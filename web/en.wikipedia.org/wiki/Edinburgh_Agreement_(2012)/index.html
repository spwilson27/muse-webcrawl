The Edinburgh Agreement is an agreement between the Government of the United Kingdom and the Scottish Government, signed on 15 October 2012 at St Andrew's House, Edinburgh, which provided the legal basis for a referendum on Scottish independence. It was signed by Prime Minister David Cameron and First Minister Alex Salmond, together with Secretary of State for Scotland Michael Moore and Deputy First Minister Nicola Sturgeon.

Background

The Scottish National Party won an outright majority in the 2011 Scottish Parliament election on a manifesto promising an independence referendum. Constitutional matters, including referendums on the Union, are reserved to Westminster, so without agreement any referendum organised by Holyrood risked legal challenge. Negotiations through 2012 settled how the referendum would be authorised and run.

Terms

The agreement, accompanied by a memorandum of understanding, provided for an Order in Council under section 30 of the Scotland Act 1998 temporarily devolving power to the Scottish Parliament to legislate for a single-question referendum. Key points included:

  • The referendum would be held before the end of 2014.
  • There would be a single question on independence — excluding a second question on further devolution ("devo-max"), which the Scottish Government had floated.
  • The franchise could be extended to 16- and 17-year-olds, as the Scottish Government wished.
  • The referendum would be regulated by the Electoral Commission in the usual way, and both governments committed to respect the result.

Outcome

The Scottish Independence Referendum Act 2013 followed, and the referendum was held on 18 September 2014. On a turnout of 84.6 percent, Scotland voted 55.3 percent to 44.7 percent against independence. The Edinburgh Agreement is frequently cited as a model of an agreed, lawful secession referendum between two governments.
