Wesleyan theology

Wesleyan theology, also known as Wesleyan–Arminian theology, is a theological tradition within Protestant Christianity based on the ministry and writings of John Wesley (1703–1791) and his brother Charles Wesley. It underlies Methodism and the Holiness and Pentecostal movements that grew from it.

Distinctive doctrines

Wesleyan theology affirms Arminian soteriology: salvation is offered to all by prevenient grace, which restores human free will and enables a response to God, contrary to Calvinist predestination. Key doctrines include justification by faith, the new birth, and the assurance of salvation given by the witness of the Holy Spirit. Most distinctive is the doctrine of Christian perfection or entire sanctification: Wesley taught that believers could, in this life, be perfected in love, freed from the dominion of intentional sin through a second, instantaneous work of grace received by faith.

Sources of authority

Wesley's method of theological reflection is often summarised by the "Wesleyan Quadrilateral" — Scripture, tradition, reason and experience — a term coined by Albert Outler. Scripture holds primacy, interpreted through the other three.

Influence

Wesleyan theology is professed by the United Methodist Church, the Church of the Nazarene, the Wesleyan Church, the Salvation Army and many Holiness and Pentecostal bodies worldwide.
