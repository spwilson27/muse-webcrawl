Þorrablót

Þorrablót is an Icelandic midwinter festival, associated with the month of Þorri of the old Icelandic calendar (mid-January to mid-February). It involves a feast of traditional foods collectively known as þorramatur.

History

The blót was a sacrifice in Norse paganism; the þorrablót is first mentioned in sagas such as Orvar-Odds saga. The modern festival was revived in the 19th and 20th centuries, notably by Icelandic student associations in Copenhagen and later in Reykjavík from 1958.

Food

Þorramatur includes cured and fermented delicacies such as hákarl (fermented shark), súrmatur (pickled foods in whey), singed sheep's heads (svið), blood pudding and dried fish, usually accompanied by brennivín schnapps.
