A National Insurance number (also known as a NINO or NI number) is a number used in the United Kingdom in the administration of the National Insurance system and the tax system. It is described as a quasi-identifier, unique to each person, and is used to record an individual's National Insurance contributions and entitlement to state benefits.

Allocation

Children born and resident in the United Kingdom are allocated a number automatically shortly before their 16th birthday if their parents receive Child Benefit for them. People coming from abroad to work or claim benefits in the UK must apply for a number through the Department for Work and Pensions. The number stays with the individual for life and does not change on marriage or change of name.

Format

The number consists of two prefix letters, six digits, and one suffix letter (A, B, C, or D) — for example, QQ123456C. Certain letter combinations are not used, and some prefixes (such as those beginning GB, NK, TN, and ZZ) are reserved or have been used for temporary or special purposes. Unlike an identity card number, the National Insurance number is not proof of identity or of the right to work, although employers must record it for payroll purposes.
