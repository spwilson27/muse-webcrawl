Tate & Lyle

Tate & Lyle PLC is a British-headquartered multinational food-ingredients company, listed on the London Stock Exchange. It was formed in 1921 from the merger of two sugar refining businesses — Henry Tate & Sons and Abram Lyle & Sons — and for much of the 20th century was one of the world's largest sugar companies. It has since transformed into a provider of food and beverage ingredients and solutions, specializing in sweeteners, texturants, and fortification.

History

Henry Tate began refining sugar in Liverpool in the 1850s and adopted the sugar cube; Abram Lyle's refinery in Plaistow, London, produced golden syrup from 1883, sold in tins decorated with the company's trademark of a lion and bees, taken from the biblical story of Samson — a design among the world's oldest unchanged brand packagings, recognized by Guinness World Records as Britain's oldest brand. After the merger, Tate & Lyle dominated British sugar refining, with refineries at Silvertown in London's East End.

Following the UK's entry into the European Economic Community and changes in sugar quotas, the company diversified into starches, sweeteners, and industrial ingredients. In 2010 it sold its sugar refining businesses, including the right to the Tate & Lyle brand on retail sugar, to American Sugar Refining. In the 2020s, the company combined with US food-ingredients business CP Kelco, strengthening its position in speciality ingredients.

Products

The company's portfolio includes the high-intensity sweetener sucralose (marketed as SPLENDA Sucralose), soluble fibres, plant proteins, starches, and stabiliser systems used by food and beverage manufacturers to reduce sugar, add fibre, and improve texture.
