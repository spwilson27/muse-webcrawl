Unification of Saudi Arabia

The unification of Saudi Arabia was a military and political campaign from 1902 to 1932 by which Abdulaziz ibn Saud and his House of Saud conquered the disparate regions of the Arabian Peninsula — Najd, Hejaz, Al-Ahsa, Jabal Shammar, Asir and others — and united them into the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, formally proclaimed on 23 September 1932. The campaign restored Saudi rule, which had been exercised by the earlier Emirate of Diriyah (1744–1818) and Emirate of Nejd (1824–1891).

Stages in the unification of Saudi Arabia

Course of the campaign

In January 1902 Abdulaziz, in exile in Kuwait, recaptured Riyadh from the Rashidi garrison in a daring night attack. Over the following decade he consolidated Najd and in 1913 took Al-Ahsa from the Ottomans. The long war with the Rashidis of Jabal Shammar ended with the fall of Ha'il in 1921. Abdulaziz's forces, including the Ikhwan tribal levies, then turned on the Hashemite Kingdom of Hejaz: Taif and Mecca fell in 1924 and Medina and Jeddah in 1925, ending Hashemite rule. In 1926 Abdulaziz was proclaimed King of Hejaz, and in 1927, by the Treaty of Jeddah, Britain recognised his sovereignty over Najd and Hejaz.

Aftermath

The Ikhwan, who opposed Abdulaziz's modernisation and his restraint toward Britain, revolted in 1927 and were defeated at the Battle of Sabilla in 1929. The southern regions of Asir and Najran were incorporated by 1934, following a brief war with Yemen. A royal decree of 18 September 1932 renamed the dual kingdom the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia, effective 23 September — now celebrated as Saudi National Day.
