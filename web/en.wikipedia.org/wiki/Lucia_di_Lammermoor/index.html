Lucia di Lammermoor

Lucia di Lammermoor is a dramma tragico (tragic opera) in three acts by Gaetano Donizetti, with an Italian libretto by Salvadore Cammarano, based on Sir Walter Scott's 1819 novel The Bride of Lammermoor. It premiered on 26 September 1835 at the Teatro di San Carlo in Naples and is one of the leading works of the bel canto repertoire.

Synopsis

In late 17th-century Scotland, Lucia Ashton is in love with Edgardo di Ravenswood, the enemy of her family. Her brother Enrico, whose fortunes are failing, schemes to marry her to the wealthy Lord Arturo Bucklaw, forging a letter to convince her that Edgardo has been unfaithful. Forced into the marriage, Lucia signs the contract just as Edgardo bursts in and curses her.

On her wedding night Lucia, driven mad, stabs Arturo to death and appears before the wedding guests in her blood-stained dress, singing the famous "Mad Scene" (Il dolce suono), one of the most celebrated coloratura showpieces in opera. She dies, and Edgardo, learning of her death, kills himself to be united with her in heaven.

Music

The opera is noted for the sextet Chi mi frena in tal momento, the Mad Scene with its extended cadenza traditionally accompanied by flute (originally written for glass harmonica), and Edgardo's final aria Tu che a Dio spiegasti l'ali. The role of Lucia became a signature part for sopranos such as Maria Callas and Joan Sutherland.
