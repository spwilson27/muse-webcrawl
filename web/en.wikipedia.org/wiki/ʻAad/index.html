ʻAad

ʿAad (Arabic: عاد, ʿĀd) was an ancient Arab tribe mentioned in the Quran and in Islamic tradition. The people of ʿAad were said to have lived in southern Arabia, in a land of sand dunes often placed in the region of al-Ahqaf (in present-day Oman and eastern Yemen), and to have built the fabled city of Iram of the Pillars. According to the Quran, God sent them the prophet Hud, whom they rejected, and the tribe was destroyed by a violent windstorm.

In the Quran

ʿAad is one of the most frequently cited "destroyed nations" in the Quran, often paired with the later tribe of Thamud. The Quran describes the ʿAad as powerful and tall in stature, builders of monuments and fortresses, who grew arrogant and worshipped idols. When their prophet Hud warned them, they mocked him; a savage wind (described in Surah Al-Haqqa as lasting seven nights and eight days) annihilated them, leaving only their dwellings standing. Surah Al-Fajr links them with "Iram of the Pillars, the like of which was never created in the lands."

Tradition and scholarship

Post-Quranic genealogists made ʿAad descendants of Aram, son of Shem, and elaborated the story of Iram, whose splendour later entered folklore and modern fiction. Archaeological candidates for their homeland include the frankincense lands of Dhofar; the excavated site of Shisr in Oman, popularly identified with the lost city of Ubar/Iram in the 1990s, has been proposed but the identification remains speculative. Some scholars connect the ʿAad with early South Arabian peoples attested in inscriptions, though a direct historical identification is unresolved.
