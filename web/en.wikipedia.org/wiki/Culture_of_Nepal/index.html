Culture of Nepal

Kathmandu's Durbar Square, heart of Newar culture and festival life.

The culture of Nepal is a mosaic of more than 120 ethnic groups and as many languages, shaped by Hinduism and Buddhism, which have coexisted and interwoven for centuries in the Himalayan foothills. Nepal is the birthplace of the Buddha at Lumbini and home to Kathmandu Valley, a UNESCO World Heritage landscape of temples and palaces.

Religion and festivals

About four-fifths of Nepalis are Hindu and about a tenth Buddhist, with Kirat, Muslim, Christian and other minorities. Festivals fill the calendar: Dashain and Tihar are the great national celebrations, while Indra Jatra, Gai Jatra, Holi and the chariot festival of Rato Machhindranath animate the Kathmandu Valley. The Kumari, a living goddess chosen from the Newar community, is a unique institution of the valley.

Arts and architecture

Newar craftsmen created the multi-tiered pagoda style that spread across Asia, as well as masterful woodcarving, bronze casting and paubha painting. Thangka painting, pottery, and the music of the sarangi and madal remain living traditions. Nepal's national instrument ensemble and folk dances vary by region and caste.

Cuisine and customs

The staple meal is dal bhat — lentil soup with rice, vegetables and pickles — eaten twice daily. Momos (dumplings), sel roti, gundruk and Newari dishes such as yomari and chhoyla are beloved. Greetings are made with palms joined and the word "Namaste," and guests are treated as gods (atithi devo bhava).
