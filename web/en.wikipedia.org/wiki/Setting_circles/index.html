Setting circles

Setting circles are graduated measurement scales on the axes of a telescope mount, used to point the telescope at an astronomical object by its coordinates. On an equatorial mount there are two circles: one for right ascension (or hour angle) on the polar axis and one for declination on the declination axis. The observer reads the object's coordinates from a catalogue, moves the telescope until the circles read those values, and the object should then be in or near the field of view.

Use

Manual setting circles require a well-aligned mount and, for the right ascension circle, a known reference: the observer first points at a bright star with known coordinates and sets (or offsets) the circles accordingly before slewing to the target. Analogue circles on small amateur telescopes are often too small to be very precise, so the technique works best for locating bright objects and as a check against star hopping.

Digital setting circles

Digital setting circles (DSCs) replace the graduated scales with rotary encoders on both axes connected to a small computer. After alignment on one or two known stars, the unit displays the angular distance and direction to move the telescope to reach a selected object from an internal database, effectively giving a manual "push-to" mount the pointing help of a GoTo system without motors.
