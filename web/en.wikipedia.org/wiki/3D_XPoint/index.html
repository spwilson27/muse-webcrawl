3D XPoint

3D XPoint (pronounced "three-dee cross point") is a non-volatile memory (NVM) technology developed jointly by Intel and Micron Technology. Announced in July 2015, it was marketed as the first new mainstream memory category since the introduction of NAND flash in 1989, promising far lower latency and much higher endurance than NAND while being slower and more expensive than DRAM.

Technology

The memory stores data in a three-dimensional grid of cells at the intersections ("cross points") of word and bit lines. Each cell's resistance changes in bulk — a property associated with phase-change behavior, though Intel never fully disclosed the mechanism — and cells are selected by separate selector devices, allowing bit-level addressing without a transistor per cell.

Products and demise

Intel sold products under the Optane brand, including SSDs and persistent-memory DIMMs; Micron briefly sold the QuantX-based X100 SSD. Micron exited the partnership and sold its Lehi, Utah fab in 2021, and Intel wound down the Optane business in 2022, taking a write-down of over half a billion dollars, ending the technology's commercial run.
