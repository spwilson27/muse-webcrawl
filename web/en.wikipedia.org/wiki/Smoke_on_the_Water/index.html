Smoke on the Water

"Smoke on the Water" is a song by the English rock band Deep Purple. It was released on their 1972 album Machine Head and quickly became the band's signature song, famous for its central four-note blues-scale guitar riff played by Ritchie Blackmore.

Background

The lyrics tell the true story of a fire at the Montreux Casino in Switzerland on 4 December 1971. Deep Purple had travelled to Montreux to record Machine Head using the Rolling Stones Mobile Studio, rented and parked outside the casino. During a concert by Frank Zappa and the Mothers of Invention, an audience member fired a flare gun toward the ceiling, and the venue burned to the ground. The band watched the smoke drift over Lake Geneva from their hotel — "smoke on the water, fire in the sky."

The band relocated and eventually recorded the album in the corridors and rooms of the empty Grand Hôtel de Territet. Bassist Roger Glover came up with the title "Smoke on the Water", and the song was assembled from a riff Blackmore had developed.

Release and reception

Though not initially released as a single, the song was issued as one in 1973 and became a hit, reaching No. 4 on the US Billboard Hot 100. Its opening riff is widely regarded as one of the most recognisable in rock music and is a rite of passage for beginner guitarists. In 2004, the song was ranked number 434 on Rolling Stone magazine's list of the 500 Greatest Songs of All Time.

Legacy

A memorial in Montreux commemorates the song and the casino fire. Deep Purple have performed it at virtually every concert since its release, and it remains a staple of classic rock radio.
