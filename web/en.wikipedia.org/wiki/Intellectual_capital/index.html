Intellectual capital

Intellectual capital is the set of intangible assets of an organization—knowledge, skills, relationships, processes, and intellectual property—that contribute to its value and competitive advantage but are not fully captured in traditional financial statements.

Components

  • Human capital – the knowledge, skills, experience, and creativity of employees.
  • Structural (organizational) capital – codified knowledge that remains when employees leave: databases, processes, patents, trademarks, and culture.
  • Relational (customer) capital – the value of relationships with customers, suppliers, partners, and other external stakeholders, including brand reputation.

Measurement and reporting

Interest in intellectual capital grew in the 1990s as the gap widened between firms' market values and book values. Frameworks such as the Skandia Navigator, Karl-Erik Sveiby's Intangible Assets Monitor, and the balanced scorecard of Kaplan and Norton attempted to identify and report intellectual capital indicators. Integrated reporting frameworks later incorporated intellectual capital as one of several "capitals" an organization uses and affects.

Management

Knowledge management practices—communities of practice, mentoring, documentation, and innovation processes—are used to create, share, and protect intellectual capital. Critics note the difficulty of measuring intangibles reliably and the risk that reported indicators are treated as precise values.
