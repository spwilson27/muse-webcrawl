Torque

Common symbolsτ, M
SI unitNewton-metre (N·m)
DimensionForce × distance

In physics and mechanics, torque is the rotational analogue of linear force. It is also referred to as the moment of force (or simply moment). It describes the rate of change of angular momentum of an isolated body, or equivalently the tendency of a force to rotate an object about an axis. Just as a linear force is a push or a pull applied to a body, a torque can be thought of as a twist applied to an object.

Definition

Torque is defined as the cross product of the position vector of the point where the force is applied and the force vector: τ = r × F. Its magnitude is τ = rF sin θ, where r is the length of the lever arm and θ the angle between the force vector and the lever arm. The direction of the torque vector follows the right-hand rule, along the axis of rotation.

Applications

The principle of the lever, known since antiquity, rests on torque: a small force at a long distance from a fulcrum produces the same torque as a large force nearer to it. In engineering, torque specifications govern the tightening of threaded fasteners, and engine output is commonly quoted as torque (for example in pound-feet) alongside power, since power is the product of torque and angular velocity. Torque converters, wrenches and dynamometers all exploit or measure the quantity.
