FICON

FICON links mainframes to storage over fibre

FICON (Fibre Connection) is the IBM proprietary name for the ANSI FC-SB-3 Single-Byte Command Code Sets-3 Mapping Protocol, a mapping layer that carries IBM channel I/O protocols over Fibre Channel. Introduced in 1998 as the successor to the copper-based ESCON, FICON connects IBM mainframes to storage devices, tape systems, and printers.

Performance

Successive generations of FICON increased link speeds from 100 MB/s to 200, 400, and 800 MB/s and beyond, and extended distances to tens of kilometres using optical fibre and DWDM. High Performance FICON (HPF) reduced protocol overhead for large transfers.

Use in channel I/O

Within the channel I/O architecture, FICON channels execute channel programs of command words to control devices such as the IBM DS8000 storage series, allowing a single mainframe to manage enormous numbers of volumes with high throughput.
