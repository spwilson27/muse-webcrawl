IBM Z

An IBM Z mainframe

IBM Z is a family name for IBM's mainframe computers, the direct descendants of the System/360 line announced in 1964. The "Z" stands for "zero downtime", reflecting the platform's emphasis on extreme reliability and availability. Recent generations include the z13, z14, z15, and z16 systems.

Characteristics

IBM Z systems are optimized for very high-volume transaction processing and I/O throughput. A channel subsystem, descended from System/360 channel I/O, offloads input/output work to dedicated processors connected over FICON and other fabrics, letting the central processors focus on transactions. The machines support extensive virtualization through logical partitions (LPARs) and the z/VM hypervisor, and run operating systems such as z/OS, z/VM, and Linux on IBM Z.

Market role

Although mainframes are a niche of the server market, IBM Z systems process a large share of the world's banking, insurance, and airline reservation transactions, valued for security features such as pervasive encryption and for decades of backward compatibility.
