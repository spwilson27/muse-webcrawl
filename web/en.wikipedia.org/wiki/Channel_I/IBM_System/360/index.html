Channel architecture of the IBM System/360

The IBM System/360, announced in 1964, generalized the concept of the I/O channel: a self-contained processor, sharing the machine's main storage, that executes I/O programs independently of the central processing unit. Channels connected to control units and devices through a standard I/O interface, so that the same peripherals could be attached across the whole range of System/360 models. The architecture defined on the System/360 remains the basis of input/output on IBM mainframes today.

Channels connect the System/360 CPU to its control units and devices

Channel types

TypeBehaviourTypical devices
Selector channelTransfers data with one device at a time in burst mode, monopolizing the channel for the duration of a block transferMagnetic tape drives, disk storage
Byte multiplexor channelInterleaves bytes from many slow devices, so dozens of low-speed devices share one channelCard readers, printers, terminals
Block multiplexor channelIntroduced later; interleaves blocks rather than bytes and can disconnect during device delays such as disk seeksDisk and tape storage

Channel programs

The CPU starts an I/O operation with the privileged START I/O instruction, pointing to a channel address word (CAW) that locates the first channel command word (CCW). The channel fetches and executes the CCW chain itself: each CCW commands an operation such as READ, WRITE, CONTROL (for example, rewind a tape or seek a disk), or SENSE, and specifies the main-storage area and byte count for the transfer. Command chaining links successive operations into one channel program; data chaining lets one operation scatter data across discontiguous storage areas. When the program ends, the channel causes an I/O interruption and stores a channel status word (CSW) reporting device and channel status.

Legacy

Later systems — System/370, System/390, and IBM Z — kept the programming model while replacing the physical implementation: the channel subsystem, subchannels, and fibre-optic FICON channels all descend directly from the System/360 design.
