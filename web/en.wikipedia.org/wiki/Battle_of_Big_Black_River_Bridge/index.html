Battle of Big Black River Bridge

The Battle of Big Black River Bridge was fought on 17 May 1863, near the Big Black River in Mississippi, as part of the Vicksburg Campaign of the American Civil War. Union forces under Major General Ulysses S. Grant routed the Confederate rear guard defending the river crossings, capturing some 1,700 prisoners and clearing the way to Vicksburg.

The Union assault on the Big Black River bridgehead, 17 May 1863.

Background

After his defeat at the Battle of Champion Hill on 16 May, Confederate Lieutenant General John C. Pemberton retreated west toward Vicksburg. He ordered Brigadier General John S. Bowen to hold the crossings of the Big Black River with about 5,000 men, buying time for the main army to cross and for another division, under William W. Loring, to rejoin — Loring's men were cut off and never returned.

The battle

On the morning of 17 May, Union corps under John A. McClernand advanced against Bowen's fortified bridgehead on the east bank. After a brief artillery preparation, Brigadier General Michael K. Lawler's brigade charged across an open meadow and overran the Confederate works in minutes. The defenders fled across the railroad bridge and a steamer serving as a bridge, both of which they set on fire to prevent pursuit — trapping much of their own force on the east bank, where some 1,700 men and 18 cannon were captured.

Aftermath

Grant's troops quickly bridged the river by other means and closed on Vicksburg, beginning the siege on 18 May. The defeat at Big Black River, following Champion Hill, left Pemberton's army demoralised and sealed inside the city's defences.
