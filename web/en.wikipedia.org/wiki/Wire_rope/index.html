Wire rope

Wire rope is a type of cable composed of several strands of metal wire twisted into a helix, typically around a central core. Wire ropes are used dynamically for lifting and hoisting in cranes and elevators, and for support in suspension bridges, cable-stayed structures and aerial tramways.

Construction

Individual wires are helically twisted into a strand, and strands are in turn laid around a core, which may be a fibre core (FC), an independent wire rope core (IWRC) or a wire strand core. Ropes are classified by the number of strands and wires per strand — for example, 6×36 classification — and by the direction and type of lay (regular lay or lang lay).

History

Modern wire rope was developed in the 1830s by the German mining engineer Wilhelm Albert for use in mines in the Harz mountains, replacing chains and hemp ropes. The invention rapidly spread to mining, cable railways and bridge building.

Applications and safety

Wire ropes require inspection for broken wires, corrosion and deformation, and are discarded according to standardised criteria. Their strength depends on material, construction and diameter; termination efficiency (splices, sockets and clips) also affects the safe working load.
