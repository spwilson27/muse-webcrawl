Prototyping

Prototyping
PurposeTest concepts, validate requirements, reduce design risk
FormsPaper/sketch, wireframe, mock-up, working model, minimum viable product
MethodsRapid prototyping / 3D printing, breadboarding, software prototyping

Prototyping is the process of building an early sample, model or release of a product to test a concept or process. A prototype is typically incomplete and inexpensive compared with the final product, and is used by designers, engineers and users to explore ideas, uncover misunderstandings about requirements, evaluate ergonomics and gather feedback before committing to full production.

Types and fidelity

Prototypes range from low-fidelity representations — hand-drawn sketches and paper prototypes of screens — to high-fidelity interactive mock-ups and functional engineering models. "Throwaway" (rapid) prototyping produces a model that will be discarded once its lessons are learned; evolutionary prototyping refines the prototype itself into the final system. In software, a proof of concept and a minimum viable product are related forms used to test feasibility and market demand.

In engineering and manufacturing

Physical prototypes allow fit, strength, manufacturing and user tests. Traditional model-making has been transformed by rapid prototyping technologies — additive manufacturing (3D printing), CNC machining and laser cutting — which turn CAD models into parts in hours. Prototypes for vehicles and buildings include clay models, wind-tunnel models and digital twins.

In software and interaction design

Software prototyping ranges from wireframes and clickable screen flows to working implementations of core features. It supports user-centred design and usability testing, and underpins iterative and agile development, in which each iteration functions as a refined prototype of the product.

Advantages and risks

Prototyping finds errors early, when changes are cheap, and engages stakeholders with something concrete. Risks include users treating a polished prototype as nearly finished, scope creep, and throwaway code quietly migrating into production systems.
