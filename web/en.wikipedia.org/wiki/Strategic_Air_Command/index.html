Strategic Air Command

Strategic Air Command (SAC)Active: 1946–1992
Country: United States
Branch: United States Air Force
Headquarters: Offutt Air Force Base, Nebraska
Motto: "Peace Is Our Profession"

Strategic Air Command (SAC) was a major command of the United States Air Force and the successor to the wartime Continental Air Forces. From 1946 to 1992 it controlled the land-based strategic bomber force and (for most of its life) the intercontinental ballistic missile force, forming two legs of the United States nuclear triad. Its motto, adopted under General Curtis LeMay, was "Peace Is Our Profession."

History

SAC was established on 21 March 1946 and headquartered from 1948 at Offutt Air Force Base, Nebraska. Under LeMay (1948–1957) it became a highly disciplined, always-ready nuclear deterrent force, operating bombers such as the B-36, B-47, B-52, and B-58, later the FB-111 and B-1B, along with KC-97 and KC-135 tankers and Minuteman and Titan ICBMs. During the Cold War SAC kept bombers on ground and airborne alert, and reconnaissance aircraft such as the U-2 and SR-71 flew under its direction. It also conducted conventional bombing operations in Korea and Vietnam, including the B-52 Arc Light missions and Operation Linebacker II.

Disestablishment

With the end of the Cold War, SAC was disestablished on 1 June 1992. Its bomber and tanker assets passed largely to the new Air Combat Command, its missiles to Air Force Space Command, and its strategic deterrence mission to the new United States Strategic Command, also headquartered at Offutt.
