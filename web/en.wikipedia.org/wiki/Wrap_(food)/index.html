Wrap (food)

A wrap is a food dish made with a soft flatbread rolled around a filling. The usual flatbreads are wheat tortillas, lavash, or pita; the filling may include cold sliced meat, poultry, or fish, lettuce, diced tomato, guacamole, sautéed mushrooms, bacon, grilled onions, cheese, and a sauce or spread such as hummus. Wraps containing a large quantity of filling wrapped in a large flour tortilla, sometimes grilled, are closely related to the burrito.

History

Rolled flatbread dishes are ancient and widespread: Mexican burritos, Armenian and Turkish dürüm, Greek gyros, Middle Eastern shawarma rolls, and Indian kathi rolls all wrap fillings in bread. The modern American "wrap" as a named menu item is generally traced to the 1980s and early 1990s, when it spread through delicatessens and cafés in California and New York as a lighter alternative to the sandwich; the Bobby Valentine sports bar in Stamford, Connecticut, has claimed to have invented the wrap sandwich in 1982 after a toaster broke down during lunch service. By the late 1990s wraps had become a standard fast-casual offering across the United States and Britain, marketed as a fresher and healthier option, though large wraps can contain as many calories as a conventional sandwich.

Preparation

The flatbread is often warmed to make it pliable, spread with sauce, layered with fillings, and rolled tightly — the sides folded in burrito-style — then sliced diagonally. Wraps may be served cold, toasted in a press, or grilled.
