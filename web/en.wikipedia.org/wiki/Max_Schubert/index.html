Max Schubert

Max Schubert AM (9 February 1915 – 6 March 1994) was an Australian winemaker, the long-serving chief winemaker of Penfolds and the creator of Grange Hermitage (later Penfolds Grange), widely regarded as Australia's most famous wine.

Early life and career

Schubert was born at Moculta in the Barossa Valley, South Australia, of German settler stock. He joined Penfolds at the Magill winery near Adelaide as a messenger boy in 1931, trained in the laboratory, and rose through the company, becoming chief winemaker in 1948.

Grange

After a 1950 study trip to Bordeaux, where he observed the cellaring potential of great Cabernet-based wines and the use of new small oak barrels, Schubert resolved to make an Australian red capable of ageing for twenty years or more. Using Shiraz (then called Hermitage) from Magill and Morphett Vale, controlled fermentation, and maturation in new American oak hogsheads, he produced the experimental 1951 Grange. Initially criticised, and ordered discontinued in 1957 after poor early tastings and management doubts, Schubert continued making Grange in secret for several vintages. Once the wines matured and began winning gold medals, the decision was reversed, and Grange went on to become an icon — the 1955 vintage was later listed as a heritage icon of South Australia, and Grange is routinely ranked among the world's great wines.

Legacy

Schubert also developed much of the Penfolds "Bin" range of wines. He retired as chief winemaker in 1975 and was made a Member of the Order of Australia in 1984. His insistence on multi-vineyard blending, rigorous selection and long-term cellaring reshaped Australian fine winemaking.
