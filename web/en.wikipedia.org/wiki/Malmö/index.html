Malmö (Swedish: [ˈmâlmøː]) is the largest city in the Swedish county of Scania (Skåne), the third-largest city in Sweden, and the sixth-largest in Scandinavia. It is situated on the Öresund strait, directly across from Copenhagen, Denmark, to which it is connected by the Öresund Bridge, opened in 2000.

History

Malmö was founded in the 13th century and grew wealthy as a port for the herring trade of the Hanseatic League. For centuries it belonged to Denmark; the city passed to Sweden under the Treaty of Roskilde in 1658. In the 19th century Malmö industrialized, with shipbuilding at the Kockums yard a major employer, and in the late 20th century it reinvented itself as a center of education, biotechnology, and sustainable urban design.

Landmarks

The city's best-known building is the Turning Torso (2005), a twisting residential skyscraper designed by Santiago Calatrava that was, at completion, the tallest building in Scandinavia. Older landmarks include Malmö Castle (Malmöhus), St. Peter's Church (Sankt Petri kyrka), and the old town square Stortorget with its city hall. The Western Harbour district (Västra hamnen) is noted internationally for sustainable housing developments such as Bo01.

Transport

Malmö Central Station is a hub for trains to Stockholm, Gothenburg, and Copenhagen; the City Tunnel, opened in 2010, carries trains under the city center. The Öresund Bridge carries both road and rail traffic to Denmark.
