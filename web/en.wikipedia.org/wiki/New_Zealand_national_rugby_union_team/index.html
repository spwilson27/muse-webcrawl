The New Zealand national rugby union team, commonly known as the All Blacks, represents New Zealand in men's international rugby union. The team is administered by New Zealand Rugby and is widely regarded as one of the most successful national teams in any sport, having won the Rugby World Cup three times (1987, 2011 and 2015) and holding a winning record against every other national team.

The All Blacks compete annually in The Rugby Championship against Argentina, Australia and South Africa, and contest the Bledisloe Cup with Australia. They are famous for their all-black playing strip, the silver fern emblem, and the haka, a Māori challenge performed before matches.

History

Rugby was introduced to New Zealand by Charles Monro in 1870. The first New Zealand representative team toured New South Wales in 1884, and the celebrated "Originals" team of 1905–06 toured the British Isles, France and North America, losing only once — to Wales — and popularising the All Blacks name. The name is generally held to derive from the team's all-black uniform, though a popular legend attributes it to a newspaper misprint of "all backs".

New Zealand won the inaugural Rugby World Cup on home soil in 1987 and repeated the triumph in 2011, again at home, before becoming the first team to retain the trophy in 2015 in England. Legendary players include Colin Meads, Jonah Lomu, Richie McCaw — who captained the side to two World Cup titles — and Dan Carter, the highest points scorer in test rugby history.

Haka

Before most test matches the team performs a haka, most famously "Ka Mate", composed by the Ngāti Toa chief Te Rauparaha around 1820. Since 2005 the team has also occasionally performed "Kapa o Pango", written specifically for the All Blacks.

Records

  • Three Rugby World Cup titles: 1987, 2011, 2015.
  • First team to reach 500 test victories.
  • Record winning streak of 18 consecutive test wins (shared).
