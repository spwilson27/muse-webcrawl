Indexation

Indexation is an economic mechanism by which wages, prices, pensions, tax brackets, interest rates or other monetary values are automatically adjusted in line with a price index — most commonly a consumer price index — so as to maintain their real value in the face of inflation.

Uses

Governments index social security benefits, public pensions and income tax thresholds in many countries; landlords and employers may index rents and wages; and inflation-indexed bonds, such as US Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) and UK index-linked gilts, adjust principal with inflation. Historically, some high-inflation economies, notably Brazil before the 1994 Plano Real, indexed nearly all contracts.

Debate

Supporters argue indexation protects incomes and savings from arbitrary erosion. Critics argue it can entrench inflation by building past price rises automatically into future costs ("inertial inflation") and reduce the effectiveness of monetary policy.
