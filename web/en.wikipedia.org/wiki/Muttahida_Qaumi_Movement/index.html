Muttahida Qaumi Movement (MQM)

Founded: 1984 (as Muhajir Qaumi Movement)

Founder: Altaf Hussain

Headquarters: Karachi, Pakistan

Base: Urban Sindh, especially Karachi and Hyderabad

An MQM rally in Karachi, the party's stronghold.

The Muttahida Qaumi Movement (MQM; Urdu: متحدہ قومی موومنٹ, "United National Movement") is a political party in Pakistan, historically rooted in Karachi and Hyderabad in Sindh province. Founded in 1984 as the Muhajir Qaumi Movement by Altaf Hussain, it championed the rights of the Muhajirs — Urdu-speaking migrants who settled in urban Sindh after the Partition of India in 1947 — and for decades dominated Karachi's politics.

History

The party grew out of the All Pakistan Muhajir Students Organisation (APMSO), founded by Altaf Hussain in 1978 at the University of Karachi. The MQM swept urban Sindh seats in the 1988 general election and became a coalition partner in national governments several times, alternating alliances with the Pakistan Peoples Party and the Pakistan Muslim League. In 1997 it changed its name from "Muhajir" to "Muttahida" (United), aiming to broaden its appeal beyond a single ethnic community.

The party's history is controversial: during the 1990s Karachi suffered waves of political and ethnic violence, and a military-led "clean-up" operation targeted the MQM in 1992. Altaf Hussain lived in self-exile in London from 1992 onwards, leading the party from abroad. Journalists and human-rights organisations have accused the MQM of extortion, targeted killings and maintaining militant wings, charges the party has denied.

Split and recent years

In August 2016, after a speech by Hussain that was widely condemned in Pakistan, the party's Pakistan-based leadership under Farooq Sattar publicly distanced itself from him, and the party split into "MQM-Pakistan" and a London-based faction. A further breakaway, the Pak Sarzameen Party (later merged back), was formed by former Karachi mayor Mustafa Kamal. The MQM-P performed poorly in the 2018 general election as the Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf surged in Karachi, but it remained part of governing coalitions and regained ground in later local elections.
