Fines herbes

Fines herbes (French pronunciation: [fin zɛʁb]) is a classic combination of finely chopped fresh herbs in French cuisine. The principal herbs are parsley, chives, tarragon and chervil, sometimes extended with marjoram, cress, lemon balm or burnet.

OriginFrance
Core herbsParsley, chives, tarragon, chervil
UsesOmelettes, salads, sauces, poultry, fish

Use

Unlike the robust bouquet garni, which is simmered, fines herbes are delicate and added near the end of cooking or raw, so their flavour is not lost. They are essential to omelette aux fines herbes, herb salads, beurre aux fines herbes and light sauces for chicken and fish.

Composition and variations

Auguste Escoffier codified the blend as parsley, chervil, tarragon and chives in roughly equal parts. Dried versions exist but are considered poor substitutes because chervil and tarragon lose character. The blend is one of the pillars of French herb cookery alongside herbes de Provence — though the latter uses dried, stronger Mediterranean herbs and is quite different in character.
