West Coast Main Line

The West Coast Main Line (WCML) is one of the most important railway corridors in the United Kingdom, connecting London Euston with the West Midlands, North West England, North Wales and Scotland. It is one of the busiest mixed-traffic railways in Europe, carrying inter-city passenger trains, regional services, suburban trains and heavy freight over some 399 miles (642 km) between Euston and Glasgow Central.

History

The route was assembled from pioneering companies: the London and Birmingham Railway (1838), the Grand Junction Railway, and the Lancaster and Carlisle Railway over Shap Fell, reaching Glasgow via the Caledonian Railway. The LMS ran its famous Coronation Scot expresses in the 1930s. British Rail electrified the line in stages between 1959 and 1974, but the Advanced Passenger Train tilting project was abandoned in the 1980s.

Modernisation

The West Coast Route Modernisation (1998–2008) rebuilt much of the line, eventually costing around £9 billion, and enabled 125 mph running by Class 390 Pendolino tilting trains for Virgin Trains and, from 2019, Avanti West Coast. Branches serve Liverpool, Manchester, Birmingham, Edinburgh and Holyhead (for ferries to Ireland). Chronic congestion on the southern section was a principal justification for the HS2 project. Landmarks include the Trent Valley line, Rugby station, and the climb to Shap and Beattock summits.
