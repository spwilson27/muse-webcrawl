Felidae

Felidae is the family of mammals in the order Carnivora colloquially referred to as cats. A member of this family is also called a felid. The term "cat" refers both to felids in general and specifically to the domestic cat (Felis catus).

The 41 known Felidae species exhibit great diversity in fur patterns and sizes, from the small rusty-spotted cat (Prionailurus rubiginosus) at about 1 kg to the tiger (Panthera tigris) at over 300 kg. All species are obligate carnivores with sharp retractable claws (with the exception of the cheetah), specialised teeth, and powerful senses adapted to hunting, often at night.

Evolution

The first cats are thought to have emerged during the Oligocene, about 25 million years ago, with the genera Proailurus and Pseudaelurus. Modern felids are divided into two subfamilies: Pantherinae, containing the big cats (Panthera and Neofelis), and Felinae, containing all other cats including the cheetah, lynxes, ocelots and the domestic cat. DNA studies suggest the family's living lineages diverged from a common ancestor in Asia roughly 10–15 million years ago.

Characteristics

Felids have soft bodies, flexible limbs, and rounded heads with short faces. They are digitigrade, walking on their toes, and possess a highly developed sense of hearing and smell. The tongue is covered with sharp, backward-facing papillae used for grooming and rasping flesh from bone. Most species are solitary hunters, though the lion is famously social, living in prides.

Classification

Subfamily Pantherinae includes the tiger, lion, jaguar, leopard, snow leopard and clouded leopards. Subfamily Felinae includes the cheetah, puma, lynxes, ocelot, serval, caracal, fishing cat, and the small wild cats of the genus Felis, from which the domestic cat descends.

Conservation

Many felid species are threatened by habitat loss and poaching; the tiger, snow leopard and Iberian lynx are among the most endangered, though the Iberian lynx has recovered significantly through conservation breeding.
