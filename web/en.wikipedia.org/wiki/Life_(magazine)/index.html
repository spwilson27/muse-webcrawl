Life (magazine)

Life
Type: Magazine
First issue: 23 November 1936 (as a photojournalism weekly)
Publisher: Time Inc. / Dotdash Meredith
Known for: Photojournalism

Life was an American magazine published as a weekly from 1936 to 1972, with later revivals as a monthly and as newspaper supplements. Famous for its photojournalism, it defined the visual record of the mid-twentieth century.

History

The name was bought from an earlier humour magazine, and publisher Henry Luce relaunched Life as a picture magazine edited by John Shaw Billings, with staff photographers including Margaret Bourke-White, Alfred Eisenstaedt, and W. Eugene Smith. Its large format, picture essays, and memorable covers — from World War II reporting to the space programme — made it one of America's most popular magazines.

Decline and legacy

Television competition and rising postal costs forced the weekly to close in 1972. It reappeared as a monthly (1978–2000) and as a weekend supplement (2004–2007). Life's photographic archive, held with Getty Images, remains one of the great collections of twentieth-century photography.
