Water harvesting

Water harvesting is the collection and storage of rainwater and runoff for later use.

Water harvesting is the accumulation and storage of rainwater and surface runoff for reuse, rather than allowing it to run off or evaporate. Harvested water may be stored for domestic use, livestock, irrigation or groundwater recharge. The practice is ancient — cisterns and runoff farms were used in the Negev desert and around the Mediterranean thousands of years ago — and remains important in arid and semi-arid regions.

Techniques

Techniques include rooftop rainwater harvesting, in which gutters divert roof runoff to tanks or cisterns; surface runoff harvesting into ponds, check dams and percolation pits; contour bunds, terraces and half-moon pits that slow runoff and increase infiltration in fields; and traditional structures such as India's stepwells, johads and Sri Lanka's tank cascades. Fog harvesting, using mesh nets to condense atmospheric moisture, is practised in some coastal mountains.

Benefits and limits

Harvesting can improve water security, recharge aquifers, reduce flooding and erosion, and support dry-season irrigation. Its limits include seasonal rainfall variability, storage costs, and water-quality concerns for drinking use, which usually requires filtration and disinfection.
