Positive-definite matrix

In linear algebra, a symmetric matrix M is positive-definite if the quadratic form xTMx is positive for every non-zero real vector x. More generally the term applies to Hermitian matrices with complex vectors, where the corresponding scalar is real and positive.

Equivalent characterizations

For a symmetric (or Hermitian) matrix, the following are equivalent: all eigenvalues are positive; all leading principal minors are positive (Sylvester's criterion); there exists a unique Cholesky decomposition M = LLT with L lower triangular with positive diagonal entries; and the bilinear form defined by M is an inner product. A matrix is positive-semidefinite if the quadratic form is non-negative for all x, equivalently all eigenvalues are non-negative.

Properties and applications

The set of positive-definite matrices forms an open convex cone; sums and positive scalar multiples of positive-definite matrices are positive-definite, as is the inverse of a positive-definite matrix. Positive-definite matrices arise as covariance matrices in statistics, Hessian matrices at local minima in optimization, stiffness matrices in finite element analysis, and kernels in machine learning (Mercer's theorem).
