Delta (uppercase Δ, lowercase δ; Greek: δέλτα, délta) is the fourth letter of the Greek alphabet. In the system of Greek numerals it has a value of 4. It was derived from the Phoenician letter dalet, and letters that derive from delta include the Latin D and the Cyrillic Д.

Uses

Uppercase delta (Δ) is widely used in mathematics and science to denote change or difference in a variable, as in Δx, and appears in the discriminant of polynomial equations and in the definition of limits. In physics and engineering it denotes differences, the Laplace operator's square-integrable relatives, and is used for particles in particle physics. Lowercase delta (δ) is used for, among other things, small changes, the Kronecker delta and Dirac delta function notations, chemical shifts in NMR spectroscopy, and partial charges in chemistry.

Other uses

The triangular shape of the uppercase letter gave its name to river deltas, such as the Nile Delta — Herodotus noted the resemblance between the letter and the triangular landform at the Nile's mouth — and to the delta wing of aircraft.
