RC circuit

An RC circuit is an electric circuit composed of resistors and capacitors. The simplest RC circuit consists of one resistor and one capacitor in series or parallel. RC circuits are used as filters, timers, and integrators/differentiators in analog electronics, and their response is governed by a first-order linear differential equation.

Time constant

The characteristic time of the circuit is the time constant τ = RC. When a step voltage V is applied to a series RC circuit, the capacitor voltage rises as V(1 − e^(−t/τ)); when discharging, it falls as V e^(−t/τ). After one time constant the voltage reaches about 63.2% of its final change.

Filters

A series RC circuit with output taken across the capacitor forms a first-order low-pass filter with cutoff frequency fc = 1/(2πRC); output across the resistor gives a high-pass filter. The roll-off of a first-order filter is 20 dB per decade. Combinations of RC stages underpin tone controls, coupling networks, and anti-aliasing filters.

Applications

RC circuits appear in timing circuits (such as the classic 555 timer's RC network), snubbers, power-supply filtering, flash photography, and as models of neuron membranes in computational neuroscience, where the membrane behaves like an RC circuit (compare the filtering analysis used for specular signal models in graphics and the timing networks of System/390 hardware).
