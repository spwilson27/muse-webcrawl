Aimé Fernand David Césaire (26 June 1913 – 17 April 2008) was a French poet, author, and politician from Martinique. He was one of the founders of the Négritude movement in Francophone literature, along with Léopold Sédar Senghor and Léon-Gontran Damas, and his works are celebrated for their surrealist imagery and fierce critique of colonialism.

Life and career

Césaire won a scholarship to study in Paris, where he attended the Lycée Louis-le-Grand and the École Normale Supérieure, and co-founded the journal L'Étudiant noir in 1934. His long poem Cahier d'un retour au pays natal ("Notebook of a Return to the Native Land", 1939), published in full in 1947, is regarded as a masterpiece of modern French poetry.

Returning to Martinique, he taught at the Lycée Schoelcher, where Frantz Fanon was among his students. In 1945 he was elected mayor of Fort-de-France and deputy to the French National Assembly, positions he held for over half a century. Initially a member of the French Communist Party, he broke with it after the Soviet invasion of Hungary, publishing his Letter to Maurice Thorez (1956) and founding the Martinican Progressive Party. He was instrumental in the 1946 law that made Martinique an overseas department of France.

Works

His prose polemic Discours sur le colonialisme (1950) remains a foundational anticolonial text. Plays include La Tragédie du roi Christophe (1963) and Une tempête (1969), a postcolonial reworking of Shakespeare's The Tempest. On his death in 2008 he was given a state funeral, and in 2011 a plaque in his honour was unveiled in the Panthéon in Paris.
