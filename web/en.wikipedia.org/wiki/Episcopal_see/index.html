Episcopal see

An episcopal see is, in a wide sense, the area of a bishop's ecclesiastical jurisdiction; the word is derived from the Latin sedes, meaning "seat". In the strict sense, it refers to the seat (throne, or cathedra) of the bishop of a diocese, and by extension the city or place where that seat is located. When a see is vacant — between the death or resignation of one bishop and the installation of a successor — it is described as sede vacante.

Usage

In the Catholic Church, each diocese is a see headed by a bishop; archdioceses are metropolitan sees with authority over an ecclesiastical province of suffragan sees. The pre-eminent see is the Holy See — the jurisdiction of the Pope as bishop of Rome — which is the central governing body of the Catholic Church and a subject of international law. Other historic sees include the patriarchates of Constantinople, Alexandria, Antioch, and Jerusalem, which together with Rome formed the ancient Pentarchy.

The term is also used in the Anglican Communion and in Eastern Orthodox and Oriental Orthodox churches. A titular see is a see that no longer functions as a residential diocese — for example, a diocese in territory lost to another religion centuries ago — whose title is conferred on auxiliary or diplomatic bishops. The movement of a see from one city to another, or its suppression and union with another, is effected by the competent church authority.
