Melisende, Queen of Jerusalem

Queen of Jerusalem

Reign: 1131 – 1153 (sole/ruling with Baldwin III until 1161)

Born: c. 1105, Hierges

Died: 11 September 1161, Jerusalem

Spouse: Fulk V of Anjou

Issue: Baldwin III, Amalric I

Father: Baldwin II of Jerusalem; Mother: Morphia of Melitene

Melisende (c. 1105 – 11 September 1161) was Queen of Jerusalem from 1131 to 1153, the eldest daughter of King Baldwin II and the Armenian princess Morphia of Melitene. She was the first woman to inherit and rule the crusader Kingdom of Jerusalem in her own right.

Reign

Melisende married Fulk V, Count of Anjou, in 1129, and the pair were crowned jointly after Baldwin II's death in 1131. Relations with Fulk were strained — a revolt by her cousin Hugh II of Jaffa (1134) was widely suspected to involve plots around her position — but after Fulk's death in a hunting accident in 1143 she ruled as regent for her son Baldwin III and was crowned alongside him. She proved an able ruler, patron of the Church and the arts (the Melisende Psalter, a masterpiece of crusader illuminated manuscript art, is associated with her court), and presided over the kingdom during the fall of Edessa (1144) and the Second Crusade (1147–49).

Conflict with Baldwin III and later life

In 1152 Baldwin III demanded sole power, and a brief civil war split the kingdom before a settlement left Melisende ruling from Nablus; she remained influential, attended court, and died in 1161. Contemporary chronicler William of Tyre praised her as a wise and experienced ruler who nearly surpassed the capabilities attributed to her sex by contemporaries.
