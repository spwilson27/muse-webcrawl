JA Douglas McCurdy Sydney Airport

JA Douglas McCurdy Sydney Airport (IATA: YQY, ICAO: CYQY) is a regional airport serving Sydney, Nova Scotia, and the Cape Breton Regional Municipality, located about 8 nautical miles east of downtown Sydney. It is named after John Alexander Douglas McCurdy, the Canadian aviation pioneer who made the first powered flight in Canada at nearby Baddeck in 1909.

Operations

The airport has two runways and a passenger terminal served seasonally and year-round by regional carriers, with scheduled services linking Cape Breton to Halifax and Toronto. It is owned and operated by the Sydney Airport Authority and is an important transport link for Cape Breton Island, whose nearest major alternative airport is several hours away by road.

History

Airfield development began during the Second World War, when the region's steel and coal industries and military installations made air access strategically important. Commercial service grew after the war as Sydney became a centre for heavy industry and tourism in Atlantic Canada.
