Wilhelma, Palestine

Wilhelma was a German Templer colony established in 1902 in Ottoman Palestine, on land purchased near the village of Yahudiya, northeast of Jaffa. It was named in honour of Kaiser Wilhelm II. Today the site lies within Israel, at the location of the moshav Bnei Atarot, near Ben Gurion Airport.

Wilhelma's main street, laid out on the model of a German village.

History

Wilhelma was founded by members of the Templer movement, German Protestants who settled in the Holy Land in the nineteenth century and established colonies including Sarona, Wilhelma, and settlements in Haifa and Jerusalem. The colony was planned with a broad central street and farmsteads combining residences, stables, and barns. Its roughly 500 hectares supported mixed farming, and a dairy industry supplied milk and cheese to Jaffa and Jerusalem. Wilhelma was the terminus of a railway line and later hosted an aerodrome used by the British.

During the First World War, the British under General Allenby took the area in 1917; Wilhelma itself was a point of conflict in the fighting of November 1917. During the Second World War, the British Mandate authorities converted Wilhelma into an internment camp for enemy aliens, holding German, Austrian, and Italian civilians from the Templer colonies and elsewhere from 1941 onward. Most German residents were deported to Australia or repatriated to Germany, and the colony effectively ceased to exist by 1948. After the establishment of Israel, the site became the moshav Bnei Atarot, founded in 1948 in part by former residents of the evacuated settlement of Atarot.
