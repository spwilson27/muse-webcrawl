Lie algebra representation

In the mathematical field of representation theory, a representation of a Lie algebra is a way of writing elements of a Lie algebra as linear transformations of a vector space, in such a way that the Lie bracket corresponds to the commutator of the transformations. Formally, a representation of a Lie algebra 𝔤 on a vector space V is a Lie algebra homomorphism ρ: 𝔤 → 𝔤𝔩(V) into the Lie algebra of endomorphisms of V, satisfying ρ([x, y]) = [ρ(x), ρ(y)].

Basic concepts

A subspace invariant under all ρ(x) is a subrepresentation; a representation with no nontrivial subrepresentations is irreducible, and one expressible as a direct sum of irreducibles is completely reducible. Every Lie algebra has an important representation on itself, the adjoint representation, given by ad(x)(y) = [x, y]. Representations of 𝔤 correspond one-to-one with modules over its universal enveloping algebra.

Classification

For finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebras over ℂ, Weyl's theorem guarantees complete reducibility, and irreducible representations are classified by their highest weights (the theorem of the highest weight). For 𝔰𝔩(2, ℂ) there is exactly one irreducible representation of each dimension n + 1, on which the standard basis acts by explicit raising and lowering operators. The theory underpins much of modern physics, where Lie algebra representations describe particle multiplets and symmetries.
