Fors Clavigera

Fors Clavigera: Letters to the Workmen and Labourers of Great Britain is a series of 96 letters by the English art critic and social thinker John Ruskin, published monthly from 1871 to 1878 and intermittently until 1884. Addressed to British workers, it expounded Ruskin’s critique of industrial capitalism and his vision of a just, craft-based society.

Fors Clavigera
Author: John Ruskin
Published: 1871–1884
Letters: 96
Subjects: Political economy, labour, education, environment

Content

The title plays on Fors (chance/fortune) and clavigera (club-, key- or nail-bearing). The letters range widely — economics, education, agriculture, art, obituaries and autobiographical asides — advocating honest work, fair wages, public education and stewardship of nature, and announcing the founding of Ruskin’s utopian Guild of St George.

Influence

Fors Clavigera deeply influenced the British labour movement; early Labour MPs cited Ruskin as formative, and his ideas fed into guild socialism and the Arts and Crafts movement. The letters are also notorious for the Whistler v. Ruskin libel background of the era and for their digressive, prophetic style.
