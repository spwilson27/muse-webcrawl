Em (typography)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The em is a relative unit equal to the font's point size

An em is a unit in the field of typography, equal to the currently specified point size. For example, one em in a 16-point typeface is 16 points. The em is therefore a relative unit: it scales with the font size wherever it is used. Its name derives from the capital letter M, whose width in many typefaces was historically equal to the body size of the type; the related unit the en is half an em.

Usage

Historically, metal type was cast on bodies measured in points, and spacing material was supplied in ems and ens; the em quadrat ("mutton quad") was the standard paragraph indent and the width basis for the em dash (—). In digital typography, fonts are designed on an em square (often 1000 or 2048 units per em) that is scaled to the requested size.

In CSS, the em unit is relative to the computed font size of the element (historically the height of the em square), and is widely used for scalable layouts and typographic spacing; rem is relative to the root element's font size. Indents, margins and media queries expressed in ems keep proportions as text size changes, and an em-based indent remains a common style for the first line of paragraphs.
