Stunt performer

A stunt performer, often called a stuntman or stuntwoman, is a trained professional who performs daring acts, often as a career. Stunt performers usually appear in films or on television, taking the place of an actor in scenes involving physical risk — a practice known as doubling. Their work includes falls from heights, staged fights, car chases and crashes, fire stunts, underwater work, and aerial work on wires.

History

Early cinema relied on daredevil performers from circus, vaudeville, and Wild West shows. In the silent era, comedy stars such as Buster Keaton performed many of their own dangerous gags, while studios increasingly hired specialists. The profession became more organized and safety-conscious over the twentieth century, with stunt coordinators planning sequences and professional associations — such as the Stuntmen's Association of Motion Pictures (founded 1961) — promoting standards. In the modern era, coordinators design sequences combining practical stunts with rigging, airbags, descenders, and digital effects.

Types of stunts

Common categories include fights and falls; vehicle work (precision driving, motorcycle jumps, car rolls and crashes); fire burns performed with protective gel and clothing; water work including tank and open-water sequences; and aerial work using wires, harnesses, and ratchets. Stunt doubles are matched to actors by build and appearance and may wear prosthetics or wigs; "ND" (non-descript) stunt performers fill background action roles.

Safety and recognition

Stunt work remains hazardous: serious injuries and occasional fatalities have occurred despite planning and rehearsal. Safety meetings, engineered rigs, and incremental rehearsal at reduced speed or height are standard. Despite the craft's importance, major film awards long lacked a stunt category; the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences announced an Academy Award for Achievement in Stunt Design beginning with the 100th Academy Awards (for films of 2027).

Notable performers

Well-known stunt performers and coordinators include Yakima Canutt, who pioneered many techniques and the standardized "falling off a horse" gag; Hal Needham, founder of Stunts Unlimited; Vic Armstrong; Dar Robinson, noted for record high falls; and Zoë Bell, who doubled Uma Thurman in Kill Bill and later acted in lead roles.
