Twenty-foot equivalent unit

The twenty-foot equivalent unit (abbreviated TEU or teu) is a unit of measure used to describe the capacity of container ships and container terminals, based on the volume of a 20-foot-long (6.1 m) intermodal container. Because containers come in different lengths, the TEU provides a standardised approximation: a 40-foot container counts as two TEU, sometimes separately designated a forty-foot equivalent unit (FEU).

Dimensions

A standard 20-foot container is 20 feet long, 8 feet wide and 8 feet 6 inches high, with an internal volume of about 33 cubic metres. Exact capacity varies, and the TEU is an inexact measure — it expresses nominal capacity rather than precise cargo volume or weight.

Usage

Port throughput statistics, shipping line capacities and vessel sizes are routinely quoted in TEU. The largest container ships in service carry over 24,000 TEU. World container port traffic grew from roughly 225 million TEU in 2000 to well over 800 million TEU annually in the 2020s, with ports such as Shanghai and Singapore leading in volume.

History

The unit arose with the standardisation of container sizes in the 1960s under ISO standards, as pioneered by Malcom McLean's Sea-Land and adopted worldwide. High-cube and extended-length containers are still converted to TEU equivalents for capacity reporting.
