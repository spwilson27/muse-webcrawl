The Milken Institute is an American nonprofit, nonpartisan economic think tank headquartered in Santa Monica, California. Founded in 1991 by former financier Michael Milken, its stated mission is to increase global prosperity by advancing collaborative solutions that widen access to capital, create jobs and improve health. Its best-known activity is the annual Milken Institute Global Conference in Beverly Hills, which draws thousands of executives, investors and policymakers and is sometimes compared to Davos for finance.

Activities

The institute publishes research on capital markets, financial regulation, regional economics and public health, and runs centres devoted to topics such as the future of aging, public finance and medical research (including FasterCures, which promotes faster development of therapies). Its "Best-Performing Cities" index ranks US metropolitan areas by economic performance. The Milken Institute School of Public Health at George Washington University, named following a major gift, opened in 2014.

Founder

Michael Milken, a pioneer of the high-yield ("junk") bond market at Drexel Burnham Lambert in the 1980s, pleaded guilty in 1990 to securities violations and served about two years in prison; he was pardoned in 2020. After his release he devoted himself to philanthropy, medical-research funding (including the Prostate Cancer Foundation) and the institute.
