Established1997
Location1616 E. 18th Street, Kansas City, Missouri
TypeMusic museum

The American Jazz Museum is a museum in Kansas City, Missouri, dedicated to preserving and celebrating the history of American jazz music. It opened in 1997 in the historic 18th and Vine District, the neighbourhood that was the centre of Kansas City jazz in the 1930s and home to musicians such as Charlie Parker and Count Basie.

Exhibits

The museum's permanent exhibits include listening stations, interactive displays and artefacts such as Charlie Parker's Grafton saxophone, a dress worn by Ella Fitzgerald and memorabilia from Louis Armstrong, Duke Ellington and other jazz greats. Changing exhibitions explore the lives of individual artists and themes in jazz history.

Facilities

The complex includes the Blue Room, a working jazz club named after a famous club in the historic Street Hotel, and the Gem Theater, a restored 500-seat performing arts venue across the street. The museum shares its building with the Negro Leagues Baseball Museum, and the two institutions form the core of the Museums at 18th & Vine.

Programmes

Alongside exhibitions, the museum hosts concerts, educational programmes and community events. It has faced financial challenges and governance changes over the years, with the city of Kansas City taking a greater role in its oversight.
