Kyūya Fukada

Died21 March 1978 (aged 75)

Kyūya Fukada (深田 久弥, Fukada Kyūya, 3 March 1903 – 21 March 1978) was a Japanese mountaineer and writer of the Shōwa period. He is best known as the author of Nihon Hyaku-meizan (One Hundred Famous Japanese Mountains), a classic of Japanese mountain literature published in 1964.

Life

Fukada was born in Kaga, Ishikawa Prefecture (now Kaga City). He fell in love with the mountains as a schoolboy in Fukui and began climbing while a student at Keio University, travelling widely in the Japanese Alps and the Hida Mountains. He left university without graduating and pursued writing, producing novels and essays about the mountains and their people.

One Hundred Famous Japanese Mountains

In Nihon Hyaku-meizan (日本百名山), serialised in the journal Yama to Kōgen and published in book form in 1964, Fukada selected 100 mountains from those he had personally climbed, judging them by their grace, history and individual character, generally requiring an elevation above 1,500 metres. The list — from Mount Rishiri and Mount Shari in Hokkaidō to Mount Miyanoura on Yakushima — became a national phenomenon: completing all 100 peaks (hyaku-meizan bagging) turned into a popular life goal for Japanese hikers, spawning guidebooks, television programmes and a surge of interest in mountaineering. Fukada received a cultural award for the work, which remains in print.

Fukada died in 1978 while climbing Mount Kayagatake in Yamanashi Prefecture. A monument to him stands at Kamikōchi in the Japanese Alps.
