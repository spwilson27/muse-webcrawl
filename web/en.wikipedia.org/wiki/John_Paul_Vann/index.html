John Paul Vann (born John Paul Tripp; 2 July 1924 – 9 June 1972) was a lieutenant colonel in the United States Army and later a senior civilian adviser in South Vietnam during the Vietnam War. He became one of the most prominent internal critics of the American and South Vietnamese conduct of the war.

Adviser and critic

Vann arrived in Vietnam in 1962 as an adviser to the Army of the Republic of Vietnam (ARVN). After the Battle of Ap Bac in January 1963, in which ARVN forces performed poorly against a smaller Viet Cong unit, Vann bluntly reported the failure to journalists and superiors, contradicting the optimistic official assessments of General Paul Harkins's headquarters. His outspokenness effectively ended his Army career, and he retired in 1963.

Return as a civilian

Vann returned to Vietnam in 1965 as a civilian with the Agency for International Development, later becoming a senior official in the CORDS pacification programme. He rose to become the senior American adviser in the II Corps region and, in 1972, effectively directed the defence of Kon Tum during the Easter Offensive, for which he was awarded the Distinguished Service Cross. He was killed when his helicopter crashed near Kon Tum on 9 June 1972. He was posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom.

Vann's life and the war are chronicled in Neil Sheehan's Pulitzer Prize-winning book A Bright Shining Lie (1988).
