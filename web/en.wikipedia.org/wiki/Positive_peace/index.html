Positive peace

Positive peace is a concept in peace studies describing the presence of conditions that sustain a peaceful society — social justice, equitable distribution of resources, rule of law, and functioning institutions — beyond the mere absence of direct violence. It contrasts with negative peace, defined simply as the absence of war or physical violence.

Origins

The distinction was developed by Johan Galtung, a founder of peace and conflict studies, from the 1960s onward. Galtung linked positive peace to the absence of "structural violence" — harm built into social structures that prevents people from meeting their basic needs — so that achieving positive peace requires transforming unjust structures as well as stopping direct violence.

Measurement

The Institute for Economics and Peace produces a Positive Peace Index, ranking countries on pillars such as well-functioning government, equitable distribution of resources, free flow of information, good relations with neighbours, high levels of human capital, acceptance of the rights of others, low levels of corruption, and a sound business environment. Research using the index finds that high positive peace predicts resilience and sustained negative peace over time.
