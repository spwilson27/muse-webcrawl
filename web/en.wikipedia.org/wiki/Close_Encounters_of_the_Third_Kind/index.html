Close Encounters of the Third Kind is a 1977 American science fiction drama film written and directed by Steven Spielberg, starring Richard Dreyfuss, Melinda Dillon, Teri Garr, Bob Balaban, Cary Guffey, and François Truffaut.

Plot

Indiana electrical lineman Roy Neary witnesses an unidentified flying object and becomes obsessed with a mental image of a flat-topped mountain (the Devils Tower in Wyoming), sculpting it from mashed potatoes and later from earth in his living room. Meanwhile, scientists led by French ufologist Claude Lacombe (Truffaut) investigate worldwide UFO phenomena. Neary and Jillian Guiler, whose young son Barry was abducted, converge on the tower, where a secret government landing site receives a vast alien mothership; Neary ultimately boards the craft.

Production and themes

The film's title derives from ufologist J. Allen Hynek's classification of close encounters, in which the third kind denotes observation of animate beings. The aliens communicate through a sequence of five musical tones, composed by John Williams. Special photographic effects were supervised by Douglas Trumbull. The film was a critical and commercial success, won the Academy Award for Best Cinematography, and is preserved in the National Film Registry. A "Special Edition" with additional scenes was released in 1980.
