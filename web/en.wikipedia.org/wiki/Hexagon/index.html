In geometry, a hexagon (from Greek ἕξ, hex, meaning "six", and γωνία, gonía, meaning "corner, angle") is a six-sided polygon. The total of the internal angles of any simple (non-self-intersecting) hexagon is 720°.

Regular hexagon

A regular hexagon has Schläfli symbol {6} and can also be constructed as a truncated equilateral triangle, t{3}, which alternates two types of edges. A regular hexagon is defined as a hexagon that is both equilateral and equiangular. It is bicentric, meaning that it is both cyclic (has a circumscribed circle) and tangential (has an inscribed circle).

The common length of the sides equals the radius of the circumscribed circle, and also equals half the diameter of the inscribed circle-related measurements: the apothem (radius of the inscribed circle) equals (√3/2) times the side length. The area of a regular hexagon with side length a is (3√3/2)a². A regular hexagon can be divided into six equilateral triangles by its centre.

Regular hexagons tile the plane with no gaps — the hexagonal tiling — one of only three regular tilings, alongside squares and equilateral triangles. Hexagons have the largest area per unit perimeter among these tilings, which is related to their prevalence in nature.

Hexagons in nature and technology

  • Honeycombs — bees build hexagonal cells, an arrangement conjectured (and in 1999 proved) to use the least wax for a given volume.
  • Snowflakes — the hexagonal symmetry of ice crystals follows from the molecular structure of ice.
  • Basalt columns — cooling lava can fracture into hexagonal columns, as at the Giant's Causeway in Northern Ireland and Devils Postpile in California.
  • Benzene — the six-carbon ring of aromatic compounds is represented as a hexagon.
  • Saturn's polar hexagon — a persistent hexagonal cloud pattern surrounds Saturn's north pole.
  • Nuts and bolts — hexagonal heads allow wrenching from six sides and compact turning angles.
  • France — mainland France is nicknamed l'Hexagone for its roughly six-sided shape.

Related figures

The hexagon is related to the Pascal's theorem configuration, the hexagram (six-pointed star), and hexagonal numbers in number theory. In three dimensions, the hexagonal prism and hexagonal antiprism extend the form. Spherical and hyperbolic geometry also admit regular hexagonal tilings with different angle sums.
