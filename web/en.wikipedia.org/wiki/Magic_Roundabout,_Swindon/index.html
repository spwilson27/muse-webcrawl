Magic Roundabout, Swindon

The Magic Roundabout in Swindon, Wiltshire, England, is a celebrated road junction constructed in 1972. It consists of five mini-roundabouts arranged in a circle around a central island, with traffic circulating anticlockwise around the outer ring — the reverse of a normal British roundabout — while traffic moves clockwise around each individual mini-roundabout. It is officially named County Islands Ring Junction, though it has been known by its nickname, borrowed from the children's television programme The Magic Roundabout, since the 1980s.

Design and operation

The junction was designed under the guidance of Frank Blackmore of the British Transport and Road Research Laboratory, inventor of the mini-roundabout. Drivers entering the system can choose from several paths around the ring, which spreads traffic more evenly than a conventional roundabout and allows short journeys to cut across in either direction. Despite its intimidating appearance to first-time users, the junction has an excellent safety record and handles high traffic volumes efficiently; in its first years, police officers were stationed at each mini-roundabout during the initial bedding-in period.

Reception

The Magic Roundabout is a source of local pride and national bemusement in roughly equal measure. It has repeatedly been voted Britain's scariest junction in surveys, while also being praised by traffic engineers as an elegant and effective design. Similar (smaller) magic roundabouts exist in Hemel Hempstead, Colchester and High Wycombe. A calendar featuring the roundabout was produced by local residents and became an unlikely bestseller, and the junction is celebrated in the song "Magic Roundabout" by the Swindon band XTC.
