William Christopher Handy (November 16, 1873 – March 28, 1958) was an American composer and musician who referred to himself as the Father of the Blues. He was one of the most influential songwriters in the United States. One of many musicians who played the distinctively American blues music, Handy did not create the blues genre but was the first to publish music in the blues form, thereby taking the blues from a regional music style (Delta blues) with a limited audience to a new level of popularity.

Handy was an educated musician who used folk material in his compositions. He was scrupulous in documenting the sources of his works, which frequently combined stylistic influences from various performers.

Early life

Handy was born in Florence, Alabama, the son of Elizabeth Brewer and Charles Barnard Handy. His father was the pastor of a small church in Guntersville, another small town in northeast central Alabama. Handy wrote in his 1941 autobiography, Father of the Blues, that his father was a stern man who believed that musical instruments were tools of the devil.

Growing up he apprenticed in carpentry, shoemaking and plastering. He was deeply religious and his musical influences were from church music, which he sang in and played in, plus the sounds of nature. The sounds of birds, the whistles of trains, and the music of work gangs would all influence his later compositions.

Career

In 1909, Handy and his band moved to Memphis, Tennessee, where they played in clubs on Beale Street. His composition "Mr. Crump", written for the mayoral campaign of Edward Hull "Boss" Crump, was later retitled "The Memphis Blues" and published in 1912. Although the sheet music did not make him wealthy, it brought blues to a national audience.

Handy's most enduring composition is "St. Louis Blues" (1914), one of the most recorded songs of the 20th century. Other notable works include "Beale Street Blues" (1916), "Yellow Dog Blues" (1914), and "Loveless Love". In 1917 he moved his publishing company, Handy Brothers Music Co., which he co-founded with Harry Pace, to New York City.

Legacy

The W. C. Handy Music Festival is held annually in Florence, Alabama. The W. C. Handy Award, later renamed the Blues Award, was established in his honor. In 1969 he was honored with a United States postage stamp. His hometown of Florence preserves his log cabin birthplace as a museum. In 2018, anthropologist Daniel Beaumont published a biography examining Handy's life and work.
