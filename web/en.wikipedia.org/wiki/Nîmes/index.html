Nîmes (French pronunciation: [nim]; Occitan: Nimes) is a city in southern France, the prefecture of the Gard department in the Occitanie region. Renowned for the density and preservation of its Roman monuments, it is often called the "French Rome". Its historic centre, the Maison Carrée and the surrounding Roman fabric were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2023.

History

The city grew around a sacred spring dedicated to the god Nemausus, from whom its name derives. Veterans of Julius Caesar's Egyptian campaigns were settled here under Augustus, and the colony of Nemausus flourished on the Via Domitia between Italy and Spain. Coins minted in the city bore a crocodile chained to a palm — an emblem still used in Nîmes's coat of arms and street furniture.

After Roman decline and a period of Visigothic and Arab rule, Nîmes became a textile town of consequence; it gave its name to serge de Nîmes, the fabric that became denim. The city was a stronghold of Protestantism and suffered severely in the Wars of Religion, including the massacre known as the Michelade in 1567.

Roman monuments

The Arena of Nîmes, a remarkably intact amphitheatre built around 70 CE, still hosts concerts and bullfights. The Maison Carrée, a temple of the early first century CE dedicated to Gaius and Lucius Caesar, is among the best-preserved Roman temples anywhere. Other survivals include the Tour Magne, the castellum aquae that distributed water brought by the aqueduct whose most famous stretch is the Pont du Gard, and the Temple of Diana within the Jardins de la Fontaine.

Culture

Nîmes is famous for its ferias — the Feria de Pentecôte and Feria des Vendanges — centred on bullfighting in the arena. The city museum of Roman history, the Musée de la Romanité, opened opposite the arena in 2018. The architect Norman Foster designed the Carré d'Art, a glass library and contemporary art museum facing the Maison Carrée.

Geography and climate

Nîmes lies between the Mediterranean lowlands and the limestone scrubland of the Garrigues, with a hot-summer Mediterranean climate moderated occasionally by the mistral wind. It is served by the Nîmes–Alès–Camargue–Cévennes Airport and a station on the high-speed line to Montpellier and Paris.
