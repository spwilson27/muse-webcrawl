Geomungo

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Geomungo
Hangul: 거문고
Hanja: 玄琴
Type: plucked zither (chordophone)
Origin: Goguryeo, Korea

The geomungo (also spelled komungo or kŏmun'go) is a traditional Korean plucked zither with six strings. It is a representative stringed instrument of Korea, made of paulownia wood, and is played with a short bamboo stick called a suldae held in the right hand, while the left hand presses the strings to produce pitch changes and vibrato.

Description

The geomungo is roughly 150 cm long. Its body is hollowed from paulownia wood, with a harder wood such as chestnut for the back. Six silk strings pass over sixteen convex frets (gwae) arranged along the soundboard; the middle strings pass over movable bridges. The strings are struck or plucked with the suldae, producing a deep, percussive, and resonant sound that has often been described as masculine and majestic, in contrast to the softer twelve-string gayageum.

History

According to tradition, the geomungo was developed in the kingdom of Goguryeo around the 4th–7th centuries CE. The Samguk Sagi records that Wang San-ak, a prime minister of Goguryeo, adapted a seven-string Chinese zither (the qin) after one was sent from Jin China, and composed over a hundred pieces for the new instrument. Tomb murals of Goguryeo depict musicians playing an instrument resembling the geomungo. The instrument became a central part of the scholarly culture of the Joseon dynasty, prized by Confucian literati for self-cultivation as well as court and ensemble music.

Repertoire and role

The geomungo is the leading melodic instrument in court ensemble music and in the chamber genre yeongsan hoesang. It also features in the lyric song accompaniment and, in the 20th century, in the solo improvisatory genre sanjo — geomungo sanjo was developed by Baek Nak-jun in the 1890s. In 2011-era cultural listings, geomungo craftsmanship and performance have been recognised within Korean intangible heritage programmes.
