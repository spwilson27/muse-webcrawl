Hydrogen iodide

Hydrogen iodide (HI) is a diatomic molecule and hydrogen halide. Its aqueous solution is hydroiodic acid, a strong acid. HI is a colourless gas with a pungent odour; it fumes in moist air and is highly soluble in water.

Properties

Hydrogen iodide has a boiling point of about −35.4 °C. It is the strongest of the hydrohalic acids in water because the H–I bond is relatively weak. It is also a powerful reducing agent, readily oxidised by air to elemental iodine, which colours aged solutions yellow-brown.

Preparation and uses

HI can be prepared by direct combination of hydrogen and iodine (catalysed by platinum at elevated temperature), or by reacting iodine with hydrazine or hydrogen sulfide. It is used in organic synthesis, for example in the cleavage of ethers and in reductions, and historically as a disinfectant and in pharmaceutical preparations.
