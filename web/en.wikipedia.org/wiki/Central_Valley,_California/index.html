Central Valley, California

Central Valley
Length: about 450 mi (720 km)
Width: 40–60 mi (60–100 km)
Major cities: Sacramento, Stockton, Modesto, Fresno, Bakersfield
Rivers: Sacramento River, San Joaquin River

The Central Valley is a great, flat valley that dominates the interior of California, United States. It runs roughly northwest to southeast for about 450 miles (720 km) between the Sierra Nevada to the east and the Coast Ranges to the west. It comprises the Sacramento Valley in the north and the San Joaquin Valley in the south.

Agriculture

The Central Valley is one of the most productive agricultural regions in the world. With less than 1% of US farmland it produces about 8% of the nation's agricultural output by value, including most of the country's tomatoes, almonds, grapes, cotton, apricots and asparagus. This productivity depends on large-scale irrigation from Sierra snowmelt, delivered by federal and state water projects, and on groundwater pumping, which has caused significant land subsidence.

Geography and climate

The valley floor lies mostly between sea level and 400 feet elevation. Summers are hot and dry, winters cool with dense tule fog. The Sacramento and San Joaquin rivers meet in the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta, the valley's only outlet to the sea.

People

About 6.5 million people live in the valley. Its largest metropolitan areas are Sacramento (the state capital), Fresno, Bakersfield, Stockton and Modesto. The region's workforce includes large Latino and immigrant communities central to its farm economy.
