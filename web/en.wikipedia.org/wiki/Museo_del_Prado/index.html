The Museo del Prado (Prado Museum) is the main Spanish national art museum, located in central Madrid. It is widely considered to house one of the world's finest collections of European art, dating from the 12th century to the early 20th century, based on the former Spanish Royal Collection, and is unquestionably the best single collection of Spanish art. Founded as a museum of paintings and sculpture in 1819, it also contains important collections of drawings, prints, coins and decorative arts.

History

The museum's home, the Villanueva building, was designed in 1785 by architect Juan de Villanueva on the orders of Charles III as a cabinet of natural history. His grandson Ferdinand VII, encouraged by his wife Queen María Isabel de Braganza, converted it into a royal museum of paintings, which opened on 19 November 1819 with some 1,500 works from the royal collections. The collection grew through the 19th and 20th centuries, absorbing the paintings of the suppressed Museo de la Trinidad — including works confiscated from religious orders — and through major donations and bequests. During the Spanish Civil War the masterpieces were evacuated, first to Valencia and then to Geneva, returning safely in 1939. A major extension designed by Rafael Moneo opened in 2007.

Collection highlights

The Prado holds the world's largest and finest collections of Hieronymus Bosch (including The Garden of Earthly Delights), Diego Velázquez (Las Meninas), Francisco Goya (including the Black Paintings and The Third of May 1808), El Greco, Peter Paul Rubens, Titian and Raphael. Together with the nearby Reina Sofía and Thyssen-Bornemisza museums, it forms Madrid's "Golden Triangle of Art" along the Paseo del Prado, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2021.
