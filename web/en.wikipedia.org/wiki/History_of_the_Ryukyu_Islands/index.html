History of the Ryukyu Islands

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The history of the Ryukyu Islands covers the chain of islands stretching from Kyushu to Taiwan, home to the Ryukyuan peoples, the medieval Ryukyu Kingdom, and modern Okinawa Prefecture of Japan.

Early period

Archaeological evidence shows human habitation from the Palaeolithic; the Minatogawa Man fossils date to roughly 20,000 years ago. The Shellmound period gave way to the Gusuku period (c. 12th–15th centuries), when local lords (aji) built fortified castles (gusuku) and competed for power. Ryukyu developed a thriving maritime trade network linking China, Japan, Korea, and Southeast Asia, trading sulphur, horses, and Japanese goods for Chinese ceramics, silk, and Southeast Asian products.

The Ryukyu Kingdom

In 1429 Shō Hashi unified Okinawa's three kingdoms (Sanzan) into the Ryukyu Kingdom, which entered tributary relations with Ming China while maintaining a distinct culture and language. The kingdom's golden age under Shō Shin saw disarmament of the lords and a flourishing court at Shuri. In 1609 Japan's Satsuma Domain invaded, making Ryukyu a vassal of both Satsuma and China — a dual subordination that concealed Satsuma's control from Chinese envoys.

Annexation and modern era

The Meiji government abolished the kingdom in 1879 (the Ryukyu Disposition) and created Okinawa Prefecture, imposing assimilation policies. The islands suffered devastation in the Battle of Okinawa (1945), one of World War II's bloodiest battles. Under United States administration until 1972, Okinawa then reverted to Japan, though a large US military presence remains a source of local tension. Ryukyuan languages are now endangered, though cultural revival movements persist.
