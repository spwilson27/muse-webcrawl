Aries Merritt

Aries Merritt, world record holder in the 110 metres hurdles.

Aries Merritt (born July 24, 1985) is an American track and field athlete specialising in the hurdles, best known for winning the gold medal in the 110 metres hurdles at the 2012 London Olympics and for setting the world record of 12.80 seconds in the event later that season — a mark that still stands. He is the only hurdler in history to run under 12.90 seconds and is widely regarded as one of the greatest sprint hurdlers of all time.

Career

Merritt, from Chicago and raised in Georgia, attended the University of Tennessee, winning NCAA titles. His breakthrough came in 2012, when he won the US Olympic trials and then the Olympic final in 12.92 seconds. On 7 September 2012 in Brussels he broke Dayron Robles's world record, running 12.80 s to cap an extraordinary season in which he ran under 13 seconds eight times.

Illness and comeback

In 2013 Merritt was diagnosed with a rare genetic disorder causing acute kidney failure. Despite declining health he won bronze at the 2015 World Championships in Beijing, days before undergoing a kidney transplant with a kidney donated by his sister. He returned to competition in 2016 and narrowly missed the US Olympic team, and continued competing at elite level for several more seasons.
