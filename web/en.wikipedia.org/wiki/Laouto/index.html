Laouto

The laouto (Greek: λαούτο, plural laouta) is a long-necked lute of Greek origin, a member of the lute family played with a long plectrum. It is closely related to the Turkish lavta and the Arabic oud, and is widely used in Greek folk music, especially on the mainland and in Crete, Cyprus and the Aegean islands.

Construction

The laouto has a large, deep, ribbed bowl body, a long fretted neck and typically four double courses of strings (eight strings in total), tuned in fifths and fourths. The Cretan laouto is larger and tuned lower than the mainland instrument. Historically strings were gut; modern instruments use steel or nylon.

Role in music

The laouto usually provides rhythmic and harmonic accompaniment — a bass line and chords — beneath a lead instrument such as the violin, lyra, clarinet or bouzouki. It can also take melodic lines, and its driving off-beat accompaniment patterns are characteristic of Greek island dance music.
