Waterfall

A waterfall is an area where water flows over a vertical drop or a series of steep drops in the course of a stream or river. Waterfalls occur where a river crosses a change in rock type, a fault, or a glacially deepened valley, and they gradually retreat upstream as erosion undercuts the lip.

Formation and types

Common forms include plunges, where water falls free of the rock face; horsetails, which maintain contact with the rock; cascades over slopes; tiered falls in steps; and segmented falls split by rock outcrops. The plunge pool at the base deepens as the falling water excavates it, undermining the cliff until the lip collapses and the falls migrate upstream, sometimes leaving a gorge, as at Niagara Falls.

Notable waterfalls

Angel Falls in Venezuela is the world's tallest waterfall at 979 m. Other giants include Tugela Falls in South Africa, Victoria Falls on the Zambezi — the largest sheet of falling water — Iguazu Falls on the Argentina–Brazil border, and Niagara Falls, famous for both its power generation and tourism.

Significance

Waterfalls are barriers to fish migration, sources of hydroelectric power, sacred sites in many cultures, and major tourist attractions. Their spray zones support distinctive microclimates and endemic species.
