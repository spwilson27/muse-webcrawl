Geiranger

The village of Geiranger at the head of the Geirangerfjord.

Geiranger is a small tourist village in Stranda Municipality in Møre og Romsdal county, Norway. It lies at the head of the Geirangerfjorden, a branch of the Storfjorden, about 40 kilometres south of the town of Ålesund's wider region and is one of Norway's most visited natural attractions. The village has roughly 250 permanent residents, but receives several hundred thousand visitors each year.

Geography and tourism

Geiranger sits where the Geirangerelva river empties into the fjord, surrounded by steep mountainsides with abandoned fjord farms clinging to the slopes. The Geirangerfjord, together with the Nærøyfjord, was inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List in 2005 as part of the West Norwegian Fjords. Cruise ships call during the summer season, anchoring in the fjord and tendering passengers ashore. Famous viewpoints include Dalsnibba, Flydalsjuvet, and Ørnesvingen (the Eagle Road), the steep road with eleven hairpin bends climbing towards Eidsdal.

Waterfalls visible around the village and fjord include the Seven Sisters, the Suitor, and the Bridal Veil. Hiking trails lead to farms such as Skageflå and Knivsflå, abandoned after a 1934 landslide warning and accessible only by boat and on foot.

Risk of landslide

The mountain Åkerneset across the fjord is slowly splitting apart, and geologists monitor a large unstable rock mass that could eventually slide into the fjord and generate a tsunami threatening Geiranger. Monitoring systems are in place to give early warning, and the scenario inspired the 2015 Norwegian disaster film The Wave (Bølgen).

Transport

The village is served by County Road 63, closed in winter on the mountain sections, and by the Hurtigruten and car ferries linking Geiranger with Hellesylt during the tourist season.
