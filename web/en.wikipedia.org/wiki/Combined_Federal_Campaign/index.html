Combined Federal Campaign

Administered byU.S. Office of Personnel Management
TypeWorkplace charitable giving programme

The Combined Federal Campaign (CFC) is the official workplace giving campaign of the United States federal government. It allows federal employees, military personnel, postal workers, and federal retirees to contribute to approved charitable organisations through payroll deduction or one-time gifts, and is administered by the Office of Personnel Management (OPM).

History

Workplace fundraising in the federal service was consolidated by executive order of President John F. Kennedy in 1961, and the campaign took its modern form in 1971. Before consolidation, dozens of separate agency-by-agency charity drives competed throughout the year. The CFC traditionally runs each autumn, from September into January.

Operation

Only non-profit organisations meeting OPM eligibility standards may participate; thousands of national, international, and local charities are listed in the annual catalogue. Donors may designate contributions to specific charities or leave them undesignated. Since its inception the campaign has raised more than $8.6 billion, though annual totals have declined from a peak of about $282.6 million in 2009 to under $100 million in recent years, reflecting changes in the federal workforce and giving habits.
