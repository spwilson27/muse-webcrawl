Feast of Fools

The Feast of Fools was a festival celebrated by the clergy in Europe during the Middle Ages, mainly in France, in which participants elected a mock bishop or "Lord of Misrule" and parodied church rituals. It was generally held around the Feast of the Circumcision on 1 January.

Origins

The feast developed from earlier traditions of role reversal such as the Roman Saturnalia and the feast of the Holy Innocents, and was associated with subdeacons, the lowest order of clergy. Lower clergy would elect one of their number to preside in a burlesque of ecclesiastical office.

Suppression

Church authorities repeatedly condemned excesses at the feast; the Council of Basel prohibited it in 1431, and it declined through the 15th and 16th centuries, though survivals persisted in some places. It is referenced in Victor Hugo's The Hunchback of Notre-Dame.
