Cher (born Cherilyn Sarkisian; 20 May 1946) is an American singer, actress, and television personality. Often referred to as the "Goddess of Pop", she has been described as embodying female autonomy in a male-dominated entertainment industry. Known for her distinctive contralto singing voice and for having worked in numerous areas of entertainment, as well as adopting a variety of styles and appearances, Cher rose to fame in 1965 as one half of the folk rock husband-wife duo Sonny & Cher, with the hit single "I Got You Babe".

Career

After success with Sonny Bono in the 1960s and on the television variety programme The Sonny & Cher Comedy Hour in the 1970s, Cher established a disco-era solo career with hits including "Gypsys, Tramps & Thieves", "Half-Breed" and "Dark Lady". She reinvented herself as a film actress in the 1980s, winning acclaim for Silkwood (1983), Mask (1985) and Moonstruck (1987), for which she won the Academy Award for Best Actress.

In 1998 her album Believe, and its title track's pioneering use of Auto-Tune pitch-correction (widely called the "Cher effect"), returned her to the top of the charts worldwide; "Believe" remains one of the best-selling singles of all time. Her farewell and later tours, including the Las Vegas residencies and the Dressed to Kill and Here We Go Again tours, rank among the most successful by a solo artist.

Legacy

Cher is one of the world's best-selling music artists, with estimated sales of over 100 million records. She is the only artist to date to have had a number-one single on a Billboard chart in seven consecutive decades, from the 1960s to the 2020s. Her honours include an Academy Award, a Grammy Award, an Emmy Award, three Golden Globe Awards and a Kennedy Center Honor. In 2024 she was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a solo artist.
