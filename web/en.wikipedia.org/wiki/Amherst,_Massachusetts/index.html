Amherst (/ˈæmərst/) is a town in Hampshire County, Massachusetts, United States, in the Connecticut River valley of the Pioneer Valley region. It is best known as a college town: it is home to Amherst College, Hampshire College, and the flagship campus of the University of Massachusetts Amherst. Together with nearby institutions, Amherst forms part of the Five College Consortium. The town was also the lifelong home of the poet Emily Dickinson.

History

The area was inhabited by the Norwottuck people before English settlement from Hadley began in the early eighteenth century. The town was incorporated in 1759 and named for Jeffery Amherst, a British commander in the French and Indian War — a namesake whose role in distributing smallpox-infected blankets to Native Americans has prompted recurring local debate about the town's name. Amherst remained an agricultural community until the founding of Amherst College in 1821 and of the Massachusetts Agricultural College (now UMass Amherst) in 1863 transformed it into an educational center.

Geography and demographics

Amherst covers about 27.8 square miles in the eastern Pioneer Valley, bordered by Hadley, South Hadley, Belchertown, Pelham, Shutesbury, Leverett, and Sunderland. Its population swells considerably during the academic year; the 2020 census counted roughly 39,000 residents, with students making up a very large share, giving the town one of the lowest median ages in Massachusetts.

Culture and notable residents

The Emily Dickinson Museum preserves the poet's homestead and her brother's house, The Evergreens. The town is also associated with Noah Webster, who worked on his dictionary while living in Amherst, and with the poet Robert Frost, who taught at Amherst College. Amherst is known for progressive politics, a farmers' market on the town common, and in 2018 it replaced its representative town meeting with a 13-member town council form of government.
