Quantum state

In quantum physics, a quantum state is a mathematical entity that embodies the knowledge of a quantum system. Quantum mechanics specifies the construction, evolution, and measurement of a quantum state. The result is a prediction for the system represented by the quantum state.

The Bloch sphere gives a geometric picture of the possible pure states of a two-level quantum system (a qubit).

Description

A pure quantum state can be represented by a vector (a "ket", written |ψ⟩) in a complex Hilbert space, or equivalently by a ray in that space. A mixed state, representing a statistical ensemble of pure states, is described by a density operator: a positive semidefinite, trace-one operator. Measurements are described by observables, self-adjoint operators whose expectation values give the predicted average outcomes.

Evolution

Isolated quantum states evolve unitarily according to the Schrödinger equation. Open systems interacting with an environment are described by more general completely positive trace-preserving maps, such as those given by the Lindblad master equation.

Superposition and entanglement

Pure states may be superpositions of basis states, and composite systems may occupy entangled states that cannot be written as products of states of their parts. These features underpin quantum information processing, including quantum computing and quantum teleportation.
