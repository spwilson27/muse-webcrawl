Ethiopia is one of the most linguistically diverse countries in the world, with more than 90 languages spoken. Almost all belong to the Afroasiatic family: the Cushitic branch (Oromo, Somali, Sidamo, Afar), the Semitic branch (Amharic, Tigrinya, Gurage languages, and the liturgical Ge'ez), and the Omotic languages of the southwest; a smaller number of Nilo-Saharan languages are spoken along the western borderlands.

Major languages

Oromo (Afaan Oromoo) is the most widely spoken first language, used by roughly a third of the population. Amharic, the language of the historic imperial court, is the working language of the federal government and a lingua franca nationwide; it is written in the Ge'ez (fidäl) script, as is Tigrinya, spoken in the north and in Eritrea. Somali predominates in the east, and Sidamo and Wolaytta in the south.

Language policy

Under the imperial and Derg regimes Amharic dominated education and administration. The 1995 constitution of the Federal Democratic Republic established ethnic federalism with regional states organised largely by language, granting nations and nationalities the right to develop their languages and to primary education in the mother tongue; regional working languages include Oromo (written in the Latin-based Qubee alphabet), Tigrinya, Somali, and Afar. In 2020 the federal government adopted Oromo, Afar, Somali, and Tigrinya as additional federal working languages alongside Amharic. English is the medium of instruction in secondary schools and universities and the principal foreign language.
