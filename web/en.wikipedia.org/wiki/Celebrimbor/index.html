Celebrimbor (Sindarin for "silver fist" or "silver hand") is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium. An Elven smith of the Noldor, grandson of Fëanor, he was the lord of Eregion in the Second Age and the greatest of the Elven jewel-smiths of his time. He led the Gwaith-i-Mírdain, the brotherhood of Elven craftsmen, and under the tutelage of the disguised Sauron — calling himself Annatar, "Lord of Gifts" — forged the Rings of Power.

Biography in the legendarium

Celebrimbor was the son of Curufin, fifth son of Fëanor, and followed his father and grandfather into exile from Valinor during the First Age, though he renounced their deeds and remained in Nargothrond, later surviving the fall of Gondolin's refugees to settle in the Second Age in Eregion, west of Moria. There the smiths entered into friendship with the Dwarves of Khazad-dûm.

Around S.A. 1200, Sauron came to Eregion in fair disguise and taught the smiths the craft of ring-making. Under his guidance they forged the lesser rings and then the Sixteen Rings later given to Dwarf-lords and Men. But Celebrimbor, working alone and without Sauron's knowledge, forged the Three Rings of the Elves — Narya, Nenya and Vilya — untouched by Sauron's hand. When Sauron put on the One Ring and spoke its verse, Celebrimbor perceived his treachery and hid the Three.

Death and legacy

In the ensuing war, Eregion was destroyed (S.A. 1697). Celebrimbor was captured, tortured to reveal the whereabouts of the rings, and killed; his body was hung on a pole and used as a banner by Sauron's armies. He revealed the location of the Seven and the Nine but refused to disclose the Three. His craftsmanship is also remembered in the West-gate of Moria, whose inscription and doors he made with the Dwarf Narvi. Celebrimbor features prominently in modern adaptations, including the video games Middle-earth: Shadow of Mordor and Shadow of War, and the television series The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power.
