Claus von Amsberg

Claus von Amsberg (German: Klaus-Georg Wilhelm Otto Friedrich Gerd von Amsberg; 6 September 1926 – 6 October 2002) was the husband of Queen Beatrix of the Netherlands and was Prince of the Netherlands from Beatrix's accession in 1980 until his death in 2002. Upon his marriage in 1966 he was known as Prince Claus.

Early life

He was born at Haus Dötzingen, near Hitzacker, Germany, into the untitled German nobility. He was educated in Germany and spent part of his youth in German East Africa (Tanganyika), where his father managed a plantation. During the Second World War he served in the German army, and after the war he studied law and worked as a diplomat for the Federal Republic of Germany, serving in Santo Domingo and Abidjan.

Marriage

His engagement to Princess Beatrix, announced in 1965, provoked controversy in the Netherlands because of his German background and wartime service. The couple married in Amsterdam on 10 March 1966 amid public protests. Over time, however, Claus became one of the most popular members of the Dutch royal house, admired for his work on development cooperation, culture, and social issues, and for speaking openly about his depression.

Death

Prince Claus died in Amsterdam on 6 October 2002 after a long illness. The Prince Claus Fund for Culture and Development, established in 1996, is named in his honour.
