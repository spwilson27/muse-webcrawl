Duane Allman

Allman in performance, 1971

Howard Duane Allman (November 20, 1946 – October 29, 1971) was an American rock and blues guitarist and the founder and original leader of the Allman Brothers Band, for which he is remembered principally. Renowned for his inventive slide guitar playing and melodic improvisation, he was also a sought-after session musician, contributing to landmark recordings by Aretha Franklin, Wilson Pickett, Eric Clapton and others, all before his death in a motorcycle accident at the age of 24.

Early life and session work

Allman was born in Nashville, Tennessee, and raised largely in Daytona Beach, Florida, with his younger brother Gregg Allman. After early bands including the Escorts, the Allman Joys and Hour Glass, Duane became a staff guitarist at FAME Studios in Muscle Shoals, Alabama. His playing on Wilson Pickett's version of "Hey Jude" (1968) drew wide attention; he subsequently recorded with Aretha Franklin, King Curtis, Boz Scaggs and the Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section, and his suggestion of recording the song is often credited with launching his session career.

The Allman Brothers Band

In 1969 Allman formed the Allman Brothers Band in Jacksonville, Florida, with Gregg, Dickey Betts, Berry Oakley, Butch Trucks and Jai Johanny "Jaimoe" Johanson. The band's fusion of blues, rock, jazz and country, built around twin lead guitars and extended improvisation, defined Southern rock. The live album At Fillmore East (1971) is widely regarded as one of the greatest live albums ever recorded.

Layla and death

Allman contributed most of the lead and slide guitar to Derek and the Dominos' Layla and Other Assorted Love Songs (1970), including the celebrated slide coda to the title track, after Eric Clapton invited him to the sessions in Miami. On October 29, 1971, in Macon, Georgia, Allman crashed his Harley-Davidson Sportster while swerving to avoid a truck, and died of internal injuries hours later.

Legacy

Rolling Stone ranked Allman among the greatest guitarists of all time, placing him second only to Jimi Hendrix in its original 2003 list. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with the Allman Brothers Band in 1995. His favoured instruments were Gibson Les Pauls, including the 1959 cherry sunburst "Hot 'Lanta" guitar, and he played slide using a Coricidin medicine bottle.
