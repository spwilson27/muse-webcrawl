Ecosystem restoration

Ecosystem restoration is the process of assisting the recovery of an ecosystem that has been degraded, damaged or destroyed. It differs from conservation, which seeks to protect existing ecosystems, in that it actively intervenes to re-establish structure, species composition and ecological functions. Restoration may involve removing the causes of degradation, reintroducing native species, replanting vegetation, restoring natural water flows, or simply allowing natural regeneration to proceed.

The United Nations declared 2021–2030 the UN Decade on Ecosystem Restoration, aiming to prevent, halt and reverse the degradation of ecosystems worldwide. Restoration is promoted as a tool for biodiversity conservation, climate change mitigation and adaptation, and the provision of ecosystem services such as clean water, pollination and carbon storage.

Approaches

Active restoration

Active restoration involves direct interventions such as planting native trees, removing invasive species, re-meandering straightened rivers, or reintroducing extirpated wildlife — a process known as rewilding when it focuses on restoring trophic complexity and natural processes.

Passive restoration

Passive restoration removes pressures such as grazing, logging or pollution and allows ecosystems to recover through natural succession. It is generally cheaper but may be slower, and can fail where seed sources or soil conditions have been lost.

Examples

Large-scale projects include the reforestation of the Loess Plateau in China, the restoration of the Florida Everglades, Atlantic Forest restoration in Brazil, and the Great Green Wall initiative across the Sahel. Wetland restoration is widely practised to recover flood control and water filtration functions.

Challenges

Restoration ecology faces questions about reference conditions — what state a restored ecosystem should aim for, especially under a changing climate — and about timescales, since mature ecosystems may take centuries to develop. Monitoring and community involvement are widely considered essential to success.
