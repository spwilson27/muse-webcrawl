Choke (electronics)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In electronics, a choke is an inductor used to block higher-frequency alternating current (AC) while passing lower-frequency or direct current (DC) in an electrical circuit. A choke usually consists of a coil of insulated wire wound on a magnetic core, though some consist of a doughnut-shaped ferrite bead threaded on a wire. The name reflects the function: the device "chokes" off unwanted high-frequency signals.

Design and types

The choke's impedance increases with frequency, so it presents high resistance to AC ripple and noise but low resistance to DC. Audio-frequency chokes, used in power-supply filters, typically have laminated iron cores and inductance of several henries. Radio-frequency chokes (RFCs) are much smaller, using air or ferrite cores, and are designed to block radio frequencies while passing audio and DC.

Common-mode choke

A common-mode choke has two windings on a single core, wound so that differential (wanted) currents cancel magnetically and pass freely, while common-mode currents — noise flowing in the same direction on both lines — see high impedance and are suppressed. Such chokes are ubiquitous in mains filters and data lines such as USB and Ethernet to reduce electromagnetic interference (EMI).

Applications

Chokes are used in power-supply smoothing (historically in valve/tube equipment, where the choke formed an LC filter with a reservoir capacitor), in fluorescent lamp and discharge-lighting ballasts, in EMI suppression, and as the energy-storage element in switching power supplies and DC–DC converters. Ferrite beads are a miniature form of choke fitted around cables to suppress high-frequency noise.
