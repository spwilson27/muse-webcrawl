Theistic evolution

Theistic evolution (also known as theistic evolutionism or God-guided evolution) is a view that reconciles religious teachings about God with modern scientific understanding about biological evolution. It holds that God created the universe and life and that evolution — change in the heritable characteristics of populations over generations, including natural selection — is the divinely ordained (or at least divinely permitted) means by which life developed.

Overview

Adherents generally accept the scientific consensus on the age of the Earth, the common descent of organisms and the mechanisms of evolution, while maintaining that God is the ultimate author of nature's laws. Theistic evolution differs from creationism in accepting evolution rather than special creation of species, and from a purely naturalistic view in affirming divine purpose or guidance. Related terms include "evolutionary creation", promoted by the organisation BioLogos, founded by geneticist Francis Collins.

History and acceptance

After the publication of Charles Darwin's On the Origin of Species (1859), many theologians and scientists — including Asa Gray, Darwin's chief American advocate — argued that evolution was compatible with faith. Today theistic evolution is the position of the Roman Catholic Church (which since 1950 has permitted belief in evolution of the human body while affirming the special creation of the soul), of many mainline Protestant denominations, and of numerous Jewish and Muslim thinkers. It is rejected by young Earth creationists and by advocates of intelligent design who dispute undisputed evolutionary mechanisms, as well as by atheist critics who regard it as an unnecessary compromise.
