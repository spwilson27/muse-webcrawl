In broadcasting and radio communications, a call sign (also known as a call name or call letters) is a unique identifier for a transmitter station. A call sign can be formally assigned by a government agency, informally adopted by individuals or organisations, or even cryptographically encoded to disguise a station's identity.

Structure and allocation

International call sign series are allocated by the International Telecommunication Union (ITU): each country receives one or more prefixes (letters and/or digits) indicating nationality, followed by a suffix unique to the station. In the United States, broadcast call signs traditionally begin with K west of the Mississippi River and W east of it. Amateur radio call signs encode the operator's licence class and region in many countries.

Uses

Aviation uses call signs such as an airline designator plus flight number (and, in general aviation, the aircraft registration). Ships use maritime call signs linked to their flag registry, and military units use tactical call signs that may change for security. Individual military aviators also receive personal "call signs" — nicknames bestowed by their peers.
