Facsimile

A facsimile (from Latin fac simile, "make alike") is a copy or reproduction of an old book, manuscript, map, art print, or other item of historical value that is as true to the original source as possible. The term is also used for the transmission of scanned printed material by telephone, usually known simply as fax.

Fax technology

Fax machines scan a document, encode the image, and transmit it over the public switched telephone network using protocols standardized by the ITU, principally Group 3 fax with modems operating at up to 33.6 kbit/s. Scottish inventor Alexander Bain received a patent for an early facsimile device in 1843, and commercial services followed in the 1860s with Giovanni Caselli's pantelegraph.

Decline and persistence

Email and document-sharing services displaced faxing from the 1990s onward, but fax remains in use in healthcare, law, government, and Japanese business, where signatures, legacy systems, and regulatory habits sustained the technology longer than expected.

Facsimile editions

In publishing, facsimile editions reproduce manuscripts such as the Book of Kells or early Shakespeare folios for scholars and collectors, preserving layout, handwriting, and defects of the originals.
