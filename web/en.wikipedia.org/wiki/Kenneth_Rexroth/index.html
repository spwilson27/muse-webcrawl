Kenneth Charles Marion Rexroth (22 December 1905 – 6 June 1982) was an American poet, translator and critical essayist. Largely self-educated, he became a central figure of the San Francisco Renaissance and a mentor to the Beat Generation — Allen Ginsberg called him a father figure of the movement — though he famously distanced himself from the Beats' celebrity with the quip that "an entomologist is not a bug".

Life and work

Orphaned young and raised in the Midwest, Rexroth was active in Chicago's avant-garde and anarchist circles before settling in San Francisco in 1927. A conscientious objector during the Second World War, he was instrumental in securing the release of Japanese American poets from internment camps. His libertarian salon and his KPFA radio book reviews made him a hub of West Coast letters. On 7 October 1955 he served as master of ceremonies at the Six Gallery reading, where Ginsberg first performed Howl — the event often taken as the Beat Generation's public debut.

Poetry and translations

Rexroth's own poetry — in collections such as In What Hour (1940), The Phoenix and the Tortoise (1944) and The Signature of All Things (1950) — combines lucid, musical free verse with themes of erotic and sacramental love, the Sierra Nevada wilderness he backpacked for decades, and anarchist and Buddhist thought. His translations, especially One Hundred Poems from the Chinese (1956), his versions of Japanese poetry and of the Greek Anthology, and his translations from French and Spanish, were widely read and helped open postwar American poetry to Asian literature.
