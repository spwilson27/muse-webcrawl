Raimondi Stela

The Raimondi Stela is a carved granite monolith from the archaeological site of Chavín de Huántar in the Peruvian Andes. It is one of the most famous examples of Chavín art, conventionally dated to roughly 900–200 BC.

Description

The stela, nearly two metres tall, depicts the Staff God, a front-facing anthropomorphic figure holding a staff in each hand and wearing an elaborate headdress of radiating appendages ending in animal heads. The carving uses contour rivalry, a technique in which the image changes when inverted: upside down, the headdress becomes a row of grinning faces and the figure reads differently. The stela is named after the Italian-Peruvian naturalist Antonio Raimondi, who drew attention to it in the 19th century; it is now held by the Museo Nacional de Arqueología, Antropología e Historia del Perú in Lima.
