Rossby number

The Rossby number (Ro), named for Carl-Gustav Arvid Rossby, is a dimensionless number used in describing fluid flow, particularly in geophysical fluid dynamics. It is the ratio of inertial forces to Coriolis forces:

Ro = U / (f L)

where U is a characteristic velocity, L a characteristic length scale, and f the Coriolis parameter (f = 2Ω sin φ, with Ω the angular velocity of the rotating body and φ the latitude).

Interpretation

When the Rossby number is small (Ro ≪ 1), Coriolis forces dominate and the flow is strongly affected by rotation: large-scale atmospheric and oceanic flows, such as mid-latitude weather systems and ocean gyres, have Ro of order 0.1 or less and are close to geostrophic balance. When the Rossby number is large (Ro ≫ 1), inertial forces dominate and rotation can be neglected — as in small-scale phenomena such as tornadoes (Ro ~ 10³) or water draining in a bathtub, contrary to popular myth.

Related quantities

A temporal Rossby number, RoT = 1/(f T), compares the Coriolis timescale to a characteristic time T. The Rossby number appears throughout rotating fluid dynamics, including in the theory of Rossby waves — large-scale planetary waves driven by the variation of f with latitude — and in the classification of flow regimes in oceans, atmospheres and stellar interiors.
