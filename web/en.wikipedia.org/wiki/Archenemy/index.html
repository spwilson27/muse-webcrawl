Archenemy

An archenemy (also spelled arch-enemy) or archnemesis is the main enemy of a character in a work of fiction, or, by extension, the principal adversary of a real person or group. In storytelling, the archenemy is typically the antagonist most personally opposed to the protagonist: the two are bound together by history, ideology, or obsession, and their conflict often defines the narrative. The term derives from the Greek prefix archi- ("chief") combined with "enemy", and was historically applied to Satan as the chief adversary of humanity.

Characteristics

An archenemy differs from an ordinary villain in the depth and permanence of the relationship. Archenemies usually recur across a hero's adventures, know the hero intimately, and mirror the hero's abilities or origins — sometimes as a dark inversion of them. Narratives frequently make the pair interdependent: each gives the other's story meaning, so that the hero cannot be fully understood without the villain, and vice versa. Moriarty, created by Arthur Conan Doyle as a worthy opponent for Sherlock Holmes, is the archetype in detective fiction, though he appeared in only a handful of stories.

Examples in fiction

Famous pairings include Superman and Lex Luthor, Batman and the Joker, Spider-Man and the Green Goblin, the Doctor and the Master in Doctor Who, Harry Potter and Lord Voldemort, and Luke Skywalker and Darth Vader in Star Wars. In older literature, Satan functions as the archenemy of God and mankind in Paradise Lost, and Captain Ahab's obsession casts the whale Moby Dick as his nemesis. Frequently the archenemy is the one villain a hero cannot permanently defeat, because the rivalry itself sustains an ongoing series.

Use outside fiction

In journalism and everyday speech, the word is applied loosely to bitter rivals in politics, sport, and business, and to the foremost opponent of an institution — for example, a nation's principal military adversary or a disease described as medicine's archenemy.
