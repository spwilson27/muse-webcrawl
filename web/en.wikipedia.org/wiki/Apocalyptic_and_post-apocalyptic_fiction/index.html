Apocalyptic and post-apocalyptic fiction

Apocalyptic fiction concerns the end of civilisation through catastrophe such as nuclear war, pandemic, climate change, alien invasion or supernatural judgement. Post-apocalyptic fiction is set after such a catastrophe and follows survivors in a world where social order, technology and population have collapsed or radically changed.

History

Early examples include Mary Shelley's The Last Man (1826), about a plague-emptied world. The genre expanded in the twentieth century with works such as Richard Matheson's I Am Legend, John Wyndham's The Day of the Triffids, Walter M. Miller Jr.'s A Canticle for Leibowitz and Cormac McCarthy's The Road. Nuclear anxieties during the Cold War made the destroyed-world setting especially prominent.

Themes

Post-apocalyptic stories often explore survival, scarcity, the rebuilding or failure of communities, moral choices under pressure and the fragility of modern infrastructure. Settings range from realistic ruins to stylised wastelands in film, television, comics and video games, including franchises such as Mad Max and Fallout.

Subtypes

Catastrophes may be natural, technological, extraterrestrial or religious. Some works focus on the event itself, while others begin generations later, when the lost civilisation survives only as legend or ruins.
