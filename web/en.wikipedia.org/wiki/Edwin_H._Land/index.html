Edwin Herbert Land (May 7, 1909 – March 1, 1991) was an American scientist and inventor, best known as co-founder of the Polaroid Corporation. He invented inexpensive filters for polarizing light, a practical system of in-camera instant photography, and the retinex theory of color vision, among other things.

Instant photography

Legend holds that Land conceived instant photography after his young daughter asked why she could not see a photograph immediately. The first Polaroid Land Camera went on sale in 1948, and the fully integral SX-70 system followed in 1972.

Legacy

Land held more than 500 patents and was one of the most prolific American inventors. He also advised the U.S. government on reconnaissance technology, including the U-2 spy plane programme, and co-founded the Rowland Institute for Science.
