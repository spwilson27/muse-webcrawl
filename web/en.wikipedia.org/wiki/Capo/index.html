Capo

This article is about the guitar accessory. For other uses, see Capo (disambiguation).

A capo (short for capotasto, Italian for "head of fretboard") is a device a musician uses on the neck of a stringed instrument (typically a guitar, mandolin, banjo, or ukulele) to shorten the playable length of the strings, thereby raising the pitch. It is a clamp that presses the strings against a fret, acting as a movable nut or "zero fret", allowing the player to use open-string fingerings and chord shapes in different keys without retuning.

Use

By placing a capo at, for example, the second fret, a guitarist can play an E-major chord shape that sounds as F♯ major, preserving the ringing open-string voicings of the original shape. Capos are widely used in folk, country, flamenco (where the device is called a cejilla), pop, and singer-songwriter styles to match a song to a singer's vocal range, to change the timbre of open chords, or to let two guitarists play the same harmony in different positions. Partial capos, which press only some strings, are used to simulate alternate tunings.

Types

  • Spring/clamp capos — squeezed open and released onto the neck; quick to move with one hand (e.g., the Kyser style).
  • Trigger and lever capos — similar quick-action designs.
  • Screw/elastic capos — adjustable tension, including older elastic-strap models.
  • Roller capos — can be rolled up or down the neck between or within songs.
  • Spider and partial capos — clamp individual strings.

History

Precursors of the capo appear in the 17th and 18th centuries for lutes and guitars; the Spanish cejilla was in use for flamenco by the 19th century. The word derives from Italian capo tasto, "head fret". Modern commercial designs proliferated in the 20th century as the guitar became central to popular music.

Other meanings

"Capo" (Italian for "head" or "boss") also denotes a rank in Mafia organisations — a caporegime or capodecina who leads a crew of soldiers — and appears in terms such as da capo ("from the head") in musical notation.
