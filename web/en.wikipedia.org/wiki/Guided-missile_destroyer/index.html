Guided-missile destroyer

A guided-missile destroyer (hull classification symbol DDG in the United States Navy) is a destroyer whose primary armament is guided missiles, typically deployed for fleet air defence, anti-submarine warfare and land-attack strike missions.

Development

The type emerged in the 1950s as surface-to-air missiles replaced guns as the main anti-aircraft weapon of escorts. Early classes such as the American Charles F. Adams class were followed by the Spruance-derived Ticonderoga cruisers and, from 1991, the Arleigh Burke class, built around the Aegis Combat System and vertical launching systems. Comparable ships serve in many navies, including Japan's Atago class, China's Type 052D, and the Royal Navy's Type 45 Daring class.

Role

Modern guided-missile destroyers are multi-mission warships capable of area air and ballistic missile defence, anti-surface and anti-submarine warfare, and Tomahawk cruise-missile strikes, usually operating as escorts within a carrier strike group or as independent units.
