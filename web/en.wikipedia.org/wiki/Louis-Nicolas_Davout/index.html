Louis-Nicolas Davout

Louis-Nicolas Davout (10 May 1770 – 1 June 1823), better known as Davout, 1st Duke of Auerstaedt and 1st Prince of Eckmühl, was a French military commander and Marshal of the Empire who served during the French Revolutionary Wars and Napoleonic Wars. Undefeated in battle, he is widely regarded as one of Napoleon's finest commanders, known as "the Iron Marshal" for his discipline and rigour.

Career

Born at Annoux in Burgundy to a family of minor nobility, Davout was educated at the military academy at Brienne and in Paris. Rising through the revolutionary armies, he served in Egypt and distinguished himself at Marengo. In 1804 he was among the first marshals created by Napoleon and was given command of III Corps of the Grande Armée, which he trained to an exceptional standard.

Auerstedt and later service

His greatest achievement came on 14 October 1806 at the Battle of Auerstedt, where his single corps of about 26,000 men defeated the main Prussian army of more than twice that strength on the same day Napoleon won at Jena. He also fought with distinction at Eckmühl and Wagram in 1809 and commanded the largest corps in the invasion of Russia in 1812. During the Hundred Days he served as Napoleon's Minister of War and organised the defence of Paris. After the Bourbon Restoration he was stripped of his titles, later partially restored, and died in Paris in 1823.
