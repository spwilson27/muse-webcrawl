Word (computer architecture)

In computing, a word is the natural unit of data used by a particular processor design: the fixed-sized group of bits handled as a unit by the instruction set and hardware registers.

Word size

Common word sizes have been 8, 16, 24, 32, 48, 60 and 64 bits. Modern general-purpose processors are predominantly 64-bit, meaning registers, addresses and data paths are 64 bits wide, allowing direct addressing of vast memory spaces. Word size affects precision, addressable memory, and performance.

Terminology

On x86-derived conventions, a 'word' historically means 16 bits, with DWORD (32) and QWORD (64) for larger units — a source of confusion distinct from the architectural sense.
