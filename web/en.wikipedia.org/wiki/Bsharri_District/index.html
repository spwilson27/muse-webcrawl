Bsharri District (Arabic: قضاء بشري, Qaḍāʾ Bšarrī) is one of the seven districts of the North Governorate of Lebanon. Its capital is the town of Bsharri. The district lies in the mountainous north of the country, encompassing part of the Mount Lebanon range and the upper Qadisha Valley.

Geography

The district includes Qurnat as Sawda', the highest peak in Lebanon and the Levant at 3,088 metres, and the Cedars of God forest near Bsharri, a remnant of the ancient cedar forests and a UNESCO World Heritage site as part of the Qadisha Valley listing. Skiing is practised at the Cedars resort in winter.

Population and heritage

The population is predominantly Maronite Christian. The town of Bsharri is the birthplace of the poet and artist Khalil Gibran, whose museum and tomb are located there. The Qadisha ("Holy") Valley, with its rock-cut monasteries such as Qozhaya and Mar Elisha, has been a centre of Maronite monasticism since the early medieval period.
