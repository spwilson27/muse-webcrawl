Basic English is a controlled language based on standard English, created by the British linguist and philosopher C. K. Ogden and described in his book Basic English: A General Introduction with Rules and Grammar (1930). It uses a core vocabulary of only 850 words — including just 18 verbs ("operators") — supplemented by rules for forming plurals, derivatives and compounds, and by additional word lists for science, commerce and general purposes.

History

Ogden, working with I. A. Richards, promoted Basic English as an international second language and a means of clearer thinking. It attracted notable interest in the 1930s and 1940s; Winston Churchill spoke in its favour in 1943, though he later grew cooler. The system influenced later controlled languages, including the Voice of America's Special English (now Learning English), and the Simple English Wikipedia uses an approach inspired by it.

Criticism

Critics have noted that expressing ideas with 18 verbs requires heavy reliance on phrasal combinations and idiom, which can be harder for learners than a larger vocabulary, and that the selection of the 850 words reflects Ogden's own theoretical commitments.
