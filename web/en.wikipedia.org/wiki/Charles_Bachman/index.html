Charles Bachman

Charles William Bachman III (11 December 1924 – 13 July 2017) was an American computer scientist who spent his career as an industrial researcher rather than in academia. He is regarded as one of the founding figures of database management: at General Electric in the 1960s he led the development of the Integrated Data Store (IDS), one of the first database management systems, and his work on the network data model shaped the CODASYL database standards. He received the Turing Award in 1973 for "his outstanding contributions to database technology."

Career

Bachman studied mechanical engineering at Michigan State University and earned a master's degree from the University of Pennsylvania. After working at Dow Chemical, he joined General Electric in 1960, where his team built IDS for GE's manufacturing systems; IDS ran in production by 1964 and demonstrated that applications could navigate a shared, structured database instead of maintaining private files. He became a central figure in the CODASYL Database Task Group, whose 1971 report standardized the network model. His 1973 Turing Award lecture, "The Programmer as Navigator," memorably contrasted record-at-a-time navigation with the emerging set-oriented, relational approach of Edgar F. Codd.

Later work

Bachman later worked at Honeywell and Cullinane Database Systems, and in 1983 founded Bachman Information Systems, which developed computer-aided software engineering (CASE) tools for data modelling and reverse engineering of databases. His graphical notation for data structure diagrams — Bachman diagrams — was widely used in data modelling. He received the National Medal of Technology and Innovation from President Barack Obama in 2014.
