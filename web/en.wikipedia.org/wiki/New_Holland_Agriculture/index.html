New Holland Agriculture

New Holland is an agricultural machinery manufacturer and a global brand of CNH Industrial. Founded in New Holland, Pennsylvania, in 1895, the company produces tractors, combine harvesters, balers, forage harvesters, seeders and other farm equipment, sold in around 170 countries.

History

The company began when Abram Zimmerman opened a repair shop in New Holland, Pennsylvania, in 1895; it became known for stationary hay balers and, from 1940, the first successful automatic pickup hay baler. It was acquired by the Sperry Corporation in 1947 and later became part of Ford's tractor operations. Ford New Holland acquired the Versatile tractor company and in 1991 Fiat Geotech took a controlling stake, merging the businesses under the New Holland name. In 1999 New Holland merged with Case Corporation to form CNH Global, now part of CNH Industrial.

Products

New Holland's range includes the T series tractors, CR and CX combine harvesters, BigBaler large square balers, and FR forage harvesters. The brand has promoted alternative-fuel machinery, including the methane-powered T6 Methane Power tractor, as part of its "Clean Energy Leader" strategy, and has developed autonomous and precision-farming technologies.
