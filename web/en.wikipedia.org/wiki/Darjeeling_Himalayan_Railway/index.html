Darjeeling Himalayan Railway

The Darjeeling Himalayan Railway, also known as the DHR or the "Toy Train", is a 610 mm (2 ft) narrow-gauge railway in the Indian state of West Bengal, running about 88 km (55 mi) between New Jalpaiguri and Darjeeling. Built between 1879 and 1881, it climbs from about 100 m (328 ft) above sea level at New Jalpaiguri to about 2,200 m (7,218 ft) at Darjeeling, using six zig-zags and five loops to gain altitude.

In 1999 UNESCO designated the DHR a World Heritage Site, later extended as part of the Mountain Railways of India listing.

History

Proposed by Franklin Prestage of the Eastern Bengal Railway, construction began in 1879; the line reached Darjeeling in July 1881. It served the hill station's tea industry and its role as a summer retreat from Calcutta. The railway was nationalised in 1948 and is now operated by Indian Railways (Northeast Frontier Railway). Its headquarters and workshops are at Kurseong and Tindharia.

Route and engineering

The line follows the Hill Cart Road for much of its length, passing Siliguri, Sukna, Tindharia, Kurseong, Mahanadi, Sonada, Tung, Ghum and Batasia before Darjeeling. The most famous feature is the Batasia Loop near Ghum, a spiral loop offering panoramic views of Kangchenjunga. The class B steam locomotives, built between 1889 and 1927, remain the line's emblem, supplemented by modern diesel locomotives on the lower section.

Heritage and tourism

UNESCO inscribed the railway in 1999 as an outstanding example of a bold, ingenious engineering solution to mountain transport. Joyride services between Darjeeling and Ghum are especially popular with tourists. Landslides occasionally interrupt operations, notably in 2010–2015 when a section near Paglajhora was closed for several years.
