The School of Computer Science (SCS) at Carnegie Mellon University in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, is one of the world's leading institutions for research and education in computer science. It consistently ranks at or near the top of graduate computer-science rankings and was one of the first standalone schools of computer science in the United States.

History

Computing at Carnegie Mellon grew from work by Allen Newell and Herbert A. Simon in the 1950s; a Computer Science Department was founded in 1965, and the Robotics Institute in 1979. In 1988 the department was elevated to a school. The school's headquarters since 2009 has been the Gates and Hillman Centers on the university's Forbes Avenue campus.

Organisation and research

SCS comprises seven units, including the Computer Science Department, the Robotics Institute, the Machine Learning Department (the first such department anywhere), the Language Technologies Institute and the Human–Computer Interaction Institute. Research strengths include artificial intelligence, machine learning, robotics, computer vision, natural-language processing, software systems and human–computer interaction. Notable achievements include the Navlab autonomous vehicles, the Tartan Racing team that won the 2007 DARPA Urban Challenge, and foundational work in AI by Turing Award laureates including Newell, Simon, Raj Reddy and Manuel Blum.

Education

The school offers a highly selective undergraduate Bachelor of Science in Computer Science — the first such undergraduate programme at CMU, established in 1989 — as well as master's and doctoral programmes and interdisciplinary majors in artificial intelligence, computational biology and human–computer interaction.
