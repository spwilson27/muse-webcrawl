Bounding volume hierarchy

A bounding volume hierarchy (BVH) is a tree data structure in which geometric objects are enclosed in bounding volumes that are grouped into progressively larger volumes toward the root. BVHs are the standard acceleration structure for ray tracing, and are also used in collision detection, physics simulation and visibility culling: a query that misses a parent volume can skip its entire subtree without testing the individual objects inside.

[ Image: Nested bounding boxes of a 3D scene arranged as a tree, from root volume to leaf primitives ]

Structure

Each node stores a bounding volume — most commonly an axis-aligned bounding box (AABB) — that encloses all geometry beneath it. Leaf nodes reference a small number of primitives, typically triangles. Building a good BVH usually proceeds top-down: objects are split at the median or by a cost heuristic such as the surface-area heuristic (SAH), which chooses splits minimising the expected cost of traversal. Bottom-up and insertion-based builders are also used, especially for dynamic scenes.

Traversal

For a ray query, the algorithm tests the ray against the root box; only children whose boxes are hit are visited, recursively, usually in front-to-back order, until leaf primitives are intersected exactly. Because whole subtrees are pruned, intersection cost grows roughly logarithmically with scene size rather than linearly. Modern ray-tracing APIs and GPUs (for example via DXR and Vulkan ray tracing) build and traverse BVHs in hardware.

Related structures

BVHs differ from space-subdivision structures such as octrees and kd-trees in that their volumes may overlap and are attached to objects rather than to space. Hybrid and multi-level schemes (such as two-level BVHs for instanced geometry) are standard in production renderers.
