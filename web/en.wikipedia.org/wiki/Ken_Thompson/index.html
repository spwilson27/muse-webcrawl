Ken Thompson

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Ken Thompson, portrait photograph with beard at a terminal]

Kenneth Lane Thompson (born 1943) is an American pioneer of computer science. He worked at Bell Labs for most of his career, where he designed and implemented the original Unix operating system (1969) with Dennis Ritchie, and created the B programming language, a precursor of C.

Thompson also co-created the UTF-8 character encoding (with Rob Pike, 1992) — now the dominant encoding of the web — and Plan 9, and later at Google co-created the Go programming language (announced 2009). With Ritchie he received the 1983 Turing Award.

Work

Thompson wrote an early, formidable chess program (Belle, with Joe Condon) that won computer chess championships around 1980. His 1983 Turing lecture, 'Reflections on Trusting Trust', showed how a compiler backdoor could be self-perpetuating and undetectable — a foundational text of computer security, relevant to modern supply-chain attacks.

At Bell Labs he also built the QED editor, contributed regular-expression implementations (his 1968 paper and the grep lineage), and worked on the Multics predecessor environment before Unix.

Recognition

National Medal of Technology (1998, with Ritchie), Japan Prize (2011), and the IEEE John von Neumann Medal among his honours. His terse, pragmatic style ('When in doubt, use brute force' is attributed to him) remains a design touchstone.
