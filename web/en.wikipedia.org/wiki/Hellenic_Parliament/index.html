Hellenic Parliament
Type: Unicameral
Seats: 300
Meeting place: Old Royal Palace, Syntagma Square, Athens

The Hellenic Parliament (Greek: Ελληνικό Κοινοβούλιο, romanized: Elliniko Kinovoulio), in English also called the Greek Parliament, is the unicameral legislature of Greece, located in the Old Royal Palace in central Athens, overlooking Syntagma Square. It consists of 300 Members of Parliament (βουλευτές) elected for four-year terms.

Electoral system

Members are elected by direct popular vote under a system of proportional representation with a majority bonus: since reforms, the winning party receives bonus seats so that it can form a stable government; under the rules applied in 2023, the first party receives a sliding bonus of up to 50 seats depending on its vote share. A 3% national threshold applies for entry into Parliament. Voting is compulsory in principle for citizens aged 17 and over, though sanctions are not enforced.

Functions

The Parliament enacts legislation, approves the budget, elects the President of the Republic, and grants or withdraws confidence in the government. It can revise the Constitution subject to special procedures, ratify international treaties, and exercise parliamentary control over the executive. Its presiding officer is the Speaker (President of the Hellenic Parliament).

History

Representative institutions in modern Greece date from the revolutionary assemblies of the 1820s and the constitution of 1844, which established an elected chamber. The Parliament has occupied the Old Royal Palace, built for King Otto in 1836–1843, since 1935, after moving from the building now known as the Old Parliament House.
