Gaslamp Quarter, San Diego

LocationDowntown San Diego, California, U.S.
Area16.5 blocks (about 38 acres)
Built1867–1930
Architectural styleVictorian commercial

The Gaslamp Quarter is a historic neighbourhood in downtown San Diego, California. It extends across 16½ blocks bounded roughly by Broadway and Harbor Drive and Fourth and Sixth Avenues, and contains some 94 historic buildings, most built in the Victorian era. Today it is the city's principal dining, nightlife and entertainment district.

History

The area began to develop in the 1860s when Alonzo Horton built a wharf at the foot of Fifth Avenue and promoted "New Town" San Diego near the bay. In its early decades the quarter was home to saloons, gambling halls and brothels; the Stingaree red-light district flourished there, and Wyatt Earp ran gambling halls in the neighbourhood in the 1880s.

After decades of decline, preservationists in the 1970s campaigned to save the district's Victorian commercial buildings. The Gaslamp Quarter was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1980, and redevelopment transformed it into a popular entertainment zone, symbolised by the Gaslamp Quarter Archway sign erected over Fifth Avenue in 1990. The name refers to the gas lamps that once lit the streets; replica lamps remain a feature of the district.
