Technical communication

Technical communication is the communication of technical or specialised topics using technology, primarily through documents and digital media.

Technical communication (or Tech Comm) is communication of technical subject matter—such as scientific and engineering information, instructions, and specifications—by a professional in the field, or communication translated by a technical writer for a non-technical audience. It encompasses user manuals, online help, API documentation, white papers, standard operating procedures, proposals, and instructional video, among many other genres.

Practice

Technical communicators analyse audiences and tasks, structure information for findability and reuse, write in plain language, and design documents with typography, graphics and layout that support comprehension and safe use. Modern practice relies on structured authoring standards such as DITA and tools for content management, single-source publishing and localisation. Work is governed by usability testing, accessibility requirements and, in regulated industries, legal standards for warnings and documentation.

History and profession

Instructional and technical writing has roots in antiquity, but the profession took shape during the Second World War with the documentation demands of military technology, expanding with the postwar aerospace, computing and software industries. Professional bodies include the Society for Technical Communication (founded 1953) and IEEE's Professional Communication Society; university programmes award degrees and certificates in technical communication, and practitioners specialise in areas such as UX writing, information architecture and content strategy.
