Cannery Row (novel)

Cannery Row is a novel by American author John Steinbeck, published in 1945. Set in Monterey, California, during the Great Depression, it follows a community of people living along a street lined with sardine canneries — Cannery Row.

Plot

The loosely connected episodes centre on Doc, a marine biologist based on Steinbeck's friend Ed Ricketts, and Mack and the boys, a group of good-hearted down-and-out men living in the Palace Flophouse. Twice they plan a party for Doc; the first ends in disaster, and the second, better-planned celebration becomes a warm affirmation of the community.

Themes

Steinbeck portrays the Row's inhabitants — prostitutes, grocers, bums and biologists — with humour and compassion, finding dignity in people on the margins of society, and weaving in intercalary chapters of observation on nature and human behaviour.

Reception and adaptations

The novel was a bestseller. A sequel, Sweet Thursday (1954), continued the story, and a film adaptation of Cannery Row appeared in 1982 starring Nick Nolte and Debra Winger.
