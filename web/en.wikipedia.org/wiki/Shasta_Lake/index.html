Shasta Lake

Location: Shasta County, California
Type: Reservoir
Surface area: ~30,000 acres (121 km²) at full pool
Capacity: ~4.55 million acre-feet
Formed by: Shasta Dam (completed 1945)

Shasta Lake is a reservoir in Shasta County, California, created by the construction of Shasta Dam across the Sacramento River. With a capacity of about 4.55 million acre-feet, it is the largest reservoir in California by storage volume, and a key component of the federal Central Valley Project, storing water for irrigation, flood control, hydroelectric power, and downstream water quality.

Geography

The lake has four major arms, formed where its principal inflow rivers — the Sacramento, the McCloud, the Squaw Creek, and the Pit River — were submerged. At full pool it has roughly 365 miles (587 km) of shoreline, set among forested mountain terrain south of Mount Shasta. The city of Shasta Lake (formerly Central Valley) grew from the construction camps built for dam workers in the late 1930s.

Recreation

Shasta Lake is a major recreation area within the Shasta–Trinity National Forest, popular for houseboating, fishing (including bass, trout, and salmon), water-skiing, and camping. Nearby attractions include the Lake Shasta Caverns. The reservoir's level fluctuates sharply with drought cycles in California, and falling water levels sometimes expose the remains of settlements flooded by its creation.
