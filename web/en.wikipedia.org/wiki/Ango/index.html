Ango (Japanese: あんご) is a Japanese confection (wagashi) from the Niigata region, consisting of a small slab or roll of castella sponge cake sandwiched with a thick layer of sweet red bean paste — anko — or, in a popular modern variant, matcha-flavoured bean paste. The name is a play on ango, the Buddhist term for a period of intensive secluded retreat, and on anko itself.

Description

Ango is typically sold as individual rectangular portions: two thin slices of golden castella enclose a filling of sweetened azuki bean paste nearly as thick as the cake layers combined. The matcha version uses green tea–flavoured white bean paste and is especially associated with Niigata's tea-growing districts. The confection is served at room temperature, often with green tea, and keeps for several days sealed.

Origin

The sweet was popularised by confectioners in Niigata Prefecture in the early 21st century as a regional souvenir (meibutsu). Its witty name — suggesting a monk's retreat sustained by bean paste — helped it gain attention in Japanese media, and it has since been sold at department-store food halls and regional product fairs across Japan.
