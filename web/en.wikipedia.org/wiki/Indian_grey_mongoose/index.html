Indian grey mongoose

Scientific name: Urva edwardsii

Family: Herpestidae

Range: Indian subcontinent and West Asia

Conservation status: Least Concern

The Indian grey mongoose (Urva edwardsii) is a mongoose species native to the Indian subcontinent and West Asia. It is a small carnivore with grizzled grey-brown fur, short legs, and a long, bushy tail, and it is famous for its ability to fight and kill venomous snakes such as cobras — a trait celebrated in Rudyard Kipling's story "Rikki-Tikki-Tavi".

Description

Adults measure roughly 36–45 cm in head-and-body length with a tail of similar length, and weigh between about 0.9 and 1.7 kg. The coarse fur appears grizzled because of alternating light and dark bands on the hairs. Compared with the smaller Indian mongoose, the grey mongoose is larger, paler, and has a tail tip that is dark or reddish rather than white.

Behaviour and diet

The species is mainly diurnal and largely solitary. It hunts rats, mice, lizards, snakes, birds, eggs, and invertebrates, and it also scavenges carrion. Its snake-fighting ability depends on speed and agility rather than true immunity, although its altered acetylcholine receptors give it partial resistance to cobra venom. Females give birth to litters of two to four young, often in burrows or termite-mound cavities.

Distribution and conservation

The Indian grey mongoose ranges from the Arabian Peninsula and Iran through Pakistan and Nepal to India, Sri Lanka, and Bangladesh, inhabiting open forest, scrubland, and cultivated fields, and frequently living near human habitation. It is listed as Least Concern by the IUCN, though it is persecuted in places, trapped for the brush-hair trade, and killed by traffic.
