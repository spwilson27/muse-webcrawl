South Pacific (musical)

South Pacific is a musical composed by Richard Rodgers, with lyrics by Oscar Hammerstein II and book by Hammerstein and Joshua Logan. The work premiered on Broadway in 1949 and was an immediate hit, running for 1,925 performances and winning ten Tony Awards, including Best Musical. It remains the only musical production ever to win all four Tony Awards for acting. The plot centers on an American nurse stationed on a South Pacific island during World War II, who falls in love with a middle-aged French plantation owner but struggles to accept his mixed-race children. A secondary romance, between a U.S. lieutenant and a young Tonkinese woman, explores his fears of the social consequences of marrying an Asian woman.

Background

The musical is based on James A. Michener's Pulitzer Prize–winning 1947 book Tales of the South Pacific, combining several of its stories. Rodgers and Hammerstein believed they could write a musical that would be both commercially successful and send a strong progressive message about racism — most famously in the song "You've Got to Be Carefully Taught," whose pointed lyric survived threats to cut it during the production's Southern tour.

Plot

On a South Pacific island during the war, U.S. Navy nurse Nellie Forbush of Little Rock, Arkansas, falls for French planter Emile de Becque, whose late wife was Polynesian. Nellie's ingrained prejudices nearly destroy the romance. Meanwhile, Lieutenant Joe Cable falls in love with Liat, the daughter of Bloody Mary, a Tonkinese trader, but fears his family's reaction back home. Cable is killed on a reconnaissance mission, and Nellie overcomes her prejudice, returning to Emile and his children.

Musical numbers

The score produced several standards, including "Some Enchanted Evening," "Bali Ha'i," "I'm Gonna Wash That Man Right Outa My Hair," "This Nearly Was Mine," "Younger Than Springtime," "There Is Nothing Like a Dame," and "Happy Talk."

Productions and adaptations

The original production starred Mary Martin as Nellie and Ezio Pinza as Emile. A film version followed in 1958, and a television film in 2001. The 2008 Broadway revival at Lincoln Center won seven Tony Awards, including Best Revival of a Musical.
