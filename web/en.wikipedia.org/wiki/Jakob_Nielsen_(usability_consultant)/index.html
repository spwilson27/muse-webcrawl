Jakob Nielsen (born October 5, 1957) is a Danish web usability consultant, human–computer interaction researcher, and co-founder of the Nielsen Norman Group with Donald Norman. He is best known for his "10 Usability Heuristics" for user interface design and for advocating "discount usability engineering" — inexpensive, fast evaluation methods such as heuristic evaluation and testing with small numbers of users.

Career

Nielsen earned a PhD in human–computer interaction from the Technical University of Denmark. He worked at Bellcore and IBM before joining Sun Microsystems in 1994 as a Distinguished Engineer, where he worked on the usability of Sun's websites and was the company's lead usability specialist. In 1998 he co-founded the Nielsen Norman Group, a usability consultancy.

Work

Since 1995 Nielsen published the Alertbox newsletter, whose columns on web usability reached a wide audience of designers. His books include Usability Engineering (1993), Designing Web Usability: The Practice of Simplicity (1999), and Prioritizing Web Usability (2006, with Hoa Loranger). His ten heuristics — including visibility of system status, consistency and standards, error prevention, and recognition rather than recall — remain a standard teaching framework. He also coined heuristics-based findings such as the observation that users typically read only a fraction of the words on a web page, and promoted the F-shaped reading pattern identified through eye-tracking studies. Nielsen's sometimes blunt pronouncements (for example, that "users don't read" and his long-running criticism of Flash and PDF for web content) have drawn both influence and controversy in the design community.
