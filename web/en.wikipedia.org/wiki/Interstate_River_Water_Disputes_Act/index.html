The Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956 is an Act of the Parliament of India enacted under Article 262 of the Constitution of India, which empowers Parliament to provide by law for the adjudication of disputes relating to waters of inter-state rivers and river valleys, and to exclude the jurisdiction of the courts in such matters.

Mechanism

Under the Act, a state government may request the central government to refer a water dispute to a tribunal for adjudication if it believes its interests are affected. The central government first attempts to resolve the dispute by negotiation; if that fails, it constitutes a tribunal chaired by a sitting or retired judge, whose award, once published, is final and binding on the parties and has the force of a decree of the Supreme Court.

Disputes

Tribunals constituted under the Act have addressed some of India's most contentious inter-state conflicts, including the Godavari, Krishna, Narmada, Ravi-Beas and Cauvery disputes. The Cauvery tribunal, constituted in 1990, delivered its final award in 2007, and implementation disputes continued before the Supreme Court, leading to the creation of the Cauvery Water Management Authority in 2018.

Amendments and reform

Amendments in 2002 set time limits for tribunals to give their awards and for the Centre to constitute them. A 2019 amendment bill proposed replacing the multiplicity of tribunals with a single permanent Inter-State River Water Disputes Tribunal, with a disputes resolution committee to attempt negotiated settlement first; the bill was passed by the Lok Sabha but drew objections from several states.
