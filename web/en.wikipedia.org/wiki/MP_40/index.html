MP 40

The MP 40 (Maschinenpistole 40) is a submachine gun chambered for the 9×19mm Parabellum cartridge. It was developed in Germany by Erma Werke and used extensively by the Axis powers during the Second World War.

MP 40
Type: Submachine gun
Origin: Germany
Designer: Heinrich Vollmer / Erma Werke
Cartridge: 9×19mm Parabellum
Produced: 1940–1945 (over 1 million)

Design

The MP 40 is a blowback-operated, open-bolt weapon made largely of stamped steel, with a folding metal stock and Bakelite fittings. It fires only in fully automatic mode at about 500–550 rounds per minute from a 32-round magazine.

Service

Derived from the earlier MP 38, the MP 40 was issued to infantry squad leaders, paratroopers and armoured vehicle crews. Allied troops sometimes incorrectly called it the "Schmeisser", although Hugo Schmeisser was not its designer.
