Maya Angelou

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Maya Angelou reciting at the presidential inauguration in January 1993: the poet at the lectern in winter sunlight]

Maya Angelou (born Marguerite Annie Johnson; 4 April 1928 – 28 May 2014) was an American memoirist, poet and civil rights activist. Her seven-volume autobiography, beginning with I Know Why the Caged Bird Sings (1969) — a landmark account of Black girlhood, rape, muteness and self-making in the segregated South — made her one of the most read and taught American writers, and her poem 'On the Pulse of Morning', delivered at President Clinton's inauguration in 1993, made her a national voice.

Angelou's early life reads like a century: raised in Stamps, Arkansas; silent for nearly five years after her rapist's murder; San Francisco's first Black woman streetcar conductor as a teenager; dancer, singer (the calypso album Miss Calypso, 1957), actress in Porgy and Bess on tour; coordinator for Martin Luther King Jr.'s SCLC; journalist and editor in Egypt and Ghana in the independence years, among Malcolm X's circle.

The autobiographies and poetry

Caged Bird, encouraged by James Baldwin and edited by Robert Loomis, broke the frame of American memoir; sequels (Gather Together in My Name, The Heart of a Woman and others) traced her to Ghana and back. Her poetry collections (Just Give Me a Cool Drink of Water 'fore I Diiie, 1971; 'Still I Rise', 1978) work in sermon, blues and anthem; the performance of the poems, in her trained, majestic voice, was inseparable from their effect.

Honours

Angelou received the National Medal of Arts (2000) and the Presidential Medal of Freedom (2011); her image appeared on a US postage stamp in 2015 and on the American quarter in 2022, the first in the American Women Quarters series. She died in Winston-Salem, North Carolina, where she had taught at Wake Forest University for three decades.
