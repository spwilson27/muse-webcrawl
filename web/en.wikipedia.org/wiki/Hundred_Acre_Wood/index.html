Hundred Acre Wood

The Hundred Acre Wood is the fictional forest setting of A. A. Milne's Winnie-the-Pooh stories (1926) and The House at Pooh Corner (1928). It is the home of Winnie-the-Pooh, Piglet, Eeyore, Owl, Rabbit, Kanga, Roo, and Tigger, and of their friend Christopher Robin. The wood was inspired by the Five Hundred Acre Wood in Ashdown Forest, East Sussex, England, near Milne's country home at Cotchford Farm, Hartfield.

The Hundred Acre Wood as drawn by E. H. Shepard in the original books.

Locations

Familiar places within the wood include Pooh's house, inscribed "Mr Sanders"; Piglet's house in a beech tree; Owl's house, the Chestnuts; Eeyore's Gloomy Place; Rabbit's burrow; the House at Pooh Corner; the Poohsticks Bridge; the Heffalump Trap; Galleons Leap; the Sandy Pit Where Roo Plays; the Six Pine Trees; and the Enchanted Place at the top of the forest, where, in the final chapter, Christopher Robin takes his leave of the animals.

Inspiration

Many of the locations can be identified in Ashdown Forest: the Five Hundred Acre Wood itself, Gills Lap (Galleons Lap), and the bridge rebuilt in 1979 and officially renamed Poohsticks Bridge, where the game of Poohsticks described in The House at Pooh Corner is still played by visitors. E. H. Shepard's illustrations drew directly on the Ashdown landscape of pine trees and gorse.

In other media

The Hundred Acre Wood has been carried into the many Disney adaptations of Winnie the Pooh beginning in 1966, into stage and television versions, and into the 2018 film Christopher Robin, much of which was filmed in and around Ashdown Forest and Windsor Great Park.
