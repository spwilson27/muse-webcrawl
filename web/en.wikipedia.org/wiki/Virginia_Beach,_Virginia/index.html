Virginia Beach, Virginia

Virginia Beach is an independent city on the southeastern coast of Virginia, United States, at the mouth of the Chesapeake Bay on the Atlantic Ocean. With a population of about 458,000 at the 2020 census, it is the most populous city in Virginia and the 43rd largest in the United States. It is the principal city of the Hampton Roads metropolitan area.

History

The area was the site of the first landing of English colonists on 26 April 1607 at Cape Henry, days before the founding of Jamestown. The modern city was formed in 1963 by the merger of the small resort city of Virginia Beach (developed after the arrival of the railroad in the 1880s) with Princess Anne County, creating a sprawling suburban city of some 497 square miles — the largest land area of any city in Virginia.

Geography and economy

Virginia Beach is a resort city with miles of beaches and hundreds of hotels and restaurants along the oceanfront, a 3-mile boardwalk, and the 28-mile Chesapeake Bay Bridge–Tunnel to the north. Its economy rests on tourism, agriculture (especially in the rural southern part of the city), and the military: Naval Air Station Oceana, the largest employer, and several other installations are located within the city. First Landing State Park and Back Bay National Wildlife Refuge preserve coastal habitats.

Culture

Landmarks include the Cape Henry Lighthouses, the Virginia Aquarium & Marine Science Center, and the King Neptune statue on the boardwalk. The city hosts the North American Sand Soccer Championships and the Rock 'n' Roll Half Marathon.
