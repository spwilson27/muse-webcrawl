The Secretariat of the Interior (Spanish: Secretaría de Gobernación, abbreviated SEGOB) is the Mexican cabinet department responsible for the country's internal affairs. Its head, the Secretary of the Interior, is appointed by the President of Mexico and is traditionally one of the most powerful figures in the cabinet, often regarded as the second most important post in the government.

Responsibilities

The secretariat coordinates relations between the federal executive and the other powers of the Union, state governments, and political parties. Its remit includes domestic security policy and federal law-enforcement coordination, migration through the National Institute of Migration, civil protection and disaster response, population policy and the national population register (RENAPO), regulation of religious associations, and oversight of official publications and the federal archives. It also plays a central role in managing relations with Congress and in federal electoral-era liaison, though elections themselves are run by the autonomous National Electoral Institute (INE).

History

A ministry of internal affairs has existed in Mexico since independence in the 19th century. Over the 20th century the secretariat accumulated wide powers over political control and internal security, and its head was, for much of the PRI's long rule, the president's presumptive successor. Reforms since the democratic transition have trimmed some functions — notably the transfer of the federal police and public security responsibilities to a separate secretariat in 2018 — while leaving SEGOB at the centre of domestic political management.
