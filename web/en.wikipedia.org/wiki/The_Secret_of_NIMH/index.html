The Secret of NIMH

Directed by: Don Bluth
Based on: Mrs. Frisby and the Rats of NIMH by Robert C. O'Brien
Starring (voices): Elizabeth Hartman, Dom DeLuise, Derek Jacobi, Peter Strauss
Released: 2 July 1982
Box office: $14.7 million

The Secret of NIMH is a 1982 American animated fantasy adventure film directed by Don Bluth and based on Robert C. O'Brien's 1971 children's novel Mrs. Frisby and the Rats of NIMH. It was the first feature film of Bluth and his studio after he and other animators left Walt Disney Productions in 1979 seeking to revive classical hand-drawn animation.

Plot

Mrs. Brisby, a widowed field mouse, must move her family before the farmer ploughs their field, but her son Timothy is too ill with pneumonia to be moved. Seeking help, she learns that her late husband Jonathan was one of a group of rodents who escaped from the National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH), where experiments gave them human-level intelligence and longevity. The rats of NIMH, led by Nicodemus, agree to move her house, while a faction led by Jenner plots against them; Brisby's courage, empowered by a magical amulet, saves her children when their home sinks into the mud.

Production

Bluth's team worked under severe financial constraints, using traditional techniques including multiplane camera work and backlit animation to give scenes a rich, glowing quality. The heroine's name was changed from Frisby to Brisby to avoid conflict with the Frisbee trademark. The score, by Jerry Goldsmith, includes the song "Flying Dreams", performed by Paul Williams.

Reception and legacy

The film was praised by critics for its dark, serious tone and its animation, though it was only a modest box-office success, competing in a summer dominated by E.T. It later became a cult favourite on home video and is regarded as one of the finest works of American animation of its era. A direct-to-video sequel, The Secret of NIMH 2: Timmy to the Rescue (1998), was made without Bluth's involvement and poorly received.
