Lake Kissimmee State Park is a Florida State Park located on the western shore of Lake Kissimmee near Lake Wales, Florida. Covering nearly 6,000 acres of floodplain, prairie, flatwoods, and oak hammock, it preserves one of the largest remaining stretches of the Kissimmee River basin landscape.

Features

The park is noted for its living-history "Cow Camp", a re-creation of an 1876 Florida cattle camp where interpreters demonstrate the life of the "cracker" cow hunters who worked the open range in the era before barbed wire. Activities include camping, hiking and equestrian trails, canoeing and kayaking on the lake and creeks, fishing, and birdwatching; the park is part of the Great Florida Birding and Wildlife Trail.

Wildlife

Habitats in the park support white-tailed deer, bobcats, gopher tortoises, sandhill cranes, bald eagles, scrub jays, and seasonal populations of whooping cranes from Florida's reintroduced flock. Prescribed burning maintains the open prairie and pine flatwoods that many of these species require.
