HSBC Holdings plc is a British multinational banking and financial services organisation, one of the largest banks in the world. Its name derives from the Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation, founded in 1865 in British Hong Kong to finance trade between China and Europe. HSBC operates in dozens of countries and territories, serving around 40 million customers through retail banking, commercial banking, and global banking and markets businesses.

History

The Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation opened in Hong Kong and Shanghai in 1865, founded by the Scottish merchant Thomas Sutherland. It grew with Asian trade, issued banknotes, and survived wars and financial crises to become the dominant bank in Hong Kong. The modern group took shape with the acquisition of the UK's Midland Bank in 1992, when HSBC moved its headquarters to London; it subsequently expanded in the Americas and Europe, acquiring banks including Marine Midland in the United States and Crédit Commercial de France.

Criticism and restructuring

HSBC paid substantial penalties in the 2010s, including a US$1.9 billion settlement with US authorities in 2012 over money-laundering controls related to Mexican drug cartels, and faced scrutiny after the 2015 "Swiss Leaks" disclosures concerning its Swiss private bank. In the 2020s the group has refocused on Asia — its historic profit engine — selling retail operations in several Western markets, including its Canadian bank, while continuing to navigate tensions between its London listing and its Asian business.
