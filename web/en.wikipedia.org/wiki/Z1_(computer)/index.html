Z1 (computer)

Z1

Designer: Konrad Zuse

Completed: 1938

Type: Mechanical binary computer

The Z1 was a mechanical computer designed by German engineer Konrad Zuse between 1935 and 1938. It is considered one of the first freely programmable computers, using binary floating-point arithmetic and a punched-tape program input read from 35 mm film stock.

Design

The machine was built in Zuse's parents' apartment in Berlin from thousands of thin metal sheets cut with a jigsaw. It contained roughly 20,000 parts and could perform about one multiplication every five seconds. Numbers were stored as binary floating-point values with a 7-bit exponent and 16-bit mantissa, and the memory held 64 words.

Legacy

The original Z1 was destroyed in an Allied air raid in 1943 together with its construction plans. Zuse later built the Z2, Z3 and Z4; a replica of the Z1, reconstructed by Zuse in 1989, is held by the German Museum of Technology in Berlin.
