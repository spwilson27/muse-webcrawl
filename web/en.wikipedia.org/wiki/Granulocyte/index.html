Granulocyte

Granulocytes are a category of white blood cells characterised by the presence of granules in their cytoplasm. They are the most numerous type of white blood cell and a central part of the innate immune system.

Types

The three types found in circulating blood are named for their staining reactions: neutrophils, which phagocytose bacteria and are the first responders to infection; eosinophils, which combat parasites and participate in allergic responses; and basophils, which release histamine and other mediators of inflammation. Mast cells, resident in tissues, are closely related.

Function

Granulocytes are short-lived, terminally differentiated cells released from the bone marrow. Their granules contain antimicrobial enzymes, defensins, and inflammatory mediators, which are discharged by degranulation or during the formation of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs). A low granulocyte count (agranulocytosis) leaves patients highly vulnerable to infection.
