Haseki Sultan (Ottoman Turkish: خاصكي سلطان, Ḫāṣekī Sulṭān) was the title used for the chief consort of an Ottoman sultan. In later years, the meaning of the title changed to "imperial consort". The title was created for Hürrem Sultan, the favourite and later legal wife of Suleiman the Magnificent, around 1534, and it elevated its holder for the first time to a rank rivalling that of the sultan's mother, the Valide Sultan, within the imperial harem.

Origins and status

Before the 16th century, Ottoman sultans rarely married, and their concubines bore titles based on motherhood of princes. Suleiman's elevation of Hürrem — and his unprecedented legal marriage to her — broke with this practice. A Haseki received a large daily stipend (Hürrem's reached 2,000 aspers a day, far exceeding other harem salaries), resided prominently in the Topkapı Palace harem after it moved from the Old Palace, and could become a major patron of architecture and charity, commissioning mosques, soup kitchens, and baths in Istanbul and the holy cities.

Holders and the Sultanate of Women

Famous Hasekis include Hürrem Sultan (Suleiman I), Nurbanu Sultan (Selim II), Safiye Sultan (Murad III), and Kösem Sultan (Ahmed I). Their political influence during the late 16th and early 17th centuries, when a succession of young or weak sultans reigned, is known as the "Sultanate of Women". From the mid-17th century the title was borne by several consorts simultaneously and lost its singular status; by the 18th century it had largely been superseded by the rank of kadınefendi.
