Diamond Light Source (or Diamond) is the United Kingdom's national synchrotron science facility, located at the Harwell Science and Innovation Campus in Oxfordshire. Its synchrotron produces brilliant beams of light — from infrared to X-rays — that scientists use to study the structure and behaviour of materials, molecules, and biological specimens at atomic and molecular resolution.

The facility

The machine accelerates electrons to 3 GeV and stores them in a 561.6-metre ring of 24 cells, housed inside a distinctive steel-clad toroidal building some 234 metres across — the largest scientific facility built in the UK for over forty years when it opened. Bending magnets and insertion devices (undulators and wigglers) generate synchrotron radiation directed down beamlines to experimental stations. Diamond succeeded the Synchrotron Radiation Source at Daresbury, Cheshire.

Research

Diamond operates more than thirty beamlines serving structural biology (including work on foot-and-mouth disease virus and drug targets), chemistry, materials science, engineering, environmental science, and cultural heritage. Beamtime is allocated by peer review to academic and industrial users; the facility hosts several thousand visiting researchers each year. Diamond is operated by Diamond Light Source Ltd, funded principally by the UK government through UK Research and Innovation (Science and Technology Facilities Council) together with the Wellcome Trust.

History

The project was announced in 2000, construction began in 2003, and the first users arrived in January 2007; the facility was formally opened by Queen Elizabeth II in October 2007. A major upgrade programme, Diamond-II, plans a new machine lattice to increase brightness and coherence.
