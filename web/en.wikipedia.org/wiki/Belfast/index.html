Belfast (from Irish Béal Feirste, meaning "mouth of the sand-bank ford") is the capital and largest city of Northern Ireland, standing on the banks of the River Lagan on the east coast of Ireland. It is the second-largest city on the island of Ireland, after Dublin. Belfast is administered as one of the eleven local government districts created in 2014 and is the seat of the Northern Ireland Assembly and the Northern Ireland Executive at Stormont.

The city suffered greatly during the period of conflict known as the Troubles (roughly 1968–1998), when sectarian violence between unionist/loyalist and nationalist/republican communities claimed many lives. Since the Good Friday Agreement of 1998, Belfast has undergone considerable social, economic and cultural renewal, and it is now a major centre for industry, the arts, higher education, business and law.

History

The site of Belfast has been occupied since the Bronze Age, and the Giant's Ring, a 5,000-year-old henge, lies near the city. A castle was built in the area by the Normans in the 12th century. Belfast received its modern foundation in the early 17th century as part of the Plantation of Ulster, when it was settled by English and Scottish settlers. Granted a charter by James I in 1613, the town grew rapidly as a centre of the Irish linen industry — for a time it was nicknamed "Linenopolis".

During the Industrial Revolution Belfast became Ireland's largest city and a world centre of shipbuilding. The Harland & Wolff shipyard, founded in 1861, built many famous vessels including RMS Titanic, launched in 1911. Belfast was granted city status by Queen Victoria in 1888. When Ireland was partitioned in 1921, Belfast became the capital of Northern Ireland.

Geography

Belfast sits at the western end of Belfast Lough, a long sea inlet, at the mouth of the River Lagan. It is flanked to the north-west by a series of hills, including Divis Mountain and Cavehill, the latter said to have inspired Jonathan Swift's Gulliver's Travels. The city spreads across the historic counties of Antrim (north of the Lagan) and Down (to the south).

Economy and culture

Historically dominated by linen, tobacco, rope-making and shipbuilding, Belfast's modern economy rests on services, aerospace (Bombardier/Spirit AeroSystems), financial services, and a growing technology and film sector — HBO's Game of Thrones was filmed largely at the Titanic Studios. The city is home to Queen's University Belfast, founded in 1845, and Ulster University's city-centre campus. Landmarks include Belfast City Hall (1906), the Titanic Belfast visitor attraction (opened 2012 on the centenary of the ship's sinking), the Albert Memorial Clock and St Anne's Cathedral.

Transport

Belfast is served by two airports: George Best Belfast City Airport, close to the city centre, and Belfast International Airport at Aldergrove. Ferries link the port with Liverpool, Cairnryan and the Isle of Man, and rail services connect the city with Dublin and Derry/Londonderry.
