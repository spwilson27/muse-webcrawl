George Clinton (vice president)

George Clinton

Born: 26 July 1739, Little Britain, New York

Died: 20 April 1812, Washington, D.C.

4th Vice President of the United States (1805–1812)

1st Governor of New York (1777–1795, 1801–1804)

George Clinton (26 July 1739 – 20 April 1812) was an American soldier, statesman and Founding Father of the United States. A prominent opponent of British rule, he served as the first Governor of New York from 1777 to 1795 and again from 1801 to 1804, and as the fourth vice president of the United States from 1805 until his death in 1812.

Revolutionary War

Clinton served in the French and Indian War and later as a delegate to the Continental Congress, which he left to serve as a brigadier general in the Continental Army, defending the Hudson Highlands. As governor of New York throughout the Revolutionary War, he was a leading figure in the Patriot cause and a political rival within the state.

Anti-Federalism and governorship

Initially an opponent of the United States Constitution, Clinton led the Anti-Federalist opposition in New York before eventually supporting ratification once a bill of rights was promised. His long tenure as governor made him one of the most powerful state politicians of the early republic.

Vice presidency

Clinton served as vice president under two presidents, Thomas Jefferson and James Madison — one of only two men to hold the office under two different presidents. He died in office in 1812, the first vice president to do so.
