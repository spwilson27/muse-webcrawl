Barry (Welsh: Y Barri) is a town in the Vale of Glamorgan, Wales, situated on the north coast of the Bristol Channel approximately 9 miles (14 km) south-west of Cardiff. Barry is a seaside resort, with attractions including beaches and the resurrected Barry Island Pleasure Park. The town grew dramatically in the late 19th century as a coal-exporting port, at one time the largest coal port in the world.

History

The area shows evidence of settlement from the Mesolithic period onward, and the ruins of Barry Castle overlook the old harbour. The modern town developed after 1884, when railway promoters and coal owners, frustrated by congestion and charges at Cardiff docks, built the Barry Docks under David Davies of Llandinam. The first dock opened in 1889; by 1913 Barry was exporting over 11 million tons of coal a year, making it briefly the world's busiest coal port.

Decline and regeneration

Coal exports collapsed after the Second World War, and the docks declined, though they still handle bulk cargoes. The Woodham Brothers scrapyard at Barry became famous for preserving more than 200 steam locomotives, most of which were later restored by heritage railways. Former dock offices and the surrounding area have seen regeneration, and the town now functions partly as a commuter town for Cardiff.

Barry Island

Barry Island, connected to the mainland by a causeway, is a popular seaside resort with sandy Whitmore Bay, a pleasure park with fairground rides, and promenades. It gained renewed fame as a principal filming location for the BBC sitcom Gavin & Stacey.
