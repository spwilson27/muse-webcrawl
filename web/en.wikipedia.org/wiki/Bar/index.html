Bar

Bar is a word with many meanings. This article presents an overview of its principal uses.

A bar is most commonly an establishment that serves alcoholic beverages, such as beer, wine, liquor, and cocktails, for consumption on the premises. The term may also refer to the counter at which drinks are served. Bars range from small neighborhood taverns and pubs to cocktail lounges and nightclub venues, and in many jurisdictions their operation is regulated by liquor licensing laws, including minimum drinking ages and permitted opening hours.

Etymology

The word "bar" in the drinking sense derives from the barrier or counter separating the server from customers in taverns and inns; in legal usage, "the Bar" refers to the same physical barrier in a courtroom that once separated lawyers and judges from the public, and by extension to the legal profession as a whole.

Other uses

  • Bar (unit) — a metric unit of pressure, equal to 100,000 pascals, roughly the atmospheric pressure at sea level.
  • Bar (form) — a long rigid piece of metal or wood; by extension, a solid block of a substance such as soap, chocolate, or gold.
  • Bar (music) — a segment of time in musical notation, bounded by vertical bar lines, containing a set number of beats.
  • Bar (law) — the legal profession, or the examination and admission process ("bar exam", "called to the bar"); also a procedural obstacle, as in "time-barred."
  • Bar (river morphology) — a ridge or mound of sediment deposited in a river or along a coast.
  • BAR — the Browning Automatic Rifle, a family of American automatic rifles.
  • Bar (heraldry) — a horizontal band across a shield, a diminutive of the fess.
