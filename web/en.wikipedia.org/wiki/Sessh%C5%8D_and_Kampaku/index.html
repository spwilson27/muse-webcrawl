Sesshō and Kampaku

Sesshō / Kampaku
Country: Japan
Type: Imperial regent titles

In Japanese history, the Sesshō (摂政) and Kampaku (関白) were the highest offices of regency at the imperial court. A sesshō governed on behalf of a child emperor or an empress regnant, while a kampaku served as regent and chief advisor to an adult emperor.

Fujiwara regency

From the 9th to the 11th century the offices were dominated by the Fujiwara clan, whose leaders married their daughters to emperors and ruled through the regency — a system known as sekkan seiji. Fujiwara no Michinaga epitomised its power at its height.

Later use

Toyotomi Hideyoshi, though of peasant origin, took the title of kampaku in 1585 to legitimise his rule. The offices became largely ceremonial in later centuries; a modern sesshō served during the minority of Emperor Hirohito in the 1920s.
