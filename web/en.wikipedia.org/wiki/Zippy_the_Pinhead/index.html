Zippy the Pinhead

Zippy the Pinhead is a fictional character in underground comics by American cartoonist Bill Griffith. Zippy is an adult man dressed in a yellow muumuu with red polka dots, wearing clown shoes and sporting a lance-shaped head with a small topknot of hair, inspired by microcephalic circus performers historically billed as “pinheads.”

Zippy the Pinhead
First appearance: Real Pulp Comics No. 1 (1971)
Created by: Bill Griffith
Publisher: King Features Syndicate (daily strip, from 1986)

History

Griffith created Zippy in 1970 after recalling the Schlitzie-like performers in Tod Browning’s 1932 film Freaks and other sideshow figures. The strip began in underground comix, appeared in High Times and the San Francisco Chronicle and Berkeley Barb circles, and was syndicated by King Features in 1986, eventually reaching hundreds of newspapers.

Character and style

Zippy speaks in surreal non sequiturs, catchphrases such as “Are we having fun yet?” and “Am I a consumer, or am I supposed to be amused?”, and questions about pop culture, roadside Americana, diners, and consumerism. He is often paired with Griffy, a more grounded alter ego of the cartoonist, and with characters such as Mr. Toad and the dog Fuelrod. The strip mixes hand-drawn panels, collage-like references, and philosophical jokes.

Themes

Despite his absurd appearance, Zippy often serves as a Zen-like observer, commenting innocently on mainstream American culture while remaining cheerfully outside it.
