Haymitch Abernathy is a fictional character in Suzanne Collins's The Hunger Games trilogy. He is the victor of the 50th Hunger Games (the second Quarter Quell) and, as District 12's only living victor for most of the series, serves as mentor to Katniss Everdeen and Peeta Mellark. He is portrayed by Woody Harrelson in the film adaptations.

Character biography

Reaped at age sixteen for the Second Quarter Quell, in which twice the usual number of tributes competed, Haymitch won by exploiting the arena's force field: in the final confrontation he positioned himself so that his opponent's thrown axe rebounded off the field and killed her. President Snow, furious at the defiance implied by the victory, had Haymitch's mother, younger brother and girlfriend killed within weeks. Traumatised and isolated, Haymitch sank into alcoholism, and for twenty-four years watched every pair of District 12 tributes he mentored die in the arena.

When Katniss and Peeta are reaped for the 74th Hunger Games, Haymitch — recognising fighters — sobers up enough to coach them seriously, devising the "star-crossed lovers" strategy and channelling sponsor gifts during the Games. In Catching Fire he is revealed as part of the underground rebellion and is extracted from the Capitol after the arena break-out; in Mockingjay he works with District 13's command while struggling with enforced sobriety. After the war he returns to District 12, where he raises geese and remains a gruff neighbour to Katniss and Peeta.

Characterisation

Haymitch is sarcastic, blunt and habitually drunk, calling Katniss "sweetheart" with heavy irony. Beneath the abrasion, Collins presents him as one of the series' most perceptive strategists and a study in survivor's guilt: his alcoholism and isolation are the long-term price of his victory. His backstory is expanded in Collins's 2025 prequel novel Sunrise on the Reaping, which retells his Games and their aftermath.
