Zappos.com, Inc. is an American online shoe and clothing retailer based in Las Vegas, Nevada. Founded in 1999 by Nick Swinmurn as ShoeSite.com, the company grew rapidly under chief executive Tony Hsieh into one of the world's largest online shoe stores, distinguished by its emphasis on customer service and corporate culture. In 2009 Zappos was acquired by Amazon in an all-stock deal valued at about $880 million.

History

Swinmurn launched the site after finding that no major online retailer sold shoes well. Venture investor Tony Hsieh, through his firm Venture Frogs, invested in the company in 2000 and became co-CEO and then CEO. Sales grew from $1.6 million in 2000 to more than $1 billion in gross merchandise sales by 2008. The company moved its headquarters from San Francisco to Henderson, Nevada, in 2004, drawn by the availability of call-centre labour, and later relocated to downtown Las Vegas.

Business model and culture

Zappos built its brand on service: free shipping both ways, a 365-day return policy, and a 24-hour call centre staffed by employees empowered to spend generously to satisfy customers. Its ten core values and unusually flat, sociable culture were widely studied in management literature. In 2013–2015 the company adopted Holacracy, a system of self-management without traditional managers, a controversial experiment that prompted a significant number of employees to take buyouts.

Amazon acquisition

Amazon announced its acquisition of Zappos in July 2009; the deal closed in November of that year. Zappos continued to operate as an independent subsidiary with its own brand and culture. Hsieh retired as CEO in August 2020 and died later that year; he was succeeded by Kedar Deshpande.

Downtown Project

Hsieh invested some $350 million of his own money into the Downtown Project, an effort to revitalise downtown Las Vegas around the company's new headquarters in the former City Hall building, funding start-ups, restaurants, and arts projects such as the Life Is Beautiful festival.
