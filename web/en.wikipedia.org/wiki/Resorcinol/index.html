Resorcinol

Resorcinol (also called resorcin) is an organic compound with the formula C6H4(OH)2. It is one of three isomeric benzenediols, the 1,3-isomer (the meta-isomer), the others being catechol (1,2) and hydroquinone (1,4). It is a white, crystalline solid that is soluble in water and turns pink on exposure to air and light.

Production

Resorcinol is produced industrially by sulfonating benzene with fuming sulfuric acid to give benzene-1,3-disulfonic acid, followed by fusion with molten sodium hydroxide and acidification. An alternative route proceeds via oxidation of 1,3-diisopropylbenzene, analogous to the cumene process for phenol.

Uses

The largest use of resorcinol is in resorcinol–formaldehyde resins and related adhesives for tyres (resorcinol–formaldehyde–latex dips that bond rubber to tyre cord) and for waterproof wood adhesives used in laminated timber and marine plywood. It is also a precursor to chemicals including hexylresorcinol, and is used in hair dyes, pharmaceuticals and as a chemical intermediate. Medicinally, resorcinol has been used in topical preparations as an antiseptic and keratolytic for skin conditions such as acne, seborrhoeic dermatitis and eczema, often combined with sulfur.

Safety

Resorcinol is moderately toxic and an irritant; it can interfere with thyroid function at high exposures and its use in cosmetics is regulated in some jurisdictions.
