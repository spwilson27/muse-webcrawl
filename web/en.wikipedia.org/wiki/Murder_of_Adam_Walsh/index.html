Murder of Adam Walsh

Adam John Walsh (November 14, 1974 – July 27, 1981) was an American boy who was abducted from a Sears department store at the Hollywood Mall in Hollywood, Florida, on July 27, 1981. His severed head was found two weeks later in a drainage canal alongside Highway 60 in rural Indian River County, Florida. His death earned national attention and was made into the 1983 television film Adam, watched by tens of millions of viewers.

Investigation

Serial killer Ottis Toole confessed to the murder but recanted, and was never tried for it; police lost key evidence including the carpet from Toole's car. On December 16, 2008, Hollywood police formally closed the case and announced that Toole was responsible. Adam's father, John Walsh, accepted the conclusion.

Legacy

The case transformed American responses to missing children. John Walsh became a victims' advocate, co-founded the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children in 1984, and began hosting America's Most Wanted in 1988. The case also contributed to the "Code Adam" store-lockdown programme used in retail stores, the Adam Walsh Child Protection and Safety Act of 2006, which strengthened sex-offender registration, and the introduction of missing-child photographs on milk cartons.
