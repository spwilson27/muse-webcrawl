Paul Ehrlich Institute

The Paul Ehrlich Institute (German: Paul-Ehrlich-Institut, PEI) is a German federal agency responsible for the regulation, testing, and research of vaccines and biomedical medicines. Headquartered in Langen, Hesse, it is an agency of the Federal Ministry of Health and serves as Germany's national regulatory authority for biological medicinal products, including vaccines, sera, blood products, allergens, and advanced therapy medicinal products.

History

The institute traces its origins to the Institute for Serum Research and Serum Testing founded in Berlin in 1896 under the direction of Paul Ehrlich, the immunologist and Nobel laureate whose work on diphtheria serum standardization defined its mission. It moved with Ehrlich to Frankfurt am Main in 1899, and after the Second World War was re-established in Frankfurt before relocating to Langen in 1988. It received its modern name in 1972.

Functions

The PEI evaluates and approves clinical trials of vaccines and biomedicines in Germany, performs official batch release testing for vaccines and blood products, monitors the safety of marketed products through pharmacovigilance, and grants national marketing authorizations. Within the European Union it works closely with the European Medicines Agency and serves as a reference laboratory. The institute gained wide public visibility during the COVID-19 pandemic, when it assessed vaccine clinical trials, evaluated suspected adverse events, and published regular safety reports for Germany.
