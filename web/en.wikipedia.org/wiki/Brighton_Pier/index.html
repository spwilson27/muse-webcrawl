Brighton Pier

Brighton Pier may refer to the pleasure piers of Brighton, East Sussex, England. The surviving pier, officially the Palace Pier (renamed "Brighton Pier" in 2000, a change never formally sanctioned), opened in 1899 and remains one of Britain's most visited tourist attractions, with a funfair, amusement arcades, and food outlets. The earlier West Pier (1866) closed in 1975 and was largely destroyed by storm and arson fires in 2002–2003; its skeletal remains still stand offshore. The original Chain Pier of 1823 was destroyed by a storm in 1896.

History

Construction of the Palace Pier began in 1891 to designs by R. St George Moore; it opened in 1899, though its concert hall and winter garden were finished later. A funfair was added at the seaward end in the 1930s, replacing the earlier bandstand attractions. The pier head concert hall hosted notable variety performers, and the pier appears frequently in film and television. Ownership passed in 1986 to the Noble Organisation and in 2016 to the Pier Group.

The West Pier

Designed by Eugenius Birch and opened in 1866, the West Pier was the first pier to be Grade I listed. Declining attendance led to closure in 1975; despite restoration plans, sections collapsed in storms in 2002 and fires in 2003 destroyed the pavilion and concert hall, leaving the famous iron skeleton that is now a landmark in its own right.
