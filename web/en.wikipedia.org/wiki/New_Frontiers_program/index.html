New Frontiers program

The New Frontiers program is a series of space exploration missions being conducted by NASA with the purpose of researching several of the Solar System bodies, including the dwarf planet Pluto. The program selects medium-class missions — larger than the Discovery Program and smaller than flagship missions — through competitive announcements of opportunity, with a cost cap of roughly one billion US dollars. Candidate mission themes are drawn from the priorities of the National Research Council's planetary science decadal survey.

New Frontiers is managed for NASA by the Marshall Space Flight Center. The fourth mission, Dragonfly, was selected in 2019 under principal investigator Elizabeth Turtle of the Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory, beating a comet sample-return finalist, CAESAR. A fifth competition, for missions to targets including Saturn probes, lunar sample return, and comet surface sample return, has been planned under subsequent decadal priorities.
