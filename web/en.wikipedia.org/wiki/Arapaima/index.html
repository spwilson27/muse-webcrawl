The arapaima, also known as pirarucu or paiche, is a genus of giant freshwater fish native to the Amazon basin — among the largest scaled freshwater fishes in the world. Arapaima are obligate air-breathers, surfacing every 15–20 minutes, and are prized food fish whose populations have been heavily depleted by overfishing.

Biology

Arapaima have torpedo-shaped bodies with large, red-edged scales so tough they resist piranha bites, a bony tongue (the source of the order name Osteoglossiformes, "bony tongues") and a swim bladder modified for breathing air. Males guard eggs and fry, which cluster around the parent's head.

Taxonomy and conservation

Once treated as a single species (Arapaima gigas), the genus is now divided into several species. Community-based management in Brazil, with fishing quotas and closed seasons, has allowed some populations to recover, and arapaima are increasingly farmed and have been introduced to waters in Bolivia and Asia.
