Crux

Crux is a constellation of the southern sky that is centred on a cross-shaped asterism commonly known as the Southern Cross. It is the smallest of the 88 modern constellations, but one of the most distinctive, and it appears on the national flags of several southern hemisphere countries including Australia, Brazil, New Zealand, Papua New Guinea and Samoa.

Stars

The Southern Cross is formed by four bright stars: Acrux (Alpha Crucis), Mimosa (Beta Crucis), Gacrux (Gamma Crucis) and Imai (Delta Crucis), with a fifth, fainter star, Ginan (Epsilon Crucis). Acrux is a multiple star system about 320 light-years away and is the brightest star in the constellation. Near the constellation lies the Coalsack Nebula, a prominent dark nebula silhouetted against the Milky Way, and the Jewel Box open cluster beside Mimosa.

Navigation and history

The long axis of the cross points approximately toward the south celestial pole, making Crux a traditional navigational aid in the southern hemisphere. The stars of Crux were visible from the Mediterranean in antiquity and were catalogued by Ptolemy as part of Centaurus; they became a separate constellation in the late 16th century as precession carried them below the northern horizon.
