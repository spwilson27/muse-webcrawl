Fractional freezing

Fractional freezing is a process used in process engineering and chemistry to separate substances with different melting points from a liquid mixture. It is a type of fractional crystallization. The mixture is cooled so that one component freezes out as relatively pure crystals, which can then be removed; repeating the process increases purity. It is the freezing analogue of fractional distillation.

Applications

Fractional freezing is used to concentrate alcoholic beverages by "freeze distillation" — as in traditional applejack and ice beer (Eisbock) — where ice (mostly water) is removed to raise the alcohol content. It has also been used for desalination of seawater, concentration of fruit juices and coffee extracts (freeze concentration), purification of chemicals, and refining of edible oils (winterization). Because only the latent heat of fusion must be removed, freeze concentration can preserve flavours better than evaporation, though separating crystals cleanly is a practical difficulty.
