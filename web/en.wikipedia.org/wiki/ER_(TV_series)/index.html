ER is an American medical drama television series created by Michael Crichton that aired on NBC from September 19, 1994, to April 2, 2009, with a total of 331 episodes spanning 15 seasons. It was produced by Constant C Productions and Amblin Television, in association with Warner Bros. Television.

The series is set in the emergency room (ER) of the fictional County General Hospital in Chicago, Illinois, and follows the professional and personal lives of the doctors, nurses and staff who work there. Crichton, who had trained as a physician, based the show on his experiences as a medical student; he originally wrote the story as a film screenplay in 1974.

Overview

ER was noted for its fast pace, ensemble cast, overlapping dialogue, Steadicam "oners" and technically dense depiction of emergency medicine. The original cast included Anthony Edwards as Dr. Mark Greene, George Clooney as Dr. Doug Ross, Julianna Margulies as nurse Carol Hathaway, Noah Wyle as medical student John Carter, Eriq La Salle as Dr. Peter Benton and Sherry Stringfield as Dr. Susan Lewis. Over its long run the ensemble turned over almost completely; Wyle remained the longest-serving regular.

Reception and legacy

The show was a huge ratings success, ranking as the most-watched drama in the United States for several seasons, and it received 124 Emmy Award nominations, winning 23, including Outstanding Drama Series in 1996. Margulies won an Emmy for her role, the only regular cast member to do so. ER is the second-longest-running primetime medical drama in American television history (after Grey's Anatomy) and helped launch the film career of George Clooney. Its success influenced later medical dramas and popularized the frenetic, documentary-like style of workplace drama.
