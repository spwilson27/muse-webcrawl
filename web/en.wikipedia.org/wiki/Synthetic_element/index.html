Synthetic element

A synthetic element is one of the 24 known chemical elements that do not occur naturally on Earth; they have been created by human manipulation of fundamental particles in a nuclear reactor, a particle accelerator, or the detonation of an atomic bomb. As a result, they are commonly called "synthetic" or "artificial" elements. The synthetic elements are those with atomic numbers 95–118, together with technetium (43) and promethium (61), which were first produced artificially although trace quantities occur naturally.

Properties

All synthetic elements are unstable, with half-lives ranging from a few years down to a few milliseconds. Their instability means that any primordial quantities would have long since decayed by the time the Earth formed. Five of them — plutonium and the transuranium elements neptunium among them — have since been found in trace amounts in nature, produced by neutron capture in uranium ores or, in the case of plutonium-244, surviving in extremely small primordial quantities.

History

The first element discovered through synthesis was technetium, produced by Carlo Perrier and Emilio Segrè in 1937 by bombarding molybdenum with deuterons; it filled a long-standing gap at atomic number 43 in the periodic table. Promethium was identified in 1945 among fission products at Oak Ridge. The actinide elements americium and curium were created in 1944 during the Manhattan Project, and the race to create ever heavier elements continued through the Cold War, with competing claims from laboratories in Berkeley, California and Dubna, Russia — the "transfermium wars".

Elements up to oganesson (118), completing the seventh period of the periodic table, have been confirmed. The most recent, tennessine and oganesson, were formally named in 2016. Research continues toward element 119 and beyond, and theorists predict an "island of stability" of relatively long-lived superheavy nuclei.

List of synthetic elements

  • Technetium (43) and promethium (61) — also found in nature in trace amounts.
  • Americium (95) through oganesson (118) — the transplutonium elements, none found in appreciable natural quantities.
  • Notable: plutonium (94) and neptunium (93) are often classed with the synthetic elements because they were first identified through synthesis, though trace natural occurrences exist.

Production

Synthetic elements are produced by bombarding a heavy target nucleus with neutrons in a reactor (building up heavier nuclei through successive neutron captures and beta decays) or with accelerated light nuclei in a particle accelerator, fusing them into a heavier nucleus. The heaviest elements have been made only a few atoms at a time, detected through their characteristic decay chains.
