Orbetello is a town and comune in the province of Grosseto, in the southern Maremma area of Tuscany, Italy. It is located about 35 kilometres (22 miles) south of Grosseto, on a narrow strip of land — a tombolo — that juts into the middle of the Lagoon of Orbetello, facing Monte Argentario.

History

The area was inhabited in Etruscan times, and the nearby ruins of Cosa testify to Roman colonization. In the Middle Ages Orbetello was controlled successively by the Aldobrandeschi and Orsini families and the Republic of Siena. From the mid-16th century it was the capital of the State of the Presidi, a Spanish Habsburg possession on the Tuscan coast, and its fortifications date largely from that period. It later passed to the Kingdom of Naples, the Grand Duchy of Tuscany, and finally unified Italy.

Geography

The Orbetello lagoon, divided by an artificial causeway built in 1842, is an internationally important wetland and nature reserve managed in part by the WWF, home to flamingos, herons, and many migratory birds. The municipality includes the coastal resorts and villages along the Argentario promontory and the Tombolo della Giannella and Tombolo della Feniglia sandbars, the latter largely covered by a pine forest nature reserve.

Economy

Historically a fishing town — bottarga (cured fish roe) is a local specialty produced by the Orbetello fishermen's cooperative — the town's economy now centers on tourism, lagoon aquaculture, and services.
