Royal Palace of Madrid

Royal Palace of Madrid Spanish: Palacio Real de Madrid
Location: Madrid, Spain
Construction: 1738–1755
Architects: Filippo Juvarra, Giovanni Battista Sacchetti, Francesco Sabatini
Style: Baroque / Classical
Floor area: 135,000 m² (3,418 rooms)

The Royal Palace of Madrid (Spanish: Palacio Real de Madrid) is the official residence of the Spanish royal family at the city of Madrid, although it is now used only for state ceremonies. The palace is the largest functioning royal palace in Europe by floor area, with 135,000 square metres and 3,418 rooms. King Felipe VI and the royal family live instead at the more modest Palace of Zarzuela on the outskirts of the city.

History

The palace stands on the site of a 9th-century Muslim-era fortress (alcázar) and the Old Alcázar of Madrid, the residence of the Spanish Habsburgs, which was destroyed by fire on Christmas Eve 1734. King Philip V, the first Bourbon king of Spain, commissioned a grand new palace in the Italian Baroque style. The design, initially by Filippo Juvarra and executed by Giovanni Battista Sacchetti, was built entirely of stone and brick—without wood—to prevent a repetition of the fire. Charles III became the first king to live in the palace, in 1764, and architects such as Francesco Sabatini completed and embellished the interiors.

Interior and collections

The palace contains richly decorated state rooms, including the Throne Room with its ceiling by Tiepolo, the Hall of Columns, and the Royal Chapel. Its collections include paintings by Velázquez, Goya, Caravaggio and Mengs, frescoes by Giaquinto and Tiepolo, the world's only complete Stradivarius string quintet, and the Royal Armoury, one of the finest collections of arms and armour in Europe. The palace is administered by Patrimonio Nacional and is open to the public when not in use for official functions.
