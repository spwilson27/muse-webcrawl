Scottish Prison Service

Formed: 1993 (as an executive agency)
Type: Executive agency of the Scottish Government
Jurisdiction: Scotland
Headquarters: Edinburgh
Establishments: 15 prisons (13 public, 2 private)
Population: ~8,000 prisoners (approx.)

The Scottish Prison Service (SPS) is an executive agency of the Scottish Government responsible for running prisons and young offenders institutions in Scotland and for the custody and rehabilitation of those sentenced or remanded by the Scottish courts. It operates within Scotland's separate legal system and answers to the Cabinet Secretary for Justice.

Establishments

The SPS directly operates 13 public prisons, including HMP Barlinnie (Glasgow), HMP Edinburgh, HMP Perth, HMP Grampian, HMP Shotts (for long-term prisoners), HMP Polmont (young offenders), and HMP Stirling, a purpose-built women's facility that replaced Cornton Vale in 2023. Two prisons, HMP Addiewell and HMP Kilmarnock, are operated by private contractors. The estate also includes open prisons such as HMP Castle Huntly. Scotland's prison population is among the highest per capita in Western Europe, and overcrowding, drug supply, and the ageing Victorian estate — notably Barlinnie, slated for replacement by a new HMP Glasgow — are persistent policy issues.

Functions and oversight

Beyond custody, the SPS is tasked with rehabilitation: education, work programmes, addiction services, and throughcare for release, increasingly framed by the Scottish Government's community-justice model and presumption against short sentences. Prisons are inspected by His Majesty's Inspectorate of Prisons for Scotland, and deaths and complaints are overseen through independent mechanisms including the Scottish Public Services Ombudsman.
