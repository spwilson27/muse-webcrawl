Naranjo

Naranjo is a pre-Columbian archaeological site of the Maya civilization in the Petén Basin region of Guatemala. It was inhabited from about 500 BC to about 950 AD, with its height of power in the Late Classic (c. 600–900 AD). It was discovered in 1905 by Teobert Maler, who named it for a hill covered with orange trees ('naranjo').

History

Naranjo's Early Classic history is partly recorded on a hieroglyphic stairway at Caracol in Belize, which describes a 631 defeat of Naranjo. Naranjo recovered under K'ahk' Tiliw Chan Chaahk ('Smoking Squirrel', r. 693–c. 728), who launched a 'star war' against Caracol in 696 and sponsored a burst of monument building — about 32 stelae record his campaigns. Later rulers included Yax Mayuy Chan Chaahk and 'Dark Sun'. The city suffered at the hands of Yaxhá in the late 8th century and declined during the Classic Maya collapse, though occupation continued into the Terminal Classic.

Archaeology

The site covers about 4 km² with more than 800 structures, six major acropolises, two ballcourts, and notable sculptural traditions showing fine-line carving. Looting damaged the site in the 20th century; mapping and consolidation projects (including under Vilma Fialko) have documented its monuments.
