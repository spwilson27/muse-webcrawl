Financing for development

Financing for development refers to the global framework, coordinated largely through the United Nations, for mobilising financial resources to achieve internationally agreed development goals, including the Sustainable Development Goals. Sources include domestic public finance (taxation), official development assistance (ODA), foreign direct investment, remittances, private philanthropy, and borrowing on international capital markets.

Conferences and agreements

Three major international conferences have defined the framework. The 2002 Monterrey Consensus emphasised domestic resource mobilisation, good governance, and aid effectiveness; wealthy countries reaffirmed the target of giving 0.7% of gross national income as ODA. The 2008 Doha Declaration reviewed progress amid the global financial crisis. The 2015 Addis Ababa Action Agenda, adopted alongside the Sustainable Development Goals, broadened the agenda to tax cooperation, blended finance, and aligning private finance with sustainable development.

Issues

Debates concern the scale and quality of aid, debt sustainability of developing countries, illicit financial flows and tax evasion, climate finance, and reform of international financial institutions such as the IMF and World Bank. The UN's Financing for Sustainable Development Report tracks progress annually.
