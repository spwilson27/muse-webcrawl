United States embargo against Cuba

The United States embargo against Cuba, known in Cuba as el bloqueo, is a comprehensive set of trade, travel and financial restrictions imposed after the Cuban Revolution. A partial arms and trade embargo began in 1960 and was expanded to nearly all trade in February 1962 by President John F. Kennedy.

Legal framework

The embargo rests on the Trading with the Enemy Act, the Foreign Assistance Act, the Cuban Assets Control Regulations, the Cuban Democracy Act of 1992 and the Helms-Burton Act of 1996, which codified the embargo into statute.

Developments

Food and medicine sales were permitted under a 2000 law. The Obama administration restored diplomatic relations in 2015 and eased travel and remittances; many measures were later tightened again. The UN General Assembly has repeatedly voted, nearly unanimously, for an end to the embargo.

Effects and debate

Supporters link the embargo to human rights and property claims; critics argue it harms ordinary Cubans and has not changed Cuba's political system.
