Radiant barrier

A radiant barrier is a type of building material that reflects thermal radiation and reduces heat transfer by radiation between surfaces. Unlike mass insulation, which slows conductive heat flow, a radiant barrier — typically a highly reflective sheet of aluminium foil or aluminised film with low emissivity — blocks radiant heat transfer from a hot surface (such as a sun-heated roof deck) toward a cooler one (such as attic insulation).

How it works

Heat travels through a roof assembly by conduction, convection and radiation. On a sunny day, solar energy is absorbed by the roof and re-radiated downward into the attic. A radiant barrier installed facing an air space on the underside of the roof reflects most of that radiation and emits very little itself, reducing attic temperatures and the cooling load on the building below. The barrier must face an open air space to work; foil pressed directly against other materials transfers heat by conduction instead.

Applications

Radiant barriers are most effective in hot climates with strong sun and high air-conditioning loads, where studies have shown cooling cost savings of roughly 5–15%. They are stapled to roof rafters, laid over attic insulation (perforated types, to avoid trapping moisture), or integrated into roof sheathing. Dust accumulation and moisture condensation can reduce performance over time.

History

Reflective insulation grew out of work by NASA in the 1950s and 1960s, which developed aluminised films to protect spacecraft and astronauts, including the Apollo programme, from extreme temperature swings. The technology was later adapted to buildings, metal structures and protective clothing.
