Inwood Hill Park

Inwood Hill Park is a public park in the Inwood neighbourhood of Manhattan, New York City, operated by the New York City Department of Parks and Recreation. Covering about 196 acres (79 hectares) at the northern tip of Manhattan Island, it contains the last remaining natural forest in Manhattan and some of the island's highest ground.

Geography and features

The park rises steeply from the Harlem River and the Hudson River to a ridge of Manhattan schist. It contains caves once used by the Lenape people, glacial potholes, the last salt marsh in Manhattan at its northern edge, and playing fields and courts along the water. The Henry Hudson Bridge crosses above the park's Spuyten Duyvil Creek inlet, and a giant letter "C" (for Columbia University) is painted on the rock face across the water.

History

According to legend, Inwood Hill is near the site where Peter Minuit purchased Manhattan Island from the Lenape in 1626; a plaque and the Shorakapok Rock mark the traditional spot. The area was farmland and country estates through the nineteenth century, and the park was assembled by the city between 1917 and the 1940s.

Ecology

The park's forest of oak, tulip tree, and hickory shelters a notable bird fauna, and the salt marsh hosts herons and egrets. A bald eagle release and viewing programme has operated in the park. The Inwood Hill Nature Center, opened in 2001, exhibits local wildlife and serves as a base for urban park rangers.
