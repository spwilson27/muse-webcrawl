Montgomery Ward

Montgomery Ward was an American mail-order business and later a department store chain founded by Aaron Montgomery Ward in Chicago in 1872. It pioneered the mail-order catalogue, selling goods directly to rural customers who had previously depended on local general stores, and its famous catalogue became known as the "Wish Book."

History

Ward's innovation was to sell at fixed, low prices with a money-back guarantee — "Satisfaction Guaranteed or Your Money Back" — undercutting local merchants. The catalogue grew from a single sheet in 1872 to hundreds of pages offering everything from clothing and tools to farm equipment and kit houses. Montgomery Ward opened its first retail stores in 1926, following rival Sears, Roebuck and Co.

Under chairman Sewell Avery after World War II, the company conserved cash expecting a postwar depression that never came, and it fell behind competitors. Ward's introduced the Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer character in a 1939 promotional booklet. After decades of decline and ownership changes, the original company filed for bankruptcy in late 2000 and closed its stores in 2001. The brand name was later revived as an online and catalogue retailer.
