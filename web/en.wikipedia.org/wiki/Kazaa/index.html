Kazaa

Placeholder image: the Kazaa logo.

Kazaa Media Desktop (commonly shortened to Kazaa or KaZaA) was a peer-to-peer file sharing application used to exchange music, films, and software over the Internet. Released in March 2001 by the Dutch company Consumer Empowerment, Kazaa used the FastTrack protocol and quickly became one of the most downloaded programs in the world following the shutdown of Napster.

History

Kazaa was created by Niklas Zennström and Janus Friis, who later founded Skype. Ownership of the software passed to Sharman Networks, based in Australia with incorporation in Vanuatu. At its peak in 2003, Kazaa had tens of millions of users and was reported to be the most downloaded software in history at the time.

Legal issues

The service was the target of sustained litigation from the recording and film industries. In MGM Studios, Inc. v. Grokster, Ltd. (2005), the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that distributors of file-sharing software could be liable for inducing copyright infringement. In 2006 Sharman Networks settled with the entertainment industry for over $115 million and agreed to convert Kazaa into a licensed service; the peer-to-peer network was shut down.

Criticism

The Kazaa client was widely criticised for bundling adware and spyware, and for files on the network that were mislabeled or infected with malware. A third-party stripped version, Kazaa Lite, circulated in defiance of the official client.
