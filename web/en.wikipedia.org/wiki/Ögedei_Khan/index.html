Ögedei Khan

Ögedei Khan (c. 1186 – 11 December 1241) was the third son of Genghis Khan and the second Great Khan of the Mongol Empire, reigning from 1229 until his death. Chosen as successor by his father, he consolidated the empire, founded its capital at Karakorum, and oversaw invasions of Persia, China and Europe.

Administration

Ögedei, famed for generosity and fondness for drink, proved an able organiser. With advisers such as Yelü Chucai he regularised taxation, built roads and the yam relay-post system across the empire, adopted a script and law code, and walled Karakorum in 1235, making it a cosmopolitan capital.

Conquests

Under his generals the Mongols destroyed the Jin dynasty in northern China (1234, briefly allied with the Song), completed the conquest of Persia under Chormaqan, and — under Batu and Subutai — overran the Rus' principalities (1237–40) and invaded Poland and Hungary in 1241, winning at Legnica and Mohi. His sudden death in December 1241, attributed to alcohol and overindulgence, forced the European armies' recall for the succession kurultai — a withdrawal often credited with saving western Europe from further invasion.

Succession

His widow Töregene ruled as regent until their son Güyük was elected in 1246. Under Ögedei the empire reached its greatest unified extent.
