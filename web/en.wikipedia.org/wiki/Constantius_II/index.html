Constantius II

Constantius II (Latin: Flavius Julius Constantius; 7 August 317 – 3 November 361) was Roman emperor from 337 to 361. The second son of Constantine the Great, he inherited the eastern third of the empire and, after civil wars against his brother Constans's killers and the usurper Magnentius, became sole ruler in 353.

Reign

Constantius fought long wars against Sasanian Persia under Shapur II, losing and retaking frontier fortresses in Mesopotamia, including campaigns around Amida (359). In the West he defeated the usurper Magnentius at Mursa (351) and Mons Seleucus (353), and appointed his cousins Gallus and then Julian as Caesars; Julian's successes in Gaul and subsequent proclamation as Augustus in 360 set up a civil war averted only by Constantius's death from fever in 361.

Religious policy

A Christian sympathetic to Arian and Homoean positions, Constantius intervened heavily in church councils, exiled opponents including Athanasius of Alexandria and Pope Liberius, and promoted formulas of faith intended to unify the empire — earning a hostile portrait in much orthodox historiography.

Assessment

The historian Ammianus Marcellinus gives a mixed account: diligent and frugal in administration, but suspicious, influenced by courtiers and eunuchs, and cruel toward perceived threats, including the purge of his relatives in 337.
