Cusp form

In number theory, a cusp form is a particular kind of modular form with a zero constant coefficient in its Fourier series expansion. In other words, a cusp form is a modular form that vanishes at all cusps of the fundamental domain of the modular group (or of the relevant congruence subgroup).

Definition

A cusp form for the full modular group is a holomorphic function f on the upper half-plane satisfying the usual modular transformation law of some weight k, and whose Fourier expansion f(τ) = Σ an qn (with q = e2πiτ) has a0 = 0. For congruence subgroups, the condition must hold at every cusp, not just at infinity.

Examples

The simplest example is the modular discriminant Δ(τ), a cusp form of weight 12 for the full modular group, defined by Δ(τ) = q ∏ (1 − qn)24. Its coefficients give Ramanujan's tau function τ(n). The Eisenstein series, by contrast, are modular forms that are not cusp forms because their constant terms are nonzero.

Properties

The vector space of cusp forms of a given weight and level is finite-dimensional. Cusp forms are central to the theory of Hecke operators: the subspace of cusp forms decomposes into simultaneous eigenforms, whose Fourier coefficients encode deep arithmetic information, such as the number of points on elliptic curves over finite fields (via the modularity theorem). The Petersson inner product makes the space of cusp forms into a Hilbert space. The Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture, proved by Deligne as a consequence of the Weil conjectures, bounds the coefficients of cusp forms.
