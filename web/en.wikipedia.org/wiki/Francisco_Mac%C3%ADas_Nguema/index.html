Francisco Macías Nguema

Born1 January 1924, Nsegayong, Spanish Guinea
Died29 September 1979, Malabo, Equatorial Guinea
OfficePresident of Equatorial Guinea (1968–1979)

Francisco Macías Nguema (1 January 1924 – 29 September 1979) was the first President of Equatorial Guinea, ruling from independence in 1968 until his overthrow and execution in 1979. His dictatorship is regarded as one of the most brutal of post-colonial Africa.

Rule

Macías concentrated all power in himself, banned opposition parties, and unleashed a reign of terror in which tens of thousands were killed or driven into exile — by some estimates a third of the population. He persecuted the educated classes, banned the word "intellectual", closed schools and churches, and destroyed the cocoa economy that had been the country's mainstay.

Overthrow

On 3 August 1979 he was deposed in a coup led by his nephew Teodoro Obiang Nguema Mbasogo. Tried for genocide, mass murder and embezzlement, Macías was executed by firing squad on 29 September 1979.
