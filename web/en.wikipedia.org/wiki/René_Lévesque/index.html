René Lévesque (24 August 1922 – 1 November 1987) was a Canadian political figure and sovereigntist leader, best known as the founder of the Parti Québécois and the 23rd Premier of Quebec (1976–1985). Born in Campbellton, New Brunswick, and raised in New Carlisle in the Gaspé, he first became prominent as a journalist and television host of the Radio-Canada program Point de mire.

Political career

As a Liberal minister in Jean Lesage's government during the Quiet Revolution, Lévesque spearheaded the nationalisation of Quebec's electricity companies into Hydro-Québec. Leaving the Liberals over sovereignty, he founded the Parti Québécois in 1968 and led it to victory in 1976 — the first sovereigntist government in Quebec's history.

Premiership

His government enacted the Charter of the French Language (Bill 101), political party financing reform, and other landmark legislation, and held the 1980 referendum on sovereignty-association, in which the "No" side won with about 60% of the vote. Lévesque was also a central figure in the 1981 constitutional negotiations and the patriation of the Canadian Constitution, from which Quebec felt excluded. He resigned in 1985 and died two years later in Montreal.
