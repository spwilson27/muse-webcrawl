Turkish makam

The Turkish makam is a system of melodic modes used in Ottoman and Turkish classical and folk music. A makam specifies not only a scale but also rules for melodic development: characteristic phrases, important notes, points of rest, the direction of melodic progression (seyir), and customary modulations. The word derives from the Arabic maqām, "place" or "position".

Theory

Turkish theory divides the octave into 53 microtonal intervals (Holderian commas), of which a practical system of 24 unequal pitches per octave is used in the Arel-Ezgi-Uzdilek theory. Makams are built from tetrachords and pentachords (çeşni), each with a characteristic intervallic flavour. Well-known makams include Rast, Uşşak, Hicaz, Hüseyni, Nihavend, Kürdilihicazkâr, and Segâh.

Each makam has a final (karar, usually the tonic), a dominant (güçlü), and a characteristic melodic course: ascending (çıkıcı), descending (inici), or ascending-descending. Compositions follow forms such as the peşrev, saz semaisi, şarkı, and the vocal beste, traditionally performed in a suite (fasıl) of pieces in one makam.

Relation to other systems

The makam system is related to the Arabic maqam, the Persian dastgah, and the Byzantine Greek echoi, all descending from the shared musical culture of the medieval Near East. Turkish makam music uses instruments such as the tanbur, ney, ud, kanun, and kemençe.
