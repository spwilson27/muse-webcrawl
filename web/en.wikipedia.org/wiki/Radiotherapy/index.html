Radiation therapy

Radiation therapy or radiotherapy, often abbreviated RT or XRT, is a treatment using ionising radiation to control or kill malignant cells, delivered by a medical linear accelerator (external-beam) or by radioactive sources placed inside the body (brachytherapy). It may be curative, given with surgery and chemotherapy, or palliative, to relieve symptoms.

Radiation damages DNA in the beam's path; cancer cells' poorer repair capacity gives a therapeutic window, sharpened by modern techniques — 3D-conformal planning, intensity-modulated RT (IMRT), stereotactic radiosurgery (e.g. Gamma Knife), image guidance and proton therapy — that sculpt dose to the tumour and spare healthy tissue. Treatment is fractionated over days or weeks. Side effects depend on the site (skin reddening, fatigue; late fibrosis or second cancers rarely). About half of all cancer patients receive radiotherapy at some point (common estimate).
