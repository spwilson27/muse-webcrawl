Triple superphosphate

Triple superphosphate (TSP) is a highly concentrated phosphate fertilizer whose main component is monocalcium phosphate, Ca(H2PO4)2. It typically contains about 44–48% available phosphate expressed as P2O5 — roughly three times the concentration of ordinary (single) superphosphate, hence its name.

Production

TSP is manufactured by reacting ground phosphate rock with phosphoric acid (rather than the sulfuric acid used for single superphosphate). Because no gypsum is formed as a diluent byproduct, the resulting product is much richer in phosphorus but contains little of the sulfur that single superphosphate supplies.

Use

TSP is applied to phosphate-deficient soils in agriculture, especially for legumes and other crops with high phosphorus demand, and is a common ingredient in blended fertilizers. Its high analysis reduces transport and handling costs per unit of nutrient. Like other soluble phosphate fertilizers, its use is managed to limit phosphorus runoff, which contributes to eutrophication of waterways.
