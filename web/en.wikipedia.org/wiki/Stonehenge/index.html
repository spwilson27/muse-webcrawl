Stonehenge

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Stonehenge at sunrise: massive grey standing stones with lintels, mist over Salisbury Plain]

Stonehenge is a prehistoric monument on Salisbury Plain in Wiltshire, England, consisting of an outer ring of vertical sarsen stones, each about 13 feet (4 m) high, topped by connecting lintels, and an inner arrangement of smaller bluestones. It is one of the most famous landmarks in the United Kingdom and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Archaeologists believe it was constructed in stages from about 3000 BC to 2000 BC. The bluestones were transported from the Preseli Hills in Wales, over 150 miles away, and the larger sarsens from a site about 25 miles distant — achievements implying a large, organised society.

Purpose

Stonehenge's exact purpose remains debated — proposed functions include a burial ground (cremated remains are found there), a healing sanctuary, and an astronomical calendar: its axis aligns with the midsummer sunrise and midwinter sunset.

Folklore and today

Medieval legend credited the wizard Merlin with moving the stones from Ireland. Stonehenge, managed by English Heritage, draws over a million visitors a year and hosts solstice gatherings.
