Duke of Hamilton is a title in the Peerage of Scotland, created in 1643. It is the senior dukedom in Scotland, and its holder is the premier peer of Scotland. The duke also holds the British title Duke of Brandon, created in 1711.

History

The Hamilton family rose to prominence in the 14th and 15th centuries and stood close to the Scottish throne; the 2nd Earl of Arran was heir presumptive to Mary, Queen of Scots and acted as Regent of Scotland. The first Duke, James Hamilton, was a leading Royalist commander in the Civil War and was executed in 1649. His brother William, the 2nd Duke, died of wounds after the Battle of Worcester in 1651.

The dukedom passed through the female line to Anne Hamilton, 3rd Duchess, whose descendants took the name Douglas-Hamilton. The family's historic seat, Hamilton Palace, was demolished in the 1920s. The current holder is Alexander Douglas-Hamilton, 16th Duke of Hamilton.
