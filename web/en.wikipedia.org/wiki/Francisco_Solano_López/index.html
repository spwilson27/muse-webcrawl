Francisco Solano López Carrillo (24 July 1826 – 1 March 1870) was a Paraguayan politician who served as President of Paraguay from 1862 until his death in 1870. He is best known for leading his country through the Paraguayan War (1864–1870), also called the War of the Triple Alliance, in which Paraguay fought against an alliance of Brazil, Argentina and Uruguay. The war devastated Paraguay, costing it a large share of its population and territory.

Francisco Solano López, president of Paraguay during the War of the Triple Alliance.

Early life and rise

López was the eldest son of President Carlos Antonio López, who had ruled Paraguay since 1844 and modernised its army, economy and infrastructure. The younger López received a military education, rose rapidly through the army, and was sent on a diplomatic mission to Europe in 1853–1854, where he purchased arms and recruited engineers and officers. He served as minister of war and as vice-president before succeeding his father in 1862.

Presidency and the road to war

As president, López continued his father's programme of state-led modernisation while concentrating power in his own hands. His intervention in Uruguay's civil war in 1864, and his determination to maintain a balance of power in the Río de la Plata region, brought conflict with Brazil. After Brazilian forces intervened in Uruguay, López seized a Brazilian steamer and invaded the province of Mato Grosso. When Argentina refused him permission to cross its territory to attack Brazil in the south, Paraguayan forces occupied Corrientes, bringing Argentina into the war as well.

The Paraguayan War

The Treaty of the Triple Alliance (May 1865) united Brazil, Argentina and Uruguay against Paraguay. After early Paraguayan successes, the war turned decisively against López: the allied victory at the Battle of Riachuelo and the surrender of Paraguayan forces at Uruguaiana in 1865 ended his offensives. The long and bloody siege of the fortress of Humaitá, and battles such as Tuyutí — one of the largest ever fought in South America — ground down the Paraguayan army. Asunción fell in January 1869.

Death and legacy

Refusing to surrender, López retreated into the northeastern hills with a small remnant force, pursued by Brazilian troops. He was killed at the Battle of Cerro Corá on 1 March 1870, reportedly declaring "I die with my country" (Muero con mi patria). His reputation remains deeply contested: long portrayed abroad as a reckless tyrant, he was rehabilitated in Paraguay during the twentieth century as a national hero who defended his country's independence to the end.
