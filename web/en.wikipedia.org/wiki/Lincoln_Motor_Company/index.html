Lincoln Motor Company

Founded: August 1917, Detroit, Michigan
Founders: Henry M. Leland, Wilfred Leland
Parent: Ford Motor Company (since 1922)
Products: Luxury vehicles

The Lincoln Motor Company, or simply Lincoln, is the luxury vehicle division of American automobile manufacturer Ford Motor Company. Marketed among the top luxury vehicle brands in the United States, Lincoln is positioned to compete against American rivals such as Cadillac, as well as imported luxury brands. The division helped to establish the personal luxury car segment with the 1940 Lincoln Continental.

History

Lincoln was founded in 1917 by Henry Leland, the precision engineer who had earlier co-founded Cadillac, and named after Abraham Lincoln, the first president for whom Leland had voted. During the First World War the company built Liberty aircraft engines. Its postwar Model L luxury car was an engineering success but a commercial disappointment, and in 1922 the bankrupt company was purchased by Ford for $8 million. Under Edsel Ford's direction, Lincoln became a styling leader; the streamlined Lincoln-Zephyr of 1936 and the Continental (1939–1948) are regarded as design classics.

Later decades

Lincolns, including the Continental Mark series and the Town Car, served as official state limousines for several U.S. presidents, beginning with Franklin D. Roosevelt's "Sunshine Special". The Navigator, launched for 1998, was the first full-size luxury SUV and a major success. In the 2010s and 2020s Lincoln refocused on crossover and SUV models such as the Corsair, Nautilus, and Aviator, and expanded sales in China, its largest market alongside North America.
