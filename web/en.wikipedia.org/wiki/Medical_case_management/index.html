Medical case management

A medical case manager coordinating care for a patient

Medical case management is a collaborative process of assessment, planning, facilitation, care coordination, evaluation, and advocacy for options and services to meet an individual's and family's comprehensive health needs through communication and available resources, while promoting quality, cost-effective outcomes. It is practised by nurses, social workers, and other professionals known as case managers, in hospitals, health insurance plans, rehabilitation settings, and workers' compensation programmes.

Process

The case management process typically follows a sequence of steps: screening and assessment of the patient's medical, functional, and psychosocial needs; development of an individualised care plan; coordination and referral to appropriate providers and community services; monitoring of the plan's implementation; and evaluation and revision as the patient's condition changes. In hospital settings, case managers also lead discharge planning, arranging post-acute care such as home health, rehabilitation, or skilled nursing placement.

Settings and roles

Health plan case managers focus on members with complex or catastrophic conditions, aiming to ensure appropriate care and avoid unnecessary hospitalisations. Provider-based case managers work within hospitals and clinics. Independent and workers' compensation case managers coordinate care and return-to-work plans for injured workers. Certification, such as the Certified Case Manager (CCM) credential administered by the Commission for Case Manager Certification in the United States, is widely recognised.

History

The roots of case management lie in early 20th-century public health nursing and social work, and in the coordination of services for returning soldiers after the World Wars. The modern profession grew rapidly in the 1980s with the spread of managed care and prospective hospital payment, which created demand for professionals who could coordinate care across settings and manage utilisation.
