The Barenboim–Said Akademie is a music academy in Berlin, Germany, founded by the conductor and pianist Daniel Barenboim. It is named after Barenboim and the Palestinian literary scholar Edward W. Said, with whom Barenboim founded the West–Eastern Divan Orchestra in 1999. The academy carries that project's ideal — bringing young musicians from Israel, Palestine and other Arab countries together — into higher education.

History and programme

The academy was founded in 2012 and opened for teaching in the autumn of 2016. It is housed in a former scenery warehouse of the Berlin State Opera on Französische Straße, converted to designs by Frank Gehry and others. The building's centrepiece is the Pierre Boulez Saal, an oval concert hall of about 680 seats named for the French composer and conductor Pierre Boulez, which opened in March 2017 and hosts both academy concerts and public performances.

Students, most of them from the Middle East and North Africa, study for a four-year Bachelor of Music (and later postgraduate diplomas) in orchestral instruments, with a curriculum that unusually integrates humanities coursework — history, philosophy and literature — alongside intensive musical training, reflecting Edward Said's belief in the humanities as a foundation for musicianship. Instruction is supported by scholarships so that tuition is not a barrier to admission.
