Hadwiger conjecture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hugo Hadwiger (1908–1981)

Hadwiger conjecture may refer to two conjectures of the Swiss mathematician Hugo Hadwiger (1908–1981):

  • Hadwiger conjecture (graph theory) — a far-reaching open problem in graph colouring, posed in 1943, which asserts that every graph with chromatic number k has a complete graph on k vertices as a minor. It is equivalent to the four colour theorem for k = 5 and remains unproved for k ≥ 7.
  • Hadwiger conjecture (combinatorial geometry) — the claim that any convex body in n-dimensional Euclidean space can be covered by at most 2n smaller copies of itself obtained by homothety, or equivalently lit so that at most 2n floodlights suffice. It is proved for n ≤ 3 and open in higher dimensions.
