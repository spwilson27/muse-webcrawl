IS–LM model

The IS–LM model, or Hicks–Hansen model, is a two-dimensional macroeconomic model used to demonstrate the relationship between interest rates and real output in the goods and services market and the money market. The model was developed by John Hicks in 1937 and later extended by Alvin Hansen, as a mathematical representation of Keynesian macroeconomic theory.

The curves

The IS curve (investment–saving) slopes downward: it traces combinations of interest rate and output at which the goods market clears, since lower rates stimulate investment and hence income. The LM curve (liquidity preference–money supply) slopes upward: it traces combinations at which the money market clears, since higher income raises money demand and thus the interest rate. Their intersection gives the general equilibrium of the two markets.

Use and criticism

The IS–LM framework is used to analyse fiscal and monetary policy: fiscal expansion shifts IS right, monetary expansion shifts LM right. It dominated macroeconomic teaching for decades, though it has been criticised for lacking microfoundations and dynamic expectations, and extended versions (such as IS–LM–BP for open economies) address some of these limits.
