The 2014 FIFA World Cup was the twentieth FIFA World Cup, held in Brazil from 12 June to 13 July 2014 — the country's second time hosting, 64 years after 1950. Germany won their fourth world title, defeating Argentina 1–0 after extra time in the final at the Maracanã in Rio de Janeiro, with Mario Götze's volley in the 113th minute. Germany thereby became the first European team to win the World Cup in the Americas.

Tournament summary

The tournament opened with shocks: defending champions Spain were beaten 5–1 by the Netherlands and eliminated in the group stage, as were Italy and England. Costa Rica improbably topped a group containing three former champions and reached the quarter-finals, while Colombia's James Rodríguez won the Golden Boot. Brazil reached the semi-finals despite losing Neymar to a fractured vertebra, then suffered the "Mineirazo" — a catastrophic 7–1 defeat by Germany in Belo Horizonte on 8 July, the heaviest loss in Brazil's history and a national trauma ranking with the 1950 Maracanazo. Miroslav Klose scored his 16th World Cup goal in that match, breaking Ronaldo's all-time record.

Technology and context

Brazil 2014 was the first World Cup to use goal-line technology and vanishing spray for free kicks. Its organisation was preceded by mass protests in 2013 over stadium costs and public services, though the tournament itself passed largely peacefully and is remembered by many as one of the most entertaining World Cups, with a high goals-per-game average and attacking football.

Final

The final repeated the 1986 and 1990 pairings. After Gonzalo Higuaín and Lionel Messi missed clear chances for Argentina and Benedikt Höwedes struck the post for Germany, substitute Götze controlled André Schürrle's cross on his chest and volleyed the winner seven minutes from the end of extra time.
