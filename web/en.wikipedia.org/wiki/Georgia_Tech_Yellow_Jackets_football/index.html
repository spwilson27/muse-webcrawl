Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets football

The Georgia Tech Yellow Jackets football program represents the Georgia Institute of Technology in American football. The Yellow Jackets compete in the NCAA Division I Football Bowl Subdivision and the Atlantic Coast Conference (ACC). The team plays its home games at Bobby Dodd Stadium at Historic Grant Field in Atlanta, Georgia, one of the oldest on-campus stadiums in college football, opened in 1913.

History

Georgia Tech fielded its first football team in 1892. Under coach John Heisman, for whom the Heisman Trophy is named, Tech won the 1917 national championship with the "Golden Tornado" team, which outscored opponents 491–17. Heisman's 1916 team defeated Cumberland 222–0, still the most lopsided result in college football history. William Alexander succeeded Heisman and led Tech to the 1928 national title, capped by a win over California in the 1929 Rose Bowl — a game famous for Cal's Roy Riegels running the wrong way after a fumble.

Bobby Dodd, Alexander's successor (1945–1966), won the 1952 national championship and made Tech a power in the Southeastern Conference before the school left the SEC in 1964. Georgia Tech joined the ACC in 1979. In 1990, under Bobby Ross, the Yellow Jackets went 11–0–1 and won a share of the national championship, finishing No. 1 in the UPI Coaches Poll after beating Nebraska in the Citrus Bowl.

Recent era

Paul Johnson coached Tech from 2008 to 2018, bringing his spread-option ("flexbone") attack and winning the 2009 ACC championship and the 2014 Orange Bowl. Geoff Collins succeeded him from 2019 to 2022; Brent Key, a former Tech lineman, took over in 2022, first as interim coach and then on a permanent basis.

Rivalries

Georgia Tech's principal rivalry is with the University of Georgia, known as "Clean, Old-Fashioned Hate." Other rivals include Clemson, Auburn, and Virginia Tech. The school's fight song is "Ramblin' Wreck from Georgia Tech."
