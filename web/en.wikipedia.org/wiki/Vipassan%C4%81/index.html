Vipassanā

Vipassanā
Tradition: Theravāda Buddhism
Meaning: "Insight", "clear seeing"
Related: Samatha; Satipaṭṭhāna

Vipassanā (Pāli) or vipaśyanā (Sanskrit) literally means "to see things as they really are" and denotes insight meditation practices in Buddhism aimed at insight into the three marks of existence: impermanence (anicca), suffering (dukkha) and non-self (anattā).

Practice

In the Theravāda tradition, vipassanā is developed on the foundation of concentration (samatha) and the four foundations of mindfulness described in the Satipaṭṭhāna Sutta: contemplation of the body, feelings, mind and mental phenomena. Noting techniques popularised by the Burmese master Mahasi Sayadaw and the lay courses of S. N. Goenka spread vipassanā worldwide in the 20th century.

Stages of insight

Classical commentaries describe a sequence of insight knowledges (ñāṇa), culminating in the realisation of nirvana. Vipassanā strongly influenced the modern mindfulness movement in the West. See also Buddhist meditation.
