Khalistan movement

The Khalistan movement is a separatist movement seeking to create an independent Sikh homeland called Khalistan ("Land of the Khalsa") in the Punjab region of India. Rooted in Sikh political grievances and the Punjabi Suba movement, it gained momentum in the late 1970s and 1980s, culminating in a violent insurgency in Punjab that lasted into the early 1990s.

History

Demands for Sikh autonomy grew after Indian independence, leading to the creation of the Punjabi-speaking state of Punjab in 1966. The Anandpur Sahib Resolution of 1973 sought greater state autonomy. In the 1980s the preacher Jarnail Singh Bhindranwale and armed militants occupied the Golden Temple complex in Amritsar; the Indian Army's Operation Blue Star in June 1984 stormed the temple, killing Bhindranwale and many others. The assassination of Prime Minister Indira Gandhi by her Sikh bodyguards followed, triggering anti-Sikh riots. A decade of insurgency and counterinsurgency ensued, in which tens of thousands died.

Present day

Within India the movement has little active support today, though Khalistan advocacy persists among parts of the Sikh diaspora in Canada, the United Kingdom and elsewhere, periodically causing diplomatic tensions with India.
