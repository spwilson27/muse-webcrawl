Bulguksa

Bulguksa (Korean: 불국사) is a Buddhist temple complex in Gyeongju, North Gyeongsang Province, South Korea. A masterpiece of Silla-dynasty art and architecture, it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1995 together with the nearby Seokguram Grotto, and houses several of South Korea's National Treasures.

History

A small temple was first built on the site in 528 during the reign of King Beopheung of Silla. The present complex was constructed between 751 and 774 under the direction of chief minister Kim Daeseong, who, according to legend, built Bulguksa for his parents in his present life and Seokguram for his parents in a previous life. The wooden halls were largely destroyed during the Japanese invasions of the 1590s; the stone structures survived, and the temple was extensively restored in the 20th century.

Architecture

The temple is laid out as an earthly representation of the Buddha's Pure Land. Two famous stone staircases, the Blue Cloud and White Cloud bridges, lead up to the main courtyard. Stone terraces and balustrades are considered among the finest examples of Silla masonry.

Treasures

The courtyard holds two celebrated stone pagodas: the simple three-story Seokgatap (Sakyamuni Pagoda) and the ornate Dabotap (Many Treasures Pagoda), the latter depicted on the South Korean 10-won coin. In 1966 a copy of the Great Dharani Sutra, among the world's oldest printed texts, was found inside Seokgatap. The temple also preserves gilt-bronze statues of Vairocana and Amitabha Buddha.
