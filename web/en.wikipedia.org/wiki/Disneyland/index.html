Disneyland

Disneyland is an amusement park in Anaheim, California, United States. It opened on July 17, 1955, and is the only theme park designed and built to completion under the direct supervision of Walt Disney. The park is owned and operated by Disney Experiences, a division of The Walt Disney Company. It was originally called "Disneyland Park" to distinguish it from the larger Disneyland Resort complex of which it is a part.

History

Walt Disney conceived a small amusement park beside his Burbank studios in the late 1940s, inspired by watching his daughters ride a merry-go-round in Griffith Park. The concept grew into a full theme park with themed lands, funded partly through a television deal with ABC. Construction began in 1954 on a 160-acre orange grove in Anaheim. Opening day, July 17, 1955, was marred by heat, overcrowding, and unfinished attractions, but the park quickly became a commercial success.

Lands

The park is organized into nine themed "lands" radiating from a central hub in front of Sleeping Beauty Castle:

  • Main Street, U.S.A. — an idealized early-20th-century American small town.
  • Adventureland — exotic jungle locales, home to the Jungle Cruise and Indiana Jones Adventure.
  • New Orleans Square — featuring Pirates of the Caribbean and the Haunted Mansion.
  • Frontierland — the American Old West, with Big Thunder Mountain Railroad.
  • Critter Country — formerly Bear Country, home to Tiana's Bayou Adventure.
  • Fantasyland — fairy-tale attractions including It's a Small World and Peter Pan's Flight.
  • Mickey's Toontown — a cartoon neighbourhood for the classic characters.
  • Tomorrowland — futuristic attractions including Space Mountain and the Monorail.
  • Star Wars: Galaxy's Edge — opened 2019, set on the planet Batuu.

Attendance and significance

Disneyland is one of the most visited theme parks in the world, with cumulative attendance exceeding 750 million guests since opening. It served as the template for the "Magic Kingdom" parks built in Florida, Tokyo, Paris, Hong Kong, and Shanghai, and its design principles — themed immersion, controlled sightlines, and the hub-and-spoke layout — became industry standards.
