Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design (LEED) is a green building certification program used worldwide. Developed by the non-profit U.S. Green Building Council (USGBC), it includes a set of rating systems for the design, construction, operation, and maintenance of green buildings, homes, and neighborhoods, which aims to help building owners and operators be environmentally responsible and use resources efficiently.

Rating systems and certification levels

LEED provides a framework for healthy, efficient, carbon and cost-saving green buildings. Projects earn points across categories including sustainable sites, water efficiency, energy and atmosphere, materials and resources, indoor environmental quality, and innovation. Depending on the number of points achieved, a project receives one of four certification levels: Certified (40–49 points), Silver (50–59), Gold (60–79), and Platinum (80+).

Rating systems exist for new construction and major renovations, existing buildings, commercial interiors, core and shell, schools, healthcare, retail, and homes, as well as neighborhood development. Certification is administered by Green Business Certification Inc. (GBCI), and professionals may become credentialed as LEED Green Associates or LEED Accredited Professionals (LEED AP).

History

Development of LEED began in 1993, led by Natural Resources Defense Council scientist Robert Watson. The pilot version, LEED NCv1.0, was launched in 1998, followed by LEED v2.0 in 2000, LEED 2009 (v3), and LEED v4 in 2013. LEED v5 was released in the mid-2020s with a stronger emphasis on decarbonization, equity, and resilience. By the 2020s, more than 100,000 projects had participated in LEED worldwide.

Criticism

Critics have questioned whether LEED-certified buildings consistently outperform conventional buildings in measured energy use, and have raised concerns about the cost of certification, the weight given to some categories, and "point chasing" design decisions. The USGBC has responded with performance-reporting requirements and successive revisions of the standard.
