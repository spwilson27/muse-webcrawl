Rolling Thunder is a United States veterans' advocacy organization, founded in 1987 by Vietnam veterans Artie Muller and Ray Manzo, that seeks to bring full accountability for American prisoners of war and service members missing in action (POW/MIA). It is best known for its annual motorcycle demonstration ride in Washington, D.C.

The Ride for Freedom

The first Rolling Thunder ride took place on Memorial Day weekend in 1988, drawing about 2,500 motorcyclists to the Pentagon and the Vietnam Veterans Memorial. The event, known as the "Ride for Freedom", grew into one of the largest annual motorcycle gatherings in the world, drawing hundreds of thousands of riders. In 2019 the national organisation announced the Washington ride would end after that year, citing costs, though local chapters continued their own events.

Activities

Beyond the ride, Rolling Thunder chapters engage in charitable work for veterans, visit VA hospitals, award scholarships, and lobby Congress on veterans' legislation. The organisation's name echoes Operation Rolling Thunder, the bombing campaign over North Vietnam.
