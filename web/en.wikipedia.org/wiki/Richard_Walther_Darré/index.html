Richard Walther Darré

Richard Walther Darré (born Ricardo Walther Óscar Darré; 14 July 1895 – 5 September 1953) was a Nazi German politician and ideologist who served as Reich Minister of Food and Agriculture from 1933 to 1942. He was a leading proponent of the "Blood and Soil" (Blut und Boden) ideology.

Early life

Born in Buenos Aires, Argentina, to a German father and Swedish-Argentine mother, Darré was educated in Germany and served as an officer in the First World War. He studied agriculture and developed theories linking racial purity to a landed peasantry.

Nazi career

Darré joined the Nazi Party in 1930 and organised its agrarian apparatus, helping win rural votes. As minister he enacted the Reich Hereditary Farm Law, protecting supposedly racially pure hereditary farms, and promoted autarky and settlement in the east. He also held high SS rank and headed the SS Race and Settlement Main Office.

Later life

He lost influence to rivals and was replaced by Herbert Backe in 1942. After the war he was convicted at the Ministries Trial in Nuremberg for crimes against humanity and membership in a criminal organisation, sentenced to seven years, released in 1950, and died in Munich in 1953.
