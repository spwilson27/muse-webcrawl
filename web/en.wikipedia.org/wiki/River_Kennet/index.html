The River Kennet is a tributary of the River Thames in Southern England. Its upper reaches are a winterbourne — flowing mainly in winter from chalk springs — and it is one of England's most celebrated chalk streams. The river rises at Swallowhead Spring near Silbury Hill in Wiltshire and flows east for about 72 kilometres (45 mi) through Marlborough, Hungerford, Newbury and Theale to join the Thames at Reading in Berkshire.

Course

From its source near the prehistoric monuments of Avebury, the Kennet flows through the North Wessex Downs, an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty. Its principal tributaries include the River Og, the River Lambourn, the River Enborne and the Foudry Brook. Below Newbury the river runs alongside (and in places shares its channel with) the Kennet and Avon Canal.

Ecology

The Kennet is designated a Site of Special Scientific Interest for its chalk stream habitat, plant communities and invertebrate fauna. Its clear, alkaline, spring-fed water supports water crowfoot beds, brown trout and grayling, and the river is famous for fly fishing. Concerns over water abstraction reducing flows in the upper river led to campaigning by the Action for the River Kennet group and subsequent reductions in abstraction from the chalk aquifer.

History

The Kennet valley has been settled since prehistory, lying close to the Avebury stone circles and Silbury Hill. The river powered numerous watermills and drove the woollen trade of Marlborough and Newbury. It was made navigable between Reading and Newbury in the early 18th century as the Kennet Navigation, later incorporated into the Kennet and Avon Canal.
