Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib

Bornc. 568 CE, Mecca, Arabia
Died22 March 625 CE (15 Shawwal 3 AH), Mount Uhud, near Medina
Known forCompanion and paternal uncle of Muhammad; commander at the Battle of Badr
TitleSayyid al-Shuhada ("Chief of the Martyrs"); "Lion of Allah"

Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib (Arabic: حمزة ابن عبد المطلب; c. 568 – 625 CE) was a foster brother, paternal uncle, and companion of the Islamic prophet Muhammad. Renowned for his strength and skill in battle, he is honoured in Islamic tradition with the titles Asad Allah ("Lion of Allah") and Sayyid al-Shuhada ("Chief of the Martyrs").

Early life

Hamza was a son of Abd al-Muttalib, chief of the Banu Hashim clan of the Quraysh tribe, and Hala bint Wuhayb. He was close in age to Muhammad and, by tradition, his foster brother, both having been nursed by Thuwaybah. Before his conversion, Hamza was known in Mecca as a powerful huntsman and wrestler who took little interest in the religious disputes of the city.

Conversion to Islam

According to tradition, Hamza embraced Islam around 616 CE after returning from a hunt to find that Muhammad had been insulted and struck by Abu Jahl. Enraged, Hamza confronted Abu Jahl at the Kaaba, struck him with his bow, and declared himself a follower of Muhammad's religion. His conversion strengthened the small Muslim community, since few dared to challenge him.

Military career

After the Hijra to Medina in 622, Hamza became one of the foremost Muslim commanders. He led the first expedition dispatched by Muhammad, a raid on a Quraysh caravan near the coast. At the Battle of Badr in 624 he fought prominently, killing several leading Meccans in single combat before the general engagement.

Death at Uhud

Hamza was killed at the Battle of Uhud on 22 March 625 by Wahshi ibn Harb, an Abyssinian slave who had been promised his freedom by Hind bint Utbah for avenging her father's death at Badr. Wahshi killed Hamza with a thrown javelin. Muhammad was deeply grieved by his death, and Hamza's tomb at Uhud remains a site of visitation.

Legacy

Hamza is revered by both Sunni and Shia Muslims. He is a central, heroic figure of the popular epic cycle known as the Hamzanama ("Book of Hamza"), a vast collection of fantastical adventures hugely popular in Persian, Turkish, Arabic, and later Mughal storytelling, and the subject of one of the most celebrated illustrated manuscripts of the Mughal court.
