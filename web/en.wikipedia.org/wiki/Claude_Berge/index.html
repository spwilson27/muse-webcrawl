Claude Berge

Born5 June 1926, Paris, France
Died30 June 2002 (aged 76)
FieldsMathematics, graph theory

Claude Jacques Berge (5 June 1926 – 30 June 2002) was a French mathematician, recognized as one of the modern founders of combinatorics and graph theory. He is particularly known for Berge's lemma and the concept of Berge graph.

Work

Berge's 1958 book Théorie des graphes et ses applications helped establish graph theory as a major field in France. He formulated the strong perfect graph conjecture (proved in 2002 as the strong perfect graph theorem) and introduced hypergraphs as a generalisation of graphs. He obtained his PhD in 1953 under André Lichnerowicz.

Oulipo

Berge was also a founding member of the Oulipo literary group, co-founded in 1960 by François Le Lionnais and Raymond Queneau, and wrote mathematical and literary works combining both interests.
