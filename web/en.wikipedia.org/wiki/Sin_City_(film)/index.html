Sin City (film)

Frank Miller's Sin City (2005), noted for its stark black-and-white visuals.

Frank Miller's Sin City is a 2005 American neo-noir crime anthology film produced and directed by Robert Rodriguez and Frank Miller. Quentin Tarantino directed one scene. It is based on Miller's graphic novel series of the same name, principally adapting the stories "The Hard Goodbye", "The Big Fat Kill", "That Yellow Bastard", and the short story "The Customer Is Always Right". The film stars an ensemble cast including Jessica Alba, Benicio del Toro, Brittany Murphy, Clive Owen, Mickey Rourke, Bruce Willis, and Elijah Wood.

Plot

The film is divided into segments set in the corrupt Basin City. In "The Customer Is Always Right", a salesman seduces and murders a woman. In "That Yellow Bastard", aging policeman John Hartigan saves the young Nancy Callahan from the disfigured child-killer Roark Junior, and years later protects the adult Nancy at the cost of his own life. In "The Hard Goodbye", the hulking Marv avenges the murder of Goldie, a prostitute who showed him kindness, killing the cannibal Kevin and Cardinal Roark. In "The Big Fat Kill", Dwight McCarthy helps the women of Old Town cover up the killing of a corrupt undercover cop, Jack Rafferty, after a battle with Manute's mercenaries.

Production

The film was shot almost entirely on digital backlot: actors performed on green screens, and the environments were created in post-production to replicate the high-contrast black-and-white look of Miller's comics, with occasional splashes of colour. Miller initially refused film offers but agreed after Rodriguez shot a short scene as proof of concept. Miller is credited as co-director, and Tarantino as "special guest director".

Reception

Sin City premiered at the Cannes Film Festival, where it was nominated for the Palme d'Or and won the Technical Grand Prize for visual shaping. It grossed over $158 million worldwide against a $40 million budget, and received generally positive reviews, with particular praise for its visual style. A sequel, Sin City: A Dame to Kill For, was released in 2014.
