Hambach Castle (German: Hambacher Schloss) is a castle near the city of Neustadt an der Weinstraße in Rhineland-Palatinate, Germany. It is celebrated as a symbol of German democracy because of the Hambach Festival held there in 1832.

History

The castle began as the Kästenburg, probably built in the 11th century as part of the defensive system of the Speyer bishopric. It was destroyed during the Palatine Peasants' War and later conflicts, and remained a picturesque ruin. In the 19th century the ruined castle, then a Bavarian possession, passed as a wedding gift to Crown Prince Maximilian of Bavaria.

The Hambach Festival

Between 27 and 30 May 1832, some 20,000–30,000 people — students, workers, and citizens, including Polish exiles — gathered at the castle for what had been announced as a folk festival. They demanded national unity, civil liberties, and democracy, and raised the black-red-gold flag that later became the flag of Germany. The festival is often called the "cradle of German democracy", and the ensuing conservative repression, including the Six Articles, failed to extinguish the movement it symbolised.

Today

Restored in phases since the 1980s, the castle houses a permanent exhibition on the road to German democracy and hosts concerts, ceremonies, and conferences. In 2015 it was awarded the European Heritage Label.
