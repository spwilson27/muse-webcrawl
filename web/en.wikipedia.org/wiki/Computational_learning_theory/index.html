Computational learning theory

In theoretical computer science, computational learning theory (COLT) is the mathematical study of machine learning algorithms: their efficiency, sample requirements, and the conditions under which learning is possible.

PAC learning

The probably approximately correct (PAC) framework, introduced by Leslie Valiant in 1984, asks when a learner can, with high probability, produce a hypothesis with low error using a number of examples polynomial in the relevant parameters. Valiant received the Turing Award partly for this work.

VC dimension

The Vapnik–Chervonenkis (VC) dimension measures the capacity of a hypothesis class and determines the sample complexity of learning: a class is PAC learnable (in the realizable setting) if and only if its VC dimension is finite.

Other models

The field also studies online learning and mistake bounds, exact learning with queries, and statistical query learning, as well as connections to boosting and Occam's razor arguments.
