Sheet music
FormPrinted or digital musical notation
NotationStaff notation (five-line staff, clefs, key and time signatures)

Sheet music is a handwritten or printed form of musical notation that uses musical symbols to indicate the pitches, rhythms, or chords of a song or instrumental musical piece. Like its analogs — printed books or pamphlets in English, Arabic, or other languages — the medium of sheet music typically is paper (or, in earlier centuries, papyrus or parchment).

Types

A score shows all the instrumental and vocal parts of an ensemble composition aligned vertically. A part contains only the music played by an individual musician. Piano music is typically written on two staves joined by a brace (a grand staff). Lead sheets give only the melody, lyrics, and chord symbols, while chord charts and fake books serve jazz and popular musicians. Tablature ("tab") is a common alternative notation for fretted instruments such as the guitar.

History

Printed sheet music dates to the late 15th century; Ottaviano Petrucci's Harmonice Musices Odhecaton (1501) was the first book of polyphonic music printed from movable type. Music engraving on copper and pewter plates dominated the 18th and 19th centuries, followed by lithography and, in the 20th century, offset printing. Engraving was traditionally done by hand, a skilled craft yielding the elegant style still imitated by notation software.

Modern production

Computer notation programs such as Finale, Sibelius, Dorico, and the open-source MuseScore have replaced hand engraving. Sheet music is now widely distributed digitally as PDF files and through projects such as the International Music Score Library Project (IMSLP), which hosts hundreds of thousands of public-domain scores.
