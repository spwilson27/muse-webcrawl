Air Force Nuclear Weapons Center

The Air Force Nuclear Weapons Center (AFNWC) is a United States Air Force organisation headquartered at Kirtland Air Force Base, New Mexico. It is the Air Force's centre of expertise for the management, sustainment and modernisation of nuclear weapons systems, supporting both the bomber and intercontinental ballistic missile legs of the US nuclear triad.

History and role

The centre traces its lineage to post-war special-weapons organisations at Kirtland. It was activated in 2006 as the Nuclear Weapons Center to consolidate nuclear sustainment functions, and renamed the Air Force Nuclear Weapons Center in 2015. After the 2007 Minot–Barksdale nuclear weapons incident, the Air Force moved to strengthen nuclear enterprise oversight, and the AFNWC became a key part of that structure, working closely with Air Force Global Strike Command.

Responsibilities

The AFNWC manages the Air Force's nuclear weapons portfolio across its life cycle, including the B61 and B83 gravity bombs, the W78 and W87 ICBM warheads (in coordination with the National Nuclear Security Administration), the AGM-86 air-launched cruise missile, and ground systems for the Minuteman III. It supports development of the LGM-35 Sentinel ICBM and the AGM-181 Long Range Stand Off weapon, oversees nuclear certification of delivery aircraft, and runs directorates at Kirtland and Hill AFB, Utah, among other locations.
