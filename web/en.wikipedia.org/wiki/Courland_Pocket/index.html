Courland Pocket

The Courland Pocket (German: Kurland-Kessel) was a battlefield on the Courland Peninsula in western Latvia during the Second World War, where German forces of Army Group North — renamed Army Group Courland in January 1945 — were cut off by the Red Army from October 1944 until the end of the war in Europe in May 1945.

Formation of the pocket

During the Soviet Baltic Offensive of autumn 1944, the Red Army reached the Baltic Sea near Memel on 10 October 1944, severing Army Group North's land connection with East Prussia. Around thirty German divisions were trapped in Courland, supplied only by sea through the ports of Libau (Liepāja) and Windau (Ventspils). Adolf Hitler refused to evacuate the force by sea, believing it could tie down Soviet troops and protect the Baltic training areas used by U-boats.

The battles of Courland

Between October 1944 and April 1945 the Soviets launched six major offensives to crush the pocket, all of which failed to break the German defences, at heavy cost to both sides. The trapped army group, commanded successively by Ferdinand Schörner, Lothar Rendulic and Carl Hilpert, held its positions on the Tukums line. Only after Germany's unconditional surrender did Army Group Courland capitulate, on 8–9 May 1945, with roughly 180,000–200,000 men marching into Soviet captivity.
