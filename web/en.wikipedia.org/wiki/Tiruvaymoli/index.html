Tiruvaymoli

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Tiruvaymoli (Tamil: திருவாய்மொழி, "sacred utterance") is a celebrated work of Tamil Vaishnava devotional poetry composed by Nammalvar, the most revered of the twelve Alvar saints. Comprising 1,102 verses arranged in 110 decades, it forms the fourth and crowning section of the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, the collected hymns of the Alvars.

Content and significance

The poems express intense devotion (bhakti) to Vishnu, moving through longing, union, separation and surrender, and are regarded within Sri Vaishnavism as the essence of the Vedas rendered in Tamil — sometimes called the "Tamil Veda". Tradition holds that Nammalvar composed the work while seated in meditation in the hollow of a tamarind tree at Alwarthirunagari.

Commentaries and practice

The Tiruvaymoli generated an extensive commentarial tradition in Manipravalam (a Sanskrit–Tamil blend), beginning with works associated with the lineage of Ramanuja, including the celebrated commentary tradition of the "Bhagavad Vishayam". Its verses are recited daily in Sri Vaishnava temples and homes, and feature centrally in festivals such as the Adhyayana Utsavam at Srirangam, where the whole Divya Prabandham is chanted before the deity.
