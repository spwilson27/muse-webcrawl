Bab al-Nasr (Arabic: باب النصر, "Gate of Victory") is one of three remaining gates in the historic walls of Cairo, Egypt. Built in 1087 under the Fatimid vizier Badr al-Jamali, it stands in the northern wall of the old city, a short distance east of Bab al-Futuh.

History

The gate was originally named Bab al-'Izz ("Gate of Prosperity") but was renamed Bab al-Nasr by the army commander in charge of its construction. Tradition states that armies departing Cairo on campaign marched out through Bab al-Nasr and returned victorious through Bab al-Futuh. The gate retains inscriptions and carved shields on its towers, and later rulers, including Napoleon's expeditionary forces, used the surrounding towers — the French renamed its towers and used them as defensive positions.

Architecture

Unlike the rounded towers of Bab al-Futuh, Bab al-Nasr is flanked by tall square towers. The gate is noted for its massive stone construction, a vaulted passage, and decorative carved emblems and inscriptions, including names of caliphs. A staircase inside gives access to the top of the walls and guard rooms built into the towers.
