Spirited Away

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Chihiro standing before Yubaba's towering bathhouse at dusk, lanterns lit, the train tracks running into the sea behind her]

Spirited Away (Japanese: 千と千尋の神隠し, Sen to Chihiro no Kamikakushi) is a 2001 Japanese animated fantasy film written and directed by Hayao Miyazaki and produced by Studio Ghibli. The ten-year-old Chihiro, moving house with her parents, wanders into a bathhouse for the gods; when the witch Yubaba turns her parents into pigs and steals her name, Chihiro must work in the bathhouse, remember herself, and free them — aided by the river spirit Haku, the boiler-man Kamaji and the silent spirit No-Face.

It became the highest-grossing film in Japanese history to that date, won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature (2003) and the Golden Bear at Berlin — the only animated film to win it — and tops polls as the greatest animated film ever made.

Making

Miyazaki came out of a declared retirement to make the film for the ten-year-old daughters of friends, wanting a heroine who was an ordinary girl, not a chosen one. Ghibli's artists built the bathhouse from Edo-period architecture and the Dōgo Onsen; the film's wonders — the stink spirit's cleansing, the train gliding over flooded tracks in near-silence — are hand-drawn patience made visible. Joe Hisaishi's piano-and-orchestra score is inseparable from its spell.

Themes

Names and memory as identity, greed (No-Face gorging on gold and bathhouse flattery), work as dignity, and Shinto animism's porous border between worlds: Spirited Away works for children as adventure and for adults as a meditation on growing up inside a Japan consuming its own past. Its English dub (supervised by Pixar's John Lasseter, Miyazaki's friend and champion) set the standard for localisation care.
