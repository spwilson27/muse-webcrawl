Raising the Flag on Iwo Jima is the iconic World War II photograph by Joe Rosenthal of the Associated Press, taken on 23 February 1945, showing six United States Marines raising the US flag atop Mount Suribachi during the Battle of Iwo Jima. It became the most reproduced photograph of the war and the defining image of the Marine Corps.

The second flag-raising

The Rosenthal photograph depicts not the first flag raised on the summit but a replacement: a smaller flag raised earlier that day by another patrol was to be swapped for a larger battle ensign so it could be seen from across the island. Rosenthal, arriving as the flag was being raised, nearly missed the shot — he took the exposure from atop a pile of rocks without looking through the viewfinder, asking an assistant afterward whether the scene was "in focus". The six flag-raisers were members of the 5th Marine Division's Easy Company, 2nd Platoon. Three of them — Harlon Block, Franklin Sousley, and Michael Strank — were killed in the fighting that followed.

Identification controversies and legacy

Postwar investigations and later scholarship twice corrected the identification of the men in the photograph: in 1947, Corporal Harlon Block (long misidentified as Hank Hansen) was confirmed as one of the raisers; in 2016, research by amateur historians prompted a Marine Corps review that identified PFC Harold Schultz (not John Bradley) as a raiser; and in 2019, PFC Harold Keller was identified in place of Rene Gagnon. The image inspired the Seventh War Bond drive poster, the US Marine Corps War Memorial (the Iwo Jima Memorial) in Arlington Ridge Park, and the 2006 Clint Eastwood films Flags of Our Fathers and Letters from Iwo Jima.
