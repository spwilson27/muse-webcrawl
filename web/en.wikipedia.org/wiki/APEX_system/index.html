The APEX system (Additive System of Photographic Exposure) is a method of calculating photographic exposure in which the familiar multiplicative quantities — f-number, shutter speed, scene luminance, and film speed — are replaced by small whole-number additive values derived from their base-2 logarithms. It was proposed in the late 1950s and adopted as the American standard ASA PH2.7-1960, and elements of it survive today in the EXIF metadata recorded by digital cameras.

Values

In the APEX system each doubling of exposure corresponds to an increment of one unit:

  • Aperture value Av: f/1 = 0, f/1.4 = 1, f/2 = 2, f/2.8 = 3, f/4 = 4, and so on.
  • Time value Tv: 1 s = 0, 1/2 s = 1, 1/4 s = 2, 1/30 s ≈ 5, 1/250 s ≈ 8.
  • Speed value Sv is defined relative to film speed, and brightness (luminance) value Lv (or Bv) describes the scene.

The exposure equation then becomes simple addition: the exposure value Ev = Av + Tv must equal Lv + Sv for correct exposure. A photographer or meter needs only add and subtract small integers rather than manipulate ratios.

Use and legacy

APEX was intended to simplify the design of coupled light meters and automatic cameras, and some cameras and meters of the 1960s displayed APEX values, but the traditional f-number and shutter-speed markings proved too entrenched for the system to win general adoption among photographers. Its values nonetheless persist in the exposure value (EV) concept and in EXIF fields such as ApertureValue, ShutterSpeedValue, BrightnessValue, and ExposureBiasValue, which are all recorded in APEX units.
