Venkateswara

Venkateswara (Sanskrit: वेंकटेश्वर, Veṅkaṭeśvara), also known as Srinivasa, Balaji and Venkatachalapati, is a form of the Hindu god Vishnu, worshipped as the preserver who appears on Earth in the Kali Yuga to guide humanity. His principal temple is the Sri Venkateswara Temple at Tirumala, near Tirupati in Andhra Pradesh, one of the most visited and wealthiest religious sites in the world.

Legend

According to tradition preserved in the Puranas and temple lore, Vishnu descended to the Venkata hill after a dispute involving the goddess Lakshmi and the sage Bhrigu. He was discovered as a child by the king Akasha Raja and married the princess Padmavati. To pay for the wedding, Vishnu is said to have borrowed a vast sum from Kubera, the god of wealth, which devotees help repay through their offerings — a theological rationale for the temple's famous donations.

Worship

Devotees undertake pilgrimage to Tirumala, often climbing the hill on foot, tonsuring their hair as an offering, and receiving darshan of the deity and the prasadam of Tirupati laddu. The temple is administered by the Tirumala Tirupati Devasthanams and follows the Vaikhanasa Agama tradition of worship.
