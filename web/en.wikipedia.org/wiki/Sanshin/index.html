Sanshin

The sanshin (三線, literally "three strings") is an Okinawan three-stringed lute and the principal instrument of traditional music in Okinawa and the Amami Islands of Japan. It is the direct ancestor of the Japanese shamisen.

Description

The sanshin consists of a long neck, traditionally carved from ebony or other dark hardwood, and a body traditionally covered with python skin (now often synthetic), which gives the instrument its distinctive warm, soft timbre. Its three strings are plucked with a plectrum traditionally made of water buffalo horn worn on the index finger. Standard tunings are named honchōshi and variants thereof.

History and use

The sanshin developed in the Ryukyu Kingdom from the Chinese sanxian, which arrived through trade in the 14th–16th centuries. It became central to Ryukyuan court music and folk song, accompanying singing in genres such as classical uzagaku and folk min'yō. In the late 16th and 17th centuries the instrument was carried to mainland Japan, where it evolved into the larger, sharper-toned shamisen. The sanshin remains a potent symbol of Okinawan identity, heard everywhere from formal ceremonies to pop music.
