Red wolf

The red wolf (Canis rufus) is a canine native to the southeastern United States. Its coat is generally a reddish-tawny colour, and it is intermediate in size between the coyote and the gray wolf. The taxonomic status of the red wolf has long been debated: it has variously been regarded as a distinct species, a subspecies of the gray wolf, or a recent hybrid of wolves and coyotes. A 2021 review by the National Academies supported its recognition as a distinct species.

Description

Adults typically weigh 20–39 kg (45–85 lb) and measure 136–160 cm in total length. The fur is coarse and predominantly cinnamon or tawny with gray and black shading along the back. Red wolves are social animals that live in family packs and hunt deer, raccoons, rabbits and other small mammals in coastal prairies, marshes and bottomland forests.

Decline and recovery

By the mid-20th century, predator control and habitat loss had reduced the red wolf to a remnant population in coastal Texas and Louisiana, where it extensively hybridised with coyotes. The species was declared extinct in the wild in 1980 after the last wild animals were captured for a captive breeding programme. In 1987, red wolves were reintroduced into the Alligator River National Wildlife Refuge in northeastern North Carolina — the first reintroduction of a species declared extinct in the wild. The wild population has since fluctuated amid poaching, vehicle strikes and coyote hybridisation, and remains critically endangered, with only a few dozen known individuals in the wild supplemented by more than 200 in captivity.
