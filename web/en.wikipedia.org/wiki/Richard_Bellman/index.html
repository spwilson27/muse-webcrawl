Richard Bellman

Richard Ernest Bellman (August 26, 1920 – March 19, 1984) was an American applied mathematician, who introduced dynamic programming in 1953, and made important contributions in many fields of mathematics.

Biography

Bellman was born in Brooklyn, New York, studied mathematics at Brooklyn College and the University of Wisconsin, and received his PhD from Princeton in 1947 under Solomon Lefschetz. During the Second World War he worked on the Manhattan Project at Los Alamos. He later worked at the RAND Corporation, then joined the University of Southern California as a professor in 1965.

Contributions

His principle of optimality — that an optimal policy has the property that whatever the initial state and decision, the remaining decisions must constitute an optimal policy — underlies the Bellman equation used across control theory, economics, and computer science. He also coined the phrase "curse of dimensionality" for the exponential growth of computational difficulty with dimension, and co-developed the Bellman–Ford shortest-path algorithm. Bellman authored dozens of books and several hundred papers, and received the first Norbert Wiener Prize in Applied Mathematics and the IEEE Medal of Honor in 1979.
