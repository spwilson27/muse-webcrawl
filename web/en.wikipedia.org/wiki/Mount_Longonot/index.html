Mount Longonot is a dormant stratovolcano in the Great Rift Valley of Kenya, southeast of Lake Naivasha. Its name derives from the Maasai word Oloonong'ot, meaning "mountain of many spurs" or "steep ridges". The mountain and its surroundings form Mount Longonot National Park, administered by the Kenya Wildlife Service.

Geology and landscape

Longonot is a young trachytic volcano with a large caldera, roughly 8 by 12 km, formed by eruptions about 21,000 years ago; a smaller crater caps the summit cone. Dense forest grows on the crater floor, and small steam vents issue from fissures around the caldera walls. Geologists believe it last erupted in the 1860s.

Visiting and geothermal energy

A trail of about 3 km climbs from the park gate to the crater rim, from which a path circles the crater in a popular day hike with views across the Rift Valley floor. Wildlife in the park includes buffalo, zebra, giraffe, Thomson's gazelle and leopards. On the mountain's flanks Kenya exploits the Olkaria geothermal field — among the largest in Africa — whose power stations supply a substantial share of the country's electricity.
