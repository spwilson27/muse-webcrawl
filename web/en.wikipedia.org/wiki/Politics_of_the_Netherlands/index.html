Politics of the Netherlands

The politics of the Netherlands take place within a parliamentary constitutional monarchy and a highly fragmented multi-party system. The monarch is head of state; the Prime Minister heads the government. The Netherlands is often described as a consociational democracy built on consultation and compromise — the "polder model".

Institutions

The legislature, the States General, consists of the House of Representatives (Tweede Kamer, 150 seats, directly elected) and the Senate (Eerste Kamer, 75 seats, elected by provincial councils). Elections use pure proportional representation with a very low effective threshold, so many parties win seats and coalition governments of three or four parties are usual. Formation negotiations, led by a formateur, can take many months.

Political history

For much of the twentieth century Dutch society was organised into "pillars" (verzuiling) — Catholic, Protestant, socialist and liberal — each with its own parties, unions, schools and broadcasters. Depillarisation from the 1960s and the rise of new parties increased volatility. Long-dominant parties include the Christian Democratic Appeal (CDA), the Labour Party (PvdA) and the People's Party for Freedom and Democracy (VVD) of Mark Rutte, prime minister from 2010 to 2024. Populist and issue parties — from Pim Fortuyn's LPF to Geert Wilders' Party for Freedom (PVV), which won the 2023 election — have reshaped the landscape.
