Abimelech (Hebrew: אֲבִימֶלֶךְ, "my father is king") was the son of the judge Gideon (Jerubbaal) by a concubine from Shechem, and the first figure in the Hebrew Bible to have himself crowned king in Israel. His story, told in Judges 9, is a cautionary tale of usurpation and violent retribution, and contains Jotham's fable of the trees — the oldest fable in the Bible.

Rise to power

After Gideon's death, Abimelech persuaded the lords of Shechem — his mother's kin — to support him over his seventy half-brothers, arguing "Which is better for you, that all seventy of the sons of Jerubbaal rule over you, or that one rule over you?" With seventy pieces of silver from the temple of Baal-berith he hired "worthless and reckless fellows" and murdered his brothers at Ophrah, "on one stone"; only the youngest, Jotham, escaped. The men of Shechem then made Abimelech king "by the oak of the pillar" (Judges 9:6).

Jotham's fable and the fall of Shechem

From Mount Gerizim Jotham denounced the choice with the fable of the trees who, refused by the olive, fig and vine, chose the bramble as king, and cursed both Abimelech and Shechem. After three years, God "sent an evil spirit" between them; the Shechemites revolted under Gaal son of Ebed. Abimelech defeated Gaal, destroyed Shechem, sowed it with salt, and burned its tower with about a thousand people inside.

Death at Thebez

Advancing on Thebez, Abimelech was storming its tower when a woman dropped an upper millstone on his head and crushed his skull. Mortally wounded, he ordered his armour-bearer to kill him with his sword "so men will not say about me, 'A woman killed him'" (Judges 9:54). The narrative concludes that God repaid the wickedness of Abimelech and of the men of Shechem, fulfilling Jotham's curse.
