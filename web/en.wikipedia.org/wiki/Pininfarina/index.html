Pininfarina

Pininfarina S.p.A. is an Italian car design firm and coachbuilder, headquartered in Cambiano, Turin. The company was founded in 1930 by Battista "Pinin" Farina. It is famous for designing many classic automobiles for manufacturers including Ferrari, Alfa Romeo, Peugeot, Fiat, and Maserati.

History

Battista Farina founded Carrozzeria Pinin Farina in 1930 after leaving his brother's coachbuilding firm. The company became known for elegant, aerodynamic designs, exemplified by the 1947 Cisitalia 202, one of the first cars exhibited at the Museum of Modern Art in New York. Under Battista's son Sergio Pininfarina, the firm began its long association with Ferrari in 1951-52, designing nearly every Ferrari road car for decades.

Design work

Famous Pininfarina designs include the Ferrari Testarossa, Ferrari F40 (with in-house Ferrari styling input), the Alfa Romeo Spider "Duetto", the Peugeot 406 Coupé, and the Maserati GranTurismo. Beyond automobiles, the firm has designed trains, yachts, furniture, and consumer products. In the electric era, Automobili Pininfarina launched the Battista electric hypercar.

Ownership

In 2015, India's Mahindra Group acquired a controlling stake in Pininfarina S.p.A. The Pininfarina family remains involved in the company's heritage and design activities.
