Tesla Fremont Factory

Location: Fremont, California, U.S.
Built: 1962 (GM); reopened by Tesla 2010
Industry: Automotive manufacturing

The Tesla Fremont Factory is an automobile manufacturing plant in Fremont, California, operated by Tesla, Inc. The facility opened as General Motors' Fremont Assembly in 1962, and was later operated as New United Motor Manufacturing, Inc. (NUMMI), a joint venture between GM and Toyota, from 1984 until 2010. Tesla purchased the plant in 2010 and retooled it to produce its electric vehicles.

Production

The factory began producing the Tesla Model S in June 2012, followed by the Model X in 2015, the Model 3 in 2017, and the Model Y in 2020 — making it, for a time, the only plant producing all four vehicles. The site covers about 370 acres (150 ha) with roughly 5.3 million square feet of manufacturing and office space, and employs more than 10,000 workers. During the Model 3 production ramp in 2018, Tesla added a large tent-like structure, "GA4", as an additional general assembly line. The Fremont plant has at times been the most productive automobile factory in North America by weekly output.

History

Under NUMMI, the plant was celebrated as a turnaround story: a formerly troubled GM workforce, rehired and retrained in the Toyota Production System, achieved near-Toyota levels of quality. After NUMMI closed in April 2010, Tesla bought the facility for $42 million, and Toyota invested $50 million in Tesla.
