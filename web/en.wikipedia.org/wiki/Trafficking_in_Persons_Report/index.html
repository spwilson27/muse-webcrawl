Trafficking in Persons Report

The Trafficking in Persons Report (often abbreviated TIP Report) is an annual report issued since 2001 by the United States Department of State's Office to Monitor and Combat Trafficking in Persons. It ranks governments worldwide based on their perceived efforts to acknowledge and combat human trafficking.

The report divides nations into tiers based on their compliance with standards outlined in the Trafficking Victims Protection Act (TVPA) of 2000. Countries whose governments fully comply with the TVPA's minimum standards are placed on Tier 1; countries that do not fully comply but are making significant efforts to do so are placed on Tier 2; and countries whose governments do not fully comply and are not making significant efforts are placed on Tier 3.

History

The first TIP Report was released in 2001 following the passage of the Trafficking Victims Protection Act of 2000, which required the Secretary of State to submit an annual report to Congress describing the efforts of governments to eliminate severe forms of trafficking in persons. The report has grown over the years to cover nearly 190 countries and territories.

Tier rankings

Tier 1

Tier 1 is the highest ranking, indicating that a government's efforts have fully met the TVPA's minimum standards for the elimination of trafficking. A Tier 1 ranking does not mean a country has no trafficking problem; it means the government has acknowledged the existence of the problem, invested efforts to address it, and complies with the TVPA's minimum standards.

Tier 2

Countries placed on Tier 2 do not yet fully meet the TVPA's minimum standards but are making significant efforts to bring themselves into compliance. A Tier 2 Watch List exists for countries where the absolute number of victims is severe or significantly increasing while efforts remain inadequate.

Tier 3

Tier 3 is the lowest ranking. Countries on Tier 3 do not fully meet the minimum standards and are not making significant efforts to do so. They may be subject to sanctions, including the withholding of non-humanitarian, non-trade-related foreign assistance.

Methodology

The State Department gathers information from U.S. embassies, government officials, nongovernmental and international organizations, published reports, and research trips to countries. The report assesses governments' efforts to prosecute traffickers, protect victims, and prevent trafficking in persons.

Criticism

The report has been criticized at times for alleged political bias, with some observers arguing that rankings for strategically important countries have been adjusted for diplomatic reasons. Scholars and activists have also debated the methodology used to assign tier placements.
