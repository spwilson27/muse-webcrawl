Clownfish or anemonefish are fishes of the subfamily Amphiprioninae in the family Pomacentridae. About thirty species are recognized, in the genera Amphiprion and Premnas. They inhabit the warm waters of the Indian and Pacific Oceans, including the Great Barrier Reef and the Red Sea.

Symbiosis

Clownfish live in a mutualistic symbiosis with sea anemones. The anemone's stinging tentacles shelter the fish from predators, while the fish defend the anemone from polyp-eating butterflyfish and deposit nutrient-rich waste. A mucus layer protects the clownfish from the anemone's sting; the fish acclimates to a new host gradually.

Biology and reproduction

Clownfish are sequential hermaphrodites: all are born male, and the dominant individual of a group becomes female. A breeding pair lives in an anemone with several smaller non-breeding males in a strict size-based hierarchy. If the female dies, the breeding male changes sex and takes her place. The best-known species, the orange ocellaris clownfish (Amphiprion ocellaris), was popularized by the film Finding Nemo.

In aquaria

Clownfish are popular marine aquarium fish and are widely bred in captivity, which reduces pressure on wild populations. Captive-bred designer varieties with unusual stripe patterns are common in the trade.
