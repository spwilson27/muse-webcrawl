Distribution of wealth

The distribution of wealth is a comparison of the wealth of various members or groups in a society. It differs from income distribution in that it looks at accumulated assets — property, investments, savings minus debts — rather than annual income.

Global distribution

Studies such as the UBS/Credit Suisse Global Wealth Report find wealth is highly concentrated: the top 1% of adults own roughly 38–47% of global household wealth, while the bottom half own only a few percent. The Gini coefficient for global wealth is around 0.88.

Factors

Inheritance, capital returns, housing, education, taxation and welfare systems shape distribution. Thomas Piketty's Capital in the Twenty-First Century argued returns on capital tend to exceed growth, increasing concentration.

By country

Wealth inequality exceeds income inequality in most countries, including the United States, where the top 1% hold about a third of net worth. See American Dream and Social equality.
