Mount Marcy is the highest point in the U.S. state of New York, with an elevation of 5,344 feet (1,629 metres). It lies in the Adirondack High Peaks region, in the town of Keene in Essex County, about 14 miles from Lake Placid. The mountain is named for William L. Marcy, who was governor of New York when its first recorded ascent was made in 1837 by a party led by Ebenezer Emmons, the geologist who named the Adirondacks.

Features

Near the summit lies Lake Tear of the Clouds, a small tarn often described as the highest source of the Hudson River; from it, Feldspar Brook and the Opalescent River carry water down to the Hudson. The summit cone rises above treeline and supports fragile alpine tundra vegetation unusual at this latitude. On 14 September 1901, Vice President Theodore Roosevelt was hiking Marcy when he received word that President William McKinley was dying, prompting his famous midnight ride down to North Creek to take the train — and, on McKinley's death, the presidency.

Climbing

The most popular route is the Van Hoevenberg Trail from Adirondak Loj, a round trip of about 14.8 miles; longer approaches come from the south via Lake Colden. Marcy is the centrepiece of the Adirondack Forty-Sixer challenge and draws heavy traffic, with summit stewards posted to protect the alpine plants.
