Cantabria

Cantabria is an autonomous community in northern Spain, with Santander as its capital. It borders the Basque Country to the east, Castile and León to the south, Asturias to the west, and the Bay of Biscay (the Cantabrian Sea) to the north. It is both a single-province autonomous community and a historic region.

Geography

Cantabria is part of "Green Spain", with a humid oceanic climate. The Cantabrian Mountains and the Picos de Europa rise in the south, while the coast has rias, beaches and fishing ports. Nature reserves include Picos de Europa National Park (shared with neighbouring regions) and the Santoña marshes.

History

The region was home to the ancient Cantabri, who resisted Roman conquest in the Cantabrian Wars. It later formed part of the Kingdom of Asturias and then Castile. The province of Santander was created in 1833, and Cantabria gained autonomy in 1982. Prehistoric art in caves such as Altamira is a UNESCO World Heritage attraction.

Economy and culture

Tourism, fishing, dairy farming and industry are important. Cantabrian cuisine features seafood, anchovies from Santoña, and cheeses such as queso picón.
