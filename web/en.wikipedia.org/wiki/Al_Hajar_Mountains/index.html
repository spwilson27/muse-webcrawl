Al Hajar Mountains

The Al Hajar Mountains are a mountain range in northeastern Oman and the eastern United Arab Emirates. They are the highest range in the eastern Arabian Peninsula, rising to Jebel Shams at about 3,009 metres, the highest point in Oman.

The range runs roughly parallel to the coast of the Gulf of Oman and is divided into western and eastern sections by the Sumail Gap. Geologically the Hajar are famous for their ophiolite complex — sections of oceanic crust and mantle thrust onto the continent — among the largest and best-exposed in the world.

The mountains support terraced agriculture in wadis and villages, including the production of roses and pomegranates on the Jebel Akhdar plateau, and they contain important archaeological and fossil sites.
