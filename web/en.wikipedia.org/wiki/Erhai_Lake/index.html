Erhai Lake

Location: Yunnan, China
Coordinates: 25°45′48″N 100°11′15″E
Surface area: approx. 250 km²
Max. depth: approx. 11.5 m

Erhai Lake (Chinese: 洱海, Ěrhǎi, literally "Ear-shaped Sea") is an alpine fault lake in Yunnan province, China, lying between the Cangshan mountains to the west and the city of Dali. It is the seventh largest freshwater lake in China, with a surface area of about 250 square kilometres, and reaches a maximum depth of roughly 11.5 metres. Its name comes from its elongated shape, which resembles an ear.

Geography and ecology

The lake is fed by rivers including the Miju and drains via the Xi'er River into the Lancang (Mekong) basin. It was historically famed for cormorant fishing by the Bai people. Many fish species once found in the lake were endemic, but several have declined or become extinct following the introduction of exotic species and pollution; remediation efforts have been underway since the 1990s to control eutrophication and protect water quality.

Tourism

The lakeshore is dotted with Bai villages such as Shuanglang and Xizhou, and the lake can be circumnavigated by road or cycle path. Islands in the lake include Jinsuo Island and the "Nanzhao Folklore Island". Together with the Cangshan range and old town of Dali, Erhai is one of Yunnan's principal scenic attractions.
