The Nahe is a river in Rhineland-Palatinate and Saarland, Germany, a left-bank tributary of the Rhine. It rises in the hills near Nohfelden in the Saarland and flows about 125 kilometres (78 mi) north-eastward past Idar-Oberstein, Kirn, Bad Sobernheim, Bad Kreuznach and Bad Münster am Stein, joining the Rhine at Bingen am Rhein.

Course and region

The river gives its name to the Nahe wine region, one of Germany's thirteen quality wine regions (Anbaugebiete), noted for Riesling grown on exceptionally varied soils — volcanic porphyry and melaphyre, red sandstone and slate — compressed into a small area. The narrow valley between Bad Münster am Stein and Bad Kreuznach is famous for the Rotenfels, a sheer porphyry cliff massif, and for the spa towns' salt works and graduation towers.

History

The name is of Celtic origin, from a root meaning "wild flowing water". Celtic and Roman settlements and later gemstone cutting at Idar-Oberstein made the valley prosperous; the river was historically prone to severe flooding, and much of its lower course is now regulated.
