Diversity, equity, and inclusion

Diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI)
Type: Organizational framework
Settings: Workplaces, universities, government
Related: Affirmative action, Equal opportunity

Diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) is a framework used by organizations to promote fair treatment, access and participation for groups historically underrepresented or discriminated against, based on characteristics such as race, gender, disability, age and sexual orientation.

Concepts

Diversity concerns representation; equity concerns fairness in outcomes and processes, sometimes requiring different treatment to address disadvantage; inclusion concerns whether people feel valued and able to participate fully.

History

DEI grew out of U.S. civil rights-era workplace training, equal opportunity and affirmative action policies, expanding from the 1980s onward into corporate programs, university offices and government initiatives.

Debate and policy changes

Supporters argue DEI improves fairness, decision-making and performance; critics argue some programs are ineffective, ideological or themselves discriminatory. Following court decisions and political shifts, a number of U.S. states, universities and — from 2025 — federal agencies under the second Trump administration restricted or dismantled DEI programs, prompting litigation and rebranding elsewhere.
