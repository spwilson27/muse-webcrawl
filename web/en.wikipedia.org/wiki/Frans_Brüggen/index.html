Frans Brüggen (30 October 1934 – 13 August 2014) was a Dutch conductor, recorder player, and baroque flautist, and one of the leading figures of the historically informed performance movement. He first achieved fame as a virtuoso of the recorder — an instrument he helped restore to serious concert status — and later as founder and conductor of the Orchestra of the Eighteenth Century.

Career

Brüggen studied the recorder with Kees Otten at the Amsterdam Conservatory and also read musicology at the University of Amsterdam. At 21 he was appointed professor of recorder at the Royal Conservatory in The Hague, becoming one of the youngest professors in the institution's history. His virtuoso playing and extensive recordings of baroque repertoire reinvigorated interest in the recorder and in period performance practice generally.

In 1981 Brüggen co-founded the Orchestra of the Eighteenth Century, an ensemble of some fifty-five players from many countries who perform on period instruments and share profits equally. With the orchestra he toured worldwide and recorded extensively, including the complete Beethoven symphonies, works by Haydn, Mozart, Schubert, and Mendelssohn, and music by Rameau and other baroque composers.

Conducting and legacy

Brüggen also guest-conducted many of the world's leading modern orchestras and other period ensembles, and co-directed the Orchestra of the Age of Enlightenment for a period. He taught generations of early-music performers and was widely regarded as a transformative figure in the performance of baroque and classical music. He died in Amsterdam in 2014 at the age of 79.
