Père Fouettard

Père Fouettard ("Father Whipper") is a character in French folklore who accompanies Saint Nicholas on his rounds on and around Saint Nicholas Day (6 December), particularly in northeastern France and French-speaking areas. Where Saint Nicholas rewards good children with gifts, Père Fouettard — dark, unkempt, and carrying whips, switches, or a martinet — threatens to punish the disobedient, sometimes distributing coal or onions instead of presents.

Legends

Several legends explain his servitude. In the most famous, from Lorraine, Père Fouettard was an innkeeper or butcher who murdered three children (in some versions, three rich students) and hid their bodies in a salting tub; Saint Nicholas discovered the crime, resurrected the children, and condemned the villain to accompany him forever as penance. Another tale makes him a bystander burned or disgraced at the siege of Metz in 1552. The figure belongs to a wider European family of Saint Nicholas companions, alongside Krampus, Knecht Ruprecht, Belsnickel, and Zwarte Piet.
