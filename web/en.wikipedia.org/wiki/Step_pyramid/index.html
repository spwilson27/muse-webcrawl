Step pyramid

A step pyramid or stepped pyramid is an architectural structure that uses flat platforms, or steps, receding from the ground up, to achieve a completed pyramidal shape. Step pyramids are found in several cultures across history.

Step pyramid
Earliest famous example: Pyramid of Djoser, Saqqara, c. 2670 BCE
Architect: Imhotep
Other examples: Ziggurats, Mesoamerican pyramids, Monte d'Accoddi

Egypt

The Pyramid of Djoser at Saqqara, designed by Imhotep for Pharaoh Djoser in the 27th century BCE, stacked six mastaba-like tiers to about 62 metres, the earliest colossal stone building in Egypt and a precursor to the smooth-sided true pyramids of Giza. The later Meidum pyramid began as a step pyramid before conversion.

Other cultures

Mesopotamian ziggurats such as that of Ur were stepped temple towers. In Mesoamerica, the pyramids of Teotihuacan, the Maya (El Castillo at Chichen Itza) and the Aztecs are stepped, usually supporting a summit temple. The Mesara structures and Monte d'Accoddi in Sardinia are earlier European examples.
