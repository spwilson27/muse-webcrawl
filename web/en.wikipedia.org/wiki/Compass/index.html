Overview

A compass is an instrument that indicates direction relative to Earth's magnetic field, by means of a magnetised needle or card free to align itself with the field. It points not to true north but to magnetic north, and the difference at any place — the declination or variation — is printed on charts and changes slowly over the years, an inconvenience navigators have lived with since the fifteenth century.

The compass originated in China, where lodestone devices were used for geomancy by the Han dynasty and for navigation by the Song dynasty; knowledge of it reached Europe by the late twelfth century and the Mediterranean soon after. Combined with the chart and later the chronometer, it made systematic oceanic navigation possible. Its card is traditionally divided into 32 points — north, north-north-east, and so on — the 'boxing of the compass' every sailing apprentice once learned by heart.
