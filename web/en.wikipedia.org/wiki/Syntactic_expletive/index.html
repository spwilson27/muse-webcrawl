Syntactic expletive

A syntactic expletive is a word in a sentence that performs a syntactic role but contributes nothing to the sentence's meaning — a "dummy" or "pleonastic" element. Unlike expletive profanity, the term in linguistics refers to words such as English it and there in sentences where they fill the subject position without referring to anything.

Examples

In "It is raining," the pronoun it is an expletive subject: English requires a subject, but no entity is doing the raining. Similarly, in "There are three men in the garden," there — sometimes called existential there — occupies the subject position while the logical subject follows the verb.

Theory

In generative grammar, expletives are analyzed in relation to the Extended Projection Principle, which requires clauses to have subjects; expletives satisfy this requirement when no thematic subject is available. Languages differ: many allow null subjects and so lack weather-it analogues, while French uses il and German es in comparable constructions.
