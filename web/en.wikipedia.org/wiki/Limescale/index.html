Limescale

Limescale is a chalky, hard deposit of calcium carbonate (CaCO3) left behind by hard water. It builds up on the inside of pipes, kettles, boilers, and central heating systems, and on surfaces frequently in contact with hard water, such as taps and shower heads. The scale reduces heat transfer and flow, increasing energy consumption and maintenance.

Formation

Hard water contains dissolved calcium bicarbonate, acquired as water percolates through limestone and chalk deposits. When such water is heated or its pressure drops, carbon dioxide is released and the bicarbonate converts to insoluble calcium carbonate, which precipitates as scale:

Ca(HCO3)2 → CaCO3↓ + H2O + CO2

Limescale is most pronounced in areas supplied with water from limestone aquifers, such as much of southern and eastern England.

Removal and prevention

Scale can be dissolved with weak acids: household descalers use citric acid, acetic acid (vinegar), or sulfamic acid, while industrial systems use inhibited hydrochloric acid. Prevention methods include ion-exchange water softeners, which replace calcium and magnesium ions with sodium, and physical water conditioners. Drip-by-drip deposits on bathroom fixtures form similar scale, sometimes stained by metal oxides.

Related deposits include boiler scale in steam systems and the calcium buildup in evaporative coolers; a softer, related material formed in caves from the same chemistry is travertine and speleothem calcite.
