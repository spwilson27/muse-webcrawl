Persian culture

Persian culture (Persian: فرهنگ ایرانی) is one of the oldest continuous cultures in the world, centred on Iran (historically Persia) and extending across Greater Iran, from Anatolia and Central Asia to the Indian subcontinent and the Persian Gulf. For over two millennia Persian language, art, architecture, and courtly customs influenced neighbouring civilisations, and Persian served as a lingua franca and language of administration across much of Asia.

Language and literature

The Persian language, written in a modified Arabic script, produced one of the world's great literary traditions. Ferdowsi's epic Shahnameh (completed c. 1010) preserved Iranian mythology and history; the poetry of Omar Khayyam, Saadi, Hafez, and Rumi remains central to cultural identity. Literary gatherings, recitation, and memorisation of verse are deeply embedded in daily life.

Art and architecture

Persian art is renowned for miniature painting, calligraphy, illuminated manuscripts, carpets, metalwork, and ceramics. Persian architecture contributed the iwan, the pointed dome on squinches, intricate tilework (haft rang), and the chahar bagh four-part garden — the origin of the word "paradise", from Old Persian pairidaeza, a walled enclosure. The Persian carpet is recognised by UNESCO as intangible heritage, as are the skills of miniature painting.

Customs and celebrations

Nowruz, the Persian New Year at the spring equinox, is celebrated with the Haft-Sin table of seven symbolic items beginning with the letter sin. Other observances include Chaharshanbe Suri (fire-jumping before Nowruz), Yalda Night (the winter solstice), and Sizdah Bedar. Hospitality, elaborate politeness (taarof), poetry quotation, tea culture, and Persian cuisine — saffron rice, kebabs, herb stews such as ghormeh sabzi, and sweets like gaz and baklava — are hallmarks of the culture.

Religion

Historically Zoroastrian, the majority of Persians have been Shia Muslims since the Safavid period, and religious observance shapes festivals, architecture, and public life, alongside pre-Islamic traditions that persist in Nowruz and other customs.
