Indian classical dance

Indian classical dance is an umbrella term for the major classical dance forms of India, rooted in the ancient Sanskrit text Natya Shastra and in temple and court traditions. India's Sangeet Natak Akademi officially recognises eight forms: Bharatanatyam (Tamil Nadu), Kathak (northern India), Kathakali and Mohiniyattam (Kerala), Kuchipudi (Andhra Pradesh), Manipuri (Manipur), Odissi (Odisha) and Sattriya (Assam).

Common elements

The forms share a theoretical foundation of rasa (aesthetic flavour or emotion), bhava (expression), elaborate hand gestures (mudras), facial expression (abhinaya), rhythmic footwork and storytelling drawn from Hindu epics and mythology such as the Ramayana and Mahabharata. Performance typically combines pure dance (nritta), expressive dance (nritya) and dramatic elements (natya), accompanied by classical music.

History

Many forms were nurtured in temples by devadasis and declined under colonial rule before being revived and reconstructed for the stage in the 20th century by figures such as Rukmini Devi Arundale (Bharatanatyam) and Vallathol (Kathakali).
