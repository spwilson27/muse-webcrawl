Look Back in Anger

Look Back in Anger
Written by: John Osborne
Premiere: 8 May 1956, Royal Court Theatre, London
Genre: Kitchen sink drama

Look Back in Anger (1956) is a play by John Osborne. It concerns a disillusioned, anti-heroic young man, Jimmy Porter, his upper-middle-class wife Alison, and their life in a cramped Midlands attic flat. The play premiered at the Royal Court Theatre in London on 8 May 1956, directed by Tony Richardson, with Kenneth Haigh as Jimmy Porter and Mary Ure as Alison.

Impact

The play is credited with transforming post-war British theatre. Its raw language, working-class setting and furious protagonist gave a name to a generation of writers — the "angry young men" — and it is a foundational text of "kitchen sink realism". Critic Kenneth Tynan famously wrote that he could not love anyone who did not wish to see it. Osborne's Jimmy Porter rages against class privilege, the establishment, the church and his own powerlessness, in tirades that became emblematic of 1950s youth disillusionment.

Plot

Jimmy Porter, a university-educated sweet-stall owner, directs his bitterness at his wife Alison, daughter of a colonel, and at their friend Cliff. When Alison's friend Helena arrives, Alison leaves and loses the baby she was carrying; Helena takes her place, then gives Jimmy up when Alison returns, broken, at the play's end.

Adaptations

The play was filmed in 1959, directed by Tony Richardson and starring Richard Burton as Jimmy Porter, with Mary Ure and Claire Bloom. It has been repeatedly revived in Britain and internationally.
