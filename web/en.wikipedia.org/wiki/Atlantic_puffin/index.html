Atlantic puffin

The Atlantic puffin (Fratercula arctica), also known as the common puffin, is a species of seabird in the auk family. It is the only puffin native to the Atlantic Ocean; two related species, the tufted puffin and the horned puffin, are found in the northeastern Pacific. The Atlantic puffin breeds in Russia, Iceland, Ireland, the Faroe Islands, Norway, the United Kingdom, France, Newfoundland and Labrador in Canada, and Maine in the United States. It spends the autumn and winter at sea in the cold northern Atlantic, returning to coastal areas to breed in the spring.

Description

The Atlantic puffin is sturdily built with a thick neck, large head and a strikingly coloured triangular beak, which is bright orange-red during the breeding season and duller in winter. It stands about 28–30 cm tall with a wingspan of roughly 47–63 cm. The plumage is black above and white below, with grey cheeks, giving it an appearance sometimes compared to a penguin or a small clown.

Behaviour

Puffins are excellent swimmers, using their wings to "fly" underwater in pursuit of fish, and awkward but fast fliers in the air, beating their wings up to 400 times per minute. They nest in burrows in cliff-top soil, where each pair raises a single chick, called a puffling, feeding it whole fish carried crosswise in the beak — a puffin can hold a dozen or more small fish at once thanks to spines on its tongue and palate.

Diet

The diet consists almost entirely of fish, especially sand eels, sprats, herring and capelin, supplemented by crustaceans and molluscs in winter.

Conservation

The species is listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN, having declined sharply at many European colonies because of overfishing of prey fish, climate change, predation by introduced mammals and marine pollution. Iceland and the Faroe Islands hold the majority of the world population. The puffin is the official bird symbol of Newfoundland and Labrador.
