Fast casual restaurant

A fast casual restaurant, found primarily in the United States and Canada, is a restaurant that does not offer full table service, but advertises higher quality food than fast food restaurants, with fewer frozen or processed ingredients. It is an intermediate concept between fast food and casual dining.

History

The category emerged in the United States in the early to mid-1990s. Chains such as Panera Bread, Chipotle Mexican Grill and Five Guys helped popularize the format, which grew rapidly during the 2000s and 2010s as consumers sought fresher ingredients and customizable meals at prices below full-service dining.

Characteristics

Fast casual restaurants typically feature a limited or no table service model, ordering at a counter, menus emphasizing fresh preparation, and decor that is more upscale than conventional fast food outlets. Average checks are higher than fast food, commonly in the range of US$8 to US$15 per person.

Examples

Notable chains include Chipotle Mexican Grill, Panera Bread, Shake Shack, Sweetgreen, and Blaze Pizza.
