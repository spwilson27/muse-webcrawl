The Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act (PPACA), commonly called the Affordable Care Act (ACA) or Obamacare, is a United States federal statute enacted by the 111th Congress and signed into law by President Barack Obama on 23 March 2010. Together with the Health Care and Education Reconciliation Act of 2010, it represents the most significant regulatory overhaul of the US healthcare system since Medicare and Medicaid in 1965.

Provisions

The act requires insurers to cover all applicants without charging more or denying coverage based on pre-existing conditions, allows young adults to remain on their parents' plans until age 26, created state and federal health insurance exchanges with income-based subsidies, and expanded Medicaid eligibility to 138 percent of the federal poverty level where states adopted the expansion. It introduced an individual mandate to maintain coverage; its tax penalty was reduced to zero from 2019. Preventive services must be covered without cost-sharing, and annual and lifetime benefit caps were prohibited.

Legal challenges

The Supreme Court upheld most of the act in NFIB v. Sebelius (2012), ruling the mandate constitutional as a tax while making Medicaid expansion optional for states. Further challenges were rejected in King v. Burwell (2015) and California v. Texas (2021). The uninsured rate fell from about 16 percent in 2010 to under 9 percent by the early 2020s.
