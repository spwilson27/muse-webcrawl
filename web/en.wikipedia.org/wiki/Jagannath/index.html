Jagannath (Sanskrit: जगन्नाथ, romanized: Jagannātha, literally "Lord of the Universe") is a Hindu deity worshipped principally in the eastern Indian state of Odisha, above all at the great Jagannath Temple in Puri. He is regarded as a form of Krishna, or of Vishnu, and is worshipped together with his elder brother Balabhadra and his sister Subhadra as a triad of large, stylised wooden images.

The deity and the triad

Unlike the anthropomorphic images usual in Hindu temples, the Puri deities are carved from neem wood in an abstract, mask-like form, with large round eyes and rudimentary limbs. The images are ritually replaced in the Nabakalebara ceremony, held at intervals of eight, twelve or nineteen years when an extra month falls in the Hindu calendar. Jagannath's consort in theology is the goddess Lakshmi, and the temple kitchen, said to be the largest in the world, prepares the mahaprasad food offerings.

Ratha Yatra

Jagannath is famous worldwide for the annual Ratha Yatra, or chariot festival, at Puri, in which the three deities are drawn through the streets to the Gundicha Temple on enormous wooden chariots by crowds of devotees. The festival is replicated by Jagannath temples across India and by Hare Krishna congregations around the world. The English word "juggernaut" derives from Jagannath, via early European accounts of the festival.

Significance

The Jagannath cult is notable for its inclusiveness and its tribal and regional roots, blending Vedic, Tantric, Buddhist and Jain elements. Jagannath is the presiding deity of Odisha, invoked in its literature and state song, and is venerated in Bengal Vaishnavism as the form of Krishna especially associated with ecstatic devotion.
