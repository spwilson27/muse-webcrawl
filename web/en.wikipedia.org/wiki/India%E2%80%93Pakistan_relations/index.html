India–Pakistan relations

India–Pakistan relations are the bilateral relations between the Republic of India and the Islamic Republic of Pakistan. The two countries, both born of the Partition of British India in 1947, have a complex and largely hostile relationship rooted in religious, territorial, and political disputes, most prominently the Kashmir conflict.

Wars and crises

India and Pakistan have fought wars in 1947–48, 1965, 1971 (which led to the creation of Bangladesh), and 1999 (the Kargil War), along with numerous border skirmishes and crises, including the 2001–02 standoff and the 2019 Pulwama–Balakot crisis. Both countries possess nuclear weapons, which has made their rivalry a persistent international concern.

Disputes and diplomacy

Beyond Kashmir, disputes cover water sharing (governed since 1960 by the Indus Waters Treaty), the Siachen Glacier, and Sir Creek. Periods of dialogue, such as the Lahore Declaration (1999) and composite dialogue processes, have alternated with breakdowns following militant attacks in India attributed to Pakistan-based groups. Trade and people-to-people contacts remain limited relative to the countries' size and shared culture.
