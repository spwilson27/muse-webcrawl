Isocitrate

Isocitrate (isocitric acid) is a tricarboxylic acid and a structural isomer of citric acid, best known as an intermediate in the citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle) of cellular respiration. It is formed from citrate via the intermediate cis-aconitate by the enzyme aconitase.

Role in metabolism

Isocitrate is oxidatively decarboxylated by isocitrate dehydrogenase to form alpha-ketoglutarate, releasing CO2 and generating NADH (or NADPH) — one of the energy-yielding steps of the cycle. Isocitrate is also a substrate in the glyoxylate cycle, where isocitrate lyase cleaves it into succinate and glyoxylate. Mutations in isocitrate dehydrogenase genes (IDH1 and IDH2) are found in several cancers, including gliomas and acute myeloid leukaemia. Commercially, isocitric acid is much less common than citric acid but is used as a marker of authenticity in fruit juices.
