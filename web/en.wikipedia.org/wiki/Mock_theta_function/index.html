Mock theta function

In mathematics, a mock theta function is a q-series resembling a theta function that Srinivasa Ramanujan described in his last letter to G. H. Hardy in January 1920 and in his lost notebook. Ramanujan listed 17 examples in orders 3, 5 and 7 without a precise definition; for decades their nature was a famous mystery, resolved when Sander Zwegers (2002) showed they are the holomorphic parts of harmonic weak Maass forms.

Definition and examples

Ramanujan's prototypical third-order example is f(q) = Σ qn² / (1+q)²...(1+qn)². Mock theta functions have infinite radius-of-convergence singularities at roots of unity and cannot be completed to ordinary theta functions by simple corrections; instead a non-holomorphic "shadow" term — a unary theta series — completes them to harmonic Maass forms that transform like modular forms.

History

George Andrews and others recovered most of Ramanujan's examples from the lost notebook (1976), and Freeman Dyson mused on the functions' hidden structure. Zwegers' Utrecht thesis placed them within Maass-form theory, sparking intensive research by Bringmann, Ono and others.

Applications

Mock theta functions and their generalisations (mock modular forms) now appear in partition asymptotics, quantum modular forms, representation theory, and physics — notably in counting microstates of black holes and in the umbral moonshine conjectures linking finite groups to mock modular forms.
