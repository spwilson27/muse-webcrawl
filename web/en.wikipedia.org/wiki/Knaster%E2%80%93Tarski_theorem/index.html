Knaster–Tarski theorem

In order theory and lattice theory, the Knaster–Tarski theorem, named after Bronisław Knaster and Alfred Tarski, states that any monotone function on a complete lattice has a fixed point, and moreover the set of its fixed points is itself a complete lattice.

Statement

Let L be a complete lattice and let f : L → L be an order-preserving (monotone) function. Then the set of fixed points of f in L is also a complete lattice. It follows in particular that f has a least fixed point and a greatest fixed point.

Applications

The theorem is fundamental in denotational semantics of programming languages, abstract interpretation, and the theory of inductive definitions. Tarski's version was published in 1955, building on Knaster's earlier work from 1928 related to the Schröder–Bernstein theorem.
