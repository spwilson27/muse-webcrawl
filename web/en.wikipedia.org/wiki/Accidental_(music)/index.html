Accidental (music)

In musical notation, an accidental is a symbol that raises or lowers the pitch of a note from the pitch implied by the key signature. The common accidentals are the sharp (♯), which raises a pitch by one semitone; the flat (♭), which lowers it by one semitone; and the natural (♮), which cancels a previous accidental or one from the key signature.

Rules of use

An accidental applies to every repetition of the same note within the same measure and octave, unless cancelled by another accidental; its effect ends at the bar line. Double sharps (𝄪) and double flats (𝄫) raise or lower a pitch by two semitones, and are used mainly to preserve correct spelling in keys with many sharps or flats. Courtesy (cautionary) accidentals remind performers of the prevailing key signature after a change.

History

Accidentals arose from the medieval practice of musica ficta, in which singers adjusted pitches not written in the score. The terms "accidental" derives from the note being outside — accidental to — the regular system of hexachords and the key signature.

Microtonal accidentals

Twentieth-century composers introduced additional signs for quarter tones and other microtonal inflections, such as the half-sharp and half-flat, extending the accidental system beyond semitones.
