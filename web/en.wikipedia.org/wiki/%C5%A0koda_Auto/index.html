Škoda Auto

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Škoda Auto a.s. is a Czech automobile manufacturer headquartered in Mladá Boleslav, Bohemia, and a wholly owned subsidiary of the Volkswagen Group. Founded in 1895 as Laurin & Klement, it is one of the oldest car makers in the world still producing vehicles.

History

Founders Václav Laurin and Václav Klement began building bicycles in 1895, moved to motorcycles in 1899, and produced their first automobile, the Voiturette A, in 1905. In 1925 the firm was acquired by the Škoda Works industrial conglomerate of Plzeň and renamed Škoda. Under state ownership after 1945, Škoda models became ubiquitous across the Eastern Bloc, valued for their simplicity and low cost.

After the Velvet Revolution, Škoda entered a partnership with Volkswagen in 1991 — the first major Western investment in a Czech company — and became a full VW Group brand by 2000. Models such as the Octavia, Fabia, and Superb rebuilt the marque's reputation in Western Europe for value and quality.

Products and markets

Škoda sells more than a million cars a year in over 100 markets, with important production sites in the Czech Republic, Slovakia, India, and China. It is especially strong in India and Europe, and its rally arm, Škoda Motorsport, has a long record of success in international rallying.
