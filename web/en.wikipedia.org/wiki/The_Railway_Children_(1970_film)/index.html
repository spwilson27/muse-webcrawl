The Railway Children is a 1970 British family film directed by Lionel Jeffries, adapted from E. Nesbit's 1906 children's novel of the same name. It is regarded as one of the finest British family films and is regularly placed in lists of the greatest British films.

Plot

After their father is mysteriously taken away by two men one night, Roberta ("Bobbie"), Peter, and Phyllis Waterbury move with their mother from a comfortable London home to a cottage, "Three Chimneys", near a railway line in the Yorkshire countryside. The children befriend the station porter, Perks, and an elderly gentleman who commutes on the 9:15 train. Their adventures include rescuing an injured schoolboy from a tunnel, helping a Russian exile searching for his family, and averting a train disaster caused by a landslip — for which they are rewarded. In the end their father, whose innocence has been established through the old gentleman's efforts, returns home; the reunion scene on the station platform, with Bobbie crying "Daddy! My Daddy!", is one of the most famous in British cinema.

Production

The film was shot largely on the Keighley and Worth Valley Railway in West Yorkshire, which has remained closely associated with it; Oakworth station serves as the film's station. Jenny Agutter, who played Bobbie, later played the mother in a 2000 television adaptation. In 1999 the British Film Institute ranked the film 66th on its list of the top 100 British films.
