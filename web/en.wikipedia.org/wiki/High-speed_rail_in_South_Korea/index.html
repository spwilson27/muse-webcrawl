High-speed rail in South Korea

High-speed rail in South Korea began with the opening of the Gyeongbu high-speed railway between Seoul and Busan in 2004, operated by Korail under the KTX (Korea Train eXpress) brand. A second operator, SR Corporation, launched SRT services from Suseo station in Seoul in 2016. Trains run at up to 305 km/h in service.

Network

The principal lines are the Gyeongbu HSR (Seoul–Busan), the Honam HSR (Osong–Gwangju–Mokpo), and the Suseo–Pyeongtaek high-speed railway, supplemented by semi-high-speed services over upgraded conventional lines to Gangneung, Pohang, Jinju and other cities. Construction of the first line drew on French TGV technology; later trainsets, such as the KTX-Sancheon and the distributed-traction KTX-Eum and KTX-Cheongryong, were developed domestically by Hyundai Rotem.

Impact

High-speed rail transformed domestic travel in South Korea: the Seoul–Busan journey fell from over four hours to about two hours fifteen minutes, domestic air routes on the corridor shrank dramatically, and KTX ridership grew to well over 100 million passenger journeys cumulatively within its first years and tens of millions annually. South Korea became one of the few countries able to design, build and export high-speed rail technology.
