The Roberts Court is the period of the Supreme Court of the United States since 2005, under the leadership of Chief Justice John Roberts. It succeeded the Rehnquist Court.

Composition

Appointees during this era include Samuel Alito, Sonia Sotomayor, Elena Kagan, Neil Gorsuch, Brett Kavanaugh, Amy Coney Barrett, and Ketanji Brown Jackson. After Barrett's confirmation in 2020 the Court had a 6–3 conservative supermajority.

Major decisions

Landmark rulings include Citizens United v. FEC (2010) on campaign finance, National Federation of Independent Business v. Sebelius (2012) upholding most of the Affordable Care Act, Obergefell v. Hodges (2015) on same-sex marriage, Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization (2022) overturning Roe v. Wade, and Students for Fair Admissions (2023) ending race-based affirmative action in college admissions. Roberts has at times acted as a swing vote and institutionalist.

Assessment

Commentators debate the Court's legitimacy, originalism, and changes to precedent. See also Ronald Reagan, who appointed earlier justices.
