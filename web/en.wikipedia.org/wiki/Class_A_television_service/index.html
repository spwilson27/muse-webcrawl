Class A television service

The Class A television service is a class of low-power television stations in the United States established by the Federal Communications Commission (FCC) in 2000 under the Community Broadcasters Protection Act of 1999. Class A stations are low-power stations that broadcast an average of at least 18 hours per day, air at least three hours per week of locally produced programming, and comply with the FCC's rules for full-power stations in areas such as children's programming and the Emergency Alert System. In exchange, they receive primary status: their channels are protected from displacement by new full-power stations and, unlike ordinary low-power (LPTV) stations, they cannot be forced off the air by spectrum changes without being offered a replacement channel.

Background

Low-power television was introduced in the 1980s to serve niche and community audiences, but LPTV stations held only secondary spectrum rights and many faced displacement as full-power television expanded. Congress responded with the Community Broadcasters Protection Act to protect qualifying community-oriented stations. Roughly 500 stations obtained Class A status in the program's initial window. Class A stations use call signs with the suffix -CA (analog era) or -CD (digital).

Requirements and obligations

To qualify and retain status, a Class A station must broadcast at least 18 hours a day, air a minimum of three hours per week of programming produced locally within its service area, and meet the same public-interest obligations as full-service stations, including children's educational programming reports, political broadcasting rules, and maintenance of a public inspection file. Failure to meet these requirements can result in loss of Class A status and reversion to ordinary LPTV classification.

Digital transition

Unlike full-power stations, which switched to digital broadcasting in 2009, Class A and LPTV stations were given a later deadline. The FCC ultimately required low-power and translator stations to complete their digital transition by July 13, 2021 (with analog operations ending then), though many converted far earlier; Class A stations that digitalised adopted the -CD suffix. The incentive auction of broadcast spectrum in 2016–17 repacked the television band, and although Class A stations were not auction-eligible, their protected status guaranteed them channels in the reduced band, subject to displacement procedures with relocation funding eligibility in some cases.
