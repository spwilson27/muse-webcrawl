In quantum field theory, the conformal anomaly, trace anomaly or Weyl anomaly is a quantum phenomenon: a symmetry of the classical theory that is violated upon quantisation. Classically, a theory with conformal symmetry has a traceless energy–momentum tensor (in four dimensions, Tμμ = 0), but the regularisation required to define the quantum theory breaks this, generating a non-zero trace proportional to curvature invariants.

Structure

In four dimensions the anomalous trace takes the form

Tμμ = (c / 16π²) F − (a / 16π²) G + (b / 16π²) □R,

where F is the square of the Weyl tensor and G the Euler density. The coefficients a and c depend on the field content; for example, a free real scalar contributes a = 1/360 and c = 1/120, a Dirac fermion a = 11/360 and c = 1/20.

Significance

The a-theorem (proved by Komargodski and Schwimmer in 2011 building on Cardy's 1988 conjecture) states that the a-coefficient decreases monotonically along renormalisation-group flows, constraining possible quantum field theories much as Zamolodchikov's c-theorem does in two dimensions. The trace anomaly also appears in cosmology (particle creation in expanding universes) and in the physics of graphene, where interactions with curvature give an anomalous contribution to transport.
