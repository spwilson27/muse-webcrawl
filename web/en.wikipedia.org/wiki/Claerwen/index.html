Claerwen

The Claerwen reservoir and dam in Powys, Wales, were the last additions to the Elan Valley Reservoirs system built to supply water to Birmingham, England. The Claerwen dam, a massive concrete gravity dam faced with dressed stone to blend with the earlier Victorian dams of the Elan Valley, was completed in 1952 and officially opened by Queen Elizabeth II in October of that year, in one of her first major engagements after her accession. The reservoir impounds the River Claerwen, a tributary of the River Wye, west of Rhayader in the Cambrian Mountains.

History

The original Elan Valley scheme, authorised in 1892 and completed in 1904–06, dammed the rivers Elan and Claerwen's neighbouring valleys to supply Birmingham by a gravity aqueduct some 73 miles (117 km) long. Demand soon outgrew the original reservoirs, and a further dam on the Claerwen was authorised but postponed by two world wars. Construction finally began in 1946 to designs by Sir William Halcrow and Partners. Because the dam is sited in a remote and rugged valley, a temporary village and narrow-gauge works were constructed for the workforce. The dam is 355 feet (108 m) long and 184 feet (56 m) high above the river bed, and the reservoir holds about 48,300 megalitres of water.

Landscape and nature conservation

The Claerwen valley forms part of the Elenydd uplands, much of which is protected as a Site of Special Scientific Interest and within the Elan Valley estate managed by Dŵr Cymru Welsh Water in partnership with the Elan Valley Trust. The surrounding blanket bog and moorland, known as Claerwen National Nature Reserve, supports breeding birds such as golden plover, dunlin, and merlin. The reservoir is a popular destination for walking, cycling on the Elan Valley Trail, and scenic driving, and its dam was used as a filming location: scenes for the television series Hinterland and others were shot in the area.
