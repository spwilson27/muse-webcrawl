The Oompa-Loompas are fictional characters from Roald Dahl's children's novel Charlie and the Chocolate Factory (1964) and its sequel Charlie and the Great Glass Elevator. They are the small workers of Willy Wonka's chocolate factory, brought by Wonka from "Loompaland" and paid in cacao beans, their favourite food.

Role in the story

In the novel, the Oompa-Loompas comment in verse and song on the fates of the badly behaved children — Augustus Gloop, Veruca Salt, Violet Beauregarde, and Mike Teavee — as each is eliminated from the tour of the factory, delivering moralising choruses that structure the narrative.

Depictions and revisions

Dahl's earliest draft described the Oompa-Loompas as African pygmies; after criticism, he revised the 1973 edition to make them fantastic beings with rosy-white skin from Loompaland. In the 1971 film Willy Wonka & the Chocolate Factory they were portrayed with orange skin and green hair by actors including Deep Roy in the 2005 film, where a single performer's image was digitally replicated.
