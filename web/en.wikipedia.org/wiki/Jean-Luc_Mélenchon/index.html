Jean-Luc Mélenchon

Jean-Luc Mélenchon addressing a rally

Jean-Luc Mélenchon (born 19 August 1951) is a French politician, founder of the left-wing movement La France Insoumise (LFI). A former minister and member of both houses of Parliament and the European Parliament, he has been a candidate for President of France three times, becoming one of the central figures of the French radical left.

Early life and Socialist Party career

Born in Tangier, Morocco, to Spanish and Italian immigrant parents, Mélenchon grew up in France and studied philosophy. He joined the Socialist Party (PS) in 1976, rising through its left wing in the Essonne department. Elected senator in 1986 — then the youngest in France — he later served as minister delegate for vocational education under Lionel Jospin (2000–2002).

Break with the PS and the Left Front

After campaigning for a "No" vote in the 2005 referendum on the European Constitutional Treaty, Mélenchon left the PS in 2008 and co-founded the Left Party (Parti de gauche). In 2009 he was elected to the European Parliament, and in 2012 he ran for president with the Left Front coalition, finishing fourth with 11.1 percent of the vote.

La France Insoumise

In 2016 he founded La France Insoumise around the program L'Avenir en commun, advocating a Sixth Republic, withdrawal from EU treaties by referendum if not renegotiated, ecological planning, and retirement at 60. He won 19.6 percent in the 2017 presidential election and 21.95 percent in 2022 — narrowly missing the second round in both. Elected to the National Assembly for Marseille in 2017, he stepped back from Parliament in 2022 while remaining LFI's dominant figure; the movement joined the NUPES alliance in 2022 and the New Popular Front in 2024.
