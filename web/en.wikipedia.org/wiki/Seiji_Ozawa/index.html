Seiji Ozawa

Seiji Ozawa (小澤 征爾)
Born: 1 September 1935, Mukden, Manchukuo (now Shenyang, China)
Died: 6 February 2024, Tokyo, Japan
Nationality: Japanese
Occupation: Conductor

Seiji Ozawa (1 September 1935 – 6 February 2024) was a Japanese conductor, known internationally for his work as music director of the Boston Symphony Orchestra and, later, the Vienna State Opera. He was one of the most prominent classical musicians of the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries, and the first Japanese conductor to achieve sustained success at the highest level of Western orchestral life.

Early life and training

Ozawa was born in Mukden (now Shenyang), in Japanese-occupied Manchuria, to Japanese parents. The family returned to Japan in 1944. He began piano lessons as a child but, after breaking both hands playing rugby at school, turned to conducting. He studied at the Toho Gakuen School of Music in Tokyo, where his mentors included Hideo Saito, the founder of the school and a formative influence on Japanese orchestral training.

In 1959 Ozawa won first prize at the International Competition of Orchestra Conductors in Besançon, France. The victory brought him to the attention of Charles Munch, then music director of the Boston Symphony Orchestra, who invited him to Tanglewood. There he also studied with Pierre Monteux and caught the eye of Herbert von Karajan, with whom he later worked in Berlin. Leonard Bernstein appointed him assistant conductor of the New York Philharmonic for the 1961–62 season.

Career

Ozawa served as music director of the Toronto Symphony Orchestra (1965–1969) and the San Francisco Symphony (1970–1976). In 1973 he became music director of the Boston Symphony Orchestra, a post he held for 29 years, until 2002 — the longest tenure of any music director in the orchestra's history. His Boston years combined core Germanic repertoire with championing of contemporary music, extensive touring, and major recordings.

In 1984 he co-founded the Saito Kinen Orchestra in Japan, named for his teacher, and its associated festival in Matsumoto. From 2002 to 2010 he was music director of the Vienna State Opera. He remained an influential figure in Japan, founding the Seiji Ozawa Music Academy and conducting the Saito Kinen Orchestra into his later years despite periods of ill health, including oesophageal cancer diagnosed in 2010. He died of heart failure in Tokyo in February 2024.

Style and legacy

Ozawa conducted largely from memory and was noted for fluid, dance-like movements and an ability to draw warm, flexible sound from orchestras. His repertoire ranged widely, from Haydn and Beethoven through Mahler, Messiaen, and Takemitsu, whose works he frequently premiered and recorded. He received numerous honours, including the Praemium Imperiale, Kennedy Center Honors, and appointment to the French Légion d'honneur.
