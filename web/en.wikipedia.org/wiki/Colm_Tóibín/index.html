Colm Tóibín

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Colm Tóibín, Irish novelist, journalist, and critic.

Colm Tóibín (born 30 May 1955) is an Irish novelist, short story writer, essayist, journalist, critic, playwright and poet. He is regarded as one of Ireland's leading contemporary writers, known for a spare, restrained prose style and for fiction exploring exile, emigration, identity, and the interior lives of his characters.

Life and career

Tóibín was born in Enniscorthy, County Wexford, and educated at University College Dublin. He worked as a journalist in the late 1970s and 1980s, including a period as editor of the Irish current affairs magazine Magill, and travelled widely in Spain and South America, experiences reflected in books such as Homage to Barcelona (1990) and The South (1990), his first novel.

Works

His novels include The Heather Blazing (1992), The Story of the Night (1996), The Blackwater Lightship (1999), The Master (2004), a fictionalised portrait of Henry James that won the International Dublin Literary Award, Brooklyn (2009), winner of the Costa Novel Award and adapted into an acclaimed 2015 film, The Testament of Mary (2012), Nora Webster (2014), House of Names (2017), The Magician (2021), a novel about Thomas Mann, and Long Island (2024), a sequel to Brooklyn. Several of his works have been shortlisted for the Booker Prize.

Tóibín has taught at Princeton University, Columbia University, and the University of Manchester, where he succeeded Martin Amis as professor of creative writing. From 2017 to 2022 he served as Chancellor of the University of Liverpool.
