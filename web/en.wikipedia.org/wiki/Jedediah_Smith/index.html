Jedediah Smith

Image placeholder: Jedediah Smith

Jedediah Strong Smith (January 6, 1799 – May 27, 1831) was an American clerk, transcontinental pioneer, frontiersman, hunter, trapper, author, cartographer, mountain man, and explorer of the Rocky Mountains, the Western United States, and the Southwest. During his travels, Smith became the first European American to cross the Sierra Nevada and traverse the Great Basin, and he recorded significant information about the geography of the American West.

Explorations

Smith joined William Ashley's fur-trading expedition in 1822 and survived a famous grizzly bear mauling in 1823 that scarred his face and cost him an ear. In 1824 he helped rediscover South Pass, the key route through the Rockies used by the Oregon Trail. Leading a party southwest from Utah in 1826, he crossed the Mojave Desert into California — the first American to reach California overland from the east — and in 1827 became the first European American to cross the Sierra Nevada and the Great Basin desert on his return, a gruelling journey that proved the region's aridity.

In 1828 his party was attacked by Umpqua people in Oregon, with most of his men killed. Smith later sold out of the fur trade and, in 1831, while travelling the Santa Fe Trail, was killed by Comanche warriors near the Cimarron River in present-day Kansas, aged 32.

Legacy

Smith's journals and maps, long overlooked because they were published decades after his death, established his reputation as one of the foremost explorers of the American West. Jedediah Smith Redwoods State Park in California is named for him.
