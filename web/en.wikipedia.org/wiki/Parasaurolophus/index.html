Parasaurolophus is a genus of hadrosaurid ("duck-billed") dinosaur that lived in what is now western North America during the Late Cretaceous Period, about 76.5 to 66 million years ago. Its name means "near crested lizard", referring to its resemblance to Saurolophus; fossils come from Alberta (Dinosaur Park Formation), New Mexico (Fruitland and Kirtland formations), and Utah (Kaiparowits Formation).

Description

Parasaurolophus was a large herbivore, roughly 9–10 metres long, that could walk both bipedally and quadrupedally. Its most striking feature is its cranial crest: a long, hollow, tube-like structure extending up to 1–1.8 metres back from the skull in the species P. walkeri and P. tubicen, while P. cyrtocristatus had a shorter, curved crest.

Crest function

The hollow crest contained looping nasal passages. Proposed functions include visual display and species recognition, sound resonance — computer modelling suggests the crest acted like a resonating chamber to produce low-frequency calls — and possibly thermoregulation.
