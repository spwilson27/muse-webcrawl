The BBC Home Service was a national radio network in the United Kingdom, broadcast from 1939 until 1967. It was created on 1 September 1939, days before the outbreak of the Second World War, by merging the BBC's pre-war National Programme and Regional Programme into a single service, and it became the BBC's main domestic channel for news, talks, drama, and serious programming.

Wartime and post-war years

During the war the Home Service carried news bulletins, government announcements, and morale-boosting output, with the separate Forces Programme (later the General Forces Programme) providing lighter entertainment. After the war the BBC's domestic radio settled into three networks: the Light Programme (1945) for popular entertainment, the Third Programme (1946) for high culture, and the Home Service in between, retaining regional variations produced in Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland, and the English regions.

Programming

The Home Service was home to news, current affairs, radio drama, schools broadcasting, and long-running features such as Woman's Hour (launched 1946), The Archers (from 1951, after a regional pilot), Today (from 1957), and Desert Island Discs, which had moved from the Forces Programme. Its regional opt-outs provided news and programming for the nations and regions.

Closure

On 30 September 1967 the BBC reorganised its radio output in response to competition from pirate radio: the Home Service became BBC Radio 4, the Light Programme became Radio 2, the Third Programme became Radio 3, and the new pop station Radio 1 was launched.
