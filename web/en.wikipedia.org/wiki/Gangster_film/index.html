Gangster film

A gangster film is a film genre that focuses on gangs and organized crime. The genre emerged in the early 1930s in the United States, during Prohibition, with films depicting the rise and fall of criminal antiheroes.

History

The classic cycle includes Little Caesar (1931), The Public Enemy (1931) and Scarface (1932). After enforcement of the Hays Code, gangsters were increasingly shown being punished, but the genre revived with Bonnie and Clyde (1967) and reached new heights with Francis Ford Coppola's The Godfather (1972) and its sequel.

Later landmark entries include Goodfellas (1990), The Departed (2006) and television's The Sopranos. Related traditions include British gangster films, Hong Kong triad films and Japanese yakuza films.

Common themes include ambition, loyalty, family, betrayal and the corrupting allure of the American Dream.
