Psamtik III

Psamtik III (also known as Psammenitus in Greek sources) was the last pharaoh of the Twenty-sixth Dynasty of Egypt, reigning for about six months in 526–525 BC. His brief reign ended with the Persian conquest of Egypt by Cambyses II, inaugurating the period of Achaemenid rule known as the Twenty-seventh Dynasty.

Reign

Psamtik succeeded his father Amasis II at a moment of crisis: the Persian king Cambyses II was already preparing the invasion of Egypt. According to Herodotus, Psamtik took his position at Pelusium, guarding the eastern approach through the Sinai. The Egyptian army was defeated at the Battle of Pelusium in 525 BC — Herodotus adds the picturesque and probably legendary claim that the Persians carried sacred cats before them, paralysing the Egyptian archers.

Capture and death

Psamtik retreated to Memphis, which was besieged and taken. Cambyses had the captured king treated honourably at first, but after Psamtik was implicated in rebellion, Herodotus reports he was forced to drink bull's blood, which killed him. Egypt was annexed as a Persian satrapy.
