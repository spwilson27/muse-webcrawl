The Great Western Main Line (GWML) is a main line railway in England, running westward from London Paddington to Bristol Temple Meads. It opened in stages between 1838 and 1841 as the original route of the Great Western Railway, engineered by Isambard Kingdom Brunel. Major stations include Reading, Didcot Parkway, Swindon and Bath Spa. Extensions continuing the historic Great Western corridor reach Cardiff and Swansea via the South Wales Main Line (through the Severn Tunnel), and Exeter, Plymouth and Penzance via the Bristol to Exeter and Cornish Main Lines.

History

Brunel built the line to exceptionally high standards of alignment and gradient, originally with his broad gauge of 7 ft ¼ in (2,140 mm). Landmark structures include the Maidenhead Railway Bridge, Box Tunnel between Chippenham and Bath, and the Bristol Temple Meads terminus. After the "gauge wars" the line was gradually converted; the last broad-gauge trains ran in 1892. Under British Rail the route was famous for the InterCity 125 High Speed Trains introduced in 1976, long the world's fastest diesel trains in regular service.

Modernisation

A long-running modernisation programme electrified the line from Paddington to Cardiff (completed 2020) after cost overruns and a descoped scheme that left sections west of Cardiff and to Bristol via Bath un-electrified; Intercity Express Trains (Classes 800/802) run bi-mode operation beyond the wires. The line also carries the Heathrow Express and Elizabeth line services on its London suburban section.
