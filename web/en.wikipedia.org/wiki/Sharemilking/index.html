Sharemilking

Sharemilking is a form of sharefarming in the dairy industry, most developed in New Zealand, in which a farm owner and a sharemilker split the income from milk production in return for dividing the operating costs and responsibilities of running the farm.

Arrangements

In a typical arrangement, the farm owner provides the land, buildings and major fixed equipment, while the sharemilker owns the herd and machinery and employs the farm staff. Common contract types include variable-order sharemilking, in which the sharemilker receives a smaller share (often around 20–30 per cent) of the milk income, and 50/50 sharemilking, in which costs and income are shared more evenly. Herd-owning sharemilking gives the sharemilker a pathway to eventual farm ownership.

Significance

Sharemilking has historically been an important route into farm ownership in New Zealand, allowing people without the capital to buy land to build equity through herd ownership. The industry is represented by bodies such as Federated Farmers' sharemilker sections.
