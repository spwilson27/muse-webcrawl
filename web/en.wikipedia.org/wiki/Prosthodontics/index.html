Prosthodontics

Prosthodontics, also known as dental prosthetics or prosthetic dentistry, is the area of dentistry that focuses on dental prostheses. It is one of twelve dental specialties recognized by the American Dental Association (ADA), the Royal College of Surgeons of England, the Royal College of Surgeons of Edinburgh, and the Royal Australasian College of Dental Surgeons. The ADA defines it as "the dental specialty pertaining to the diagnosis, treatment planning, rehabilitation and maintenance of the oral function, comfort, appearance and health of patients with clinical conditions associated with missing or deficient teeth or oral and maxillofacial tissues using biocompatible substitutes."

History

Dental prostheses date back thousands of years. The Etruscans used gold bridgework as early as 700 BC, and carved ivory or animal-bone dentures were known in the ancient world. In the 18th century, Pierre Fauchard advanced the craft with early dentures made of human teeth and ivory. The 19th century brought vulcanite denture bases, and the 20th century introduced acrylic resins, cast-metal frameworks, and eventually titanium dental implants, following Per-Ingvar Brånemark's work on osseointegration in the 1960s.

Branches

Prosthodontics is commonly divided into several branches:

  • Fixed prosthodontics — crowns, bridges, inlays, onlays and veneers cemented permanently in the mouth.
  • Removable prosthodontics — complete and partial dentures that the patient can remove.
  • Implant prosthodontics — restorations supported by or retained on dental implants.
  • Maxillofacial prosthetics — prostheses replacing missing parts of the face, jaws or ears, often after cancer surgery or trauma.

Training

In the United States, prosthodontics requires a three-year postgraduate residency program accredited by the ADA after dental school. Board certification is granted by the American Board of Prosthodontics. Similar specialist training pathways exist in the United Kingdom, Australia, and many other countries.

Treatment

A prosthodontist plans and coordinates multidisciplinary care, designing restorations that restore chewing function, speech, and facial appearance. Treatments range from a single crown to full-mouth rehabilitation, and may involve coordination with periodontists, oral surgeons, orthodontists and endodontists. Digital workflows — including intraoral scanning, CAD/CAM milling and 3D printing — have become standard in modern practice.
