Queen regnant

A queen regnant (plural: queens regnant) is a female monarch who rules in her own right over a kingdom, possessing and exercising all of a sovereign's powers. This is distinct from a queen consort, who is the wife of a reigning king and shares her husband's rank and titles but does not share his sovereignty. A queen regnant is sometimes also called a female king in older usage, and a few women, such as Jadwiga of Poland, were formally crowned as "king" rather than queen.

Elizabeth II, queen regnant of the United Kingdom and other Commonwealth realms

History

Queens regnant have ruled in many cultures, though inheritance laws favouring male heirs (such as Salic law in parts of medieval Europe) limited their frequency. Notable historical queens regnant include Hatshepsut of Egypt, Isabella I of Castile, Elizabeth I of England, Christina of Sweden, Maria Theresa of Austria, Catherine the Great of Russia, and Victoria of the United Kingdom.

In modern times, as most surviving monarchies have moved toward absolute primogeniture or equal succession, queens regnant have become more common. Margrethe II of Denmark, who abdicated in 2024, and Elizabeth II of the United Kingdom, the longest-reigning queen regnant in history, are prominent recent examples.

Related titles

The male equivalent is a king regnant. A queen who rules on behalf of a minor or absent monarch is a queen regent, and the mother of a reigning monarch may be styled queen mother. An empress regnant is the corresponding rank in an empire.
