Marquess of Bute

Marquess of Bute is a title in the Peerage of Great Britain, created in 1796 for John Stuart, 4th Earl of Bute. The Stuart family descends from a younger branch of the royal Stewart line and took its name from the Isle of Bute in the Firth of Clyde, where the family seat, Mount Stuart House, stands.

History

The earldom of Bute was created in the Peerage of Scotland in 1703. John Stuart, 3rd Earl of Bute (1713–1792), became a close adviser and tutor to George III and served as Prime Minister of Great Britain from 1762 to 1763, the first Scottish prime minister after the Union. His son was raised to the marquessate in 1796.

The family's fortunes grew enormously in the 19th century through coal and dock development in South Wales. John Crichton-Stuart, 3rd Marquess of Bute (1847–1900), one of the richest men in Britain, rebuilt Cardiff Castle and Mount Stuart House in lavish Gothic Revival style and was a noted scholar and patron of architecture and learning.

Seats and subsidiary titles

The family seats are Mount Stuart House on the Isle of Bute and Dumfries House in Ayrshire. Subsidiary titles include Earl of Bute, Earl of Dumfries, Viscount Ayr, and Lord Crichton of Sanquhar. The heir apparent traditionally uses the courtesy title Earl of Dumfries.
