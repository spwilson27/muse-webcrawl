Jōmon period

Periodc. 14,000 – c. 1000 BC
RegionJapanese archipelago
Named forCord-marked (jōmon) pottery
Followed byYayoi period

The Jōmon period is the time in Japanese prehistory, traditionally dated between about 14,000 BC and 1000 BC, during which Japan was inhabited by a diverse hunter-gatherer culture that achieved a considerable degree of sedentism and cultural complexity. The period is named after the cord-marked patterns on its pottery, among the oldest ceramic traditions in the world.

Culture

Jōmon communities lived in pit dwellings, exploited rich marine and forest resources, and produced distinctive pottery, clay figurines (dogū), lacquerware and stone circles. Regional cultures included large settlements such as Sannai-Maruyama in Aomori, a major site of the period. Fishing, shellfish gathering and the harvesting of nuts such as chestnuts and acorns formed the subsistence base, supplemented increasingly by small-scale plant management.

End of the period

The Jōmon period gave way to the Yayoi period as wet-rice agriculture and metalworking were introduced from the continent through Korea, along with new populations ancestral in part to modern Japanese people.
