UNRRA

The United Nations Relief and Rehabilitation Administration (UNRRA) was an international relief agency founded in 1943 to provide aid to areas liberated from Axis occupation during World War II. Despite its name, it predated the United Nations Organization and was created by an agreement signed at the White House on 9 November 1943 by 44 nations; "United Nations" referred to the Allied coalition.

Operations

UNRRA distributed food, clothing, medicine, and agricultural and industrial supplies worth about $4 billion, the great majority financed by the United States. It operated in China, the Philippines, Korea, Ethiopia, and throughout Europe, including Poland, Yugoslavia, Greece, Czechoslovakia, and Italy, and later in the Byelorussian and Ukrainian Soviet republics. Its largest undertaking was the care of displaced persons: at its peak UNRRA administered hundreds of DP camps in Germany and Austria and helped repatriate millions of refugees and forced labourers.

Leadership and end

Its directors general were Herbert H. Lehman, former governor of New York; Fiorello La Guardia, former mayor of New York City; and Major General Lowell Rooks. UNRRA's operations in Europe wound down by mid-1947 and elsewhere by 1948; its remaining functions passed to successor bodies, including the International Refugee Organization, the World Health Organization (whose Interim Commission it funded), and UNICEF, to which it transferred residual assets.

Legacy

UNRRA was the first great experiment in multilateral humanitarian relief and a direct precursor of the post-war international aid system, including the Marshall Plan, which took over the task of European economic reconstruction in 1948.
