Tteokbokki (Korean: 떡볶이) is a popular Korean food made from small cylinder-shaped rice cakes called tteokmyeon or garaetteok, simmered in a thick, sweet and spicy sauce. The best-known modern version is seasoned with gochujang (chili paste) and is a staple of street food stalls and snack bars (bunsikjip) throughout South Korea, typically served with sliced fish cakes (eomuk), boiled eggs, and spring onions.

History

The dish descends from tteokbokki served at the Joseon royal court, where it was a savoury, non-spicy dish of sliced rice cake stir-fried with meat, vegetables and a soy-sauce-based seasoning — today's gungjung tteokbokki ("royal court tteokbokki"). The red gochujang version emerged in the 1950s in Seoul's Sindang-dong neighbourhood, credited popularly to vendor Ma Bok-rim, who is said to have created it after accidentally dropping rice cake into a spicy sauce. Inexpensive wheat-flour tteok soon made it a mass snack, and Sindang-dong became a dedicated tteokbokki district.

Variations

Modern variations include cheese tteokbokki, curry tteokbokki, jjajang (black bean) tteokbokki, and rosé tteokbokki with cream, as well as rabokki, which adds instant ramen noodles. Instant and export versions have carried the dish worldwide with the spread of Korean popular culture, and it consistently ranks among the Korean foods best known to foreigners.
