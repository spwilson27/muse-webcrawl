Johns Hopkins University (often abbreviated as Johns Hopkins, Hopkins, or JHU) is a private research university in Baltimore, Maryland, United States. Founded in 1876, it was the first university in the United States based on the European research institution model, combining advanced scholarship with graduate training. It is considered one of the world's leading universities, particularly in medicine, public health and international studies, and is consistently among the largest recipients of federal research funding of any American university.

History

The university is named for its first benefactor, the Quaker philanthropist and abolitionist Johns Hopkins, who bequeathed US$7 million — at the time the largest philanthropic gift in American history — to found a university and hospital. Under its first president, Daniel Coit Gilman, Johns Hopkins opened in 1876 with an emphasis on graduate education, research and academic freedom, importing the German seminar model of scholarship. The Johns Hopkins Hospital (1889) and School of Medicine (1893) revolutionised medical education in America, requiring a bachelor's degree and scientific training for admission and pioneering bedside teaching; the School of Hygiene and Public Health (1916) was the first of its kind.

Organisation and campuses

The university's main Homewood campus in north Baltimore houses the Krieger School of Arts and Sciences and the Whiting School of Engineering. Other major divisions include the School of Medicine, the Bloomberg School of Public Health and the School of Nursing in East Baltimore; the Peabody Institute conservatory; the Carey Business School; the School of Education; and the Paul H. Nitze School of Advanced International Studies (SAIS) in Washington, D.C. The university also operates the Applied Physics Laboratory, a major defence research centre, and the Space Telescope Science Institute.

Research and rankings

Johns Hopkins has been associated with dozens of Nobel laureates and landmark research, from the discovery of restriction enzymes to the development of CPR and the first effective treatments for sickle cell disease. Its COVID-19 dashboard, maintained by the Center for Systems Science and Engineering, became the world's most cited source of pandemic data. The Blue Jays men's lacrosse team is one of the most successful in collegiate history, with 44 national championships.

Notable people

Alumni and faculty include US president Woodrow Wilson, physiologist and Nobel laureate Riccardo Giacconi, geneticist Barbara McClintock (faculty), and numerous heads of state, scientists and writers. The university press, founded in 1878, is the oldest continuously running university press in the United States.
