George Ashley Campbell

George A. Campbell

Born: 27 November 1870, Hastings, Minnesota, USA

Died: 10 November 1954

Known for: Loading coil theory, electric wave filter

George Ashley Campbell (27 November 1870 – 10 November 1954) was an American engineer who worked for the American Telephone and Telegraph Company (AT&T). He was a pioneer in the mathematical theory of transmission lines and is credited with inventing the electric wave filter.

Loading coils

Around 1899–1900 Campbell worked out the theory of the loading coil: by inserting inductors at regular intervals along a telephone line, the line's characteristics could be made to approximate the distortionless condition derived by Oliver Heaviside, greatly extending the range and quality of telephone transmission. His work ran parallel to that of Michael Pupin, leading to a famous patent interference case that AT&T ultimately settled by purchasing Pupin's patents while Campbell's theoretical contributions were recognised within the company.

Wave filter

Campbell's 1915 design of the electric wave filter — networks of inductors and capacitors that pass selected frequency bands — laid the foundation of modern filter theory and made frequency-division multiplexing practical, allowing many telephone conversations to share one line. His 1922 paper "Physical Theory of the Electric Wave-Filter" in the Bell System Technical Journal is a classic of electrical engineering.

Honours

Campbell received the AIEE Edison Medal in 1940 "for his contributions to the theory of electrical networks and particularly to the development of the electric wave filter". His mathematical work on network theory also fed into later developments in feedback amplifiers and communication theory at Bell Labs.
