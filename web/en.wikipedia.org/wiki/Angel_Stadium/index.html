Angel Stadium

Angel Stadium is a baseball stadium in Anaheim, California, United States. Since its opening in 1966, it has served as the home ballpark of the Los Angeles Angels of Major League Baseball (MLB). It is the fourth-oldest active MLB ballpark, behind Fenway Park, Wrigley Field and Dodger Stadium. The stadium is known for its iconic 230-foot (70 m) "Big A" scoreboard structure and the rock waterfall feature in left-center field known as the "California Spectacular."

History

Angels owner Gene Autry chose the Anaheim site in 1964, and the stadium—originally called Anaheim Stadium—opened for the 1966 season. Its signature "Big A" scoreboard, topped by a halo, became a landmark along the Orange Freeway. The stadium hosted the 1967 MLB All-Star Game, the first played at night during prime time.

Football years and renovation

In 1980 the stadium was enclosed and expanded to about 69,000 seats so that the Los Angeles Rams of the NFL could move in; the Rams played there until departing for St. Louis after the 1994 season. After the Walt Disney Company took control of the Angels, the stadium underwent a major renovation between 1997 and 1999 that removed much of the outfield seating added for football, restored an open outfield view, and added the rock fountain "California Spectacular." The renovated park reopened in 1998.

Major events

Angel Stadium hosted the 2002 World Series, in which the Angels defeated the San Francisco Giants in seven games for the franchise's only championship, and the 1989 and 2010 All-Star Games. It has also hosted the AMA Supercross motorcycle series for decades, monster truck events, concerts, and was the site of the 2005 and 2015 U.S. editions of large evangelistic crusades. The stadium was a venue for the 2023 World Baseball Classic.

Naming

The stadium's full sponsored name from 2004 to 2024 was Angel Stadium of Anaheim; naming arrangements have changed several times, including Edison International Field (1998–2003).
