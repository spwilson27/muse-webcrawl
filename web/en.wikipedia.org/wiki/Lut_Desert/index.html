The Lut Desert, widely referred to as Dasht-e Lut (Persian: دشت لوت, "Emptiness Desert"), is a large salt desert in south-eastern Iran, in the provinces of Kerman, South Khorasan, and Sistan and Baluchestan. It is the world's 33rd-largest desert and was inscribed on UNESCO's World Heritage List in 2016 for its spectacular wind-eroded landforms.

Geography and climate

The desert stretches about 480 km long and 320 km wide. Its central depression contains yardangs (wind-carved ridges) among the largest on Earth, vast salt pans, and the Gandom Beryan plateau, covered in dark volcanic lava. It is one of the hottest and driest places on Earth: NASA's Aqua satellite recorded a land surface temperature of 70.7 °C (159.3 °F) in 2005 — the highest ever measured on the planet's surface. The core of the desert is a hyper-arid zone where even bacteria cannot persist, earning it the description of an "abiotic zone".

Name and exploration

Lut means "bare" or "empty" in Persian. The region was traversed by Marco Polo and later explored and mapped in detail by, among others, the Swedish explorer Sven Hedin and the Iranian geographer Parviz Rajabi.
