California Battalion

The California Battalion (also called the California Mounted Riflemen) was a volunteer military unit formed during the Mexican–American War in present-day California. It was organized in 1846 under the authority of Brevet Captain John C. Frémont of the United States Army Corps of Topographical Engineers.

Formation

Following the outbreak of the Bear Flag Revolt in June 1846, American settlers and adventurers in Mexican Alta California formed a volunteer force. Frémont took command and the unit was formally mustered into United States service as the California Battalion in July 1846, after Commodore John D. Sloat raised the American flag at Monterey. The battalion grew to several companies of mounted riflemen, numbering several hundred men, drawing recruits from settlers, trappers, and immigrants arriving overland.

Service

The battalion participated in the American conquest of California, marching south from Monterey, occupying Santa Barbara and Los Angeles alongside forces under Robert F. Stockton and Stephen W. Kearny. Its members fought in engagements including the Battle of San Pasqual (in support roles), the Battle of Rio San Gabriel, and the Battle of La Mesa in January 1847. The war in California ended with the Treaty of Cahuenga, signed at Campo de Cahuenga by Frémont and Andrés Pico on 13 January 1847. The battalion was disbanded later in 1847.

Legacy

Many battalion members remained in California and became prominent in the early American period. The California Battalion is often regarded as a forerunner of California's state militia and National Guard traditions.
