Double Irish arrangement

The Double Irish is a base erosion and profit shifting (BEPS) corporate tax tool designed for U.S. multinational corporations, in which a series of transactions shifts intellectual-property (IP) income from a higher-tax country to a lower-tax country using two Irish companies. Pre-2015 it was the largest BEPS tool in history, shielding US$1 trillion in profits; an extension, the "Double Irish with a Dutch sandwich," routed payments through a Netherlands company to avoid Irish withholding taxes.

Mechanism

In the classic structure, a US parent licenses IP to a first Irish company that is tax-resident in a zero-tax jurisdiction such as Bermuda but incorporated in Ireland; this company sub-licenses to a second Irish operating company, tax-resident in Ireland, which sells to customers worldwide. Royalty payments flow from the operating company to the Bermuda-resident company as deductible expenses, leaving little taxable profit in Ireland while Ireland's trading rate (12.5%) applies to the small remainder. Check-the-box US tax rules allowed the arrangement to be disregarded for US purposes, deferring US tax indefinitely.

Users and scrutiny

The structure was associated with US technology and pharmaceutical firms including Apple, Google, Facebook, and Microsoft. The 2013 US Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations hearings on Apple, and the European Commission's 2016 state-aid decision ordering Apple to repay up to €13 billion in back taxes to Ireland, made the Double Irish a symbol of aggressive corporate tax avoidance.

Closure

In October 2014, Ireland's finance minister Michael Noonan announced the closure of the arrangement: from 2015 no new entrants were admitted, and existing users were grandfathered until the end of 2020. Successor Irish BEPS tools — notably the "Single Malt" and capital-allowances-for-intangible-assets (CAIA) structures — were subsequently identified by academics, while the US 2017 Tax Cuts and Jobs Act's GILTI regime also reduced the benefit of such structures.
