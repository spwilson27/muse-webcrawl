Botany Bay

Botany Bay (Aboriginal name: Kamay) is an open ocean embayment on the coast of New South Wales, Australia, about 13 km south of the Sydney central business district. It was the site of the first landing on the east coast of Australia by Europeans, when Lieutenant (later Captain) James Cook and the crew of HMS Endeavour came ashore in April 1770.

History

The bay's botanists Joseph Banks and Daniel Solander collected so many plant specimens during the Endeavour's eight-day stay that Cook named it Botany Bay. In January 1788 the First Fleet under Captain Arthur Phillip arrived to establish a penal colony at Botany Bay, but finding the anchorage exposed and the soil and water inadequate, Phillip moved the settlement north to Sydney Cove in Port Jackson, where Sydney was founded on 26 January 1788.

The bay's northern shore, at La Perouse, and its southern shore, at Kurnell — the traditional lands of the Gweagal people — are commemorated in Kamay Botany Bay National Park.

Modern use

Today Botany Bay is heavily developed: Port Botany, one of Australia's largest container terminals, occupies the northern shore, Sydney Airport's runways extend into the bay, and the bay is an important recreational and commercial fishing area. The Georges and Cooks rivers flow into it.
