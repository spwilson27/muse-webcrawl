The Twenty-sixth Dynasty of Egypt (notated Dynasty XXVI, alternatively 26th Dynasty or Dynasty 26) was the last native dynasty of Ancient Egypt before the Persian conquest in 525 BC (although there were later native dynasties). The dynasty's reign (664–525 BC) is also called the Saite Period after the city of Sais, where its pharaohs had their capital, and marks the beginning of the Late Period of ancient Egypt.

History

The dynasty traces its origins to the Twenty-fourth Dynasty of local rulers at Sais in the western Delta. Psamtik I, initially installed as a vassal of the Assyrian Empire, gradually reunited Egypt, expelled Assyrian control with the help of Greek and Carian mercenaries, and by 656 BC had unified the country under his rule.

Under the Saite kings, Egypt enjoyed a final great flowering. Psamtik I's son Necho II commissioned the canal linking the Nile to the Red Sea and, according to Herodotus, sent a Phoenician expedition that circumnavigated Africa. Apries campaigned in the Levant and against the Greek colony of Cyrene. Amasis II, a general who seized the throne in 570 BC, enjoyed a long and prosperous reign marked by close relations with the Greek world, including the trading colony of Naukratis in the Delta.

Culture

The Saite period saw a deliberate cultural renaissance: artists and scribes consciously revived the styles and conventions of the Old and Middle Kingdoms, and ancient religious texts were recopied and edited (the so-called Saite recension of the Book of the Dead). At the same time Egypt became more integrated into the Mediterranean world of Greek trade and mercenary service.

End

Amasis died shortly before the Persian invasion. His son Psamtik III reigned only briefly before being defeated by Cambyses II at the Battle of Pelusium in 525 BC, after which Egypt became a satrapy of the Achaemenid Empire.
