Sir Douglas Quintet

The Sir Douglas Quintet was an American rock band formed in San Antonio, Texas, in 1964. Led by Doug Sahm, the group was an early exponent of the Tex-Mex sound, blending rock and roll, country, blues, rhythm and blues and conjunto music. Their best-known songs are "She's About a Mover" (1965) and "Mendocino" (1969).

History

Sahm, a child prodigy who had performed with Hank Williams as a boy, formed the band with lifelong friend Augie Meyers, whose Vox organ playing became central to the group's sound. Producer Huey P. Meaux gave the band its misleadingly British-sounding name to capitalise on the British Invasion, but its music was steeped in Texas traditions. "She's About a Mover", built on a conjunto rhythm, reached the US top 20 in 1965.

After a drug arrest disrupted the band's career, Sahm moved to California, re-formed the Quintet, and scored again with "Mendocino" in 1969. The group recorded through the 1970s, and Sahm's later projects included the Texas Tornados supergroup. Members of the Quintet, including Meyers and bassist Jack Barber, continued to work with Sahm for decades.

Legacy

The Sir Douglas Quintet is regarded as a founding influence on Tex-Mex rock and Americana, and "She's About a Mover" has been inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame.
