Ecoregion

An ecoregion (ecological region) is an ecologically and geographically defined area that is smaller than a bioregion, which in turn is smaller than a biogeographic realm. Ecoregions cover relatively large areas of land or water and contain distinctive assemblages of natural communities and species.

Ecoregion
Systems: WWF (867 terrestrial), EPA, Bailey
Hierarchy: Realm > Bioregion > Ecoregion

Definitions and systems

The World Wildlife Fund (WWF) defined 867 terrestrial ecoregions grouped into 14 biomes and 8 realms, and selected 200 priority "Global 200" ecoregions for conservation. The US EPA and Commission for Environmental Cooperation use hierarchical levels in North America; Robert Bailey developed a system for the US Forest Service.

Importance

Ecoregions are used for conservation planning, biodiversity assessment, and ecosystem management because boundaries reflect climate, geology, soils, and biota rather than political borders. Marine and freshwater ecoregion systems have also been developed.
