International Baccalaureate

The International Baccalaureate (IB), formerly known as the International Baccalaureate Organization (IBO), is a nonprofit foundation headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland, and founded in 1968. It offers four educational programmes for students aged 3 to 19, taught in thousands of schools in over 150 countries.

Programmes

The four programmes are the Primary Years Programme (PYP), the Middle Years Programme (MYP), the Diploma Programme (DP), and the Career-related Programme (CP). The Diploma Programme, taken at ages 16–19, requires study in six subject groups, an extended essay, the Theory of Knowledge course, and Creativity, Activity, Service (CAS).

History

The IB grew out of the International School of Geneva, where teachers in the 1960s sought a common curriculum and university-entry qualification for internationally mobile students. The first Diploma examinations were held in 1970.

Recognition

The IB Diploma is recognized by universities worldwide as an admissions qualification, often with advanced standing or credit for higher-level subjects. Critics have raised concerns about cost and workload, while supporters cite its emphasis on critical thinking and international-mindedness.
