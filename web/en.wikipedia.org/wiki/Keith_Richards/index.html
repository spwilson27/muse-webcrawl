Keith Richards

Richards performing with the Rolling Stones

Keith Richards (born 18 December 1943) is an English musician, songwriter and record producer, best known as the co-founder, guitarist, secondary vocalist and co-principal songwriter of the Rolling Stones. His partnership with Mick Jagger — as the "Glimmer Twins" — produced many of the most enduring songs in rock music, and Rolling Stone magazine has ranked him among the greatest guitarists of all time.

Early life

Richards was born in Dartford, Kent. He met Mick Jagger, a former primary-school classmate, in 1961; their shared love of American blues and R&B led them to form a band with Brian Jones, which became the Rolling Stones in 1962.

With the Rolling Stones

Richards's guitar style — built on Chuck Berry-derived riffs, rhythm playing with arrested lead figures, and from 1969 an open G tuning with five strings — defined such songs as "(I Can't Get No) Satisfaction", "Jumpin' Jack Flash", "Brown Sugar", "Tumbling Dice" and "Start Me Up". He also sings lead vocals on at least one song per album, including "Happy", "Before They Make Me Run" and "You Got the Silver". His 2010 autobiography, Life, written with James Fox, became a bestseller.

Solo work and other projects

Richards released solo albums including Talk Is Cheap (1988) and Crosstown Traffic-era work with his band the X-Pensive Winos; his third solo album, Crosseyed Heart, appeared in 2015. He appeared as Captain Jack Sparrow's father, Captain Teague, in the Pirates of the Caribbean films.

Personal life

Richards's history of drug use and near-legendary constitution made him a tabloid fixture for decades; he has been clean of heroin since the late 1970s. He has been married to the model Patti Hansen since 1983 and lives principally in Connecticut and at Redlands, his Sussex estate.
