Eastern Depot

Eastern Depot (Dongchang)
Founded: 1420, Ming dynasty
Founder: Yongle Emperor
Function: Secret police / intelligence

The Eastern Depot or Eastern Factory (Chinese: 東廠; pinyin: Dōngchǎng) was a Ming dynasty spy and secret police agency run by eunuchs, established by the Yongle Emperor in 1420.

Role

Headquartered near the Donghua Gate in Beijing, the Depot reported directly to the emperor, bypassing regular bureaucracy. Its agents investigated officials and commoners for sedition and corruption, with powers of arrest and interrogation, often working with — and later dominating — the Embroidered Uniform Guard (Jinyiwei).

Notoriety

The Depot became notorious under powerful eunuchs such as Wang Zhen, Liu Jin and especially Wei Zhongxian in the 1620s, whose persecution of Donglin partisans made 'Eastern Depot' a byword for eunuch tyranny. A later Western Depot (1477) briefly supplemented it. The institution survived until the fall of the Ming in 1644. See also Ming dynasty.
