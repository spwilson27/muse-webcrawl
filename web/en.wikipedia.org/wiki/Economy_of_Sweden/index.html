The economy of Sweden is a highly developed, export-oriented mixed economy. Timber, hydropower, and iron ore constitute the resource base of an economy heavily oriented toward foreign trade. Sweden's engineering sector accounts for a large share of output and exports, and the country is home to major multinational firms including Volvo, Ericsson, IKEA, H&M, and Spotify.

Overview

Sweden combines a capitalist economy with a large welfare state — the "Nordic model" — funded by high taxes, with public spending around half of GDP providing universal health care, education, and social insurance. The country joined the European Union in 1995 but rejected adopting the euro in a 2003 referendum, retaining the krona and an independent monetary policy under the Riksbank.

History

Sweden industrialised rapidly from the late 19th century and by the 1970s had one of the world's highest standards of living. A severe banking and fiscal crisis in the early 1990s led to reforms including central bank independence, a fiscal framework with a surplus target, pension reform, and deregulation, after which growth recovered strongly. Sweden weathered the 2008 financial crisis comparatively well, though export dependence makes it sensitive to global demand.
