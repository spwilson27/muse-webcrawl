United Nations Truce Supervision Organization

The United Nations Truce Supervision Organization (UNTSO) is a United Nations peacekeeping organisation founded on 29 May 1948 to supervise the truce called for by the UN Security Council during the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. It is the United Nations' first peacekeeping operation and remains in operation today, making it the longest-running peacekeeping mission in UN history.

Mandate and work

UNTSO's military observers — unarmed officers contributed by member states — monitor ceasefires, armistice agreements and disengagement lines in the Middle East. Following the 1949 Armistice Agreements, UNTSO observers assisted the Mixed Armistice Commissions between Israel and Egypt, Jordan, Lebanon and Syria. Its personnel are headquartered at Government House in Jerusalem and deploy in support of later missions, including UNDOF on the Golan Heights (since 1974) and UNIFIL in southern Lebanon (since 1978), observing and reporting violations of the relevant agreements.

History

Created by Security Council Resolution 50 (1948), UNTSO observers were the first UN "blue berets" (initially without helmets) in the field. Observers served through the wars of 1956, 1967 and 1973, adapting as armistice lines were replaced by disengagement agreements. Notably, the UN mediator Count Folke Bernadotte, whom UNTSO supported, was assassinated in Jerusalem in September 1948. Several dozen UNTSO personnel have died in service over the mission's history. Because its authorising resolutions have no expiry date, UNTSO endures as a flexible pool of experienced observers for the region.
