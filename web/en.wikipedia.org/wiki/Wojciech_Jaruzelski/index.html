Wojciech Jaruzelski

Wojciech Witold Jaruzelski (Polish: [ˈvɔjt͡ɕɛx ˈvitɔlt jaruˈzɛlskʲi]; 6 July 1923 – 25 May 2014) was a Polish military commander, politician and de facto leader of the Polish People's Republic from 1981 to 1989. He was the last leader of communist Poland, serving as First Secretary of the Polish United Workers' Party (1981–1989), Prime Minister (1981–1985), Chairman of the Council of State (1985–1989) and finally President of Poland (1989–1990). He is remembered above all for imposing martial law in Poland on 13 December 1981 to crush the Solidarity movement, and then, eight years later, for presiding over the Round Table negotiations and semi-free elections that peacefully ended communist rule in Poland.

Early life and military career

Born into a landowning family in Kurów, Jaruzelski was deported with his family to Siberia after the Soviet occupation of eastern Poland in 1940; forced labour in the taiga left him with snow blindness, the reason for his trademark tinted glasses. His father died in exile. In 1943 Jaruzelski joined the Soviet-formed Polish People's Army, fighting with the 1st Polish Army through the Vistula–Oder Offensive to Berlin. After the war he rose through intelligence and staff posts, fought bands of anti-communist partisans, and in 1960 became chief of the army's political directorate. He was Chief of the General Staff (1965–1968) and Minister of National Defence from 1968 — in which capacity Polish troops took part in the Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia — until 1983.

Martial law

Appointed Prime Minister in February 1981 amid economic crisis and the rise of the independent trade union Solidarity, Jaruzelski became party First Secretary in October. On 13 December 1981, citing the danger of civil war and (he later argued) of Soviet invasion, he proclaimed martial law: Solidarity was suspended and later banned, some 10,000 people were interned, borders and communications were sealed, and at least dozens were killed, including nine miners shot at the Wujek coal mine. Martial law was lifted in July 1983, but repression continued, including the 1984 murder of the priest Jerzy Popiełuszko by security officers.

Transition and later life

In 1988–89, facing renewed strikes, Jaruzelski authorised the Round Table Talks with Solidarity, leading to the semi-free elections of 4 June 1989, in which the communists were overwhelmingly defeated. Jaruzelski was elected President by the National Assembly by a single vote and served until Lech Wałęsa succeeded him in December 1990. In retirement he published memoirs defending martial law as the "lesser evil". From 2006 he faced prosecutions connected with martial law and the 1970 shootings of workers on the Baltic coast; proceedings were repeatedly suspended because of his ill health. He died in Warsaw in 2014 and was buried at Powązki Military Cemetery amid public controversy.
