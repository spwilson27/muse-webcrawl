Unto These Hills

Unto These Hills

Written by: Kermit Hunter

First performed: 1950

Venue: Mountainside Theatre, Cherokee, North Carolina

Unto These Hills is an outdoor historical drama by playwright Kermit Hunter first performed in 1950 in Cherokee, North Carolina. It dramatises the history of the Cherokee people of the Eastern Band from first contact with Europeans through the Trail of Tears, the forced removal of the Cherokee from their ancestral lands in 1838.

Production

The play is staged each summer at the Mountainside Theatre, an outdoor amphitheatre in Cherokee operated in association with the Cherokee Historical Association. It is one of the longest-running outdoor dramas in the United States and has been seen by millions of visitors. The cast features members of the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians.

Story

The drama follows Cherokee history through figures such as the peace chief Attakullakulla, the warrior Dragging Canoe, and Tsali, who sacrificed himself so that a remnant of his people could remain in the mountains — the ancestors of today's Eastern Band. Later revisions of the script, including a 2006 rewrite, sought to correct historical inaccuracies and present a more authentic Cherokee perspective.
