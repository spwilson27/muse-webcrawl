Yabusame
Japanese流鏑馬 ("flowing arrow horse")
TypeTraditional Japanese mounted archery (kyūjutsu)
SettingShinto shrine festivals
SchoolsTakeda school, Ogasawara school

Yabusame (流鏑馬) is a traditional form of Japanese mounted archery in which an archer on a running horse shoots three special "turnip-headed" arrows (kabura-ya, whose bulbous whistling heads were designed to make noise rather than to wound) at three wooden targets along a track. It is performed as a ritual at Shinto shrines, where it is believed to entertain the kami and pray for peace, abundant harvests, and the well-being of the community.

The performance

The archer, dressed in the hunting costume of a Kamakura-period samurai, gallops down a straight track about 255 metres long. At three points, marked by target posts, the archer must draw, aim, and loose an arrow — controlling the galloping horse with the knees alone — in a matter of seconds, shouting "In-yō-i!" ("darkness and light!") with each release. The blunt, whistling turnip-shaped arrowheads strike small wooden targets designed to shatter spectacularly. Because the ritual demands both martial skill and purity, archers traditionally undergo purification and abstinence before performing.

History

Mounted archery was the defining skill of the early samurai, and Minamoto no Yoritomo, alarmed by his warriors' poor archery, institutionalised yabusame practice in the Kamakura period. The two surviving schools, Takeda and Ogasawara, maintain the tradition today; famous performances are held at the Tsurugaoka Hachimangū in Kamakura and at the Shimogamo Shrine in Kyoto during the Aoi Matsuri season.
