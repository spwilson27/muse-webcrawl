The Highwaymen

The Highwaymen were an American country music supergroup formed in 1985 by four of the genre's most influential outlaw-country figures: Johnny Cash, Waylon Jennings, Willie Nelson, and Kris Kristofferson.

History

The four artists began collaborating in the mid-1980s, first appearing together on a Christmas special filmed in Switzerland. Their debut album, Highwayman (1985), featured the title track written by Jimmy Webb, which became a number-one country hit and earned a Grammy Award for Best Country Song. The group recorded two further studio albums, Highwayman 2 (1990) and The Road Goes On Forever (1995), and toured widely.

Legacy

The Highwaymen are credited with reviving the outlaw country movement and demonstrating the commercial power of country supergroups. Individual members continued solo careers until Jennings died in 2002 and Cash in 2003.
