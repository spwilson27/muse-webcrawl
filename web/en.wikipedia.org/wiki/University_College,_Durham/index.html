University College, Durham

University College, informally known as Castle, is the oldest college of Durham University in England. Founded in 1832, it is housed primarily in Durham Castle, a Norman castle that has been the college's home since 1837 and is part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Durham University was founded by Act of Parliament in 1832, and University College was created as its first college. In 1837 Bishop Edward Maltby granted the use of Durham Castle — formerly a palace of the Bishop of Durham — to the university, and it has accommodated students ever since, making it one of the oldest inhabited university buildings in the world.

The college today

The college has around a thousand students, with about a hundred living within the castle itself at any time; the rest live in nearby college accommodation at Moatside and Kepier Court. It operates the usual structures of a Durham college, with a Junior Common Room for undergraduates and a Middle Common Room for postgraduates. Students dine in the castle's Great Hall, beneath portraits, and formals are held regularly.

Motto

The college motto is Non nobis solum — "not for ourselves alone."
