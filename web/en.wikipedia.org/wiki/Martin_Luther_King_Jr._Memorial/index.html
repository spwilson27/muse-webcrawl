Martin Luther King Jr. Memorial

Location1964 Independence Ave. SW, West Potomac Park, Washington, D.C.
EstablishedAugust 28, 2011
DedicatedOctober 16, 2011
SculptorLei Yixin
Governing bodyNational Park Service

The Martin Luther King Jr. Memorial is a national memorial located in West Potomac Park next to the National Mall in Washington, D.C., United States. It covers four acres (1.6 ha) and includes the Stone of Hope, a granite statue of civil rights movement leader Martin Luther King Jr. carved by sculptor Lei Yixin. The memorial opened to the public on August 22, 2011, after more than two decades of planning, fundraising, and construction.

The Stone of Hope, depicting King emerging from granite, faces the Tidal Basin.

Design

The design, by the ROMA Design Group, draws on a line from King's 1963 "I Have a Dream" speech: "Out of the mountain of despair, a stone of hope." Visitors pass through two pieces of granite symbolizing the Mountain of Despair before reaching the Stone of Hope, from which King's likeness emerges. A 450-foot (137 m) crescent-shaped inscription wall carries fourteen quotations from King's speeches, sermons, and writings.

History

The memorial was authorized by Congress in 1996, championed by the Alpha Phi Alpha fraternity, of which King was a member. Its address, 1964 Independence Avenue SW, references the Civil Rights Act of 1964. It is the first memorial on or near the National Mall dedicated to an African American and to a non-president.

Quotation controversy

One inscription paraphrased King's 1968 "Drum Major" sermon in a way critics said made him sound arrogant. Following public criticism, the paraphrase was removed entirely in 2013.
