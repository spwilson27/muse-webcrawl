Opportunity zone

An opportunity zone is a designation and investment program created by the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act of 2017 in the United States to encourage long-term private investment in low-income urban, suburban and rural communities. The program provides tax incentives for investors to re-invest unrealized capital gains into dedicated Qualified Opportunity Funds, which in turn invest in businesses, real estate and other property within designated zones.

Designation

Governors of each state and territory nominated low-income census tracts (generally those with poverty rates of at least 20 percent or median family income below 80 percent of the area median), and the U.S. Department of the Treasury certified 8,764 opportunity zones across all 50 states, the District of Columbia, and five U.S. territories.

Tax incentives

Investors who roll capital gains into a Qualified Opportunity Fund within 180 days can defer tax on the gains, receive a step-up in basis for investments held for specified periods (under the original rules), and — most significantly — exclude tax on gains from the fund itself if the investment is held for at least ten years.

Debate

Supporters argue that the program channels private capital into distressed areas at low public cost. Critics contend that benefits have flowed disproportionately to wealthier investors and to areas already gentrifying, and that the program lacks reporting requirements sufficient to measure its effect on residents' employment and incomes.
