Joseph Paxton


Sir Joseph Paxton
Born: 3 August 1803, Milton Bryan, Bedfordshire, England
Died: 8 June 1865 (aged 61), Sydenham, London
Occupation: Gardener, architect, MP
Known for: The Crystal Palace; Chatsworth House gardens

Sir Joseph Paxton (3 August 1803 – 8 June 1865) was an English gardener, architect, engineer and Member of Parliament, best known for designing the Crystal Palace in London for the Great Exhibition of 1851.

Chatsworth

Paxton became head gardener at Chatsworth House, Derbyshire, seat of the Duke of Devonshire, where he built celebrated glasshouses, including the Great Conservatory, laid out the gardens and Emperor Fountain, and first flowered the giant water lily Victoria amazonica in Britain — an achievement whose ribbed leaf structure inspired his later architecture.

The Crystal Palace

For the Great Exhibition, Paxton's design for a vast prefabricated building of cast iron and plate glass — based on his Chatsworth lily house — was chosen over hundreds of entries. Erected in Hyde Park in just a few months in 1851, the Crystal Palace was later rebuilt at Sydenham, where it stood until destroyed by fire in 1936.

Later life

Knighted in 1851, Paxton served as MP for Coventry from 1854 until his death and was involved in railway promotion and public park design, including Birkenhead Park.
