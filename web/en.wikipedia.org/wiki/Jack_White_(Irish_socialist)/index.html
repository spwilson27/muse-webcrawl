Jack White (Irish socialist)

Captain Jack White DSO (22 May 1879 – 2 February 1946) was an Irish political activist, socialist, and co-founder of the Irish Citizen Army. The son of Field Marshal Sir George White, he moved from a decorated career in the British Army to revolutionary republicanism and later anarchism.

From soldier to socialist

Educated at Winchester and Sandhurst, White served in the Boer War, where he was decorated with the Distinguished Service Order. Commanding the family's regiment in India and serving as Governor of Gibraltar's aide, he became disillusioned with imperialism, resigned his commission, and embraced Tolstoyan and socialist ideas, settling his family at a communal experiment.

Irish Citizen Army

During the 1913 Dublin Lockout, White proposed and helped organize a workers' militia to protect pickets from police — the Irish Citizen Army, founded in November 1913 with James Larkin and James Connolly. He trained its first volunteers before resigning in 1914 after disputes over its direction; the ICA later fought in the Easter Rising of 1916, in which White did not take part, having been imprisoned in Wales for anti-recruiting agitation.

Later life

White drifted toward anarchism, supported republican prisoners, and in 1936 traveled to Spain during its civil war, working with the CNT anarchists and writing for the movement press. He died in Belfast in 1946; his autobiography, Misfit (1930), remains a classic account of a class-defector revolutionary.
