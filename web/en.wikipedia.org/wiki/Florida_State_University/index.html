Florida State University (Florida State or FSU) is a public research university in Tallahassee, Florida, United States. It is a senior member of the State University System of Florida and was founded in 1851; it is located on the oldest continuous site of higher education in Florida. The university is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity" and enrolls more than 44,000 students from all 50 states and over 130 countries.

History

Florida State traces its origins to 1823, when the Territorial Legislature planned a system of higher education, and to an 1851 legislative act establishing two seminaries of learning. The West Florida Seminary, which became Florida State College in 1901, became coeducational in 1947 after serving as the Florida State College for Women from 1905, and took the name Florida State University. The campus is noted for its collegiate Gothic architecture, much of it built in the first decades of the twentieth century.

Academics

The university comprises 16 colleges offering more than 300 degree programs, including highly ranked programs in law, business, education, criminology, creative writing, information studies and the sciences. FSU is home to the National High Magnetic Field Laboratory, the only such laboratory in the United States and headquarters of a worldwide network of high-field magnet facilities. The Florida State University College of Medicine, founded in 2000, was the first new medical school of the twenty-first century in the United States.

Student life and athletics

FSU's athletic teams are the Seminoles, competing in the Atlantic Coast Conference. The football team plays at Doak Campbell Stadium and has won three national championships (1993, 1999 and 2013). The university's Marching Chiefs are one of the largest collegiate marching bands in the country. Student traditions include the flying trapeze of the FSU Flying High Circus, one of only two collegiate circuses in the United States.

Notable alumni

Alumni include actors Burt Reynolds and Norman Reedus, musician Jim Morrison, golfer Brooks Koepka, football players Deion Sanders and Jameis Winston, and several Florida governors and members of Congress.
