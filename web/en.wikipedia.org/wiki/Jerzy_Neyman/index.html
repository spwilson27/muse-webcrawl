Jerzy Neyman (16 April 1894 – 5 August 1981; born Jerzy Spława-Neyman) was a Polish mathematician and statistician who spent most of his professional career at the University of Warsaw and then at the University of California, Berkeley. He was one of the principal architects of the modern theory of statistical inference.

Work

With Egon Pearson, Neyman developed the Neyman–Pearson lemma (1933), which gives a rigorous foundation for hypothesis testing, including the concepts of Type I and Type II errors and the power of a test. In 1937 he introduced the concept of the confidence interval, one of the most widely used tools of applied statistics. His work on sampling theory, including the theory of stratified sampling and the "Neyman allocation", also remains standard, as does his early 1923 formulation of potential outcomes in experimental design.

Life

Neyman studied at Kharkov University and later in Warsaw, Paris and London, where he collaborated with Karl and Egon Pearson. In 1938 he moved to Berkeley, where he built the Statistical Laboratory into one of the world's leading centres for statistics and founded the Berkeley Symposia on Mathematical Statistics and Probability. He received the U.S. National Medal of Science in 1968. Neyman died in Oakland, California, in 1981.
