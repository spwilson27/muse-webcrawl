Khedivate of Egypt

The Khedivate of Egypt (Arabic: خديوية مصر) was an autonomous tributary state of the Ottoman Empire, established in 1867 and lasting until 1914. Its rulers bore the title Khedive, granted by the Ottoman sultan to Isma'il Pasha and his successors of the Muhammad Ali dynasty.

History

Muhammad Ali, who seized power in Egypt in 1805, built a de facto independent state while remaining nominally an Ottoman governor. In 1867 his grandson Isma'il obtained the hereditary title of Khedive. Isma'il's ambitious modernization — and the opening of the Suez Canal in 1869 — left Egypt deeply in debt; he was deposed in 1879. The 'Urabi revolt led to British occupation in 1882, and Egypt remained under British control while formally Ottoman until 1914, when Britain declared a protectorate and the Sultanate of Egypt was proclaimed.

Khedives

  • Isma'il Pasha (1867–1879)
  • Tewfik Pasha (1879–1892)
  • Abbas II (1892–1914)
