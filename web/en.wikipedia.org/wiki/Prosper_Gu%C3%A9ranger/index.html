Prosper Guéranger

Born4 April 1805, Sablé-sur-Sarthe, France
Died30 January 1875, Solesmes, France
OccupationBenedictine monk, abbot, liturgist

Dom Prosper Guéranger (1805–1875) was a French Benedictine monk and priest who refounded the Abbey of Solesmes and became a leading figure of the nineteenth-century liturgical movement. He is regarded as the restorer of monastic life in France after its suppression during the Revolution.

Solesmes

Ordained in 1827, Guéranger purchased the former priory of Solesmes in 1833 and established a Benedictine community there; in 1837 it was raised to an abbey with Guéranger as its first abbot. Under his leadership Solesmes became the centre of the restoration of Gregorian chant, work carried on by his monks through the study of medieval chant manuscripts.

Writings and influence

His best-known work, L'Année liturgique (The Liturgical Year), a multi-volume commentary on the Mass and offices of the seasons, spread widely and helped popularise active participation in the liturgy. Guéranger also campaigned for the adoption of the Roman liturgy in place of local French (Neo-Gallican) uses, and defended papal authority, contributing to the climate that led to the definition of papal infallibility at the First Vatican Council.
