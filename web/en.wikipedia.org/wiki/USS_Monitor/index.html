USS Monitor

USS Monitor was an ironclad warship built for the Union Navy during the American Civil War and completed in early 1862, the first such ship commissioned by the Navy. Designed by Swedish-American engineer John Ericsson, she was revolutionary: nearly her entire hull was submerged, presenting a tiny target, and her two 11-inch Dahlgren guns were mounted in the world's first rotating naval gun turret. She gave her name to an entire class of warships — the monitors.

Battle of Hampton Roads

Monitor arrived at Hampton Roads, Virginia, on 8 March 1862, the day the Confederate ironclad CSS Virginia had destroyed two Union wooden warships. The next day, 9 March, the two ironclads fought the first battle between armored warships in history. The four-hour duel ended in a tactical draw, but Monitor prevented Virginia from breaking the Union blockade, and the engagement rendered wooden navies obsolete overnight.

Design and loss

Nicknamed the "cheesebox on a raft," Monitor was 179 feet long with a flat iron deck barely above the waterline and a cylindrical turret amidships. Her low freeboard made her unseaworthy in heavy weather: on 31 December 1862 she foundered in a storm off Cape Hatteras, North Carolina, while under tow, with the loss of sixteen of her crew.

Legacy and wreck

The wreck was located in 1973 and designated the Monitor National Marine Sanctuary, the first U.S. national marine sanctuary. Her propeller, engine, and the turret itself — recovered in 2002 with the remains of two sailors inside — are conserved and displayed at the Mariners' Museum in Newport News, Virginia.
