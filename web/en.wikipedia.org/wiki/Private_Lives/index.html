Private Lives is a 1930 comedy of manners in three acts by Noël Coward. It concerns a divorced couple who, while honeymooning with their new spouses, discover that they are staying in adjacent rooms at the same hotel. Despite a perpetually stormy relationship, they realise that they still have feelings for each other. Coward wrote one of his most popular songs, "Some Day I'll Find You", for the play.

Plot

Elyot Chase and Amanda Prynne, divorced for five years, arrive at the same hotel on the French coast to honeymoon with their new spouses, Victor and Sibyl. Spotting each other on adjoining balconies, they quickly discover that their old attraction is intact; within hours they have run away together to Amanda's flat in Paris, abandoning their spouses. There, after a few days of bliss, their old quarrels resume with full force — interrupted whenever either plays the agreed safety signal, "Solomon Isaacs" ("Sollocks"). Victor and Sibyl arrive in pursuit; after a chaotic breakfast confrontation, Elyot and Amanda slip away together again, leaving Victor and Sibyl — now quarrelling in exactly the same way — behind.

Original production and reception

Coward wrote the play in four days in Shanghai in 1930 and starred as Elyot opposite Gertrude Lawrence as Amanda, with a young Laurence Olivier as Victor. After a tryout opening in Edinburgh in August 1930, it opened in London at the Phoenix Theatre and then transferred to Broadway in 1931. The lightness of the writing — Coward called it "a frothy thing" — conceals dialogue now regarded as some of the wittiest in the English theatre, including the exchange "Don't quibble, Sibyl!" and Elyot's observation that it is "very, very flat, Norfolk."

Revivals

The play has been revived frequently, notably in a 2001 production with Alan Rickman and Lindsay Duncan that transferred from London to Broadway, and a 2013 London production with Toby Stephens and Anna Chancellor. It was adapted for film in 1931 starring Norma Shearer and Robert Montgomery.
