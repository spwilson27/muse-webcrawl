Ravanica

Ravanica (Serbian: Раваница) is a Serbian Orthodox monastery near Ćuprija in central Serbia, founded by Prince Lazar Hrebeljanović in the 1370s. It is the most important monument of the Morava school of medieval Serbian architecture and the burial place of Prince Lazar, the hero of the Battle of Kosovo (1389).

Architecture and art

The monastery church of the Ascension is a triconch building crowned by five domes, built of alternating courses of stone and brick with rich carved ornament — the prototype of the Morava style. Its frescoes, painted toward the end of the 14th century, include portraits of Prince Lazar and his family and are considered masterpieces of late medieval Serbian painting.

History

After Lazar's death at Kosovo, his relics were brought to Ravanica, making it a major pilgrimage site. The monastery suffered repeated Ottoman attacks and burnings, and its monks fled north in the Great Migration of 1690, carrying the prince's relics with them; the relics were eventually returned to Ravanica in 1988, on the eve of the 600th anniversary of the battle. Ravanica is protected as a Monument of Culture of Exceptional Importance in Serbia.
