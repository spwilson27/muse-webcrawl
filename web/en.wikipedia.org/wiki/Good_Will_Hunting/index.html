Good Will Hunting is a 1997 American drama film directed by Gus Van Sant and written by Ben Affleck and Matt Damon. It tells the story of Will Hunting, a 20-year-old janitor at MIT from South Boston whose unrecognized genius for mathematics is discovered by a professor, and whose court-ordered therapy with psychologist Sean Maguire forces him to confront the trauma of his abusive childhood.

Plot

Will anonymously solves graduate-level mathematics problems left on an MIT blackboard, drawing the attention of Professor Gerald Lambeau. After an assault charge, Lambeau arranges Will's release on condition that he study mathematics and attend therapy. After Will mocks a string of therapists, Lambeau turns to his former roommate Sean Maguire (Robin Williams), a widowed psychologist teaching at Bunker Hill Community College. Their combative sessions gradually break through Will's defenses, culminating in Sean's repeated insistence — "It's not your fault" — and Will's decision to follow his girlfriend Skylar to California.

Production and reception

Damon began the story as a Harvard playwriting exercise; he and Affleck expanded it into a screenplay that, after a circuitous path through studios, was made by Miramax for about $10 million. The film grossed over $225 million worldwide and received nine Academy Award nominations, winning Best Original Screenplay for Affleck and Damon and Best Supporting Actor for Williams. It launched its young writers' careers and remains noted for its Boston setting and the park-bench monologue in which Sean reflects on love and loss.
