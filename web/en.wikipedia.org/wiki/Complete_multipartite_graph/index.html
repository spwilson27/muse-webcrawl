Complete multipartite graph

In graph theory, a complete multipartite graph is a graph that is the complement of a disjoint union of cliques — equivalently, a graph whose vertices can be partitioned into independent sets (called parts) such that two vertices are adjacent if and only if they belong to different parts. A complete multipartite graph with part sizes n1, n2, …, nk is denoted Kn1,n2,…,nk.

Special cases

Complete multipartite graphs generalise several familiar families. A complete graph Kn is the case in which every part has exactly one vertex. A complete bipartite graph Ka,b has two parts; special complete bipartite graphs include the star graphs K1,n and the utility graph K3,3. The Turán graph T(n, r) — the complete r-partite graph on n vertices whose part sizes differ by at most one — is the extremal example in Turán's theorem, the unique graph with the maximum number of edges among n-vertex graphs containing no (r+1)-clique.

Properties and characterisations

Complete multipartite graphs can be characterised as the graphs containing no induced subgraph that is an independent set of size three together with an edge (the graph 2K1 + K2, i.e. they are the cluster graphs' complements), and equivalently as the graphs whose complement is a disjoint union of cliques. They are perfect graphs, and they can be recognised in linear time. The chromatic number of a complete multipartite graph equals the number of parts in its coarsest partition, and its clique number equals the number of parts as well when all parts are non-empty. Complete multipartite graphs arise naturally in the study of extremal graph theory, network design, and the modelling of relationships between distinct classes of objects.
