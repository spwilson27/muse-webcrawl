MacBook

The MacBook is a line of Mac laptop computers developed and sold by Apple Inc. The original MacBook was introduced in May 2006 as the successor to the iBook, as part of Apple's transition from PowerPC to Intel processors. It used a polycarbonate enclosure, initially in white and black, and was marketed to consumers and education users below the MacBook Pro tier.

The name has been applied to several distinct machines: the polycarbonate (later aluminium unibody) MacBook sold 2006–2012; the ultrathin 12-inch Retina MacBook sold 2015–2019; and, in current usage, as the umbrella term covering the MacBook Air and MacBook Pro ranges.

History of the original line

The first-generation MacBook combined Intel Core Duo processors, an integrated webcam ("iSight"), and a magnetic MagSafe power connector in a 13.3-inch format. Later revisions adopted Core 2 Duo processors, LED-backlit displays, and in 2008 an aluminium unibody design before Apple rebranded the 13-inch aluminium model into the MacBook Pro line in 2009, leaving a white polycarbonate model as the sole MacBook until its discontinuation in 2011 (with education sales continuing into 2012).

Retina MacBook (2015)

In March 2015 Apple unveiled a new 12-inch MacBook with a Retina display, a fanless Intel Core M design, USB-C as its single port, a Force Touch trackpad, and a redesigned butterfly-mechanism keyboard. It was discontinued in 2019 after generational updates, with its role assumed by the MacBook Air.
