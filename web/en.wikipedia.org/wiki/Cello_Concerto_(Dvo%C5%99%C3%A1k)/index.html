Cello Concerto (Dvořák)

Cello Concerto in B minor
Composer: Antonín Dvořák
Opus: 104, B. 191
Composed: 1894–95
Premiere: 19 March 1896, London

The Cello Concerto in B minor, Op. 104, by Antonín Dvořák is one of the most widely performed of all cello concertos. Dvořák wrote it in New York in the winter of 1894–95, near the end of his tenure as director of the National Conservatory, initially for the cellist Hanuš Wihan, though Leo Stern premiered it in London in 1896.

Music

The work has three movements: a broad Allegro, a lyrical Adagio ma non troppo that quotes Dvořák's song "Leave Me Alone" (a favourite of his sister-in-law Josefina, whose illness and death prompted a further quotation in the finale's coda), and a rondo-like Allegro moderato. Brahms is reported to have said that had he known such a cello concerto could be written, he would have tried to write one himself.
