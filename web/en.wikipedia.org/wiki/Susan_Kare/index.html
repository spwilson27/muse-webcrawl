Susan Kare

Susan Kare (born 1954) is an American artist and graphic designer best known for creating many of the interface graphics and typefaces for the original Apple Macintosh in the 1980s. Working on a grid of pixels, she designed the icons — including the trash can, the paint bucket, the "Happy Mac" startup face, and the Clarus the Dogcow figure — and fonts such as Chicago and Geneva that defined the early Macintosh user experience.

Career

Kare studied fine art at Mount Holyoke College and earned a PhD from New York University. She was recruited to Apple in 1983 through her high-school friend Andy Hertzfeld. After leaving Apple with Steve Jobs in 1985, she joined NeXT as its creative director. As an independent designer she has created icons and graphics for Microsoft (including the Windows 3.0 solitaire cards and numerous icons), IBM, Facebook, and many other clients. Her work is held in the collection of the Museum of Modern Art in New York, and she received the Cooper Hewitt National Design Award for lifetime achievement in 2019.
