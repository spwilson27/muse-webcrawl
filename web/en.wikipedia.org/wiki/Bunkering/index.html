Bunkering
Industry: Maritime transport
Activity: Supplying ships with fuel
Major hubs: Singapore, Rotterdam, Fujairah

Bunkering is the process of supplying fuel to a ship for its own use. The term derives from the coal "bunkers" of steamships, and today refers chiefly to the delivery of marine fuel oils, and increasingly liquefied natural gas and other alternative fuels, by bunker barges, pipelines, or trucks at ports.

Process and market

Fuel oil is stored in port tank farms and delivered to vessels alongside or at anchorage, with quantity measured by mass flow meters or tank soundings — measurement disputes have historically been common. Singapore is the world's largest bunkering port, selling on the order of 50 million tonnes of marine fuel annually, followed by hubs such as Rotterdam and Fujairah. Since 2020, IMO rules have capped the sulphur content of marine fuels at 0.5%, shifting demand from high-sulphur fuel oil to low-sulphur blends, while LNG, methanol, and ammonia bunkering are growing as the industry decarbonises.

Illegal bunkering

In some regions, notably Nigeria's Niger Delta, "bunkering" also denotes the illegal tapping of oil pipelines and theft of crude oil, a major source of pollution and lost revenue.
