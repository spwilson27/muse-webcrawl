Ibn Taymiyyah — an alternative transliteration of Ibn Taymiyya (1263–1328) — was a medieval Sunni scholar of the Hanbali school in Mamluk Damascus. The spelling "Taymiyyah", with a final h, is a common variant rendering of the Arabic name ابن تيمية, and works catalogued under either spelling refer to the same person.

Summary

Ibn Taymiyyah advocated strict adherence to the Qur'an and Sunna as interpreted by the earliest Muslims, opposed the veneration of saints and visits to tombs, attacked the Mongol invaders of Syria in fatwas that remain debated today, and was repeatedly imprisoned for his teachings. He died in the Citadel of Damascus in 1328. His writings, including the Majmu' al-Fatawa, went on to shape later Islamic reform movements, including the thought of his pupil Ibn Qayyim al-Jawziyya and, in the 18th century, Muhammad ibn Abd al-Wahhab.
