Chingaza National Park

Chingaza National Natural Park (Spanish: Parque Nacional Natural Chingaza) is a protected area in the Eastern Cordillera of the Colombian Andes, northeast of Bogotá, spanning the departments of Cundinamarca and Meta. Covering some 76,600 hectares at elevations from about 800 to 4,020 metres, it protects páramo and high Andean forest ecosystems that are a principal source of drinking water for Bogotá.

Ecology

Most of the park lies above 3,000 metres in páramo, a high-altitude grassland ecosystem characterised by frailejones (Espeletia), cushion plants and tussock grasses. The park contains dozens of glacial lakes, including Lake Chingaza and the Siecha Lakes, sacred to the pre-Columbian Muisca people, whose gold offerings at such lakes helped inspire the legend of El Dorado. Wildlife includes the spectacled bear, white-tailed deer, puma, Andean condor and numerous endemic birds and amphibians.

Water supply

Chingaza's wetlands and reservoirs form the core of the Chingaza System, which supplies roughly 80% of Bogotá's potable water. Because of this strategic role, access to much of the park is restricted, and visitor entry is controlled through authorised ecotourism operators and designated trails.

Conservation

Declared a national park in 1977 and a Ramsar wetland of international importance, Chingaza faces pressures from climate change affecting páramo hydrology, illegal hunting and encroachment at its lower margins. Restoration projects focus on native vegetation recovery in former agricultural areas.
