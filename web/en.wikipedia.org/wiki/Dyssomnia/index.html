Dyssomnia

Dyssomnia
SpecialtySleep medicine; psychiatry
ExamplesInsomnia, hypersomnia, narcolepsy, sleep apnea, circadian rhythm disorders

Dyssomnias are a broad class of sleep disorders involving difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or excessive sleepiness — that is, disturbances in the amount, quality or timing of sleep. They contrast with parasomnias, which involve abnormal behaviours during sleep such as sleepwalking.

Classification

Traditional classification divides dyssomnias into intrinsic disorders (arising from within the body, such as insomnia, narcolepsy, obstructive sleep apnea and restless legs syndrome), extrinsic disorders (caused by external factors such as poor sleep hygiene or environmental disturbance), and circadian rhythm sleep disorders (such as delayed sleep phase disorder, jet lag and shift-work disorder). Modern classifications, including the International Classification of Sleep Disorders, organise these conditions somewhat differently.

Diagnosis and treatment

Assessment uses sleep history, diaries and sometimes polysomnography or actigraphy. Treatment targets the specific disorder: cognitive behavioural therapy for insomnia (CBT-I), continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) for sleep apnea, light therapy and scheduling for circadian disorders, and medication in selected cases.
