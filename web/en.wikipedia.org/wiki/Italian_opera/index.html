Italian opera

Italian opera is both the art of opera in Italy and opera in the Italian language. Opera originated in Italy around 1600, in the circle of the Florentine Camerata, with works such as Jacopo Peri's Dafne (1598) and Euridice (1600), and Claudio Monteverdi's L'Orfeo (1607).

Development

Venice opened the first public opera houses in the 1630s. In the eighteenth century opera seria dominated serious stages while opera buffa developed a lighter comic style. The nineteenth century brought the bel canto of Rossini, Bellini and Donizetti, then the towering works of Giuseppe Verdi, and at the century's end the verismo of Mascagni, Leoncavallo and Puccini, whose La bohème, Tosca and Madama Butterfly remain core repertory.

Influence

Italian conventions — recitative and aria, the libretto, terms such as soprano, tempo and crescendo — shaped opera worldwide, and Italian remains the traditional language of musical terminology.
