Tekezé River

Countries: Ethiopia, Eritrea, Sudan
Length: approx. 608 km (378 mi)
Mouth: Atbarah River (as the Setit in Sudan)

The Tekezé River (Amharic: ተከዜ; also spelled Tekeze, Italian: Tacazzè) is a major river of Ethiopia and a tributary of the Nile. For part of its course it forms the westernmost border between Ethiopia and Eritrea, and below the tripoint with Sudan it is known in Sudan as the Setit before joining the Atbarah River.

Course

The Tekezé rises in the Ethiopian Highlands near Mount Qachen in the Lasta region, at over 3,000 metres elevation, and flows west and north through a deep canyon — in places the gorge is more than 2,000 metres deep, among the deepest river canyons in Africa. The river is about 608 km long, and its principal tributaries include the Tahali, Meri, Tellare, Sullo, Arequa, Gheoa and Wari rivers.

Flows are highly seasonal, swelling greatly during the summer kiremt rains. Historically the river was dangerous to cross in flood and formed a natural frontier, a role reflected in its role in the Ethiopian–Eritrean border demarcation following the war of 1998–2000.

Development

The Tekezé Hydroelectric Power Plant, with a double-curvature arch dam 188 metres high (the tallest in Africa at completion in 2009) and an installed capacity of 300 MW, is a major Ethiopian power project on the river. The dam's reservoir also supports irrigation development in the surrounding lowlands.
