Kaliningrad Sea Canal

Location: Kaliningrad Oblast, Russia
Length: 43 km (27 mi)
Construction began: 1901
Completed: 15 November 1927

The Kaliningrad Sea Canal (Russian: Калининградский морской судоходный канал) is a dredged channel, formerly known in German as the Königsberger Seekanal, that allows ships to reach the ports of Kaliningrad, Russia, from the Baltic Sea. The canal runs through the northern part of the Vistula Lagoon (Frisches Haff) from the strait at Baltiysk (Pillau) to the mouth of the Pregolya river and the port of Kaliningrad.

History

Before the canal was built, larger ships bound for Königsberg had to lighten their loads at Pillau because of the shallow waters of the lagoon. Construction of the canal began in 1901, and its first stage was completed that same year, initially allowing vessels with a draught of about 6.5 metres to pass. The channel was progressively deepened, and the completed canal was formally opened on 15 November 1927.

After the Second World War, northern East Prussia was transferred to the Soviet Union, Königsberg was renamed Kaliningrad, and the canal passed to Soviet, then Russian, administration. It remains the only sea access for the ports of Kaliningrad and Baltiysk.

Characteristics

The canal is approximately 43 kilometres (27 miles) long, with a maintained depth of about 9–10.5 metres. Much of its length is a single-lane channel protected by embankments through the shallow lagoon, so traffic is regulated and vessels pass at designated wider sections. An important feature on the canal is the Mamonovo–Vistula Lagoon shore and the small artificial islands built from dredged material. Navigation is possible year-round, though icebreakers may be needed in severe winters.
