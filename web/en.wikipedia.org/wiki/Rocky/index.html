Rocky is a 1976 American sports drama film directed by John G. Avildsen and written by and starring Sylvester Stallone. It tells the story of Rocky Balboa, an uneducated but kind-hearted debt collector and small-time club boxer in Philadelphia, who gets a once-in-a-lifetime shot at the world heavyweight championship held by Apollo Creed (Carl Weathers). The film also stars Talia Shire as Rocky's love interest Adrian, Burt Young as her brother Paulie, and Burgess Meredith as trainer Mickey Goldmill.

Production

Stallone wrote the screenplay in about three days, inspired by the 1975 title fight between Muhammad Ali and underdog Chuck Wepner. He refused to sell the script unless he could star, despite being nearly broke. Produced on a budget of roughly $1 million and shot in 28 days, largely in Philadelphia, the film made pioneering use of the Steadicam for the famous training run up the steps of the Philadelphia Museum of Art — the "Rocky Steps."

Reception

Rocky was a sleeper hit and the highest-grossing film of 1976, earning about $225 million worldwide. It received ten Academy Award nominations and won three, including Best Picture and Best Director, and launched one of the most successful film franchises of all time: five sequels written by and starring Stallone (1979–1990), the 2006 coda Rocky Balboa, and the Creed spin-off series beginning in 2015. Bill Conti's score and its theme "Gonna Fly Now" became iconic.

Legacy

The film is credited with establishing the modern sports-underdog template and with reshaping Stallone's career. A bronze statue of Rocky, commissioned by Stallone for Rocky III, stands near the bottom of the museum steps in Philadelphia, and the character was inducted into the International Boxing Hall of Fame's observer category. The film is preserved in the US National Film Registry.
