Chagas disease

Chagas disease, also known as American trypanosomiasis, is a tropical parasitic disease caused by the protozoan Trypanosoma cruzi. It is spread mostly by insects in the subfamily Triatominae, known as "kissing bugs", which feed on blood and deposit infected faeces near the bite. The disease is named after the Brazilian physician Carlos Chagas, who discovered it in 1909.

Course of disease

The acute phase lasts weeks to months and often causes few symptoms beyond fever, swelling at the infection site (a chagoma) or the characteristic one-sided eyelid swelling called Romaña's sign. Parasites then enter a long chronic indeterminate phase. In roughly 30 percent of infected people, chronic disease develops years or decades later, most seriously as Chagas cardiomyopathy — heart failure, arrhythmias and sudden death — and in some as digestive complications such as megaesophagus and megacolon.

Transmission

Besides the vector route, T. cruzi can be transmitted congenitally, through blood transfusion or organ transplantation, and by ingestion of food or drink contaminated with infected bug faeces, a route responsible for acute outbreaks in the Amazon. Screening of blood donations has sharply reduced transfusion transmission.

Diagnosis and treatment

Acute infection is diagnosed by detecting parasites in blood; chronic infection relies on serological tests. The antiparasitic drugs benznidazole and nifurtimox are effective in acute and early chronic infection, less so later, though treatment is increasingly offered to chronically infected adults. There is no vaccine. Prevention rests on insecticide spraying of houses, improved housing, and screening of blood and pregnant women. Chagas disease is classified by the WHO as a neglected tropical disease.

History

Carlos Chagas identified the parasite, its vector and the disease in 1909 in Minas Gerais, Brazil — an unmatched feat in which a single researcher described a complete new disease cycle. Charles Darwin's chronic ill health after his Beagle voyage, during which he recorded being bitten by triatomine bugs in Argentina, has led to retrospective speculation that he suffered from Chagas disease.
