GameOver Zeus


GameOver Zeus was a peer-to-peer banking trojan and botnet, disrupted in 2014.

GameOver Zeus (GOZ) was a variant of the Zeus banking trojan that emerged around 2011. Unlike the original Zeus, which communicated with centralized command-and-control servers, GameOver Zeus used a peer-to-peer botnet architecture, making it substantially harder to take down, since infected machines could receive commands and updates from each other without contacting a single server.

Operation

The malware stole banking credentials and other sensitive information using keystroke logging and form grabbing, and was used to commit fraudulent wire transfers. The U.S. Department of Justice alleged that the botnet infected between 500,000 and one million computers and was responsible for more than $100 million in losses. GameOver Zeus was also used to distribute the CryptoLocker ransomware.

Takedown

In June 2014, an international law-enforcement and industry effort — Operation Tovar — seized servers and disrupted the botnet's peer-to-peer network, temporarily knocking both GameOver Zeus and CryptoLocker offline. The U.S. charged Russian national Evgeniy Bogachev as the alleged administrator of the botnet; the FBI offered a reward of $3 million for information leading to his arrest, at the time its largest reward for a cybercriminal. Attempts to rebuild the botnet appeared within weeks of the takedown. See also botnet.
