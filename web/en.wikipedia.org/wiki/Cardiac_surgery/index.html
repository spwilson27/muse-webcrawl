Cardiac surgery

Cardiac surgery, or cardiovascular surgery, is surgery on the heart or great vessels performed by cardiac surgeons. It treats coronary artery disease, heart valve disease, congenital heart defects, heart failure and aortic disease.

Common proceduresCoronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), valve repair/replacement, heart transplantation
Key technologyCardiopulmonary bypass

History

Successful heart surgery began in the late 19th and early 20th centuries; Ludwig Rehn repaired a heart wound in 1896. The heart–lung machine, developed by John Gibbon (first successful use 1953), enabled open-heart surgery. Christiaan Barnard performed the first human heart transplant in 1967.

Procedures

CABG reroutes blood around blocked coronary arteries using vein or artery grafts. Valve surgery repairs or replaces mitral and aortic valves, increasingly with minimally invasive and transcatheter (TAVR) approaches. Other operations include congenital defect repair, aortic aneurysm repair, surgery for arrhythmias, and ventricular assist devices.

Risks and outcomes

Risks include bleeding, stroke, infection and arrhythmia; outcomes depend on patient condition and centre experience. Postoperative care occurs in intensive care with cardiac rehabilitation. See Coronary artery bypass surgery.
