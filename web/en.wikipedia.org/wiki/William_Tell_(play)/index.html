William Tell (play)

William Tell (German: Wilhelm Tell) is a drama written by Friedrich Schiller in 1804, dramatising the legendary Swiss folk hero's struggle against Austrian tyranny.

Author: Friedrich Schiller
Written: 1804
Premiere: Weimar, 1804
Subject: Swiss independence legend

Plot

Marksman William Tell refuses to bow to the hat of bailiff Gessler, is forced to shoot an apple off his son's head, escapes, and kills Gessler — sparking the Swiss cantons' revolt, sworn in the Rütli Oath.

Influence

Schiller's last completed play became a Swiss national drama and a universal symbol of resistance to tyranny. Rossini's opera Guillaume Tell (1829) adapted it. See William Tell and Friedrich Schiller.
