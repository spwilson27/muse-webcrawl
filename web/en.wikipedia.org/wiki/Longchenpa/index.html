Longchen Rabjam Drimé Özer (Tibetan: ᚲᛞᚱᙬᚹ᚞ᚺᛞᚺᙬᚱ, 1308–1364), commonly known as Longchenpa, was a Tibetan Buddhist scholar and yogi of the Nyingma school. He is regarded as one of the greatest systematizers of Dzogchen ("Great Perfection") thought and a key figure in transmitting the Nyingma teachings.

Life and works

Longchenpa studied at Sangphu monastery and with many masters, most importantly the Dzogchen teacher Kumaradza, from whom he received the Vima Nyingtik transmission. He spent long periods in retreat, including years of exile in Bhutan, where he founded monasteries such as Tharpaling. Persecution by political rivals late in his life forced him to flee Tibet shortly before his death in 1364.

His enormous literary output — over 250 works — includes the Seven Treasuries (Dzödün), commentaries on the Guhyagarbha Tantra, and the Trilogy of Rest. He compiled and arranged the Nyingma Gyubum (the collected tantras of the Nyingma school) and systematised the Heart Essence (Nyingtik) teachings, profoundly shaping all later Nyingma practice.
