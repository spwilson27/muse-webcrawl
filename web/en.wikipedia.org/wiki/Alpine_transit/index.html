Alpine transit

Alpine transit refers to the movement of passengers and freight across the Alps, the mountain range that forms a natural barrier between northern and southern Europe. Because a small number of passes and tunnels carry enormous volumes of traffic — in particular north–south trade between Italy and the industrial regions of Germany, France and the Benelux — Alpine transit has long been a major political, environmental and engineering issue, especially in Switzerland and Austria.

Principal crossings

The most important transalpine corridors are:

  • The Brenner Pass (Austria–Italy), the busiest freight crossing of the Alps, used by well over two million trucks per year at its peak.
  • The Gotthard axis (Switzerland–Italy), historically served by the 1882 Gotthard railway tunnel and the 1980 road tunnel, and since 2016 by the Gotthard Base Tunnel, at 57 km the world's longest railway tunnel.
  • The Mont Cenis / Fréjus route (France–Italy), with the planned Lyon–Turin base tunnel.
  • The Simplon and Lötschberg routes in Switzerland, the Tauern route in Austria, and the Mont Blanc tunnel between France and Italy.

Modal shift policy

Switzerland, through which much transit traffic passes without Swiss origin or destination, adopted a constitutional "Alpine Initiative" in 1994 requiring transalpine freight to be transferred from road to rail. To implement this, the country built the New Railway Link through the Alps (NRLA/NEAT): the Lötschberg Base Tunnel (opened 2007), the Gotthard Base Tunnel (2016) and the Ceneri Base Tunnel (2020), financed in part by a distance-based heavy vehicle fee (LSVA/HVF) charged to trucks. Switzerland also negotiated a "rolling highway" and combined-transport subsidies, and set a target of cutting truck crossings drastically, though rail's share of transalpine freight through Switzerland (around 70% or more) remains far above that of France or Austria.

Environmental concerns

Alpine valleys concentrate noise and air pollution from traffic: temperature inversions trap exhaust gases, and pollutant concentrations in narrow valleys such as the Inn and the upper Rhône valleys can exceed those of large cities. Road transit was implicated in forest dieback debates of the 1980s and remains politically sensitive. The EU and Alpine states coordinate through the Alpine Convention and the EU's Trans-European Transport Networks (TEN-T) programme, which co-finances base tunnels such as the Brenner Base Tunnel (under construction between Austria and Italy).
