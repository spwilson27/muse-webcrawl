The United States Department of Energy (DOE) is an executive department of the U.S. federal government that oversees U.S. national energy policy and energy production, the research and development of nuclear power, the military's nuclear weapons program, nuclear reactor production for the United States Navy, energy-related research, and domestic energy production and energy conservation.

The department was established by the Department of Energy Organization Act, signed by President Jimmy Carter in 1977 in response to the 1973 oil crisis, consolidating the Federal Energy Administration, the Energy Research and Development Administration and other agencies. The first Secretary of Energy was James R. Schlesinger.

Organization

The DOE is headed by the Secretary of Energy, a cabinet-level position. Major components include the National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA), which manages the nation's nuclear weapons stockpile; the Office of Science, the largest single supporter of basic research in the physical sciences in the United States; and the Energy Information Administration, which collects energy statistics.

National laboratories

The department operates a system of 17 national laboratories and technology centers, including Lawrence Livermore, Los Alamos, Oak Ridge, Sandia, Argonne and Fermilab. Much of the department's budget is devoted to the nuclear weapons complex and to environmental cleanup of Cold War-era sites such as Hanford.

History

The DOE's origins lie in the Manhattan Project and its successor, the Atomic Energy Commission (1946). Energy functions passed through the Atomic Energy Commission, the Energy Research and Development Administration (1975) and the Federal Energy Administration before consolidation in 1977. The department played a central role in the Human Genome Project through its Office of Science.
