Vitamin deficiency

Vitamin deficiency is the condition of a long-term lack of a vitamin. When caused by insufficient dietary intake it is a primary deficiency; when due to an underlying disorder such as malabsorption it is a secondary deficiency. Deficiency of a single vitamin produces characteristic diseases, many of which were historically major public-health scourges. The general term for vitamin deficiency is avitaminosis or, for insufficient (rather than absent) intake, hypovitaminosis.

Causes and prevalence

Causes include poverty and food insecurity, monotonous diets (e.g., polished white rice and beriberi; maize-based diets and pellagra), restrictive eating, alcoholism, malabsorptive diseases (coeliac disease, cystic fibrosis, bariatric surgery), and increased requirements in pregnancy. Vitamin D deficiency is widespread at northern latitudes because of limited sunlight. Globally, vitamin A deficiency remains a leading preventable cause of childhood blindness, and folate deficiency is targeted by flour fortification programmes. Diagnosis is by diet history, clinical signs, and blood levels; treatment is supplementation and dietary correction. Excess intake of some vitamins (notably A and D) is itself toxic — hypervitaminosis.
