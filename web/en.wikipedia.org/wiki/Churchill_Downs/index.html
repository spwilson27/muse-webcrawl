Churchill Downs

Churchill Downs is a horse racing complex located on Central Avenue in south Louisville, Kentucky, United States. It is famed for hosting the annual Kentucky Derby, the first leg of the American Triple Crown. The racetrack is named for John and Henry Churchill, who leased the land to its founder, Colonel Meriwether Lewis Clark Jr., grandson of explorer William Clark.

History

The track formally opened on May 17, 1875, when it hosted the first Kentucky Derby, won by Aristides. The famous twin spires atop the grandstand, designed by architect Joseph Dominic Baldez and added in 1895, have become the universally recognized symbol of the racetrack and the Derby. The facility covers 175 acres and normally can accommodate up to 50,000 spectators, though crowds for the Derby can exceed 150,000.

Facilities and events

In addition to the spring and fall race meets, Churchill Downs hosts the Kentucky Oaks for fillies on the Friday before the Derby. The track has hosted the Breeders' Cup on multiple occasions. In 2005 a $121 million renovation modernized the clubhouse and added luxury suites. A bronze statue of 1875 Derby winner Aristides stands in the garden, and the Kentucky Derby Museum is located on the grounds.
