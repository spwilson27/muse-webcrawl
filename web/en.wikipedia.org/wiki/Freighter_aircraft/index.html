Cargo aircraft

Freighter aircraft
RoleTransport of freight rather than passengers
Typical featuresLarge cargo doors, reinforced floor, few or no windows, high wing on military types
Major typesBoeing 747F, 777F; Antonov An-124; Lockheed C-130 (military)

A cargo aircraft (also known as a freight aircraft, freighter, airlifter or cargo jet) is a fixed-wing aircraft designed or converted for the carriage of cargo rather than passengers. Such aircraft usually have one or more large doors for loading cargo, a wide fuselage cross-section, numerous wheels for soft-field operation (in military types), a high-mounted wing to keep the cargo floor low, and few or no cabin windows.

Types

Dedicated freighters

Aircraft designed from the outset for freight, such as the Boeing 747-8F and Boeing 777F, feature a strengthened main-deck floor, roller conveyors and large side or nose cargo doors. The 747 freighter's hinged nose allows drive-on loading of long items. Historically, the Antonov An-225 Mriya — destroyed in 2022 during the Russian invasion of Ukraine — was the largest cargo aircraft ever operated, and its smaller sibling, the An-124 Ruslan, remains a mainstay of outsize cargo charter.

Converted freighters

Many freighters are retired passenger aircraft converted by removing seats and cabin fittings, cutting a main-deck cargo door and reinforcing the floor ("P2F" — passenger-to-freighter conversions, e.g. 737-800BCF, A321P2F). Conversions extend airframe life at lower capital cost than new-build freighters.

Combi and belly cargo

"Combi" aircraft carry passengers and main-deck cargo behind a partition. Ordinary passenger airliners also carry substantial freight in their lower holds ("belly cargo"), which in normal years accounts for roughly half of world air freight by weight.

Operations

Freighters are flown by all-cargo airlines (such as FedEx Express, UPS Airlines and Cargolux), combination carriers, charter operators and air forces. Cargo is typically containerised in unit load devices (ULDs) shaped to the fuselage. Air freight favours high-value, perishable or time-sensitive goods; express integrators run hub-and-spoke overnight networks.
