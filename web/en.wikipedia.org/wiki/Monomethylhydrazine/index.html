Monomethylhydrazine

Monomethylhydrazine (MMH) is a highly toxic, volatile hydrazine chemical with the formula CH3NHNH2. It is a clear liquid with an ammonia-like odor. MMH is widely used as a rocket propellant: it is hypergolic with oxidizers such as nitrogen tetroxide and red fuming nitric acid, igniting spontaneously on contact, and is stable enough to be stored in a regeneratively cooled engine. It was used in the Space Shuttle's orbital maneuvering system, in spacecraft thrusters, and in missile systems, and in blends such as Aerozine 50 with unsymmetrical dimethylhydrazine (UDMH).

MMH is extremely hazardous: it is flammable, corrosive, absorbed through the skin, and a suspected carcinogen; exposure can cause convulsions and damage to the liver, kidneys, and central nervous system. The compound also occurs naturally, in modified forms, as the toxin gyromitrin in false morel mushrooms, which hydrolyzes to MMH in the body.
