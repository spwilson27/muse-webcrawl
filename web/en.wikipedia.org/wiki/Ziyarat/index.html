Ziyarat (Arabic: زيارة, romanized: ziyāra, lit. 'visitation') is a form of pilgrimage to sites associated with the Islamic prophet Muhammad, his family members and descendants, his companions, or other venerated figures in Islam such as prophets, Sufi saints and Islamic scholars. Sites of pilgrimage include mosques, maqams, battlefields, mountains, and caves. Ziyarat is distinct from the Hajj, the obligatory pilgrimage to Mecca, and is a voluntary act of devotion undertaken to seek blessings and express reverence.

Practice

Pilgrims visiting a tomb or shrine typically recite prescribed supplications and texts — also called ziyarat — greet the buried figure as if alive, pray, recite Qur'anic verses, and ask for the figure's intercession. In Shia Islam, ziyarat to the shrines of the Imams is elaborately developed, with collections of visitation liturgies compiled in works such as Ibn Qulawayh's Kamil al-Ziyarat. The Arba'in pilgrimage to Karbala, commemorating the martyrdom of Husayn ibn Ali, draws tens of millions of participants and is among the largest annual gatherings in the world.

Major sites

  • Medina — the Prophet's Mosque and Muhammad's tomb; also the Baqi cemetery.
  • Karbala — shrines of Husayn ibn Ali and Abbas ibn Ali.
  • Najaf — shrine of Ali ibn Abi Talib.
  • Mashhad — shrine of Imam Reza, the eighth Shia Imam.
  • Qom — shrine of Fatimah bint Musa.
  • Damascus — Sayyidah Zaynab Mosque and other shrines.
  • Jerusalem — Al-Aqsa Mosque and the Dome of the Rock.

Views and debates

While ziyarat is embraced by Shia Muslims and many Sunnis, particularly within Sufi traditions, some movements — notably Salafi and Wahhabi schools — regard visitation practices involving supplication at graves as impermissible innovation or even polytheism, citing hadith against taking graves as places of worship. This disagreement has historically led to the destruction of shrines in areas under such movements' control.
