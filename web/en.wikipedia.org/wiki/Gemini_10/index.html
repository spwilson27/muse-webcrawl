Gemini 10

Gemini 10 (officially Gemini X) — 1966 crewed spaceflight, Project Gemini. Crew: John W. Young (command pilot), Michael Collins (pilot). Launched 18 July 1966; landed 21 July 1966. Duration: 2 days, 22 hours, 46 minutes.

Gemini 10 (officially Gemini X) was the eighth crewed spaceflight in NASA's Gemini program, launched in July 1966. It was commanded by John Young with Michael Collins as pilot. The mission achieved rendezvous and docking with an Agena target vehicle, used the Agena's engine to reach a record altitude, and included two spacewalks by Collins.

Objectives and flight

The primary objectives were to rendezvous and dock with the Gemini Agena Target Vehicle (GATV-10), to use its propulsion system to maneuver, and to conduct extravehicular activity (EVA). Gemini 10 docked with its Agena about five and a half hours after launch, then fired the Agena's main engine to raise the apogee to roughly 764 km (475 miles), a new crewed altitude record at the time. The crew later rendezvoused with the passive Agena left over from Gemini 8.

Spacewalks

Collins performed two EVAs. During the second, he traversed to the Gemini 8 Agena using a hand-held maneuvering unit to retrieve a micrometeorite collection package — one of the earliest successful spacewalk transfers between vehicles, though he lost a camera in the process.

Splashdown

The spacecraft landed in the Atlantic Ocean on 21 July 1966, close to the recovery ship USS Guadalcanal, completing a highly successful mission that demonstrated the rendezvous, docking, and high-altitude operations needed for Apollo.
