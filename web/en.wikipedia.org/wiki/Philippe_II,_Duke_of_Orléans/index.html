Philippe II, Duke of Orléans

Born: August 2, 1674, Saint-Cloud, France
Died: December 2, 1723, Versailles, France
Office: Regent of France (1715–1723)
Father: Philippe I, Duke of Orléans
Mother: Elizabeth Charlotte of the Palatinate

Philippe d'Orléans governed France as regent during the minority of Louis XV.

Philippe II, Duke of Orléans (Philippe Charles d'Orléans; 2 August 1674 – 2 December 1723) was a French prince, soldier, and statesman who served as Regent of the Kingdom of France from 1715 until 1723, during the minority of his grand-nephew Louis XV. The period is known as the Régence. He was the nephew of Louis XIV and the son of Philippe I, Duke of Orléans and Elizabeth Charlotte of the Palatinate.

Early life and military career

Philippe distinguished himself as a cavalry commander in the Nine Years' War, fighting at Steenkerque and Neerwinden, and later commanded French forces in Italy and Spain during the War of the Spanish Succession. A talented amateur of the arts and sciences, he was also dogged by scandalous rumours at court, including wild — and unfounded — accusations connected with the deaths of other princes in 1711–1712.

The Régence

On Louis XIV's death in September 1715, Philippe had the late king's will set aside by the Parlement of Paris and secured full regency powers. He reversed many of Louis XIV's policies: he replaced ministerial government with aristocratic councils (the Polysynody), eased religious persecution of Protestants and Jansenists, and began his rule from the Palais-Royal in Paris. His ministry is remembered for the financial experiments of John Law, whose Mississippi Company and paper-money system collapsed spectacularly in 1720, and for the foreign policy of his adviser Cardinal Dubois. The regency ended formally with Louis XV's majority in February 1723; Philippe became chief minister but died in December of that year.
