Jonathan (1 Samuel)

Jonathan (Hebrew: יְהוֹנָתָן / יוֹנָתָן, "Yahweh has given") is a figure in the Hebrew Bible, described in the Books of Samuel as the eldest son of King Saul and a close friend of David. Renowned for his courage, loyalty, and military skill, Jonathan is remembered above all for his covenant friendship with David, whom he defended against his own father's attempts on David's life, even though David's rise meant Jonathan would not succeed to the throne.

Exploits against the Philistines

Jonathan first appears leading a thousand men and striking a Philistine garrison at Geba (1 Samuel 13). His most celebrated exploit followed at Michmash, where, accompanied only by his armour-bearer, he climbed to a Philistine outpost and killed about twenty men, triggering panic in the Philistine camp and a rout of their army. Saul nearly executed Jonathan for unwittingly violating a fast Saul had imposed, but the army ransomed him because of the victory he had won.

Friendship with David

After David's victory over Goliath, "the soul of Jonathan was knit with the soul of David, and Jonathan loved him as his own soul" (1 Samuel 18:1). Jonathan gave David his robe, sword, bow, and belt, and made a covenant with him. When Saul repeatedly sought David's life, Jonathan warned him, interceded with Saul, and finally met David in the wilderness of Ziph, where, shortly before his death, he acknowledged that David would be king and that he himself would stand next to him.

Death

Jonathan died alongside his father Saul and his brothers Abinadab and Malchishua at the Battle of Mount Gilboa, fighting the Philistines. David lamented them in the "Song of the Bow" (2 Samuel 1), declaring of Jonathan: "thy love to me was wonderful, passing the love of women." Jonathan's young son Mephibosheth was later received at David's table for his father's sake.
