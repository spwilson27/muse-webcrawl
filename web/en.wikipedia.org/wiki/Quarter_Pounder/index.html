The Quarter Pounder is a hamburger sold by the international fast-food chain McDonald's, first introduced in 1971. It is named for its beef patty, which weighed a quarter of a pound (4 ounces, about 113 grams) before cooking. The sandwich typically consists of the patty with cheese, onions, pickles, ketchup, and mustard in a sesame-seed bun.

History

The Quarter Pounder was created in 1971 by Al Bernardin, a McDonald's franchisee in Fremont, California, who wanted a burger for adult appetites; its slogan ran "Today Fremont, tomorrow the world". It rolled out across the United States in 1973 and became one of the chain's core products alongside the Big Mac. In 2015 McDonald's increased the pre-cooked patty weight to 4.25 ounces, and in 2018 it switched the North American Quarter Pounder to fresh, never-frozen beef cooked when ordered.

Name in metric countries

In most of the world the burger keeps its imperial name, but in France it is famously sold as the Royal Cheese — a quirk immortalised in a dialogue scene in Quentin Tarantino's 1994 film Pulp Fiction, in which the characters discuss what a Quarter Pounder with Cheese is called in Paris. In some markets it has also been sold under names such as "McRoyal".

2024 E. coli outbreak

In October 2024, a US outbreak of E. coli O157:H7 linked to Quarter Pounders sickened over one hundred people in several states, with one death; investigators traced the likely source to slivered onions supplied to the chain, and the sandwich was temporarily withdrawn in affected regions.
