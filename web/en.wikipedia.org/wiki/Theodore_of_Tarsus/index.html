Theodore of Tarsus (c. 602 – 19 September 690) was a Byzantine Greek monk who became the seventh Archbishop of Canterbury. Sent to England by Pope Vitalian at the age of about 66, he reorganized the English Church, filled its vacant sees, and presided over a notable revival of learning; the historian Bede praised the era such that "never had there been such happy times" since the English came to Britain.

Life

Theodore was born in Tarsus in Cilicia and educated in Constantinople and probably Athens, acquiring learning in Greek and Latin literature, medicine, astronomy, and law before fleeing westward from the Arab conquests to Rome, where he lived as a monk. When the see of Canterbury fell vacant and the first appointee, Wigheard, died of plague, Pope Vitalian chose Theodore, who was consecrated in March 668 — first having to wait four months for his hair to grow so he could receive the Roman tonsure. He reached Canterbury in May 669, accompanied by the African abbot Hadrian.

Archbishopric

Theodore conducted a visitation of the whole English Church, deposed or confirmed bishops, divided oversized dioceses, and summoned the first general synod of the English Church at Hertford (672), which established rules on episcopal authority, monastic discipline, and the calculation of Easter. With Hadrian he ran a celebrated school at Canterbury whose pupils learned Greek and Latin, metrics, astronomy, and computus; glosses from its teaching survive as the Canterbury biblical commentaries. He died in 690 and was buried at St Augustine's Abbey, Canterbury.
