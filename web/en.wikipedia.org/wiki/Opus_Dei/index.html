Opus Dei

Opus Dei (Latin for "Work of God"), formally the Prelature of the Holy Cross and Opus Dei, is an institution of the Catholic Church founded in Madrid in 1928 by Saint Josemaría Escrivá. It teaches that ordinary life is a path toward holiness and that laypeople are called to sanctify their everyday work and activities.

In 1982 Pope John Paul II made Opus Dei a personal prelature, a structure governed by a prelate and comprising lay faithful and priests. Its members include numeraries, who typically live celibately in centres, supernumeraries, who usually live with families, and associates, along with diocesan priests of the Priestly Society of the Holy Cross.

Opus Dei runs schools, universities, residences and social projects in many countries. It has also attracted criticism and controversy, including accusations of secrecy and elitism, which the organisation disputes; its portrayal in popular fiction has further shaped public perceptions.
