The Tri-Cities are three closely linked cities — Kennewick, Pasco, and Richland — in the southeastern part of the U.S. state of Washington, at the confluence of the Yakima, Snake, and Columbia Rivers in the Columbia Basin. Together they form the Tri-Cities metropolitan statistical area, one of the fastest-growing metro areas in the state.

Geography and economy

The cities occupy both banks of the Columbia River: Kennewick and Pasco face each other across the river (joined by the Cable Bridge and the Blue Bridge), while Richland lies upstream at the bend where the Yakima River joins. The region's economy was transformed by the Hanford Site, established in 1943 as part of the Manhattan Project, which made Richland a government-built atomic city and left a major ongoing environmental cleanup mission. The Pacific Northwest National Laboratory is headquartered in Richland. Agriculture and food processing — potatoes, apples, wheat and wine grapes — are also major industries; the surrounding Columbia Valley is a notable wine region.

Culture and sport

The three cities share institutions such as the Tri-Cities Airport (in Pasco) and the Toyota Center arena in Kennewick. Local sports teams include the Tri-City Americans of the Western Hockey League and the Tri-City Dust Devils baseball team. Lewis and Clark's Corps of Discovery camped at the confluence of the Snake and Columbia in October 1805, commemorated at Sacagawea Heritage Trail and Sacajawea State Park.
