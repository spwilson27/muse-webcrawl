Wilhelm von Schadow

Born: September 6, 1788, Berlin

Died: March 19, 1862, Düsseldorf

Movement: Nazarene movement, Düsseldorf school

Friedrich Wilhelm von Schadow (September 6, 1788 – March 19, 1862) was a German Romantic painter. He was the son of the sculptor Johann Gottfried Schadow, and brother of the sculptor Rudolf Schadow.

Schadow studied at the Prussian Academy of Arts in Berlin and served as a soldier during the Napoleonic Wars. In 1811–1817 he lived in Rome, where he joined the Nazarene movement — a group of German painters seeking to revive the spirituality and technique of early Renaissance art — and converted to Catholicism, a step reflected in his many religious subjects.

Düsseldorf

Returning to Berlin, he founded a successful teaching studio, and in 1826 he became director of the Kunstakademie Düsseldorf, which under his leadership became one of the most influential art schools in Europe and America. Schadow emphasised master-class teaching and the study of nature alongside idealised composition; his pupils included many painters of the Düsseldorf school of painting, a style that profoundly influenced the American Hudson River School through students such as Worthington Whittredge and Emanuel Leutze.

His own works include altarpieces, portraits — notably a celebrated portrait of his sister-in-law and of Felix Mendelssohn — and large religious compositions. He was ennobled in 1843 and became Wilhelm von Schadow.
