The Three Sisters (Ireland)

The Three Sisters (Irish: Na Trí Siúracha) is the name given in south-east Ireland to the three rivers the Barrow, the Nore and the Suir. The three rivers rise in the same general area — the Slieve Bloom Mountains and Devil's Bit in County Tipperary — and flow southwards through counties including Kilkenny, Carlow, Wexford and Waterford, entering the sea together at Waterford Harbour.

The rivers

The River Suir, the longest of the three, rises on Devil's Bit Mountain and flows past Thurles, Cahir, Clonmel and Waterford. The River Nore passes Durrow and Kilkenny city before joining the Barrow. The River Barrow, Ireland's second-longest river, flows through Athy, Carlow and New Ross. The Nore joins the Barrow north of New Ross, and the combined flow meets the Suir at Waterford Harbour.

Significance

The three rivers drain much of Ireland's south-east and have been navigable arteries, fishery resources (notably for salmon) and boundaries since early times; the region they water is sometimes marketed as the "sunny south east". A long-distance walking route, the Barrow Way, follows the Barrow towpath.
