Col de Turini

The hairpin bends of the Col de Turini, famous from the Monte Carlo Rally.

The Col de Turini is a high mountain pass in the Maritime Alps of south-eastern France, in the Alpes-Maritimes department near the Italian border. At an elevation of about 1,607 metres (5,272 ft), the pass links the villages of Moulinet and La Bollène-Vésubie and is crossed by the D2566 road.

Monte Carlo Rally

The pass is legendary as a special stage of the Monte Carlo Rally, traditionally run at night—the famous "Night of the Long Knives"—when thousands of spectators line the road. The stage from Sospel over the Turini is one of the most technical in rallying, with dozens of hairpin bends and road conditions that can change from dry asphalt to ice and snow within a few kilometres.

Cycling

The climb has occasionally featured in professional cycle races including Paris–Nice, and is popular with amateur cyclists for its sustained gradients and switchbacks.

Geography

The pass sits above the Vésubie and Bévéra valleys, not far from the Authion massif and the Mercantour National Park, an area fortified in the 1930s as part of the Alpine Line.
