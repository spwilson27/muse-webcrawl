River Sow

River Sow
LocationStaffordshire, England
SourceNear Brocton, Staffordshire
MouthRiver Trent near Great Haywood
LengthApprox. 24 mi (38 km)

The River Sow is a tributary of the River Trent in Staffordshire, England. It rises between the villages of Brocton and Milford, close to Cannock Chase, and flows eastwards through the county town of Stafford before joining the Trent near Great Haywood, downstream of Shugborough Hall. It is joined in Stafford by its own principal tributary, the Penk, whose name gives Penkridge.

Course

From springs below Brocton, the Sow runs north-east through Stafford, where it historically looped around the town centre and powered its mills; the river through Stafford was re-aligned and trained in the 1970s as part of a flood-alleviation scheme after repeated flooding of the town centre. Below Stafford it flows through low-lying meadows—the Sow floodplain around Doxey Marshes, now a Staffordshire Wildlife Trust nature reserve noted for breeding waders and wintering wildfowl—then past Tixall, where the wide floodplain was landscaped in the 18th century as part of the Tixall estate, before reaching the Trent near Great Haywood, close to the famous Essex Bridge packhorse bridge over the Trent.

History

Stafford grew up at a crossing of the Sow, and the river defended the medieval town on its northern side. Its name is Old English in origin, though its precise derivation is uncertain. Doxey Marshes, toward the town's northern edge, remains a locally important bird reserve partly created by the river's flooding regime, and otter and water vole have been recorded along the Sow corridor.
