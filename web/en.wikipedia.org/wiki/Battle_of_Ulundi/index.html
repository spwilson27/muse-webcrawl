Battle of Ulundi

The Battle of Ulundi took place on 4 July 1879 at Ulundi, the royal kraal of King Cetshwayo in Zululand. It was the final major battle of the Anglo-Zulu War, in which a British force under Lord Chelmsford decisively defeated the Zulu army and burned the royal homestead, effectively ending the war.

Background

After the shock defeat at Isandlwana in January 1879, Chelmsford advanced cautiously on Ulundi with a large, heavily reinforced column. On the morning of 4 July his force of about 5,000 men, drawn up in a large hollow square with artillery and Gatling guns, crossed the Mhlatuze River and approached the royal kraal.

The battle

Some 12,000–15,000 Zulu warriors attacked the square repeatedly but could not break it; disciplined rifle volleys and artillery fire inflicted devastating losses. When the attacks faltered, British cavalry — including the 17th Lancers — charged out and pursued the fleeing Zulus across the plain. Zulu casualties numbered around 1,500; British losses were about 100 killed and wounded. Ulundi was then burned to the ground.

Aftermath

Cetshwayo fled but was captured in August and exiled to Cape Town. The Zulu kingdom was broken up into thirteen chiefdoms, a settlement that led to civil war in Zululand; Zululand was later annexed to Natal in 1897.
