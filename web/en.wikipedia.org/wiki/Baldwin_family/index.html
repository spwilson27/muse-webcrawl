The Baldwin family is an American family of actors from Massapequa, Long Island, New York. Its most famous members are the four Baldwin brothers — Alec, Daniel, William and Stephen — all actors, sons of Carol (née Martineau) and Alexander Rae Baldwin Jr., a high-school social studies teacher and football coach.

The brothers

Alec Baldwin (born 1958) is the eldest and most prominent: a film star of the 1980s–90s (The Hunt for Red October, Glengarry Glen Ross), he later won acclaim and Emmy Awards for playing Jack Donaghy on the sitcom 30 Rock and for his impersonation of Donald Trump on Saturday Night Live. Daniel Baldwin (born 1960) is known for Homicide: Life on the Street. William "Billy" Baldwin (born 1963) starred in Backdraft, Sliver and Flatliners. Stephen Baldwin (born 1966), the youngest, is known for The Usual Suspects and Bio-Dome, and later became an evangelical Christian activist.

Next generation

Stephen's daughter Hailey Bieber (née Baldwin, born 1996) is a model and founder of the skincare brand Rhode; she is married to singer Justin Bieber. Alec's daughter Ireland Baldwin (with actress Kim Basinger) is a model and actress. The family's cousins include actor Joseph Baldwin.

Public profile

The brothers have been collectively prominent in American popular culture since the late 1980s, notable as one of several sibling groups in Hollywood. Alec Baldwin's career was overshadowed in October 2021 by the fatal shooting on the set of the film Rust, in which cinematographer Halyna Hutchins was killed by a live round discharged from a prop revolver he was holding; involuntary manslaughter charges against Baldwin were dismissed in July 2024.
