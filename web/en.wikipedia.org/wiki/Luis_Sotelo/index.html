Blessed Luis Sotelo (6 September 1574 – 25 August 1624) was a Spanish Franciscan friar who served as a missionary in Japan and accompanied the Keichō Embassy of the samurai Hasekura Tsunenaga to Mexico, Spain and Rome. He was martyred in Japan in 1624 and was beatified by Pope Pius IX in 1867.

Missionary work

Sotelo studied at the University of Salamanca before joining the Franciscan order. He travelled to the Philippines, and in 1603 went to Japan, where he ministered to Christians in Edo (Tokyo) and won the favour of the daimyō Date Masamune of Sendai, who permitted him to proselytise in his domain. He founded churches and a hospital for lepers and became an interpreter and adviser to Date.

The Keichō Embassy

Seeking trade and missionaries, Date Masamune dispatched Hasekura Tsunenaga across the Pacific in 1613 aboard the Japanese-built galleon San Juan Bautista. Sotelo accompanied the embassy through New Spain (Mexico) to Spain, where the ambassadors were received by King Philip III, and on to Rome, where they were granted an audience with Pope Paul V in 1615. Sotelo presented a letter requesting the establishment of a diocese in Sendai with himself as bishop.

Return and martyrdom

By the time the embassy returned to Japan in 1620, Christianity had been outlawed and the political climate had turned decisively against missionaries. Sotelo was arrested in 1622 after re-entering Japan in disguise aboard a Chinese ship, and after two years' imprisonment he was burned at the stake at Shimabara on 25 August 1624, together with companions including the Jesuit Miguel de Carvalho.
