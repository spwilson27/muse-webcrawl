Katowice (German: Kattowitz) is a city in southern Poland, the capital of the Silesian Voivodeship and the centre of the Upper Silesian industrial region and metropolitan area. Historically a centre of coal mining and heavy industry, it has reinvented itself as a hub for services, culture and events.

History

The area was settled for centuries before industrialisation; the village of Katowice was first recorded in the 16th century. In the 19th century, under Prussian rule, the discovery and exploitation of coal and the arrival of the railway transformed it into an industrial boomtown, and it received city rights in 1865. After the First World War and the Silesian Uprisings, a 1921 plebiscite and League of Nations decision assigned the city to Poland in 1922, though much of its population was German-speaking.

During the communist era the city was renamed Stalinogród from 1953 to 1956. In December 1981, nine striking miners were killed by riot police at the Wujek coal mine during martial law — an event commemorated by a memorial and museum. The post-1989 decline of coal mining forced a long economic restructuring.

Present day

Landmarks include the Spodek ("saucer") arena of 1971, the Silesian Museum built on a former mine site, the concert hall of the Polish National Radio Symphony Orchestra (NOSPR, opened 2014), and the International Congress Centre. Katowice hosted the COP24 United Nations climate summit in 2018 and is recognised by UNESCO as a City of Music. Intel Extreme Masters esports tournaments at Spodek draw large international audiences.
