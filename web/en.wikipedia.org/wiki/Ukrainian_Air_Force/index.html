Ukrainian Air Force

The Ukrainian Air Force (Ukrainian: Повітряні сили Збройних сил України, romanized: Povitryani syly Zbroynykh syl Ukrainy) is the air force of Ukraine and one of the eight branches of the Armed Forces of Ukraine. Its current form was created on 17 March 1992, largely from units of the Soviet Air Forces and Air Defence Forces stationed on Ukrainian territory after the dissolution of the Soviet Union.

Founded: 17 March 1992
Headquarters: Vinnytsia
Role: Aerial warfare, air defence
Aircraft: MiG-29, Su-24, Su-25, Su-27, F-16, Mirage 2000

History

Ukraine inherited one of the largest air fleets in Europe — some 1,800 aircraft — including Tu-160 and Tu-95 strategic bombers, many of which were returned to Russia, scrapped under cooperative threat-reduction programs, or sold. Chronic underfunding through the 1990s and 2000s left much of the fleet grounded. Following Russia's annexation of Crimea and the war in Donbas from 2014, the force rebuilt readiness, losing several aircraft to ground fire and air defences.

Russo-Ukrainian War

During Russia's full-scale invasion from February 2022, the Air Force, though heavily outnumbered, preserved much of its combat fleet by dispersing aircraft, and continued flying strike, air-defence and interception missions. Ukrainian air defences — fighters combined with Soviet-era and Western-supplied surface-to-air missiles (S-300, Buk, NASAMS, IRIS-T, Patriot) — denied Russia air superiority. From 2024, allied-supplied F-16 Fighting Falcons and French Mirage 2000-5s began entering Ukrainian service, marking a transition toward Western aircraft, while Bayraktar TB2 and other drones performed reconnaissance and strike roles early in the war.

Structure

The Air Force encompasses tactical aviation brigades (fighter, bomber, attack and transport), air-defence missile and radio-technical (radar) troops, and support units, administered through regional air commands (West, Centre, South and East). Its distinctive blue-and-grey disruptive camouflage and yellow-blue roundel are widely recognised.
