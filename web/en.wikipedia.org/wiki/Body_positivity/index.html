Body positivity

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Body positivity is a social movement that promotes acceptance of all bodies, regardless of size, shape, skin tone, gender and physical ability, and challenges unrealistic beauty standards as a means of building confidence and self-worth. The slogan commonly associated with the movement is that all bodies are "good bodies".

History

The movement has roots in the fat acceptance movement of the late 1960s, including the founding of the National Association to Advance Fat Acceptance (NAAFA) in 1969 and "fat-in" protests. A second wave in the early 2000s took the message to blogs and social media, broadening its scope from size to include disability, race, scars and other visible difference. A third wave from around 2012 onward saw the term enter mainstream culture, as brands, advertisers and plus-size models adopted its language.

Criticism and debate

Critics within the movement argue that mainstream body positivity has been commercialised and diluted, centring bodies that remain close to conventional attractiveness standards while marginalising the fat, disabled and darker-skinned people the movement began with. Others debate its relationship to health, arguing either that it usefully separates worth from health status or that it can obscure medical risks associated with obesity. Related concepts include body neutrality, which emphasises appreciating what the body does rather than how it looks.
