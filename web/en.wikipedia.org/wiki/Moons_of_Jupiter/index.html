Moons of Jupiter

As of the mid-2020s, Jupiter has 274 confirmed moons with formally recognised orbits, far more than any other planet in the Solar System. The largest are the four Galilean moons — Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto — discovered by Galileo Galilei in 1610. Ganymede is the largest moon in the Solar System and is larger than the planet Mercury. The remaining moons are mostly small irregular satellites, many only a few kilometres across, thought to be captured asteroids or fragments of larger bodies.

Other groups

Inside the Galilean orbits lie four small inner moons, including Amalthea. Beyond them, dozens of irregular moons follow distant, eccentric, and often retrograde orbits, grouped into collisional families such as the Himalia, Ananke, Carme, and Pasiphae groups. New moons continue to be announced as surveys improve; large batches were confirmed in 2023 and 2025.

Exploration

The moons have been studied by the Pioneer, Voyager, Galileo, Cassini (flyby), New Horizons (flyby), and Juno spacecraft. ESA's JUICE mission, launched in 2023, and NASA's Europa Clipper, launched in 2024, will study the icy Galilean moons in the 2030s.
