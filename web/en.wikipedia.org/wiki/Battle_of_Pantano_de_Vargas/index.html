The Battle of Pantano de Vargas (Spanish: Batalla del Pantano de Vargas) was fought on 25 July 1819, during the Colombian War of Independence, between the patriot army of Simón Bolívar and the royalist forces of Colonel José María Barreiro. It took place near Paipa, in the swampy terrain of the Vargas valley, and was one of the hardest-fought battles of Bolívar's campaign to liberate New Granada.

The monument to the Lancers of the Vargas Swamp, near Paipa.

Background

Bolívar's army had crossed the flooded plains and the high Andes pass of Pisba in June–July 1819, emerging exhausted into the highlands of Boyacá. Barreiro's royalist division moved to intercept him before he could reach Tunja and Bogotá. The two armies met on 25 July around the swamp (pantano) de Vargas.

The battle

The fighting raged for hours on the hills around the swamp, with the exhausted patriots repeatedly pressed back. At the critical moment, Colonel Juan José Rondón led a charge of llanero lancers against the royalist position — Bolívar's famous order, "¡Coronel, salve usted la patria!" ("Colonel, save the fatherland!") — while the British Legions under Colonel James Rooke also advanced. The royalists were driven from the field in disorder; Rooke was mortally wounded. Patriot losses numbered several hundred, and royalist losses were comparable or higher.

Aftermath

Although not immediately decisive, the battle broke royalist momentum and allowed Bolívar to advance on Tunja. Twelve days later he destroyed Barreiro's army at the Battle of Boyacá (7 August 1819), securing the independence of New Granada. The Vargas Swamp Lancers Monument, one of the largest in Colombia, commemorates the battle.
