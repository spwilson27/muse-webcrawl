Sunderland

Country: England

Region: North East England

Ceremonial county: Tyne and Wear

City status: 1992

Population: approx. 277,000 (city area)

Sunderland is a city in Tyne and Wear, England, at the mouth of the River Wear on the North Sea coast. It is the administrative centre of the City of Sunderland metropolitan borough, which also includes the towns of Washington and Houghton-le-Spring. Historically part of County Durham, Sunderland grew from monastic settlements at Monkwearmouth and Bishopwearmouth, the former home to the Venerable Bede's monastery.

History

Sunderland developed as a major port, with shipbuilding on the Wear from the 14th century and a large coal-exporting trade. By the 19th century it was one of the largest shipbuilding towns in the world, building a substantial share of British shipping tonnage. Glassmaking was also a historic industry, dating to the 7th century at Monkwearmouth. The decline of shipbuilding and coal mining in the late 20th century caused significant economic hardship; the last shipyard closed in 1988.

Economy and regeneration

Modern employers include the Nissan car plant at Washington—one of the largest in the UK—financial services, call centres, and the University of Sunderland. The city is home to Sunderland A.F.C., who play at the Stadium of Light, and to cultural venues including the Sunderland Empire Theatre and the National Glass Centre.

Transport

Sunderland is served by the Tyne and Wear Metro, mainline rail services, and the A19 and A1(M) trunk roads. The Northern Spire bridge, opened in 2018, crosses the Wear west of the city centre.
