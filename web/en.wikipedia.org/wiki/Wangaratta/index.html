Wangaratta

Wangaratta is a city in the northeast of Victoria, Australia, situated at the junction of the Ovens and King rivers, which both drain from the Victorian Alps. It is the administrative centre and most populous city in the Rural City of Wangaratta local government area, with a population of about 19,000. The city is roughly 236 km (147 mi) by road from Melbourne along the Hume Highway.

History

The region was originally inhabited by the Pallanganmiddang people. European settlement followed the explorations of Hume and Hovell in 1824, and the town grew as a service centre on the route to the Beechworth goldfields. Wangaratta became a proclaimed city in 1959. Its cathedral is the seat of the Anglican Diocese of Wangaratta.

Attractions

The city is a gateway to the Milawa gourmet food and wine region and the Victorian Alps ski fields. The Wangaratta Festival of Jazz and Blues, held annually since 1990, is one of Australia's leading jazz festivals.
