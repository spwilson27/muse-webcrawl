Continuous glucose monitor

A continuous glucose monitor (CGM) is a medical device that measures glucose levels in the interstitial fluid on an ongoing basis, typically every one to five minutes, for people with diabetes. A small sensor worn on the skin — commonly on the abdomen or the back of the upper arm — transmits readings to a receiver, insulin pump, or smartphone app, showing current values, trend arrows, and alerts for high and low glucose.

PurposeGlucose monitoring in diabetes
MethodSubcutaneous sensor measuring interstitial glucose
Sensor lifeTypically 10–15 days depending on model
Major makersDexcom, Abbott (FreeStyle Libre), Medtronic

Technology

Most CGMs use an enzyme-based electrochemical sensor inserted just under the skin; glucose oxidase generates a current proportional to glucose concentration. Because the sensor measures interstitial rather than blood glucose, readings lag blood values by several minutes, especially when glucose is changing rapidly. Accuracy is expressed by the mean absolute relative difference (MARD) compared with reference measurements; modern systems have MARD values around 8–10% and most no longer require calibration with fingerstick tests.

Clinical use

Studies show CGM use improves glycaemic control (HbA1c) and reduces hypoglycaemia in type 1 diabetes and in insulin-treated type 2 diabetes. Metrics derived from CGM data, such as "time in range" (the percentage of readings between 70 and 180 mg/dL), are now standard treatment targets. Integrated with insulin pumps, CGMs enable hybrid closed-loop "artificial pancreas" systems that automate basal insulin delivery. Over-the-counter CGMs for people without diabetes have also entered the market, though their benefit in that population is debated.
