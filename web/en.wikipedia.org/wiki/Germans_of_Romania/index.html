Germans of Romania

The Germans of Romania (German: Rumäniendeutsche) are the ethnic German minority of Romania. Numbering about 745,000 in the 1930 census, their population has shrunk dramatically through war, deportation, and emigration, to roughly 23,000 in the 2021 census. The best-known groups are the Transylvanian Saxons and the Banat Swabians.

Historical groups

German settlement in present-day Romania proceeded in several waves. The Transylvanian Saxons were settled in Transylvania from the 12th century by the kings of Hungary, building fortified towns and the famous fortified churches of southern Transylvania. The Banat Swabians, Satu Mare Swabians, and Bukovina Germans arrived in the 18th and 19th centuries under Habsburg colonisation; other groups included the Zipser Germans, Landler Protestants, and the Black Sea Germans of Dobruja.

20th century

After World War I, Transylvania, the Banat, and Bukovina became part of Romania, and their German populations became Romanian citizens. World War II brought catastrophe: resettlements, service in the Waffen-SS, and in January 1945 the deportation of some 70,000 Romanian Germans to forced labour in the Soviet Union. Under the communist regime, emigration to West Germany was progressively permitted, effectively ransomed through payments by the Federal Republic. Most remaining Germans emigrated after the fall of communism in 1989–1992.

Today

The Democratic Forum of Germans in Romania (DFDR) represents the community politically. Klaus Iohannis, a Transylvanian Saxon and former mayor of Sibiu (Hermannstadt), served as President of Romania from 2014 to 2025. Saxon and Swabian cultural heritage — including UNESCO-listed fortified churches — remains a prominent part of Romania's landscape.
