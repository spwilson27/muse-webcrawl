Herbert Austin, 1st Baron Austin
Born: 8 November 1866, Little Missenden, Buckinghamshire
Died: 23 May 1941 (aged 74), Birmingham
Known for: Founder of the Austin Motor Company

Herbert Austin

Herbert Austin, 1st Baron Austin (8 November 1866 – 23 May 1941) was an English automobile designer and builder who founded the Austin Motor Company at Longbridge in 1905. His Austin 7 of 1922 brought motoring within reach of ordinary British families and was copied and licensed around the world.

Early career

Austin emigrated to Australia as a young engineer and worked for the Wolseley Sheep Shearing Machine Company, improving its machinery and experimenting with shearing equipment and early vehicles. Returning to England, he managed Wolseley's motor business before leaving to start his own company.

Austin Motor Company

At the former print works at Longbridge, Birmingham, Austin built cars ranging from luxury models to taxis and commercial vehicles. The little Austin 7 — designed with the young draughtsman Stanley Edge — became one of Britain's best-selling cars and was produced under licence in Germany (as the first BMW car), France, Japan, and the United States.

Politics and honours

Austin served as Conservative MP for Birmingham King's Norton from 1918 to 1924 and was raised to the peerage as Baron Austin in 1936. He died in 1941; his company later became part of the British Motor Corporation.
