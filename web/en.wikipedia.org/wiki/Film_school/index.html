Film school

A film school is an educational institution, or a department within a larger institution, dedicated to education in film theory and the practice of filmmaking. Courses cover screenwriting, directing, cinematography, editing, sound design, and producing, typically combining academic study with the production of student films.

History

The first film school was the Gerasimov Institute of Cinematography (VGIK) in Moscow, founded in 1919. In the United States, the University of Southern California's School of Cinematic Arts, founded in 1929, is the oldest film school in the country; later institutions include UCLA, NYU's Tisch School of the Arts, and the American Film Institute Conservatory (1969). In the United Kingdom, the National Film and Television School opened in 1971, and in France La Fémis was founded in 1986.

Notable schools and debates

Famous alumni include directors such as George Lucas, Martin Scorsese, and Steven Spielberg (who famously left film school early and received his degree decades later). Whether film school is worthwhile is a perennial debate: proponents cite training, equipment access, and professional networks, while critics note the cost and that many successful filmmakers never attended.
