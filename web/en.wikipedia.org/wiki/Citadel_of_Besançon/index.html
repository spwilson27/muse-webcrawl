Citadel of Besançon

The Citadel of Besançon (French: Citadelle de Besançon) is a fortress in Besançon, Franche-Comté, eastern France. It sits atop Mount Saint-Étienne, more than 100 metres above the old town, within a loop (oxbow) of the river Doubs, and is one of the masterpieces of the military engineer Sébastien Le Prestre de Vauban.

History

After the French conquest of Franche-Comté, Vauban designed the citadel between 1668 and 1711 on the site of an earlier Spanish fortress. Its massive ramparts, up to 20 metres high, enclose about 11 hectares. During the Second World War the citadel was used by the German occupation forces; a memorial honours members of the Resistance executed there.

Today

The citadel is one of France's most visited heritage sites in the region and houses several museums, including the Museum of the Resistance and Deportation, a museum of Franche-Comté life, a natural history museum, and gardens and a small zoo. With the other Fortifications of Vauban, it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2008.
