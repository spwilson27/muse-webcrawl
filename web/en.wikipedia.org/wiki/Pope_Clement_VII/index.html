Pope Clement VII (Latin: Clemens VII; born Giulio de' Medici; 26 May 1478 – 25 September 1534) was head of the Catholic Church and ruler of the Papal States from 19 November 1523 to his death on 25 September 1534. His pontificate was overwhelmed by political and military crises of the Italian Wars: the Sack of Rome in 1527, the collapse of Medici rule in Florence, and the beginning of the English Reformation, precipitated by his refusal to annul the marriage of Henry VIII of England to Catherine of Aragon.

Early life

Giulio de' Medici was the posthumous son of Giuliano de' Medici, who was assassinated in the Pazzi conspiracy a month before Giulio's birth; his mother was Fioretta Gorini. Raised by his uncle Lorenzo the Magnificent, he rose rapidly once his cousin Giovanni became Pope Leo X in 1513: he was made Archbishop of Florence and a cardinal, and as vice-chancellor of the Church he effectively ran the papal administration and directed Florentine affairs. He was elected pope on 19 November 1523 after a long conclave, succeeding the austere Adrian VI.

The Italian Wars and the Sack of Rome

Clement tried to steer between France and the Holy Roman Empire of Charles V. In 1526 he formed the League of Cognac with France, Venice, Florence and Milan against imperial power. The war went disastrously: on 6 May 1527 an unpaid, mutinous imperial army of Spanish troops and German Landsknechte stormed Rome and subjected it to a prolonged, brutal sack. Clement escaped to Castel Sant'Angelo and, after months of captivity, fled in disguise to Orvieto. The sack shocked Europe and is often taken to mark the end of the Roman High Renaissance.

Meanwhile the Medici were expelled from Florence, which proclaimed a republic. Clement eventually reconciled with Charles V (Treaty of Barcelona, 1529, and the imperial coronation at Bologna, 1530); imperial arms then besieged and restored Medici rule in Florence, where Clement installed his relative Alessandro de' Medici.

The English annulment and schism

From 1527 Henry VIII sought annulment of his marriage to Catherine of Aragon, Charles V's aunt, in order to marry Anne Boleyn. Dependent on the emperor after the Sack of Rome, Clement delayed and finally, in March 1534, ruled the marriage valid. Henry responded by breaking with Rome: the English Parliament passed the Act of Supremacy later in 1534, making the king head of the Church of England — the decisive step of the English Reformation.

Death and assessment

Clement died on 25 September 1534, reportedly poisoned by death cap mushrooms, though the cause is uncertain. A patron of Michelangelo — he commissioned the Last Judgment and the Medici Chapel and Laurentian Library in Florence — Clement is often judged an intelligent but indecisive man whose pontificate was overtaken by events he could not control.
