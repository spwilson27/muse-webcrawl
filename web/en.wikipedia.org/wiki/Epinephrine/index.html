Overview

Epinephrine, also known as adrenaline, is a hormone and neurotransmitter produced by the adrenal glands (and a small number of neurons) that prepares the body for the "fight-or-flight" response: it increases heart rate and contractility, dilates airways and pupils, constricts many blood vessels while dilating those in skeletal muscle, and mobilises glucose. It is a catecholamine, synthesised from noradrenaline (norepinephrine).

Medical use

As a drug, epinephrine is indispensable: it is the first-line treatment for anaphylaxis (given by auto-injector such as the EpiPen), a core drug in cardiac-arrest resuscitation, an adjuvant to local anaesthetics (prolonging action and limiting bleeding), and a treatment for severe asthma and croup (nebulised). Discovered in adrenal extracts in the 1890s (Takamine Jōkichi isolated and marketed "Adrenalin" around 1900–01), it was among the first hormones isolated and synthesised.
