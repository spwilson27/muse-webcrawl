The Carnegie Endowment for International Peace is an American foreign-policy think tank founded in 1910 by the industrialist and philanthropist Andrew Carnegie. It is one of the oldest international-affairs organisations in the United States and among the most influential think tanks in the world, describing itself as non-partisan and dedicated to advancing cooperation between nations.

History

On his 75th birthday, 25 November 1910, Carnegie announced a gift of $10 million to establish the Endowment, charging its trustees to use the fund "to hasten the abolition of war". Elihu Root, former Secretary of State, was its first president. The Endowment's early work covered international law and economics; it funded reconstruction studies after the First World War and later financed the influential journal Foreign Policy, founded in 1970. Its presidents have included John Foster Dulles, Morton Abramowitz, Jessica Mathews (1997–2015) and William J. Burns (2015–2021), later CIA director.

Global network

In 2007 the Endowment launched its "New Vision" as a global think tank, opening centres in Beijing (with Tsinghua University), Beirut, Brussels and New Delhi alongside its Washington headquarters, and later a Europe centre and a Berlin office. Its Moscow Center, opened in 1994, closed in 2022 after Russia's invasion of Ukraine, when the Russian authorities forced its closure. Programmes cover democracy and governance, nuclear policy, climate, technology, and regional studies of Asia, Europe, the Middle East and Russia/Eurasia.
