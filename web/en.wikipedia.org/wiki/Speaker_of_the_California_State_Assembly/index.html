The Speaker of the California State Assembly is the presiding officer and highest-ranking member of the California State Assembly, the lower house of the California State Legislature. The Speaker is elected by the members of the Assembly, customarily from the majority party, and is one of the most powerful political figures in California state government, often described as second in influence only to the Governor.

Powers and duties

The Speaker presides over sessions of the Assembly, maintains order, recognizes members to speak, and rules on points of order. More importantly, the Speaker directs the legislative agenda: appointing committee chairs and members, referring bills to committees, and controlling the flow of legislation. The Speaker also leads the majority party's caucus, plays a central role in budget negotiations with the Governor and the Senate President pro Tempore (together the "Big Three"), and is a principal fundraiser for the party.

History

The office dates to the first session of the Assembly in 1849–1850, during California's transition to statehood. In the modern era, Jesse M. Unruh (Speaker 1961–1969) greatly increased the power and institutional capacity of the speakership, professionalizing the Legislature. Willie Brown, Speaker from 1980 to 1995, was the longest-serving Speaker and the first African American to hold the post; his tenure ended after voters approved term limits (Proposition 140 of 1990), which initially capped Assembly service at six years; Proposition 28 (2012) later allowed up to 12 years in the Legislature overall. Notable recent Speakers include John Pérez, the first openly gay Speaker (2010–2014); Toni Atkins, the first woman and first openly LGBT Speaker (2014–2016); Anthony Rendon (2016–2023); and Robert Rivas, who took office on 30 June 2023.
