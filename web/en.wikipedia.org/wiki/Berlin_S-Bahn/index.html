Berlin S-Bahn

The Berlin S-Bahn (German: S-Bahn Berlin) is a rapid transit railway system in and around Berlin, the capital of Germany. It is operated by S-Bahn Berlin GmbH, a subsidiary of Deutsche Bahn, and forms part of the Verkehrsverbund Berlin-Brandenburg (VBB) transport association.

Network

The network comprises 16 lines and about 166 stations over roughly 340 kilometres (210 mi) of track. Key routes include the east–west Stadtbahn, the north–south tunnel, and the circular Ringbahn. S-Bahn services are distinguished by their green and white livery and the green "S" symbol.

History

Steam-hauled suburban services began in the 19th century; the system was electrified with third-rail DC power from 1924, and the term S-Bahn (short for Stadtschnellbahn) was coined in 1930. After the Second World War the S-Bahn in both sectors was operated by East Germany's Deutsche Reichsbahn. The building of the Berlin Wall in 1961 split the network, and many West Berliners boycotted it. Operation in the west passed to BVG in 1984. After reunification in 1990 the network was progressively reintegrated, with the Ring completed again in 2002.
