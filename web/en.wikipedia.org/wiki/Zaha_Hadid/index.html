Zaha Hadid

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Heydar Aliyev Center in Baku: a single white surface rising from the plaza and folding into roof, wall and wave without a straight line]

Dame Zaha Mohammad Hadid DBE RA (31 October 1950 – 31 March 2016) was an Iraqi-British architect, artist and designer, recognised as a defining figure of late-20th and early-21st century architecture. Known as the 'Queen of the Curve', she liberated architectural geometry from the right angle: her buildings sweep, fold and flow — the Vitra Fire Station (1993), the MAXXI Museum in Rome (2009), the Guangzhou Opera House (2010), the Heydar Aliyev Center in Baku (2012) and the London Aquatics Centre (2011).

In 2004 she became the first woman to receive the Pritzker Architecture Prize; she twice won the RIBA Stirling Prize (2010, 2011) and in 2016 became the first woman awarded the RIBA Royal Gold Medal in her own right. Born in Baghdad to an industrialist-politician father, she studied mathematics in Beirut and architecture at London's Architectural Association, worked for Rem Koolhaas at OMA, and founded Zaha Hadid Architects in 1980.

From paper to buildings

For a decade Hadid was architecture's most famous unbuilt architect — her 1983 competition-winning design for Hong Kong's Peak Club (never realised) and her suprematist paintings made her reputation before commissions followed. The Vitra Fire Station (1993), all blades and tension, proved the drawings buildable; the digital tools of the 2000s then let her office of hundreds realise the fluid megastructures that define her late work on every continent.

Death and legacy

Hadid died of a heart attack in Miami in March 2016 while being treated for bronchitis — mid-career by her own plans, with some fifty projects in progress. Her practice under Patrik Schumacher continues as one of the world's largest; her furniture, jewellery and product designs (for Alessi, Swarovski, and her own label) extended the same geometry to the hand. The argument she embodied — that architecture is form-making at civic scale, not mere problem-solving — remains the profession's live wire.
