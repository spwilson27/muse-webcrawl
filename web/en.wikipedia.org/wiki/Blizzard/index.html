Blizzard

A blizzard is a severe snowstorm characterised by strong sustained winds and low visibility. The United States National Weather Service defines a blizzard as a storm with winds of at least 35 mph (56 km/h) and falling or blowing snow that reduces visibility to less than a quarter of a mile (400 m) for at least three hours. A ground blizzard produces the same conditions by whipping up snow already on the ground, without new snowfall.

Formation

Blizzards occur when cold polar air collides with moist warmer air, typically on the northwest side of a strong extratropical cyclone where the tight pressure gradient drives powerful winds. Nor'easters on the North American east coast, Alberta clippers, and Siberian and Antarctic storms are classic blizzard producers. Temperatures are usually below freezing, though a blizzard does not require extreme cold; the wind-chill produced by high winds and low temperatures is the principal danger.

Dangers

Whiteout conditions disorient travellers and make roads impassable; motorists stranded in blizzards risk hypothermia. Blizzards also bring down power lines, halt air and rail transport, and can kill livestock in huge numbers on open range. Historic storms include the Great Blizzard of 1888 in the northeastern United States, the 1940 Armistice Day Blizzard, the 1978 northeastern blizzard, and the 1972 Iran blizzard — the deadliest on record, which killed more than 4,000 people.

Warning systems

Meteorological agencies issue blizzard warnings when conditions are expected. In the United States the National Weather Service issues blizzard warnings and winter storm warnings; comparable alert systems operate in Canada, Russia and other affected countries.
