Friday (Robinson Crusoe)

Crusoe meets Friday on the beach, in an 18th-century illustration.

Friday is a fictional character in Daniel Defoe's 1719 novel Robinson Crusoe. A native of one of the Caribbean islands, he becomes Crusoe's companion, servant and friend on the desert island where Crusoe has been shipwrecked for 27 years. Friday is one of the most recognisable and frequently adapted characters in English literature.

Character

In the novel, Crusoe rescues Friday from cannibals who had brought him to the island to be eaten. He names him "Friday" because the rescue took place on a Friday, and teaches him to speak English, to wear clothes, to eat grain instead of human flesh and to practise Christianity. Friday proves intelligent and loyal: he learns English quickly, explains the customs of his own people, and helps Crusoe escape the island in the sequel The Farther Adventures of Robinson Crusoe (1719).

Cultural legacy

Friday's name entered the language as a generic term for a loyal, resourceful assistant — hence "Man Friday" and, in the feminine form, "Girl Friday" — and his character is often cited in postcolonial criticism as a symbol of the colonial relationship: an indigenous figure whose identity is reshaped by the European protagonist. The character was drawn from Defoe's reading of contemporary accounts of Indigenous peoples, including the Scottish sailor Alexander Selkirk's real-life castaway story and the Carib people of the Caribbean. He has been portrayed in countless adaptations, most famously by Robert Culp in the 1964 film Robinson Crusoe on Mars re-imagined, and by Mehdi Dehbi in the 2021 film Robinson.
