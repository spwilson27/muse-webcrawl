The Arsacid dynasty (Armenian: Արշակունի, Arshakuni) was a branch of the Parthian Arsacids that ruled the Kingdom of Armenia from 52 AD to 428 AD. Under the Arsacids Armenia became a buffer kingdom contested between the Roman and Persian (Parthian, then Sasanian) empires, and in the early 4th century it became the first state to adopt Christianity as its official religion.

Establishment

The dynasty was founded by Tiridates I, brother of the Parthian king Vologases I. After war between Rome and Parthia, the compromise of Rhandeia (63 AD) provided that the Arsacid prince would receive his crown from the Roman emperor; Tiridates duly travelled to Rome in 66 AD and was crowned by Nero. Thereafter Armenia was ruled by Iranian Arsacid kings nominally under Roman suzerainty, a settlement that endured, with interruptions, for centuries.

Christianisation

Under Tiridates III (the Great), and through the mission of Gregory the Illuminator, Armenia adopted Christianity around 301–314 AD — traditionally dated 301 — making it the first Christian state. In 405/406 the monk Mesrop Mashtots, under royal and ecclesiastical patronage, created the Armenian alphabet, inaugurating a golden age of Armenian literature.

Partition and fall

In 387 Rome and Sasanian Persia partitioned Armenia; the larger eastern part (Persarmenia) kept Arsacid kings as Persian vassals until the Armenian nobility, dissatisfied with King Artaxias IV, petitioned for his removal. The Sasanian king Bahram V deposed him in 428, ending the dynasty and converting the kingdom into a Persian marzpanate. Arsacid cadet branches and claimed descendants survived among the Armenian princely houses, and the family name endured in Armenian tradition.
