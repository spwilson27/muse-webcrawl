The Dolly Sisters is a 1945 American Technicolor biographical musical film directed by Irving Cummings and released by 20th Century Fox. It stars Betty Grable and June Haver as the Dolly Sisters, the Hungarian-born identical-twin dancers Jenny and Rosie Dolly, who conquered American vaudeville and Broadway in the 1910s and 1920s, with John Payne as their songwriter love interest.

As with most Hollywood biographical musicals of the period, the film takes considerable liberties with the facts of the sisters' lives, focusing on their rise from a New York dance hall act to international fame in Paris and London, and on Jenny's romance with singer Harry Fox (John Payne), the inventor of the fox trot.

Production and reception

The film was Grable's most expensive production to that date and one of Fox's biggest hits of 1945, earning nearly $6 million. Its musical sequences feature standards of the era, including "I'm Always Chasing Rainbows", an interpolation of Chopin's Fantaisie-Impromptu, and the sisters' signature number "Darktown Strutters' Ball". The film was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Music, Scoring of a Musical Picture, and a novelty song from its score, "I Can't Begin to Tell You", was nominated for Best Original Song at the following year's ceremony as part of the studio's output.
