Borel summation

Borel summation is a summation method for divergent series introduced by Émile Borel in 1899. It is particularly useful for summing divergent asymptotic series, and in some cases gives the proper analytic continuation of the original series beyond its disk of convergence. Borel summation and its generalizations play a central role in resurgence theory and in the study of Stokes phenomena.

Definition

Given a formal power series, one forms its Borel transform by dividing each coefficient by a corresponding factorial-like factor, producing a new series with a positive radius of convergence. If that transformed series can be analytically continued along the positive real axis with at most exponential growth, one applies the Laplace transform to recover a function. When the original series converges, the Borel sum coincides with the ordinary sum; when it diverges, the procedure may still produce a well-defined value.

Example

The prototypical example is the Euler series, whose partial sums diverge rapidly. Its Borel transform sums easily, and the Laplace transform of that closed form gives an integral representation of the Borel sum, which agrees with the exponential integral function. This is the historical prototype for the resummation of factorially divergent perturbation series in quantum field theory.

Applications

Borel summation is used throughout asymptotic analysis, mathematical physics, and ordinary and partial differential equations wherever divergent series appear as formal solutions. Variants such as Borel–Padé and Borel–Écalle resummation extend its range in numerical and theoretical work.
