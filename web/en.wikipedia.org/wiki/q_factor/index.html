Q factor

TypeDimensionless parameter of oscillators
Definition2π × (energy stored / energy lost per cycle)
High QLow damping, narrow bandwidth

In physics and engineering, the quality factor or Q factor is a dimensionless parameter that describes how underdamped an oscillator or resonator is. It is approximately the ratio of the initial energy stored in the resonator to the energy lost in one radian of oscillation.

Definition

Q is defined as 2π times the ratio of the total energy stored to the energy lost in a single cycle. Equivalently, for a lightly damped resonator, Q equals the resonant frequency divided by the bandwidth of the resonance.

Physical systems

High-Q systems ring for a long time: tuning forks, quartz crystals (Q in the tens of thousands), superconducting RF cavities, and optical cavities in lasers. Low-Q systems dissipate energy quickly, as in shock absorbers. In RLC circuits, Q depends on resistance, inductance, and capacitance; in mechanical systems it reflects friction losses.

Applications

Q determines the selectivity of radio tuners, the linewidth of lasers, and the sensitivity of resonant sensors and atomic clocks.
