Cooley Peninsula

The Cooley Peninsula (Irish: Cuaille) is a peninsula in County Louth, Ireland, projecting into the Irish Sea between Carlingford Lough to the north and Dundalk Bay to the south. The peninsula is dominated by the Cooley Mountains, whose highest point is Slieve Foy at 589 metres, and includes the village and port of Carlingford and the settlements of Omeath, Greenore, and Lordship.

History and mythology

In Irish mythology, Cooley (Cúailnge) is the setting of the Táin Bó Cúailnge ("The Cattle Raid of Cooley"), the central epic of the Ulster Cycle, in which Queen Medb of Connacht invades Ulster to seize the Brown Bull of Cooley and is resisted by the hero Cú Chulainn. The peninsula is rich in prehistoric and early Christian remains, including the Proleek dolmen near Ballymascanlon and the medieval walled town of Carlingford with its King John's Castle.

Geography and economy

Greenore is a deep-water port built in the 19th century and remains the only privately owned port in Ireland. Tourism, hill walking, and water sports on Carlingford Lough are significant local industries, and the area has a substantial oyster and mussel fishery. The Cooley Distillery, founded in 1987, revived whiskey distilling on the peninsula.
