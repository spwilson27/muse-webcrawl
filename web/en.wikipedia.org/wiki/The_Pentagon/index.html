The Pentagon is the headquarters building of the United States Department of Defense, located in Arlington County, Virginia, across the Potomac River from Washington, D.C. As a symbol of the U.S. military, "the Pentagon" is often used metonymically to refer to the Department of Defense itself. It is among the world's largest office buildings, with about 6.5 million square feet (600,000 m²) of floor space, of which about 3.7 million square feet are offices.

History

Designed by architect George Bergstrom and built by contractor John McShain, construction began on 11 September 1941 and the building was dedicated on 15 January 1943, after only about sixteen months of work. The five-sided design was partly dictated by the road layout of the original site near Arlington Farms; although the site was changed, the pentagonal plan was retained. General Brehon Somervell led the project, and the building consolidated War Department offices scattered across the Washington area.

The building consists of five concentric pentagonal rings connected by ten corridors, with five floors above ground and two basement levels. Despite its size — 17.5 miles (28 km) of corridors — any two points in the building can be reached on foot within about seven minutes.

September 11 attacks

On 11 September 2001, exactly 60 years after construction began, hijackers crashed American Airlines Flight 77 into the building's western side, killing 184 people: 59 aboard the aircraft and 125 in the building. The damaged section had recently been renovated with blast-resistant features, which reduced casualties. The Pentagon Memorial, dedicated in 2008, commemorates the victims.

Renovation

The Pentagon Renovation Program (PENREN), begun in the 1990s and completed in 2011, gutted and rebuilt the building wedge by wedge, modernizing systems, removing asbestos, and adding security features.
