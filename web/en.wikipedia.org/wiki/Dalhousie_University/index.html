Dalhousie University (commonly known as Dal) is a large public research university in Nova Scotia, Canada, with three campuses in Halifax, a fourth in Bible Hill, and a second medical school campus in Saint John, New Brunswick. It is one of Canada's oldest universities, founded in 1818.

History

Dalhousie was founded by George Ramsay, 9th Earl of Dalhousie and Lieutenant Governor of Nova Scotia, using funds from customs duties collected during the War of 1812 occupation of Castine, Maine; he modelled it as a non-denominational college open to all. Teaching was intermittent in its early decades, and the university was reorganized in 1863.

Academics

Dalhousie offers more than 200 degree programs across 13 faculties, including law (the Schulich School of Law), medicine, dentistry, engineering, architecture and planning, and agriculture. It is a member of the U15 group of Canadian research-intensive universities and is noted for ocean research. The university enrolls roughly 20,000 students, with a significant international population.

Campus and student life

The main Studley campus is in Halifax's South End. Dalhousie's sports teams are the Tigers (and the Rams at the Agricultural Campus). The Dalhousie Gazette, founded in 1869, is among Canada's oldest student newspapers.
