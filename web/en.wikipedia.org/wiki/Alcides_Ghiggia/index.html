Alcides Edgardo Ghiggia Pereyra (22 December 1926 – 16 July 2015) was a Uruguayan footballer who achieved worldwide fame for scoring the winning goal for Uruguay against Brazil in the decisive match of the 1950 FIFA World Cup, an event known in Brazil as the Maracanazo.

Club career

Ghiggia began his career with Peñarol in Montevideo, winning Uruguayan league titles in 1949 and 1951. In 1953 he moved to Italy, playing for Roma and later AC Milan, with whom he won the Serie A title in 1961–62 and the Latin Cup. He returned to Uruguay to finish his career with Danubio.

International career

In the final round of the 1950 World Cup, Uruguay needed only a draw against Brazil at the Maracanã Stadium, but fell behind early in the second half. After Juan Alberto Schiaffino equalised, Ghiggia scored the winning goal in the 79th minute, beating goalkeeper Barbosa at the near post and silencing a crowd of around 200,000. Ghiggia later also represented Italy, being of Italian descent, earning five caps in 1957–59.

Later life and legacy

Ghiggia remained a revered figure in Uruguay and was honoured by FIFA and the Brazilian football authorities late in life; in 2009 he was received at the Maracanã and left his footprints in its hall of fame. He died in 2015, on the 65th anniversary of the Maracanazo, and was the last surviving member of Uruguay's 1950 winning team.
