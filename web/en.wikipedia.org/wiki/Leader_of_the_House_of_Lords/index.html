Leader of the House of Lords

StyleThe Right Honourable
AppointerThe Prime Minister
OfficeMember of the Cabinet

The Leader of the House of Lords is a member of the Cabinet of the United Kingdom who arranges government business in the House of Lords and presides over the government's party within it. The office is appointed by the Prime Minister and is traditionally combined with a sinecure office such as Lord President of the Council or Lord Privy Seal, since the Leader receives no separate salary for the leadership role itself.

Responsibilities

The Leader is responsible for the conduct of government business in the Lords: scheduling legislation, negotiating through the "usual channels" with the opposition and crossbench convenor, and advising the House on procedure. The Leader also represents the Lords end of government, repeats statements made in the House of Commons to the upper house, and answers for the government in Lords debates. Unlike the Speaker of the Commons, the Leader has an explicitly partisan role, although they are expected to act with regard to the interests of the House as a whole.

History

Although peers informally led the House earlier, the office emerged in recognisable form in the eighteenth century. For much of the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries the Leader was frequently the Prime Minister when the premier sat in the Lords, as with the Marquess of Salisbury. Since 1902, prime ministers have sat in the Commons, and the leadership of the Lords became a distinct appointment. Notable holders include Lord Curzon, Lord Halifax, the Earl of Home (later Prime Minister as Alec Douglas-Home), Baroness Jay, and Baroness Amos, the first Black woman to hold the office and to sit in Cabinet.

Deputy and shadow counterparts

The Leader is assisted by a Deputy Leader, and each opposition party in the Lords has its own leader, of whom the Leader of the Opposition in the House of Lords is the principal counterpart.
