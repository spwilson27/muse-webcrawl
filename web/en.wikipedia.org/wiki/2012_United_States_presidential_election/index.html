The 2012 United States presidential election was the 57th quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, 6 November 2012. Incumbent Democratic President Barack Obama and Vice President Joe Biden were re-elected, defeating the Republican ticket of former Massachusetts governor Mitt Romney and Congressman Paul Ryan. Obama became the first president since Franklin D. Roosevelt to win two terms with a majority of the popular vote in both elections since the 1940s era pattern.

Campaign

The election took place amid a slow recovery from the Great Recession, with unemployment falling below 8 per cent only in the autumn of 2012. Obama's campaign emphasised the auto-industry rescue, the killing of Osama bin Laden, and the Affordable Care Act, and used sophisticated data-driven voter targeting. Romney, emerging from a long primary, was damaged by a leaked recording of his "47 percent" remarks about Americans dependent on government. A weak first debate performance by Obama in Denver briefly put Romney ahead in polls, but Obama recovered in the second and third debates. Superstorm Sandy's landfall a week before the election also drew praise for Obama's response.

Results

Obama won 332 electoral votes to Romney's 206, carrying every battleground state except North Carolina and Indiana, which had flipped Republican after voting Democratic in 2008. He received 51.1 per cent of the popular vote — the first Democratic majority re-election since 1944 in popular-vote terms (FDR). Democrats gained seats in the Senate and slightly narrowed the Republican House majority, leaving divided government largely intact.
