Mubahala

Mubahala (Arabic: مباهلة, mubāhala, "mutual imprecation") is an Islamic ritual in which two disputing parties invoke the curse of God upon whichever of them is lying. The term is most famous from the Event of Mubahala, an episode in early Islamic history in which the Prophet Muhammad challenged a Christian delegation from Najran to a mubahala concerning the nature of Jesus.

The event

According to Islamic tradition, in the year 10 AH (631–632 CE) a delegation of Christians from Najran, in present-day southwestern Saudi Arabia, came to Medina to debate Muhammad about the status of Jesus. After days of discussion without agreement, Muhammad proposed that both parties bring their families and jointly pray for God's curse to fall on the liars, following the instruction of verse 3:61 of the Quran: "Come, let us call our sons and your sons, our women and your women, ourselves and yourselves, then let us pray earnestly and invoke the curse of Allah on the liars."

Muhammad is said to have arrived accompanied by his daughter Fatima, his son-in-law Ali ibn Abi Talib, and his grandsons Hasan and Husayn. Seeing this, the Christian delegation declined the mubahala and instead negotiated a treaty (the Treaty of Najran), under which they kept their religion in return for tribute.

Significance

The event is commemorated by Shia Muslims on 24 Dhu al-Hijjah as Eid al-Mubahala, and the five persons who accompanied Muhammad are revered as the Ahl al-Kisa ("People of the Cloak"). Sunni sources also record the event, though with varying details about who was present. The episode is a key proof-text in Shia theology for the elevated status of the Ahl al-Bayt.
