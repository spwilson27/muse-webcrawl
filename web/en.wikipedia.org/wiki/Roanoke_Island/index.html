Roanoke Island

Roanoke Island
Location: Dare County, North Carolina, United States
Between: Albemarle Sound and Pamlico Sound
Main settlement: Manteo
Known for: The Roanoke Colony ("Lost Colony"), Fort Raleigh

Roanoke Island is an island in Dare County on the Outer Banks of North Carolina, United States, lying between the mainland and the barrier islands of Bodie Island and Hatteras Island. It is about 13 kilometres (8 miles) long and 3 kilometres (2 miles) wide, separated from the mainland by Croatan Sound and from Bodie Island by Roanoke Sound. The island's principal town is Manteo, the county seat of Dare County, with the village of Wanchese at the southern end.

The Roanoke Colony

Roanoke Island is famous as the site of the first English attempts to settle North America. Expeditions sponsored by Sir Walter Raleigh reached the island in 1584, and a first colony of soldiers was established in 1585 under Ralph Lane. In 1587 a second colony of about 115 men, women, and children, led by John White, landed on the island; White's granddaughter, Virginia Dare, was the first English child born in the Americas. White returned to England for supplies and was delayed by the war with Spain; when he finally returned in 1590, the colonists had vanished, leaving only the word "CROATOAN" carved on a post. The fate of the "Lost Colony" remains one of the enduring mysteries of American history, and is commemorated at the Fort Raleigh National Historic Site and in the long-running outdoor drama The Lost Colony.

Later history

During the American Civil War, Union forces under Ambrose Burnside captured the island in the Battle of Roanoke Island in February 1862. A freedmen's colony was established on the island for formerly enslaved people. In the 20th century the island became a centre for boatbuilding and commercial fishing at Wanchese, while Manteo developed as a tourist town. The island is connected to the mainland and to Bodie Island by bridges.
