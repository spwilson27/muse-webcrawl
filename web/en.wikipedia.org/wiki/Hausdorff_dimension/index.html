Hausdorff dimension

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In mathematics, the Hausdorff dimension is a measure of roughness, or more specifically, a notion of dimension defined in terms of Hausdorff measures. It was introduced by Felix Hausdorff in 1918. Unlike topological dimension, the Hausdorff dimension can take non-integer real values, which makes it a fundamental tool in fractal geometry: classical fractals such as the Cantor set, the Sierpiński triangle and the Koch curve have Hausdorff dimensions strictly between their topological dimension and the dimension of the ambient space.

Definition

Let X be a metric space. For s ≥ 0 and δ > 0, the s-dimensional Hausdorff content is defined by covering X with sets of diameter at most δ and summing the s-th powers of their diameters. Letting δ → 0 gives the s-dimensional Hausdorff measure Hs(X). As s increases, Hs(X) drops from infinity to zero at a critical value; that critical value is the Hausdorff dimension:

dimH(X) = inf{ s ≥ 0 : Hs(X) = 0 } = sup{ s : Hs(X) = ∞ }.

Properties

  • The Hausdorff dimension of a smooth n-dimensional surface is n, agreeing with intuition.
  • It is countably stable: the dimension of a countable union of sets is the supremum of their dimensions.
  • It is invariant under bi-Lipschitz maps, but can decrease under Lipschitz maps.
  • Hausdorff dimension is always at least the topological (Lebesgue covering) dimension, and at most the ambient dimension.
  • It is greater than or equal to the lower box-counting dimension in general comparisons, and equal to the box-counting dimension for many self-similar sets.

Examples

  • The middle-thirds Cantor set has Hausdorff dimension log 2 / log 3 ≈ 0.631.
  • The Sierpiński triangle has dimension log 3 / log 2 ≈ 1.585.
  • The Koch curve has dimension log 4 / log 3 ≈ 1.262.
  • Brownian motion in the plane almost surely has Hausdorff dimension 2.
  • The boundary of the Mandelbrot set has Hausdorff dimension 2 (Shishikura, 1991).

Self-similar sets and the open set condition

For a self-similar set generated by similarities with contraction ratios r1, …, rm satisfying the open set condition, the Hausdorff dimension equals the unique s solving Σ ris = 1 — the similarity dimension. Most classical fractals fall into this class.
