Autoregressive integrated moving average


Image placeholder: ARIMA forecast plot

In statistics and econometrics, and in particular in time series analysis, an autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA) model is a generalisation of an autoregressive moving average (ARMA) model. Both types of model are fitted to time series data either to better understand the data or to predict future points (forecasting).

Definition

An ARIMA model is specified by three orders, (p, d, q): p is the order of the autoregressive part (the number of lagged values used), d is the degree of differencing applied to make the series stationary (the "integrated" part), and q is the order of the moving-average part (the number of lagged forecast errors). Differencing subtracts consecutive observations to remove trends; a series differenced d times is modelled as ARMA(p, q).

Box–Jenkins methodology

George Box and Gwilym Jenkins popularised the approach in Time Series Analysis: Forecasting and Control (1970). Model building proceeds iteratively: identification (using autocorrelation and partial autocorrelation plots and stationarity tests), estimation (usually by maximum likelihood), and diagnostic checking of residuals. An extension, SARIMA (seasonal ARIMA), adds seasonal orders (P, D, Q) at the seasonal period.

Applications

ARIMA models are widely used in economic forecasting, inventory control, and signal processing. Extensions include ARIMAX (with exogenous regressors), vector autoregression (VAR) for multivariate series, and hybrid approaches combining ARIMA with machine-learning methods.
