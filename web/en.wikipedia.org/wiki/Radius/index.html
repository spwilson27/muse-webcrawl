Radius

In classical geometry, a radius (plural: radii) of a circle or sphere is any of the line segments from the centre to the perimeter, and in more modern usage also the length of any such segment. The name comes from the Latin radius, meaning ray but also the spoke of a chariot wheel.

Properties

The radius is half the diameter: d = 2r. The circumference of a circle is 2πr and its area is πr². For a sphere, the surface area is 4πr² and the volume is (4/3)πr³.

Generalizations

The concept generalizes to other shapes: the radius of a regular polygon is the distance from the centre to a vertex (its circumradius), and in graph theory the radius of a graph is the minimum eccentricity over all vertices.
