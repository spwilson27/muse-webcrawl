Deller Consort

The Deller Consort, pioneers of the early-music revival

The Deller Consort was an English early-music vocal ensemble founded in 1948 by the countertenor Alfred Deller. It was among the first groups devoted to performing Renaissance and Baroque vocal music with an emphasis on historically informed style, and it played a major role in the post-war early music revival.

History

Deller formed the consort after Michael Tippett invited him to sing Purcell's Welcome to all the pleasures. The ensemble specialized in English madrigals, lute songs, and the music of Henry Purcell, John Dowland and their contemporaries, touring widely and making many influential recordings, including complete madrigal collections. After Alfred Deller's death in 1979, the group continued under the direction of his son, Mark Deller, until 1992.

Legacy

The Deller Consort helped establish the countertenor voice and small vocal-ensemble performance as fixtures of concert life, and its recordings contributed greatly to renewed interest in pre-Classical repertoire.
