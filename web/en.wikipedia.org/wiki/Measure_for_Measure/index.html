Measure for Measure

Measure for Measure is a play by William Shakespeare, believed to have been written in 1603 or 1604 and first performed in 1604. It was published in the First Folio of 1623, where it was listed as a comedy, though its tone and themes have led modern critics to describe it as one of Shakespeare's "problem plays". The play's main themes include justice, mercy, and the tension between corruption and purity, epitomized in the biblical title phrase about judgment.

Plot

Vincentio, Duke of Vienna, announces that he is leaving the city and deputes the strict Angelo to govern in his absence. Angelo revives long-dormant laws against fornication and sentences Claudio to death for impregnating his fiancée Juliet. Claudio's sister Isabella, a novice nun, pleads for mercy; Angelo offers to spare Claudio if Isabella will yield her virginity to him. The Duke, who has not in fact left Vienna but disguised himself as a friar, engineers a "bed trick" in which Angelo's former betrothed Mariana takes Isabella's place. In the final act the Duke returns, exposes Angelo, pardons the condemned, and disposes multiple marriages, proposing to Isabella herself — to which the text gives her no reply.

Sources and themes

The plot derives from Cinthio's Hecatommithi (1565) and George Whetstone's play Promos and Cassandra (1578). Critics debate whether the play advocates mercy over strict justice or offers a darker view of power, sexuality, and coercion; its ambiguous ending and moral complexities have made it a frequent subject of modern reinterpretation.

References: Shakespeare, Measure for Measure (First Folio, 1623); Oxford and Cambridge editions with critical introductions.
