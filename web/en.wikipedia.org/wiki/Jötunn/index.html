Jötunn

A jötunn (Old Norse: jǫtunn; plural jötnar) is a type of being in Germanic mythology. In Norse mythology the jötnar are often contrasted with the gods — the Æsir and Vanir — though they are not always enemies: gods and jötnar intermarry, and several gods, including Odin and Thor's mother Jörð, are of jötunn descent.

The jötnar dwell primarily in Jötunheimr, one of the Nine Worlds, a land of mountains, forests and winter. They include the frost and fire giants (such as Surtr and the hrímþursar) as well as figures of great wisdom, like Mímir and Vafþrúðnir. The first jötunn, Ymir, was formed from primordial ice, and the world was created from his body.

At Ragnarök the jötnar are prophesied to fight against the gods. The word survives in English as "ettin" and in Scandinavian place names, and the beings are commonly rendered as "giants" in translation, though their size varies greatly in the sources.
