Deekshabhoomi is a sacred monument of Navayana Buddhism in Nagpur, Maharashtra, India, where B. R. Ambedkar, the architect of the Indian Constitution, embraced Buddhism together with about 600,000 of his followers on 14 October 1956, Ashoka Vijaya Dashami. It is the largest stupa in Asia and a major pilgrimage centre for Buddhists, especially followers of Ambedkar. The word deeksha means the act of ordaining or initiating, and bhoomi means ground, so Deekshabhoomi is "the ground where people got ordained to Buddhism."

History

Ambedkar had declared in 1935 that he would not die a Hindu, and after two decades of study he chose Nagpur for the conversion ceremony. The site was chosen, he explained, because Nagpur was the historic centre of the Naga people and of early Buddhism in the region. Ambedkar died less than two months after the conversion, on 6 December 1956. The memorial society formed to commemorate the event built a huge hollow stupa on the site; construction began in 1978 and the monument was inaugurated on 18 December 2001 by the President of India, K. R. Narayanan.

Celebrations

Every year on Ashoka Vijaya Dashami (Dussehra), hundreds of thousands of Ambedkarite Buddhists gather at Deekshabhoomi to commemorate the 1956 conversion, making it one of the largest annual Buddhist gatherings in the world. The urns containing Ambedkar's ashes are enshrined at the site, and a Bodhi tree grown from a sapling of the Mahabodhi tree at Bodh Gaya stands beside the stupa.
