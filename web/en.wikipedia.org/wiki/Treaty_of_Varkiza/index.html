Treaty of Varkiza

Signed12 February 1945
LocationVarkiza, near Athens, Greece
PartiesGreek government; EAM (National Liberation Front)
EffectEnd of the Dekemvriana clashes; disarmament of ELAS

Athens in 1945, weeks after the fighting the Varkiza agreement ended.

The Treaty of Varkiza (also known as the Varkiza Agreement) was signed on 12 February 1945 between the Greek Minister of Foreign Affairs and the leadership of the left-wing National Liberation Front (EAM), its military wing ELAS having clashed with government and British forces during the Dekemvriana events of December 1944.

Background

Following the liberation of Greece from Axis occupation in October 1944, tensions between the EAM-led resistance and the returning government of national unity, backed by Britain, erupted into open fighting in Athens in December 1944 — the Dekemvriana. After weeks of street battles, both sides agreed to a ceasefire in January 1945, and negotiations opened at the seaside suburb of Varkiza.

Terms

The agreement provided for the complete disarmament of ELAS and all other armed resistance formations, an amnesty for political offences committed during the December events, a plebiscite on the monarchy, followed by free elections, and the purging of collaborators from the state apparatus. EAM accepted the terms expecting constitutional politics to follow.

Aftermath

In practice, implementation was one-sided. While ELAS handed over its weapons, right-wing paramilitary groups launched a campaign of persecution of former resistance members known as the "White Terror." The promised purge of collaborators largely failed to materialize. The breakdown of the Varkiza settlement contributed directly to the outbreak of the full-scale Greek Civil War in 1946, which lasted until 1949.
