Middle Persian

Middle Persian, also known as Pahlavi, is the Middle Iranian language of southwestern Iran that served as the official and literary language of the Sasanian Empire (224–651 AD). It developed from Old Persian, the language of the Achaemenids, and is the direct ancestor of modern Persian (Farsi), as well as of the literary forms Dari and Tajik. The name "Pahlavi" properly refers to the writing systems used for the language, derived from Aramaic script.

Language

Compared with Old Persian, Middle Persian had undergone extensive simplification: most of the old case system disappeared, grammatical gender was lost, and the complex Old Persian verb system was reduced, with new periphrastic constructions. Its vocabulary, however, is heavily masked in writing by the use of Aramaic heterograms — words written in Aramaic but read aloud as their Persian equivalents, a feature inherited from Achaemenid scribal practice. The Book Pahlavi script, with only fourteen letter shapes for many more sounds, is notoriously ambiguous, and later Zoroastrian scribes devised the phonetic Avestan-based Pazend transcription to clarify texts.

Literature

Middle Persian literature is dominated by Zoroastrian religious works, much of it compiled in the 9th–10th centuries from older material: the Denkard, an encyclopaedia of the faith; the Bundahishn, on cosmology and creation; and the Arda Wiraz Namag, describing a visionary journey through heaven and hell. Secular works include the romance Vis o Ramin tradition (via later versions), the Book of the Deeds of Ardashir, wisdom (andarz) literature, and the probable source of the frame tales that flowed through Arabic and Persian into world literature. Manichaean Middle Persian texts, written in a clearer script, survive from Central Asian sites such as Turfan.

Legacy

After the Arab conquest, Middle Persian evolved into New Persian, written in Arabic script and enriched with Arabic vocabulary, by around the 9th–10th centuries. Zoroastrian communities in Iran and the Parsi diaspora in India preserved the Pahlavi books as scripture and scholarship.
