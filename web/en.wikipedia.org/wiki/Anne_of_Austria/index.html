Anne of Austria (Spanish: Ana María Mauricia; 22 September 1601 – 20 January 1666) was Queen of France as the wife of King Louis XIII, and regent of France during the minority of her son, Louis XIV, from 1643 to 1651. An Infanta of Spain and Portugal by birth, and an Archduchess of Austria by her Habsburg descent, she married Louis XIII in 1615 as part of a Franco-Spanish double marriage alliance.

Queen consort

The marriage was distant and marked by political tension during the Thirty Years' War, in which France opposed her Spanish Habsburg family. Her friendship with the Duke of Buckingham and the affair of the diamond studs — dramatised in Alexandre Dumas's The Three Musketeers, where she is a central figure — reflected real court intrigues. After 23 years of marriage and several miscarriages, she gave birth to Louis XIV in 1638, followed by Philippe in 1640.

Regency

On Louis XIII's death in 1643, Anne had his will — which limited her powers — annulled by the Parlement of Paris and became sole regent. She governed with Cardinal Mazarin as chief minister, guiding France through the final phase of the Thirty Years' War, the Peace of Westphalia (1648), and the civil wars of the Fronde (1648–1653), which briefly forced the court to flee Paris. Her regency formally ended with Louis XIV's majority in 1651, though she remained influential until Mazarin's death in 1661.

Death

Anne retired to the Val-de-Grâce abbey church she had founded in gratitude for the birth of her son, and died of breast cancer in 1666.
