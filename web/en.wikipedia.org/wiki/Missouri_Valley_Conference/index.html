The Missouri Valley Conference (MVC), commonly called "the Valley", is a collegiate athletic conference affiliated with NCAA Division I. Founded in 1907, it is the second-oldest NCAA Division I athletic conference in the United States, after the Big Ten. Its members are located primarily in the Midwest, and its headquarters are in St. Louis, Missouri.

History

The conference was founded as the Missouri Valley Intercollegiate Athletic Association in Kansas City in 1907 by schools including Kansas, Missouri, Nebraska, Iowa and Drake. It split in 1928, with most large state schools forming what became the Big Eight (later Big 12). The Valley re-established itself around private and regional universities and became famous in basketball: Cincinnati's Oscar Robertson, Wichita State teams, and later Bradley, Creighton and Loyola Chicago — which reached the 2018 Final Four — carried its banner. Football was sponsored until 1985; the separate Missouri Valley Football Conference now operates as a related but legally distinct league.

Members and tournaments

As of 2022–23, members include Belmont, Bradley, Drake, Evansville, Illinois State, Indiana State, Missouri State, Murray State, Northern Iowa, Southern Illinois, UIC and Valparaiso. The men's basketball tournament, held in St. Louis since 1991 and marketed as "Arch Madness", is one of the oldest conference tournaments in the country and the league's signature event.
