Ezhou is a prefecture-level city in eastern Hubei Province, China. It lies on the southern bank of the Yangtze River, east of the provincial capital Wuhan and west of Huangshi. Ezhou is one of the smallest prefecture-level cities in China by area but has a long history and a dense concentration of lakes.

History

The area was the capital region of the state of E in antiquity, from which the city takes its name. During the Three Kingdoms period, Sun Quan, founder of Eastern Wu, made Wuchang (modern Ezhou) his capital for a time. The Guanyin Pavilion, built on a rock in the Yangtze in the Yuan dynasty, is a famous landmark.

Geography and administration

Ezhou administers three districts: Echeng, Huarong, and Liangzihu. The city is known as a "city of a hundred lakes," including Liangzi Lake, one of Hubei's largest freshwater lakes.

Economy and transport

Steel, building materials, pharmaceuticals, and textiles are major industries. Ezhou Huahu Airport, opened in 2022, is a major cargo hub—Asia's first dedicated freight airport—anchored by SF Airlines, complementing the city's river ports and rail links in the Wuhan metropolitan area.
