Svante Paabo

Svante Paabo (unaccented rendering of Svante Pääbo; born 20 April 1955) is a Swedish geneticist specialising in evolutionary genetics, and one of the founders of paleogenetics. He was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 2022 "for his discoveries concerning the genomes of extinct hominins and human evolution".

Work

Paabo developed methods for extracting and sequencing ancient DNA from archaeological and paleontological remains, beginning with Egyptian mummies in the 1980s. At the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology in Leipzig, which he joined in 1997, his team published the first draft sequence of the Neanderthal genome in 2010, demonstrating that Neanderthals interbred with the ancestors of present-day non-African humans. DNA from a Siberian finger bone the same year revealed a previously unknown hominin group, the Denisovans, who also contributed ancestry to modern populations, particularly in Oceania and Asia.

Note on the name

The surname is natively spelled Pääbo in Swedish; the unaccented form "Paabo" appears in databases and media that do not support the ä character. His mother was the Estonian chemist Karin Pääbo, and his father was the biochemist Sune Bergström, himself a Nobel laureate.
