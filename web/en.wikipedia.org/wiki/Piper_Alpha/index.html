Piper Alpha

Piper Alpha was an oil platform located in the North Sea about 120 miles (190 km) north-east of Aberdeen, Scotland. It was operated by Occidental Petroleum (Caledonia) Limited and began production in 1976, initially as an oil-only platform, later converted to gas production.

The disaster

On 6 July 1988, a series of explosions and fires destroyed Piper Alpha. Of the 226 men aboard, 167 were killed, making it the world's deadliest offshore oil disaster. The disaster began with a leak of gas condensate from a pump whose pressure safety valve had been removed for maintenance; a missing blank flange and a breakdown in the permit-to-work system allowed the pump to be started. Subsequent explosions ruptured risers carrying gas and oil from neighbouring platforms, which continued to feed the fire because those platforms were not shut down in time.

Cullen Inquiry

The public inquiry led by Lord Cullen produced a landmark report in 1990 that criticised Occidental's safety management and the regulatory regime. Its 106 recommendations transformed offshore safety regulation in the UK, introducing the safety case regime and transferring regulatory responsibility to the Health and Safety Executive.

Legacy

A memorial stands in Aberdeen's Hazlehead Park. The disaster remains a central case study in process safety and organisational failure, and 6 July is commemorated annually by the offshore industry.
