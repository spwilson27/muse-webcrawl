Likert scale

A Likert scale (pronounced "lick-urt") is a psychometric scale commonly used in questionnaires to measure attitudes or opinions. Respondents specify their level of agreement or disagreement on a symmetric agree–disagree scale for a series of statements. The scale is named after its inventor, psychologist Rensis Likert, who introduced it in 1932.

Format

A typical five-point item runs: strongly disagree, disagree, neither agree nor disagree, agree, strongly agree. The responses to several items may be summed to form a scale score, and individual items are properly called Likert items, the sum being the Likert scale in the strict sense.

Analysis

There is longstanding debate about whether Likert data are ordinal or interval, and hence whether means and parametric statistics are appropriate. Common practice treats summed scores as interval-level, while item-level data are often analyzed with medians, modes, and non-parametric tests.
