Unique Forms of Continuity in Space

Unique Forms of Continuity in Space (Italian: Forme uniche della continuità nello spazio) is a 1913 bronze Futurist sculpture by the Italian artist Umberto Boccioni. It depicts a human-like figure apparently in motion, with surfaces stretched and fragmented by the wind of its own speed — an attempt to capture dynamism rather than a fixed pose. It is considered a masterpiece of Futurist sculpture.

Boccioni's striding figure, modelled in 1913.

Creation and casts

Boccioni modelled the work in plaster in 1913; he died in 1916 without casting it in bronze. Several bronze casts were made posthumously, beginning in 1931, and are now held by major museums including the Museum of Modern Art (MoMA) in New York, the Metropolitan Museum of Art, and Tate Modern in London. The original plaster is in the Museu de Arte Contemporânea in São Paulo, Brazil.

Interpretation

The figure strides forward on two block-like feet, its body merging with flame-like and sail-like forms that suggest speed, machinic power, and the Futurist celebration of modernity expressed in F. T. Marinetti's 1909 Futurist Manifesto. Boccioni's own writings on "plastic dynamism" argued that sculpture should render the interaction of an object with its surrounding space rather than a closed, static form.

Cultural presence

The sculpture is depicted on the obverse of the Italian-issue 20 cent euro coin. It remains one of the most reproduced images of early modernist sculpture.
