The Twentieth Dynasty of Egypt (notated Dynasty XX, alternatively 20th Dynasty or Dynasty 20) is the third and last dynasty of the Ancient Egyptian New Kingdom period, lasting from 1189 BC to 1077 BC. The dynasty was founded by Setnakhte, and its best-known ruler is Ramesses III, often considered the last great pharaoh of the New Kingdom.

History

Setnakhte seized the throne after the chaos at the end of the Nineteenth Dynasty, founding a dynasty whose rulers all took the name Ramesses. His son Ramesses III (reigned c. 1186–1155 BC) defended Egypt against invasions by the Libyans and the Sea Peoples, commemorated in the reliefs of his mortuary temple at Medinet Habu. The Battle of the Delta (c. 1175 BC) is among the earliest naval battles recorded in detail.

The later years of Ramesses III were troubled: the first recorded labour strike in history took place in his reign, when the royal tomb-builders of Deir el-Medina went unpaid, and he was eventually assassinated in the harem conspiracy described in the Judicial Papyrus of Turin.

Decline

Ramesses III's successors, Ramesses IV through XI, presided over a steady decline. Civil unrest, tomb robbery trials, corruption, food shortages and war with Libyan tribes weakened the state. Power increasingly passed to the High Priests of Amun at Thebes. By the reign of Ramesses XI, the High Priest Herihor effectively ruled the south, and with the death of Ramesses XI around 1077 BC the New Kingdom ended and the Third Intermediate Period began.

Pharaohs

The pharaohs of the Twentieth Dynasty are: Setnakhte, Ramesses III, Ramesses IV, Ramesses V, Ramesses VI, Ramesses VII, Ramesses VIII, Ramesses IX, Ramesses X and Ramesses XI.
