National Rail is the generic term and brand used to promote and coordinate passenger railway services in Great Britain. It is not a company itself; services are run by train operating companies, while the brand, journey planner and timetable information are managed through the Rail Delivery Group.

History

Following the privatisation of British Rail in the mid-1990s, the National Rail brand was introduced in 1999 to give passengers a single point of reference for the fragmented network. Its double-arrow logo derives from the British Rail symbol and appears at stations and on signage across the network.

Services

National Rail Enquiries provides timetables, live departure information, fare details and disruption news via its website, apps and phone service. Most tickets sold under the brand are inter-available between operators on a given route, and national conditions of travel govern their use.

Distinctions

The brand covers England, Scotland and Wales but not Northern Ireland, whose railways operate as NI Railways. London Underground, light rail and heritage railways are likewise outside the brand.
