Keto–enol tautomerism

TypeTautomerism (prototropy)
Keto formCarbonyl compound (ketone or aldehyde)
Enol formAlkene with a hydroxyl group on a doubly bonded carbon
RequirementAn α-hydrogen on the carbonyl compound

Keto–enol tautomerism is a chemical equilibrium between a keto form — a ketone or aldehyde — and an enol, an alcohol with a carbon–carbon double bond adjacent to the hydroxyl-bearing carbon. The two forms are tautomers: they interconvert by the migration of a proton (an α-hydrogen) and the shifting of bonding electrons, a process catalysed by traces of acid or base.

The equilibrium

For simple carbonyl compounds such as acetone, the keto form is overwhelmingly favoured (the enol content is a tiny fraction of a percent), because the C=O bond is stronger than the C=C bond formed in the enol. The enol proportion rises when the enol is stabilised, for example by conjugation or intramolecular hydrogen bonding: in β-dicarbonyl compounds such as acetylacetone and ethyl acetoacetate, the enol can predominate, especially in non-polar solvents.

Importance

Although usually a minor species, the enol is often the reactive partner: α-halogenation, racemisation at α-stereocentres, aldol chemistry and enolate formation all proceed through enol or enol-like intermediates. Keto–enol tautomerism also matters in biochemistry — for instance in the isomerisation steps of glycolysis and in the tautomeric forms of the DNA bases, which can cause mispairing.
