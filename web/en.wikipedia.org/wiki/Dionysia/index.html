Dionysia

Dionysia
Location: Athens, ancient Greece
Deity: Dionysus
Main events: Tragedy and comedy competitions
Related: Dionysus, Theatre of Dionysus

The Dionysia was a large ancient Greek festival in honor of the god Dionysus, the central event of which was the performance of tragedies and, later, comedies.

City Dionysia

The City or Great Dionysia, held in Athens in the month Elaphebolion (roughly late March), drew visitors from across the Greek world. It included processions, sacrifices, dithyrambic choruses and dramatic competitions at the Theatre of Dionysus on the Acropolis slope.

Drama

Playwrights such as Aeschylus, Sophocles, Euripides and Aristophanes competed for prizes. Three tragedians each presented three tragedies and a satyr play; comic poets presented one comedy each.

Rural Dionysia

Smaller local celebrations, the Rural Dionysia, were held in winter in Attic villages, with processions and plays.
