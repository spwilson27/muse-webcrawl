Opinion poll

An opinion poll, often simply referred to as a survey or a poll, is a human research survey of public opinion from a particular sample. Opinion polls are usually designed to represent the opinions of a population by conducting a series of questions and then extrapolating generalities in ratio or within confidence intervals.

Methods

Polling uses probability sampling so that results can be generalised to a wider population, with a quantifiable margin of error. Interviews may be conducted by telephone, online, by mail, or in person. Wording and ordering of questions can significantly affect results.

History

Scientific polling emerged in the 1930s with firms such as Gallup, which correctly predicted the 1936 US presidential election while the widely-read Literary Digest straw poll failed.

Accuracy

Modern polls face challenges from falling response rates, the difficulty of reaching mobile-only households, and late swings in voter intention.
