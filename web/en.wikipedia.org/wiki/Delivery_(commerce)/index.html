Delivery (commerce)

Delivery is the process of transporting goods from a source location to a predefined destination. Cargo (physical goods) are primarily delivered via roads and railroads on land, shipping lanes on the sea, and airline networks in the air. Certain specialised goods may be delivered via other networks, such as pipelines for liquid goods, power grids for electrical energy, and computer networks such as the Internet for information goods.

The process of bringing goods from a manufacturing plant or a warehouse to a retail store or directly to a customer's home is a central part of logistics and supply chain management. Delivery to the final customer is often referred to as the last mile, and is typically the most expensive and complex leg of the journey.

History

Organised delivery is ancient: courier systems such as the Persian angareion and the Roman cursus publicus moved messages and goods across empires, and national postal services from the 19th century onwards made parcel delivery a routine commercial service. The 20th century added integrated express carriers such as FedEx and UPS, and the growth of e-commerce from the 1990s transformed consumer delivery expectations, culminating in same-day and even sub-hourly services in dense urban areas.

Types of delivery

  • Postal and parcel delivery — letters and packages carried by postal operators and courier companies.
  • Freight delivery — bulk and palletised goods moved by truck, train, ship or aircraft.
  • Food delivery — prepared meals transported from restaurants to customers, increasingly organised through app-based platforms.
  • Home delivery — groceries, furniture and other goods delivered directly to residences, often in scheduled time slots.
  • Digital delivery — software, media and documents transmitted electronically rather than physically.

Last mile

The last mile presents particular challenges: many small stops, failed delivery attempts, returns, and high customer expectations for speed and tracking. Responses include parcel lockers and pickup points, route optimisation software, delivery by bicycle in city centres, and experiments with drones and autonomous vehicles.
