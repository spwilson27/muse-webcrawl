Northern Fleet

The Northern Fleet (Russian: Северный флот, Severnyy flot) is the Russian Navy's fleet responsible for the Arctic and for defence of Russia's northwestern maritime approaches in the Barents Sea, White Sea, and the Atlantic and Arctic oceans. Its headquarters is at Severomorsk, near Murmansk on the Kola Peninsula. It is the youngest of Russia's fleets and the most powerful in strategic terms, operating the bulk of the navy's nuclear-powered ballistic missile submarines.

History

The fleet traces its origins to the Arctic Ocean Flotilla formed during the First World War to protect convoys into Murmansk and Arkhangelsk. The modern Northern Fleet dates from 1933, when a northern naval flotilla was established, becoming a full fleet in 1937. During the Second World War it protected Allied Arctic convoys bringing Lend-Lease supplies to the Soviet Union.

During the Cold War the fleet grew into the Soviet Navy's principal force, home to its nuclear submarine fleet operating from bases along the Kola coast such as Gadzhiyevo, Polyarny, and Zapadnaya Litsa. After the collapse of the Soviet Union the fleet contracted sharply, but it retained its strategic role. In 2010 it became the core of the Northern Fleet Joint Strategic Command, and in 2021 it was granted the status of a separate military district.

Composition

The fleet's major surface units have included the aircraft carrier Admiral Kuznetsov and the nuclear-powered battlecruiser Pyotr Velikiy, its flagship, together with Slava- and Udaloy-class cruisers and destroyers. Its submarine force comprises Borei- and Delta IV-class ballistic missile submarines and Yasen-, Oscar-, and Akula-class attack submarines, supported by naval aviation and coastal defence troops. The fleet is heavily involved in Russian activity along the Northern Sea Route.
