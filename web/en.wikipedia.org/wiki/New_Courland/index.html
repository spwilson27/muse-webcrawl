New Courland

New Courland (Neu-Kurland) was the name of the colony established by the Duchy of Courland and Semigallia on the island of Tobago in the Caribbean. Founded in 1654, it was one of the smallest colonial ventures of early modern Europe and a notable episode in the history of Couronian colonization of the Americas.

History

Duke Jacob Kettler of Courland, who had built up a substantial merchant fleet, sent an expedition that arrived at Tobago in May 1654 and founded the settlement of Jacobsstadt (present-day Plymouth). The colonists built a fort, Fort Jacobus, and began cultivating tobacco and sugar.

The colony struggled from the start. A rival Dutch settlement was established on the island the same year, and Courland, weakened by war with Sweden — during which the duke himself was taken prisoner — could not reliably supply or defend it. The settlers surrendered to the Dutch in 1659, and although Courland's claim lingered on paper and colonists briefly returned, the colony effectively ended in the following decades as Tobago passed among Dutch, French and British hands.
