Kinbasket Lake is a reservoir on the Columbia River in southeastern British Columbia, Canada, created by the completion of the Mica Dam in 1973 under the terms of the Columbia River Treaty. The reservoir extends along two flooded valley reaches: the Columbia reach, running northwest from Mica Dam, and the Canoe reach, running southeast along the former Canoe River valley, meeting near Valemount.

Name and history

The name Kinbasket derives from a 19th-century chief of the Shuswap (Secwepemc) people who lived in the region. The original Kinbasket Lake was a much smaller natural lake on the Columbia; the reservoir drowned it along with low-lying settlements and the historic Big Bend Highway corridor when the waters rose after the dam's completion. The lake lies within the traditional territories of the Secwepemc, Ktunaxa, and other First Nations, and its flooding is the subject of ongoing historical and compensation discussions.

Geography

The reservoir is long and narrow, bounded by the Monashee and Selkirk mountains on the Columbia reach and by the Rocky Mountain Trench on the Canoe reach. It stores water for power generation at Mica Dam and the downstream Revelstoke Dam, and for flood control and power benefits in the United States under the Treaty. The surrounding area is sparsely populated, with the village of Valemount near its northern end.
