Hardcore mode

In video games, a hardcore mode or hardcore character is a game mode or character option in which the player character's death is permanent: once the character dies, the playthrough ends and the save file or character is deleted, a mechanic known as permadeath. Hardcore modes typically offer no additional lives, respawns, or reloads, and are offered as an optional, higher-stakes way to play.

History

Permadeath originated in early roguelike games of the 1980s, such as Rogue (1980) and NetHack, in which starting over after death was the standard mode of play. The term "hardcore" for a permadeath character was popularised by Blizzard Entertainment's Diablo II (2000), which allowed players to create hardcore characters whose deaths were final; their ghosts could chat with other players but the character could never be played again. Blizzard continued the feature in Diablo III and Diablo IV, and in 2023 launched dedicated Hardcore realms for World of Warcraft Classic.

Notable implementations

  • Minecraft includes a Hardcore mode (added 2011) that locks the difficulty to Hard and deletes the entire world when the player dies; in multiplayer, dead players are placed in spectator mode.
  • Terraria offers hardcore characters who drop all items and become ghosts on death.
  • Path of Exile runs hardcore leagues in which dead characters are transferred to the standard league rather than deleted.
  • XCOM and other strategy games offer "ironman" modes that enforce a single autosave, preventing save-scumming, sometimes combined with permadeath of individual soldiers.
  • Old School RuneScape offers Hardcore Ironman mode, in which a single death downgrades the account to a regular ironman account.

Player culture

Hardcore modes appeal to players seeking tension and lasting consequences, and they are popular with streamers and challenge runners. Entire community events and races have been organised around hardcore playthroughs, and some games award special achievements, titles, or cosmetic rewards for hardcore completion. Critics note that deaths caused by lag or disconnections rather than player error are a persistent frustration in online hardcore modes.
