Linderhof Palace

Location: Ettal, Bavaria, Germany
Architect: Georg von Dollmann
Built: 1869–1886
Commissioned by: Ludwig II of Bavaria

Linderhof Palace (German: Schloss Linderhof) is a palace in southwest Bavaria near the village of Ettal. It is the smallest of the three palaces built by King Ludwig II of Bavaria and the only one which he lived to see completed, in 1886. Ludwig called the palace "Meicost Ettal"; the name Linderhof derives from a mighty linden tree (Linde) that stood near the original hunting lodge on the site.

Description

The palace, built in an ornate French Rococo revival style inspired by Versailles and the Petit Trianon, is lavishly decorated with scenes celebrating absolutist monarchy, the Bourbon kings, and Wagnerian themes. Notable rooms include the Hall of Mirrors and the dining room with its "magic table" (Tischlein deck dich), which could be lowered to the kitchens below so the king could dine without seeing servants. Ludwig in fact used Linderhof as his favourite retreat and spent much of his later reign there.

Park

The formal gardens, designed by court garden director Carl von Effner, combine Renaissance and Baroque parterres with an English landscape park. Features include the Venus Grotto — an artificial cave with a lake, shell-shaped boat, and coloured electric lighting (among the earliest in Bavaria), built for private performances of Wagner's operas — the Moorish Kiosk, and the Moroccan House.

In 2025 Linderhof was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List together with Ludwig II's other palaces, Neuschwanstein, Herrenchiemsee, and Schachen.
