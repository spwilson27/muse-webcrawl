The Indian National Congress (INC), colloquially known as the Congress Party or simply the Congress, is a political party in India with deep roots in the Indian independence movement. Founded in 1885, it was the first modern nationalist movement to emerge in the British Empire in Asia and Africa, and it became the principal leader of the struggle for independence from the United Kingdom.

History

The Congress was founded in 1885 by Allan Octavian Hume, a retired civil servant, along with Dadabhai Naoroji, Dinshaw Wacha and others; its first session was held in Bombay in December 1885. Under the leadership of Mahatma Gandhi from the 1920s, it transformed into a mass movement employing non-violent civil disobedience, including the Non-Cooperation Movement, the Salt March of 1930, and the Quit India Movement of 1942.

After independence

Following independence in 1947, the Congress became India's dominant political party. Jawaharlal Nehru, India's first prime minister, led it to victories in the first three general elections. The party has produced six prime ministers, including Indira Gandhi, Rajiv Gandhi, P. V. Narasimha Rao, and Manmohan Singh. Nehru's descendants — the Nehru–Gandhi family — have long been central to the party's leadership.

Recent decades

The Congress led the United Progressive Alliance governments of 2004–2014 under Prime Minister Manmohan Singh, introducing measures such as the rural employment guarantee (MGNREGA) and the Right to Information Act. It suffered heavy defeats to the Bharatiya Janata Party in the 2014 and 2019 general elections, recovering partially in 2024 as the largest party of the opposition INDIA alliance, with its leader Rahul Gandhi becoming Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha.
