The 6th Army (German: 6. Armee) was a field army of the German Army during World War II. It is best known for its destruction at the Battle of Stalingrad in the winter of 1942–43, one of the turning points of the war on the Eastern Front.

Early campaigns

Formed from the former 4th Army command, the 6th Army took part in the invasion of Belgium and France in 1940 under Walther von Reichenau. During Operation Barbarossa in 1941 it advanced through Ukraine, fighting at Kiev and Kharkov. Under Reichenau, and later Friedrich Paulus, the army was implicated in war crimes, including the enforcement of the Commissar Order and cooperation with Einsatzgruppen in the murder of Jews, notably at Babi Yar.

Stalingrad

In the summer of 1942 the 6th Army spearheaded the drive into Stalingrad as part of Case Blue. After months of brutal street fighting, the Soviet counteroffensive Operation Uranus in November 1942 encircled the army in a pocket (the Kessel). Forbidden to break out and supplied inadequately by air, the army starved through the winter. Paulus, promoted to field marshal on 30 January 1943 in the expectation he would not surrender, capitulated the following day; the northern pocket surrendered on 2 February. Roughly 91,000 men went into captivity, of whom only about 5,000–6,000 survived to return to Germany.

Reformation

A new 6th Army was formed in March 1943 and fought in Ukraine, Romania, and Hungary before surrendering in Austria at the end of the war.
