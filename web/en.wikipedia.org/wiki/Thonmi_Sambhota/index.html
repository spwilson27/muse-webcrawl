Thonmi Sambhota

Thonmi Sambhota (born 7th century) was a minister of the Tibetan emperor Songtsen Gampo, traditionally credited with inventing the Tibetan script and composing the foundational works of Tibetan grammar, thereby enabling the translation of Buddhist scriptures into Tibetan.

Mission to India

According to tradition, Songtsen Gampo sent Thonmi, son of the minister Thonmi Anu, to India around 632–633 with a party of youths to study languages and devise a script for Tibet. Most of his companions died in the Indian climate, but Thonmi returned having studied Sanskrit under learned teachers.

The Tibetan script and grammar

Thonmi modelled the new script on Indian alphabets — usually identified with a Gupta-derived or Kashmiri form — adapting it to Tibetan phonology with thirty consonants and four vowel signs. Two grammatical treatises are attributed to him, the Sum cu pa (Treatise in Thirty Verses) and the Rtags kyi 'jug pa, which remain the basis of classical Tibetan grammar, although only parts survive and their attribution is debated by scholars.

Legacy

The creation of a written language under Songtsen Gampo and Thonmi Sambhota enabled Tibet's imperial administration and the great translation project that made Tibetan a principal vehicle of Mahayana Buddhism. Thonmi is honoured in Tibet as one of the enlightened ministers of the Dharma king.
