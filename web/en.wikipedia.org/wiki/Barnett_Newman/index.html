Barnett Newman

Barnett Newman (January 29, 1905 – July 4, 1970) was an American artist associated with Abstract Expressionism and a leading figure of color field painting. His work is characterised by large fields of flat colour divided by thin vertical lines he called "zips."

Career

Born in New York City to Polish Jewish immigrant parents, Newman studied philosophy at City College and worked in his father's clothing business before turning fully to art. His breakthrough painting, Onement I (1948), introduced the zip: a single vertical band of colour running down the centre of the canvas, which he described as giving the work its life and unity.

His monumental Vir Heroicus Sublimis (1950–51) measures roughly 2.4 by 5.4 metres. Newman also produced the series The Stations of the Cross (1958–66), fourteen black-and-white canvases, and important sculptures such as Broken Obelisk (1963–67).

Influence

Newman's writings, including the essay "The Sublime is Now" (1948), helped define the ambitions of postwar American painting. His work profoundly influenced later generations of minimal and color field artists.
