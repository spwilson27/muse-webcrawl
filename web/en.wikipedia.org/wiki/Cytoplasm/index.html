Cytoplasm

In cell biology, the cytoplasm is all of the material within a eukaryotic cell, enclosed by the cell membrane, except for the cell nucleus. It consists of the gel-like cytosol, the organelles suspended in it, and various inclusions.

Components

The cytosol is an aqueous solution of salts, proteins, and organic molecules in which metabolic reactions occur. Suspended organelles include mitochondria, the endoplasmic reticulum, the Golgi apparatus, ribosomes, and lysosomes. In prokaryotic cells, which lack a nucleus, the cytoplasm contains the cell's genetic material directly.

Function

The cytoplasm is the site of many metabolic pathways, including glycolysis and protein synthesis, and provides a medium for intracellular transport. Cytoplasmic streaming, observed in many plant and amoeboid cells, circulates nutrients and organelles within the cell.
