Preserved counties of Wales

The preserved counties of Wales are the eight areas of Wales that were the counties created by the Local Government Act 1972 for local government purposes between 1974 and 1996. Since the reorganization of Welsh local government in 1996, they have been retained as areas for ceremonial purposes, in particular as the areas of the Lords Lieutenant and High Sheriffs, and for some statistical and electoral purposes.

The eight counties

  • Clwyd — the north-east, including Wrexham and Flintshire
  • Dyfed — the south-west (Pembrokeshire, Carmarthenshire, Ceredigion)
  • Gwent — the south-east, including Newport
  • Gwynedd — the north-west, including Anglesey
  • Mid Glamorgan — the central South Wales Valleys
  • Powys — the rural mid-Wales county
  • South Glamorgan — including Cardiff and the Vale of Glamorgan
  • West Glamorgan — including Swansea and Neath Port Talbot

Current role

The Local Government (Wales) Act 1994 abolished the eight county councils in 1996 and replaced them with 22 unitary principal areas, but named the former counties as "preserved counties" for lieutenancy and shrievalty. Their boundaries have been slightly adjusted by later orders so that they follow the boundaries of the principal areas.
