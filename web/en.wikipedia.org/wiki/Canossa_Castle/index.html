The ruins of Canossa Castle

Canossa Castle (Italian: Castello di Canossa) is a castle in ruins in the comune of Canossa, in the province of Reggio Emilia, northern Italy. Perched on a rocky outcrop in the Apennine foothills, it is famous as the site of the Walk to Canossa in January 1077, when the Holy Roman Emperor Henry IV stood in penitence in the snow outside the castle to beg forgiveness of Pope Gregory VII.

History

The castle was founded around 940 by Adalbert Atto, founder of the House of Canossa. It passed to the powerful countess Matilda of Tuscany, who made it a centre of her resistance to imperial power during the Investiture Controversy. In 1077, mediated by Matilda and Abbot Hugh of Cluny, Gregory VII received the penitent Henry IV at Canossa and lifted his excommunication — an episode that became a lasting symbol of the conflict between church and state; in German, "going to Canossa" (Gang nach Canossa) remains an idiom for humiliating submission.

Later history and today

The castle was destroyed by the men of Reggio in 1255 and passed through various hands before falling into ruin. Excavations in the 19th and 20th centuries recovered parts of the walls and the church of Sant'Apollonio. The site is now protected as a national monument, with a small museum displaying finds from the excavations.
