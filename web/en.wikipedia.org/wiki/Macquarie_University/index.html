Macquarie University is a public research university in Sydney, New South Wales, Australia. Founded in 1964 by the New South Wales Government, it was the third university established in metropolitan Sydney. The university is named after Lachlan Macquarie, an early Governor of New South Wales.

History

The idea of a third Sydney university was raised in the early 1960s. The New South Wales Government established the university by act of parliament in 1964, and the first students enrolled in 1967. The campus in Macquarie Park was designed on a modern, modular plan set in extensive native parkland, and the area around it developed into a major high-technology and research precinct.

Campus

The 126-hectare campus includes the Macquarie University Hospital, the Australian Hearing Hub, a railway station, a sculpture park and the Macquarie University Library, noted for its automated storage and retrieval system. The university also operates an astronomical observatory and research stations.

Academics

Macquarie is organised into faculties covering arts, business and economics, human sciences, medicine and health sciences, and science and engineering. It is known for research in linguistics, ancient history, earth and planetary sciences, cognitive science and laser physics. The university enrols roughly 40,000 students and offers the Macquarie University International College pathway programs.

Rankings and reputation

Macquarie regularly places within the top 200 universities worldwide in global league tables and is a member of the Association of Commonwealth Universities.
