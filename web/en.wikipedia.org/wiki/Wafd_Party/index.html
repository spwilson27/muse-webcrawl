Wafd Party

The Wafd Party ("Delegation Party"; Arabic: حزب الوفد) was a nationalist liberal political party in Egypt. It was said to be Egypt's most popular and influential political party for a period in the 1920s and 1930s. During this time, it was instrumental in the development of the 1923 constitution, and supported moving Egypt from the dynastic rule of the Muhammad Ali dynasty to a constitutional monarchy.

History

The Wafd was founded in November 1918 when Saad Zaghloul and colleagues asked the British authorities for permission to travel to the Paris Peace Conference to argue Egypt's case for independence. They were refused and Zaghloul was exiled, sparking the Egyptian Revolution of 1919. The party won repeated elections under Zaghloul and his successor Mustafa el-Nahas, governing at intervals until all parties were dissolved after the Egyptian revolution of 1952.

New Wafd

A successor party, the New Wafd Party, was founded in 1978 and remains active in Egyptian politics.
