Charles Curtis

Charles Curtis (January 25, 1860 – February 8, 1936) was an American attorney and Republican politician from Kansas who served as the 31st vice president of the United States from 1929 to 1933 under President Herbert Hoover. A member of the Kaw Nation, he is the highest-ranking enrolled Native American ever to serve in the United States government and the only vice president from Kansas.

Early life

Curtis was born in Topeka to a father of English and Scotch descent and a mother who was part Kaw (Kansa), Osage, and Potawatomi. Orphaned young, he was partly raised by his Kaw grandparents on the Kaw reservation, where he spoke Kansa and French before English. He studied law and was admitted to the bar in 1881.

Congressional career

Curtis was elected to the U.S. House of Representatives in 1892 and to the Senate in 1907, serving, with a brief interruption, until 1929. He became Senate Majority Leader in 1925. In Congress he specialized in Native American affairs; he authored the Curtis Act of 1898, which extended allotment to the "Five Civilized Tribes" and dissolved their tribal governments — legislation now widely viewed as damaging to tribal sovereignty.

Vice presidency

Chosen as Herbert Hoover's running mate in 1928 to balance the ticket, Curtis served one term during the onset of the Great Depression. He and Hoover lost the 1932 election to Franklin D. Roosevelt and John Nance Garner. Curtis remained in Washington, practising law, until his death in 1936.
