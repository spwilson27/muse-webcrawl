The Oath of the Horatii (French: Le Serment des Horaces) is a large painting by the French artist Jacques-Louis David, painted in Rome in 1784 and exhibited in Paris in 1785. It is one of the best-known works in the Neoclassical style and is now held by the Louvre.

Subject

The painting depicts a scene from Roman legend, drawn from Livy and retold in Corneille's play Horace. Rome and Alba Longa, at war, agree to settle the conflict by combat between two sets of triplet brothers: the Roman Horatii and the Alban Curiatii. David shows the moment before battle: the three Horatii brothers salute in unison, their arms outstretched, as their father holds up the three swords they swear to defend Rome with. At the right, the women of the family — bound by marriage and kinship to both sides — collapse in grief, providing an emotional counterweight to the men's rigid civic virtue. No ancient source actually describes such an oath; David invented the scene's central gesture.

Reception and influence

The painting's severe composition, sculptural figures, and message of patriotic self-sacrifice made it an immediate sensation and a rallying point for Neoclassicism. In the years before the French Revolution its exaltation of duty to the state over private feeling was read as politically charged, and the image later became emblematic of revolutionary virtue. It established David's reputation and remains a landmark of European art history.
