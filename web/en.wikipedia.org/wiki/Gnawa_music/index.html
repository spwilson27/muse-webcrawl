Gnawa music

Gnawa music
Stylistic originsSub-Saharan African ritual music, Sufi traditions
Cultural originsGnawa people, Morocco and Algeria
Typical instrumentsGuembri (sintir), qraqeb, tbal

Gnawa music is a body of Moroccan and Algerian ritual music and ceremony developed by the Gnawa people, descendants of sub-Saharan Africans brought to the Maghreb through historical trade and slavery routes. Centred on the all-night healing and trance ceremony known as the lila (or derdeba), Gnawa music blends African musical and spiritual traditions with Sufi Islam, invoking spirits (mluk) through song, rhythm, dance, colour and incense.

Instruments

The central instrument is the guembri (also called sintir or hajhuj), a three-stringed bass lute carved from a hollowed log and covered with camel skin, played by the master musician, the maalem. Metal castanets called qraqeb provide the characteristic driving rhythm, alongside large drums (tbal) and handclapping. The maalem leads the ceremony, singing invocations in a mixture of Arabic, Bambara, Songhai and other languages.

The lila ceremony

During the lila, suites of songs are performed for a sequence of spirit families, each associated with particular colours, incenses and dances. Participants may enter trance states, and the ceremony serves purposes of healing, thanksgiving and communal gathering.

Modern developments

Since the 1990s, Gnawa music has reached international audiences through fusion with jazz, blues, reggae and electronic music, and through festivals such as the Gnaoua World Music Festival held annually in Essaouira, Morocco, since 1998. Maalems such as Hassan Hakmoun and Mahmoud Guinia became internationally known. In 2019, UNESCO inscribed "Gnawa" on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
