Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs is a 1937 American animated musical fantasy film produced by Walt Disney and released by RKO Radio Pictures. Based on the Brothers Grimm fairy tale "Snow White", it was the first full-length cel-animated feature film and the first animated feature produced in the United States. Doubters in Hollywood nicknamed the project "Disney's Folly", but it became a landmark success that established the animated feature as a major art form.

Plot

Snow White, a princess, is forced to work as a servant by her vain stepmother, the Queen, whose magic mirror declares Snow White the fairest in the land. The Queen orders her Huntsman to kill Snow White, but he cannot bring himself to do it and warns her to flee. Lost in the forest, Snow White befriends woodland animals and finds the cottage of seven dwarfs — Doc, Grumpy, Happy, Sleepy, Bashful, Sneezy and Dopey — who work in a diamond mine and welcome her into their home.

The Queen, learning Snow White lives, transforms herself into an old hag and offers her a poisoned apple. Snow White takes a bite and falls into a sleeping death. The grieving dwarfs place her in a glass coffin in the forest. The Prince, who had fallen in love with her, finds her and kisses her, breaking the spell. The Queen, pursued by the dwarfs during a storm, falls from a cliff to her death. Snow White and the Prince depart together.

Production

Disney began developing the film around 1934, personally supervising story meetings and investing heavily in new technology, notably the multiplane camera, which gave scenes a sense of depth. The budget swelled from an initial $250,000 to nearly $1.5 million, requiring Disney to mortgage his house. Hundreds of artists produced roughly two million drawings, of which about 250,000 were used. The seven dwarfs were given distinct personalities and names — an innovation over the nameless dwarfs of the tale — and Adriana Caselotti, an 18-year-old unknown, provided Snow White's singing and speaking voice.

Music

The songs, by Frank Churchill, Larry Morey and Leigh Harline, include "Someday My Prince Will Come", "Heigh-Ho", "Whistle While You Work" and "I'm Wishing". The soundtrack album was among the first commercially released film soundtrack recordings.

Reception and legacy

Premiering at the Carthay Circle Theatre on December 21, 1937, and released nationally in February 1938, the film became the highest-grossing sound film of its time, earning about $8 million on its initial run. Walt Disney received an honorary Academy Award in 1939 — one full-size statuette and seven miniature ones — presented by Shirley Temple. The film was selected for the United States National Film Registry in 1989 and is credited with launching Disney's feature-animation tradition and the modern animation industry.
