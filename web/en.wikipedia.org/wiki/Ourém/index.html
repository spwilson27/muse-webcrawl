Ourém (Portuguese pronunciation: [owˈɾɐ̃j]) is a municipality in the Santarém District of central Portugal. Its seat, the town of Ourém, sits at the foot of a medieval castle hill about 130 kilometres north of Lisbon. The municipality is best known for containing the sanctuary town of Fátima, one of the most important Roman Catholic pilgrimage sites in the world.

History

Ourém takes its name from the Moorish period; tradition connects it with a Moorish princess named Fátima who converted to Christianity and took the name Oriana (Oureana), from which "Ourém" is said to derive. The medieval castle of Ourém, rebuilt in the 15th century by Dom Afonso, 4th Count of Ourém and grandson of Nuno Álvares Pereira, crowns the old town. The counts of Ourém were a major branch of the House of Braganza.

Fátima

The civil parish of Fátima, in the south of the municipality, became world-famous after the reported Marian apparitions of 1917 to three shepherd children. The Sanctuary of Fátima now receives millions of pilgrims each year, and the parish has grown into a small city rivalling the municipal seat.

Parishes

The municipality is subdivided into 13 civil parishes, including Alburitel, Atouguia, Caxarias, Fátima, Freixanda, Gondemaria e Olival, Matas e Cercal, Nossa Senhora da Piedade, Nossa Senhora das Misericórdias, Rio de Couros e Casal dos Bernardos, Seiça and Urqueira.

Geography

Ourém borders Tomar, Ferreira do Zêzere, Alvaiázere, Pombal, Leiria, Batalha, Alcanena and Torres Novas. The terrain is hilly limestone country on the edge of the Maciço Calcário Estremenho.
