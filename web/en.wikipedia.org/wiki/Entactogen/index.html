Entactogen

Entactogens are a class of psychoactive drugs characterised by effects of emotional openness, empathy, euphoria and a sense of inner touch or communion with oneself and others. The term was proposed in 1986 by the American pharmacologist David E. Nichols, built from roots meaning "touching within", as a neutral scientific alternative to empathogen, which had been coined a few years earlier for the same family of substances. The prototypical entactogen is MDMA (ecstasy); related compounds include MDA, MDEA and various substituted methylenedioxyphenethylamines and amphetamines.

Effects and pharmacology

Entactogens act chiefly by promoting release of the monoamine neurotransmitters serotonin, norepinephrine and dopamine, and blocking their reuptake, with serotonin release predominating. Users typically report heightened empathy, emotional closeness, reduced fear and defensiveness, mild stimulation and enhanced sensory pleasure, without the strong perceptual distortions of classical psychedelics. Adverse effects can include elevated body temperature, jaw clenching, dehydration and a period of low mood ("comedown") in the days after use.

Research and history

MDMA was first synthesised by Merck in 1912 and rediscovered in the 1970s, when therapists including those around chemist Alexander Shulgin used it as an adjunct to psychotherapy. Following widespread recreational use it was placed in the most restrictive drug schedules in the United States in 1985 and internationally soon after. Since the 2010s, clinical trials sponsored by organisations such as MAPS have investigated MDMA-assisted psychotherapy for post-traumatic stress disorder, with large phase 3 trials reporting significant symptom reductions; regulatory decisions on medical approval have remained contentious.

Terminology

Although entactogen and empathogen are often used interchangeably, Nichols proposed "entactogen" to avoid the pathological connotations of the Greek root pathos ("suffering") in "empathogen". The category remains pharmacologically somewhat loose, since many of these compounds also have stimulant and mild psychedelic properties.
