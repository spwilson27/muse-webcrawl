Scuttling of the German fleet at Scapa Flow

On 21 June 1919, the interned ships of the German High Seas Fleet were scuttled by their own crews at Scapa Flow in the Orkney Islands, Scotland, where they had been held since the Armistice of November 1918 pending a decision on their fate at the Paris Peace Conference.

Background

Seventy-four German warships were interned at Scapa Flow under Rear Admiral Ludwig von Reuter, manned by skeleton crews. Fearing that the Allies intended to seize and divide the fleet — especially as the deadline for Germany to sign the Treaty of Versailles approached — Reuter ordered the scuttling using a pre-arranged signal.

The scuttling

Sea cocks were opened, watertight doors wedged, and ensigns hoisted. British guard ships, caught by surprise during exercises, managed to beach a few vessels, but 52 of the 74 ships sank, including 15 of the 16 capital ships. Nine German sailors were killed by British fire — among the last German casualties of the First World War. Most of the wrecks were salvaged in the 1920s and 1930s in one of the largest marine salvage operations ever undertaken; the seven wrecks remaining are popular dive sites and a source of low-background steel.
