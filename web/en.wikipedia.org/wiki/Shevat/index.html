Shevat (Hebrew: שְׁבָט, Šəḇāṭ) is the fifth month of the civil year and the eleventh month of the ecclesiastical year on the Hebrew calendar. It is a winter month of 30 days, usually corresponding to mid-January to mid-February in the Gregorian calendar. The name derives from the Akkadian Šabātu, and the month is mentioned in the Bible in Zechariah 1:7.

Holidays and observances

The best-known observance in Shevat is Tu BiShvat, the 15th of Shevat, designated in Mishnah as the New Year for Trees — the date used for calculating tithes and the age of fruit trees. In modern times Tu BiShvat has become a celebration of ecology and tree planting in Israel, often marked by a seder of fruits, especially the Seven Species of the Land of Israel. The 30th of Shevat is the first day of Rosh Chodesh Adar (or Adar I in leap years).

Events

According to tradition, Moses began his farewell discourse, the book of Deuteronomy, on the 1st of Shevat. The 10th of Shevat (Yud Shevat) is significant in Chabad-Lubavitch tradition as the yahrzeit of the sixth Rebbe, Yosef Yitzchak Schneersohn, and the day his successor Menachem Mendel Schneerson formally accepted leadership in 1951.
