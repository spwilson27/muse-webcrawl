The economy of Norway is a highly developed mixed economy in which the state owns large stakes in strategic sectors, above all petroleum. Norway has one of the world's highest standards of living, with GDP per capita among the global top tier, and channels much of its oil and gas revenue into the Government Pension Fund Global, the world's largest sovereign wealth fund.

Petroleum

Oil was discovered in the Norwegian sector of the North Sea in 1969 (the Ekofisk field), and production began in 1971. Petroleum activities grew to account for a large share of exports, government revenue and GDP. The state participates through direct ownership, the majority state-owned company Equinor, and high taxation of the sector. Norway became Europe's largest supplier of natural gas after 2022.

The sovereign wealth fund

The Government Pension Fund Global, established in 1990 and first funded in 1996, invests petroleum revenues abroad in shares, bonds and property. By the mid-2020s it was worth well over US$1.5 trillion — roughly US$280,000 per Norwegian citizen — and owned on the order of 1.5 percent of all listed shares worldwide. A fiscal rule limits annual budget spending to the fund's expected real return, estimated at 3 percent.

Other sectors

Norway is a major maritime nation with a large merchant fleet and offshore supply industry. It is one of the world's largest exporters of seafood; farmed Atlantic salmon is a signature product. Abundant hydropower supplies nearly all domestic electricity and supports energy-intensive industries such as aluminium and ferroalloys. Agriculture is small and heavily subsidised.

Welfare and labour

Norway operates a comprehensive Nordic welfare state funded by high taxation, with low unemployment, high labour-force participation (including among women), coordinated wage bargaining, and comparatively low income inequality. Statistics Norway and Norges Bank anchor a policy framework emphasising stability; mainland (non-oil) GDP growth is typically modest.
