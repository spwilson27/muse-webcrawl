New Forum (German: Neues Forum) was a political movement in East Germany formed in September 1989, in the months leading up to the collapse of the German Democratic Republic and German reunification. It was the first opposition movement to attempt to gain official recognition, and it became one of the principal vehicles of the Peaceful Revolution.

Founding

On the night of 9–10 September 1989, some thirty dissidents met at the home of Katja Havemann in Grünheide near Berlin and issued the founding appeal "Aufbruch 89 – Neues Forum" ("Awakening 89 – New Forum"), written largely by the lawyer Rolf Henrich and the artist Bärbel Bohley. The appeal declared that communication between state and society in the GDR was evidently disturbed and called for a democratic dialogue about the rule of law, the economy, culture and the environment. It deliberately avoided demanding reunification or a change of system, framing itself instead as a platform open to all citizens.

Within weeks the appeal had gathered some 200,000 signatures. On 19 September 1989 the regime declared New Forum an "anti-state platform" and refused to register it, but popular support was so broad that registration was granted on 8 November — the day before the fall of the Berlin Wall.

Role in the Peaceful Revolution

New Forum helped organise the Monday demonstrations and other protests of autumn 1989 and participated in the Central Round Table talks between the opposition and the government from December 1989. Its leading figures, including the molecular biologist Jens Reich, became nationally known. In the free Volkskammer election of March 1990, New Forum ran as part of Alliance 90 with other civic movements, winning a modest share of the vote as the electorate swung behind parties promising rapid reunification. Many of its members went on to play roles in the merged Alliance 90/The Greens party.
