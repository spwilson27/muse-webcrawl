Hannes Kolehmainen

Juho Pietari "Hannes" Kolehmainen
Born: 9 December 1889, Kuopio, Finland
Died: 11 January 1966, Helsinki
Sport: Long-distance running
Olympic golds: 1912 (5000 m, 10,000 m, cross country), 1920 (marathon)

Juho Pietari "Hannes" Kolehmainen (9 December 1889 – 11 January 1966) was a Finnish four-time Olympic gold medalist and a world record holder in middle- and long-distance running. He was the first in a line of world-class Finnish long-distance runners, often called "the Flying Finns", and is considered the first great star of modern track and field.

Career

At the 1912 Stockholm Olympics, Kolehmainen won the 5000 metres in a famous duel with France's Jean Bouin, setting a world record, and also won the 10,000 metres and the individual cross country. Living and training in the United States, where he worked as a bricklayer, he missed the cancelled 1916 Games, but at Antwerp in 1920 — now representing an independent Finland — he won the marathon. He lit the Olympic flame at the 1952 Helsinki Games together with Paavo Nurmi.
