Happy Mac

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Happy Mac is the normal bootup icon of the classic Macintosh computer. It is a smiling Macintosh computer screen displayed when the machine successfully boots: the hardware self-test has found a bootable disk and the operating system is loading. The icon was designed by Susan Kare, who created most of the original Macintosh icons and fonts, and first appeared with the original Macintosh 128k in 1984.

Sad Mac and successors

Its counterpart, the Sad Mac, showed a Macintosh with a downturned mouth and X-shaped eyes, accompanied by a chime of doom, when a serious hardware or software failure occurred at startup; later models displayed a hexadecimal error code. With the introduction of the Macintosh II and subsequent machines the startup icons evolved; the New World Macs beginning with the iMac replaced the Happy Mac with a Mac OS logo screen, and modern macOS systems show an Apple logo and progress bar.

Cultural presence

The Happy Mac became one of the most recognisable symbols of the Macintosh line and of personal computing generally. A variant opens the startup of the iPod's diagnostic modes, and the icon survives in retro-computing culture, merchandise and Apple's design history. The smiling computer face also inspired the Finder icon used in Mac OS from version 7.6 onward and retained in macOS.
