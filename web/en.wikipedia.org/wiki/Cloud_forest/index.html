Cloud forest

A cloud forest, also called a water forest, primas forest or montane rainforest, is a generally tropical or subtropical, evergreen, montane, moist forest characterised by a persistent, frequent or seasonal low-level cloud cover, usually at canopy level.

BiomeTropical/subtropical moist broadleaf forest
ElevationTypically 500–4,000 m
FeaturePersistent cloud immersion; abundant epiphytes

Characteristics

Trees are shorter and more gnarled than in lowland rainforest, with dense crowns laden with mosses, ferns, orchids and bromeliads. Cloud forests capture water directly from fog — 'horizontal precipitation' — making them vital watersheds. Famous examples include Monteverde in Costa Rica and the elfin forests of Puerto Rico's Luquillo Mountains.

Biodiversity and threats

Though covering only a small fraction of the tropics, cloud forests harbour exceptional endemism, including the resplendent quetzal and many frogs and salamanders. They are threatened by clearing for agriculture and by climate change lifting the cloud base upslope, drying the forest.
