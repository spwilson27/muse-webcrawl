Poynting vector

Poynting vector
SymbolS
Definition (vacuum)S = (1/μ0) E × B
SI unitWatt per square metre (W/m2)
Named afterJohn Henry Poynting

In physics, the Poynting vector (or Umov–Poynting vector) represents the directional energy flux — the energy transfer per unit area per unit time — of an electromagnetic field. It is named after John Henry Poynting, who first derived it in 1884; Nikolay Umov had earlier formulated a similar concept for energy flow in media. The vector's magnitude gives the irradiance of an electromagnetic wave, and its direction is the direction of energy propagation.

In a plane electromagnetic wave, S points along the direction of propagation, perpendicular to both E and B.

Definition

In a vacuum, and generally in terms of the magnetic field strength H:

S = E × H

where E is the electric field. Using the magnetic flux density B in a vacuum, S = (1/μ0) E × B. For a plane wave propagating in free space, the time-averaged magnitude is related to the peak electric field amplitude E0 by <S> = (1/2) c ε0 E02.

Poynting's theorem

Poynting's theorem is the energy-conservation statement for electromagnetic fields: the divergence of the Poynting vector equals the negative rate of change of the electromagnetic energy density minus the rate at which the fields do work on charges:

∇ · S = − ∂u/∂t − J · E

where u is the electromagnetic energy density and J the current density. Integrated over a volume, it says the net power flowing out through the surface equals the decrease in field energy plus the work done on charges inside.

Interpretation and examples

  • In a DC circuit, the Poynting vector shows energy flowing through the space around the wires — not inside the metal — from source to load.
  • A static field configuration (for example, a charged magnet) can have a non-zero Poynting vector forming closed loops; this is connected with the field angular momentum stored in such systems.
  • The solar constant, about 1,361 W/m2 at the top of Earth's atmosphere, is the magnitude of the time-averaged Poynting vector of sunlight.
  • In antennas and radio propagation, the Poynting vector describes radiation patterns and received power density.
