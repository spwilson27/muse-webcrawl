Uniform Probate Code

The Uniform Probate Code (UPC) is a uniform act drafted by the National Conference of Commissioners on Uniform State Laws (Uniform Law Commission) in partnership with the American Bar Association's Real Property, Probate and Trust Law Section, first promulgated in 1969. It seeks to modernize and standardize state laws governing wills, estates, intestacy, and trusts in the United States.

Contents

The Code covers intestate succession (including shares for spouses and descendants, and provisions for omitted spouses and children), wills (execution formalities, holographic wills, revocation, and rules of construction), estate administration (offering informal, unsupervised probate as a simplified alternative to court-supervised proceedings), guardianship and conservatorship, and non-probate transfers such as pay-on-death accounts and transfer-on-death deeds. Significant revisions followed in 1975, 1982, 1990 (particularly for intestacy and spousal elective shares), and 2008.

Adoption

Around eighteen states have enacted the UPC in whole or substantial part, including Colorado, Minnesota, Montana, Nebraska, and North Dakota, while many others have adopted individual provisions. Its simplified informal probate procedures in particular influenced reforms nationwide.
