Nameplate capacity

Nameplate capacity, also known as the rated capacity, nominal capacity, installed capacity, maximum effect or gross capacity, is the intended full-load sustained output of a facility such as a power station, an electric generator, a chemical plant, a fuel plant, a mine or an industrial facility. For dispatchable facilities, this capacity is the maximum output safely and continuously producible at a point in time, as determined by the manufacturer's design ratings, which are usually printed on a nameplate attached to the machine.

Non-dispatchable resources

For non-dispatchable generation such as wind and solar power, nameplate capacity refers to generation under ideal conditions, which are seldom achieved in practice. Actual output varies with weather and time of day; the ratio of actual average output to nameplate capacity is the capacity factor, typically 10–25% for solar photovoltaics and 25–45% for wind.

Capacity versus output

Even for thermal and nuclear plants, sustained output is usually somewhat below nameplate capacity because of maintenance outages, equipment derating in hot weather, and fuel or cooling constraints. Grid operators therefore plan around de-rated or dependable capacity rather than nameplate figures.
