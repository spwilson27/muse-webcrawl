Total angular momentum quantum number

In quantum mechanics, the total angular momentum quantum number, denoted j, characterises the total angular momentum of a particle or system, combining its orbital angular momentum and its intrinsic spin. It arises because spin and orbital angular momenta interact through spin–orbit coupling and are not separately conserved, while their vector sum is.

Values

Given an orbital angular momentum quantum number l and a spin quantum number s, the total angular momentum quantum number takes values from |l − s| to l + s in integer steps. For a single electron (s = 1/2), j = l ± 1/2. The magnitude of the total angular momentum is √(j(j+1))ħ, and its component along a chosen axis is quantised in units of ħ, with quantum number mj running from −j to +j.

Spectroscopic notation

Atomic states are labelled in term symbols of the form 2S+1LJ, where the subscript J gives the total angular momentum quantum number for the whole atom. The splitting of spectral lines by spin–orbit interaction is called fine structure.
