Yamaha DX7

The Yamaha DX7 is a synthesizer manufactured by the Yamaha Corporation from 1983 to 1989. It was the first successful digital synthesizer and is one of the best-selling synthesizers in history, selling over 200,000 units.

Synthesis

The DX7 uses frequency modulation (FM) synthesis, based on research by John Chowning at Stanford University, which Yamaha licensed. Its engine provides six operators (sine-wave oscillators with envelopes) arranged in 32 algorithms. The keyboard has 61 velocity- and aftertouch-sensitive keys and 16-voice polyphony.

Impact

The DX7's bright, glassy electric piano, bell and bass presets became defining sounds of 1980s popular music, appearing on records by artists including Whitney Houston, Chicago, Brian Eno and many others. Its low price relative to earlier digital instruments, stable tuning and MIDI implementation (it was among the first synthesizers with MIDI) helped reshape the music industry and displaced many analog synthesizers and electric pianos.

Programming reputation

Programming the DX7 through its membrane buttons and single data slider was widely considered difficult, so most users relied on factory presets and third-party sounds — a factor in the homogeneity of its sound across 1980s pop. Later models in the DX series and software emulations kept the design influential for decades.
