Frank Sinatra

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Frank Sinatra at a Capitol Records session in the 1950s: fedora pushed back, tie loosened, leaning into a ribbon microphone over Nelson Riddle's orchestra]

Francis Albert Sinatra (12 December 1915 – 14 May 1998) was an American singer and actor. Nicknamed the 'Chairman of the Board' and later called 'Ol' Blue Eyes', Sinatra was one of the most popular entertainers of the mid-20th century. He is among the world's best-selling music artists, with an estimated 150 million record sales.

A Hoboken saloon singer turned bobby-soxer idol with Tommy Dorsey, Sinatra survived career collapse in the early 1950s, won an Oscar for From Here to Eternity (1953) and reinvented himself at Capitol Records: the concept albums In the Wee Small Hours (1955) and Songs for Swingin' Lovers! (1956) defined the LP era's art of phrasing. As leader of the Rat Pack with Dean Martin and Sammy Davis Jr., he embodied Las Vegas cool; his signature songs — 'My Way' (1969), 'New York, New York' (1980) — are standards of the Great American Songbook.
