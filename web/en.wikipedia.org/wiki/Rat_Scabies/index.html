Rat Scabies (born Christopher John Millar, 25 July 1955) is an English musician best known as the drummer of the punk rock band the Damned, which he co-founded in 1976. Scabies was a member from the band's formation until 1995, and rejoined in later years for reunions of the original line-up. He is regarded as one of the defining drummers of first-wave British punk.

Career

Millar took his stage name after contracting scabies while working in a council office job — according to band folklore, the "Rat" prefix came after a rat appeared (or he was likened to one) during his first rehearsal room period with Brian James. With James, Dave Vanian and Captain Sensible, he formed the Damned in London in 1976. The band released "New Rose" in October 1976, generally credited as the first single by a British punk group, followed by the album Damned Damned Damned (1977), produced by Nick Lowe.

Scabies' explosive, Keith Moon-influenced drumming powered the band through its punk, psychedelic and goth phases, including Music for Pleasure (1977), Machine Gun Etiquette (1979), The Black Album (1980), Strawberries (1982) and Phantasmagoria (1985), and singles such as "Love Song", "Smash It Up" and "Eloise". He left the Damned in 1995 after disputes over songwriting royalties and the release of the album Not of This Earth.

Later work

After leaving, Scabies pursued projects including The Mutants, collaborations with various punk contemporaries, and a long-standing fascination with the Holy Grail and Rennes-le-Château, which led to the book Rat Scabies and the Holy Grail (2005), written with journalist Christopher Dawes, chronicling his grail-hunting expeditions. He later played with Professor and the Madman and, in 2021–2022, rejoined the original Damned line-up (Vanian, James, Sensible and Scabies) for reunion concerts performing material from the band's first years.
