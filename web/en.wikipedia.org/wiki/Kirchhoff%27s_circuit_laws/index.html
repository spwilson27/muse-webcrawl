Kirchhoff's circuit laws

Proposed by: Gustav Kirchhoff (1845)
Laws: Current law (KCL); Voltage law (KVL)

Kirchhoff's circuit laws are two equalities that deal with the current and potential difference (voltage) in the lumped element model of electrical circuits. They were first described in 1845 by German physicist Gustav Kirchhoff and form the basis of network analysis.

Current law (KCL)

The algebraic sum of currents entering and leaving any node in a circuit is zero. This expresses conservation of electric charge: charge neither accumulates at nor vanishes from a node.

Voltage law (KVL)

The algebraic sum of voltages around any closed loop in a circuit is zero. This follows from conservation of energy for a charge traveling around the loop, assuming slowly varying magnetic fields.

Applications

Together with Ohm's law, Kirchhoff's laws allow systematic solution of complex networks via nodal and mesh analysis, and they underlie most circuit simulation software.
