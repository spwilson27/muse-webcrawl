Dusty in Memphis is the fifth studio album by English singer Dusty Springfield, released in 1969. It was recorded at American Sound Studio in Memphis with the Memphis Cats, produced by Jerry Wexler, Tom Dowd and Arif Mardin, and backed by the vocal group the Sweet Inspirations.

Recording

Springfield, nervous about recording alongside musicians who had played on records by Aretha Franklin and Elvis Presley, ultimately recorded her final vocals in New York. The album's best-known track, "Son of a Preacher Man" — written by John Hurley and Ronnie Wilkins — became an international top-10 hit and was later revived in the 1994 film Pulp Fiction. Other tracks include interpretations of songs by Randy Newman and of "The Windmills of Your Mind".

Reception and legacy

Initial sales were modest, but the album's critical reputation grew enormously; it is now widely considered one of the greatest albums of all time. It was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame in 2001 and selected for the US National Recording Registry in 2020. Rolling Stone has ranked it among the top 100 albums ever made, praising Springfield's restrained, intimate vocal performances over the Memphis soul arrangements.

Track listing highlights

Side one includes "Just One Smile", "I Don't Want to Hear It Anymore" and "The Windmills of Your Mind"; side two includes "Son of a Preacher Man", "Breakfast in Bed" and "Just a Little Lovin'".
