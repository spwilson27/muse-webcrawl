Waste

Municipal solid waste separated by type for collection.

Waste (or wastes) are unwanted or unusable materials. Waste is any substance discarded after primary use, or that is worthless, defective and of no use. A by-product, by contrast, is a jointly produced product of relatively minor economic value. A waste product may become a by-product, joint product or resource through an invention that raises a waste product's value above zero.

Examples include municipal solid waste (household trash), hazardous waste, wastewater (such as sewage, which contains bodily wastes — feces and urine), radioactive waste, and others. The OECD defines waste as "substances or objects which are disposed of or are intended to be disposed of or are required to be disposed of by the provisions of national law."

Types

Wastes are commonly classified by source and by their effect on health and the environment. Municipal solid waste consists of everyday household and commercial refuse. Hazardous waste is waste that poses substantial threats to public health or the environment, exhibiting properties such as ignitability, corrosivity, reactivity or toxicity. Biomedical waste arises from healthcare facilities, while electronic waste (e-waste) covers discarded electrical and electronic devices. Radioactive waste requires specialized long-term management.

Management

Waste management encompasses collection, transport, treatment and disposal, together with monitoring and regulation. The waste hierarchy ranks options by environmental preference: prevention first, then preparing for re-use, recycling, other recovery (such as energy recovery through incineration), and disposal (such as landfill) last. Modern policy increasingly follows the principles of the circular economy, seeking to keep materials in use and design waste out of production systems. See waste sorting and wastewater treatment.

International regulation

The Basel Convention on the Control of Transboundary Movements of Hazardous Wastes and Their Disposal, adopted in 1989, restricts the international shipment of hazardous waste, particularly from developed to less developed countries.
