Juan Ponce de León

Juan Ponce de León (c. 1474 – July 1521) was a Spanish explorer and conquistador who led the first known European expedition to Florida, which he named La Florida in 1513. He served as the first governor of Puerto Rico and is popularly — though apocryphally — associated with the legend of the Fountain of Youth.

Early career and Puerto Rico

Ponce de León sailed with Christopher Columbus's second voyage in 1493 and later took part in the conquest of Hispaniola. In 1508–1509 he led the colonisation of Puerto Rico (then San Juan Bautista), founded Caparra, and became the island's first governor before being displaced in a political dispute with the Columbus family.

Florida expeditions

Seeking new lands, he sailed northwest in March 1513, landing on Florida's east coast near present-day Melbourne Beach or St. Augustine during the Easter season (Pascua Florida). On the return voyage his expedition encountered the powerful Gulf Stream. In 1521 he returned with settlers to Florida's Gulf coast, where he was wounded by a Calusa arrow and died after retreating to Havana; he is buried in San Juan Cathedral in Puerto Rico.

Fountain of Youth

The story that Ponce de León searched for a fountain restoring youth was attached to him by chroniclers such as Gonzalo Fernández de Oviedo after his death and is generally regarded as legend rather than fact.
