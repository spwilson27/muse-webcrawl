1. FC Kaiserslautern

1. Fußball-Club Kaiserslautern e. V., also known as 1. FCK, FCK, or simply Kaiserslautern, is a German professional association football club based in Kaiserslautern, Rhineland-Palatinate. Founded on 2 June 1900, the club is one of Germany's traditional footballing names and is nicknamed Die Roten Teufel ("The Red Devils").

History

The club was formed through the merger of local football clubs in 1900. Kaiserslautern rose to prominence in the early 1950s, winning German championships in 1951 and 1953 with a team built around the brothers Fritz and Ottmar Walter, who formed the core of the West Germany side that won the 1954 FIFA World Cup — the "Miracle of Bern". The club's stadium on the Betzenberg hill is named the Fritz-Walter-Stadion in honour of its most famous player.

FCK were founding members of the Bundesliga in 1963 and remained in the top flight for decades. Their most famous triumph came in the 1997–98 season, when, managed by Otto Rehhagel, Kaiserslautern won the Bundesliga title immediately after being promoted — an unprecedented achievement in German football. The club also won the DFB-Pokal in 1990 and 1996.

Recent years

After financial difficulties and relegations, the club dropped into the third division in 2018, its first time outside the top two tiers. Kaiserslautern returned to the 2. Bundesliga in 2022 and reached the DFB-Pokal final in 2024, narrowly losing to Bayer Leverkusen. The club retains one of the largest and most loyal fan bases in German football relative to the size of its city.
