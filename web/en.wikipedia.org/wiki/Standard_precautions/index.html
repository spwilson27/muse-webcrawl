Standard precautions

Standard precautions are the minimum infection prevention practices that apply to all patient care, regardless of the suspected or confirmed infection status of a patient, in any setting where healthcare is delivered. They are designed to protect both healthcare personnel and patients from infection with pathogens that can be spread by blood or other bodily fluids, secretions and excretions.

History

Standard precautions were introduced by the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) in 1996, combining the major features of the earlier Universal Precautions (introduced in 1985–88 in response to the HIV epidemic, and treating all blood and certain body fluids as potentially infectious) and Body Substance Isolation. The CDC's 2007 guideline for isolation precautions in hospitals updated and reaffirmed standard precautions and added respiratory hygiene/cough etiquette and safe injection practices as formal components.

Components

  • Hand hygiene — before and after patient contact and after contact with potentially infectious material
  • Personal protective equipment — gloves, gowns, masks and eye protection selected according to anticipated exposure
  • Respiratory hygiene and cough etiquette — covering coughs, masks for symptomatic patients, spatial separation
  • Safe injection practices — one needle, one syringe, single-dose vials where possible
  • Sharps safety — no recapping, sharps containers at point of use
  • Cleaning and disinfection of equipment and environmental surfaces, and safe handling of linen and waste

Relationship to transmission-based precautions

Where a patient is known or suspected to have an infection spread by a particular route, standard precautions are supplemented by transmission-based precautions: contact, droplet and airborne precautions.
