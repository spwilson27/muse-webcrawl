Mosquito net

A mosquito net hung over a bed

A mosquito net is a type of meshed curtain that is circumferentially draped over a bed or a sleeping area, to offer the sleeper protection against bites and stings from mosquitoes and other insects, and thus against the diseases they may carry. Examples of such preventable insect-borne diseases include malaria, dengue fever, yellow fever, Zika, and various forms of encephalitis.

Construction

Mosquito nets are usually made of polyester or nylon mesh woven finely enough to exclude mosquitoes while still allowing air circulation. For protection against mosquitoes, the holes in the mesh should be no larger than about 1.2 millimetres. Nets are hung from the ceiling or from a frame and are tucked under the mattress to prevent insects entering.

Insecticide-treated nets

When treated with a suitable insecticide, such as a pyrethroid, mosquito nets offer considerably enhanced protection: insects are repelled or killed on contact, and protection extends somewhat beyond the net itself. Long-lasting insecticidal nets (LLINs) are designed to remain effective for several years without re-treatment. Trials in Africa showed that insecticide-treated nets substantially reduce child mortality from malaria, and mass distribution of treated nets by organisations such as the World Health Organization and the Against Malaria Foundation has become a cornerstone of malaria control.

History

Mosquito nets have been used since antiquity; the Greek historian Herodotus described Egyptians using nets by day and night against gnats, and Cleopatra is said to have slept under one. The English word "mosquito" derives from Spanish, and the net's use spread widely in the tropics during the 19th and 20th centuries.
