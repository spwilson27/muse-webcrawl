Leyland Motors

Leyland Motors Limited was a British manufacturer of cars, trucks, and buses based in Leyland, Lancashire. Founded in 1896 as the Lancashire Steam Motor Company, it grew into one of the world's leading commercial-vehicle makers before driving the 1968 merger that created the British Leyland Motor Corporation.

History

The company began building steam-powered lawn mowers and wagons, producing its first petrol-engined vehicle in 1904 and adopting the Leyland name in 1907. Leyland built tanks and military trucks in both world wars and dominated the British bus market with models such as the Titan and Atlantean; the Lynx was among its last bus designs. Postwar acquisitions included Albion Motors, Scammell, and Standard-Triumph, and in 1962 Leyland acquired the Associated Commercial Vehicles group (AEC), gaining the London bus Routemaster business.

British Leyland and decline

In 1968 Leyland merged with British Motor Holdings to form British Leyland, in an effort to create a national champion. Chronic industrial disputes, underinvestment, and competition eroded the group; after the 1975 Ryder Report it was effectively nationalised. The bus business was sold to Volvo in 1988, and the truck operation — Leyland DAF — was formed with Dutch maker DAF in 1987.

Legacy

After DAF's 1993 receivership, the Leyland truck plant was bought by management and in 1998 acquired by Paccar, which still builds DAF trucks at Leyland, Lancashire, under the Leyland Trucks name. The Leyland name also survives in India's Ashok Leyland, founded in 1948 as a joint venture.
