Kristoff Bjorgman is a fictional character in Walt Disney Animation Studios' Frozen franchise. Voiced by Jonathan Groff, Kristoff is a rugged Sami ice harvester who lives in the mountains outside the kingdom of Arendelle with his reindeer companion, Sven. He serves as the love interest of Princess Anna, whom he accompanies on her journey to find her sister, Elsa, in Frozen (2013).

Character

Kristoff was orphaned at a young age and adopted by the rock trolls of the Valley of the Living Rock, whose leader, Pabbie, raised him. As an adult he earns his living harvesting ice blocks and selling them in Arendelle. He is gruff, pragmatic and something of a loner, preferring the company of Sven, for whom he supplies both sides of their "conversations" — a habit reflected in the duet "Reindeer(s) Are Better Than People".

Appearances

Frozen (2013)

After Elsa's powers plunge Arendelle into eternal winter, Anna recruits Kristoff to guide her up the North Mountain. Along the way they meet the snowman Olaf and battle wolves and Elsa's snow monster, Marshmallow. Kristoff falls in love with Anna but brings her back to Arendelle believing an act of true love from Prince Hans will save her; when Hans is revealed as a villain, Olaf tells Kristoff that Kristoff himself is Anna's true love, and he races back across the fjord as Anna sacrifices herself for Elsa.

Frozen II (2019)

In the sequel Kristoff repeatedly tries and fails to propose to Anna amid the crisis of the Enchanted Forest; his solo ballad "Lost in the Woods" (a 1980s power-ballad pastiche by Kristen Anderson-Lopez and Robert Lopez) is a highlight. By the film's end the couple are engaged, with Anna crowned Queen of Arendelle.

Other media

Kristoff appears in the shorts Frozen Fever (2015), Olaf's Frozen Adventure (2017) and Once Upon a Snowman (2020), in the Broadway musical adaptation, and in video games including Kingdom Hearts III.

Reception

Kristoff has been praised as a refreshingly ordinary Disney male lead — a working-class hero whose devotion is expressed through action rather than grand gestures — and Groff's performance and songs have been widely singled out.
