Television in Scotland

Television in Scotland is dominated by the United Kingdom-wide broadcasters BBC and ITV, each of which operates Scottish services with a substantial volume of Scotland-specific programming, alongside the Gaelic-language channel BBC Alba and the commercial channel STV.

History

Television broadcasts reached Scotland in 1952 with the opening of the Kirk o' Shotts transmitter. Commercial television arrived in 1957 with Scottish Television (STV) in the central belt, followed by Grampian Television (1961) in the north and Border Television (1961) in the south. BBC Scotland expanded from radio into television production in Glasgow, moving in 2007 to a purpose-built headquarters at Pacific Quay on the River Clyde.

Broadcasters and channels

  • BBC Scotland — produces Scottish news (Reporting Scotland), sport, drama, and comedy, and since 2019 has operated a dedicated BBC Scotland channel.
  • BBC Alba — the Scottish Gaelic channel, launched in 2008 as a partnership between the BBC and MG Alba.
  • STV — the ITV licence holder for central and northern Scotland, producing STV News and entertainment programming.
  • ITV Border — serves southern Scotland jointly with northern England.

Programming

Distinctively Scottish productions include the soap River City, comedies such as Still Game and Burnistoun, the crime drama Taggart (once Britain's longest-running police drama), Gaelic children's and documentary output on BBC Alba, and extensive coverage of Scottish football. Devolution since 1999 increased demand for Scottish political coverage, and debates over a possible separate Scottish Six O'Clock news bulletin have recurred for decades.
