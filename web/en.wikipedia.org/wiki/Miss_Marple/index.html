Miss Marple

Jane Marple, usually referred to as Miss Marple, is a fictional amateur detective created by Agatha Christie. An elderly spinster living in the village of St Mary Mead, she solves crimes by drawing parallels between suspects and the village characters she has observed all her life.

Appearances

Miss Marple first appeared in the short story collection The Thirteen Problems (1932) and in the novel The Murder at the Vicarage (1930). She features in twelve novels, ending with Sleeping Murder, written during the Second World War but published posthumously in 1976, and in twenty short stories.

Character

Gentle, sharp-eyed and fond of knitting and gardening, Miss Marple's apparently fluffy manner conceals a shrewd, unsentimental view of human nature. Her nephew is the novelist Raymond West.

Adaptations

She has been played on screen by Margaret Rutherford, Angela Lansbury, Joan Hickson — widely regarded as definitive — Geraldine McEwan and Julia McKenzie, among others.
