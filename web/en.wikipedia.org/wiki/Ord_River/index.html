Ord River

The Ord River is a 650-kilometre (400 mi) river in the Kimberley region of Western Australia. It rises in the Albert Edward Range near the Northern Territory border, flows generally north through rugged ranges and gorges, is impounded by the Ord River Dam to form Lake Argyle — one of Australia's largest artificial freshwater storages — and discharges into Cambridge Gulf near Wyndham, a tidal arm of the Timor Sea. The river was named by Alexander Forrest in 1879 in honour of Sir Harry Ord, Governor of Western Australia.

Ord River Irrigation Scheme

The Ord River Irrigation Scheme, promoted since the 1940s by state premier-era visions of "developing the north," was built in two stages: the diversion dam and the town of Kununurra (1963), then the main Ord River Dam, completed in 1972 and officially opened by Prime Minister William McMahon. Lake Argyle stores some 10.7 billion cubic metres in its main basin. Early crops (cotton, rice, sugar) struggled with pests, prices and distance, but the scheme area has since expanded to roughly 20,000+ hectares growing sandalwood, melons, mangoes, chia, cotton and other crops; a second-stage expansion into the Northern Territory lands proceeded in the 2010s.

Environment

Regulating the river transformed the downstream ecosystem: constant irrigation flows replaced the natural boom-and-bust cycle of wet and dry seasons, changing sediment movement and estuarine habitats. The lower Ord floodplain is a Ramsar-listed wetland of importance for waterbirds, and the river supports freshwater crocodiles and barramundi. The catchment is the traditional country of Miriwoong and other Aboriginal peoples.
