Buffy the Vampire Slayer is an American supernatural drama television series created by Joss Whedon. It premiered on March 10, 1997, on The WB and concluded on May 20, 2003, on UPN. The series follows Buffy Summers (played by Sarah Michelle Gellar), a teenage girl chosen by fate to battle vampires, demons, and other forces of darkness, aided by her friends — the "Scooby Gang" — and her Watcher, Rupert Giles (Anthony Stewart Head).

Premise

Buffy is the latest in a line of young women known as Slayers, endowed with supernatural strength and skill. Living in the fictional town of Sunnydale, California, situated on a mystical "Hellmouth," she balances the demands of high school and later young adult life with her calling.

Cast

The ensemble included Nicholas Brendon as Xander Harris, Alyson Hannigan as Willow Rosenberg, Charisma Carpenter as Cordelia Chase, David Boreanaz as Angel, Seth Green as Oz, and James Marsters as Spike.

Reception and legacy

The series is widely acclaimed as one of the greatest television shows of all time, praised for its writing, serialized storytelling, and use of horror as metaphor for adolescent experience. Standout episodes include the musical "Once More, with Feeling," the nearly silent "Hush," and "The Body." It spawned the spin-off Angel, a franchise of comics, novels, and video games, and has been the subject of extensive academic study sometimes called "Buffy studies." The series followed a 1992 film of the same name starring Kristy Swanson.
