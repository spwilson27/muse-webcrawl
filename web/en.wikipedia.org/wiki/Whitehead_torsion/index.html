Whitehead torsion

In geometric topology, Whitehead torsion is an algebraic invariant of a homotopy equivalence between finite CW complexes. Introduced by J. H. C. Whitehead in the 1940s and 1950s, it detects whether a homotopy equivalence is a simple homotopy equivalence — one that can be built up from elementary expansions and collapses of complexes. Whitehead torsion takes values in the Whitehead group Wh(π), an abelian group constructed from the fundamental group π of the space, and its vanishing is a fundamental ingredient in the classification of manifolds, notably in the s-cobordism theorem.

An elementary expansion (adding a pair of cells) and its reverse, an elementary collapse. Simple homotopy equivalences are generated by such moves.

Definition

Given a homotopy equivalence f : X → Y between finite connected CW complexes, one forms the mapping cylinder of f and considers the long exact chain complex of the pair, with coefficients twisted by the fundamental group. The resulting chain complex is acyclic and based; its Reidemeister–Franz torsion, taken modulo the trivial units ±π, defines the Whitehead torsion τ(f) in the Whitehead group Wh(π1Y), defined as the quotient of the abelianisation of the group of units of the group ring Z[π] by the subgroup generated by the trivial units ±g.

Two finite complexes have the same simple homotopy type if they are related by a homotopy equivalence with vanishing torsion. A homotopy equivalence is simple if and only if its Whitehead torsion is zero.

The s-cobordism theorem

Whitehead torsion classifies h-cobordisms. In the s-cobordism theorem, proved by Stephen Smale for simply connected manifolds and extended by Barry Mazur, John Stallings and Dennis Barden to the general case, an h-cobordism between manifolds of dimension at least five is a product if and only if the Whitehead torsion of the inclusion of one boundary component vanishes. Every element of the Whitehead group is realised by some h-cobordism, so the group precisely measures the possible obstructions.

Examples and computations

The Whitehead group vanishes for trivial groups, free groups and many others, including free abelian groups and fundamental groups of many aspherical manifolds; the Farrell–Jones conjectures in algebraic K-theory predict exactly when Wh(π) is zero. On the other hand, finite cyclic groups of order five or more have nontrivial Whitehead groups, a fact connected with the existence of inequivalent lens spaces that are homotopy equivalent but not simply homotopy equivalent: Reidemeister torsion and its refinement by Whitehead torsion distinguish them.
