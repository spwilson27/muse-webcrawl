Bouldering

[Image placeholder: Bouldering]

Bouldering is a form of rock climbing performed on small rock formations or artificial climbing walls without the use of ropes or harnesses. Problems (the paths up a boulder) are usually short — rarely more than about 6 metres — and climbers use crash pads, spotters and climbing shoes for protection and grip.

History

Bouldering began as training for larger ascents in the late 19th century in places such as Fontainebleau, France, and the Lake District and Peak District of England. In the 20th century, figures such as John Gill popularised it as a discipline in its own right, introducing gymnastic standards and chalk. Modern bouldering grades use the V-scale (Hueco) in North America and the Font scale in Europe.

Competition

Indoor bouldering is hugely popular in climbing gyms and became an Olympic sport as part of sport climbing at the Tokyo 2020 Games; at Paris 2024 bouldering was combined with lead climbing in a single medal event.
