The Principality of Hungary (Hungarian: Magyar Fejedelemség) was the earliest documented Hungarian state, existing roughly from 895 until 1000. It was formed in the Carpathian Basin after the Hungarian conquest (Honfoglalás) and was ruled by grand princes of the Árpád dynasty.

Formation

Around 895, under pressure from the Pechenegs and Bulgarians, the Magyar tribal alliance led by Álmos and his son Árpád crossed the Carpathian Mountains from Etelköz and settled the basin, displacing the remnants of Avar, Slavic, and Frankish authority. The principality functioned as a confederation of seven Magyar tribes, with dual leadership in the early period: the sacred kende and the military gyula.

Raids and transformation

Throughout the first half of the 10th century Hungarian mounted archers raided western Europe and the Balkans. Defeats — at Riade (933) and decisively at the Battle of Lechfeld (955) — ended the western raids and pushed the principality toward settled agriculture and Christianity. Grand Prince Géza (r. 972–997) opened relations with the Holy Roman Empire, invited missionaries, and began centralising power.

Transition to kingdom

Géza's son Stephen was crowned king — traditionally on Christmas Day 1000 or 1 January 1001 — with a crown sent by Pope Sylvester II, transforming the principality into the Christian Kingdom of Hungary.
