United Kingdom Census 2021

The United Kingdom Census 2021 was the national population census conducted in England, Wales and Northern Ireland on 21 March 2021. Scotland's census was postponed because of the COVID-19 pandemic and was held in 2022. The censuses are administered separately by the Office for National Statistics in England and Wales, the Northern Ireland Statistics and Research Agency, and National Records of Scotland.

Design

The 2021 census was designed as “digital-first”, with most households expected to complete the questionnaire online, although paper forms remained available. It took place during pandemic restrictions and used address registers, field follow-up and administrative information to maximize coverage. Participation was compulsory for most questions, with fines possible for refusal, though new questions on sexual orientation and gender identity for people aged 16 and over were voluntary. A question also identified veterans of the UK armed forces.

Results

The usual resident population of England and Wales on census day was about 59.6 million, an increase of 6.3 percent from 2011. Northern Ireland's population was about 1.90 million. Results covered age, sex, ethnicity, religion, national identity, language, health, disability, housing, education, labour market status and migration. In England and Wales, less than half of respondents identified as Christian for the first time in a census, while the proportions reporting no religion and identifying as Muslim, Hindu and other faiths grew.

Use

Census results guide central and local government funding, public services, transport, housing and equality monitoring, and provide denominators for statistics until the next census. Detailed outputs are released in stages and are subject to statistical disclosure controls to protect confidentiality. Individual census records are normally closed for 100 years.
