The Australia national cricket team represents Australia in men's international cricket. It is administered by Cricket Australia. Along with England, it is the oldest team in Test cricket history, playing in the first ever Test match in 1877. The team also plays One Day International (ODI) and Twenty20 International (T20I) cricket, participating in the first ODI, against England in the 1970–71 season, and the first T20I, against New Zealand in the 2004–05 season.

History

Australia won the inaugural Test at the Melbourne Cricket Ground in 1877, and its rivalry with England quickly produced The Ashes, contested since 1882. The team has produced many of the game's greatest players, most notably Sir Donald Bradman, whose career batting average of 99.94 is regarded as one of the most extraordinary statistics in sport. Other celebrated figures include Victor Trumper, Keith Miller, Richie Benaud, Dennis Lillee, Greg Chappell, Allan Border, Shane Warne, Glenn McGrath, Ricky Ponting and Steve Smith.

Tournament record

Australia is the most successful team in the Cricket World Cup, winning the tournament a record six times — in 1987, 1999, 2003, 2007, 2015 and 2023 — and also won the ICC Champions Trophy twice and the ICC T20 World Cup in 2021. In Test cricket, Australia won the ICC World Test Championship in 2023, defeating India in the final at The Oval. The side dominated world cricket for long periods, notably in the late 1990s and 2000s under captains Steve Waugh and Ricky Ponting.

Colours and identity

The team is known as the "Baggy Greens" after the dark green caps its Test players receive on debut, a symbol of great prestige in Australian sport. Test whites are worn with the green and gold of Australia, colours also used in limited-overs uniforms.
