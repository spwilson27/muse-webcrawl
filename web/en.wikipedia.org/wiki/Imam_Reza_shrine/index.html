The Imam Reza shrine (Persian: حرم امام رضا) in Mashhad, Iran, is a vast mosque and mausoleum complex containing the tomb of Imam Reza (Ali al-Rida), the eighth Imam of Twelver Shia Islam. It is one of the largest mosques in the world and the most important pilgrimage destination in Iran, drawing tens of millions of visitors each year; the very name Mashhad means "place of martyrdom", referring to the Imam's burial there in 818 CE.

History

Ali al-Rida died in 818 in the village of Sanabad, reportedly poisoned on the orders of the Abbasid caliph al-Ma'mun, who had summoned him to Khorasan and named him heir apparent. A shrine grew over his tomb, and the town that developed around it became Mashhad. The complex was repeatedly destroyed and rebuilt — after Ghaznavid, Mongol and Uzbek raids — and received its most lavish patronage under the Safavid shahs, who promoted Shia pilgrimage to Mashhad as an alternative to Mecca and Karbala. The golden dome, tilework and minarets were extensively developed under Shah Abbas I and later rulers; Nader Shah added the golden iwan and other features in the eighteenth century.

The complex

The shrine comprises a series of courtyards (sahn) surrounded by prayer halls, madrasas and museums, centred on the gilded dome over the Imam's burial chamber, where pilgrims gather at the silver zarih (lattice enclosure) over the tomb. The complex includes the Goharshad Mosque (1418), commissioned by the wife of the Timurid ruler Shah Rukh and famed for its turquoise tilework, as well as libraries holding one of the world's great collections of Islamic manuscripts and Qur'ans, and museums displaying gifts donated over centuries.

Pilgrimage

Mashhad is a destination of ziyarat (visitation) for Shia Muslims from Iran, Iraq, Afghanistan, Pakistan, Azerbaijan and beyond. The shrine's administering foundation, Astan Quds Razavi, is one of the largest religious endowments in the Muslim world, funding extensive services for pilgrims.
