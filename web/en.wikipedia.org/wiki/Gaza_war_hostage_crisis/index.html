Gaza war hostage crisis

Date7 October 2023 – 2025
LocationSouthern Israel; Gaza Strip
Abducted251 people
ContextGaza war; October 7 attacks

The Gaza war hostage crisis refers to the abduction of 251 people from Israel to the Gaza Strip during the Hamas-led attack of 7 October 2023. The hostages — Israeli civilians and soldiers, children, elderly people, and foreign nationals including Thai and Nepali workers — were held by Hamas and other Palestinian armed groups, largely in tunnels and private homes, becoming a central focus of the ensuing Gaza war and of Israeli and international public life.

Abduction

Hostages were seized from kibbutzim and towns of the Gaza envelope, from the Nova music festival near Re'im, and from military bases. Among the most widely known captives were the Bibas family, kidnapped from Nir Oz with their two small children, and Noa Argamani, whose abduction on a motorcycle was filmed.

Releases and rescues

During a week-long ceasefire in late November 2023, 105 hostages were released in exchange for Palestinian prisoners. A small number were freed in Israeli military rescue operations, most prominently the June 2024 raid in Nuseirat that freed four hostages. Others died in captivity, some killed by their captors and some in Israeli strikes or friendly-fire incidents; the recovery of bodies became a recurring feature of military operations.

A ceasefire agreement in January 2025 provided for the phased release of 33 hostages in its first stage. A further agreement in October 2025, under the United States-brokered peace plan, led to the release of the remaining living hostages in exchange for Palestinian prisoners and detainees, with the return of deceased hostages' remains proceeding in stages thereafter.

Public response

The Hostages and Missing Families Forum, formed by relatives, ran a sustained international campaign under the slogan "Bring Them Home Now". Yellow ribbons, empty chairs and poster campaigns with the hostages' faces became global symbols, and the fate of the hostages dominated Israeli politics throughout the war.
