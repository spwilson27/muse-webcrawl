Chromebook

A Chromebook laptop computer

A Chromebook (sometimes stylized in lowercase as chromebook) is a laptop or tablet running the Linux-based ChromeOS operating system. Initially designed to rely heavily on web applications for tasks using the Google Chrome browser, Chromebooks have since expanded to be able to run Android and full Linux apps since 2017 and 2018, respectively.

All supported apps can be installed and launched alongside each other in the same interface. These apps can be stored locally on the device's internal storage or run from the web. Chromebook models are manufactured by several vendors, including Acer, Asus, Dell, HP, Lenovo, and Samsung, as well as Google's own Pixelbook line.

History

The first Chromebooks for sale, by Acer Inc. and Samsung, began shipping on June 15, 2011. In addition to laptop models, a desktop version, called a Chromebox, was introduced in May 2012, and an all-in-one device, called a Chromebase, was introduced in January 2014 by LG Electronics.

Chromebooks proved especially successful in the education market, where their low cost, simple management, and integration with Google Workspace made them popular with schools, particularly in the United States. By 2020 Chromebooks outsold Apple Macs worldwide for a period, taking second place behind Windows PCs.

Features

Chromebooks are designed around cloud computing: files are primarily stored in Google Drive, and the devices boot quickly owing to their lightweight operating system and solid-state storage. Security is provided through sandboxing, verified boot, and automatic updates. Most models are inexpensive, though premium devices such as the Pixelbook targeted higher-end users.
