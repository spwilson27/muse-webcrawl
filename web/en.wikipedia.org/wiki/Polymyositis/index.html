Polymyositis

Polymyositis (PM) is a type of chronic inflammation of the muscles (an inflammatory myopathy) related to dermatomyositis and inclusion body myositis. Its name means "inflammation of many muscles" (poly- = many, myo- = muscle, -itis = inflammation). The inflammation is predominantly of the endomysium in polymyositis, whereas dermatomyositis is characterised by perimyseal inflammation.

Signs and symptoms

The defining symptom is symmetric weakness of the proximal muscles — those closest to the trunk, such as the shoulders, hips, and thighs — developing over weeks to months. Patients may struggle to climb stairs, rise from a chair, or lift objects overhead. Muscle pain and tenderness may occur, and in some cases the muscles of swallowing and breathing can be affected. Unlike dermatomyositis, polymyositis lacks the characteristic skin rashes.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is supported by elevated muscle enzymes (such as creatine kinase) in the blood, electromyography findings, autoantibodies, and confirmation by muscle biopsy showing endomysial inflammation with invasion of non-necrotic muscle fibres by T cells. Treatment generally begins with high-dose corticosteroids such as prednisone, often combined with immunosuppressants such as methotrexate or azathioprine. Physical therapy helps maintain strength and function.
