Guildhall, London

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Guildhall is a municipal building in the Moorgate area of the City of London, England. It has been the centre of administration of the City since the Middle Ages: the site of the seat of the City of London Corporation and the meeting place of its Court of Common Council. The present Great Hall, built between 1411 and 1440, is the only secular stone building in the City to survive both the Great Fire of 1666 and the Blitz.

History

A guildhall existed on or near the site by the 12th century; tradition holds that the Roman amphitheatre of Londinium — whose outline is marked in the modern Guildhall Yard — lay beneath it. The current Great Hall, designed in the Perpendicular Gothic style under master mason John Croxtone, hosted famous state trials including those of Lady Jane Grey (1553) and Henry Garnet after the Gunpowder Plot. It was gutted in the Great Fire and again by incendiary bombing on 29 December 1940, and restored under Sir Giles Gilbert Scott, reopening in 1954.

The complex

Guildhall Yard is flanked by the Guildhall Art Gallery (opened 1999, incorporating the revealed remains of the amphitheatre in its basement), the Guildhall Library — London's largest public reference library for the capital's history — and the offices of the Corporation. Within the Great Hall stand monuments to Pitt the Elder, Pitt the Younger, Nelson, Wellington and Churchill, and the Gog and Magog statues, the giants of City legend.

Functions

Guildhall hosts the Lord Mayor's Banquet, state and livery banquets addressed by the Prime Minister and Chancellor of the Exchequer, elections of the Lord Mayor and sheriffs, and meetings of Common Hall.
