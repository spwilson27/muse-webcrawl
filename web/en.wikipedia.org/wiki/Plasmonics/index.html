Plasmonics

Plasmonics is the study and application of plasmons — quanta of plasma oscillation — particularly of surface plasmons, the collective oscillations of free electrons at metal–dielectric interfaces. The field exploits the ability of metallic nanostructures to confine and manipulate light at scales far below the diffraction limit.

Surface plasmons

When light interacts with a metal surface under suitable conditions, it can couple to oscillating surface charges to form surface plasmon polaritons, electromagnetic waves bound to the interface. In nanoparticles, localized surface plasmon resonances concentrate electric fields and scatter or absorb light strongly, which is the origin of the vivid colours of colloidal gold and silver.

Applications

Plasmonics underpins surface-enhanced Raman spectroscopy (SERS), highly sensitive biosensors, plasmonic waveguides and nanocircuitry, metamaterials, photothermal cancer therapy, and enhanced solar cells and photodetectors. The field sits at the intersection of optics, materials science, and nanotechnology.

Challenges

Optical losses in metals at visible frequencies limit propagation lengths and device efficiencies, driving research into alternative materials such as transparent conducting oxides, nitrides, and graphene.
