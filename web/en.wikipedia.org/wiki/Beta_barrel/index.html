Beta barrel

A beta barrel is a beta sheet composed of tandem repeats that twists and coils to form a closed toroidal structure in which the first strand is hydrogen bonded to the last strand. Beta barrels are found in many proteins, most notably the outer membrane proteins of Gram-negative bacteria, mitochondria and chloroplasts, and in pore-forming toxins.

Structure

Beta barrels are formed from an even or odd number of antiparallel beta strands — typically between 8 and 22 — arranged around a central axis. In membrane proteins the exterior surface facing the lipid bilayer is hydrophobic, while the interior of the barrel is often hydrophilic and can act as a channel or pore.

Examples

Well-known beta barrel proteins include the porins of bacterial outer membranes, the green fluorescent protein (GFP), whose 11-stranded barrel encloses its chromophore, and transmembrane enzymes such as outer membrane phospholipase A.
