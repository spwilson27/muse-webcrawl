Pisa

Pisa is a city in Tuscany, central Italy, straddling the Arno just before it empties into the Ligurian Sea. It is the capital of the Province of Pisa and is known worldwide for the Piazza dei Miracoli, the cathedral square containing the Leaning Tower, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Pisa is also the birthplace of Galileo Galilei and home to the University of Pisa, founded in 1343.

History

An ancient port of Etruscan and Roman origin, Pisa became one of the great maritime republics of medieval Italy, rivalling Genoa, Venice, and Amalfi; Pisan fleets fought in the Crusades and traded across the Mediterranean. Defeat by Genoa at the Battle of Meloria (1284) began its decline, and the silting of its harbour ended its sea power. Pisa passed under Florentine rule in 1406. Its university, and the elite Scuola Normale Superiore founded by Napoleon in 1810, made it a lasting centre of learning.

Sights

Besides the cathedral, baptistery, and campanile (the Leaning Tower, stabilised by restoration works completed in 2001), visitors see the Camposanto cemetery, the churches of Santa Maria della Spina and San Paolo a Ripa d'Arno, and the Palazzo Blu. The city hosts the Giugno Pisano festivals, including the Luminara and the Gioco del Ponte, each June.
