Tetrahedral number

Tetrahedral number
First terms1, 4, 10, 20, 35, 56, 84, 120…
Formulan(n+1)(n+2)/6
TypeFigurate number

A tetrahedral number, or triangular pyramidal number, is a figurate number that represents a pyramid with a triangular base and three sides — a tetrahedron. The nth tetrahedral number is the sum of the first n triangular numbers, just as the nth square pyramidal number is the sum of the first n square numbers.

Ten = n(n + 1)(n + 2) / 6 = C(n + 2, 3)

Formula and examples

The first tetrahedral numbers are 1, 4, 10, 20, 35, 56, 84, 120, 165, 220. They arise naturally when spheres are stacked in a tetrahedral arrangement: a pile with five layers contains 35 spheres, and so on. The generating function is x / (1 − x)4.

Properties

Tetrahedral numbers can be read from Pascal's triangle: they form the third diagonal from either edge. A curiosity discovered by Pollock (the Pollock tetrahedral numbers conjecture) proposes that every positive integer is the sum of at most five tetrahedral numbers; this has been verified computationally for very large bounds but remains unproved in general. Only three tetrahedral numbers are also perfect squares: 1, 4, and 19600.

Applications

Tetrahedral numbers appear in combinatorics as binomial coefficients C(n+2, 3), counting combinations with repetition, and in the classic problem of stacking cannonballs or oranges in a tetrahedral pile.
