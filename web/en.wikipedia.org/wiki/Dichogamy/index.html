Dichogamy

Dichogamy is a form of sequential hermaphroditism in flowering plants in which the male and female reproductive organs of a flower mature at different times, thereby reducing or preventing self-pollination and encouraging cross-pollination (outcrossing).

Types

In protandry, the anthers release pollen before the stigma of the same flower becomes receptive; examples include many members of the carrot and sunflower families. In protogyny, the stigma matures first, as in avocado and many magnolias. Dichogamy may be complete, when the periods of male and female function do not overlap, or incomplete, when they partially overlap.

Significance

By separating pollen presentation and stigma receptivity in time, dichogamy promotes genetic diversity. It often works together with herkogamy (spatial separation of anthers and stigma) and self-incompatibility systems.
