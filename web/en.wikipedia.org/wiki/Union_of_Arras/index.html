The Union of Arras (Dutch: Unie van Atrecht) was an accord signed on 6 January 1579 in Arras, in the Spanish Netherlands, by the largely French-speaking Catholic provinces of the south — the Counties of Artois and Hainaut, the city of Douai and associated Walloon territories — during the Dutch Revolt.

Background

Following the Pacification of Ghent (1576), which had united the provinces against Spanish rule, religious and political tensions grew between the Calvinist-dominated north and the Catholic south. The excesses of Calvinist regimes in cities such as Ghent, together with the diplomacy of the Spanish governor Alexander Farnese, Duke of Parma, drove the southern "Malcontent" nobles back toward reconciliation with Philip II of Spain.

Terms and consequences

The signatories declared their loyalty to the Catholic faith and to the King of Spain, while demanding the withdrawal of foreign troops and the maintenance of provincial privileges. In direct response, the northern provinces concluded the Union of Utrecht later the same month (23 January 1579). The two unions crystallised the permanent division of the Low Countries: the south remained under Spanish (later Austrian) rule and eventually formed part of Belgium, while the north developed into the independent Dutch Republic.
