Bass clarinet

The bass clarinet is a member of the clarinet family, sounding one octave below the standard B♭ soprano clarinet. It is usually pitched in B♭, has a predominantly cylindrical bore with a flared, often upturned metal bell, and a curved neck; low keys extend most instruments to written low E♭ or C.

History

Various deep-voiced clarinets were built from the late eighteenth century, but the modern bass clarinet took shape through the work of Adolphe Sax in the 1830s, whose design greatly improved intonation and tone. Wagner, Liszt and later Mahler, Strauss, Stravinsky and Shostakovich gave it prominent orchestral roles; it features famously in The Nutcracker, The Rite of Spring and the opening of Tchaikovsky's Sixth Symphony.

Use

The bass clarinet is a standard member of the concert band and orchestra and is common in clarinet choirs and contemporary chamber music, where its wide range and dark, vocal timbre are prized. In jazz it was championed by Eric Dolphy, whose improvisations established it as a solo instrument.
