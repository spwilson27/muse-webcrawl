False Dmitry I

False Dmitry I (Russian: Лжедмитрий I) reigned as Tsar of all Russia from 10 June 1605 until his death on 17 May 1606. He was the first of three impostors who claimed to be Dmitry Ivanovich, the youngest son of Ivan the Terrible, who had died in Uglich in 1591 under suspicious circumstances. His rise and fall were central episodes of Russia's Time of Troubles.

Identity and rise

The impostor is widely identified as Grigory Otrepyev, a former monk and clerk who fled to the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, where he gained the support of magnates including Jerzy Mniszech, and (unofficially) King Sigismund III. He claimed the child Dmitry had escaped assassination and been hidden away. Having converted to Catholicism (at least outwardly) and promised concessions to Poland, he invaded Russia in 1604 with a small Polish-backed force. After Tsar Boris Godunov's sudden death in April 1605, support collapsed for Godunov's son; Dmitry entered Moscow in triumph in June 1605 and was crowned tsar, recognised even by the real Dmitry's mother, Maria Nagaya.

Reign and death

Intelligent and energetic but culturally westernising, Dmitry alienated the boyars and Muscovite populace with his Polish entourage, Catholic wife Marina Mniszech (married in May 1606), and disregard for Orthodox customs. On 17 May 1606 a boyar conspiracy led by Vasily Shuisky stormed the Kremlin; Dmitry was killed attempting to flee, his body burned and — legend holds — his ashes fired from a cannon toward Poland. Shuisky succeeded him as tsar, but further False Dmitrys soon appeared.
