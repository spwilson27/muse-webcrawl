Tannaim

The Tannaim (Hebrew: תנאים, singular Tanna, "teachers" or "repeaters") were the rabbinic sages whose views are recorded in the Mishnah, from approximately 10–220 CE. They worked in Roman Palestine, first at Yavne and later in Galilee, and were succeeded by the Amoraim of the Talmudic period.

History

Tannaitic activity is traditionally divided into generations spanning the last century of the Second Temple, the destruction of Jerusalem in 70 CE, and the Bar Kokhba revolt (132–135 CE). Leading figures include Hillel and Shammai, Yohanan ben Zakkai, Rabbi Akiva, Rabbi Meir, and Judah ha-Nasi, who redacted the Mishnah around 200 CE.

Works

Beyond the Mishnah, Tannaitic literature includes the Tosefta, halakhic midrashim such as the Mekhilta, Sifra, and Sifre, and passages (baraitot) cited in the Talmuds. The Tannaim laid the foundation of rabbinic Judaism, systematising Jewish law and interpretation after the loss of the Temple.
