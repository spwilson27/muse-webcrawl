Rhoda Island

Rhoda Island (Arabic: Jazīrat ar-Rauḍa) is an island in the River Nile in central Cairo, Egypt, situated between the historic centre of the city on the east bank and Giza on the west. It is one of the oldest districts of Cairo and is known for its historic monuments and gardens.

History

The island was settled in the Islamic era and became important under the Ayyubid and Mamluk sultans. The Ayyubid sultan as-Salih Ayyub built a palace and fortress on its northern end in the 1240s, recruiting his Bahri Mamluk guard there — the corps that would overthrow the Ayyubids and found the Mamluk Sultanate in 1250.

Landmarks

The island's most famous monument is the Nilometer at its southern tip, a structure used since Pharaonic times, in its surviving Islamic form dating to the ninth century, to measure the Nile flood and set tax levels. At the northern end stands the Manial Palace and Museum, built by Prince Muhammad Ali Tewfik in the early twentieth century, combining many Islamic architectural styles. The island also contains the Al-Manyal district, historic mosques, and the Qasr El Ainy area opposite on the mainland.

Modern day

Today Rhoda Island is a largely residential district linked to both banks by bridges, valued for its relative calm and greenery within the dense metropolis of Cairo.
