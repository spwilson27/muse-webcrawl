Hinkley Point C nuclear power station

Hinkley Point C
Location: Somerset, England
Reactors: 2 × EPR
Capacity: 3,200 MW
Status: under construction

Hinkley Point C is a nuclear power station under construction on the Somerset coast of England, beside the earlier Hinkley Point A (closed 2000) and B (closed 2022) stations. It will consist of two European Pressurised Reactor (EPR) units with a combined output of 3,200 megawatts, enough to supply around six million homes — roughly 7 per cent of the United Kingdom's electricity.

Background and financing

The project, led by EDF Energy with China's China General Nuclear Power Group as minority partner, is the first new nuclear station built in Britain since Sizewell B in 1995. The government agreed a Contract for Difference guaranteeing a strike price of £92.50 per megawatt-hour (in 2012 prices) for 35 years, an arrangement that attracted criticism as costs of competing low-carbon generation fell. Final investment approval was given in 2016.

Construction

Major construction began in 2018–19. The vast site involves twin reactor buildings, a sea wall, cooling-water tunnels bored beneath the Severn Estuary, and some of the world's largest cranes, including "Big Carl". Progress has been slowed by the COVID-19 pandemic, inflation and design and supply-chain difficulties; by 2024 EDF estimated costs at £31–35 billion (in 2015 prices) with first generation targeted for around the end of the decade, subject to risk of further delay. A near-identical station, Sizewell C in Suffolk, is planned as a follow-on.
