Din-i Ilahi

Din-i Ilahi
Founder: Akbar, Mughal emperor
Proclaimed: c. 1582
Region: Mughal Empire, India
Related: Akbar, Mughal Empire

Din-i Ilahi, the 'Religion of God', also known as Tawhid-i Ilahi, was a syncretic religious movement or ethical system promulgated by the Mughal emperor Akbar around 1582.

Beliefs and practices

Drawing on Islam, Hinduism, Zoroastrianism, Jainism and Christianity — faiths debated at Akbar's Ibadat Khana in Fatehpur Sikri — Din-i Ilahi emphasized devotion to God, virtue, abstinence and loyalty to the emperor as spiritual guide. It had no scripture, priesthood or mass rituals, and its adherents were a small circle of courtiers.

Debate

Historians debate whether it was a new religion, a Sufi-like discipleship, or a political instrument to unify a diverse empire under Akbar's policy of sulh-i kul (peace with all).

Decline

The movement did not survive Akbar's death in 1605 and left no continuing community.
