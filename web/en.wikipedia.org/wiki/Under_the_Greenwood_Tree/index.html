Under the Greenwood Tree: A Rural Painting of the Dutch School is the second of Thomas Hardy's novels to be published and the first of his Wessex novels. It was published anonymously in 1872 by Tinsley Brothers. It is Hardy's lightest and sunniest novel, a pastoral story named after the song in Shakespeare's As You Like It.

Plot

The novel follows the Mellstock village choir — a traditional "quire" of strings and voices, led by figures such as the tranter Reuben Dewy — whose customs are threatened when the progressive new vicar, Mr. Maybold, plans to replace them with a harmonium played by the schoolmistress Fancy Day. Against this gentle conflict of old and new, Reuben's son Dick Dewy courts Fancy, competing with the wealthy farmer Shiner and, secretly, the vicar himself. Dick wins her hand, but the novel closes on the suggestion that Fancy keeps a secret — her acceptance of Maybold's proposal, withdrawn — from her husband.

Hardy drew on his own family history: his grandfather and father had played in a church quire of the kind depicted, displaced in the same way by an organ. The book is structured by the seasons, its five parts running from Winter through Spring, Summer and Autumn to a Conclusion.

Reception and adaptations

The novel was well received and remains among Hardy's most popular. Hardy himself regarded it fondly, though he came to feel its pastoral simplicity was untypical of his mature work. It has been adapted for television in 2005 by ITV, starring Keeley Hawes as Fancy Day.
