Vinyl alcohol

Vinyl alcohol, also called ethenol, is the simplest enol, with the formula CH2CHOH. It is an unstable compound that readily tautomerizes to acetaldehyde, its much more stable carbonyl form, and therefore cannot be isolated or stored under ordinary conditions.

Tautomerism

Vinyl alcohol and acetaldehyde are tautomers: the hydrogen of the hydroxyl group migrates to the terminal carbon while the double bond shifts, converting the enol into the aldehyde. The equilibrium lies overwhelmingly toward acetaldehyde; the enol fraction at room temperature is on the order of one part in a million or less. Vinyl alcohol has nevertheless been generated and observed transiently in the gas phase, in pyrolysis experiments, in matrix isolation studies, and by spectroscopic means, and it has even been detected in interstellar molecular clouds.

Polyvinyl alcohol

Despite the monomer's instability, its polymer, poly(vinyl alcohol) (PVA or PVOH), is a major industrial material used in adhesives, paper coatings, textiles, and water-soluble films. Because vinyl alcohol cannot be polymerized directly, PVA is manufactured indirectly, by polymerizing vinyl acetate and then hydrolyzing the resulting poly(vinyl acetate).
