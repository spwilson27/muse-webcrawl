Ismail Enver Pasha (Ottoman Turkish: اسماعیل انور پاشا; Turkish: İsmail Enver Paşa; 22 November 1881 – 4 August 1922) was an Ottoman Turkish military officer, revolutionary, and convicted war criminal who became a leader of the Young Turk Revolution and, as Minister of War, one of the most powerful figures in the Ottoman Empire during the First World War. Together with Talaat Pasha and Djemal Pasha, he formed the ruling triumvirate known as the "Three Pashas".

Enver Pasha, Ottoman Minister of War during the First World War.

Early life and career

Enver was born in Constantinople (Istanbul) in 1881, the son of a bridge-keeper and public works employee. He graduated from the Ottoman Military Academy in 1903 as a staff captain and served in Macedonia, where he joined the Committee of Union and Progress (CUP). In 1908 he was a leading figure in the Young Turk Revolution that restored the constitution, and he became a national hero celebrated as the "Hero of Liberty". He served as military attaché in Berlin, fought in the Italo-Turkish War in Libya in 1911, and in the Balkan Wars helped recapture Edirne in 1913, after which he took part in the CUP's seizure of power in the Raid on the Sublime Porte.

First World War

Appointed Minister of War in January 1914, Enver purged the officer corps, promoted Germany's military mission, and concluded the secret Ottoman–German alliance that brought the empire into the First World War on the side of the Central Powers. His personal direction of the Caucasus campaign ended in catastrophe at the Battle of Sarikamish (December 1914 – January 1915), where the Ottoman Third Army was largely destroyed by Russian forces and winter conditions. Historians also identify Enver as one of the architects of the Armenian genocide of 1915–1917; in 1919 an Ottoman court-martial sentenced him to death in absentia for his role in the empire's wartime crimes and defeats.

Exile and death

When the empire collapsed in October 1918, Enver fled aboard a German submarine. He travelled to Moscow, where he sought Soviet support for his ambitions in Central Asia, then joined — and eventually turned against — the Basmachi movement in Turkestan, presenting himself as a leader of a pan-Turkic revolt against Bolshevik rule. He was killed on 4 August 1922 near Baljuvon (in present-day Tajikistan) in a clash with Red Army forces, reportedly struck down in a cavalry charge led by the Armenian Bolshevik commander Hakob Melkumov. His remains were repatriated to Turkey in 1996 and reburied in Istanbul.
