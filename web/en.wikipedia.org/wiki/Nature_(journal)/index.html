Nature (journal)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The cover of a recent issue of Nature: the word 'nature' in white serif type over a dramatic science photograph]

Nature is a British weekly scientific journal founded in 1869, among the world's most cited and prestigious scientific publications. It publishes peer-reviewed research across the sciences, alongside news, reviews, correspondence and other features. Its publisher is Nature Portfolio, part of Springer Nature.

Many landmark papers first appeared in Nature, including the discovery of the structure of DNA — Watson and Crick's famous 1953 letter — early reports of X-rays and wave mechanics, and much modern genomics and climate research.

History

The journal's first editor was Norman Lockyer; its first issue was published on 4 November 1869. It was aimed at providing 'an accessible forum for the communication of scientific research' to scientists and the educated public alike.

Editorial process

Only a small fraction of submitted manuscripts are sent for peer review, and fewer still accepted; publication in Nature is highly prized in academic careers, and its impact factor has commonly been around 40-50 in recent years.
