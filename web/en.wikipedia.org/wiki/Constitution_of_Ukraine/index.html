Constitution of Ukraine

The Constitution of Ukraine (Ukrainian: Конституція України) is the fundamental law of Ukraine. It was adopted and ratified at the fifth session of the Verkhovna Rada, the parliament of Ukraine, on 28 June 1996. The constitution was adopted with 321 votes in favour, and 28 June is celebrated as Constitution Day, a public holiday.

Overview

The constitution declares Ukraine a sovereign, democratic, social, law-based state and guarantees human and citizen rights and freedoms. It establishes a semi-presidential system: the President is head of state, the Verkhovna Rada is the sole legislative body of 450 deputies, and executive power is exercised by the Cabinet of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister.

Amendments

Major amendments were adopted in 2004, shifting powers toward parliament; these were annulled by the Constitutional Court in 2010 and restored in 2014. Amendments in 2019 enshrined the strategic course toward membership of the European Union and NATO.
