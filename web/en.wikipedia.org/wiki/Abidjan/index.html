Overview

Abidjan is the largest city of Côte d'Ivoire and its economic capital, although Yamoussoukro has been the official political capital since 1983. The city spreads around the Ébrié Lagoon on the Gulf of Guinea and is made up of distinct districts — the glass-towered Plateau business quarter, the residential Cocody, the commercial Treichville and the vast working-class commune of Yopougon among them.

Abidjan grew from a small fishing village after the French made it a colonial capital in 1934 and especially after the Vridi Canal opened the lagoon to the sea in 1950, creating one of West Africa's most important deep-water ports.

Economy and culture

The Port of Abidjan handles the great majority of Côte d'Ivoire's trade and much transit trade for landlocked neighbours such as Mali and Burkina Faso. The city is the country's centre of banking, industry and media, and it is known for a lively music scene — it is a historic home of zouglou and coupé-décalé — and for the striking modernist St. Paul's Cathedral.

Rapid growth since independence has made Abidjan one of the largest French-speaking cities in the world; its population figures vary considerably between estimates.
