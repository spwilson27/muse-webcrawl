Sizewell B nuclear power station

Sizewell B
Location: Sizewell, Suffolk, England
Operator: EDF Energy
Reactors: 1 × pressurised water reactor
Output: ~1,198 MW

Sizewell B is a nuclear power station on the Suffolk coast in England, near the village of Sizewell. It is the United Kingdom's only commercial pressurised water reactor (PWR), based on the Westinghouse design, and the last large nuclear power station to be built in Britain in the 20th century. It is operated by EDF Energy.

History

The decision to build a PWR at Sizewell followed a lengthy public inquiry held between 1983 and 1985, one of the longest in British planning history. Construction began in 1988, and the reactor first supplied power to the National Grid in February 1995. The station's distinctive white dome encloses the reactor containment building.

Design

Sizewell B uses a four-loop pressurised water reactor derived from plants built by Westinghouse in the United States, adapted to British requirements with additional safety and control systems. The unit has a net electrical output of about 1,198 megawatts, supplying roughly 3 per cent of the UK's electricity. The neighbouring Sizewell A station, with two Magnox reactors, ceased generation in 2006 and is being decommissioned.

Future

The station is expected to operate until at least 2035, with EDF exploring a possible lifetime extension. A new station, Sizewell C, comprising two EPR reactors, has been approved for construction adjacent to the site, replicating the design of Hinkley Point C.
