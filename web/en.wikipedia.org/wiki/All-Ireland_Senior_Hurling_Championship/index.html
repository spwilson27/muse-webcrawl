The All-Ireland Senior Hurling Championship is an annual inter-county hurling competition organised by the Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA). First held in 1887, it is the premier competition in the sport of hurling and one of Ireland's most attended sporting events. The winning team receives the Liam MacCarthy Cup.

History

The first championship was contested in 1887, three years after the foundation of the GAA, and was won by Tipperary (represented by Thurles). For most of its history the championship was a straight knockout competition contested by the winners and runners-up of the provincial championships in Leinster and Munster, with Galway and a representative from Ulster entering at the semi-final stage. Reforms in 2018 introduced provincial round-robin league formats in Leinster and Munster, ensuring the strongest counties more matches.

Structure

Teams compete first in the Leinster and Munster championships, played as round-robin groups. The provincial finalists qualify directly for the All-Ireland semi-finals, while the third-placed teams meet the finalists of the Joe McDonagh Cup (the second tier) in preliminary quarter-finals. The All-Ireland final is traditionally held at Croke Park in Dublin in July.

Dominant teams

The championship has been dominated by the "big three" of Kilkenny (36 titles), Cork (30) and Tipperary (28). Limerick, Wexford, Galway, Offaly, Dublin, Waterford and Clare have also won titles. Under manager Brian Cody, Kilkenny won eleven All-Irelands between 2000 and 2015, including four in a row (2006–2009). Limerick matched the four-in-a-row feat from 2020 to 2023 under John Kiely.
