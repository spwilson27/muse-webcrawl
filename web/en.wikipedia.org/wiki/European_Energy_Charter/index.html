European Energy Charter

The European Energy Charter was a political declaration adopted in The Hague on 17 December 1991, committing its signatories to cooperation in the energy sector between Eastern and Western Europe after the end of the Cold War. It formed the political basis for the legally binding Energy Charter Treaty of 1994, which established a multilateral framework for cross-border energy trade, transit, investment protection, and energy efficiency.

Background

The initiative originated with Dutch Prime Minister Ruud Lubbers, who proposed in 1990 that the energy resources of the Soviet Union and Eastern Europe be developed with Western investment and technology in exchange for secure supplies for Western Europe. Following the dissolution of the Soviet Union, the Charter sought to integrate the energy sectors of the former communist states into European and global markets on market-economy principles.

From declaration to treaty

The 1991 declaration was non-binding, but negotiations under its mandate produced the Energy Charter Treaty and the Energy Charter Protocol on Energy Efficiency and Related Environmental Aspects, signed in Lisbon in December 1994 and in force from April 1998. The Energy Charter Conference and its secretariat in Brussels oversee the process.
