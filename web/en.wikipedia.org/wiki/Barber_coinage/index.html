Barber coinage

Obverse of a Barber dime.

The Barber coinage is a series of United States silver coins — the dime, quarter dollar, and half dollar — designed by Chief Engraver Charles E. Barber of the United States Mint. The three denominations share a common design: a head of Liberty wearing a Phrygian cap and laurel wreath on the obverse, and (for the quarter and half dollar) a heraldic eagle on the reverse. The dime's reverse carries a wreath surrounding the words ONE DIME.

Background and design

By 1891 the Seated Liberty designs then in use had been struck for more than fifty years and were widely unpopular. After a design competition failed to satisfy Mint officials, Barber, who had succeeded his father William Barber as Chief Engraver in 1879, was directed to prepare the new coinage himself. The coins entered production in 1892.

Production and successor designs

Barber dimes and quarters were struck from 1892 to 1916, and Barber half dollars from 1892 to 1915, at the mints in Philadelphia, New Orleans, San Francisco and Denver. The series includes one of the most famous American rarities, the 1894-S dime, of which only 24 were struck and nine are known. When the designs became eligible for change after 25 years, the Treasury held a new competition in 1916 that produced the Mercury dime, Standing Liberty quarter and Walking Liberty half dollar. Because most Barber coins circulated heavily, examples in uncirculated condition are scarce, though the series as a whole remains relatively affordable and popular with collectors.
