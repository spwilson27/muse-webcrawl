Common carotid artery

Common carotid artery
SourceLeft: aortic arch; Right: brachiocephalic trunk
BranchesInternal and external carotid arteries
SuppliesHead and neck

In anatomy, the common carotid artery is a large artery on each side of the neck that supplies blood to the head and neck. The left common carotid arises directly from the aortic arch, while the right arises from the bifurcation of the brachiocephalic trunk.

Course and branches

Each common carotid ascends the neck within the carotid sheath alongside the internal jugular vein and vagus nerve. At about the level of the upper border of the thyroid cartilage it divides into the external carotid artery, supplying the face and scalp, and the internal carotid artery, which continues upward to supply the brain. Near the bifurcation lie the carotid sinus, a pressure receptor, and the carotid body, a chemoreceptor.

Clinical significance

The carotid pulse is palpated along the anterior border of the sternocleidomastoid muscle. Narrowing (stenosis) of the carotid bifurcation by atherosclerosis is a major cause of stroke and may be treated by carotid endarterectomy or stenting.
