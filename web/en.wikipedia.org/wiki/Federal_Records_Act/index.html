Federal Records Act

Enacted1950 (as the Federal Records Act of 1950)
Codified44 U.S.C. ch. 21, 29, 31, 33
Administered byNational Archives and Records Administration (NARA)

The Federal Records Act is a body of United States law governing how federal agencies create, manage, preserve, and dispose of records. Its foundations were laid by the Federal Records Act of 1950, and its provisions now appear in chapters 21, 29, 31, and 33 of Title 44 of the U.S. Code.

Requirements

The act requires each agency head to make and preserve records documenting the organization, functions, policies, decisions, and essential transactions of the agency, and to maintain an active records management programme. Records may be destroyed only under schedules approved by the Archivist of the United States; unauthorized destruction must be reported to the National Archives and Records Administration (NARA), which oversees the federal records system, operates records centres, and takes permanent custody of historically valuable records. The Presidential Records Act, a related statute, applies the same principles to presidential records.

Amendments and digital records

Later amendments — notably the Federal Records Act Amendments of 2014 — extended the statutory definition of records to electronic formats, clarified officials' obligations when using private email and messaging accounts for government business, and strengthened NARA's authority. The transition to electronic recordkeeping, including an end to accepting most paper transfers, has been the act's central modern challenge.
