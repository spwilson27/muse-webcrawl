Théodore Simon

Théodore Simon (10 July 1873 – 4 September 1961) was a French psychologist and physician who, together with Alfred Binet, developed the first practical intelligence test, the Binet–Simon scale. His work laid the foundation for modern psychometric testing of mental ability.

Early life and career

Simon studied medicine in Paris and worked in psychiatric institutions, including the asylum at Saint-Yon near Rouen and the colony for intellectually disabled children at Perray-Vancluse. His clinical experience with children of differing abilities brought him into contact with Alfred Binet.

The Binet–Simon scale

In 1904 the French Ministry of Public Instruction appointed a commission to find a way to identify children who would struggle in ordinary classrooms. Binet and Simon published their first scale in 1905, consisting of 30 tasks of increasing difficulty; revised versions appeared in 1908 and 1911, introducing the concept of mental age. After Binet's death in 1911, Simon continued to advocate for the test and for careful, humane assessment of children.

Later work

Simon served as a psychiatrist during the First World War and later directed psychiatric services. He remained active in French psychology for decades, defending Binet's legacy against purely mechanical uses of intelligence testing.
