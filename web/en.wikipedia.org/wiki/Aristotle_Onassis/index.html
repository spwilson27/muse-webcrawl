Aristotle Socrates Onassis (Greek: Αριστοτέλης Ωνάσης; 20 January 1906 – 15 March 1975) was a Greek and Argentine business magnate. He amassed the world's largest privately owned shipping fleet and was one of the world's richest and most famous men. He was married to Athina Mary Livanos, had a long-standing affair with opera singer Maria Callas, and was later married to American former First Lady Jacqueline Kennedy.

Early life

Onassis was born in Smyrna (now İzmir), in the Ottoman Empire, to a prosperous tobacco merchant family. After the Greco-Turkish War and the Great Fire of Smyrna in 1922, the family lost its fortune and fled as refugees to Greece. In 1923 Onassis emigrated to Buenos Aires, Argentina, where he worked as a telephone operator by night and traded tobacco by day, building his first fortune importing Turkish tobacco.

Shipping empire

During the Great Depression Onassis bought freight ships at bargain prices and expanded into tankers; in the 1950s he pioneered very large crude carriers and ordered among the first supertankers. He founded Olympic Airways, the Greek national airline, in 1957. His bitter rivalry with fellow shipping tycoon Stavros Niarchos was a staple of the society press.

Personal life and death

Onassis's marriage to Jacqueline Kennedy in 1968, on his private island of Skorpios, drew worldwide attention. His son Alexander died in a plane crash in 1973, a loss from which Onassis never recovered; he died of myasthenia gravis-related respiratory failure in Paris in 1975. His daughter Christina inherited much of the empire, and his granddaughter Athina Onassis became the principal heir.
