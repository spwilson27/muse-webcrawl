Giuseppe Meazza

Giuseppe Meazza (Italian pronunciation: [dʒuˈzɛppe meˈattsa]; 23 August 1910 – 21 August 1979) was an Italian footballer who played principally for Internazionale (then Ambrosiana-Inter) and the Italy national team. A prolific forward and one of the first true stars of Italian football, he captained Italy to victory at the 1938 FIFA World Cup and also won the 1934 tournament, making him one of the few players of his era with two World Cup titles.

Career

Born in Milan and rejected by AC Milan as a slight teenager, Meazza was taken on by Inter, debuting in 1927 at seventeen — his nickname il Balilla came from a journalist's surprise at the youngster's selection. He was Serie A's top scorer three times, won three league titles (1930, 1938, 1940) and the Coppa Italia in 1939 with Inter, scoring a remarkable 284 goals for the club in 408 appearances — still a club record. A brief, less happy spell at AC Milan and later Juventus, Atalanta and a return to Inter followed, as injuries and the war years took their toll; he retired in 1947. For Italy he scored 33 goals in 53 matches between 1930 and 1939, a national record that stood until surpassed by Gigi Riva — his tally included goals in both World Cup-winning campaigns under Vittorio Pozzo.

Style and legacy

Meazza is remembered as a complete attacking player — a dribbling, inventive inside-forward or centre-forward with a powerful shot, famed for his invitation to goalkeepers to commit before rounding them, a move Italian fans called a goal alla Meazza. Milan's San Siro stadium is officially named the Stadio Giuseppe Meazza in his honour.
