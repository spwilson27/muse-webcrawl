Louisville, Kentucky

Louisville is the most populous city in Kentucky, located on the Ohio River in north-central Kentucky. Founded by George Rogers Clark in 1778 and named for King Louis XVI of France, it is the seat of Jefferson County and merged with the county government in 2003.

History and economy

Growing as a portage and river port around the Falls of the Ohio, Louisville became a shipping and industrial centre. Today it hosts UPS's global air hub, major healthcare and logistics employers, and a noted bourbon distilling heritage.

Culture

The city is best known for the Kentucky Derby at Churchill Downs each May. It was the hometown of boxer Muhammad Ali, honoured at the Muhammad Ali Center, and holds a large collection of Victorian homes in Old Louisville.
