Persian traditional music

Persian traditional music (Persian: musiqi-ye sonnati-ye irani) or Persian classical music is the traditional and indigenous music of Iran and Persian-speaking countries. It is based on the modal system of the dastgah: a collection of melodic figures (gusheh) organized into a set of modes, codified in a repertoire called the radif.

The dastgah system

The radif is traditionally divided into twelve dastgah and avaz (subsidiary modes), including Shur, Mahur, Homayun, Segah, Chahargah, Nava and Rast-Panjgah. A performance typically proceeds from an introduction (daramad) through increasingly ornamented melodic developments (owj, the climax) and back down. Improvisation (bedahsazi) within the modal framework is central, and musicians learn the radif by rote from a master (ostad) over many years.

Instruments

Principal instruments include the long-necked lutes tar and setar, the santur (hammered dulcimer), the kamancheh (spike fiddle), the ney (reed flute), the daf and tombak (percussion), and the robab. The vocal tradition sets classical Persian poetry — by Rumi, Hafez, Saadi and others — to song (avaz). In 2009 the radif was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
