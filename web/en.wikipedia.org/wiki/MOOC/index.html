Massive open online course

MOOCs deliver courses to large online audiences

A massive open online course (MOOC) is an online course aimed at unlimited participation and open access via the web. MOOCs provide interactive course materials such as video lectures, readings, problem sets and quizzes, along with discussion forums supporting interaction among students and instructors.

History

The term was coined in 2008 by Dave Cormier for connectivist courses run by Stephen Downes and George Siemens. The format exploded in popularity in 2011–2012 — dubbed "the year of the MOOC" by The New York Times — when Stanford spin-offs Udacity and Coursera launched, Harvard and MIT founded the nonprofit edX, and Sebastian Thrun's and Andrew Ng's online courses attracted hundreds of thousands of enrolments.

Features and criticism

MOOCs are typically free to audit, with fees for certificates, graded assignments and degree pathways such as MicroMasters and online master's degrees. They promise to widen access to education worldwide, but are criticized for low completion rates (often under 10%), limited instructor interaction, and cohorts skewed toward already-educated learners. Platforms have increasingly pivoted toward professional development and corporate training.
