Florence Cathedral (Italian: Duomo di Firenze), formally the Cathedral of Saint Mary of the Flower (Cattedrale di Santa Maria del Fiore), is the cathedral of Florence, Italy. It was begun in 1296 in the Gothic style to a design of Arnolfo di Cambio and was structurally completed by 1436, with the dome engineered by Filippo Brunelleschi. The exterior of the basilica is faced with polychrome marble panels in shades of green and pink, bordered by white, and has an elaborate 19th-century Gothic Revival façade by Emilio De Fabris.

The cathedral complex, in Piazza del Duomo, includes the Baptistery of St. John and Giotto's Campanile. These three buildings are part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site covering the historic centre of Florence. The basilica is one of Italy's largest churches and until the modern era, its dome was the largest in the world; it remains the largest brick dome ever constructed.

History

The cathedral was built on the site of the earlier church of Santa Reparata. The ambitious design, promoted by the wealthy Florentine republic, was begun under Arnolfo di Cambio in 1296 and continued by Giotto — who designed the campanile (bell tower) in 1334 — and later by Francesco Talenti and others, who enlarged the plan in the mid-14th century.

The dome

The vast octagonal dome posed an unprecedented engineering problem: it was too large for traditional wooden centering. In 1418 a competition was won by Filippo Brunelleschi, whose double-shell brick dome, built with herringbone brickwork and innovative hoisting machines of his own design, was completed in 1436. The lantern on top was finished after Brunelleschi's death. The dome remains an icon of Renaissance engineering and of the city of Florence.

Interior and art

The interior is comparatively austere, in keeping with Florentine taste, but contains notable works including Paolo Uccello's equestrian monument to Sir John Hawkwood, the monumental clock by Uccello, Giorgio Vasari and Federico Zuccari's fresco of the Last Judgment inside the dome, and Domenico di Michelino's painting of Dante and the Divine Comedy (1465).
