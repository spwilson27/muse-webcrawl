Street dance
OriginsUnited States, 1970s
StylesBreaking, locking, popping, house, waacking, krump
CultureHip-hop culture
SettingsStreets, block parties, battles, cyphers

Street dance is an umbrella term for a large number of social dance styles created outside dance studios, in everyday spaces such as streets, schoolyards, and nightclubs. Many are improvisational and social in nature, encouraging interaction and creative expression. Street dance is connected with the culture of hip-hop and funk music.

History

The earliest styles emerged in the 1970s: breaking (b-boying/b-girling) in the Bronx, New York, alongside DJing, MCing, and graffiti as elements of hip-hop culture; and the funk styles locking (created by Don Campbell in Los Angeles) and popping (from Fresno, California, associated with Boogaloo Sam) on the West Coast. Later styles include house dance from Chicago and New York clubs, waacking, voguing from Harlem ballroom culture, and krump.

Culture and competition

Street dance is practised in cyphers — circles where dancers take turns improvising — and in judged battles. International competitions such as Juste Debout (Paris) and Red Bull BC One drew global participation. Breaking made its Olympic debut at the Paris 2024 Summer Games.
