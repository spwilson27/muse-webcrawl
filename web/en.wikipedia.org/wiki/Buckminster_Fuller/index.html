Buckminster Fuller

Richard Buckminster Fuller
Born: 12 July 1895, Milton, Massachusetts, U.S.
Died: 1 July 1983, Los Angeles, California, U.S.
Known for: Geodesic dome, Dymaxion, Spaceship Earth, synergetics

Richard Buckminster Fuller (July 12, 1895 – July 1, 1983) was an American architect, systems theorist, writer, designer, inventor, philosopher, and futurist. He styled his name as R. Buckminster Fuller in his writings, publishing more than thirty books and coining or popularising terms such as "Spaceship Earth", "Dymaxion" (e.g., Dymaxion house, Dymaxion car, Dymaxion map), "ephemeralization", "synergetics", and "tensegrity".

Work

Fuller developed numerous inventions, mainly architectural designs, and popularised the widely known geodesic dome; carbon molecules known as fullerenes were later named by scientists for their structural and mathematical resemblance to geodesic spheres. He also served as the second World President of Mensa International from 1974 to 1983.

Fuller was awarded 28 United States patents and many honorary doctorates. In 1960, he was awarded a professorship at Southern Illinois University Carbondale, and in 1968, he was made University Professor. In 1970, he received the Gold Medal of the American Institute of Architects. In 1983, shortly before his death, President Ronald Reagan presented him with the Presidential Medal of Freedom.

Philosophy

Fuller was a pioneer in thinking globally; he developed the concept of "Spaceship Earth" to emphasise that humanity must manage the planet's finite resources cooperatively. His "World Game" proposed distributing resources equitably through simulation. He coined the term "ephemeralization" to describe the trend of doing "more with less" through technology. Fuller also kept a meticulous chronological archive of his life, the Dymaxion Chronofile, recording his activities every 15 minutes from 1920 to 1983.

Major projects

  • Dymaxion house (1929)
  • Dymaxion car (1933)
  • Dymaxion map (1943)
  • Geodesic domes, including the United States pavilion at Expo 67 in Montreal (now the Biosphère)
  • Operating Manual for Spaceship Earth (1969)
  • Critical Path (1981)
