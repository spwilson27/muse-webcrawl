William Booth

Born: 10 April 1829, Nottingham, England
Died: 20 August 1912, London, England
Known for: Founding the Salvation Army
Spouse: Catherine Booth

William Booth (10 April 1829 – 20 August 1912) was a British Methodist preacher who, with his wife Catherine Booth, founded the Salvation Army and became its first General. The organisation developed into a worldwide Christian church and charitable movement.

Early life and ministry

Booth was born in Sneinton, Nottingham, to a family that fell into poverty after his father's business failures. Apprenticed to a pawnbroker as a teenager, he experienced a religious conversion and began preaching, first with the Methodists and later with the Methodist New Connexion, from which he resigned in 1861 to become an independent itinerant evangelist.

The Salvation Army

In 1865 Booth began mission work among the poor of London's East End, founding the Christian Mission. In 1878 the mission was reorganised along military lines as the Salvation Army, with Booth as its General. Its officers wore uniforms, used military ranks, and combined evangelism with practical relief work — soup kitchens, shelters, and employment bureaux.

In Darkest England

Booth's 1890 book In Darkest England and the Way Out set out an ambitious scheme of social reform, including labour exchanges, emigration assistance, and farm colonies. It became a bestseller and shaped the Army's social programme.

Death and legacy

Booth remained General until his death in 1912, when he was succeeded by his son Bramwell Booth. His funeral drew enormous crowds in London, and the Salvation Army continues to operate in more than 130 countries.
