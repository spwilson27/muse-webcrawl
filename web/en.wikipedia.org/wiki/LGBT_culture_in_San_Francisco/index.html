LGBT culture in San Francisco

LGBT culture in San Francisco is among the oldest and most prominent in the United States. San Francisco is widely regarded as one of the world's leading centres of lesbian, gay, bisexual, and transgender culture, with the Castro District serving as its best-known neighbourhood. The city has been described as a "gay mecca" and played a central role in the American gay liberation movement.

History

San Francisco's gay history traces back to the Gold Rush era and the Barbary Coast, but it accelerated after the Second World War, when thousands of servicemen discharged for homosexuality settled in the city. By the 1960s the city had a visible gay bar scene, and in 1964 the Council on Religion and the Homosexual and the Society for Individual Rights were founded. The Compton's Cafeteria riot of 1966 in the Tenderloin was an early act of transgender resistance.

In the 1970s the Castro transformed from a working-class Irish neighbourhood into a gay village, driven by figures such as Harvey Milk, who in 1977 became one of the first openly gay elected officials in the United States when he won a seat on the Board of Supervisors. Milk and Mayor George Moscone were assassinated in 1978 by former supervisor Dan White; the light sentence White received provoked the White Night riots of 1979.

Institutions and events

The San Francisco Pride parade, first held in 1970, is one of the largest in the world. Other major events include the Folsom Street Fair and the Castro Street Fair. Institutions include the GLBT Historical Society, the James C. Hormel Gay and Lesbian Center at the San Francisco Public Library, and the giant rainbow flag at Harvey Milk Plaza. The city was also at the centre of the AIDS crisis of the 1980s, which devastated its gay community but also produced influential activist and caregiving organisations such as the San Francisco AIDS Foundation.

Demographics

San Francisco consistently ranks among U.S. cities with the highest proportion of residents identifying as LGBT. Historically centred on the Castro, LGBT residents are now spread across neighbourhoods including the Mission, Bernal Heights, and the Tenderloin.
