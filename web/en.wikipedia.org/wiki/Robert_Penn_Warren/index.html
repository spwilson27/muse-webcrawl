Robert Penn Warren

Robert Penn Warren
Born: April 24, 1905, Guthrie, Kentucky
Died: September 15, 1989, Stratton, Vermont
Notable work: All the King's Men (1946)

Robert Penn Warren (April 24, 1905 – September 15, 1989) was an American poet, novelist and literary critic. He is the only person to have won Pulitzer Prizes for both fiction and poetry: for his novel All the King's Men (1946), widely regarded as one of the great American political novels, and for the poetry collections Promises: Poems 1954–1956 (1957) and Now and Then (1978). In 1986 he was named the first Poet Laureate Consultant in Poetry of the United States.

Life and career

Warren was born in Guthrie, Kentucky, and educated at Vanderbilt University, where he joined the Fugitive poets and contributed to the Agrarian manifesto I'll Take My Stand (1930). A Rhodes Scholarship took him to New College, Oxford. With Cleanth Brooks, he co-founded The Southern Review at Louisiana State University and co-authored the influential New Criticism textbooks Understanding Poetry (1938) and Understanding Fiction (1943). He later taught at Yale University for many years.

All the King's Men

All the King's Men follows the rise and assassination of Willie Stark, a populist Southern governor modeled in part on Louisiana's Huey Long, narrated by journalist Jack Burden. The novel won the Pulitzer Prize for fiction and was adapted into a 1949 film that won the Academy Award for Best Picture, and again into a 2006 film. Warren's other novels include World Enough and Time (1950) and Band of Angels (1955).

Later work

Warren's later career engaged directly with the civil rights movement, notably in Segregation: The Inner Conflict in the South (1956) and Who Speaks for the Negro? (1965), recanting his earlier Agrarian defence of segregation. He received the National Medal of Arts and a MacArthur "Genius" grant, and was elected to the American Academy of Arts and Letters.
