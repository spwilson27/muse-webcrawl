Zika virus (ZIKV) is a member of the virus family Flaviviridae, spread primarily by Aedes mosquitoes, which bite mainly during the day. It is named after the Ziika Forest of Uganda, where it was first isolated in 1947 from a sentinel rhesus monkey. Infection, known as Zika fever, is usually mild or asymptomatic, with symptoms similar to a mild form of dengue fever.

Transmission and symptoms

Besides mosquito bites, the virus can be transmitted sexually, from mother to fetus, and through blood transfusion. In most adults illness is brief — fever, rash, conjunctivitis, joint and muscle pain lasting a few days — and most infections cause no symptoms at all. Severe outcomes are rare in adults but include Guillain–Barré syndrome.

2015–2016 epidemic

A large epidemic that began in Brazil in 2015 spread across the Americas and was declared a Public Health Emergency of International Concern by the World Health Organization in February 2016. The epidemic established that infection during pregnancy can cause microcephaly and other severe congenital brain abnormalities (congenital Zika syndrome), transforming Zika from an obscure virus into a global health priority. No licensed vaccine or specific treatment exists; prevention focuses on mosquito control and bite avoidance.
