Sindarin

Sindarin is a fictional language devised by J. R. R. Tolkien, one of the languages of the Elves in his legendarium. It is the language most commonly spoken by the Elves of Middle-earth during the Third Age, in which The Lord of the Rings is set — the everyday tongue of the Sindar (Grey Elves) and the language of the Elven realms of Lothlórien and the Woodland Realm, as well as of Gondor's courtly usage in names and greetings.

Development

Tolkien, a philologist, gave Sindarin a Welsh-like flavour: its phonology and its system of initial consonant mutation resemble those of Welsh, which he greatly admired. Within the fiction, Sindarin descends from Common Telerin, the language of the Teleri who remained in Beleriand, diverging from the Quenya carried to Valinor. Tolkien worked on the language for decades, earlier calling it "Gnomish" and later "Noldorin" before settling its history in its final form.

Features

Sindarin is written in the Tengwar (as in the inscription on the West-gate of Moria) or in the Cirth runes. Notable features include plural formation by vowel change (i-affection, as in adan "man" → edain), lenition of initial consonants after certain words, and a rich stock of names in the narrative: Mithrandir (Grey Pilgrim, Gandalf), Aragorn, Galadriel's realm Lothlórien, and the greeting mae govannen ("well met"). The language is much less fully documented than Quenya, which limits how far new texts can be composed in it.

Use in adaptations

Sindarin dialogue features prominently in Peter Jackson's film adaptations of The Lord of the Rings and The Hobbit, with linguistic consultants constructing lines from Tolkien's published vocabulary. Fans and scholars study the language through journals such as Parma Eldalamberon and Vinyar Tengwar, which publish Tolkien's linguistic manuscripts.
