Life

James Price Johnson (1 February 1894 – 17 November 1955) was an American pianist and composer, the originator of the stride style of jazz piano and a central figure of the Harlem Renaissance. Raised in New York, he absorbed ragtime, blues, and the competitive pianism of Harlem's rent parties and clubs such as The Jungles Casino, where "cutting contests" decided reputations. He recorded piano rolls from 1917 and made landmark records in the 1920s ("Carolina Shout," "Keepin' Out of Mischief Now").

Stride piano

Stride took its name from the left hand's sweeping "stride" across the keyboard — bass note on beats one and three, chord on two and four, sometimes spanning tenths — while the right hand played syncopated, orchestral figures with shouts, rolls, and variations. Johnson's pupils and rivals included Willie "The Lion" Smith, Fats Waller (his protégé), and, in the next generation, Art Tatum and Duke Ellington learned from his example.

Compositions

Johnson wrote hundreds of songs, a ballet, and orchestral works reflecting ambitions beyond jazz. His tune "The Charleston" (1923, from the revue Runnin' Wild) supplied the signature dance craze of the Roaring Twenties. He also composed the opera De Organizer (with libretto by Langston Hughes), the folk opera The Dreamy Kid, and the symphonic Yamekraw — A Negro Rhapsody, performed at Carnegie Hall in 1928. Long undervalued in his lifetime, he was rediscovered by the traditional-jazz revival before a stroke ended his playing career.
