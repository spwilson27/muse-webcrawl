George Lee "Sparky" Anderson (February 22, 1934 – November 4, 2010) was an American Major League Baseball player and manager. He managed the Cincinnati Reds to two World Series championships in the 1970s and the Detroit Tigers to a third in 1984, becoming the first manager to win the World Series in both the National and American leagues. He retired with 2,194 managerial wins, then third on the all-time list, and was inducted into the Baseball Hall of Fame in 2000.

Playing career

Anderson played one major-league season as a second baseman, batting .218 for the Philadelphia Phillies in 1959, after a long apprenticeship in the minor leagues, principally in the Brooklyn/Los Angeles Dodgers organisation. His nickname "Sparky" came from his scrappy, argumentative play as a minor leaguer.

Managing career

After success managing in the minors, Anderson was a surprise appointment by the Reds in October 1969. His "Big Red Machine" teams — built around Johnny Bench, Joe Morgan, Pete Rose and Tony Pérez — won pennants in 1970 and 1972 and back-to-back World Series in 1975 and 1976, the 1975 victory over Boston being one of the most celebrated Series ever played. Anderson's quick use of his bullpen earned him the nickname "Captain Hook".

Dismissed by Cincinnati after the 1978 season, he joined the Detroit Tigers in June 1979 and rebuilt them into champions: the 1984 Tigers started 35–5 and won the World Series in five games over San Diego. Anderson also won the AL pennant in 1987 and was twice AL Manager of the Year. He retired after the 1995 season, later working as a broadcaster.

Anderson died in 2010 of complications from dementia. Both the Reds and Tigers retired tributes to him — Cincinnati retired his No. 10 and Detroit his No. 11.
