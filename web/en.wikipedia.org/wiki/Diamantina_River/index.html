Diamantina River

The Diamantina River is a river in central-western Queensland and northeastern South Australia. It is one of the main tributaries of the Lake Eyre basin and part of the Channel Country, a network of braided channels that flow only after heavy rain.

Course

The river rises in the Selwyn Range north-west of Mount Isa, flows south-west through towns including Winton, Diamantina National Park and Birdsville, crossing the border into South Australia, where it joins the Georgina River to form the Warburton River, which in flood reaches Lake Eyre.

History and name

Explorer William Landsborough named the river in 1862 in honour of Lady Diamantina Bowen, wife of Sir George Bowen, the first Governor of Queensland. Aboriginal peoples including the Pitta Pitta and Wangkanguru have long lived along its channels.

Ecology

Flood pulses across its vast floodplain — among the largest in Australia — trigger booms of waterbirds, fish and vegetation in an otherwise arid landscape. The catchment supports extensive cattle grazing on Mitchell grass downs.
