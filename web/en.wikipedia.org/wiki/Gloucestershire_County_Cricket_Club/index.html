Gloucestershire County Cricket Club

Gloucestershire County Cricket Club is one of eighteen first-class county clubs within the domestic cricket structure of England and Wales. It represents the historic county of Gloucestershire. Founded in 1870, its home ground is the Bristol County Ground (the Seat Unique Stadium) in Bristol.

History

The club is inseparably linked with the Grace family: W. G. Grace, the most famous cricketer of the Victorian era, captained the county in its early decades, alongside his brothers E. M. and G. F. Grace. Gloucestershire won the unofficial "Champion County" title in the 1870s. Later stars included Wally Hammond, one of England's greatest batsmen, and Tom Graveney. Although the club has never won the official County Championship, it enjoyed a celebrated run of one-day trophies under captain Mark Alleyne, winning five limited-overs titles between 1999 and 2004, plus the Royal London One-Day Cup in 2015 and the T20 Blast in 2024.

Grounds

As well as Bristol, the club plays annually at the Cheltenham Festival on the College Ground, one of the oldest cricket festivals in the world, dating to 1872.
