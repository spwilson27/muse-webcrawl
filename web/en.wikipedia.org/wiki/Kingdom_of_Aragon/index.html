The Kingdom of Aragon (Aragonese: Reino d'Aragón; Catalan: Regne d'Aragó; Spanish: Reino de Aragón) was a medieval and early modern kingdom on the Iberian Peninsula, corresponding to the modern-day autonomous community of Aragon in Spain. It emerged in 1035 from the partition of the Kingdom of Navarre and grew during the Reconquista, conquering Zaragoza in 1118 and expanding south to the mountains of Teruel.

History

In 1137 the kingdom was united dynastically with the County of Barcelona through the marriage of Queen Petronilla of Aragon and Count Ramon Berenguer IV, creating the Crown of Aragon, a confederation that went on to rule Catalonia, Valencia, Mallorca, and later Sicily, Sardinia and Naples. Within the Crown, the Kingdom of Aragon proper retained its own laws (the fueros), courts (the Cortes of Aragon) and chief magistrate, the Justicia de Aragón.

The dynastic union of Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile in 1469 laid the foundations of the Spanish monarchy, but Aragon kept its separate institutions until Philip V's Nueva Planta decrees abolished them in 1707, after the War of the Spanish Succession, formally ending the kingdom.

Legacy

The four red pallets on a gold field of Aragon's arms became one of the most recognisable heraldic symbols of the western Mediterranean. Today Aragon is an autonomous community of Spain with its capital at Zaragoza, and the historic kingdom's institutions are remembered in its statute of autonomy.
