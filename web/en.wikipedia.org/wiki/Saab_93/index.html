Saab 93

The Saab 93 (pronounced ninety-three) is a small family car produced by the Swedish manufacturer Saab from 1955 to 1960. Announced on 18 August 1955 in Stockholm, it was the successor to the Saab 92, featuring a redesigned body by Sixten Sason, a three-cylinder two-stroke engine, and a split windscreen replaced by a single piece in later production.

Design

The 93 was developed under project manager Rolf Mellde. Its new 748 cc three-cylinder two-stroke engine produced about 33 hp (25 kW), derived from a Heinkel/DKW design, and drove the front wheels through a three-speed gearbox, with a four-speed offered later. Coil-spring suspension replaced the transverse leaf springs of the 92. Versions included the standard 93, the improved 93B (1957, with one-piece windscreen), and the 93F (1960, with front-hinged doors), of which only about 600 were built before the Saab 96 took over.

Motorsport

The 93 was a successful rally car in the hands of Erik Carlsson, whose giant-killing results in the lightweight, front-wheel-drive car — including class wins and strong finishes in the RAC Rally and the 1000 Lakes Rally — built Saab's sporting reputation. The sporting GT750 (Granturismo) variant, introduced for the American market in 1958, offered a tuned engine and upgraded trim.
