Battle of Sainte-Foy

The Battle of Sainte-Foy (also called the Battle of Quebec) was fought on 28 April 1760 near Quebec City during the Seven Years' War (the French and Indian War in North America). A French army under the Chevalier de Lévis defeated the British garrison force of James Murray, who had marched out of Quebec to fight on the same ground where the Battle of the Plains of Abraham had been fought the previous year. It was the last French victory of the war in Canada and one of its bloodiest battles.

The battle

Murray, whose garrison had suffered grievously from scurvy and cold over the winter, chose to fight outside the city walls. The French, reinforced and attacking in strength, overlapped the British flanks; Murray's artillery, bogged in mud and snow, was overrun, and the British retreated into Quebec in disorder.

Aftermath

Lévis besieged Quebec but lacked heavy guns, and the arrival of a British naval squadron in May — which destroyed the French support ships on the St. Lawrence — forced him to withdraw to Montreal. The outcome of the war in Canada was thus decided by sea power: when both sides waited for the first sails up the river in spring 1760, they were British. Montreal capitulated in September 1760.
