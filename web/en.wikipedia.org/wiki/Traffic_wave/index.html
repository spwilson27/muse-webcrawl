Traffic wave

A traffic wave (also called a phantom traffic jam or stop-and-go traffic) is a travelling disturbance in the distribution of cars on a road in which vehicles alternately slow or stop and accelerate, even though no accident, obstruction, or other external cause is present. Traffic waves propagate upstream, against the direction of travel, typically at a fairly constant speed.

Causes

Waves arise when traffic density is high enough that small perturbations — a driver braking or changing lanes — are amplified by the delayed reactions of following drivers, who brake harder than the driver ahead. Experiments on circular tracks have shown that phantom jams form spontaneously once vehicle density exceeds a critical threshold.

Mitigation

Maintaining a large, steady following distance, variable speed limits, ramp metering, and adaptive cruise control can damp traffic waves. Traffic flow theory models them as kinematic waves using frameworks such as the Lighthill–Whitham–Richards model.
