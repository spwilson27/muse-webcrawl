Guillermo del Toro's Pinocchio (or simply Pinocchio) is a 2022 stop-motion animated musical fantasy film directed by Guillermo del Toro and Mark Gustafson, and written by del Toro and Patrick McHale. Based on Carlo Collodi's 1883 Italian novel The Adventures of Pinocchio, the film reimagines the classic story, setting it in Fascist Italy during the interwar period and the Second World War.

Plot

Geppetto, a woodcarver grieving the death of his young son Carlo during the First World War, drunkenly carves a wooden boy from the pine tree that grew over Carlo's grave. A Wood Sprite gives the puppet life, and Geppetto awakens to find the boy, Pinocchio, in his home. As Pinocchio struggles to live up to his father's expectations and navigates a world of fascist officers, a greedy circus count, and the afterlife itself, he learns what it means to be a real boy. The story explores themes of grief, disobedience, love, and mortality.

Production

Del Toro had developed the project since 2008, and it was finally produced by Netflix Animation with the Jim Henson Company and ShadowMachine. The film was animated using stop motion, with puppets built at ShadowMachine's studio in Portland, Oregon, and in Guadalajara, Mexico. It premiered at the BFI London Film Festival in October 2022 and was released on Netflix in December 2022.

Reception

The film received widespread critical acclaim for its animation, emotional depth, and darker take on the source material. It won the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature at the 95th Academy Awards, as well as the Golden Globe Award for Best Animated Feature Film and the BAFTA Award for Best Animated Film.
