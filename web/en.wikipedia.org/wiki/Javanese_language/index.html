Javanese language

A manuscript written in Javanese script

Javanese (basa Jawa) is an Austronesian language spoken by the Javanese people of the central and eastern parts of the island of Java, Indonesia. With roughly 68 million native speakers, it is the largest Austronesian language by number of first-language speakers and the most widely spoken regional language of Indonesia, though Indonesian dominates in education and national media.

Speech levels

Javanese is famous for its system of speech levels reflecting social hierarchy and politeness. The basic registers are ngoko (informal, used among intimates and to inferiors), madya (intermediate), and krama (polite, used to elders and superiors), supplemented by honorific vocabulary (krama inggil) that elevates the addressee. Choice of register pervades vocabulary, so that many common concepts have two or three entirely different words depending on level.

History and literature

Old Javanese (Kawi), heavily influenced by Sanskrit, was the language of a rich court literature from the 9th to 15th centuries, including the Ramayana and Mahabharata adaptations and the poem Arjunawiwaha. Middle and modern Javanese developed under the Mataram Sultanate; the Surakarta and Yogyakarta courts codified the modern standard. Literary and shadow-puppet (wayang) traditions remain important carriers of the language.

Writing systems

Javanese was traditionally written in its own Brahmic-derived script (hanacaraka) and in an Arabic-based script (pegon) for religious texts. Today the Latin alphabet predominates in everyday use, and the native script is taught in schools in Central and East Java as cultural heritage.
