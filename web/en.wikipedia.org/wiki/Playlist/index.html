Playlist

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A playlist is a list of video or audio files that can be played back on a media player, either sequentially or in a shuffled order. In its most general form, an audio playlist is simply a list of songs. The term originated in radio broadcasting, where station managers and disc jockeys drew up lists of songs to be played, and it was later adopted by television and by personal computing.

History

Early computer media players such as Winamp generated playlists in formats like M3U, which stores the locations of media files in plain text. The arrival of portable players such as the iPod and of music-download stores made personal playlists a central way of organising digital music libraries. With the rise of streaming services such as Spotify, Apple Music and YouTube, editorial and algorithmic playlists became an important means of music discovery and promotion, and placement on major playlists can significantly affect a recording's commercial performance.

Formats

  • M3U / M3U8 — plain-text format originating with Winamp; M3U8 uses UTF-8 and underlies HTTP Live Streaming.
  • PLS — an INI-like format introduced by Nullsoft.
  • XSPF — an XML-based open format ("spiff").
  • WPL / ASX — formats associated with Microsoft's Windows Media Player.

Types

Playlists may be user-made, collaboratively edited, generated automatically from listening habits, or curated by editors and DJs. Video-sharing sites use playlists to chain clips into continuous sequences, and television channels publish playlists of scheduled programming. "Smart playlists" update automatically when library items meet specified criteria.
