Alexios I Komnenos

Alexios I Komnenos (c. 1057 – 15 August 1118) was Byzantine emperor from 1081 to 1118. He seized the throne in a coup, halted the empire's collapse after Manzikert, and appealed to the West for help — an appeal that helped trigger the First Crusade.

Early career

Son of John Komnenos and nephew of Emperor Isaac I, Alexios served as a general against Turks, rebels and the Normans. With support of the Doukas family and his mother Anna Dalassene, he entered Constantinople in 1081.

Wars

He fought Robert Guiscard's Normans in the Balkans, suffering defeat at Dyrrhachium (1081) but eventually expelling them with Venetian naval help — at the price of major trade concessions to Venice. He defeated the Pechenegs decisively at Levounion in 1091 and stabilised the Danube frontier.

The Crusade

Alexios appealed to Pope Urban II at Piacenza in 1095 for mercenaries against the Seljuk Turks. The response far exceeded expectations: the armies of the First Crusade arrived in 1096–97. Alexios extracted oaths that former Byzantine lands would be returned, supplied and guided the crusaders, and recovered Nicaea and much of western Anatolia. Relations soured over Antioch, which Bohemond kept.

Reforms and legacy

Alexios reformed the coinage (introducing the hyperpyron), rebuilt the army and administration around the imperial family — the Komnenian system — and founded a restoration continued by John II and Manuel I. His daughter Anna Komnene chronicled his reign in the Alexiad.
