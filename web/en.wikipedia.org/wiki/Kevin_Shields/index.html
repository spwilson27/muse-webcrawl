Kevin Patrick Shields (born 21 May 1963) is an American-born Irish musician, singer-songwriter and record producer, best known as the vocalist and guitarist of the band My Bloody Valentine. He is a central figure in the creation of the shoegaze genre, famed for his "glide guitar" technique—strumming chords while manipulating the tremolo arm to produce bending, blurred textures—and for treating the recording studio as an instrument.

Early life

Shields was born in Queens, New York, to Irish immigrant parents and moved with his family to Dublin, Ireland, as a child. He formed My Bloody Valentine in Dublin in 1983 with drummer Colm Ó Cíosóig.

My Bloody Valentine

After early EPs, the band released Isn't Anything (1988) and Loveless (1991) on Creation Records. Loveless, produced obsessively over two years at enormous cost—contributing to Creation's financial troubles—became one of the most acclaimed albums of all time and the defining shoegaze record. The band stalled in the 1990s amid Shields's perfectionism and writer's block; Island Records dropped them. My Bloody Valentine reformed for live shows in 2007 and surprise-released the third album m b v in February 2013.

Other work

Shields remixed and produced other artists, was briefly a touring member of Primal Scream, and contributed music to Sofia Coppola's film Lost in Translation (2003), including the track "City Girl", earning film-score recognition. He is also known for the band's extreme live volume and his tinnitus advocacy.
