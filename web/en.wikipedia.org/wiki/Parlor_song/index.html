Parlor song

Parlor songs (also spelled parlour songs) were a popular genre of music in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, composed for performance in the home — specifically in the parlor, where a piano was the centrepiece of respectable middle-class entertainment. They were published as sheet music and sung by amateurs, typically with piano accompaniment.

Characteristics

Parlor songs were usually strophic or in simple ternary form, with lyrical melodies in a modest vocal range and undemanding piano accompaniments that amateur players could manage. Their texts were sentimental and decorous, dealing with love, longing, home, motherhood, nature, lost innocence and death, carefully avoiding anything considered improper.

History

The genre flourished in Britain and the United States from about the 1820s to the early 1900s, driven by the mass production of pianos and cheap sheet music. American composers such as Stephen Foster wrote emblematic examples — "Jeanie with the Light Brown Hair" and "Beautiful Dreamer" among them. In Britain, drawing-room ballads were staples of domestic music-making and of the publishing trade.

The parlor song declined with the arrival of ragtime, Tin Pan Alley, the phonograph and radio, which shifted popular music toward professional performance and recording. Its legacy persists in the art song and in the standard repertory of sentimental ballads.
