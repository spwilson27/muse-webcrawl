Environmental impact of meat production

The environmental impact of meat production includes greenhouse gas emissions, land and water use, deforestation, biodiversity loss and pollution associated with raising livestock for meat. Impacts vary widely by species and production system: ruminants (cattle, sheep) generally have far higher footprints per kilogram than pigs and poultry, which in turn exceed most plant foods.

Greenhouse gases

Livestock supply chains account for roughly 12–14.5% of anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions (FAO estimates). Sources include enteric methane from ruminants, nitrous oxide from manure and fertilized feed crops, and CO2 from land-use change and energy.

Land and water

Animal agriculture occupies about three-quarters of agricultural land while supplying a minority of calories. Meat production drives deforestation in the Amazon and Cerrado for pasture and soy feed. Water footprints for beef are typically an order of magnitude above those of staple crops per kilogram.

Other impacts

Nutrient runoff causes eutrophication; overgrazing degrades soils; intensive systems raise concerns over antibiotic resistance and air quality.

Mitigation

Options include dietary shifts, improved feed and manure management, methane-reducing feed additives, breeding, and alternative proteins such as plant-based and cultivated meat.
