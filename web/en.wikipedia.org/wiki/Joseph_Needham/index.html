Joseph Needham

BornNoel Joseph Terence Montgomery Needham, 9 December 1900, London, England
Died24 March 1995, Cambridge, England
FieldsBiochemistry; history of science

Noel Joseph Terence Montgomery Needham (9 December 1900 – 24 March 1995) was a British biochemist, historian of science and sinologist, best known for his monumental multi-volume work Science and Civilisation in China, which transformed Western understanding of the history of Chinese science and technology.

Early life and biochemistry

Needham studied at Gonville and Caius College, Cambridge, and worked in Frederick Gowland Hopkins's laboratory on embryology and morphogenesis, publishing the three-volume Chemical Embryology (1931). He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1941. His interest in China began in 1937 when Chinese research students — including Lu Gwei-djen, who became his collaborator and later his second wife — came to Cambridge.

China and Science and Civilisation

From 1942 to 1946 Needham directed the Sino-British Science Co-operation Office in Chongqing, travelling widely, collecting books and meeting Chinese scholars. Returning to Cambridge, he began Science and Civilisation in China; the first volume appeared in 1954, and the series — documenting Chinese achievements from astronomy and mathematics to gunpowder, printing, the magnetic compass and engineering — continued for decades with collaborators, published by Cambridge University Press.

The "Needham Question" — why modern science developed in Europe rather than in China, despite China's earlier technological lead — became a central problem in the history of science. Needham served as Master of Gonville and Caius College (1966–1976) and founded the Needham Research Institute in Cambridge.
