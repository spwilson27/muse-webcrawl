Pre-replication complex

The pre-replication complex (pre-RC) is a protein complex assembled at eukaryotic origins of replication during the G1 phase of the cell cycle. Its formation, called origin licensing, prepares the DNA for replication in the following S phase and ensures that each origin fires only once per cell cycle, so that the genome is copied exactly once.

Assembly

Assembly begins when the six-subunit origin recognition complex (ORC) binds to an origin. ORC recruits the proteins Cdc6 and Cdt1, which together load the replicative helicase, the MCM2-7 complex, onto the DNA as an inactive double hexamer encircling double-stranded DNA. This completes the pre-RC. Licensing can only occur when cyclin-dependent kinase (CDK) activity is low, which restricts it to late mitosis and G1.

Activation and control

At the onset of S phase, rising CDK and DDK (Dbf4-dependent kinase) activity phosphorylate pre-RC components and recruit additional factors — including Cdc45 and the GINS complex — converting MCM2-7 into the active CMG helicase that unwinds the DNA ahead of the replication fork. Re-licensing during S and G2 is prevented by CDK activity and by the protein geminin, which inhibits Cdt1; failure of these controls can cause re-replication and genome instability.
