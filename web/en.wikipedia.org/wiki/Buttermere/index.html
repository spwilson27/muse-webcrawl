Buttermere

Buttermere is a lake in the Lake District in North West England, in the county of Cumbria. It is about 2 kilometres (1.2 mi) long and 570 metres (1,870 ft) wide at most, and around 23 metres (75 ft) deep. The lake lies at the head of the valley of the River Cocker and is surrounded by fells, including Fleetwith Pike, Haystacks, and High Stile. The village of Buttermere stands at the lake's north-western end.

Geography

Buttermere is separated from neighbouring Crummock Water by a flat alluvial strip at its northern end; the two were once a single post-glacial lake. A short stream, Buttermere Dubs, connects them. A path runs around the entire lake, passing through a short rock tunnel on the south-western shore cut in the 19th century for the landowner of Hassness. The lake is owned by the National Trust, as is much of the surrounding land.

Name and history

The name is generally explained as Old English or Old Norse for "lake by the dairy pastures" (butter + mere). The lakeshore village was the home of Mary Robinson, the "Maid of Buttermere", whose beauty brought her fame in the 1790s and whose marriage to an impostor posing as a wealthy aristocrat — actually the forger and bigamist John Hatfield — became a national sensation in 1802 and inspired works by Wordsworth, Coleridge, and later the novelist Melvyn Bragg.

Walking

The circuit of the lake is one of the Lake District's most popular low-level walks. Alfred Wainwright considered Haystacks, on the southern shore, his favourite fell and asked for his ashes to be scattered there beside Innominate Tarn.
