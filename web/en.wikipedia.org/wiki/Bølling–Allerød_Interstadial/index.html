Bølling–Allerød Interstadial

Periodapprox. 14,700–12,900 years ago
TypeAbrupt warm interstadial
Named fromBølling lake and Allerød, Denmark

The Bølling–Allerød Interstadial was an abrupt warm and moist interstadial period that occurred during the final stages of the last glacial period, roughly 14,700 to 12,900 years before present, ending with the sudden onset of the colder Younger Dryas.

Phases and evidence

The interval is named for pollen sequences from Bølling Lake in Jutland and Allerød in Zealand, Denmark. It comprises the Bølling oscillation, a brief Older Dryas cooling, and the Allerød oscillation. Greenland ice cores show warming of several degrees within decades at its start, associated with a reinvigoration of the Atlantic overturning circulation and rising sea levels (meltwater pulse 1A).

Ecology and human history

Warming allowed birch and pine woodland to spread into northern Europe, expanded animal ranges, and coincided with Upper Palaeolithic cultures such as the Magdalenian and the emergence of the Natufian culture in the Levant.
