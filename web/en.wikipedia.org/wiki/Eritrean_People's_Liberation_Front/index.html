The Eritrean People's Liberation Front (EPLF) was an armed Eritrean nationalist organisation that fought for the independence of Eritrea from Ethiopia during the Eritrean War of Independence (1961–1991). It emerged around 1970 from dissidents of the earlier Eritrean Liberation Front (ELF), formally organising in the 1970s, and by the 1980s had become the dominant Eritrean insurgent movement, defeating the ELF in a civil war between the fronts.

War of independence

From its base areas around Nakfa in the northern highlands, the EPLF built a self-reliant movement with extensive underground facilities, workshops, schools and health services in the liberated zones, and a large guerrilla army (the EPLA) that combined guerrilla tactics with increasingly conventional operations. In alliance with the Ethiopian Tigray People's Liberation Front, it won decisive victories at Afabet (1988) and Massawa (1990), and in May 1991 EPLF forces took Asmara, ending the thirty-year war.

After independence

The EPLF formed the provisional government of Eritrea, organised the 1993 referendum in which independence was overwhelmingly approved, and in 1994, at its third congress, renamed itself the People's Front for Democracy and Justice (PFDJ), which remains the ruling — and only legal — party of Eritrea under President Isaias Afwerki, the EPLF's wartime leader.
