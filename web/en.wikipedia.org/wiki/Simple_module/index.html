Simple module

In mathematics, specifically in ring theory, a simple module over a ring R is a module M that is not the zero module and whose only submodules are the zero module and M itself. Simple modules are the analogues for modules of simple groups in group theory.

Properties

By Schur's lemma, the endomorphism ring of a simple module is a division ring. Every simple module is a quotient of R by a maximal left ideal, and every module of finite length can be built from simple modules via a composition series, whose factors are unique up to isomorphism and ordering by the Jordan–Hölder theorem. A module that is a direct sum of simple modules is called semisimple.

Examples

For a field k, the simple k-modules are exactly the one-dimensional vector spaces. For the ring of integers ℤ, the simple modules are the cyclic groups ℤ/pℤ for prime numbers p. In representation theory, the simple modules over a group algebra correspond to the irreducible representations of the group.
