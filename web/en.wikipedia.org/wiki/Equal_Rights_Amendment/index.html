Equal Rights Amendment

The Equal Rights Amendment (ERA) is a proposed amendment to the United States Constitution that would explicitly prohibit sex discrimination: "Equality of rights under the law shall not be denied or abridged by the United States or by any State on account of sex." First drafted by Alice Paul and Crystal Eastman and introduced in Congress in 1923, it was passed by Congress in 1972 and sent to the states with a seven-year ratification deadline, later extended to 1982.

Ratification campaign

Thirty-five of the required thirty-eight states ratified by 1977, but an organised opposition campaign led by Phyllis Schlafly's STOP ERA movement stalled the amendment, arguing it would remove protections for women and lead to measures such as women in combat and unisex facilities; five states voted to rescind their ratifications, the legal effect of which remains disputed. The deadline expired in 1982 with the ERA three states short.

Renewed efforts

Beginning in 2017, Nevada, Illinois and finally Virginia (in January 2020) ratified the amendment, bringing the count to thirty-eight — the "three-state strategy". Whether the amendment is now part of the Constitution remains contested: supporters argue the deadline and rescissions are legally ineffective, while federal officials and courts have debated the Archivist's duty to certify it. In January 2025 President Joe Biden declared the ERA "the law of the land", though the Archivist and the Justice Department did not act on the statement, leaving its legal status unresolved.
