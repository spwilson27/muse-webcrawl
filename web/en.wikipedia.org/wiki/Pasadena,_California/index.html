Pasadena is a city in Los Angeles County, California, United States, 11 miles (18 km) northeast of downtown Los Angeles. It is the most populous city and the primary cultural center of the San Gabriel Valley. Old Pasadena is the city's original commercial district.

Pasadena City Hall (1927), seen from Garfield Avenue.

Pasadena is known for hosting the annual Rose Bowl football game and the Tournament of Roses Parade each New Year's Day. It is also home to the California Institute of Technology (Caltech), the Norton Simon Museum, and the Pasadena Playhouse. NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory is located in nearby La Cañada Flintridge and is managed by Caltech.

History

The area was originally inhabited by the Hahamogna band of the Tongva people. Pasadena was founded in 1873 by settlers from Indiana led by Thomas Elliott, informally known as the Indiana Colony; the name "Pasadena" was chosen in 1875 and derives from a Chippewa (Ojibwe) phrase rendered as "Crown of the Valley". The city incorporated in 1886. It grew as a winter resort for wealthy Easterners and later as a centre of the Arts and Crafts movement, exemplified by the Gamble House (1908), designed by Charles and Henry Greene.

Geography

Pasadena lies at the base of the San Gabriel Mountains, at the western edge of the San Gabriel Valley. It is bordered by the San Rafael Hills to the west and the Arroyo Seco canyon, spanned by the Colorado Street Bridge (1913).

Culture

The Tournament of Roses Parade, first held in 1890, precedes the Rose Bowl Game at the Rose Bowl stadium. The city's museums include the Norton Simon Museum, noted for European and Asian art, and the Pacific Asia Museum. Pasadena is also associated with the annual Doo Dah Parade, a satirical counterpoint to the Rose Parade.

Education

Caltech, founded in 1891, is one of the world's leading science and engineering universities. Other institutions include ArtCenter College of Design, Pasadena City College, and Fuller Theological Seminary.
