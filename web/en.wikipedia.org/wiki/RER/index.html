The RER (Réseau Express Régional, "Regional Express Network") is a hybrid commuter rail and rapid transit system serving Paris and its suburbs in the Île-de-France region of France. It combines deep-level central tunnels with metro-like frequency and through-running onto suburban railway lines, a model that has inspired similar systems worldwide.

Network

The network has five lines, designated A through E, with about 257 stations over roughly 602 kilometres of track, much of it shared with suburban services. RER A — running from the western suburbs through central Paris to Marne-la-Vallée (Disneyland Paris) in the east — is one of the busiest urban rail lines in the world, carrying over a million passengers a day. RER B links Charles de Gaulle Airport and the northern suburbs to the south, including Orly Airport connections. Lines C and D follow largely SNCF suburban routes, and Line E, opened in 1999 as "Éole", is being extended westward.

History

The concept dates to the 1936 and postwar regional plans: existing suburban lines, which terminated at separate Parisian stations, were to be joined by new east–west and north–south tunnels beneath the city. The first cross-city section opened between Auber and Nation in stages from 1969; the network was formally named RER when the central link of line A opened in 1977. Its vast underground interchange stations, like Châtelet–Les Halles, are among the largest in the world.

Operation

The RER is jointly operated by RATP (which runs the Paris Métro) and the national railway SNCF, with dual-voltage trains running across both networks. Within central Paris it functions like a metro with integrated ticketing; fares become distance-based in the outer zones.
