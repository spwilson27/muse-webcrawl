Álava (Spanish: [ˈalaβa]) or Araba (Basque: [aˈɾaβa]), officially Araba/Álava, is a province and historical territory of Spain and of the Basque Country. It is the southernmost and largest of the three provinces in the Basque Autonomous Community, and the only landlocked one. Its capital, Vitoria-Gasteiz, is also the capital of the autonomous community and seat of the Basque government and parliament.

Geography

Álava consists largely of a broad plateau drained by the Ebro and its tributaries, bounded by mountains to the north (including Gorbea, 1,482 m, the province's highest point) and the Sierra de Cantabria to the south, beyond which lies the Rioja Alavesa wine region, famous for its Rioja wines. The province wholly surrounds the small Castilian enclave of Treviño.

History and government

The medieval Lordship and Brotherhood of Álava joined the Crown of Castile while retaining its own fueros (chartered privileges). Like the other Basque territories, Álava enjoys extensive fiscal autonomy through the concierto económico, and it is governed by a provincial council (Diputación Foral) and a general assembly (Juntas Generales). Vitoria-Gasteiz was named European Green Capital in 2012.
