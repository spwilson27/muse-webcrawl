Platoon

A platoon on patrol

A platoon is a military unit typically composed of two to four squads, sections, or patrols. It is usually the smallest unit commanded by a commissioned officer – a lieutenant – with a senior non-commissioned officer (a platoon sergeant) as second-in-command.

Organisation

In most armies a platoon numbers roughly 25 to 50 soldiers. In the United States Army, a rifle platoon consists of a headquarters and three rifle squads of nine soldiers each, while the British Army platoon likewise has three sections of eight. Three or four platoons, together with a headquarters element, normally form a company.

Platoons may be specialised – infantry, tank, mortar, anti-tank, reconnaissance, engineer, or signals platoons – and armoured platoons typically field four tanks or armoured vehicles. In cavalry units of the US Army the equivalent echelon is the platoon within a troop; in artillery the corresponding unit is organised as platoons within a battery.

History

The word derives from the French peloton ("little ball", from pelote), originally a small body of soldiers who fired together. The platoon as a standing tactical subunit became standard in European armies during the late 17th and 18th centuries with the development of platoon fire by volleys, and was retained as armies adopted modern squad-based tactics in the First World War.
