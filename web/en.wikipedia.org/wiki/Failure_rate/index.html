Failure rate

Failure rate is the frequency with which an engineered system or component fails, expressed in failures per unit of time. It is usually denoted by the Greek letter λ (lambda) and is important in reliability engineering. The failure rate of a system usually depends on time, with the rate varying over the life cycle of the system.

Bathtub curve

Many systems exhibit a "bathtub curve": a high but decreasing failure rate early in life (infant mortality, from manufacturing defects), a roughly constant rate during useful life, and an increasing rate as wear-out mechanisms dominate. For a constant failure rate, the reliability follows an exponential distribution and the mean time between failures is the reciprocal of λ.

Measurement

Failure rates are estimated from life testing, field data, and handbooks such as MIL-HDBK-217. Units include failures per hour and FIT (failures in time), one FIT being one failure per 109 device-hours. The failure rate of an assembly can be summed from component rates under the assumption of series reliability and independent failures.
