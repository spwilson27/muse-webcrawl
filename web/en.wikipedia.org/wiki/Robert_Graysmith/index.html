Robert Graysmith (born 17 September 1942) is an American author and former cartoonist best known for his books about the Zodiac Killer, the unidentified serial killer who operated in Northern California in the late 1960s and early 1970s. As a political cartoonist at the San Francisco Chronicle — one of the newspapers to which the Zodiac sent taunting letters and ciphers — Graysmith became personally absorbed in the case and spent years compiling evidence.

Zodiac investigation

His book Zodiac (1986) assembled the case record from police files and interviews, and Zodiac Unmasked (2002) argued explicitly that the killer was Arthur Leigh Allen, the prime suspect of Vallejo detective George Bawart. Allen, who died in 1992, was never charged; DNA and fingerprint comparisons have not conclusively linked him to the crimes, and Graysmith's thesis remains contested among researchers.

In film

Graysmith was portrayed by Jake Gyllenhaal in David Fincher's film Zodiac (2007), which drew on both of his books and dramatised his obsession with the case and his work decoding the Zodiac's ciphers. He has written several other true-crime books, including works on Bob Crane's murder, the anthrax attacks (2001), and the Unabomber.
