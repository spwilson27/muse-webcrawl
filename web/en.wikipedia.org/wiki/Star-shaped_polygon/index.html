Star-shaped polygon

A star-shaped polygon is a polygonal region in the plane that contains at least one point from which the entire polygon is visible — that is, the segment from that point to any point in the region lies entirely within it. The set of all such points is called the kernel of the polygon.

Examples

Every convex polygon is star-shaped with its whole interior as kernel. Star polygons such as the five-pointed star are star-shaped but not convex. Not every simple polygon is star-shaped; a spiral-shaped polygon may have an empty kernel.

Applications

Star-shaped domains arise in computational geometry, notably in the art gallery problem, and in computer vision and robotics visibility problems.
