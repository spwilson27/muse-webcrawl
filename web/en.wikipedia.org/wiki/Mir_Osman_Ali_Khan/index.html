Mir Osman Ali Khan

Mir Osman Ali Khan, Asaf Jah VII GCSI GBE (5 April 1886 – 24 February 1967) was the last Nizam (ruler) of the princely state of Hyderabad, the largest princely state in British India, reigning from 1911 until 1948. Known for his immense wealth — he was named the richest man in the world by Time magazine in 1937 — he was also a major benefactor of education and development in his state, while remaining famous in his private habits for frugality and eccentricity.

Reign

Succeeding his father in 1911, Osman Ali Khan ruled Hyderabad, a state of some 16 million people, for 37 years. His administration founded Osmania University (1918, among the first Indian universities teaching in an Indian language, Urdu), Osmania General Hospital, the State Bank of Hyderabad, Hyderabad House in Delhi, and the Begumpet airport; it also built the Nizam Sagar and Himayat Sagar reservoirs and electrification projects. He donated generously to institutions across India, including Banaras Hindu University, and gave grants to temples as well as mosques.

Wealth

The Nizam's fortune was estimated in the 1940s at about US$2 billion (roughly £500 million), including the famous Jacob Diamond (184.75 carats), which he reportedly used as a paperweight. His jewels collection became the subject of a long legal dispute with the Government of India, which purchased the Nizam's jewellery in the 1990s.

Accession crisis and later life

Osman Ali Khan sought to keep Hyderabad independent after the Partition of India in 1947, but in September 1948 the Indian Army annexed the state in Operation Polo. He was retained as Rajpramukh (governor) of Hyderabad State from 1950 to 1956, then lived in retirement at King Kothi Palace, Hyderabad, until his death in 1967. His funeral was among the largest in Hyderabad's history.
