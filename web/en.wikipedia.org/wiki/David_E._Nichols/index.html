David E. Nichols

David Earl Nichols (born December 23, 1944) is an American pharmacologist and medicinal chemist, internationally known for his research on psychedelic drugs. He is a Distinguished Professor Emeritus at the Purdue University College of Pharmacy and an Adjunct Professor at the University of North Carolina. In 1993 he founded the Heffter Research Institute, which funds scientific studies of psychedelics such as psilocybin.

Research

Nichols earned his PhD at the University of Iowa in 1973 and joined Purdue in 1974. His laboratory studied the structure–activity relationships of hallucinogens at serotonin 5-HT2A receptors, developing widely used research tools such as DOB, DOI, and the "FLY" compounds (including bromo-dragonFLY), and synthesised MDMA, LSD analogues, and psilocybin for legitimate clinical research — for many years he was among the few scientists legally permitted to produce such compounds in the United States. His supplied LSD was used in studies of psychedelic-assisted therapy, and his assays helped establish how psychedelics act in the brain.

Heffter Institute

Frustrated by the lack of funding for psychedelic science, Nichols co-founded the Heffter Research Institute in 1993, which supported landmark trials at Johns Hopkins and NYU on psilocybin for end-of-life anxiety and addiction.
