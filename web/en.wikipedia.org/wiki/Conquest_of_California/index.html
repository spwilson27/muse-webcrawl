Conquest of California

Commodore John D. Sloat raised the U.S. flag at Monterey on 7 July 1846.

The conquest of California, also known as the conquest of Alta California or the California campaign, was a campaign of the Mexican–American War from 1846 to 1847 for control of Alta California, then a department of Mexico. It succeeded after military operations by U.S. naval and land forces, assisted by the Bear Flag Revolt of American settlers.

In June 1846 American settlers at Sonoma proclaimed the California Republic and raised the Bear Flag. On 7 July 1846, Commodore John D. Sloat of the U.S. Pacific Squadron occupied Monterey, the capital, without bloodshed, and claimed California for the United States. His successor, Commodore Robert F. Stockton, organised the California Battalion under John C. Frémont. Mexican Californio forces under José María Flores and Andrés Pico won a notable victory over Stephen W. Kearny's Army of the West at the Battle of San Pasqual in December 1846, but U.S. forces prevailed at the battles of Río San Gabriel and La Mesa in January 1847.

The campaign ended with the Treaty of Cahuenga on 13 January 1847. California was formally ceded by Mexico to the United States under the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo in 1848, and was admitted to the Union as a state in 1850.
