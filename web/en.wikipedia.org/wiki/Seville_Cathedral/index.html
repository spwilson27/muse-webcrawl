Seville Cathedral

Seville Cathedral
Location: Seville, Andalusia, Spain
Built: 1401–1528
Style: Gothic
UNESCO World Heritage Site: 1987

The Cathedral of Saint Mary of the See, better known as Seville Cathedral, is a Catholic cathedral in Seville, Spain. It is the largest Gothic cathedral in the world and the third-largest church overall. Construction began in 1401 on the site of the city's Almohad mosque; the builders reportedly resolved to make a church "so great that those who see it finished will think us mad".

Features

The Giralda, originally the mosque's minaret (1184–1198), serves as its bell tower. Inside are the tomb of Christopher Columbus, an enormous gilded retablo mayor, and works by Murillo, Zurbarán and Goya. The adjoining Alcázar and Archive of the Indies share its UNESCO listing.
