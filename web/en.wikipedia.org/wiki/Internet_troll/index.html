Internet troll
Term originUsenet, late 1980s–1990s
Etymology"Trolling for suckers" (fishing); later associated with the folk-tale troll
Motivations studiedAmusement, attention, revenge, political manipulation

In slang, an Internet troll is a person who posts deliberately inflammatory, off-topic or provocative messages in an online community — such as a forum, chat room, comment thread or social media platform — intending to upset others, elicit angry responses or disrupt discussion. The practice is called trolling.

History of the term

The term appeared on Usenet in the late 1980s, originally from the fishing technique of trolling — dragging a baited line to lure fish — in the phrase "trolling for newbies". By the mid-1990s the folk-tale troll lurking under a bridge had merged with the fishing metaphor in popular understanding. Early community responses produced the enduring maxim "Don't feed the trolls", advising users to ignore provocations rather than reward them with attention.

Forms and research

Trolling ranges from individual mischief-making and "concern trolling" (feigned sympathy used to undermine a position) to organised state-sponsored operations, such as Russia's Internet Research Agency, which used troll accounts to interfere in foreign politics. Psychology research associates habitual trolling with the "dark tetrad" traits — everyday sadism, narcissism, Machiavellianism and subclinical psychopathy — though context, anonymity and community norms also matter. Platforms counter trolling through moderation, rate limits, reputation systems and, in some jurisdictions, laws against online harassment and malicious communications.
