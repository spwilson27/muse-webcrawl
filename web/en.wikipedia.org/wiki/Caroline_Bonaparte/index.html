Marie-Annonciade-Caroline Bonaparte (25 March 1782 – 18 May 1839), known as Caroline Murat, was the youngest sister of Napoleon Bonaparte and, through her marriage to Marshal Joachim Murat, Queen consort of Naples from 1808 to 1815. Ambitious and politically astute, she was regarded by Napoleon as the most capable of his siblings after himself.

Life

Born in Ajaccio, Corsica, Caroline was educated at Madame Campan's school at Saint-Germain-en-Laye. In 1800 she married Joachim Murat, one of Napoleon's most brilliant cavalry commanders. When Murat became King of Naples in 1808, Caroline became queen and, during her husband's absences on campaign, ruled as regent, promoting reforms, education for girls, and the excavations at Pompeii and Herculaneum.

Fall and later life

After Napoleon's first abdication, Caroline sought to preserve the Neapolitan throne by treaty with Austria, but the Murat regime collapsed in 1815 and her husband was executed at Pizzo in October of that year. Caroline fled to Austria, where she lived under the title Countess of Lipona (an anagram of Napoli). She later settled in Florence, where she died in 1839.
