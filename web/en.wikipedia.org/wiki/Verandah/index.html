Verandah

A verandah (also spelled veranda) is a roofed, open-air gallery or porch attached to the outside of a building, usually partly enclosed by a railing and extending along the front, and often the sides, of the structure. Verandahs shade walls and windows from sun and rain while allowing air to circulate, making them well suited to warm climates.

Origins and spread

The word entered English from Portuguese varanda and related South Asian terms during the colonial period. The form flourished in British India, where it was adapted from the Bengali bangla style, and spread through the British world to Australia — where the wraparound verandah became emblematic of the Queenslander house — the Caribbean, and the American South, where related forms include the gallery and the deep front porch.

Design

A verandah is distinguished from a porch by its length along a façade and from a balcony by being at ground level. Materials range from timber posts and corrugated-iron roofs in Australia to masonry colonnades in colonial India. Verandahs serve as transitional living space for sitting, dining and sleeping in hot weather, and in many cultures they are a key social space between the public street and the private interior.
