Neonatal jaundice

Neonatal jaundice, also known as neonatal hyperbilirubinemia, is the yellow discolouration of the skin and whites of the eyes in a newborn infant, caused by elevated levels of bilirubin in the blood. It affects a majority of term babies and most preterm babies in the first week of life. In most cases it is physiological and resolves without treatment, but very high bilirubin levels can cause a form of brain damage called kernicterus, so significant jaundice is monitored and treated.

Causes

Newborns produce bilirubin rapidly as fetal haemoglobin is broken down, while the immature liver conjugates and excretes it slowly — hence physiological jaundice, which typically appears after the first 24 hours and peaks around day 3–5. Jaundice is exaggerated by prematurity, bruising or cephalohematoma, breastfeeding difficulties, and blood group incompatibility (ABO or Rh) causing haemolysis. Pathological causes include glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency, infection, and inherited conditions such as Gilbert and Crigler–Najjar syndromes. Jaundice within the first 24 hours of life is considered pathological and requires urgent evaluation.

Complications

Unconjugated bilirubin can cross the immature blood–brain barrier and deposit in the basal ganglia, causing acute bilirubin encephalopathy and, if untreated, kernicterus — permanent choreoathetoid cerebral palsy, hearing loss, and gaze abnormalities. Kernicterus is now rare where screening and treatment are available but remains a significant problem in low-resource settings.

Diagnosis and treatment

Bilirubin is measured in blood or transcutaneously and plotted against age in hours on nomograms (such as the Bhutani nomogram) to assess risk and guide treatment per guidelines such as those of the American Academy of Pediatrics. The standard treatment is phototherapy: blue-green light converts bilirubin into water-soluble isomers that can be excreted without conjugation. Severe or haemolytic cases may need intravenous immunoglobulin or exchange transfusion. Adequate feeding support helps by promoting excretion.
