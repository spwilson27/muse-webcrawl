In-band on-channel

In-band on-channel (IBOC) is a method of transmitting digital radio and analogue radio broadcast signals simultaneously on the same frequency. By adding subcarriers to an existing AM or FM analogue signal, a station can broadcast digital audio without requiring new spectrum allocations.

HD Radio

The best-known implementation is HD Radio, developed by iBiquity Digital (now part of DTS), which was selected by the U.S. Federal Communications Commission in 2002 as the digital radio standard for the United States. In FM hybrid mode, digital subcarriers are placed on either side of the analogue host signal; in all-digital modes the analogue signal is dropped and the digital signal occupies the channel.

Technical features

IBOC systems use orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) and perceptual audio coding. One advantage is a gradual upgrade path; a disadvantage is that digital sidebands can cause interference to adjacent-channel stations, a criticism particularly directed at AM IBOC operation at night.
