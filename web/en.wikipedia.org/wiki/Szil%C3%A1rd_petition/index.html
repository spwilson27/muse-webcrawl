Szilárd petition

The Szilárd petition, drafted and circulated by physicist Leó Szilárd in July 1945, was a petition signed by 70 scientists working on the Manhattan Project in Oak Ridge and Chicago. Addressed to President Harry S. Truman, it urged that the United States not use atomic bombs against Japan without first giving Japan an opportunity to surrender on stated terms.

Background

Szilárd, who had been instrumental in initiating the American atomic bomb programme through the 1939 Einstein–Szilárd letter to President Roosevelt, became concerned about the moral and political consequences of using the weapon, particularly the prospect of a postwar arms race with the Soviet Union. The petition argued that atomic weapons would make the United States responsible for "opening the door to an era of devastation on an unimaginable scale".

Fate

The petition was forwarded through official channels but was delayed by the Army and never reached President Truman before the bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki in August 1945. It was classified and remained unpublished until 1949. A related document, the Franck Report of June 1945, had made similar recommendations.
