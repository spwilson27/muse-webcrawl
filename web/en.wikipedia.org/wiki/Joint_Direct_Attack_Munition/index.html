The Joint Direct Attack Munition (JDAM) is a guidance kit, produced by Boeing, that converts unguided bombs ("dumb bombs") into all-weather precision-guided munitions. JDAM-equipped bombs are guided by an integrated inertial guidance system coupled to a Global Positioning System receiver.

Description

The kit consists of a tail section containing the guidance and control system, along with aerodynamic strakes fitted to the bomb body. JDAM kits are produced for bomb bodies ranging from 500 to 2,000 pounds (230 to 910 kg). A later variant, the Laser JDAM, adds a laser seeker for engaging moving targets.

Operational use

JDAMs were first used in combat during the 1999 NATO bombing of Yugoslavia and have since been employed extensively by the United States and allied air forces in Afghanistan, Iraq, and other conflicts. They can be carried by most American combat aircraft, including fighters and heavy bombers.
