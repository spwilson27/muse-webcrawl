War of the Ring

The War of the Ring is the conflict at the end of the Third Age of Middle-earth in J. R. R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings. It was fought between the Dark Lord Sauron and his allies on one side, and the Free Peoples of Middle-earth — Men, Elves, Dwarves and Hobbits — on the other. The war culminated in 3019 of the Third Age with the destruction of the One Ring and the fall of Sauron.

Background

Sauron, having regained much of his former strength in Mordor, sought the One Ring, into which he had poured a great part of his power in the Second Age, in order to dominate all the other Rings of Power and rule Middle-earth. The Ring had been found by the hobbit Bilbo Baggins and passed to his heir Frodo. On the advice of the wizard Gandalf, a Fellowship of nine companions set out from Rivendell to destroy the Ring in Mount Doom, the only place where it could be unmade.

Major battles

  • Battle of the Hornburg (Helm's Deep) — Rohan under King Théoden, aided by Aragorn, Legolas and Gimli, withstood Saruman's army of Uruk-hai; the tide turned with the arrival of Gandalf and Erkenbrand, and the Huorns.
  • Destruction of Isengard — the Ents, led by Treebeard, flooded Saruman's stronghold.
  • Battle of the Pelennor Fields — Gondor under the Steward Denethor II was besieged by the forces of the Witch-king of Angmar; the Rohirrim arrived at dawn, and Aragorn came by the Corsairs' ships. Minas Tirith was saved at heavy cost, including Théoden and the Witch-king.
  • Battle of the Morannon (Black Gate) — a diversionary assault by the Captains of the West to draw Sauron's eye away from Frodo and Sam.
  • Battle of Bywater — the hobbits' uprising that ended Saruman's occupation of the Shire.

Outcome

When Frodo claimed the Ring at the Cracks of Doom, the creature Gollum seized it and fell into the fire, destroying it. Sauron's power collapsed, Barad-dûr fell, and the Third Age ended. Aragorn was crowned King Elessar of the Reunited Kingdom of Gondor and Arnor.
