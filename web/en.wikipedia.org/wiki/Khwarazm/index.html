Khwarazm (also Chorasmia) is a large oasis region on the delta of the Amu Darya (Oxus) river in western Central Asia, south of the Aral Sea, now divided among Uzbekistan, Turkmenistan, and Kazakhstan. One of the cradles of Iranian civilisation, it maintained its own language, calendar, and traditions for over two thousand years and gave its name to the mathematician Muhammad ibn Musa al-Khwarizmi—and, through his name, to the word "algorithm."

Ancient history

Irrigation agriculture in the delta goes back to the Bronze Age. The Chorasmians appear in Achaemenid inscriptions as a subject people, and Herodotus mentions them among the empire's tributaries. The region was later ruled from fortresses such as Toprak-Kala by kings whose coins and palaces testify to a flourishing Zoroastrian culture; al-Biruni, Khwarazm's great native scholar, preserved much of its chronology and learning in his Chronology of Ancient Nations. Khwarazm successively passed under Kushan, Hephthalite, Turkic, and Arab sway, but local dynasties such as the Afrighids of Kath long retained power.

The Khwarazmian Empire and the Mongols

Under the Anushteginid Khwarazmshahs, the region's rulers built the largest empire in the Islamic world by 1217, stretching from Iraq to Transoxiana. The Mongol invasion of 1219–1221 destroyed it catastrophically: Genghis Khan's armies diverted rivers, massacred populations, and wrecked the irrigation system, from which cities such as Konye-Urgench never truly recovered.

Later history

After periods under the Timurids and Shaybanids, Khwarazm became the territory of the Khanate of Khiva (1511–1920). Its Khwarezmian language died out by about the 14th century, replaced by Turkic languages. In the Soviet era the oasis was split between the Uzbek and Turkmen republics; the diversion of the Amu Darya for cotton irrigation has since shrunk the delta and contributed to the Aral Sea disaster.
