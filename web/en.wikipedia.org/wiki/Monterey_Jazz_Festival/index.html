The Monterey Jazz Festival is an annual music festival that takes place in Monterey, California, United States. It was founded on October 3, 1958, by jazz disc jockey Jimmy Lyons and jazz critic Ralph J. Gleason, and is widely regarded as one of the world's longest consecutively running jazz festivals.

The festival is held annually on the 20-acre, oak-studded Monterey County Fairgrounds, the site of the first Monterey Folk Festival and the Monterey Pop Festival. It takes place on the third full weekend in September and features performances on multiple stages, along with jazz conversations, panel discussions, workshops, exhibitions, and an international array of food and merchandise.

History

The inaugural festival in 1958 presented artists such as Louis Armstrong, Dizzy Gillespie, Billie Holiday, Dave Brubeck, and John Lewis. Lyons served as the festival's director until 2005. The festival's long association with the Dave Brubeck Quartet and with performers such as Duke Ellington, Miles Davis, and Thelonious Monk helped establish its reputation.

Education programs

The festival maintains a strong commitment to jazz education, including the Next Generation Jazz Festival, held each spring, which brings student musicians from across the United States to Monterey, and the Next Generation Jazz Orchestra, an all-star ensemble of the nation's top high school jazz musicians.
