Samnite Wars

Date343–290 BC
LocationCentral and southern Italy
BelligerentsRoman Republic vs. the Samnites
ResultRoman victory; Rome dominant in Italy

The Samnite Wars were a series of three conflicts fought between the Roman Republic and the Samnites, a tribal confederation of the Apennine highlands of southern Italy, between 343 and 290 BC. The wars were decisive in establishing Rome as the dominant power of the Italian peninsula.

First Samnite War (343–341 BC)

The first war began when the Campanian city of Capua appealed to Rome against Samnite attack. Roman forces won engagements at Mount Gaurus and Suessula, but unrest among Rome's Latin allies ended the war quickly; Rome made peace, keeping influence in Campania while conceding Samnite predominance in their own sphere.

Second Samnite War (326–304 BC)

Also called the Great Samnite War, this longer conflict began over Naples and Roman expansion at Samnium's borders. In 321 BC a Roman army was trapped in the mountain pass at the Caudine Forks and forced to surrender and pass under the yoke, Rome's great humiliation of the era. Rome recovered, reformed its army — the manipular legion is often linked to lessons from fighting in Samnite hill country — and after hard campaigns including the disaster at Lautulae (315 BC), prevailed. Victory at Bovianum in 305 BC effectively decided the war.

Third Samnite War (298–290 BC)

The final war saw the Samnites ally with Etruscans, Umbrians and Gauls. The coalition was broken at the Battle of Sentinum in 295 BC, one of the largest battles of the age, where Roman and Picentine forces defeated the Samnites and Gauls. Continued Roman campaigning, including victories near Aquilonia, ended organised Samnite resistance by 290 BC.

Consequences

Rome extended control over central and much of southern Italy, binding defeated peoples through its alliance system and colonies. The Samnites remained restive and later sided with Pyrrhus and Hannibal, and rose again in the Social War (91–88 BC), but their independence was never restored.
