The Upper East Side is a neighborhood in the borough of Manhattan in New York City, bounded roughly by 59th Street to the south, 96th Street to the north, the East River to the east, and Fifth Avenue and Central Park to the west. Long associated with wealth and old-money society, it contains some of the most expensive real estate in the United States, including the Fifth Avenue and Park Avenue corridors and the historic district of Carnegie Hill.

History

Before development the area held farms and the village of Yorkville, a center of German and later Hungarian immigrant life. In the late 19th century, wealthy families built mansions along Fifth Avenue facing Central Park, and in the early 20th century grand apartment houses rose on Park Avenue after the New York Central Railroad tracks were covered. The "Silk Stocking District" became a byword for affluence.

Culture and institutions

The stretch of Fifth Avenue along the park is known as Museum Mile, home to the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum, the Frick Collection, the Cooper Hewitt, and the Jewish Museum. The neighborhood also hosts major medical institutions, including Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Center, Weill Cornell Medicine, and Rockefeller University, plus elite private schools and the luxury shopping of Madison Avenue.

Transportation

The neighborhood is served by the Lexington Avenue subway lines (4, 5, 6) and, since 2017, by the first phase of the Second Avenue Subway (Q train) at 72nd, 86th, and 96th Streets.
