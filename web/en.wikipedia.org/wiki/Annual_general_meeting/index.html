Annual general meeting

An annual general meeting (AGM, also known as the annual meeting) is a meeting of the general membership of an organisation. These organisations include membership associations and companies with shareholders. Such meetings are legally required in many jurisdictions for companies, and are held to conduct business on behalf of the organisation.

Purpose

At companies, the AGM allows shareholders to receive the annual report and financial statements, elect directors, appoint auditors and vote on resolutions such as dividend declarations and executive remuneration. It provides a forum in which shareholders may question the board of directors.

Legal requirements

Under the UK Companies Act 2006, public companies must hold an AGM within six months of their financial year end. In the United States, state corporation laws (notably Delaware's) require annual meetings of stockholders. Extraordinary general meetings (EGMs) may be called for urgent business between AGMs.

Other organisations

Charities, trade unions, professional bodies and sports clubs also typically hold AGMs to elect officers, approve accounts and amend their constitutions.
