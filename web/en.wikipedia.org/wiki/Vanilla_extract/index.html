Vanilla extract

Main ingredientsVanilla pods, ethanol, water
Key flavour compoundVanillin
US standard≥35% alcohol; ≥100 g pods per litre

Vanilla extract is a solution made by macerating and percolating vanilla pods in a solution of ethanol and water. It is the most common form of vanilla used in cooking and baking in North America, and gives flavour to ice cream, custards, cakes, and many other foods.

Production and standards

In the United States, the Food and Drug Administration defines pure vanilla extract as containing at least 35% alcohol by volume and at least 100 grams of vanilla beans per litre. During production, cured pods are extracted over weeks to months, drawing out vanillin and hundreds of other aromatic compounds. "Double" and "triple" extracts use proportionally more beans; vanilla flavourings and imitation extracts use synthetic vanillin, often derived from lignin or petrochemicals, and are much cheaper.

Use

Vanilla extract is added in small quantities — commonly a teaspoon at a time — because its flavour is concentrated. Although some alcohol and aroma are lost in baking, enough flavour compounds survive to season the finished dish. Extract should be stored sealed, cool, and dark; properly kept, it keeps almost indefinitely and its flavour can deepen with age.
