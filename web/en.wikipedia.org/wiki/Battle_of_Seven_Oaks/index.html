The Battle of Seven Oaks was a violent confrontation on 19 June 1816 during the Pemmican War between the Hudson's Bay Company (HBC) and the North West Company (NWC) in what is now Winnipeg, Manitoba. A party of Métis, led by Cuthbert Grant and acting for the North West Company, clashed with Governor Robert Semple and a group of HBC men and Red River Colony settlers. Semple and 20 of his men were killed; the Métis lost one man. It was the bloodiest incident of the fur-trade rivalry.

Background

The Red River Colony, founded by Lord Selkirk under HBC auspices, threatened the NWC's supply lines and the Métis pemmican trade. Governor Miles Macdonell's Pemmican Proclamation of 1814 banning food exports from the colony ignited open conflict, including the seizure of NWC forts and supplies by Semple in 1816.

The encounter

On 19 June 1816, Grant's party of about 60 Métis carrying pemmican toward NWC canoes was confronted near a stand of oak trees by Semple and 28 men. A parley broke down, shots were fired, and in a brief fight the skilled Métis horsemen overwhelmed Semple's party, killing Semple and 20 others. The survivors fled to Fort Douglas, which the settlers abandoned days later.

Aftermath

Selkirk responded by seizing the NWC post at Fort William and sending troops, and lengthy legal proceedings followed in Canada. The rivalry ended with the forced merger of the two companies in 1821. The battle remains a foundational — and contested — episode in Métis history and identity.
