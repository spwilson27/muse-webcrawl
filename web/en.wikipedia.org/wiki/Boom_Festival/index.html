Boom Festival

The Boom Festival is a biennial transformational festival and psychedelic trance music event held near Idanha-a-Nova, in the Castelo Branco district of Portugal, on the shores of the Marechal Carmona reservoir. Founded in 1997, it is one of the largest and best-known psytrance gatherings in the world, attracting tens of thousands of participants from over a hundred countries, and is noted for its emphasis on sustainability, art, and cross-cultural exchange.

History

Boom began in 1997 as a gathering centred on Goa trance and psychedelic trance music, inspired by the beach parties of Goa, India. Early editions were held near Águas de Moura and later at Herdade do Zambujeiro before the festival settled in 2002 at its long-term home by the lake in Idanha-a-Nova. Over successive editions it expanded beyond music into a multidisciplinary event with areas devoted to visionary art, lectures and workshops (the "Liminal Village"), healing practices, film, and performance, while remaining independent and free of corporate sponsorship.

Ethos and sustainability

The festival brands itself around the motto of unity in diversity and oneness with the environment. It has won recognition for environmental measures including composting toilets, water treatment and reuse, biodiesel generators, large-scale recycling, and the planting of trees on the site. In 2010 it received the Green'n'Clean award for festivals, and the organisers publish environmental reports for each edition. A harm-reduction project, Kosmicare, provides drug checking and psychedelic crisis support on site, in cooperation with Portuguese health authorities.
