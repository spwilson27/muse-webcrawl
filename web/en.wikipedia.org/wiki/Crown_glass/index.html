Crown glass

Crown glass is a type of optical glass used in lenses and other optical components. It is produced from alkali-lime silicates containing approximately 10% potassium oxide, and has a low refractive index (around 1.52) and low dispersion (high Abbe number, around 58–59). Crown glass is contrasted with flint glass, which has higher refractive index and greater dispersion.

History

The name derives from an early method of making window glass: a gather of molten glass was blown and then spun into a flat disc, or "crown", with a characteristic central thickening known as the bull's-eye. The technique was developed in Normandy and introduced to England in the 14th century; crown glass windows were prized for their clarity and brilliance.

Optical use

Paired with flint glass in an achromatic doublet, crown glass allows lens systems to correct chromatic aberration. The Schott catalogue designates many crown glasses with the "K" prefix, such as BK7, a borosilicate crown glass widely used for precision optics. See also Flint glass, Achromatic lens and Abbe number.
