The International Organization for Standardization (ISO) is an independent, international non-governmental organisation that develops and publishes voluntary, consensus-based International Standards. Its members are the national standards bodies of more than 170 countries, and its Central Secretariat is in Geneva, Switzerland. Since its founding in 1947, ISO has published well over 25,000 standards covering almost all aspects of technology and business, from screw threads and shipping containers to information security and food safety.

Name and abbreviation

"ISO" is not an acronym of the organisation's name in any one language (which would differ in English, French and Russian); it derives from the Greek isos, meaning "equal", chosen so that the organisation's short name would be the same in every language.

History

ISO grew out of earlier international standardisation work by the International Federation of the National Standardizing Associations (ISA, founded 1926) and a wartime United Nations Standards Coordinating Committee. Delegates from 25 countries meeting in London in October 1946 agreed to create a new body, and ISO officially began operations on 23 February 1947. Its first standard, ISO/R 1:1951, specified the standard reference temperature for industrial length measurements.

How standards are made

Standards are developed by technical committees of experts nominated by member bodies, passing through proposal, preparatory, committee, enquiry, approval and publication stages; adoption requires a qualified majority of participating members and a majority of all voting members. Standards are reviewed at least every five years. Well-known ISO standards include ISO 9001 (quality management), ISO 14001 (environmental management), ISO/IEC 27001 (information security), ISO 3166 (country codes), ISO 4217 (currency codes), ISO 8601 (dates and times) and the ISO container standards that underpin world shipping.

Membership and structure

Each member country is represented by its principal national standards body — for example, ANSI in the United States, BSI in the United Kingdom and DIN in Germany. Governance rests with a General Assembly, a Council, and a Technical Management Board overseeing several hundred technical committees. ISO works closely with the International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) and the International Telecommunication Union (ITU).
