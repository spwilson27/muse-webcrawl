Multinomial logistic regression (often just multinomial logit, or mlogit) is a classification method that generalises logistic regression to problems where the dependent variable can take more than two possible discrete outcomes — for example, predicting which of several political parties a voter will choose, or which of several products a consumer will buy. It models the probability of each category as a function of a set of explanatory variables.

The model

For an outcome with K categories, the model assigns each category k (except a chosen reference category) a linear predictor built from the covariates, and converts these into probabilities through the softmax function:

Pr(Y = k) = exp(βk · x) / Σj exp(βj · x)

Equivalently, the log-odds of each category relative to the reference category are linear in the covariates. Coefficients are estimated by maximum likelihood, typically using Newton–Raphson or quasi-Newton optimisation; predicted probabilities are guaranteed to lie between 0 and 1 and to sum to 1.

Assumptions and applications

The multinomial logit rests on the assumption of independence of irrelevant alternatives (IIA): the relative odds between two outcomes do not depend on what other alternatives are available. Where IIA is implausible — the classic example being transport choices involving similar options such as a red bus and a blue bus — more flexible models such as the nested logit or multinomial probit are used instead. Multinomial logistic regression is a workhorse of the social sciences, marketing, biostatistics and machine learning, where it also appears, trained by gradient methods, as the softmax layer of classifiers and neural networks.
