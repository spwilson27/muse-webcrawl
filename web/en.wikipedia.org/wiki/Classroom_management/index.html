Classroom management

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Classroom management is the process by which teachers maintain appropriate student behavior, establish productive learning environments, and maximize instructional time. Research in education consistently identifies it as among the most important influences on student achievement.

Key approaches

  • Preventive management: clear routines, procedures, seating arrangements, and transitions planned in advance.
  • Behaviorist approaches: rules, reinforcement, and consequences, drawing on Skinner's operant conditioning.
  • Democratic management: student involvement in rule-making and restorative practices, emphasizing community and belonging.
  • Withitness: Jacob Kounin's term for a teacher's awareness of the whole class — positioning, eye contact, and overlapping — shown to reduce disruption.

Evidence

Meta-analyses find proactive strategies (engaging instruction, positive relationships, clear expectations) substantially more effective than reactive discipline. Culturally responsive management adapts expectations to students' backgrounds rather than imposing a single norm.
