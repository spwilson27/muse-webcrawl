Rundetaarn (Round Tower)
LocationCopenhagen, Denmark
Completed1642
Height34.8 m
Commissioned byChristian IV of Denmark

The Rundetaarn, or Round Tower, is a 17th-century tower located in central Copenhagen, Denmark, and one of the many architectural projects of Christian IV of Denmark. Built as an astronomical observatory, it is most famous for its equestrian staircase, a 7.5-turn helical corridor leading to the platform at the top, and its views over Copenhagen.

History

Construction began in 1637 and finished in 1642. The tower formed part of the Trinitatis Complex, which combined a student church (Trinitatis Church), the university library in the loft above the church, and the observatory. The astronomer Christian Sørensen Longomontanus, a pupil of Tycho Brahe, was involved in the planning, and the observatory was used by the University of Copenhagen until the 19th century; Ole Rømer worked there. Peter the Great is said to have ridden up the spiral ramp on horseback in 1716, and his wife Catherine drove up in a carriage.

Architecture

The tower is 34.8 metres high and built of brick in the Dutch Baroque style. Instead of stairs, a spiral ramp 192 metres long winds seven and a half times around the hollow core, allowing horses and carts to carry heavy instruments to the top. The viewing platform offers panoramic views of the old town; an annual unicycle race is held up and down the ramp.
