Magdala

Magdala was an ancient Jewish town on the western shore of the Sea of Galilee, in present-day Israel. It is best known as the hometown of Mary Magdalene, and in the Roman period it was a significant fishing and fish-processing centre. In Greek sources the town appears as Tarichaeae, "pickling vats", reflecting its trade in salted fish.

History

Magdala flourished in the Hellenistic and early Roman periods. The historian Josephus describes Tarichaeae as a fortified town that resisted the Romans during the First Jewish–Roman War before being captured by Titus in 67 CE; many of its inhabitants were killed or enslaved. Excavations have uncovered streets, houses, ritual baths and one of the oldest synagogues in Israel, dating to the first century CE.

The Magdala stone

In 2009 excavators found in the synagogue a carved stone block, the Magdala stone, decorated with a relief of a seven-branched menorah — among the earliest depictions of the Second Temple's menorah. A modern church and pilgrimage centre, Duc in Altum, now stands at the site.

Mary Magdalene

The New Testament names Mary "called Magdalene" (Luke 8:2), generally understood to mean Mary of Magdala. She is portrayed as a follower of Jesus, a witness of the crucifixion and, in the Gospel of John, the first witness of the resurrection.
