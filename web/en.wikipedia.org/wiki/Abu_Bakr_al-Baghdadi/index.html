Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi

BornIbrahim Awad Ibrahim al-Badri, 1971, Samarra, Iraq
Died27 October 2019, Barisha, Syria
Known forLeader ("caliph") of the Islamic State

Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi (born Ibrahim Awad Ibrahim al-Badri; 1971 – 27 October 2019) was an Iraqi militant and the leader of the Islamic State (IS), a jihadist organization that seized large parts of Iraq and Syria in 2014 and proclaimed a caliphate with al-Baghdadi as "Caliph Ibrahim."

Early life and rise

Born near Samarra, al-Baghdadi studied Islamic theology in Baghdad, reportedly earning a doctorate in Quranic studies. After the 2003 US-led invasion of Iraq he became involved in insurgent groups and was detained by US forces at Camp Bucca in 2004. He rose through the ranks of al-Qaeda in Iraq's successors and became leader of the Islamic State of Iraq in 2010.

The caliphate

Exploiting the Syrian civil war, his organization expanded, broke with al-Qaeda, and in June 2014 captured Mosul. On 29 June 2014 it declared a caliphate; days later al-Baghdadi appeared publicly at Mosul's Great Mosque of al-Nuri to deliver a sermon — his only confirmed public appearance as leader. Under his rule, IS committed mass atrocities, including the genocide of the Yazidis, and inspired or directed terrorist attacks worldwide.

Death

After a US-led coalition and local forces destroyed the territorial caliphate by 2019, al-Baghdadi was located in Barisha, Idlib Province, Syria. On 26–27 October 2019, US special forces raided the compound; cornered in a tunnel, he detonated a suicide vest, killing himself and two children. He was succeeded as IS leader by Abu Ibrahim al-Hashimi al-Qurashi.
