Satellite television

Satellite television is a service that delivers television programming to viewers by relaying it from a communications satellite orbiting the Earth directly to the viewer's location, where it is received by a parabolic dish antenna and a set-top box.

Satellite television
Delivery: Geostationary satellite to dish
Bands: C-band, Ku-band
Providers include: DirecTV, Dish Network, Sky

History

Early satellite relays in the 1960s (Telstar, Syncom) led to distribution of channels to cable headends in the 1970s, e.g. HBO. Direct-to-home (DTH / DBS) services expanded in the 1990s with digital compression (DVB-S, MPEG-2) allowing many channels per transponder, from providers such as DirecTV and Dish Network in the US and Sky in Europe.

Technology

Signals are uplinked to geostationary satellites, amplified and downlinked on C or Ku-band frequencies. A low-noise block downconverter (LNB) at the dish converts the signal for a receiver, which decrypts conditional-access programming.
