HaKirya

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

HaKirya (Hebrew: הַקִּרְיָה, "The Campus") is a central district of Tel Aviv, Israel, containing the city's main government and military complex. The name is also used more specifically for the headquarters compound of the Israel Defense Forces (IDF), whose General Staff and the Ministry of Defense are located there; the IDF headquarters itself is often referred to metonymically as "the Kirya", in much the same way as "the Pentagon" is used in the United States.

History

The site originated as Sarona, a Templer colony founded in the 1870s by German settlers. During the Second World War the British authorities interned the Templers and took over the settlement, and after 1948 the young State of Israel used its buildings for government offices and the nascent IDF General Staff. Over the following decades the military compound — Camp Rabin (Mahane Rabin), named after Yitzhak Rabin — expanded on the site, while the old Templer houses were preserved and later restored as the Sarona heritage and leisure quarter.

The district today

Modern HaKirya combines the fenced military headquarters, dominated by the Marganit Tower and the Matcal Tower, with one of Israel's densest commercial districts. The Azrieli Center's three towers stand at its northern edge, alongside government ministry buildings, the Tel Aviv District Court and other office towers along Kaplan Street and Begin Road. High land values have made the district a focus of planning disputes over the future relocation of the military base, which would free a large tract of central Tel Aviv for civilian development.
