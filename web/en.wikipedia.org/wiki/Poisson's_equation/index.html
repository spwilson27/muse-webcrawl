Poisson's equation

Poisson's equation is an elliptic partial differential equation of broad utility in theoretical physics. It is named after the French mathematician and physicist Siméon Denis Poisson, and generalises Laplace's equation.

Statement

The equation takes the form

Δφ = f,

where Δ is the Laplace operator, and φ and f are (real- or complex-valued) functions on some domain. In three-dimensional Cartesian coordinates it reads:

(∂²/∂x² + ∂²/∂y² + ∂²/∂z²) φ(x,y,z) = f(x,y,z).

When f = 0 the equation reduces to Laplace's equation. The equation typically arises with boundary conditions specifying φ (Dirichlet) or its normal derivative (Neumann) on the boundary of the domain.

Applications

Electrostatics: Poisson's equation relates the electric potential φ to the charge density ρ: Δφ = −ρ/ε0. It follows from Gauss's law combined with the definition of the potential, and reduces to Laplace's equation in charge-free regions.

Gravitation: In Newtonian gravity, the gravitational potential Φ satisfies ΔΦ = 4πGρ, an exact analogue of the electrostatic equation.

Fluid mechanics and heat conduction: Variants of the equation govern steady-state temperature distributions with heat sources and the pressure field in incompressible flow.

Solution methods

In free space, solutions can be written using Green's functions, which for three dimensions take the form of convolutions with the kernel 1/(4π|r − r′|). Numerically, Poisson's equation is solved by finite difference, finite element, and spectral methods; the Fast Fourier Transform provides efficient solvers on regular grids, and multigrid methods are widely used for large problems.
