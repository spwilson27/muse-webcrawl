Neolog Judaism

Neolog Judaism (Hungarian: Neológ irányzat, "new trend") is one of the two large communal organisations among Hungarian Jews and a denomination of Judaism. Historically it represented the religiously moderate, acculturated wing of Hungarian Jewry, standing between Orthodoxy and the more radical Reform movement of Germany.

History

From the 1820s, modernising congregations in the Kingdom of Hungary introduced synagogue innovations such as vernacular sermons, choirs, organs, and decorous services. After the Hungarian Jewish Congress of 1868–69 failed to produce a single communal structure, the movement organised separately from the Orthodox, receiving official recognition as a distinct denomination. Neolog rabbis were trained at the Jewish Theological Seminary of Budapest, founded in 1877.

Practice

Neolog Judaism retained Hebrew liturgy, traditional dietary laws, and most ritual observance while embracing Hungarian language and culture, modern scholarship (the Wissenschaft des Judentums), and moderate synagogue reforms. Its theology is often compared to American Conservative Judaism, though it developed independently.

Today

The Holocaust destroyed most Hungarian Jewish communities. Neolog congregations were reconstituted after the war and remain the larger stream in Hungary today, centred on Budapest's Dohány Street Synagogue, the largest synagogue in Europe.
