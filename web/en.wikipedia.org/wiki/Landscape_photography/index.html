Landscape photography

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Landscape photography (also called landscape art in its painterly tradition) is the art of capturing pictures of nature and the outdoors in a way that brings the viewer into the scene. Landscape photographs typically capture large-scale vistas — mountains, valleys, coasts, deserts, forests — with minimal human presence, though "urban landscapes" and industrial scenes form recognised sub-genres. It is among the oldest and most popular genres of photography.

History

Landscape was a favourite subject from photography's earliest years, partly because scenery holds still during long exposures. Nineteenth-century practitioners included Roger Fenton, Francis Frith, and, in the American West, Carleton Watkins and Timothy O'Sullivan, whose survey photographs influenced the creation of the US national parks. In the twentieth century, Ansel Adams's sharply focused, high-contrast images of Yosemite and the Sierra Nevada — developed through his Zone System and the Group f/64 — set the standard for the genre. Later photographers such as Galen Rowell brought a more active, wilderness-travel approach, while the New Topographics movement of the 1970s turned to ordinary, human-altered landscapes.

Technique

Landscape photographers typically seek great depth of field (small apertures), sharpness from foreground to horizon, and dramatic natural light — often in the "golden hours" after sunrise and before sunset, or in broken weather. Wide-angle lenses, tripods, graduated neutral-density and polarising filters, and panoramic formats are characteristic tools. Composition commonly rests on a strong foreground element, leading lines, and layered planes of foreground, middle ground and background.

Themes and practice

Common themes include wilderness and conservation, seasonal change, night skies and astrophotography, seascapes, and aerial landscape photography from aircraft and drones. Landscape photography has repeatedly served environmental advocacy, from Watkins's Yosemite images to modern campaigns documenting glacier retreat.
