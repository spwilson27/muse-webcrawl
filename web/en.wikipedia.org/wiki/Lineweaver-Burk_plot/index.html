Lineweaver–Burk plot

In biochemistry, a Lineweaver–Burk plot (or double reciprocal plot) is a graphical representation of the Lineweaver–Burk equation of enzyme kinetics, described by Hans Lineweaver and Dean Burk in 1934. It plots the reciprocal of reaction velocity against the reciprocal of substrate concentration.

Derivation

Taking reciprocals of the Michaelis–Menten equation gives 1/v = (Km/Vmax)(1/[S]) + 1/Vmax, a straight line whose y-intercept is 1/Vmax and whose slope is Km/Vmax. The plot makes it easy to distinguish competitive, non-competitive, and uncompetitive inhibition patterns.

Criticism

The double-reciprocal transformation badly distorts experimental error, overweighting the least precise low-concentration measurements; modern practice favors direct non-linear fitting of the Michaelis–Menten equation, with the plot retained mainly for display and teaching.
