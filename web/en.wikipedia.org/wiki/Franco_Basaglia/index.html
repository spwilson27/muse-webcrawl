Franco Basaglia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Franco Basaglia (11 March 1924 – 29 August 1980) was an Italian psychiatrist, neurologist, and professor who pioneered the modern concept of mental health in Italy. He was the principal proponent of Law 180, the 1978 Italian reform law that dismantled the country's psychiatric asylum system and mandated community-based mental health care — a model widely influential internationally and praised by the World Health Organization as revolutionary.

Career and the Gorizia experiment

Born in Venice, Basaglia studied medicine at the University of Padua and took a chair in mental health. In 1961 he became director of the psychiatric hospital of Gorizia and, drawing on phenomenology, existentialism, and the work of Maxwell Jones and Erving Goffman, began transforming the closed asylum into an open therapeutic community: locks, restraints, and uniforms were removed, patients participated in assemblies, and the institution was progressively opened to the town. The experience, documented in the collective book L'istituzione negata ("The Negated Institution", 1968), made him the leading figure of the Psichiatria Democratica (Democratic Psychiatry) movement, founded in 1973. At Trieste from 1971 he went further, closing the psychiatric hospital entirely and replacing it with community mental health centres, protected residences, and work cooperatives.

Law 180 and legacy

Basaglia argued that the asylum itself produced much of the "chronicity" attributed to mental illness, and that psychiatry should address the patient's social and material conditions rather than merely segregating them. Law 180 (1978), often called the "Basaglia Law", prohibited new admissions to mental hospitals and ordered their closure, transferring care to small inpatient units in general hospitals and community services — making Italy the first country to abolish the asylum system by law. Basaglia died of a brain tumour in 1980, aged 56, two years after the law passed; the full closure of Italian asylums was completed in the 1990s. His approach continues to influence debates on deinstitutionalisation worldwide, both celebrated for restoring rights and debated where community services proved underfunded.
