A business trading under an assumed name

Doing business as

The term doing business as (DBA), also known as a trade name, fictitious name, or assumed business name, is a legal term used in the United States and Canada for the name under which a person or company carries on business when that name differs from the legal name of the person or entity responsible.

Registration

In most U.S. states and counties, a business operating under a fictitious name must register it — often by filing a fictitious business name statement with the county clerk — and in some jurisdictions publish notice in a newspaper. The requirement exists largely for consumer protection, so that the identity of the person behind a business name can be traced in case of legal action or fraud. A DBA registration does not by itself create a separate legal entity or confer trademark rights.

Usage

Sole proprietors commonly register a DBA to trade under a business name rather than their personal name; corporations and LLCs register DBAs to operate multiple brands or lines of business under one parent entity. Equivalent concepts exist elsewhere: in the United Kingdom a "trading as" name, and in parts of Canada a "master business licence" name.
