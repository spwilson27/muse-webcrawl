Ten-code

Ten-codes, officially known as ten signals, are brevity codes used to represent common phrases in voice communication, particularly by law enforcement and in Citizens Band (CB) radio transmission in North America. Each code begins with the word "ten" followed by a number, e.g. 10-4 for "message received / acknowledged".

History

The codes were developed in 1937–1940 by Charles Hopper of the Illinois State Police and adopted by the Association of Public-Safety Communications Officials (APCO). They were designed for brevity and clarity on poor-quality early radio channels: the initial "ten" gave receivers time to tune in before the meaningful digit arrived.

Variation and plain language

Meanings vary between jurisdictions — a notorious source of confusion in multi-agency responses. After the 2005 Hurricane Katrina response, US federal guidance moved toward plain English, though ten-codes remain widespread in local policing and popular culture through CB radio slang of the 1970s.
