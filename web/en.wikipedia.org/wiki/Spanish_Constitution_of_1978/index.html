Spanish Constitution of 1978

The Constitution of Spain (Spanish: Constitución española) is the supreme law of the Kingdom of Spain, promulgated on 6 December 1978 following a referendum, culminating the Spanish transition to democracy after Franco's death.

Drafting

A seven-member committee, the 'fathers of the Constitution', drawn from major parties, drafted the text by consensus. It was approved overwhelmingly by the Cortes Generales and by 87.8% of voters in the referendum; 6 December is now Constitution Day, a public holiday.

Contents

The Constitution declares Spain a social and democratic state under the rule of law, establishes a parliamentary monarchy, guarantees extensive rights, abolishes the death penalty (except wartime military law, later fully abolished), and creates the 'State of Autonomies' — 17 autonomous communities with devolved powers. Article 2 proclaims the indissoluble unity of the Spanish nation while recognising regional autonomy.

Amendments

It has been amended only three times (1992, 2011 budget-stability reform, and 2024 wording on disability rights), reflecting its consensus status.
