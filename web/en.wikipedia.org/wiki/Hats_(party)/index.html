Hats (party)

The Hats (Swedish: hattarna) were a Swedish political faction — often described as one of the first political parties — that dominated the politics of Sweden during much of the Age of Liberty (1719–1772). Their rivals were the Caps. The names derived from headgear: the Hats took theirs from the tricorne hat associated with military officers, symbolising a more assertive, warlike policy, while the Caps were mockingly named for a soft nightcap, implying timidity.

Policies

The Hats favoured an alliance with France, from which they received subsidies, and sought to recover Sweden's standing as a great power, including revenge against Russia for territorial losses. In economics they promoted mercantilist policies, supporting manufactures and export industries through state subsidies. Their dominance from 1738 led to the disastrous Russo-Swedish War of 1741–43, launched in an attempt to reverse the Treaty of Nystad, and later to Sweden's involvement in the Seven Years' War in Pomerania.

Decline

War failures, economic difficulties and corruption scandals weakened the Hats, and the Caps won the election of 1765–66. The two parties' alternation in power ended when Gustav III's coup of 1772 restored royal authority and closed the Age of Liberty.
