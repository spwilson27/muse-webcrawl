The Jack Hills are a range of hills in Mid West Western Australia. They are best known as the source of the oldest material of terrestrial origin yet identified: detrital zircon crystals extracted from metamorphosed sedimentary rocks, the oldest of which have been dated at approximately 4.4 billion years old, formed only about 160 million years after the formation of the Earth itself.

Geology

The hills lie within the Narryer Gneiss Terrane on the northern margin of the Yilgarn Craton. The zircons occur in a metaconglomerate deposited around 3 billion years ago. Because zircon is highly resistant to weathering and metamorphism, the crystals preserve isotopic information from the Hadean eon, for which no intact rock record survives.

Scientific significance

Uranium–lead dating of Jack Hills zircons in 2001 yielded an age of 4,404 ± 8 million years for the oldest grain, making it the oldest dated material formed on Earth. Oxygen isotope ratios in the zircons have been interpreted as evidence that liquid water existed at the surface surprisingly early, suggesting a cooler early Earth than once supposed. Carbon isotope measurements in a graphite inclusion in one ~4.1-billion-year-old zircon have been discussed as possible, though disputed, evidence of very early life.

Mining

The range also hosts iron ore deposits; the Jack Hills mine, operated by Crosslands Resources, shipped direct-shipping haematite ore during its period of operation.
