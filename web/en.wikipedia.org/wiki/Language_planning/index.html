Language planning

Official multilingual signage is a visible product of status planning.

Language planning (or language engineering) is a deliberate effort to influence the function, structure, or acquisition of language varieties within a speech community. It is often carried out by governments and language academies to standardise a language, promote a national language, or manage multilingualism.

Types

Einar Haugen's influential framework divides planning into status planning (choosing which language is used for government, education, and media), corpus planning (developing the language itself — spelling reforms, new terminology, standard grammars), and later acquisition planning (teaching and spreading the language).

Examples

Famous cases include the revival of Hebrew in Israel, Atatürk's Turkish script and vocabulary reforms, the promotion of Bahasa Indonesia as a unifying national language, the work of the Académie française, and the standardisation of Norwegian (Bokmål and Nynorsk). In Wales and Ireland, planning supports endangered Celtic languages.
