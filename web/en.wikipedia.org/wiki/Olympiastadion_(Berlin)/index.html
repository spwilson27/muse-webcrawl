The Olympiastadion (German pronunciation: [oˈlʏmpi̯aˌʃtaːdi̯ɔn]) is a sports stadium at Olympiapark Berlin in Berlin, Germany. It was originally built by Werner March for the 1936 Summer Olympics. During the Olympics, the record attendance was thought to be over 100,000. Today the stadium is part of the Olympiapark Berlin.

Since a major renovation in 2004, the Olympiastadion has a permanent capacity of 74,475 seats and is the largest stadium in Germany for international football matches. The Olympiastadion is a UEFA category four stadium.

History

The stadium stands on the site of the Deutsches Stadion, which had been built for the cancelled 1916 Summer Olympics. When the Nazis came to power in 1933, they decided to use the Olympic Games of 1936 for propaganda purposes, and Werner March was charged with building a grand new stadium. Construction took place from 1934 to 1936, and the stadium was inaugurated on 1 August 1936 with the opening ceremony of the Games, at which Jesse Owens won four gold medals.

After the Second World War, the stadium lay in the British occupation zone and was used by British forces. It hosted matches of the 1974 FIFA World Cup and, after reunification, was comprehensively renovated between 2000 and 2004, gaining a distinctive roof that covers all seats while leaving the field open to the sky. It hosted six matches of the 2006 FIFA World Cup, including the final, and the 2015 UEFA Champions League Final. It was one of the venues for UEFA Euro 2024, hosting the final.

Tenants and events

Since 1963, it has been the home of the football club Hertha BSC. It also hosts the final of the DFB-Pokal every year, and has staged major athletics events including the 2009 World Championships in Athletics, concerts, and American football games.
