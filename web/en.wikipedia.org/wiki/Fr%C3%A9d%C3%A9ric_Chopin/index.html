Frédéric Chopin
Born: 1 March 1810, Żelazowa Wola, Poland
Died: 17 October 1849, Paris
Occupation: Composer, pianist
Era: Romantic
Known for: Nocturnes, ballades, études, mazurkas

Frédéric François Chopin (1810–1849) was a Polish composer and virtuoso pianist of the Romantic period who wrote primarily for solo piano. He has maintained worldwide renown as a leading musician of his era, one whose "poetic genius was based on a professional technique that was without equal in his generation".

Life

Born in Żelazowa Wola to a Polish mother and French father, Chopin was a child prodigy who completed his musical education in Warsaw before leaving Poland at 20, months before the November 1830 Uprising. He settled in Paris, where he taught piano, gave rare salon performances and composed nearly all his major works. His decade-long relationship with the writer George Sand ended shortly before his death from tuberculosis at 39.

Music

Chopin's compositions — nocturnes, ballades, scherzos, études, preludes, waltzes, mazurkas and polonaises — expanded the expressive and technical possibilities of the piano and influenced virtually all later piano music.
