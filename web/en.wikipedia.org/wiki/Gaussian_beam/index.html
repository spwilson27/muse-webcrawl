Gaussian beam

In optics, a Gaussian beam is a beam of electromagnetic radiation whose transverse amplitude distribution is given by a Gaussian function. Many lasers emit beams that approximate a fundamental Gaussian profile, making the model central to laser physics and optical system design.

Beam parameters

The beam is characterised by its waist radius w₀, the radius at the focus where the field profile is narrowest, and by the Rayleigh range z_R = πw₀²/λ, over which the beam area doubles. At distance z from the waist the beam radius grows as w(z) = w₀√(1 + (z/z_R)²), the wavefront curvature is described by R(z), and the beam accumulates an extra axial phase shift, the Gouy phase. The divergence angle in the far field is θ = λ/(πw₀): a smaller waist produces a more strongly diverging beam.

Higher-order modes

General solutions of the paraxial wave equation include Hermite–Gaussian and Laguerre–Gaussian modes, which describe the transverse modes of laser resonators. Real beams are quantified by the beam quality factor M², which equals 1 for an ideal Gaussian beam.

Applications

Gaussian beam optics is used to design lens systems for focusing and collimating laser beams, in optical data storage, laser cutting, interferometry and fibre coupling.
