Lamar Hunt

Lamar Hunt (August 2, 1932 – December 13, 2006) was an American businessman and sports promoter, one of the most influential figures in the history of American professional sports. He founded the American Football League (AFL) in 1959 and owned the Dallas Texans/Kansas City Chiefs of the AFL and NFL. Hunt is credited with coining the term "Super Bowl". He also championed professional soccer in the United States — the U.S. Open Cup in soccer is named the Lamar Hunt U.S. Open Cup in his honour, and he was a founding investor in Major League Soccer — and helped popularise professional tennis through World Championship Tennis. He is one of very few people inducted into the halls of fame of three different sports: pro football, soccer, and tennis.

Early life

Hunt was the youngest son of oil tycoon H. L. Hunt. He graduated from Southern Methodist University in Dallas with a degree in geology in 1956 and played football at SMU as a reserve end. After being rebuffed in attempts to buy an NFL expansion team or the Chicago Cardinals, he resolved to start a rival league.

American Football League and the Chiefs

In 1959 Hunt recruited fellow oilmen and businessmen — the self-deprecating "Foolish Club" — to launch the American Football League, which began play in 1960. Hunt's own team, the Dallas Texans, won the 1962 AFL championship and moved to Kansas City in 1963 as the Chiefs. The AFL's success forced the NFL into the 1966 merger agreement and the AFL–NFL World Championship Game, which Hunt jokingly dubbed the "Super Bowl" after his children's toy "Super Ball"; the name stuck. The Chiefs won Super Bowl IV. The AFL championship trophy, the Lamar Hunt Trophy (now given to the AFC champion), bears his name.

Soccer, tennis, and other ventures

Hunt co-founded World Championship Tennis in 1968, a key step in the open era of professional tennis. A soccer enthusiast after attending the 1966 World Cup, he owned NASL's Dallas Tornado, then invested in MLS at its 1996 founding, operating the Kansas City Wizards and Columbus Crew; the Crew's Columbus Crew Stadium (1999) was the league's first soccer-specific stadium. He was also a co-founder of the Chicago Bulls and part-owner during their championship era, and was involved in Worlds of Fun and Oceans of Fun theme parks in Kansas City. Hunt died in Dallas in 2006 of complications from prostate cancer; his family, through Hunt Sports Group, continues to own the Chiefs.
