Overview

The Namib is a coastal desert in Southern Africa, stretching more than 2,000 km along the Atlantic coasts of Angola, Namibia and South Africa. Its name, from the Nama/Damara language, means "vast place". The cold Benguela Current keeps it hyper-arid yet foggy — fog is a lifeline for its endemic life — and it is often called the world's oldest desert, with arid or semi-arid conditions for tens of millions of years.

Regions

The Namib divides into the Skeleton Coast in the north (named for shipwrecks and whale bones), the great dune sea of the Namib Sand Sea around Sossusvlei in the centre (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), and the Sperrgebiet diamond fields in the south around Lüderitz and the ghost town of Kolmanskop. Welwitschia plants, fog-basking beetles and desert elephants are emblematic of its adapted life.
