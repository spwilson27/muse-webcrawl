Sampling (statistics)

FieldStatistics, survey methodology
GoalInference about a population from a subset
Key methodsSimple random, stratified, cluster sampling

In statistics, quality assurance, and survey methodology, sampling is the selection of a subset or sample of individuals from within a statistical population to estimate characteristics of the whole population.

Probability sampling

In simple random sampling every member has an equal chance of selection. Stratified sampling divides the population into strata and samples within each, improving precision for subgroups. Cluster sampling selects whole groups, and systematic sampling takes every k-th element from a list. Probability samples permit estimates of sampling error.

Non-probability sampling

Convenience, quota, and snowball samples are cheaper but risk selection bias, as illustrated by the 1936 Literary Digest poll failure. Weighting and calibration can partly correct imbalances.

Sample size

Required sample size depends on population variability, desired margin of error, and confidence level; for large populations, a few thousand respondents typically estimate proportions within a few percentage points. See also plain communication of results in survey reporting.
