Miami Vice
Genre: Crime drama / neo-noir
Created by: Anthony Yerkovich
Starring: Don Johnson, Philip Michael Thomas, Edward James Olmos
Network: NBC
Original run: September 16, 1984 – January 25, 1990 (5 seasons, 114 episodes)

Miami Vice is an American crime drama television series created by Anthony Yerkovich and executive produced by Michael Mann for NBC. The series stars Don Johnson as James "Sonny" Crockett and Philip Michael Thomas as Ricardo "Rico" Tubbs, two undercover Metro-Dade Police detectives working against drug trafficking and organised crime in Miami. Edward James Olmos co-starred as their taciturn lieutenant, Martin Castillo. The show aired five seasons from 1984 to 1989 (with final episodes syndicated in 1990).

Style and production

Miami Vice revolutionised the look and sound of television drama. Produced on location in Miami with feature-film cinematography, the series adopted a palette of pastels and whites — Mann's dictum was famously "no earth tones" — showcasing Art Deco South Beach, speedboats, fashion by designers such as Versace and Armani, and luxury sports cars (Crockett's Ferrari Daytona Spyder replica, later replaced by a white Testarossa). Music was integral rather than background: original scoring by Jan Hammer (whose "Miami Vice Theme" hit No. 1 in 1985) combined with licensed pop and rock tracks, most famously Phil Collins's "In the Air Tonight" in the pilot.

Reception and influence

The series was a major ratings and cultural phenomenon, spawning fashion trends in men's clothing (linen blazers over T-shirts, loafers without socks) and helping drive the preservation-led revitalisation of Miami Beach. It won Emmy Awards including for Olmos and for its music, and critics credit it with ushering in a more cinematic era of television. A darker, more violent feature film adaptation, written and directed by Mann, was released in 2006 starring Colin Farrell and Jamie Foxx.

Themes

Beneath the glamour, episodes frequently ended ambiguously or tragically, exploring the corrosive effect of undercover work, the futility of the drug war, and the blurred line between cop and criminal — a neo-noir sensibility that anticipated later prestige crime dramas.
