Music of Ukraine

The music of Ukraine includes a diverse variety of musical elements, from indigenous folk music to modern rock, pop, classical and traditional repertoires. Ukraine's folk tradition features instruments such as the bandura, kobza and tsymbaly, and an extensive body of ritual, dance and epic songs.

Folk music

Ukrainian folk songs are traditionally divided into ritual songs (connected to the calendar and to weddings and funerals), historical songs and dumas — sung epic poems performed by blind itinerant minstrels called kobzars, who accompanied themselves on the kobza or bandura. Dance music accompanies dances such as the hopak. The Christmas song "Shchedryk", by Mykola Leontovych, became world-famous in its English adaptation as "Carol of the Bells".

Classical and modern music

Composers associated with Ukraine include Dmytro Bortniansky, Mykola Lysenko — often regarded as the founder of a Ukrainian national school — and, in the Soviet and modern eras, Borys Lyatoshynsky and Valentyn Silvestrov. Since independence in 1991, Ukrainian popular music has flourished; Ukrainian acts have won the Eurovision Song Contest three times: Ruslana (2004), Jamala (2016) and Kalush Orchestra (2022).
