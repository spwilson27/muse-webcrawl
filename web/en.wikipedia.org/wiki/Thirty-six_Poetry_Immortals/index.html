Thirty-six Poetry Immortals

The Thirty-six Poetry Immortals (sanjūrokkasen) are a group of Japanese poets of the Asuka, Nara and Heian periods selected by the eleventh-century poet and critic Fujiwara no Kintō as exemplars of poetic excellence in the waka tradition. The list is preserved in the Sanjūrokunin kasen (Selection of Thirty-six Poets).

The selection

Kintō, compiler of influential anthologies and author of the Shinsen zuinō, chose poets he regarded as masters of waka. The group includes celebrated figures such as Kakinomoto no Hitomaro, Yamabe no Akahito, Ōtomo no Yakamochi, Ariwara no Narihira, Ono no Komachi, Ki no Tsurayuki, Mibu no Tadamine, Lady Ise, Fujiwara no Okikaze and Sarumaru no Taifu. Five women are among the thirty-six.

Cultural influence

The list became canonical in Japanese literary culture. Portraits of the thirty-six poets, often accompanied by one of their poems, decorated screens, albums and temple collections; notable painted sets include those attributed to the Tosa school. The concept was later mirrored by the "Thirty-six Immortals of Kanshi" and, in the Edo period, by related groupings such as the Six Poetry Immortals (rokkasen), an earlier and smaller canon drawn from the preface of the Kokinshū.

Legacy

The compilation helped define the classical canon of Japanese poetry, and the poets of the list continue to feature prominently in the Ogura Hyakunin Isshu and in the traditional card game karuta.
