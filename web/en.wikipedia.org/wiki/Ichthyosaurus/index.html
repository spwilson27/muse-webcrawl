Ichthyosaurus (Greek for "fish lizard", from ichthys, "fish", and sauros, "lizard") is a genus of ichthyosaurs that lived during the Early Jurassic period in what is now Europe, including Belgium, England, Germany, Switzerland and Indonesia. It is among the best known ichthyosaur genera, known from hundreds of specimens, and was one of the first large fossil vertebrates to be recognised by science.

Adults reached about 2 metres (6.6 ft) in length. They were air-breathing marine predators, streamlined for fast swimming, with large eyes adapted for hunting in dim water, and they gave birth to live young rather than laying eggs.

Discovery and history

The first complete fossil skeleton attributable to the group was found at Lyme Regis, Dorset, in 1811 by Mary Anning and her brother Joseph, although the animal was not formally named until 1821, when Henry De la Beche and William Conybeare described Ichthyosaurus communis. Many classic specimens come from the Lower Jurassic rocks of Lyme Regis and Street in Somerset. During the nineteenth century nearly every ichthyosaur fossil was assigned to this genus, and it was later recognised that many belonged to distinct genera such as Stenopterygius and Temnodontosaurus.

Description

Ichthyosaurus was smaller than most of its relatives, with a total length of roughly 2 metres. It had a long, pointed snout armed with numerous conical teeth suited to catching fish and squid, very large orbits housing sclerotic rings, and a crescent-shaped tail fin supported by a downward-bent tail vertebrae column. The limbs were transformed into steering paddles with tightly packed polygonal bones. Coprolites show that its diet consisted mainly of fish and belemnites.

Classification

The genus belongs to the family Ichthyosauridae within the order Ichthyosauria. Recognised species include I. communis, I. breviceps, I. conybeari, I. anningae (named in 2015 after Mary Anning), I. larkini and I. somersetensis. Together with relatives such as Stenopterygius, it represents the classic dolphin-like body plan of Jurassic ichthyosaurs.
