Jennifer Shrader Lawrence (born August 15, 1990) is an American actress. The world's highest-paid actress in 2015 and 2016, she appeared in Time's 100 most influential people in the world list in 2013 and the Forbes Celebrity 100 list from 2013 to 2016. Lawrence's films have grossed over $6 billion worldwide.

Lawrence began her career in television, playing a main role on the sitcom The Bill Engvall Show (2007–2009). Her film breakthrough came as a poverty-stricken teenager in the independent drama Winter's Bone (2010), earning her first Academy Award nomination. She gained stardom playing the mutant Mystique in the X-Men film series (2011–2019) and Katniss Everdeen in The Hunger Games series (2012–2015), the latter making her the highest-grossing action heroine of all time.

Her performance as a young widow in the romance film Silver Linings Playbook (2012) won her the Academy Award for Best Actress, aged 22 — the second-youngest winner in the category. She won a BAFTA Award for playing a grieving trophy wife in American Hustle (2013) and Golden Globe Awards for Silver Linings Playbook, American Hustle, and for playing inventor Joy Mangano in Joy (2015). Lawrence has since starred in the science fiction romance Passengers (2016), the psychological horror film Mother! (2017), the spy thriller Red Sparrow (2018), the satire Don't Look Up (2021), the drama Causeway (2022), which she produced, and the comedy No Hard Feelings (2023).

Early life

Lawrence was born and raised in Indian Hills, Kentucky, near Louisville, to Gary, a construction worker, and Karen, a summer camp manager. She has two older brothers. A hyperactive and anxious child, she acted in church plays and school musicals; aged 14, on a family trip to New York City, she was spotted by a talent scout and signed with an agency, leaving school at 14 and earning a GED while building early television work in Los Angeles.

Breakthrough

After guest roles (Cold Case, Medium, Monk) and the TBS sitcom The Bill Engvall Show, for which she won a Young Artist Award, Lawrence transitioned to film with The Burning Plain (2008), winning the Marcello Mastroianni Award at Venice. Her leading role as Ree Dolly in Debra Granik's Winter's Bone (2010) brought universal acclaim and her first Oscar nomination, aged 20.

Franchise stardom and awards

In 2011 Lawrence played Mystique in X-Men: First Class. In 2012 she headlined The Hunger Games as Katniss Everdeen; the series finale split grossed over $3 billion combined and established her as a global star. Her collaborations with director David O. Russell — Silver Linings Playbook (2012), American Hustle (2013), and Joy (2015) — brought an Oscar win and two further nominations, making her the youngest actor to accrue four Academy Award nominations (by age 25).

Later work and production

Following Passengers (2016) and Darren Aronofsky's Mother! (2017), Lawrence took a hiatus, then co-founded the production company Excellent Cadaver. Through it she produced and starred in Lila Neugebauer's Causeway (2022) and the sex comedy No Hard Feelings (2023), and produced documentaries including Bread and Roses (2023) about Afghan women under Taliban rule.

Public image and activism

Lawrence is known for an unfiltered, self-deprecating public persona. A vocal feminist, she wrote a widely discussed 2015 essay on the Hollywood gender pay gap after the Sony Pictures hack revealed her compensation, founded the Jennifer Lawrence Foundation supporting charities in Kentucky, and has campaigned with RepresentUs against political corruption. She was a victim of the 2014 iCloud celebrity photo leak and condemned it as a sex crime. In 2019 she married art gallery director Cooke Maroney; they have two children.
