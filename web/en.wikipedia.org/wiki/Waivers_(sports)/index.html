Waivers (sports)

In North American professional sports, waivers are a contractual and procedural mechanism by which a team relinquishes its rights to a player, giving the other teams in the league a period of time to claim the player and assume his contract. If no team claims the player during the waiver period, the player is said to have "cleared waivers" and may then be assigned to a minor-league affiliate, released, or traded more freely, depending on the league's rules.

How it works

When a player is placed on waivers, teams may submit claims, usually in an order determined by reverse standings (worst record first). The claiming team typically assumes the player's existing contract. In Major League Baseball, the NHL, the NFL, and the NBA the details vary: the NHL requires most established players to clear waivers before being assigned to the AHL; the NFL uses waivers for players with fewer than four accrued seasons; NBA waivers run for 48 hours.

Related concepts

Waivers differ from an outright release, after which a player becomes a free agent immediately. Designated-for-assignment (DFA) in baseball is a related roster move that often precedes a waiver placement. The system is intended to promote competitive balance by giving weaker teams the first chance to acquire discarded players.
