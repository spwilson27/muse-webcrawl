Sarpsfossen (the Sarp Falls) is a waterfall on the river Glomma at the city of Sarpsborg in south-eastern Norway. It is one of the largest waterfalls in Europe by volume of water, with a greater average flow than many more famous falls, and it has been a defining feature of the city since its founding by Saint Olaf in 1016.

Industry and power

The falls powered sawmills and other industry from the sixteenth century onwards and were central to the development of Sarpsborg as an industrial city. In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries hydroelectric power stations were built at the falls, and the Borregaard industrial complex grew up beside them, making Sarpsborg a major centre of the Norwegian timber and chemical industries.

Crossings

The Sarp Bridge (Sarpsbrua) has spanned the falls since 1854 in successive forms, carrying both road and, nearby, railway traffic over the Glomma. The falls remain a symbol of Sarpsborg and appear in the city's coat of arms imagery of a bear over the waterfall.
