Hiroaki "Rocky" Aoki (October 9, 1938 – July 10, 2008) was a Japanese-born American restaurateur, professional wrestler and daredevil, best known as the founder of the Benihana chain of Japanese teppanyaki restaurants.

Rocky Aoki, founder of Benihana.

Early life and wrestling

Aoki was born in Tokyo, the son of the founder of the Benihana coffee shop and restaurant in Tokyo. He was a champion wrestler in Japan and qualified for the Japanese Olympic wrestling team before moving to the United States in 1960 on a wrestling scholarship, competing while studying restaurant management in New York.

Benihana

In 1964, with savings from driving an ice-cream truck in Harlem, Aoki opened the first Benihana restaurant in Manhattan, introducing American diners to teppanyaki — food cooked on a steel grill at the table by theatrical knife-wielding chefs. The format was a major success, and the chain grew across the United States and internationally.

Adventures

Aoki was famous for daredevil pursuits: offshore powerboat racing, in which he won championships despite serious crashes, and a 1981 attempt to cross the Pacific Ocean in a double-reeled helium balloon, the Double Eagle V flight from Japan to California — the first successful transpacific balloon crossing.

Personal life and death

Aoki was the father of DJ and producer Steve Aoki and model Devon Aoki. He died in New York City in 2008 of pneumonia aggravated by diabetes and liver disease.
