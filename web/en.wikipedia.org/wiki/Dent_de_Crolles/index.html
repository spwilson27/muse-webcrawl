Dent de Crolles

The Dent de Crolles is a mountain in the Chartreuse Mountains in the French Prealps, in the department of Isère, southeastern France. Rising to 2,062 metres (6,765 ft), it is the second-highest summit of the Chartreuse massif. Its name, "tooth of Crolles", refers to its distinctive tooth-shaped limestone profile, visible from Grenoble and the Grésivaudan valley, and to the nearby commune of Crolles.

Caving

The mountain is internationally famous among cavers for the Réseau de la Dent de Crolles, one of the most complex cave systems in the world: a network of more than 100 km of mapped passages linking entrances such as the Trou du Glaz, the Guiers Mort, and the Grotte Chevalier, with a depth of roughly 690 m. The system permits celebrated underground traverses from one side of the mountain to the other and has been explored since the 19th century.

Access

The usual ascent starts from the Col du Coq or from Saint-Hilaire-du-Touvet (also known for its funicular and paragliding). Routes such as the Pas de l'Œille and the Trou du Glaz path combine hiking and easy scrambling through striking limestone scenery within the Chartreuse Regional Natural Park.
