El Capitan (supercomputer)

El Capitan is an exascale supercomputer hosted at the Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory in California, United States. Built by Hewlett Packard Enterprise using AMD processors and accelerators, it was deployed for the National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA) to support the United States' nuclear stockpile stewardship programme. On its debut in November 2024 it topped the TOP500 list of the world's fastest supercomputers, with a measured performance of about 1.742 exaFLOPS on the High Performance LINPACK benchmark, becoming the third system to break the exascale barrier after Frontier and Aurora.

Architecture

El Capitan is based on HPE's Cray EX "Shasta" architecture. Its compute nodes use AMD Instinct MI300A accelerated processing units, which combine Zen 4 CPU cores and CDNA 3 GPU compute units on a single package with shared high-bandwidth memory. The system contains over 11 million combined CPU and GPU cores and is interconnected with HPE's Slingshot fabric. Its peak theoretical performance is around 2.7 exaFLOPS. A companion system, Tuolumne, built on the same technology at roughly a tenth of the scale, supports unclassified work at the laboratory.

Purpose

As part of the NNSA's Advanced Simulation and Computing programme, El Capitan performs high-resolution 3D simulations of nuclear weapons performance and safety, allowing the United States to maintain its stockpile without underground nuclear testing. It is also used for other national-security and scientific workloads, including materials science and high-energy-density physics.

History

The system was announced in 2019 as part of the US Department of Energy's CORAL-2 procurements, with a contract value reported around $600 million. Installation at Livermore took place through 2023–2024, and the machine was formally dedicated in early 2025. It succeeded Sierra, Livermore's previous flagship system, and took its name, like Sierra, from a prominent peak — El Capitan in Yosemite National Park.
