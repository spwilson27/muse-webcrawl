Video CD

Video CD (abbreviated VCD, also called View CD) is a home video format and a standard digital format for storing video onto a compact disc. The format was created in 1993 by Sony, Philips, Matsushita, and JVC as part of the "White Book" standard. VCDs are playable in dedicated VCD players, most DVD players, personal computers, and some video game consoles.

Technical specifications

A Video CD stores MPEG-1 video at a constant bit rate of about 1.15 Mbit/s, roughly the data rate of an audio CD. Resolution is 352×240 for NTSC or 352×288 for PAL, comparable to VHS quality. Audio is MPEG-1 Audio Layer II at 224 kbit/s. A standard 74-minute disc holds about 74 minutes of video, so feature films were typically distributed on two discs.

Adoption

VCD never gained much popularity in North America or Europe, where it was quickly overshadowed by DVD. It became enormously popular across Asia, however, because players and discs were inexpensive, discs were cheap to manufacture, and the format tolerated scratches and humid climates better than videotape. In China and Southeast Asia, VCD was the dominant home movie format through the late 1990s, and piracy of films on VCD was widespread. It was succeeded by DVD and later by streaming, and in China partly by the higher-resolution China Video Disc (CVD) and Super Video CD (SVCD) variants.
