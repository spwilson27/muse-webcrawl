A Negroni is an Italian cocktail, made of one part gin, one part vermouth rosso (red, semi-sweet) and one part Campari, garnished with orange peel. It is considered an apéritif. A properly made Negroni is stirred, not shaken, and built in the glass over ice.

History

The most widely reported account is that it was first mixed in Florence, Italy, in 1919 at Caffè Casoni (formerly Caffè Giacosa, later renamed Caffè Cavalli). Count Camillo Negroni is said to have asked the bartender, Fosco Scarselli, to strengthen his favourite cocktail, the Americano, by adding gin rather than the normal soda water. The bartender also added an orange garnish rather than the lemon garnish typical of the Americano to signify the drink was different. After the drink's success, the Negroni family founded the Negroni Distillerie in Treviso, producing a ready-made version.

The story has been questioned by historians — no member of the Negroni family named Camillo has been firmly documented in Florence at the time, and a Corsican branch of the family promoted a rival claim involving Count Pascal Olivier de Negroni — but the Florentine account remains the standard tradition, and the cocktail is listed among the International Bartenders Association's official cocktails.

Variations

  • Americano: the parent drink — Campari, sweet vermouth and soda water.
  • Boulevardier: bourbon or rye whiskey in place of gin.
  • Negroni sbagliato: prosecco in place of gin ("sbagliato" means "mistaken").
  • White Negroni: made with Suze and Lillet Blanc in place of Campari and vermouth.
  • Old Pal: rye whiskey and dry vermouth.

In popular culture

Since 2013, "Negroni Week" has been held annually as a charitable initiative by the drinks industry. In 2022 the Negroni was reported by industry surveys to have overtaken the Old Fashioned as the world's best-selling classic cocktail in bars.
