Dravidian architecture

Dravidian architecture is the style of temple architecture that emerged in southern India, characterised by pyramid-shaped towers, massive enclosing walls and elaborately sculpted gateways. It developed over more than a thousand years under dynasties including the Pallavas, Cholas, Pandyas, Vijayanagara and Nayakas.

Principal features

A classic Dravidian temple complex includes the vimana, the stepped pyramidal tower over the main shrine (garbhagriha); the gopuram, a monumental entrance gateway tower covered in painted sculpture, which in later temples grew taller than the vimana itself; pillared halls (mandapas); temple tanks; and concentric enclosing walls (prakarams). Unlike the curvilinear shikhara of northern Nagara temples, the vimana rises in distinct storeys capped by a barrel-vaulted or domed roof.

Notable examples

  • Shore Temple and Kanchipuram temples (Pallava)
  • Brihadisvara Temple, Thanjavur (Chola, 11th century)
  • Meenakshi Temple, Madurai (Pandya/Nayak)
  • Vittala Temple, Hampi (Vijayanagara)
