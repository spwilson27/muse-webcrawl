The Michigan Wolverines comprise 29 varsity sports teams at the University of Michigan. These teams compete in the NCAA's Division I and are members of the Big Ten Conference in all sports. The football team plays at Michigan Stadium, nicknamed "The Big House", the largest stadium in the Western Hemisphere by capacity, exceeding 107,000.

Michigan Stadium ("The Big House") in Ann Arbor.

Football

The Michigan Wolverines football program, begun in 1879, is among the most successful in college football history, with the most all-time wins in the sport, multiple claimed national championships (most recently winning the College Football Playoff national championship for the 2023 season), and famous coaches including Fielding Yost, Fritz Crisler (who introduced the winged helmet in 1938), Bo Schembechler, and Jim Harbaugh. Its rivalry with Ohio State — "The Game" — is one of the fiercest in American sports; other rivals include Michigan State and Notre Dame. Three Wolverines have won the Heisman Trophy: Tom Harmon, Desmond Howard, and Charles Woodson.

Other sports

Michigan's men's basketball team has won one NCAA championship (1989, under interim coach Steve Fisher and star Glen Rice) and produced the celebrated "Fab Five" freshmen of 1991–93. The ice hockey team is among the most decorated in the NCAA, with numerous national titles. The softball team won the 2005 Women's College World Series, and Michigan's swimming, gymnastics, wrestling, and Olympic-sport programs have produced many Olympians, including swimmer Michael Phelps, who trained at the university's Club Wolverine.
