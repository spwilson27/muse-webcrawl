Terek Cossacks

The Terek Cossacks are a Cossack host that historically inhabited the basin of the Terek River in the North Caucasus. They were one of the Cossack hosts of the Russian Empire, settled along the empire's southern frontier in what is now North Ossetia, Chechnya, Ingushetia, Dagestan, and Stavropol Krai.

History

Free Cossack communities appeared along the Terek as early as the 16th century, including the Greben Cossacks who settled in the foothills of the Caucasus. In 1577 a community of Volga Cossacks founded the town of Terki, and these settlers are often regarded as the founders of the Terek host. The Terek Cossack Host was formally established in 1860, combining earlier line Cossack formations.

Role and culture

The Terek Cossacks served as military colonists guarding the Caucasian Line against raids by highland peoples, and they participated in the Caucasian War and numerous Russian campaigns. Living among Caucasian peoples, they adopted elements of local dress, weaponry (such as the shashka and kindjal), horsemanship, and customs, producing a distinctive frontier culture reflected in the works of Leo Tolstoy, who served in the Caucasus and wrote about the Terek Cossacks in his novella The Cossacks.

Twentieth century

After the Russian Revolution, the Terek Cossacks suffered heavily during decossackization, deportations, and collectivization under the Soviet regime. In the post-Soviet period, registered Cossack societies in the region have revived, and the Terek host was re-established as a military Cossack society in modern Russia.
