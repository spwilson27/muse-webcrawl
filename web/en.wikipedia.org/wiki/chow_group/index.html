Chow group

In mathematics, the Chow group of an algebraic variety is an analogue of homology or cohomology groups in algebraic topology, constructed from algebraic cycles. The Chow groups are named after Wei-Liang Chow, who developed their foundations with André Weil in the 1940s and 1950s.

Definition

For an algebraic variety X, the group of cycles of codimension p is the free abelian group on the irreducible closed subvarieties of codimension p. Cycles arising as divisors of rational functions on subvarieties are declared rationally equivalent to zero; the quotient group of cycles modulo rational equivalence is the Chow group CHp(X). There are also Chow groups of dimension k, CHk(X).

Properties

Chow groups carry an intersection product given by intersecting cycles and moving them into proper position (Chow's moving lemma), which makes the total Chow group of a smooth variety into a graded ring. For smooth projective space, CHp(Pn) ≅ Z, generated by the class of a linear subspace. Rational equivalence is the finest adequate equivalence relation on cycles, so the Chow groups map to the groups defined by algebraic, homological, or numerical equivalence.

Importance

Chow groups play a central role in intersection theory, enumerative geometry, and the theory of motives. The Hodge conjecture and related problems concern which cohomology classes come from algebraic cycles and hence from Chow groups.
