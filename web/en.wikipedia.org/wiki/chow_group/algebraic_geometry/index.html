Chow group

In algebraic geometry, the Chow groups of an algebraic variety are algebraic-geometric analogues of the homology groups of a topological space. They are built from the variety's algebraic cycles modulo rational equivalence, and they admit an intersection product making them (for smooth varieties) into a graded ring, the Chow ring. The groups are named after Wei-Liang Chow, who studied them in the 1950s.

Definition

Let X be a smooth projective variety. The group of cycles of codimension p is the free abelian group Zp(X) on the irreducible subvarieties of codimension p. A cycle is rationally equivalent to zero if it can be written as a sum of divisors of rational functions on subvarieties of codimension p−1. The Chow group CHp(X) is the quotient of the cycle group by the subgroup of cycles rationally equivalent to zero. For curves, CH1 recovers the Picard group of divisors; for points, CH0 records zero-cycles up to rational equivalence.

Intersection product

Chow's moving lemma ensures that cycles can be moved within their rational equivalence classes so as to intersect properly, allowing an intersection product to be defined on the Chow groups of a smooth variety. William Fulton later developed a more general intersection theory via the "deformation to the normal cone", defining Chow groups and intersection products for singular varieties as well.

Relation to other theories

Cycle maps relate Chow groups to ordinary (co)homology, K-theory, and other cohomology theories; for complex varieties a cycle class lands in Hodge cohomology, and the Hodge conjecture concerns the image of this map for projective varieties. Chow groups also feature in Arakelov geometry and in the theory of motives, where the category of Chow motives organizes varieties by their cycle-theoretic content.
