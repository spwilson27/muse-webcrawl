Breve

A breve (from Latin brevis, "short") may refer to several different things united by the idea of brevity or by a curved, cup-shaped mark (˘). The best-known senses are the diacritic used in writing systems, a note value in music, and a short papal or legal document.

[ Image: Breve mark ]
The breve diacritic (˘) shown over a letter.

Diacritic

As a diacritic, the breve is a curved mark placed over a vowel to indicate a short vowel, as opposed to the macron which marks a long vowel. It is used in Latin and Greek scholarly transcription (ă, ĕ, ĭ, ŏ, ŭ), in Romanian for the letter ă, in Turkish and Azerbaijani for ğ (where it indicates lengthening or softening rather than shortness), and in Vietnamese orthography for ă and the vowel in words written with â-like contrasts. In the International Phonetic Alphabet, a breve below a letter marks extra-short duration.

Music

In musical notation, a breve or double whole note is a note lasting twice the duration of a whole note (semibreve). In mensural notation of the medieval and Renaissance periods the breve was a principal unit; in modern notation it is written as a filled or open notehead with vertical strokes on both sides, or as a small filled rectangle. The names of shorter notes — semibreve, minim, and others — derive from their fractional relation to the breve.

Documents

A papal brief (from the same Latin root) is a less formal form of papal document than a bull. Similarly, in Scots and older English law, a brieve or breve was a writ issuing from the chancery.
