Richard "Dick" Marcinko (November 21, 1940 – December 25, 2021) was a United States Navy officer and writer. A veteran of the Vietnam War, he is best known as the first commanding officer of SEAL Team Six, the Navy's premier counter-terrorism unit, and of Red Cell, a unit tasked with testing the security of naval installations. His 1992 autobiography Rogue Warrior became a bestseller and spawned a long-running series of novels.

Early life

Marcinko was born in Lansford, Pennsylvania, the son of Croatian and Slovak immigrants, and grew up in New Brunswick, New Jersey. After dropping out of high school, he enlisted in the United States Navy in 1958, initially serving as a radioman. He later completed a high school equivalency diploma and earned a bachelor's degree from the Naval Postgraduate School system while on active duty.

Naval career

Marcinko completed Basic Underwater Demolition/SEAL (BUD/S) training with class 26 and was commissioned as an officer. He served two tours in Vietnam with SEAL Team Two, leading operations in the Mekong Delta. During the Tet Offensive in January 1968 he led an assault on Chau Doc, for which he received the Silver Star. He also served as the Navy's liaison to the Central Intelligence Agency and commanded SEAL Team Two from 1974 to 1976.

SEAL Team Six and Red Cell

Following the failure of Operation Eagle Claw in 1980, the Iranian hostage rescue attempt, the Navy tasked Marcinko with creating a dedicated maritime counter-terrorism unit. He selected its operators from across the SEAL community and named the unit SEAL Team Six, reportedly to confuse Soviet intelligence about the number of existing SEAL teams. He commanded the unit from its founding in November 1980 until 1983. Afterwards he created the Naval Security Coordination Team, known as Red Cell (OP-06D), which conducted mock attacks and infiltrations to expose weaknesses at U.S. naval bases.

Later life and writing

Marcinko retired from the Navy in 1989. In 1990 he was convicted of conspiracy in a kickback scheme involving grenade procurement and served 15 months in federal prison, a prosecution he maintained was retaliation for Red Cell's embarrassing security findings. His memoir Rogue Warrior (1992), written with John Weisman, reached the New York Times bestseller list and was followed by a series of Rogue Warrior novels. He also worked as a motivational speaker, radio host, and consultant, and appeared in the 2009 video game Rogue Warrior, which was named after his book series.

Death

Marcinko died of a heart attack at his home in Fauquier County, Virginia, on Christmas Day, December 25, 2021, at the age of 81.
