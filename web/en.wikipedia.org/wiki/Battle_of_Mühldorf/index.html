The last great knightly battle on German soil, 1322.

The Battle of Mühldorf, also known as the Battle of Ampfing, was fought on 28 September 1322 near Mühldorf am Inn in Bavaria. The army of Louis IV of Bavaria decisively defeated his Habsburg rival Frederick the Fair of Austria, capturing him and ending the disputed double election of 1314 for the imperial crown. It is often described as the last great battle of knights fought without gunpowder weapons.

Background

The imperial election of 1314 had produced two kings: Louis of Wittelsbach and Frederick of Habsburg. Years of intermittent war followed. In 1322 Frederick invaded Bavaria from Austria with support from his brother Leopold, while Louis mustered his own host, including John I of Bohemia, between the Inn and Isen rivers.

Battle

Frederick attacked before Leopold's reinforcements could arrive. The battle was a hard-fought melee of mounted knights; tradition credits the intervention of a Bavarian reserve under the burgrave of Nuremberg — in some accounts arriving disguised in Austrian colours — with turning the day. The Austrian army was routed and Frederick was taken prisoner by a Bavarian knight, Albrecht Rindsmaul.

Aftermath

Frederick was held at Trausnitz Castle for nearly three years. In the Treaties of Trausnitz and Munich (1325), he renounced his claim and was released, thereafter ruling alongside Louis as a kind of co-king until his death in 1330. Louis was crowned emperor in Rome in 1328.
