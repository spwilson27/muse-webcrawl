Autonomic nervous system

DivisionsSympathetic, parasympathetic, enteric
Part ofPeripheral nervous system

The autonomic nervous system (ANS) is a component of the peripheral nervous system that regulates involuntary physiological processes including heart rate, blood pressure, respiration, digestion and pupillary response. It operates largely unconsciously.

Divisions

The sympathetic division mobilises the body in “fight or flight,” increasing heart rate and dilating airways, using noradrenaline. The parasympathetic division promotes “rest and digest,” slowing the heart and stimulating digestion, chiefly via the vagus nerve and acetylcholine. The enteric nervous system governs the gastrointestinal tract and can function semi-independently.

Function and disorders

The ANS maintains homeostasis through reflex arcs monitored by the hypothalamus and brainstem. Dysfunction, dysautonomia, can cause orthostatic hypotension, gastroparesis and abnormal sweating.
