Golden Jubilee Bridges

The Golden Jubilee Bridges are two parallel pedestrian bridges in London which flank the Hungerford Bridge over the River Thames. Built in 2002 to mark the Golden Jubilee of Queen Elizabeth II, they link Charing Cross on the north bank with the South Bank and Waterloo.

Design and construction

The bridges were designed by architects Lifschutz Davidson Sandilands with engineers WSP Group. Each bridge is a cable-stayed structure whose white steel masts lean outward, with fan-shaped arrays of slender steel rods supporting the decks; the stays are arranged to avoid the piers of the railway bridge between them. The project replaced narrow, unsatisfactory footways attached to the sides of the railway bridge and won a Royal Fine Art Commission award for bridge design.

History

A design competition was held in 1996. Construction was complicated by the proximity of the Bakerloo and Northern line tunnels beneath the river and the need to keep the mainline station open. The bridges opened in 2002, fifty years after Elizabeth II's accession. They carry millions of pedestrians a year between the West End and the South Bank cultural quarter.
