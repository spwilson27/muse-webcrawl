History of Finland

The history of Finland begins with settlement after the last Ice Age. Finland was part of the Kingdom of Sweden for centuries, then an autonomous grand duchy of the Russian Empire, before declaring independence in 1917.

Swedish and Russian rule

Swedish crusades and settlement integrated Finland into the Swedish realm from the 12th–13th centuries. Russia annexed Finland in 1809 after the Finnish War, ruling it as the Grand Duchy of Finland until 1917.

Independence

Finland declared independence on 6 December 1917, followed by a civil war in 1918. It fought the Soviet Union in the Winter War (1939–40) and Continuation War (1941–44), retaining independence while ceding territory, and later joined the European Union in 1995 and NATO in 2023.
