Alpenkorps (German Empire)

The Alpenkorps was a mountain division-sized formation of the Imperial German Army in the First World War. Raised in May 1915, principally from Bavarian units, to help defend Austria-Hungary's Tyrolean border after Italy entered the war, its men wore the edelweiss badge granted by their Austrian allies, a tradition continued by German mountain troops to this day.

Service

Though trained and equipped for mountain warfare, the Alpenkorps fought on many fronts: in the Dolomites in 1915, in Serbia, at Verdun and in Romania in 1916 — where the young Erwin Rommel served with its Württemberg mountain battalion — and in Italy in 1917, where it took part in the breakthrough at Caporetto. In 1918 it fought on the Western Front in the German spring offensives and the final defensive battles.

Organisation

The corps was built around Jäger and infantry regiments with mountain artillery, machine-gun and pioneer units, and included elite formations such as the Bavarian Life Guards. It was disbanded after the Armistice in 1919. The Nazi-era Wehrmacht later revived the Alpenkorps name informally for mountain formations, and its edelweiss symbol remains the insignia of today's Bundeswehr Gebirgsjäger.
