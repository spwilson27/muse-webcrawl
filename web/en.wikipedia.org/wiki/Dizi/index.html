Dizi

The dizi (Chinese: 笛子) is a major Chinese musical instrument. It is a transverse flute made of bamboo, widely used in many genres of Chinese folk music, Chinese opera, and the modern Chinese orchestra, as well as being popular among Chinese people in their daily lives as a folk instrument.

Construction

Most dizi are made of bamboo, though they may also be made of other woods, stone, jade or plastic. The instrument has six finger holes and, distinctively, an extra hole between the embouchure hole and the finger holes covered by a thin membrane called a mokong, traditionally made from the inner skin of bamboo or reed. This membrane gives the dizi its characteristic bright, buzzing timbre.

Types and playing

Dizi come in many sizes and pitches. The two most common types are the bangdi, a shorter, higher-pitched flute used in northern Chinese music and opera accompaniment, and the qudi, longer and lower, used in the Jiangnan sizhu ensembles and Kunqu opera of the south. Playing techniques include rapid finger articulations, vibrato, trills, and circular breathing.

History

Transverse flutes have existed in China for over two thousand years; bone flutes from the Neolithic Jiahu site date back some 9,000 years and are among the oldest playable instruments known. The dizi in its modern form has been central to Chinese music-making for centuries.
