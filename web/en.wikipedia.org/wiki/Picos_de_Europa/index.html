Picos de Europa

LocationAsturias, Cantabria and Castile and León, Spain
Highest pointTorre de Cerredo, 2,650 m (8,690 ft)
GeologyCarboniferous limestone
Protected areaPicos de Europa National Park (1918)

The Picos de Europa ("Peaks of Europe") are a mountain range in northern Spain forming part of the Cantabrian Mountains, extending across the autonomous communities of Asturias, Cantabria and Castile and León. Despite their modest altitude — the highest summit, Torre de Cerredo, reaches 2,650 metres — the pale limestone peaks rise dramatically close to the Atlantic coast, only about 20 kilometres inland. Tradition holds that the range owes its name to returning sailors, for whom the peaks were the first sight of Europe.

Geography

The range consists of three main massifs: the Western Massif (Cornión), the Central Massif (Urrieles), which contains Torre de Cerredo and the iconic Naranjo de Bulnes (Picu Urriellu, 2,519 m), and the Eastern Massif (Ándara). The limestone has been carved by glaciers and water into deep gorges, notably the Cares Gorge, and extensive cave systems; the region contains some of the deepest caves in Spain. Rivers such as the Deva and Cares drain the mountains.

National park

The western part of the range was declared Spain's first national park in 1918 (originally the Parque Nacional de la Montaña de Covadonga), and the park was expanded in 1995 to cover 646 km² across all three provinces and renamed Picos de Europa National Park. It is also a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve. Wildlife includes Cantabrian brown bears, Iberian wolves, chamois (rebeco), golden eagles and griffon vultures, and the park preserves traditional transhumant pastoralism and cheeses such as Cabrales, matured in limestone caves.
