Synaspismos (Greek: Συνασπισμός, "Coalition"), formally the Coalition of the Left of Movements and Ecology (Συνασπισμός της Αριστεράς των Κινημάτων και της Οικολογίας), was a Greek political party of the democratic socialist left. It was the largest component of the Syriza coalition, into which it merged in 2013 when Syriza became a unitary party.

History

Synaspismos originated in the late 1980s as an electoral alliance between the Communist Party of Greece (KKE) and the Greek Left (EAR), along with other groups. After the KKE left the alliance in 1991 following the collapse of the Soviet Union, the remaining members, led by Maria Damanaki and later Nikos Konstantopoulos, transformed Synaspismos into an independent party. Alekos Alavanos led the party from 2004, followed by Alexis Tsipras from 2008.

Syriza

In 2004 Synaspismos helped form the Coalition of the Radical Left (Syriza) with smaller left-wing and ecological groups. As Syriza's popularity surged during the Greek debt crisis under Tsipras, the party voted in July 2013 to dissolve itself into a unitary Syriza.
