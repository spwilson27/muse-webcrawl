MacRobert Award

The MacRobert Award is a United Kingdom award for engineering innovation, presented annually by the Royal Academy of Engineering. It is widely regarded as the UK's most prestigious prize for engineering, recognising outstanding innovation with proven commercial success and benefit to society.

History

The award was first presented in 1969 and is named after Lady Rachel MacRobert, whose family established the MacRobert Trusts. The winning team receives a gold medal and a cash prize, originally £25,000 and later increased to £50,000.

Selection

Entries are judged by a panel of fellows of the Royal Academy of Engineering. The criteria emphasise engineering innovation, commercial success and social benefit. Finalists are typically announced in the spring, with the winner revealed at the Academy's annual awards dinner.

Notable winners

Past winners include the team behind the CT scanner at EMI, the developers of the Harrier jump jet's Pegasus engine, and more recent winners recognised for work in medical devices, renewable energy and advanced manufacturing.
