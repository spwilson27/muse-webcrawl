Steganography

Steganography is the practice of concealing a message within another message or physical object, so that the very existence of the hidden communication is not apparent. The term comes from the Greek steganographia, combining steganós ("covered") and graphein ("writing"). Unlike cryptography, which scrambles a message so it cannot be read, steganography aims to hide that a secret message exists at all; the two techniques are often combined.

History

Historical examples include messages tattooed on a slave's shaved head and concealed as the hair regrew (recorded by Herodotus), invisible inks, and microdots. Johannes Trithemius coined the term in his Steganographia (written c. 1499).

Digital steganography

In digital media, information can be hidden in the least significant bits of image, audio or video files, in text formatting, or in network protocols. The analysis and detection of hidden content is called steganalysis. Steganography has legitimate uses (watermarking, metadata) as well as illicit ones, which has made it a topic of interest in computer security.
