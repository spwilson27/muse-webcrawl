The Curly-coated Retriever (often called simply the Curly) is a British breed of gundog, developed in England in the early 19th century for retrieving game from land and water. It is distinguished from all other retrievers by its coat of small, tight, crisp curls, and it is regarded as the oldest of the retriever breeds, though today also one of the rarest.

History

The breed's ancestors are thought to include the now-extinct English Water Spaniel, the St John's water dog from Newfoundland and, later, poodle crosses introduced to tighten the curl. Curly-coated Retrievers were exhibited at the earliest dog shows — a class was provided at Birmingham in 1860 — and the breed was exported widely, becoming especially established in Australia and New Zealand, where it worked in rough terrain and cold water. Numbers collapsed after the two World Wars, and although the breed recovered, annual registrations remain low; it is classed as a vulnerable native breed in the United Kingdom.

Appearance

The Curly is the tallest of the retrievers, standing about 69 cm at the shoulder for males, with a strong, slightly longer-than-tall body. The coat is its hallmark: a dense mass of tight curls covering the body, tail and legs, with smooth short hair on the face. The curls shed water readily and protect the dog in icy water and thick cover. Only two colours are recognised: black and liver (a deep brown). The head is wedge-shaped with dark eyes and small ears covered in curls.

Temperament and work

Curlies are intelligent, independent and somewhat more aloof with strangers than other retrievers, while devoted and playful with their families. Bred as an all-purpose shooting dog, the breed combines a keen nose, strong swimming ability and natural marking skill with the stamina for upland game as well as waterfowl. The breed is slow to mature and requires firm, consistent training and abundant exercise.
