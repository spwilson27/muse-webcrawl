Calligraphy

Calligraphy (from Greek kalligraphia, 'beautiful writing') is a visual art related to writing: the design and execution of lettering with a pen, ink brush, or other writing instrument. It is practised in many cultures, including Western, East Asian, South Asian and Islamic traditions.

MediumInk, pen, brush on paper or parchment
Major traditionsWestern, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Islamic, Indian

History

Beautiful handwriting was prized wherever manuscripts were copied. In medieval Europe monks produced illuminated manuscripts in scripts such as Carolingian minuscule, blackletter and italic. In China calligraphy became one of the highest arts, associated with scholars and painters, using brush and ink on paper or silk.

Western calligraphy

Latin calligraphy developed from Roman capitals and uncials through medieval book hands to Renaissance italic. The 20th-century revival led by Edward Johnston, author of Writing & Illuminating & Lettering (1906), restored broad-edged pen lettering.

East Asian calligraphy

Chinese shūfǎ, Japanese shodō and Korean seoye emphasise brush strokes, balance and expressive energy. Tools are traditionally the 'Four Treasures': brush, ink, paper and inkstone.

Islamic calligraphy

Because depiction of figures is restricted in many religious contexts, Arabic calligraphy became a major decorative art in mosques and manuscripts, in styles such as Kufic, Naskh, Thuluth and Diwani.

Modern practice

Today calligraphy is used for invitations, logos, typography, graffiti art and personal expression, alongside digital lettering. See also Typography, Illuminated manuscript and Penmanship.
