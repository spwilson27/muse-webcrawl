Slurm

The Slurm Workload Manager, formerly Simple Linux Utility for Resource Management (SLURM), also known as Slurm, is a free and open-source job scheduler for Linux and Unix-like kernels, used by many of the world's supercomputers and computer clusters.

It provides three key functions. First, it allocates access to resources (computer nodes) to users for some duration of time so they can perform work. Second, it provides a framework for starting, executing, and monitoring work (typically a parallel job such as MPI) on a set of allocated nodes. Finally, it arbitrates contention for resources by managing a queue of pending jobs.

Features

Slurm's features include: no single point of failure (with backup daemons), a fault-tolerant job step parallel launch, a hierarchical bank accounts system, and a large set of plugin modules for extending its behaviour — for authentication, scheduling policies, and resource selection, among others. It is highly scalable, supporting tens of thousands of independent jobs and hundreds of thousands of processors.

Adoption

Slurm is the workload manager on a majority of the TOP500 supercomputers, including many of the most powerful systems in the world. Its development began as a collaborative project involving Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory, SchedMD, Linux NetworX, Hewlett-Packard, and Groupe Bull. Since 2010, Slurm development and support has been provided by SchedMD.

Commands

Common user commands include sbatch (submit a batch script), srun (run a parallel job), squeue (view the job queue), sinfo (view system information), scancel (cancel a job), and scontrol (administrative view and control).
