Michael Ellis DeBakey (September 7, 1908 – July 11, 2008) was an American general and cardiovascular surgeon widely regarded as one of the most influential physicians of the 20th century. Over a career spanning more than seven decades at Baylor College of Medicine in Houston, he pioneered numerous operations and devices in the treatment of diseases of the heart and blood vessels.

Early work and inventions

While still a medical student at Tulane University, DeBakey devised the roller pump, a device that became a key component of the heart–lung machine and made open-heart surgery possible. During the Second World War he helped develop the US Army's system of mobile surgical hospitals (MASH units) and an improved system for treating battlefield vascular injuries, experience he later applied to civilian surgery through the landmark Korean War–era vascular registry.

Surgical achievements

DeBakey performed the first successful removal of a carotid artery blockage (endarterectomy, 1953), the first successful patch-graft angioplasty, and pioneered repair of aortic aneurysms using Dacron grafts — a material he first fabricated on his wife's sewing machine. In 1964 he led the team that performed the first successful coronary artery bypass operation. He was involved in the early use of an artificial heart in 1966 and performed some of the first heart transplants in the United States. His long professional rivalry with fellow Houston surgeon Denton Cooley became famous in medical history.

Institution building and honours

DeBakey transformed Baylor's surgery department and the Texas Medical Center into world-leading institutions, advised US presidents, and received the Presidential Medal of Freedom (1969), the National Medal of Science (1987) and the Congressional Gold Medal (2008), shortly before his death at age 99. The classification of aortic dissections still bears his name.
