Diehard tests

The Diehard tests are a battery of statistical tests for measuring the quality of a random number generator. They were published by statistician George Marsaglia on a CD-ROM in 1995 and became a standard benchmark for pseudorandom number generators.

The battery

The suite contains about fifteen tests, including the birthday spacings test, the overlapping permutations test, the binary rank tests for matrices, the bitstream and "count the 1's" tests, the parking lot test, the minimum distance test, the random spheres test, the squeeze test, the overlapping sums test, the runs test, and the craps test. Each test produces p-values that should be uniformly distributed if the generator is truly random.

Successors

The Diehard battery was later extended and largely superseded by Marsaglia's Dieharder suite and by the NIST Statistical Test Suite and TestU01, but it remains historically important in the evaluation of random number generators.
