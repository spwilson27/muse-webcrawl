Indoor air pollution

Indoor air pollution is the degradation of indoor air quality by harmful chemicals and other materials. It can be up to ten times worse than outdoor air pollution and is a major global health risk, because people spend most of their time indoors and because pollutants are often concentrated in enclosed spaces.

Sources

The largest source worldwide is the burning of solid fuels — wood, crop waste, dung and coal — for cooking and heating in poorly ventilated homes, affecting billions of people. Other sources include tobacco smoke, radon gas seeping from the ground, mould and microbial pollutants, asbestos and lead in building materials, and volatile organic compounds (VOCs) from paints, cleaning products and furnishings.

Health effects

Exposure is linked to pneumonia in children, chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, lung cancer, stroke and cardiovascular disease. The World Health Organization attributes several million premature deaths per year to household air pollution, mostly in low- and middle-income countries, with women and young children most exposed.

Mitigation

Solutions include switching to clean fuels (electricity, LPG, biogas), improved cookstoves, ventilation, building standards, and bans on smoking indoors.
