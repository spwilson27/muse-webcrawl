Loans for shares scheme

The loans for shares scheme (Russian: zalogovye auktsiony, "collateral auctions") was a programme of privatisation carried out in Russia in 1995, during the presidency of Boris Yeltsin. Under the scheme, the Russian government borrowed money from commercial banks, pledging shares in major state-owned companies — particularly in oil, metals, and mining — as collateral. When the government predictably failed to repay the loans, the banks were allowed to keep or auction the shares, and did so at prices far below their market value.

Background

The earlier voucher privatisation programme had left many large industrial enterprises with dispersed and insider-dominated ownership. Faced with a budget crisis and hoping to bind powerful businessmen to Yeltsin's re-election in 1996, the government accepted a proposal associated with banker Vladimir Potanin to raise revenue through loans secured on the state's remaining stakes in prized companies.

The auctions

Shares in enterprises such as Norilsk Nickel, Yukos, Sibneft, Sidanko, and Lukoil changed hands in auctions widely criticised as rigged: competing bids were excluded on technicalities, and the winning banks frequently administered the very auctions they won. The transactions transferred some of Russia's most valuable natural-resource assets to a small group of financiers who became known as the oligarchs.

Consequences

The scheme is regarded by many historians and economists as a decisive moment in post-Soviet Russian history: it consolidated a new class of super-wealthy owners, deepened public disillusionment with privatisation and market reform, and fuelled later efforts by the state under Vladimir Putin to reassert control over strategic industries.
