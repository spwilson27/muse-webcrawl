Independent living

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Independent living is a philosophy and a movement of people with disabilities working for self-determination, equal opportunity, and self-respect. It holds that people with disabilities are the best experts on their own needs, and that they should be able to live in the community of their choice with whatever support they require, rather than in institutions. In this context, "independent" does not mean doing everything without help; it means having control over one's life and decisions.

History

The movement grew out of the civil rights struggles of the 1960s and 1970s in the United States. A pivotal figure was Ed Roberts, a severely disabled student at the University of California, Berkeley, who, with fellow students known as the "Rolling Quads," demanded accessible housing and personal assistance services. In 1972 Roberts and his peers founded the Center for Independent Living in Berkeley, the first organization run by and for people with disabilities providing peer support, advocacy, and services. The model spread worldwide, and in the US the Rehabilitation Act of 1978 provided federal support for a national network of such centres.

Services and policy

Centres for Independent Living typically provide core services including information and referral, independent living skills training, peer counselling, individual and systems advocacy, and transition services — helping people move from nursing homes back into the community and diverting others from institutionalization. The philosophy is reflected internationally in the UN Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (2006), whose Article 19 recognizes the right to live independently and be included in the community.
