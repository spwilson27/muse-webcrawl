Avalanche diode

An avalanche diode is a diode made of silicon or other semiconductor material that has been designed to experience avalanche breakdown at a specified reverse bias voltage. The junction of an avalanche diode is designed to prevent current concentration at hot spots, so that the diode is undamaged by the breakdown. The nearly constant voltage maintained during breakdown makes the device useful as a voltage reference and for overvoltage protection.

Avalanche breakdown

When a sufficiently high reverse voltage is applied, charge carriers accelerated in the junction's electric field gain enough energy to free additional carriers by impact ionisation. These in turn free more carriers, producing a rapidly multiplying "avalanche" of current at a well-defined breakdown voltage. Diodes designed for this effect typically have breakdown voltages above about 5.6 volts, where avalanche multiplication dominates over the Zener effect; the two mechanisms have opposite temperature coefficients, which can be balanced in precision references.

Applications

Avalanche diodes are used in surge protection circuits, voltage clamping and reference voltage sources. A specialised form, the avalanche photodiode, exploits the gain of avalanche multiplication to detect very low light levels, and is used in fibre-optic receivers and lidar. Operated in Geiger mode above breakdown, such devices become single-photon avalanche diodes (SPADs) capable of registering individual photons.
