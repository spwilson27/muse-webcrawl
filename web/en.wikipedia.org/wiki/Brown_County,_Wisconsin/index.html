Brown County, Wisconsin

Brown County is a county in the U.S. state of Wisconsin. As of the 2020 census, the population was 268,047, making it the fourth-most populous county in Wisconsin. Its county seat is Green Bay. The county was created in 1818, when Wisconsin was part of Michigan Territory, and is named for Major General Jacob Brown, a military leader during the War of 1812.

Geography

Brown County lies in northeastern Wisconsin at the southern end of Green Bay, an arm of Lake Michigan. The Fox River flows northward through the county to the bay. According to the U.S. Census Bureau, the county has a total area of about 616 square miles, of which roughly 530 square miles are land. Other municipalities include De Pere, Ashwaubenon, Bellevue, Howard, Suamico, Hobart, Allouez and Wrightstown.

Economy and culture

Green Bay is known for its paper industry — long styled the "Toilet Paper Capital of the World" — as well as meatpacking, shipping through the Port of Green Bay, and the Green Bay Packers of the National Football League, whose Lambeau Field is in Green Bay. The county is part of the Green Bay metropolitan statistical area.

Demographics

The population grew steadily through the twentieth century, from about 61,889 in 1920 to over a quarter-million by 2020, with growing Hispanic and Hmong communities alongside residents of Belgian, German, Polish and Oneida heritage. The Oneida Nation reservation lies partly within the county.
