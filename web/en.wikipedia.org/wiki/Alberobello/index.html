Alberobello (Italian: [ˌalberoˈbɛllo]; literally "beautiful tree") is a small town and comune in the Metropolitan City of Bari, Apulia, southern Italy. It has around 10,700 inhabitants and is famous for its unique trullo buildings. The trulli of Alberobello have been designated as a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1996.

History

The settlement grew in the 16th century when the Counts of Conversano, the Acquaviva family, encouraged peasants to clear and cultivate the land. The trulli — whitewashed limestone dwellings with conical roofs built without mortar — are said to have been constructed so they could be quickly dismantled when royal tax inspectors arrived, since the Kingdom of Naples taxed new settlements.

The trulli

A trullo is built from roughly worked limestone boulders collected from nearby fields, using a dry-stone technique. The conical roofs, often decorated with painted religious or zodiacal symbols, are formed of limestone slabs called chiancarelle. The densest concentrations are in the Rione Monti and Rione Aia Piccola districts, which together contain more than 1,500 trulli.

Main sights

  • The Trullo Sovrano, the town's only two-storey trullo
  • The Church of Saint Anthony (Chiesa di Sant'Antonio), built in trullo form in 1927
  • The Basilica of Saints Cosmas and Damian, the town's patron saints
