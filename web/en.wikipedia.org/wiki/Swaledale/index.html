Swaledale

Swaledale is one of the northernmost dales in the Yorkshire Dales National Park in northern England. The valley of the River Swale runs from the Cumbrian border eastward past the villages of Keld, Muker, Gunnerside and Reeth to Richmond.

Landscape

The dale is known for its pattern of small hay meadows divided by drystone walls and stone field barns (cow houses), among the most characteristic landscapes in the Yorkshire Dales. Wildflower meadows around Muker are protected for their traditional management.

History

Swaledale was an important lead-mining district in the 18th and 19th centuries, and the Swaledale breed of sheep takes its name from the valley. The dale also gave its name to Swaledale cheese, traditionally made from sheep's milk.
