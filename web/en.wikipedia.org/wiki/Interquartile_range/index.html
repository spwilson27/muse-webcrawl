Interquartile range


A box plot illustrating the interquartile range.

In descriptive statistics, the interquartile range (IQR) is a measure of statistical dispersion, the spread of the data. It is defined as the difference between the 75th and 25th percentiles of the data — the third quartile (Q3) minus the first quartile (Q1): IQR = Q3 − Q1. It corresponds to the range of the middle half of the data.

Use

Unlike the total range, the interquartile range is robust to outliers because it is based solely on the middle portion of a distribution. It is the standard measure of spread in a box plot, where the box spans Q1 to Q3 with a line at the median, and whiskers or plotted points are often used to flag values lying more than 1.5 × IQR beyond the quartiles as potential outliers.

Example

For the data set {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8}, the first quartile is 2.5 and the third quartile is 6.5 under common conventions, giving an IQR of 4. Several methods exist for computing quartiles, and they can produce slightly different values, especially for small data sets.
