Transfer case

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A transfer case is a part of the drivetrain of a four-wheel-drive, all-wheel-drive, or other multiple-powered-axle vehicle. The transfer case transfers power from the transmission to the front and rear axles by means of drive shafts. It is usually located immediately behind the transmission and includes one or two sets of additional low-range gears in most off-road applications.

Function

In part-time four-wheel-drive systems, the transfer case directs power to the rear axle in two-wheel-drive mode and engages the front axle when four-wheel drive is selected with a lever, dial, or switch. In full-time systems it incorporates a centre differential that allows the front and rear axles to rotate at different speeds on paved surfaces, often with a locking function for low-traction conditions. Many transfer cases also provide a low-range gearset (commonly around 2:1 to 4:1 reduction) for steep, slow off-road driving, and some provide a power take-off (PTO) to run auxiliary equipment.

Types

Transfer cases are either gear-driven or chain-driven; chain-drive units are quieter and lighter and dominate in modern vehicles, while heavy gear-drive cases are prized for strength in dedicated off-road vehicles. They may be married (bolted directly) to the transmission or divorced (separate, connected by a short shaft).
