Google Play, formerly known as the Android Market, is a digital distribution service operated and developed by Google. It serves as the official app store for certified Android devices and also offers ebooks and, in some regions, other digital media. Launched in 2008 alongside the first Android phone, it was rebranded Google Play in March 2012, unifying Google's music, books, movies and app stores. The store — browsable at play.google.com — lists millions of apps and is pre-installed on nearly all Android phones sold outside China.

History and features

The Android Market opened with just a few dozen apps; paid apps, in-app purchases, carrier billing and curation features followed. Today the Play Store includes app and game charts, editorial collections, Play Pass subscription (games and apps without ads or in-app purchases), Play Points rewards, and safety features under Google Play Protect, which scans apps for malware. Google generally takes a 15–30 percent commission on app sales and subscriptions — a policy challenged by developers and regulators.

Criticism

Google's Play policies were at the centre of the antitrust case Epic Games v. Google, in which a US jury found in December 2023 that Google held an illegal monopoly in Android app distribution and in-app billing; remedies ordered in 2024 required Google to open the Play Store to rival stores in the US. Play has also been criticised for hosting malicious and copycat apps despite screening, and for removing apps under government pressure in various countries.
