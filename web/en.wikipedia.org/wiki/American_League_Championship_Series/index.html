The American League Championship Series (ALCS) is a best-of-seven playoff and one of two League Championship Series comprising the penultimate round of Major League Baseball's (MLB) postseason. The winner of the ALCS wins the American League pennant and advances to play the winner of the National League Championship Series (NLCS) in the World Series. The series is played in a 2–3–2 format.

History

The ALCS was created in 1969 when the American League split into two divisions, East and West, with the winners of each division meeting for the pennant. From 1969 through 1984 it was a best-of-five series; it expanded to best-of-seven in 1985, a change made famous by the 1985 Toronto–Kansas City series. Since the addition of the Wild Card in 1994 (first played in 1995 after the 1994 strike), the ALCS participants are the winners of the two American League Division Series. A further postseason expansion in 2022 added a Wild Card Series round.

The New York Yankees have won the most ALCS titles, while memorable series include the 2004 edition, in which the Boston Red Sox overcame a 3–0 deficit against the Yankees — the first such comeback in MLB history — en route to ending their 86-year championship drought.

Championship Trophy

The winning team receives the William Harridge Trophy, named for the former American League president. Since 2017, a Most Valuable Player award (the Lee MacPhail Award) is presented to the series' outstanding player; earlier honorees were recognized retroactively by various selections.
