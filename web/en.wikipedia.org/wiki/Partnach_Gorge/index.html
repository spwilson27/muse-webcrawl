Partnach Gorge

LocationGarmisch-Partenkirchen, Bavaria, Germany
LengthApprox. 700 m
DepthUp to 80 m

The Partnach Gorge (German: Partnachklamm) is a deep gorge near Garmisch-Partenkirchen in Bavaria, southern Germany, carved by the Partnach river through limestone rock. About 700 metres long and up to 80 metres deep, it is one of the most popular natural attractions in the Bavarian Alps.

Visiting

A path with tunnels blasted into the rock runs along the gorge, offering views of waterfalls and rapids. In winter, ice formations cling to the walls and the gorge is a popular evening torch-lit walk destination.

History

The gorge was historically used for floating timber down to Partenkirchen; the path was made safe for tourists in the early twentieth century. It was used as a filming location and lies on routes toward the Reintal and the Zugspitze massif.
