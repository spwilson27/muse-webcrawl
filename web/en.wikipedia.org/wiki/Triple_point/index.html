Triple point

Triple point of water
Temperature273.16 K (0.01 °C, 32.018 °F)
Pressure611.657 Pa (0.00604 atm)
PhasesIce, liquid water, water vapour in equilibrium

In thermodynamics, the triple point of a substance is the temperature and pressure at which the three phases (gas, liquid and solid) of that substance coexist in thermodynamic equilibrium. It is the point on a phase diagram where the sublimation, fusion and vaporisation curves meet.

Triple point of water

The triple point of water—273.16 K at a pressure of 611.657 pascals—was historically used to define the kelvin, the SI unit of temperature: until the 2019 redefinition of the SI, the kelvin was defined as 1/273.16 of the thermodynamic temperature of the triple point of water. Because the triple point is a fixed, reproducible state, triple-point cells filled with highly pure water were the standard artefacts for calibrating thermometers.

Other substances

Many substances have characteristic triple points; that of carbon dioxide, at 216.58 K and 5.12 atm, explains why dry ice sublimates rather than melts at atmospheric pressure. Some substances have multiple triple points where different solid polymorphs participate.
