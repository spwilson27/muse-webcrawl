Shoe

A shoe is an item of footwear intended to protect and comfort the human foot. Shoes are also used as an item of decoration and fashion. The design of shoes has varied enormously through time and from culture to culture, with form originally being tied to function.

History

The earliest known shoes are sagebrush bark sandals from Fort Rock Cave in Oregon, dated to roughly 8000 BCE. Humans likely wore simple foot coverings much earlier; evidence from toe bone morphology suggests footwear was in use by the Upper Paleolithic. Ötzi the Iceman (c. 3300 BCE) wore shoes of bearskin and deer skin stuffed with grass. Leather moccasins, sandals in Egypt and Rome, and wooden clogs and pattens in medieval Europe are among the many regional forms. The high heel originated in Persia for cavalry use and spread to European aristocratic fashion in the 17th century.

Construction

A shoe consists of an upper, a sole (outsole, midsole and insole), a heel, and often a fastening system such as laces, straps, buckles, zippers or hook-and-loop closures. Traditional construction methods include the Goodyear welt, Blake stitch, and cementing. Modern athletic shoes use synthetic materials, foam cushioning and specialized designs for particular sports.

Types

Common types include boots, dress shoes, sneakers (trainers), sandals, loafers, Oxford and Derby shoes, brogues, pumps, and moccasins. Shoe sizes are measured with devices such as the Brannock Device, and follow regional systems (US, UK, EU, Mondopoint).
