St. Lambert's Church, Münster

St Lamberti tower, showing the three cages of 1536.

St. Lambert's Church (German: St. Lamberti) is a Roman Catholic parish church in Münster, Westphalia, Germany. Built between 1375 and 1525 in the late Gothic style, it stands at the northern end of the Prinzipalmarkt and its tower, modelled on that of Freiburg Minster, is a landmark of the city. It belongs to the Diocese of Münster.

The cages

The church is famous for the three iron cages hanging from its tower. In 1536, after the suppression of the Münster Anabaptist rebellion, the corpses of the movement's leaders — Jan van Leiden, Bernhard Krechting, and Bernhard Knipperdolling — were displayed in the cages as a warning after their execution in the marketplace. The bones were removed around fifty years later, but the cages remained, were preserved through later restorations, and were returned to the tower after war damage was repaired in the 20th century. They remain the most notorious relics of the Anabaptist kingdom of Münster.

Architecture and history

St Lamberti was the church of the city's merchants, standing at the boundary between the market quarter and the cathedral immunity. The hall church was built in Westphalian late Gothic, and its tower's openwork spire echoed Freiburg's. The building suffered damage in the Second World War and was restored in the post-war decades. The church houses significant late medieval sculpture and an organ used for regular concerts. A night watchman (Türmer) still sounds a horn from the tower each evening, a tradition maintained since the 14th century.
