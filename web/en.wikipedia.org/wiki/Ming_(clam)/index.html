Ming (clam)

Species: Ocean quahog (Arctica islandica)
Born: c. 1499
Died: 2006 (aged about 507)
Found: Off the coast of Iceland
Known for: Oldest recorded non-colonial animal

Ming was an ocean quahog clam (Arctica islandica) that was dredged off the coast of Iceland in 2006 during a research cruise by scientists from Bangor University. By counting the annual growth rings in its shell — a technique called sclerochronology — researchers determined that Ming had lived for about 507 years, making it the longest-lived non-colonial animal known to science.

Name and age

The clam was nicknamed "Ming" by journalists because, on the initial estimate of 405–410 years, it would have been born during China's Ming dynasty. More precise counting later revised the age upward to 507 years, meaning the animal settled on the seabed around 1499. The clam died in the course of the research — it was frozen on board ship with the rest of the catch before its exceptional age was known, a fact that drew considerable media comment.

Scientific value

Ocean quahog shells record annual growth bands whose widths reflect sea temperatures and food availability, so long-lived specimens like Ming provide a continuous archive of North Atlantic marine climate extending back centuries before instrumental records. The specimen and its shell chronology have been used in studies reconstructing past ocean conditions.
