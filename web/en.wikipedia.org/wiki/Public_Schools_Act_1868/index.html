Public Schools Act 1868

Citation: 31 & 32 Vict. c. 118
Royal assent: 1868
Status: Repealed (superseded by later statutes)

The Public Schools Act 1868 (31 & 32 Vict. c. 118) was an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that reformed the governance of seven leading English boys' boarding schools — the "public schools" Eton, Winchester, Westminster, Charterhouse, Harrow, Rugby and Shrewsbury.

Background

The Act followed the report of the Clarendon Commission (1861–1864), chaired by the Earl of Clarendon, which had been appointed to investigate the revenues, management and studies of the nine great schools (the seven above, plus St Paul's and Merchant Taylors', which as day schools were treated separately and dealt with by other arrangements). The commission found much to praise in the schools' classical education but criticised their antiquated statutes, the unchecked power or neglect of governing bodies, weak teaching of modern subjects such as science, mathematics and modern languages, and a lack of financial accountability.

Provisions

The Act dissolved the existing governing bodies of the seven schools and replaced them with new ones on which outside bodies — the universities of Oxford and Cambridge, the Royal Society, and the Lord Chancellor, among others — were represented. The new governing bodies were empowered to make statutes for their schools regulating admissions, fees, curricula, the appointment and dismissal of headmasters and staff, and the use of endowment income, subject to the approval of special commissioners appointed under the Act. The Act sought to preserve the schools' independence and charitable foundations while ending the self-perpetuating closed corporations that had governed them.

Legacy

Together with the Endowed Schools Act 1869, which applied similar scrutiny to hundreds of grammar schools, the 1868 Act began the modern era of regulated independent schooling in England. Its framework was progressively replaced by later legislation in the twentieth century; the charitable status framework for these schools was ultimately reshaped under the Charities Acts and reforms of the 2000s.
