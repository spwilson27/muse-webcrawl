Lease

A lease is a contract by which one party (the lessor or landlord) conveys land, property, services, or other assets to another party (the lessee or tenant) for a specified period of time, usually in return for periodic payment. A lease of land or buildings creates both a contractual relationship and an estate in land known as a leasehold estate; a lease of goods or equipment is a form of hiring governed largely by contract and, for businesses, by accounting rules on leasing.

Leases of land

A leasehold is one of the two main forms of land tenure in English law, the other being freehold. The tenant acquires exclusive possession for a fixed or periodic term while the landlord retains the reversion. Leases may run for any length from a week to 999 years; long residential leases (commonly 99 or 125 years) are effectively a form of home ownership, with the leaseholder paying ground rent and service charges. The lease document sets out covenants on rent, repair, use, assignment, and subletting, and statutory regimes in many jurisdictions protect tenants against unfair terms and arbitrary eviction.

Types of tenancy

  • Fixed-term tenancy — ends automatically on a specified date.
  • Periodic tenancy — continues period to period (e.g. monthly) until terminated by notice.
  • Tenancy at will — terminable by either party at any time.
  • Tenancy at sufferance — a tenant remains in possession after the lease expires.

Gross and net leases

In commercial property, a gross lease sets a single rent from which the landlord pays operating expenses, while in a net lease the tenant pays some or all of property taxes, insurance, and maintenance (a "triple net" lease passes on all three). Percentage leases, used in retail, add a share of the tenant's turnover to a base rent.
