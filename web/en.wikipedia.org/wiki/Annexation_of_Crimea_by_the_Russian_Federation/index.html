Annexation of Crimea by the Russian Federation

Annexation of Crimea

Date20 February – 26 March 2014 (de facto)
LocationCrimean Peninsula
ParticipantsRussia, Ukraine
OutcomeRussia administers Crimea as two federal subjects; annexation not recognised by most states
[Image: Unmarked Russian soldiers ("little green men") in Crimea, March 2014]

The annexation of Crimea by the Russian Federation took place in February–March 2014, in the aftermath of Ukraine's Euromaidan revolution. Russian forces without insignia seized strategic sites across the Crimean Peninsula, the Crimean parliament installed a pro-Russian government, and a referendum on joining Russia — held on 16 March 2014 and not recognised as legitimate by Ukraine or most of the international community — preceded a treaty of accession signed in Moscow on 18 March.

Background

Crimea had been transferred from the Russian SFSR to the Ukrainian SSR in 1954 and remained part of independent Ukraine after 1991, hosting Russia's Black Sea Fleet at Sevastopol under a leasing agreement. A majority of its population was ethnically Russian.

International response

The UN General Assembly adopted Resolution 68/262 affirming Ukraine's territorial integrity. Western states imposed sanctions on Russia, and Russia was suspended from the G8. Russia administers Crimea as the Republic of Crimea and the federal city of Sevastopol, while Ukraine and most countries continue to regard it as Ukrainian territory under occupation. The annexation preceded the war in Donbas and the full-scale Russian invasion of Ukraine in 2022.
