Manubrium

The manubrium (Latin for "handle") is the broad upper part of the sternum (breastbone). It is roughly trapezoidal in shape. The top of the manubrium has a central depression, the jugular (suprasternal) notch, flanked by the clavicular notches where the clavicles articulate.

Anatomy

The manubrium articulates below with the body of the sternum at the manubriosternal joint, a landmark known as the sternal angle (of Louis), which lies opposite the second costal cartilage and is an important clinical reference point for counting ribs and locating thoracic structures. The first costal cartilages and part of the second attach to its sides. Muscles attaching to its anterior surface include the sternocleidomastoid and pectoralis major; posteriorly lie structures of the superior mediastinum, including the great vessels and thymic remnants.

Other uses

In zoology the term is used for handle-shaped structures in various animals: the stomach-bearing stalk hanging from the bell of a jellyfish, and the central process of the springing organ (furcula) of springtails.
