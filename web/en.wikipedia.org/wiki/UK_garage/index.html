UK garage, abbreviated as UKG, is a genre of electronic dance music that emerged in London in the early to mid-1990s. It developed from the British reception of American garage house, fused with elements of jungle, contemporary R&B and dancehall. The genre is characterised by shuffling, off-beat percussion, chopped and pitch-shifted vocal samples, deep sub-bass and a syncopated rhythm — in its 2-step variant, a beat pattern that deliberately omits the kick drum on some beats. UK garage reached mainstream chart success at the turn of the 2000s before mutating into dubstep, grime and other pillars of UK dance music.

Origins

The name derives from the Paradise Garage club in New York and the soulful house sound associated with it. London DJs playing US garage imports at clubs and on pirate radio in the early 1990s began speeding the records up and blending them with the breakbeat science of the jungle scene. Producers such as Grant Nelson and the outfit Tuff Jam helped define a distinctively British sound — sometimes initially called "the Sunday Scene" after its fashionable club sessions — with swung hi-hats, bouncy basslines and cut-up diva vocals over a steady four-to-the-floor kick.

Speed garage and 2-step

Around 1996–97, speed garage brought heavier jungle-style "warp" basslines and time-stretched vocal effects; Armand Van Helden's remix of "Spin Spin Sugar" by Sneaker Pimps is often cited as a defining record. Producers then loosened the formula into 2-step: the kick pattern shed its rigid four-on-the-floor pulse, the snare moved to unusual positions, and the rhythm took on a halting, lurching swing. El-B and the Ghost crew, Steve Gurley and Zed Bias were among its architects.

Mainstream success

In 1999–2001 UK garage crossed into the pop charts. Artful Dodger's "Re-Rewind" featuring Craig David reached number 2 in the UK in December 1999; Craig David's solo career, MJ Cole's "Sincere", and hits by Mis-Teeq, So Solid Crew and Ms. Dynamite ("Dy-Na-Mi-Tee") followed. Champagne-fuelled club nights in London and Ayia Napa defined the scene's glamorous image, while pirate stations such as Deja Vu 92.3 nurtured its MC-led edge.

Legacy

As 2-step waned commercially, its darker strains split into new genres: in south London, producers stripping garage to sparse half-time rhythms created dubstep; in east London, MC crews rapping over colder, minimal beats created grime. Bassline (niche) in Sheffield and UK funky continued other branches. The 2010s brought a sustained revival — Disclosure carried garage swing to global pop audiences, and Burial's critically acclaimed work, especially Untrue (2007), reimagined 2-step as haunted, rain-soaked electronica. UK garage's rhythmic DNA — shuffled drums, sub-bass and vocal chops — now runs through much of contemporary British electronic music.
