2016 Conservative Party leadership election

The 2016 Conservative Party leadership election was held in the United Kingdom following the resignation of David Cameron as Prime Minister and leader of the Conservative Party after the 2016 referendum vote to leave the European Union. The contest ended on 11 July 2016 with the withdrawal of Andrea Leadsom, leaving Theresa May as the only remaining candidate; May became party leader forthwith and Prime Minister on 13 July 2016.

Background

On 24 June 2016, the morning after the referendum, Cameron announced he would resign by the autumn, judging that the country needed new leadership to negotiate Brexit. The election was conducted under Conservative Party rules: MPs held a series of ballots to reduce the field to two candidates, between whom the wider party membership would choose.

Candidates and parliamentary ballots

Five candidates stood: Stephen Crabb, Liam Fox, Michael Gove, Andrea Leadsom and Theresa May. Boris Johnson, widely expected to run, withdrew after Gove — his campaign manager — announced his own candidacy. In the first ballot on 5 July, May led with 165 votes; Fox (16) was eliminated and Crabb (34) withdrew, endorsing May. In the second ballot on 7 July, May won 199 votes, Leadsom 84 and Gove 46, eliminating Gove.

Withdrawal of Leadsom and result

On 11 July, following controversy over remarks she had made about motherhood and her candidacy, Leadsom withdrew, saying May had the support needed to govern immediately. May was declared leader that day, addressed the nation outside 10 Downing Street on 13 July, and formed a government committed to delivering Brexit, famously summarised in her phrase "Brexit means Brexit."
