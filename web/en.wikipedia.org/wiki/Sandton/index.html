Sandton is an affluent suburb and financial district in the northern part of Johannesburg, Gauteng, South Africa. Often called "the richest square mile in Africa", it is the country's most important business and financial hub, home to the Johannesburg Stock Exchange, Sandton City shopping centre, Nelson Mandela Square, and the headquarters of major banks, law firms, and mining companies. Its name is a portmanteau of the neighbouring suburbs Sandown and Bryanston.

History

The area was farmland and smallholdings — home to estates such as the one attached to the Zandfontein farm — until the Sandton municipality was established in 1969. Development accelerated in the 1970s and 1980s as businesses moved north from Johannesburg's central business district. After 1994 growth intensified, and Sandton displaced the old CBD as the financial heart of South Africa; the Johannesburg Stock Exchange relocated there in 2000.

Transport and landmarks

Sandton hosts the flagship station of the Gautrain rapid-rail system, linking it to OR Tambo International Airport, Pretoria, and Johannesburg Park Station. Landmarks include the 20-metre bronze statue of Nelson Mandela on Nelson Mandela Square (unveiled 2004), the Michelangelo Towers, and The Leonardo, among Africa's tallest buildings.
