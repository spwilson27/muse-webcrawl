Humectant

A humectant is a hygroscopic (water-absorbing) substance used to keep things moist. It is the opposite of a desiccant, which dries things out. Humectants are used in many products, including foods, cosmetics, medicines, and tobacco, to prevent drying and to maintain texture.

Mechanism

Humectants typically contain many hydrophilic groups, often hydroxyl (–OH) groups, which form hydrogen bonds with water molecules. By binding water, they reduce its evaporation and lower the water activity of a product, which can also inhibit microbial growth.

Examples and uses

Common humectants include glycerol (glycerin), sorbitol, propylene glycol, urea, lactic acid, and honey. In skin care, humectants draw moisture into the outer layer of the skin; in foods such as baked goods and chewing gum they preserve softness and shelf life.
