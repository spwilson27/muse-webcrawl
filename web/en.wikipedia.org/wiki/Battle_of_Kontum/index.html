Battle of Kontum

The Battle of Kontum was fought from May to June 1972 during North Vietnam's Easter Offensive, for the city of Kontum in South Vietnam's Central Highlands. South Vietnamese (ARVN) forces, heavily supported by United States airpower and advised by John Paul Vann, repelled the People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN) assault, frustrating the offensive's plan to cut South Vietnam in two.

Background

The Easter Offensive, launched on 30 March 1972, struck on three fronts. In the Highlands, PAVN forces overran ARVN outposts at Tan Canh and Dak To in late April, destroying the 22nd Division, and advanced on Kontum, whose fall would open the road to the coast at Qui Nhơn and split the country. The ARVN 23rd Division under Colonel Lý Tòng Bá took over the defence, coordinated by senior American advisor John Paul Vann, who commanded all US support in the region.

The battle

The main assault began on 14 May with massed artillery and tank-infantry attacks. ARVN troops, backed by round-the-clock B-52 strikes, tactical airpower and — for the first time in combat in Vietnam — helicopter-launched TOW anti-tank missiles that knocked out PAVN T-54 and PT-76 tanks, held the perimeter. PAVN units penetrated parts of the city in late May, but by early June their attacks had been ground down and they began to withdraw; Kontum was cleared by mid-June 1972.

Aftermath

PAVN losses ran to several thousand men and most of its committed armour. The victory at Kontum, alongside the successful defences of An Lộc and Quảng Trị, blunted the Easter Offensive, though at the cost of heavy South Vietnamese casualties and devastation. Vann was killed in a helicopter crash on 9 June, days after the battle's climax, and was posthumously awarded the Medal of Honor.
