Kraków Ghetto

Kraków Ghetto
LocationPodgórze, Kraków, German-occupied Poland

The Kraków Ghetto was one of five major metropolitan Nazi ghettos created by Nazi Germany in the General Government territory of German-occupied Poland during World War II. It was established for the purpose of exploitation, terror and persecution of local Polish Jews. The ghetto was later used as a staging area for separating the "able workers" from those to be deported to extermination camps, in Operation Reinhard in Kraków.

History

Establishment

On 3 March 1941, Hans Frank, head of the General Government, ordered the establishment of a ghetto in the Podgórze district of Kraków, south of the Vistula river. By 20 March 1941 all Jews were required to resettle there. Some 15,000 Jews were crowded into an area previously inhabited by about 3,000 people, in a district consisting of 30 streets, 320 residential buildings and 3,167 rooms. The ghetto was enclosed by walls whose tops were shaped like the rounded arches of Jewish tombstones.

Life in the ghetto

Life in the ghetto was administered by a Jewish Council (Judenrat). Among its notable residents was the pharmacist Tadeusz Pankiewicz, whose pharmacy "Under the Eagle" (Apteka Pod Orłem) was the only business permitted to remain open inside the ghetto; it became a meeting place and a centre of aid and resistance. The young filmmaker Roman Polanski survived the ghetto as a child. The ghetto also saw resistance activity by the Jewish Fighting Organisation (ŻOB).

Deportations

Large-scale deportations began in 1942. In the "June Aktion" (28 May – 8 June 1942) thousands of Jews were deported to the Bełżec extermination camp. A further deportation in October 1942 sent thousands more to their deaths.

Liquidation

The ghetto was liquidated on 13–14 March 1943 under the command of SS officer Amon Göth. Approximately 8,000 Jews deemed able to work were transferred to the Kraków-Płaszów labour camp. About 2,000 were murdered in the streets of the ghetto during the operation, and the remainder — some 3,000 people — were sent to Auschwitz.

Legacy

The liquidation of the ghetto is depicted in the film Schindler's List (1993). The main square of the ghetto, now Plac Bohaterów Getta (Ghetto Heroes Square), contains a memorial of large metal chairs commemorating the victims. Tadeusz Pankiewicz's pharmacy is now a branch of the Historical Museum of Kraków.
