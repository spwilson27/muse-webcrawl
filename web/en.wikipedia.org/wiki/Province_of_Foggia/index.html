The province of Foggia (Italian: provincia di Foggia) is a province in the Italian region of Apulia. It is the largest province of Apulia and the second largest in Italy, and is often called the Capitanata, its historical name. Its capital is the city of Foggia.

Geography

The province brings together several distinct landscapes: the Tavoliere delle Puglie, Italy's second largest plain and its great wheat-growing "granary"; the Gargano promontory, a mountainous, forested headland in the Adriatic, much of it a national park; and the Daunian Mountains (Monti Dauni) along the Apennine border with Campania and Molise. The Tremiti Islands in the Adriatic also belong to the province.

Economy and sights

Agriculture dominates: durum wheat for pasta, tomatoes, olives and wine. Coastal towns such as Vieste, Peschici and Rodi Garganico are major beach resorts. San Giovanni Rotondo draws pilgrims to the shrine of Saint Padre Pio, and Lucera preserves a great castle of Frederick II. The province historically depended on transhumant sheep farming along the tratturi (drove roads) linking Abruzzo to the Tavoliere.
