Bogie

A bogie (in North American English, a truck) is a chassis or framework that carries a wheelset, attached to a vehicle—a modular subassembly of wheels and axles. Bogies take various forms in various modes of transport. A bogie may remain normally attached (as on many railway cars and semi-trailers) or be quickly detachable (as the dolly in a road train or in railway bogie exchange); it may contain a suspension within it, or be solid; and it may be mounted on a swivel, as traditionally on a railway carriage or locomotive, in addition to being articulating and sprung.

Railway bogies

On railways, a bogie usually carries two axles (four wheels) and is pivoted under the vehicle body, allowing the vehicle to negotiate curves while the body remains relatively steady. Suspension elements—coil springs, air springs, swing hangers or elastomeric pads—are interposed between the bogie frame and the axleboxes and between the frame and the bolster to isolate vibration and control hunting oscillation at speed.

Common designs include the Commonwealth, Barber and Scheffel bogies for freight, and many passenger designs with primary and secondary suspension. Powered bogies carry traction motors in locomotives and multiple units; Jacobs bogies are shared between adjacent articulated carbodies, as on many modern trams and the TGV.

Other uses

In road transport, "bogie" describes the tandem rear axle assembly of a truck or trailer. In aviation slang the word can refer to landing gear assemblies, and in military usage to the road-wheel assemblies of tracked vehicles. Tracked vehicles such as tanks use a different arrangement and do not have bogies in the railway sense, though the term is sometimes applied to their wheel stations.
