University of Delaware

The University of Delaware (UD) is a privately governed, state-assisted land-grant research university located in Newark, Delaware. Its roots trace to Newark Academy, founded in 1743, making it one of the oldest institutions of higher education in the United States. It received its present charter in 1843 as Delaware College and became the University of Delaware in 1921 on merging with the Women's College of Delaware.

Academics

UD enrols roughly 24,000 students in eight colleges and more than 150 undergraduate majors. It is classified as an R1 research university and is notable for programmes in chemical engineering (supported by Delaware's chemical industry, including DuPont), art conservation (one of few such programmes in North America), hotel and hospitality management, and marine science through its Lewes campus. It was the first American university to offer a study-abroad programme, begun in 1923, sending students to Paris.

People and athletics

Alumni include US President Joe Biden and his wife, educator Jill Biden. The university's athletic teams are the Fightin' Blue Hens, named for the Delaware state bird, and its mascot is YoUDee.
