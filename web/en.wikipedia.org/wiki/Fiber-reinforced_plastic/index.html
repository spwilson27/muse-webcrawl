Fiber-reinforced plastic (FRP; also called fibre-reinforced polymer) is a composite material made of a polymer matrix reinforced with fibres. The fibres are usually glass (fiberglass), carbon, aramid, or basalt, while the polymer is usually an epoxy, vinyl ester, or polyester thermosetting plastic, though phenol formaldehyde resins are still in use.

Properties

FRPs combine high strength with low weight, and their properties can be tailored by choosing the fibre type, fibre orientation, and matrix. The fibres carry most of the load while the matrix holds them in position, transfers load between them, and protects them from environmental damage. Fibre orientation makes many FRPs strongly anisotropic, being much stronger along the fibre direction than across it.

Manufacturing

Common processes include hand lay-up, filament winding, pultrusion, resin transfer moulding, and compression moulding. FRP is commonly produced in forms including sheets, pipes, and custom-moulded parts.

Applications

FRPs are used in aerospace, automotive, marine, and construction industries, as well as in sporting goods, wind turbine blades, chemical storage tanks, and corrosion-resistant piping. Glass-reinforced plastic (GRP) is widely used for boat hulls and vehicle body panels; carbon-fibre-reinforced polymer (CFRP) is used where the highest strength-to-weight ratio is required.
