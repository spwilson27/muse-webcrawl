Lillehammer (Norwegian pronunciation: [ˈlɪ̂ləˌhɑmːər]) is a municipality and town in Innlandet county, Norway, situated at the northern end of Lake Mjøsa in the Gudbrandsdalen valley. It is best known internationally as the host of the 1994 Winter Olympics.

History

The name derives from Old Norse Litlihamarr, "the little rock" or "little crag". Lillehammer received market town rights in 1827 and city status in 1842, growing as a trading and later tourist centre served by steamships on Mjøsa and by the railway. Its dry, sunny inland climate made it a popular health resort, and the surrounding mountains — with the Birkebeiner ski terrain — established it as one of Norway's classic winter sports towns.

The 1994 Winter Olympics

Lillehammer hosted the XVII Olympic Winter Games from 12 to 27 February 1994, the first Winter Games held in a year different from the Summer Games. The Olympics were widely praised for their compact venues, environmental sensitivity, and festive atmosphere. Venues such as the Lysgårdsbakken ski jump and the Håkons Hall arena remain in use, and the town hosted the Winter Youth Olympic Games in 2016.

Culture and sights

The town's attractions include Maihaugen, one of Europe's largest open-air museums, with more than 200 historic buildings; the home of Nobel Prize-winning novelist Sigrid Undset at Bjerkebæk; and the Norwegian Olympic Museum. The pedestrian main street, Storgata, preserves many wooden 19th-century buildings. Lillehammer is also the setting of the Norwegian-American television series Lilyhammer.
