Gender-neutral pronoun

A gender-neutral pronoun is a pronoun that does not associate a gender with the individual who is being discussed. Some languages, such as English, do not have a third-person singular personal pronoun that is completely gender neutral in traditional usage (see the English gender-neutral pronouns they, one, and invented forms), whereas other languages, such as Finnish, Turkish, and Mandarin Chinese, use the same pronoun for all genders in the third person.

In various languages

Many languages lack grammatical gender entirely, so their pronouns are inherently neutral: Finnish hän, Turkish o, and spoken Mandarin tā each cover both "he" and "she". Languages with grammatical gender may still develop neutral usages or neologisms; in Swedish, the pronoun hen was proposed in the 1960s and gained wide currency in the 2010s, entering the Swedish Academy's dictionary in 2015.

English

English usage has long included singular they for an indefinite or unknown referent (attested since the 14th century), and its use for specific nonbinary people became common in the 21st century. Alternatives have included generic he (criticized as male-biased), he or she, alternating pronouns, and coined pronouns such as ze/hir or the Spivak pronouns. Style guides including the APA Publication Manual now endorse singular they.

Neopronouns

Since the 19th century many gender-neutral pronouns have been coined for English, from "thon" (proposed in 1858 and briefly listed in dictionaries) to modern neopronouns used in some communities. Their adoption has generally remained limited compared to singular they.
