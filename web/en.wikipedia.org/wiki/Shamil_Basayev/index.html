Shamil Basayev

Born: January 14, 1965, Dyshne-Vedeno, Chechen-Ingush ASSR, Soviet Union
Died: July 10, 2006 (aged 41), Ingushetia, Russia
Allegiance: Chechen Republic of Ichkeria; later Caucasus Emirate-aligned forces

Shamil Salmanovich Basayev (14 January 1965 – 10 July 2006) was a Chechen militant and separatist commander who became the most notorious figure of the Chechen wars against Russia. Designated a terrorist by Russia, the United States, and the United Nations, he claimed responsibility for or led a series of mass-casualty attacks on Russian civilians.

Early life and First Chechen War

Basayev was born in the village of Dyshne-Vedeno in southeastern Chechnya. He studied in Moscow and worked in business before joining the Chechen national movement in 1991, when he gained attention by hijacking an airliner to Turkey to publicize the Chechen cause. In the First Chechen War (1994–1996) he emerged as a daring field commander; after Russian forces killed much of his family, he led the June 1995 raid on Budyonnovsk, seizing a hospital and more than 1,000 hostages, an operation that forced Moscow into ceasefire talks. He subsequently directed the 1996 recapture of Grozny that preceded the Khasavyurt Accord and Russian withdrawal.

Interwar politics and Second Chechen War

Basayev ran unsuccessfully for president of Chechnya in 1997 and briefly served as prime minister in 1998. In 1999 his incursion into Dagestan helped trigger the Second Chechen War. Increasingly aligned with radical Islamist ideology, he claimed responsibility for attacks including the 2002 Moscow theatre hostage crisis (through his subordinate Movsar Barayev) and the 2004 Beslan school siege, in which more than 330 people, most of them children, were killed.

Death

Basayev was killed on July 10, 2006, in Ingushetia when a truck loaded with explosives he was accompanying detonated; Russian officials stated the explosion resulted from an FSB operation. His death removed the most prominent of the remaining separatist warlords.
