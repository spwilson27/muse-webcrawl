Linear transformation

In mathematics, a linear transformation (also called a linear map or linear operator) is a function between two vector spaces that preserves the operations of vector addition and scalar multiplication. Formally, a map T: V → W satisfies T(u + v) = T(u) + T(v) and T(cv) = cT(v) for all vectors u, v in V and all scalars c.

Properties

A linear transformation always maps the zero vector to the zero vector, and it maps lines to lines (or points): straight lines through the origin remain straight lines through the origin. The set of vectors mapped to zero is the kernel (or null space); the set of outputs is the image (or range). The rank–nullity theorem relates their dimensions: dim(domain) = rank + nullity.

Matrices

In finite dimensions, once bases are chosen for V and W, every linear transformation can be represented by a matrix, and applying the transformation is matrix multiplication; conversely every matrix defines a linear transformation. Rotations, reflections, scalings, shears and projections of the plane and of three-dimensional space are all linear transformations, as are differentiation and integration of functions and the Fourier transform. Composition of linear maps corresponds to multiplication of their matrices, and a linear transformation is invertible exactly when its matrix has a non-zero determinant.

Examples

Scaling all vectors by a constant, rotating a vector about the origin, and projecting a vector onto an axis are linear transformations of the plane; by contrast, translations (moving every point by a fixed vector) are not, since they move the origin — they are affine transformations.
