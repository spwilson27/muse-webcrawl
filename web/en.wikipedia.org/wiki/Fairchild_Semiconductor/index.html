Fairchild Semiconductor International, Inc. was an American semiconductor company based in San Jose, California. Founded in 1957, it was a pioneer in the manufacture of transistors and integrated circuits and is widely credited as one of the companies that gave rise to Silicon Valley.

History

Fairchild was founded by the "traitorous eight" — Julius Blank, Victor Grinich, Jean Hoerni, Eugene Kleiner, Jay Last, Gordon Moore, Robert Noyce, and Sheldon Roberts — who left Shockley Semiconductor Laboratory to work under Fairchild Camera and Instrument. In 1959 the company developed the planar process and produced one of the first commercially practical integrated circuits. Robert Noyce's design, based on Jean Hoerni's planar process, became foundational to modern chipmaking.

Former Fairchild employees went on to found dozens of companies, the "Fairchildren", including Intel (Noyce and Moore, 1968), AMD, and National Semiconductor's modern form. After a series of ownership changes — Schlumberger (1979), National Semiconductor (1987) — Fairchild was spun off in 1997 as an independent company focused on power and analog semiconductors. In 2016, ON Semiconductor acquired Fairchild for about $2.4 billion.
