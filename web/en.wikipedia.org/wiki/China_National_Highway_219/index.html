China National Highway 219

China National Highway 219 (G219) runs along the western border of the People's Republic of China, from Kanas in Xinjiang in the north to Dongxing in Guangxi in the south. The original highway ran from Yecheng (Kargilik) in Xinjiang to Lhatse in Tibet, a distance of about 2,342 km (1,455 mi), and formed the western section of the Xinjiang–Tibet Highway across the disputed Aksai Chin region. Under China's expanded national highway plan, the route was greatly extended to follow the country's entire western and southwestern frontier, making the new G219 one of China's longest national highways at over 10,000 km.

Route

The original Xinjiang–Tibet Highway climbs from Yecheng in the Tarim Basin onto the Kunlun mountains and across the Aksai Chin plateau at elevations exceeding 5,000 metres, passing through some of the most remote and inhospitable terrain in Asia, before descending into Tibet and ending at Lhatse, where it meets China National Highway 318. The road was completed in 1957, having been built in secret across territory claimed by India; its existence, revealed after completion, contributed to the tensions leading to the Sino-Indian War of 1962.

Extended highway

In the 2013 national trunk highway plan, G219 was extended northward to the Altai Mountains and southward along the borders with Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Pakistan, India, Nepal, Bhutan, Myanmar, Laos and Vietnam, ending at the port of Dongxing on the South China Sea. The extended route is promoted as a scenic border touring road passing Mount Kailash, Everest viewpoints and tropical sections of Yunnan and Guangxi.
