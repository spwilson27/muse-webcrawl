Fargo, North Dakota

Fargo is the most populous city in the U.S. state of North Dakota and the county seat of Cass County. It sits on the western bank of the Red River of the North, across from Moorhead, Minnesota, in the flat Red River Valley. Founded in 1871 as a Northern Pacific Railway crossing point, Fargo grew into the state's principal economic and cultural centre, home to North Dakota State University.

History

Fargo was established where the Northern Pacific Railway crossed the Red River, and was named for William G. Fargo, a director of the railroad and co-founder of Wells Fargo. The town became a jumping-off point for homesteaders claiming Bonanza farms in the fertile valley. A devastating fire in 1893 destroyed much of the downtown, which was rebuilt in brick. The city acquired an international pop-culture profile from the Coen brothers' 1996 film Fargo and the subsequent television series, though both are set mostly in Minnesota.

Geography and climate

Fargo occupies the bed of glacial Lake Agassiz, making its terrain exceptionally flat and prone to spring flooding when the Red River overflows; major floods in 1997 and 2009 prompted record flood fights and a long-debated diversion project. The climate is humid continental, with very cold winters and warm summers.

Economy and education

Agriculture, healthcare (Sanford Health is headquartered in the region), technology (a major Microsoft campus) and higher education anchor the economy. North Dakota State University, a land-grant institution, is known for its Bison football programme, which has won numerous FCS national championships, with games played at the Fargodome.
