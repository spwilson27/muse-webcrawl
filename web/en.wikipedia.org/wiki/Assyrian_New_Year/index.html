Assyrian New Year

Assyrian New Year (Kha b-Nisan)
Observed byAssyrians worldwide
Date1 April (1 Nisan)
Also calledKha b-Nisan, Resha d'Sheta ("head of the year")
CelebrationsParades, traditional dress, dancing, picnics, weddings

The Assyrian New Year, known in Assyrian Neo-Aramaic as Kha b-Nisan ("the first of Nisan") or Resha d'Sheta ("head of the year"), is the spring festival of the Assyrian people, celebrated on 1 April. It marks the beginning of spring and of the Assyrian calendar year, whose numbering — reckoned from the legendary founding of the temple of Ashur — makes celebrations in the 2020s fall in years numbered around 6770.

Origins

The festival descends from the ancient Mesopotamian Akitu new year celebrations of Babylon and Assyria, among the oldest recorded festivals in the world, which marked the sowing season and the renewal of kingship and creation myths. Elements of the spring festival survived among the Christian Assyrians of northern Mesopotamia (northern Iraq, south-eastern Turkey, north-western Iran and north-eastern Syria) across millennia of religious and political change.

Celebrations

Traditional celebrations include communal picnics and gatherings, dancing in traditional clothing (joma), and parades. In Duhok in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq, the annual parade draws tens of thousands of Assyrians in traditional dress. In the diaspora — in cities such as Chicago, Sydney, Toronto and Södertälje — parades and festivals are major expressions of Assyrian identity. The season around the new year is a traditional time for weddings, and customs include decorating homes with spring flowers and, in some areas, hanging a "beard" of sprouted wheat or grass (daqna d'Nisan) over doorways for fertility and good fortune.
