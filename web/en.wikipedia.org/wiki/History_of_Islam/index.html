History of Islam

The history of Islam concerns the political, social, economic and cultural developments of the Islamic world, beginning with the revelations received by the prophet Muhammad in Arabia in the early 7th century CE. Within a century of Muhammad's death in 632, Muslim rule extended from Iberia to Central Asia; Islam subsequently spread through trade, scholarship and empire to Africa, South and Southeast Asia, becoming one of the world's largest religions.

Muhammad and the Rashidun Caliphate

Muhammad united the tribes of Arabia under Islam before his death in 632. His successors, the Rashidun ("rightly guided") caliphs Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and Ali, conquered Syria, Egypt, Mesopotamia and Persia. The assassination of Uthman and the ensuing civil war produced the enduring division between Sunni and Shia Islam, the latter holding that leadership belonged to Ali and his descendants.

Umayyad and Abbasid caliphates

The Umayyad Caliphate (661–750), ruling from Damascus, extended the empire to Spain and the Indus, built the Dome of the Rock in Jerusalem, and made Arabic the language of administration. The Abbasid Caliphate (750–1258), centred on Baghdad, presided over an Islamic Golden Age of science, medicine, mathematics, philosophy and translation, even as effective power fragmented among regional dynasties such as the Fatimids in Egypt and the Umayyads of Córdoba. The Mongol sack of Baghdad in 1258 ended the Abbasid caliphate in Iraq, though a shadow caliphate continued under the Mamluks of Egypt.

Later empires

From the 16th century the Islamic world was dominated by three great empires: the Ottoman Empire in the Middle East, Balkans and North Africa; Safavid Persia, which established Twelver Shi'ism as its state religion; and Mughal India. Islam also spread along Saharan and Indian Ocean trade routes into West Africa, the Swahili coast and maritime Southeast Asia.

Colonial era and the modern world

During the 19th and 20th centuries most Muslim-majority lands came under European colonial rule, prompting reform movements ranging from modernist reinterpretation to revivalist and Islamist movements. The Ottoman Empire was dissolved after the First World War and the caliphate abolished in 1924. Post-colonial nation-states, the creation of Israel in 1948, the Iranian Revolution of 1979, and conflicts in Afghanistan, Iraq and Syria have shaped modern Muslim politics. Today Islam has roughly two billion adherents worldwide.
