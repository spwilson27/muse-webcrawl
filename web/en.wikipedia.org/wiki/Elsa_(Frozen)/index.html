Elsa is a fictional character who appears in Walt Disney Animation Studios' Frozen franchise, principally voiced by Broadway star Idina Menzel. Created by directors Chris Buck and Jennifer Lee, Elsa is the Queen of Arendelle, born with magical powers of ice and snow, and the elder sister of Princess Anna. She first appeared in the animated film Frozen (2013) and returned in the sequel Frozen II (2019).

Development

Elsa is loosely based on the title character of Hans Christian Andersen's fairy tale "The Snow Queen" (1844). In early drafts she was the film's villain, as in Andersen's tale, but the production team reconceived her as a misunderstood heroine after writing the song "Let It Go", which reframed her flight from Arendelle as an anthem of self-acceptance. Her design, with platinum-blonde hair and a sparkling ice-blue gown created during that sequence, became one of Disney's most recognisable character looks.

Role in the films

In Frozen, Elsa's powers are accidentally revealed at her coronation, and she flees into the mountains, inadvertently plunging Arendelle into eternal winter. Her sister Anna's act of true love breaks the spell, and Elsa learns that love, not fear, controls her gift. In Frozen II, Elsa is drawn by a mysterious voice to the Enchanted Forest and the glacier Ahtohallan, where she discovers she is the "fifth spirit" bridging humans and the elements; she abdicates Arendelle's throne to Anna and remains as guardian of the forest.

Reception and impact

Elsa has been widely praised as a departure from traditional Disney princesses: she has no love interest, and the story's emotional core is the sisters' relationship. "Let It Go" won the Academy Award for Best Original Song, and the character became a cultural phenomenon, frequently read as an allegory for difference, repression, and self-liberation. Elsa also appears in the short films Frozen Fever and Olaf's Frozen Adventure, the Broadway musical adaptation, and Disney theme-park attractions.
