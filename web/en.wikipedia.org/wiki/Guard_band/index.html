Guard band

Field: Telecommunications

Purpose: Prevent interference between adjacent channels

A guard band is a narrow part of the radio spectrum left unused between adjacent allocated channels or bands. It acts as a buffer so that transmissions in neighbouring channels do not interfere with each other through imperfect filtering, frequency drift or out-of-band emissions.

Use

Guard bands appear in frequency-division multiplexing systems, television and radio channel plans, and mobile telephony allocations, where regulators leave small gaps between licensees' blocks. In digital systems with well-controlled waveforms the required guard bands can be narrower than in analogue systems, freeing more spectrum for use, though some separation is generally retained, for example between 4G/5G carriers and adjacent services such as radio altimeters or satellite bands.
