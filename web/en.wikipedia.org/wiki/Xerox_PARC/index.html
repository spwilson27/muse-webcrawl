Xerox PARC

Xerox PARC (Palo Alto Research Center, formerly Xerox Palo Alto Research Center) is a research and development company in Palo Alto, California, founded in 1970 by Xerox. It is famous for inventing many elements of modern computing, including the graphical user interface (GUI), the computer mouse in its popularised form, Ethernet, laser printing, and object-oriented programming.

History

Xerox founded PARC in 1970, deliberately placing it 3,000 miles from company headquarters in Rochester, New York, to give researchers freedom from corporate bureaucracy. Led initially by George Pake and by computer scientist Bob Taylor in its computing laboratory, PARC attracted many of the leading researchers of the day, including Alan Kay, Butler Lampson, Charles Thacker and Charles Simonyi.

Inventions

In 1973 PARC completed the Alto, one of the first personal computers: it had a bitmapped display, a mouse, removable storage, and networking. Building on Douglas Engelbart's earlier work and Alan Kay's Dynabook concept, researchers developed the first graphical user interface with windows, icons and menus, operated through the Smalltalk programming environment. Other PARC inventions include Ethernet (developed by Robert Metcalfe), the laser printer (Gary Starkweather), WYSIWYG text editing (Bravo, by Lampson and Simonyi), and the Interpress page-description language, a forerunner of PostScript.

Commercialisation and legacy

Xerox was famously slow to commercialise PARC's inventions. Its own Xerox Star (1981) introduced the GUI commercially but at high cost. Visits by Steve Jobs in 1979 exposed Apple's engineers to the Alto, directly influencing the Lisa and Macintosh. Metcalfe left to found 3Com; Simonyi took GUI word-processing ideas to Microsoft. In 2002 Xerox spun PARC out as a wholly owned subsidiary, and in 2023 donated it to SRI International.

Despite Xerox's failure to dominate the industries PARC created, the centre is regarded as one of the most fertile research organisations in history; the modern personal computer is in large part a PARC design.
