Kassel (German pronunciation: [ˈkasl̩]; spelled Cassel until 1926) is a city on the Fulda River in northern Hesse, in central Germany. It is the administrative seat of the Regierungsbezirk Kassel and the district of the same name, with a population of about 200,000. Kassel is internationally renowned as the host city of documenta, the major exhibition of contemporary art held every five years since 1955.

History

Kassel was first mentioned in documents in 913. It became the residence of the Landgraves of Hesse and later of the Electors of Hesse-Kassel, whose court shaped the city with palaces, gardens, and collections. In the 18th century Landgrave Charles and his successors built the vast baroque water features of the Wilhelmshöhe park. During World War II, Kassel, an armaments center, was devastated by Allied bombing, most catastrophically in the firestorm raid of 22–23 October 1943, which killed around 10,000 people and destroyed most of the old town. The city was rebuilt largely in 1950s modernist style.

Sights

The Bergpark Wilhelmshöhe, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2013, features the monumental Hercules statue, water cascades, the Löwenburg castle, and Wilhelmshöhe Palace with its Old Masters gallery, which includes works by Rembrandt. In the city center lie the Fridericianum — one of the oldest public museums in Europe and a principal documenta venue — the Karlsaue park with its orangery, and the Ottoneum natural history museum.

Culture

Every five years documenta transforms Kassel into a center of the international art world for 100 days. The Brothers Grimm lived and worked in Kassel for decades, collecting fairy tales there; the Grimmwelt museum is devoted to their legacy. The University of Kassel, founded in 1971, enrolls around 25,000 students.
