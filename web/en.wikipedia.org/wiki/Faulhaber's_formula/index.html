Faulhaber's formula

In mathematics, Faulhaber's formula, named after the German mathematician Johann Faulhaber, expresses the sum of the p-th powers of the first n positive integers as a polynomial in n of degree p+1, whose coefficients involve the Bernoulli numbers. Famously, the sum of the first n integers is n(n+1)/2, the sum of squares is n(n+1)(2n+1)/6, and the sum of cubes equals the square of the sum of the first n integers.

History

Faulhaber published formulas for power sums up to the 17th power in his Academia Algebrae (1631), without giving the general form. The general formula was discovered by Jacob Bernoulli, who recognised the sequence of constants now called Bernoulli numbers; it was also found independently by Seki Takakazu in Japan.

Statement

The sum 1p + 2p + … + np equals 1/(p+1) times a sum over j from 0 to p of binomial(p+1, j) · Bj · np+1−j, where Bj are the Bernoulli numbers.
