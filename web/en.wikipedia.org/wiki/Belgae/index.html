Belgae
RegionNorthern Gaul (modern Belgium, northern France, Luxembourg, Netherlands south of the Rhine)
Era3rd century BC – Roman conquest, 1st century BC
Major tribesNervii, Remi, Bellovaci, Suessiones, Atrebates, Menapii, Eburones

The Belgae were a confederation of tribes living in northern Gaul, between the English Channel, the west bank of the Rhine, and the northern bank of the Seine, from at least the 3rd century BC. They were conquered by Julius Caesar during the Gallic Wars in 57–53 BC and organised under Augustus into the Roman province of Gallia Belgica.

Origins and language

Caesar, in his Commentarii de Bello Gallico, describes the Belgae as distinct from the Celtic Gauls, of partly Germanic origin from east of the Rhine. Their languages remain debated: many Belgic tribal and personal names appear Celtic, and scholars have proposed a distinct "Nordwestblock" language. Of all the Gauls, Caesar famously wrote, the Belgae were the bravest (horum omnium fortissimi sunt Belgae).

Roman conquest and legacy

The Belgic tribes mounted fierce resistance, notably the Nervii at the Battle of the Sabis in 57 BC and the Eburones under Ambiorix, who destroyed a Roman legion in 54 BC before being annihilated in reprisal. Related Belgic groups also settled in southern Britain. The modern country of Belgium takes its name from the Belgae.
