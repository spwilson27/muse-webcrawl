Cool jazz

Cool jazz
Stylistic originsBebop, swing, classical music
Cultural originsLate 1940s, United States (New York; later California)
Key artistsMiles Davis, Dave Brubeck, Chet Baker, Gerry Mulligan, Modern Jazz Quartet

Cool jazz is a style of modern jazz that emerged in the United States in the late 1940s and 1950s, characterised by relaxed tempos, lighter tones, smoother textures, and a more formal, arrangement-oriented approach than the frenetic style of bebop. The recordings made by Miles Davis's nonet in 1949–50, later compiled as Birth of the Cool (1957), gave the style its name.

Cool jazz favoured muted tones, arranged ensembles, and a relaxed feel.

Characteristics

Cool jazz players tended to play without vibrato, at soft dynamics, with emphasis on composition and counterpoint — partly under the influence of classical music. Instruments unusual in jazz, such as French horn, tuba, flute, and cello, appeared in ensembles like Davis's nonet and the Modern Jazz Quartet. Pianist Lennie Tristano and saxophonist Lee Konitz developed a related linear, "cool" approach on the East Coast, while the style became especially associated with the West Coast scene in California (West Coast jazz) through figures such as Gerry Mulligan, Chet Baker, Dave Brubeck, Paul Desmond, and Stan Getz.

History

The style partly reacted against bebop's intensity and drew on the smoother playing of earlier musicians such as Lester Young and the Claude Thornhill Orchestra, whose arranger Gil Evans helped shape the Davis nonet sessions. Cool recordings sold well to college audiences in the 1950s; Brubeck's Time Out (1959), with "Take Five", became one of the best-selling jazz albums of all time. Cool jazz influenced bossa nova — Getz's collaborations with Charlie Byrd and João Gilberto carried its sensibility into the 1960s.

Criticism

Some critics associated "cool" with a marketing-driven whitening of jazz and contrasted it unfavourably with hard bop's return to blues and gospel roots; others note that many of its originators, including Davis himself, were Black innovators also central to other styles.
