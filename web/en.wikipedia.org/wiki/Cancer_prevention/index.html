Cancer prevention

Cancer prevention is the practice of taking active measures to decrease the incidence of cancer and its mortality. The World Health Organization estimates that between 30% and 50% of all cancer cases are preventable through avoidance of risk factors and implementation of evidence-based prevention strategies, including vaccination, screening, and lifestyle changes.

Primary prevention

Primary prevention aims to stop cancer from developing. Tobacco use is the single largest preventable cause of cancer, responsible for roughly a fifth of cancer deaths worldwide, and smoking cessation markedly reduces the risk of lung and many other cancers. Other major measures include limiting alcohol consumption, maintaining a healthy weight and physical activity, avoiding excessive ultraviolet exposure, and controlling occupational and environmental exposures such as asbestos and air pollution.

Infections cause an estimated 13% of cancers globally. Vaccination against human papillomavirus (HPV) prevents cervical and several other cancers, and hepatitis B vaccination prevents hepatocellular carcinoma. Treatment of Helicobacter pylori and hepatitis C likewise reduces gastric and liver cancer risk.

Secondary prevention (screening)

Screening detects precancerous lesions or early cancers in asymptomatic people. Established programs include cervical cytology and HPV testing, mammography for breast cancer, fecal testing and colonoscopy for colorectal cancer, and low-dose CT for heavy smokers at high risk of lung cancer. Screening involves trade-offs, including overdiagnosis and false positives, and recommended tests vary by age, sex, and risk profile.

Chemoprevention and risk-reducing surgery

Certain medications reduce risk in selected populations: tamoxifen and aromatase inhibitors for women at high risk of breast cancer, and aspirin, which is associated with reduced colorectal cancer incidence. Individuals with strong hereditary syndromes, such as BRCA1/BRCA2 mutations or Lynch syndrome, may choose intensified surveillance or risk-reducing surgery.
