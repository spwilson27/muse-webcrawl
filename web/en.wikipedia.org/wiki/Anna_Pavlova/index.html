Anna Pavlova

Anna Pavlovna Pavlova (12 February [O.S. 31 January] 1881 – 23 January 1931) was a Russian prima ballerina of the late 19th and early 20th centuries. She was a principal artist of the Imperial Russian Ballet and of Sergei Diaghilev's Ballets Russes, and is most recognised for her creation of the role of The Dying Swan. With her own company she became the first ballerina to tour ballet around the world.

Career

Born prematurely to a laundress mother, Pavlova was inspired at age eight by a performance of The Sleeping Beauty and entered the Imperial Ballet School in 1891. Despite a slight physique unsuited to the academic ideal, she rose quickly at the Mariinsky Theatre. In 1905 Mikhail Fokine choreographed The Dying Swan for her to Saint-Saëns' music; the brief solo became her signature. After seasons with the Ballets Russes she formed her own company in London and spent two decades touring across Europe, the Americas, Asia, Australia and South Africa, giving thousands of performances and popularising ballet worldwide.

Death and legacy

Pavlova died of pleurisy in The Hague in January 1931, days before her fiftieth birthday, while on tour. Her ashes are held at Golders Green Crematorium in London. The dessert pavlova, popular in Australia and New Zealand, is named in her honour, and her London home, Ivy House, later became a cultural centre.
