Laurentius Petri (Swedish: Lars Petersson; 1499 – 26 October 1573) was a Swedish clergyman and the first Evangelical Lutheran Archbishop of Sweden. Appointed in 1531 under King Gustav Vasa, he held the see of Uppsala for 42 years — the longest tenure of any Swedish archbishop — and, with his elder brother Olaus Petri, was the chief architect of the Swedish Reformation.

Life

Laurentius was born in Örebro, the son of a blacksmith. He studied in Germany, including at Wittenberg, where he absorbed the teachings of Martin Luther and Philipp Melanchthon. Returning to Sweden, he was elected archbishop in 1531 and consecrated by bishops in apostolic succession — a continuity the Swedish church prized — though without papal approval, which Rome regarded as void.

Work

As archbishop, Laurentius steered a moderate course between the king's fiscal and political demands and the preservation of much of the church's older order: Sweden retained bishops, vestments, and much traditional ceremony. He worked with his brother on the Swedish Bible translation of 1541 (the Gustav Vasa Bible). His most lasting achievement was the Swedish Church Ordinance of 1571, the first complete Lutheran church order, which regulated worship, clergy discipline, education, and poor relief and remained foundational for the Church of Sweden for centuries. He also resisted, late in life, the Calvinist leanings of King Erik XIV and began the defence of Swedish Lutheran identity that his successors continued against Johan III's liturgical innovations.

Legacy

Laurentius Petri is remembered as the consolidator of the Swedish national church. His church ordinance, catechisms, and hymnody shaped Swedish religious life, and the Church of Sweden's claim to unbroken episcopal succession runs through his consecration.
