Filter feeder

Filter feeders are animals that feed by straining suspended matter and food particles from water, typically by passing the water over a specialized filtering structure. Some animals that use this method of feeding are clams, krill, sponges, baleen whales, and many fish (including some sharks). Some birds, such as flamingos and certain species of duck, are also filter feeders.

Mechanisms

Filter feeders employ various mechanisms: bivalves draw water over gills coated in mucus that traps plankton; sponges filter water through pores using flagellated choanocyte cells; baleen whales engulf huge mouthfuls of water and expel it through keratinous baleen plates, trapping krill; whale sharks and basking sharks strain water through gill rakers. Many filter feeders are sessile and rely on currents, while others actively swim through patches of prey.

Ecological importance

Filter feeders play a major role in clarifying water and linking planktonic production to higher trophic levels. Oyster reefs, mussel beds, and krill populations are foundational to many marine food webs, and the decline of filter-feeding bivalves has measurably reduced water quality in estuaries such as the Chesapeake Bay.
