Martin Johnson Heade (August 11, 1819 – September 4, 1904) was an American painter known for his salt marsh landscapes, seascapes, and depictions of tropical birds such as hummingbirds, as well as lotus blossoms and other still lifes. His painting style and subject matter, while derived from the romanticism of his time, were regarded by art historians as a significant departure from those of his peers.

Heade's salt marsh scenes, painted under dramatic skies, are among his most celebrated works.

Biography

Heade was born in Lumberville, Pennsylvania, the son of a farmer. He studied art first with the folk artist Edward Hicks and received further training in Rome and London during a trip to Europe in 1838. He exhibited for the first time at the Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts in 1841 and moved between numerous American cities as an itinerant portrait painter before gradually turning to landscape.

In the 1860s Heade became fascinated with the salt marshes of New England, particularly those around Newburyport, Massachusetts. His marsh paintings, built around low horizons, stacks of salt hay, and luminous skies, are considered masterworks of Luminism for their calm surfaces and clear, glowing light.

Tropical subjects

Heade traveled to Brazil in 1863 intending to produce a lavishly illustrated book of hummingbirds, to be called "The Gems of Brazil." The publishing project failed, but Heade produced some forty small paintings of hummingbirds in lush tropical settings. He later journeyed to Nicaragua, Colombia, Jamaica, and Panama, and the hummingbird pictures remained among his most popular works. In his later years he painted magnolias and other lush flowers, often arranged on velvet cloth.

Later life and legacy

In 1883 Heade settled in St. Augustine, Florida, where he married and remained for the rest of his life. He sold few works late in his career and was largely forgotten after his death in 1904. Scholars rediscovered his paintings in the 1940s, and he is now ranked among the most distinctive American painters of the nineteenth century.
