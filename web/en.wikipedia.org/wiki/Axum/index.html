Axum (also Aksum, Tigrinya: ኣኽሱም) is a town and separate woreda in the Tigray Region of northern Ethiopia. It was the capital of the Kingdom of Aksum, a major ancient trading empire, and is one of the holiest cities in Ethiopian Orthodox Christianity.

History

The Aksumite Empire flourished from about the 1st to 7th centuries CE, trading with Rome, Persia and India, minting its own coins, and adopting Christianity in the 4th century under King Ezana — among the earliest states to do so. Axum is famed for its giant carved granite stelae (obelisks); the largest, taken to Rome by Italian forces in 1937, was returned in 2005.

Religious significance

The Church of Our Lady Mary of Zion is claimed to house the Ark of the Covenant. Axum is a major pilgrimage site, especially for the Timkat and Mary of Zion festivals.
