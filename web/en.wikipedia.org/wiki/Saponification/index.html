Saponification

Saponification of a triglyceride with sodium hydroxide, producing glycerol and soap.

Saponification is a process of converting esters into soaps and alcohols by the action of aqueous alkali, usually sodium hydroxide or potassium hydroxide. Soaps are salts of fatty acids, while the alcohol produced is typically glycerol. The reaction is especially important in the manufacture of soap from fats and oils, in which a triglyceride reacts with an alkali to give glycerol and three molecules of soap.

In general, saponification involves the hydrolysis of an ester under basic conditions. When the ester is treated with a strong base, the carboxylic acid product is deprotonated to the carboxylate salt, which makes the reaction effectively irreversible and drives it to completion, in contrast to acid-catalysed hydrolysis, which is reversible.

Mechanism

The mechanism of base-promoted ester hydrolysis proceeds by nucleophilic acyl substitution. A hydroxide ion attacks the carbonyl carbon of the ester, forming a tetrahedral intermediate, which then collapses, expelling an alkoxide leaving group. The resulting carboxylic acid is immediately deprotonated by the alkoxide or by hydroxide, giving the carboxylate product and the alcohol. Because the final acid–base step consumes the base, saponification requires a stoichiometric amount of alkali.

Saponification value

The saponification value (or saponification number) is the amount of alkali, expressed in milligrams of potassium hydroxide, required to saponify one gram of a fat or oil. It is inversely related to the average molecular weight of the fatty acids in the sample and is used in the soap and food industries as an analytical measure of the type of fat present.

Applications

Traditional hard soaps are made with sodium hydroxide, while potassium hydroxide produces softer or liquid soaps. Saponification is also relevant in the conservation of paintings, where lead-based pigments can react with fatty acids in the paint medium to form lead soaps, and in the formation of adipocere, the waxy substance produced when body fat is saponified under moist, anaerobic conditions.
