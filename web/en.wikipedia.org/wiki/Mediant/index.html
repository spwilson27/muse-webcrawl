Mediant

In music, the mediant is the third scale degree (3̂) of a diatonic scale, being the note midway between the tonic and the dominant. In the movable do solfège system, the mediant note is sung as mi. While the fifth scale degree is almost always a perfect fifth, the mediant can be a major or minor third.

Schenkerian analysts consider the mediant as the defining note of the scale: "the [diatonic] scale is shown to be a prolongation of the mediant triad". The mediant chord is the triad built on the third degree: in major keys it is a minor triad (iii), and in minor keys it is usually a major triad (III) when the tonic is natural minor.

Use

The mediant chord has a weak function in tonal harmony and is often used as a substitute for or prolongation of the tonic. Deceptive cadences frequently resolve to the submediant or mediant chord. In popular music, chord progressions making extensive use of the mediant are common in styles that avoid strong dominant-tonic closure.

The mediant also plays a role in mediant relationships between keys: third-related key changes (for example, from C major to E major or A-flat major) are sometimes called "chromatic mediants" and are common in Romantic music and film scores.
