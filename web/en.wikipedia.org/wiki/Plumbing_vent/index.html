Plumbing vent

A plumbing vent, also known as a vent stack or soil vent pipe, is a pipe used to remove sewer gases from a building and to regulate air pressure within the pipes, preventing the creation of a vacuum and the siphoning of water from traps. It is a vertical component of a building's drain-waste-vent system.

Function

Every plumbing fixture in a building is connected to a trap, a curved section of pipe that retains a small amount of water and prevents sewer gases from entering the living space. Without proper venting, the negative pressure created when water drains could siphon this water out of the trap, breaking the seal. Vent pipes extend upward, typically through the roof, allowing fresh air into the drainage system and harmful gases — including methane, hydrogen sulfide, and carbon monoxide — to escape safely above the roofline.

Types

Common venting configurations include:

  • Main vent stack — the principal vertical vent continuing from the main soil stack.
  • Individual (branch) vents — vents serving a single fixture.
  • Wet venting — a pipe that serves as both a drain for one fixture and a vent for another, permitted under certain codes when properly sized.
  • Air admittance valves (AAVs) — mechanical devices that admit air into the system but do not let gases out; used where a roof penetration is impractical.
  • Studor vents — a common trade name for air admittance valves in some regions.

Regulation

Building codes, such as the International Plumbing Code and the Uniform Plumbing Code in the United States, specify minimum vent pipe diameters, maximum distances between fixtures and their vents, and requirements that vent terminals extend a minimum height above the roof surface and be located away from windows and air intakes.
