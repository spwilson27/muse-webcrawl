Seat belt

Type: Vehicle safety device

Inventor of three-point belt: Nils Bohlin (Volvo, 1959)

A seat belt (also seatbelt or safety belt) is a vehicle safety device designed to secure the driver and passengers against harmful movement that may result during a traffic collision or a sudden stop. It reduces the risk of death among front-seat occupants by roughly half.

Design

The modern three-point belt, invented by Nils Bohlin for Volvo in 1959 and shared patent-free, combines lap and shoulder straps with a retractor, buckle, and load-limiting and pretensioner systems that manage crash forces across the pelvis and rib cage. Two-point lap belts, four- and five-point harnesses (racing, child seats) and inflatable belts are variants.

History and law

Belts appeared in 19th-century carriages and early aircraft; cars offered them optionally in the 1950s. Victoria, Australia passed the first compulsory wearing law in 1970, followed by most countries; usage rates and enforcement vary, and primary-enforcement laws and reminders raise wearing substantially.

Effectiveness

Belts prevent ejection, spread deceleration forces, and keep occupants positioned for airbags. Non-use remains a major factor in road deaths worldwide.
