Chimayo

Country: United States

State: New Mexico

County: Rio Arriba

Known for: El Santuario de Chimayó; Chimayó chiles and weaving

Chimayo is a census-designated place in Rio Arriba County, New Mexico, United States. It is a small Hispano community in the foothills of the Sangre de Cristo Mountains, about 25 miles (40 km) north of Santa Fe. The name is generally derived from a Tewa word, Tsi Mayoh, referring to a local hill.

El Santuario de Chimayó

Chimayo is best known for El Santuario de Chimayó, an adobe Roman Catholic church built in the early 19th century by Bernardo de la Encarnación Abeyta. The shrine is a major pilgrimage site: a room adjoining the chapel contains a pit of earth (el pocito) believed by many faithful to have healing powers. During Holy Week, tens of thousands of pilgrims walk to the santuario, some from as far away as Albuquerque.

Chile and weaving

The Chimayó chile, a landrace pepper grown in the valley, is prized for its flavour and is an ingredient in New Mexican cuisine; it carries an official designation as the "Chimayo Chile" among growers of the region. Chimayo also has a long tradition of Hispanic weaving, exemplified by the Ortega and Trujillo families, whose blankets and textiles continue a craft practised in the area for generations.

History

The settlement grew from Spanish colonial land grants in the Santa Cruz valley. The Abeyta family's chapel, completed around 1816, became the centre of devotion after a crucifix — the image of Our Lord of Esquipulas — was associated with the site and with miraculous earth found nearby.
