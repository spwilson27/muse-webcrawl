Nectanebo I (Egyptian: Nḫt-nb.f, "strong is his lord") was an ancient Egyptian pharaoh, the founder and first king of the Thirtieth Dynasty, the last native dynasty of Egypt. He reigned from 380 to 362 BC.

Reign

An army general from Sebennytos, Nectanebo came to power by deposing and probably murdering the last pharaoh of the 29th Dynasty, Nepherites II. His reign was dominated by the threat of a renewed Persian invasion, since Egypt had thrown off Achaemenid rule in 404 BC. In 373 BC a Persian army under Pharnabazus, with the Greek commander Iphicrates, invaded Egypt but failed to take Memphis and withdrew after disputes between its commanders and the arrival of the Nile inundation.

Building programme

Nectanebo was a prolific builder and restorer of temples throughout Egypt, inaugurating a final flowering of pharaonic art. Projects included work at Philae (the earliest part of the Isis temple), the temple of Hibis in Kharga Oasis, Bubastis, and the avenue of sphinxes between Luxor and Karnak. His son Teos succeeded him after serving as co-regent.
