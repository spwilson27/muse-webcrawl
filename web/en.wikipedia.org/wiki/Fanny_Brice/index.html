Fanny Brice

Fania Borach (October 29, 1891 – May 29, 1951), known professionally as Fanny Brice or Fannie Brice, was an American comedian, singer, and actress who made many stage, radio, and film appearances. She is best known as the creator and star of the top-rated radio comedy series The Baby Snooks Show.

BornFania Borach, October 29, 1891, New York City
DiedMay 29, 1951, Los Angeles, California
OccupationComedian, singer, actress

Career

Brice rose to fame in burlesque and vaudeville before headlining the Ziegfeld Follies from 1910 into the 1930s, introducing songs such as "My Man" and "Second Hand Rose". Despite her comic, dialect-driven stage persona, she was also a respected dramatic singer. Her radio character Baby Snooks, a mischievous child, became a fixture of American broadcasting in the 1930s and 1940s.

Legacy

Her life story inspired the Broadway musical Funny Girl (1964) and its 1968 film adaptation, in which Barbra Streisand recreated the role and won an Academy Award.
