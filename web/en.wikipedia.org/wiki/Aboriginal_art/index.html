Aboriginal art

Aboriginal art is art made by Indigenous peoples of Australia, encompassing rock art tens of thousands of years old, bark painting, body painting, carving, weaving and the contemporary acrylic painting movement.

Traditional forms

Rock engravings and paintings — such as Gwion Gwion (Bradshaw) figures in the Kimberley and sites in Kakadu and the Sydney basin — record Dreaming (Tjukurrpa) stories, ancestral beings, animals and contact history. Motifs encode law, country, water sources and journeys, and many are restricted or sacred.

Contemporary movement

In 1971 at Papunya, teacher Geoffrey Bardon encouraged men painting traditional designs in acrylics, launching the Western Desert art movement. Artists such as Clifford Possum Tjapaltjarri and Emily Kame Kngwarreye gained international acclaim. Dot painting, cross-hatching (rarrk) in Arnhem Land bark art, and urban Aboriginal art address identity and politics.

Protection

Issues include authenticity, the Indigenous Art Code, resale royalties and protection of Indigenous Cultural and Intellectual Property.
