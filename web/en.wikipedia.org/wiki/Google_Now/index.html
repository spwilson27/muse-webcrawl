Google Now

DeveloperGoogle
Initial releaseJuly 9, 2012
TypeIntelligent personal assistant
PlatformAndroid, iOS
SuccessorGoogle Assistant

Google Now was a feature of Google Search of the Google app for Android and iOS, and of the Chrome browser. Google Now proactively delivered information to users in the form of "cards" that it predicted they might want, based on search habits, location, calendar entries, email, and other signals.

Google Now was first launched in July 2012 on Android 4.1 "Jelly Bean". It was one of Popular Science's "Innovations of the Year" for 2012. It proactively displayed information about weather, traffic, sports scores, flights, package deliveries, and nearby events and photo spots.

History

First included in Android 4.1, Google Now was released for iOS in April 2013 as part of the Google Search app. In 2016 Google began replacing Now functionality with the Google Assistant, and the "Now" branding was retired; the card feed continued in various forms as Google Discover.

Functionality

Google Now used a natural language user interface to answer questions, make recommendations, and perform actions by delegating requests to a set of web services. It could parse information from Gmail messages — such as flight itineraries and hotel reservations — and present it at the time it was needed. Developers could integrate third-party app cards beginning in 2015.
