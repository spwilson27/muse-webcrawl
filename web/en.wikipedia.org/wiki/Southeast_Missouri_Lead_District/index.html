Southeast Missouri Lead District

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Southeast Missouri Lead District, also known as the Missouri Lead Belt, is a major lead-mining region in south-eastern Missouri, United States. It has been one of the world's most productive lead districts, yielding lead — together with zinc, copper, silver and cobalt — from Mississippi Valley-type deposits hosted in Cambrian sedimentary rocks.

Geology

Ore occurs mainly in the Bonneterre Dolomite around the flanks of the Precambrian St. Francois Mountains. Mineralisation is of Mississippi Valley-type, precipitated from warm basinal brines, with galena as the principal ore mineral.

History

French colonists mined surface lead near Mine La Motte in the early eighteenth century, and the Old Lead Belt around Park Hills, Desloge and Bonne Terre was worked intensively in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries. Deeper deposits of the Viburnum Trend, discovered in 1955 beneath several hundred metres of cover, revitalised the district and remain in production under operators such as the Doe Run Company.

Environmental legacy

Historic mining left extensive chat piles and contamination, and several areas of the district are designated Superfund sites, notably in Herculaneum and the Big River corridor.
