Our Lord of Esquipulas

Our Lord of Esquipulas (Spanish: Nuestro Señor de Esquipulas), also known as the Black Christ of Esquipulas (Cristo Negro de Esquipulas), is a wooden crucifix housed in the Cathedral of Esquipulas in Guatemala. It is venerated throughout Guatemala and Central America.

The image was carved in 1594 by the sculptor Quirio Cataño from dark balsam wood, which over centuries of candle smoke darkened further, giving rise to the name Black Christ. In 1737 Archbishop Pedro Pardo de Figueroa, who credited the image with his recovery from illness, commissioned the great basilica that now houses it.

Each year on 15 January hundreds of thousands of pilgrims visit Esquipulas for the feast of the Black Christ, making it one of the most important Catholic pilgrimage sites in Latin America. Pope John Paul II visited in 1996.
