Tour Montparnasse, formerly Tour Maine-Montparnasse, is a 210-metre (689 ft) office skyscraper in the Montparnasse district of Paris, France, built between 1969 and 1973 above the Paris Montparnasse railway station. For decades it was the tallest building in France and remains the only skyscraper within the historic core of Paris proper, outside the La Défense business district.

History

The tower formed the centrepiece of a controversial redevelopment of the Montparnasse station area in the 1960s. Its stark, monolithic black form provoked intense public criticism for clashing with the low-rise Parisian cityscape; the backlash contributed to a ban on buildings over seven storeys in central Paris imposed in 1977, just four years after the tower's completion. A well-known Parisian quip holds that the tower's viewing platform offers the best view of Paris because it is the only place from which the tower itself cannot be seen.

Observation deck

The 56th floor houses an observatory and terrace with panoramic views over Paris, reached by one of the fastest lifts in Europe when installed. The tower contains about 120,000 m² of office space across 59 floors and receives well over a million visitors annually.

Renovation

A major renovation project, designed under the "Demain Montparnasse" competition won by Nouvelle AOM in 2017, aims to lighten the façade with glass, add greenery including a large greenhouse level, and improve the building's environmental performance; works were under way in the 2020s, with the tower's appearance set to change from dark cladding to a lighter, more transparent envelope.

Climbs and incidents

The tower has been climbed unauthorised by "urban climbers" including Alain Robert, and in 1995 by the French philosopher and mountaineer Catherine Destivelle without publicity. In 2015, Polish daredevil Marcin Banot also performed a notable ascent.
