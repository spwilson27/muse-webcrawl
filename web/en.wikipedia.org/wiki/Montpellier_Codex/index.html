Montpellier Codex

The Montpellier Codex (Montpellier, Bibliothèque Inter-Universitaire, Section Médecine, H196) is the largest and most important surviving collection of 13th-century French polyphonic music. Compiled around 1300, probably in Paris, it contains 336 polyphonic works — chiefly motets, along with organa, conductus, and hoquetus pieces — spanning the period from about 1170 to the end of the 13th century.

Contents and structure

The codex is organised in eight fascicles arranged roughly chronologically and by number of voices. Its contents include works associated with the Notre Dame school of Léonin and Pérotin, motets on chant tenors in Latin and French (including double and triple motets with different texts sung simultaneously), and early examples of the motets of Petrus de Cruce, whose subdivision of the breve into semibreves pushed against the limits of Franconian notation.

Significance

Because it gathers music from across the whole century in one manuscript, the Montpellier Codex is a principal source for the study of the medieval motet and of the development of mensural notation. It was first described in detail in the 19th century and has been published in modern editions, notably by Hans Tischler. The manuscript is preserved at the library of the University of Montpellier's medical faculty, which gives it its name.
