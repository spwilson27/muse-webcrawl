Ladder paradox

The ladder paradox (or barn–pole paradox) is a thought experiment in special relativity. It involves a ladder, too long to fit into a garage when at rest, travelling at relativistic speed parallel to the garage. Due to Lorentz contraction, observers at rest with the garage measure the moving ladder as shortened, so it momentarily fits inside. But observers moving with the ladder measure the garage as contracted instead, so the ladder cannot fit. The apparent contradiction is resolved by the relativity of simultaneity.

The paradox

Suppose a ladder of rest length 20 m and a garage of depth 10 m with a front and back door. Moving at a speed where its length contracts by half, the ladder fits inside the garage for an instant, during which both doors can be simultaneously closed (in the garage's frame) with the ladder entirely inside. In the ladder's rest frame, however, the garage contracts to 5 m, and the ladder never fits: the events of the front of the ladder reaching the back door and the back of the ladder entering the front door are not simultaneous, and the doors do not close at the same time.

Resolution

The key is that "the doors close at the same time" is frame-dependent. In the ladder's frame the back door closes before the front door opens, sequentially, so the ladder passes through without ever being fully enclosed. If the ladder is instead brought to rest inside the garage (for example by crashing into the back wall), further analysis involving acceleration and the non-rigidity of real objects—no perfectly rigid bodies exist in relativity—shows that the ladder compresses and, once at rest in the garage frame, does end up fitting only if it is genuinely shortened by the stopping process; the classic treatment by Wolfgang Rindler analyses this "man and pole" variant in detail.

Related paradoxes

The ladder paradox is closely related to Bell's spaceship paradox and the Ehrenfest paradox, all of which turn on length contraction, simultaneity, and the impossibility of Born-rigid motion for extended bodies.
