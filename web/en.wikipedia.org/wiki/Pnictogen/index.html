Pnictogen

A pnictogen (also pnicogen) is any of the chemical elements in group 15 of the periodic table: nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), arsenic (As), antimony (Sb), bismuth (Bi), and moscovium (Mc). The group is also known as the nitrogen group or family.

The name derives from the Greek πνίγειν (pnigein, "to choke"), referencing nitrogen's suffocating properties. All pnictogens have five valence electrons (ns² np³ configuration) and commonly form compounds in oxidation states −3, +3 and +5.

Properties

The group shows a transition from nonmetals (nitrogen, phosphorus) through metalloids (arsenic, antimony) to a metal (bismuth). Nitrogen is essential to life as a component of proteins and nucleic acids; phosphorus is vital in ATP and bones; arsenic and antimony have industrial and semiconductor uses.
