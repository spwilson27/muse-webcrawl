MacLehose Trail

The MacLehose Trail is a 100-kilometre hiking trail that crosses much of the New Territories in Hong Kong, from Pak Tam Chung in Sai Kung in the east to Tuen Mun in the west. It is the longest of Hong Kong's long-distance trails and is divided into ten sections. The trail opened on 26 October 1979 and was named after Sir Murray MacLehose, the then Governor of Hong Kong and an enthusiastic hiker.

Route

The trail passes through eight country parks, crosses the High Island Reservoir, climbs Tai Mo Shan (Hong Kong's highest peak at 957 metres), and traverses the ridge of hills known as the "Eight Peaks of Kowloon". Distance posts are placed every 500 metres. The annual Oxfam Trailwalker charity race follows the trail, with teams of four aiming to complete it within 48 hours.
