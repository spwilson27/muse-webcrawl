Meteorite classification

Meteorite classification is the scheme by which meteorites are grouped according to their composition, structure, and inferred parent-body history. The fundamental division is between chondrites, which are undifferentiated and preserve material nearly unchanged since the formation of the Solar System, and achondrites and the iron and stony-iron meteorites, which come from differentiated parent bodies that melted and separated into core, mantle, and crust.

Major divisions

  • Chondrites — named for the chondrules (millimetre-sized silicate spheres) they contain. They are subdivided into ordinary chondrites (H, L, LL groups), carbonaceous chondrites (CI, CM, CV, CO, CR and others), enstatite chondrites, and the R and K groups. Ordinary chondrites are further assigned petrologic types 1–6 reflecting aqueous alteration and thermal metamorphism.
  • Primitive achondrites — partially melted residues such as acapulcoites and lodranites.
  • Achondrites — igneous rocks from differentiated bodies, including the HED clan (howardites, eucrites, diogenites) from asteroid 4 Vesta, Martian meteorites (shergottites, nakhlites, chassignites), and lunar meteorites.
  • Stony-iron meteorites — pallasites (olivine crystals in iron-nickel metal) and mesosiderites.
  • Iron meteorites — composed mainly of iron-nickel alloys (kamacite and taenite), classified structurally (hexahedrites, octahedrites, ataxites) and chemically into groups such as IAB, IIAB, IIIAB, and IVA; octahedrites display the characteristic Widmanstätten pattern when etched.

Purpose

Classification links each meteorite to a parent body and a formation process, letting researchers reconstruct the early history of the planets. Oxygen isotope ratios are a key tool: meteorites from the same parent body plot along a common fractionation line.
