BIBO stability

In signal processing and control theory, BIBO stability (bounded-input, bounded-output stability) is a form of stability for signals and systems: a system is BIBO stable if every bounded input produces a bounded output.

Definition

For a continuous-time linear time-invariant (LTI) system, BIBO stability is equivalent to the impulse response being absolutely integrable: ∫| h(τ) | dτ < ∞. For a discrete-time LTI system, the condition is that the impulse response be absolutely summable.

Frequency-domain condition

For a rational transfer function, BIBO stability is equivalent to all poles lying in the open left half of the s-plane (continuous time) or strictly inside the unit circle in the z-plane (discrete time) — that is, the region of convergence of the transfer function includes the imaginary axis (or unit circle).

Relation to other stability notions

BIBO stability concerns only input–output behaviour; a system may be BIBO stable yet internally (asymptotically) unstable if unstable modes are hidden by pole–zero cancellation. For LTI systems with minimal realisations, the two notions coincide.
