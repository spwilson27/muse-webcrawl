The Hulk is a superhero appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Stan Lee and artist Jack Kirby, he debuted in The Incredible Hulk #1 (May 1962). The character is the alter ego of physicist Dr. Robert Bruce Banner, who, after exposure to gamma radiation while saving a teenager from a bomb test, transforms into a giant, superhumanly strong green (originally grey) creature whenever enraged or stressed: the mild-mannered scientist and his raging alter ego are a modern retelling of Dr Jekyll and Mr Hyde, with Frankenstein's monster as another acknowledged inspiration.

Characterisation

The Hulk's strength is classically described as increasing with his anger, potentially without limit. Over decades of publication the character has been portrayed in multiple incarnations, including the childlike "savage" Hulk, the articulate grey "Joe Fixit", and merged or "Professor" versions; later stories revealed dissociative identities rooted in Banner's childhood trauma. Supporting characters include Betty Ross, Rick Jones, and adversaries such as the Leader, the Abomination and General "Thunderbolt" Ross, later the Red Hulk. The Hulk is a founding member of the Avengers.

In other media

The character became a pop-culture icon through the 1978–1982 television series The Incredible Hulk, starring Bill Bixby as Banner and Lou Ferrigno as the Hulk, and through films including Ang Lee's Hulk (2003) and The Incredible Hulk (2008). In the Marvel Cinematic Universe the role has been played by Mark Ruffalo since The Avengers (2012). The character's catchphrases — "Hulk smash!" and "You wouldn't like me when I'm angry" — are widely recognised.
