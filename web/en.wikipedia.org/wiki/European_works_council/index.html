European works council

European works councils (EWCs) are bodies that represent the European employees of a company through information and consultation on transnational issues. They were established under the European Works Council Directive of 1994, recast in 2009, to improve workers' rights in undertakings operating across the European Union.

European works council
Legal basis: Directive 94/45/EC; recast Directive 2009/38/EC
Threshold: 1,000+ employees in the EEA, with 150+ in each of two member states

Scope and establishment

The directive applies to companies with at least 1,000 employees within the European Economic Area and at least 150 in each of two member states. An EWC can be requested by employees or initiated by management, and its composition and procedures are negotiated between management and a special negotiating body of employee representatives; subsidiary requirements apply if negotiations fail.

Functions

Councils must be informed and consulted on transnational matters such as restructuring, closures, collective redundancies and major changes to work organisation. Unlike German-style works councils, EWCs generally have consultation rather than co-determination rights. Around a thousand EWCs exist, covering millions of workers in multinational firms.

Criticism

Unions argue consultation often comes after decisions are effectively made, and enforcement is weak; employers argue the bodies help communication across borders. Brexit removed UK employees from the directive's framework, prompting renegotiation of many agreements.
