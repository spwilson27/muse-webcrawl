Manto

Manto may refer to several people and things. Most prominently, it is the surname of Saadat Hasan Manto (1912–1955), an Indian and later Pakistani writer of Urdu short stories, essays, radio plays and film scripts, widely regarded as one of the greatest short-story writers in South Asian literature.

Saadat Hasan Manto

Born in Samrala, Punjab, Manto worked in Bombay's film industry before migrating to Lahore after the Partition of India in 1947. His stories — including "Toba Tek Singh", "Thanda Gosht" (Cold Meat), "Khol Do" and "Bu" — deal unsparingly with the human cost of Partition, prostitution, poverty and hypocrisy. He was repeatedly tried for obscenity in both British India and Pakistan, though never convicted. He died in Lahore in 1955 at the age of 42. His work has been the subject of renewed critical attention, translations, and films, including the 2018 biopic Manto.

Other uses

Manto is also a figure in Greek mythology: a prophetess, daughter of the seer Tiresias, associated with Delphi and with the founding of the oracle of Apollo at Claros. The name also occurs as a place name and as a surname in Italy and elsewhere.
