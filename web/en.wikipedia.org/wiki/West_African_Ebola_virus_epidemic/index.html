West African Ebola virus epidemic

The West African Ebola virus epidemic (2013–2016) was the largest outbreak of Ebola virus disease in history. Beginning in Guéckédou Prefecture, Guinea, in December 2013 — the index case is believed to have been a two-year-old child, Emile Ouamouno — it spread to neighbouring Liberia and Sierra Leone, with smaller numbers of cases in Nigeria, Mali, Senegal and several non-African countries. By the time the World Health Organization declared the end of the public health emergency in March 2016, some 28,600 cases and more than 11,300 deaths had been reported.

Course and response

The epidemic overwhelmed weak health systems and was fuelled by urban spread, funerary practices involving contact with the deceased, and initial mistrust of authorities. The WHO's declaration of a Public Health Emergency of International Concern came in August 2014. An unprecedented international response followed: treatment centres were built by governments, NGOs such as Médecins Sans Frontières, and foreign militaries; thousands of local health workers — many of whom died — carried out contact tracing, safe burials and community engagement. Ring vaccination trials of the rVSV-ZEBOV vaccine in Guinea in 2015 showed high efficacy, and the vaccine was later licensed.

Impact

Beyond its direct toll, the epidemic devastated health services, economies and education in the three worst-affected countries, orphaned thousands of children, and led to lasting reforms in global health emergency response, including the creation of the WHO Health Emergencies Programme and the Coalition for Epidemic Preparedness Innovations (CEPI).
