Organization for Transformative Works

The Organization for Transformative Works (OTW) is a nonprofit organization founded in 2007 by fans to provide access to and preserve the history of fanworks and fan cultures. Its projects include the Archive of Our Own (AO3), a fanfiction archive; the Fanlore wiki; the Open Doors preservation project; legal advocacy for fanworks; and the academic journal Transformative Works and Cultures.

Founding

The OTW was founded after the 2007 launch and failure of the commercial, advertising-supported fanfiction site FanLib, which many fans viewed as an attempt to profit from fan labour without fan control. Writer Naomi Novik, an early board member, was prominent in the call for a fan-owned, nonprofit archive with servers owned by the community. The organisation was incorporated in 2007 and its principal project, the Archive of Our Own, entered open beta in November 2009.

Projects

Archive of Our Own (AO3) hosts millions of fanworks — fiction, art, audio, and video — in tens of thousands of fandoms, using a volunteer-maintained tagging system; it won the Hugo Award for Best Related Work in 2019. Fanlore is a wiki documenting fandom history and culture. Open Doors rescues at-risk fan archives by importing them into AO3 or a special collections programme that also preserves print fanzines in university libraries. The OTW's Legal Advocacy committee defends the position that fanworks are transformative and protected under fair use, filing amicus briefs and comments with bodies such as the US Copyright Office; it helped secure exemptions to the DMCA for remix video.

Governance

The OTW is run by volunteers organised into committees, overseen by a board elected by dues-paying members. It is funded by donations and holds regular membership drives; membership confers voting rights in board elections.
