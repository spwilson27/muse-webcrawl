Geography of South Korea

The southern Korean Peninsula

Area: 100,363 km²

Highest point: Hallasan, 1,947 m

Longest river: Nakdong, 510 km

South Korea (the Republic of Korea) occupies the southern portion of the Korean Peninsula in East Asia, covering about 100,363 square kilometres. Its only land border, with North Korea, follows the Korean Demilitarized Zone near the 38th parallel; on all other sides it is bounded by sea — the Yellow Sea to the west, the East China Sea to the south and the Sea of Japan (East Sea) to the east.

Terrain

About 70% of the country is mountainous. The Taebaek Mountains run down the eastern coast, and the Sobaek range crosses the southwest. The highest peak is Hallasan (1,947 m), a dormant shield volcano on Jeju Island, South Korea's largest island. Lowlands are concentrated in the west and southeast, where the Han, Geum and Nakdong rivers have formed the plains that hold most of the population and rice cultivation.

Coastline and islands

The western and southern coasts are deeply indented rias coasts with thousands of islands, large tidal flats and some of the world's largest tidal ranges. The eastern coast is straighter and more mountainous, with narrow beaches.

Climate

South Korea has a humid continental and subtropical climate influenced by the East Asian monsoon: cold, dry winters, hot humid summers, and a summer rainy season (jangma). Typhoons occasionally strike the southern coast in late summer.
