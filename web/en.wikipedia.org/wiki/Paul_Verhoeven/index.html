Paul Verhoeven (born 18 July 1938) is a Dutch filmmaker. His films are known for their graphic violence, sexual content, and satirical social commentary. After success in the Netherlands with films such as Turkish Delight (1973) and Soldier of Orange (1977), he moved to Hollywood and directed a string of commercially successful science fiction and thriller films, including RoboCop (1987), Total Recall (1990), Basic Instinct (1992), Starship Troopers (1997), and Hollow Man (2000).

Early life

Verhoeven was born in Amsterdam during the Second World War; memories of the German occupation and its violence marked his childhood and recur throughout his work. He studied mathematics and physics at Leiden University, graduating in 1964, and made short films during his national service with the Royal Netherlands Navy's film unit.

Career

Verhoeven's Dutch features established him as the country's leading director: Turkish Delight was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film and was later voted the best Dutch film of the twentieth century, and the war epic Soldier of Orange brought him international attention. In Hollywood, RoboCop combined ultraviolence with media satire; Total Recall and Basic Instinct were major hits; Showgirls (1995) was a famous critical and commercial failure on release but has since been reappraised as a cult work. Starship Troopers, widely misunderstood on release as militaristic, is now read as an anti-fascist satire.

Returning to Europe, Verhoeven directed the Dutch Second World War film Black Book (2006), the French psychological thriller Elle (2016), which won the Golden Globe for Best Foreign Language Film and earned Isabelle Huppert an Academy Award nomination, and the controversial religious drama Benedetta (2021).
