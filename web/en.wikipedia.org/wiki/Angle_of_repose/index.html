Angle of repose

The angle of repose is the steepest angle at which a pile of granular material remains stable without slumping. It is measured between the sloping surface of the pile and a horizontal plane. The angle depends on particle size, shape, roughness, moisture, cohesion and the way the material is deposited.

Behaviour

Dry sand commonly has an angle of repose around 34 degrees, but values vary widely. Angular or interlocking grains tend to stand more steeply than smooth rounded grains. Adding a small amount of water can increase stability through capillary forces, whereas saturation may lubricate grains or add pore pressure and trigger flow.

Applications

The concept is used in geology, soil mechanics, mining, agriculture, pharmaceutical powder handling and the design of stockpiles, embankments and hoppers. Related phenomena include avalanching, talus slopes and the angle of internal friction, although these are not identical measures.
