Eldrick Tont "Tiger" Woods (born 30 December 1975) is an American professional golfer. He is tied for first in PGA Tour wins, ranks second in men's major championships, and holds numerous golf records. Woods is widely regarded as one of the greatest golfers of all time and one of the most famous athletes in modern history.

A child prodigy introduced to golf by his father Earl, Woods won three US Amateur titles and turned professional in 1996 with the famous "Hello, world" Nike campaign. In April 1997 he won the Masters at 21 by a record twelve strokes, becoming the tournament's youngest champion and transforming golf's popularity and prize money — the "Tiger Effect". Between 1999 and 2002 he completed the "Tiger Slam" (holding all four major titles simultaneously, 2000–01), dominating the sport for a decade.

Comebacks

After scandal in 2009, injuries and multiple back surgeries, Woods's career appeared over; his win at the 2019 Masters — his 15th major, eleven years after his 14th — is considered one of the greatest comebacks in sport. A serious car crash in February 2021 caused major leg injuries; he has since played a limited schedule while remaining the game's biggest draw.
