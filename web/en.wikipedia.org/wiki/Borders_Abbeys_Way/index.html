The Borders Abbeys Way is a long-distance footpath in the Scottish Borders region of Scotland. The circular route, some 109 kilometres (68 miles) long, links the four great ruined abbeys of the Borders — Kelso, Jedburgh, Melrose and Dryburgh — together with the towns of Hawick and Selkirk. It is one of Scotland's Great Trails, managed by the Scottish Borders Council.

Route

The way is usually described starting at Kelso, where the trailhead stands near Kelso Abbey. From Kelso the path follows the valley of the River Teviot to Jedburgh, passing the ruins of Roxburgh Castle. Jedburgh Abbey, founded in the 12th century for Augustinian canons, dominates its town. The route then crosses farmland and low hills westwards to Hawick, the largest town on the route, before turning north through the Ettrick Forest fringe to Selkirk.

From Selkirk the path descends to the valley of the River Tweed and follows it to Melrose, site of Melrose Abbey, traditionally the burial place of the heart of Robert the Bruce. The final section runs along the Tweed to Dryburgh Abbey, a Premonstratensian house in a secluded loop of the river where Sir Walter Scott is buried, before returning to Kelso.

The abbeys

  • Kelso Abbey — founded in 1128 by David I for Tironensian monks; only fragments of the great church survive.
  • Jedburgh Abbey — Augustinian, founded c. 1138; its church is substantially complete roofless.
  • Melrose Abbey — Cistercian, founded 1136; famed for its ornate stone carving.
  • Dryburgh Abbey — Premonstratensian, founded c. 1150; burial place of Sir Walter Scott.
