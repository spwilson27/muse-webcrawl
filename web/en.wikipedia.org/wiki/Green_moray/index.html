Green moray

Scientific nameGymnothorax funebris
FamilyMuraenidae
RangeWestern Atlantic Ocean
Maximum length2.5 m (8.2 ft)
Conservation statusLeast Concern (IUCN)

The green moray (Gymnothorax funebris) is a moray eel found in the western Atlantic Ocean from Long Island, New York, at the northern end of its range, to Brazil, including the Gulf of Mexico and the Caribbean Sea. It is the largest moray species in the Atlantic, and one of the largest of all morays.

Description

The green moray's dark brown or black skin secretes a protective yellowish mucus, giving the animal its characteristic green appearance. It can grow to 2.5 m (8.2 ft) in length and weigh up to 29 kg (64 lb), though most individuals are considerably smaller. Its muscular, scaleless body is laterally compressed toward the tail.

Habitat and behaviour

Green morays inhabit rocky shorelines, coral reefs, and mangroves, sheltering in crevices by day and hunting at night. They feed mainly on fish, crabs, shrimp, and octopus, locating prey largely by smell. Despite a fearsome appearance — the mouth is often held open to pump water over the gills — they are generally shy of divers unless provoked.

Relationship with humans

The species is a popular display animal in large public aquariums. Its flesh may carry ciguatera toxin, so it is rarely eaten.
