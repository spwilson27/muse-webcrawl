The politics of Afghanistan have undergone dramatic transformations over the past century, from monarchy to republic, communist state, civil war, the first Taliban emirate (1996–2001), the internationally backed Islamic Republic (2004–2021), and since August 2021 the restored Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan under the Taliban.

The Arg in Kabul, seat of Afghan governments.

Islamic Emirate (2021–present)

After the withdrawal of United States and NATO forces and the collapse of the Islamic Republic in August 2021, the Taliban re-established the Islamic Emirate. Supreme authority rests with the Amir al-Mu'minin (Supreme Leader), Hibatullah Akhundzada, based in Kandahar, who rules by decree under the movement's interpretation of Hanafi Islamic law. A cabinet of acting ministers administers the state from Kabul; there is no constitution in force and no elections. The government has not been formally recognised by any UN member state, though several countries maintain working relations with it.

Islamic Republic (2004–2021)

The 2004 constitution created a presidential system with a bicameral National Assembly — the Wolesi Jirga (lower house) and Meshrano Jirga (upper house) — and a Loya Jirga as the highest traditional consultative body. Presidents Hamid Karzai (2004–2014) and Ashraf Ghani (2014–2021) governed amid an ongoing Taliban insurgency and heavy dependence on foreign aid and security assistance.

Historical background

Afghanistan was ruled as a monarchy until 1973, when Mohammed Daoud Khan proclaimed a republic. The 1978 Saur Revolution brought a communist government, triggering the Soviet–Afghan War (1979–1989), followed by civil war among mujahideen factions and the Taliban's rise in the 1990s.
