Scott Adams

Born: June 8, 1957, Windham, New York, U.S.
Died: January 13, 2026 (aged 68)
Occupation: Cartoonist, author, commentator
Known for: Dilbert

Scott Raymond Adams (June 8, 1957 – January 13, 2026) was an American cartoonist and author, best known as the creator of the comic strip Dilbert, which satirised white-collar corporate culture and at its peak appeared in some 2,000 newspapers in 65 countries. He also wrote business-humour and self-help books, including The Dilbert Principle (1996) and How to Fail at Almost Everything and Still Win Big (2013).

Early life and career

Adams grew up in Windham, New York. He earned a bachelor's degree in economics from Hartwick College (1979) and an MBA from the University of California, Berkeley (1986). While working as an engineer and project manager at Crocker National Bank and then Pacific Bell (1979–1995), he drew cartoons based on his office experiences; Dilbert was launched in syndication on April 16, 1989, and Adams left Pacific Bell in 1995 to cartoon full-time. He suffered from spasmodic dysphonia, a voice disorder, which he discussed publicly, and regained normal speech after surgery in 2008.

Later commentary and controversy

From the mid-2010s Adams became a prominent political commentator and livestreamer, notably analysing Donald Trump's persuasion techniques. In February 2023, after remarks in a livestream in which he called Black Americans a "hate group" and advised white people to "get the hell away" from them, hundreds of newspapers dropped Dilbert and his syndicator terminated their relationship. Adams relaunched the strip as a subscription webcomic, Dilbert Reborn, and continued daily commentary until his death from prostate cancer in January 2026.
