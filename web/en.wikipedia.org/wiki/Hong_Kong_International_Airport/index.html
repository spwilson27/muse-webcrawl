Hong Kong International Airport (IATA: HKG, ICAO: VHHH) is an international airport on the island of Chek Lap Kok in western Hong Kong. It is also known as Chek Lap Kok Airport to distinguish it from its predecessor, the former Kai Tak Airport. Opened in 1998, it is one of the world's busiest cargo airports and one of the busiest passenger airports in Asia, and a major hub for Cathay Pacific.

History

The airport was built on a largely artificial island, formed by levelling and merging the islands of Chek Lap Kok and Lam Chau and reclaiming land from the sea; the site covers about 12.5 square kilometres. It was the centrepiece of the Airport Core Programme, one of the largest infrastructure projects of its time, which also included the Airport Express railway, the Tsing Ma Bridge, and the new town of Tung Chung. The airport opened on 6 July 1998, replacing Kai Tak, whose approach over Kowloon was famously demanding; its opening was marred by serious cargo-handling and computer problems that took months to resolve.

Facilities

Terminal 1, designed by Foster + Partners, was among the largest airport terminal buildings in the world when it opened; Terminal 2 opened in 2007 and a Midfield Concourse in 2015. Satellite Concourse 1 is linked by an automated people mover. The airport originally had two runways (07L/25R and 07R/25L); a third runway (07C/25C) opened in 2022 as part of the Three-runway System project, after which the original north runway was closed for reconfiguration.

Operations

Before the COVID-19 pandemic, the airport handled some 74.7 million passengers in 2019 and was consistently the world's busiest airport by cargo traffic, with over 4 million tonnes annually. It is a focus city for carriers across Asia-Pacific and connects to more than 200 destinations. The Hong Kong–Zhuhai–Macau Bridge, which opened in 2018 adjacent to the airport island, integrated it further into the Pearl River Delta catchment area.

Ground transport

The Airport Express train reaches Hong Kong Station in the central business district in about 24 minutes. The airport is also served by buses, coaches to mainland China, and the SkyPier ferry terminal connecting passengers to ports in the Pearl River Delta.
