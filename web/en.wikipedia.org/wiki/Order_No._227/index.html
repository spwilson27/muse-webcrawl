Order No. 227

Order No. 227
Issued: 28 July 1942
Issuer: Joseph Stalin, People's Commissar of Defence
Known as: "Not a step back!" (Ни шагу назад!)

Order No. 227 was issued by Joseph Stalin, in his capacity as People's Commissar of Defence, on 28 July 1942, during the Second World War. It is famous for its injunction "Not a step back!" (Ni shagu nazad!), which became a rallying cry of the Red Army in the defence of Stalingrad and other threatened positions.

Contents

The order declared that panic-mongers and cowards who retreated without orders were to be eliminated on the spot. It required each front to create penal battalions of disgraced soldiers and officers, and each army to form "blocking detachments" (zagraditelnye otriady) stationed behind unreliable divisions to prevent unauthorised retreat. It also forbade commanders to withdraw without orders from higher headquarters.

Context and effect

The order came as German forces advanced deep into the southern Soviet Union toward Stalingrad and the Caucasus oilfields. While it was intended to stiffen resistance, its harshest measures were applied unevenly; blocking detachments were largely reassigned or disbanded by October 1942, though penal units remained in use throughout the war. Historians continue to debate the order's balance of motivational and coercive effects on the Red Army's subsequent performance.
