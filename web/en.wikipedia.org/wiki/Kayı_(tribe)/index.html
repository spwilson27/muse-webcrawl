Kayı (tribe)

The Kayı tamga, a symbol associated with the tribe in Oghuz tradition.

The Kayı or Kayï (Turkish: Kayı boyu) were one of the Oghuz Turkic tribes. In Oghuz tradition they ranked among the leading tribes of the Bozok branch, descended from Kayı Khan, a son of Gün Khan and grandson of Oghuz Khan. The name is often interpreted as meaning "the one who has might and power".

History

According to later Ottoman tradition, the Kayı were the ancestral tribe of the Ottoman dynasty: Ertuğrul, father of Osman I, the founder of the Ottoman Empire, was said to be a Kayı chieftain who was granted lands around Söğüt by the Seljuk Sultanate of Rum in the thirteenth century. Modern historians debate how much of this genealogy is historical and how much was constructed later to legitimise Ottoman rule.

Legacy

The Kayı tamga, formed of the letters resembling "IYI", appears on some early Ottoman coins and remains a popular symbol in Turkey. Settlements named Kayi exist in Turkey and elsewhere in the former Oghuz world, and tribal groups identifying as Kayı survive among Turkmen and in Anatolia. The tribe was popularised internationally by the television series Diriliş: Ertuğrul.
