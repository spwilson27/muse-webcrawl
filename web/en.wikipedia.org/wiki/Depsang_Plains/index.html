LocationLadakh / Aksai Chin frontier

The Depsang Plains, also called the Depsang Bulge, are a high-altitude gravel plain in the western sector of the disputed China–India border, at the southern end of Aksai Chin and the northern edge of Ladakh, near the Karakoram Pass. At roughly 5,300 metres above sea level, the plains form a rare stretch of flat terrain in the Karakoram region suitable for vehicle movement, giving them strategic significance.

Strategic significance

The Depsang area overlooks the route to the Karakoram Pass, one of the few passes linking the region with Xinjiang, and lies close to India's Daulat Beg Oldi airstrip, one of the world's highest. The Indian patrol routes in the plains run up to points near the Line of Actual Control, whose alignment here is not mutually agreed.

Standoffs

In April–May 2013, a platoon of Chinese troops camped on the plains in territory claimed by India, causing a three-week standoff resolved by mutual withdrawal. The area again featured in the wider 2020–2021 China–India border crisis, in which access to traditional Indian patrolling points in Depsang was a central issue in disengagement negotiations.
