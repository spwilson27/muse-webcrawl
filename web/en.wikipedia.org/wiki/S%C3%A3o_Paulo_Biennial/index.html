São Paulo Biennial

First held1951
VenueCiccillo Matarazzo Pavilion, Ibirapuera Park, São Paulo
FrequencyEvery two years

The São Paulo Biennial (Bienal de São Paulo) is an international contemporary art exhibition held every two years in São Paulo, Brazil. Founded in 1951 by industrialist Ciccillo Matarazzo, it was modelled on the Venice Biennale and is the second-oldest art biennial in the world.

History

The first edition brought major European modernists to Brazil and helped establish São Paulo as an art centre. Early editions were organised within the São Paulo Museum of Modern Art; the Fundação Bienal de São Paulo was created in 1962 to run the event independently. The exhibition has been held since 1957 in the Ciccillo Matarazzo Pavilion in Ibirapuera Park, a building designed by Oscar Niemeyer.

Format

Historically organised around national representations, the Biennial shifted toward thematic, curator-led exhibitions in later decades. Notable editions include the 1985 "Great Canvas" exhibition and controversial editions held during Brazil's military dictatorship, some boycotted by artists.

Significance

The Biennial remains one of the largest contemporary art events in the Southern Hemisphere and a key platform for Latin American art.
