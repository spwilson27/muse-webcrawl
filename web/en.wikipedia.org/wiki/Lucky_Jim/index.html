Lucky Jim

Lucky Jim is a comic novel by Kingsley Amis, first published in 1954. It is widely regarded as one of the finest comic novels of the 20th century and helped define the "campus novel" genre, satirising the stuffiness of provincial academia in post-war Britain.

Plot

Jim Dixon is a reluctant junior lecturer in medieval history at a provincial English university, holding a probationary post he does not want and despises. Anxious to secure tenure, he must ingratiate himself with his pompous department head, Professor Welch, and survive the Welches' excruciating arty weekend parties and his entanglement with a fellow lecturer, the manipulative Margaret Peel.

A series of comic disasters follows — a burnt bedsheet concealed from his hosts, a catastrophically drunken public lecture on "Merrie England" — yet Dixon is rescued in the end by the wealthy Gore-Urquhart, who offers him a job in London, and he leaves the university with Christine, the girl he loves. It is this pattern of escaping the consequences of disaster that makes him "Lucky Jim".

Reception and legacy

The novel won the Somerset Maugham Award and launched Amis's career. It became associated with the "Angry Young Men" of 1950s British literature, though Amis distanced himself from the label. It was adapted into a 1957 film starring Ian Carmichael.
