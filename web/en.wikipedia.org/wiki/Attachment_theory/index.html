Attachment theory

Key figuresJohn Bowlby, Mary Ainsworth
FieldDevelopmental psychology

Attachment theory is a psychological framework concerning enduring emotional bonds between humans, developed by psychiatrist John Bowlby from the 1950s. It holds that infants have an innate need to form a close attachment to at least one primary caregiver for normal social and emotional development.

Ainsworth's patterns

Mary Ainsworth's “Strange Situation” experiments in the 1970s identified attachment patterns: secure, anxious-avoidant, and anxious-ambivalent/resistant; Mary Main later added disorganised attachment. Securely attached children use the caregiver as a secure base for exploration.

Adult attachment and criticism

The theory was extended to adult romantic relationships by Hazan and Shaver. Critics note cultural variation in caregiving, the role of temperament, and warn against over-deterministic readings; attachment is influential but not destiny.
