Corporate group

A corporate group or group of companies is a collection of parent and subsidiary corporations that function as a single economic entity through a common source of control. The concept of a group is frequently used in tax law, accounting and (less commonly) company law to attribute the rights and duties of one member of the group to another or the whole. If the corporations are wholly separate, the group cannot be a single legal person, though groups typically prepare consolidated financial statements.

Legal independence

Each company in a group is normally a separate legal person with limited liability, following the principle established in Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd [1897]. The courts may, in limited circumstances, pierce the corporate veil to treat group companies as one, as discussed in Adams v Cape Industries plc [1990].

Accounting

Under IFRS and most national accounting standards, a parent company must prepare consolidated financial statements combining the results of all subsidiaries it controls, eliminating intra-group transactions.

Group structures

Groups may be organised horizontally (companies under common ownership), vertically (a chain of subsidiaries), or as conglomerates across unrelated industries. Closely related concepts include the Japanese keiretsu and Korean chaebol.
