Bouncing ball

The physics of a bouncing ball concerns the behaviour of a ball bouncing against a surface, governed by mechanics, the coefficient of restitution, spin, and air resistance. It is a classic problem in physics education and sports science.

Flight and impact

In flight the ball follows a projectile trajectory under gravity, slowed by drag and deflected by the Magnus effect if spinning. On impact it compresses, loses energy as heat and sound, and rebounds with reduced speed determined by the coefficient of restitution e, so successive bounce heights form a geometric sequence.

Applications

Analysis informs ball and surface design in tennis, golf, basketball and squash, and the bouncing-ball animation principle in computer graphics and early animation exercises.

The bouncing ball in animation

In animation, the bouncing ball exercise teaches timing, squash and stretch, and arcs.
