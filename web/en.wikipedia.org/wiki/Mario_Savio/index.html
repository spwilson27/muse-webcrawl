Mario Savio (8 December 1942 – 6 November 1996) was an American activist and a leading figure of the Free Speech Movement at the University of California, Berkeley in 1964. His impassioned speeches on the steps of Sproul Hall became emblematic of the student movements of the 1960s.

Background

Savio was born in New York City to Italian immigrant parents and raised in Queens. A gifted student, he entered UC Berkeley in 1963 to study philosophy. In the summer of 1964 he joined the Freedom Summer project in Mississippi, registering Black voters, an experience that deeply radicalised him about institutional injustice.

Free Speech Movement

When the Berkeley administration banned political advocacy on a strip of campus used by civil rights groups, students formed the Free Speech Movement. Savio emerged as its most eloquent spokesman. On 2 December 1964, before the occupation of Sproul Hall, he delivered his famous "Bodies upon the gears" speech, declaring that when the operation of the machine becomes so odious, "you've got to put your bodies upon the gears and upon the wheels... and you've got to make it stop." He was among some 800 students arrested in the ensuing sit-in, one of the largest mass arrests in California history. The movement ultimately won the right to political speech on campus.

Later life

Savio later distanced himself from the national spotlight, completed his studies, and became a lecturer in mathematics, physics and philosophy at Sonoma State University. He remained active in campus causes, campaigning against fee increases in the 1990s. He died in 1996 following a heart attack. The Mario Savio Memorial Lecture is held annually at Berkeley in his honour, and the steps of Sproul Hall where he spoke are named the Mario Savio Steps.
