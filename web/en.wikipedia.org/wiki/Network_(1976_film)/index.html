Network (1976 film)

Network is a 1976 American satirical drama film written by Paddy Chayefsky and directed by Sidney Lumet, about a fictional television network, the Union Broadcasting System (UBS), and its struggle with poor ratings. The film stars Faye Dunaway, William Holden, Peter Finch (in his final film role), and Robert Duvall.

Plot

Veteran news anchor Howard Beale (Finch) learns he will be fired because of declining ratings. On air, he announces that he will kill himself during a future broadcast. Instead of removing him, the network's ambitious programming executive Diana Christensen (Dunaway) exploits Beale's increasingly unhinged on-air rants, which make him a ratings sensation as the "mad prophet of the airwaves". Beale's most famous outburst urges viewers to open their windows and shout, "I'm as mad as hell, and I'm not going to take this anymore!" When Beale's preaching begins to threaten a corporate merger, the network arranges to have him assassinated on live television.

Reception and legacy

Network was a critical and commercial success and is widely regarded as one of the greatest films ever made. At the 49th Academy Awards it won four Oscars: Best Actor for Finch (awarded posthumously), Best Actress for Dunaway, Best Supporting Actress for Beatrice Straight, and Best Original Screenplay for Chayefsky. The film was selected for preservation in the National Film Registry in 2000, and Chayefsky's screenplay has been praised as prescient about the blurring of news and entertainment. A stage adaptation by Lee Hall premiered at the National Theatre in London in 2017 and transferred to Broadway.
