Knot polynomial

In the mathematical field of knot theory, a knot polynomial is a knot invariant that takes the form of a polynomial whose coefficients encode some of the properties of a given knot. Knot polynomials are among the most computable and widely used invariants in the subject.

Examples

The first knot polynomial, the Alexander polynomial, was introduced by J. W. Alexander in 1928 and can be computed from a Seifert matrix. Vaughan Jones's discovery of the Jones polynomial in 1984, via von Neumann algebras, revolutionised the field and earned him a Fields Medal; it distinguishes some knots from their mirror images, which the Alexander polynomial cannot. The HOMFLY polynomial generalises both, and further families include the Kauffman polynomial and Khovanov homology, a categorification of the Jones polynomial.

Computation

Most knot polynomials can be computed from a knot diagram using skein relations, which relate the values on three diagrams differing at a single crossing. No known polynomial invariant distinguishes all knots, and it remains unknown whether any, including the Jones polynomial, detects the unknot.
