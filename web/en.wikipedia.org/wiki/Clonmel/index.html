Clonmel (Irish: Cluain Meala, meaning "honey meadow") is the county town of County Tipperary in Ireland. It lies in the valley of the River Suir, between the Comeragh Mountains and Slievenamon, and is the largest inland town in the south of the country.

History

Clonmel grew around a medieval walled settlement and was a significant stronghold of the Butler Earls of Ormond. In May 1650 it was the scene of Oliver Cromwell's bloodiest defeat in Ireland: the Siege of Clonmel, in which Hugh Dubh O'Neill's garrison inflicted heavy casualties on the New Model Army before slipping away by night. The novelist Laurence Sterne was born in Clonmel in 1713, and the town was a centre of the nineteenth-century coach-building trade of Charles Bianconi.

Economy and culture

Clonmel is known for cider production — Bulmers Irish Cider (sold internationally as Magners) is made in the town — and for pharmaceutical and medical-technology manufacturing. The town hosts the annual Clonmel Junction Festival and is the home of the Tipperary Museum of Hidden History.
