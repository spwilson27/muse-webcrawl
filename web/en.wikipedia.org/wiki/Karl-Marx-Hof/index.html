Karl-Marx-Hof

The Karl-Marx-Hof is a famous municipal housing complex (Gemeindebau) in Heiligenstadt, in Döbling, the 19th district of Vienna, Austria. Built between 1927 and 1930 by the architect Karl Ehn, a pupil of Otto Wagner, during the period of "Red Vienna", it is the longest single residential building in the world, at just over 1,100 metres in length.

Design

The complex contains about 1,382 apartments housing some 5,000 people, yet occupies only about a fifth of its site; the rest is courtyards and gardens. It includes laundries, baths, kindergartens, a library, medical offices and shops, embodying the socialist programme of collective amenities for workers. Its rhythmic façade of ochre and red render, with tall arched gateways and balconies, gives it a fortress-like monumentality.

History

During the February Uprising of 1934, the Karl-Marx-Hof became a stronghold of the Social Democratic Republikanischer Schutzbund, and was shelled by government forces. After the Second World War it was repaired and renamed only briefly in the Nazi era; today it is a protected landmark and one of the best-known symbols of Red Vienna's housing programme.
