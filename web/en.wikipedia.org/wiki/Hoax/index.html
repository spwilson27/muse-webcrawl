Hoax

A hoax is a deliberate attempt to deceive the public

A hoax is a widely publicized falsehood so fashioned as to invite reflexive, unthinking acceptance by the greatest number of people of the greatest number of varied geographical, age, social, cultural, and ethnic groups. In this concept, a hoax differs from other forms of fakery — notably falsehoods whose creation is intended to deceive anyone into potentially falling for it — in that a hoax is specifically designed to attract and persuade the most gullible subgroup of a population.

The word hoax is thought to be a contraction of the words "hocus pocus", which in turn is possibly derived from the common magician's incantation "hocus pocus, tontus talontus, vade celeriter jubeo" used during tricks in the 17th century.

Definition

Robert Nares (1753–1829) says the word hoax was coined in the late 18th century as a contraction of the verb hocus, which means "to cheat" or "to impose upon" or "to befuddle often with drugged liquor". Hocus is a shortening of the magic incantation hocus pocus.

A hoax differs from a magic trick or from fiction in that those engaging in the hoax intend it to be believed by the public as true, or at least presented as a genuine occurrence. Hoaxes may target the media, academia, the general public, or specific institutions, and they can serve motives ranging from humour and satire to financial fraud, political manipulation, or publicity.

Famous hoaxes

Well-known historical hoaxes include the Piltdown Man, a fabricated fossil "missing link" presented in 1912 and not definitively exposed until 1953; the Cottingley Fairies photographs of 1917; Orson Welles's 1938 radio adaptation of The War of the Worlds, often said (perhaps exaggeratedly) to have caused panic; the Hitler Diaries of 1983, forged diaries sold to the magazine Stern; and crop circles, later revealed to have been made by human pranksters. In the internet era, hoaxes spread rapidly through social media and email forwarding.

Types of hoaxes

Hoaxes are frequently classified by their subject matter: religious hoaxes, hoaxes involving science or history, literary hoaxes and forged documents, hoaxes about celebrities and death hoaxes, urban legends presented as news, computer virus hoaxes and other false warnings, and April Fools' Day jokes published by the media. Some hoaxes begin as jokes but are repeated as fact, while others are designed maliciously to cause harm or panic.
