Piedmont (wine)


Vineyards in the Langhe hills

Piedmont wine is the range of Italian wines made in the region of Piedmont in the northwest of Italy. The best-known wines from the region include Barolo and Barbaresco, made from the Nebbiolo grape. These wines are among Italy's most prestigious and long-lived red wines. Other notable wines include Barbera (from the grape of the same name), Dolcetto, and the sweet sparkling wine Moscato d'Asti (Asti Spumante).

Geography and climate

Piedmont (Italian: Piemonte) is bordered by the Alps to the north and west and the Apennines to the south. The name means "foot of the mountain". Vineyards are concentrated in the hills of the Langhe, Roero, and Monferrato in the south of the region around the towns of Alba, Asti, and Alessandria. The continental climate, with cold winters and foggy autumns (the nebbia from which Nebbiolo may take its name), shapes the wines.

Wines and grapes

Nebbiolo produces structured, tannic wines with aromas of tar and roses that age for decades. Barbera is the most widely planted red variety, giving high-acid, fruity wines. Dolcetto yields softer, early-drinking reds. White grapes include Arneis, Cortese (used for Gavi), Erbaluce, and Moscato Bianco. Piedmont has more DOCG-designated wines than any other Italian region.

History

Viticulture in Piedmont dates to Roman times. In the 19th century, winemakers influenced by French techniques, including the dry fermentation of Nebbiolo, transformed the region's wines; Barolo became known as "the wine of kings, the king of wines". The Langhe-Roero and Monferrato vineyard landscapes were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2014.
