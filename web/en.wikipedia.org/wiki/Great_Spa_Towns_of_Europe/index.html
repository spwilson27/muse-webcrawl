The Great Spa Towns of Europe is a transnational World Heritage Site inscribed by UNESCO in July 2021, comprising eleven spa towns in seven European countries that together testify to the international spa culture which developed from the early 18th century to the 1930s. The towns are: Baden bei Wien (Austria); Spa (Belgium); Františkovy Lázně, Karlovy Vary, and Mariánské Lázně (Czech Republic); Vichy (France); Baden-Baden, Bad Ems, and Bad Kissingen (Germany); Montecatini Terme (Italy); and Bath (United Kingdom).

Outstanding universal value

UNESCO recognized the towns' shared urban form built around natural mineral springs: ensembles of spa buildings (pump rooms, colonnades, bathhouses, kurhaus assembly rooms), resort architecture (hotels, villas, casinos, theatres), and carefully landscaped parks and promenades designed for the ritual of "taking the cure". The spas were also crucibles of European high society, diplomacy, music, and literature — Spa gave its name to the whole genre, Bath was inscribed already in 1987 in its own right, and Vichy lent its name to the wartime French regime headquartered there.

Selection

The nomination was coordinated by the Czech Republic and developed over more than a decade; several candidate towns, including Wiesbaden and Bad Homburg (Germany), Luhačovice (Czech Republic), and Budapest's baths, were part of earlier drafts but did not appear in the final inscription grouping. The eleven towns cooperate through the Great Spa Towns of Europe Association in conservation and promotion.
