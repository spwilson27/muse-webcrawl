Age Discrimination in Employment Act of 1967

The Age Discrimination in Employment Act of 1967 (ADEA) is a United States federal law that prohibits employment discrimination against persons aged 40 or older. It applies to employers with 20 or more employees, including federal, state, and local governments, employment agencies, and labour organisations.

Provisions

The ADEA forbids discrimination in hiring, firing, pay, job assignments, promotions, layoffs, training, benefits, and other terms of employment, and generally prohibits specifying age in job advertisements except where age is a bona fide occupational qualification. Amendments in 1978 and 1986 raised and then largely abolished the mandatory-retirement age it covered; the Older Workers Benefit Protection Act of 1990 regulated waivers of ADEA rights in severance agreements.

Enforcement and case law

Charges are filed with the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission. Supreme Court decisions such as Smith v. City of Jackson (2005), recognising disparate-impact claims, and Gross v. FBL Financial Services (2009), requiring age to be the "but-for" cause in disparate-treatment cases, have shaped its application.
