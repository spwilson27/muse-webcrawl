Michael Tomasello

Michael Tomasello (born January 18, 1950) is an American developmental and comparative psychologist. He is a professor of psychology at Duke University and formerly co-director of the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology in Leipzig, Germany. His research focuses on the cognitive and cultural processes that distinguish humans from their nearest primate relatives — particularly shared intentionality — and on how children acquire language.

Career

Tomasello received his Ph.D. from the University of Georgia in 1980 and taught at Emory University before moving to Leipzig in 1998, where he co-founded and co-directed the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Anthropology and led its department of developmental and comparative psychology. In 2018 he joined Duke University.

Research

Comparing children with great apes, Tomasello argues that what makes human cognition unique is the capacity for shared intentionality: the ability to participate with others in joint attention, collaborative activities with shared goals, and cooperative communication. These capacities, emerging in infancy (the "nine-month revolution"), underpin imitation learning, language acquisition, and cumulative culture. In language research he is a leading proponent of the usage-based (constructionist) theory of language acquisition, challenging nativist accounts associated with Noam Chomsky.

Selected works

  • The Cultural Origins of Human Cognition (1999) — winner of the William James Book Award.
  • Constructing a Language: A Usage-Based Theory of Language Acquisition (2003)
  • Origins of Human Communication (2008)
  • Why We Cooperate (2009)
  • A Natural History of Human Thinking (2014)
  • A Natural History of Human Morality (2016)
  • Becoming Human: A Theory of Ontogeny (2019)

He received the Jean Nicod Prize in 2006 and the Klaus J. Jacobs Research Prize in 2011, and was elected a member of the National Academy of Sciences.
