Oath Keepers

Formation: March 2009

Founder: Stewart Rhodes

Type: Far-right anti-government militia movement

Headquarters: United States

Status: Defunct as a national organization following prosecutions after the January 6 Capitol attack

The Oath Keepers was an American far-right anti-government militia whose members asserted that the federal government was encroaching on citizens' rights and that they were prepared to defend the United States Constitution against all enemies, foreign and domestic. The group drew its membership largely from current and former military personnel, law enforcement officers, and first responders, all of whom had sworn an oath to support and defend the Constitution.

History

The organization was founded in March 2009 by Stewart Rhodes, a Yale Law School graduate and former paratrooper. Rhodes launched the group at a ceremony in Lexington, Massachusetts, invoking the symbolism of the American Revolutionary War. The group grew during the presidency of Barack Obama and became one of the most prominent organizations within the broader American militia movement and the patriot movement.

Oath Keepers participated in a number of armed standoffs and protests, including the 2014 Bundy standoff in Nevada and the 2015 armed protests outside a military recruiting station in Chattanooga, Tennessee, after a shooting there. Members also patrolled the streets of Ferguson, Missouri, during unrest in 2014 and 2015.

January 6 Capitol attack and prosecutions

Members of the Oath Keepers were among those who took part in the attack on the United States Capitol on January 6, 2021. Federal prosecutors charged Rhodes and other members with seditious conspiracy, alleging that they planned to use force to prevent the peaceful transfer of presidential power and stockpiled weapons at a hotel in Virginia for a quick reaction force. In November 2022, Rhodes and another leader, Kelly Meggs, were convicted of seditious conspiracy; in May 2023 Rhodes was sentenced to 18 years in prison. Several other members pleaded guilty or were convicted of related offenses. In January 2025, President Donald Trump commuted the sentences of Rhodes and other convicted Oath Keepers leaders and pardoned others convicted in connection with January 6.

Ideology and criticism

The group's rhetoric centered on a list of "orders we will not obey," including disarmament of citizens and imposition of martial law. Anti-extremism researchers, including the Southern Poverty Law Center and the Anti-Defamation League, described the Oath Keepers as extremist and steeped in conspiracy theories, characterizations the group disputed.
