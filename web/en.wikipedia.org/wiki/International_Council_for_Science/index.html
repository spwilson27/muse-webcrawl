International Council for Science

The International Council for Science (ICSU, after its former name International Council of Scientific Unions) was an international non-governmental organization devoted to international cooperation in the advancement of science. Founded in 1931 and based in Paris, it united national scientific academies and international disciplinary unions.

History

The ICSU evolved from the International Research Council (1919) and the International Association of Academies. Over the 20th century it sponsored major global research programmes, including the International Geophysical Year (1957–58), and global-change programmes such as the International Geosphere-Biosphere Programme and the World Climate Research Programme.

Merger

In October 2016, ICSU members voted to merge with the International Social Science Council. The merger took effect in July 2018, creating the International Science Council (ISC), which represents both the natural and social sciences and is likewise headquartered in Paris.

Activities

The ICSU defended the principle of universality of science — the free movement of scientists and open access to data — and coordinated interdisciplinary research on hazards, ecosystems, and science policy, particularly in developing regions through its regional offices.
