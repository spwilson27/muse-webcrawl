1,000 Guineas Stakes

The 1,000 Guineas Stakes is a Group 1 flat horse race in Great Britain open to three-year-old fillies. It is run on the Rowley Mile course at Newmarket over a distance of one mile (1,609 metres), and it is traditionally held on the Sunday of the Guineas Festival at the beginning of May.

1,000 Guineas Stakes

Location: Rowley Mile, Newmarket, England

Inaugurated: 1814

Race type: Flat / Thoroughbred

Distance: 1 mile (1,609 m)

Qualification: Three-year-old fillies

Classification: Group 1; British Classic

History

The race was first run in 1814, five years after the equivalent race for both sexes, the 2,000 Guineas. Both were established by the Jockey Club under the direction of Sir Charles Bunbury. The 1,000 Guineas is named after its original prize fund of 1,000 guineas. It is the first of the season's five British Classic races and the first leg of the Fillies' Triple Crown, followed by the Oaks at Epsom and the St Leger at Doncaster — a treble completed most famously by Oh So Sharp in 1985.

Notable winners

Leading winners include Pretty Polly (1904), Sceptre (1902, the only filly to win four Classics outright), Sun Chariot (1942), Oh So Sharp (1985), Bosra Sham (1996), Virginia Waters (2005), Speciosa (2006), Sky Lantern (2013), Minding (2016), Hermosa (2019), Love (2020), Mother Earth (2021) and Mawj (2023). The most successful trainer in the race's history is Robert Robson, with nine victories in the early 19th century; in the modern era Aidan O'Brien has trained a record number of winners. Lester Piggott rode a record seven winners of the race.

Records

The fastest winning time on the Rowley Mile is held by Ghanaati, who won in 1 minute 34.22 seconds in 2009. The widest winning margin is eight lengths, recorded by Mayonaise in 1859.
