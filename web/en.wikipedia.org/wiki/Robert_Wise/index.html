Robert Earl Wise (September 10, 1914 – September 14, 2005) was an American film director, producer and editor. He won Academy Awards for Best Director and Best Picture for both West Side Story (1961, co-directed with Jerome Robbins) and The Sound of Music (1965), two of the most successful musicals of all time.

Career

Wise began at RKO as an editor, cutting Orson Welles's Citizen Kane (1941) and The Magnificent Ambersons (1942). Given his directing chance by producer Val Lewton, he made atmospheric horror films including The Curse of the Cat People (1944) and The Body Snatcher (1945). His range over a 40-year directing career was exceptional: the science-fiction classic The Day the Earth Stood Still (1951), the boxing noir The Set-Up (1949), the death-row drama I Want to Live! (1958), the gothic horror The Haunting (1963), the war epic The Sand Pebbles (1966), and Star Trek: The Motion Picture (1979).

Honours

Wise served as president of the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences and of the Directors Guild of America, receiving the AFI Life Achievement Award in 1998.
