The University of Amsterdam (Dutch: Universiteit van Amsterdam, abbreviated UvA) is a public research university located in Amsterdam, Netherlands. Founded in 1632 as the Athenaeum Illustre, it is one of the largest and most prestigious universities in Europe and the largest university in the Netherlands by enrollment.

History

The institution began as the Athenaeum Illustre, where scholars such as Gerardus Vossius and Caspar Barlaeus gave inaugural lectures in 1632. It was recognized as a university in the 19th century, receiving the right to confer doctorates in 1877, when it also came under municipal control as the Municipal University of Amsterdam; it became a national institution in 1961 and took its present name.

Organization

The university is organized into seven faculties: Humanities, Social and Behavioural Sciences, Economics and Business, Science, Law, Medicine (at Amsterdam UMC), and Dentistry (ACTA). Its campuses are spread across the city, including the historic city-centre campus, the Roeterseiland campus, and the Amsterdam Science Park.

Notable people

Affiliates include Nobel laureates such as Jacobus Henricus van 't Hoff (the first Nobel laureate in chemistry) and physicists Pieter Zeeman and Frits Zernike.
