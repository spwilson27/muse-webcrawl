CinemaDNG is an open format promoted by Adobe for storing digital cinema footage as raw image data. It extends Adobe's Digital Negative (DNG) still-image format to motion sequences, storing each frame as an individual DNG file within a directory structure, together with audio and metadata files.

Design

Because each frame is a standards-based DNG image (itself built on TIFF/EP), CinemaDNG sequences can be opened by the large ecosystem of raw-photo software, and post-production can apply the familiar raw workflow — white balance, highlight recovery and non-destructive grading — to moving images. The specification defines folder layouts, frame-rate and timecode metadata, and support for lossless compression of the raw sensor data.

Adoption

The format was championed by camera makers without proprietary raw-video formats of their own; Blackmagic Design cameras (such as the original Blackmagic Cinema Camera and Pocket Cinema Camera) recorded CinemaDNG before the company introduced its own Blackmagic RAW in 2018. Editing support is provided natively by DaVinci Resolve and Adobe Premiere Pro. Competing raw-video systems include Apple ProRes RAW and REDCODE.
