West Indies Federation

The West Indies Federation was a short-lived political union of British Caribbean colonies that existed from 3 January 1958 to 31 May 1962. Its members included Jamaica, Trinidad and Tobago, Barbados, the Leeward and Windward Islands, and other territories. The capital was Chaguaramas in Trinidad, and the federation's motto was "To dwell together in unity".

History

The federation was created by the British Caribbean Federation Act 1956, intended as a step toward independence as a single state. Sir Grantley Adams of Barbados became its first and only Prime Minister; the Governor-General was Lord Hailes. Federal elections were held in 1958, but the largest members, Jamaica and Trinidad, retained much real power in their own legislatures.

Collapse

Disputes over taxation, freedom of movement and the strength of the federal government weakened the union. In a 1961 referendum, Jamaican voters chose to leave; Premier Eric Williams of Trinidad then declared that "one from ten leaves nought" and withdrew his colony as well. The federation was dissolved on 31 May 1962. Many of its members later cooperated through CARICOM, formed in 1973.
