Los Nevados National Natural Park (Spanish: Parque Nacional Natural Los Nevados) is a national park in the Cordillera Central of the Colombian Andes, spanning the departments of Caldas, Risaralda, Quindío, and Tolima. It protects a chain of snow-capped volcanoes — the nevados — and the high-altitude páramo and super-páramo ecosystems around them.

Geography

The park's principal peaks are the volcanoes Nevado del Ruiz (5,321 m), Nevado del Tolima (5,216 m), Nevado de Santa Isabel (4,965 m), Nevado del Quindío, Nevado del Cisne, and Paramillo de Santa Rosa. Glaciers cap the highest summits, though they have retreated dramatically in recent decades. The park is a crucial watershed: meltwater feeds rivers supplying millions of people in the coffee-growing region below.

Ecology

Within the park lie Andean forest, páramo grassland dominated by frailejones (Espeletia), and barren super-páramo near the snowline. Wildlife includes the spectacled bear, mountain tapir, Andean condor, and the endemic buffy helmetcrest hummingbird. The Otún lagoon is a notable wetland.

Nevado del Ruiz eruption

On 13 November 1985 the eruption of Nevado del Ruiz melted part of its glacier and generated lahars (volcanic mudflows) that buried the town of Armero, killing roughly 23,000–25,000 people in one of the deadliest volcanic disasters of the 20th century. The volcano remains active and closely monitored, and access to parts of the park is periodically restricted for safety.

Tourism

Visitors enter mainly from Manizales and Pereira for hiking, mountaineering, and hot springs. The park forms part of the UNESCO-recognised Coffee Cultural Landscape region's wider setting and is one of Colombia's most visited national parks.
