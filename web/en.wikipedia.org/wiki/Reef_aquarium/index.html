Reef aquarium

Image placeholder: a mature reef aquarium display.

A reef aquarium is a marine aquarium that prominently displays live corals and other marine invertebrates, together with fish that play a functional role in maintaining the tropical reef environment. It is widely considered the most demanding branch of the fishkeeping hobby because reef organisms require stable, carefully controlled water conditions.

Components

A typical reef system combines an aquarium with intense lighting (commonly LED or metal halide) to sustain photosynthesising corals, strong water movement, a protein skimmer, and live rock that provides biological filtration. A sump and refugium may house additional equipment and macroalgae. Temperature, salinity, calcium, alkalinity and magnesium must be monitored and supplemented as corals consume them for growth.

Livestock

Reef tanks are stocked with stony and soft corals, anemones, shrimp, crabs and reef-safe fish such as clownfish, tangs and gobies. Because many specimens are collected from wild reefs, captive propagation and aquaculture are increasingly promoted, and collection is regulated in many jurisdictions.
