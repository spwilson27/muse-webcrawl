Impact event

Impact event
Caused by: asteroids, comets
Famous examples: Chicxulub, Tunguska, Chelyabinsk
Risk scale: Torino scale

An impact event is a collision between astronomical objects, such as an asteroid or comet striking a planet, releasing energy far beyond ordinary geological processes. Earth bears more than 200 confirmed impact craters, though erosion and plate tectonics erase most.

Effects

A large impact excavates a crater, triggers earthquakes, tsunamis and wildfires, and injects dust and sulfur aerosols into the stratosphere, causing an 'impact winter' that collapses photosynthesis. The 10–15 km Chicxulub impactor of 66 million years ago, which struck the Yucatán Peninsula, is the leading cause of the Cretaceous–Paleogene mass extinction that ended the non-avian dinosaurs.

Frequency and observed events

Small meteoroids burn up constantly. The 1908 Tunguska event flattened some 2,000 km² of Siberian forest; the 2013 Chelyabinsk meteor, about 20 m across, injured around 1,500 people, mostly from broken glass. Kilometre-scale impacts occur on million-year timescales.

Planetary defence

Surveys (Spaceguard, Pan-STARRS, Catalina) catalogue near-Earth objects; NASA's DART mission in 2022 successfully changed an asteroid moonlet's orbit by kinetic impact, the first planetary-defence test. See Chicxulub crater and Near-Earth object.
