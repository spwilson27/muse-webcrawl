Ziehl–Neelsen stain

Named after: Franz Ziehl, Friedrich Neelsen

Type: Acid-fast staining technique

Used for: Detecting Mycobacterium tuberculosis

The Ziehl–Neelsen stain is a bacteriological staining method used to identify acid-fast organisms, principally mycobacteria such as Mycobacterium tuberculosis, the cause of tuberculosis. It was developed in the 1880s by Franz Ziehl and Friedrich Neelsen, building on work by Paul Ehrlich.

Procedure

A smear is flooded with hot carbol fuchsin, whose phenol content helps the dye penetrate the waxy, mycolic-acid-rich cell wall. Acid-fast cells retain the red dye even after decolorisation with acid-alcohol, while other bacteria are decolorised and take up the methylene blue counterstain, appearing blue.

Clinical use

Sputum smear microscopy with Ziehl–Neelsen staining remains a front-line diagnostic test for tuberculosis in much of the world, though fluorescence microscopy and molecular tests offer higher sensitivity.
