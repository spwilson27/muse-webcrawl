Concordat of Worms

The Concordat of Worms (1122), also called the Pactum Calixtinum, was an agreement between Pope Callixtus II and Holy Roman Emperor Henry V that ended the Investiture Controversy, the dispute over whether secular rulers could appoint (invest) bishops and abbots.

Concordat of Worms
Date23 September 1122
PartiesPope Callixtus II; Emperor Henry V
EffectEnded Investiture Controversy

Terms

The emperor renounced investiture with ring and crosier — the symbols of spiritual authority — and granted free election of bishops by cathedral chapters. In return, in Germany the emperor (or his representative) could be present at elections and invest the bishop-elect with his temporal regalia by the sceptre before consecration; elsewhere in the empire the sequence differed slightly.

Significance

The compromise distinguished spiritual from temporal authority, strengthened canon law and papal claims, and became a model for later church–state settlements, though disputes over appointments continued for centuries.
