Gift Aid

Gift Aid is a United Kingdom tax incentive that increases the value of donations made by UK taxpayers to charities and community amateur sports clubs. Under the scheme, a charity can reclaim from HM Revenue and Customs the basic rate of income tax that the donor has already paid on the money donated, at no extra cost to the donor.

How it works

When a donor signs a Gift Aid declaration, the charity treats the donation as made net of basic-rate tax and reclaims the tax from HMRC. At the long-standing basic rate of 20%, a donation of £80 is grossed up to £100, so the charity claims an additional £25 — equivalent to 25p for every £1 donated. Higher-rate and additional-rate taxpayers can claim the difference between their marginal rate and the basic rate on their own tax returns, and may themselves donate that relief to charity.

History

Gift Aid was introduced in the Finance Act 1990, initially for one-off donations of £600 or more. The minimum threshold was lowered in stages and abolished for all donations in the Finance Act 2000, which extended the scheme to regular giving and payroll donations. The Gift Aid Small Donations Scheme (GASDS), introduced in 2013, allows charities to claim a Gift-Aid-like top-up on small cash and contactless donations (up to £30 per donation, subject to an annual cap) without individual declarations.

Conditions

The donor must be a UK taxpayer who has paid at least as much income tax or capital gains tax in the tax year as the charity reclaims; if not, HMRC may recover the difference from the donor. Benefits received in return for a donation (such as event tickets or goods) must fall below set value limits for the donation to qualify. Donations made through the scheme run to billions of pounds of relief annually, making Gift Aid one of the largest sources of tax-advantaged income for the UK charitable sector.
