Ukrainian Christmas

Christmas in Ukraine is celebrated according to traditions that blend Orthodox and Greek Catholic practice with older folk customs. Traditionally observed on 7 January under the Julian calendar, Christmas was officially moved to 25 December in 2023, when Ukraine adopted the Revised Julian calendar for fixed feasts — a change accelerated by the break with Russian religious influence following the 2022 invasion.

Holy Supper

The centerpiece of Christmas Eve (Sviatvechir) is the Holy Supper: a meatless, dairy-free meal of twelve dishes, symbolizing the twelve apostles, served when the first star appears in the evening sky. The meal begins with kutia, a porridge of wheat berries, honey, and poppy seeds, and typically includes borscht, varenyky, holubtsi, fish, and uzvar, a drink of stewed dried fruit. A sheaf of wheat called a didukh, representing the spirits of ancestors, is placed in a place of honor, and an extra place is often set for unexpected guests or the departed.

Caroling and customs

Groups of carolers (koliadnyky) go house to house singing koliadky, sometimes carrying a large illuminated star on a pole. The vertep, a portable puppet nativity theater, is performed in many regions. Festivities continue through the Jordan feast (Epiphany, Vodokhreshcha) on 6 or 19 January, when water is blessed and some faithful plunge into icy rivers through cross-shaped holes.

Modern changes

Under Soviet rule Christmas observance was suppressed and New Year celebrations with Did Moroz (Grandfather Frost) promoted in its place; after 1991 the religious holiday revived strongly. The 2023 calendar reform, endorsed by the Orthodox Church of Ukraine and the Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church, aligned most Ukrainian Christmas observance with 25 December for the first time.
