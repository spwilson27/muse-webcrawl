Jubilee 2000

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Campaigners form a human chain at the 1998 Birmingham G8 summit.

Jubilee 2000 was an international coalition movement, founded in the United Kingdom in 1996, that campaigned for the cancellation of the debts of the world's poorest countries by the year 2000. Inspired by the biblical concept of the Jubilee — a periodic forgiveness of debts described in the Book of Leviticus — the movement united churches, aid agencies, trade unions and campaigners in more than 40 countries.

Campaign

The campaign argued that heavily indebted poor countries were spending more on debt service to wealthy creditors than on health and education, and that much of the debt was odious or already effectively repaid. Jubilee 2000 collected some 24 million petition signatures worldwide, then the largest petition ever assembled. Its most famous action surrounded the 1998 G8 summit in Birmingham, England, where about 70,000 people formed a human chain around the summit venue.

Outcomes and legacy

At the 1999 Cologne G8 summit, leaders agreed to an enhanced debt-relief package linked to the IMF and World Bank's Heavily Indebted Poor Countries (HIPC) initiative, promising on the order of $100 billion in nominal debt relief. Jubilee 2000 wound up at the end of 2000, with successor campaigns — including the Jubilee Debt Campaign in the UK, Jubilee USA, and the broader Make Poverty History coalition of 2005 — continuing to press for full cancellation. Campaigners credit the movement with shifting public debate about sovereign debt and with substantial real reductions in poor-country debt burdens, while critics note that relief was slower and more conditional than promised.
