National Security Advisor (United States)

Assistant to the President for National Security Affairs
AppointerPresident of the United States
FormationMarch 23, 1953
First holderRobert Cutler
OfficeWest Wing, White House

The Assistant to the President for National Security Affairs, commonly referred to as the National Security Advisor (NSA), is a senior aide in the Executive Office of the President, based in the West Wing of the White House, who serves as the chief in-house advisor to the President of the United States on national security issues.

Role

The National Security Advisor heads the staff of the National Security Council, coordinates policy among the State and Defense departments, the intelligence community and the military, and offers the President independent advice. Unlike cabinet secretaries, the advisor is appointed without Senate confirmation.

History

The position was created in 1953 under President Dwight D. Eisenhower, with Robert Cutler as its first holder. Its influence has varied with each presidency: advisors such as Henry Kissinger and Zbigniew Brzezinski became central foreign-policy figures in their own right, sometimes overshadowing the Secretary of State.

Notable holders

Twenty-seven men and women have held the post, among them McGeorge Bundy, Walt Rostow, Brent Scowcroft, Samuel Berger, Condoleezza Rice (the first woman), Susan Rice, H. R. McMaster, John Bolton and Jake Sullivan.
