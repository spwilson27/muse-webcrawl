Specific learning disorder

Specific learning disorder (SLD) is a neurodevelopmental disorder characterised by persistent difficulties in learning and using academic skills, despite adequate instruction and intelligence. It is the diagnostic category used in the DSM-5, which specifies impairment in reading, written expression, or mathematics.

Types

Impairment in reading includes inaccurate or slow word reading and poor comprehension, and is often associated with dyslexia. Impairment in written expression involves spelling, grammar and organisation difficulties, historically linked to dysgraphia. Impairment in mathematics involves difficulties with number sense, calculation and mathematical reasoning, associated with dyscalculia. The difficulties must persist for at least six months despite targeted intervention and must not be better explained by intellectual disability, sensory problems or lack of instruction.

Diagnosis and prevalence

Diagnosis is based on academic history, standardised achievement testing and clinical assessment. Prevalence estimates vary, commonly around 5–15 percent of school-age children across academic domains, and the disorder frequently co-occurs with ADHD and language disorders.

Management

There is no cure, but structured, explicit instruction in the affected skill, accommodations such as extra time or assistive technology, and early intervention substantially improve outcomes.
