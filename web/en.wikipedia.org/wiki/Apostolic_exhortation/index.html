Apostolic exhortation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An apostolic exhortation (Latin: exhortatio apostolica) is a type of communication from the Pope, the head of the Catholic Church. It is a pastoral document in which the Pope encourages a particular virtue, activity, or pastoral priority; unlike an encyclical, it does not generally define doctrine, and unlike an apostolic constitution it does not promulgate binding law. In the hierarchy of papal documents, an apostolic exhortation typically ranks below an apostolic constitution and an encyclical letter in solemnity, but above apostolic letters and messages.

Post-synodal exhortations

A prominent subtype is the post-synodal apostolic exhortation, issued after a meeting of the Synod of Bishops to gather and present the Synod's conclusions and the Pope's pastoral guidance. Important examples include:

  • Evangelii Nuntiandi (1975), Paul VI on evangelisation in the modern world.
  • Familiaris Consortio (1981), John Paul II on the Christian family.
  • Christifideles Laici (1988), John Paul II on the vocation of the lay faithful.
  • Verbum Domini (2010), Benedict XVI on the Word of God.
  • Amoris Laetitia (2016), Francis on love in the family.
  • Querida Amazonia (2020), Francis on the Amazon region.

Other notable exhortations

Popes have also issued apostolic exhortations outside the synodal process, often as programmatic documents of their pontificates. Francis's Evangelii Gaudium (2013), "The Joy of the Gospel", is widely regarded as the manifesto of his pontificate, treating evangelisation, social inclusion, and the renewal of the Church. His Gaudete et Exsultate (2018) addresses the call to holiness, and Laudate Deum (2023) is a follow-up to the encyclical Laudato Si' on the climate crisis. John Paul II's Redemptoris Custos (1989) treats Saint Joseph, and Benedict XVI's Sacramentum Caritatis (2007) treats the Eucharist.

Authority

Because exhortations are pastoral rather than legislative or definitively doctrinal, their teaching carries the authority of the ordinary papal magisterium and is owed religious submission of intellect and will by the faithful, but they do not characteristically introduce new binding norms. Controversy has occasionally surrounded interpretations of exhortations — most notably the debate over Amoris Laetitia and the admission of divorced and civilly remarried Catholics to the sacraments, which prompted the dubia submitted by four cardinals in 2016.
