Quadric

In mathematics, a quadric or quadric surface is a geometric object defined as the set of points satisfying a polynomial equation of degree two in three variables. Quadrics are the three-dimensional analogues of conic sections in the plane.

Types

The principal non-degenerate quadrics in Euclidean space include the sphere, ellipsoid, elliptic paraboloid, hyperbolic paraboloid (a saddle), hyperboloid of one sheet, hyperboloid of two sheets, and the cone; degenerate cases include cylinders and pairs of planes. Their normal forms include:

  • Ellipsoid: x²/a² + y²/b² + z²/c² = 1
  • Hyperboloid of one sheet: x²/a² + y²/b² − z²/c² = 1
  • Elliptic paraboloid: z = x²/a² + y²/b²

Projective geometry

In projective geometry, all non-degenerate quadrics over the complex numbers are equivalent under projective transformations, and over the reals they fall into a small number of classes determined by the signature of the defining quadratic form. Quadrics appear in architecture (cooling towers, saddle roofs) and in optics, as paraboloid and hyperboloid mirrors.
