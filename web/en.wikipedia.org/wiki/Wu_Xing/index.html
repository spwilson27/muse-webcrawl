Wuxing (Chinese philosophy)

Wuxing 五行
PhasesWood (木), Fire (火), Earth (土), Metal (金), Water (水)
Key figureZou Yan (c. 305–240 BCE)
Used inTraditional Chinese medicine, feng shui, astrology, martial arts

Wuxing (Chinese: 五行; pinyin: wǔxíng), usually translated as the Five Phases or Five Agents, is a fivefold conceptual scheme used in many traditional Chinese fields to explain a wide array of phenomena, including cosmic cycles, the succession of dynasties, and the workings of the human body. The five phases are Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal and Water.

Cycles

The phases interact through two principal cycles. In the generating (sheng) cycle, Wood feeds Fire, Fire creates Earth (ash), Earth bears Metal, Metal collects Water, and Water nourishes Wood. In the overcoming (ke) cycle, Wood parts Earth, Earth dams Water, Water extinguishes Fire, Fire melts Metal, and Metal chops Wood. Balance between the cycles maintains harmony; imbalance causes disorder in nature, politics or health.

History and applications

The scheme was systematised by the Warring States thinker Zou Yan and became central to Han dynasty correlative cosmology under Dong Zhongshu. It underlies traditional Chinese medicine (where the phases correspond to organ systems), feng shui, Chinese astrology and fortune-telling, the calendar, music theory and martial arts such as xingyiquan, whose five fists correspond to the five phases.

Correspondences

Each phase is associated with a season, direction, colour, taste, planet and many other categories — for example Wood with spring, east and green; Fire with summer, south and red; Earth with the centre and yellow; Metal with autumn, west and white; Water with winter, north and black or dark blue.
