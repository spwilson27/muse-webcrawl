Kac–Moody algebra

In mathematics, a Kac–Moody algebra (named for Victor Kac and Robert Moody, who independently introduced them around 1968) is a Lie algebra, usually infinite-dimensional, that can be defined by generators subject to relations determined by a generalised Cartan matrix. Kac–Moody algebras include as special cases the finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras and the affine Lie algebras central to conformal field theory and string theory.

Definition

Let A = (aij) be an n × n generalised Cartan matrix: an integer matrix with 2's on the diagonal, non-positive integers off the diagonal, and the property that aij = 0 implies aji = 0. The Kac–Moody algebra associated to A is generated by elements ei, fi, and hi (i = 1, …, n) subject to relations generalising the Chevalley relations of a semisimple Lie algebra: [hi, hj] = 0, [hi, ej] = aij ej, [hi, fj] = −aij fj, [ei, fj] = δij hi, together with the Serre relations ad(ei)1−aij(ej) = 0 and ad(fi)1−aij(fj) = 0 for i ≠ j.

Classification

Kac–Moody algebras are divided into three classes according to the properties of their Cartan matrix. If A is positive definite, the algebra is a finite-dimensional simple Lie algebra. If A is positive semidefinite of corank 1, the algebra is an affine Lie algebra, realisable as a central extension of a loop algebra of a simple Lie algebra, possibly twisted. All remaining matrices give indefinite type algebras, which are far less understood; the hyperbolic ones, a small subclass, have attracted attention for their connections with the Monster group and Borcherds' generalised Kac–Moody (or Borcherds) algebras.

Representation theory and applications

Kac developed a theory of highest-weight representations for Kac–Moody algebras, including the Weyl–Kac character formula, which generalises the Weyl character formula to the infinite-dimensional setting and underlies the Macdonald identities. Affine Kac–Moody algebras appear throughout theoretical physics — in two-dimensional conformal field theory (the Wess–Zumino–Witten models and the Sugawara construction), in string theory, and in the study of integrable systems such as the KdV hierarchy. The E10 and E11 conjectures propose roles for very-extended Kac–Moody algebras in M-theory.
