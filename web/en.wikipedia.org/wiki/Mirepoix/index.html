Mirepoix

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The classic mirepoix: two parts onion to one part each carrot and celery.

A mirepoix (pronounced "meer-PWAH") is a mixture of diced aromatic vegetables cooked gently in fat, used as a flavour base for soups, sauces, stews, and braised dishes in French cuisine. The classic combination is onions, carrots, and celery, in the proportion of roughly two parts onion to one part each carrot and celery, by weight.

History and use

The preparation is named, according to tradition, after Charles-Pierre-Gaston François de Lévis, duc de Mirepoix, an eighteenth-century French aristocrat whose cook is said to have popularised or lent his name to the mixture. The vegetables are cut to a size appropriate to the cooking time of the dish — fine for quick sauces, larger for long braises — and are usually sweated slowly in butter or oil without browning, so they soften and sweeten; a browned variant is used for dark stocks and sauces. Mirepoix may be strained out before serving or left in the finished dish.

Related preparations

Many cuisines have an equivalent aromatic base: Italian soffritto (onion, carrot, celery, often cooked in olive oil), Spanish sofrito, the Cajun and Creole "holy trinity" of onion, celery, and bell pepper in Louisiana cooking, and German Suppengrün (leek, carrot, celeriac). A white mirepoix, using parsnip or the white of leeks instead of carrot, is used for pale stocks and sauces. The addition of ham, bacon, or pancetta produces a mirepoix au gras (with meat), as opposed to the plain or maigre version.
