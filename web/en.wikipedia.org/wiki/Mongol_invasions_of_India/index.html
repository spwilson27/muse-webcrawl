Mongol invasions of India

The Mongol invasions of India were a series of incursions by the Mongol Empire and its successor khanates into the Indian subcontinent between the 13th and 14th centuries. Beginning with Genghis Khan's pursuit of Jalal al-Din Mangburni to the Indus in 1221, repeated Mongol and Chagatai armies attacked the Delhi Sultanate, reaching the outskirts of Delhi several times, but they never succeeded in conquering and holding northern India permanently.

Genghis Khan and the Indus (1221)

In 1221 Genghis Khan pursued the Khwarazmian prince Jalal al-Din to the Indus River and defeated him there. The Mongols sacked towns in the Punjab but did not push deeper into India; Genghis turned back, deterred, according to later accounts, by the heat and by unfavourable omens.

Thirteenth-century raids

Mongol forces occupied Lahore in 1241 and raided the Punjab repeatedly. The sultans of Delhi maintained a strong north-western defensive line of forts and garrisons, and commanders such as Balban organised the frontier against annual incursions from the Mongols of the Chagatai Khanate.

The Chagatai invasions under Alauddin Khalji

The most determined attacks came between 1297 and 1308, when the Chagatai Khanate launched repeated large-scale invasions. Mongol armies under Qutlugh Khwaja and others were defeated by the forces of Alauddin Khalji at battles including Jalandhar, Kili (1299), Amroha (1305) and the Ravi (1306). Generals such as Zafar Khan and Malik Kafur played prominent roles. After 1308 the invasions dwindled, and Delhi's frontier defences held.

Later incursions

Tarmashirin invaded in the early 14th century, and in 1398 Timur — a Turkic conqueror in the Mongol tradition — sacked Delhi catastrophically, though his was a separate episode. The Mughal Empire, founded in 1526 by Babur, a descendant of both Timur and Genghis Khan, finally brought India under a dynasty of Mongol origin, but by conquest from Central Asia rather than by steppe invasion.
