Ivan Mozzhukhin

Ivan Mozzhukhin (1889–1939), star of Russian and French silent cinema.

Ivan Ilyich Mozzhukhin (Russian: Иван Ильич Мозжухин; 26 September 1889 – 18 January 1939), often billed in France as Ivan Mosjoukine, was a Russian silent film actor who became one of the most popular leading men of European cinema in the 1910s and 1920s.

Career

Born in Kondol, in the Saratov Governorate, Mozzhukhin studied law before joining a touring theatre troupe and then the Moscow Art Theatre's orbit. He entered films in 1911 and rose to stardom in productions of the Khanzhonkov studio, including Yakov Protazanov's The Queen of Spades (1916) and Father Sergius (1918), adaptations of Pushkin and Tolstoy that made him the pre-eminent screen actor of late Imperial Russia.

Emigrating after the Revolution, he settled in Paris and became the leading star of the Albatros studio, famed for émigré Russian productions such as Kean (1924), The Late Mathias Pascal (1926), and Casanova (1927). His face is immortalised in film theory through the "Kuleshov effect": Lev Kuleshov's montage experiment intercut a single neutral close-up of Mozzhukhin with images of soup, a child in a coffin, and a woman, and audiences praised the actor's subtle shifts of emotion — demonstrating that viewers derive meaning from editing context.

Later years

A brief Hollywood venture under contract produced the unsuccessful Surrender (1927), and the coming of sound, against which his accent worked, ended his stardom. He died in poverty in Neuilly-sur-Seine in 1939.
