Rudradaman I (Brahmi: 𑀭𑀼𑀤𑁆𑀭𑀤𑁆𑀤𑀸𑀫𑀦) (r. c. 130–150 CE) was a ruler of the Western Kshatrapas dynasty of India, of Saka origin. He is famous for issuing the Junagadh rock inscription, one of the earliest long inscriptions in classical Sanskrit prose, dated to around 150 CE.

Reign

Rudradaman was the grandson of the celebrated satrap Chashtana. He assumed the title of Mahakshatrapa ("Great Satrap") and reigned from Ujjain over a territory that included Gujarat, Sindh, northern Maharashtra and parts of Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh. He is described as having won his territories by his own prowess, and he defeated the Satavahana dynasty to the south, though he spared its ruler owing to a matrimonial alliance.

Junagadh inscription

The Junagadh (Girnar) rock inscription records Rudradaman's repair of the embankment of Sudarshana Lake, originally built under Chandragupta Maurya and improved under Ashoka. The inscription is notable as an early example of chaste Sanskrit prose in an Indian royal inscription, praising Rudradaman's military victories, his patronage of learning, and his mastery of grammar, music, logic and other sciences.

Legacy

Rudradaman is regarded as the greatest of the Western Satraps; under him the dynasty reached its greatest extent and prosperity. His silver coinage became the model for later Gupta coinage.
