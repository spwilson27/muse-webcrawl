Holy water

Holy water is water that has been blessed by a member of the clergy or derived from a well or spring considered holy. It is used in the Roman Catholic Church, Eastern Orthodox Church, Oriental Orthodox Churches, Anglicanism, and some other traditions as a sacramental — for baptism, the blessing of persons, churches, homes, and objects, and as a means of repelling evil.

Preparation and use

In the Latin tradition, holy water is blessed by a priest (or, in some rites, a deacon); traditional formulas mixed exorcised salt into the water. Stoups of holy water are placed at church entrances, where the faithful dip their fingers and sign themselves with the cross, recalling their baptism. A priest sprinkles people or objects using an aspergillum during blessings — at funerals, house blessings, and the Asperges rite before Sunday Mass. In Eastern Orthodoxy, the Great Blessing of Waters is performed at Theophany (6 January), when waters are blessed outdoors — in rivers, lakes, and seas — and the faithful drink and take home the blessed water.

Significance

Holy water is a sacramental, not a sacrament: its efficacy is understood to derive from the prayer of the Church and the disposition of the user, evoking purification, the waters of baptism, and protection from evil. Special forms include Easter water (blessed at the Easter Vigil), Gregorian water (used in the consecration of altars, traditionally mixed with salt, ashes, and wine), and water from pilgrimage shrines such as Lourdes, which, while not formally "holy water" unless blessed, is sought by millions of pilgrims.

In other religions

Lustration with sanctified water appears in many religions — in Hindu ritual (water from the Ganges), Sikh practice (amrit), Shinto purification, and ancient Greek and Roman religion, from which some early Christian usage adapted.
