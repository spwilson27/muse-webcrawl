The Little St Bernard Pass (French: Col du Petit Saint-Bernard; Italian: Colle del Piccolo San Bernardo) is a mountain pass in the Alps on the border between France and Italy, at an elevation of 2,188 metres. It connects Savoie in France with the Aosta Valley in Italy and lies south of the Mont Blanc massif. It is not to be confused with the higher Great St Bernard Pass further north.

History

The pass has been used since prehistoric times; a stone circle some 72 metres in diameter near the summit dates to the Iron Age. The Romans knew it as Alpis Graia and built a road and a way-station (mansio) there. In the 11th century Saint Bernard of Menthon founded a hospice at the summit to shelter travellers, maintained for centuries by Augustinian canons. The pass saw military action several times, notably during the French Revolutionary Wars and in June 1940 during the Second World War, when Italian forces attacked France across it.

Road and sport

The modern road, opened in the nineteenth century, is generally closed by snow from late autumn to late spring. The pass has featured in the Tour de France a handful of times and is a popular route for cyclists and motorists in summer. The ski area of La Rosière lies on the French approach.
