Deflate (stylised DEFLATE) is a lossless data compression algorithm that combines the LZ77 algorithm with Huffman coding. It was designed by Phil Katz for version 2.0 of his PKZIP archiving tool and later specified in RFC 1951. Deflate is the compression at the heart of the ZIP archive format, gzip files, the zlib stream format, and the PNG image format, and it is a standard option for HTTP content encoding.

How it works

A deflate stream consists of a series of blocks. In compressed blocks, the input is first processed with LZ77: repeated sequences are replaced by length–distance pairs referring back to earlier data within a 32-kilobyte sliding window. Literals and the components of those pairs are then encoded with Huffman codes — either fixed trees defined by the specification or dynamic trees transmitted with the block when that saves space. Data may also be stored uncompressed in "stored" blocks.

Implementations

The free zlib library by Jean-loup Gailly and Mark Adler became the near-universal implementation; its compression levels 1–9 trade speed against ratio, with level 6 the default. The patent-free status of deflate — in contrast to the then-patented LZW used in GIF — was a key reason for its adoption in PNG and across the early web. Modern optimised variants such as Zopfli and libdeflate squeeze out extra ratio or speed while remaining format-compatible.
