Dwarka is a town and a municipality in Devbhoomi Dwarka district in the state of Gujarat in Western India. It is located on the western shore of the Okhamandal Peninsula on the right bank of the Gomti River at the mouth of the Gulf of Kutch facing the Arabian Sea. It is one of the seven ancient cities (Sapta Puri) in Hinduism and one of the four sacred pilgrimage sites of the Char Dham.

Legend

Dwarka is identified in Hindu tradition with the Dvaraka, the legendary capital city of the god Krishna, said to have been founded after Krishna left Mathura. According to the Mahabharata and the Bhagavata Purana, the city was built on land reclaimed from the sea and, following Krishna's death, was swallowed by the ocean. Marine archaeology off the modern coast has found submerged structures, though their interpretation remains debated.

Dwarkadhish Temple

The town's principal temple, the Dwarkadhish Temple (Jagat Mandir), is a five-storeyed limestone and sandstone temple supported by 72 pillars, believed to have been built or rebuilt over many centuries, with its main shrine dated to roughly the 15th–16th century. The temple is a major centre of the Pushtimarg tradition associated with the saint Vallabha.

Geography and economy

Dwarka has a hot semi-arid climate. The economy depends heavily on pilgrimage tourism, with agriculture and fishing also important. The nearby island of Bet Dwarka, reached by ferry, contains further Krishna temples, and the Nageshvara Jyotirlinga temple lies nearby.
