Philosophy of sex

Philosophy of sex
Scope: Conceptual, metaphysical and ethical questions about sex and sexuality
Key figures: Plato, Augustine, Aquinas, Kant, Sartre, Foucault, Thomas Nagel, Alan Soble, Raja Halwani

The philosophy of sex concerns the conceptual analysis and ethical evaluation of human sexual activity, desire and identity. Long neglected by mainstream philosophers — who often treated sex as morally suspect or philosophically trivial — it emerged as a recognised field in the later twentieth century, with the founding of the Society for the Philosophy of Sex and Love and works by philosophers such as Alan Soble.

Conceptual questions

Philosophers ask what makes an act a sexual act: definitions have appealed to sexual desire, sexual arousal, sexual pleasure, or contact with parts of the body conventionally deemed sexual — each proposal facing counterexamples. Related debates concern the nature of sexual desire and attraction, the definition of perversion (statistical, teleological and moral senses have all been proposed), and the concepts of sexual orientation and gender.

Ethical questions

Sexual ethics addresses consent (its nature, and conditions under which it is undermined by deception, coercion or incapacity), objectification, prostitution, pornography, adultery, casual sex and sexual relationships between unequals in power. Historical theories include natural law accounts (Augustine, Aquinas) that tie sex to procreation, Kant's view that sex outside marriage treats persons as mere means, and Thomas Nagel's account of sexual interaction as reciprocal embodied recognition. Michel Foucault's History of Sexuality reshaped discussion by arguing that "sexuality" itself is a historically constructed apparatus of knowledge and power.
