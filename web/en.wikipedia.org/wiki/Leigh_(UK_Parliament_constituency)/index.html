Leigh was a parliamentary constituency in Greater Manchester, England, centred on the town of Leigh. Created for the 1885 general election, it returned one Member of Parliament (MP) to the House of Commons of the Parliament of the United Kingdom, elected by the first-past-the-post system. The constituency was abolished for the 2024 general election, when its area was incorporated mainly into the new seat of Leigh and Atherton.

History

The seat covered a former coal-mining and mill town area in the south of the Metropolitan Borough of Wigan. From 1922 until 2019 Leigh was continuously represented by the Labour Party, becoming one of its safest seats in the North West; its long-serving MP Andy Burnham (2001–2017) held cabinet office as Chief Secretary to the Treasury, Secretary of State for Culture, Media and Sport and Shadow Home Secretary before leaving Parliament to become Mayor of Greater Manchester.

In the December 2019 general election, amid the collapse of Labour's "Red Wall," the seat was won by the Conservative candidate James Grundy — the first non-Labour MP for Leigh in 97 years. Grundy stood down at the 2024 election, at which the successor constituency of Leigh and Atherton was won by Labour's Jo Platt, who had previously represented Leigh from 2017 to 2019.

Boundaries

In its final form (2010–2024) the constituency comprised the wards of Astley Mosley Common (part), Atherleigh, Atherton (part), Golborne and Lowton West, Leigh East, Leigh South, Leigh West, Lowton East and Tyldesley (part) — in practice the town of Leigh with Golborne and Lowton to the south. The area is largely residential and suburban in character toward Wigan and Greater Manchester, with the Leigh Sports Village and the Leeds and Liverpool Canal among local landmarks.
