Ents are a species of sentient, tree-like beings in J. R. R. Tolkien's fantasy world of Middle-earth. They appear in The Lord of the Rings as ancient shepherds of the trees, created to protect the forests from the depredations of Orcs, Dwarves, and Men. The oldest and best known Ent is Treebeard (also called Fangorn), who describes himself as one of the oldest living things in Middle-earth.

Description

Ents resemble the trees they guard, standing roughly fourteen feet tall, with bark-like skin, deep-set eyes, and the ability to move with surprising speed when roused. They are immensely strong — able to tear apart stone and iron — but famously slow to decide: their language, Entish, is so deliberate that an Entmoot, a gathering of Ents, may take days to reach a conclusion. Their female counterparts, the Entwives, loved gardens and cultivated lands; they vanished long before the War of the Ring, and the Ents have slowly dwindled, some becoming "treeish" and ceasing to move at all — a state reflected in the related Huorns.

In the story

In The Two Towers, the hobbits Merry and Pippin encounter Treebeard in Fangorn Forest, and their account of Saruman's destruction of the trees persuades the Entmoot to march on Isengard. The Ents destroy Saruman's fortress works, dam the river Isen, and flood the pits of Isengard — one of the decisive blows against Saruman's power. The name "Ent" comes from the Old English word for a giant, and Tolkien drew on the talking trees of folklore and on his own love of trees and hatred of industrial destruction.
