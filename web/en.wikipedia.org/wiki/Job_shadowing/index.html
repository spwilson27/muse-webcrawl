Job shadowing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Job shadowing
TypeCareer exploration; workplace learning
Typical durationA few hours to several days
Used byStudents, new employees, career changers

Job shadowing is a type of on-the-job learning in which a person interested in a particular occupation follows and observes a trained and experienced employee performing that job, in order to gain a realistic understanding of the work. It is widely used in career exploration for students, in onboarding for new hires, and in leadership development when preparing employees for promotion.

Purpose

Unlike an internship, job shadowing is primarily observational: the shadower does not normally perform the work or take responsibility for output. Its goals are to expose the observer to the daily tasks, skills, workplace culture and challenges of a role, helping them make informed career choices or transition smoothly into a new position.

Programmes

Schools commonly arrange shadowing days—such as the "Take Our Kids to Work" initiatives in North America—for students in their early teens. Employers also use shadowing within graduate schemes and cross-training, and in professions such as medicine, applicants are often expected to shadow practitioners before applying, to evidence informed commitment.

Benefits and limitations

Shadowing is inexpensive and low-risk for both parties, and can improve recruitment fit and retention. Its limitations include its passive nature, the possibility that a short visit gives an unrepresentative picture, and confidentiality or safeguarding constraints in settings such as healthcare and law.
