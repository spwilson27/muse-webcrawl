The Canadian Red Ensign is a flag consisting of a red field with the Union Jack in the upper hoist canton and the shield of the royal arms of Canada in the fly. It served as Canada's civil ensign and, for most practical purposes, as the country's national flag from the late 19th century until 15 February 1965, when it was replaced by the current Maple Leaf flag.

History

From about 1868, Canadians began informally defacing the British Red Ensign with the arms of the Canadian provinces. An Admiralty warrant in 1892 authorised the red ensign with the composite provincial shield for Canadian merchant vessels, and versions proliferated as provinces were added. In 1921 King George V proclaimed a new coat of arms for Canada, and the shield on the ensign was standardised; a 1924 order authorised its use on Canadian government buildings abroad, and in 1945 it was authorised for use on federal buildings within Canada pending adoption of a national flag.

Canadian soldiers carried the Red Ensign in both world wars — it flew at Vimy Ridge and over Juno Beach — and it was under this flag that Canada fought at Dieppe and in Korea. The 1957 version, adopted after a minor redesign of the shield (red maple leaves replacing green), was the last official form.

Replacement

After the Great Canadian Flag Debate of 1964, Parliament adopted George Stanley's red-and-white maple leaf design. The new flag was proclaimed by Queen Elizabeth II and first flown on 15 February 1965 — now marked as National Flag of Canada Day — ending the Red Ensign's official use, though it remains flown by some veterans' groups and appears in the provincial flags of Manitoba and Ontario, both of which adopted red ensigns bearing their own arms in 1965–66.
