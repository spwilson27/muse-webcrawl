Highland dance


The sword dance (Gillie Callum)

Scottish Highland dance (also Highland dancing) is a style of competitive dancing developed in Scotland in the 19th and 20th centuries, at events such as the Highland games, where it is performed to the accompaniment of the Great Highland bagpipe. It shares the technical vocabulary of turnout, pointed feet, and precise arm positions with ballet, but is danced in a distinctive style in Highland dress.

The dances

The traditional Highland dances include the Highland Fling, the sword dance (Gillie Callum, performed over two crossed swords), the Seann Triubhas, and the strathspey and reel. A second category, the "national dances," includes such dances as the Scottish Lilt, the Flora MacDonald's Fancy, the Irish Jig (a character dance), and the Sailor's Hornpipe, which are performed in different costumes. Legend associates several dances with battle traditions — the sword dance is said to date back to Malcolm III of Scotland in the 11th century — though most choreographed forms were standardized much later.

Competition and governance

Highland dancing is today practised worldwide, especially in countries of the Scottish diaspora such as Canada, Australia, New Zealand, South Africa, and the United States. The Royal Scottish Official Board of Highland Dancing (RSOBHD), founded in 1950, sets the technique standards and rules used by most competitions, at which dancers — the large majority of them female in the modern era — are judged on timing, technique, and presentation across age and skill categories culminating in championship events.
