Stranger in a Strange Land is a 1961 science fiction novel by American author Robert A. Heinlein. It tells the story of Valentine Michael Smith, a human who comes to Earth in early adulthood after being born on the planet Mars and raised by Martians, and explores his interaction with—and eventual transformation of—Terran culture.

The title is an allusion to the phrase in Exodus 2:22. The novel won the 1962 Hugo Award for Best Novel and became an international bestseller and a defining text of the 1960s counterculture.

Plot summary

Valentine Michael Smith, the son of astronauts from the first expedition to Mars, is orphaned when the crew dies and is raised by the native Martians. Years later, a second expedition brings him back to Earth. Because of the legal doctrine of the Larkin Decision, Smith is heir to the entire exploration party's fortune and, arguably, owner of Mars itself, making him a political pawn of the world government.

Smith, who shares water and forms deep bonds in the Martian fashion, is sheltered by the curmudgeonly author Jubal Harshaw. As Smith learns human customs—including religion, money, jealousy, and sex—he develops psychic abilities taught by Martian discipline, including telekinesis and the ability to "discorporate" others. He eventually founds his own church, the Church of All Worlds, preaching free love and communal living, and is stoned to death by a mob, accepting his martyrdom.

Themes

The novel questions institutional religion, sexual mores, government, and the nature of language. The Martian word grok—to understand something so thoroughly that the observer becomes part of the observed—entered the English language through the novel's popularity. Heinlein drew on themes from Rudyard Kipling's The Jungle Book and the feral child tradition.

Publication history

Heinlein conceived the story idea in 1948 and worked on it intermittently for over a decade. The published 1961 version was substantially cut from his manuscript; the uncut version was published posthumously in 1991, after the original manuscript was recovered from the author's archives.

Legacy

The novel influenced the counterculture of the 1960s, and the water-sharing ritual and Church of All Worlds inspired a real neo-pagan church of the same name founded in 1962. It remains one of the best-known works of science fiction of the twentieth century.
