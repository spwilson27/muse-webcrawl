Overview

Hans Poulsen Egede (1686–1758) was a Dano-Norwegian Lutheran missionary who founded the Danish mission and colony in Greenland, earning the title "Apostle of Greenland". Sailing in 1721 with royal support to re-establish contact with the lost Norse settlements (and to convert their presumed Catholic descendants to Lutheranism), he found instead the Inuit, whose language he learned and among whom he worked for fifteen years.

Work and legacy

Egede founded Godthåb ("Good Hope") in 1728 — modern Nuuk, Greenland's capital — and documented Inuit life and Greenlandic nature in his Description of Greenland (1745), including reports (and a famous drawing) of a great sea serpent. The Norse Eastern Settlement he sought had vanished centuries earlier. Egede's legacy in Greenland is contested today: he is honoured as a founder of the modern Greenlandic church and written language tradition (his successors, especially his son Poul, translated scripture into Greenlandic), while his colonising mission is criticised in Inuit and Greenlandic debates; his statue in Nuuk was vandalised in 2020.
