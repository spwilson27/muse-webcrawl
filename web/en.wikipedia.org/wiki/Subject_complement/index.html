Subject complement

In grammar, a subject complement is a predicative expression that follows a linking verb (copula) and complements the subject of the sentence by renaming it or describing it. In "She is a doctor", a doctor is a subject complement; in "She is happy", happy is one.

Types

Subject complements take two main forms: a predicate nominative (a noun or pronoun that renames the subject, as in "He became chairman") and a predicate adjective (an adjective describing the subject, as in "The soup smells good"). Some grammars also count certain prepositional phrases after copular verbs as complements.

Linking verbs

The most common linking verb is be; others include become, seem, appear, feel, look, smell, taste, sound, grow and remain. Subject complements contrast with objects: they follow intransitive copulas and agree with the subject rather than receiving an action.
