The Sabaean kingdom (Kingdom of Saba, or Sheba) was an ancient South Arabian kingdom centred in what is now Yemen, flourishing from roughly the 8th century BC until the late 3rd century AD. Its wealth rested on control of the incense trade routes carrying frankincense and myrrh from southern Arabia to the Mediterranean world.

The ruins of ancient Ma'rib, capital of the Sabaean kingdom.

History

Sabaean rulers, initially bearing the title mukarrib (federator) and later malik (king), built their capital at Ma'rib, where the great Ma'rib Dam irrigated a large oasis. The kingdom expanded at times to dominate much of South Arabia, competing with the neighbouring kingdoms of Minaea, Qataban and Hadhramaut, and later with Himyar, which finally absorbed Saba around 275 AD.

Culture and religion

The Sabaeans wrote in the monumental Sabaic script and built temples such as the Awwam temple (Mahram Bilqis) near Ma'rib, dedicated to the moon god Almaqah. Tradition associates Saba with the biblical Queen of Sheba, who is said to have visited King Solomon; the identification is debated by historians. Monumental irrigation works, inscriptions recording building and warfare, and distinctive alabaster sculpture characterise the kingdom's material culture.
