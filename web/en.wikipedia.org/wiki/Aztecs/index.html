The Aztecs were a Mesoamerican culture of central Mexico whose empire, ruled from the island city of Tenochtitlan (founded 1325), dominated the region from 1428 until the Spanish conquest of 1521. Their language, Nahuatl, gave English words including tomato, avocado and chocolate.

Conquest

Hernán Cortés, allied with the Aztecs' enemies and aided unknowingly by smallpox, besieged and destroyed Tenochtitlan; Mexico City rose on its ruins. Aztec art, calendar, chinampa farming and a ritual tradition including human sacrifice remain subjects of study and debate.
