The Four Books

The Four Books (Chinese: 四書) is a 2011 novel by Chinese writer Yan Lianke, set in a labour camp during the Great Leap Forward and the ensuing Great Chinese Famine. The novel's title refers ironically to the Confucian Four Books, and its four interwoven narrative strands are styled as a criminal's confessional record, a dying writer's manuscript, a folk-mythological account, and an official bureaucratic report.

Plot and themes

In the "99th district" re-education camp near the Yellow River, intellectuals — among them the Writer, the Scholar, the Musician, and the Theologian — are driven by the camp's childlike, godlike commandant, "the Child", to produce impossible quantities of steel and wheat. In exchange for promises of freedom, the Writer secretly documents the camp in reports to the authorities and in a private chronicle. Famine reduces the inmates to desperation and, ultimately, to acts they record with horrified detachment. The novel is a savage satire of utopian political campaigns and of intellectuals' complicity.

Publication

Unable to publish the novel in mainland China — Yan reportedly rewrote it many times over some two decades — he released it in Hong Kong and Taiwan in 2011. An English translation by Carlos Rojas appeared in 2015 and was shortlisted for the Man Booker International Prize in 2016.
