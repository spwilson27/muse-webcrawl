Krämerbrücke

The Krämerbrücke, Erfurt, lined with half-timbered houses

The Krämerbrücke (German for "Merchants' Bridge") is a medieval arch bridge in the Thuringian city of Erfurt, Germany, lined on both sides with inhabited half-timbered houses. Spanning the Breitstrom, a branch of the Gera River, it is the longest bridge in Europe continuously inhabited along its entire length—125 metres—with 32 houses standing upon it.

History

A wooden bridge on the site is documented from 1117, forming part of the Via Regia trade route. After repeated fires, the stone bridge with its five arches was completed in 1325. Originally 62 stalls and houses stood on the bridge, later consolidated into the present buildings. Churches once stood at both ends; only the Ägidienkirche at the eastern end survives. The houses were originally occupied by merchants selling luxury goods—hence the name Krämer (merchants or grocers).

Today

The bridge remains a landmark of Erfurt's old town. Its houses contain small shops, galleries, workshops and residences, many specialising in crafts and regional products. The Krämerbrückenfest, held each June, is one of Thuringia's largest old-town festivals.
