Stress–energy tensor

Components of the stress–energy tensor in a coordinate basis

The stress–energy tensor, sometimes called the stress–energy–momentum tensor or energy–momentum tensor, is a tensor physical quantity in general relativity and relativistic field theory that describes the density and flux of energy and momentum in spacetime. It appears on the right-hand side of the Einstein field equations, where it acts as the source of the gravitational field, generalising the role of mass density in Newtonian gravity.

Definition

The tensor, conventionally written Tμν, is symmetric in its two indices. Its component T00 is the density of relativistic mass-energy; components T0i describe the flux of energy (equivalently, momentum density); components Ti0 describe momentum flux; and the spatial block Tij is the flux of momentum — that is, stress, including pressure and shear. Because the Einstein tensor is divergence-free, the stress–energy tensor must satisfy local conservation of energy and momentum: ∇μTμν = 0.

Examples

For a perfect fluid, the tensor is determined by the energy density ρ, pressure p and four-velocity u: Tμν = (ρ + p/c²)uμuν + p gμν. For the electromagnetic field, the stress–energy tensor is built from the field tensor F and is traceless, reflecting the masslessness of the photon. In field theory the canonical tensor obtained from Noether's theorem can be symmetrised by the Belinfante–Rosenfeld procedure; the equivalent Hilbert definition varies the matter action with respect to the metric.

Variants

Related objects include the Landau–Lifshitz pseudotensor, an attempt to describe the energy of the gravitational field itself within a tensor-like object, which general relativity does not allow to be defined in a fully coordinate-independent way.
