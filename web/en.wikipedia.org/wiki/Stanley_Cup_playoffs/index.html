Stanley Cup playoffs

The Stanley Cup playoffs (French: Les séries éliminatoires de la Coupe Stanley) are the annual elimination tournament of the National Hockey League (NHL), held after the regular season to determine the league champion and winner of the Stanley Cup. Sixteen teams — eight from each conference — qualify, and the champion must win sixteen games across four best-of-seven rounds, making the two-month tournament one of the most grueling in professional team sports.

History

The Stanley Cup was donated in 1893 by Lord Stanley of Preston, Canada's Governor General, and was originally a challenge cup for amateur Canadian clubs. Professional teams began competing for it in the 1900s, and in 1915 the National Hockey Association and Pacific Coast Hockey Association agreed to an annual championship series. The NHL took sole possession of the Cup in 1926 after the western leagues folded. Formats changed many times over the decades: early rounds varied from total-goals series to best-of-three and best-of-five, before the league settled on an all best-of-seven format in 1987. Great dynasties emerged in the tournament, including the Montreal Canadiens of the 1950s (five straight Cups) and 1970s, the New York Islanders (four straight, 1980–83), and the Edmonton Oilers (five in seven years, 1984–90).

Current format

Since the 2013–14 season, each conference sends its top three teams from each division plus two wild-card teams. The division winners play the wild cards and the second- and third-place teams in each division meet in the first round, a bracket-style format intended to build divisional rivalries; winners advance through the second round to the conference finals and then the Stanley Cup Final. Home-ice advantage goes to the team with the better regular-season record. Playoff games cannot end in a tie: teams play successive 20-minute sudden-death overtime periods at five-on-five until a goal is scored, which has produced some of the longest games in hockey history, including the 1936 Detroit–Montreal Maroons game decided in the sixth overtime.

Traditions

Famous playoff traditions include the handshake line at the end of each series, the growing of "playoff beards," the crowd custom of throwing octopuses onto the ice in Detroit, the Conn Smythe Trophy awarded to the most valuable player of the playoffs, and the presentation of the Stanley Cup itself — uniquely among major North American trophies, it is handed first to the winning team's captain, and each champion player traditionally gets the Cup for a day during the off-season. Players also traditionally refuse to touch the conference championship trophies, to avoid jinxing the final.
