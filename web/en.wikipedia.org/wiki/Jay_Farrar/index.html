Jay Farrar

Jay Farrar performing with Son Volt

Jay Farrar (born December 26, 1966) is an American songwriter and musician best known as a founding figure of the alternative country movement, first as co-leader of Uncle Tupelo and later as the frontman of Son Volt.

Uncle Tupelo

Farrar formed Uncle Tupelo in Belleville, Illinois, with Jeff Tweedy and Mike Heidorn. Between 1990 and 1994 the band fused punk rock energy with country and folk traditions on albums such as No Depression, Still Feel Gone, March 16–20, 1992, and Anodyne. Internal tensions between Farrar and Tweedy led to the band's breakup in 1994; Tweedy went on to form Wilco.

Son Volt and solo work

Farrar immediately formed Son Volt, whose debut Trace (1995) — featuring the songs "Windfall" and "Tear Stained Eye" — was critically acclaimed as a landmark of alt-country. After several albums and a hiatus in the early 2000s, during which Farrar released solo records such as Sebastopol (2001), Son Volt re-formed with a new lineup and continued recording, including American Central Dust (2009) and Union (2019).

Farrar also collaborated with Ben Gibbard on the soundtrack One Fast Move or I'm Gone (2009), setting Jack Kerouac's writing to music, and with the supergroup Gob Iron. His gravel-voiced singing and plainspoken, literate lyrics remain defining features of the genre he helped create.
