Skid row

A skid row is a neighbourhood in a city characterised by concentrated poverty, homelessness and inexpensive single-room-occupancy hotels. The term is most associated with districts in North American cities, notably Skid Row in Los Angeles, the Downtown Eastside of Vancouver and the Tenderloin in San Francisco.

Etymology

The phrase is generally traced to "skid road", a track of greased logs used to skid timber downhill in logging camps of the Pacific Northwest. Seattle's Yesler Way, along which logs were moved to Henry Yesler's mill, became a district of cheap lodging and saloons; over time "skid road" was corrupted to "skid row" and applied generally.

Characteristics

Skid rows developed around rail yards and docks where transient labourers gathered. They typically feature shelters, missions, soup kitchens and social services, alongside high rates of substance use disorder and mental illness among residents. Urban renewal and gentrification have shrunk many such districts.
