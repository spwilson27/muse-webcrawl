Haitham bin Tariq Al Said (Arabic: هيثم بن طارق آل سعيد; born 13 October 1955) is the Sultan of Oman, having succeeded his cousin Qaboos bin Said on 11 January 2020. He previously served as Minister of Heritage and Culture in the Sultanate of Oman.

Early life and education

Haitham bin Tariq was born in Muscat, the capital of Oman. He is a member of the Al Said royal family and a grandson of Sultan Taimur bin Feisal. His father, Tariq bin Taimur, served as prime minister under Sultan Qaboos. Haitham graduated from Pembroke College, University of Oxford, in 1979, where he studied in the Foreign Service Programme.

Career

In the 1980s, Haitham served as the first head of the Oman Football Association. He later worked in the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, holding positions including undersecretary for political affairs and secretary general. From 2002 until his accession he was Minister of Heritage and Culture, overseeing the preservation of Oman's cultural sites and traditions. He was also closely involved in developing Oman Vision 2040, the country's long-term economic development plan.

Reign

Following the death of Sultan Qaboos on 10 January 2020, a sealed letter left by the late sultan named Haitham as his successor. He was sworn in the following day. As sultan, Haitham is head of state and holds wide-ranging executive authority. His reign has focused on economic diversification, fiscal reform — including the introduction of a value-added tax in 2021 — and maintaining Oman's traditional role as a neutral mediator in regional diplomacy. In January 2021 he issued a new Basic Law establishing the formal position of crown prince, and his eldest son, Theyazin bin Haitham, was named to the role.

Family

Haitham is married to Ahad bint Abdullah Al Busaidiyah. Their children include Theyazin bin Haitham and Bilarab bin Haitham.
