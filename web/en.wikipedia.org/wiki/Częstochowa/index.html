Częstochowa is a city in southern Poland on the Warta River, with a population of roughly 214,000 inhabitants, making it one of the larger cities of the Silesian Voivodeship. The city is known worldwide for the Jasna Góra Monastery, home to the revered icon of the Black Madonna of Częstochowa, and is one of the most important Catholic pilgrimage destinations in the world.

History

Częstochowa was founded in the 11th century and received town rights in the 14th century. The Pauline monastery of Jasna Góra was established in 1382, and the Black Madonna icon was credited with miraculously saving the monastery during the Swedish siege of 1655, an event that became a defining moment in Polish national history and was immortalised in Henryk Sienkiewicz's novel The Deluge. In the 19th century the city grew into an industrial centre, aided by the Warsaw–Vienna railway.

Pilgrimage

Millions of pilgrims visit Jasna Góra each year, many travelling on foot in organised pilgrimages during the summer months, particularly for the Feast of the Assumption in August and the Feast of Our Lady of Częstochowa on 26 August. Pope John Paul II, himself Polish, visited the shrine several times, and the World Youth Day of 1991 was held in Częstochowa.

Economy and culture

Historically a centre of iron and steel production, mining and textiles, the city's economy has diversified into services, trade and higher education; it hosts the Jan Długosz University and the Częstochowa University of Technology. The city is also known for speedway, with the Włókniarz Częstochowa club among Poland's leading teams.
