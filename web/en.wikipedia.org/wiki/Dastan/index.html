Dastan

A dastan (Persian: داستان, "story") is an ornate form of oral history in Persian, Turkic and Central Asian literature, a lengthy epic tale in prose or verse that recounts the heroic deeds, romances and adventures of a hero. Dastans formed a central genre of folk literature across Iran, Central Asia, Azerbaijan, Turkey and northern India.

Characteristics

Dastans were traditionally performed by professional storytellers — the Persian naqqal or the Turkish meddah and aşık — in coffeehouses and public squares. Performances combined narration, impersonation and sometimes illustration with painted scrolls or banners. Though rooted in oral tradition, many dastans were written down and circulated as popular romances.

Famous examples

Well-known dastans include the Dastan-e Amir Hamza (the Hamzanama), relating the adventures of Amir Hamza, uncle of the Prophet Muhammad; the Turkish Book of Dede Korkut, the dastans of Köroğlu, and Persian heroic cycles connected with the Shahnameh of Ferdowsi. In Mughal India, dastangoi flourished in Urdu, producing enormous multi-volume cycles such as the Tilism-e Hoshruba.

Legacy

The dastan tradition declined in the 20th century with the spread of print and broadcast media, though the art of dastangoi has seen a revival in India and Pakistan in recent decades.
