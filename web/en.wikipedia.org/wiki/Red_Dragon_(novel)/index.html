Red Dragon (novel)

Red Dragon
Author: Thomas Harris
Published: 1981
Genre: Horror, crime thriller
Series: Hannibal Lecter
Followed by: The Silence of the Lambs

Red Dragon is a 1981 horror novel by American author Thomas Harris. It introduced the character of Dr. Hannibal Lecter, a brilliant psychiatrist and cannibalistic serial killer, and FBI profiler Will Graham, the man who caught him. The novel was adapted to film as Manhunter (1986) and again as Red Dragon (2002), and forms a major arc of the television series Hannibal.

Plot

Former FBI profiler Will Graham, who retired after nearly being killed capturing Hannibal Lecter, is persuaded by Jack Crawford to help hunt a serial killer dubbed the "Tooth Fairy", who has murdered two families on lunar cycles. Graham visits the imprisoned Lecter for insight, while the killer — Francis Dolarhyde, a film-processing technician obsessed with William Blake's painting The Great Red Dragon and the Woman Clothed in Sun — corresponds with Lecter through personal ads, endangering Graham's family. The investigation converges through tabloid reporter Freddy Lounds, Dolarhyde's relationship with the blind Reba McClane, and a final confrontation at Graham's Florida home.

Themes and reception

The novel is noted for its forensic detail, its dual portrait of investigator and killer as mirror images, and for Lecter — a relatively minor character here — whose popularity drove the sequels. It is regarded as a landmark of the modern serial-killer thriller.
