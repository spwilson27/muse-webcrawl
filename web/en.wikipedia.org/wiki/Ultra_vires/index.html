Ultra vires

Ultra vires is a Latin phrase meaning "beyond the powers". In law it describes acts attempted by a person, company or public body that exceed the authority granted to them, and which are therefore invalid or unenforceable. The opposite is intra vires, "within the powers".

Public law

In administrative law, the ultra vires doctrine is a foundation of judicial review: government ministers, agencies and local authorities may only act within the powers conferred on them by statute, and courts can quash decisions that go further. The doctrine was central to landmark cases such as Anisminic v Foreign Compensation Commission.

Company law

Historically, a company's objects clause limited its capacity, and contracts beyond those objects were void even against outsiders, a rule illustrated by Ashbury Railway Carriage and Iron Co v Riche (1875). Modern company statutes in most common-law jurisdictions have largely abolished the external effect of ultra vires for companies, protecting third parties dealing with them in good faith.
