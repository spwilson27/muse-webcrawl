Wounded Knee (Lakota: Čhaŋkpé Ópi, "wounded knee") is a census-designated place on the Pine Ridge Indian Reservation in Oglala Lakota County, South Dakota, United States. It is named for Wounded Knee Creek, which runs through the community, and is one of the most historically significant places in Native American history.

Wounded Knee Massacre

On 29 December 1890, the United States Army's 7th Cavalry killed roughly 250–300 Lakota men, women, and children near Wounded Knee Creek in the Wounded Knee Massacre, the culminating tragedy of the Ghost Dance movement. The dead were buried in a mass grave on a hill beside the creek, where a memorial now stands. The event is widely regarded as the end of armed resistance by Plains Indians.

1973 occupation

From 27 February to 8 May 1973, about 200 Oglala Lakota and followers of the American Indian Movement occupied the town in the Wounded Knee incident, protesting the tribal government of Richard Wilson and the U.S. government's treatment of Native peoples. The 71-day standoff with federal marshals and the FBI drew worldwide attention; two occupiers were killed and a federal marshal was paralyzed.

Geography and community

The community is small and rural, with a population of a few hundred, predominantly Oglala Lakota. Wounded Knee Creek is a tributary of the White River. The site of the massacre and the 1973 occupation is preserved informally, with a small museum and memorial maintained locally.
