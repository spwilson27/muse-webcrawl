Dating sims, or romance simulation games, are a subgenre of simulation video games, usually Japanese, in which the player's goal is to develop a romantic relationship with one of several characters. The player typically manages a schedule and statistics — studying, working, exercising, choosing gifts and dialogue — to raise a chosen character's affection over an in-game period of weeks or months, pursuing one of several endings.

History

The genre was defined by Konami's Tokimeki Memorial (1994), set in a high school, whose stat-raising and "bomb" mechanics (neglected characters spreading bad rumours about the player) became templates for countless successors. Dating elements were combined with other genres in series such as Sakura Wars (tactics and theatre) and Persona (role-playing). Games aimed at women players, with casts of male love interests, form the related otome game category.

Characteristics

Dating sims overlap heavily with visual novels but are distinguished by gameplay systems rather than pure branching narrative: parameter management, calendars, and repeatable daily choices. Adult-oriented variants (eroge) are a significant part of the Japanese market, though many major titles are all-ages. Outside Japan the genre remained niche until the 2010s, when indie and Western titles such as Dream Daddy (2017) and numerous itch.io and mobile games reached wide audiences.
