The 2007–08 Writers Guild of America strike was a labour action by the Writers Guild of America, East (WGAE) and the Writers Guild of America, West (WGAW) against the Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers. More than 12,000 film and television writers went on strike on 5 November 2007 and remained out for 100 days, until 12 February 2008.

Issues

The central dispute concerned compensation for work distributed through new media — downloads and internet streaming — as well as residuals for DVD sales. Writers sought a share of revenue from digital distribution, which studios initially offered only under the much older home-video formula. Jurisdiction over reality television and animation writers was also contested.

Impact and settlement

The strike shut down production of most American scripted television, shortened seasons, delayed films, and forced the cancellation of the Golden Globe Awards ceremony; it also sped the development of unscripted and online content. The eventual agreement gave writers new residuals for streaming and downloads, generally regarded as establishing the template for guild contracts in the streaming era.
