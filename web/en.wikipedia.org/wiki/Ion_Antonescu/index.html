Ion Antonescu

Ion Antonescu (14 June 1882 – 1 June 1946) was a Romanian military officer and Marshal who served as Conducător (leader) and Prime Minister of Romania during the Second World War, leading the country as an ally of Nazi Germany.

Rise to power

Born in Pitești, Antonescu distinguished himself in the Second Balkan War and the First World War. In September 1940, after territorial losses and political crisis, King Carol II appointed him prime minister; Antonescu forced the King's abdication and briefly shared power with the fascist Iron Guard before crushing its rebellion in January 1941.

War and the Holocaust

Antonescu joined Operation Barbarossa in June 1941 to recover Bessarabia and Bukovina. His regime was responsible for the deaths of between 280,000 and 380,000 Jews and some 11,000 Roma, including the Iași pogrom and massacres in Transnistria, as documented by the Wiesel Commission (2004), which established Romania's participation in the Holocaust.

Downfall and execution

Arrested in King Michael's coup of 23 August 1944, Antonescu was tried by a People's Tribunal in 1946, convicted of war crimes, and executed by firing squad at Jilava on 1 June 1946.
