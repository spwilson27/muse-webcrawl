Complex geometry

In mathematics, complex geometry is the study of geometric structures and constructions arising from the complex numbers. It encompasses the study of complex manifolds, complex analytic varieties, and complex algebraic varieties, together with the holomorphic functions, vector bundles and coherent sheaves defined on them. The field sits at the intersection of differential geometry, algebraic geometry and several complex variables, and differs markedly from real geometry because holomorphic functions are far more rigid than smooth functions.

Basic objects

A complex manifold is a manifold whose transition maps are holomorphic; examples include Riemann surfaces (complex dimension one), complex projective space, tori, and the complex structures underlying Kähler manifolds. On such spaces one studies holomorphic and meromorphic functions, divisors, and line bundles, using tools such as Dolbeault cohomology and sheaf cohomology.

Key results and themes

The Riemann–Roch theorem relates the dimensions of spaces of meromorphic functions to topological data, generalising to the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem and the Atiyah–Singer index theorem. The Kodaira embedding theorem characterises which compact complex manifolds are algebraic. In Kähler geometry, the Calabi conjecture — proved by Shing-Tung Yau in 1978 — established the existence of Ricci-flat metrics on manifolds with vanishing first Chern class, producing the Calabi–Yau manifolds that later became central to string theory. Classification theory, from the Enriques–Kodaira classification of surfaces to modern minimal model theory, seeks to organise complex manifolds by their birational and metric properties.
