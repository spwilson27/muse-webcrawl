Hamsa

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The hamsa (Arabic: خمسة, romanized: khamsa; Hebrew: חמסה) is a palm-shaped amulet popular throughout West Asia, North Africa and the Levant. It depicts an open right hand and is commonly used in jewellery, wall hangings and other decorative forms. It is believed to provide defence against the evil eye.

Names and origins

The word hamsa means "five" in Arabic, referring to the five fingers of the hand. The symbol is also called the Hand of Fatima, after Fatima bint Muhammad, daughter of the Prophet Muhammad, and within Jewish tradition the Hand of Miriam, after the sister of Moses. Hand motifs used as protective symbols are far older than either faith, appearing in Mesopotamian and Carthaginian art, and similar palm amulets were associated with goddesses such as Ishtar and Tanit.

Design and symbolism

The hamsa is typically shown as a symmetrical hand with two thumbs, though natural single-thumb designs also occur. An eye, often blue, frequently appears in the centre of the palm, reinforcing its protective purpose against the evil eye. The fingers may be spread apart, to ward off evil, or joined together, to bring good luck.

The five fingers are sometimes associated with the five senses, the five pillars of Islam, or the five books of the Torah. The hamsa has become a widely recognised symbol of protection, blessing, power and strength, and in modern times is also used as a shared symbol of the peoples of the Middle East.

Use today

Hamsa imagery is found throughout the region in amulets, door knockers, jewellery and household ornaments, and has become popular worldwide as a fashion motif and tattoo design.
