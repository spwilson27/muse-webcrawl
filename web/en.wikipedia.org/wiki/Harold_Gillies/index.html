Harold Gillies

Sir Harold Delf Gillies (17 June 1882 – 10 September 1960) was a New Zealand otolaryngologist and surgeon widely considered the father of modern plastic surgery. His pioneering work reconstructing the faces of soldiers wounded in the First World War transformed reconstructive surgery.

First World War work

After witnessing early French and German efforts at skin grafting, Gillies persuaded the British Army to establish a dedicated facial injury unit, first at Aldershot and then at the Queen's Hospital in Sidcup, opened in 1917. There he and his team treated over 5,000 servicemen, developing techniques such as the tubed pedicle flap for transferring skin to damaged faces.

Later career

Between the wars Gillies built a private practice and, with Archibald McIndoe — his cousin and protégé — advanced cosmetic and reconstructive surgery. During the Second World War he organised plastic surgery units across Britain. In 1946 he performed one of the first modern female-to-male gender-affirming surgeries, on Michael Dillon.

Legacy

Gillies's principles of reconstruction — meticulous planning, staged repair, and restoring function as well as appearance — remain foundations of plastic surgery training. See plastic surgery and Archibald McIndoe.
