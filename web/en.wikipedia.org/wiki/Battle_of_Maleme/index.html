The Battle of Maleme was one of the main engagements of the Battle of Crete during the Second World War. Fought from 20 to 23 May 1941, it centred on the struggle for Maleme airfield, whose capture gave the German airborne invasion of Crete its decisive foothold.

Background

After the German conquest of mainland Greece in April 1941, Allied forces withdrew to Crete. The German plan, Operation Mercury (Unternehmen Merkur), called for the island to be taken by parachute and glider troops under General Kurt Student, supported by mountain troops flown in once an airfield was secured. Maleme airfield, on the north-western coast, was one of three principal objectives, alongside Rethymno and Heraklion.

The battle

On the morning of 20 May 1941, German paratroopers of the Fallschirmjäger dropped around Maleme. The airfield was defended by the 22nd Battalion of the New Zealand 2nd Division, commanded by Lieutenant Colonel Leslie Andrew, who held the dominating feature of Hill 107 overlooking the strip. Fierce fighting raged through the day; the New Zealanders inflicted heavy losses, but communications broke down and Andrew, believing his battalion was being overwhelmed, withdrew from Hill 107 during the night of 20–21 May.

The German seizure of Hill 107 proved decisive. With the high ground lost, German transport aircraft began landing on the airfield on 21 May, bringing in reinforcements despite continuing Allied artillery fire. A New Zealand counterattack on the night of 21–22 May failed to retake the field, and by 23 May the position was firmly in German hands, allowing the build-up that ultimately doomed the Allied defence of Crete.

Aftermath

The loss of Maleme is often regarded as the turning point of the Battle of Crete. Over the following days German forces pushed east, and the Allies evacuated much of their force from Sfakia on the south coast. The battle was nevertheless costly for the Germans, whose paratroop arm suffered such heavy casualties that large-scale airborne operations were never again attempted by Germany in the war.
