Puzzle video game

Puzzle video games are a broad genre of video games that emphasize puzzle solving. The types of puzzles can test many problem-solving skills, including logic, pattern recognition, sequence solving, spatial recognition, and word completion. Many puzzle games place the player in a timed, score-based challenge or a more deliberative environment in which the player must think through the solution without time pressure.

History

Puzzle video games owe their origins to brain teasers and puzzles throughout human history. Early examples in video games include Nintendo's Donkey Kong (1981) elements and games such as Lode Runner (1983). The genre was propelled into the mainstream by Tetris (1984), created by Alexey Pajitnov, in which falling tetromino pieces must be arranged into complete lines. Tetris remains one of the best-selling video games of all time.

Subgenres

Major subgenres include falling-block puzzles (Tetris, Columns, Puyo Puyo), tile-matching games (Bejeweled, Candy Crush Saga), physics puzzles (Angry Birds, Portal), hidden object games, logic puzzles adapted from paper puzzles such as Sudoku, and puzzle-platformers such as Braid and Fez. Point-and-click adventure games often incorporate puzzle elements as well.

Design

Puzzle games typically rely on easily grasped rules that deepen in complexity. Because success depends on reasoning rather than reflexes or accumulated character statistics, puzzle games have broad demographic appeal and were among the most popular genres on mobile platforms.
