Phrase (music theory)


In music theory, a phrase is a unit of musical form — a complete musical thought, typically ending with a cadence.

In music theory, a phrase is a unit of musical meter that has a complete musical sense of its own, built from figures, motifs and cells, and combining to form melodies, periods and larger sections. Analogous to a clause or sentence in language, a phrase typically expresses a single musical idea and comes to resting points marked by cadences.

Structure

Phrases in the Classical style often last four bars. Two phrases frequently combine into a period: an antecedent phrase ending with a weak (half) cadence poses a musical "question", answered by a consequent phrase ending with a stronger perfect cadence. In a sentence, a basic idea and its repetition are followed by a continuation and cadence. Phrases may be extended by repetition, sequence, interpolation or cadential delay, or compressed by elision, in which the last bar of one phrase serves as the first of the next.

Phrasing in performance

Phrasing also denotes the performer's shaping of the notes of a phrase — through dynamics, articulation, agogics and breathing — often indicated in scores by slur-like phrase marks. Singers and wind players plan breaths at phrase boundaries, and the identification of phrase structure is a central concern of musical analysis (from Heinrich Schenker to William Caplin's theory of formal functions).
