Digital Audio Broadcasting

Digital Audio Broadcasting (DAB) is a digital radio standard for broadcasting digital audio radio services in many countries, designed in the 1980s and developed under the European Eureka 147 project. Its successor, DAB+, uses the more efficient HE-AAC v2 codec.

Technology

DAB uses COFDM modulation and MPEG audio coding to transmit an ensemble (multiplex) of stations in a single frequency block, offering CD-like audio quality, programme-associated data, and resistance to multipath fading in mobile reception.

Adoption

Norway became the first country to switch off national FM radio (in 2017) in favour of DAB; Switzerland and the United Kingdom have also pursued extensive DAB rollouts, while adoption elsewhere has been slower, with some countries favouring internet streaming or other standards.
