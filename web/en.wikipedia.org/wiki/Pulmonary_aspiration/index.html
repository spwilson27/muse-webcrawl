Pulmonary aspiration

Pulmonary aspiration is the entry of material — such as food, liquid, saliva, vomit or foreign objects — into the airways and lungs instead of the oesophagus. It can cause choking, aspiration pneumonia, chemical pneumonitis, airway obstruction and, in severe cases, death.

Risk factors and prevention

Risk is increased by impaired consciousness, anaesthesia and sedation, neurological disorders affecting swallowing (dysphagia), stroke, gastro-oesophageal reflux and emergency surgery on a full stomach. Preventive measures include pre-operative fasting, rapid sequence induction of anaesthesia, head-up positioning, swallowing assessment, modified food textures and oral hygiene in at-risk patients.

Management

Immediate management may involve positioning, suctioning, oxygen and bronchoscopy for particulate matter; antibiotics are used when bacterial aspiration pneumonia develops.
