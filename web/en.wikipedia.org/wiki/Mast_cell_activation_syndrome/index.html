Mast cell activation syndrome

Mast cell activation syndrome
SpecialtyImmunology; allergy
SymptomsFlushing, itching, hives, abdominal pain, diarrhea, low blood pressure, anaphylaxis
CausesInappropriate activation of mast cells; primary (clonal), secondary (allergic), or idiopathic
TreatmentTrigger avoidance; H1/H2 antihistamines; mast cell stabilisers; epinephrine for anaphylaxis

Mast cell activation syndrome (MCAS) is a term for a collection of conditions in which mast cells inappropriately release their chemical mediators — including histamine, tryptase, prostaglandins and leukotrienes — causing symptoms in several organ systems that can range from itching and flushing to life-threatening anaphylaxis. Unlike mastocytosis, in which there is an abnormal accumulation of mast cells, MCAS is defined by abnormal activation; the two can overlap, and diagnostic criteria for MCAS remain an area of medical debate.

Classification

Proposed consensus criteria divide mast cell activation disorders into primary (clonal) forms, in which a mutation such as KIT D816V is present (including monoclonal MCAS and systemic mastocytosis); secondary forms, driven by a known trigger such as IgE-mediated allergy or other disorders; and idiopathic MCAS, in which no cause is identified.

Diagnosis

Consensus diagnostic criteria proposed by Valent and colleagues require: (1) episodic, multi-system symptoms consistent with mast cell mediator release; (2) a documented rise in a validated mediator — classically serum tryptase rising by at least 20% plus 2 ng/mL over the individual's baseline during an episode; and (3) response of symptoms to medications that block mast cell mediators or stabilise mast cells. The specificity of these criteria and of commonly ordered mediator tests is debated, and overdiagnosis is a recognised concern.

Treatment

Management emphasises identification and avoidance of individual triggers, H1- and H2-receptor antihistamines, mast cell stabilisers such as cromolyn sodium, leukotriene modifiers, and carrying epinephrine auto-injectors for patients at risk of anaphylaxis.
