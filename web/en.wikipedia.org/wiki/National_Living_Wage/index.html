National Living Wage
Country: United Kingdom
Introduced: 1 April 2016
Applies to: Workers aged 21 and over (from 2024)
Advised by: Low Pay Commission

The National Living Wage is a mandatory minimum wage payable to workers in the United Kingdom. It was introduced on 1 April 2016 by Chancellor of the Exchequer George Osborne as a new top rate of the National Minimum Wage for workers aged 25 and over, initially set at £7.20 per hour.

Development

The rate was designed to rise towards a target share of median earnings — first 60 percent of median earnings by 2020, and subsequently two-thirds of median earnings. The age threshold was lowered to 23 in 2021 and to 21 in April 2024. Rates are announced annually by the government on the recommendation of the Low Pay Commission and take effect in April.

Distinction from the voluntary living wage

The statutory National Living Wage should not be confused with the voluntary "Real Living Wage" calculated by the Living Wage Foundation from the cost of living, which is higher and is paid voluntarily by accredited employers.
