Colossal statue

MediumStone, bronze, modern composite materials
SubjectsDeities, rulers, national symbols

A colossal statue is a statue of very large size, typically several times life size or larger. The tradition of colossal statuary extends from antiquity to the present day, and such statues have been created in many cultures to express divine power, royal authority, or national identity.

Antiquity

Among the most famous colossal statues of the ancient world was the Colossus of Rhodes, one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, a bronze statue of the sun god Helios erected in 280 BC and standing about 33 metres (108 ft) tall. Other ancient examples include the Great Sphinx of Giza and the colossal statues of Ramesses II in Egypt, the Colossus of Nero in Rome, and the Colossus of Constantine.

Religious colossi

Buddhist art produced some of the largest religious statues of the pre-modern world, including the Leshan Giant Buddha in China, carved into a cliff face in the 8th century and standing 71 metres (233 ft) tall, and the great bronze Buddhas of Japan. The destroyed Buddhas of Bamiyan in Afghanistan, carved in the 6th century, stood 55 and 38 metres tall.

Modern colossi

The modern era has continued the tradition. The Statue of Liberty (1886) in New York Harbor stands 46 metres (151 ft) tall excluding its pedestal. The Spring Temple Buddha in China, completed in 2008, stands 128 metres (420 ft), while the Statue of Unity in India, completed in 2018 and depicting Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, is currently the world's tallest statue at 182 metres (597 ft).
