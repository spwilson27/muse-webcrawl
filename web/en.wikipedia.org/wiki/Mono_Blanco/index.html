Mono Blanco

El Mono Blanco ("The White Monkey") is a landmark of Cuban music history in Veracruz, Mexico, and the name of the celebrated son jarocho group founded there in 1977 by Gilberto Gutiérrez Silva. The phrase refers to a famous café and gathering place in the port of Veracruz associated with the mid-20th-century flourishing of son jarocho, and the group took its name in homage to that tradition.

The group

Grupo Mono Blanco was formed in 1977 in Veracruz and became the leading force in the movimiento jaranero, the revival of traditional son jarocho that resisted the genre's commercialisation. Under Gutiérrez's direction, the group researched older styles and lyrics, trained younger musicians, organised community fandangos, and helped revive the making of traditional instruments such as the jarana jarocha, requinto jarocho and leona.

Style and influence

The group's performances centre on the tarima (wooden platform) danced upon by couples during the fandango, with improvised verses exchanged among singers. Mono Blanco's albums and tours, including collaborations with artists from other traditions, brought son jarocho to international attention and inspired revival movements in Mexico and the United States. The group's best-known alumnus association is with the festival culture that also fed into the success of Los Lobos' recording of regional Mexican music.
