Transport in Singapore

Transport in Singapore comprises an extensive public transport network of Mass Rapid Transit (MRT) trains, buses and taxis, integrated under the planning of the Land Transport Authority (LTA), alongside one of the world's busiest ports and a major international aviation hub at Changi Airport. Because of land scarcity, Singapore manages private car use through the Certificate of Entitlement (COE) vehicle quota system and Electronic Road Pricing (ERP), making car ownership among the most expensive in the world.

Public transport

The MRT network, which opened in 1987, comprises lines including the North South, East West, North East, Circle, Downtown and Thomson–East Coast lines, operated by SMRT Trains and SBS Transit, and is supplemented by Light Rail Transit (LRT) feeder lines in housing estates. Public buses, operated mainly by SBS Transit and SMRT Buses along with Tower Transit and Go-Ahead Singapore, complete the network. Fares are integrated and paid with EZ-Link or SimplyGo cards.

Roads

Singapore has about 3,500 km of roads and a network of expressways including the Pan Island Expressway (PIE), Ayer Rajah Expressway (AYE) and Central Expressway (CTE). Vehicle ownership is controlled by the COE auction system, in place since 1990, and congestion is priced through ERP gantries, introduced in 1998 as the world's first electronic road pricing system for urban congestion management.

Ports and aviation

The Port of Singapore is among the world's busiest transshipment container ports, and a new mega-port at Tuas is progressively consolidating container operations. Singapore Changi Airport is a leading global air hub, consistently ranked among the world's best airports, and is served by Singapore Airlines, the flag carrier.

Cross-border links

Singapore is connected to Peninsular Malaysia by the Johor–Singapore Causeway and the Malaysia–Singapore Second Link, carrying heavy road and rail traffic; a rapid transit link to Johor Bahru is under construction.
