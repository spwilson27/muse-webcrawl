The 1837 Galilee earthquake, often called the Safed earthquake, shook the Galilee region of Ottoman Syria on January 1, 1837. The earthquake struck at about 4:00 in the afternoon, accompanied by a severe aftershock about 25 minutes later. Contemporary estimates of its magnitude range from 6.5 to 7.1, with the epicentre located along the Dead Sea Transform fault system near the Jordan Valley.

Damage

The city of Safed was devastated: the Jewish quarter, built on a steep hillside, collapsed in a landslide, and nearly all its stone houses were destroyed. Tiberias was also largely destroyed, with the shock triggering a seiche on the Sea of Galilee that drowned residents. Damage was reported across the region, including in Acre, Nazareth, Sidon, Tyre, Beirut, and numerous villages in the Galilee and southern Lebanon. In total, between 6,000 and 7,000 people are estimated to have been killed, the majority in Safed.

Aftermath

The disaster struck a region already weakened by years of instability following the Egyptian occupation of Syria under Ibrahim Pasha. Safed's Jewish community, which lost a large share of its population, slowly rebuilt over the following decades. The earthquake remains one of the largest and deadliest seismic events in the recorded history of the Levant, and is an important data point in studies of the Dead Sea Transform's seismic hazard.
