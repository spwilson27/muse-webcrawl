Representable functor

In mathematics, particularly category theory, a representable functor is a functor from a locally small category to the category of sets that is naturally isomorphic to a hom-functor. Representable functors encode the idea that an object can be understood through the maps into or out of it, an idea systematised by the Yoneda lemma.

Definition

Let C be a locally small category. A covariant functor F : C → Set is representable if there is an object A of C and a natural isomorphism between F and the hom-functor Hom(A, –). The object A is called a representing object for F; by the Yoneda lemma it is unique up to unique isomorphism, and the isomorphism corresponds to a "universal element" of F(A). Dually, a contravariant functor is representable if it is isomorphic to Hom(–, A).

Examples

  • The forgetful functor from groups to sets is represented by the infinite cyclic group Z.
  • The functor sending a ring to its group of units is representable in algebraic geometry by the multiplicative group scheme.
  • In algebraic geometry, a moduli problem is representable when a fine moduli space exists; many moduli functors are not representable and require stacks.
  • Brown representability in homotopy theory gives conditions under which a contravariant functor on a triangulated category is representable.

Universal properties

Most universal properties — products, pullbacks, tensor products, classifying spaces — can be phrased as the representability of an associated functor, with the universal object being its representing object.
