Glomerular filtration rate

Glomerular filtration rate (GFR) is the volume of fluid filtered from the renal glomerular capillaries into Bowman's capsule per unit time. It is the standard overall index of kidney function: central to the production of urine, GFR describes the flow rate of filtered fluid through the kidneys and is used to detect, stage and monitor chronic kidney disease and to guide drug dosing.

Measurement and estimation

GFR can be measured directly as the clearance of an ideal filtration marker — inulin is the historical gold standard; iohexol and iothalamate are used in practice. Because direct measurement is cumbersome, GFR is usually estimated (eGFR) from serum creatinine using equations such as Cockcroft–Gault, MDRD, and the CKD-EPI equations; the 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine equation, refit without a race coefficient, is widely recommended. Cystatin C provides an alternative marker less dependent on muscle mass.

Normal values and staging

In healthy young adults GFR is approximately 90–120 mL/min/1.73 m² and declines gradually with age. Chronic kidney disease is classified by GFR category:

Physiology

GFR is determined by the hydrostatic and colloid osmotic pressures across the glomerular capillary wall and by the filtration surface area and permeability. It is autoregulated over a wide range of arterial pressures by tubuloglomerular feedback at the juxtaglomerular apparatus and by the renin–angiotensin system, which adjusts afferent and efferent arteriolar tone.
