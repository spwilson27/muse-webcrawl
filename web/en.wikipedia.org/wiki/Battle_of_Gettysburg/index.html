Battle of Gettysburg

The Battle of Gettysburg (July 1–3, 1863) was a major battle of the American Civil War, fought in and around Gettysburg, Pennsylvania, between the Union Army of the Potomac under Major General George G. Meade and the Confederate Army of Northern Virginia under General Robert E. Lee. Often described as the war's turning point, it was the bloodiest battle of the conflict fought on American soil.

Course of the battle

July 1: Advancing Confederate divisions collided with Union cavalry under John Buford west of Gettysburg. Fighting escalated; Union forces were driven back through the town to Cemetery Hill, where they rallied on strong high ground.

July 2: Lee attacked both Union flanks. Fierce fighting raged at Little Round Top, Devil's Den, the Wheatfield, the Peach Orchard and Cemetery Ridge. The Union line bent but held; Colonel Joshua Chamberlain's 20th Maine famously defended the extreme left at Little Round Top.

July 3: After the morning fight at Culp's Hill, Lee ordered a massive frontal assault on the Union centre — Pickett's Charge — by some 12,500 men across open ground. It was repulsed with heavy losses. Lee retreated to Virginia on July 4, the same day Vicksburg surrendered.

Aftermath

Gettysburg ended Lee's second invasion of the North and, with Vicksburg, marked the strategic turning point of the war. In November 1863 President Abraham Lincoln dedicated the Soldiers' National Cemetery at the site, delivering the Gettysburg Address, one of the most famous speeches in American history.
