Pairing-based cryptography is the use of a bilinear pairing — a map between elements of two groups on an elliptic curve and elements of a third group — to build cryptographic protocols and schemes. Pairings, originally studied through the Weil and Tate pairings of algebraic geometry, were at first used to attack elliptic-curve cryptosystems; from around 2000 they were turned into a constructive tool that enabled entirely new classes of schemes, most famously identity-based encryption.

Bilinear pairings

A pairing is a map e: G1 × G2 → GT between groups of prime order r which is bilinear, meaning e(aP, bQ) = e(P, Q)ab for all points P, Q and scalars a, b; non-degenerate; and efficiently computable. In practice G1 and G2 are groups of points on an elliptic curve over finite fields and GT is a multiplicative group of a larger extension field. Security rests on variants of the bilinear Diffie–Hellman problem.

History and applications

In 1993 Menezes, Okamoto, and Vanstone showed that the Weil pairing could reduce the discrete logarithm problem on certain supersingular curves to a weaker field (the MOV attack). The constructive era began in 2000–2001: Joux presented a one-round tripartite Diffie–Hellman key exchange; Sakai, Ohgishi, and Kasahara proposed identity-based key agreement; and Boneh and Franklin gave the first practical identity-based encryption scheme, realising a concept Shamir had posed in 1984. Boneh, Boyen, and others added hierarchical IBE and attribute-based encryption.

Other landmark pairing-based constructions include the BLS short signature scheme of Boneh, Lynn, and Shacham — signatures half the size of comparable DSA signatures, with natural aggregate-signature properties — and pairing-based non-interactive zero-knowledge proofs (Groth–Sahai) and zk-SNARKs. Pairing-friendly curves such as Barreto–Naehrig (BN) curves and BLS12-381 are widely deployed, for example in the Zcash cryptocurrency and in Ethereum's proof-of-stake consensus (BLS signatures), and pairing operations were added to Ethereum via precompiled contracts.
