Polarizability

An atom's electron cloud distorted by an external electric field

Polarizability measures how readily the electron cloud of an atom, ion, or molecule is distorted by an external electric field, producing an induced electric dipole moment. The induced dipole moment is proportional to the applied field, and the constant of proportionality is the polarizability, usually denoted α.

Definitions

For an isotropic particle, the relationship is written p = αE in SI units, or with a factor of 4πε0 depending on convention, so polarizability is often quoted as a "polarizability volume" in cubic ångströms. For anisotropic molecules, polarizability is a tensor, and a molecule's response depends on the field direction relative to its axes. Polarizability generally increases with atomic size, because larger, more diffuse electron clouds are more easily deformed — a concept important in chemistry through the notion of "soft" and "hard" species in HSAB theory.

Related phenomena

Polarizability connects the microscopic response of matter to macroscopic quantities such as the refractive index and dielectric constant, through relations like the Clausius–Mossotti equation and the Lorentz–Lorenz equation. It also governs the strength of London dispersion forces and the intensity of Raman scattering.
