Geneva is a city in and the county seat of Kane County, Illinois, United States. It is located on the Fox River, about 36 miles (58 km) west of Chicago. The population was about 21,000 at the 2020 census. Geneva is part of the "Tri-City" area along with St. Charles and Batavia, and is known for its historic downtown with shops, restaurants, and preserved 19th-century buildings.

History

The area was first settled in the 1830s, after the Black Hawk War, and was initially called Big Spring. A settlement named LaFox also existed nearby. The community was renamed Geneva after Geneva, New York, and became the county seat of Kane County in 1841. The arrival of the railroad in the 1850s spurred growth, and Geneva became a centre for dairy and manufacturing, including foundries and wagon works.

Geography and culture

Geneva is situated along the west bank of the Fox River, with a riverwalk and bike trails following the water. Third Street, its main commercial thoroughfare, is noted for independent shops and dining. The city hosts annual events including Swedish Days, the Festival of the Vine, and Christmas Walk. The Fabyan Forest Preserve, south of the city, preserves the estate of George and Nelle Fabyan, including a windmill and a villa remodelled by Frank Lloyd Wright.

Transportation

Geneva is served by a station on Metra's Union Pacific West Line, providing commuter rail service to Chicago. Illinois Route 31 and Route 38 pass through the city.
