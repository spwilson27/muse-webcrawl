Sunrise (or sunup) is the moment when the upper limb of the Sun appears on the horizon in the morning, at the start of the solar day. The term can also refer to the entire process of the solar disk crossing the horizon and its accompanying atmospheric effects, which produce the characteristic red and orange colours of the early-morning sky due to Rayleigh scattering of sunlight through a longer path in the atmosphere.

Astronomical definition

Astronomically, sunrise is defined as the moment when the upper edge of the Sun's disk is on the horizon and the Sun's centre is 0.833 degrees below it, accounting for atmospheric refraction (about 0.583°) and the Sun's apparent radius (about 0.25°). Because of this refraction, the Sun is already geometrically below the horizon when it appears to rise. The time of sunrise varies by latitude and throughout the year, driven by the Earth's axial tilt and orbital eccentricity; the earliest and latest sunrises do not coincide with the summer and winter solstices. Near the equator sunrise times vary little, while in polar regions the Sun may remain above or below the horizon for weeks at a time.

Stages of twilight

Sunrise is preceded by dawn twilight in three conventional stages: astronomical twilight, when the Sun is 18°–12° below the horizon; nautical twilight (12°–6°); and civil twilight (6°–0°), when there is generally enough light for ordinary outdoor activities. After sunrise, the low, warm light of the "golden hour" is prized in photography.

Cultural significance

Sunrise has deep symbolic associations with renewal, hope and beginnings across religions and cultures, and is observed in festivals such as the Hindu Chhath and in daily prayer traditions. The first sunrise of the year is celebrated in Japan (hatsuhinode) and elsewhere.
