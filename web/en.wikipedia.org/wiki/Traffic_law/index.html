Traffic law
ScopeVehicles, drivers, pedestrians and other road users
Key areasLicensing, speed limits, right of way, vehicle standards, offences
International frameworkVienna Convention on Road Traffic (1968)

Traffic law (or road traffic law) is the body of law governing traffic and regulating vehicles and other road users. It covers driver licensing, vehicle registration and roadworthiness, rules of the road such as speed limits and right of way, and the offences and penalties applied for violations, from parking infractions to dangerous driving.

Rules of the road

Core rules include which side of the road traffic keeps (left in the UK, Ireland, Japan and much of the Commonwealth; right in most of the world), priority at intersections, signalling, overtaking, and the hierarchy of traffic signals, signs and road markings. International harmonisation is promoted by the 1949 Geneva Convention and the 1968 Vienna Convention on Road Traffic and on Road Signs and Signals, though the United States is not a party to the latter.

Enforcement and offences

Traffic laws are enforced by police and, increasingly, by automated systems such as speed cameras and red-light cameras. Offences are typically graded from minor civil infractions punishable by fines and penalty points, to criminal offences such as driving under the influence, reckless driving and causing death by dangerous driving. Many jurisdictions operate demerit-point systems leading to licence suspension.

History

Modern traffic law emerged with the motor car: Britain's Locomotive Acts of the 1860s famously imposed low speed limits and a man walking ahead with a red flag, while the first traffic signals appeared in London in 1868 and electric signals in Cleveland in 1914. Mass motorisation in the 20th century produced comprehensive highway codes in most countries.
