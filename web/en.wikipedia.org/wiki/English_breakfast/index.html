Full English breakfast
Course: Breakfast
Place of origin: England
Main ingredients: Bacon, eggs, sausage, baked beans, tomato, mushrooms, toast or fried bread

The full English breakfast, often called a "fry-up", is a cooked breakfast meal traditionally served in England (and, in regional variants, throughout the United Kingdom and Ireland). A typical plate combines back bacon, sausages, eggs (fried, scrambled or poached), grilled or fried tomato, mushrooms, baked beans, black pudding and toast or fried bread, accompanied by tea or coffee.

History

The cooked breakfast became established among the English gentry in the Victorian era, when elaborate morning meals showcased country-house produce, and was popularised more widely by the Edwardians. As it spread to hotels, cafés and the working-class "greasy spoon", the components settled into the now-familiar canon. During and after the Second World War the fry-up became a staple of transport cafés and bed-and-breakfast establishments.

Regional variants

The full Scottish breakfast adds haggis, tattie scones and Lorne (square) sausage; the Ulster fry includes soda bread and potato farls and may omit beans; the full Welsh breakfast features laverbread and cockles; Cornish versions sometimes include hog's pudding. Beans, once considered an American-influenced addition, are now standard in most of England.

Cultural status

The full breakfast is regarded as one of England's iconic dishes, frequently voted among the nation's favourite meals. It has also attracted criticism on health grounds for its high fat and salt content, and modern cafés commonly offer grilled rather than fried cooking, vegetarian substitutions such as plant-based sausages, and smaller portions.
