Reflexive modernization

Reflexive modernization is a theory of social change developed by the sociologists Ulrich Beck, Anthony Giddens and Scott Lash, most fully in their joint 1994 volume Reflexive Modernization: Politics, Tradition and Aesthetics in the Modern Social Order.

Theory

The theory holds that advanced modern societies are entering a "second modernity" in which modernization turns back upon its own foundations: the institutions and certainties of industrial society — class, the nuclear family, full employment, the nation-state and faith in scientific progress — are themselves transformed by modernization's consequences. Central to the idea is Beck's notion of the risk society, in which the manufactured risks of modern technology (pollution, nuclear accidents, climate change) become the dominant problems of politics.

Key features

Associated processes include detraditionalization, individualization — people being released from inherited roles and forced to construct their own biographies — and "sub-politics", political action outside formal institutions. Critics have questioned how sharply the second modernity can be distinguished from the first, and the empirical reach of the concept.
