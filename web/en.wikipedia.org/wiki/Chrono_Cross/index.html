Chrono Cross is a role-playing video game developed and published by Square (now Square Enix) for the PlayStation. Released in Japan in 1999 and in North America in 2000, it is a sequel to the 1995 Super Nintendo game Chrono Trigger and draws on themes from the earlier text adventure Radical Dreamers.

Plot

The story follows Serge, a teenage boy from the fishing village of Arni in the tropical archipelago of El Nido, who slips into a parallel world where he died ten years earlier. Moving between the "Home" and "Another" worlds, Serge becomes entangled with the thief Kid, the dragoon Lynx, and the supercomputer FATE, uncovering a plot involving the frozen flame, the dragon gods, and the fate of Schala from Chrono Trigger. The game features more than 40 recruitable party members and multiple endings.

Gameplay

Battles use an element-based system: characters spend stamina on attacks of increasing power, then cast Elements — spells and consumables assigned to a grid — whose strength depends on the colour of the battlefield field effect. There are no random encounters; enemies are visible on the map, and the party levels up through star levels awarded after boss fights, with small stat gains between them.

Reception and legacy

Chrono Cross received widespread critical acclaim, including perfect or near-perfect scores from several publications, and praise for Yasunori Mitsuda's Celtic- and Mediterranean-influenced soundtrack, which includes the celebrated opening theme "Scars of Time." Some Chrono Trigger fans debated its looser connection to the original. A remaster, Chrono Cross: The Radical Dreamers Edition, was released in 2022 for modern platforms, bundling an English version of Radical Dreamers.
