Battle of the Alamo

The Battle of the Alamo (February 23 – March 6, 1836) was a pivotal event in the Texas Revolution. Following a 13-day siege, Mexican troops under President General Antonio López de Santa Anna reclaimed the Alamo Mission near San Antonio de Béxar (modern-day San Antonio, Texas, United States), killing most of the Texian defenders.

Background

Texian rebels had driven Mexican forces out of Texas in late 1835 and occupied the former mission and fortress of the Alamo. Determined to crush the rebellion, Santa Anna personally led a large army north in the winter of 1836. The Alamo garrison, commanded jointly by William Barret Travis and the ailing James Bowie, included the famous frontiersman and former congressman Davy Crockett.

The siege and assault

Mexican troops arrived on February 23 and began a siege. Travis's letters pleading for reinforcements — most famously the "Victory or Death" letter — went largely unanswered; only 32 men from Gonzales reached the Alamo, bringing the defenders to roughly two hundred. Before dawn on March 6, Santa Anna launched a full assault. The attackers overwhelmed the walls after fierce fighting, and nearly all the defenders were killed. A small number of noncombatants, including Susanna Dickinson, survived and were released.

Legacy

"Remember the Alamo!" became the rallying cry of the Texian army, which defeated Santa Anna six weeks later at the Battle of San Jacinto, securing Texan independence. The Alamo chapel is preserved today as a shrine and museum in downtown San Antonio and is one of the most visited historic sites in Texas.
