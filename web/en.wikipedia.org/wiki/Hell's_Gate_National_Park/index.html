Hell's Gate National Park

Hell's Gate National Park is a national park situated south of Lake Naivasha in Kenya, north-west of Nairobi. Established in 1984, it covers an area of about 68 square kilometres (26 sq mi). The park is named after a narrow break in the cliffs, once a tributary of a prehistoric lake that fed early humans in the Rift Valley. It is known for its dramatic scenery — towering red cliffs, gorges, rock towers and belching geothermal plumes — and for being one of the few Kenyan parks where visitors may walk or cycle among wildlife.

Fischer's Tower, a volcanic plug in Hell's Gate, is popular with rock climbers.

Geography and features

The park's landmarks include Fischer's Tower and the Central Tower, volcanic plugs named in part for German explorer Gustav Fischer, and the Ol Njorowa Gorge, a narrow water-carved canyon. Geothermal activity is exploited nearby at the Olkaria geothermal power stations, among the largest in Africa, and steam vents dot the park. There are also Maasai cultural centres presenting the heritage of the Maasai people, for whom the area held significance, and a circuit suitable for hiking, cycling and rock climbing.

Wildlife

Although it lacks large predators in significant numbers — which is what makes walking and cycling safe — the park hosts African buffalo, zebra, eland, hartebeest, Thomson's gazelle, giraffe, baboons and klipspringer, with occasional leopard and lion. More than 100 bird species have been recorded, including Verreaux's eagles and the rare lammergeier (bearded vulture), for which the cliffs are a breeding stronghold.

In popular culture

The park's landscapes inspired settings in Disney's The Lion King (1994), and it served as a location for the film Lara Croft: Tomb Raider – The Cradle of Life (2003).

Safety

The Ol Njorowa Gorge is subject to dangerous flash flooding; in September 2019 seven people, including six tourists and a guide, were killed by a flash flood in the gorge, prompting the Kenya Wildlife Service to review visitor safety and close the gorge during rains.
