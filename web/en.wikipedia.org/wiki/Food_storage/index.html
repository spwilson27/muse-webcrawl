Food storage is the practice of keeping food in conditions that preserve its safety, nutritional value and flavour from the time of production or harvest until consumption. Effective storage slows spoilage by limiting the growth of bacteria, yeasts and moulds, retarding oxidation and enzymatic changes, and protecting food from pests, moisture and contamination.

Principles

The main agents of spoilage are micro-organisms, enzymes, oxygen and light. Storage methods counter them by reducing temperature, removing water (lowering water activity), excluding air, adding preservatives such as salt or sugar, or destroying microbes by heat. Different foods have characteristic safe storage lives: perishable foods such as milk, meat and cooked dishes require refrigeration at or below about 4 °C, whereas dried grains and pulses keep for months in a cool, dry, dark place.

Methods

  • Dry storage – cupboards and pantries for grains, flour, canned goods and dried foods, kept cool, dry and sealed against pests.
  • Refrigeration – slows microbial growth and is essential for dairy, raw meat and leftovers.
  • Freezing – at −18 °C or below, halts microbial growth almost entirely and stores food for months, though texture may change.
  • Canning and bottling – heat-sterilised sealed containers give shelf lives of years.
  • Vacuum packing and modified-atmosphere packaging – reduce oxygen to delay spoilage and freezer burn.
  • Traditional methods – drying, salting, smoking, fermenting and pickling, used since antiquity.

Commercial and industrial storage

Warehouses, grain elevators and cold stores handle food in bulk, with temperature- and humidity-controlled environments and pest management. Logistics systems such as FIFO (first in, first out) rotation ensure older stock is used first. Governments and aid agencies also maintain strategic food reserves against famine and emergencies.

Home preparedness

Some households keep extended food storage as insurance against emergencies; the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints notably encourages members to maintain a supply of food and essentials. Preppers similarly stockpile long-life foods. Safe practice emphasises rotating supplies, labelling dates, and discarding food showing signs of spoilage or damaged packaging.
