Airplane! (alternatively titled Flying High! in several countries) is a 1980 American comedy film written and directed by the team of Jim Abrahams and brothers David and Jerry Zucker (ZAZ). A parody of the disaster-film genre — closely modeled on the 1957 film Zero Hour!, whose plot and dialogue it mirrors — it stars Robert Hays as a traumatized ex-fighter pilot who must land a passenger airliner after its crew is struck down by food poisoning. The film was a major critical and commercial success and is widely ranked among the greatest comedies ever made.

Plot

Ted Striker, a former military pilot haunted by a failed mission, boards a Los Angeles–to–Chicago flight to win back his flight-attendant girlfriend Elaine. When dinner service leaves both pilots and many passengers violently ill — tainted fish — Elaine and Dr. Rumack recruit Striker to fly the plane, guided by hard-bitten air traffic controller Steve McCroskey and Striker's old commander Rex Kramer. Amid escalating absurdity — an inflatable autopilot, hysterical passengers, and a chain of visual and verbal gags — Striker lands the aircraft safely and reconciles with Elaine.

Style and production

The film's humor rests on deadpan delivery: the ZAZ team deliberately cast actors known for dramatic roles — Leslie Nielsen, Peter Graves, Lloyd Bridges, and Robert Stack — and had them play ludicrous dialogue with total sincerity. Nielsen's Dr. Rumack ("I am serious — and don't call me Shirley") relaunched his career as a comedy star. The screenplay adapts Zero Hour! so closely that the producers bought the remake rights. Made for about $3.5 million, the film grossed roughly $171 million worldwide.

Reception and legacy

Airplane! helped define the modern spoof film and launched the ZAZ team's subsequent successes (Top Secret!, The Naked Gun). It was selected for the United States National Film Registry in 2010 as culturally and historically significant, and consistently appears near the top of critics' and the American Film Institute's comedy lists. A sequel, Airplane II: The Sequel (1982), was made without the original creators.
