Surgeonfish

Surgeonfish, also called tangs, are marine ray-finned fishes of the family Acanthuridae, comprising about 86 species in six genera, including the unicornfishes (genus Naso). They are named for the sharp, scalpel-like spines on each side of the caudal peduncle, which can be used defensively and can inflict painful wounds. Surgeonfish are characteristic inhabitants of tropical coral reefs, especially in the Indo-Pacific, where most feed on benthic algae and play an important role in reef ecology.

Family: Acanthuridae
Genera: 6 (~86 species)
Habitat: Tropical coral reefs

Characteristics

Surgeonfish have deep, laterally compressed oval bodies, small terminal mouths with fine teeth adapted to grazing algae, and continuous dorsal fins. Many are brightly coloured — familiar species include the palette surgeonfish (regal blue tang, Paracanthurus hepatus), the yellow tang (Zebrasoma flavescens) and the powder blue tang. The caudal spine of Acanthurus species folds into a groove, while unicornfishes bear fixed spines and, in some species, a horn-like rostral projection.

Ecology and human use

Most surgeonfish are herbivores whose grazing prevents algae from overgrowing coral; a few feed on zooplankton or detritus. They are popular in the marine aquarium trade, though some species are difficult to keep, and are eaten locally in parts of the Pacific, occasionally causing ciguatera poisoning. The regal blue tang achieved worldwide fame as the character Dory in the films Finding Nemo (2003) and Finding Dory (2016).
