Universal design

Universal design is the design of buildings, products, and environments to make them accessible and usable by all people, to the greatest extent possible, without the need for adaptation or specialized design. The term was coined by American architect Ronald Mace of North Carolina State University, who founded the Center for Universal Design in 1989.

Principles

In 1997 the Center published seven principles of universal design: equitable use; flexibility in use; simple and intuitive use; perceptible information; tolerance for error; low physical effort; and size and space for approach and use. The principles are applied across architecture, product design, information technology, and education — the related framework of Universal Design for Learning applies the same ideas to curricula.

Examples

Everyday examples include lever door handles operable without gripping, curb cuts that serve wheelchair users, parents with strollers, and travelers with luggage alike, automatic doors, and captions on video. Universal design is related to, but broader than, accessibility standards required by laws such as the Americans with Disabilities Act, because it aims for usability by everyone rather than accommodation of a defined group.
