Tea brick

Tea bricks are blocks of whole or finely ground tea leaves packed into moulds and pressed into brick form. They were once widely used across China, Tibet, Mongolia and Siberia both as a beverage and as a form of currency.

Production and use

Traditionally made from the coarsest leaves and stems of the tea plant, bricks were convenient to transport along tea-horse routes and to store for long periods. To prepare tea, pieces were shaved off, ground, and boiled — in Tibet, churned with yak butter and salt to make butter tea.

Currency

From roughly the 9th to the 20th century, tea bricks circulated as money in Tibet, Mongolia and parts of Siberia; small change could be made by breaking off pieces, and the value of goods was sometimes quoted in bricks. Compressed teas such as pu-erh cakes remain popular with collectors today.
