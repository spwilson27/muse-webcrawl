Roe v. Wade

Roe v. Wade, 410 U.S. 113 (1973), was a landmark decision of the U.S. Supreme Court holding that the Constitution of the United States protected a right to have an abortion. The Court ruled 7–2 that the Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment protects a 'right to privacy' encompassing the decision.

The case was brought by 'Jane Roe' (Norma McCorvey) against Dallas County district attorney Henry Wade, challenging Texas abortion laws. Justice Harry Blackmun's majority opinion balanced the right against state interests using a trimester framework, later modified by Planned Parenthood v. Casey (1992), which substituted the viability standard and the undue burden test.

Overruling

On June 24, 2022, Roe and Casey were overturned by Dobbs v. Jackson Women's Health Organization, which held the Constitution does not confer a right to abortion and returned regulation to the states. Roe remains one of the most debated decisions in American law.
