Machzor

A machzor (Hebrew: מחזור, plural machzorim) is the prayer book used by Jews on the High Holy Days of Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, and in many traditions also for the festivals of Passover, Shavuot and Sukkot. The word derives from a Hebrew root meaning "cycle", referring to the yearly cycle of festivals. The weekday and Sabbath prayer book, by contrast, is called a siddur.

Contents

The machzor contains the standard daily prayers with special additions for the festivals, including the piyyutim (liturgical poems) composed over many centuries, the Musaf service, and for Yom Kippur the Kol Nidre declaration and the Ne'ila concluding service. Distinctive melodies, known as MiSinai tunes, are traditionally used for much of the High Holy Day liturgy.

History

The earliest machzorim were compiled in the early medieval period, drawing on the prayer orders of Amram Gaon and Saadia Gaon in ninth- and tenth-century Babylonia. Lavishly illuminated machzorim were produced in medieval Germany, among them the Worms Machzor of 1272 and the Leipzig Machzor.

Traditions

Different communities — Ashkenazi, Sephardi, Italian and Yemenite — maintain their own machzor traditions, differing in the selection of piyyutim and in details of the service.
