Airstream mechanism

In phonetics, an airstream mechanism is the process by which a body of air is set in motion to produce speech sounds, by generating a difference in air pressure between the vocal tract and the outside atmosphere. It is one of the components of speech production, along with phonation, articulation and resonance.

Types

The initiator of the airstream is usually an organ such as the lungs, glottis or tongue. The most common mechanism is pulmonic egressive, in which air is pushed out of the lungs and used by nearly all sounds in all languages. Other mechanisms include glottalic airstreams, producing ejective consonants (egressive) and implosive consonants (ingressive); lingual (velaric) airstreams, producing click consonants; and rare percussive sounds produced without an airstream.

Direction

An airstream may be egressive, flowing out of the vocal tract, or ingressive, flowing inward. Most speech uses egressive airstreams.
