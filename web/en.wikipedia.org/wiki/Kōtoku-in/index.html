Kōtoku-in (高徳院) is a Buddhist temple of the Jōdo sect in the city of Kamakura in Kanagawa Prefecture, Japan. Its mountain name is Taiizan. The temple is renowned for the Great Buddha of Kamakura (鎌倉大仏, Kamakura Daibutsu), a monumental outdoor bronze statue of Amida Buddha that is one of the most famous icons of Japan and a designated National Treasure.

The Great Buddha

The statue, cast beginning in 1252 during the Kamakura period, stands approximately 11.4 metres tall (13.35 metres including the base) and weighs about 121 tonnes. It was originally housed inside a great temple hall, which was repeatedly destroyed by storms and a tsunami in the 14th and 15th centuries; since 1495 the Buddha has stood in the open air. The figure is hollow, and visitors can enter its interior. Fundraising for the original statue is traditionally credited to the priest Jōkō and the lady Inada-no-Tsubone, with the sanction of the shogunate of Minamoto no Yoritomo's successors.

Temple history

The precise founding of Kōtoku-in is unclear; records of the temple in its present form go back to the 13th century. The Great Buddha was preceded on the site by a wooden statue, completed around 1243, which was damaged by a storm in 1247 — prompting the decision to recast the image in bronze. The temple declined after the fall of the Kamakura shogunate but was revived in the Edo period.

In culture

The Great Buddha appears in Rudyard Kipling's poem "Buddha at Kamakura" and is a standard stop on tours of Kamakura's temples, alongside Tsurugaoka Hachimangū and Hase-dera. An enormous pair of straw sandals (waraji), woven by children and renewed every few years, hangs inside the temple gate.
