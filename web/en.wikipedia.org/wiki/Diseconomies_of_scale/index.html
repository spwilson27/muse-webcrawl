Diseconomies of scale

In microeconomics, diseconomies of scale are the cost disadvantages that economic actors accrue due to an increase in organisational size or output, resulting in the production of goods and services at increased per-unit costs. They are the opposite of economies of scale.

Diseconomies of scale
Field: Microeconomics
Effect: Rising long-run average cost

Causes

  • Communication problems — messages are distorted or delayed as they pass through more layers of management.
  • Coordination and control loss — managers struggle to monitor very large workforces.
  • Motivation — workers in large firms may feel alienated and less motivated, lowering productivity.
  • Slower decision-making and bureaucratic rigidity.
  • Geographical and input factors — congestion, higher local wages or rents, and depletion of cheap local resources.

The cost curve

On the long-run average cost curve, diseconomies set in beyond the minimum efficient scale, causing the curve to slope upward. Firms that grow too large may respond by decentralising, splitting into divisions, or outsourcing.
