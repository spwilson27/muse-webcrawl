Ethan Allen (January 21, 1738 – February 12, 1789) was an American farmer, businessman, land speculator, philosopher, writer, politician and patriot. Allen was a founder of the State of Vermont, and he led the Green Mountain Boys in capturing Fort Ticonderoga from the British in the early months of the American Revolutionary War.

Early life and the New Hampshire Grants

Born in Litchfield, Connecticut, Allen moved to the New Hampshire Grants (present-day Vermont), where he acquired land and became embroiled in the dispute between New York and New Hampshire over jurisdiction of the territory. He helped organise the Green Mountain Boys, a militia that resisted New York attempts to enforce its claims, and was declared an outlaw by New York's governor.

Revolutionary War

In May 1775, Allen and Benedict Arnold led the Green Mountain Boys in a surprise dawn capture of Fort Ticonderoga, the first offensive American victory of the war, securing vital artillery later hauled to Boston by Henry Knox. Allen demanded the fort's surrender "in the name of the Great Jehovah and the Continental Congress", as he later reported. Later that year he was captured during an ill-fated attempt to take Montreal and was held prisoner by the British until 1778.

Vermont and later life

After his release Allen returned to Vermont, which had declared itself an independent republic in 1777, and advocated for its admission to the Union—achieved only in 1791, after his death. His deist philosophical work Reason: the Only Oracle of Man (1784) was one of the first attacks on organised Christianity published in America. Allen died in Burlington in 1789.
