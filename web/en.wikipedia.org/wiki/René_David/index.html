René David (1906–1990)

René David (3 January 1906 – 26 May 1990) was a French legal scholar known for his work in the field of comparative law, and in particular for his classification of the world's legal systems into families. He was a professor at the University of Paris (Paris II) and one of the most influential comparative lawyers of the 20th century.

Work

David's major work, Les grands systèmes de droit contemporains (first published 1964; in English as Major Legal Systems in the World Today), classified legal systems into the Romano-Germanic (civil law) family, the common law family, the socialist law family, and a residual category including religious and traditional systems such as Islamic law, Hindu law, Jewish law and the laws of the Far East and Africa. His taxonomy, alongside that of Konrad Zweigert and Hein Kötz, long dominated the teaching of comparative law.

David also played a practical role in law reform: he directed the drafting of the Ethiopian Civil Code of 1960, commissioned by Emperor Haile Selassie, and advised on codification projects in other countries. He was a leading figure in UNIDROIT, the International Institute for the Unification of Private Law, and promoted the unification and harmonization of private law.

Honours

David received honorary doctorates from numerous universities and was elected to several learned academies, including the Institut de France. In 1976 he shared the Erasmus Prize (with Werner Kaegi) in the field of law.
