Vandal Kingdom

The Vandal Kingdom (Latin: Regnum Vandalum), also known as the Kingdom of the Vandals and Alans, was a kingdom established by the Germanic Vandals under Gaiseric in North Africa and the Mediterranean in the 5th and 6th centuries AD. At its height it ruled North Africa, Sardinia, Corsica, the Balearic Islands, Sicily in part, and Malta.

History

The Vandals crossed the Rhine in 406, moved through Gaul and Hispania, and crossed to Africa in 429 under Gaiseric. They captured Carthage in 439, an event that shocked the Roman world, and in 455 sacked Rome. Treaties with the Western and Eastern empires recognised their control.

Society and religion

The Vandals were Arian Christians ruling a largely Nicene Catholic population, leading to periods of persecution, notably under Huneric. The kingdom maintained Roman administration, taxation, coinage and trade, and Carthage remained a major Mediterranean port. Its fleet dominated the western Mediterranean.

Decline and conquest

Succession disputes and conflict with Berber (Moorish) tribes weakened the kingdom. In 533–534 the Eastern Roman (Byzantine) general Belisarius, sent by Justinian I, defeated kings Gelimer at Ad Decimum and Tricamarum, ending Vandal rule and restoring North Africa to the empire.
