Wilhelm I (German: Wilhelm Friedrich Ludwig; 22 March 1797 – 9 March 1888) was King of Prussia from 1861 and German Emperor from 1871 until his death in 1888. A member of the House of Hohenzollern, he was the first head of state of a united Germany.

Wilhelm was the second son of King Frederick William III and Queen Louise. Under the leadership of his chancellor Otto von Bismarck, Prussia fought and won three wars of unification — against Denmark (1864), Austria (1866), and France (1870–71) — culminating in Wilhelm's proclamation as German Emperor in the Hall of Mirrors at the Palace of Versailles on 18 January 1871.

Early life and military career

Wilhelm grew up during the Napoleonic Wars and accompanied the Prussian army as a young officer, fighting at the battles of Ligny and Waterloo in 1815. He developed a reputation as a competent soldier and a conservative, deeply monarchist figure. During the revolutions of 1848 he was associated with the suppression of liberal uprisings, earning the nickname "Kartätschenprinz" (Prince of Grapeshot), and briefly fled to England.

Reign as King of Prussia

After his elder brother Frederick William IV suffered a debilitating stroke, Wilhelm became regent in 1858 and succeeded him as king on 2 January 1861. His reign began with a constitutional conflict over army reform, which led him to appoint Otto von Bismarck as Minister President in 1862 — a partnership that would dominate German politics for the rest of his reign.

German Emperor

Following Prussia's victory in the Franco-Prussian War, Wilhelm was proclaimed German Emperor (Deutscher Kaiser) on 18 January 1871. Though he personally preferred the title "Emperor of Germany", he accepted the compromise title. As emperor he presided over a rapidly industrializing empire, lending Bismarck wide latitude in domestic and foreign policy, including the Kulturkampf and the first modern social welfare legislation.

Death and succession

Wilhelm survived two assassination attempts in 1878. He died on 9 March 1888 at the age of 90 and was succeeded by his son Frederick III, who died of cancer after only 99 days; the year 1888 thus became known as the Year of the Three Emperors. He was succeeded in turn by his grandson Wilhelm II.
