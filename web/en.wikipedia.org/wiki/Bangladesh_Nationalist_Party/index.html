The Bangladesh Nationalist Party (Bengali: বাংলাদেশ জাতীয়তাবাদী দল, abbreviated BNP) is a major political party in Bangladesh. Founded on 1 September 1978 by President Ziaur Rahman, the BNP promotes "Bangladeshi nationalism" as its central ideology. Along with the Awami League, it has been one of the two dominant parties in Bangladeshi politics for most of the period since the restoration of parliamentary democracy in 1991.

History

The BNP won the 1979 parliamentary election and governed until Ziaur Rahman's assassination in 1981. Under his widow Khaleda Zia, the party returned to power after the 1991 election, and again in 2001 in alliance with Jamaat-e-Islami and other parties. Khaleda Zia served as Prime Minister from 1991 to 1996 and from 2001 to 2006. The party boycotted the 2014 general election and spent a long period in opposition to the Awami League government of Sheikh Hasina.

Leadership

Khaleda Zia has served as chairperson since 1984. Her son Tarique Rahman, based in London since 2008, became acting chairman in 2018. Mirza Fakhrul Islam Alamgir has served as secretary general.
