National Tartan Day

National Tartan Day is a North American celebration of Scottish heritage held on 6 April, the date of the Declaration of Arbroath in 1320, in which Scottish nobles asserted Scotland's independence. The day recognizes the contributions of Scots and Scottish descendants to the United States and Canada.

Origins

Tartan Day originated in Nova Scotia, Canada, in 1987, promoted by the Federation of Scottish Clans in Nova Scotia, and spread to other Canadian provinces in the early 1990s. In the United States, the day gained national recognition through the efforts of the Scottish Coalition USA; the U.S. Senate passed a resolution in 1998 designating 6 April as National Tartan Day, and the House of Representatives followed in 2005. A presidential proclamation in 2008 further affirmed the observance.

Celebrations

The best-known celebration is the New York City Tartan Week, culminating in a parade along Sixth Avenue with pipe bands, dancers, and clan societies; similar parades and events occur in Washington, D.C., and cities across Canada. The date was chosen to link Scottish-American heritage with the Declaration of Arbroath, which some historians regard as an influence on the American Declaration of Independence; several signers of the Declaration were of Scottish descent.
