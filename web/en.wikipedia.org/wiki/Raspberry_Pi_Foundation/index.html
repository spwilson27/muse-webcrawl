The Raspberry Pi Foundation is a British charity and company founded in 2009 to promote the study of basic computer science in schools and developing countries. It is best known as the organisation behind the Raspberry Pi, a series of small, inexpensive single-board computers launched in February 2012 that became among the best-selling British computers of all time.

History

The Foundation was established in Cambridge by Eben Upton and colleagues from the University of Cambridge Computer Laboratory, alarmed by the decline in applicants for computer science degrees and by school students' shrinking exposure to programming. With trustees including Jack Lang, Pete Lomas, Rob Mullins, Alan Mycroft and David Braben, the charity developed a credit-card-sized computer cheap enough — US$35 for the first Model B — to put programmable hardware into children's hands in the spirit of the BBC Micro. The first batch of 10,000 boards, manufactured in China, sold out within hours of launch on 29 February 2012.

Structure

The Foundation is a charity registered in England and Wales. Its trading subsidiary, Raspberry Pi (Trading) Ltd — renamed Raspberry Pi Ltd — designs and sells the computers and accessories, gifting its profits to the charity; manufacturing is licensed to partners such as RS Components and Premier Farnell/element14, with most boards made in Sony's Pencoed factory in Wales. In 2024 Raspberry Pi Ltd listed on the London Stock Exchange, while the Foundation remained its majority shareholder.

Educational work

Beyond hardware, the Foundation runs large-scale education programmes. It founded Code Club, a network of volunteer-led coding clubs for children; merged with the CoderDojo Foundation in 2017; runs the Astro Pi challenge, in which school students' code is executed on Raspberry Pi computers aboard the International Space Station; and publishes the magazine The MagPi and books through Raspberry Pi Press. It also develops free teaching resources and teacher training, and provides the Raspberry Pi OS software.
