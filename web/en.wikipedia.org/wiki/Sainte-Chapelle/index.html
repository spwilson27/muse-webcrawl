The Sainte-Chapelle ("Holy Chapel") is a royal palace chapel in the Rayonnant Gothic style within the medieval Palais de la Cité on the Île de la Cité in Paris. King Louis IX built it in the 1240s to house his collection of Passion relics — above all the Crown of Thorns, acquired from the Latin Emperor of Constantinople in 1239 for a sum exceeding the cost of the chapel itself. It is famed for its stained glass: among the most extensive and finest surviving ensembles of 13th-century glass anywhere.

Architecture

The chapel is in two storeys: a low, richly painted lower chapel for palace servants, and an upper chapel for the king whose walls are almost entirely glass — fifteen windows, each about 15 metres high, separated by the slenderest of buttresses, depicting 1,113 biblical scenes from Genesis to the arrival of the relics in Paris. The building resolves into a lantern of coloured light, crowned by a flèche and framed by deep buttresses with pinnacles.

History

Desecrated during the Revolution, the chapel lost most of its relics (the Crown of Thorns was transferred to Notre-Dame) and served as a club and archive store. A celebrated 19th-century restoration under Duban, Lassus and Viollet-le-Duc recovered its polychromy and most of its glass, some two-thirds of which is original. A seven-year restoration of the windows was completed in 2015. The Sainte-Chapelle now functions as a museum and concert venue administered by the Centre des monuments nationaux.
