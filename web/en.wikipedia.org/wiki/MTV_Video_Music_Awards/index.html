The MTV Video Music Awards (commonly abbreviated as the VMAs) is an award show presented by the cable channel MTV to honour the best in the music video medium and popular culture. Originally conceived as an alternative to the Grammy Awards (in the video category), the annual ceremony has often been called the "Super Bowl for youth" for its ability to draw millions of young viewers. Winners receive a statuette of an astronaut on the Moon — one of MTV's earliest symbols — originally called the "Moonman" and renamed the "Moon Person" in 2017.

The first VMAs were held on September 14, 1984, at Radio City Music Hall in New York City, hosted by Dan Aykroyd and Bette Midler; the Cars' "You Might Think" won the first Video of the Year. The show has since rotated among New York, Los Angeles, Miami, and Las Vegas.

Notable moments

The VMAs are famous for provocative and controversial performances: Madonna's 1984 wedding-dress performance of "Like a Virgin" and her 2003 opening kiss with Britney Spears and Christina Aguilera; Michael Jackson and Lisa Marie Presley's 1994 kiss; Lady Gaga's 2010 meat dress; Miley Cyrus's 2013 performance with Robin Thicke; and Kanye West's 2009 interruption of Taylor Swift's acceptance speech, which drew comment even from President Barack Obama. Beyoncé is the most awarded artist in VMA history, and her 2016 medley performance is frequently cited among the show's best. Taylor Swift has won Video of the Year a record five times.

Categories

The general categories include Video of the Year, Artist of the Year, Song of the Year, and Best New Artist, alongside genre awards (pop, hip-hop, rock, Latin, K-pop, R&B, country) and professional categories such as Best Direction, Best Cinematography, and Best Visual Effects. The Michael Jackson Video Vanguard Award is the ceremony's lifetime-achievement honour, given to performers who have made an outstanding contribution to music video and popular culture.
