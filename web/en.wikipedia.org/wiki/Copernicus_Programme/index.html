Copernicus is the Earth observation component of the European Union Space Programme, managed by the European Commission in partnership with the European Space Agency (ESA), EUMETSAT and other agencies. It provides information services based on satellite Earth observation and in-situ (non-space) data for environmental monitoring, climate change, civil protection and security. The programme was established in 2014, building on the earlier Global Monitoring for Environment and Security (GMES) initiative, and is named after the astronomer Nicolaus Copernicus. Its data policy is famously full, free and open.

The Sentinel satellites

The space component consists of dedicated families of Sentinel missions developed by ESA. Sentinel-1 (radar imaging, launched 2014) works in all weather, day and night; Sentinel-2 (launched 2015) provides high-resolution multispectral optical imagery for land monitoring; Sentinel-3 observes oceans and land (altimetry, sea and land surface temperature); Sentinel-4 and Sentinel-5/5P monitor air quality and atmospheric composition (5P launched in 2017); and Sentinel-6 measures global sea level. Contributing missions from commercial and national operators supplement the Sentinels.

Services

Six thematic services turn the data into products: the Copernicus Atmosphere Monitoring Service (CAMS), Marine Environment Monitoring Service (CMEMS), Land Monitoring Service (CLMS), Climate Change Service (C3S), Emergency Management Service (CEMS) and Security service. Copernicus data support flood and wildfire mapping, crop monitoring, air-quality forecasts, ocean forecasting and measurement of greenhouse gases and ice loss.

Access

Data are distributed through the Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem and DIAS cloud platforms. With a budget of several billion euros per funding period, Copernicus is considered the world's largest single Earth-observation programme and the third-largest EU programme overall.
