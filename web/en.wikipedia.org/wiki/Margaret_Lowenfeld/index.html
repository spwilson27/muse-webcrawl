Margaret Lowenfeld

Margaret Frances Jane Lowenfeld (4 February 1890 – 2 February 1973) was a British pioneering child psychologist and psychotherapist. She developed the “world technique”, a non-verbal method of child therapy using trays of sand and miniature figures, which influenced later play therapy and the sandplay therapy of Dora Kalff.

Life and work

Lowenfeld trained in medicine, qualifying in 1918, and worked in Poland on relief efforts after the First World War, an experience that shaped her interest in children’s minds. In 1928 she founded a clinic for nervous and difficult children in London, which became the Institute of Child Psychology in 1931.

World technique

Observing that young children express experience more readily in play and images than in words, Lowenfeld provided a shallow tray partly filled with sand, water, and a cabinet of small models — people, animals, houses, trees, and everyday objects. Children built “worlds” in the tray which the therapist recorded and studied as direct expressions of the child’s mental life. She described the method in Play in Childhood (1935) and the posthumously published The World Technique (1979).
