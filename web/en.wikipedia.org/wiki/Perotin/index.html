Perotin

Flourished: c. 1200
Era: Medieval
Associated with: Notre Dame school

Perotin (fl. c. 1200), also called Perotinus and Magister Perotinus, was a medieval European composer associated with the Notre Dame school of polyphony in Paris. Almost nothing is known of his life; much of what is attributed to him comes from the writings of the theorist known as Anonymous IV.

Works

Perotin is credited with expanding organum from two voices to three and four voices. His surviving works include the four-voice organa Viderunt omnes and Sederunt principes, composed for the liturgies of Christmas and St Stephen's Day, probably around 1198–1199, as well as three-voice organa and conductus.

Significance

Together with his predecessor Leonin, Perotin is linked to the Magnus liber organi, the great book of organum for Notre Dame Cathedral. His rhythmic innovations, using the rhythmic modes, were foundational for the later motet and for measured polyphony in Western music.
