Basal shoot

Basal shoots, also called basal breaks, are vigorous new shoots that arise from the base of a plant, typically from buds at or just below ground level near the crown or graft union. The term is used most often in horticulture, especially in the cultivation of roses, where strong new canes growing from the bud union are prized as the foundation of future flowering wood.

In horticulture

In roses, basal breaks usually appear in spring and after deadheading or feeding, and gardeners encourage them with adequate water, fertiliser, and by removing weak or twiggy growth. Basal shoots should be distinguished from suckers, which in grafted plants arise from the rootstock below the graft union and, if left, can outgrow and replace the desired cultivar. In many other plants — including raspberries, lilacs, and numerous trees — basal shoots (also called water sprouts when arising on trunks, or stool shoots) are a normal means of regeneration and are managed by pruning.
