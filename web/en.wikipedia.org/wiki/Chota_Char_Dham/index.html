Chota Char Dham


Placeholder image: Kedarnath, one of the four shrines

The Chota Char Dham (literally the "smaller circuit of four abodes") is an important Hindu pilgrimage circuit in the Garhwal Himalayas of Uttarakhand, India. It consists of four shrines — Yamunotri, Gangotri, Kedarnath and Badrinath — visited in that order, traditionally from west to east. The circuit is "smaller" in contrast to the nationwide Char Dham circuit of Badrinath, Dwarka, Puri and Rameswaram established, by tradition, by the 8th-century philosopher Adi Shankara.

The pilgrimage season runs roughly from late April or May (Akshaya Tritiya) until October or November, when the temples close for winter and the deities are moved to lower-altitude winter seats. Kedarnath, at about 3,583 metres (11,755 ft), is also one of the twelve Jyotirlingas and part of the Panch Kedar.

Access

The circuit begins at Haridwar or Rishikesh. A major all-weather road project, the Char Dham Pariyojana, has widened the highways serving the shrines, and helicopter services operate to Kedarnath and Badrinath during the season. The region was devastated by the June 2013 North India floods, which killed thousands, many of them pilgrims in the Kedarnath valley; the circuit has since been progressively rebuilt.
