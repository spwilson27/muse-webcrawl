John Cleland (baptised 24 September 1709 – 23 January 1789) was an English novelist best known for his erotic novel Fanny Hill: or, the Memoirs of a Woman of Pleasure (1748), one of the most prosecuted and banned books in history.

The son of an army officer, Cleland attended Westminster School and served with the East India Company in Bombay before returning to London and a precarious literary career. He wrote Fanny Hill while imprisoned for debt in the Fleet Prison; on publication he was prosecuted for obscenity, and the book was officially suppressed in Britain, though pirated copies circulated for two centuries. It was finally published legally in the United States in 1966 and in Britain in 1970.

Other works

Cleland wrote several further novels, including Memoirs of a Coxcomb, plays, translations and works on language, but none approached the notoriety or influence of Fanny Hill.
