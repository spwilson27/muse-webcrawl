Safavid Iran

Safavid Iran (1501–1736), also called the Safavid Empire or Safavid Persia, was one of the great Iranian empires after the Muslim conquest of Persia. Ruled by the Safavid dynasty, it established Twelver Shia Islam as Iran's official religion — shaping Iran's modern identity — and was a major power in trade, art and architecture.

Origins

The dynasty descended from Safi-ad-din Ardabili, founder of a Sufi order in Ardabil. His descendant Ismail I, supported by Qizilbash Turkmen tribesmen, captured Tabriz in 1501, proclaimed himself shah and imposed Twelver Shi'ism. His defeat by the Ottomans at Chaldiran (1514) checked expansion westward.

Golden age under Abbas I

Shah Abbas I (r. 1588–1629) reformed the army with musketeer and artillery corps and Georgian-Armenian ghulam troops, recaptured territory from Ottomans and Uzbeks, moved the capital to Isfahan, and made it a jewel of mosques, palaces and bridges such as the Shah Mosque and Naqsh-e Jahan Square. Silk exports via the Armenian merchants of New Julfa enriched the state.

Decline and fall

Later shahs weakened; in 1722 Afghan rebels under Mahmud Hotak besieged Isfahan and deposed Sultan Husayn. Safavid pretenders lingered until Nader Shah Afshar formally ended the dynasty in 1736.
