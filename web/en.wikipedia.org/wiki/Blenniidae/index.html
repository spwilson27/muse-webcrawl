Blenniidae

Blenniidae is the largest family of fishes in the order Blenniiformes, the combtooth blennies. The family contains around 400 species in 58 genera, found mainly in tropical and subtropical seas worldwide, with a few species in brackish and fresh water.

Description

Combtooth blennies are small, scaleless fishes with blunt heads, large eyes set high on the head, and long dorsal fins. Their name refers to the single row of numerous fine, comb-like teeth in the jaws. Many species have fleshy tentacles (cirri) above the eyes. Most are less than 15 cm long; the largest, the hairtail blenny, reaches about 55 cm. Some, such as the fang blennies of the genus Meiacanthus, possess venomous fangs — unusual among fishes — and are mimicked by other species.

Behaviour and ecology

Blennies are bottom-dwellers of reefs, rocky shores and tide pools, feeding on algae, detritus and small invertebrates. Males typically guard eggs laid in crevices or empty shells until they hatch. The Pacific leaping blenny and its relatives spend much of their lives out of water on splash-zone rocks, breathing through their skin and gills. Many colourful species, including Ecsenius and Salarias species, are popular in the aquarium trade.

Taxonomy

The family is divided into two subfamilies, Blenniinae and Salariinae. Together with the clinids, labrisomids and other families, they form the blennioid assemblage of perch-like fishes.
