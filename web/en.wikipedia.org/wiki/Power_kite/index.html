Power kite


Labelled image placeholder: a foil power kite

A power kite or traction kite is a large kite designed to provide significant pull to the user. They come in three main configurations: foil kites, leading-edge inflatable kites, and supported leading-edge kites.

Types

Foil kites are soft kites based on the paraglider design, with air-filled cells that give the wing its shape; they are used mainly on land. Leading edge inflatable (LEI) kites have an inflatable bladder along the front edge and are the standard for kite surfing because they can be relaunched from water. Kites range from about 1 m² trainer models to wings of more than 20 m² for light-wind riding.

Uses

Power kites provide the traction for kite surfing (kiteboarding), kite landboarding, kite buggying, and snow kiting, and can also be used simply for recreation and static flying. The user wears a harness connected to the kite's control bar or handles, and steers by moving the kite through the "wind window"; power is greatest when the kite crosses the centre of the window and can be reduced by steering the kite to the window's edge or sheeting out.

Safety

Because a power kite can lift or drag the user, safety systems are central to the sport. Modern kites have depower ranges, quick-release mechanisms that detach the kite, and kite leashes. Beginners are advised to learn with small trainer kites and take lessons, as gusty conditions have caused serious accidents.
