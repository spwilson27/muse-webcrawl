Kansai dialect

The Kansai dialect (関西弁, Kansai-ben) is a group of Japanese dialects spoken in the Kansai region of Japan, including Osaka, Kyoto, Kobe, Nara and surrounding areas. It is the most widely recognised regional dialect of Japanese, associated nationally with comedy, commerce and a warm, direct style of speech.

Features

Kansai-ben differs from standard (Tokyo) Japanese in vocabulary, grammar and pitch accent. Characteristic forms include akan ("no good") for dame, mōkarimakka greetings among merchants, the negative -hen (e.g. wakarahen, "I don't understand"), and the copula ya in place of da. Kyoto speech (Kyō-kotoba) is traditionally softer and more honorific, reflecting its history as the imperial capital; Osaka speech is brisker and mercantile.

Cultural role

Because Osaka is the home of manzai comedy, Kansai dialect is strongly associated in Japanese media with humour; comedians from the Yoshimoto agency popularised it nationwide. Characters in anime, television drama and film frequently speak Kansai-ben to signal a comic or down-to-earth personality.
