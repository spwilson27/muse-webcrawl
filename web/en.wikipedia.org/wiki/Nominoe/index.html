Nominoe (Breton: Nevenoe; French: Nominoë; died 7 March 851) was the first ruler of Brittany to lay the foundations of an independent Breton polity. Originally a missus imperatoris and count appointed by the Carolingian emperor Louis the Pious, he progressively broke with Frankish authority under Charles the Bald and is regarded by Breton tradition as the father of the Breton nation (Tad ar Vro).

Rise to power

After the Breton campaigns of Louis the Pious, Nominoe was established by about 830 as the emperor's representative in Brittany, holding the county of Vannes. Following Louis's death in 840, Nominoe at first supported Lothair I, but then, in conflict with Charles the Bald, whose nominee Bishop Actard of Nantes he expelled, he moved toward open rebellion in alliance with Lambert II of Nantes.

War with Charles the Bald

On 22 November 845, Nominoe decisively defeated Charles the Bald at the Battle of Ballon, near Redon. A truce followed, but hostilities resumed; Nominoe extended Breton power into the Frankish marches, seizing Rennes and Nantes. In 849 he asserted ecclesiastical independence by deposing Frankish bishops at the synod of Coitlouh and replacing them with Breton candidates, creating the archbishopric of Dol in opposition to Tours — a controversy that lasted for centuries.

Death and legacy

Nominoe died in 851 while campaigning near Vendôme, deep in Frankish territory. His son Erispoe succeeded him and secured recognition from Charles the Bald at the Treaty of Angers. Nominoe's reign permanently altered the map of western France: Brittany emerged as a durable, effectively independent kingdom whose borders, including Rennes and Nantes, endured for the rest of the Middle Ages.
