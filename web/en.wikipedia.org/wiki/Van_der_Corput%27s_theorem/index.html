Van der Corput's theorem

Van der Corput's theorem is a result in number theory, named after the Dutch mathematician Johannes van der Corput. Several distinct theorems bear this name, concerning exponential sums, equidistribution, and lattice points.

Exponential sum estimate

One form of the theorem gives an estimate for the exponential sum Σ e^{2πi f(n)} over an interval, in terms of the size of the second derivative of f. Such estimates, including the van der Corput method of Weyl–van der Corput differencing, are fundamental tools in analytic number theory, used for example in studying the Riemann zeta function and divisor problems.

Lattice points

Another result attributed to van der Corput bounds the number of integer lattice points under a curve or inside a plane region in terms of its perimeter and curvature, improving elementary counting arguments.
