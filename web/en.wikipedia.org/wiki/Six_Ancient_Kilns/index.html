Six Ancient Kilns

The Six Ancient Kilns (日本六古窯, Nihon rokkoyō) are six historic ceramic production centres in Japan recognised for their long, continuous traditions of pottery making dating back to the medieval period. The term was coined in the mid-20th century by the ceramics scholar Fujio Koyama, who identified the kilns of Seto, Tokoname, Shigaraki, Bizen, Tamba, and Echizen as the principal surviving ancient kiln sites among the many that once existed across Japan.

The six kilns

  • Seto ware (Aichi Prefecture) — one of the few Japanese centres to produce glazed ceramics from an early date, known for ash glazes and later for diverse wares; the generic Japanese word for ceramics, setomono, derives from Seto.
  • Tokoname ware (Aichi Prefecture) — a major producer of large jars and, in later centuries, roof tiles, teapots, and industrial stoneware pipes.
  • Shigaraki ware (Shiga Prefecture) — unglazed stoneware with natural ash effects, famously including tanuki figurines in modern times.
  • Bizen ware (Okayama Prefecture) — unglazed, high-fired stoneware known for reddish-brown surfaces and firing marks such as hidasuki.
  • Tamba ware (Hyōgo Prefecture) — also called Tachikui ware, produced in the Sasayama basin, known for ash-glazed jars and sake flasks.
  • Echizen ware (Fukui Prefecture) — a northern centre producing large storage jars and grinding bowls with subdued ash glazes.

Characteristics

The six kilns produced primarily utilitarian stoneware for everyday use — jars, pots, bowls, and mortars — fired in climbing kilns using local clays. Most of their wares were unglazed or coated with natural wood-ash glaze that accumulated during firing. Their rustic, unadorned aesthetic later proved highly influential on the tea ceremony and on the Mingei (folk craft) movement.

Recognition

In 2017 the Six Ancient Kilns were collectively designated a Japan Heritage site under the title of a journey through a thousand years of pottery history. Each centre maintains museums, kilns, and festivals celebrating its ceramic tradition.
