Blue Ridge Mountains

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Layered blue ridges fading into haze at sunset, seen from the Blue Ridge Parkway]
Layered blue ridges fading into haze at sunset, seen from the Blue Ridge Parkway

The Blue Ridge Mountains are a mountain range in the eastern United States, the eastern front of the Appalachian Mountains, running some 890 km from Pennsylvania southwest through Maryland, Virginia and North Carolina to Georgia. Their name comes from the bluish haze — isoprene released by the forests, scattering light — that softens their ridges. The range includes the highest peaks east of the Mississippi, such as Mount Mitchell (2,037 m), and is traversed by the Blue Ridge Parkway and the Appalachian Trail.

Geography and ecology

Among the world's oldest mountains — the Grenville rocks at their core exceed a billion years — the Blue Ridge's long erosion has produced a temperate rainforest of exceptional diversity, especially the cove hardwood forests of the Great Smoky Mountains with their salamander capital status. Shenandoah National Park and Great Smoky Mountains National Park (the most visited US national park) protect the range's heart.

Culture

The Blue Ridge is foundational to American folk culture: Scots-Irish settlement, shape-note singing, bluegrass music (named, via Bill Monroe's band, for Kentucky but raised in these hills) and the moonshine lore of Prohibition. John Denver's 'Take Me Home, Country Roads' hymns its West Virginia margin to the world.
