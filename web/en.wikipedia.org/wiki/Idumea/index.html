Idumea

Idumea (Greek: Ἰδουμαία, Idoumaía) is the name given in Greco-Roman sources to the region south of Judea inhabited by the Edomites, centred on the northern Negev, the southern Shephelah, and the area around Hebron and Maresha (Marisa).

History

After the Babylonian destruction of Judah in the sixth century BCE, Edomites migrated northward from their homeland east and south of the Dead Sea into the depopulated southern hill country of Judah. Their territory became known as Idumea. During the Hellenistic period the Idumeans were a distinct ethnos with their own deity, Qos, and their cities included Maresha and Adoraim.

The Hasmonean ruler John Hyrcanus conquered Idumea around 125 BCE and, according to Josephus, compelled the Idumeans to adopt Jewish customs; the region was incorporated into the Hasmonean kingdom. The most famous Idumean descendant was Herod the Great, whose father Antipater was an Idumean noble and who became king of Judea under Roman patronage.

Under Rome, Idumea was a district (toparchy) of the province of Judea. Idumean troops took part in the First Jewish–Roman War, and the region fades from history in late antiquity.
