Storming of the Bastille

Date14 July 1789
LocationParis, France
ResultBastille captured; start of the French Revolution as a popular uprising

The Storming of the Bastille (French: Prise de la Bastille) took place in Paris, France, on 14 July 1789, when revolutionary insurgents stormed and seized control of the medieval armory, fortress and political prison known as the Bastille. At the time, the Bastille represented royal authority in Paris, and the violence of the event marked the flashpoint of the French Revolution.

Background

France in 1789 was in crisis: a near-bankrupt monarchy, soaring bread prices, and the convening of the Estates-General for the first time since 1614. The Third Estate had proclaimed itself the National Assembly, and on 11 July King Louis XVI dismissed the popular finance minister Jacques Necker — an act Parisians read as the start of a royal coup against the Assembly. Royal troops were massing around the capital. On 12 July the journalist Camille Desmoulins rallied crowds at the Palais-Royal, and over the following two days rioters seized tens of thousands of muskets from the Hôtel des Invalides — but found almost no gunpowder. The powder was stored at the Bastille.

The assault

The Bastille was defended by its governor, the Marquis de Launay, with a garrison of 82 invalides (veteran soldiers) and 32 Swiss guards, and held only seven prisoners. On the morning of 14 July a crowd demanding the fortress's arms and powder gathered outside. Negotiations dragged on; in the early afternoon the crowd surged into the courtyard, chains of the drawbridge were cut amid confused firing, and the attackers — joined by mutinous Gardes françaises with cannon — began a sustained assault. Around 5:30 p.m., facing a massacre, de Launay capitulated and the gates were opened. The crowd flooded in, liberated the prisoners, and seized the powder.

Casualties and aftermath

About 93 of the attackers were killed and scores wounded; the defenders lost one man killed in the fighting. After the surrender, de Launay was seized by the mob and killed on the way to the Hôtel de Ville, and his head was paraded on a pike; the prévôt des marchands, Jacques de Flesselles, was also murdered. The king, informed the following day, withdrew his troops and recalled Necker. The Bastille itself was demolished within months, its stones sold as souvenirs, and the site became the Place de la Bastille.

Legacy

The Fête de la Fédération was held on 14 July 1790 to mark the first anniversary. Since 1880, 14 July — le Quatorze Juillet — has been France's national day (Bastille Day), commemorating both the storming and the festival. The event remains the enduring symbol of the uprising of the modern French nation against the abuses of the ancien régime.
