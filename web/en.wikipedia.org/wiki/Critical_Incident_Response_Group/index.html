Critical Incident Response Group

CIRG includes the FBI's Hostage Rescue Team and crisis negotiation units.

The Critical Incident Response Group (CIRG) is a division of the United States Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) that provides the bureau's central response capability for crisis situations, including hostage takings, barricaded suspects, kidnap investigations and high-risk tactical operations. It is headquartered at the FBI Academy in Quantico, Virginia.

History

CIRG was created in 1994, in the aftermath of criticism of federal handling of the sieges at Ruby Ridge (1992) and Waco (1993), to consolidate the FBI's tactical, negotiation and behavioural-science crisis resources under a single command and to improve coordination during major incidents.

Components

The group encompasses the Hostage Rescue Team (HRT), the FBI's full-time counter-terrorism tactical unit; the Crisis Negotiation Unit; the Behavioral Analysis Units (including the former profiling unit); tactical helicopter and surveillance support; and the Strategic Information and Operations Center support elements. CIRG personnel train and deploy with FBI field SWAT teams and assist state and local agencies during critical incidents. Among its best-known deployments were the response to the Boston Marathon bombing in 2013 and numerous hostage-rescue and barricade operations.
