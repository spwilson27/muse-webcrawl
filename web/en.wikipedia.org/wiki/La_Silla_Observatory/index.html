La Silla Observatory

La Silla Observatory is an astronomical observatory in Chile with three telescopes built and operated by the European Southern Observatory (ESO). Several other telescopes are located at the site and are partly maintained by ESO. The observatory is one of the largest in the Southern Hemisphere and was the first ESO observatory, inaugurated in 1969 on the Cerro La Silla, a 2,400-metre mountain in the southern part of the Atacama Desert, about 160 km north of La Serena.

Telescopes and discoveries

The site's major instruments include the New Technology Telescope (NTT), a 3.58-metre telescope that pioneered active optics when it began operations in 1989; the ESO 3.6-metre telescope, host of the HARPS high-precision spectrograph, long the world's most productive exoplanet hunter by the radial-velocity method; and the MPG/ESO 2.2-metre telescope with its Wide Field Imager. National and project telescopes on the mountain include the Danish 1.54-metre, the Euler Telescope (the Swiss 1.2-metre used for exoplanet searches), TRAPPIST–South, and the REM robotic telescope. La Silla observations contributed to the discovery of numerous exoplanets, the study of supernova 1987A, gamma-ray burst afterglows, and the accelerating universe; Proxima Centauri b was found with instruments including HARPS data in 2016.
