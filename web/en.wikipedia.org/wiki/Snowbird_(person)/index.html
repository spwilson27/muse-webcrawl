Snowbird (person)

A snowbird is a person who migrates seasonally from colder northern climates to warmer southern locations during the winter months — most famously from Canada and the northern United States to the American Sun Belt, Mexico, or the Caribbean — and returns in spring.

The term, adapted from the dark-eyed junco bird known colloquially as the snowbird, was applied first to seasonal workers and later, from the mid-20th century, to retirees. Popular destinations include Florida, Arizona, Texas, Southern California and coastal Mexico. Snowbirds often stay in recreational vehicle parks, condominiums, or second homes for one to six months.

Economic and social impact

Seasonal residents contribute significantly to the economies of destination communities, supporting housing, hospitality and healthcare sectors, while raising questions about healthcare coverage (for Canadians, travel insurance and provincial residency rules), tax residency, and local services used without year-round property tax burdens. Some communities actively market to snowbirds; long-term visa and residency limits shape how long they can stay.
