2019–2020 Hong Kong protests

Protest details
DateMarch 2019 – mid-2020
LocationHong Kong
CausesProposed extradition bill; demands for democratic reform

The 2019–2020 Hong Kong protests were a series of demonstrations initially triggered by the introduction of the Fugitive Offenders amendment bill, which would have allowed extraditions to mainland China. The movement grew into wider demands for democracy and police accountability.

Course of the movement

Marches in June 2019 drew crowds estimated at up to two million people. After protesters stormed the Legislative Council on 1 July and a mob attack on commuters at Yuen Long station later that month, confrontations between police and demonstrators intensified. The protesters' "five demands" included full withdrawal of the bill, an independent inquiry into police conduct, amnesty for arrested protesters, retraction of the "riot" characterisation, and universal suffrage.

Key events

The bill's withdrawal was announced in September 2019, but protests continued, culminating in sieges at the Chinese University of Hong Kong and Hong Kong Polytechnic University in November. Pro-democracy candidates won a landslide in the November District Council elections. Street protests waned in early 2020 amid the COVID-19 pandemic.

Aftermath

On 30 June 2020, Beijing imposed a national security law on Hong Kong, criminalising secession, subversion, terrorism and collusion with foreign forces. Mass arrests of activists and the closure of pro-democracy media followed, effectively ending the movement.
