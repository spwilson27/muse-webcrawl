Hancock Park

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hancock Park is a neighbourhood in the Wilshire area of Los Angeles, California. Developed in the 1920s on land formerly belonging to the Hancock family, it is known for its large, architecturally distinctive houses, broad lawns and mature canopy trees, and is recognised as a Historic Preservation Overlay Zone.

History

The neighbourhood takes its name from George Allan Hancock, an oil magnate whose father, Major Henry Hancock, had acquired the Rancho La Brea land grant in the nineteenth century. Hancock developed the residential tract from the 1920s, requiring houses to be set back from the street and designed in period revival styles, with driveways and utilities placed at the rear.

The neighbourhood surrounds the Wilshire Country Club and lies just east of the Los Angeles County Museum of Art and the La Brea Tar Pits on Wilshire Boulevard—though those institutions are technically just outside its boundaries, a point of recurring local discussion.

Character

Hancock Park's roughly 1,200 homes are mostly large two-storey houses in Tudor Revival, Spanish Colonial Revival, English Revival and other period styles, many designed by noted Los Angeles architects. Residents have included Nat King Cole, who moved there in 1948 despite opposition from some neighbours, and numerous figures from the entertainment industry. The area contains the official residence of the mayor of Los Angeles, the Getty House.
