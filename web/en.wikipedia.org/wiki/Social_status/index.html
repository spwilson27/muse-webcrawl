Social status

Social status is the position or rank of a person or group within a society, based on the honour or prestige attached to that position. Status can be determined by wealth, occupation, education, ancestry, lifestyle and other socially valued attributes, and it strongly shapes how people are treated and how they behave.

Achieved and ascribed status

Anthropologist Ralph Linton distinguished ascribed status, assigned at birth or involuntarily (such as caste, sex or inherited title), from achieved status, earned through effort and choice (such as a profession or educational degree). An individual holds many statuses at once; the one that dominates social identity is called a master status.

Theories

Max Weber treated status as one dimension of stratification alongside class and party, emphasising that status groups maintain distinctive lifestyles and exclude outsiders through social closure. Erving Goffman analysed status symbols and stigma; Pierre Bourdieu connected status to cultural capital. Sociologists also study status inconsistency — when a person ranks high on one dimension and low on another — and status attainment, how people come to occupy their positions.
