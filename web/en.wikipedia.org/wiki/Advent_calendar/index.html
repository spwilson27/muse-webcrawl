Advent calendar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An Advent calendar is a special calendar used to count the days of Advent in anticipation of Christmas. Most begin on 1 December — rather than on the first Sunday of Advent, which varies — and contain 24 small numbered doors or windows, one opened each day, revealing a picture, a piece of chocolate, a small gift or a verse. The custom originated among German Lutherans in the 19th century and has since spread worldwide, becoming a largely secular seasonal product as well as a devotional one.

History

German Protestants marked the days to Christmas with chalk lines on doors, by lighting candles, or by hanging a new religious picture each day. Printed calendars appeared in the early 20th century: Gerhard Lang of Munich is credited with the first mass-produced Advent calendar (about 1903–1908), inspired by a cardboard calendar with biscuits his mother made for him; his design of little doors, introduced in the 1920s, became the standard form. Production lapsed during the Second World War (cardboard rationing, and Nazi-era restrictions on religious imagery) and revived afterwards, spreading to Britain and the United States in the 1950s — helped by a photograph of President Eisenhower's grandchildren with one. Chocolate-filled calendars became common from the 1970s onward.

Variations

Modern calendars may contain toys, cosmetics, tea, alcohol miniatures or pet treats, and luxury versions are a significant retail category. Reusable fabric or wooden calendars with pockets are filled by hand, and some towns create "living" calendars in which a different house window is unveiled each evening. In Scandinavia, televised Advent calendars (such as Norway's and Sweden's annual TV series with accompanying paper calendars) are an institution, as are the 24-episode radio and podcast versions.
