Glutamate dehydrogenase


Glutamate dehydrogenase, a mitochondrial enzyme of amino acid metabolism.

Glutamate dehydrogenase (GLDH, or GDH) is an enzyme observed in prokaryotes and in the mitochondria of eukaryotes that converts glutamate to α-ketoglutarate, and vice versa, playing a central role in linking amino acid metabolism to the citric acid cycle and in the handling of nitrogen.

Reaction

The enzyme catalyses the reversible oxidative deamination of L-glutamate: glutamate + NAD(P)+ + H2O ⇌ α-ketoglutarate + NH4+ + NAD(P)H + H+. Mammalian glutamate dehydrogenase, encoded by the GLUD1 gene, can use either NAD+ or NADP+ as cofactor, whereas many microbial enzymes are specific for one or the other. The human enzyme is a hexamer of identical subunits of about 56 kDa and is located in the mitochondrial matrix, with the highest levels in liver, kidney, brain and pancreatic beta cells.

Regulation

Glutamate dehydrogenase is subject to sophisticated allosteric control. It is inhibited by GTP and ATP (signals of energy abundance) and activated by ADP and by the amino acid leucine. This regulation connects the enzyme to energy status: when energy is low, glutamate is oxidised to feed α-ketoglutarate into the citric acid cycle; in pancreatic beta cells, amino-acid-stimulated activity of the enzyme contributes to insulin secretion.

Clinical significance

Gain-of-function mutations in GLUD1 cause the hyperinsulinism/hyperammonaemia syndrome, in which increased enzyme activity — and heightened sensitivity to leucine — produces excessive insulin release with hypoglycaemia together with persistently elevated plasma ammonia; the hypoglycaemia responds to the drug diazoxide. A second human gene, GLUD2, arose by retrotransposition to the X chromosome and is expressed principally in the nervous system and testes, where it is thought to support glutamate recycling in astrocytes during neurotransmission. Serum GLDH is also used clinically as a relatively liver-specific biomarker of hepatocellular injury, released from damaged hepatocyte mitochondria.
