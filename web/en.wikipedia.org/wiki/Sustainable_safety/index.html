Sustainable safety

Sustainable safety (Dutch: Duurzaam Veilig) is a Dutch approach to road traffic safety that aims to reduce the risk of crashes and to limit the severity of those that do occur, by designing the road system so that human error does not lead to death or serious injury. The vision was first articulated by the Dutch Institute for Road Safety Research (SWOV) and adopted as national policy in the Netherlands in the 1990s.

Principles

The original Sustainable Safety vision rested on five principles:

  • Functionality — roads should have a single, clear function: through traffic, distribution, or access, avoiding mixes of functions.
  • Homogeneity — differences in speed, direction and mass between road users sharing a space should be minimized; where they cannot be, users should be separated in space or time.
  • Predictability — road design should be recognizable and consistent so that road users know what behavior to expect of themselves and others.
  • Forgivingness — the road environment should be forgiving of errors, so that mistakes do not lead to serious injury.
  • State awareness — road users should be able to assess their own capability to participate in traffic.

A renewed version of the vision, published in 2018, reorganized the principles around functionality, homogeneity, predictability, forgivingness (extended to the social environment), and state awareness.

Impact

The approach influenced the widespread application in the Netherlands of measures such as roundabouts, 30 km/h zones in built-up areas, physically separated cycle tracks, and area-wide speed management. The Netherlands has consistently ranked among the countries with the lowest road fatality rates in Europe, and Sustainable Safety principles have influenced Vision Zero and other safe-system approaches internationally.
