Fashoda Incident

Date: July – November 1898
Location: Fashoda (Kodok), Sudan
Participants: United Kingdom, France
Result: French withdrawal; Anglo-French convention of 1899

The Fashoda Incident was the climax of a series of territorial disputes between Britain and France in Africa, occurring in 1898 at the fortified village of Fashoda (modern Kodok) on the White Nile in Sudan. It brought the two colonial powers to the brink of war before ending in a French withdrawal and a diplomatic settlement.

Background

During the Scramble for Africa, Britain pursued a north–south axis of territory from Cairo to the Cape, while France sought an east–west belt from Senegal to Djibouti. The two ambitions intersected in the upper Nile valley. After the British-Egyptian reconquest of Sudan under General Kitchener, crowned by the victory at Omdurman on 2 September 1898, control of the Nile headwaters was a strategic priority for London, which feared any rival power able to threaten Egypt's water supply.

The confrontation

A French expedition under Major Jean-Baptiste Marchand, having marched across Africa from the Congo, reached Fashoda first, on 10 July 1898, and raised the French flag. On 18 September Kitchener arrived with a much larger Anglo-Egyptian force and gunboats. The two commanders behaved with notable courtesy — they exchanged visits and even shared drinks — while referring the dispute to their governments. In Europe the crisis inflamed public opinion: Britain, enjoying overwhelming naval superiority and supported by the Royal Navy's mobilisation, refused to negotiate while French troops remained. Isolated diplomatically and unwilling to risk war, France ordered Marchand to withdraw on 3 November 1898.

Aftermath

The Anglo-French Convention of March 1899 delimited the two empires' spheres, with France abandoning claims to the Nile valley in return for recognition of its position in the Chad basin and West Africa. The humiliation at Fashoda pushed France toward consolidating its alliance with Russia and, eventually, reconciliation with Britain in the Entente Cordiale of 1904.
