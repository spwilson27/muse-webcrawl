Concord Records

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Concord Records logo

Concord Records is an American record label owned by Concord and based in Los Angeles, California. It was founded in 1972 by Carl Jefferson, originally as a jazz label known as Concord Jazz, an offshoot of the Concord Jazz Festival in Concord, California.

History

Jefferson founded the label to document artists appearing at the Concord Summer Festival, which he had started in 1969. Early releases featured guitarists such as Herb Ellis, Joe Pass, and Barney Kessel, and the label became known for mainstream and Latin jazz, recording artists including Cal Tjader, Scott Hamilton, and Rosemary Clooney.

In 1999 the label was sold to a group led by Hal Gaba and producer Norman Lear. Concord subsequently expanded dramatically through acquisitions, including Fantasy Records in 2004 (bringing in the catalogs of Prestige, Riverside, Milestone, and Stax), Telarc, Rounder Records, and the catalog of Musart. The parent company, Concord, also became a major independent music rights holder and acquired Imagem Music and other publishing assets.

Artists and catalog

The label's releases have won numerous Grammy Awards, notably Ray Charles's final album Genius Loves Company (2004), which won eight Grammys including Album of the Year. Notable artists associated with Concord and its imprints include Esperanza Spalding, Chick Corea, Dave Brubeck (via catalog), and many others across jazz, Americana, classical, and rock.
