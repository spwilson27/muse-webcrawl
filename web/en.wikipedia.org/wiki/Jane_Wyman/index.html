Jane Wyman

Jane Wyman (born Sarah Jane Mayfield; January 5, 1917 – September 10, 2007) was an American actress. She won the Academy Award for Best Actress for her performance as a deaf-mute rape victim in Johnny Belinda (1948) and received three further Oscar nominations, for The Yearling (1946), The Blue Veil (1951) and Magnificent Obsession (1954). A later generation knew her as the scheming matriarch Angela Channing in the prime-time soap opera Falcon Crest (1981–1990). She was the first wife of future U.S. president Ronald Reagan.

Early life and career

Wyman was born in St. Joseph, Missouri, and after her father's death was raised in foster care before being taken in by her mother in Los Angeles. She began as a chorus girl and extra at Warner Bros. in the early 1930s, gradually rising through supporting roles in musicals and comedies, often as the wisecracking blonde. Her shift to dramatic roles came with Billy Wilder's The Lost Weekend (1945), followed by the family drama The Yearling.

Johnny Belinda and stardom

Her dialogue-free performance in Johnny Belinda (1948) — for which she learned sign language and spent the shoot with wax in her ears — won her the Academy Award. She went on to star in Hitchcock's Stage Fright (1950), the weepie The Blue Veil (1951), Walt Disney's Pollyanna (1960), and two Douglas Sirk melodramas opposite Rock Hudson, Magnificent Obsession (1954) and All That Heaven Allows (1955), now regarded as classics of the genre.

Television and later life

Wyman hosted the anthology series Jane Wyman Presents in the 1950s. In 1981 she returned to prominence as the imperious Angela Channing on Falcon Crest, earning a Golden Globe in 1984. Married to Ronald Reagan from 1940 to 1949, she remained on friendly terms with him and declined to comment publicly on his political career. She retired in the 1990s and died of natural causes at her home in Rancho Mirage, California, in 2007, aged 90.
