NBA Most Valuable Player Award

The National Basketball Association Most Valuable Player Award (MVP) is an annual National Basketball Association (NBA) award given since the 1955–56 season to the best performing player of the regular season. The winner receives the Michael Jordan Trophy, renamed in December 2022 in honour of five-time MVP Michael Jordan; the trophy was previously named for Maurice Podoloff, the NBA's first commissioner. Since the 2022–23 season, players must appear in at least 65 games (with limited exceptions) to be eligible for the award.

Voting

Until the 1979–80 season the MVP was selected by a vote of NBA players. Since the 1980–81 season, a panel of sportswriters and broadcasters from the United States and Canada votes, each ranking five players; a first-place vote is worth 10 points, second 7, third 5, fourth 3 and fifth 1. Since 2010 one ballot has been a collective fan vote conducted online.

History and records

Kareem Abdul-Jabbar won the award a record six times. Bill Russell and Michael Jordan each won five; Wilt Chamberlain and LeBron James won four. Russell, Chamberlain and Larry Bird are the only players to win three consecutive MVPs; Nikola Jokić and Giannis Antetokounmpo are among the international players to win the award, and Stephen Curry (2015–16) is the only unanimous winner, receiving all 131 first-place votes. Derrick Rose, at 22, is the youngest winner. The Maurice Podoloff Trophy was renamed the Michael Jordan Trophy as part of a 2022 redesign of the league's major award trophies. Winning the MVP is considered a central credential for Hall of Fame candidacy; every eligible MVP winner to date has been inducted into the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame.
