Sustainable tourism

Sustainable tourism is tourism that takes full account of its current and future economic, social and environmental impacts, addressing the needs of visitors, the industry, the environment and host communities (UN Tourism definition). It emerged from the broader sustainable development movement and gained prominence after the 1992 Rio Earth Summit.

Principles

Sustainable tourism aims to make optimal use of environmental resources, respect the socio-cultural authenticity of host communities, and ensure viable, long-term economic operations with fairly distributed benefits, including stable employment and poverty alleviation. It requires the informed participation of all relevant stakeholders and constant monitoring of impacts.

Related forms

Related concepts include ecotourism, which focuses on nature conservation and education; responsible tourism, the framework of the 2002 Cape Town Declaration emphasising the responsibilities of all actors; and regenerative tourism, which seeks to leave destinations better than they were found. Certification schemes such as those recognised by the Global Sustainable Tourism Council attempt to verify claims and combat greenwashing.

Criticism and challenges

Critics note that air travel's carbon emissions, overtourism in cities such as Venice and Barcelona, and "leakage" of tourist spending to foreign owners limit how sustainable mass tourism can be. The COVID-19 pandemic prompted calls to "build back better", though visitor numbers subsequently rebounded to record levels.
