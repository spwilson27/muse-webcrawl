Kilgore is a city in Gregg and Rusk counties in the eastern part of the U.S. state of Texas. It was founded in 1872 as a stop on the International–Great Northern Railroad and named for Constantine Buckley Kilgore, who sold the land for the townsite. For its first half-century it was a quiet farming community — until the discovery of oil transformed it overnight into one of the most famous boomtowns in America.

Oil boom

In late 1930 the great East Texas Oil Field — the largest petroleum reservoir in the contiguous United States at the time — was discovered around Kilgore. The town's population exploded from about 700 to some 10,000 within months. At the peak of the boom, more than a thousand oil wells stood within the city limits; one downtown block, packed with 24 wells, was dubbed "the World's Richest Acre". Oil production was so uncontrolled that the governor declared martial law in the field in 1931 and sent in the Texas Rangers and National Guard to enforce proration.

Steel derricks that once crowded downtown are commemorated today by replica derricks topped with stars, giving Kilgore its nickname, "City of Stars". The East Texas Oil Museum at Kilgore College recreates the boom era.

Culture and education

Kilgore is home to Kilgore College (founded 1935), famous for the Kilgore College Rangerettes, the first precision drill team of its kind, created in 1940 and still internationally known. The city also hosts the Texas Shakespeare Festival, held each summer at Kilgore College since 1986.
