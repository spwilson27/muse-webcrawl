Waltham Cross is a town in the Borough of Broxbourne, Hertfordshire, England, on the border with Greater London and Essex. It lies immediately north of Enfield and west of Waltham Abbey, at the junction of the old Roman Ermine Street with the road to Waltham Abbey.

The Eleanor Cross in the town centre

The Eleanor Cross

The town is named for the medieval cross erected by Edward I in memory of his queen, Eleanor of Castile, who died in 1290. Her body rested at Waltham on its funeral journey to Westminster, and the cross built here is one of three surviving of the twelve original Eleanor crosses (the others being at Geddington and Hardingstone). The ornate Gothic cross, with its statues of the queen, was heavily restored in the Victorian era.

History and transport

Waltham Cross grew as a coaching and market point on the road north from London. The Four Swans inn hosted the "Waltham" way of early postal services, and novelist Anthony Trollope lived nearby at Waltham House while working for the Post Office. Waltham Cross railway station, opened in 1840, provides services to London Liverpool Street. Nearby, the Lee Valley White Water Centre, built for canoe slalom at the 2012 London Olympics, lies just east of the town in the Lea Valley.
