Weight training

Weight training, also known as resistance training, is a form of exercise in which skeletal muscles contract against an external resistance — typically free weights, weight machines, or the trainee's own body weight — with the goal of increasing muscular strength, size (hypertrophy), or endurance. It is a common component of training for many sports and is widely practised for general fitness and health.

Training programmes are built from exercises performed for sets of repetitions ("reps") at a given load, organised by movements such as the squat, bench press, deadlift, overhead press and row. The principle of progressive overload — gradually increasing the demand on the muscles over time — is the foundation of long-term adaptation.

Principles

Muscles adapt to the stresses placed on them. Varying intensity (load as a percentage of one-repetition maximum), volume (sets and reps), exercise selection and rest intervals changes the stimulus: heavy loads at low repetitions emphasise maximal strength, moderate loads at moderate repetitions are commonly used for hypertrophy, and lighter loads at high repetitions emphasise muscular endurance. Adequate recovery, sleep and protein intake are required for adaptation, and correct technique reduces the risk of injury.

Health effects

Regular weight training increases muscle mass and strength, improves bone mineral density (reducing the risk of osteoporosis), supports metabolic health, and can improve balance and functional capacity in older adults. Health authorities such as the World Health Organization and the American College of Sports Medicine recommend muscle-strengthening activities on at least two days per week for adults. Risks include strains, sprains and, with poor technique or excessive load, more serious musculoskeletal injury.

History

Strength display and training with heavy objects date to antiquity, from Greek athletes and the wrestler Milo of Croton to the strongmen of the nineteenth century such as Eugen Sandow, often called the father of modern bodybuilding. Weight training was systematised in the twentieth century through the growth of commercial gyms, the founding of bodybuilding and powerlifting competitions, and scientific study of exercise physiology.
