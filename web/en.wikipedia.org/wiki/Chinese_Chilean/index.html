Chinese Chilean

Chinese Chileans (Spanish: chinos chilenos) are Chilean citizens and residents of Chinese ancestry. The community is relatively small compared with the much larger Chinese communities of neighbouring Peru, but it has a history stretching back to the 19th century and has grown with expanding trade between China and Chile.

History

Chinese migration to Chile began in the late 19th century, when Chinese labourers and merchants arrived in the nitrate-mining north, particularly around Iquique — territory Chile acquired from Peru and Bolivia after the War of the Pacific (1879–1884). Some migrants had first settled in Peru and moved south. In the 20th century the community remained small, concentrated in commerce and restaurants.

Since the 1990s, and especially after the China–Chile Free Trade Agreement of 2005 (the first such agreement China signed with a Latin American country), migration increased, with newcomers from mainland China joining the older Cantonese-speaking community. A small Chinatown district exists in the Estación Central area of Santiago.
