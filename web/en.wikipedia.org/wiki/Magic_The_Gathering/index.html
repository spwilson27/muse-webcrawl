Magic: The Gathering

DesignerRichard Garfield
PublisherWizards of the Coast
ReleasedAugust 1993
Players2 or more
TypeCollectible / trading card game

Magic: The Gathering (often shortened to Magic or MTG) is a collectible card game created by mathematician Richard Garfield and published by Wizards of the Coast in 1993. It is generally recognised as the first modern trading card game and the model for the genre. Players take the role of Planeswalkers — powerful wizards — who cast spells, summon creatures and use artefacts to reduce an opponent's life total from 20 to 0.

Gameplay

Cards are drawn from a personalised deck built around five colours of mana — white, blue, black, red and green — produced mainly by land cards and each associated with a philosophy and characteristic mechanics. Players spend mana to play creatures, sorceries, instants, enchantments, artefacts and planeswalkers across structured turns with phases and a priority system ("the stack") that governs how spells resolve. Because each player assembles their own deck from a pool of tens of thousands of distinct cards, deck construction is as central to the game as play itself.

History and formats

The game's first printings, Alpha and Beta (1993), sold out rapidly; early cards such as the Black Lotus — one of the "Power Nine" — have become among the most valuable trading cards in the world. Wizards of the Coast was acquired by Hasbro in 1999. Play is organised into formats regulating which sets are legal, including Standard, Pioneer, Modern, Legacy, Vintage and the hugely popular multiplayer casual format Commander. Digital versions include Magic: The Gathering Online (2002) and MTG Arena (2019). Wizards reported over 50 million players in the early 2020s, and the game's tournament circuit, Pro Tour and Hall of Fame have run since the mid-1990s.
