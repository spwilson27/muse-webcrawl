Nephites

In the Book of Mormon, the Nephites are one of the main peoples described as having settled in the ancient Americas. They are said to descend from the prophet Nephi, son of Lehi, who led his family out of Jerusalem around 600 BC and across the ocean to the New World.

Narrative

According to the Book of Mormon, after Lehi's death his sons' followers split into two nations: the Nephites, generally portrayed as the more righteous and civilised group, and the Lamanites, descendants of Laman and Lemuel. The Nephite record chronicles centuries of alternating righteousness and apostasy, wars with the Lamanites, the visit of the resurrected Jesus Christ to the Americas, and a period of peace followed by renewed wickedness. Around AD 385 the Nephites were destroyed in a final war culminating at the Hill Cumorah, where the last Nephite prophet-historian, Moroni, buried the golden plates from which Joseph Smith later said he translated the Book of Mormon.

Belief and scholarship

Members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints regard the Nephites as historical peoples. Mainstream archaeologists and historians have found no evidence corroborating a Nephite civilisation, and the Book of Mormon's account is generally treated by scholars outside the faith as a 19th-century religious text; the question of Nephite historicity remains a subject of religious apologetics and debate.
