La Cucaracha

"La Cucaracha" ("The Cockroach") is a traditional Spanish folk song and corrido that became popular in Mexico during the Mexican Revolution. The song's simple, repetitive melody and improvised verses have made it one of the best-known Spanish-language songs in the world.

Origins

The tune is of Spanish origin; a version of the song was already known in Spain by the mid-19th century. In Mexico, soldiers of the Revolution added countless satirical verses, many mocking the Huertista general Victoriano Huerta or, in Pancho Villa's version, celebrating the revolutionary cause.

Lyrics

The most famous refrain declares that the cockroach "can no longer walk" because it lacks one of its hind legs (in some versions, marijuana to smoke — a reference variously attributed to Huerta's habits). Because the verses are improvised, hundreds of variants exist.

In popular culture

The melody is commonly used for novelty car horns in Mexico and the United States, and it has appeared in numerous films, cartoons, and recordings.
