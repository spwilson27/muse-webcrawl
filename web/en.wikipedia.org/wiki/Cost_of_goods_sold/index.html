Cost of goods sold

Cost of goods sold (COGS) is the carrying value of goods sold during a particular period. It comprises the direct costs attributable to the production of the goods a company sells: the cost of materials, direct labour, and production overheads allocated to the units sold. COGS appears on the income statement and is subtracted from revenue to give gross profit.

Calculation

For a merchandising business, COGS is computed as:

Beginning inventory + purchases (and freight-in) − ending inventory = cost of goods sold

For a manufacturer, the calculation runs through cost of goods manufactured: beginning finished-goods inventory plus the cost of goods completed in the period (direct materials, direct labour and applied factory overhead) minus ending finished-goods inventory.

Inventory costing methods

Which costs flow into COGS depends on the inventory valuation method adopted: first-in, first-out (FIFO), last-in, first-out (LIFO — permitted under US GAAP but not IFRS), or weighted average cost. In periods of rising prices, FIFO yields lower COGS and higher reported profit; LIFO yields higher COGS and lower taxable income.

Exclusions

COGS excludes indirect expenses such as marketing, distribution and administrative salaries, which are reported as operating expenses, as well as abnormal waste and idle-capacity costs, which under IAS 2 are expensed immediately. For service businesses with little or no inventory, the analogous line is "cost of services" or "cost of revenue".
