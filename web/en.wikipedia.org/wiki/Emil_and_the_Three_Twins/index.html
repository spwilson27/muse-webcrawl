Emil and the Three Twins

Emil and the Three Twins (German: Emil und die drei Zwillinge) is a children's novel by the German author Erich Kästner, published in 1934. It is the sequel to Kästner's celebrated Emil and the Detectives (1929) and reunites Emil Tischbein with his Berlin friends, the Professor, Gustav, and the others, for a holiday adventure on the Baltic coast.

Plot

Emil is invited by the Professor's family to spend the summer holidays at a house on the Baltic Sea. The boy detectives become involved with a troupe of young acrobats, the "three twins"—in fact only one pair of twins plus a third boy billed as a triplet—whose leader plans to run off with the takings and abandon his partners. With the help of a Danish ship's boy and the local community, the children expose the scheme and ensure the young performers are properly looked after.

Background

Kästner wrote the sequel as the Nazi regime was consolidating power; his books for adults were banned and burned in 1933, but his children's works remained in print. The novel keeps the playful, cooperative spirit of the first Emil book, transposed from the big city to a seaside resort. English translations appeared within a few years of publication, and the book remains in print as a companion to Emil and the Detectives.
