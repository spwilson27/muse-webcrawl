Cephalopod limb

Cephalopod limbs are the appendages surrounding the mouth of cephalopods — squid, octopuses, cuttlefish, and nautiluses. All living cephalopods have eight or ten limbs in the coleoid groups: octopuses have eight arms, while squid and cuttlefish have eight arms plus two specialised tentacles. Nautiluses have up to ninety small tentacles.

Arms and tentacles

Arms are lined with suckers along most of their length — in octopuses in two rows, typically without hooks or stalks. Tentacles, by contrast, have a long smooth stalk and bear suckers only on the expanded club at the tip; in squid the suckers may be reinforced with chitinous rings or hooks. Tentacles shoot out rapidly to seize prey, while arms manipulate food toward the beak.

Function and anatomy

Cephalopod limbs are muscular hydrostats, like the human tongue, lacking any skeleton and capable of bending and elongating in complex ways. In males, one arm is modified as the hectocotylus for transferring spermatophores during mating. Lost limbs can be regenerated in many species.
