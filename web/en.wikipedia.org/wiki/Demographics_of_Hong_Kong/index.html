Hong Kong has a population of approximately 7.5 million, making it one of the most densely populated places in the world, with an overall density of about 6,800 people per square kilometre and far higher densities in built-up districts such as Kwun Tong and Wong Tai Sin.

Ethnicity and language

Ethnic Chinese comprise about 92 per cent of the population, most of Cantonese origin, with significant communities of other Han groups including Teochew, Hakka, and Shanghainese. The largest non-Chinese communities include Filipinos and Indonesians, many employed as foreign domestic helpers, together with long-established South Asian communities and Western expatriates. Cantonese is the mother tongue of the great majority and a co-official language alongside English; Putonghua (Mandarin) use has grown since the 1997 handover.

Population trends

Hong Kong's population grew rapidly after 1945 through natural increase and immigration from mainland China. Since the 1990s, however, its fertility rate has fallen to among the lowest in the world, around or below one child per woman, and the population has aged markedly, with a median age in the mid-40s. Immigration under the one-way permit scheme from the mainland, at a quota of 150 people per day, remains the main source of population growth.

Religion

Religious life reflects a blend of Buddhism, Taoism, and Chinese folk religion, practised by a large share of the population alongside ancestral worship. Christians make up roughly one-sixth of the population, and there are longstanding Muslim, Hindu, Sikh, and Jewish communities. A substantial minority profess no religion.

Residency

Most residents are permanent residents with the right of abode. A significant share of the population was born in mainland China. Hong Kong residents also have one of the world's highest life expectancies, exceeding 85 years on average, supported by a heavily subsidised public healthcare system.
