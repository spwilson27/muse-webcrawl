Allison Brooks Janney (born November 19, 1959) is an American actress. Known for her versatility across drama and comedy, she is the recipient of numerous accolades, including an Academy Award and seven Primetime Emmy Awards, making her one of the most decorated performers of her generation.

Career

Born in Boston and raised in Dayton, Ohio, Janney studied at Kenyon College, where Paul Newman — an alumnus — directed her in a play and encouraged her acting ambitions; she went on to train at the Neighborhood Playhouse and the Royal Academy of Dramatic Art. After small roles in films and soap operas, her breakthrough came as press secretary C. J. Cregg in Aaron Sorkin's political drama The West Wing (1999–2006), for which she won four Emmy Awards.

Janney won three further Emmys: two for playing Bonnie Plunkett, a recovering addict, in the sitcom Mom (2013–2021), and one for a guest role in Masters of Sex. In film she appeared in American Beauty, The Hours, Juno, The Help, and Spy, and won the Academy Award for Best Supporting Actress for her performance as Tonya Harding's abusive mother LaVona Golden in I, Tonya (2017). She also has a distinguished stage career, earning Tony Award nominations for A View from the Bridge and 9 to 5.

Advocacy

Janney is active in charitable causes including addiction recovery, women's health, and animal welfare, and she received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame in 2016.
