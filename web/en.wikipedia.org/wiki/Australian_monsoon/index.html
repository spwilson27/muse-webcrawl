Australian monsoon

The Australian monsoon is the seasonal weather regime that brings the wet season to northern Australia, roughly from November to April. As the austral summer heats the continent, the intertropical convergence zone and the monsoon trough move south over the Timor and Arafura Seas, drawing moist north-westerly winds across the Top End, Kimberley, and Cape York Peninsula.

Seasonal cycle

The build-up ("the build-up") of heat and humidity in October–November precedes the monsoon onset, usually around late December at Darwin. The season alternates between active phases, with widespread heavy rain, thunderstorms, and tropical cyclones, and break phases of relatively dry, easterly weather lasting a week or more. Annual rainfall at Darwin averages about 1,700 mm, most of it falling in these months.

Drivers and variability

The strength and timing of the Australian monsoon are modulated by the El Niño–Southern Oscillation, the Indian Ocean Dipole, and the Madden–Julian Oscillation, whose eastward-propagating pulses organise bursts of monsoon activity. The monsoon interacts with the broader Australian–Asian monsoon system and feeds moisture into weather systems affecting the rest of the continent.

Impacts

Monsoon rains refill rivers, wetlands, and aquifers of the north, driving the ecology of Kakadu's floodplains and the northern pastoral industry, but also causing major flooding, road closures isolating remote communities, and cyclone landfalls. Indigenous seasonal calendars of the Top End recognise up to six seasons keyed to monsoon changes.
