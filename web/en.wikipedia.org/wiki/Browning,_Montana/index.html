Browning is a town in Glacier County, Montana, United States. It is the headquarters and principal community of the Blackfeet Indian Reservation, home to the Blackfeet Nation (the Blackfeet Tribe of the Blackfeet Indian Reservation). The population was around 1,000 at the 2020 census.

Geography and climate

Browning lies on the eastern edge of the Rocky Mountain Front, west of Cut Bank and southeast of Glacier National Park. The town experiences a continental climate with cold, windy winters; it is known for extreme temperature swings caused by chinook winds, and for recording a US national record temperature drop of 100 °F (56 °C) within 24 hours in January 1916.

Culture

The town hosts the annual North American Indian Days celebration each July, one of the largest gatherings of Native American tribes in the region. The Museum of the Plains Indian, operated by the Indian Arts and Crafts Board, is located in Browning.
