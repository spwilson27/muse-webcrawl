Central Alaskan Yup'ik (Yugtun) is a Yupik language of the Eskimo–Aleut family spoken in western and southwestern Alaska, from Norton Sound to Bristol Bay including the Yukon–Kuskokwim delta. It is the Alaska Native language with the most speakers and, unusually for an Indigenous language in the US, is still acquired by children in many communities.

Grammar

Yup'ik is polysynthetic and agglutinative: single words can express whole sentences through a root, derivational suffixes ("postbases"), and inflectional endings marking dual as well as singular and plural number, and ergative–absolutive case alignment.

Dialects and education

Major dialect groupings include General Central Yup'ik, Norton Sound, Hooper Bay–Chevak (Cup'ik), and Nunivak (Cup'ig). A standard orthography was developed at the Alaska Native Language Center in the 1960s–70s, and bilingual and immersion programmes operate in the Lower Kuskokwim school district, noted for strong language maintenance.
