Sprinkler irrigation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sprinkler irrigation is a method of applying irrigation water that mimics natural rainfall. Water is distributed through a system of pipes under pressure and sprayed into the air through sprinkler heads, breaking into droplets that fall on the crop and soil. Sprinkler systems range from small hand-moved pipes and lawn sprinklers to enormous self-propelled machines covering hundreds of hectares, and they are used in agriculture, landscaping, turf management, and dust and fire control.

Types of systems

  • Center pivot — a long pipeline supported on wheeled towers rotates slowly around a central water source, irrigating a circular field; the green circles it produces are a familiar sight from aircraft over arid regions such as the Great Plains and the Middle East.
  • Lateral move (linear) — similar spans move in straight lines across rectangular fields.
  • Hand-move and wheel-line (side roll) — portable aluminium pipes moved between sets by hand or by rolling.
  • Solid set — permanently installed pipes and risers, common in orchards and high-value crops.
  • Travelling gun — a large single sprinkler pulled across the field on a hose reel.
  • LEPA/LESA low-pressure systems — drop tubes and bubblers that apply water close to the ground to reduce evaporation and wind drift.

Advantages and limitations

Sprinklers adapt to uneven terrain where surface irrigation would require levelling, deliver water at controllable rates suited to soil infiltration, and allow fertiliser and pesticide application through the system (fertigation and chemigation). Disadvantages include high energy costs for pressurisation, water losses to evaporation and wind, uneven distribution in windy conditions, and the risk of spreading some foliar diseases. Efficiency typically falls between flood irrigation and drip irrigation, though modern low-pressure pivot packages approach drip performance.

History

Mechanised sprinkler systems spread after the Second World War with cheap aluminium pipe and centrifugal pumps. Frank Zybach patented the center-pivot system in 1952, transforming irrigation on the High Plains, where pivots drawing on the Ogallala Aquifer turned semi-arid land into some of the world's most productive farmland.
