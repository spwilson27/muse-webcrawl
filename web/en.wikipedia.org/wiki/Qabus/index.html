Qabus

Qabus ibn Wushmgir (full name: Abol-Hasan Qabus ibn Wushmgir ibn Ziyar, Persian: ابوالحسن قابوس بن وشمگیر بن زیار; died 1012), also known by his laqab Shams al-Ma'ali ("Sun of the Heights"), was the Ziyarid ruler of Gorgan and Tabaristan in northern Persia, reigning in two periods (977–981 and 998–1012).

Life and reign

Qabus succeeded his brother Bisutun in 977 but was driven from Gorgan by the Buyid ruler Adud al-Dawla in 981 and spent years in exile at the Samanid court in Nishapur. Restored in 998, he became known both for his tyrannical severity and for his patronage of learning: the astronomer and polymath al-Biruni dedicated his Chronology of Ancient Nations to him, and Avicenna visited his court. In 1012 Qabus was deposed and imprisoned by his own troops and soon afterwards murdered.

Legacy

He is best remembered for the Gonbad-e Qabus, the monumental brick tower tomb built for him near modern Gonbad-e Kavus in 1006–07, one of the earliest dated Islamic monuments in Iran and a UNESCO World Heritage Site. His grandson Kaykavus authored the famous Persian mirror for princes, the Qabus-nama.
