Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem

Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem
FieldLinear algebra, combinatorics
StatementEvery doubly stochastic matrix is a convex combination of permutation matrices
Named afterGarrett Birkhoff, John von Neumann

The Birkhoff–von Neumann theorem states that the set of doubly stochastic matrices — square matrices of non-negative real numbers whose rows and columns each sum to one — forms a convex polytope (the Birkhoff polytope) whose extreme points are exactly the permutation matrices. Equivalently, every doubly stochastic matrix can be written as a convex combination of permutation matrices.

Proof and history

The result was established in the 1940s through work by Garrett Birkhoff and John von Neumann, building on earlier results by Dénes Kőnig on regular bipartite graphs. A standard proof proceeds by induction: Hall's marriage theorem guarantees that a doubly stochastic matrix has a positive diagonal, allowing one permutation matrix to be subtracted off at each step.

Applications

The theorem is fundamental in combinatorial optimization and the assignment problem, shows that the Birkhoff polytope has dimension (n − 1)², and underlies results in probability, majorization theory, and the design of switching networks, where it justifies decomposing traffic matrices into permutation schedules.
