Léopold Sédar Senghor

Léopold Sédar Senghor
Born: 9 October 1906, Joal, Senegal
Died: 20 December 2001, Verson, France
President of Senegal: 1960–1980

Léopold Sédar Senghor (1906–2001) was a Senegalese poet, politician and cultural theorist, the first president of Senegal (1960–1980) and a co-founder of the Négritude movement.

Life and thought

Educated in Senegal and Paris, Senghor became the first African agrégé in French grammar teaching. With Aimé Césaire and Léon Damas he developed Négritude, affirming Black African culture and values against colonial assimilation. Elected to the French National Assembly after World War II, he led Senegal to independence in 1960.

Presidency and poetry

As president he pursued African socialism, political stability and cultural renaissance, hosting the 1966 World Festival of Black Arts in Dakar. He voluntarily retired in 1980, the first African head of state of his generation to do so, and in 1983 was elected to the Académie française. His poetry collections include Chants d'ombre and Hosties noires. See Senegal.
