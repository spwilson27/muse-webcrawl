Rakesh Sharma

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Rakesh Sharma (born 13 January 1949) is a retired Indian Air Force pilot and the only Indian citizen to have travelled to space (as of the early 2020s). He flew aboard the Soviet spacecraft Soyuz T-11 on 3 April 1984 as part of the Interkosmos programme, spending nearly eight days aboard the Salyut 7 space station.

Early life and career

Sharma was born in Patiala, Punjab, and educated at St. George's Grammar School, Hyderabad and Nizam College. He joined the Indian Air Force in 1970 and flew combat missions during the Indo-Pakistani War of 1971. He later became a test pilot and rose to the rank of Wing Commander.

Spaceflight

Sharma was selected from hundreds of applicants for the joint Indo-Soviet manned space mission. Launched from the Baikonur Cosmodrome with commander Yury Malyshev and flight engineer Gennady Strekalov, he conducted scientific experiments in space, including studies in biomedicine and remote sensing, and performed yoga exercises to study their effects in weightlessness.

During a televised conversation, Prime Minister Indira Gandhi asked him how India looked from space. Sharma replied in Hindi with the famous line "Saare Jahan Se Achha" ("the best of all the world"), quoting the poet Muhammad Iqbal.

Honours

Sharma was awarded the Ashoka Chakra, India's highest peacetime gallantry award, and was made a Hero of the Soviet Union. After retiring from the Air Force he worked as a test pilot for Hindustan Aeronautics Limited.
