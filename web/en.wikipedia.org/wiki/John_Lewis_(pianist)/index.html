John Aaron Lewis (May 3, 1920 – March 29, 2001) was an American jazz pianist, composer and arranger, best known as the founder and musical director of the Modern Jazz Quartet (MJQ).

Career

Lewis studied music at the University of New Mexico and the Manhattan School of Music. He played with Dizzy Gillespie's big band and with Miles Davis, contributing to the Birth of the Cool sessions. In 1952 he co-founded the Modern Jazz Quartet with Milt Jackson, Percy Heath, and Kenny Clarke; the group became famous for blending jazz improvisation with classical forms such as the fugue, a style associated with the "third stream" movement.

Compositions

His best-known compositions include "Django", written in memory of guitarist Django Reinhardt, and "Afternoon in Paris". Lewis also composed film scores, including for Odds Against Tomorrow (1959).
