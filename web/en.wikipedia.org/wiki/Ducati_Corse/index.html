Ducati Corse

Ducati Corse S.r.l.
Type: Racing division of Ducati
Founded: 1999 (as a separate company)
Headquarters: Borgo Panigale, Bologna, Italy

Ducati Corse is the racing team division of the Italian motorcycle manufacturer Ducati. It is responsible for the company's factory racing activities in MotoGP and the Superbike World Championship, and for the development and customer support of Ducati's racing motorcycles.

History

Ducati's competition department was formally spun off as Ducati Corse S.r.l. in 1999. The division built on decades of racing heritage, from the single-cylinder racers of the 1950s to the V-twin superbikes that dominated the Superbike World Championship in the 1990s with riders such as Carl Fogarty and Troy Bayliss.

MotoGP

Ducati returned to Grand Prix racing in 2003 with the Desmosedici, a V4 machine named for its desmodromic valve actuation. Casey Stoner won the 2007 MotoGP world championship on the Desmosedici GP7. After a long title drought, Francesco Bagnaia won consecutive championships in 2022 and 2023, and Ducati's satellite-supported riders and teams made the Desmosedici the dominant bike on the grid, sweeping manufacturers' titles.

Superbike World Championship

Ducati is the most successful manufacturer in World Superbike history, with well over a dozen riders' championships. Its Panigale V4 R carried Álvaro Bautista to consecutive titles in 2022 and 2023.

Organisation

The general manager of Ducati Corse since 2014 has been Luigi "Gigi" Dall'Igna, widely credited with the Desmosedici's aerodynamic and technical resurgence.
