The foot (plural feet) is an anatomical structure found in many vertebrates. It is the terminal portion of a limb which bears weight and allows locomotion. In humans, the foot is a complex mechanical structure containing 26 bones, 33 joints and more than a hundred muscles, tendons and ligaments, evolved for upright bipedal walking and running.

Anatomy

The human foot is divided into the hindfoot (talus and calcaneus, the heel bone), the midfoot (navicular, cuboid and three cuneiform bones) and the forefoot (five metatarsals and fourteen phalanges forming the toes). The bones form two arches — longitudinal and transverse — held by the plantar fascia and ligaments, which store and return elastic energy with each step and distribute body weight.

Function and conditions

Feet provide balance, propulsion and shock absorption. Common conditions include plantar fasciitis, bunions (hallux valgus), flat feet, athlete's foot and fractures; diabetes can impair circulation and sensation in the feet, requiring careful monitoring. The medical specialty devoted to the foot is podiatry.

Culture

Feet feature prominently in custom and ritual, from foot washing ceremonies to Chinese foot binding, practised for roughly a thousand years into the early 20th century.
