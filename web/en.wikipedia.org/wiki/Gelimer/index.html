Gelimer (c. 480 – 553) was the last king of the Vandals and Alans in North Africa, reigning from 530 to 534. His deposition of his cousin Hilderic gave the Byzantine emperor Justinian I a pretext for the Vandalic War, which destroyed the Vandal kingdom less than a century after its foundation.

Reign and war

Gelimer, a great-grandson of the Vandal king Genseric, overthrew Hilderic in 530 because of Hilderic's pro-Byzantine policy and tolerance of Nicene Christianity, imprisoning him. Justinian demanded Hilderic's restoration and then, in 533, sent the general Belisarius with an expeditionary force to Africa. The Byzantines won the battles of Ad Decimum (September 533) and Tricamarum (December 533) and took Carthage.

Capture and later life

Gelimer fled to Mount Papua in Numidia and surrendered in the spring of 534 on condition his life be spared. He was paraded in Justinian's triumph in Constantinople — reportedly murmuring "Vanity of vanities, all is vanity" as he walked — and was then granted estates in Galatia, where he lived out his life, though as an Arian he could not receive patrician rank without converting.
