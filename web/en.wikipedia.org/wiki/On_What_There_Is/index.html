On What There Is

"On What There Is" is a 1948 paper by the American philosopher Willard Van Orman Quine, published in the Review of Metaphysics and later reprinted in his collection From a Logical Point of View (1953). One of the most influential works of 20th-century analytic philosophy, it sets out Quine's criterion of ontological commitment and his objections to positing universals and merely possible entities.

Content

Quine opens with the question "What is there?" and the short answer "Everything." Surveying rival ontologies — illustrated by the fictional disputants McX, who accepts universals, and Wyman, who populates his world with unactualized possibles such as a "possible fat man in that doorway" — Quine argues that a theory is committed to exactly those entities that the bound variables of its canonical regimentation must range over: "to be is to be the value of a bound variable." Descriptions of non-existent objects can be eliminated using Bertrand Russell's theory of descriptions, avoiding commitment to a bloated universe of entities like Pegasus, which Quine calls "Plato's beard."

Influence

The paper revived metaphysics within analytic philosophy by giving ontology a rigorous, language-relative method, and it frames Quine's own preference for a "desert landscape" of physical objects — tempered by acceptance of sets for the needs of mathematics.
