Ciliary muscle

The ciliary muscle is an intrinsic smooth muscle of the eye, forming a ring within the ciliary body. It controls accommodation — the changing of the lens shape for focusing at different distances — and regulates the flow of aqueous humour into Schlemm's canal.

Ciliary muscle
LocationCiliary body of the eye
NerveOculomotor nerve (CN III), parasympathetic via ciliary ganglion
ActionAccommodation; opens trabecular meshwork

Structure

The muscle consists of smooth muscle fibres in three orientations: longitudinal (Brücke's muscle), radial, and circular (Müller's muscle). It differs in architecture between species: in mammals it is smooth muscle, whereas birds and reptiles possess striated ciliary muscle allowing faster accommodation.

Function

When the ciliary muscle contracts under parasympathetic control, the ciliary ring moves forward and inward, releasing tension on the zonular fibres (of Zinn) that suspend the lens. The elastic lens then rounds up, increasing its refractive power for near vision. Drugs that paralyse the muscle (cycloplegics, such as atropine and cyclopentolate) are used to examine the eye, while pilocarpine, which contracts it, increases aqueous outflow and is used in glaucoma treatment.
