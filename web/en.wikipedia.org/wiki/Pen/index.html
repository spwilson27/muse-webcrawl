Pen

TypeWriting instrument
MediumInk on paper
Main typesFountain pen, ballpoint, rollerball, dip pen, quill

A pen is a writing instrument that applies ink to a surface, usually paper, for writing or drawing. Pens have been used for over five thousand years, evolving from reed pens and quills to modern mass-produced instruments.

History

Ancient Egyptians used reed pens with carbon-based inks. Quill pens, cut from large bird feathers, dominated European writing from the Middle Ages until the nineteenth century, when mass-produced steel dip-pen nibs replaced them. The fountain pen, with an internal ink reservoir, became practical in the 1880s. The ballpoint pen, patented by László Bíró in 1938 and mass-produced after the Second World War, made pens cheap and disposable; the rollerball and gel pen followed in the twentieth century.

Types

Major types include fountain pens (nib, capillary feed), ballpoints (thick oil-based ink delivered by a rolling ball), rollerballs and gel pens (water-based inks), felt-tip and brush pens, and technical pens. Despite the rise of digital devices, pens remain ubiquitous in education, business and art.
