Thomas Weelkes

Thomas Weelkes (1576 – 30 November 1623) was an English composer and organist. He became the organist of Winchester College in 1598, moving to Chichester Cathedral. His works are chiefly vocal, and include madrigals, anthems and services. Weelkes is considered one of the finest of the English madrigalists, known for his bold chromatic harmony and vivid word-painting.

Life

Weelkes was baptised at Elsted, near Chichester in Sussex, on 25 October 1576. Little is known of his early life, though he may have been a chorister. His first book of madrigals was published in 1597, when he was about twenty-one. In 1598 he was appointed organist and informator choristarum (master of the choristers) at Winchester College, and in 1599–1600 he published two further books of madrigals and contributed "As Vesta was from Latmos hill descending" to The Triumphs of Oriana (1601) — one of the most admired pieces in that collection.

Around 1602 Weelkes moved to Chichester as organist and choirmaster of the cathedral. Records of the Chichester chapter show repeated complaints about his behaviour — absenteeism, drunkenness and swearing — and he was dismissed in 1617, though later reinstated. He spent his last years partly in London, where he died on 30 November 1623.

Music

Weelkes published four sets of secular vocal music: balletts and madrigals for five voices (1598), madrigals for three to six voices (1599), madrigals for five and six voices (1600), and ayres or phantasticke spirits for three voices (1608). His madrigals are notable for daring chromaticism — as in "O Care, thou wilt despatch me" — energetic rhythms and expressive depiction of the text. His church music includes anthems such as "When David heard", a moving lament, "Hosanna to the Son of David", and several services, among them the substantial Nunc Dimittis for seven voices.

Reputation

Along with Thomas Morley, John Wilbye and Thomas Tomkins, Weelkes stands at the head of the English Madrigal School, and his anthems remain staples of the cathedral choral repertoire.
