Camogie

A camogie match in progress.

Camogie (Irish: camógaíocht) is an Irish team sport played by women, organised by the Camogie Association. It is the female counterpart of hurling, sharing the same pitch, goals and most rules: teams of fifteen players use a wooden stick called a camán (a shorter version of the hurler's hurley) to drive a leather ball, the sliotar, into the opponents' goal or over the crossbar for points.

History

The game was formalised in the early twentieth century; the Camogie Association was founded in 1904 and the first All-Ireland Senior Camogie Championship was held in 1932. Dublin dominated the early championships, winning 26 titles, while Cork, Kilkenny and Tipperary have been the leading counties in recent decades. The championship final is played at Croke Park, and the sport's premier players receive All Star awards.

Rules and differences from hurling

Matches last 60 minutes. A goal (under the crossbar) is worth three points; a point (over the bar) is worth one. Unlike hurling, camogie players may hand-pass a goal directly, drop the stick to catch the ball, and wear skirts or skorts historically mandated by the rules. Physical contact is somewhat more restricted. Protective helmets, optional for much of the sport's history, are now mandatory.

Competitions

The principal competitions are the All-Ireland Senior, Intermediate and Junior championships, the National League, and the inter-varsity Ashbourne Cup, first played in 1915 and among the oldest trophies in women's sport. The game is also played in Britain, North America and Australia under the umbrella of the Gaelic Athletic Association's world games structure, although camogie retains its own association in Ireland.
