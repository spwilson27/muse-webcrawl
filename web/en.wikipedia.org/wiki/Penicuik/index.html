Penicuik (Scots: Penicuik; Scottish Gaelic: Peighinn na Cuthaige, "hill of the cuckoo") is a town and former burgh in Midlothian, Scotland, lying on the west bank of the River North Esk, about 9 miles (14 km) south of Edinburgh. It had a population of around 16,000 at the 2011 census.

History

The settlement's name derives from Brythonic pen y cog, "hill of the cuckoo." Papermaking became the town's defining industry: the first mill on the Esk was established at Penicuik in 1708, and by the nineteenth century several mills operated along the river, employing much of the town and making it one of Scotland's leading paper centres. The last major mills closed in the early 2000s.

During the Napoleonic Wars, French prisoners of war were held at the nearby Valleyfield Camp, and a monument in the town commemorates those who died in captivity. Glencorse Barracks, just north of the town, has long been associated with the Royal Scots and is now home to the Royal Highland Fusiliers battalion of the Royal Regiment of Scotland.

Penicuik is also notable for giving its name to an early experiment in data and weather science: the Penicuik Experiments of Sir John Clerk's era are less famous than the town's association with the Clerk family of Penicuik House, a centre of the Scottish Enlightenment.

The town today

Penicuik functions largely as a commuter town for Edinburgh. It is home to the Edinburgh Technopole science park and lies close to the Roslin Institute and the Royal (Dick) School of Veterinary Studies at Easter Bush. Local schools include Penicuik High School and Beeslack Community High School.
