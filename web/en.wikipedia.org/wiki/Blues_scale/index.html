Blues scale

The hexatonic blues scale on C

The term blues scale refers to several different scales with differing numbers of pitches and related characteristics, all used in blues, jazz, and rock music.

Hexatonic blues scale

The most common form is the hexatonic (six-note) blues scale, consisting of the minor pentatonic scale plus a chromatic passing note — the flattened fifth (♭5), also called the "blue note". In C, the scale is C, E♭, F, G♭, G, B♭. The added note creates the characteristic tension and colour of blues melody against major-key accompaniment.

Other forms

A nine-note variant adds the major third and major seventh to produce the scale sometimes described as the dominant blues scale. Other writers use "blues scale" for the minor pentatonic scale itself, or for the Dorian mode with added chromatic notes. The concept is tied to the practice of blue notes — pitches bent or inflected downward, derived from African-American vocal and instrumental traditions, lying between the minor and major forms of scale degrees 3, 5, and 7.

Use

The blues scale is the basic vocabulary of improvisation in blues and much of rock and jazz. A single scale (typically based on the tonic) is commonly played over all three chords of the twelve-bar blues progression, its blue notes rubbing against the dominant seventh chords to produce the genre's signature sound.
