Takashi Murakami (村上 隆, born 1 February 1962) is a Japanese contemporary artist. He works in fine arts — painting and sculpture — as well as commercial media, fashion and animation, and is known for blurring the line between high and low art. He founded the Superflat movement, a postmodern style characterised by flat planes of colour and graphic imagery drawn from anime and manga, and he has been called "the Warhol of Japan".

Early life and education

Murakami was born in Itabashi, Tokyo. He studied nihonga (traditional Japanese painting) at Tokyo University of the Arts, earning a PhD in 1993. Increasingly drawn to contemporary art and otaku culture, in the mid-1990s he developed his alter-ego character Mr. DOB and began exhibiting internationally.

Superflat and major work

In 2000 Murakami formulated the Superflat theory, linking the flatness of classical Japanese art to postwar Japanese consumer culture and the flattening of distinctions between art and commerce. His Hiropon Factory, later the company Kaikai Kiki, employs dozens of assistants in a studio system modelled on both traditional workshops and Warhol's Factory. Famous works include the sculpture My Lonesome Cowboy (1998), which sold at auction in 2008 for over $15 million, and his multicoloured smiling flowers and "jellyfish eyes" motifs.

Commercial collaborations

Murakami collaborated with Louis Vuitton from 2002, redesigning its monogram in rainbow colours, and with musicians including Kanye West (the cover of Graduation, 2007), Pharrell Williams, and Billie Eilish. Major retrospectives include ©Murakami (2007–2009) and The Octopus Eats Its Own Leg (2017). In 2020 he announced financial difficulties for Kaikai Kiki during the COVID-19 pandemic, and in 2022 he launched NFT projects based on his flower motif.
