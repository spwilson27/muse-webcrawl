Lateral malleolus

The lateral malleolus is the bony prominence on the outer side of the ankle. It is formed by the lower end (distal extremity) of the fibula. Its counterpart on the inner side of the ankle is the medial malleolus, formed by the tibia. The word malleolus comes from Latin, meaning "small hammer".

Anatomy

The lateral malleolus extends about a centimetre lower than the medial malleolus and sits slightly behind it, helping to grip the talus and stabilise the ankle mortise. Its inner surface bears an articular facet for the talus; behind it runs a groove for the tendons of the fibularis (peroneus) longus and brevis muscles. Several ligaments of the ankle — including the anterior talofibular, calcaneofibular, and posterior talofibular ligaments — attach around it.

Clinical significance

Fractures of the lateral malleolus are among the most common ankle injuries, usually caused by twisting the foot. They are classified by the Weber (Danis–Weber) system according to their level relative to the syndesmosis. Stable isolated fractures may be treated with a brace or walking boot, while displaced or unstable injuries, especially with medial injury, often require surgical fixation with a plate and screws.
