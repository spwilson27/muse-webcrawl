The Battle of Fort Washington was fought on 16 November 1776 during the American Revolutionary War. A combined British and Hessian force under General William Howe assaulted and captured Fort Washington, the last American stronghold on Manhattan Island, taking its garrison of nearly 3,000 men prisoner. It was one of the worst American defeats of the war.

Background

Fort Washington stood on high ground at the northern end of Manhattan, paired with Fort Lee across the Hudson to obstruct British navigation. After the American retreat from New York, General George Washington doubted the fort could be held but deferred to General Nathanael Greene and the garrison commander, Colonel Robert Magaw, who believed it could resist.

Battle

Howe attacked from several directions on 16 November. Hessian troops under Knyphausen stormed the northern approaches after hard fighting up the steep heights; other columns pressed from the east and south. The American outer defences were driven in, and the garrison, crowded into the small fort, surrendered that afternoon. Among the attackers' intelligence advantages was information supplied by the American deserter William Demont, the fort's adjutant.

Aftermath

Some 2,800 officers and men were captured; many died in British prison ships and prisons. Fort Lee was evacuated days later, and the Continental Army began its retreat across New Jersey. The victory gave Britain complete control of New York City, which it held until 1783.
