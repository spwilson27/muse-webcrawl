Johann Friedrich Pfaff

Johann Friedrich Pfaff (22 December 1765 – 21 April 1825) was a German mathematician, regarded as one of the most prominent German mathematicians of the late 18th and early 19th centuries. He is known for his work on partial differential equations and for the theory of Pfaffian forms.

Career

Pfaff studied at the University of Göttingen under Abraham Gotthelf Kästner, and became professor of mathematics at Helmstedt and later at Halle. Among his students was Carl Friedrich Gauss, whose doctoral thesis Pfaff supervised. Pfaff's 1815 treatise on partial differential equations, Methodus generalis, aequationes differentiarum partialium, introduced what are now called Pfaffian systems.

Pfaffian forms

A Pfaffian form is a differential 1-form, and the theory of Pfaffian equations concerns the integration of such forms. Pfaff's work laid groundwork later developed by Élie Cartan into the theory of exterior differential systems.
