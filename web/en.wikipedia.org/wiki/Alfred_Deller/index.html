Alfred George Deller (31 May 1912 – 16 July 1979) was an English singer and one of the main figures in popularising the countertenor voice in the 20th century, reviving repertoire written for the voice in the English cathedral tradition and in Baroque opera.

Career

A self-taught singer, Deller was discovered by composer Michael Tippett, who championed him and arranged his London debut in 1946. He sang in the choirs of Canterbury and St Paul's Cathedrals, made pioneering recordings of Purcell and English lute songs, and in 1948 founded the Deller Consort, one of the earliest ensembles devoted to historically informed performance of Renaissance and Baroque vocal music. Benjamin Britten wrote the role of Oberon in A Midsummer Night's Dream (1960) for him.

Legacy

Deller founded the Stour Music Festival in Kent in 1962. He was appointed CBE in 1970. His son Mark Deller continued the Consort after his death. Deller's distinctive, delicate tone and musicianship brought the countertenor voice to a wide international audience and inspired a generation of singers.
