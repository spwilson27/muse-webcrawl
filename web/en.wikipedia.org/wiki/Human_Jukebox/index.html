The Human Jukebox is the marching band of Southern University and A&M College in Baton Rouge, Louisiana. Officially the Southern University Marching Band, it is one of the most celebrated historically Black college and university (HBCU) marching bands in the United States, renowned for its high-energy performances, precise dancing, and powerful brass sound.

History

The band was established in 1947 under director T. Leroy Davis. It grew in size and reputation over the following decades, becoming a central institution of Southern University football culture. Under long-serving directors including Isaac Greggs, who led the band from 1969 to 2005, the Human Jukebox refined the show-style marching tradition — blending traditional military precision with popular music, elaborate dance routines, and theatrical field shows.

Performances and recognition

The Human Jukebox has performed at Super Bowls, presidential inaugurations, Mardi Gras parades, and numerous nationally televised events. It is famous for its halftime "dancing" horn sections and for its performances in the annual Bayou Classic against Grambling State University's Tiger Marching Band, one of the most storied rivalries in HBCU football. The band has also performed with and for prominent recording artists and has been featured in documentaries and media celebrations of HBCU band culture.

Style

The band's style emphasizes:

  • High-step marching with dramatic knee lifts and horn swings.
  • Arrangements of contemporary R&B, hip hop, and funk alongside traditional repertoire.
  • The "dancing dolls" — the Fabulous Dancing Dolls dance team that accompanies the band.
  • Large, disciplined ranks typically numbering around 200 members.

The Human Jukebox is frequently ranked among the top HBCU marching bands in national polls, and its alumni include professional musicians and band directors across the United States.
