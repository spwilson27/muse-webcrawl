Antimony

SymbolSb
Atomic number51
Atomic mass121.760
Group15 (pnictogens)
Melting point630.63 °C

Antimony is a chemical element; it has symbol Sb (from Latin stibium) and atomic number 51. A lustrous grey metalloid, it is found in nature mainly as the sulfide mineral stibnite (Sb2S3). Antimony compounds have been known since ancient times and were powdered for use as medicine and cosmetics, most famously as kohl.

Properties

Antimony is a brittle, silvery-white metalloid with poor electrical and thermal conductivity. It has several allotropes, of which the stable form is a metallic grey solid with a layered structure. It is relatively resistant to attack by acids. China has long been the largest producer of antimony and its compounds.

Applications

The largest applications of antimony are in flame retardants (as antimony trioxide, used synergistically with halogenated materials) and in lead alloys, where small additions harden lead used in batteries, cable sheathing and ammunition. Antimony compounds are also used in semiconductors (such as indium antimonide), in glass and ceramics, and in medicine against leishmaniasis. Antimony and many of its compounds are toxic, and occupational exposure is regulated.
