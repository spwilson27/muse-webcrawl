Texas City, Texas

County: Galveston

State: Texas

Population (2020): about 51,900

Texas City is a city in Galveston County in the U.S. state of Texas, located on the southwest shoreline of Galveston Bay. It is a busy deepwater port on the Texas Gulf Coast and a major petrochemical centre, home to some of the largest oil refineries and chemical plants in the United States. Its population was about 51,900 at the 2020 census.

History

The site was promoted in the 1890s by investors from Minnesota who dredged a channel and developed the port. The city grew with the petroleum industry in the early twentieth century.

Texas City is notorious for the Texas City disaster of 16 April 1947, the deadliest industrial accident in U.S. history: a fire aboard the French ship Grandcamp, loaded with ammonium nitrate fertiliser, caused a massive explosion that killed at least 581 people, injured thousands, and devastated the port and waterfront areas. A second ship exploded the following day.

The city was also affected by the 2005 explosion at the BP Texas City refinery, which killed 15 workers and injured 180, leading to major regulatory penalties and safety reforms. The Texas City Dike, a man-made breakwater extending into Galveston Bay, is popular for fishing and recreation.

Economy

The Port of Texas City is among the busiest in the United States by tonnage. Refining and petrochemical production by companies such as Marathon Petroleum (Galveston Bay Refinery) and Valero dominate the local economy. The city is part of the Houston metropolitan area's industrial corridor along the bay.
