Milnor K-theory

Milnor K-theory organizes symbols from the units of a field into a graded ring.

In mathematics, Milnor K-theory is a comparatively elementary approach to algebraic K-theory, introduced by John Milnor, that defines a graded ring K*M(F) associated to a field F. It is built directly from the multiplicative group of the field rather than from projective modules or vector spaces.

Definition

The Milnor K-theory ring of a field F is the quotient of the tensor algebra of the abelian group F× (written additively in degrees) by the ideal generated by elements of the form a ⊗ (1 − a) for a ≠ 0, 1 — the Steinberg relation. Thus K0M(F) = ℤ, K1M(F) = F×, and K2M(F) is generated by symbols {a, b} subject to bilinearity and the Steinberg relation {a, 1 − a} = 0, matching Matsumoto's presentation of the second algebraic K-group of a field.

Applications

Milnor K-theory is linked to Galois cohomology through the norm residue isomorphism, conjectured by Milnor for degree 2 and in general by Bloch and Kato, and proved by Voevodsky and others — a landmark of modern algebraic geometry that underpins motivic cohomology. For number fields and local fields, the groups KnM connect with class field theory, quadratic forms, and symbols such as the Hilbert symbol. In higher degrees, Milnor K-theory generally differs from Quillen's K-theory, coinciding in low degrees for fields.
