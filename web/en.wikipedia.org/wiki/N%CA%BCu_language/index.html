Nǁu (also spelled Nǀu, Nǁŋǃke, or called ǂKhomani) is a critically endangered Tuu language of the Kalahari region of South Africa. It belongs to the ǃKwi branch of the Tuu family, the languages historically grouped under the label "Southern Khoisan". Nǁu is famous among linguists for its extremely large phoneme inventory, including an extensive set of click consonants.

History and revitalisation

The language was thought to be extinct until the 1990s, when linguistic research located a small number of elderly fluent speakers among the ǂKhomani San community near Upington. Nǁu has subsequently been the subject of documentation and community language-revitalisation efforts, including classes for children led by elder speakers such as Katrina Esau, who opened a school to teach the language and published a children's book in Nǁu. By the 2020s only a handful of first-language speakers remained, most famously Esau, recognised as the last fluent mother-tongue speaker after the deaths of other elders.
