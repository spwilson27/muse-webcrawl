The Franco-Prussian War, or Franco-German War, often referred to in France as the War of 1870, was a conflict between the Second French Empire and the North German Confederation led by the Kingdom of Prussia. Lasting from 19 July 1870 to 28 January 1871, the conflict was caused primarily by France's determination to restore its dominant position in continental Europe, which it had lost following Prussia's victory over Austria in 1866, and Prussia's desire to complete German unification under Otto von Bismarck.

Causes

The immediate trigger was the candidacy of a Hohenzollern prince for the Spanish throne and Bismarck's provocative editing of the Ems Dispatch, which inflamed French opinion; France declared war on 19 July 1870, widely seen in Germany as the aggressor — bringing the south German states into alliance with Prussia.

Course

Superior German mobilisation by rail, staff planning under Helmuth von Moltke, and Krupp steel artillery overwhelmed the French. After frontier defeats, the army of Marshal MacMahon was encircled and captured with Napoleon III at the Battle of Sedan (1 September 1870); another army surrendered at Metz. Paris was besieged for four months; the new French Republic fought on with improvised armies until an armistice in January 1871.

Consequences

On 18 January 1871 the German Empire was proclaimed in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles. The Treaty of Frankfurt (May 1871) ceded Alsace and northern Lorraine to Germany and imposed a five-billion-franc indemnity. In Paris, the revolutionary Paris Commune rose and was bloodily suppressed. The loss of Alsace-Lorraine nurtured French revanchism that contributed to the tensions leading to the First World War.
