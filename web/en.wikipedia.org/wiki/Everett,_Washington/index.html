Everett, Washington

County: Snohomish County

Population: approx. 110,000 (2020 census)

Incorporated: 4 May 1893

Named for: Everett Colby

Everett is the county seat and largest city of Snohomish County, Washington, United States. It lies about 25 miles north of Seattle on the Puget Sound, at the mouth of the Snohomish River on Port Gardner Bay. Founded in 1890 and named for the son of investor Charles L. Colby, Everett grew as a mill town processing timber from the Cascade foothills; for a time it promoted itself as the "City of Smokestacks" for its lumber mills, smelters and paper plants.

Economy

Modern Everett is best known as the home of Boeing's wide-body aircraft assembly plant, built in 1967 for the 747 programme and among the largest buildings in the world by volume, where the 747 (until 2023), 767, 777 and 787 have been assembled. The city also hosts Naval Station Everett, a US Navy homeport for aircraft carriers and destroyers, opened in 1994. Other employers include Providence Regional Medical Center, Funko, which has its headquarters downtown, and Everett Community College. Paine Field (Snohomish County Airport) began commercial passenger service in 2019.

History and culture

The site was inhabited by the Snohomish people, whose principal village, Hibulb, stood near the river mouth; the 1855 Treaty of Point Elliott was signed nearby. Everett's early history was turbulent, including the 1916 Everett massacre, a violent clash between labour organisers of the Industrial Workers of the World and local authorities that killed at least seven people. Today the city has a historic downtown, the Schack Art Center, Angel of the Winds Arena, home of the Everett Silvertips ice hockey team, and Funko Field, home of the Everett AquaSox baseball team.
