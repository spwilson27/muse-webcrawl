11 points in the Negev

The 11 points in the Negev (Hebrew: 11 הנקודות) were eleven Jewish settlements established in a single night — 5–6 October 1946, at the close of Yom Kippur — in the northern Negev desert of Mandatory Palestine. Organised by the Jewish Agency, the operation was intended to establish a Jewish presence in the sparsely populated Negev and thereby influence the eventual partition of Palestine in favour of including the region in a future Jewish state.

Background

British restrictions on Jewish land purchase and settlement under the 1939 White Paper and the 1940 Land Transfer Regulations largely excluded the Negev. In 1946 the Morrison–Grady plan proposed placing the Negev outside the projected Jewish province, spurring the Jewish Agency, under David Ben-Gurion's strategic advocacy of Negev settlement, to act before the political map was fixed. Three earlier experimental stations (the "three lookouts" of 1943) had tested the feasibility of farming the region.

The operation

In one coordinated night, convoys carried prefabricated huts, fencing, water tanks and settlers to eleven sites, so that by dawn each point had the rudiments of a settlement — a fait accompli difficult to reverse. The eleven were: Be'eri, Gal On, Hatzerim, Kedma, Kfar Darom, Mishmar HaNegev, Nevatim, Nirim, Shoval, Tkuma and Urim. Water was initially trucked in; a pipeline from the north followed.

Legacy

The settlements helped ensure that the 1947 UN Partition Plan assigned most of the Negev to the Jewish state. During the 1948 Arab–Israeli War several of the points — notably Kfar Darom and Nirim — became front-line positions; Kfar Darom was evacuated after siege, while others held. Most of the eleven survive today as kibbutzim and moshavim in southern Israel.
