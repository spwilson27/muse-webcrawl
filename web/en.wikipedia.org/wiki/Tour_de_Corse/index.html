The Tour de Corse is a rally first held in 1956 on the island of Corsica, France. For many years it was the French round of the World Rally Championship (WRC), held from the championship's inception in 1973 until 2008, and again from 2015 to 2019. Run entirely on narrow, twisting mountain tarmac roads, it is famously nicknamed the "Rally of 10,000 Corners".

History

The event was founded in 1956 by Count Peraldi and Dr Jean de la Chappelle as a marathon tour of the island. It evolved into a competitive special-stage rally and became one of the WRC's most demanding fixtures, with stages winding along cliff edges with sheer drops. Notable winners include Bernard Darniche, who won six times, and Didier Auriol, also a six-time winner.

The rally is also remembered for tragedy: Attilio Bettega was killed in 1985, and in 1986 Henri Toivonen and his co-driver Sergio Cresto died when their Lancia Delta S4 left the road — an accident that precipitated the end of the Group B era at the close of that season.

After 2008 the Tour de Corse lost its WRC status to the Rallye de France Alsace; it ran as a round of the Intercontinental Rally Challenge and then the European Rally Championship. The WRC returned to Corsica in 2015, basing the event around Ajaccio, Bastia and Porto-Vecchio, until it again left the calendar after 2019.

Characteristics

Unlike most tarmac rallies, the Tour de Corse traditionally featured very long stages, few straight sections, and constant elevation change. Tyre wear and brake cooling are major challenges, and the weather in Corsica's mountains can change rapidly between stages.
