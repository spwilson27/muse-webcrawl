Aichi Prefecture

Aichi Prefecture (愛知県, Aichi-ken) is a prefecture of Japan located in the Chūbu region of Honshu. Its capital and largest city is Nagoya. With a population of roughly 7.5 million, Aichi is the fourth most populous prefecture in Japan and the centre of the country's automotive and aerospace industries.

Geography

Aichi faces Ise Bay and Mikawa Bay on the Pacific coast. The flat, fertile Nōbi Plain in the west gives way to hills and the Chita and Atsumi peninsulas in the south. The prefecture was formed in 1872 from the former provinces of Owari and Mikawa; its name comes from the district of Aichi mentioned in the Man'yōshū poetry anthology.

Economy

Aichi consistently records the highest value of manufactured goods shipments in Japan, anchored by Toyota Motor Corporation, headquartered in the city of Toyota, and its supplier network. The prefecture also hosts Mitsubishi Heavy Industries aerospace plants, the ceramics tradition of Seto and Tokoname, and Chubu Centrair International Airport, built on an artificial island in Ise Bay. Nagoya hosted Expo 2005, held in Nagakute and Seto.

Culture

Landmarks include Nagoya Castle, Atsuta Shrine, and the Tokugawa Art Museum. Local cuisine — Nagoya meshi — features dishes such as miso-katsu, kishimen noodles, and hitsumabushi (grilled eel over rice).
