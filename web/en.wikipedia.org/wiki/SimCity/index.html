SimCity

SimCity is an open-ended city-building video game series originally designed by Will Wright. The first game in the series, SimCity, was published by Maxis in 1989 and was one of the first games of its genre. The success of SimCity drove the creation of numerous sequels and spin-offs, and it is considered a landmark in video game design. Players act as mayor, zoning land for residential, commercial, and industrial development, providing infrastructure and services, setting taxes, and managing budgets while responding to disasters and citizen demands.

Gameplay

In most SimCity titles, the player is given a tract of undeveloped land and must build and manage a city, balancing residential, commercial, and industrial zones ("RCI") with power plants, roads, water supply, police and fire coverage, schools, and hospitals. Cities generate income through taxes; the player adjusts tax rates and ordinances to keep citizens (called Sims) satisfied. Random disasters such as earthquakes, fires, tornadoes, and monster attacks may strike the city. There is generally no fixed winning condition; success is measured by population growth, treasury health, and citizen approval.

History

Will Wright developed the original game while working on the shooter Raid on Bungeling Bay, finding that he enjoyed building the game's maps more than playing the game itself. Maxis initially struggled to find a publisher before releasing the game in 1989. Its open-ended, non-violent simulation was unusual for the time and became a major critical and commercial success, influencing later simulation games including The Sims, Wright's subsequent franchise.

The 2013 reboot, simply titled SimCity, used the GlassBox simulation engine and originally required an internet connection; its troubled launch, with server failures preventing many players from accessing the game, was widely criticized, and an offline mode was added in 2014. The series' commercial decline contributed to the closure of Maxis Emeryville in 2015, and rival title Cities: Skylines, released the same year, took over much of the genre's audience.

Legacy

SimCity helped popularize the simulation genre and has been used in educational settings to teach urban planning concepts. The original game's source code was released under a free software license in 2008 under the name Micropolis.
