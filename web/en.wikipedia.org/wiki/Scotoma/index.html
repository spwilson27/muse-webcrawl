Scotoma

A scotoma is an area of partial alteration in the field of vision consisting of a diminished or entirely degenerated visual acuity that is surrounded by a field of normal — or relatively well-preserved — vision. The word derives from the Greek skotos, "darkness".

Example of a central scotoma in the visual field.

Types and causes

Every normal mammal has a physiological scotoma at the optic disc — the blind spot where the optic nerve leaves the retina and no photoreceptors exist. Pathological scotomas may result from damage to the retina (as in macular degeneration), the optic nerve (as in optic neuritis or glaucoma), or the visual cortex. A central scotoma affects the foveal vision used for reading; arcuate scotomas follow the retinal nerve fibre pattern in glaucoma.

Scintillating scotoma

A scintillating scotoma is a common visual aura preceding a migraine: a flickering, zigzag arc of light that expands across part of the visual field over 10–30 minutes before fading. It is thought to arise from cortical spreading depression in the occipital lobe.

Diagnosis

Scotomas are mapped by perimetry (visual field testing). Their size, shape and location help localise lesions along the visual pathway.
