Lance corporal

Lance corporal is a military rank, used by many armed forces worldwide, and in some police forces and other uniformed organisations. It is usually the lowest non-commissioned officer rank, below corporal and above private (or equivalent). The name derives from the Italian lancia spezzata, "broken lance", a veteran soldier who had broken his lance in battle and led a small group of men — a "lance".

United Kingdom

In the British Army and Royal Marines, lance corporal (LCpl or L/Cpl) is the first promotional step from private; the insignia is a single chevron worn on the upper arm. In the infantry the holder typically commands a fire team of four soldiers within a section led by a corporal. The rank goes back to the early 19th century, when it was an appointment rather than a substantive rank; it became a formal rank in 1961. The Household Cavalry uses the equivalent title lance corporal of horse. In the Royal Air Force, lance corporal exists in the RAF Regiment (introduced in 2010); the rest of the RAF uses senior aircraftman ranks instead.

United States

In the United States Marine Corps, lance corporal (LCpl) is the third enlisted rank (pay grade E-3), above private first class and below corporal, and is the most senior non-NCO rank; Marines are typically promoted to it on time-in-service. The US Army does not currently use the rank, though it existed in the 19th and early 20th centuries, and its E-3 grade is private first class.

Other countries

Comparable grades include the Commonwealth lance bombardier (artillery), lance sergeant (an appointment in some regiments), and ranks such as Gefreiter in German-speaking armies, though equivalents differ in status and duties. Many Commonwealth and NATO forces map lance corporal to NATO grade OR-3.
