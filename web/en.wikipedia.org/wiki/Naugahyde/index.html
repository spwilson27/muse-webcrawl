Naugahyde

Naugahyde is an American brand of artificial leather, a vinyl-coated fabric introduced by the United States Rubber Company (later Uniroyal) in 1914 and marketed from Naugatuck, Connecticut. The name plays on Naugatuck and "hide".

Uses

Durable, water-resistant and easy to clean, Naugahyde became popular for automobile and bus seating, furniture, restaurant booths, medical equipment and marine upholstery. A 1960s advertising campaign humorously featured the fictional "Nauga" animal whose hide was supposedly shed without harm.
