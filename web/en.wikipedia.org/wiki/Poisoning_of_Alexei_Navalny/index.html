Poisoning of Alexei Navalny
Date: 20 August 2020
Location: Flight from Tomsk to Moscow; hospitalised in Omsk, then Berlin
Agent identified: Novichok nerve agent

On 20 August 2020, Russian opposition leader and anti-corruption activist Alexei Navalny was poisoned with a Novichok nerve agent during a flight from Tomsk to Moscow. He fell ill and lost consciousness; the plane made an emergency landing in Omsk, where he was hospitalised in a coma. Two days later he was evacuated to the Charité hospital in Berlin, Germany, where he recovered.

Findings

German government laboratories reported "unequivocal proof" of a Novichok-type agent, and the finding was confirmed by laboratories in France and Sweden and by the OPCW, which identified biomarkers of a cholinesterase inhibitor in his blood and urine. Western governments and the European Union attributed the attack to the Russian state and imposed sanctions on Russian officials. In December 2020, an investigation by Bellingcat and media partners alleged that agents of Russia's Federal Security Service (FSB) had followed Navalny and applied the poison; in a recorded phone call published by Navalny, a man identified as an FSB operative appeared to describe aspects of the operation. Russia denied involvement and disputed the findings.

Aftermath

Navalny returned to Russia in January 2021 and was immediately arrested; his detention prompted nationwide protests. His poisoning became a central episode in the deterioration of relations between Russia and the West and in assessments of the treatment of Russian opposition figures.
