The Royal Australian Navy (RAN) is the naval branch of the Australian Defence Force (ADF). It was formed on 1 March 1901 as the Commonwealth Naval Forces by amalgamating the naval forces of the Australian colonies after Federation, and received its "Royal" title from King George V on 10 July 1911. Its ships bear the prefix HMAS (His Majesty's Australian Ship).

History

The young navy's first flagship, the battlecruiser HMAS Australia, arrived in 1913. In the First World War RAN ships served worldwide, and HMAS Sydney won fame by destroying the German raider SMS Emden at the Cocos Islands in November 1914. In the Second World War the RAN reached a strength of nearly 40,000 personnel and almost 350 vessels; it suffered grievous losses, including the cruisers Sydney (lost with all 645 hands to the raider Kormoran in 1941), Perth at the Battle of Sunda Strait, and Canberra at Savo Island. RAN forces later served in Korea, Vietnam, the Gulf, East Timor, and numerous peacekeeping and maritime-security operations.

The modern fleet

The RAN today operates one of the most capable fleets in the Indo-Pacific. Its major units include two Canberra-class landing helicopter docks (HMAS Canberra and Adelaide), three Hobart-class air-warfare destroyers, eight Anzac-class frigates, six Collins-class submarines, and a large force of Armidale- and Cape-class patrol boats, amphibious and replenishment ships, and MH-60R Seahawk helicopters. The principal bases are Fleet Base East (HMAS Kuttabul, Sydney) and Fleet Base West (HMAS Stirling, near Perth).

Under the AUKUS agreement announced in 2021, Australia plans to acquire conventionally armed, nuclear-powered submarines — US Virginia-class boats followed by the jointly developed SSN-AUKUS class — while surface combatants will be renewed with Hunter-class frigates and a new general-purpose frigate fleet.
