Classic cycle races

The classic cycle races are the most prestigious one-day professional road cycling races in the international calendar, most of them over a century old. The five oldest and most famous are known as the Monuments.

The Monuments

Milan–San Remo (the sprinters' classic), the Tour of Flanders and Paris–Roubaix (the "Hell of the North", famed for cobblestones) form the spring cobbled and Italian classics, followed by Liège–Bastogne–Liège in the Ardennes and the autumn Giro di Lombardia, the "Race of the Falling Leaves". Only Eddy Merckx, Roger De Vlaeminck and Rik Van Looy have won all five.

Other classics

Other major one-day races include Ghent–Wevelgem, Amstel Gold Race, La Flèche Wallonne, Strade Bianche, Paris–Tours, Clásica de San Sebastián and the E3 Saxo Classic. The Ardennes and cobbled weeks structure the spring season.

Characteristics

Classics are long (often 250 km or more), run in varied weather over historic routes, and reward endurance, tactics and bike-handling rather than pure stage-race climbing.
