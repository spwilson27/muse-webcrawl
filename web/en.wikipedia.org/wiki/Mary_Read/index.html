Mary Read (c. 1685 – 28 April 1721) was an English pirate who operated in the Caribbean during the early 18th century, the late "Golden Age of Piracy". She is best remembered as one of only two women — along with her crewmate Anne Bonny — known to have been convicted of piracy during that era. Much of her biography derives from Captain Charles Johnson's A General History of the Pyrates (1724), which blends documented fact with probable embellishment.

According to Johnson, Read was raised disguised as a boy, served as a soldier and sailor in Flanders and the West Indies, married a fellow soldier, and after his death crossed the Atlantic, where her ship was taken by pirates. She eventually joined the crew of John "Calico Jack" Rackham — disguising herself as a man named Mark Read — on whose sloop William she sailed in 1720 alongside Anne Bonny, who had made a similar discovery of her sex.

Capture, trial, and death

In October 1720 Rackham's ship was captured off Jamaica by a pirate-hunting sloop under Jonathan Barnet. Read and Bonny reportedly fought while most of the male crew hid below deck. At their trial in Spanish Town, Jamaica, in November 1720, Read and Bonny were convicted of piracy, but their executions were stayed after they "pleaded their bellies" — both were pregnant. Read died in prison in April 1721, probably of a fever following childbirth; a burial record survives in the register of St. Catherine's parish. No record exists of her burial or of her child.

Legacy

Read and Bonny remain among the most famous female pirates in popular culture, appearing in novels, plays, statues, and video games, and their story has often been retold as one of gender disguise and defiance of convention.
