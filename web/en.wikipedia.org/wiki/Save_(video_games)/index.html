Saved game

A saved game (also called a save file, save point or save state) is a record of a player's progress in a video game stored on persistent media, allowing play to be suspended and resumed later. Saving preserves variables such as the player's position, inventory, statistics, completed objectives and game-world state.

History

Early home computer games such as adventures and role-playing games saved to cassette or floppy disk. Console games initially used passwords encoding progress; battery-backed cartridge memory, popularised by The Legend of Zelda (1986), made true save files practical on consoles, followed by memory cards (PlayStation), internal hard drives and cloud saves. Handheld and PC classics like the Ultima and Wizardry series relied on saves to make long campaigns feasible.

Mechanisms

Common designs include manual saving (sometimes restricted to designated save points), autosaves triggered by events, checkpoints that respawn the player on death, and quicksave/quickload keys typical of PC games. Some designs deliberately restrict saving — roguelikes with permadeath delete the save on death ("save scumming" is the pejorative term for abusing reloads to undo failure). Emulators add save states, snapshots of the entire emulated machine that can be restored at any moment.

Technical aspects

Save files are serialised data structures ranging from a few kilobytes to many megabytes; they may be checksummed, compressed or encrypted, and are a common target for editors and modding tools. Cloud synchronisation (Steam Cloud, PlayStation Plus, Xbox cloud saves) allows progress to follow players across machines, while corrupt or version-incompatible saves remain a familiar frustration after game patches.
