Whites (Finland)

The Whites (Finnish: valkoiset) were the forces of the Senate of Finland — the legal, bourgeois government — in the Finnish Civil War of 1918, opposed to the socialist Reds. Their armed force, the White Army (White Guards), was commanded by General C. G. E. Mannerheim and emerged victorious, securing Finland's independence as a non-socialist state.

Composition

The Whites drew support from the landowning peasantry, the middle and upper classes, most of the Swedish-speaking population, and the Finnish establishment. Their military backbone included the Civil Guards (Suojeluskunta), a volunteer militia; some 1,200 Finnish Jägers trained in Germany as the 27th Royal Prussian Jäger Battalion; and direct German military intervention, including the Baltic Sea Division under Rüdiger von der Goltz, which landed in April 1918 and captured Helsinki.

The war

The war began in late January 1918. The Whites, based in Ostrobothnia around Vaasa, disarmed Russian garrisons in the north, then advanced south. Mannerheim's victory at the Battle of Tampere (March–April 1918), the largest battle in Nordic history to that time, broke Red resistance in the west; with German help the government forces took Helsinki, Viipuri, and the remaining Red strongholds by mid-May 1918.

Aftermath

Victory was followed by a harsh White terror: thousands of Reds were executed, and some 12,000–13,500 prisoners died of hunger and disease in prison camps. The Whites' political programme initially favoured a constitutional monarchy with a German king, but Germany's defeat in the First World War led instead to a republic under President K. J. Ståhlberg in 1919. The Civil Guards remained a pillar of White Finland's defence establishment until 1944.
