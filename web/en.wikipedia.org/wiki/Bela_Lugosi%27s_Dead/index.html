"Bela Lugosi's Dead" is a song by the English post-punk band Bauhaus. Released in August 1979 on the Small Wonder label, it was the band's debut single and is widely regarded as a foundational record of gothic rock.

Recording and composition

The song was recorded in a single take at Beck Studios in Wellingborough in January 1979. Over nine minutes long, it builds on a dub-influenced, echoing rhythm with sparse, descending guitar figures and Peter Murphy's funereal vocals. The lyrics refer to Bela Lugosi, the Hungarian actor famed for playing Count Dracula in the 1931 film Dracula, with the refrain declaring the actor "undead".

Legacy

Although it made little chart impression on release, "Bela Lugosi's Dead" became an anthem of the emerging goth subculture. Bauhaus performed the song in the opening sequence of the 1983 vampire film The Hunger. It has been covered and referenced by numerous artists, and the single's sleeve, featuring imagery from the expressionist film The Cabinet of Dr. Caligari, reinforced the band's gothic aesthetic.
