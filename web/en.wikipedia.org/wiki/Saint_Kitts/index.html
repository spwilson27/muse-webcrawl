Saint Kitts, officially the Island of Saint Christopher, is the larger of the two main islands that constitute the Federation of Saint Kitts and Nevis in the Leeward Islands of the Caribbean Sea. The island is separated from its sister island, Nevis, by a shallow channel called The Narrows. The capital of the federation, Basseterre, is on Saint Kitts.

Geography

Saint Kitts is roughly oval in shape, about 29 km long and 8 km wide, with a mountainous volcanic spine running north-west to south-east. Its highest peak, Mount Liamuiga (formerly Mount Misery), reaches 1,156 metres and contains a crater lake. A low, flat south-eastern peninsula extends towards Nevis and holds salt ponds and popular beaches.

History

The island was known to its Kalinago inhabitants as Liamuiga, "fertile land". Sighted by Christopher Columbus in 1493, it became in 1623 the site of the first successful English colony in the Caribbean under Thomas Warner, soon shared with French settlers — earning it the title "Mother Colony of the West Indies". The French and English cooperated in the 1626 Kalinago genocide at Bloody Point. Britain gained sole control by the Treaty of Utrecht (1713), confirmed by the Treaty of Paris (1783) after the Battle of Saint Kitts. Brimstone Hill Fortress, now a UNESCO World Heritage Site, was the island's great stronghold.

Economy

Sugar cane, grown with enslaved labour until emancipation in 1834, dominated the economy for three centuries; the state sugar industry closed in 2005. Today tourism, offshore financial services, and the federation's citizenship-by-investment programme are major income sources. A narrow-gauge railway built for the sugar industry now carries tourists as the St. Kitts Scenic Railway.
