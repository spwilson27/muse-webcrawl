Arthur Edward Waite (2 October 1857 – 19 May 1942) was an American-born British poet, scholar and mystic who wrote extensively on occult and esoteric subjects. He is best known as the co-creator, with the artist Pamela Colman Smith, of the Rider–Waite Tarot deck (1909), the first mass-market tarot deck to bear fully illustrated scenes on all of the Minor Arcana cards, and as the author of its guidebook, The Pictorial Key to the Tarot (1910).

Life

Waite was born in Brooklyn, New York, to an American father, who died at sea, and an English mother, who returned with her children to England. Raised in modest circumstances in London, he was educated at home and briefly at St Charles' College. After the death of his sister in 1874 he turned to spiritualism and then to the study of esoteric literature at the British Museum. He worked as a clerk and in business while building a prolific writing career.

Waite joined the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn in 1891 and became a central figure in its later, reformed offshoot, steering it toward Christian mysticism. In 1915 he founded his own order, the Fellowship of the Rosy Cross. He was also a Freemason and a member of the Societas Rosicruciana in Anglia.

Works

Waite wrote or edited more than fifty books. His scholarly interests centred on the Holy Grail legend, the Kabbalah, alchemy, Rosicrucianism and the history of Freemasonry, which he treated as vehicles of a hidden mystical tradition — the "Secret Tradition" — that he sought to trace through Western esotericism. His works include The Book of Black Magic and of Pacts (1898), The Holy Kabbalah and Its Relation to Divine Wisdom (1929), The Brotherhood of the Rosy Cross (1924), The Holy Grail (1933) and the two-volume A New Encyclopedia of Freemasonry (1921). He also translated Éliphas Lévi and wrote an autobiography, Shadows of Life and Thought (1938).

The Rider–Waite deck

Commissioned by the publisher William Rider & Son, Waite directed the design of a new tarot pack, executed by Pamela Colman Smith, a fellow Golden Dawn member. Published in December 1909, the deck replaced the abstract pip designs of earlier packs with narrative scenes drawn partly from the Sola Busca tarot. It became the most influential tarot deck of the twentieth century and the model for most modern decks.
