Expulsion of Germans after World War II

The flight and expulsion of Germans during and after World War II (1944–1950) involved the forced migration of at least 12 million ethnic Germans from Central and Eastern Europe to Germany and Austria — the largest population transfer in European history.

Expulsion of Germans
Date: 1944–1950
People affected: 12–14 million
Regions: East Prussia, Silesia, Sudetenland, Pomerania

Background and course

Flight ahead of the Red Army in 1944–45 was followed by "wild" expulsions and then organised transfers sanctioned at the Potsdam Conference from Poland, Czechoslovakia (the Beneš decrees, Sudeten Germans) and Hungary. Conditions were brutal; death toll estimates range from about 500,000 in recent scholarship to 2 million in older West German figures.

Aftermath

Expellees (Vertriebene) integrated into West and East Germany, formed influential organisations, and shaped debates about memory, borders finally settled by treaties including the 1990 Two Plus Four settlement.
