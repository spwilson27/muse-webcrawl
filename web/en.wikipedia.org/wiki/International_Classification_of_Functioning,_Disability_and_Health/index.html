International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health

The ICF model shows functioning and disability as an interaction between health conditions and contextual factors.

The International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF) is a classification of the health components of functioning and disability published by the World Health Organization (WHO). It was endorsed by the World Health Assembly in 2001 as the international standard for describing and measuring health and disability.

Background

The ICF replaced the International Classification of Impairments, Disabilities, and Handicaps (ICIDH, 1980). While the ICIDH framed disability largely as a consequence of disease within the individual, the ICF adopts a biopsychosocial model in which disability and functioning are outcomes of interactions between health conditions and contextual factors — environmental and personal. It is designed to complement the ICD, which classifies diseases and health conditions, by classifying the functioning associated with them.

Components

The ICF organizes information into two parts. Part 1, Functioning and Disability, comprises Body Functions and Structures (physiological and anatomical features, and their impairments) and Activities and Participation (the execution of tasks and involvement in life situations, with activity limitations and participation restrictions). Part 2, Contextual Factors, covers Environmental Factors — the physical, social, and attitudinal environment, which can act as facilitators or barriers — and personal factors, which are acknowledged but not classified. Each component is coded with alphanumeric categories and qualifiers indicating the extent of a problem.

Applications

The ICF is used in clinical practice, rehabilitation, social policy, statistics, and research as a common language for describing functioning. A version adapted for children and youth, the ICF-CY, was published in 2007 and later merged into the main classification. Sets of ICF categories for specific health conditions, called Core Sets, have been developed for practical clinical use.
