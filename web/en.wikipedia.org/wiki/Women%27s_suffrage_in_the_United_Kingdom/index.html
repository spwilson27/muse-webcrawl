Women's suffrage in the United Kingdom

Women's suffrage in the UK
Partial suffrage: 1918
Equal suffrage: 1928
Key figures: Millicent Fawcett; Emmeline Pankhurst

Women's suffrage in the United Kingdom was achieved after a long campaign by suffragists and suffragettes in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

Campaign

From 1897 Millicent Fawcett's National Union of Women's Suffrage Societies pursued constitutional methods, while Emmeline Pankhurst's Women's Social and Political Union (founded 1903) adopted militant tactics under the slogan "Deeds, not words" — including protests, hunger strikes and forcible feeding in prison. Campaigning was suspended in 1914 as many women supported the war effort.

Legislation

The Representation of the People Act 1918 enfranchised women over 30 meeting property qualifications, and the Parliament (Qualification of Women) Act 1918 allowed women to sit in the Commons. The Representation of the People (Equal Franchise) Act 1928 finally gave women the vote on the same terms as men, at age 21. See also Women's suffrage.
