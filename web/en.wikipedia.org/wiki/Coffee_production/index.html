Coffee is one of the most valuable agricultural commodities in world trade. Coffee production takes place in more than 70 countries, primarily in the equatorial "coffee belt" of Latin America, Africa, and Asia, and employs tens of millions of smallholder farmers. Brazil is by far the largest producer, followed by Vietnam, Colombia, Indonesia, and Ethiopia, the botanical homeland of Coffea arabica.

Cultivation

Coffee plants are grown from seed in nurseries and planted out in fields, often under shade trees in traditional systems or in full sun in intensive ones. Arabica favours cool highlands between roughly 600 and 2,000 metres, while robusta (Coffea canephora) tolerates hotter, lower conditions. Plants begin bearing after three to five years and flower after rains, producing fruit — the coffee cherry — that ripens over six to nine months.

Harvesting and processing

Cherries are picked by hand, selectively for ripe fruit on quality estates or by stripping whole branches, or harvested mechanically on flatter farms. Processing removes the bean from the fruit by one of three main routes: the washed (wet) process, in which fruit is pulped, fermented, and washed before drying; the natural (dry) process, in which whole cherries are dried and then hulled; and the honey process, a hybrid. The green beans are then milled, graded, bagged, and exported.

Threats and sustainability

The industry faces serious pressures from climate change, which is shrinking suitable arabica land, and from pests and diseases such as coffee leaf rust and the coffee berry borer. Certification schemes such as Fairtrade, Rainforest Alliance, and organic certification aim to improve prices, labour conditions, and environmental practice, and research into resistant varieties and agroforestry is widespread.

Trade

Green coffee is traded as a commodity, with arabica priced on the New York ICE exchange and robusta in London. Price volatility is a chronic problem for producing countries, a theme explored in works such as the documentary Black Gold. Most value is added after export, in roasting and retailing in consuming countries.
