Thus Spoke Zarathustra: A Book for All and None (German: Also sprach Zarathustra: Ein Buch für Alle und Keinen) is a philosophical novel by the German philosopher Friedrich Nietzsche, composed in four parts between 1883 and 1885. It features the fictional prophet Zarathustra, named after the ancient Iranian prophet Zoroaster, who descends from the mountains to announce Nietzsche's central ideas.

Content

In the book's prologue, Zarathustra proclaims that "God is dead" and announces the coming of the Übermensch (Overman or Superman), the goal humanity should set for itself. Key themes include eternal recurrence, the will to power, the critique of herd morality, and the call for individuals to create their own values. The work is written in a poetic, biblical style full of speeches, parables, and songs.

Composition and reception

The first three parts were published in 1883–1884 to little notice; the fourth part was printed privately in 1885 and only published commercially in 1892. The book later became one of the most influential works of modern philosophy and literature. Richard Strauss's tone poem Also sprach Zarathustra (1896), whose opening "Sunrise" fanfare was famously used in the film 2001: A Space Odyssey, is based on chapters from the book.
