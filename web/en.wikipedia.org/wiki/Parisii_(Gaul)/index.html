Parisii (Gaul)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Parisii (Gaulish: Parisioi) were a Gallic tribe dwelling in the region around the modern city of Paris, which is named after them, during the Iron Age and the Roman era. Their territory lay on the middle Seine, between the Senones to the south-east, the Carnutes to the south-west, the Bellovaci to the north, and the Veliocasses and Aulerci to the west.

History

The Parisii settled the area by the 3rd century BC at the latest, farming the fertile plains along the Seine and trading along the river. Their principal settlement was an oppidum identified with Lutetia, although its exact Iron Age location is debated; finds on the Île de la Cité and at Nanterre have both been proposed. The tribe minted high-quality gold staters, notable for their stylised horse designs, which are admired as masterpieces of Celtic coinage.

During the Gallic Wars, the Parisii joined the coalition of Vercingetorix in 52 BC, contributing troops to the relief of Alesia. Julius Caesar had earlier convened an assembly of Gallic tribes at Lutetia in 53 BC. After the Roman conquest, Lutetia developed into a Gallo-Roman town, and the Parisii's civitas preserved their name — evolving into "Paris".

Culture and religion

Like other Gallic peoples, the Parisii were organised under magistrates (vergobrets) and druids, practised La Tène material culture, and cremated or inhumed their dead in cemeteries such as those found around the oppidum. River trade in grain, wine and metals, taxed at Lutetia, was central to their economy, as Caesar noted when describing the settlement's position on the Seine.
