Emain Macha (Old Irish: [ˈeṽə̃nʲ ˈṽaxə]; Irish: Eamhain Mhacha), known in English as Navan Fort, is an ancient ceremonial monument near Armagh, Northern Ireland. In Irish mythology it is the royal capital of the Ulaid, the kings of Ulster, and a central setting of the Ulster Cycle of tales, home of King Conchobar mac Nessa and the warriors of the Red Branch, including the hero Cú Chulainn.

Etymology and legend

The name is traditionally explained in two ways: as "Macha's brooch" (eo-muinn), from the story that the goddess or queen Macha marked out the fort's boundary with her brooch pin, or as "the twins of Macha", from the legend that Macha, forced to race the king's horses while pregnant, gave birth to twins at the finish and cursed the men of Ulster with the cess noínden — the debility that leaves the Ulaid incapacitated in the Táin Bó Cúailnge (Cattle Raid of Cooley).

Archaeology

The site occupies a hilltop with a large circular enclosure about 250 metres across, defined by a ditch and external bank — a henge-like ceremonial layout rather than a defensive fort. Excavations in the 1960s–70s revealed a remarkable sequence of structures on the summit. Most famous is a huge circular timber building some 40 metres in diameter, built around 95 BC (dated precisely by tree rings), which was filled with a cairn of stones, deliberately burnt, and covered with turf to form the mound that survives today — an apparent ritual monument rather than a residence. Finds include an iron sword, spearheads, and the skull of a Barbary ape, evidence of far-flung exchange networks.

Together with sites such as the Hill of Tara, Rathcroghan, and Dún Ailinne, Emain Macha is classed among the "royal sites" of Iron Age Ireland. The nearby Navan Centre interprets the site for visitors.
