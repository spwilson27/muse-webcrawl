Dzogchen

Dzogchen
Tibetanརྫོགས་ཆེན་ (rdzogs chen)
Meaning"Great Perfection" / "Great Completion"
TraditionsNyingma (Tibetan Buddhism), Bon
Key figureGarab Dorje; Longchenpa

Dzogchen (Tibetan: རྫོགས་ཆེན་), also known as the "Great Perfection" or "Great Completion", is a tradition of teachings in Tibetan Buddhism and Bon aimed at discovering and continuing in the ultimate ground of existence. The goal of Dzogchen is knowledge of the natural, primordial state of consciousness — rigpa — which is held to be already perfect and complete.

Teachings

Dzogchen is presented as the highest of the nine vehicles in the Nyingma school. Its practice centres on "direct introduction" to rigpa by a qualified teacher, followed by practices such as trekchö ("cutting through" resistance, resting in primordial purity, kadak) and tögal ("direct crossing", visionary practice associated with spontaneous presence, lhundrup). The tradition attributes its transmission to Garab Dorje, said to have received the teachings from the buddha Vajrasattva; major systematizers include Vimalamitra, Padmasambhava and the 14th-century scholar Longchenpa, whose Seven Treasuries remain central texts.

History

Dzogchen texts emerged in Tibet from the 8th–10th centuries in the Nyingma school, with parallels in the indigenous Bon religion. Historically it was kept relatively esoteric; in the 19th–20th centuries figures of the Rimé movement, and later teachers such as Dudjom Rinpoche and Namkhai Norbu, brought it to wider audiences, including in the West.
