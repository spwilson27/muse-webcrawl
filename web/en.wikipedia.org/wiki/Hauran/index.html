The Hauran (Arabic: حَوْرَان, Ḥawrān) is a region spanning southwestern Syria and northwestern Jordan, south of Damascus and east of the Golan Heights. It includes the volcanic plateau of the Jabal al-Druze (Jabal Hauran) in the east and the fertile Nukrah plain in the west.

Geography and agriculture

The region is characterised by weathered basalt from ancient volcanic activity, which produces rich dark soils. The Hauran plain has long been known as a granary of Syria, producing wheat, barley, and other cereals for Damascus and beyond. Its distinctive architecture uses black basalt blocks, seen in towns such as Bosra, famous for its remarkably preserved Roman theatre.

History

The Hauran is mentioned in ancient Egyptian and biblical texts; the biblical Bashan largely corresponds to this region. Under Roman rule it was divided among provinces including Arabia Petraea and Syria, and cities such as Bosra, Shahba, and Qanawat flourished, leaving extensive ruins. In Ottoman times the region was a corridor for the Hajj pilgrimage caravan from Damascus to Mecca. The Druze population of the Jabal al-Druze played a central role in the Great Syrian Revolt of 1925–1927. In 2011, protests in the Hauran city of Daraa were among the sparks of the Syrian uprising.
