The Stone of Scone (also called the Stone of Destiny) is an oblong block of red sandstone that was used for centuries in the coronation of the monarchs of Scotland. Legend associates it with the biblical Jacob's Pillow Stone. It was kept at the now-ruined Scone Abbey in Scone, near Perth, Scotland.

Seizure and long absence

In 1296, during the First War of Scottish Independence, King Edward I of England seized the stone as a spoil of war and took it to Westminster Abbey, where it was fitted into a wooden throne — the Coronation Chair — on which English and later British sovereigns were crowned. It remained in London for 700 years.

The 1950 removal

On Christmas Day 1950, four Scottish students removed the stone from Westminster Abbey; it broke in two during the removal and was later repaired. After a nationwide search, it reappeared at Arbroath Abbey in April 1951 and was returned to Westminster. The incident greatly raised the stone's profile in Scottish national consciousness.

Return to Scotland

In 1996 the British government returned the stone to Scotland, stipulating that it would travel to Westminster for future coronations. It was displayed at Edinburgh Castle until 2020, then moved to Perth Museum, which opened in 2024. The stone was used at the coronation of Charles III in May 2023.
