Gilles Binchois

Born: c. 1400, probably Mons, County of Hainaut

Died: September 20, 1460, Soignies

Occupation: Composer, singer, priest

Era: Burgundian School, early Renaissance

Gilles de Bins dit Binchois (also Binchoys, c. 1400 – September 20, 1460) was a Franco-Flemish composer of the early Renaissance. A central figure of the Burgundian School, he was, with Guillaume Du Fay and John Dunstaple, one of the most famous and influential composers of the fifteenth century. He is remembered especially for his graceful secular chansons, which were widely copied and admired throughout Europe.

Life

Binchois was probably born in Mons, in the County of Hainaut (now in Belgium). He may have served as a soldier in his youth — he later referred to military service — before entering the church. By 1419 he was organist at Sainte-Waudru in Mons. In the 1420s he briefly served the English Earl of Suffolk in France, and by about 1430 he had joined the chapel of Philip the Good, Duke of Burgundy, one of the most splendid musical establishments in Europe. He spent the remainder of his career in Burgundian service, holding ecclesiastical benefices, and retired to Soignies, where he died in 1460.

Music

Binchois wrote both sacred and secular music. His sacred output includes Mass movements, Magnificats, hymns, and motets, many apparently composed for the daily use of the ducal chapel. His lasting fame rests on his more than fifty surviving chansons — rondeaux and ballades for three voices on courtly love themes — characterized by clear phrasing, smooth melodies, and delicate counterpoint. Songs such as Triste plaisir and De plus en plus remained popular for decades and served as models for other composers.

Influence

Contemporaries praised Binchois alongside Du Fay; the poet Martin le Franc ranked the two as the leading composers of the age, and Johannes Ockeghem composed a lament (Mort, tu as navré de ton dart) on Binchois's death. His melodic style helped define the Burgundian chanson and influenced the next generation of Franco-Flemish composers.
