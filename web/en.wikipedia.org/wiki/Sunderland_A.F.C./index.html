Sunderland A.F.C.

Sunderland Association Football Club is a professional football club based in Sunderland, Tyne and Wear, England. Founded in 1879, the club plays at the Stadium of Light, its home since 1997 after 99 years at Roker Park. Sunderland have won the English league championship six times (1892, 1893, 1895, 1902, 1913 and 1936) and the FA Cup twice (1937 and 1973), the latter as a Second Division side in one of the competition's most famous upsets. Nicknamed the Black Cats, the club's traditional rivals are Newcastle United, against whom it contests the Tyne–Wear derby.

History

The club was founded by schoolmaster James Allan as Sunderland and District Teachers A.F.C. and joined the Football League in 1890, quickly earning the nickname "the Team of All Talents" after winning three championships in four years. Sunderland remained in the top flight for a record 57 consecutive seasons until relegation in 1958. Managed by Bob Stokoe, the 1973 FA Cup final victory over Don Revie's Leeds United, secured by an Ian Porterfield goal and Jim Montgomery's famous double save, remains the club's defining modern moment.

Recent decades

The 1990s and 2000s brought yo-yo promotions under Peter Reid and a period of Premier League stability, followed by consecutive relegations in 2017 and 2018 that dropped the club to League One — a fall documented in the Netflix series Sunderland 'Til I Die. Under new ownership the club returned to the Championship via the play-offs in 2022, and won promotion back to the Premier League in the 2025 play-off final.
