Zhejiang Geely Holding Group
Industry: Automotive
Founded: 1986 (as refrigerator parts maker); entered automobiles 1997
Founder: Li Shufu
Headquarters: Hangzhou, Zhejiang, China

Zhejiang Geely Holding Group Co., Ltd., commonly known as Geely, is a Chinese multinational automotive company headquartered in Hangzhou, Zhejiang. Founded by entrepreneur Li Shufu, the group began making refrigerator components in 1986 and entered the automobile business in 1997, becoming the first privately owned carmaker in China. Its listed arm, Geely Automobile Holdings, sells passenger vehicles under the Geely brand.

Brands and acquisitions

Geely is best known internationally for acquiring Volvo Cars from Ford in 2010, a landmark overseas purchase by a Chinese automaker. The group's portfolio has since expanded to include Polestar and Zeekr (electric vehicles), Lynk & Co (a joint brand with Volvo), Geometry, Galaxy, the London Electric Vehicle Company (maker of London taxis), and stakes in or control of Lotus Cars and Proton of Malaysia. Geely is also the largest shareholder of Germany's Daimler/Mercedes-Benz Group, with Li Shufu holding roughly a tenth of the shares.

Operations

The company operates manufacturing plants and research centres in China and abroad, including design studios in Gothenburg, Coventry, and Shanghai. It has invested heavily in electrification, hybrid powertrains, and shared vehicle architectures developed with Volvo, such as the Compact Modular Architecture. Geely has also branched into satellites, motorcycles (through QJ Motor), and ride-hailing technology.

Sales

Geely consistently ranks among China's largest automakers by volume, selling well over a million vehicles a year across its brands, with exports to markets in Asia, Latin America, the Middle East, and Europe.
