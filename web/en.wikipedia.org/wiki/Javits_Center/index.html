Javits Center

Image placeholder: Javits Center

The Jacob K. Javits Convention Center, commonly known as the Javits Center, is a large convention center on Eleventh Avenue in the Hudson Yards neighborhood of Manhattan in New York City. It was designed by architect James Ingo Freed of Pei Cobb Freed & Partners. The space-frame structure was constructed from 1980 to 1986 and was named for United States Senator Jacob Javits, who died that year. The center is operated and managed by the New York Convention Center Operating Corporation.

History

The Javits Center opened on 3 April 1986, replacing the New York Coliseum as the city's main convention venue. Its distinctive glass-and-steel space frame covers a full city block between 34th and 40th Streets. A major expansion north of 40th Street, completed in 2021, added 1.2 million square feet, including a rooftop farm and pavilion; a renovation known as Javits North was designed to increase the total exhibition capacity substantially.

Events and use

The center hosts major trade shows and conventions, including the New York International Auto Show, New York Comic Con, and the New York Boat Show. In March 2020, during the COVID-19 pandemic, the Javits Center was converted into a 2,500-bed temporary field hospital operated with the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, and later served as a mass vaccination site.

Bird collisions and green roof

The building's glass facade was formerly notorious for bird strikes; renovations replacing the glass with fritted panels and the installation of a large green roof — one of the largest in the United States — greatly reduced collisions and created habitat for birds and other wildlife.
