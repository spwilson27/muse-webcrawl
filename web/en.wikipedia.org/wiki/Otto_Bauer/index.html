Otto Bauer (5 September 1881 – 4 July 1938) was an Austrian Social Democratic politician and political theorist, and the leading intellectual figure of Austromarxism. He served as foreign minister in the first government of the Republic of Austria after the First World War and led the Social Democratic Workers' Party (SDAP) during the interwar years.

Early life and theory

Bauer was born in Vienna to a wealthy Jewish textile-manufacturing family. He studied law at the University of Vienna, earning his doctorate in 1906, and became a leading contributor to the party's theoretical journal Der Kampf, which he co-founded in 1907. His first major work, The Question of Nationalities and Social Democracy (1907), developed an influential definition of the nation as a "community of character" arising from a shared fate, and proposed national cultural autonomy — personal, non-territorial self-government for national groups — as the socialist answer to the Habsburg Empire's multinational tensions.

War, revolution, and office

Bauer served as an officer on the Eastern Front, was captured by Russian forces, and returned from captivity in 1917. In November 1918 he became state secretary for foreign affairs in Karl Renner's government. He sought union (Anschluss) with the new German republic, negotiating secretly with Berlin, but the Allied victors forbade it in the Treaty of Saint-Germain, and he resigned in July 1919. He also oversaw negotiations associated with the settlement that ceded German-speaking South Tyrol to Italy.

Leader of the SDAP

Through the 1920s Bauer was the dominant theorist and effective leader of Austrian Social Democracy, architect of the reform achievements of "Red Vienna" and author of the Linz Programme of 1926, which combined commitment to democratic means with a defensive threat of force should the bourgeoisie destroy democracy. His strategy of waiting — building a "balance of class forces" rather than forcing revolution — was blamed by critics for the party's hesitation as Austrian democracy decayed. After the brief civil war of February 1934, in which Chancellor Dollfuss's regime crushed the party and its Schutzbund militia, Bauer fled to Brno in Czechoslovakia, directing the exiled party's underground bureau, and later moved to Paris, where he died in 1938, weeks after the Anschluss he had once sought on democratic terms was imposed by Nazi Germany.
