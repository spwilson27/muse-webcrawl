The 6th Airborne Division was an airborne infantry division of the British Army during the Second World War. Despite its designation, it was in fact the second of two airborne divisions raised by the British Army, alongside the 1st Airborne Division.

Formation

The division was formed in April–May 1943 under Major-General Richard Gale. It comprised parachute and airlanding (glider-borne) brigades, supported by airborne artillery, engineers and reconnaissance troops, all wearing the maroon beret and the Pegasus insignia.

Normandy

On D-Day, 6 June 1944, the division landed east of the River Orne to protect the left flank of the Normandy invasion. Its troops captured the Caen canal and Orne river bridges (the former becoming famous as "Pegasus Bridge"), destroyed the Merville gun battery, and held the ridge east of Caen against repeated counter-attacks until the breakout in August.

Later service

After being rushed to Belgium during the Battle of the Bulge in December 1944, the division made its second combat jump in Operation Varsity, the airborne crossing of the Rhine, in March 1945, and advanced across northern Germany to the Baltic, reaching Wismar before the war ended. It subsequently served on internal-security duties in Palestine before being disbanded in 1948.
