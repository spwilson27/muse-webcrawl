Hypersthene is a common rock-forming inosilicate mineral belonging to the pyroxene group. Its chemical formula is (Mg,Fe)SiO3, representing the intermediate compositions of the orthopyroxene solid-solution series between enstatite (the magnesium endmember) and ferrosilite (the iron endmember). The name comes from the Greek words for "over strength," referring to its hardness being greater than that of the otherwise similar amphibole hornblende with which it was once confused.

Properties and occurrence

Hypersthene crystallizes in the orthorhombic system and typically occurs as massive, foliated, or lamellar aggregates rather than well-formed crystals. It is greenish, brown, or nearly black, with prominent cleavage and sometimes displays a coppery-red schiller on cleavage surfaces. It is a characteristic mineral of igneous rocks such as norite, gabbro, and andesite, and of high-grade metamorphic rocks of the granulite facies, including charnockites, in which hypersthene is a diagnostic constituent. It also occurs in some meteorites.

Classification status

Although long established in the mineralogical literature, the name hypersthene was officially discredited as a distinct species by the International Mineralogical Association's pyroxene nomenclature revision (1988), which treats it as an intermediate member of the enstatite–ferrosilite series. The term remains in widespread use in petrology for orthopyroxene of intermediate composition.

Use

Material with attractive schiller is occasionally cut as a gemstone or used ornamentally, but hypersthene is principally of scientific and petrological interest.
