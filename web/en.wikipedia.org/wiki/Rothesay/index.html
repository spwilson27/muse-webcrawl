Rothesay

Country: Scotland

Council area: Argyll and Bute

Population: approx. 4,400

Location: Isle of Bute, Firth of Clyde

Rothesay (Scottish Gaelic: Baile Bhòid) is the principal town on the Isle of Bute, in the Firth of Clyde, Scotland. A royal burgh and former county town of Bute, it grew in the nineteenth century into a fashionable Clyde seaside resort for the people of Glasgow, and its name is carried by the dukedom of Rothesay, a title traditionally borne by the heir to the British throne.

History

The town grew around Rothesay Castle, a circular curtain-walled fortress built in the thirteenth century, which was attacked by Norwegians in 1230 and 1263 and later held by the Stewart kings. Robert III created his eldest son Duke of Rothesay in 1398 — the first dukedom created in Scotland — and the title "Duke of Rothesay" is still used by the heir apparent in Scotland.

In the Victorian era, steamers brought thousands of holidaymakers "doon the watter" from Glasgow, and Rothesay acquired its pier, promenade, winter gardens (1924), and grand hotels and villas. After the decline of Clyde resort tourism in the later twentieth century, the town settled into quieter roles as a ferry port, marina, and local service centre.

Geography and transport

Rothesay curves around a sheltered bay on Bute's east coast, facing the Cowal peninsula. Caledonian MacBrayne ferries link the town's pier with Wemyss Bay on the mainland, connecting with trains to Glasgow. Attractions include the castle, the Bute Museum, the restored Winter Gardens housing a discovery centre, and nearby Mount Stuart House, the Gothic palace of the Marquess of Bute.
