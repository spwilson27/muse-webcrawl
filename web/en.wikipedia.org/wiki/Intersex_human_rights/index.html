Intersex human rights

Intersex human rights concern the rights of people born with sex characteristics — chromosomes, gonads, hormones or genitals — that do not fit typical binary notions of male or female bodies. Advocacy focuses on bodily autonomy and integrity, protection from discrimination, legal recognition, and access to information and peer support.

Key issues

Central to the movement is opposition to medically unnecessary, non-consensual surgeries and hormone treatments performed on intersex infants and children to make their bodies conform to typical sex characteristics. Critics of such practices argue they can cause physical and psychological harm and should be deferred until the person can participate in the decision. Defenders of early intervention have argued that it eases parental distress and social integration, though major medical and human-rights bodies have increasingly urged caution.

International developments

In 2013 the Malta Declaration, adopted by the Third International Intersex Forum, set out demands including an end to non-consensual "normalising" treatments and to discriminatory registration practices. Malta became the first country to prohibit non-consensual modifications of sex characteristics by law in 2015, and several other jurisdictions have since introduced protections. In 2024 the United Nations Human Rights Council adopted its first resolution specifically addressing discrimination and harmful practices against intersex persons. Courts and legislatures in various countries have also recognised third-sex or non-binary marker options, and sports governing bodies' eligibility rules for athletes with differences of sex development have been widely contested.

Organisations

Advocacy is led by intersex-led organisations at national and regional level, many coordinated through networks such as ILGA World and OII (Organisation Intersex International) affiliates.
