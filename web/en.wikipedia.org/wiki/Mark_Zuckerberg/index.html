Mark Zuckerberg

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Mark Zuckerberg photographed at a technology conference, wearing a grey T-shirt, speaking on stage]

Mark Elliot Zuckerberg (born 14 May 1984) is an American businessman and programmer best known as the co-founder of the social-networking service Facebook and its parent company Meta Platforms, of which he is chairman, chief executive officer and controlling shareholder.

Zuckerberg launched Facebook from his Harvard University dormitory room in February 2004 with Eduardo Saverin, Andrew McCollum, Dustin Moskovitz and Chris Hughes. The company took the name Meta Platforms in 2021 as it emphasised virtual-reality and 'metaverse' ambitions alongside its social apps.

Early life and education

Born in White Plains, New York, Zuckerberg developed an early interest in programming, building a messaging program called 'ZuckNet' as a teenager. At Harvard he created Facemash, a controversial site comparing students' photos, before Facebook.

Career and influence

Under Zuckerberg, Facebook acquired Instagram in 2012 and WhatsApp in 2014, growing into one of the world's largest technology companies. He and his wife Priscilla Chan founded the Chan Zuckerberg Initiative in 2015, pledging much of their wealth to philanthropic causes. His leadership has also drawn sustained scrutiny over privacy, content moderation and market power.
