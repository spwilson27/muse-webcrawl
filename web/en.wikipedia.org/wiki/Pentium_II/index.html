Pentium II
Launched: 7 May 1997
Discontinued: 1999 (desktop)
Manufacturer: Intel
Architecture: x86 (P6 microarchitecture)
Clock speeds: 233 MHz – 450 MHz

The Pentium II in its distinctive SECC cartridge for Slot 1

The Pentium II is a microprocessor introduced by Intel on 7 May 1997 as the successor to the Pentium Pro. Based on the P6 microarchitecture, it combined the Pentium Pro's advanced core — dynamic execution, dual independent bus — with the MMX instruction set and improved 16-bit performance, making it suitable for the mainstream consumer and business desktop market as well as workstations and servers.

Design

Unlike the socketed chips before it, the Pentium II was mounted on a circuit board enclosed in a plastic and metal cartridge (the Single Edge Contact Cartridge, SECC) that plugged into a motherboard Slot 1 connector. The level-2 cache — 512 KB — sat on the cartridge beside the CPU core and ran at half the core speed. Early models (code-named Klamath) ran at 233–300 MHz on a 66 MHz front-side bus; the later Deschutes core, built on a 250 nm process, reached 450 MHz and introduced the 100 MHz bus.

Variants and legacy

The Pentium II spawned the budget Celeron line (initially Pentium II cores with reduced or no L2 cache) and the Pentium II Xeon for servers and workstations, which used larger, full-speed caches. It was also the first Pentium processor to feature Intel's famous "Intel Inside" era marketing push into mainstream retail desktops, and it powered the typical Windows 95/98-era PC. It was succeeded in 1999 by the Pentium III.
