Organic wine

Organic wine

Type: Wine from organically grown grapes

Related: Biodynamic wine, natural wine

Organic wine is wine made from grapes grown in accordance with principles of organic farming — without synthetic fertilisers, pesticides or herbicides — and produced under rules that restrict additives and processing aids. The precise legal definition differs between jurisdictions, which has long been a source of confusion for consumers.

Definitions

In the European Union, since 2012 the term "organic wine" may be used for wine made from certified organic grapes and vinified under EU organic winemaking rules, which limit additives and cap sulphur dioxide below the levels allowed in conventional wine. Copper and sulphur sprays — traditional, naturally derived fungicides — remain permitted in the vineyard, a point of contention among environmentalists because of copper accumulation in soils.

In the United States, USDA rules distinguish "organic wine", which must be made from organic grapes with no added sulphites, from wine "made with organic grapes", which may contain added sulphur dioxide up to 100 parts per million. Because sulphites are the industry's standard preservative, most American organic-grape wine is sold under the second label.

Production and market

Organic viticulture substitutes cover crops, mechanical weeding, compost, canopy management and resistant grape varieties for chemical inputs, and requires a conversion period (typically three years) before certification. France, Spain and Italy hold most of the world's certified organic vineyard area, and the global market share of organic wine has grown steadily, driven by consumer demand and by wine regions' responses to climate change and soil health. Organic production overlaps with, but is distinct from, biodynamic wine (certified by bodies such as Demeter) and the looser category of natural wine, which has no legal definition.
