Jule Styne (1905–1994) was an English-American song writer and composer for musical theater and film. He composed the scores of many Broadway classics, including Gypsy, Funny Girl and Gentlemen Prefer Blondes.

Early life

Born Julius Kerwin Stein in London to Russian Jewish immigrants, he moved with his family to Chicago as a child. A piano prodigy, he performed with orchestras before leading dance bands and working as a vocal coach in Hollywood.

Career

With lyricist Sammy Cahn, Styne wrote wartime hits including "I'll Walk Alone" and won an Academy Award for "Three Coins in the Fountain" (1954). On Broadway he collaborated with Betty Comden and Adolph Green on Bells Are Ringing and with Stephen Sondheim on Gypsy (1959), whose score includes "Everything's Coming Up Roses". Funny Girl (1964), with lyrics by Bob Merrill, produced the standard "People" and launched Barbra Streisand to stardom.

Legacy

Styne was inducted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame and received a Kennedy Center Honor in 1990. His songs, numbering in the hundreds, remain staples of the American songbook.
