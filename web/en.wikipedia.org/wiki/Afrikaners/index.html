Afrikaners

Afrikaners are a Southern African ethnic group descended from predominantly Dutch settlers who first arrived at the Cape of Good Hope in 1652. Until 1994, they dominated South Africa's politics as well as the country's commercial agricultural sector.

Afrikaners make up approximately 5.2% of the total South African population, based on the number of white South Africans who speak Afrikaans as a first language in the South African National Census of 2022.

History

The ancestors of Afrikaners were mainly Dutch Calvinists, but also included Germans and French Huguenots. Other peoples also contributed to the ancestry of Afrikaners in varying amounts, including indigenous South Africans, enslaved peoples from the rest of Africa, and many others from countries in Asia. The arrival of Jan van Riebeeck at the Cape in 1652, on behalf of the Dutch East India Company, marked the beginning of permanent European settlement. Settlers who moved inland became known as Trekboers, and later groups undertook the Great Trek of the 1830s and 1840s into the interior, founding the Boer republics, including the Orange Free State and the South African Republic (Transvaal).

The Second Boer War (1899–1902) against the British Empire ended in defeat for the republics and great suffering, including the deaths of thousands of Boer women and children in concentration camps. Political mobilisation in the twentieth century led to the rise of Afrikaner nationalism and the National Party, which governed South Africa from 1948 to 1994 and implemented the system of apartheid.

Language and culture

The Afrikaans language developed at the Cape from Dutch dialects, influenced by the languages of slaves and indigenous peoples. It was recognised as an official language in 1925 and today is spoken as a first language by millions of South Africans of all backgrounds. Afrikaner culture is traditionally associated with Calvinist religion, particularly the Dutch Reformed Church, as well as with rural farming life, braais (barbecues), and folk music and dance such as the volkspele.

Modern era

Since the first democratic elections of 1994, Afrikaners have participated in South African politics across several parties. Many Afrikaners emigrated after 1994, while others remained and continue to play a role in the country's economy, agriculture, sport and culture. Afrikaans-language media, universities and cultural festivals such as the Aardklop and Klein Karoo Nasionale Kunstefees remain prominent.
