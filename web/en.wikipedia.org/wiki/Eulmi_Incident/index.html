Eulmi Incident

The Eulmi Incident (Korean: 을미사변) was the assassination of Queen Min of Korea (posthumously Empress Myeongseong) by Japanese agents on 8 October 1895 at Gyeongbokgung Palace in Seoul. Named for the year eulmi in the traditional sexagenary cycle, the incident is one of the most notorious events of the period in which Japan and other powers contested control of the Korean Empire's predecessor, the Joseon kingdom.

Background

After Japan's victory in the First Sino-Japanese War, Queen Min sought to counter Japanese influence by drawing closer to Russia. The Japanese minister in Seoul, Miura Gorō, a former army general, conspired with Japanese military personnel, ronin, and Korean collaborators of the pro-Japanese faction to eliminate her.

The assassination

Before dawn on 8 October 1895, the conspirators forced their way into the palace despite resistance from the royal guard, located the Queen in her chambers in Geoncheonggung, murdered her, and burned her body in a nearby pine grove. King Gojong was effectively held prisoner. The killing provoked outrage in Korea and abroad; under international pressure Japan put Miura and others on trial in Hiroshima, but they were acquitted for lack of evidence.

Consequences

The incident fuelled the growth of Korean righteous armies (uibyeong) resisting Japanese encroachment and deepened Korean resentment of Japan. King Gojong fled to the Russian legation in February 1896 (the Agwan Pacheon), temporarily checking Japanese dominance. The Eulmi Incident remains a deeply emotive episode in Korean historical memory, and Myeongseong is honoured as a patriotic martyr; the event has been dramatised in Korean theatre, film, and the long-running musical The Last Empress.
