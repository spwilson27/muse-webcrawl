Marooning

Marooning is the deliberate abandonment of a person in a remote, uninhabited place such as a desert island or reef. It was practised most famously as a punishment among pirates and buccaneers in the 17th and 18th centuries, and in some navies for serious offences.

Practice

A marooned sailor might be left with a small supply of water, food, shot and powder, and sometimes a firearm. Because many marooning islands lacked fresh water and food, the punishment was often a slow death sentence, though some victims survived — most famously Alexander Selkirk, whose four years marooned on the Juan Fernández Islands helped inspire Robinson Crusoe.

In culture

Marooning features prominently in pirate literature, from Treasure Island, in which Ben Gunn is marooned for three years, to accounts of the buccaneer era in Exquemelin's Buccaneers of America.
