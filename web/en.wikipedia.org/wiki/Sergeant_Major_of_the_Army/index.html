Sergeant Major of the Army

The Sergeant Major of the Army (SMA) is the most senior enlisted member of the United States Army and a unique position of rank and protocol. The Sergeant Major of the Army serves as the personal adviser to the Chief of Staff of the Army on all matters affecting the enlisted force, particularly training, quality of life, and professional development.

History and role

The position was created in 1966, during the Vietnam era, on the recommendation of Chief of Staff Harold K. Johnson, who modelled it in part on senior enlisted advisers he had observed in other forces. The first Sergeant Major of the Army was William O. Wooldridge. The incumbent is appointed by the Chief of Staff and typically serves a term of about four years.

Although the SMA is a non-commissioned officer, the position carries protocol precedence equivalent to a three-star general, its own distinctive insignia and flag, and an office in the Pentagon. The SMA travels widely to meet soldiers, testifies before Congress on personnel issues, and sits on various boards affecting enlisted policy.

Selection

Sergeants major considered for the office are among the Army's most experienced command sergeants major, usually having served at the highest levels of command. Only a small number of soldiers have held the post since its creation.
