David Brower

David Ross Brower (July 1, 1912 – November 5, 2000) was a prominent American environmentalist and the founder of many environmental organizations, including the Sierra Club Foundation and Friends of the Earth. He served as the first executive director of the Sierra Club from 1952 to 1969 and is widely credited with transforming it from a small California hiking club into one of the most powerful environmental organizations in the United States.

Early life

Brower was born in Berkeley, California. He dropped out of the University of California, Berkeley, and worked as a publicity manager for the Yosemite Park and Curry Company. An accomplished mountaineer, he made dozens of first ascents in the Sierra Nevada and served in the U.S. Army's 10th Mountain Division during World War II.

Conservation campaigns

Under Brower, the Sierra Club successfully fought proposals to build dams in Dinosaur National Monument and in the Grand Canyon. He pioneered the use of large-format coffee-table books and full-page newspaper advertisements to mobilize public opinion. He later founded Friends of the Earth in 1969, the League of Conservation Voters, and the Earth Island Institute in 1982.

Legacy

Brower was nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize three times. He died in Berkeley in 2000. The David Brower Center in Berkeley, California, is named for him.
