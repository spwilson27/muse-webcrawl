Sheppey Crossing

Carries: A249 road
Crosses: The Swale
Locale: Kent, England
Opened: 3 July 2006

The Sheppey Crossing is a road bridge in Kent, England, carrying the A249 across the Swale channel between mainland Kent and the Isle of Sheppey. Opened on 3 July 2006, it replaced reliance on the older Kingsferry Bridge (which it runs beside and which still carries local and rail traffic), providing the first fixed dual-carriageway link to the island.

Design and construction

The crossing is a four-lane, high-level fixed bridge about 1.25 km long, built high enough (a navigational clearance of roughly 29 metres) for ships to pass beneath without lifting — a key advantage over the lifting Kingsferry Bridge, whose openings frequently delayed road traffic. The project cost around £100 million and was delivered under a private finance arrangement.

2013 pile-up

On 5 September 2013 the crossing was the scene of one of Britain's largest road collisions: around 130 vehicles crashed in dense fog in the morning rush hour, in a chain reaction stretching hundreds of metres. Miraculously no one was killed, though about 70 people were injured, eight seriously. The accident prompted debate about the absence of lighting and fog-warning systems on the bridge; several drivers were prosecuted, and calls followed for speed and visibility measures on the crossing.
