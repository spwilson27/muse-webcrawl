Ian Schrager (born July 19, 1946) is an American entrepreneur, hotelier and real estate developer. He co-founded the legendary New York nightclub Studio 54 and later pioneered the "boutique hotel" and "urban resort" concepts, and is credited with popularising the hotel lobby as a social scene.

Studio 54

Schrager and his college friend Steve Rubell opened Studio 54 in 1977 in a former television studio on West 54th Street in Manhattan. The club became the epicentre of disco-era nightlife, famous for its celebrity clientele and theatrical door policy. In 1979 the two were convicted of tax evasion and served prison terms; Schrager received a presidential pardon from Barack Obama in 2017.

Hotels

After prison, Schrager turned to hotels, opening Morgans Hotel in New York in 1984 with designer Andrée Putman — generally regarded as the first boutique hotel. Through Morgans Hotel Group he created properties including the Royalton and Paramount in New York, the Delano in Miami Beach, the Mondrian in Los Angeles and the Hudson in New York, famously collaborating with designer Philippe Starck and describing his approach as "cheap chic". He later partnered with Marriott International to create the Edition brand (launched 2010) and launched his own Public Hotels brand in 2017.
