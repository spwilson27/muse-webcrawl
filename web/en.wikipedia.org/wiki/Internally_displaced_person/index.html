Internally displaced person

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An internally displaced person (IDP) is someone who has been forced to flee their home but who remains within their country's borders — because of armed conflict, generalised violence, human rights violations or natural or human-made disasters. Unlike refugees, IDPs have not crossed an international border, so no dedicated binding global treaty protects them; the 1998 Guiding Principles on Internal Displacement restate applicable human-rights and humanitarian law, and the African Union's Kampala Convention (2009) binds its parties.

Scale and response

The Internal Displacement Monitoring Centre counts IDPs in the tens of millions — recent year-end totals have exceeded 75 million people, driven by conflicts in Sudan, Ukraine, DR Congo, Myanmar and elsewhere and by disasters. Because IDPs remain under their own government's jurisdiction — even when that government caused the displacement — the international response operates on "sovereignty as responsibility" principles through UNHCR, IOM and the cluster system. Durable solutions (return, local integration, resettlement elsewhere in-country) lag displacement's scale, producing protracted IDP situations spanning generations (Colombia, Azerbaijan historically, Iraq, Somalia).
