Kokoda Track campaign

Date21 July – 16 November 1942
LocationTerritory of Papua
ResultAllied victory
BelligerentsAustralia, United States vs. Empire of Japan

The Kokoda Track campaign or Kokoda Trail campaign was part of the Pacific War of World War II. The campaign consisted of a series of battles fought between July and November 1942 in what was then the Australian Territory of Papua. It was primarily a land battle, between the Japanese South Seas Detachment under Major General Tomitarō Horii and Australian and Papuan land forces under command of New Guinea Force.

Background

After their attempt to capture Port Moresby by sea was disrupted by the Battle of the Coral Sea, the Japanese resolved to take the town by an overland advance across the Owen Stanley Range. Japanese forces landed at Buna and Gona on 21 July 1942 and advanced south along the rugged Kokoda Track toward Kokoda village and its strategically valuable airstrip.

The fighting

Initially opposed mainly by the understrength Maroubra Force, built around the 39th Battalion of Australian militia, the Australians fought a delaying action at Kokoda, Isurava, Mission Ridge–Brigade Hill, and Ioribaiwa. At Isurava in late August the Australians were reinforced by the veteran 2/14th Battalion of the Second Australian Imperial Force. Prime Minister John Curtin and General Thomas Blamey controversially relieved commanders during the retreat, but by late September, with their supply lines overextended and ordered to withdraw in the wake of Japanese reverses at Milne Bay and Guadalcanal, Horii's force began a fighting retreat northward.

Australian forces pursued the Japanese back along the track, fighting costly actions at Eora Creek–Templeton's Crossing and Oivi–Gorari. General Horii drowned while attempting to cross the Kumusi River. Australian and United States forces then converged on the Japanese beachheads at Buna and Gona. The campaign is remembered in Australia for the role of Papuan carriers, the "Fuzzy Wuzzy Angels," who carried supplies and evacuated the wounded.
