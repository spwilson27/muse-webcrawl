Medicine in the medieval Islamic world

In the history of medicine, Islamic medicine refers to the science of medicine developed in the Middle East and the Islamic world between the 7th and 15th centuries, building on Greek, Persian, and Indian traditions.

Key figures

Al-Razi (Rhazes) wrote on smallpox and measles; Ibn Sina's The Canon of Medicine (1025) remained a standard textbook in Europe for centuries; Al-Zahrawi advanced surgery; Ibn al-Nafis described pulmonary circulation.

Institutions

Bimaristans (hospitals) in Baghdad, Damascus, and Cairo provided free care, medical training, and pharmacies. Translation movements preserved and extended ancient knowledge, later transmitting it to medieval Europe. See Medication.
