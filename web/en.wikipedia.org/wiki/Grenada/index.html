Grenada

Grenada (Grenadian Creole French: Gwinàd) is an island country of the West Indies in the eastern Caribbean Sea. The southernmost of the Windward Islands, Grenada consists of the island of Grenada itself, two smaller islands, Carriacou and Petite Martinique, and several small islands. It is located northwest of Trinidad and Tobago, northeast of Venezuela, and southwest of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines. Its size is 344 square kilometres (133 sq mi), and it has an estimated population of about 117,000. Its capital is St. George's.

History

Before the arrival of Europeans, Grenada was inhabited by the indigenous Arawaks and later Caribs. Christopher Columbus sighted Grenada in 1498 during his third voyage to the Americas. The French founded a colony in 1649, and Grenada changed hands repeatedly between France and Britain during the 18th century before being ceded to Britain by the Treaty of Paris in 1763 and again definitively in 1783.

Grenada gained independence from the United Kingdom on 7 February 1974 under Prime Minister Eric Gairy. In 1979, Maurice Bishop's New Jewel Movement seized power in a coup and established the People's Revolutionary Government. In October 1983, Bishop was executed by rival factions, and the United States and Caribbean allies invaded Grenada (Operation Urgent Fury) to restore order. Democratic elections resumed thereafter.

Geography

Grenada is of volcanic origin, with rich soil and a mountainous interior rising to Mount St. Catherine (840 m). The island is known as the "Island of Spice" because of its production of nutmeg and mace, of which it is one of the world's largest exporters. Grand Anse Beach near St. George's is among the Caribbean's best-known beaches.

Politics and economy

Grenada is a parliamentary constitutional monarchy within the Commonwealth realm, with the British monarch as head of state, represented by a governor-general, and a prime minister as head of government. The economy depends on tourism, agriculture (especially spices and cocoa), and offshore services. St. George's University, a large medical school, is a significant employer and source of foreign students.

Culture

Grenadian culture blends African, French, and British influences. The annual Spicemas carnival, calypso and soca music, and the traditional Shakespeare Mas of Carriacou are notable. The national dish is "oil down", a one-pot stew of breadfruit, salted meat, and vegetables cooked in coconut milk.
