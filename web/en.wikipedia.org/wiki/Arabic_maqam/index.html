Arabic maqam

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Arabic maqam (Arabic: مقام, maqām, pl. مقامات, maqāmāt) is the system of melodic modes used in traditional Arabic music. A maqam is much more than a scale: it is a complete melodic vocabulary and framework that specifies a set of pitches, characteristic phrases and motifs, conventional melodic development, points of emphasis, and customary modulations to related maqamat. The Arabic maqam system is related to, but distinct from, the Turkish makam, the Persian dastgah, and the Byzantine echos, all of which descend from shared traditions of Near Eastern modal music.

Structure: ajnas

Maqamat are analysed as combinations of smaller melodic building blocks called ajnas (sing. jins), most commonly trichords, tetrachords, and pentachords. A maqam typically consists of a lower (primary) jins beginning on the tonic (qarar) and an upper jins beginning on the dominant (ghammaz). Important ajnas include Bayati, Hijaz, Rast, Saba, Kurd, Nahawand, and Ajam. For example, maqam Rast is built from a Rast tetrachord on the tonic plus a Rast pentachord or tetrachord on the fifth degree, while maqam Bayati combines a Bayati tetrachord with a Nahawand tetrachord above it.

The pitch material of maqam music is not confined to the twelve equal-tempered semitones. Arabic theory divides the octave into 24 quarter-tones, and many maqamat include "half-flat" or "half-sharp" notes — such as the E half-flat of maqam Rast and the B half-flat of maqam Bayati — that give the music its characteristic colour. In modern practice, instruments with fixed Western tuning (such as the accordion or piano) must be specially adapted, and actual intonation varies by region and tradition.

Melodic behaviour and performance

Each maqam has a conventional melodic course (sayr): characteristic starting notes, a way of exploring the low and high registers, typical cadential formulas (qafla), and an emotional character or ethos. Improvisation within a maqam — the vocal mawwal and layali and the instrumental taqsim — is the summit of the art, and modulation between maqamat is a principal means of musical interest in long compositions and improvisations. Maqam performance is traditionally heterophonic: instruments of the takht ensemble (oud, qanun, nay, violin, riq) realise the same melody with individual ornamentation.

Families and repertoire

Dozens of maqamat are in common use, traditionally grouped into families by their primary jins: the Rast, Bayati, Hijaz, Saba, Sikah, Nahawand, Ajam, and Kurd families, among others. Well-known maqamat include Rast (often considered the "master" maqam), Bayati, Hijaz, Saba (associated with sadness and longing), Nahawand (analogous in pitch to Western minor), Ajam (analogous to Western major), and Kurd. The system underlies genres from the classical wasla suite and muwashshah songs to the art songs of twentieth-century composers such as Sayed Darwish and Mohammed Abdel Wahab, and it is also used in religious contexts, including the melodic recitation of the Qur'an (tajwid) and Levantine Christian chant.
