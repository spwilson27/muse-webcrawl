Groupe ADP, formerly Aéroports de Paris, is a French international airport operator based in Paris. It owns and manages the three main airports serving the French capital — Paris Charles de Gaulle, Paris Orly, and Paris Le Bourget — along with a network of smaller general aviation airfields in the Île-de-France region.

History

The company was created in 1945 as a public establishment to rebuild and operate Paris's airports after the Second World War. It opened Orly's South terminal in 1961 and inaugurated Charles de Gaulle Airport in 1974. In 2005 it was converted into a société anonyme and partially privatised, with shares listed on Euronext Paris the following year; the French state retained a majority stake. In 2016, Aéroports de Paris adopted the Groupe ADP name for the holding company, with "Paris Aéroport" as the passenger-facing brand.

International activities

Through its ADP International subsidiary, the group holds stakes in and operates airports worldwide, including in Turkey (via TAV Airports, in which it holds a major stake), Jordan (Queen Alia International Airport in Amman), Chile, Croatia, Madagascar, and Saudi Arabia. Its engineering arm, ADP Ingénierie, designed airport terminals around the world.

Operations

Before the COVID-19 pandemic, the Paris airports handled over 100 million passengers a year, making Groupe ADP one of the world's largest airport operators by passenger traffic. The group earns revenue from aeronautical fees, retail and services, real estate, and its international holdings. In 2019 the French government proposed a full privatisation of the group; the plan was suspended during the pandemic and formally abandoned in 2023.
