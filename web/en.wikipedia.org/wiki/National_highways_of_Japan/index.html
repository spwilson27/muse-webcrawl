The national highways of Japan (日本の国道) are a network of major roads designated by the national government under the Road Act of 1952. Together with the separate expressway network, they form the backbone of intercity road transport in Japan.

Designation and numbering

Highways are designated "national routes" (国道, kokudō) and numbered from 1 upward; routes numbered up to 507 exist, though some numbers in the 500s were never assigned or were later merged. Low-numbered routes date from the earliest designations and include historic corridors such as Route 1, the modern successor of the Tōkaidō between Tokyo and Kyoto via Nagoya.

Administration

Important sections designated as "specified sections" are built and maintained directly by the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism, while other sections are administered by prefectures with national subsidies. Expressways, though nationally significant, are administered separately, largely by the NEXCO companies and other toll-road operators.

Signage

National routes are marked by an inverted-triangle (onigiri-shaped) blue sign bearing the route number. Famous routes include Route 58, the longest, linking Kagoshima with Naha across island chains, and Route 339's "Staircase National Highway" in Aomori Prefecture.
