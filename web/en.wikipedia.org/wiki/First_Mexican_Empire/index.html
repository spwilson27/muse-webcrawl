First Mexican Empire

The First Mexican Empire (Spanish: Primer Imperio mexicano) was a short-lived monarchy and the first independent post-colonial state in Mexico. During this period, Mexico was commonly known as Anáhuac. The Empire lasted from 1821 to 1823, proclaimed after the Treaty of Córdoba, and the territory corresponded to the former Viceroyalty of New Spain, extending at its greatest from the present-day southwestern United States through Central America to Costa Rica.

Independence and proclamation

The Mexican War of Independence (1810–1821) ended when the royalist general Agustín de Iturbide, having allied with the insurgent Vicente Guerrero under the Plan of Iguala (February 1821), led the Army of the Three Guarantees into Mexico City in September 1821. The Plan promised independence, Catholicism as the state religion, and equality ("union") of peninsulares and criollos, and envisaged a constitutional monarchy under a European prince. When Spain repudiated the Treaties of Córdoba and no Bourbon prince accepted the throne, Iturbide's supporters proclaimed him Emperor Agustín I in May 1822; he was crowned on 21 July 1822.

Reign and fall

The Empire was beset by an empty treasury, a restive Congress, and regional resistance. Agustín dissolved Congress in October 1822 and ruled through a hand-picked junta, alienating both republicans and former royalists. In December 1822 Antonio López de Santa Anna rose at Veracruz, and in February 1823 the Plan of Casa Mata demanded a new Congress, drawing support from provincial deputations and generals including Guerrero and Nicolás Bravo. Agustín reconvened Congress but abdicated on 19 March 1823 and went into exile in Europe. The empire was replaced first by a provisional government (the Supreme Executive Power) and then, under the Constitution of 1824, by a federal republic. Iturbide returned to Mexico in 1824, unaware that Congress had declared him a traitor; he was executed at Padilla in July 1824. Central America, which had joined the Empire in 1822, seceded in 1823 to form the Federal Republic of Central America.

Legacy

The First Empire demonstrated the strength of monarchist sentiment in early Mexico and set up a recurring conservative–liberal conflict over the form of the state — a pattern that culminated in the Second Mexican Empire (1864–1867) under Maximilian I.
