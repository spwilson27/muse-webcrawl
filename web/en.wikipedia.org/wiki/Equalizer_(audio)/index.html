Equalizer (audio)

An equalizer or equaliser is a device or software used to adjust the balance between frequency components of an audio signal, a process called equalization (EQ). Originally developed for telephone systems — where "equalizers" compensated for losses in long lines — EQ became fundamental to recording, sound reinforcement and broadcasting.

Concepts

Equalizers divide the audio spectrum into frequency bands whose levels can be raised ("boosted") or lowered ("cut") independently. Filters are described by their centre frequency, bandwidth or Q (the sharpness of the peak), and gain. Common filter shapes include shelving filters, which boost or cut everything above or below a frequency, and peaking filters, which affect a band around a centre frequency.

Types

Graphic equalizers provide a bank of sliders, each controlling a fixed band (often in octave or third-octave spacing), whose positions give a visual "graph" of the response. Parametric equalizers allow continuous adjustment of frequency, Q and gain for a smaller number of bands, and are standard in mixing consoles and digital audio workstations. In consumer equipment, simple bass and treble tone controls are a basic form of EQ.

Applications

Equalization is used creatively in music production to shape timbre, correctively to reduce feedback and compensate for room acoustics and loudspeaker response, and in mastering to balance a mix. Historically, fixed EQ curves are built into audio formats and playback chains, such as the RIAA equalization curve used for vinyl records.
