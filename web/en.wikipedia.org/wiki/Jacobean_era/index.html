The Jacobean era was the period in English and Scottish history that coincides with the reign of James VI and I, who was King of Scotland as James VI from 1567 and King of England and Ireland as James I from 1603 until his death in 1625. The term derives from Jacobus, the Latin form of James. The era succeeded the Elizabethan era and preceded the Caroline era under Charles I.

Politics and religion

James's accession united the crowns of England and Scotland in one person, though the kingdoms remained legally distinct. His reign saw the Gunpowder Plot of 1605, an attempt by Catholic conspirators including Guy Fawkes to blow up Parliament; the Hampton Court Conference of 1604, which led to the commissioning of the King James Version of the Bible (published 1611); and growing friction between the Crown and Parliament over finance and the divine right of kings, arguments James advanced in works such as The True Law of Free Monarchies.

Colonization

The era saw the establishment of England's first permanent colony in North America at Jamestown, Virginia (1607), the voyage of the Mayflower and the founding of Plymouth Colony (1620), and the expansion of the East India Company's trade.

Literature and theatre

Jacobean literature includes the later works of William Shakespeare — including King Lear, Macbeth, Antony and Cleopatra, and The Tempest — alongside plays by Ben Jonson, John Webster, Thomas Middleton, and Francis Beaumont and John Fletcher. Jacobean drama is often noted for its darker, more cynical tone. In architecture, the period introduced the work of Inigo Jones, whose Banqueting House in Whitehall (1619–1622) brought Palladian classicism to England.
