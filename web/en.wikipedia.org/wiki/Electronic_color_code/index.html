Electronic color code

The electronic color code or electronic colour code (see spelling differences) is used to indicate the values or ratings of electronic components, usually for resistors, but also for capacitors, inductors, diodes and others. Coloured bands are printed on the component body because the components are too small for legible numerals, and the colour bands remain readable from any angle.

Resistor colour code

For axial-lead resistors, three or four bands give the resistance: the first two (or three) bands are significant figures, the next is a decimal multiplier, and an optional final band—separated by a gap—gives the tolerance. The colours correspond to digits in the order of the rainbow, a scheme developed in the 1920s by the Radio Manufacturers Association and now standardized as IEC 60062.

Gold and silver bands serve as multipliers of ×0.1 and ×0.01, and as tolerance markers of ±5% and ±10% respectively; a missing tolerance band implies ±20%. Mnemonics such as "Black Boys Race Our Young Girls But Violet Generally Wins" are used to memorise the sequence.

Other uses

Colour codes are also used for capacitor values and voltage ratings, inductor values, diode part numbers, and the insulation of wires and cables, where paired colours identify individual conductors in multi-core cables.
