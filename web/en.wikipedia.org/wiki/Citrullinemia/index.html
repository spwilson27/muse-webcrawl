Citrullinemia

Citrullinemia is an autosomal recessive urea cycle disorder that causes ammonia and other toxic substances to accumulate in the blood. The condition results from a deficiency of an enzyme of the urea cycle, leading to markedly raised levels of the amino acid citrulline in blood and cerebrospinal fluid.

SpecialtyMedical genetics, metabolic medicine
TypesType I (ASS1 deficiency); Type II (citrin deficiency)
CauseMutations in ASS1 or SLC25A13
InheritanceAutosomal recessive

Types

Type I citrullinemia is caused by mutations in the ASS1 gene encoding argininosuccinate synthetase. It usually presents in the first days of life with poor feeding, lethargy, vomiting, and progressive encephalopathy from hyperammonemia, though milder later-onset forms occur. Type II citrullinemia is caused by deficiency of citrin, a mitochondrial transporter encoded by SLC25A13, and typically presents in adulthood with episodes of hyperammonemia and neuropsychiatric symptoms; it is more common in East Asian populations.

Treatment

Management involves dietary protein restriction, ammonia-scavenging medications such as sodium benzoate and sodium phenylbutyrate or glycerol phenylbutyrate, supplementation with arginine, and emergency haemodialysis for severe hyperammonemic crises. Liver transplantation can correct the metabolic defect in type I. Newborn screening detects classic type I disease in many jurisdictions.
