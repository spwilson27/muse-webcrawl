Marimba

ClassificationPercussion, idiophone
RangeTypically 4.3 to 5 octaves
RelatedXylophone, vibraphone, glockenspiel

The marimba is a musical instrument in the percussion family consisting of graduated wooden bars — traditionally rosewood — arranged like a piano keyboard and struck with mallets. Beneath each bar hangs a resonator tube that amplifies the tone, giving the marimba its warm, mellow sound, deeper than that of the xylophone.

History

Instruments with wooden bars over gourd resonators have deep roots in Africa and were carried across the Atlantic, developing distinctive traditions in Central and South America; the marimba is the national instrument of Guatemala. The modern concert marimba was standardised in the early 20th century by manufacturers such as Deagan and Musser, and composers including Darius Milhaud and later Keiko Abe helped establish a solo repertoire. Abe's compositions and four-mallet technique transformed the instrument's virtuoso potential.

Technique and repertoire

Players use two or four mallets (sometimes six), held with grips such as the Burton or traditional cross grip, allowing chords and independent melodic lines. The marimba is a standard member of percussion ensembles and is used in orchestral, chamber, jazz, and popular music; marimba bands are also a fixture of schools in southern Africa.
