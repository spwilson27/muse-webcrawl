Certosa di Pavia

The Certosa di Pavia is a monastery complex in Lombardy, northern Italy, situated about 8 kilometres north of the city of Pavia. Built between the late 14th and 16th centuries for the Carthusian order, it is one of the most celebrated monuments of the Italian Renaissance, renowned for the rich marble decoration of its church facade and for the dynastic tomb monuments it was designed to house.

History

The charterhouse was founded by Gian Galeazzo Visconti, Duke of Milan, who laid the first stone on 27 August 1396, intending the monastery to serve as the mausoleum of the Visconti dynasty. Construction proceeded slowly over more than a century, interrupted by wars and plagues, and continued under the Sforza dukes. The church was consecrated in 1497, and Ludovico Sforza commissioned the tomb of himself and his wife Beatrice d'Este from Cristoforo Solari. Suppressed under Joseph II in 1782, the monastery passed through several religious orders before returning to Carthusian monks, and later Cistercians; today it is partly a national monument open to visitors.

Architecture and art

The church, laid out on a Latin cross plan with a nave and side chapels, combines late Gothic structure with Renaissance ornament. Its facade, begun in 1491 to designs associated with Giovanni Antonio Amadeo, is a dense screen of coloured marbles, reliefs, statues, and medallions depicting saints, prophets, and Visconti and Sforza rulers. Inside are frescoes by Bergognone and others, an ornate choir with inlaid wooden stalls, and the founder's tomb of Gian Galeazzo Visconti. The complex also includes the Small Cloister, the Great Cloister lined with the monks' cells, the chapter house, and the Palazzo Ducale, which houses a museum with plaster casts and works associated with the monastery.
