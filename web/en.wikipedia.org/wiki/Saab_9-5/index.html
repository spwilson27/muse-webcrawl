The Saab 9-5 is an executive car produced by the Swedish automobile manufacturer Saab Automobile. Introduced in 1997 as the successor to the Saab 9000, it was built in Trollhättan, Sweden, and sold as a four-door sedan and a five-door station wagon (SportCombi/SportWagon).

A first-generation Saab 9-5 sedan.

First generation (1997–2010)

The original 9-5 was based on the General Motors GM2900 platform, shared with the Saab 9-3 and Opel/Vauxhall Vectra, but was significantly re-engineered by Saab. Power came from turbocharged four-cylinder petrol engines, a turbocharged V6, and later diesel options. The high-performance Aero model produced up to 260 PS from a 2.3-litre turbo engine. The car was noted for safety, including Saab's active head restraints (SAHR), and for comfort over long distances. A facelift in 2006 gave the car revised styling sometimes dubbed the "Dame Edna" model for its chrome headlamp surrounds.

Second generation (2010–2012)

A completely redesigned 9-5, based on GM's Epsilon II platform and previewed by a concept at the 2009 Frankfurt Motor Show, entered production in 2010. It was larger and more luxurious, intended to compete with the BMW 5 Series and Mercedes-Benz E-Class. Production was cut short by Saab's bankruptcy in December 2011; only around 11,000 second-generation cars were built, including a small number assembled under the company's subsequent owners.
