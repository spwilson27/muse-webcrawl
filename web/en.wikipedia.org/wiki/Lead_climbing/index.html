Lead climbing

Lead climbing is a style of climbing in which the climber ascends with the rope passing below them, clipping it into successive points of protection as they go, while a belayer manages the rope from below. Unlike top-rope climbing, a fall in lead climbing can be considerably longer, since the climber falls past their last clipped protection point.

Technique

In sport climbing, protection consists of pre-placed bolts; the climber clips the rope into quickdraws attached to each bolt. In traditional ("trad") climbing, the leader places removable protection — nuts, hexes, and camming devices — into cracks, and the second removes it while following. Good rope management, including avoiding clipping errors such as back-clipping and "Z-clipping," is essential to safety.

Competition

Lead climbing is one of the disciplines of competition climbing and featured in sport climbing's Olympic debut at the Tokyo 2020 Games, where athletes attempt to climb as high as possible on a tall, overhanging wall within a time limit.
