Tk'emlúps te Secwépemc (formerly the Kamloops Indian Band) is a First Nations government of the Secwépemc (Shuswap) people, located in the Thompson River valley near the city of Kamloops in British Columbia, Canada. Its name means "where the rivers meet", referring to the confluence of the North and South Thompson Rivers.

Community

The band administers one of the larger First Nations reserves in British Columbia and has a registered population of over a thousand members, many living in Kamloops. It is a member of the Shuswap Nation Tribal Council and operates businesses, a school, and cultural facilities including the Secwépemc Museum and Heritage Park.

Kamloops Indian Residential School

The community was the site of the Kamloops Indian Residential School, which operated from 1890 to 1978. In May 2021 the band announced that ground-penetrating radar had indicated some 200 potential unmarked burials at the site, an announcement that drew worldwide attention and prompted national reckoning with Canada's residential school system.
