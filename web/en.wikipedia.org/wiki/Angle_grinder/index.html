Angle grinder

Angle grinder
Type: Power tool
Power source: Electric, pneumatic, petrol, battery
Common disc sizes: 115 mm, 125 mm, 230 mm
Uses: Cutting, grinding, polishing

An angle grinder, also known as a side grinder or disc grinder, is a handheld power tool used for grinding (abrasive cutting) and polishing. Although developed originally as tools for rigid abrasive discs, the availability of an interchangeable power source has encouraged their use with a wide variety of cutters and attachments.

Design

The tool is driven by an electric motor, petrol engine, or compressed air. The motor drives a geared head at a right angle, on which an abrasive disc or a thinner cutting disc can be mounted. Discs are typically available in sizes from 100 mm (4 in) up to 230 mm (9 in), with the most common general-purpose sizes being 115 mm and 125 mm. Larger grinders, sometimes called large angle grinders, are used with 180 mm and 230 mm discs.

Uses

Depending on the disc fitted, an angle grinder can cut metal bar, tile, stone and concrete, remove rust and paint, sharpen blades, and grind down welds. Wire brush wheels, sanding discs and diamond blades extend its range further. Diamond cutting discs are widely used in construction for cutting masonry.

Safety

Angle grinders are among the more hazardous workshop tools: the disc rotates at several thousand revolutions per minute, and a shattered disc can eject fragments at high speed. Guards, correct disc selection for the material and speed rating, and eye and face protection are considered essential, and the tool should never be used with the guard removed.
