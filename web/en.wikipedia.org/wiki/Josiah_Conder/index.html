Josiah Conder

Josiah Conder (28 September 1852 - 21 June 1920) was a British architect who worked as a foreign adviser (oyatoi gaikokujin) in Meiji-era Japan. Often called the "father of Japanese modern architecture," he designed many of Tokyo's first Western-style brick buildings and trained the first generation of Japanese architects.

Career in Japan

Conder arrived in Japan in 1877, aged 24, to teach architecture at the Imperial College of Engineering in Tokyo. His students included Tatsuno Kingo, who designed Tokyo Station, and other pioneers of Japanese architecture. Conder's own commissions included the Rokumeikan (Deer Cry Pavilion, 1883), the famous venue for the Meiji government's Westernizing social events, the Mitsubishi offices in Marunouchi, the Iwasaki residence, and the Russian Orthodox St. Nicholas Cathedral (Nikolai-do).

Later life

After leaving government service, Conder went into private practice in partnership with Japanese colleagues and remained in Japan for the rest of his life. He married a Japanese woman, Maida Kumeko, studied Japanese painting under Kawanabe Kyosai, and exhibited under the name Kyosai's pupil. He was decorated by the Japanese government with the Order of the Sacred Treasure. Conder died in Tokyo in 1920 and is buried at Gokoku-ji temple.
