Swiss National Day

Swiss National Day (German: Schweizer Bundesfeiertag; French: Fête nationale suisse) is the national day of Switzerland, celebrated annually on 1 August. It commemorates the Federal Charter of 1291, in which the cantons of Uri, Schwyz and Unterwalden pledged mutual defence, regarded as the founding act of the Swiss Confederation.

History

The date was first marked in 1891 for the 600th anniversary of the charter, and annual celebrations began in 1899. Although long observed with speeches, bonfires and fireworks, it became an official nationwide public holiday only in 1994, following a popular referendum.

Celebrations

Traditions include bonfires on hilltops, lantern parades for children, fireworks, communal brunches on farms, and an address by the President of the Swiss Confederation, traditionally broadcast from the Rütli meadow, legendary site of the founding oath.
