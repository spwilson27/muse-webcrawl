Feedlot

A feedlot or feed yard is a type of animal feeding operation (AFO) used in intensive animal farming, particularly for beef cattle, prior to slaughter. Large beef feedlots in the United States are called concentrated animal feeding operations (CAFOs). Cattle are confined in pens and fed a ration of grain, silage, hay, and protein supplements designed to promote rapid weight gain so the animals reach market weight at a younger age than pasture-raised cattle.

Operation

Feeder cattle, typically weaned calves or yearlings raised on pasture or in backgrounding operations, arrive at the feedlot weighing roughly 300–400 kg and spend about 90–300 days on feed. Diets are mostly energy-dense grains such as corn, often processed by steam-flaking, supplemented with roughage, minerals, and sometimes growth-promoting implants or feed additives such as ionophores. Feed is delivered to long concrete bunks by truck, and water is provided in troughs. When cattle reach roughly 500–650 kg, they are shipped to a packing plant for slaughter.

Industries and regions

Feedlots are concentrated in the Great Plains and Corn Belt of the United States—including Texas, Nebraska, Kansas, Colorado, and Iowa—as well as in Canada, Australia, Brazil, and other beef-producing countries. The largest operations can hold more than 100,000 head at one time.

Issues

Feedlots raise environmental and public-health concerns: management of large volumes of manure and runoff can affect water quality; concentrated herds generate odor, dust, and greenhouse gas emissions; and the routine use of antibiotics in livestock is scrutinized for its contribution to antimicrobial resistance. Animal welfare advocates also criticize confinement conditions. In the United States, large CAFOs are regulated under the Clean Water Act's permitting system.

Alternatives

Grass-fed and pasture-finishing systems finish cattle on forage rather than grain, typically producing leaner beef more slowly and at higher cost.
