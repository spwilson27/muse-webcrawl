Driver's license

A driver's license (American English) or driving licence (British English) is an official document permitting a specific individual to operate one or more types of motorized vehicles — such as motorcycles, cars, trucks or buses — on a public road. In most jurisdictions it is issued after the applicant passes written and practical driving tests and meets age, residency and medical requirements.

History

Licensing emerged with the first automobiles. The United Kingdom's Motor Car Act 1903 introduced a registration-based driving licence, while compulsory driving tests followed in the 1930s. France and Prussia were among the first jurisdictions to require driver permits in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

Categories and identification

Licences are typically divided into categories by vehicle class and weight, with separate endorsements for motorcycles, commercial vehicles and hazardous materials. In many countries, including the United States, the driver's license also serves as the most common general-purpose identity document.

International use

Conventions on road traffic allow many licences to be used abroad, sometimes together with an International Driving Permit, a standardized translation of the national licence.
