Vernalisation

Vernalisation (from Latin vernus, "of the spring") is the induction of a plant's flowering process by exposure to the prolonged cold of winter, or by an artificial equivalent. After vernalisation, plants have acquired the ability to flower, though they may still require additional seasonal cues such as lengthening days (photoperiodism) before they actually do so.

Mechanism

Many biennials and winter annuals — including winter wheat, barley, cabbage and the model plant Arabidopsis thaliana — will not flower, or flower poorly, without a cold period. In Arabidopsis, cold progressively silences the floral repressor gene FLC through epigenetic modification, a state that is remembered through mitosis for the rest of the season but reset in the next generation. The Soviet agronomist Trofim Lysenko promoted vernalisation ("jarovisation") of seed grain in the 1930s, and his broader rejection of genetics — Lysenkoism — did lasting damage to Soviet biology.
