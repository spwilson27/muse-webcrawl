Revenue recognition

Revenue recognition is the accounting principle that determines when income from a transaction is recorded in financial statements. Rather than treating every receipt of cash as revenue, modern standards generally recognize revenue when an entity satisfies a performance obligation by transferring a promised good or service to a customer.

Key idea
Revenue is recognized as control of goods or services passes to the customer, in an amount reflecting the consideration expected in exchange.

Principles

The central question is when the customer obtains control. Control may transfer at a point in time, as when a product is delivered, or over time, as with many construction, subscription and professional-service contracts. Cash collection can occur before, during or after recognition, producing contract assets, receivables or contract liabilities.

Five-step model

International Financial Reporting Standard 15 and the corresponding United States standard use a five-step model: identify the contract; identify the performance obligations; determine the transaction price; allocate that price to the obligations; and recognize revenue when or as each obligation is satisfied. Variable consideration, significant financing components, noncash consideration and contract modifications require additional judgment.

Historical development

Older guidance often emphasized when revenue was earned and realized or realizable. Industry-specific rules accumulated over time and sometimes produced inconsistent results for similar arrangements. The converged standards issued in 2014 sought a common framework across industries and jurisdictions, although implementation—especially for software, licensing and long-term contracts—remains complex.

Examples

A retailer normally recognizes revenue when a shopper takes possession of merchandise. A software company selling a one-year hosted subscription generally recognizes the fee ratably over the subscription term. A builder using an over-time method measures progress by costs incurred, work performed or another faithful depiction of transfer.
