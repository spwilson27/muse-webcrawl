Caber toss

[Image placeholder: A competitor at a Highland games in the moment of launching the caber]

The caber toss is a traditional Scottish athletic event in which competitors toss a large tapered wooden pole called a "caber" (/ˈkeɪbər/). It is normally practised at the Scottish Highland Games. In Scotland, the caber is usually made from a larch tree and is typically 19 feet 6 inches (5.94 m) long and weighs about 175 pounds (79 kg).

The toss

The object is not to throw the caber for distance, but to turn it end over end so that it lands in a prescribed direction. The tosser cups the tapered end of the caber in interlocked hands, rests it against the shoulder, lifts it upright, and runs forward building momentum before planting his feet and heaving the pole upward. A perfect toss sees the caber fall directly away from the thrower, in the "twelve o'clock" position; judges score lesser turns by their clock-face angle, and a caber that fails to turn is scored by the maximum angle it reached before falling back.

History

The event's origins are uncertain; it is said to derive from Highland drovers and lumberjacks pitching logs across streams, or from the practical throwing of logs into place for building. Caber tossing has been a fixture of Highland games since the 19th century and has spread with the games worldwide to North America, Australia and elsewhere. Famous cabers include the Braemar caber, which is notoriously difficult to turn.
