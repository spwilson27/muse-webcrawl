The Texas A&M University System is a state public university system in Texas, headquartered in College Station. It is one of the largest systems of higher education in the United States, with a statewide network of universities, a comprehensive health science center, and state agencies in agriculture, engineering, transportation, and emergency services.

History

The system's flagship institution, Texas A&M University, was founded in 1876 as the Agricultural and Mechanical College of Texas under the Morrill Land-Grant Acts. The system was formally organized in 1948 as branch institutions and agencies grew. Members include Texas A&M University at College Station, Prairie View A&M University, Tarleton State University, Texas A&M International University, Texas A&M University–Corpus Christi, Texas A&M University–Kingsville, West Texas A&M University, and others.

Agencies

The system administers eight state agencies, including the Texas A&M AgriLife Extension Service, Texas A&M Forest Service, Texas A&M Transportation Institute, and the Texas Division of Emergency Management (housed within the system until 2019). The Board of Regents governs the system, and the chancellor serves as chief executive officer.
