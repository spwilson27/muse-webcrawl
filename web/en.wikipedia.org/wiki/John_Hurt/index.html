John Hurt

Sir John Vincent Hurt (22 January 1940 – 25 January 2017) was an English actor whose career spanned over five decades. Regarded as one of Britain's finest actors, he had roles in films including A Man for All Seasons (1966), Midnight Express (1978), Alien (1979), The Elephant Man (1980), and Nineteen Eighty-Four (1984).

Early life

Hurt was born in Chesterfield, Derbyshire, the son of a vicar and an engineer-actress. He trained at the Royal Academy of Dramatic Art (RADA) in London and began his career on stage and in television in the early 1960s.

Career

Hurt gained acclaim for his portrayal of Quentin Crisp in the television play The Naked Civil Servant (1975) and of Caligula in I, Claudius (1976). His film breakthrough came with Midnight Express, which won him a Golden Globe and a BAFTA Award and an Academy Award nomination. His death scene in Alien — the "chestburster" sequence — is one of the most famous in cinema history. He received a second Oscar nomination for his portrayal of John Merrick in The Elephant Man.

Later work

Later generations knew Hurt as Mr Ollivander in the Harry Potter films and as the War Doctor in Doctor Who. He was knighted in 2015 and died of pancreatic cancer in 2017.
