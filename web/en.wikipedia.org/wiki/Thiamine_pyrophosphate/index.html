Thiamine pyrophosphate

[Hero image placeholder: chemical structure diagram of thiamine pyrophosphate.]

Thiamine pyrophosphate (TPP or thiamine diphosphate, TDP) is a derivative of thiamine (vitamin B1) and the active coenzyme form of the vitamin. It is produced by the enzyme thiamine diphosphokinase and plays a central role in carbohydrate and amino-acid metabolism in all living organisms.

Function

TPP is the cofactor for several key enzymes, including the pyruvate dehydrogenase complex (linking glycolysis to the citric acid cycle), the α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase complex, branched-chain α-keto acid dehydrogenase, and transketolase in the pentose phosphate pathway. Its chemistry centres on the thiazolium ring: deprotonation at the C2 carbon forms a nucleophilic ylide/carbene that attacks carbonyl groups, stabilising the "active aldehyde" intermediates of decarboxylation and two-carbon transfer reactions.

Clinical relevance

Because TPP-dependent enzymes are essential, thiamine deficiency — beriberi and Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome — impairs energy metabolism, particularly in the nervous system.
