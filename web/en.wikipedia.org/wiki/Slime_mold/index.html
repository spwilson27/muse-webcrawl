Slime mold

Slime mold or slime mould is an informal name given to a several kinds of unrelated eukaryotic organisms with a life cycle that includes a free-living single-celled stage and the aggregation of many such cells into a structure visible to the naked eye. Slime molds were formerly classified as fungi but are no longer considered part of that kingdom. Although not forming a single monophyletic clade, they are grouped within the paraphyletic group Protista.

More than 900 species of slime mold occur worldwide. Their common name refers to a part of some of these organisms' life cycles where they can appear as gelatinous "slime". This is mostly seen with the Myxogastria, the only macroscopic slime molds.

Taxonomy

Slime molds are divided into several groups. The most prominent are:

  • Myxogastria (myxomycetes) — plasmodial or true slime molds, which form a multinucleate mass of protoplasm called a plasmodium.
  • Dictyostelia — cellular slime molds, whose individual amoebae aggregate into a multicellular slug when food is scarce.
  • Protostelia — mostly microscopic forms that produce small fruiting bodies from individual cells.
  • Acrasidae — cellular slime molds in the group Heterolobosea.
  • Plasmodiophorids — obligate parasites of plants, including the cause of clubroot disease.
  • Labyrinthulomycetes — the "slime nets", mostly marine organisms.

Life cycle

Slime molds begin life as amoeba-like cells. These unicellular amoebae are commonly haploid and feed on bacteria. When food is scarce, the amoebae may aggregate. In the plasmodial slime molds, many nuclei-containing cells fuse together to create a large multinucleate organism called a plasmodium.

When conditions become unfavourable, the plasmodium develops into fruiting bodies (sporangia) that produce spores. The spores germinate under favourable conditions to release new amoebae, completing the cycle.

Plasmodium

The plasmodium is typically diploid and is a brightly coloured, slimy mass often visible to the naked eye. It creeps slowly across its substrate, engulfing bacteria, yeasts and decaying organic material by phagocytosis. Species such as Physarum polycephalum have been used as model organisms in studies of cell motility, chemotaxis and even simple forms of learning and problem-solving.

Ecology

Slime molds are found on decaying wood, leaf litter and soil in moist environments worldwide. They play an important role in the decomposition of dead plant material and in recycling nutrients. Some species are eaten by beetles, slugs and other small animals; a few, such as the "scrambled egg slime" Fuligo septica, are consumed by humans in parts of Mexico.
