Ring Nebula

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Ring Nebula (also catalogued as Messier 57, M57 and NGC 6720) is a planetary nebula in the northern constellation of Lyra. Such a nebula is formed when a star, during the last stages of its evolution before becoming a white dwarf, expels a vast glowing mass of ionized gas into the surrounding interstellar space.

Observational history

The nebula was discovered by the French astronomer Antoine Darquier de Pellepoix in January 1779, days before Charles Messier found it independently and catalogued it. William Herschel compared its appearance to a ring; William Huggins's spectroscopy in 1864 showed its gaseous nature.

Structure

The familiar ring is the bright inner rim of a roughly barrel- or torus-shaped shell seen nearly end-on; Hubble images reveal radial "spokes", petal-like outer loops and knots of dark material in the main ring. The central white dwarf, with a temperature of about 120,000 K, ionizes the expelled gas: doubly ionized oxygen gives the inner region its green tint, while hydrogen in the outer parts glows red. The nebula is expanding at roughly 20–30 km/s and is estimated to have been expanding for several thousand years — the dying phase of a star once similar to the Sun.

Observation

M57 lies between the stars Gamma and Beta Lyrae and is a popular target for amateur telescopes, appearing as a small smoky ring at moderate magnification, about one arcminute across.
