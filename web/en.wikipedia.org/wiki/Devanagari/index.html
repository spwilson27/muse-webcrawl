Devanagari (देवनागरी) is an Indic abugida script used to write over 120 languages, including Hindi, Sanskrit, Marathi, Nepali, Konkani, Sindhi, and Maithili. It is written from left to right and is characterised by the strong horizontal line, the shirorekha, that runs along the top of its characters. Devanagari is one of the most widely adopted writing systems in the world.

History

Devanagari descends from the Brahmi script through the Gupta script and the Nagari script, which emerged around the 7th century CE. By the 11th century it had developed into its modern form. The name derives from Sanskrit deva ("deity") and nagari ("of the city").

Structure

Devanagari is an abugida: each consonant letter carries an inherent vowel (usually /ə/) which is modified by dependent vowel signs (matras) or suppressed by a virama. The core inventory comprises 11 vowels and 33 consonants in the traditional ordering, which follows a phonetic arrangement by place and manner of articulation. Consonant clusters are commonly written as ligatures called conjuncts.

Numerals

Devanagari has its own set of decimal digits (०१२३४५६७८९), though Western Arabic numerals are also widely used.

Usage

Devanagari is the official script of Hindi, the most widely spoken language of India, and of Nepali in Nepal. It is also the principal script for Sanskrit, the liturgical language of Hinduism, and appears on Indian currency and government publications.
