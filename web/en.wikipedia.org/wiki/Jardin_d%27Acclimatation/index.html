Jardin d'Acclimatation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Jardin d'Acclimatation in the Bois de Boulogne, Paris

The Jardin d'Acclimatation is a 19-hectare children's amusement park and leisure garden in the Bois de Boulogne in Paris, France. It was opened on 6 October 1860 by Napoleon III and Empress Eugénie as a zoological garden operated by the Société zoologique d'acclimatation, intended to acclimatise exotic animals and plants to the French climate.

History

During the Siege of Paris (1870–71) many of the zoo's animals were eaten, and in the following decades the park became notorious for its "human zoo" exhibitions of foreign peoples, displayed until the early twentieth century. The collection of animals dwindled, and the park evolved into a family leisure park with rides, gardens, a miniature train and puppet theatres.

Modern era

After a major renovation in 2017–2018 by its operator (a partnership between the City of Paris and LVMH), the park reopened with a steampunk-themed redesign of its attractions, while retaining its heritage features such as the narrow-gauge train, the Guignol puppet theatre and the Korean garden.
