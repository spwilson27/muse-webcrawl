Maria Longworth Nichols Storer (1849–1932) was an American philanthropist and ceramic artist who founded Rookwood Pottery in Cincinnati, Ohio, in 1880 — the first large American art pottery founded by a woman.

Rookwood Pottery

Heir to the Longworth family fortune (her grandfather Nicholas Longworth was a pioneering wine-maker), Storer began painting china in the 1870s. Rookwood, named for her father's estate, won international acclaim for its Standard Glaze and later matte and vellum wares, becoming a leader of the American Arts and Crafts movement.

Later life

After the death of her first husband, George Nichols, she married Bellamy Storer, a congressman and diplomat. She sold her interest in Rookwood in 1890. A convert to Catholicism, she spent her later years largely in France and died in Paris in 1932.
