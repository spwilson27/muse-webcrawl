Duane–Hunt law

The Duane–Hunt law, named after the American physicists William Duane and Franklin Hunt, gives the maximum frequency (or minimum wavelength) of X-rays produced by bremsstrahlung in an X-ray tube as a function of the applied voltage. It states that the cut-off wavelength is inversely proportional to the accelerating voltage: λmin = hc / eV, where h is Planck's constant, c the speed of light, e the electron charge and V the tube voltage.

Explanation

An electron accelerated through voltage V gains kinetic energy eV. If it loses all of that energy in a single collision with a target atom, the emitted photon takes the maximum possible energy, producing the short-wavelength limit of the continuous X-ray spectrum. The law thus provided an early, precise way to measure Planck's constant and confirmed the quantum relation E = hν.

History

Duane and Hunt published the relation in 1915, following their X-ray spectroscopy experiments; the agreement between the measured cut-off and the predicted value was an important early verification of quantum theory.
