Wilhelm Maybach

Born: February 9, 1846, Heilbronn

Died: December 29, 1929, Stuttgart

Occupation: Engine designer, industrialist

Wilhelm Maybach (February 9, 1846 – December 29, 1929) was an early German engine designer and industrialist. During the 1890s he was hailed in France, then the world centre for car production, as the "King of Designers". He is regarded as one of the founding fathers of the modern automobile.

Orphaned at age ten, Maybach was educated at the Bruderhaus in Reutlingen, where he met Gottlieb Daimler. He followed Daimler through successive firms and, as his chief engineer, developed the high-speed internal combustion engine. Together they built the 1885 Reitwagen, often considered the first motorcycle, and the 1889 steel-wheeled car.

Career

As technical director of the Daimler Motoren Gesellschaft (DMG), Maybach designed the Phoenix engine and, in 1901, the Mercedes 35 hp — commissioned by Emil Jellinek and widely regarded as the first modern automobile, with its low centre of gravity, pressed-steel frame, and honeycomb radiator, a Maybach invention. After disagreements with DMG management, he left in 1907.

In 1909, with his son Karl, he founded Maybach Motorenbau, which built engines for Zeppelin airships and later luxury automobiles famed for their engineering. The Maybach name survives today as Mercedes-Maybach, the ultra-luxury sub-brand of Mercedes-Benz.
