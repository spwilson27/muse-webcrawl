Rialto Bridge

Rialto Bridge
Italian: Ponte di Rialto
Location: Venice, Italy
Crosses: Grand Canal
Designer: Antonio da Ponte
Completed: 1591

The Rialto Bridge (Italian: Ponte di Rialto) is the oldest of the four bridges spanning the Grand Canal in Venice, Italy. Connecting the sestieri of San Marco and San Polo, it has been rebuilt several times since its first construction as a pontoon bridge in 1173, and is now one of the most famous landmarks of the city.

History

The first bridge at the site was a floating pontoon bridge built in 1173. It was replaced by a wooden structure in 1255, which burned during a revolt in 1310 and collapsed under crowds in 1444 and again in 1524. Proposals for a stone bridge were debated for decades — designs were solicited from architects including Michelangelo, Palladio and Vignola — before Antonio da Ponte's single-span stone design was chosen in 1588. The bridge was completed in 1591.

Design

The bridge is a single stone arch with a span of about 28.8 metres. Two inclined ramps lead to a central portico, and the span carries two rows of shops under arcades, whose rents helped finance the bridge's maintenance. Critics at the time feared the daring single arch would collapse, but it has survived for more than four centuries.

Today

The Rialto Bridge stands at the commercial heart of Venice, beside the Rialto markets that have operated for centuries. It is among the city's most visited sights and a classic viewpoint over the Grand Canal.
