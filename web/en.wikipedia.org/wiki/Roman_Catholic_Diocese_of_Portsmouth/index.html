Roman Catholic Diocese of Portsmouth


Cathedral: St John's Cathedral, Portsmouth
Established: 19 May 1882
Province: Southwark
Territory: Hampshire, Berkshire, Isle of Wight, Channel Islands, and parts of Dorset and Oxfordshire

The Diocese of Portsmouth (Latin: Dioecesis Portus Magni) is a Latin Church diocese of the Catholic Church in England. Erected on 19 May 1882 by Pope Leo XIII, it was formed from the western part of the Diocese of Southwark. Its cathedral is the Cathedral Church of St John the Evangelist in Portsmouth, and it forms part of the ecclesiastical province of Southwark.

Territory

The diocese covers Hampshire, the Isle of Wight, Berkshire, the Channel Islands (Jersey and Guernsey), and parts of Dorset and Oxfordshire — an area of roughly 6,300 square kilometres along England's south coast and inland. Major centres include Portsmouth, Southampton, Bournemouth, Reading, Basingstoke, Winchester, and Oxford's southern parishes. The diocese is organised into deaneries and pastoral areas and contains over 100 parishes.

History

Catholic life in the region revived after emancipation in the nineteenth century; Portsmouth's position as a naval port brought a large Catholic population of sailors and Irish workers. The first bishop was John Virtue (1882–1900), who built the cathedral, consecrated in 1882. Successors included John Cahill, William Cotter, Derek Worlock — later Archbishop of Liverpool — and Crispian Hollis, who served from 1988 to 2012. Philip Egan became the eighth Bishop of Portsmouth in 2012.
