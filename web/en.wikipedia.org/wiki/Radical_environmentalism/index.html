Radical environmentalism

Radical environmentalism is a grass-roots branch of the environmental movement that emerged from an ecocentrism-based frustration with the co-option of mainstream environmentalism. It considers the current political and economic system — industrial civilization — to be the root of environmental degradation, and advocates fundamental, systemic change, often including direct action.

Philosophy

Radical environmentalism is closely associated with deep ecology, the philosophy articulated by Arne Næss, which holds that all living beings have intrinsic value independent of their usefulness to humans. Other strands include social ecology, ecofeminism, bioregionalism, and green anarchism or primitivism.

History and tactics

The movement gained visibility in the 1980s with the founding of Earth First! in the American Southwest, which used civil disobedience and "monkeywrenching" — sabotage of environmentally destructive equipment, a tactic popularized by Edward Abbey's novel The Monkey Wrench Gang. The decentralized Earth Liberation Front and Animal Liberation Front later carried out arson and property destruction campaigns, prompting law-enforcement labels such as "eco-terrorism," a term activists dispute since their actions target property rather than people.

Criticism

Critics within and outside the environmental movement argue that radical tactics alienate the public and invite repression, while proponents counter that legal, mainstream strategies have failed to halt ecological decline.
