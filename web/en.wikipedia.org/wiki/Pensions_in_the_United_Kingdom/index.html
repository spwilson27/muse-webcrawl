Pensions in the United Kingdom

Pensions in the United Kingdom are provided through a combination of the state pension, occupational pension schemes, and personal pensions. The system is regulated principally by The Pensions Regulator and the Financial Conduct Authority, with the Department for Work and Pensions responsible for policy.

State pension

The modern state pension began with the Old Age Pensions Act 1908, which introduced a means-tested pension for people over 70, and was put on a contributory social-insurance footing by the National Insurance Act 1946. A supplementary earnings-related scheme, SERPS, was added in 1978 and later reformed as the State Second Pension. From 6 April 2016, a single-tier "new State Pension" replaced the basic and additional pensions for new retirees, subject to a record of National Insurance contributions (35 qualifying years for the full amount). Since 2011 the state pension has been uprated under the "triple lock" — by the highest of earnings growth, inflation, or 2.5 per cent.

Workplace pensions

Occupational schemes were historically dominated by defined benefit (final salary) plans, especially in the public sector, but most private-sector DB schemes have closed to new entrants. The Pensions Act 2008 introduced automatic enrolment, phased in from 2012, requiring employers to enrol eligible workers into a qualifying pension scheme with minimum combined contributions of 8 per cent of qualifying earnings; the National Employment Savings Trust (NEST) was created as a low-cost public option. The Pension Protection Fund, established in 2005, compensates members of DB schemes whose sponsoring employer becomes insolvent with insufficient assets.

Personal pensions and freedoms

Personal pensions, including stakeholder pensions and self-invested personal pensions (SIPPs), allow individuals to save outside workplace schemes, with tax relief on contributions subject to annual and (until its abolition in 2024) lifetime allowances. The "pension freedoms" introduced in April 2015 gave savers aged 55 and over flexible access to defined contribution savings, ending the effective requirement to buy an annuity.
