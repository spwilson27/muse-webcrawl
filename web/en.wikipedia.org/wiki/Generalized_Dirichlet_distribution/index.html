Generalized Dirichlet distribution

In statistics, the generalized Dirichlet distribution (GD) is a generalization of the Dirichlet distribution with a more general covariance structure and is a multivariate extension of the beta distribution. It was introduced by Connor and Mosimann in 1969 in the context of modelling compositional data, such as proportions of components in a mixture.

Definition

Whereas the ordinary Dirichlet distribution forces a restrictive structure in which correlations between components are determined by a single set of concentration parameters, the generalized Dirichlet distribution is constructed from ratios of independent beta-distributed variables. If independent random variables follow beta distributions with separate pairs of parameters for each component, the transformed variables distributed as proportions summing to less than one (with a leftover category) follow the generalized Dirichlet distribution.

Properties

The generalized Dirichlet distribution has 2(k − 1) parameters for a k-category composition, twice as many as the Dirichlet distribution, permitting a more flexible correlation structure, including positive correlations between some components. The Dirichlet distribution arises as a special case when particular constraints are imposed on the parameters. The distribution is closed under amalgamation of categories in a specific ordering-dependent way, and its density has a closed form involving products of beta functions.

Applications

The distribution is used in Bayesian statistics and machine learning for modelling compositional and categorical data where the Dirichlet's restrictive covariance assumptions are inadequate, including topic modelling and the analysis of microbiome and geochemical composition data.
