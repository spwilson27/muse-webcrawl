St. Elsewhere

St. Elsewhere is an American medical drama television series created by Joshua Brand and John Falsey that originally ran on NBC from October 26, 1982, to May 25, 1988. The series stars Ed Flanders, Norman Lloyd, and William Daniels as teaching doctors at the fictional St. Eligius Hospital, a decaying urban teaching hospital in Boston's South End, nicknamed "St. Elsewhere" because it took in patients other hospitals turned away.

Overview

The ensemble cast included Denzel Washington, Howie Mandel, David Morse, Mark Harmon, and Christina Pickles. Produced by MTM Enterprises, the show was known for its gritty realism, large ensemble, serialized storylines, and frequent references to other television shows and films. Though never a ratings hit, it was critically acclaimed and won 13 Emmy Awards from 62 nominations.

Finale

The series finale, "The Last One" (May 25, 1988), is famous for its twist ending: the final scene reveals that the entire series may have taken place in the imagination of Tommy Westphall, an autistic boy who gazes into a snow globe containing a replica of the hospital. Because characters from St. Elsewhere crossed over with many other series, this has spawned the humorous fan theory known as the "Tommy Westphall Universe" hypothesis, which argues that hundreds of television shows share one fictional universe — and therefore all occur in Tommy's mind.
