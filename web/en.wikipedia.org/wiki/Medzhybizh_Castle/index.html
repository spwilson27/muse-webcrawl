Medzhybizh Castle

Medzhybizh Castle is a historic fortress in the town of Medzhybizh in Khmelnytskyi Oblast, western Ukraine, situated at the confluence of the Southern Bug and Buzhok rivers. It is one of the oldest and largest surviving fortifications in the Podolia region.

LocationMedzhybizh, Khmelnytskyi Oblast, Ukraine
BuiltOrigins 12th–14th century; stone castle 14th–16th century
StyleMedieval fortress with Renaissance elements

History

A fortified settlement existed on the site in the era of Kievan Rus'. After the region passed under Lithuanian and then Polish rule, a stone castle was built to guard the frontier against Tatar raids along the so-called Black Trail. In the 16th century the castle belonged to the Sieniawski family, powerful Polish magnates, who rebuilt it in Renaissance style, adding a palace and chapel within the walls.

The castle changed hands during the Khmelnytsky Uprising (1648–1657) and the wars between Poland, the Cossacks, and the Ottoman Empire; it suffered damage in sieges but remained in use. Under Russian rule in the 19th century it served as a military installation, and in the Soviet period it fell into disrepair.

Today

Since Ukrainian independence the castle has been a state historical and cultural reserve, with gradual restoration of its towers, walls, and the Church of St. Nicholas within the complex. The town of Medzhybizh is also famous as the home of the Baal Shem Tov, founder of Hasidic Judaism, who is buried there; the town remains a pilgrimage destination, and the castle hosts festivals and reenactments.
