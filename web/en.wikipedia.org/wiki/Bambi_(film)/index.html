Bambi is a 1942 American animated drama film produced by Walt Disney and released by RKO Radio Pictures. It is based on the 1923 novel Bambi, a Life in the Woods by Austrian author Felix Salten. The film tells the story of a young white-tailed deer (a mule deer in the novel) growing up in the forest, befriending the rabbit Thumper and the skunk Flower, losing his mother to a hunter, and eventually becoming the Great Prince of the Forest.

Plot

A young fawn is born in the forest and greeted by the animals as the new "Prince of the Forest". Guided by his mother, Bambi explores the meadow, befriends the energetic rabbit Thumper and the shy skunk he names Flower, and learns of "Man", the danger feared by all animals. During his first winter, a hunter ("Man") shoots and kills his mother. His father, the Great Prince, leads him to safety. Bambi grows into a young stag, survives a forest fire started by Man's camp, falls in love with Faline, and in the end takes his father's place as the Great Prince, watching over his own newborn twins.

Production

Disney acquired the rights in 1937 and strove for unusual realism: animators studied live deer kept at the studio, and background artist Tyrus Wong's soft, impressionistic watercolour style defined the film's look. The multiplane camera was used to create depth in the forest scenes. The death of Bambi's mother, which happens off-screen, became one of the most famous and affecting scenes in animation.

Reception and legacy

Released during the Second World War, the film initially underperformed financially but was re-released repeatedly to great success and is now regarded as a classic of animation. It was nominated for three Academy Awards (sound, score, and the song "Love Is a Song"). The American Film Institute ranked it among the greatest animated films, and in 2011 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry. The term "Bambi effect" derives from the public sympathy for deer the film generated. A direct-to-video sequel, Bambi II, was released in 2006.
