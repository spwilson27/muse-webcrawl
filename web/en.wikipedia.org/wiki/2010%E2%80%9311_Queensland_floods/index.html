2010–11 Queensland floods

DateNovember 2010 – January 2011
LocationQueensland, Australia
Deaths35
Damage~A$2.4 billion; insured losses ~A$5.6 billion

The 2010–11 Queensland floods were a series of floods in Australia, primarily in the state of Queensland, caused by exceptionally heavy rainfall during the strong 2010–11 La Niña event. Beginning in November 2010, flooding eventually forced the evacuation of thousands from towns and cities including Brisbane, Ipswich, Toowoomba, and the Lockyer Valley. Thirty-five people died, and the floods affected an area larger than France and Germany combined.

Causes

The floods resulted from one of the strongest La Niña events on record, combined with a negative Indian Ocean Dipole and record-warm seas around Australia. Tropical Cyclone Tasha crossed the coast near Cairns on Christmas Day 2010, adding further rain to already saturated catchments.

Toowoomba and the Lockyer Valley

On 10 January 2011, an intense storm sent a flash flood — described as an "inland tsunami" — through Toowoomba and down the range into the Lockyer Valley, devastating Grantham and other towns. Most of the disaster's deaths occurred in this torrent.

Brisbane floods

The Brisbane River peaked at 4.46 metres on 13 January 2011 — below the great flood of 1974 but high enough to inundate some 20,000 homes across 78 suburbs. Central Brisbane businesses closed, the Suncorp Stadium flooded, and volunteers known as the "Mud Army" helped clean up. Controversy followed over the management of releases from Wivenhoe Dam.

Aftermath

The Queensland Reconstruction Authority was established to coordinate rebuilding. A commission of inquiry examined dam operations, planning, and emergency responses. The floods, followed days later by Cyclone Yasi in North Queensland, caused billions of dollars in damage and temporarily shut much of the state's coal industry.
