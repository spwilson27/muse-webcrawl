USG Corporation — founded as the United States Gypsum Company — is an American manufacturer of building materials, best known for Sheetrock gypsum wallboard (drywall), the product that transformed interior construction in the twentieth century. Headquartered in Chicago, USG was acquired by the German building-materials group Knauf in 2019 for about US$7 billion.

History

US Gypsum was formed in 1901 by consolidating some 30 gypsum and plaster companies. In 1916 its subsidiary developed Sheetrock, a panel of gypsum plaster sandwiched between heavy paper sheets, which replaced slow wet-plaster wall finishing with fast, dry installation; wartime labour shortages in the 1940s made it universal in American housing. The company later diversified into ceiling tile and grid systems (buying back its former subsidiary Armstrong's ceiling business relationships notwithstanding), cement board (Durock, 1980s), and joint compounds.

Asbestos bankruptcy and recovery

Facing tens of thousands of asbestos personal-injury claims from products sold decades earlier, USG filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2001. It emerged in 2006 under a settlement — supported by investor Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway, a major shareholder — that funded a trust of about US$3.95 billion for claimants, a landmark resolution that preserved the operating company. Berkshire increased its stake during the housing downturn before Knauf's 2019 buyout took the company private.

Products

USG's lines include Sheetrock-brand gypsum panels (including lightweight and fire- and mould-resistant variants), Durock cement board for wet areas and exteriors, Securock sheathing, acoustic ceiling tiles and suspension systems, and setting and ready-mix joint compounds. Its gypsum comes from company quarries and, increasingly, synthetic gypsum captured from power-plant flue gas.
