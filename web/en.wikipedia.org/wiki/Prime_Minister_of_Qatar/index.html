The Prime Minister of Qatar is the head of government of the State of Qatar. The office was established in 1970, shortly before Qatar's independence from Britain in 1971, and for much of its history was held concurrently by the emir himself or by members of the ruling Al Thani family.

Role

The prime minister is appointed by the Emir of Qatar and presides over the Council of Ministers, coordinating the work of ministries and implementing the general policies of the state under the emir's authority. Qatar is a constitutional monarchy in which the emir retains wide-ranging executive powers; the prime minister and cabinet manage the day-to-day administration of the government.

History

The first prime minister was Khalifa bin Hamad Al Thani, who served from 1970 and became emir in 1972, holding both roles until he was deposed in 1995. His son, Emir Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani, also served as his own prime minister until 1996, when he appointed his half-brother Abdullah bin Khalifa Al Thani. Later prime ministers included Hamad bin Jassim Al Thani (2007–2013), a prominent figure in Qatar's foreign and investment policy; Abdullah bin Nasser Al Thani (2013–2020); Khalid bin Khalifa Al Thani (2020–2023); and Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani, who became prime minister in March 2023 while also serving as foreign minister.
