Joint European Torus

Location: Culham, Oxfordshire, United Kingdom
Type: Tokamak (magnetic confinement fusion)
First plasma: 1983
Closed: 2023
Key record: 69 MJ fusion energy output (2023)

The Joint European Torus (JET) was a magnetically confined plasma physics experiment, located at the Culham Centre for Fusion Energy in Oxfordshire, UK. Based on the tokamak design, JET was a collective venture of the European fusion programme, operated from 1983 until its decommissioning at the end of 2023. It was the largest operational tokamak in the world for most of its life and the only one capable of routinely using the deuterium–tritium fuel mixture of a future power station.

History

Construction of JET began in 1978 under the European Communities, with the machine designed to study plasma behaviour in conditions approaching those of a commercial fusion reactor. First plasma was achieved in June 1983. In 1991 JET performed the world's first controlled release of deuterium–tritium fusion power, and in 1997 it set a record of 16 megawatts of fusion power with a Q value of about 0.67.

Records and final campaigns

In December 2021 JET produced 59 megajoules of fusion energy over five seconds, more than doubling the previous record. Its final deuterium–tritium campaign in 2023 surpassed this with 69 megajoules of output. JET's results in plasma confinement, heating, exhaust handling, and materials validated design choices for ITER, the international tokamak under construction in France. Experimental operations ended in December 2023, after which JET entered a long decommissioning programme that itself serves as a testbed for remote handling.
