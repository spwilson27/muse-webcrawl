Prawn cocktail

A prawn cocktail, also known as a shrimp cocktail in North America, is a seafood dish consisting of shelled, cooked prawns served in a Marie Rose sauce — a mixture of mayonnaise and tomato ketchup, often seasoned with lemon juice, Worcestershire sauce and a dash of Tabasco — accompanied by shredded lettuce and traditionally presented in a glass.

History

Seafood cocktails originated in the United States in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, when oyster and shrimp cocktails in a spicy tomato-based sauce became fashionable; serving them in glasses is sometimes linked to Prohibition-era reuse of stemware. In Britain the prawn cocktail became a ubiquitous restaurant starter from the 1960s through the 1980s, a period when it was considered sophisticated dining. The chef Fanny Cradock is frequently credited with popularising the modern British version. The dish became so emblematic of a certain style of restaurant menu that the phrase "prawn cocktail offensive" was borrowed by the Labour Party in the 1990s for its campaign to win over business leaders.

Preparation

Cooked peeled prawns are chilled and served over crisp shredded lettuce, coated or accompanied by Marie Rose sauce, and garnished with a wedge of lemon and sometimes a sprinkle of paprika or cayenne. A whole prawn in its shell may be hung on the rim of the glass. Though later dismissed as dated, the dish has been revived by chefs emphasising fresh ingredients and well-made sauce.
