Hangzhou Bay (Chinese: 杭州湾) is a funnel-shaped inlet of the East China Sea on the coast of Zhejiang province, China, bordering the municipalities of Shanghai to the north and Ningbo and Hangzhou to the south. It is the estuary of the Qiantang River and is famous for the Qiantang tidal bore, the largest tidal bore in the world, which can reach heights of up to about 9 metres (30 ft) and travel at speeds of up to 40 km/h.

Tidal bore

The bay's rapidly narrowing, shoaling shape funnels the incoming tide into a steep wave front. Bore-watching has been a tradition for more than two thousand years, peaking during the Mid-Autumn Festival, when huge crowds line the river banks near Haining and Hangzhou. The bore is a hazard as well as a spectacle, and viewing areas are protected by seawalls.

Crossings and economy

The Hangzhou Bay Bridge, opened in 2008, is a 35.7 km (22.2 mi) cable-stayed bridge across the mouth of the bay linking Jiaxing and Ningbo; at opening it was among the longest trans-oceanic bridges in the world. The bay region is one of China's most economically dynamic areas, forming part of the Yangtze River Delta megalopolis, with major ports, wetlands of ecological significance, and extensive land reclamation.
