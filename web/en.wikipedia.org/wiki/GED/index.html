The General Educational Development (GED) tests are a group of four academic subject tests in the United States and Canada certifying academic knowledge equivalent to that of a high school graduate. Passing the GED test gives those who do not complete high school, or who do not meet requirements for a high school diploma, the opportunity to earn a Certificate of High School Equivalency or similarly titled credential.

History

The GED program was created in 1942 at the request of the United States Armed Forces Institute, to help returning World War II veterans who had left high school to serve finish their education and reintegrate into civilian life. In 1947 the American Council on Education began offering the tests to civilians. Revised versions of the test series were released in 1978, 1988, 2002, and 2014; the 2014 series is computer-based and aligned with college- and career-readiness standards.

Test structure

The current GED test consists of four subject tests:

  • Reasoning Through Language Arts — reading comprehension, writing, and editing, including an extended-response essay.
  • Mathematical Reasoning — quantitative and algebraic problem solving.
  • Science — life science, physical science, and Earth and space science.
  • Social Studies — civics and government, United States history, economics, and geography.

Each subject is scored from 100 to 200. A score of at least 145 is required to pass; scores of 165–174 indicate college readiness, and 175–200 indicate readiness for college-level coursework with possible credit.

Eligibility and recognition

Test-takers generally must be at least 16 years old and not enrolled in high school or already holding a diploma; exact rules vary by state or province. The credential is widely accepted by employers and postsecondary institutions in the United States and Canada, though some states offer alternative equivalency exams such as the HiSET or TASC historically.
