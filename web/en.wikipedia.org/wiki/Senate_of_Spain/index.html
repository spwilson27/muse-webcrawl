The Senate (Spanish: Senado) is the upper house of the Cortes Generales, the Spanish parliament, the other chamber being the Congress of Deputies. It is formally described in the Spanish Constitution of 1978 as the chamber of territorial representation. The Senate meets in the Palacio del Senado in Madrid.

Composition

The Senate is composed of 265 senators. Most are directly elected by universal suffrage in provincial constituencies: each mainland province elects four senators, while the island provinces and the autonomous cities of Ceuta and Melilla elect smaller numbers. The remaining senators are appointed by the legislative assemblies of the autonomous communities, roughly one per community plus an additional senator per million inhabitants.

Powers

Spain's bicameral system is asymmetric: the Congress of Deputies holds primacy in legislation and in the investiture of the President of the Government. The Senate may amend or veto bills, but Congress can override a Senate veto by absolute majority, or by simple majority after two months. The Senate has special roles in authorising measures under Article 155 of the Constitution, in appointing members of constitutional bodies, and in ratifying certain treaties.

History

A senate first appeared in Spain under the Royal Statute of 1834 as the Estamento de Próceres. It existed in various forms through the nineteenth and twentieth centuries before being re-established in its modern democratic form by the Law for Political Reform of 1977 and the Constitution of 1978.
