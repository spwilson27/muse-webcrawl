Overview

The Antarctic Circle is the northernmost latitude in the Southern Hemisphere at which the Sun can stay continuously above or below the horizon for 24 hours. It lies at about 66.56° south of the Equator, the mirror image of the Arctic Circle.

South of the circle, every place experiences at least one day of continuous daylight (at the December solstice) and at least one day of continuous darkness each year; at the South Pole these stretch to six months each.

Geography

Unlike its northern counterpart, the Antarctic Circle lies almost entirely over the Southern Ocean. It clips the Antarctic continent at several points and crosses the Antarctic Peninsula, which reaches northwards towards South America. There are no towns and no permanent population on the circle — only research stations, such as those along the peninsula and the coast of East Antarctica.

The circle is drifting southward by about 15 metres a year as Earth's axial tilt decreases, slowly shrinking the zone of the midnight sun.
