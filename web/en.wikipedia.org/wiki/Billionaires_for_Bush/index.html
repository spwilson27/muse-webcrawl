Billionaires for Bush was a culture jamming political street theatre organization in the United States that satirized the George W. Bush administration by posing as wealthy supporters of the president. Dressed in tuxedos, evening gowns, top hats and tiaras, the "Billionaires" staged comic demonstrations at campaign events, thanking Bush for policies they claimed benefited the rich at the expense of everyone else.

History

The group originated in 2000 as "Billionaires for Forbes", targeting Steve Forbes's flat-tax proposal, and included future participants such as Andrew Boyd, a co-founder later known for the Beautiful Trouble toolkit. It re-emerged for the 2004 presidential election with chapters in dozens of U.S. cities, performing at both Democratic and Republican events, including a prominent presence around the 2004 Republican National Convention in New York City.

Method and message

The Billionaires' comedy rested on stating the quiet part loudly: chanting "Four more wars!", holding signs such as "Leave No Billionaire Behind", and holding "Billionaires' balls" and croquet tournaments. Their targets included the Bush tax cuts, the Iraq War, deregulation and corporate welfare. The group later adapted its persona for subsequent elections, including appearances as "Billionaires for Romney" in 2012, and is frequently cited as an influential example of humorous protest tactics.
