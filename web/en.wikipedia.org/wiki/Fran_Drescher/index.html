Fran Drescher

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Francine Joy Drescher (born September 30, 1957) is an American actress, comedian, writer, activist, and trade union leader. She is best known for her role as Fran Fine in the television sitcom The Nanny (1993–1999), which she created and produced with her then-husband Peter Marc Jacobson. Her distinctive nasal voice and Queens accent became her trademarks. Since 2021 she has served as president of SAG-AFTRA, the actors' union, leading it through the 2023 actors' strike.

Early life and career

Drescher was born in Queens, New York City, and grew up in Flushing. After being named runner-up in the Miss New York Teenager pageant, she took acting classes and made her film debut with a small role in Saturday Night Fever (1977), followed by appearances in American Hot Wax (1978) and This Is Spinal Tap (1984), in which she played publicist Bobbi Flekman. She appeared in a string of sitcoms and films through the 1980s and early 1990s without a breakthrough until she and Jacobson pitched The Nanny to CBS.

The Nanny and later work

The Nanny ran for six seasons and 146 episodes, earning Drescher two Emmy Award and two Golden Globe nominations. The character of Fran Fine, a cosmetics saleswoman from Queens who becomes nanny to a Broadway producer's children, drew directly on Drescher's own background and family. Afterwards she starred in the sitcoms Living with Fran (2005–06) and Happily Divorced (2011–13), the latter inspired by her post-divorce friendship with Jacobson, and voiced Eunice in the Hotel Transylvania animated film series.

Activism and union leadership

A survivor of a violent home invasion in 1985 and of uterine cancer (diagnosed in 2000 after two years of misdiagnoses, recounted in her memoir Cancer Schmancer), Drescher founded the Cancer Schmancer Movement, a non-profit advocating early detection and prevention, and has worked as a health-care advocate and special envoy. Elected national president of SAG-AFTRA in 2021, she became one of the most visible labour figures in the United States during the union's 2023 strike against the film and television studios, and was re-elected the same year.
