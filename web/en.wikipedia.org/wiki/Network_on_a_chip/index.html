Network on a chip

A network on a chip or network-on-chip (NoC) is a network-based communications subsystem on an integrated circuit, most typically in a system on a chip (SoC). NoCs apply networking theory and methods to on-chip communication, replacing shared buses and crossbars with routers and links that forward packets between processor cores, memory controllers and accelerators.

Design

Common topologies include mesh, torus, ring and fat-tree layouts. Routers use switching techniques such as wormhole switching, and routing algorithms must avoid deadlock and livelock while meeting quality-of-service goals. NoCs were proposed as a research direction around the year 2000 as transistor counts made bus-based interconnects a bottleneck for power and scalability.

Benefits and challenges

By structuring communication, NoCs improve scalability, energy efficiency and modularity in manycore processors; costs include router area, latency and complex verification. Commercial manycore chips and AI accelerators commonly employ NoC fabrics.
