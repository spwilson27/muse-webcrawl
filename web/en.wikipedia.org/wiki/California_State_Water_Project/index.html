California State Water Project

The California Aqueduct is the principal conveyance of the State Water Project.

The California State Water Project (SWP) is a state water management project in the U.S. state of California under the supervision of the California Department of Water Resources. It is one of the largest public water and power utilities in the world, providing drinking water to more than 27 million people and generating an average of 6,500 gigawatt-hours of hydroelectricity per year. It collects water from rivers in Northern California and redistributes it to the water-scarce but populous south through a network of canals, pipelines, reservoirs and hydroelectric power plants.

History

The project was authorised by the Burns–Porter Act of 1959 and a $1.75 billion general obligation bond approved by voters in November 1960, championed by Governor Edmund G. "Pat" Brown. Its centrepiece, Oroville Dam on the Feather River — the tallest earthfill dam in the United States — was completed in 1968, and water deliveries to the San Francisco Bay Area, the San Joaquin Valley and Southern California began in the late 1960s and early 1970s.

Facilities

Water stored at Lake Oroville is released down the Feather and Sacramento rivers to the Sacramento–San Joaquin Delta, where the Harvey O. Banks Delta Pumping Plant lifts it into the 444-mile (715 km) California Aqueduct. The aqueduct carries water south along the San Joaquin Valley and, via the Edmonston Pumping Plant — one of the largest pumping lifts in the world — over the Tehachapi Mountains into Southern California reservoirs such as Castaic Lake and Lake Perris. Other major facilities include the San Luis Reservoir (shared with the federal Central Valley Project), Clifton Court Forebay, and the South Bay Aqueduct.

Contractors and controversies

Twenty-nine public water agencies hold long-term contracts for SWP water; the largest contractor is the Metropolitan Water District of Southern California. The project has long been controversial for its cost, its effects on the Delta ecosystem and endangered fish such as the Delta smelt, and shortfalls between the water contracted ("paper water") and the amount reliably deliverable.
