Siege of Lisbon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Siege of Lisbon (1 July – 25 October 1147) was the military action that brought the city of Lisbon under definitive Portuguese control, when the forces of King Afonso Henriques, aided by a fleet of northern European crusaders bound for the Holy Land, captured it from its Muslim rulers during the Second Crusade and the Reconquista.

Background

A crusader fleet of some 164 ships carrying English, Norman, Flemish, Frisian and German contingents put into Porto in June 1147. Afonso, with the mediation of the bishop, persuaded the crusaders to help attack Lisbon first; an agreement divided the expected spoils. The eyewitness account De expugnatione Lyxbonensi preserves a detailed narrative of the campaign.

The siege

The attackers besieged the city for nearly four months, building siege towers and undermining the walls, while the defenders, reinforced from the south, resisted fiercely and destroyed several siege engines. Famine and a breach in the defences at last forced negotiations; the city surrendered on 24 October 1147 under terms allowing the garrison terms of departure, though the ensuing entry was marred by plundering in breach of the agreement.

Aftermath

Lisbon became the principal city of the emerging Kingdom of Portugal. Gilbert of Hastings was installed as its first bishop, and many crusaders settled in Portugal. The conquest was a turning point in the Reconquista and in Lisbon's rise to become Portugal's capital.
