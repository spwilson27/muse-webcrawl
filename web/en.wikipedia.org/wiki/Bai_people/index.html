The Bai people (Bai: Baipho; Chinese: 白族, Bái zú) are an East Asian ethnic group native to the Dali Bai Autonomous Prefecture of Yunnan Province, China, with smaller communities in Guizhou, Sichuan, and Hunan. With roughly two million members, they are one of the 56 officially recognized ethnic groups of the People's Republic of China.

History

The Bai trace their origins to the ancient peoples of the Erhai Lake region, where the kingdoms of Nanzhao (738–937) and the Dali Kingdom (937–1253) flourished. Although the Bai claim descent from the ruling elites of these states, scholars debate the exact relationship. In 1253 the Mongol Empire conquered Dali, incorporating the region into China; thereafter Bai elites increasingly integrated into Chinese administration while preserving a distinct identity. During the Ming dynasty, Bai scholars achieved notable success in the imperial examinations.

Language

The Bai language belongs to the Sino-Tibetan family, though its precise classification is contested: it shows heavy influence from Chinese at all historical stages and is often grouped with the Sinitic languages, while other linguists see an independent branch with deep Chinese contact. It is written using Chinese characters adapted in the historical bowen tradition and, more recently, a Latin-based orthography. Many Bai also speak Southwestern Mandarin.

Culture

Bai culture is known for its distinctive architecture, characterized by whitewashed walls—reflecting the name "Bai", meaning "white"—and elaborate gatehouses, as in the old town of Xizhou. Tie-dye cloth produced in Zhoucheng is a famous craft. The Third Month Fair (Sanyue Jie) in Dali, originating over a thousand years ago, is the group's most important festival. The Bai practice Benzhuism, the veneration of village guardian deities called benzhu, alongside Mahayana Buddhism and Taoism. Tea culture, including the three-course tea ceremony, is central to Bai hospitality.

Economy

Traditionally agricultural, the Bai farm the fertile plains around Erhai Lake, growing rice, wheat, and vegetables, and fish the lake. Marble quarrying near Dali gave the stone its Chinese name, dalishi ("Dali stone"). Tourism has become a major industry in recent decades.
