Haut-Folin

The Haut-Folin is the highest peak in the Morvan massif and the highest point of the Burgundy-Franche-Comté region of France, rising to an elevation of 901 metres (2,959 ft). It lies on the border between the departments of Nièvre and Saône-et-Loire, within the Morvan Regional Natural Park.

Geography

The summit is formed of granite and is covered by dense forest, largely conifer plantations. Because of the trees, the summit itself offers only limited views, though a clearing near the top and the surroundings of the ski area provide lookouts over the Morvan hills. Its slopes drain toward both the Loire basin and the Yonne, a tributary of the Seine.

Facilities

The mountain carries a telecommunications relay and EDF transmission installations; drivers are advised that an unmarked military transponder zone lies nearby. A small ski station with drag lifts operated on the upper slopes historically, and the area remains popular for hiking and cross-country trails. The Tour du Morvan long-distance path passes near the summit.
