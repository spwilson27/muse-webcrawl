Background radiation

Background radiation is the ubiquitous ionizing radiation present in the environment. It originates from a variety of natural and artificial sources and varies widely from place to place. The largest source of natural background radiation is airborne radon, a radioactive gas that seeps from the ground and can accumulate indoors; other natural sources include cosmic rays, terrestrial gamma radiation from rocks and soil, and radioactive isotopes within the human body itself, such as potassium-40.

Sources and levels

The worldwide average annual effective dose from natural background radiation is about 2.4 millisieverts (mSv), according to the United Nations Scientific Committee on the Effects of Atomic Radiation (UNSCEAR). Medical imaging is the largest artificial source for most people in developed countries. Some locations, such as Ramsar in Iran and the monazite sands of Kerala in India and Guarapari in Brazil, have naturally high background levels many times the global average, and have been studied for possible health effects on their inhabitants.

Terminology

The term is also used in other branches of physics: in astronomy, the cosmic microwave background is the relic radiation of the early universe, and in particle physics experiments "background" refers to signals not produced by the process under study. The biological effects of low-dose background radiation remain an area of scientific discussion, commonly framed by the linear no-threshold model.
