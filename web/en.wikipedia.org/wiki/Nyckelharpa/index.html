Nyckelharpa

The nyckelharpa (Swedish for "keyed fiddle" or "key harp") is a traditional Swedish string instrument. It is played with a bow on the right hand while the left hand presses keys (nycklar), which in turn press tangents against the melody strings to change their pitch. Sympathetic resonance strings vibrate in response, giving the instrument its characteristic resonant sound.

History

Keyed fiddles appear in depictions from medieval Europe, including a relief dated to about 1350 in Källunge church on Gotland, Sweden. The instrument survived in Swedish folk tradition, particularly in the province of Uppland, when it died out elsewhere. The modern chromatic nyckelharpa was developed in the 20th century, and players such as Eric Sahlström helped revive and refine it. Today several thousand people play the instrument in Sweden, and it has gained popularity in folk music across Europe and North America.

Construction

A typical modern nyckelharpa has four bowed strings (three melody strings and a drone) and twelve sympathetic strings, with a keybox holding rows of wooden keys. Its repertoire consists largely of Swedish folk tunes, polskas in particular, and Uppsala hosts an annual gathering of players.
