Skidmore College

Skidmore College is a private liberal arts college in Saratoga Springs, New York. Founded in 1903 as the Skidmore School of Arts for women, it was chartered as a four-year college in 1922 and named for its founder, Lucy Ann Skidmore. It became coeducational in 1971. Approximately 2,500 students attend the college.

History

Lucy Skidmore, widow of coal merchant Joseph Skidmore, used her inheritance to found the Young Women's Industrial Club in 1903, offering courses in practical and fine arts to young women of the Saratoga Springs area. The club became the Skidmore School of Arts in 1911 and was chartered by the New York State Board of Regents as Skidmore College in 1922, offering bachelor's degrees. In the 1960s the college built a new campus — the Jonsson Campus — on land donated by trustee Erik Jonsson, abandoning its scattered downtown buildings. Under President David Porter in the late 1980s and 1990s the college's endowment and national profile grew substantially.

Campus

The 850-acre Jonsson Campus sits at the foothills of the Adirondack Mountains. Notable buildings include the Frances Young Tang Teaching Museum and Art Gallery, opened in 2000, which functions as both a museum and an interdisciplinary teaching space, and the Arthur Zankel Music Center, opened in 2010. The campus is known for its modernist architecture designed amid pine forests and for its green (Case Green) at the centre.

Academics

Skidmore offers more than 40 majors, with notable programs in the creative arts, business, psychology and the natural sciences. Its motto, adopted under Lucy Skidmore, is "Creative Thought Matters". The college is known for an interdisciplinary curriculum, a strong study-abroad program, and the First-Year Experience seminar. It is a member of the Liberty League for athletics; its teams are the Thoroughbreds.

Student life

More than 100 student clubs and organisations operate on campus, alongside a tradition of student-run cabaret and a cappella groups. Saratoga Springs, famous for horse racing, mineral springs and the Saratoga Performing Arts Center, provides an off-campus cultural setting.
