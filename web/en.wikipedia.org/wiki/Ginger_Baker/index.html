Ginger Baker

Born: Peter Edward Baker, August 19, 1939, Lewisham, London, England

Died: October 6, 2019 (aged 80), Canterbury, Kent, England

Genres: Rock, blues rock, jazz fusion, Afrobeat

Instruments: Drums, percussion

Associated acts: Cream, Blind Faith, Ginger Baker's Air Force, Fela Kuti

Peter Edward "Ginger" Baker (August 19, 1939 – October 6, 2019) was an English drummer, regarded as one of the most influential drummers in rock history. A co-founder of the rock supergroup Cream, he pioneered the use of two bass drums in rock and brought jazz and African rhythms into rock drumming. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of Cream in 1993 and into the Modern Drummer Hall of Fame in 1999.

Early life

Baker was born in Lewisham, South London. His father was killed in the Second World War in 1943. Baker took up drums at about fifteen, largely self-taught though he later received lessons from the jazz drummer Phil Seamen. He played in British trad jazz bands before joining the Graham Bond Organisation in 1963, where he played alongside bassist Jack Bruce — with whom he developed a famously volatile relationship.

Cream and Blind Faith

In 1966 Baker formed Cream with Jack Bruce and Eric Clapton. The trio became one of the biggest bands in the world, selling millions of records with songs such as "Sunshine of Your Love" and "White Room" before internal tensions — chiefly between Baker and Bruce — caused their breakup in 1968. Baker then joined Clapton in Blind Faith (1969), and after its dissolution led the jazz-rock big band Ginger Baker's Air Force.

Later career

In the early 1970s Baker lived in Nigeria, building a recording studio in Lagos and collaborating closely with Afrobeat pioneer Fela Kuti. He later played with Hawkwind, Public Image Ltd, and the Baker Gurvitz Army, and formed the jazz-oriented Ginger Baker Trio with Charlie Haden and Bill Frisell. Famed for his temper as much as his musicianship, he was the subject of the 2012 documentary Beware of Mr. Baker. Cream reunited for concerts in 2005. Baker died in Canterbury in October 2019 after a period of ill health, including chronic obstructive pulmonary disease.

Style and influence

Baker's drumming blended jazz technique, African polyrhythm, and rock power. His extended solo "Toad" on Cream's debut album helped establish the rock drum solo. He was ranked among the greatest drummers of all time in numerous polls, and his playing influenced generations of rock and metal drummers.
