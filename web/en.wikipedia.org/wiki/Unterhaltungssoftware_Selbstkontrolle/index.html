The Unterhaltungssoftware Selbstkontrolle (USK; English: Entertainment Software Self-Regulation Body) is the organisation responsible for video game ratings in Germany. Founded in 1994, it is a self-regulatory body supported by the German games industry, whose age ratings are recognised by the German federal states as legally binding for the sale and public display of games to minors.

Rating system

Following a reform of German youth protection law that took effect in 2021, USK ratings combine an age category with descriptors explaining the reasons for the rating, such as violence, pressure to act, or in-game purchases. The age categories are:

  • USK 0 — approved without age restriction
  • USK 6 — approved for ages 6 and up
  • USK 12 — approved for ages 12 and up
  • USK 16 — approved for ages 16 and up
  • USK 18 — no youth approval (adults only)

Ratings are displayed as coloured diamond-shaped logos on game packaging: white (0), yellow (6), green (12), blue (16), and red (18). Titles that are refused classification may be indexed by the Federal Review Board for Media Harmful to Minors (BPjM), restricting their advertising and sale.

Procedure

Publishers submit games to the USK, where trained testers play the title and present it to rating committees composed of youth-protection experts appointed with the involvement of the state youth protection authorities. The committees decide the final rating. The USK also rates apps and games distributed through the International Age Rating Coalition (IARC) system for digital storefronts.
