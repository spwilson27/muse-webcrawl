Torsion pendulum

A torsion pendulum is a pendulum that oscillates by rotating back and forth about a vertical axis, twisting a suspension wire or fibre that provides the restoring torque. It is a classic instrument of experimental physics and the basis of the torsion balance used in some of the most sensitive measurements ever made.

Physics

For small twists the wire exerts a torque proportional to the angle of twist, so the system undergoes harmonic oscillation with period T = 2π√(I/κ), where I is the moment of inertia of the suspended body and κ is the torsional constant of the wire. Because the restoring force can be made extremely weak by using a long, fine fibre, tiny forces produce measurable deflections.

Applications

Charles-Augustin de Coulomb used a torsion balance to establish the inverse-square law of electrostatic force, and Henry Cavendish used one in 1797–98 to measure the density of the Earth and hence the gravitational constant G. Loránd Eötvös refined the torsion balance for tests of the equivalence principle, experiments later repeated with modern torsion pendulums to extraordinary precision. Torsion pendulums also serve as the timebase in anniversary clocks.
