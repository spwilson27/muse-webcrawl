Donald John Trump (born June 14, 1946) is an American politician, media personality, and businessman who is the 47th president of the United States. A member of the Republican Party, he previously served as the 45th president from 2017 to 2021.

Business and media career

Born in Queens, Trump received a bachelor's degree from the Wharton School and took over his family's real-estate business, the Trump Organization, building hotels, casinos, and golf courses and licensing his name. From 2004 to 2015 he hosted the reality television show The Apprentice.

First presidency

Trump won the 2016 election against Hillary Clinton, an election marked by Russian interference. His administration cut taxes, deregulated, appointed three Supreme Court justices, pursued restrictive immigration policies, and withdrew from international agreements. He was impeached twice — in 2019 over Ukraine and in 2021 over the January 6 Capitol attack — and acquitted both times. The special counsel investigation was led by Robert Mueller.

Between presidencies and return

After losing the 2020 election to Joe Biden, Trump continued to lead the Republican Party. He won the 2024 election against Kamala Harris and began a second, non-consecutive term in January 2025, the first president since Grover Cleveland to do so.

Policies and legacy

Trump's political style, use of social media, and “America First” agenda reshaped American politics, drawing both strong support and strong opposition.
