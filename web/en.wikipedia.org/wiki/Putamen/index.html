Putamen

The putamen (from Latin, meaning "nutshell" or "husk") is a large, rounded structure located at the base of the forebrain, within the basal ganglia. Together with the caudate nucleus it forms the dorsal striatum, the main input structure of the basal ganglia; together with the globus pallidus, which lies medial to it, it makes up the lentiform nucleus. It is separated from the caudate by the internal capsule, although bridges of grey matter cross the capsule, giving the two nuclei a striped appearance from which "striatum" takes its name.

Anatomy and connections

The putamen receives its principal inputs from the motor and somatosensory cortex (topographically organised), from the substantia nigra pars compacta (dopaminergic), and from intralaminar thalamic nuclei. Its medium spiny projection neurons send inhibitory (GABAergic) outputs to the globus pallidus: via the direct pathway to the internal segment, and via the indirect pathway to the external segment. While the caudate is weighted toward associative and cognitive circuits, the putamen is weighted toward sensorimotor circuits, with more posterior regions engaged in the control of movement and habits.

Function

The putamen plays a central role in motor learning and the performance of learned, automatic movements, and in procedural (habit) learning generally. It is also involved in reinforcement learning and reward prediction, language processing including speech articulation and bilingual language control, and various non-motor functions attributed to basal-ganglia loops.

Clinical significance

Degeneration of dopaminergic input to the putamen is prominent in Parkinson's disease, contributing to bradykinesia and rigidity; the posterior putamen is the most severely affected striatal region. Putaminal haemorrhage is a classic presentation of hypertensive intracerebral bleeding. Lesions and metabolic insults — carbon monoxide poisoning, Wilson's disease and various neurodegenerations — frequently involve the putamen, and bilateral putaminal necrosis is a recognised complication of methanol poisoning.
