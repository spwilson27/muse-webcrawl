Stefan Lazarević (Serbian Cyrillic: Стефан Лазаревић; c. 1377 – 19 July 1427), also known as Stefan the Tall, was the ruler of Serbia as prince (1389–1402) and despot (1402–1427). The son of Prince Lazar Hrebeljanović, who died at the Battle of Kosovo in 1389, Stefan is considered one of the finest knights and military leaders of his time and one of the great literary figures of medieval Serbia.

Reign

Stefan initially ruled as an Ottoman vassal, fighting loyally for Sultan Bayezid I, including at the Battle of Ankara in 1402, where his Serbian heavy cavalry distinguished itself. After the Ottoman defeat there, he obtained the title of despot from the Byzantine emperor and later became a Hungarian vassal, receiving Belgrade, which he made his capital and a flourishing cultural and commercial centre. He balanced between the Ottoman Empire, Hungary and Venice, expanded and consolidated his state, and introduced reforms in administration and mining law (notably the Code of Mines for Novo Brdo, 1412).

Cultural legacy

Stefan was a patron of arts and learning and himself a writer; his works include the poetic epistle "Slovo ljubve" ("A Homage to Love"). He founded the Resava Monastery (Manasija), which became a centre of literary activity. The Serbian Orthodox Church canonised him as a saint.
