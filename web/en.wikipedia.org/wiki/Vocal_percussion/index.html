Vocal percussion

Vocal percussion is the art of creating sounds with one's mouth that approximate, replace or otherwise serve the same purpose as a percussion instrument, whether in a group of singers, an instrumentalist, a beatboxer soloist, or in North Indian classical music. In a cappella ensembles, the vocal percussionist provides the rhythmic foundation of the arrangement in place of a drum kit.

A vocal percussionist typically performs with the microphone close to the mouth to amplify low-frequency sounds.

Styles

In contemporary a cappella, vocal percussion imitates a full drum set: a "pf" or "b" sound for the kick drum, "ts" or "ch" for hi-hats, and "k" or "pfsh" for the snare. Hip hop beatboxing extends this vocabulary with lip oscillations, tongue clicks, humming basslines and electronic effects. In Carnatic music, the related art of konnakol vocalises rhythmic syllables (solkattu) corresponding to drum strokes.

Technique

Performers cup the microphone to boost bass response and use plosive consonants, inward breath sounds and lip buzzes to sustain a continuous groove. Leading practitioners include Wes Carroll, Bobby McFerrin and beatboxers such as Rahzel, who famously performs melody and beat simultaneously.
