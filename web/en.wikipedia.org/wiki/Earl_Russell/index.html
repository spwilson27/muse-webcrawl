Earl Russell, of Kingston Russell in the County of Dorset, is a title in the Peerage of the United Kingdom created in 1861 for the Whig and Liberal statesman Lord John Russell (1792–1878), who twice served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom (1846–1852 and 1865–1866). He was the third son of John Russell, 6th Duke of Bedford, and was created Earl Russell with the subsidiary title Viscount Amberley, of Amberley in the County of Gloucester and of Ardsalla in the County of Meath, having already been raised to the peerage as Baron Russell in 1861-era arrangements of the same creation.

Holders

The 1st Earl was succeeded by his grandson John Francis Stanley Russell, 2nd Earl Russell (1865–1931), eldest son of John Russell, Viscount Amberley. The 2nd Earl was a controversial figure, once imprisoned for bigamy after a sensational trial. He was succeeded by his younger brother, the philosopher Bertrand Russell (1872–1970), 3rd Earl Russell, Nobel laureate in Literature and one of the founders of analytic philosophy, who had disclaimed interest in the title but used his seat in the House of Lords for causes including nuclear disarmament.

The 4th Earl was Bertrand Russell's son John Conrad Russell (1921–1987). He was followed by his half-brother Conrad Russell (1937–2004), 5th Earl Russell, a distinguished historian of seventeenth-century England and Liberal Democrat peer. The 6th Earl was Conrad's son Nicholas Lyulph Russell (1968–2014). The title is associated with the wider Russell family, whose senior line holds the dukedom of Bedford.
