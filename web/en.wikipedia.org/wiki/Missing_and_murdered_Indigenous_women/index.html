Missing and murdered Indigenous women

Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women (MMIW) is a movement addressing the disproportionately high rates of violence, disappearance, and murder experienced by Indigenous women and girls in Canada and the United States. The red handprint and the empty red dress have become widely recognised symbols.

Background

Statistics in both countries show Indigenous women face murder and violence at rates several times higher than other populations, with many cases unsolved or inadequately investigated. Contributing factors discussed include colonial history, poverty, jurisdictional gaps between tribal, state/provincial, and federal authorities, and failures in data collection.

Response

Canada held a National Inquiry into Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women and Girls (2016–2019), which issued Calls for Justice. In the United States, measures include Savanna's Act and the Not Invisible Act (2020). May 5 is observed as a national day of awareness in the US, and October 4 in Canada.
