Holden

Industry: Automotive
Founded: 1856 (as saddlery); 1908 (motor body building)
Defunct: 2021
Headquarters: Port Melbourne, Victoria, Australia
Parent: General Motors

Holden, formerly known as General Motors-Holden, was an Australian automobile marque and the Australian subsidiary of the American manufacturer General Motors. Founded in Adelaide in 1856 as a saddlery business by James Alexander Holden, the company became a coachbuilder, then a motor body builder, and in 1948 launched the first mass-produced Australian-designed car.

History

Holden's Motor Body Builders became a major supplier of car bodies for imported chassis in the 1920s and was wholly acquired by General Motors in 1931, forming General Motors-Holden's Ltd. After the Second World War, with government encouragement, GM-Holden developed an Australian car: the Holden 48-215 (the "FX"), launched on 29 November 1948 by Prime Minister Ben Chifley. The car was a huge success, and "Holden" became a cultural icon, with successive models including the FJ, FE, EH, and the long-running Kingswood.

Commodore era and decline

In 1978 Holden launched the Commodore, adapted from Opel designs and later developed into a distinctively Australian large car that rivalled Ford's Falcon for market leadership. Changing buyer tastes toward SUVs and imports, high production costs, and the strong Australian dollar eroded local manufacturing. GM announced in December 2013 that Holden would end Australian production; the last Australian-built Holden, a red VF Commodore, left the Elizabeth, South Australia plant on 20 October 2017. Holden thereafter sold imported, rebadged GM vehicles until February 2020, when GM announced the Holden brand would be retired by 2021, ending 164 years of the Holden name.

Motorsport

Holden was a mainstay of Australian touring car racing, competing against Ford in the Bathurst 1000 and the Supercars Championship, with drivers such as Peter Brock — "King of the Mountain" — becoming national heroes.
