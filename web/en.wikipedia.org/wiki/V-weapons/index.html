V-weapons

V-weapons
Type: Long-range strike weapons
Used by: Nazi Germany
Period: June 1944 – March 1945
Principal targets: London, Antwerp, Liège

The V-weapons (German: Vergeltungswaffen, "retribution weapons" or "reprisal weapons") were a set of long-range German weapons of the Second World War developed late in the conflict and used mainly against Allied cities, above all London and Antwerp. The term derives from Nazi propaganda, which promised retaliation for Allied bombing of German cities.

The weapons

  • The V-1 flying bomb — a pulsejet-powered cruise missile, nicknamed the "buzz bomb" or "doodlebug". About 9,500 were launched against England beginning 13 June 1944.
  • The V-2 rocket — the world's first long-range guided ballistic missile, developed under Wernher von Braun at Peenemünde. First used against London on 8 September 1944, it travelled supersonically and gave no warning of arrival.
  • The V-3 cannon — a multi-charge supergun built into a hillside near Mimoyecques, France, intended to bombard London; it was destroyed by bombing before it could open fire.

Effects

The attacks killed over 9,000 people in England and many thousands more in Belgium and France. Londoners endured months of V-1 attacks during the summer of 1944, and the V-2 campaign continued until late March 1945. Antwerp, the Allies' key supply port, was the most heavily targeted city overall.

Militarily the weapons caused disruption and heavy civilian casualties but did not change the course of the war. Their production, centred on the Mittelwerk underground factory, consumed immense resources and cost the lives of thousands of concentration-camp prisoners — more people died manufacturing the V-2 than were killed by it in attacks. After the war, V-2 technology and its engineers became the foundation of American and Soviet rocket and space programmes.
