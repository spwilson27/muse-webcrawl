Western Abenaki language

Native to: United States, Canada
Region: Vermont, New Hampshire, Quebec
Family: Algic → Algonquian → Eastern Algonquian

Western Abenaki (also Wαbanakii Alαnαbaαkan in the language's modern orthography) is an Algonquian language spoken by the Abenaki people of Vermont, New Hampshire and Quebec. It forms the western branch of the Abenaki dialect continuum, closely related to but distinct from the Eastern Abenaki once spoken in Maine.

Status and revitalisation

The language became severely endangered during the 20th century as speakers shifted to English and French. The last fully fluent native speaker of the Vermont dialect, Madeline Wαbanakii'kw, died in 2013, but a revitalisation movement led by community language keepers such as Jesse Bowman Bruchac has trained new speakers and produced dictionaries, teaching materials, and immersion programmes, particularly at Odanak in Quebec and among the Nulhegan and other Vermont bands.

Features

Like other Algonquian languages, Western Abenaki is polysynthetic and uses an animate/inanimate gender system. Its orthography, developed largely from the work of 19th-century lexicographers and modern teachers, uses the character α (or ô in some conventions) for a central vowel sound.
