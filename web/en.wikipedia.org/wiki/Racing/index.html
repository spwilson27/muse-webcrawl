Racing

Racing is a form of competition in which participants attempt to complete a defined course in the shortest time, or to travel the greatest distance in a fixed time. Racing occurs in many forms, including foot races, swimming, cycling, motorsport, horse racing, sailing and winter sports, and is both an amateur pastime and a major professional spectator industry.

History

Foot races were among the earliest recorded competitions, featuring in the ancient Olympic Games from 776 BC. Horse racing was established in antiquity and formalised in Britain in the 17th and 18th centuries. The invention of the bicycle and the automobile in the 19th century created new disciplines, with the first organised motor race held in 1894 from Paris to Rouen.

Forms

Major categories include athletics (sprints, middle- and long-distance, marathons), cycling (road, track, cyclo-cross), motorsport (Formula One, rallying, NASCAR, endurance racing), equestrian racing, aquatic racing (rowing, canoeing, sailing, powerboating) and animal racing such as greyhound and camel racing.

Organisation

Races are typically governed by sanctioning bodies that set rules on equipment, courses and eligibility, and enforce anti-doping and safety standards. Timing technology ranges from hand-held stopwatches to transponder systems accurate to thousandths of a second.
