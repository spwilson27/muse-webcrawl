Personalized medicine

Personalized medicine, also called precision medicine, is a medical model that proposes customising healthcare — with decisions, treatments, practices, and products tailored to the individual patient — based on a person's genetic, environmental, and lifestyle characteristics. It seeks to select therapies according to predicted response or risk of disease, in contrast to a one-size-fits-all approach.

Methods and applications

The field draws on genomics, pharmacogenomics, molecular diagnostics, and medical imaging. Established applications include testing breast tumours for HER2 overexpression to select trastuzumab therapy, genotyping tumours for mutations such as EGFR in lung cancer to guide targeted drugs, and screening patients for genetic variants that affect drug metabolism (for example, before prescribing certain anticoagulants or the HIV drug abacavir). Companion diagnostics are frequently approved alongside the therapies they inform.

Challenges

Adoption is limited by the cost and interpretation of genetic testing, the need for large interoperable health-data systems, privacy concerns surrounding genomic information, and the risk that benefits accrue unevenly across populations, since genomic reference databases remain skewed toward participants of European ancestry.
