Santiago Jiménez Jr.

Santiago Jiménez Jr. (born April 4, 1944) is an American conjunto accordionist and vocalist from San Antonio, Texas. A son of the pioneering conjunto accordionist Don Santiago Jiménez Sr. and younger brother of Leonardo "Flaco" Jiménez, he is a leading exponent of traditional Texas-Mexican conjunto music, known for his two-row button accordion playing.

Life and career

Born into one of the great dynasties of conjunto music, Jiménez began playing professionally as a teenager, performing with his father's band. While his brother Flaco developed a more progressive, cross-genre style, Santiago Jr. devoted himself to preserving the classic conjunto sound of his father — accordion, bajo sexto, and tololoche (stand-up bass) — recording dozens of albums of polkas, rancheras, redovas, and huapangos.

Recognition

Jiménez has received two Grammy Award nominations for Best Mexican-American Album and, in 2000, was awarded the National Heritage Fellowship by the National Endowment for the Arts, the highest honour in folk and traditional arts in the United States. His albums include El Mero, Mero de San Antonio and recordings for the Arhoolie label.
