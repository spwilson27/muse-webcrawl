International Computers Limited

IndustryComputing
Founded1968
Defunct2002 (absorbed into Fujitsu)
HeadquartersLondon, United Kingdom

International Computers Limited (ICL) was a British computer hardware, software and services company, formed in 1968 by the merger of International Computers and Tabulators (ICT) and English Electric Computers, with encouragement from the British government, which wanted a national champion able to compete with IBM.

History

ICL's mainframe lines included the 1900 Series inherited from ICT and, from 1974, the 2900 Series running the VME operating system. The company supplied many UK government and large corporate systems and developed strengths in retail and local-government systems. It acquired the computer interests of Singer Business Machines and, in the US, bought into the market through acquisitions. In 1984 ICL was acquired by STC plc; in 1990 Fujitsu took a controlling stake, becoming sole owner in 1998. The ICL brand was retired in 2002 when the company was renamed Fujitsu Services.

Legacy

ICL is remembered for VME, the ICL 2900 and distributed systems such as the OSLAN networking architecture, and for its long-running role as a supplier to the British public sector.
