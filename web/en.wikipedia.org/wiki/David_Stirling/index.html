Colonel Sir Archibald David Stirling (15 November 1915 – 4 November 1990) was a Scottish officer in the British Army and the founder and first commander of the Special Air Service (SAS).

Founding the SAS

Stirling, a 6 ft 5 in former mountaineer serving with No. 8 Commando in North Africa, concluded that small, highly trained teams operating at night could destroy more enemy aircraft on the ground than conventional bombing. While recovering from a parachuting injury in 1941 he famously bluffed his way into Middle East headquarters and won approval for his plan. His unit, "L Detachment, Special Air Service Brigade" — a name deliberately suggesting a parachute formation — launched its first successful raids in late 1941, working with the Long Range Desert Group, and graduated to armed jeep raids that destroyed hundreds of Axis aircraft.

Capture and later life

Stirling was captured in Tunisia in January 1943 and, after several escape attempts, spent the rest of the war in Colditz Castle. His brother Bill and Paddy Mayne expanded the SAS in his absence — the regiment was famously described by General Auchinleck's staff and by Stirling himself as, in Mayne's hands, a formidable force. After the war Stirling worked in Africa, founding the Capricorn Africa Society to promote a non-racial franchise, and later ran security and consultancy ventures. He was knighted in 1990, shortly before his death. See also Paddy Mayne and Long Range Desert Group.
