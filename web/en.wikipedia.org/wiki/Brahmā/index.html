Brahma, the four-faced creator

Brahmā (Sanskrit: ब्रह्मा) is a Hindu god, referred to as "the Creator" within the Trimurti, the triple deity of supreme divinity alongside Vishnu, "the Preserver", and Shiva, "the Destroyer". He is associated with creation, knowledge and the Vedas, and is traditionally depicted with four faces looking in the four directions, four arms, a white beard, and seated on a lotus or riding his vehicle, the hamsa.

Origins and mythology

Brahma emerges in post-Vedic texts as the personal creator god, identified with the earlier figure Prajapati. In the Puranas he is born from a golden egg (Hiranyagarbha) or from a lotus springing from Vishnu's navel. His consort is Saraswati, goddess of knowledge and learning. According to tradition, Brahma's lifespan of 100 "Brahma years" defines the duration of the universe before it dissolves and is created anew; each Brahma day (kalpa) lasts 4.32 billion human years.

Worship

Despite his central role in cosmology, Brahma is worshipped far less than Vishnu or Shiva, and few temples are dedicated to him alone. The most famous is the Brahma Temple at Pushkar in Rajasthan, associated with the legend of Brahma's yajna there and his marriage to Savitri and Gayatri. Mythological explanations for his limited worship include curses by Shiva and by the sage Bhrigu.
