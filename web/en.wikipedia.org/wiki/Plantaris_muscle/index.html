Plantaris muscle

Origin: Lateral supracondylar ridge of femur
Insertion: Calcaneus (via calcaneal tendon region)
Artery: Sural arteries
Nerve: Tibial nerve (S1, S2)
Actions: Weak plantar flexion of ankle; knee flexion

The plantaris is a small muscle of the posterior compartment of the leg, notable for its tiny belly and disproportionately long, thin tendon — the longest tendon in the body relative to its muscle size. It is absent in roughly 10% of people and is generally considered vestigial in humans, though it is a powerful muscle in many other mammals.

Structure and function

The muscle arises from the lateral supracondylar ridge of the femur, just above the lateral head of gastrocnemius, and from the oblique popliteal ligament. Its belly, only 7–10 cm long, descends between the gastrocnemius and soleus, and its slender tendon runs down the medial border of the calcaneal (Achilles) tendon to insert on the calcaneus. Supplied by the tibial nerve, the plantaris weakly assists plantar flexion of the ankle and flexion of the knee, but contributes so little force that its tendon is routinely harvested for grafts in reconstructive surgery without measurable loss of function. Because it is richly supplied with proprioceptive receptors, some anatomists suggest it serves mainly as a sensory organ monitoring tension in the calf.

Clinical significance

Rupture of the plantaris tendon, usually during sudden pushing-off movements in sport, causes a sharp pain and popping sensation in the calf historically called "tennis leg" — a term now applied mainly to tears of the medial gastrocnemius. Plantaris rupture mimics the more serious Achilles tendon rupture but is distinguished on examination and imaging, and typically heals with conservative treatment.
