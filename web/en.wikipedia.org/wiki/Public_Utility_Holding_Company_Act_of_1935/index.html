Public Utility Holding Company Act of 1935

The Public Utility Holding Company Act of 1935 (PUHCA) was a US federal law enacted as part of the New Deal to regulate electric and gas utility holding companies. It required holding companies to register with the Securities and Exchange Commission and empowered the SEC to break up sprawling, non-contiguous utility empires so that each became a single integrated system.

History

The act followed abuses by pyramidal holding companies such as Samuel Insull's empire, whose collapse harmed investors. Its "death sentence clause" (Section 11) forced divestiture of unrelated and geographically scattered properties. PUHCA was repealed by the Energy Policy Act of 2005, effective 2006, and replaced by a narrower set of FERC and state access-to-books provisions.
