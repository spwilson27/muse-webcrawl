Match

A match is a tool for starting a fire. Typically, matches are made of small wooden sticks or stiff paper. One end is coated with a material that can be ignited by friction generated by striking the match against a suitable surface.

History

Early matches used sulfur and various chemical mixtures. The friction match was invented by English chemist John Walker in 1826. The safety match, which requires a special striking surface containing red phosphorus, was developed in Sweden in the mid-19th century by Gustaf Erik Pasch and Johan Edvard Lundström.

Types

Matches are broadly divided into safety matches, which can only be struck on a prepared surface, and strike-anywhere matches, which can be ignited by friction on many rough surfaces.
