Sacrilege

Sacrilege is the violation or injurious treatment of a sacred object, site or person. It can take the form of irreverence towards sacred persons, places and things, and is usually understood in a religious context. When the sacrilegious offence is verbal, it is called blasphemy; when physical, it is often called desecration. The word originates from the Latin sacrilegium, meaning "stealing sacred things", from sacer (sacred) and legere (to steal or gather).

Roman law

In Roman law, sacrilegium originally referred to the theft of sacred property from a temple. Under the Empire, the concept expanded to include offences against the emperor's divinity and, later, violations of imperial decrees, becoming associated with crimes against the state and religion.

Christianity

In Roman Catholic canon law, sacrilege is the profanation or unworthy treatment of sacred things, persons or places. It includes such acts as desecrating the Eucharist, violating consecrated places, and simony in some classifications. Canon law attaches penalties, including excommunication for certain offences such as the profanation of the consecrated species. Many Christian traditions distinguish sacrilege from blasphemy (speech against God) and heresy (false belief).

Other religions

Most religions treat the abuse of sacred objects and spaces as a grave offence. In Islam, desecration of the Qur'an or of mosques is considered a serious violation, and acts of sacrilege against religious symbols have historically provoked severe legal and social penalties in many societies. Modern secular states generally treat such acts under laws against vandalism, theft, hate crime or public order offences rather than under sacrilege as such.
