Arthur Surridge Hunt

Arthur Surridge Hunt (1 March 1871 – 18 June 1934) was an English papyrologist. With his friend and fellow Oxford scholar Bernard P. Grenfell, he excavated the ancient rubbish mounds of Oxyrhynchus in Egypt and recovered hundreds of thousands of papyrus fragments, transforming knowledge of the literature and everyday life of the Hellenistic and Roman worlds.

Life and excavations

Hunt was educated at Eastbourne College and The Queen's College, Oxford, where he met Grenfell. Working for the Egypt Exploration Fund, the two began digging at Oxyrhynchus in the winter of 1896–97 and almost at once struck vast deposits of papyri preserved in the dry rubbish heaps. Over successive seasons they shipped enormous quantities of fragments back to Oxford, including lost works of Greek literature, early Christian texts such as the "Sayings of Jesus" (the Logia), and documents of daily life — letters, tax receipts, contracts, and census returns.

The Oxyrhynchus Papyri

Grenfell and Hunt edited the first volumes of The Oxyrhynchus Papyri from 1898 onward, establishing the standards of modern papyrology. Discoveries attributed to their work include fragments of Sappho, Pindar, Menander, Euripides, and the Hellenica Oxyrhynchia, as well as the earliest extensive remains of early Christian writings. After Grenfell's death in 1926, Hunt continued the editorial work at Oxford, where he was professor of papyrology.

Legacy

The publication series Grenfell and Hunt founded continues today, and the collection — now numbering over half a million fragments — remains one of the largest papyrus archives in the world, held in the Sackler Library at Oxford.
