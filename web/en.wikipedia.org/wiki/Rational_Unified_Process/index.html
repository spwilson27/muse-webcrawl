Rational Unified Process

The Rational Unified Process (RUP) is an iterative software development process framework created by the Rational Software Corporation, later acquired by IBM. It is an adaptable process framework, intended to be tailored by development organisations to their projects. RUP is based on the Unified Process and the work of Ivar Jacobson, Grady Booch and James Rumbaugh, the creators of UML.

Phases

RUP divides a project into four phases:

  1. Inception — establish the business case, scope and feasibility.
  2. Elaboration — analyse the problem domain and establish a sound architecture.
  3. Construction — build and integrate the product.
  4. Transition — deliver the product to users.

Disciplines and practices

Across the phases run disciplines such as business modelling, requirements, analysis and design, implementation, test, deployment, configuration and change management, project management and environment. Its six best practices are: develop iteratively, manage requirements, use component-based architectures, model visually, verify quality continuously and control changes.

Influence

RUP was highly influential in the late 1990s and 2000s, and variants such as the Agile Unified Process and OpenUP simplified it for smaller teams. Agile methods such as Scrum later displaced it in much of industry.
