Chora Church

Chora Church
Location: Edirnekapı, Istanbul, Turkey
Style: Byzantine architecture
Known for: late Byzantine mosaics and frescoes
Other name: Kariye Camii

The Church of the Holy Saviour in Chora (Greek: Ἡ Μονὴ τῆς Χώρας), known as the Chora Church, is a medieval Eastern Orthodox church in the Edirnekapı neighborhood of Istanbul, Turkey. It is celebrated for its superb late Byzantine mosaics and frescoes, among the finest surviving examples of Byzantine art.

History

Chora means "in the country" in Greek, reflecting that the original church stood outside the early walls of Constantinople. The present building dates largely from the early 12th century, and its lavish decoration was commissioned by the scholar-statesman Theodore Metochites in 1316–1321.

Art

The mosaics narrate the lives of Christ and the Virgin Mary in remarkable detail, including the famous Anastasis (Resurrection) fresco in the parekklesion, which depicts Christ raising Adam and Eve from their tombs. After the Ottoman conquest the church was converted into a mosque (Kariye Camii) in the 16th century, and the images were covered with plaster; it served as a museum from 1958 following restoration and the uncovering of the mosaics, before being reconverted to a mosque in 2020.
