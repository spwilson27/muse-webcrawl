Dumfries and Galloway (Scots: Dumfries an Gallowa; Scottish Gaelic: Dùn Phrìs is Gall-Ghaidhealaibh) is one of the 32 unitary council areas of Scotland, located in the south-west of the country along the Solway Firth and the Irish Sea. It covers the historic counties of Dumfriesshire, Kirkcudbrightshire and Wigtownshire.

Geography

The region is one of Scotland's most rural and sparsely populated. The Southern Uplands cross it, including the Merrick (843 m), the highest peak in southern Scotland, within the Galloway Forest Park — designated in 2009 as the UK's first Dark Sky Park. Major rivers include the Nith, the Annan and the Cree. The main towns are Dumfries, Stranraer, Annan, Lockerbie, Castle Douglas and Kirkcudbright.

History and culture

Galloway was a distinct Gaelic-speaking lordship in the Middle Ages, and the region preserves strong local identities. Robert Burns spent his last years in Dumfries, and J. M. Barrie attended Dumfries Academy. The town of Wigtown is Scotland's national Book Town, and Kirkcudbright is known as an artists' town. Gretna Green, on the border with England, became famous for runaway marriages after 1754.

Economy and transport

Agriculture, forestry and tourism dominate the economy. Ferries to Northern Ireland sail from Cairnryan near Stranraer. The A75 road links the region to Carlisle and the ferry ports; Lockerbie is on the West Coast Main Line.
