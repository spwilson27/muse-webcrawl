Jules Maigret is a fictional French police detective (a commissaire of the Paris Police Judiciaire) created by the Belgian writer Georges Simenon. He is the protagonist of 75 novels and 28 short stories published between 1931 and 1972 — among the most extensive series in crime fiction — and has been portrayed by many actors on screen.

Character

Maigret is a large, patient, pipe-smoking man, usually seen in a heavy overcoat and bowler hat, working from offices at 36 Quai des Orfèvres in Paris. Happily married to the long-suffering but resourceful Louise, he has no children. His method is the opposite of the puzzle-solving detective: rather than deducing from clues, he immerses himself in the atmosphere of a place and the lives of the people involved, soaking up impressions until he understands — his aim, as he often insists, is less to judge than to comprehend how an ordinary life went wrong.

The novels

Simenon wrote the first Maigret novels at extraordinary speed beginning in 1930; Pietr le Letton (Pietr the Latvian) appeared in 1931. Nineteen novels appeared in the first burst (1931–1934), after which Simenon, believing he was moving on to "serious" literature, paused the series. He resumed it in 1942 and continued until Maigret and Monsieur Charles (1972). Standout titles include The Hanged Man of Saint-Pholien, A Man's Head, The Yellow Dog, Maigret's First Case and Maigret and the Saturday Caller.

Adaptations

Maigret has been played on French screens by Jean Gabin and Bruno Cremer, in Britain by Rupert Davies, Michael Gambon and Rowan Atkinson, and most recently by Benjamin Wainwright. The books have been translated worldwide and remain in print, with Penguin issuing retranslations of the full series.
