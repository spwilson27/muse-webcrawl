Data mart

A data mart is a structure or access pattern specific to data warehouse environments, used to retrieve client-facing data. It is a subset of the data warehouse, oriented to a specific business line or team — for example finance, marketing or sales — rather than to the enterprise as a whole.

Types

Data marts are classified by how they are built. Dependent data marts draw their data from a central warehouse, ensuring consistent definitions. Independent data marts are created directly from operational sources without a central warehouse, which is faster but can produce inconsistent "stovepipe" systems. Hybrid data marts combine inputs from a warehouse and other sources.

Design

Data marts usually implement a dimensional model — a star or snowflake schema — optimised for fast analytical queries (OLAP) rather than transaction processing. Proponents of the warehouse-first approach, such as Bill Inmon, treat marts as departmental views of the enterprise warehouse; Ralph Kimball's bottom-up approach builds the warehouse as the union of conformed data marts.

Benefits

Because they are smaller and focused, data marts are cheaper and quicker to implement than a full warehouse, give departments control over their own data, and improve query performance for their users.
