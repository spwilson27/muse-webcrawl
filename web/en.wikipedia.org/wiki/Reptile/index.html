Reptile

Most reptiles are ectothermic.

Reptiles are tetrapod vertebrates of the class Reptilia: turtles, crocodilians, lizards, snakes, the tuatara and the extinct dinosaurs and marine reptiles. About 12,000 living species are recognised; traditionally the group excluded birds, though by descent birds are reptiles (see Clade).

Biology

Reptiles are mostly ectothermic (‘cold-blooded’), breathing air, with dry scaly skin and amniotic eggs that freed them from water for reproduction. They range from geckos to the saltwater Crocodile and the Komodo dragon; a few (leatherback turtles, some dinosaurs) achieved partial warm-bloodedness.

Evolution

Reptiles arose in the Carboniferous (~320 million years ago) and dominated land in the Mesozoic ‘Age of Reptiles’. Today's groups are relicts of a far richer fossil diversity — ichthyosaurs, plesiosaurs, pterosaurs (flying, though not reptiles proper but close kin) and non-avian dinosaurs like Triceratops.
