The Waal is the main distributary branch of the river Rhine flowing approximately 80 km (50 mi) through the Netherlands. It is the major waterway connecting the port of Rotterdam to Germany. Before it reaches Rotterdam, it joins the Afgedamde Maas near Woudrichem to form the Merwede (roughly, the upper portion of the Nieuwe Merwede).

The Waal carries about two-thirds of the Rhine's total flow, making it the Rhine's largest branch in the Rhine–Meuse–Scheldt delta. The cities of Nijmegen, Tiel and Zaltbommel lie on its banks. The river is heavily used by inland shipping between Rotterdam and the German industrial regions of the Ruhr.

History

The name Waal, sometimes written as Vahal in Latin texts, is of Germanic origin and derives from the many meanders the river once had (Old Dutch: waha, "crooked"). The main channel became the principal branch of the Rhine after flood-control works in the Middle Ages and, especially, after the St. Elizabeth's flood of 1421 redirected much of the Meuse's flow.

During the Second World War, the Waal at Nijmegen was a key objective of Operation Market Garden in September 1944. American paratroopers of the 82nd Airborne Division crossed the river in canvas boats under fire — the famous "Waal crossing" — to capture the Nijmegen road bridge.

Flood management

The Waal's discharge capacity has been a central concern of Dutch water management. Under the national Room for the River programme, completed in the 2010s, the river near Nijmegen was given more space: the dike was moved inland and an ancillary channel was dug through the floodplain, creating a river island and lowering flood levels.
