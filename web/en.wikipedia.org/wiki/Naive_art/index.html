Naïve art

Naïve art is art created by artists who lack (or reject) formal training, characterised by simplicity of technique and subject matter, bright colours, flattened perspective, and a childlike freshness of vision.

Characteristics

Naïve works typically ignore academic rules of perspective, proportion and chiaroscuro, instead using strong patterning, meticulous detail, and direct, often idyllic depictions of everyday life.

Notable artists

The French postman-turned-painter Henri Rousseau is the most famous naïve artist; others include Grandma Moses (Anna Mary Robertson Moses) in the United States, Niko Pirosmani in Georgia, and the Hlebine School painters of Croatia.
