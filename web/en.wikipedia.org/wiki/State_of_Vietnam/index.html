State of Vietnam

Native name: Quốc gia Việt Nam
Existed: 1949–1955
Capital: Saigon
Chief of State: Bảo Đại (1949–1955)
Prime Minister: Ngô Đình Diệm (1954–1955, last)
Status: Associated state of the French Union
Succeeded by: Republic of Vietnam (South Vietnam)

The State of Vietnam (Vietnamese: Quốc gia Việt Nam) was a state in Southeast Asia that existed from 1949 to 1955, governing — in rivalry with the communist Democratic Republic of Vietnam — the territory that became South Vietnam. It was created by France under the Élysée Accords of 8 March 1949 as an "associated state" within the French Union, with the former emperor Bảo Đại as Chief of State, as France's answer to Hồ Chí Minh's Democratic Republic proclaimed in 1945. Its flag — a yellow field with three horizontal red stripes — later became the flag of South Vietnam and remains a symbol of the Vietnamese diaspora.

History

Following the outbreak of the First Indochina War, France sought to build a non-communist Vietnamese alternative. After negotiations, Bảo Đại returned from exile and took office in July 1949, heading a government based in Saigon that was recognised by the United States and the United Kingdom in 1950 but hampered by limited real autonomy, continuing French control of finance and defence, and competition from religious-political sects (Cao Đài, Hòa Hảo) and the Bình Xuyên syndicate.

After the French defeat at Điện Biên Phủ and the Geneva Accords of 1954 partitioned Vietnam at the 17th parallel, the State of Vietnam governed the south. Prime Minister Ngô Đình Diệm, appointed in 1954 with American backing, consolidated power in the Battle for Saigon (1955) against the sects and Bình Xuyên, then deposed Bảo Đại in the referendum of 23 October 1955 — a vote widely regarded as fraudulent — and proclaimed the Republic of Vietnam on 26 October 1955, with himself as president. The defunct State of Vietnam's institutions, army (the Vietnamese National Army) and flag passed to the new republic, which survived until 1975.
