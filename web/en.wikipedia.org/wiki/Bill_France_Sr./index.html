William Henry Getty France (September 26, 1909 – June 7, 1992), commonly known as Bill France Sr. or Big Bill, was an American racing driver and businessman who co-founded and managed NASCAR, the sanctioning body of American stock car racing.

Early life

France was born in Washington, D.C., the son of a bank teller. He skipped school as a teenager to race at local dirt tracks and worked as a mechanic and at a service station. In 1934, seeking warmer weather and better opportunities, he and his wife Anne moved to Daytona Beach, Florida, where he worked painting houses and at a local automobile dealership before opening his own service station.

Founding NASCAR

France participated in racing on the Daytona Beach and Road Course and promoted events there from 1938. On December 14, 1947, he convened a meeting of drivers, car owners and promoters at the Streamline Hotel in Daytona Beach to standardise rules for stock car racing. The result was the National Association for Stock Car Auto Racing — NASCAR — incorporated on February 21, 1948, with France as its first president.

France was central to the construction of Daytona International Speedway, which opened in 1959 with the first Daytona 500, and later Talladega Superspeedway in Alabama. He ruled NASCAR with a firm hand for nearly three decades, weathering the 1969 drivers' boycott at Talladega and keeping the France family in control of the sport. He stepped down as NASCAR president in 1972 in favour of his son, Bill France Jr., while remaining head of International Speedway Corporation.

Legacy

France was inducted into the International Motorsports Hall of Fame in 1990 and was a member of the inaugural class of the NASCAR Hall of Fame in 2010. He died of Alzheimer's disease in 1992.
