Downtempo

Downtempo (also called downbeat) is a broad genre of electronic music characterised by slow tempos, relaxed grooves and an atmospheric, often melancholic mood. Emerging in the late 1980s and 1990s from chill-out rooms in clubs and from the Bristol trip-hop scene, downtempo typically features tempos below about 110 beats per minute, hip-hop-derived beats, dub basslines, samples and elements of jazz, funk, ambient and world music.

History

The style grew out of the "chill-out" spaces of late-1980s acid house clubs, where DJs played slower, ambient mixes for dancers to recover. In the 1990s acts such as Massive Attack, Portishead and Tricky defined the related trip hop sound, while artists like Kruder & Dorfmeister, Thievery Corporation, Air, Zero 7, Morcheeba and Bonobo brought downtempo to a wide audience through albums and compilation series such as Café del Mar and Buddha-Bar.

Characteristics

Downtempo productions favour texture and mood over dancefloor energy: warm analogue timbres, vinyl crackle, sparse vocals and downtempo rhythms influenced by dub reggae and instrumental hip hop. The genre overlaps with trip hop, chillwave, lo-fi hip hop and ambient music, and is widely used in cafés, lounges and film and advertising soundtracks.
