The 2004 United States presidential election was the 55th quadrennial presidential election, held on Tuesday, 2 November 2004. Incumbent Republican President George W. Bush and Vice President Dick Cheney defeated Democratic Senator John Kerry of Massachusetts and his running mate, Senator John Edwards of North Carolina.

The election was dominated by the Iraq War, which Bush had launched in 2003, and by issues of national security following the September 11 attacks. Kerry attacked the conduct of the war and Bush's economic record, while the Bush campaign portrayed Kerry as inconsistent — a "flip-flopper" — and emphasised social issues and "moral values".

Result

Bush won 286 electoral votes to Kerry's 251 and, unlike in 2000, carried the popular vote by about three million votes. The result hinged on Ohio, whose 20 electoral votes Bush won by roughly 118,000 votes; Kerry conceded the day after the election. Bush's coattails helped Republicans increase their majorities in both houses of Congress.
