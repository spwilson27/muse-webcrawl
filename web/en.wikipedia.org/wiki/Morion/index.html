Morion

A morion (Spanish: morrión) is a type of open-faced combat helmet originally from the Kingdom of Castile (Spain), used from the middle of the 16th to the early 17th century. It is strongly associated in popular imagery with the Spanish conquistadors and early colonists of the Americas.

Design

The morion was typically forged from a single plate of steel and was characterised by a skull with a raised central comb and a broad, flat brim that curved up at the front and back, deflecting downward blows and offering shade and protection from projectiles and the sun. Two main forms existed: the Spanish "comb morion" with a high crest, and the "Spanish morion" with a lower, almond-shaped skull.

Use

Morions were standard issue for infantry, especially pikemen, musketeers, and arquebusiers, across Spain, Italy, Germany, England, and other European armies. They were cheap to produce, provided good protection, and left the wearer's vision and hearing unobstructed. The helmet remained in use by guards and ceremonial units long after it disappeared from the battlefield.
