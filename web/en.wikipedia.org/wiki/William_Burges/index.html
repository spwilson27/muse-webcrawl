William Burges

William Burges (2 December 1827 – 20 April 1881) was an English architect and designer, one of the leading figures of the Gothic Revival. Working in a vigorous early French Gothic style, he produced some of the most elaborate and fantastical buildings and interiors of the Victorian era, most famously for the 3rd Marquess of Bute at Cardiff Castle and Castell Coch.

Career

The son of a wealthy civil engineer, Burges studied engineering briefly before turning to architecture, articling with Edward Blore and later working with Matthew Digby Wyatt. He travelled extensively in France and Italy, filling sketchbooks with medieval architecture and ornament. His first major commission was St Fin Barre's Cathedral in Cork, Ireland, won in competition in 1863, which he designed as a richly sculpted early French Gothic cathedral.

Burges's most important patron was John Crichton-Stuart, 3rd Marquess of Bute, reputedly the richest man in Britain. From 1865 Burges transformed Cardiff Castle into a palace of medieval fantasy, with themed rooms covered in carving, painting, stained glass, and fireplaces populated by sculpted figures. He rebuilt the ruined Castell Coch as a romantic summer retreat, and built his own London home, the Tower House in Kensington, as a showcase of his art. Burges also designed furniture, metalwork, jewellery, and stained glass in a total-work-of-art approach.

Burges died in 1881, aged 53; several works were completed by his assistants, notably William Frame. Long regarded as an eccentric, his work was reappraised in the later twentieth century, and his buildings are now considered masterpieces of High Victorian design.
