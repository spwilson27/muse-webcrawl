Kapa haka

Kapa haka is the term for Māori performing arts in New Zealand, encompassing group song, dance, chant and movement — including the famous haka — performed standing in rows (kapa means a rank or line of performers). It is a central expression of Māori cultural identity, practised in schools, communities and national competitions.

Elements of performance

A competitive bracket typically includes: whakaeke (entrance), mōteatea (traditional chant preserving tribal history), waiata tautoko (supporting song), haka (posture dance), poi (swinging ball dance), waiata-ā-ringa (action song), and whakawātea (exit). Performers use expressive facial gestures — whetero (tongue extended) and pūkana (wide-open eyes) — and wiri, the trembling of the hands. Traditional dress includes flax cloaks (korowai), piupiu (rolled flax skirts) and tā moko designs.

Competitions

The premier event is Te Matatini, the national kapa haka championships held every two years since 1972, drawing the best senior groups (kapa) from across Aotearoa and Australia before large live and broadcast audiences. Regional competitions and the national secondary schools festival feed into it. Exponents such as Ngāpo and Pimia Wehi shaped the modern art form.

Cultural role

Kapa haka transmits the Māori language (te reo), genealogy (whakapapa), history and values, and features prominently at official welcomes, commemorations such as Anzac Day, and international performances. The haka of the All Blacks rugby team, "Ka Mate" (composed by Te Rauparaha of Ngāti Toa), is its most globally recognised fragment.
