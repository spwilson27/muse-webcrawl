The Government Code and Cypher School (GC&CS) was the British signals intelligence and code-breaking organisation responsible for intercepting and decrypting foreign communications and for advising on the security of British ciphers. Formed in 1919 from the wartime units Room 40 (Admiralty) and MI1b (War Office), it is best known for its achievements at Bletchley Park during the Second World War.

Second World War

At Bletchley Park, GC&CS cryptanalysts — including Alan Turing, Gordon Welchman, Dilly Knox, and Joan Clarke — broke the German Enigma machine ciphers and the Lorenz teleprinter cipher used by the German high command. The intelligence produced, codenamed Ultra, was credited with shortening the war, particularly in the Battle of the Atlantic and the campaigns in North Africa and Normandy. The electromechanical Bombe and the electronic Colossus computers were built to support this work.

Post-war

In 1946 GC&CS was renamed the Government Communications Headquarters (GCHQ) and eventually moved to Cheltenham. The work of Bletchley Park remained officially secret until the 1970s, when publication of accounts of Ultra brought GC&CS widespread public recognition.
