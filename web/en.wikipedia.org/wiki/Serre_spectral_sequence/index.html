Serre spectral sequence

In algebraic topology, the Serre spectral sequence (earlier called the Leray–Serre spectral sequence) is a spectral sequence for computing the (co)homology of a total space in terms of the (co)homology of its base space and fibre. It was introduced by Jean-Pierre Serre in his 1951 doctoral thesis, building on the work of Jean Leray, and became one of the foundational computational tools of homotopy theory.

Statement

Given a fibration F → E → B with B path-connected, there is a spectral sequence with E₂ page given by the (co)homology of B with coefficients in the (co)homology of F (possibly twisted by local coefficients from the action of π₁(B)), converging to the (co)homology of E. When B is simply connected the coefficients are untwisted and the E₂ page is simply a tensor product of the groups of base and fibre.

Applications

Serre used the cohomological spectral sequence to compute homotopy groups of spheres, proving for instance that πn+k(Sn) is finite for k > 0 (except for the cases that contain a copy of Z), a result that launched modern homotopy theory. The sequence remains a standard method for computing the homology of loop spaces, classifying spaces and fibre bundles.
