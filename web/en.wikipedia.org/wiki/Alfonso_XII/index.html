Alfonso XII (28 November 1857 – 25 November 1885), known as the Peacemaker, was King of Spain from 1874 until his death in 1885. His reign restored the Bourbon monarchy after the collapse of the First Spanish Republic and brought an end to the Third Carlist War.

Early life and accession

Alfonso was the son of Queen Isabella II. When his mother was deposed in the Glorious Revolution of 1868, the royal family went into exile; Alfonso was educated in Paris, Vienna and at the Royal Military College, Sandhurst, in England. Isabella abdicated in his favour in 1870. After the Sandhurst Manifesto (1874), in which he promised to be "a good Spaniard, a good Catholic and a true liberal", a military pronunciamiento by General Martínez Campos proclaimed him king in December 1874, beginning the Bourbon Restoration.

Reign

Under the political system designed by his chief minister Antonio Cánovas del Castillo — embodied in the Constitution of 1876 and the turno pacífico alternation of Conservative and Liberal governments — Spain enjoyed relative stability. Alfonso personally took the field against the Carlists and was present at the end of the Third Carlist War in 1876, and the Ten Years' War in Cuba ended with the Pact of Zanjón in 1878. His first marriage, to his cousin Mercedes of Orléans in 1878, was a popular love match cut short by her death five months later. He remarried, to Maria Christina of Austria, in 1879.

Death and succession

Alfonso died of tuberculosis at El Pardo on 25 November 1885, three days before his 28th birthday. His widow Maria Christina acted as regent, and their son, born after his death, succeeded as Alfonso XIII.
