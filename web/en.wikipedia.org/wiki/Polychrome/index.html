Polychrome

Polychrome is the practice of decorating architectural elements, sculpture or pottery in multiple colours, as opposed to monochrome. Ancient Greek and Roman statues, Egyptian art and medieval sculpture were originally brightly painted.

Polychrome

History

Polychromy was widespread in antiquity — e.g. the Alexander Sarcophagus, Chinese terracotta warriors, and Mesoamerican temples. Renaissance and neoclassical taste favoured white marble, suppressing knowledge of painted originals until 19th–20th century research and exhibitions such as Gods in Color.

Techniques

Pigments, gilding, encaustic and coloured materials (glass, stone inlay) were applied. Modern conservation uses UV and microscopy to detect traces.
