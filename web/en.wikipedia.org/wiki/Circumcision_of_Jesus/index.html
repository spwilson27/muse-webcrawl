Circumcision of Jesus

The circumcision of Jesus is an event from the life of Jesus, according to the Gospel of Luke, which states in verse 2:21 that Jesus was circumcised eight days after his birth (traditionally January 1). This is in keeping with the Jewish law which holds that males should be circumcised eight days after birth during a Brit milah ceremony, at which they are also given their name.

Feast

The Feast of the Circumcision of Christ is a Christian celebration of the circumcision, eight days (counting inclusively) after his birth, the occasion on which the child was formally given his name. The feast day has been celebrated on 1 January in many churches, including in the Catholic Church, where it is now observed as the Solemnity of Mary, Mother of God.

In art

The event was a common subject in Christian art from the 10th century onwards, one of several events in the Life of Jesus to be frequently depicted by artists. It was initially only seen as a scene in larger cycles, but by the Renaissance might be treated as an individual subject for a painting or become the central scene in an altarpiece.
