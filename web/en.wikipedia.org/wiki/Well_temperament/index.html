Well temperament

Well temperament (also good temperament, or in German wohltemperiert) is a system of musical tuning in which the twelve notes of the octave are tuned so that music can be played in all major and minor keys without any key sounding unacceptably out of tune. Well temperaments are "circulating" temperaments: unlike meantone temperament, which made distant keys unusable because of the wolf interval, a well temperament distributes the comma unevenly around the circle of fifths, so each key has a slightly different colour while all remain usable.

Historical systems

Many specific well temperaments were described in the 17th and 18th centuries, including those of Andreas Werckmeister (1691), Johann Philipp Kirnberger, Francesco Antonio Vallotti, Thomas Young and Johann Georg Neidhardt. In Werckmeister III, for instance, some fifths are pure and others are tempered by a quarter of the Pythagorean comma, producing near-pure thirds in the common keys and more pungent intervals in remote keys.

Bach and The Well-Tempered Clavier

J. S. Bach's The Well-Tempered Clavier (1722 and c. 1742), with preludes and fugues in all 24 major and minor keys, demonstrated the musical possibilities of a well-tempered tuning. Scholars debate exactly which temperament Bach intended; it was certainly not the modern twelve-tone equal temperament, which only became standard in the 19th and 20th centuries. In equal temperament all fifths are equally narrowed and all keys sound alike, whereas well temperament preserves a gradation of key character that many historically informed performers seek to recover.
