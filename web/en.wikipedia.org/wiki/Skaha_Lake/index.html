Skaha Lake

Skaha Lake
Location: Okanagan Valley, British Columbia, Canada
Length: ~11.8 km
Surface area: ~19.9 km²
Outflow: Okanagan River

Skaha Lake is a lake in the Okanagan Valley of south-central British Columbia, Canada, lying between the city of Penticton at its northern end and the community of Okanagan Falls at its southern end. It is part of the Okanagan River system, receiving water from Okanagan Lake via the channelled Okanagan River at Penticton and draining southward through Okanagan Falls toward Osoyoos Lake.

Geography

The lake is about 11.8 kilometres long and covers roughly 20 square kilometres. Its shores include the village of Kaleden on the western side. The name Skaha derives from the Okanagan language and means "dog" (or "horse"); the lake was historically also known as Dog Lake.

Recreation and ecology

Skaha Lake is a popular recreation destination with beaches, boating, and the Skaha Bluffs climbing area nearby, a well-known sport climbing site. The lake supports populations of kokanee and rainbow trout, and its inlet channel at Penticton is used for floating recreation in summer. Efforts to restore sockeye salmon to the Okanagan system have included the lakes of the basin, supported by the Okanagan Nation Alliance.
