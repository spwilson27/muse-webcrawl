Niantic, Inc. is an American software development company based in San Francisco, best known for developing the augmented reality mobile games Ingress (2012) and Pokémon Go (2016), the latter becoming one of the most successful mobile games of all time and a global cultural phenomenon.

History

The company began in 2010 as Niantic Labs, an internal start-up within Google founded by John Hanke, who had previously led Google's Geo division and co-founded Keyhole, the company behind Google Earth. Its first product was Field Trip, a location-based app for discovering local points of interest, followed by the science-fiction AR game Ingress, whose player-mapped portal network became the location database for later titles.

In 2015 Niantic was spun out as an independent company during Google's restructuring into Alphabet, with investment from Google, Nintendo and The Pokémon Company. Pokémon Go, released in July 2016 in partnership with The Pokémon Company, was downloaded hundreds of millions of times and generated billions of dollars in revenue. Later titles included Harry Potter: Wizards Unite (2019, shut down in 2022), Pikmin Bloom (2021) and Monster Hunter Now (2023).

Technology

Niantic developed the Lightship platform, an augmented-reality development kit built around a large-scale visual positioning system and a 3D map of the world built from player-contributed scans, positioning the company as an AR platform provider as well as a game studio. In 2025 Niantic announced a major restructuring, selling its games business to Scopely and spinning off its geospatial AI work as Niantic Spatial.
