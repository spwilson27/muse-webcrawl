Mirabilis

Mirabilis is a genus of flowering plants in the four-o'clock family Nyctaginaceae, native to the Americas, with a centre of diversity in the southwestern United States and Mexico; one species, the four-o'clock flower Mirabilis jalapa, is cultivated worldwide as an ornamental. The genus contains roughly fifty to sixty species of herbs, often with tuberous roots and showy, trumpet-shaped flowers that open in the late afternoon.

Notable species

  • Mirabilis jalapa — four-o'clock flower or marvel of Peru; famous in genetics because crosses between differently coloured flowers produce variegated offspring, demonstrating incomplete dominance and cytoplasmic (plastid) inheritance.
  • Mirabilis multiflora — Colorado four-o'clock, a robust desert perennial of the American Southwest.
  • Mirabilis longiflora — sweet four-o'clock, with long, night-fragrant white flowers pollinated by hawk moths.
  • Mirabilis expansa — mauka or chago, an Andean root crop.

Description and cultivation

The apparent "petals" of Mirabilis flowers are in fact a petaloid calyx; the true perianth is surrounded by an involucre of bracts that can make a cluster of flowers look like one. Flowers open in late afternoon and often close by the following morning, giving rise to the name "four o'clock". Plants are easy to grow in full sun, self-seed prolifically, and develop black, wrinkled, single-seeded fruits (anthocarps). Seeds and roots are reported to be poisonous if ingested. Carl Linnaeus gave the genus its name — mirabilis means "wonderful" in Latin — in reference to M. jalapa.
