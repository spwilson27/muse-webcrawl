Lý Thái Tổ

Lý Thái Tổ (8 March 974 – 31 March 1028), personal name Lý Công Uẩn, was the founding emperor of the Lý dynasty of Vietnam, reigning from 1009 to 1028. A former commander of the palace guard under the Early Lê dynasty, he is celebrated for moving the capital to the site of modern Hanoi.

Rise to power

Orphaned young and raised in a Buddhist pagoda, Lý Công Uẩn rose through the ranks of the court guard. When the Lê emperor Lê Long Đĩnh died in 1009, the court and military acclaimed Lý Công Uẩn as emperor, ending the Early Lê dynasty with the support of the powerful monk Vạn Hạnh.

Reign

In 1010 Lý Thái Tổ issued the Edict on the Transfer of the Capital, relocating the seat of government from Hoa Lư to the citadel of Đại La, which he renamed Thăng Long ("Ascending Dragon") — the city that became Hanoi. He reformed taxation, granted amnesties, built temples and palaces, and organised the kingdom into administrative circuits. His reign is regarded as the start of a golden age of Đại Việt, and the Lý dynasty he founded lasted for more than two centuries, until 1225. A bronze statue of the emperor stands prominently beside Hoàn Kiếm Lake in Hanoi.
