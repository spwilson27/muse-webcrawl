The Boeing B-29 Superfortress is an American four-engined propeller-driven heavy bomber designed by Boeing and flown primarily by the United States during World War II and the Korean War. One of the largest aircraft operational during World War II, the B-29 was among the most advanced bombers of its era, featuring a pressurised cabin, dual-wheeled tricycle landing gear, and an electronic fire-control system that permitted the crew to remotely operate its gun turrets.

Design and development

The B-29 originated in a 1940 United States Army Air Corps requirement for a long-range "superbomber" able to carry heavy bomb loads across the Pacific. Boeing's Model 345 was chosen, and the prototype XB-29 first flew on 21 September 1942. The programme was enormously complex and expensive — ultimately costing more than the Manhattan Project — and was plagued early on by fires in its Wright R-3350 engines.

Operational history

The B-29 entered combat in 1944, first from bases in India and China under Operation Matterhorn, and then, far more effectively, from the Mariana Islands, from where it conducted the firebombing campaign against Japanese cities, including the raid on Tokyo of 9–10 March 1945. On 6 August 1945 the B-29 Enola Gay dropped the "Little Boy" atomic bomb on Hiroshima, and on 9 August Bockscar dropped "Fat Man" on Nagasaki.

B-29s returned to combat in the Korean War (1950–53), flying bombing missions until MiG-15 jet fighters forced them largely to night operations. Variants served afterwards as aerial tankers (KB-29), weather reconnaissance aircraft, and motherships for experimental aircraft such as the Bell X-1. The Soviet Tupolev Tu-4 was an unlicensed reverse-engineered copy built from interned B-29s. The last USAF B-29s were retired in 1960; two examples, Fifi and Doc, remain airworthy.

Specifications

The B-29 had a crew of eleven, a length of 30.18 m (99 ft), a wingspan of 43.05 m (141 ft 3 in), a maximum speed of about 574 km/h (357 mph), a combat range of roughly 5,230 km (3,250 mi), and a bomb load of up to 9,100 kg (20,000 lb).
