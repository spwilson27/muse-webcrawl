The Hafsid dynasty (Arabic: الحفصيون, al-Ḥafṣiyyūn) was a Sunni Muslim dynasty of Berber descent that ruled Ifriqiya from 1229 to 1574. Descended from the Almohad governor Abu Hafs Umar ibn Yahya, the Hafsids declared independence from the declining Almohad Caliphate and made Tunis their capital.

History

Under Abu Zakariya (r. 1229–1249) and his son Muhammad I al-Mustansir, who briefly claimed the caliphate after the fall of Baghdad in 1258, the Hafsid state prospered through trade with Europe and sub-Saharan Africa. The Eighth Crusade of Louis IX ended at Tunis in 1270. Despite dynastic struggles and pressure from the Marinids, the Hafsids endured, though piracy and Ottoman–Spanish rivalry weakened them in the 16th century; Tunis finally fell to the Ottomans in 1574.

Culture

Hafsid Tunis was a centre of learning and architecture; the sultanate maintained lively diplomatic and commercial relations with Aragon, Sicily, Venice and Genoa.
