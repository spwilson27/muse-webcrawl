The grey heron (Ardea cinerea) is a long-legged wading bird of the heron family, Ardeidae, native throughout temperate Europe and Asia and also parts of Africa. It is a resident bird in much of its range, though northern populations migrate south in winter.

Description

It is a large bird, standing up to 100 cm tall and measuring 84–102 cm in length with a wingspan of 155–195 cm. The plumage is largely ash-grey above and greyish-white below, with a white head and neck, a black eyebrow extending into slender plumes, and a dagger-like yellow bill that becomes orange in the breeding season. In flight the neck is retracted into an S-shape, as is characteristic of herons.

Behaviour and diet

Grey herons feed in shallow water, standing motionless for long periods before striking with the bill at fish, amphibians, small mammals, insects and other prey. They forage in lakes, rivers, marshes, estuaries and even garden ponds. Their call is a loud, harsh croaking "fraaanh".

Breeding

The species breeds colonially in heronries, usually building large stick nests high in trees near water, though reedbeds and cliffs are also used. The female lays three to five pale blue-green eggs. Colonies may hold dozens of nests and be occupied for many decades.
