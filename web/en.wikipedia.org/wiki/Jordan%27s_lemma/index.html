Jordan's lemma

In complex analysis, Jordan's lemma, named after Camille Jordan, is a result frequently used in conjunction with the residue theorem to evaluate contour integrals and improper integrals. It provides an upper bound for the integral of certain functions over a semicircular arc in the upper half-plane, showing that the contribution of the arc vanishes as its radius tends to infinity.

Statement

If a continuous function f is defined on a semicircular contour of large radius in the upper half-plane and can be written in a form involving an exponential eiaz with a > 0 multiplied by a function tending uniformly to zero on the arc, then the integral over the arc tends to zero as the radius grows without bound.

Applications

The lemma is a standard tool for computing real improper integrals, such as integrals of rational functions multiplied by sines or cosines, by closing the contour in the appropriate half-plane and applying the residue theorem.
