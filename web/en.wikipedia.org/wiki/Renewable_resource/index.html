Renewable resource

A renewable resource, also known as a flow resource, is a natural resource that replenishes to overcome resource depletion caused by usage and consumption, either through biological reproduction or other naturally recurring processes, within a finite, humanly relevant timescale.

Examples

Renewable resources include sunlight, wind, water (hydropower and tides), geothermal heat, wood and other biomass, and agricultural products. Renewable energy sources derived from these flows are contrasted with non-renewable fossil fuels and nuclear fuels, which are consumed far faster than they form.

Sustainability

A renewable resource is not inexhaustible: if harvested faster than it regenerates — as in overfishing, deforestation, or aquifer overdraft — it can be depleted or degraded. Sustainable yield describes the maximum harvest rate consistent with regeneration. Soil, fresh water, forests, and fisheries are all renewable but conditionally so.

Management

Policies for renewable resources include quotas, protected areas, reforestation, and certification schemes such as those of the Forest Stewardship Council and Marine Stewardship Council.
