The King Baudouin Stadium (French: Stade Roi Baudouin; Dutch: Koning Boudewijnstadion) is a sports ground in north-west Brussels, Belgium, located in the Heysel section of the city. It is the largest stadium in Belgium by capacity and serves as the home of the Belgium national football team. The stadium stands on the site of the earlier Heysel Stadium and is named after King Baudouin, who reigned from 1951 until his death in 1993.

History

The original stadium on the Heysel site opened in 1930 as the Stade du Centenaire for the centenary of Belgian independence, and it hosted matches of the 1935 Brussels International Exposition. It was renamed Heysel Stadium after the Second World War and staged numerous European Cup finals, including several European Cup and Cup Winners' Cup finals, as well as matches at UEFA Euro 1972.

On 29 May 1985 the stadium was the scene of the Heysel Stadium disaster, when 39 people, mostly supporters of the Italian club Juventus, died before the European Cup final against Liverpool. Following the tragedy the stadium was largely demolished except for the athletics track, and a new stadium was built on the site. The rebuilt venue opened in 1995 under its present name.

Events

The stadium hosted the final of UEFA Euro 2000, co-hosted by Belgium and the Netherlands, in which France defeated Italy. It has also hosted the Memorial Van Damme athletics meeting of the Diamond League, cup finals of Belgian domestic football, and major concerts by international artists. Plans for a new national stadium elsewhere in Brussels have been discussed repeatedly, but the King Baudouin Stadium remains Belgium's principal national venue.
