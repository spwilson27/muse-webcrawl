Tteok

Type:Rice cake
Place of origin:Korea
Main ingredients:Rice flour (glutinous or non-glutinous)

Tteok (Korean: 떡) is a class of Korean rice cakes made with steamed flour of various grains, principally glutinous or non-glutinous rice. Hundreds of varieties exist, differing by region, season, and occasion: tteok are eaten as everyday snacks, as ceremonial foods at weddings, birthdays (notably the 100th-day baekil and first-birthday doljanchi celebrations), ancestral rites, and holidays such as Seollal (Lunar New Year) and Chuseok (harvest festival).

Types and preparation

Tteok are commonly grouped by method: steamed tteok (such as siruttteok, layered with red-bean crumbs, and the rainbow-layered mujigae-tteok); pounded tteok (injolmi, coated in bean powder, and long cylinder-shaped garae-tteok sliced into coin shapes for tteokguk soup); shaped and boiled tteok (songpyeon, half-moon cakes filled with sesame and honey and steamed over pine needles for Chuseok); and pan-fried flower tteok (hwajeon). They may be flavoured and coloured with mugwort, pumpkin, cactus, berries, and other natural ingredients.

In Korean cuisine

Beyond ceremonial use, tteok appear in everyday dishes such as tteokbokki, in which tube-shaped rice cakes are simmered in a spicy gochujang sauce — one of Korea's most popular street foods — and tteokguk, the rice-cake soup traditionally eaten at New Year, symbolically marking the gaining of a year of age. Fresh tteok harden quickly and are best eaten the day they are made; traditionally they were prepared in specialised shops called tteokjip.
