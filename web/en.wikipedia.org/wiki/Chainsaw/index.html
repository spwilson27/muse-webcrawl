Chainsaw

A chainsaw (or chain saw) is a portable power tool with a set of teeth attached to a rotating chain that runs along a guide bar. It is used in activities such as tree felling, limbing, bucking, pruning, cutting firebreaks in wildland fire suppression, and harvesting firewood. Chainsaws with specially designed bar-and-chain combinations have been developed as tools for use in chainsaw art and chainsaw mills. Most chainsaws are powered by two-stroke gasoline engines or electric motors, although early models were pneumatic or, in the nineteenth century, steam-driven.

Construction

A chainsaw consists of an engine or motor, a drive mechanism with a centrifugal clutch and sprocket, a guide bar — a long steel bar with a groove around its edge — and the saw chain, a loop of riveted metal links carrying small sharp cutting teeth, rakers and drive links. Each cutter is essentially a small L-shaped chisel of chromium-plated steel with a corner angle optimised for the type of wood. While cutting, oil from a dedicated reservoir is continuously fed onto the bar to lubricate the chain. Safety features on modern saws include a chain brake that stops the chain when kickback is detected, anti-vibration mountings, and a throttle interlock.

History

The chainsaw's ancestor was the osteotome, a hand-cranked chain instrument invented around 1830 by the German orthopaedist Bernard Heine for cutting bone in surgery. Large two-man chain saws for timber appeared in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. Andreas Stihl patented an electric chain saw in 1926 and a gasoline-powered model in 1929, founding the company that still bears his name; Joseph Buford Cox's improved cutter tooth (1947) greatly increased cutting efficiency. Lightweight one-man saws introduced in the 1950s transformed forestry work, largely replacing axes and crosscut saws.

Uses and safety

Chainsaws are the primary tool of professional loggers and arborists and are widely used by homeowners. They are among the most dangerous power tools in common use: kickback — the sudden upward rotation of the bar tip — is a leading cause of injury. Operators are advised to wear protective clothing including chainsaw chaps or trousers with clogging fibres, helmets with visors and ear defenders, gloves and safety boots. Specialist variants include concrete-cutting saws, pole saws for high branches, and rescue saws used by fire services.
