Denizli

Denizli is a city in the Aegean Region of southwestern Turkey and the capital of Denizli Province, situated in a valley on the edge of a high plateau where the Çürüksu stream joins the Menderes (Meander) river system. It is an industrial and commercial centre celebrated for its textile industry and for the nearby travertine terraces and ruins of Pamukkale and Hierapolis, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The site's principal ancient predecessor was Laodicea on the Lycus, a major Hellenistic and Roman city a few kilometres away, famous in antiquity for banking, medicine, and black wool, and addressed in the Book of Revelation as one of the Seven Churches of Asia. Modern Denizli developed during the Seljuk and Ottoman periods as a market town — its name is commonly derived from deniz, "sea", a reference to its abundant waters. It was occupied by Greek forces in 1920 during the Greco-Turkish War and retaken by Turkish forces the same year.

Economy and culture

Denizli is one of the "Anatolian Tigers", cities that industrialized rapidly from the 1980s. Its textile sector, producing towels, bathrobes and home textiles exported worldwide, is supplied by famous cotton from the surrounding plains. The city is also a marble-processing centre and the gateway for the millions of visitors to Pamukkale's thermal pools and the necropolis of Hierapolis. The Denizli rooster, bred for its extraordinarily long crow, is a civic emblem, celebrated by a glass statue in the city centre.
