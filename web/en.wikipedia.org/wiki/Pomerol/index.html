Pomerol is a commune in the Gironde department of southwestern France and one of the most prestigious red wine appellations of Bordeaux. Lying on the "Right Bank" of the Dordogne, just northwest of Saint-Émilion, it is the smallest of Bordeaux's major wine regions, at roughly 800 hectares.

Merlot vineyards on the Pomerol plateau.

Wines

Unlike the Médoc and Saint-Émilion, Pomerol has never had an official classification. Its wines are dominated by Merlot, usually blended with Cabernet Franc, grown on a distinctive plateau of gravel over clay rich in iron oxide (crasse de fer), with a famous small outcrop of blue clay. The wines are renowned for richness, plush texture, and notes of plum, truffle, and violet, and tend to be approachable earlier than Left Bank Cabernet-based wines while ageing well.

Notable estates

The most celebrated estate is Château Pétrus, among the most expensive wines in the world. Other leading properties include Château Le Pin (whose tiny production commands extreme prices), Château Lafleur, Vieux Château Certan, Château Trotanoy, Château L'Évangile, and Château La Conseillante.

History

Vines were planted in the area by the Romans and later developed by the Knights Hospitaller, but Pomerol long stood in the shadow of Saint-Émilion; its rise to fame came chiefly in the twentieth century, driven by merchants in nearby Libourne and by demand for its Merlot-rich style.
