Spreewald


Punting on the Spreewald canals.

The Spreewald (Lower Sorbian: Błota, "the marshes") is a lowland region in the state of Brandenburg, Germany, about 100 kilometres southeast of Berlin. It is famous for its traditional irrigation network of more than 200 small canals — with a total length of some 1,300 kilometres — fed by the river Spree, and for its alder forests on wetlands, meadows, and fields. UNESCO designated it a biosphere reserve in 1991.

Culture

The region is a centre of the Sorbs, a Slavic minority recognised in Germany; many villages have bilingual names and signs. Traditional houses are reached by boat, and flat-bottomed punts (Kähne) remain a standard means of transport and the basis of a large tourist trade. The Spreewald is also renowned for its pickled gherkins (Spreewälder Gurken), protected by EU geographical indication, and for horseradish and linseed oil.

Towns

The main towns are Lübbenau, Lübben, and Burg; the village of Lehde, with its island farmsteads, is often called the Venice of the Spreewald.
