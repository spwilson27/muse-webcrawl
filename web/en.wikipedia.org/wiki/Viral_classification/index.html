Virus classification

Virus classification is the process of naming viruses and placing them into a taxonomic system similar to that used for cellular organisms. Because viruses are obligate parasites with diverse origins, their classification combines genomic, structural and host-range criteria.

ICTV classification

The International Committee on Taxonomy of Viruses (ICTV) maintains the official taxonomy. Since a major revision in 2019, the system uses a 15-rank hierarchy mirroring cellular taxonomy: realm, subrealm, kingdom, subkingdom, phylum, subphylum, class, subclass, order, suborder, family, subfamily, genus, subgenus and species. As of recent releases, viruses are grouped into a handful of realms — including Riboviria (RNA viruses and retroviruses), Duplodnaviria (most double-stranded DNA viruses of animals, bacteria and archaea), Monodnaviria (single-stranded DNA viruses) and Varidnaviria.

Baltimore classification

Proposed by Nobel laureate David Baltimore, this scheme groups viruses by how their genome is converted into messenger RNA:

Other criteria

Viruses are also described by capsid symmetry (helical, icosahedral or complex), the presence or absence of a lipid envelope, host range, and disease caused. Satellite viruses and viroids, which depend on helper viruses, have their own classifications.
