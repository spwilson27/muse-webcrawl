Puissance

The puissance is a high-jump competition in show jumping, named from the French word for "power". Unlike a standard jumping round over a course of varied fences, the puissance centres on a single large wall — traditionally built to resemble a brick wall and painted red — which is raised in successive rounds until only one horse and rider remain able to clear it, or the remaining competitors agree to share first place.

Format

A competition begins with a short qualifying course of four to six jumps, including the wall at a moderate height. In each subsequent round, those who jumped clear return over a reduced course, and the wall is raised. The wall may exceed 2 metres (6 ft 7 in) in the final rounds, and jumping it remains the deciding test; faults at the wall normally settle the placings, with riders who knock a brick out of the wall eliminated or ranked below those who clear it.

Records and notable competitions

The world record for the puissance-style high jump is 2.47 m (8 ft 1¼ in), set by Captain Alberto Larraguibel Morales of Chile on the horse Huaso in Viña del Mar in 1949 — a record that still stands as the FEI high-jump record. Indoor puissance competitions became celebrated spectator events, notably at the Olympia London International Horse Show, where riders such as Nick Skelton became famous specialists. In 2019 the Olympia puissance was rebranded following sponsorship sensitivities, and its future as a regular fixture has been debated amid animal-welfare concerns about repeatedly jumping very high walls.

Welfare debate

Critics argue that the format encourages jumping beyond safe limits and may stress horses, while supporters note that experienced puissance horses jump voluntarily, that only a tiny number of elite rounds reach extreme heights, and that the wall is designed to collapse safely. Several major shows have reduced or retired their puissance competitions in recent decades.
