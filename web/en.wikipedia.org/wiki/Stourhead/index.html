Stourhead

Stourhead is a 1,072-hectare (2,650-acre) estate at the source of the River Stour in the southwest of the English county of Wiltshire, extending into Somerset. The estate is about 4 km (2.5 mi) northwest of the town of Mere and includes a Grade I listed 18th-century Palladian mansion, the village of Stourton, one of the most famous gardens in the English landscape gardening style, farmland, and woodland. Stourhead has been part-owned by the National Trust since 1946.

The Pantheon at Stourhead, a centrepiece of the landscape garden.

History

The Stourton family had lived in the Stourhead estate for 700 years when they sold it to Henry Hoare I, son of wealthy banker Sir Richard Hoare, in 1717. The original manor house was demolished and a new house, one of the first of its kind, was designed by Colen Campbell and built by Nathaniel Ireson between 1721 and 1724. Over the next 200 years the Hoare family collected many heirlooms, including a large library and art collection. In 1901 the house was gutted by fire, but many of the heirlooms were saved and the house was rebuilt in a near-identical style.

The gardens at Stourhead were laid out by Henry Hoare II between 1741 and 1780 in a classical 18th-century design set around a large lake. The garden is celebrated for its picturesque design, incorporating classical temples and monuments arranged around the artificial lake. Among its features are the Pantheon (1753–54), modelled on the Pantheon in Rome and containing statues of classical deities; the Temple of Apollo (1765), overlooking the lake; and the Temple of Flora (1744–46), the first garden building.

The garden

Visitors walk around the lake, encountering a sequence of carefully composed views. The inclusion of the medieval St Peter's Church and the Bristol High Cross (relocated to Stourhead in 1765) adds a sense of antiquity. The grotto, constructed in 1744 and extended later, houses a statue of a nymph beside the spring that feeds the lake.
