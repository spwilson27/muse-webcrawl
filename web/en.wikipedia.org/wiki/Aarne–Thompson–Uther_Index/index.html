Aarne–Thompson–Uther Index

The Aarne–Thompson–Uther Index (ATU Index) is a catalogue of folktale types used in folkloristics to classify traditional narratives from around the world. Each "type" is a stable plot pattern — for example ATU 333, "Little Red Riding Hood", or ATU 510A, "Cinderella" — under which individual variants collected by folklorists can be grouped.

History

Finnish folklorist Antti Aarne published the first Verzeichnis der Märchentypen in 1910, as part of the historic-geographic ("Finnish") method of folktale research. The American scholar Stith Thompson translated and greatly expanded it in 1928 and again in 1961 (The Types of the Folktale), producing the Aarne–Thompson (AT) system. In 2004, Hans-Jörg Uther issued a thoroughly revised three-volume edition, The Types of International Folktales, which renamed, merged and renumbered many types and broadened the corpus beyond Europe.

Structure

Types are grouped in broad ranges: animal tales (1–299), tales of magic (300–749), religious tales (750–849), realistic tales or novellas (850–999), tales of the stupid ogre (1000–1199), anecdotes and jokes (1200–1999), and formula tales (2000–2399). The index is used together with Thompson's Motif-Index of Folk-Literature, which classifies the smaller narrative elements from which tales are built.

Criticism

Scholars have criticised the index for its Eurocentric origins, its privileging of plot over performance context and meaning, and the artificiality of some type boundaries; nevertheless it remains the standard reference framework for comparative folktale research.
