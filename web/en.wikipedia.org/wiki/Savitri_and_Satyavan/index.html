Savitri and Satyavan

Savitri and Satyavan (Sanskrit: सावित्री सत्यवान्) is a legendary tale from the Vana Parva of the Mahabharata, narrated by the sage Markandeya to Yudhishthira. Savitri, a princess of Madra, chooses Satyavan as her husband although the sage Narada prophesies that he will die within a year. Through her devotion and her brilliant dialogue with Yama, the god of death, she wins back her husband's life. The story is a classic exemplar of wifely devotion (pativrata) and of the power of wisdom and steadfastness against fate.

Story

Savitri, the beautiful and learned daughter of King Ashvapati of Madra — born by the grace of the goddess Savitri — chose as her husband Satyavan, the virtuous son of the blind, exiled king Dyumatsena, who lived in a forest hermitage. Warned by Narada that Satyavan was fated to die in exactly one year, Savitri refused to choose another, declaring that she chose only once.

Counting the days, she undertook a three-day fast before the appointed date and accompanied Satyavan into the forest. As he lay down to rest with his head in her lap, Yama himself appeared to claim his soul. Savitri followed the god, engaging him in discourse on dharma so subtle and righteous that Yama, pleased, granted her boon after boon — her father-in-law's sight and kingdom restored, a hundred sons for her father, a hundred sons for herself and Satyavan — until, trapped by his own gifts, he was obliged to restore Satyavan's life, since her sons required a living father.

Legacy

Savitri's vow, the Vat Savitri vrat, is still observed by married women in Maharashtra, Odisha, Bihar, and other regions, fasting and tying threads around a banyan tree for their husbands' longevity. The tale inspired Sri Aurobindo's English epic poem Savitri: A Legend and a Symbol, one of the longest poems in English, which transforms the story into a spiritual allegory of the soul's conquest of death and ignorance.
