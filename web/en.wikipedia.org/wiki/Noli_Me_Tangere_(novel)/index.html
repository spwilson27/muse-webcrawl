Noli Me Tangere (novel)

Noli Me Tangere (Latin for "Touch Me Not") is a novel by Filipino national hero José Rizal, first published in 1887 in Berlin. Written in Spanish, it exposed the corruption and abuses of Spanish colonial rule and the Catholic friars in the Philippines, and is regarded as one of the foundational works of Philippine literature and nationalism.

Plot summary

Juan Crisóstomo Ibarra, a young Filipino educated in Europe, returns to the Philippines to marry María Clara and build a school. He discovers that his father died in prison after being falsely accused by the friar Padre Dámaso. Betrayed, persecuted by the friars and the civil guards, and framed in a staged revolt, Ibarra escapes and is presumed dead, while María Clara, revealed to be Dámaso's daughter, enters a convent. The idealistic reformer Ibarra is contrasted with the radical Elías, who dies saving him.

Impact

Banned in the colony, the novel circulated clandestinely and inspired the Propaganda Movement and, later, the Philippine Revolution. Together with its sequel El filibusterismo, it is required reading in Philippine schools, and "Noli" characters such as Sisa and Basilio remain cultural touchstones.
