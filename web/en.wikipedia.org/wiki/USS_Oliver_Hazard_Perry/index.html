USS Oliver Hazard Perry (FFG-7) was the lead ship of the Oliver Hazard Perry class of guided-missile frigates of the United States Navy. She was named for Commodore Oliver Hazard Perry, the American naval hero of the War of 1812 best known for his victory at the Battle of Lake Erie and his report, "We have met the enemy and they are ours."

Class and design

The Perry class was conceived in the 1970s under Admiral Elmo Zumwalt's "high-low" fleet concept as a low-cost escort for convoy and amphibious groups, providing anti-air and anti-submarine defence. The ships were built around a single-shaft gas-turbine propulsion plant, a Mk 13 launcher for Standard SM-1 and Harpoon missiles, a 76 mm gun, and hangars for two helicopters. Seventy-one ships of the class were built for the U.S. and allied navies, including license-built examples in Spain, Australia, and Taiwan, and the type proved notably survivable — USS Stark and USS Samuel B. Roberts survived missile and mine hits respectively.

Service

Commissioned in 1977 at Bath Iron Works, Oliver Hazard Perry served in the Atlantic Fleet for two decades before decommissioning in 1997 as part of the post-Cold War drawdown. Unlike many of her sisters, she was not transferred to an allied navy and was dismantled in 2006.
