Dial-up Internet access

Dial-up Internet access is a form of Internet access that uses the public switched telephone network to establish a connection to an Internet service provider by dialling a telephone number on a conventional phone line. A modem modulates digital data into analogue audio signals — producing the characteristic handshake tones familiar to early Internet users.

History

Dial-up was the dominant means of home Internet access during the 1990s. Speeds rose from 300 bit/s in early modems to a theoretical maximum of 56 kbit/s under the V.90 and V.92 standards. Because the connection occupied the household telephone line, calls could not be received while online.

Decline

From the early 2000s, broadband technologies such as DSL and cable Internet displaced dial-up in most developed markets, offering always-on connections at far higher speeds. Dial-up persists in some rural and remote areas and as a backup service.
