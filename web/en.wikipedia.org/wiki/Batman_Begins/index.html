Batman Begins

Batman Begins is a 2005 superhero film directed by Christopher Nolan and written by Nolan and David S. Goyer. Based on the DC Comics character Batman, it stars Christian Bale as Bruce Wayne / Batman, with Michael Caine, Liam Neeson, Katie Holmes, Gary Oldman, Cillian Murphy, Tom Wilkinson, Rutger Hauer, Ken Watanabe, and Morgan Freeman in supporting roles. The film reboots the Batman film series, telling the origin story of Bruce Wayne from the death of his parents to his journey to become Batman and his fight to stop Ra's al Ghul and the Scarecrow from plunging Gotham City into chaos.

Plot

As a child in Gotham City, Bruce Wayne falls into a well and develops a fear of bats. After witnessing his parents' murder by mugger Joe Chill, Bruce is raised by the family butler, Alfred Pennyworth. Fourteen years later, Bruce intends to murder Chill, but a mobster beats him to it. Bruce travels the world for seven years, training in martial arts and immersing himself in the criminal underworld. In a Bhutanese prison he is recruited by Henri Ducard, who invites him to join the League of Shadows, led by Ra's al Ghul. Bruce completes his training but refuses to kill a prisoner, destroying the League's temple.

Returning to Gotham, Bruce takes over Wayne Enterprises and develops a prototype armored suit and the Tumbler with Lucius Fox. Adopting the persona of "Batman", he intercepts a drug shipment and learns the drugs were supplied by Dr. Jonathan Crane, the "Scarecrow", working for Ra's al Ghul. Crane sprays Batman with a fear-inducing toxin; Alfred and Fox save him and develop an antidote. At his birthday party, Bruce confronts Ducard, who reveals himself as the real Ra's al Ghul. The League attacks Gotham, releasing the toxin through the city's water supply. Batman overcomes Ra's aboard a monorail train carrying a microwave emitter, refusing to save him as the train crashes. Gotham is saved, and Bruce embraces his dual life as Batman, with the newly promoted Commissioner Gordon warning him about a criminal known as the Joker.

Production

Warner Bros. hired Nolan in January 2003 to direct a fresh start for the Batman franchise after the critical failure of Batman & Robin (1997). Inspired by Blade Runner, Superman (1978), and Frank Miller's Batman: Year One, Nolan sought a darker, more realistic tone grounded in humanity rather than fantasy. Filming took place in England, Iceland, and Chicago, with much of Gotham built as a massive set at Shepperton Studios; the Tumbler was built as a full-size practical vehicle.

Reception and legacy

The film received generally positive reviews, with praise for its tone, Bale's performance, action sequences, and emotional depth, though some critics found the fight scenes hard to follow. It grossed $373.7 million worldwide and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Cinematography. It was followed by The Dark Knight (2008) and The Dark Knight Rises (2012), completing Nolan's Dark Knight trilogy.
