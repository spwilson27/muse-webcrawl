Albula Railway

The Albula Railway (German: Albulabahn; Italian: Ferrovia dell'Albula) is a metre-gauge railway line of the Rhaetian Railway in the Swiss canton of Graubünden, running 61.7 km from Thusis over the Albula Pass region to the spa town of St. Moritz in the Engadin. Opened in 1903–1904, it is celebrated as a masterpiece of Alpine railway engineering, climbing 1,119 metres through 55 bridges and 39 tunnels — including the 5,866-metre Albula Tunnel and the famous curved Landwasser Viaduct — without rack assistance. Since 2008 the Albula and Bernina lines have together been inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

A train on the Landwasser Viaduct, the emblem of the Albula line.

Route and engineering

Between Bergün and Preda the line overcomes a height difference of 417 metres in a short distance by means of five spiral tunnels and two curved tunnels, a configuration long demonstrated in model form at the Bahnmuseum Albula in Bergün. The original Albula Tunnel, driven under the pass between Preda and Spinas from 1898 to 1903, suffered from water ingress and deterioration; a second, parallel tube entered service in 2024, with the old tunnel converted to a safety tunnel. The line carries the Glacier Express and regional trains and is electrified at 11 kV AC. See also Bernina Railway, Rhaetian Railway and Landwasser Viaduct.
