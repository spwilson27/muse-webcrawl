Ski resort

A ski resort is a resort developed for skiing, snowboarding, and other winter sports. In Europe and Canada it may also be called a ski area; a purpose-built village at the base of the slopes is sometimes termed a ski station. Ski resorts provide lift access — chairlifts, gondolas, cable cars, and surface lifts — to groomed pistes graded by difficulty, along with lodging, ski schools, equipment rental, and off-slope ("après-ski") amenities.

Major resort regions include the European Alps (France, Switzerland, Austria, Italy), the Rocky Mountains of North America (Colorado, Utah, British Columbia), Japan's Hokkaido and Honshu ranges, and the Southern Alps of New Zealand. Among the largest linked areas is France's Les Trois Vallées, with some 600 km of pistes, while Whistler Blackcomb is the largest ski resort in North America.

Operations

Resorts employ snowmaking and grooming vehicles (piste bashers) to maintain snow cover, and ski patrollers manage safety and avalanche control. Because climate change shortens reliable snow seasons — especially at low altitude — many resorts have diversified into summer activities such as mountain biking and hiking, and into year-round tourism.

Environmental impact

Ski area development can fragment habitat, consume large volumes of water for snowmaking, and increase energy use; these concerns have prompted sustainability programmes at many resorts.
