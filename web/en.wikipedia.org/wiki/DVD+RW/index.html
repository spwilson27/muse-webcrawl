DVD+RW

DVD+RW is a physical format for rewritable optical discs and can hold up to 4.7 GB of data. DVD+RW was created by the DVD+RW Alliance, an industry consortium of drive and disc manufacturers including Philips, Sony, Hewlett-Packard and others, that also developed the DVD+R and DVD+R DL formats. Unlike its rival format DVD-RW, adopted by the DVD Forum, DVD+RW's rewritable standard was not adopted by the DVD Forum at the time; instead the Alliance promoted its own standard.

Features

DVD+RW supports random write access and sector-level rewriting, lossless linking (allowing sectors to be replaced without loss of adjacent data), and defect management, making it behave more like a removable hard disk than DVD-RW. Discs can be rewritten roughly 1,000 times. The format also allows writing without finalizing the disc for playback in many players, and supports the +VR video recording mode used by standalone DVD recorders, which enables editing of recorded video on the disc itself.

Comparison with DVD-RW

Both formats hold 4.7 GB. DVD+RW generally offers faster formatting ("background formatting"), more robust linking, and better editing features for video recording, while early DVD-RW discs enjoyed slightly wider compatibility with first-generation DVD players. Later "multi" drives supported both camps, ending the format war in practice.

Dual-layer variant

A dual-layer rewritable version, DVD+RW DL, with 8.5 GB capacity, was announced but saw very limited commercial release.
