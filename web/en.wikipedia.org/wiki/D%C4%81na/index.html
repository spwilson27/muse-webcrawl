Dāna

Dāna (Pali and Sanskrit: दान) is the concept of generosity and charitable giving in Indian religions, including Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism and Sikhism. It is regarded as a fundamental virtue and a form of spiritual practice.

In Buddhism

Dāna is the first of the pāramitās (perfections) in Mahāyāna Buddhism and the first of the pāramīs in Theravāda. Giving to monks, nuns and the needy — food, robes or support — generates merit and loosens attachment, preparing the mind for higher practice.

In Hinduism and Jainism

Classical Hindu texts praise dāna as a duty of householders, with gifts to worthy recipients given without expectation of return. In Jainism, giving is one of the daily duties of laypeople, classically divided into gifts of food, medicine, learning and protection from fear.
