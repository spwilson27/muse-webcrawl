Sanibel, Florida

Sanibel is a city in Lee County, Florida, United States, occupying Sanibel Island, a barrier island in the Gulf of Mexico off Fort Myers. The population was about 6,400 at the 2020 census. Sanibel is famous for its shell-strewn beaches — its unusual east–west orientation acts as a scoop for shells carried by Gulf currents — and for its strong conservation ethic, with more than half of the island preserved as wildlife refuge.

Geography and wildlife

The J.N. "Ding" Darling National Wildlife Refuge, named for the conservationist cartoonist Jay Norwood "Ding" Darling, covers a large part of the island and protects mangrove forests, wetlands, and habitat for migratory birds, manatees, and alligators. The island has no traffic lights, low-rise buildings, and extensive bicycle paths.

History

The island was inhabited by the Calusa people before European contact. Sanibel was settled by homesteaders in the nineteenth century, and the Sanibel Island Light was built in 1884. The Sanibel Causeway, opened in 1963, linked the island to the mainland and brought development; the City of Sanibel incorporated in 1974 in large part to control that growth. In September 2022, Hurricane Ian made landfall nearby, severely damaging the causeway and devastating the island, from which it has been rebuilding.

Shelling

Sanibel is consistently ranked among the best shelling beaches in the world; the stooped posture of collectors is jokingly called the "Sanibel stoop". The Bailey-Matthews National Shell Museum, opened in 1995, is one of the few museums in the United States devoted entirely to shells and mollusks.
