Washington Redskins name controversy

The Washington Redskins name controversy concerned the name and logo of the Washington American football franchise, whose former name "Redskins" is regarded by many Native Americans and others as a dictionary-defined racial slur. After decades of protest, the team retired the name in 2020.

Background

The franchise adopted the name in 1933 while based in Boston. Native American activists campaigned against it from the 1960s onward, arguing the term is derogatory; legal challenges sought cancellation of the team's trademarks, culminating in cases that reached the U.S. Supreme Court's jurisprudence on trademark law in Matal v. Tam (2017), which was decided on free-speech grounds unrelated to the name's offensiveness.

Resolution

Following the George Floyd protests of 2020, major sponsors including FedEx and Nike pressured the team to change. Owner Daniel Snyder, who had long resisted, announced a review and retired the name and logo in July 2020. The franchise played two seasons as the Washington Football Team before adopting the name Commanders in February 2022.
