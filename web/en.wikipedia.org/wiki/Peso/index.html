Peso

The peso is the monetary unit of several countries in the Americas and the Philippines. The word peso means "weight" in Spanish, and the currency originates in the Spanish colonial silver coin known as the peso de ocho reales — the "piece of eight" or Spanish dollar — which circulated widely from the 16th to the 19th centuries and influenced the design and name of currencies worldwide, including the United States dollar.

History

The original peso was a silver coin weighing about 27 grams, minted in Spain and its colonies from 1497 onward and standardized in the 16th century. Minted in enormous quantities at Mexico City, Potosí, and other colonial mints, it became the first global currency, accepted in trade from Europe to China, and remained legal tender in the United States until 1857. Its symbol, and that of the Spanish dollar, is one origin proposed for the "$" sign.

Current peso currencies

Countries that currently use a currency called the peso include:

  • Argentina — Argentine peso (ARS)
  • Chile — Chilean peso (CLP)
  • Colombia — Colombian peso (COP)
  • Cuba — Cuban peso (CUP)
  • Dominican Republic — Dominican peso (DOP)
  • Mexico — Mexican peso (MXN), the most traded peso and one of the most traded currencies in the world
  • Philippines — Philippine peso (PHP), spelled piso in Filipino on coins and notes
  • Uruguay — Uruguayan peso (UYU)

Former pesos

Many other countries formerly used pesos, including Spain itself (until replaced in everyday use by the peseta, whose name is a diminutive of peso), Bolivia, Costa Rica, Ecuador, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras, Nicaragua, Paraguay, Peru, and Venezuela. Most modern pesos are subdivided into 100 centavos (or céntimos/sentimo).
