Stortorget

Stortorget (Swedish: "the big square") is a public square in Gamla Stan, the old town in central Stockholm, Sweden. It is the oldest square in Stockholm and the historic centre around which the medieval urban agglomeration gradually came into being. Many of Stockholm's early institutions were located around the square, and it remains surrounded by prominent historic buildings.

History

The square is mentioned in historical records as early as 1420. It was the site of the Stockholm Bloodbath in November 1520, when scores of Swedish nobles and clergy were executed following the coronation of Christian II of Denmark. The old Stockholm Stock Exchange Building, which now houses the Swedish Academy, the Nobel Prize Museum, and the Nobel Library, stands on the northern side of the square.

Surroundings

Notable buildings around Stortorget include the colourful 17th-century merchants' houses on its eastern side, among the most photographed facades in Stockholm, and the Börshuset (Stock Exchange Building). Streets radiating from the square include Köpmangatan, the oldest named street in Stockholm, and Svartmangatan.
