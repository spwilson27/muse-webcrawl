United States Note

Currency details
Issued: 1862–1971
Nickname: Greenback, Legal Tender Note
Distinguishing feature: Red Treasury seal

A United States Note, also known as a Legal Tender Note, is a type of paper money issued by the United States Treasury from 1862 to 1971. It was a fiat currency, originally not backed by gold or silver, and was the first national paper currency of the United States.

History

Facing financial crisis during the American Civil War, Congress passed the Legal Tender Act on February 25, 1862, authorizing $150 million in notes that were legal tender for all debts except customs duties and interest on the public debt. Because the backs were printed in green ink, they became known as "greenbacks." The Supreme Court upheld their constitutionality in the Legal Tender Cases of 1871.

Characteristics

United States Notes were distinguished from Federal Reserve Notes and National Bank Notes by their red Treasury seal and serial numbers. They circulated alongside other currency types. The amount outstanding was fixed by law at $346,681,016 after 1878. Although issuance ceased with the 1963 series, the notes remained redeemable and in circulation until 1994, when the Treasury ended their special reserve accounting.

Series

Denominations ranged from $1 to $10,000 on early large-size issues; later small-size series (1928, 1953, 1963) were issued in $2 and $5 denominations with a red seal.
