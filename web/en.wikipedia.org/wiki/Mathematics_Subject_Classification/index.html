Mathematics Subject Classification

The Mathematics Subject Classification (MSC) is an alphanumerical classification scheme collaboratively produced by staff of, and based on the coverage of, the two major mathematical reviewing databases, Mathematical Reviews and zbMATH Open. The MSC is used by many mathematics journals, which ask authors of research papers and expository articles to list subject classification codes from the Mathematics Subject Classification in their papers.

Structure

The MSC is a hierarchical scheme with three levels. A top-level code consists of two digits representing a broad discipline, such as 11 (Number theory), 20 (Group theory), 35 (Partial differential equations), 53 (Differential geometry), or 90 (Operations research). A second level adds a letter, for example 11B (Elementary number theory), and a third level adds two more digits to give five-character codes such as 11B68 (Bernoulli and Euler numbers and polynomials). A code ending in "-99" denotes topics in the section but not in any of its subsections, while "00" codes refer to general or cross-disciplinary topics.

History and revisions

The classification originated in the classification used by Mathematical Reviews, with antecedents going back to the AMS classification of the early 20th century. Major revisions have appeared roughly once a decade: MSC 1980, MSC 1991, MSC 2000, MSC 2010, and MSC 2020. The 2020 revision contains 63 top-level two-digit classes and several thousand finer subdivisions, reflecting growth in fields such as mathematics of computing and data science.

Use

Authors select the codes that best describe their work; reviewers and editors of MathSciNet and zbMATH assign codes to classify the literature. The MSC is also used in library cataloguing and bibliometric studies of mathematics.
