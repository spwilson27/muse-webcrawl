Hythe, Kent

Town in Kent, England

Hythe seafront and the Royal Military Canal

Hythe is a small coastal market town on the edge of Romney Marsh, in the Folkestone and Hythe district of Kent, England. The word Hythe means landing place or port in Old English. The town was once one of the Cinque Ports, and its long history is reflected in its medieval churches, its military canal, and the narrow streets of its old town.

History

Hythe was an important port in the Middle Ages and a limb of the Cinque Ports confederation, contributing ships and men for the defence of the realm. As the harbour silted up over the centuries, the town's maritime importance declined, while nearby Folkestone grew. In the early nineteenth century the Royal Military Canal was dug along the edge of Romney Marsh as a defensive line against possible invasion during the Napoleonic Wars, and the canal still forms a green boundary to the town.

The crypt of St Leonard's Church contains one of the largest collections of human skulls and bones in England, known as the Hythe ossuary, which has long been an object of study and local legend.

Transport and amenities

The town is served by the Romney, Hythe and Dymchurch Railway, a miniature steam railway running along the coast to Dungeness. The seafront promenade and shingle beach are popular with walkers, and the canal towpath forms part of a long-distance cycle route.
