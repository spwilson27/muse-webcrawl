Combined events

Combined-events athletes compete in running, jumping, and throwing disciplines

Combined events are competitions in track and field athletics in which athletes contest a series of different events over one or two days, earning points in each according to scoring tables, with the winner being the athlete with the highest total. The best-known combined events are the men's decathlon (ten events) and the women's heptathlon (seven events), both contested at the Olympic Games.

Events

The outdoor decathlon consists of the 100 metres, long jump, shot put, high jump, and 400 metres on the first day, and the 110 metres hurdles, discus throw, pole vault, javelin throw, and 1500 metres on the second. The women's heptathlon consists of the 100 metres hurdles, high jump, shot put, and 200 metres on day one, and the long jump, javelin throw, and 800 metres on day two. Indoors, men contest a heptathlon and women a pentathlon; a women's decathlon is also held outside the major championships.

Scoring

Performances are converted to points using the World Athletics scoring tables, which are designed so that comparable elite performances across disciplines earn similar points — roughly 1,000 points corresponds to a world-class mark in each event. Athletes who fail to record a valid mark in an event score zero for it and are effectively eliminated from contention.

History

Combined events descend from the ancient Greek pentathlon and from 19th-century all-around competitions. The decathlon entered the Olympics in 1912, where Jim Thorpe won famously; the women's event evolved from the pentathlon (introduced at the 1964 Olympics) to the heptathlon in 1984.
