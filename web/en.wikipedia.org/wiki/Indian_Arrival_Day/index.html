Indian Arrival Day

Indian Arrival Day is a public holiday celebrated in several Caribbean and other countries to commemorate the arrival of Indians, brought as indentured labourers under the British, Dutch and French colonial systems, to work on sugar plantations following the abolition of slavery.

By country

In Trinidad and Tobago, Indian Arrival Day is observed on 30 May, marking the landing of the Fatel Razack in 1845 with the first group of Indian indentured immigrants; it was first celebrated in 1945 and became an official public holiday in 1995. In Guyana, Arrival Day is celebrated on 5 May, commemorating the 1838 arrival of the first ships, the Whitby and Hesperus. In Suriname, the arrival of the Lalla Rookh in 1873 is marked on 5 June. Mauritius observes the arrival of indentured labourers on 2 November, and similar commemorations occur in Fiji, Grenada, Jamaica, Saint Lucia and Saint Vincent.

Significance

The day honours the resilience and cultural contributions of Indo-Caribbean and other Indian diaspora communities — in religion, cuisine, music, politics and public life — and serves as an occasion for reflection on the hardships of indentureship.
