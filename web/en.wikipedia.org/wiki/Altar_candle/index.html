Altar candle

Altar candles are candles set on or near the altar of a church and lit during services. In Roman Catholic, Anglican, Lutheran, and some other traditions, they symbolize Christ as the Light of the World and the presence of the divine during worship.

Usage

Traditionally two candles are placed on or beside the altar for celebrations of the Eucharist, with additional candles for greater feasts — six for solemn occasions and seven when a bishop celebrates. Altar candles are traditionally made of beeswax; modern regulations generally require a high proportion of beeswax for candles used at Mass. Candles are lit before the service and extinguished afterward, often by acolytes using a candle snuffer.

Symbolism and history

The use of lights in worship dates to early Christianity, when lamps and candles were used practically in catacombs and evening services and acquired symbolic meaning. Candles may also be carried in procession and placed on candlesticks flanking the altar.
