The Channel Country is a region of remote, arid interior Australia, centred on south-western Queensland and extending into South Australia, New South Wales, and the Northern Territory. It takes its name from the network of intertwining, braided river channels that cross its flat floodplains — principally the Georgina River, the Diamantina River, and Cooper Creek — all draining toward Lake Eyre in the Lake Eyre basin.

Hydrology and ecology

The rivers are fed by monsoon rains far to the north and flow only intermittently. In big flood years, water spreads across the floodplains in vast shallow sheets, triggering booms of grasses, waterbirds, and fish in one of the world's last free-flowing desert river systems; in drought, the rivers shrink to chains of permanent waterholes that serve as refuges. Towns of the region include Birdsville, Bedourie, Boulia, and Windorah.

Land use

The Channel Country is renowned cattle country: floodwaters produce rich natural pasture ("herbage") on which large pastoral stations fatten beef cattle, and the region is a stronghold of organic, grass-fed beef production. Graziers time stock movements to the flood cycle. The region's remoteness, gibber plains, and the Simpson Desert to the west define its outback character, and it holds significant Aboriginal cultural heritage, including traditional trade routes and songlines.
