Laser rangefinder

A laser rangefinder, also known as a laser telemeter, is a rangefinder that uses a laser beam to determine the distance to an object. The most common form operates on the time-of-flight principle: it sends a laser pulse toward the target and measures the time taken for the pulse to be reflected back, computing distance from the speed of light.

Principle

Distance = (speed of light × time of flight) / 2. Because light travels about 300,000 km per second, timing must be extremely precise; modern electronics achieve millimetre accuracy at short range. Phase-shift instruments compare the phase of a modulated continuous beam for very accurate surveying measurements.

Applications

  • Military: tank and artillery fire control, handheld target designator rangefinders.
  • Surveying and construction: distance meters replacing tape measures.
  • Sport: golf and hunting rangefinders measuring distance to flag or game.
  • Space: Lunar Laser Ranging measured the Earth–Moon distance using retroreflectors left by Apollo missions.
  • Industry and robotics: components of lidar systems, including autonomous vehicles.

Limitations

Fog, rain and smoke scatter the beam; highly absorptive or mirror-like surfaces at oblique angles may return weak signals. Eye safety limits laser power for consumer devices.
