Cauchy distribution

The Cauchy distribution, named after Augustin-Louis Cauchy, is a continuous probability distribution also known in physics as the Lorentz distribution or Breit–Wigner distribution, describing resonance phenomena.

f(x; x0, γ) = (1/πγ) · 1 / (1 + ((x − x0)/γ)2)

Properties

The Cauchy distribution has heavy tails: its mean, variance, and higher moments are undefined. The sample mean of Cauchy-distributed variables has the same distribution as a single observation, so the law of large numbers fails in its usual form. It is a stable distribution with stability parameter 1, and arises as the ratio of two independent standard normal variables, and as the distribution of the tangent of a uniform angle.

Applications

It models spectral line shapes in spectroscopy, is used in robust statistics as an example of a pathological distribution, and serves as a prior in Bayesian work on some problems.
