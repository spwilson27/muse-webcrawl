Perseverance rover

Perseverance
Type: Mars rover
Operator: NASA / JPL
Launched: 30 July 2020
Landed: 18 February 2021, Jezero Crater
Mission: Mars 2020

Perseverance, nicknamed Percy, is a car-sized Mars rover designed to explore the Jezero Crater on Mars as part of NASA's Mars 2020 mission. Built by the Jet Propulsion Laboratory, it was launched on 30 July 2020 and landed successfully on 18 February 2021.

Mission

The rover's goals are to study Mars's geological processes and history, assess past habitability, search for signs of ancient microbial life (biosignatures), and collect and cache samples of rock and regolith for possible return to Earth by a future mission. Jezero Crater was chosen because it once held a lake and river delta more than 3.5 billion years ago.

Instruments and technology

Perseverance carries seven science instruments, including the Mastcam-Z camera system, the SuperCam laser instrument, the PIXL and SHERLOC spectrometers, and MOXIE, an experiment that produced oxygen from the carbon dioxide atmosphere. It also carried the Ingenuity helicopter, which in April 2021 achieved the first powered flight on another planet and completed 72 flights before its mission ended in January 2024.

Sample caching

The rover sealed dozens of sample tubes containing drilled rock cores, regolith, and atmospheric samples. A backup depot of ten tubes was deposited at Three Forks in early 2023 as part of the Mars Sample Return planning.

Name

The name "Perseverance" was chosen from a student naming contest and submitted by Virginia seventh-grader Alexander Mather.
