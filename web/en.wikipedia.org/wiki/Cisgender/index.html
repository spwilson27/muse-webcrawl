Cisgender

Cisgender (often shortened to cis) describes a person whose gender identity corresponds to their sex assigned at birth.

The word cisgender (often shortened to cis; sometimes cissexual) describes a person whose gender identity corresponds to their sex assigned at birth, i.e., the sex they were identified as having at birth. It is the antonym of transgender. The prefix cis- comes from Latin meaning 'on this side of'. The term cisgender was coined in 1994 as an antonym for transgender, and entered into dictionaries starting in 2015 as a result of changes in social discourse about gender.

Related concepts are cisnormativity (the presumption that cisgender identity is preferred or normal) and cissexism (bias or prejudice favoring cisgender people).

Etymology and terminology

Cisgender has its origin in the Latin-derived prefix cis-, meaning 'on this side of', which is the opposite of the prefix trans-, meaning 'across from' or 'on the other side of'. This usage can be seen in the cis–trans distinction in chemistry, the cis and trans sides of the Golgi apparatus in cellular biology, the cis–trans or complementation test in genetics, and in the ancient Roman term Cisalpine Gaul (i.e., 'Gaul on this side of the Alps').

The term cissexual was coined in 1991 by German sexologist Volkmar Sigusch. In 1994, the term cisgender appeared in print on a Usenet newsgroup post by Dana Leland Defosse, and the term gained wider use in the 2000s and 2010s through academic writing and social media.

Academic use

The term and its derivative forms are used in gender studies, sociology, and medicine to provide neutral vocabulary for discussing gender without treating any identity as the unmarked default. Some critics, including some feminists and gender-critical commentators, have objected to the term, arguing it imposes an identity framework; others defend it as a useful descriptive term comparable to terms like heterosexual.
