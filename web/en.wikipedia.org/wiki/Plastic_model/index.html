Plastic model

A finished injection-moulded plastic model of a Second World War fighter aircraft.

A plastic model is a scale model manufactured from plastic, usually sold as a kit to be assembled by the buyer. Most plastic models depict vehicles — aircraft, ships, automobiles and armoured fighting vehicles — though figures, buildings, and science-fiction subjects are also popular. The hobby of building them is known as scale modelling.

Manufacture

Most kits are produced by injection moulding of polystyrene: molten plastic is injected into steel moulds, producing frames ("sprues") of parts that the builder cuts out, cleans up, and cements together with solvent-based plastic cement. Kits typically include decals for markings and an instruction sheet. Other manufacturing methods include vacuum forming (vacuform kits), resin casting for limited-run "garage kits", and more recently 3D printing.

Scales

Standard scales include 1:72, 1:48 and 1:32 for aircraft; 1:35 and 1:48 for armour; 1:24 and 1:25 for cars; and 1:350 and 1:700 for ships. Common modelling practice includes painting with acrylic or enamel paints (often with an airbrush), weathering with washes and pigments, and adding aftermarket detail parts in photo-etched metal or resin.

History

The first plastic model kits appeared in the late 1930s (Frog Penguin kits in Britain) and proliferated after the Second World War, when manufacturers such as Airfix, Revell, Monogram, Aurora, Tamiya and Hasegawa built mass markets from the 1950s onward. The hobby peaked in popularity in the 1960s and 1970s, declined with competition from video games, and has since sustained a large adult hobbyist market served by international competitions, magazines, and clubs such as the International Plastic Modellers' Society (IPMS), founded in 1963.
