Stele

A stele (plural stelae) is a stone or wooden slab, generally taller than it is wide, erected in the ancient world as a monument, often for funerary or commemorative purposes, and frequently inscribed or carved in relief.

Stele
Materials: Stone, wood
Uses: Grave markers, law codes, boundary stones, commemorations
Famous examples: Rosetta Stone, Code of Hammurabi stele

History

Stelae were used in ancient Egypt (including the Rosetta Stone, a granodiorite stele), Mesopotamia (the stele bearing the Code of Hammurabi), Greece (painted and sculpted grave stelae), the Maya world (tall carved stelae recording dynastic history, e.g. at Tikal and Copán), and China (commemorative and Nestorian stelae such as the Xi'an Stele of 781 CE).

Functions

Stelae recorded laws, treaties, victories, decrees, boundary markers and tomb commemorations. Their texts are primary sources for historians and linguists.
