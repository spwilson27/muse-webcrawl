Siege of Myitkyina

DateMay 17 – August 3, 1944
LocationMyitkyina, Burma
ResultAllied victory
BelligerentsUnited States, Republic of China vs. Empire of Japan

The Siege of Myitkyina was a battle of the Burma campaign of the Second World War, fought from May 17 to August 3, 1944, for the town of Myitkyina in northern Burma (now Myanmar). A combined American and Chinese force under the Northern Combat Area Command of General Joseph Stilwell attacked the Japanese garrison commanded by Major General Genzō Mizukami.

Background

Myitkyina possessed the only all-weather airfield in northern Burma and sat on the Irrawaddy River and the rail line from Rangoon; capturing it would secure the air route over "the Hump" to China and support the Ledo Road. On May 17, 1944, after a long jungle march, Chinese troops and the American long-range penetration unit Merrill's Marauders seized the airfield by surprise, but an attack on the town itself failed, and the exhausted Marauders were controversially evacuated or pressed back into the line, of which only a fraction remained fit.

The siege

The battle settled into a grueling siege through the monsoon. Japanese reinforcements raised the garrison to roughly 4,600 men, while the Allies built up a larger force including the US 5307th Composite Unit's replacements, Chinese divisions flown in and marched overland, and elements of the British-led Chindits reassigned to Stilwell. General Mizukami, ordered to hold to the end, committed suicide as the position collapsed; on August 3, 1944, the town fell, with the surviving Japanese withdrawing across the Irrawaddy.

Aftermath

Allied casualties were heavy — the Chinese suffered over 3,000 casualties and the Americans some 2,700, many from disease — but the capture of Myitkyina secured the Hump airlift's lower, safer route and cleared the way for the eventual reopening of the Burma Road corridor to China in early 1945.
