Old Beijing

Old Beijing refers to the historic urban fabric of Beijing prior to the large-scale redevelopment of the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries: the walled Ming and Qing dynasty city of courtyard houses (siheyuan), narrow lanes (hutongs), temples, markets, and city gates.

City layout

The Ming-dynasty city was arranged on a north–south central axis, with the Forbidden City at its heart, surrounded by the Imperial City and the Inner and Outer Cities, each enclosed by walls and gates. Residential districts consisted of siheyuan courtyard compounds arranged along hutongs running mostly east–west.

Demolition and preservation

The city walls were demolished in the 1950s–1960s, and from the 1990s large numbers of hutongs and siheyuan were cleared for modern development, a process that accelerated before the 2008 Olympics. In response, conservation districts were designated, and areas such as Shichahai, Nanluoguxiang, and the Drum and Bell Tower neighbourhood survive as protected fragments of the old city, popular with visitors.

Culture

"Old Beijing" also evokes a distinctive local culture: Beijing dialect, traditional snacks, tea houses, Peking opera, and the courtyard way of life celebrated in the writings of Lao She.
