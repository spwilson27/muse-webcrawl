Villa I Tatti

Villa I Tatti is a Renaissance villa in Fiesole, overlooking Florence, Italy, that serves as the Harvard University Center for Italian Renaissance Studies. The estate was the home of the art historian and connoisseur Bernard Berenson (1865–1959) and his wife Mary Berenson, who bequeathed the villa, its library, and its art collection to Harvard University.

History

The Berensons settled at I Tatti in 1900, first renting and then purchasing the property, which they transformed into a center of scholarship and collecting. Bernard Berenson assembled one of the finest private libraries on Italian art and a notable collection of Italian primitive paintings, Chinese and Islamic art, and period furniture. The formal Italian gardens, designed in the early twentieth century, are celebrated as a revival of the Renaissance garden style.

The Harvard Center

After Berenson's death in 1959, the villa passed to Harvard, which opened the Center for Italian Renaissance Studies in 1961. The center hosts a residential fellowship program for scholars of Italian Renaissance art, history, literature, and music, and maintains the Berenson Library — one of the world's great research libraries in the field — along with the Fototeca Berenson, a vast photographic archive.
