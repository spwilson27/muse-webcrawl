Fort Meade

Fort George G. Meade
Location: Anne Arundel County, Maryland, U.S.
Established: 1917
Named for: Gen. George G. Meade
Major tenants: NSA, US Cyber Command, DISA

Fort George G. Meade is a United States Army installation in Anne Arundel County, Maryland, between Baltimore and Washington, D.C. Established in 1917 as Camp Meade for First World War mobilisation, it is named after Major General George G. Meade, commander of the Army of the Potomac at the Battle of Gettysburg. Fort Meade is best known as the home of the National Security Agency (NSA) and of United States Cyber Command, and it is one of the largest employers in Maryland, with a workforce of tens of thousands of military, civilian, and contractor personnel.

History

The post was authorised in 1917 and trained hundreds of thousands of soldiers in both world wars. In the Second World War it served as a training centre and housed a prisoner-of-war camp and an internment facility. In 1952 the NSA established its headquarters adjacent to and later on the post, and Fort Meade grew into a centre for signals intelligence, information operations, and defence media. The Defence Information School and the Defence Media Activity are also located there.

Tenants and role

Principal organisations at Fort Meade include the National Security Agency/Central Security Service, US Cyber Command, the Defense Information Systems Agency (DISA), US Fleet Cyber Command elements, and the headquarters of the US Army's recruitment and other support commands. The co-location of the NSA and Cyber Command — led for years by a single dual-hatted officer — made Fort Meade the centre of US government signals intelligence and military cyber operations. The post covers roughly 5,000 acres and supports a large surrounding community in Odenton and nearby towns.
