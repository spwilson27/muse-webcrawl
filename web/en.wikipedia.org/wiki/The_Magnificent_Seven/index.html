The Magnificent Seven is a 1960 American Western film directed by John Sturges. It is a remake of Akira Kurosawa's 1954 Japanese film Seven Samurai, transposing the story to the Old West: seven gunfighters are hired to defend a poor Mexican village from a bandit gang led by Calvera (Eli Wallach).

Plot

Gunslinger Chris Adams (Yul Brynner) recruits six others — Vin Tanner, Bernardo O'Reilly, Britt, Lee, Harry Luck and the young Chico — to protect the village. Four of the seven are killed in the final battle, but the village is saved; Chris concludes that "the farmers have won. Not us."

Music and legacy

Elmer Bernstein's rousing score was nominated for an Academy Award and became one of the most famous in Western film, later used in Marlboro cigarette advertising. The film spawned three sequels — Return of the Seven (1966), Guns of the Magnificent Seven (1969) and The Magnificent Seven Ride! (1972) — a television series (1998–2000), and a 2016 remake directed by Antoine Fuqua starring Denzel Washington. In 2013 the 1960 film was selected for preservation in the US National Film Registry.
