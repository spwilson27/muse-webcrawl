Germania (city)

Model of the planned Welthauptstadt Germania

Germania, formally to be renamed Welthauptstadt Germania ("World Capital Germania"), was the projected renewal of Berlin planned by Adolf Hitler during Nazi Germany, as part of his vision for the future of a victorious Germany. Albert Speer, the "First Architect of the Third Reich", prepared most of the plans, centred on enormous monumental boulevards and buildings dwarfing those of Paris and Rome.

Plans

The centrepiece was a 5-kilometre grand boulevard, the North–South Axis, lined with triumphal monuments and crowned by a gigantic domed hall, the Volkshalle (People's Hall), some 290 metres high and capable of holding over 150,000 people. At the southern end stood a triumphal arch 117 metres tall, inscribed with the names of Germany's war dead. Demolition work began in the late 1930s, and the Chancellery and a few other elements were completed, but construction halted with the outbreak and course of the Second World War.

Legacy

Almost nothing of the scheme was built; surviving traces include the Schwerbelastungskörper, a massive concrete cylinder built to test the bearing capacity of Berlin's sandy soil for the triumphal arch. Germania has become a byword for totalitarian megalomania in architecture.
