Navarre

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Navarre (Spanish: Navarra; Basque: Nafarroa), officially the Chartered Community of Navarre (Spanish: Comunidad Foral de Navarra; Basque: Nafarroako Foru Komunitatea), is a landlocked autonomous community and province in northern Spain, bordering the Basque Autonomous Community, La Rioja, and Aragon in Spain and Nouvelle-Aquitaine in France. The capital city is Pamplona (Basque: Iruña).

Navarre covers about 10,391 km² and has a population of roughly 660,000. It is a single-province autonomous community with a distinctive fiscal regime: under its historic fueros (chartered rights), Navarre collects its own taxes and negotiates a payment (the cupo) to the Spanish state for common services.

History

The region corresponds broadly to the medieval Kingdom of Navarre, which grew out of the Kingdom of Pamplona founded in the 9th century under Íñigo Arista. At its height in the 11th century under Sancho III the Great, the kingdom briefly dominated Christian Iberia. The southern, peninsular part of the kingdom was conquered and annexed by Castile in 1512–1515, while the small northern portion beyond the Pyrenees (Lower Navarre) passed eventually to the French crown; Henry of Navarre became Henry IV of France, and French monarchs styled themselves "King of France and of Navarre" until the Revolution.

Navarre retained its fueros and institutions as a viceroyalty within the Spanish monarchy until the 19th century; after the First Carlist War, the Ley Paccionada of 1841 converted it into a province while preserving substantial fiscal autonomy — the root of its present chartered status, reaffirmed in the Amejoramiento del Fuero of 1982 under the Spanish Constitution.

Geography and culture

The north of Navarre is mountainous, traversed by the western Pyrenees and the humid Basque-speaking valleys; the south (the Ribera) is a drier Ebro-valley plain known for wine and horticulture. The Basque language is co-official in the northern part of the community, under a linguistic zoning system. Pamplona's festival of San Fermín (6–14 July), with its famous running of the bulls, popularised by Ernest Hemingway's The Sun Also Rises, draws visitors from around the world. The Camino de Santiago pilgrimage route enters Spain from France through Roncesvalles in Navarre.

Economy

Navarre is among Spain's most prosperous regions, with strong automotive (a major Volkswagen plant at Landaben), renewable-energy, agri-food and pharmaceutical sectors, and one of the lowest unemployment rates in the country.
