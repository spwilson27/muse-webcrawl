Joan Littlewood

Joan Maud Littlewood (6 October 1914 – 20 September 2002) was an English theatre director who trained at RADA and developed her Theatre Workshop, bringing working-class theatre to the stage. Her production of Oh, What a Lovely War! (1963) is regarded as a landmark of modern British theatre.

Life and career

Born in Stockwell, Littlewood won a scholarship to RADA but left, moving to Manchester in 1934 where she met folk singer Ewan MacColl; together they founded the Theatre of Action and later Theatre Union. After the Second World War they formed Theatre Workshop in 1945, touring for years before settling at the Theatre Royal, Stratford East in 1953.

Theatre Workshop

Using improvisation, ensemble methods influenced by Stanislavski, Meyerhold, and Laban, Littlewood championed new writers including Brendan Behan (The Quare Fellow, The Hostage) and Shelagh Delaney (A Taste of Honey). Fings Ain't Wot They Used T'Be and Oh, What a Lovely War! transferred to the West End and Broadway; the latter became a 1969 film directed by Richard Attenborough.

Later years

After the death of her partner Gerry Raffles, Littlewood left Britain for France in the 1970s, published her autobiography Joan's Book (1994), and died in Paris in 2002.
