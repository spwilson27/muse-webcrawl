The National Audubon Society, commonly known as Audubon, is an American non-profit environmental organization dedicated to the conservation of birds and their habitats. Founded in 1905, it is one of the oldest organizations of its kind in the world and is named after John James Audubon, the Franco-American ornithologist and naturalist famous for his detailed illustrations of North American birds.

History

The society grew out of late 19th-century campaigns against the slaughter of birds for the feather trade. In 1905 the National Association of Audubon Societies was incorporated in New York, later becoming the National Audubon Society. Early wardens guarded rookeries in Florida, where warden Guy Bradley was killed by plume hunters in 1905.

Activities

Audubon operates a network of state offices, local chapters, nature centers and important bird area programs across the United States. Its work includes science-based conservation, policy advocacy, and community engagement. Signature citizen-science programs include the Christmas Bird Count, run since 1900, and the Great Backyard Bird Count. The organization publishes Audubon magazine and climate reports warning that many North American bird species are threatened by climate change.
