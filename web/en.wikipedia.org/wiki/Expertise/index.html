Expertise

Expertise refers to the high level of knowledge or skill that an expert possesses in a particular field or activity. Experts consistently outperform novices, recognize meaningful patterns quickly, and represent problems at a deeper level.

Acquisition

Research by K. Anders Ericsson emphasizes deliberate practice — focused, feedback-rich practice over many years, popularized (and somewhat simplified) as the 10,000-hour rule by Malcolm Gladwell. Chess studies by Adriaan de Groot and by William Chase and Herbert Simon showed masters recall realistic board positions far better than random ones, pointing to chunking.

Models

The Dreyfus model describes stages from novice through advanced beginner, competence, proficiency, to expertise. Hubert and Stuart Dreyfus argued expert performance is largely intuitive rather than rule-based. See Deliberate practice and Tacit knowledge.

Criticism and limits

Expert judgment can be biased, overconfident, or domain-bound, and expertise does not always transfer between domains.
