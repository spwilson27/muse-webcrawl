Ray class group

In mathematics, a ray class group is a group of algebraic number theory generalizing the ideal class group. It classifies abelian extensions of a number field according to their ramification behavior at a chosen set of places, called a modulus.

Definition

Let K be a number field and let m be a modulus, a formal product of an ideal of the ring of integers and a set of real embeddings of K. The ray modulo m consists of fractional ideals coprime to m generated by elements congruent to 1 modulo m in the multiplicative sense and totally positive at the specified real places. The quotient of the group of fractional ideals coprime to m by the ray is the ray class group modulo m, denoted Clm(K).

Properties

The ray class group is finite. When the modulus is trivial, it reduces to the ordinary (narrow or wide, depending on convention) ideal class group. By class field theory, ray class groups correspond to ray class fields, the maximal abelian extensions of K whose conductor divides the modulus.
