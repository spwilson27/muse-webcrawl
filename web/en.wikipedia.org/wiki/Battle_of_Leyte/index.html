Battle of Leyte

The Battle of Leyte (17 October – 26 December 1944) was the amphibious invasion of the island of Leyte in the Philippines by American forces, opening the Philippines Campaign of 1944–45 in the Second World War. General Douglas MacArthur famously waded ashore at Palo, declaring "I have returned".

Landings

Following preliminary operations by the Sixth Army under Walter Krueger, some 200,000 troops landed on Leyte's eastern coast on 20 October 1944. The landings triggered the massive naval Battle of Leyte Gulf (23–26 October), in which the Imperial Japanese Navy was effectively destroyed as an organised force and the first organised kamikaze attacks were launched.

Fighting on the island

Japanese forces under Tomoyuki Yamashita reinforced Leyte through Ormoc on the west coast, prolonging the campaign into December. Torrential rain, mud, and airfield construction difficulties hampered American operations. Organised resistance ended by late December, though mopping-up continued into 1945.

Significance

The capture of Leyte severed Japan's access to resources in Southeast Asia and provided bases for the subsequent invasion of Luzon.
