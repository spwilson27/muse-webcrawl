Monoceros (Greek: 'unicorn') is a faint constellation on the celestial equator. Its name is Greek for unicorn. Its brightest stars are only of fourth magnitude, and it is overshadowed by its prominent neighbours Orion, Gemini, Canis Major, and Hydra. The constellation was introduced in the early 17th century, appearing on a globe by the Dutch cartographer Petrus Plancius in 1612 or 1613 and charted by Jakob Bartsch in 1624.

Features

Monoceros contains several notable open clusters and nebulae, including the Rosette Nebula (NGC 2237), a vast emission nebula with an embedded cluster, and the Christmas Tree Cluster (NGC 2264) with the Cone Nebula. The variable red supergiant V838 Monocerotis became famous in 2002 when a major outburst produced a spectacular expanding light echo.

The Monoceros Ring, a ring of stars around the Milky Way's disk, may be the tidal stream of a disrupted dwarf galaxy or a feature of the galactic disk itself. The constellation also hosts the black hole system A0620−00 (V616 Monocerotis).
