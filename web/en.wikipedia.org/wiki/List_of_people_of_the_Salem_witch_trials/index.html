List of people of the Salem witch trials

The Salem witch trials (1692–1693) in colonial Massachusetts saw more than 200 people accused of witchcraft. Twenty were executed — nineteen hanged and one, Giles Corey, pressed to death — and at least five others died in prison. This list names the best-known of the accused, the accusers, and the officials involved.

Pressed to death

Giles Corey (died 19 September 1692) refused to enter a plea and was pressed under heavy stones for two days — the only victim of peine forte et dure in the trials.

Died in prison

Sarah Osborne, Roger Toothaker, Ann Foster and Lydia Dustin (acquitted but unable to pay jail fees) were among at least five who died in custody.

Principal accusers

  • Betty Parris (9) and Abigail Williams (11) — the first "afflicted" girls, in the household of Rev. Samuel Parris.
  • Ann Putnam Jr. (12) — named more than 60 people; made a public apology in 1706.
  • Mercy Lewis, Elizabeth Hubbard, Mary Walcott, Elizabeth Parris and others.
  • Tituba — an enslaved woman in the Parris household, among the first accused; her confession ignited the crisis.

Officials and clergy

  • William Stoughton — chief judge of the Court of Oyer and Terminer.
  • John Hathorne and Jonathan Corwin — magistrates who conducted the examinations.
  • Samuel Sewall — judge who later publicly apologised (1697).
  • Samuel Parris — minister of Salem Village.
  • Cotton Mather and Increase Mather — Boston ministers influential in shaping opinion about spectral evidence.
  • Governor William Phips — established, then dissolved, the special court.
