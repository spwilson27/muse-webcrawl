The Exploratorium is a museum of science, technology, and arts in San Francisco, California. Founded in 1969 by physicist Frank Oppenheimer, it pioneered the modern interactive science museum: its hundreds of hands-on exhibits, built largely in its own workshops, invite visitors to experiment with natural phenomena rather than merely observe them. In 2013 the museum moved from the Palace of Fine Arts to a new home on Pier 15 along the Embarcadero waterfront.

History

Frank Oppenheimer, younger brother of J. Robert Oppenheimer, conceived the Exploratorium after his own career in physics and teaching convinced him that people learn science best by direct manipulation and observation. It opened in 1969 in the cavernous Palace of Fine Arts, a Beaux-Arts structure surviving from the 1915 Panama–Pacific International Exposition. Early exhibits were built cheaply from everyday materials, and the museum's ethos — inquiry through play, phenomena over facts, and no "do not touch" signs — influenced the worldwide science centre movement.

After Oppenheimer's death in 1985, the museum continued to expand its exhibit development, teacher training, and publishing programmes; its exhibit "cookbooks" have been used by museums around the world to replicate its designs. In April 2013 it reopened at Piers 15 and 17 on the San Francisco waterfront, in a renovated pier shed with over 300,000 square feet (about 28,000 m²) of indoor and outdoor exhibit space, designed to be a net-zero energy facility.

Exhibits and programmes

The museum contains more than 600 exhibits at any time, grouped in galleries covering human phenomena, tinkering, seeing and listening, the living world, and the outdoor environment of the bay. Signature exhibits include the Tactile Dome, a pitch-black crawl-through environment experienced entirely by touch (originally created in 1971), and numerous exhibits on perception, electricity, waves, and mechanics. The Tinkering Studio provides a dedicated making space, and the museum's Explainers — a corps of young staff in orange vests — demonstrate and discuss exhibits with visitors, a programme Oppenheimer modelled partly on his own teaching practice.

Beyond the museum floor, the Exploratorium runs teacher professional-development programmes, publishes digital resources and apps, and develops exhibits for partner institutions internationally.

Influence

The Exploratorium is widely credited with transforming science museums from repositories of objects into laboratories of experience; its model was emulated by hundreds of science centres founded in subsequent decades, and Oppenheimer's writings on teaching remain a reference point in informal science education.
