Nagano Prefecture (Japanese: 長野県, Nagano-ken) is a landlocked prefecture of Japan in the Chūbu region of Honshu. Its mountainous interior — the Japanese Alps — gives it some of the country's most celebrated scenery, ski resorts and hot springs, and the longest life expectancy of any Japanese prefecture.

Geography

Nagano is bordered by eight other prefectures, more than any other in Japan. The Hida, Kiso and Akaishi mountain ranges (the Northern, Central and Southern Alps) run through it, and nine of Japan's twelve highest mountains lie within or on its borders. Rivers including the Shinano — Japan's longest — and the Tenryū drain its valleys. The climate is an inland one of cold, snowy winters and cooler summers than the Pacific lowlands, especially in resort towns such as Karuizawa.

History

The prefecture corresponds largely to the old province of Shinano (hence the alternative name Shinshū). Zenkō-ji, the great Buddhist temple in Nagano City founded in the seventh century, drew pilgrims for over a millennium. The feudal castles of Matsumoto — one of Japan's twelve surviving original keeps and a National Treasure — and Ueda recall the Sengoku period, while the Nakasendō post towns of the Kiso Valley, such as Tsumago and Narai, preserve Edo-period streetscapes.

1998 Winter Olympics

Nagano hosted the 1998 Winter Olympic Games, the last of the twentieth century, remembered for the debut of snowboarding, NHL players in ice hockey, and ski jumping from the Hakuba valley. The Hokuriku Shinkansen was extended to Nagano for the Games, cutting the journey from Tokyo to under ninety minutes.

Economy and culture

Agriculture thrives in the basins: Nagano leads Japan in apples along with grapes, soba (buckwheat) and miso production. Precision engineering, electronics and tourism anchor the modern economy. The prefecture is famous for the wild snow monkeys of Jigokudani, the mountain shrines of Togakushi, and some of Japan's largest ski areas. Nagano's residents — men in particular — have repeatedly recorded Japan's highest life expectancy, commonly attributed to diet and active rural lifestyles.
