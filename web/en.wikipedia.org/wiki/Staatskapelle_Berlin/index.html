Staatskapelle Berlin

The Staatskapelle Berlin is a German orchestra and the resident orchestra of the Berlin State Opera (Staatsoper Unter den Linden). Founded in 1570 as the electoral court chapel of the Margraves of Brandenburg, it is one of the oldest orchestras in the world.

History

The ensemble began as the Hofkapelle of the Elector of Brandenburg. In 1742 it moved into Frederick the Great's new court opera house on Unter den Linden as the Königliche Kapelle. Over the centuries its chief conductors and music directors included Gaspare Spontini, Giacomo Meyerbeer, Felix Mendelssohn Bartholdy, and Richard Strauss, who was its principal conductor from 1899 to 1913.

In the 20th century the orchestra was led by such figures as Erich Kleiber, Wilhelm Furtwängler, Herbert von Karajan, Otmar Suitner and, from 1992, Daniel Barenboim as Generalmusikdirektor — a tenure that lasted more than three decades and included the orchestra's activity as a symphony orchestra of international standing as well as in the opera pit.

The orchestra today

Besides its opera commitments at the Staatsoper, the Staatskapelle gives subscription concerts at the Philharmonie and the Konzerthaus Berlin, tours internationally, and makes numerous recordings. In 2024 Christian Thielemann succeeded Barenboim as General Music Director of the Staatsoper and chief conductor of the orchestra.
