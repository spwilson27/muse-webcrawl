Algirdas (c. 1296 – May 1377) was Grand Duke of Lithuania from 1345 to 1377. With his brother Kęstutis, who ruled the ethnic Lithuanian west and defended it against the Teutonic Order, Algirdas presided over the greatest expansion of the Grand Duchy, extending its rule deep into the former lands of Kievan Rus' until Lithuania stretched, in the later formula, "from sea to sea" — the Baltic to the Black Sea steppe.

Rise to power

A son of Gediminas, Algirdas ruled Vitebsk as a prince through his first marriage to Maria of Vitebsk, absorbing her principality into the Lithuanian sphere. In 1345, with Kęstutis's help, he deposed their younger brother Jaunutis and took the grand-ducal throne, thereafter sharing power in a remarkably durable diarchy: Kęstutis in the west, Algirdas in the east.

Expansion eastward

Algirdas annexed or subordinated a succession of Rus' principalities — Bryansk, Smolensk (for periods), Chernihiv, Kyiv, Volhynia and Podolia. His victory over the Tatars of the Golden Horde at the Battle of Blue Waters (1362/1363) brought Kyiv and the steppe frontier under Lithuanian control and is often treated as the high point of Lithuanian expansion. Three times (1368, 1370, 1372) he marched on Moscow in support of his brother-in-law Mikhail of Tver, each time reaching the Kremlin's walls without taking the citadel. Within his realm he tolerated Orthodoxy — both his wives were Orthodox Rus' princesses and many of his sons were baptised Orthodox — while remaining personally pagan in the eyes of most chroniclers, though some sources claim a deathbed conversion. Vilnius tradition associates him with the martyrdom of the Orthodox saints Anthony, John and Eustathius.

Death and succession

Algirdas died in 1377 and was succeeded by his son Jogaila, whose marriage to Jadwiga of Poland would found the Jagiellonian union. Algirdas's descendants, the Algirdaičiai, supplied many of the great magnate lines of the Grand Duchy.
