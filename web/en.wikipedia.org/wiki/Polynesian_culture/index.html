Polynesian culture

Polynesian culture is the culture of the indigenous peoples of Polynesia who share common traits in language, customs, and society. The indigenous people who inhabit the islands of Polynesia are termed Polynesians, and they share many similar traits, including language families, a general belief in gods and religion, housing, myths, and food.

History and voyaging

Polynesians are among history's greatest navigators, settling the vast triangle of the Pacific Ocean bounded by Hawaiʻi, Rapa Nui (Easter Island), and Aotearoa (New Zealand) using double-hulled voyaging canoes and sophisticated non-instrument navigation based on stars, winds, waves, and bird flight. They descend from Austronesian-speaking peoples who spread from the region of Taiwan through Southeast Asia and the Lapita culture into the Pacific.

Society and religion

Traditional Polynesian societies were often chiefly, organised around kinship and governed by ariki (chiefs), with concepts such as mana (spiritual power) and tapu (sacred restrictions, the origin of the word "taboo") central to social life. Religion encompassed gods such as Tangaloa, Tane, and Pele, celebrated in myth and ceremony.

Arts and practices

Polynesian culture is known for tattooing (tatau), haka and other dance traditions, bark cloth (tapa) making, wood carving, and oral traditions preserving genealogies and voyaging histories.
