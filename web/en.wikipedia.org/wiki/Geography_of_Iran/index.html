Geography of Iran

Location: Western Asia
Area: 1,648,195 km²
Highest point: Mount Damavand (5,610 m)
Coastline: 2,440 km

The geography of Iran is dominated by the Iranian Plateau, ringed by mountain ranges and bordered by the Caspian Sea to the north and the Persian Gulf and Gulf of Oman to the south. Iran is the second-largest country in the Middle East, with an area of about 1.65 million km², and one of the most mountainous countries in the world.

Topography

The Zagros Mountains run from the northwest to the southeast for more than 1,500 km, formed by the collision of the Arabian Plate with the Eurasian Plate. The Alborz range fringes the southern shore of the Caspian Sea and contains Mount Damavand (5,610 m), a dormant stratovolcano and the highest peak in the Middle East. The interior plateau contains two great salt deserts: the Dasht-e Kavir in the north-central region and the Dasht-e Lut in the east, among the hottest and driest places on Earth. Lowlands are confined to the Caspian coastal plain and the Khuzestan plain at the head of the Persian Gulf.

Climate

Iran's climate is mostly arid and semi-arid, with a humid subtropical strip along the Caspian coast receiving heavy rainfall. Winters can be severe in the highlands, while summers are extremely hot, particularly in the southern lowlands and deserts. Most precipitation falls in winter and spring.

Natural resources and hazards

Iran holds some of the world's largest proven reserves of petroleum and natural gas, concentrated in the Zagros foothills and offshore in the Persian Gulf. The country lies in an active seismic zone along the Alpine belt and experiences frequent destructive earthquakes, such as the 2003 Bam earthquake. Lake Urmia in the northwest, once one of the largest salt lakes in the world, has shrunk drastically due to drought and water diversion.
