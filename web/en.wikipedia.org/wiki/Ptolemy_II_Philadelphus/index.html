Ptolemy II Philadelphus (309 BC – 246 BC) was Pharaoh of Ptolemaic Egypt from 284 to 246 BC, succeeding his father Ptolemy I Soter, who had made him co-regent in 285 BC. His epithet Philadelphus means "sibling-loving", reflecting his marriage to his full sister Arsinoe II, after the Egyptian royal custom.

Reign

Under Ptolemy II, Egypt reached the height of its power and wealth. He reorganised the administration and tax system, expanded the canal linking the Nile to the Red Sea, fought the First Syrian War against the Seleucid Empire, intervened in Greece during the Chremonidean War, and maintained a large navy dominating the Aegean. Diplomatic and commercial links stretched from Rome to India.

Culture

His court at Alexandria was the centre of Hellenistic learning. Ptolemy II lavishly patronised the Great Library and Mouseion, where scholars such as Euclid's successors, Eratosthenes (appointed late in the reign), the poets Callimachus and Theocritus, and the engineer Ctesibius worked. Tradition credits his reign with the Septuagint translation of the Hebrew scriptures and the completion of the Pharos lighthouse, built by Sostratus of Cnidus — one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World. He also instituted the Ptolemaieia festival and was deified alongside Arsinoe as the Theoi Adelphoi.
