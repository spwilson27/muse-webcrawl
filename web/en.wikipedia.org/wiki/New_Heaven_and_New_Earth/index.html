New Heaven and New Earth

The New Heaven and the New Earth is an expression used in the Bible — in Isaiah, 2 Peter, and the Book of Revelation — to describe the final state of redeemed humanity and creation after the Last Judgment. In Christian eschatology it represents the renewal of the entire cosmos, in which God dwells permanently with his people and death, sorrow, and pain are abolished.

Biblical passages

"For behold, I create new heavens and a new earth, and the former things shall not be remembered or come into mind." — Isaiah 65:17

Isaiah 65 and 66 promise a divinely created "new heavens and a new earth" in which Jerusalem is a joy and the wolf and lamb feed together. In the New Testament, 2 Peter 3:13 states that believers "look for new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness," following the dissolution of the present heavens and earth by fire.

Revelation 21–22

The most detailed description appears in the closing chapters of Revelation. After the millennium and the final judgment, John sees "a new heaven and a new earth, for the first heaven and the first earth had passed away, and the sea was no more." The holy city, the New Jerusalem, descends from heaven "prepared as a bride adorned for her husband." A voice declares that God will dwell with humanity, wipe away every tear, and that there will be no more death, mourning, crying, or pain. The city is described as having streets of gold, gates of pearl, the river of the water of life, and the tree of life, with no need of sun or temple because God and the Lamb are present.

Interpretations

Interpreters debate whether the new creation is an entirely different cosmos replacing the old or the present creation radically renewed and liberated from corruption — a reading supported by Romans 8:18–25, which speaks of creation itself being set free. Most Christian traditions affirm a bodily resurrection and a material, eternal dwelling place for the redeemed, rather than a purely spiritual heaven. Some traditions, such as Jehovah's Witnesses, understand the "new earth" as a restored paradise earth governed from heaven, while others interpret the imagery largely symbolically of the perfected church and the presence of God.
