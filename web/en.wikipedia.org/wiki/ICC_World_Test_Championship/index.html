ICC World Test Championship

The ICC World Test Championship (WTC) is a league competition for Test cricket run by the International Cricket Council (ICC), which started on 1 August 2019. It is intended to be the premier championship for Test cricket, giving context to bilateral Test series. The top two teams in the league table compete in a final, the winners being crowned world Test champions and awarded the ICC Test Championship mace.

History

A world championship for Tests had been proposed for many years; a tournament was planned for 2013 in England but was cancelled for financial reasons, and a second attempt in 2017 also fell through. The ICC finally approved the league format in 2017, and the first cycle ran from 2019 to 2021.

Finals

The inaugural final was played at the Rose Bowl in Southampton in June 2021, where New Zealand defeated India to become the first champions. The second final, at The Oval in London in June 2023, was won by Australia, who beat India. The third final was held at Lord's in June 2025, where South Africa defeated Australia to claim their first title.

Format

Nine full-member teams participate in each cycle. Each team plays six series — three at home and three away — over roughly two years. Points are awarded for each match, standardized so that all matches in a series carry equal total value regardless of the series length. The two teams with the highest percentage of available points won contest the final.
