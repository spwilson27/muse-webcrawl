Messianic Judaism

Messianic Judaism is a modern religious movement that combines elements of Judaism, including Jewish tradition and holiday observance, with evangelical Christian theology centred on belief in Jesus (called Yeshua) as the Jewish Messiah. The movement emerged in its modern form in the United States in the 1960s and 1970s, growing out of the older Hebrew Christian movement.

Beliefs and practices

Messianic Jews affirm Jesus as the Messiah and generally accept the New Testament as scripture alongside the Hebrew Bible. Congregations typically worship on Saturday (Shabbat), observe Jewish festivals such as Passover and Sukkot, use Hebrew liturgy, and read from the Torah in synagogue-style services. Most adherents also hold evangelical doctrines such as salvation by faith in Jesus.

Reception

Mainstream Jewish denominations do not consider Messianic Judaism a form of Judaism, viewing belief in Jesus as the Messiah as incompatible with Judaism, and regard the movement as a form of Christianity. Israeli courts have likewise ruled that Messianic Jews who believe in Jesus are not entitled to automatic citizenship under the Law of Return as Jews if they belong to another religion, though ethnic Jews who adopt the belief may immigrate under other provisions. Messianic organisations dispute this characterisation, regarding themselves as a Jewish movement.

Major umbrella organisations include the Union of Messianic Jewish Congregations (founded 1979) and the Messianic Jewish Alliance of America.
