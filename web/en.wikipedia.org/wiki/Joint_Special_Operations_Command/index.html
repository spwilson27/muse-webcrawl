RoleCommand of special mission units; counterterrorism

The Joint Special Operations Command (JSOC) is a component command of the United States Special Operations Command, headquartered at Fort Bragg, North Carolina. It was established in 1980 following the failed Operation Eagle Claw hostage-rescue mission in Iran, to standardise the training, equipment, and tactics of the military's highest-readiness special mission units.

Subordinate units

JSOC controls the Army's 1st Special Forces Operational Detachment–Delta (Delta Force) and Intelligence Support Activity, the Navy's Naval Special Warfare Development Group (DEVGRU), and the Air Force's 24th Special Tactics Squadron, together with supporting aviation and intelligence elements such as Task Force Orange.

Operations

JSOC forces have conducted counterterrorism and hostage-rescue missions worldwide, including in Panama, Somalia (1993), Afghanistan, and Iraq. Its best-known operation was the 2011 raid in Abbottabad, Pakistan, in which DEVGRU operators killed al-Qaeda leader Osama bin Laden. Since 2001 JSOC has become a central instrument of U.S. counterterrorism campaigns, conducting raids and providing intelligence support across the Middle East, the Horn of Africa, and beyond — work that has drawn both praise and scrutiny over civilian casualties and secrecy.
