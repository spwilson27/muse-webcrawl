Cognac (French pronunciation: [kɔɲak]) is a commune in the Charente department, southwestern France. It is a sub-prefecture situated on the river Charente between the towns of Angoulême and Saintes. The town gives its name to the world-famous brandy Cognac, which must be produced in the surrounding region to bear the name.

History

The town grew around a castle on the Charente; Francis I, one of France's most admired kings, was born at the Château de Cognac in 1494. From the 17th century, Dutch and English merchants developed the local trade in double-distilled eau-de-vie, creating great houses such as Hennessy, Martell, Rémy Martin and Courvoisier, several headquartered in or near the town.

Economy

Beyond the distilleries and cooperages of the brandy industry, Cognac hosts related industries of glassmaking, cork and packaging, and the nearby airbase BA 709 Cognac–Châteaubernard. The town also hosts the annual Cognac Blues Passions festival.
