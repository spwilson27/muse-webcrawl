Edmund Campion (24 January 1540 – 1 December 1581) was an English Catholic Jesuit priest and martyr. A brilliant scholar at Oxford, he abandoned a promising career in the Church of England, became a Jesuit, and returned to England on a clandestine mission in 1580. Within little more than a year he was captured, tortured and executed for treason. He was canonised in 1970 as one of the Forty Martyrs of England and Wales.

Early life

Campion was educated at Christ's Hospital and St John's College, Oxford, where his eloquence made him famous; he welcomed Queen Elizabeth I on her visit to the university in 1566. He took Anglican orders as a deacon but grew troubled in conscience about the Reformation. Leaving Oxford in 1569, he went to Ireland and then to Douai, where he reconciled with the Catholic Church. In 1573 he joined the Society of Jesus in Rome and taught rhetoric at the Jesuit college in Prague.

Mission and death

In 1580 Campion and Robert Persons were sent on the first Jesuit mission to England. Travelling in disguise, Campion ministered secretly to Catholic families and composed his famous "Brag," a letter challenging the Privy Council to a public disputation, along with the tract Decem Rationes ("Ten Reasons"). He was betrayed and arrested at Lyford Grange in July 1581, racked in the Tower of London, tried for treason on fabricated charges of conspiracy, and hanged, drawn and quartered at Tyburn on 1 December 1581.

Legacy

Campion is commemorated in numerous schools bearing his name, and his feast day is kept on 1 December. Campion Hall at Oxford is named after him.
