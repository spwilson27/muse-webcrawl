Sully (also titled Sully: Miracle on the Hudson in some markets) is a 2016 American biographical drama film directed by Clint Eastwood and written by Todd Komarnicki, based on the memoir Highest Duty by Chesley "Sully" Sullenberger. Tom Hanks stars as Sullenberger, with Aaron Eckhart as first officer Jeff Skiles and Laura Linney as Sullenberger's wife, Lorraine.

Plot

On 15 January 2009, US Airways Flight 1549 strikes a flock of Canada geese shortly after takeoff from LaGuardia Airport, losing both engines. Unable to reach any airport, Captain Sullenberger ditches the Airbus A320 in the Hudson River; all 155 people aboard survive in the event dubbed the "Miracle on the Hudson". The film interweaves the flight and water evacuation with the subsequent National Transportation Safety Board investigation, in which simulations initially suggest Sullenberger could have returned to LaGuardia — until human reaction time is factored in, vindicating his decision.

Production

The film was shot almost entirely with IMAX cameras and on location in New York, using a retired Airbus airframe floated on water tanks and in the Hudson's rescue locations. Eastwood and Hanks aimed for a restrained, procedural tone centred on professionalism and institutional scrutiny.

Reception

Sully premiered at the Telluride Film Festival on 2 September 2016 and grossed over $240 million worldwide against a $60 million budget. It received generally positive reviews, with praise for Hanks's performance and Eastwood's direction, and was named one of the top ten films of 2016 by the American Film Institute and the National Board of Review. It was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Sound Editing. Some NTSB investigators criticised the film's portrayal of the inquiry as more adversarial than the real proceedings.
