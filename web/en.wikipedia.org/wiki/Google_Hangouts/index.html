Google Hangouts

Google Hangouts was a cross-platform instant messaging service developed by Google. Originally a feature of Google+, Hangouts became a standalone product in 2013, when Google also began integrating features from its other messaging products, including Google Talk. It supported text messaging, voice and video calls, and group video conferences of up to ten participants (later increased).

In 2017, Google began developing two separate enterprise communication products, Google Meet and Google Chat, as part of the Google Workspace office suite, and began transitioning Workspace users from Hangouts. Google transitioned all remaining users from Hangouts to Chat and Meet in 2022 and Hangouts was shut down on November 1, 2022.

History

Hangouts was launched on May 15, 2013, at the I/O developers' conference, combining Google Talk, Google+ Messenger, and the video-chat system of Google+ into a single product. In 2015 a dedicated Hangouts website was launched. Google announced in 2016 that Hangouts would increasingly focus on enterprise use, while consumer messaging would move to the (later discontinued) Allo and Duo apps.

Features

Hangouts allowed conversations between two or more users across the web, Chrome extension, and Android and iOS apps. Chat histories were saved online and synced between devices. A "watermark" showed how far a correspondent had read. Video calls could be broadcast publicly using the "Hangouts on Air" feature, which was discontinued in 2019.
