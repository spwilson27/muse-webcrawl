Noise reduction

Noise reduction is the process of removing noise from a signal. Noise reduction techniques exist for all kinds of signals, including audio and images. To reduce noise, systems typically attenuate or remove frequency components, apply statistical smoothing, or use models of the expected signal to separate it from random corruption.

Audio

In audio recording and reproduction, noise reduction may target hum, hiss, clicks, and background sounds. Analogue systems such as Dolby noise reduction used companding — boosting quiet high-frequency content during recording and attenuating it on playback — to push tape hiss below audibility. In the digital era, spectral subtraction and adaptive filtering estimate the noise spectrum (for example during a pause in speech) and subtract it from the signal. Modern conferencing and recording software uses machine-learning models trained to isolate the human voice from other sounds.

Images

Image noise reduction (denoising) seeks to remove grain, "salt-and-pepper" artefacts, and sensor noise while preserving edges and detail. Classical methods include:

  • Median filtering — replaces each pixel with the median of its neighbours, effective against impulse noise.
  • Gaussian smoothing — weighted averaging that reduces noise but blurs fine detail.
  • Bilateral filtering — smooths while respecting edges by weighting pixels by both distance and intensity similarity.
  • Non-local means — averages pixels whose surrounding patches are similar, preserving textures.
  • Wavelet and total-variation methods — threshold or regularize transform coefficients to suppress noise.

Deep-learning denoisers trained on paired noisy/clean images now achieve state-of-the-art results in photography, medical imaging, and video.

Trade-offs

All noise reduction involves a trade-off: aggressive smoothing removes genuine detail along with noise, producing waxy textures in images or muffled, artefacts-laden audio. The challenge is to model which parts of a signal are "noise" and which are wanted content.
