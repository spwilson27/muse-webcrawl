Polyphenol oxidase

Polyphenol oxidase (PPO)
EC numbers: 1.10.3.1, 1.14.18.1
Cofactor: copper
Includes: tyrosinase, catechol oxidase, laccase (related)

Polyphenol oxidase (PPO; also polyphenol oxidase I, chloroplastic) is a group of copper-containing enzymes that catalyze the oxidation of phenolic compounds in the presence of molecular oxygen. The family includes catechol oxidase (o-diphenol oxidase) and tyrosinase, which additionally hydroxylates monophenols.

Reactions

PPOs catalyze two reactions: the hydroxylation of monophenols to o-diphenols (cresolase activity, characteristic of tyrosinase) and the oxidation of o-diphenols to o-quinones (catecholase activity). The resulting quinones react non-enzymatically to form brown, red, or black polymers — the melanins. In plants this enzymatic browning occurs when tissue is cut or bruised and cells' compartmentation is broken, bringing the enzyme (stored in plastids) into contact with phenolic substrates (in the vacuole).

Biological role

In plants, PPO products can deter herbivores and pathogens; the quinones cross-link proteins and reduce nutritional quality for insects. In animals, tyrosinase is essential for melanin pigmentation, and its loss causes forms of albinism. In fungi and bacteria, related enzymes contribute to pigmentation and spore resistance.

Food science

Enzymatic browning degrades the appearance of many fruits, vegetables, and seafood (e.g., shrimp melanosis), and is controlled by refrigeration, acidulants such as lemon juice, ascorbic acid, sulfites, blanching, and exclusion of oxygen. Conversely, browning by PPO is desirable in the production of tea, coffee, cocoa, raisins, and prunes. A transgenic non-browning apple (Arctic apple) was created by silencing PPO genes and approved for sale in the United States and Canada.
