Willard Carroll Smith II (born 25 September 1968) is an American actor, rapper and producer. Known for his work in film, television and music, he has received numerous accolades, including an Academy Award, a BAFTA Award, and four Grammy Awards. Films in which he has starred have grossed over $9.5 billion worldwide, making him one of Hollywood's most bankable stars.

Early career in music and television

Smith began his career as part of the hip hop duo DJ Jazzy Jeff & the Fresh Prince with childhood friend Jeffrey Townes, scoring hits such as "Parents Just Don't Understand" (1988), which won the first Grammy Award for Best Rap Performance, and "Summertime" (1991). He went on to star as a fictionalised version of himself in the NBC sitcom The Fresh Prince of Bel-Air (1990–1996), which made him a household name.

Film stardom

Smith transitioned to leading film roles with Bad Boys (1995), Independence Day (1996) and Men in Black (1997). He earned Academy Award nominations for portraying Muhammad Ali in Ali (2001) and stockbroker Chris Gardner in The Pursuit of Happyness (2006). Other major successes include I, Robot (2004), Hitch (2005), I Am Legend (2007), Hancock (2008), Aladdin (2019) and Bad Boys for Life (2020).

Academy Award and 2022 ceremony incident

At the 94th Academy Awards in March 2022, Smith won the Academy Award for Best Actor for playing Richard Williams in King Richard. Earlier in the ceremony he had walked onstage and slapped presenter Chris Rock in response to a joke about his wife. The incident drew worldwide attention; Smith resigned from the Academy, which banned him from attending its events for ten years.
