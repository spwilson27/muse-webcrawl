Vow

A vow (Latin: votum) is a promise or oath made to a deity or, by extension, a solemn commitment made to another person or oneself. Vows are found in nearly all religious traditions and in secular ceremonies such as weddings.

Religious vows

In Roman Catholicism, members of religious orders take the evangelical counsels as vows of poverty, chastity, and obedience; a distinction is drawn between simple and solemn vows, and private vows may be made by any believer. Monastic vows also structure Buddhist monastic life, where monks and nuns undertake the precepts of the Vinaya. In the Hebrew Bible, the Nazirite vow and the vows of figures such as Hannah and Jephthah illustrate the binding force — and danger — of rash vows.

Marriage and other vows

Marriage vows are the promises exchanged by partners during a wedding ceremony, varying by tradition and increasingly written by couples themselves. Other familiar vows include the Hippocratic Oath of physicians, vows of office, and vows of silence kept by some monastic communities.
