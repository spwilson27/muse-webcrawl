British Aerospace Sea Harrier

The British Aerospace Sea Harrier was a naval short take-off and vertical landing (STOVL) jet fighter, reconnaissance and strike aircraft operated by the Royal Navy's Fleet Air Arm from 1980 to 2006 and by the Indian Navy from 1983 to 2016. Derived from the Hawker Siddeley Harrier, it is best known for its service in the 1982 Falklands War, where Sea Harriers provided air defence for the British task force.

Variants and service

The Sea Harrier FRS1 had a raised cockpit for better visibility, a Blue Fox radar, and was designed to operate from the Invincible-class carriers' ski jumps. In the Falklands War, FRS1s flew over 1,100 combat air patrol sorties, and are credited with 20 air-to-air kills against Argentine aircraft without a single air-to-air loss, losses being to ground fire and accidents. The upgraded FA2 (1993) added the Blue Vixen pulse-Doppler radar and AIM-120 AMRAAM beyond-visual-range missiles, making it among the most capable naval fighters of its day.

Retirement

The FA2 was withdrawn in 2006 amid defence economies, leaving the Royal Navy temporarily without a dedicated carrier fighter until the F-35B era. India's Sea Harrier FRS51s, based aboard INS Viraat and equipped latterly with Derby missiles, were retired in May 2016.
