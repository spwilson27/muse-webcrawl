Kaur

Kaur is a name borne by Sikh women as a symbol of equality and sovereignty.

Kaur (Punjabi: ਕੌਰ, romanized: kaura, literally "prince" or "crown prince") is a surname or middle name given to women of the Sikh faith. It was bestowed by the tenth Sikh Guru, Guru Gobind Singh, at the founding of the Khalsa in 1699, when Sikh men were given the name Singh ("lion") and women the name Kaur.

History

Before 1699, Sikh women typically took the surname of their husband or family caste after marriage. Guru Gobind Singh's reform was intended to abolish caste distinctions and to assert the independent identity and equal status of women. By giving all Sikh women a single royal name meaning "prince", the Guru declared that a woman need not derive her identity from her father or husband.

Usage

Kaur is commonly used as a middle name (for example, "Harpreet Kaur") or as a surname. Not all women named Kaur are Sikh, and the name also occurs among some Hindu Punjabi families, but its widespread use is strongly associated with Sikh identity. In the Sikh diaspora, Kaur remains one of the most common female surnames.
