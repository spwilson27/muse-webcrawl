Dawn Therese Brancheau (née LoVerde; April 16, 1969 – February 24, 2010) was an American animal trainer at SeaWorld Orlando. A senior trainer with fifteen years of experience, she was killed on February 24, 2010, by Tilikum, a bull orca (killer whale), during a public performance-related session—an incident that drew international attention and contributed to major changes in the keeping and performance of captive orcas.

Early life

Brancheau grew up in Cedar Lake, Indiana, the youngest of six children. Inspired by a childhood visit to SeaWorld, she pursued a career working with marine mammals, earning a degree in psychology from the University of South Carolina. She began her career at Six Flags Great Adventure in New Jersey before joining SeaWorld Orlando in 1996, initially working with sea lions and otters before becoming a killer whale trainer.

Death

On February 24, 2010, following a "Dine with Shamu" show, Tilikum pulled Brancheau into the water by her ponytail and killed her in front of guests. The autopsy attributed her death to drowning and blunt-force trauma. Tilikum had previously been involved in two human deaths, in 1991 and 1999. The U.S. Occupational Safety and Health Administration fined SeaWorld and barred trainers from close water contact with orcas during performances, a restriction that became effectively permanent.

Legacy

Brancheau's death was a central event in the 2013 documentary film Blackfish, which critically examined orca captivity and was followed by sharp declines in SeaWorld's attendance and share price, the end of its orca breeding program, and the phase-out of theatrical orca shows. The Dawn Brancheau Foundation was established in her memory to support causes she cared about, including children and animals.
