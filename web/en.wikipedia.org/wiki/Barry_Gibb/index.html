Barry Gibb performing

Sir Barry Alan Crompton Gibb AC CBE (born 1 September 1946) is a British-American musician, singer-songwriter and record producer, best known as a co-founder of the Bee Gees with his younger brothers Robin and Maurice. Raised in Manchester and then in Redcliffe, Queensland, Australia, Gibb became, with his brothers, one of the best-selling recording artists of all time; his high falsetto became the signature sound of the group's disco era.

Career

The Bee Gees first found success in the late 1960s with orchestral pop hits such as "Massachusetts", "To Love Somebody" and "Words". After relocating to Miami and working with producer Arif Mardin, the group reinvented itself with the R&B-inflected Main Course (1975) and then dominated popular music through the Saturday Night Fever soundtrack (1977), for which Barry wrote or co-wrote "Stayin' Alive", "Night Fever" and "How Deep Is Your Love". He also wrote hits for others, including Barbra Streisand's "Woman in Love", Dionne Warwick's "Heartbreaker", Kenny Rogers and Dolly Parton's "Islands in the Stream", and Diana Ross's "Chain Reaction".

Later life

Following Maurice's death in 2003 and Robin's in 2012, Barry is the last surviving Gibb brother; he was knighted in 2018 for services to music and charity. His solo albums include In the Now (2016) and Greenfields (2021), a country reinterpretation of Bee Gees songs recorded with guests including Parton and Keith Urban.
