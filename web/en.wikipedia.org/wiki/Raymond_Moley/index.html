Raymond Moley

Raymond Charles Moley
Born: 27 September 1886, Berea, Ohio, US
Died: 18 February 1975 (aged 88)
Occupation: Political scientist, journalist
Known for: Leader of Roosevelt's "Brain Trust"

Raymond Charles Moley (27 September 1886 – 18 February 1975) was an American political scientist and journalist. He was a leading member of President Franklin D. Roosevelt's "Brain Trust" during the first New Deal and helped draft much of Roosevelt's early economic programme, before breaking with the administration and becoming one of its most prominent critics.

Career

A professor of government at Columbia University, Moley was recruited to advise Roosevelt during his governorship of New York and his 1932 presidential campaign; he is credited with coining the phrase "a new deal" in Roosevelt's acceptance speech. As Assistant Secretary of State in 1933 he clashed with Secretary Cordell Hull at the London Economic Conference, after which he resigned.

Moley then became a columnist for Newsweek and the editor of Today magazine. His memoir After Seven Years (1939) was a notable insider account critical of the later New Deal. In later life he moved steadily rightward, supporting conservative candidates and causes.
