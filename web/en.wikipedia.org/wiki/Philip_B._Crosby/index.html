Philip Bayard Crosby (18 June 1926 – 18 August 2001) was an American businessman and author who contributed influential ideas to quality management. He is best known for the concepts of "Do It Right The First Time", Zero Defects, and the argument of his bestselling 1979 book Quality Is Free: that investment in prevention costs less than the money companies waste making, inspecting and fixing defective products.

Career

Crosby grew up in Ohio, served in the US Navy in the Second World War and Korea, and began his quality career in 1952 as a reliability technician. Working for Crosley, Bendix and the Martin Company — where, on the Pershing missile programme, he developed the Zero Defects concept — he rose through quality management. In 1965 he became vice president of quality at ITT, where he served 14 years and rolled out his quality improvement process worldwide.

In 1979, the year Quality Is Free became a bestseller, he founded Philip Crosby Associates, a management consultancy and "Quality College" in Winter Park, Florida, whose courses trained executives from many of the largest US corporations.

Four absolutes of quality management

  1. Quality is defined as conformance to requirements, not as "goodness".
  2. The system for causing quality is prevention, not appraisal.
  3. The performance standard must be Zero Defects, not "that's close enough".
  4. The measurement of quality is the price of nonconformance — the cost of doing things wrong.

Crosby summarised his approach in a 14-step quality improvement programme and the "vaccine" of management commitment, communication and organisation. He wrote numerous further books, including Quality Without Tears (1984), and was inducted into the Quality Hall of Fame debates alongside contemporaries such as W. Edwards Deming and Joseph M. Juran, with whom his motivational, management-centred approach was sometimes contrasted.
