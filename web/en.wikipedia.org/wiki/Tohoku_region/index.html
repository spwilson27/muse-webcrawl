The Tohoku region (Japanese: 東北地方, Tōhoku-chihō, literally "north-east region") consists of the northeastern portion of Honshu, the largest island of Japan. It is made up of six prefectures: Aomori, Iwate, Miyagi, Akita, Yamagata and Fukushima. The region's largest city is Sendai.

Geography

The Ou Mountains run down the spine of Tohoku, dividing the Pacific side from the Sea of Japan side and producing sharply different climates: the Pacific side has cooler summers, while the Japan Sea side receives some of the heaviest snowfall in the world. The region is known for its mountains, ski resorts, hot springs and extensive rice farming, and it has long been considered the traditional, rural heartland of Japan.

History

Historically the region was the territory of the Emishi peoples and was incorporated into the Japanese state only gradually. In the feudal period powerful domains such as Sendai (Date clan) and Aizu dominated. Tohoku's long winters and famines gave it a reputation for hardship, but its festivals — Sendai's Tanabata, Aomori's Nebuta Matsuri, Akita's Kanto Matsuri and Yamagata's Hanagasa Matsuri — are among Japan's most famous summer events.

2011 earthquake and tsunami

On 11 March 2011 a magnitude 9.0–9.1 undersea earthquake off the Tohoku coast triggered a devastating tsunami that inundated Pacific coastal communities, killing more than 18,000 people and causing the Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster. Reconstruction of the region has continued for years since.
