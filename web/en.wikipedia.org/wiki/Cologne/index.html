Cologne

Cologne (German: Köln)

Country: Germany
State: North Rhine-Westphalia
Founded: 50 AD (Colonia Claudia Ara Agrippinensium)
Population: ~1.1 million
Landmark: Cologne Cathedral (UNESCO)

Cologne is the largest city of Germany's North Rhine-Westphalia state and the fourth-most populous city in Germany, with around 1.1 million inhabitants within city limits and a larger Rhine-Ruhr metropolitan population. It lies on the River Rhine about 35 km southeast of Düsseldorf.

History

Founded by the Romans in 50 AD as Colonia Claudia Ara Agrippinensium, Cologne became an important trading and religious centre in the Middle Ages, a member of the Hanseatic League, and home to one of Europe's oldest universities (1388). Its archbishop was an elector of the Holy Roman Empire. The city was heavily bombed in the Second World War and rebuilt in the postwar period.

Landmarks and culture

Cologne Cathedral, a vast Gothic cathedral begun in 1248 and completed in 1880, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and houses the Shrine of the Three Kings. The city is famous for its Carnival, its 12 Romanesque churches, museums such as the Museum Ludwig, and for Eau de Cologne, created here in the early 18th century. Its local beer is Kölsch, and its dialect is also called Kölsch.

Economy

Cologne is a media, insurance, trade-fair and chemical/industrial hub, home to broadcasters, the Koelnmesse fairs, and Ford's European operations nearby.
