The Hawaiian Kingdom, or Kingdom of Hawaii, was a sovereign state located in the Hawaiian Islands. It was established in 1795 when Kamehameha I, then aliʻi of Hawaii island, conquered most of the archipelago; the islands of Kauaʻi and Niʻihau joined peacefully in 1810, completing the unification.

History

The kingdom developed from a traditional chiefdom into a constitutional monarchy recognised by the great powers, including Britain, France, and the United States. Kamehameha III promulgated constitutions in 1840 and 1852 and enacted the Great Māhele land reform. Successive monarchs maintained Hawaii's independence through diplomacy amid growing foreign — especially American — economic influence through the sugar industry. King Kalākaua, the "Merrie Monarch", revived Hawaiian cultural practices and built Iolani Palace in Honolulu.

Overthrow and annexation

In January 1893, Queen Liliʻuokalani was overthrown by a committee of American and European businessmen backed by the U.S. minister and naval forces. A Provisional Government, then the Republic of Hawaii, followed. The United States annexed Hawaii by the Newlands Resolution in 1898, and it became the Territory of Hawaii in 1900 and a U.S. state in 1959. In 1993, the U.S. Congress passed the Apology Resolution acknowledging the illegality of the overthrow.
