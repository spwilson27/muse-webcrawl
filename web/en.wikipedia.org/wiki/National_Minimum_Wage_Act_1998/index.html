National Minimum Wage Act 1998

The National Minimum Wage Act 1998 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that created a national minimum wage applying across the country. It was a flagship policy of the Labour government of Tony Blair, elected in 1997, and the first rates came into force on 1 April 1999.

Provisions

The Act established the Low Pay Commission to advise on rates and set an initial main rate of £3.60 per hour for workers aged 22 and over, with lower rates for younger workers. Enforcement is carried out by HM Revenue and Customs. In 2016 the government introduced a higher "National Living Wage" for older workers under the framework of the Act, and rates are uprated annually.

Background

Before 1999 the United Kingdom had no general statutory minimum wage; sectoral Wages Councils, which had set minimum rates in some low-paid industries, had been abolished in 1993. The Act followed decades of campaigning by trade unions and the Labour Party.
