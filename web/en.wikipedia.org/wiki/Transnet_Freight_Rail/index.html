Transnet Freight Rail (TFR) is a South African rail transport company, formerly known as Spoornet. It is the largest division of Transnet, the state-owned freight transport and logistics company. TFR operates South Africa's national freight railway network of about 20,000 km of track (of which the core network is roughly 12,800 km), representing about 80 per cent of Africa's rail infrastructure by route length.

Operations

TFR is organized into business areas including General Freight, which moves containers, motor vehicles, agricultural products and general goods, and two heavy-haul export lines:

  • The Coal Line (formerly the Richards Bay coal line) carries export coal from the Mpumalanga coalfields to the Richards Bay Coal Terminal.
  • The Iron Ore Line (the Sishen–Saldanha line, or Orex line) carries iron ore from mines at Sishen in the Northern Cape to the port of Saldanha, using some of the longest and heaviest trains in the world, with trains up to 4 km long hauled by diesel and electric locomotives.

History

South African railways trace their origins to the 19th-century colonial lines and the South African Railways administration. Spoornet was formed as the rail arm of the transport parastatal in 1990, and was renamed Transnet Freight Rail in 2007 as part of the Transnet group's restructuring.

Challenges

In the 2010s and 2020s, TFR's performance declined sharply due to underinvestment in maintenance, theft of copper cable and other infrastructure crime, locomotive procurement disputes, and derailments. Export volumes of coal and iron ore fell, prompting government reform plans including opening the network to third-party/private operators and restructuring Transnet's rail businesses.
