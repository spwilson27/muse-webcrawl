Louis, Dauphin of France

Louis, Dauphin of France (Louis Ferdinand; 4 September 1729 – 20 December 1765) was the elder and only surviving son of King Louis XV of France and his wife, Queen Marie Leszczyńska. As a son of the king, he was a fils de France. As the heir apparent, he was styled Dauphin of France. Although he was expected to become king, he died before his father and thus never ascended the French throne. He was the father of three future kings of France: Louis XVI, Louis XVIII and Charles X.

Early life

Louis was born at the Palace of Versailles on 4 September 1729. His birth was widely celebrated, as the succession of the House of Bourbon had appeared precarious: his grandfather, Louis, Duke of Burgundy, and his great-grandfather Louis, Grand Dauphin, had both died in the early 18th century. He was baptised privately and given the name Louis Ferdinand. From birth he was styled Dauphin of France.

The young dauphin was raised under the supervision of the Duchess of Ventadour and educated by carefully chosen tutors. He was considered intelligent, devout and studious, with a taste for learning, particularly history, geography and the sciences, though his reserved temperament contrasted with the liveliness of his father.

Marriages

On 23 February 1745 Louis married Infanta Maria Teresa Rafaela of Spain, daughter of King Philip V of Spain. The marriage was intended to strengthen Franco-Spanish ties. Maria Teresa died in July 1746, days after giving birth to a daughter, Marie Thérèse, who herself died in infancy.

On 9 February 1747 Louis married Marie-Josèphe of Saxony, daughter of Frederick Augustus II, Elector of Saxony and King of Poland, and Maria Josepha of Austria. The second marriage proved a durable and affectionate one and produced numerous children.

Children

By Marie-Josèphe of Saxony, Louis had several sons who survived to adulthood: Louis-Auguste, the future Louis XVI; Louis-Stanislas, Count of Provence, the future Louis XVIII; and Charles-Philippe, Count of Artois, the future Charles X. His daughters included Marie Zéphyrine, who died young; Marie Adélaïde Clotilde, later Queen of Sardinia; and Élisabeth, who was guillotined during the French Revolution.

Personality and the devot party

Louis was deeply religious and became associated with the parti dévot, the devout faction at court which opposed the influence of Madame de Pompadour and supported the Jesuits. He took an interest in military matters and accompanied his father during the War of the Austrian Succession, though chronic ill health limited his activities. Contemporaries regarded him as virtuous and dutiful, and his early death was widely mourned.

Death

The dauphin's health declined steadily in the 1760s. He died of tuberculosis at Fontainebleau on 20 December 1765, aged 36. He was buried in the cathedral of Saint-Étienne at Sens, while his heart was placed in the Basilica of Saint-Denis near Paris. His widow Marie-Josèphe died in 1767, also of tuberculosis.
