Elisha

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Elisha (Hebrew: Elisha, "My God is salvation") was, according to the Hebrew Bible, a prophet and wonder-worker of the northern kingdom of Israel in the ninth century BCE. He was the disciple and successor of the prophet Elijah, whose mantle he received when Elijah was taken up to heaven in a whirlwind.

Biblical narrative

Introduced in 1 Kings 19 ploughing with twelve yoke of oxen, Elisha left his farm to follow Elijah. The bulk of his ministry is narrated in 2 Kings, which credits him with a long series of miracles: parting the Jordan, purifying the waters of Jericho, multiplying a widow's oil, raising the Shunammite woman's son from the dead, healing Naaman the Syrian of leprosy, making an iron axe head float, and feeding a hundred men with twenty loaves. After his death, a corpse thrown into his tomb was revived on touching his bones.

In other traditions

Elisha is venerated as a prophet in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam, where he is known as Alyasa. His traditional tomb sites are claimed at several locations, including Eğil in Turkey. In Christian tradition he is commemorated as a saint, and his name day is 14 June in the Roman martyrology.
