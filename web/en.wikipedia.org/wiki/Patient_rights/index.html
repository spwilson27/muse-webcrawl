Patient rights

Placeholder image

Patient rights are the rights afforded to individuals receiving medical care. They commonly include the right to informed consent, to refuse treatment, to confidentiality and privacy of medical records, to access one's own health information, to be treated without discrimination, and to participate in decisions about one's care.

History and frameworks

The modern articulation of patient rights developed in the second half of the twentieth century, alongside medical ethics frameworks such as the Declaration of Lisbon on the Rights of the Patient (1981) by the World Medical Association and national measures such as the American Hospital Association's Patient's Bill of Rights (1973). In health privacy law, legislation such as HIPAA in the United States gives patients enforceable rights over their health information.

Patient rights are balanced against duties of care providers and, in some situations, public health considerations. See also informed consent and medical ethics.
