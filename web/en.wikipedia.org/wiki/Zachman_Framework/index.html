Zachman Framework

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Zachman Framework is an enterprise ontology and is a fundamental structure for enterprise architecture which provides a formal and structured way of viewing and defining an enterprise. The ontology is a two-dimensional classification schema that reflects the intersection between two historical classifications: the perspectives (rows) and the interrogatives (columns).

Structure

Developed by John Zachman at IBM and first published in 1987, the framework is presented as a 6×6 matrix. The rows represent six perspectives: Planner (contextual), Owner (conceptual), Designer (logical), Builder (physical), Subcontractor (out of context/detailed), and the functioning enterprise. The columns represent six fundamental questions: What (data), How (function), Where (network), Who (people), When (time), and Why (motivation).

Each cell of the matrix holds a unique model or artifact type (for example, the owner's "what" is a business entity model). The framework is a taxonomy rather than a methodology: it prescribes no processes or methods, but defines the complete set of architectural representations of an enterprise. It strongly influenced later enterprise architecture approaches such as TOGAF and FEAF.
