The Austrian Parliament (German: Österreichisches Parlament) is the bicameral federal legislature of the Republic of Austria. It consists of the National Council (Nationalrat), with 183 directly elected members, and the Federal Council (Bundesrat), with 61 members chosen by the state legislatures (Landtage) of Austria's nine states. The National Council is by far the more powerful house; the Federal Council generally has only a suspensive veto on most legislation.

Functions

Parliament enacts federal legislation, approves the federal budget and international treaties, and exercises oversight of the federal government: the National Council can dismiss the government or an individual minister by a vote of no confidence, and the Federal President can dissolve the National Council. Together, the two houses sit as the Federal Assembly (Bundesversammlung) for special purposes, such as swearing in the Federal President or declaring war.

History

The parliament's institutional ancestors include the Imperial Council (Reichsrat) of the Austrian Empire, created after 1861, and the Provisional and Constituent National Assemblies of 1918–1920, which founded the republic and adopted the Federal Constitutional Law of 1920, largely drafted by Hans Kelsen. The current parliament building on Vienna's Ringstrasse, designed by Theophil Hansen in Greek Revival style and opened in 1883, was extensively renovated between 2017 and 2023, during which parliament met in temporary quarters in the Hofburg.
