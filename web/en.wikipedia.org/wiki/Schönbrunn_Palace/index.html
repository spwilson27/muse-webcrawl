Schönbrunn Palace

Schönbrunn Palace, Vienna

Schönbrunn Palace (German: Schloss Schönbrunn) is a former imperial summer residence in Vienna, Austria. The palace, with its extensive Baroque gardens, is one of Austria's most important cultural monuments and its most popular tourist attraction. Its name derives from a "beautiful spring" (schöner Brunnen) found on the site.

History

The site was acquired by the Habsburgs in the 16th century. Emperor Leopold I commissioned the architect Johann Bernhard Fischer von Erlach to design a grand palace there in the 1690s; construction began in 1696. The palace took its present form under Maria Theresa in the mid-18th century, with remodelling by Nikolaus Pacassi. It was the birthplace of Emperor Franz Joseph I (1830) and the setting of his long reign; he died there in 1916. In 1918, in the palace's Blue Chinese Salon, Emperor Charles I signed the declaration renouncing participation in government, effectively ending the Habsburg monarchy.

The palace and gardens

The palace contains some 1,441 rooms, of which about 45 can be visited, including the Rococo Millions Room, panelled in rosewood and inset with Persian miniatures, and the Great Gallery where state banquets were held. The gardens feature the Neptune Fountain, the Gloriette on the hill above, an orangery, a maze, and the Tiergarten Schönbrunn, founded in 1752 and the world's oldest continuously operating zoo.

World Heritage

Schönbrunn and its gardens were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1996.
