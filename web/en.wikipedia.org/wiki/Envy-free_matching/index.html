Envy-free matching

In economics and computer science, an envy-free matching is a matching in a bipartite graph in which no agent prefers the item assigned to another agent over their own item. The concept is an adaptation of the notion of envy-freeness from fair division to assignment problems where each agent receives at most one item, such as assigning students to dormitories or workers to shifts.

Definitions

Consider a set of agents and a set of items, with each agent having a preference ranking over items (and possibly over being unmatched). A matching assigns distinct items to agents. The matching is envy-free if every matched agent weakly prefers their own item to the item of any other agent. A stronger notion, envy-free up to one item, and the concept of a popular matching, relax the condition in various ways.

Existence and computation

An envy-free matching always exists: the empty matching is trivially envy-free. The interesting question is finding a maximum-cardinality envy-free matching. Polynomial-time algorithms exist for finding a maximum envy-free matching when preferences are strict, based on reductions to bipartite matching and the structure of "envy graphs". When agents may also envy unmatched agents or have ties in their preferences, finding large envy-free matchings becomes computationally harder.

Applications

Envy-free matchings are used in course allocation, school choice, refugee resettlement, and the assignment of tasks in cloud computing, where fairness between participants is a primary concern.
