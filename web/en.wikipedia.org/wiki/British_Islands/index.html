British Islands

The British Islands is a legal and political term in the law of the United Kingdom which collectively denotes the following four polities:

  • the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland;
  • the Bailiwick of Jersey;
  • the Bailiwick of Guernsey (including Alderney, Sark and Herm); and
  • the Isle of Man.

The territories comprising the British Islands.

Legal definition

The term was defined by the Interpretation Act 1949 and retained in the Interpretation Act 1978: "'British Islands' means the United Kingdom, the Channel Islands and the Isle of Man." The Channel Islands and the Isle of Man are Crown Dependencies — self-governing possessions of the Crown that are not part of the United Kingdom, though the UK is responsible for their defence and international representation.

Distinction from "British Isles"

The legal term is not to be confused with the geographical expression "British Isles", which denotes the archipelago including the island of Ireland. "British Islands" deliberately excludes the Republic of Ireland, and its use avoids the offence the geographical term causes in Ireland. Passports issued in the dependencies are inscribed "British Islands" in place of "United Kingdom" where appropriate, e.g. "British Islands — Bailiwick of Jersey".
