Universal Classic Monsters

The Universal Classic Monsters (also known as the Universal Monsters) are a series of horror, suspense, and science fiction films made by Universal Pictures between the 1930s and the 1950s, whose iconic characters — Dracula, Frankenstein's monster, the Mummy, the Invisible Man, the Wolf Man, and the Creature from the Black Lagoon — defined the horror genre.

Silent era and early sound

Universal's horror tradition began with Lon Chaney in The Hunchback of Notre Dame (1923) and The Phantom of the Opera (1925). The sound era opened with Dracula (1931), starring Bela Lugosi, and Frankenstein (1931), in which Boris Karloff played the Monster under James Whale's direction, with Jack Pierce's celebrated makeup designs.

Golden age

Sequels and new series followed: The Mummy (1932), The Invisible Man (1933), Bride of Frankenstein (1935), The Wolf Man (1941) with Lon Chaney Jr., and "monster rally" ensemble films such as House of Frankenstein (1944). The cycle closed with The Creature Walks Among Us (1956).

Legacy

The films' imagery — Karloff's flat-headed Monster, Lugosi's caped Count — became the default visual language of Halloween and horror. Universal repeatedly attempted revivals, including the short-lived "Dark Universe" franchise launched with The Mummy (2017), and standalone successes such as The Invisible Man (2020).
