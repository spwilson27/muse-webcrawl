Anarchism in Japan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Kōtoku Shūsui, early Japanese anarchist

Anarchism in Japan emerged in the early 20th century from the socialist movement, influenced by European figures such as Peter Kropotkin and by the translation of anarchist texts. Its pioneering figure was the journalist and translator Kōtoku Shūsui, who moved from socialism to anarcho-syndicalism and direct action.

History

The movement suffered a devastating blow in the High Treason Incident of 1910–11, when Kōtoku and eleven others were executed on charges of plotting to assassinate the Emperor Meiji. In the 1920s the movement revived through anarcho-syndicalist unions and federations such as Kokuren and Zenkoku Jiren, and through thinkers like Ōsugi Sakae, who was murdered by military police in the Amakasu Incident following the 1923 Great Kantō earthquake. State repression in the 1930s effectively destroyed the pre-war movement.

Post-war

Anarchism reappeared after 1945 in the Japanese Anarchist Federation (founded 1946) and in the student movements of the 1960s, though it never regained its earlier influence. Small anarchist groups, publishers and social centres remain active in Japan today.
