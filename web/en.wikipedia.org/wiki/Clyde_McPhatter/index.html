Clyde Lensley McPhatter (November 15, 1932 – June 13, 1972) was an American rhythm and blues, soul, and rock and roll singer. He was one of the most widely imitated R&B singers of the 1950s and early 1960s, and a key figure in the shaping of doo-wop and R&B.

Career

McPhatter first gained fame as the high-tenor lead singer of Billy Ward and his Dominoes, fronting hits such as "Have Mercy Baby" (1952). In 1953 he formed The Drifters for Atlantic Records, recording classics including "Money Honey" and the original version of "White Christmas." After military service, his solo career produced hits like "Treasure of Love" (1956), "Just to Hold My Hand", and "A Lover's Question" (1958).

Later years and death

His popularity faded in the 1960s amid changing tastes and personal struggles with alcohol. He died in 1972 of complications from heart, liver, and kidney disease, aged just 39.

Legacy

McPhatter was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1987, and into the Grammy Hall of Fame for "White Christmas" with the Drifters. His soaring tenor influenced generations of soul singers.
