The Hound of the Baskervilles

SeriesSherlock Holmes

The Hound of the Baskervilles is the third of the four crime novels by British writer Arthur Conan Doyle featuring the detective Sherlock Holmes. Serialised in The Strand Magazine from August 1901 to April 1902, it is set largely on Dartmoor in Devon and features a supposedly supernatural hellhound that haunts the Baskerville family.

Plot

Dr James Mortimer consults Holmes after the death of Sir Charles Baskerville, found dead on his Dartmoor estate with the footprints of a gigantic hound nearby. Fearing for the new heir, Sir Henry Baskerville, Holmes sends Dr Watson to Baskerville Hall to investigate while he works in secret. The trail leads through escaped convicts, an entomologist neighbour, a mysterious woman, and a legendary family curse, before Holmes unmasks the would-be murderer — Jack Stapleton, a secret Baskerville relative who kept a starved, phosphorescent hound to terrify his victims to death.

Background

The novel marked Holmes's return after Conan Doyle had apparently killed him off in "The Final Problem" (1893); immense public demand prompted the author to set this adventure before Holmes's death. The story draws on West Country legends of spectral hounds such as the Wisht Hounds and on the tale of Squire Richard Cabell of Buckfastleigh.

Legacy

Widely considered the finest of the Holmes novels, it has been adapted for film and television dozens of times, including the 1939 Basil Rathbone version and the 1959 Hammer production.
