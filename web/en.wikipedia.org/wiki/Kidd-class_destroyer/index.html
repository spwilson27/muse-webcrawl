The Kidd-class destroyers were a series of four guided missile destroyers based on the Spruance class. Originally ordered by the Imperial Iranian Navy and sometimes called the "Ayatollah class", they were taken over by the United States Navy after the Iranian Revolution of 1979 cancelled the order. They combined the anti-submarine Spruance hull and machinery with the anti-air warfare weapon systems of the Virginia-class cruisers, and were considered among the most powerful general-purpose destroyers of their era.

Design and service

The class carried two Mk 26 twin-arm missile launchers for Standard SM-2MR surface-to-air missiles, eight Harpoon anti-ship missiles, ASROC and torpedo tubes for anti-submarine warfare, and two 5-inch guns. Extra air conditioning and dust separation equipment, fitted for Iranian service in the Persian Gulf, also proved useful to the US Navy.

Decommissioned by the United States in the late 1990s as the Arleigh Burke class entered service, all four ships were sold to Taiwan. After extensive refit they were commissioned into the Republic of China Navy in 2005–2006 as the Kee Lung class, where they remain the largest destroyers in Taiwanese service and provide area air defence for the fleet.
