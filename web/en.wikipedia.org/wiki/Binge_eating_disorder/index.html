Binge eating disorder (BED) is an eating disorder characterized by frequent and persistent episodes of binge eating accompanied by marked distress and feelings of loss of control. Unlike bulimia nervosa, episodes are not followed by compensatory behaviors such as self-induced vomiting, fasting, or excessive exercise. BED is the most common eating disorder in many countries and is associated with obesity, metabolic problems, depression, and anxiety.

Diagnosis

BED was recognized as a distinct diagnosis in the DSM-5 (2013), having previously been listed in DSM-IV as a condition for further study. Diagnostic criteria require recurrent binge episodes — eating an objectively large amount of food in a discrete period with a sense of lack of control — occurring at least once a week for three months, accompanied by features such as eating rapidly, eating until uncomfortably full, eating alone due to embarrassment, and feeling disgusted or guilty afterward. ICD-11 includes a similar diagnosis.

Causes

The causes are multifactorial: genetic vulnerability, dieting and weight stigma, adverse life events, and alterations in reward and impulse-control circuitry have all been implicated. BED frequently co-occurs with major depressive disorder, anxiety disorders, and substance use disorders.

Treatment

Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), including guided self-help formats, is the best-established treatment and reduces binge frequency in a substantial proportion of patients. Interpersonal therapy and dialectical behavior therapy are also used. The stimulant prodrug lisdexamfetamine is approved in the United States for moderate-to-severe BED in adults — the only medication with that indication — while antidepressants and topiramate have shown some efficacy off-label.
