Tanztheater

Tanztheater (German: "dance theatre") is a form of dance that emerged from German expressionist dance (Ausdruckstanz) and blends movement, spoken text, music, and theatrical staging. It rejects the purely virtuosic display of classical ballet in favour of expressive, often unsettling explorations of human relationships.

History

Its roots lie with Rudolf Laban and Mary Wigman in the early twentieth century, and with Kurt Jooss, whose anti-war ballet The Green Table (1932) is a landmark. After the Second World War, the form was transformed by Pina Bausch, whose Tanztheater Wuppertal, founded in 1973, achieved worldwide fame with works such as Café Müller and Kontakthof, built from dancers' own memories and improvisations.

Characteristics

Tanztheater pieces often use repetition, everyday gesture, elaborate stage sets (water, soil, carnations), and spoken fragments in several languages. The movement vocabulary mixes ballet, modern dance, and pedestrian action. The form has influenced contemporary dance and physical theatre worldwide, with notable companies including Sasha Waltz's in Berlin.
