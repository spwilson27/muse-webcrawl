Interior Salish

The Interior Salish are a group of Indigenous peoples of the Pacific Northwest Interior Plateau, speaking languages of the Interior branch of the Salishan language family. Their traditional territories lie in what is now interior British Columbia in Canada and Washington, Montana, and Idaho in the United States.

Peoples

The Interior Salish include the Secwepemc (Shuswap), St'át'imc (Lillooet), Nlaka'pamux (Thompson), Syilx (Okanagan), Sinixt (Lakes), Kalispel, Spokane, Coeur d'Alene, and the Confederated Tribes of the Colville Reservation, among others. They are distinguished from the Coast Salish peoples of the Pacific coast.

Culture

Traditionally, Interior Salish peoples lived in semi-subterranean pit houses (kekuli) in winter and mat lodges in summer, subsisting on salmon, game, roots such as camas, and berries. Salmon fishing at falls and rapids on the Columbia and Fraser river systems was central to the economy and ceremonial life. Trade networks connected the Plateau with the coast and the Plains.

Languages

Interior Salishan languages include Nlaka'pamux (Thompson), Secwepemctsín, St'át'imcets, nsyilxcən (Okanagan), and the southern languages Spokane–Kalispel–Flathead, Coeur d'Alene, and Columbia–Moses. All are endangered, with active revitalization programs.
