Deneb (α Cygni) is the brightest star in the constellation of Cygnus and one of the brightest stars in the night sky. It forms one vertex of the Summer Triangle asterism, together with Vega and Altair, and marks the tail of the swan — its name derives from the Arabic dhanab, "tail". Deneb is a blue-white supergiant and one of the most luminous stars known; despite being roughly 2,600 light-years away (the exact distance remains uncertain), it shines at first magnitude.

Properties

Deneb is classified as a spectral type A2 Ia supergiant with a surface temperature of about 8,500 K. Its luminosity is estimated at around 200,000 times that of the Sun, and its radius at roughly 200 solar radii — if placed at the centre of the Solar System it would extend beyond Earth's orbit. It is losing mass through a strong stellar wind and is slightly variable, the prototype of the Alpha Cygni variables. Deneb is thought to be evolving toward a red supergiant phase and will eventually end its life in a supernova, probably within the next few million years. Because of the precession of Earth's axis, Deneb was the pole star around 9800 BC and will be again around AD 9800.

Observational history and culture

Known since antiquity, Deneb appears in Chinese astronomy as the fourth star of the "Magpie Bridge" and figures in the myth of the Weaver Girl and the Cowherd. Its brightness and position make it an important navigational and seasonal marker in northern latitudes, where it is circumpolar from mid-northern locations.
