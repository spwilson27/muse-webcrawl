Saw

A hand saw for cutting wood.

A saw is a tool consisting of a tough blade, wire, or chain with a toothed edge, used to cut through material, most commonly wood, but also metal, stone and plastic. The cut is made by placing the toothed edge against the material and moving it back and forth, or continuously in the case of powered saws, so that each tooth shears away a small particle and the cumulative action forms a groove called the kerf.

History

Saws are among the oldest tools: serrated flint implements date back tens of thousands of years, and bronze and later iron saws were used in ancient Egypt, Greece and Rome. The Romans improved tooth design and invented the frame saw. Saws were essential to shipbuilding, carpentry and logging, and the Industrial Revolution brought steam-, water- and later electric-powered sawmills and circular saws.

Types

Hand saws include the crosscut saw and rip saw for lumber, the hacksaw for metal, the coping saw and jigsaw for curves, the backsaw for precise joinery, and the Japanese pull saw, which cuts on the pull stroke. Powered saws include the circular saw, table saw, bandsaw, reciprocating saw and chainsaw. Saw teeth are set (bent alternately to either side) so that the kerf is wider than the blade, preventing binding.
