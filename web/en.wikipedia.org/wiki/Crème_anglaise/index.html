Crème anglaise

Crème anglaise (French for "English cream"), also known as custard sauce, is a light pouring custard used as a dessert sauce or base in classical French cuisine. It is made by gently whisking egg yolks and sugar into hot milk or cream, usually flavoured with vanilla, and cooking the mixture until it thickens enough to coat the back of a spoon.

Preparation

The yolks and sugar are whisked until pale, hot milk is tempered into them, and the mixture is returned to gentle heat, stirring constantly, until it reaches the nappe stage — thick enough to coat a spoon and hold a line drawn through it. It must not boil, or the eggs will scramble. The sauce is then strained and cooled.

Uses

Crème anglaise is traditionally served with chocolate desserts, fruit, poached pears, and the meringue dessert île flottante (floating island). Churned, the same base becomes vanilla ice cream; set with gelatin, it becomes bavarois. In England a similar pouring custard is simply called "custard" and is a standard accompaniment to pies and puddings.
