James Douglas Morrison (December 8, 1943 – July 3, 1971) was an American singer, songwriter and poet who was the lead vocalist and primary lyricist of the rock band the Doors. Known for his baritone voice, poetic lyrics, distinctive stage persona and unpredictable performances, Morrison is regarded as one of the most charismatic frontmen in rock history. He was also known by the self-bestowed nickname "the Lizard King".

Early life

Morrison was born in Melbourne, Florida, the son of a US Navy officer who later became an admiral. A military childhood involved frequent moves. He studied film at UCLA, where he met keyboardist Ray Manzarek. In 1965 the two formed the Doors with guitarist Robby Krieger and drummer John Densmore, taking the band's name from Aldous Huxley's The Doors of Perception.

Career with the Doors

The Doors' self-titled debut album (1967) included the number-one single "Light My Fire" and the dramatic "The End". Five more studio albums followed, including Strange Days, Waiting for the Sun, Morrison Hotel and L.A. Woman. Morrison's lyrics drew on poetry, mythology and philosophy. His confrontational stage behavior led to legal trouble, including an arrest after a 1969 concert in Miami, where he was charged with lewd and lascivious behavior; he was convicted in 1970 and posthumously pardoned in 2010.

Death and legacy

In 1971 Morrison moved to Paris with his longtime companion Pamela Courson. He was found dead in a bathtub on July 3, 1971, aged 27; no autopsy was performed, and heart failure was listed as the cause. He is buried in Père Lachaise Cemetery, one of the most visited graves in Paris. Morrison published two volumes of poetry in his lifetime, and his image remains a staple of rock iconography. He is frequently grouped with the so-called "27 Club" of musicians who died at that age.
