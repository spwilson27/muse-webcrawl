Local council (Israel)

A local council (Hebrew: מועצה מקומית, mo'atza mekomit) is one of the three types of local government in Israel, alongside cities and regional councils. Local councils govern towns that are larger than villages but not large or urbanised enough to be declared cities; the status is granted by the Ministry of the Interior, generally to localities with more than about 2,000 residents.

Governance

A local council is headed by a mayor and a council, both elected by residents every five years in municipal elections held simultaneously across the country. The number of council members depends on the population of the locality. Councils are responsible for municipal services including local planning, roads, water, education, welfare, religious services and cultural activities within their jurisdiction.

Scale

There are over one hundred local councils in Israel, ranging from small towns such as Kfar Vradim to large localities of tens of thousands of residents — some of which exceed small cities in population but retain council status. Local councils may be elevated to city status by ministerial decision, as happened with a number of growing towns.
