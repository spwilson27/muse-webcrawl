Gyromagnetic ratio

Gyromagnetic ratio
Common symbolsγ (gamma)
SI unitrad·s−1·T−1 (or Hz/T)
Dimension[M−1 T I] (charge per unit mass)

In physics, the gyromagnetic ratio (also sometimes known as the magnetogyric ratio in other disciplines) of a particle or system is the ratio of its magnetic moment to its angular momentum, and it is often denoted by the symbol γ (gamma). Its SI unit is the radian per second per tesla (rad·s−1·T−1) or, equivalently, coulomb per kilogram (C/kg).

γ = μ / L

where μ is the magnetic moment and L is the angular momentum. The gyromagnetic ratio determines the frequency at which a magnetic moment precesses in an external magnetic field — the Larmor frequency, ω = γB — and is therefore fundamental to nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR) and magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), as well as to the physics of electron paramagnetic resonance.

Classical and quantum values

For a classical charged body in which the charge and mass are distributed identically, the gyromagnetic ratio is simply q/2m, half the charge-to-mass ratio. For the orbital motion of an electron this classical result is essentially correct. For the electron's intrinsic spin, however, the ratio is approximately twice the classical value: the electron's gyromagnetic ratio is about −1.760859630 × 1011 rad·s−1·T−1, the factor of roughly two being described by the g-factor, g ≈ 2.00231930436. The tiny deviation of the electron g-factor from exactly 2 — the anomalous magnetic moment — is predicted by quantum electrodynamics to extraordinary precision and is one of the most precisely tested quantities in all of physics.

Nuclear gyromagnetic ratios

Atomic nuclei also possess gyromagnetic ratios, scaled by the nuclear magneton rather than the Bohr magneton and therefore roughly three orders of magnitude smaller than the electron's. Each isotope has its own characteristic value, which fixes the frequency of its NMR signal in a given field.

The free proton's gyromagnetic ratio, γp/2π = 42.577478 MHz/T, is a measured fundamental constant and is used in precision determinations of magnetic fields: NMR magnetometers measure the field by finding the proton precession frequency.

Larmor precession

When a magnetic moment with gyromagnetic ratio γ is placed in a magnetic field B, it experiences a torque that causes it to precess about the field direction at the Larmor angular frequency ω = −γB. This precession is the physical basis of magnetic resonance: a radio-frequency field applied at the Larmor frequency can tip the magnetisation and produce a measurable signal. In MRI, the dependence of the Larmor frequency on field strength is also what allows spatial encoding, because gradient fields make the precession frequency vary with position.

Sign conventions

The sign of γ depends on the sign of the charge and the direction of the angular momentum relative to the magnetic moment. For the electron γ is negative because its magnetic moment is opposite to its spin angular momentum; for most nuclei of interest γ is positive, although some nuclei such as 15N and 29Si have negative gyromagnetic ratios, which reverses the sense of precession and has practical consequences in NMR pulse sequences.
