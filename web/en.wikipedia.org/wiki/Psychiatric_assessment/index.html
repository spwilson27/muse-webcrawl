Psychiatric assessment


A psychiatric consultation

A psychiatric assessment, or psychological screening, is the process of gathering information about a person within a psychiatric service, with the purpose of making a diagnosis. The assessment is usually the first stage of a treatment process, but psychiatric assessments may also be used for various legal purposes. The assessment includes social and biographical information, direct observations, and data from specific psychological tests. It is typically carried out by a psychiatrist, but it can be a multidisciplinary process involving nurses, psychologists, occupational therapists and social workers.

Purpose

The purposes of a psychiatric assessment include establishing a diagnosis, formulating a case (an explanatory account of the origins and maintenance of the person's difficulties), assessing risk of harm to self or others, and planning treatment. Assessments may be requested in emergency, outpatient or inpatient settings, or for legal purposes such as assessing fitness to stand trial.

Components

History

The history of the presenting complaint covers the nature, onset and development of the person's symptoms, previous episodes, past psychiatric and medical history, medication, substance use, developmental and personal history, family history, and current social circumstances.

Mental state examination

The mental state examination (MSE) is a structured record of the person's appearance and behaviour, speech, mood and affect, thought form and content, perceptions (including hallucinations), cognition, insight and judgement at the time of interview.

Physical examination and investigations

Because physical illness can present with psychiatric symptoms, an assessment may include a physical examination and investigations such as blood tests or brain imaging where indicated.

Risk assessment

Assessing risk of suicide, self-harm, violence and vulnerability forms a standard part of psychiatric assessment and informs decisions about the level of care and any need for detention under mental health legislation.
