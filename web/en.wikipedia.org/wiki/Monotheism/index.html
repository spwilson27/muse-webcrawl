Monotheism

Monotheism is the belief that there is only one God, or in the oneness of God. It is distinguished from polytheism (many gods), henotheism (devotion to one god without denying others), and monolatry. The word derives from Greek monos (single) and theos (god), and was coined in the 17th century.

History

An early arguably monotheistic system was Atenism in 14th-century BCE Egypt under Akhenaten, centered on the sun disc Aten, though its classification is debated. Zoroastrianism in ancient Persia centered on Ahura Mazda. Israelite religion developed into the strict monotheism of Second Temple Judaism, expressed in the Shema: "Hear, O Israel: the LORD our God, the LORD is one."

Abrahamic religions

Christianity affirms one God in the doctrine of the Trinity; Islam affirms absolute oneness (tawhid), with the shahada declaring no god but God; Judaism, Christianity and Islam are collectively called Abrahamic religions, tracing descent from Abraham. Sikhism and the Baháʼí Faith are also monotheistic.

Philosophy

Philosophical monotheism appears in Greek thought (Xenophanes, Plato, Aristotle's unmoved mover), in deism, and in concepts such as the Absolute in some Hindu traditions (e.g., Advaita Vedanta's Brahman), which is sometimes classified as monism rather than monotheism.
