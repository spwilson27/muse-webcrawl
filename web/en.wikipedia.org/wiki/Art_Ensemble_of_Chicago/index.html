The Art Ensemble of Chicago is an avant-garde jazz group that grew out of the Association for the Advancement of Creative Musicians (AACM) in the late 1960s. The ensemble is known for integrating music from throughout the history of jazz and African musical traditions, performing with an enormous array of instruments — including whistles, bicycle horns, party favours and other "little instruments" — and appearing in African clothing and face paint. Their motto is "Great Black Music, Ancient to the Future."

History

The group began as the Roscoe Mitchell Sextet in 1966, which recorded the landmark album Sound for Delmark Records. Saxophonist Joseph Jarman and bassist Malachi Favors joined, and trumpeter Lester Bowie became a member in 1968. In 1969 the group travelled to Paris, where they were billed as the Art Ensemble of Chicago and recorded a series of albums for the BYG Actuel label, including A Jackson in Your House and Message to Our Folks. Drummer Famoudou Don Moye joined in 1970, completing the classic quintet line-up.

Returning to the United States in 1971, the group settled in Chicago and recorded for Atlantic Records (Bap-Tizum, Fanfare for the Warriors) and later ECM (Nice Guys, Full Force, Urban Bushmen). Their concerts combined free improvisation, composed pieces, poetry, theatre and mime, and were famously unpredictable in length and content.

Later years

The ensemble continued to record and tour through the 1980s and 1990s. Lester Bowie died in 1999, and Malachi Favors died in 2004; the group carried on with new members including trumpeter Corey Wilkes and bassist Jaribu Shahid, while Jarman retired in 1993 and briefly returned before his death in 2019. In 2019 the group released the expansive album We Are on the Edge: A 50th Anniversary Celebration on Pi Recordings, featuring a large ensemble with strings and guest vocalists, marking five decades since their Paris debut.

Musical style

The Art Ensemble's performances typically move between tightly arranged themes and open improvisation, often beginning and ending with the ringing of bells and percussion. Each member is a multi-instrumentalist, and the group's music deliberately spans the whole of African-American musical history, from New Orleans jazz and bebop to free jazz, funk and silence itself, which the members treat as a compositional element.
