Ray Kurzweil

Raymond Kurzweil (born February 12, 1948) is an American computer scientist, author, inventor, and futurist. He is involved in fields such as optical character recognition (OCR), text-to-speech synthesis, speech recognition technology, and electronic keyboard instruments. He has written books on health, artificial intelligence (AI), transhumanism, the technological singularity, and futurism.

Inventions and companies

Kurzweil founded Kurzweil Computer Products in 1974, developing the first omni-font optical character recognition system and the Kurzweil Reading Machine, which read printed text aloud for blind people. He later founded Kurzweil Music Systems, whose K250 synthesizer was developed in consultation with Stevie Wonder, and companies in speech recognition and medical education software. He joined Google in 2012 as a director of engineering working on machine intelligence and natural language understanding.

Futurism

In books including The Age of Intelligent Machines (1990), The Age of Spiritual Machines (1999), The Singularity Is Near (2005), and The Singularity Is Nearer (2024), Kurzweil argues that technological progress follows an exponential "law of accelerating returns". He predicts that computing will enable a technological singularity around 2045, preceded by human-level AI in the late 2020s, radical life extension through biotechnology and nanotechnology, and eventually the merging of human and machine intelligence. His predictions have been both influential and controversial; critics question his timelines and his interpretation of exponential trends.

Honours

Kurzweil received the National Medal of Technology and Innovation in 1999 and was inducted into the National Inventors Hall of Fame in 2002. He has received numerous honorary doctorates.
