Byurakan Observatory

The Byurakan Astrophysical Observatory is an astronomical observatory owned and operated by the Armenian Academy of Sciences. It is located on the slope of Mount Aragats near the village of Byurakan in Armenia, at an altitude of about 1,400 metres, and was founded in 1946 by the astrophysicist Viktor Ambartsumian, who served as its first director.

Instruments and surveys

The observatory's main instrument is a 2.6-metre Cassegrain reflector, commissioned in 1976, alongside a 1-metre Schmidt camera. Byurakan is best known for the First Byurakan Survey (FBS, the Markarian survey), conducted with the Schmidt telescope from 1965, which catalogued galaxies with ultraviolet excess — the Markarian galaxies — and the Second Byurakan Survey, which found many quasars and active galactic nuclei.

Legacy

Under Ambartsumian the observatory became a leading centre for the study of stellar associations and unstable phenomena in galaxies. It hosted major international conferences, including the 1966 meeting associated with the early SETI discussions and IAU symposia on active galaxies. The observatory remains Armenia's principal astronomical research institute, and its digitised survey plates are used worldwide.
