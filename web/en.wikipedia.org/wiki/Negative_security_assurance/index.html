Negative security assurance

Negative security assurances (NSA) are pledges by nuclear-weapon states not to use or threaten to use nuclear weapons against non-nuclear-weapon states. They are the counterpart of positive security assurances, in which nuclear powers promise to come to the aid of a non-nuclear state if it is attacked with nuclear weapons.

Background

The concept emerged during negotiations over the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons (NPT). Non-nuclear-weapon states sought legally binding commitments that they would not be targeted by nuclear weapons in return for renouncing them. In 1995, in connection with the indefinite extension of the NPT, the five recognized nuclear-weapon states issued declarations on security assurances, noted by the United Nations Security Council in Resolution 984.

Status

These assurances have generally been given as unilateral political declarations rather than as a legally binding treaty, and most contain qualifications — for example, exceptions in the case of an attack carried out in alliance with a nuclear-weapon state. China has maintained an unconditional no-first-use policy and gives unqualified negative security assurances. Non-nuclear states and the Non-Aligned Movement have repeatedly called for a legally binding international convention on the subject.
