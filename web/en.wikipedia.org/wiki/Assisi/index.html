Assisi (Italian: Assisi, from Latin Asisium) is a town and comune of Italy in the province of Perugia in the Umbria region, on the western flank of Monte Subasio. It is generally regarded as the birthplace of St. Francis, who founded the Franciscan religious order in the town in 1208, and of St. Clare (Chiara d'Offreducci), co-founder of the Order of Poor Ladies. Assisi is one of the world's major centres of Christian pilgrimage.

History

The settlement was an Umbrian and then Roman town (Asisium); the Temple of Minerva in its main piazza survives with its Corinthian portico intact. In the Middle Ages Assisi became a free commune, frequently at war with Perugia — the young Francis was captured in one such war in 1202. After his conversion Francis gathered followers at the little chapel of the Porziuncola, and on his death in 1226 construction began of the great Basilica of Saint Francis, whose Upper Church is decorated with fresco cycles attributed to Giotto and his school depicting the life of the saint.

Main sights

Besides the Basilica of St. Francis, landmarks include the Basilica of Santa Chiara (St. Clare), the cathedral of San Rufino, the hermitage of the Eremo delle Carceri on Monte Subasio, the Rocca Maggiore fortress, and the sanctuary of San Damiano. On 26 October 1986 Pope John Paul II convened the first World Day of Prayer for Peace in Assisi, bringing together leaders of many religions, an event repeated by his successors. The town was damaged by an earthquake on 26 September 1997, which caused partial collapses in the Upper Basilica, killing four people; the basilica reopened in 1999 after restoration. UNESCO inscribed "Assisi, the Basilica of San Francesco and Other Franciscan Sites" on the World Heritage List in 2000.
