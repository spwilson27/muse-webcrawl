Differentiated instruction

Differentiated instruction is a framework for effective teaching that involves providing all students within a classroom with different avenues to acquiring content, processing ideas, and demonstrating what they have learned, so that students of differing abilities, interests, and learning needs can be taught effectively.

Principles

The approach, popularised by educational researcher Carol Ann Tomlinson, holds that teachers should proactively plan varied approaches according to students' readiness levels, interests, and learning profiles. Differentiation may apply to content (what students learn), process (how they learn it), product (how they show learning), and the learning environment.

Strategies

Common strategies include tiered assignments, flexible grouping, choice boards, compacting the curriculum for advanced learners, and ongoing formative assessment to guide adjustments. Critics note the practical difficulty of differentiating for large classes and the variable evidence base for some claimed effects.
