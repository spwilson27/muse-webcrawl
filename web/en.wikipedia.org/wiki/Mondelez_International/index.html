Mondelez International, Inc., styled as Mondelēz International, is an American multinational confectionery, food, and beverage company based in Chicago. It was formed on October 1, 2012, when Kraft Foods split its global snack business from its North American grocery business (which retained the Kraft name, later Kraft Heinz). The name combines the Latin-derived words monde ("world") and delez (a play on "delicious").

History

Mondelēz inherited Kraft's international snack portfolio, including Cadbury (acquired controversially by Kraft in 2010), Nabisco, LU biscuits, Milka and Toblerone chocolate, Trident gum and Tang. Under CEO Irene Rosenfeld and, from 2017, Dirk Van de Put, the company focused on snacking categories — biscuits, chocolate, gum and candy — divested its coffee business into the JDE Peet's joint venture, and expanded in emerging markets and premium snack acquisitions such as Clif Bar (2022).

Products

  • Biscuits: Oreo (the world's best-selling cookie), Belvita, Ritz, Chips Ahoy!, LU.
  • Chocolate: Cadbury Dairy Milk, Milka, Toblerone, Lacta.
  • Gum and candy: Trident, Halls, Sour Patch Kids, Swedish Fish.
  • Other: Philadelphia-adjacent bakery and snack lines in some regions.

Operations and criticism

Mondelēz sells in about 150 countries and derives most revenue outside North America. It has faced criticism over plant closures and offshoring (including the Cadbury takeover's aftermath in the UK and Nabisco's Chicago bakery dispute), palm-oil sourcing and deforestation, sugar content and marketing to children, and its decision to continue limited operations in Russia after 2022, which led some Nordic customers to boycott its brands in 2023. The company runs the "Cocoa Life" sustainability programme for its cocoa supply chain.
