British Antarctic Territory
Status: British Overseas Territory
Administered from: London
Research stations: Rothera, Halley VI

The British Antarctic Territory (BAT) is a sector of Antarctica claimed by the United Kingdom as one of its 14 British Overseas Territories. It comprises the region south of 60°S latitude and between longitudes 20°W and 80°W, a wedge-shaped claim extending to the South Pole that includes the Antarctic Peninsula and the Weddell Sea coast. The claim overlaps those of Argentina and Chile and is not widely recognised internationally; like all Antarctic claims it is held in abeyance under the Antarctic Treaty of 1959.

History

The territory's origins lie in letters patent of 1908 and 1917 asserting British sovereignty over South Atlantic dependencies. The Falkland Islands Dependencies Survey, later renamed the British Antarctic Survey (BAS), was established in 1943–44 partly to strengthen the claim. The BAT was formally constituted as a separate territory on 3 March 1962.

Administration and research

The territory has no permanent population; it is administered by a Commissioner based in the Foreign and Commonwealth Office in London. The British Antarctic Survey operates the Rothera Research Station on Adelaide Island and the Halley VI station on the Brunt Ice Shelf, supporting scientific research in geology, glaciology, biology, and atmospheric science. Revenue is raised from postage stamps and income tax on personnel wintering in the territory.

Wildlife

The territory's coastal areas support penguin colonies, seals, and seabirds, and its waters are subject to conservation measures under the Antarctic Treaty System.
