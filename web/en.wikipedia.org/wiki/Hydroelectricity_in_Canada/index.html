Hydroelectricity in Canada

Canada is one of the world's largest producers of hydroelectricity, which supplies roughly 60% of the country's electricity generation. With more than 600 hydropower stations and installed hydro capacity on the order of 100 GW, Canada typically ranks second or third globally in hydroelectric output. Almost all production comes from provincial Crown corporations such as Hydro-Québec, BC Hydro, Manitoba Hydro, and Ontario Power Generation.

Major developments

Quebec's James Bay Project, begun in the 1970s under Premier Robert Bourassa, created a complex of huge generating stations — the largest, Robert-Bourassa, has 5,616 MW of capacity — behind some of the largest dams in the world. Other giant stations include Churchill Falls in Labrador (5,428 MW), the Mica and Revelstoke dams on the Columbia River in British Columbia, and the W.A.C. Bennett Dam on the Peace River. Newer megaprojects include Site C in British Columbia and Muskrat Falls in Labrador, both marked by major cost overruns.

Issues

Hydro development in Canada has repeatedly raised questions about the rights of Indigenous peoples, whose lands were flooded; the 1975 James Bay and Northern Quebec Agreement with the Cree and Inuit became a landmark modern treaty. Environmental concerns include methylmercury contamination of reservoir fisheries and alterations to river ecosystems. Canada is a major exporter of electricity to the United States.
