Panda Express is an American fast-food restaurant chain that specializes in American Chinese cuisine. It is the largest Asian-segment restaurant chain in the United States, with more than 2,300 locations — chiefly in food courts, airports, universities, and increasingly freestanding drive-thru sites — and additional outlets in several other countries.

History

The chain descends from the Panda Inn, a full-service restaurant opened in Pasadena, California, in 1973 by Ming-Tsai Cherng. In 1983 his son Andrew Cherng and daughter-in-law Peggy Cherng opened the first Panda Express as a fast-food adaptation in the Glendale Galleria mall. The Cherngs, both engineers by training, built the business around operational systems and a steam-table format adapted to mall food courts; Peggy Cherng's systems work is often credited for the chain's early scalability. The parent company, Panda Restaurant Group, remains privately held and family owned.

Menu

The chain's signature dish is Orange Chicken — battered, fried chicken in a sweet orange-chilli sauce, developed in 1987 — of which it sells tens of millions of pounds annually. Other staples include Beijing Beef, Kung Pao Chicken (see also the Sichuan original), Broccoli Beef, chow mein, and fried rice. Entrées are generally prepared in woks in small batches in view of customers.

Operations and culture

Panda Express is notable for remaining company-owned rather than franchising most locations, and for comparatively high hourly wages in the fast-food sector. The Cherngs' philanthropy is channelled through the Panda Cares foundation, which supports health and education programmes for underserved youth.
