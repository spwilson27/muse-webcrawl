Cornplanter

Cornplanter (Gaiänt'wakê; John Abeel III)
Bornc. 1732–1740, Canawaugus (Avon, New York)
DiedFebruary 18, 1836, Cornplanter Tract, Pennsylvania
NationalitySeneca
Known forWar chief and diplomat; negotiating with the United States after the Revolutionary War

Cornplanter, known in the Seneca language as Gaiänt'wakê ("By What One Plants") and sometimes as John Abeel III, (c. 1740 – February 18, 1836) was a Seneca war chief and diplomat of the Wolf clan. As a war chief, Cornplanter fought in the French and Indian War and the American Revolutionary War on the side of the British. After the war, Cornplanter led negotiations with the United States and was a signatory of the Treaty of Fort Stanwix (1784) and an important figure in the Treaty of Canandaigua (1794).

Early life

Cornplanter was born at Canawaugus, a Seneca village on the Genesee River in present-day New York, the son of a Seneca woman and John Abeel II, a Dutch fur trader descended from a prominent Albany family. Raised among the Seneca, he rose to prominence as a war leader during the French and Indian War.

Revolutionary War

During the American Revolution, Cornplanter reluctantly supported the Seneca's alliance with the British and Loyalists, taking part in campaigns including the Battle of Wyoming and raids in New York and Pennsylvania. After the devastating Sullivan Expedition of 1779 destroyed Seneca villages, he helped lead his people through the post-war land cessions.

Later life and diplomacy

Cornplanter became the Seneca's principal negotiator with the new United States, meeting President George Washington in Philadelphia in 1790 to protest land fraud. Pennsylvania granted him a tract of land on the Allegheny River (the Cornplanter Tract), where he lived until his death in 1836, reputedly around a century old. His descendants were displaced in the 1960s when the Kinzua Dam flooded the tract; his grave was relocated to higher ground.
