La sonnambula

La sonnambula (The Sleepwalker) is an opera semiseria in two acts by Vincenzo Bellini, with an Italian libretto by Felice Romani, based on Eugène Scribe's ballet-pantomime La somnambule (1827). It premiered at the Teatro Carcano in Milan on 6 March 1831, with Giuditta Pasta in the title role, and is a cornerstone of the bel canto repertoire.

Synopsis

In a Swiss village, the orphan Amina is engaged to the farmer Elvino. The mysterious Count Rodolfo returns to the village and takes a room at Lisa's inn; Amina, sleepwalking, enters his room at night and is found asleep there, and the jealous Elvino breaks the engagement, believing her unfaithful. Only when Amina is seen sleepwalking again — crossing the village mill-bridge in her sleep — is her innocence proved, and the lovers are reunited.

Music

The opera is celebrated for Bellini's long-breathed, melancholic melodies, epitomizing the bel canto style. Amina's final aria, "Ah! non giunge uman pensiero," and her sleepwalking scene became vehicles for great coloratura sopranos; Maria Callas's performances at La Scala in 1955, in a production by Luchino Visconti, famously restored the work's prestige. Other renowned Aminas include Joan Sutherland and Natalie Dessay.

Performance history

La sonnambula was an immediate success and remains one of Bellini's most frequently performed operas alongside Norma and I puritani. The role of Amina, demanding both lyrical purity and brilliant coloratura, is among the most challenging in the soprano repertoire.
