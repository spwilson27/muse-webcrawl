Washington Dulles International Airport (/ˈdʌlɪs/ DUL-iss; IATA: IAD, ICAO: KIAD) is an international airport in Loudoun County and Fairfax County in Northern Virginia, United States, 26 miles (42 km) west of Downtown Washington, D.C. It is the primary international airport serving the Washington metropolitan area and a major hub for United Airlines.

History

The airport is named after John Foster Dulles, U.S. Secretary of State under President Dwight D. Eisenhower. It was dedicated by President John F. Kennedy on 17 November 1962, the first airport in the United States designed specifically for jet aircraft. Since 1987 it has been operated by the Metropolitan Washington Airports Authority.

Architecture

The main terminal, designed by Finnish-American architect Eero Saarinen, is a celebrated work of modernist architecture: its roof is a suspended catenary curve, hung between rows of outward-leaning columns, intended to suggest the flight of a bird. The terminal was extended in 1996 to a design consistent with Saarinen's original. Dulles was famous for its "mobile lounges" — large vehicles that carried passengers directly between the terminal and aircraft; most have been replaced by the underground AeroTrain, which opened in 2010, though mobile lounges still serve some international arrivals.

Operations

The airport occupies about 52.6 km² (20.3 sq mi) and has four runways. It handles the great majority of international traffic for the Washington–Baltimore region, with flights to destinations across Europe, Asia, Africa, the Middle East and Latin America. The Washington Metro's Silver Line reached the airport in November 2022, connecting it to downtown Washington. The Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center, the annex of the National Air and Space Museum, stands just south of the airport.
