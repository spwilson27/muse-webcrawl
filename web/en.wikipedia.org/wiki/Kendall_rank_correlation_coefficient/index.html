Kendall rank correlation coefficient

In statistics, the Kendall rank correlation coefficient, commonly referred to as Kendall's tau coefficient (after the Greek letter τ), is a statistic used to measure the ordinal association between two measured quantities. It is a nonparametric measure of rank correlation: it assesses the similarity of the orderings of the data when ranked by each quantity.

Definition

Let (x1, y1), …, (xn, yn) be pairs of observations. Any pair (xi, yi) and (xj, yj) is said to be concordant if the orderings agree (both xi > xj and yi > yj, or both less), and discordant if they disagree. The original coefficient, tau-a, is defined as τ = (number of concordant pairs − number of discordant pairs) / (n choose 2). A value of +1 indicates perfect agreement of rankings, −1 perfect disagreement, and 0 indicates independence of the orderings.

Variants and use

Because tied ranks are common in real data, adjusted versions are used in practice: tau-b corrects for ties in both variables, and tau-c (Stuart's tau-c) is used for contingency tables of unequal dimensions. Kendall's tau is closely related to Spearman's rank correlation coefficient but has a direct probabilistic interpretation: it equals the probability that a random pair is concordant minus the probability that it is discordant. It is widely used in social science, psychometrics, genetics and machine learning evaluation wherever ordinal data or ranked outputs must be compared.
