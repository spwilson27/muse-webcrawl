Channel (communications)

A communication channel links a transmitter and a receiver

A communication channel is a medium used to convey information from a sender (transmitter) to a receiver. In telecommunications and information theory, a channel is the medium through which a signal travels, and it may be characterized by its bandwidth, its noise, and the maximum rate at which information can be reliably transmitted—its channel capacity, as given by the Shannon–Hartley theorem.

Types of channels

Channels may be physical, such as a copper wire, optical fiber, or free space for radio waves, or logical, such as a frequency band within a shared medium. A television channel or radio channel typically refers to an assigned frequency range used by a broadcaster.

Channel models

Information theorists model channels mathematically to study error rates and coding schemes. Well-known models include the binary symmetric channel, the additive white Gaussian noise (AWGN) channel, and fading channel models used for mobile radio.

Multiplexing

A single physical transmission path often carries several logical channels at once through multiplexing, dividing the resource by frequency, time, code, or wavelength.
