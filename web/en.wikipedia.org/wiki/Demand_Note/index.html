Demand Note

Demand Notes were United States government paper money issued in 1861 and 1862 to help finance the Union effort in the American Civil War. They were the first paper money issued directly by the United States federal government to circulate widely and were nicknamed "greenbacks" for the distinctive green ink used on their backs.

History

The Act of July 17, 1861 authorised $60 million in Treasury notes payable on demand in coin at assistant treasurer offices in Philadelphia, Boston and New York. Denominations of $5, $10 and $20 were issued, bearing the signatures of Treasury officials — initially handwritten, which led clerks (many of them women, employed for the task) to sign "for the" Register or Treasurer before printed signatures were adopted.

Convertibility and replacement

Because the notes were redeemable in gold coin on demand, they maintained par value longer than the Legal Tender Notes ("United States Notes") that replaced them under the Legal Tender Act of 1862. Holders could also exchange Demand Notes for the new 6% twenty-year bonds. Most were redeemed, making surviving examples collectible.

Legacy

Demand Notes established the precedent for federal paper currency in the United States. Their green reverse set the colour convention that persists on US currency today.
