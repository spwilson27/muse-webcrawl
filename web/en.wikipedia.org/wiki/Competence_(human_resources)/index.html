Competence (human resources)

Competence in human resources is a standardised requirement for an individual to properly perform a specific job. It encompasses a combination of knowledge, skills, abilities, behaviours, and attitudes that can be defined, observed, and measured, and that distinguish superior from average performance.

History

The competence movement grew from David McClelland's 1973 paper "Testing for competence rather than for intelligence," which argued that aptitude tests poorly predicted job success. Richard Boyatzis's The Competent Manager (1982) popularised competency modelling in organisations.

Types

  • Core competencies — values and capabilities expected of all employees.
  • Functional/technical competencies — job-specific knowledge and skills.
  • Behavioural competencies — e.g., communication, teamwork, leadership.
  • Leadership competencies — strategic thinking, change management.

Competency models

A competency model (or framework) lists competencies with proficiency levels and behavioural indicators. Organisations use models for recruitment, performance appraisal, training and development, succession planning, and pay decisions.

Criticism

Critics note competencies can become vague checklists, quickly outdated, culturally biased, or focused on past success rather than future needs. The Dreyfus model of skill acquisition is an alternative account of expertise development.
