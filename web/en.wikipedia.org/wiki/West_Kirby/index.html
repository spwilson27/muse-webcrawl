West Kirby

West Kirby is a town on the north-west corner of the Wirral Peninsula in Merseyside, England, at the mouth of the River Dee. It lies within the Metropolitan Borough of Wirral. At the 2011 Census, the population was 12,733.

History

The name is thought to derive from the Old Norse Kirkjubyr, meaning "village with a church". The village was recorded in the Domesday Book of 1086 as part of the hundred of Wirral. St Bridget's Church, which stands on a hill to the west of the town, is of medieval origin, with a tower dating largely from the early 16th century.

West Kirby developed as a fashionable seaside resort in the Victorian era, following the arrival of the railway in 1866. The Hoylake and West Kirby line was later electrified, and the station remains a terminus of the Wirral line of the Merseyrail network.

Geography

The town faces the Dee estuary and the hills of north Wales. At low tide it is possible to walk across the sands to Hilbre Island, a small tidal island and nature reserve about a mile offshore, passing Middle Eye and Little Eye. Walkers must check tide times carefully, as the estuary fills quickly.

The West Kirby Marine Lake, constructed in the late 19th century and rebuilt after storm damage in 1985, is enclosed by a promenade walkway of just over a mile and is used for sailing, windsurfing and kayaking; it has hosted the Wilson Trophy team-racing sailing event.

Landmarks

The town's war memorial, designed by Charles Sargeant Jagger, stands on Grange Hill and was unveiled in 1922. West Kirby is the starting point of the Wirral Way, a footpath and cycleway along the trackbed of the former railway to Hooton, which forms part of Wirral Country Park, Britain's first designated country park.

Transport

West Kirby railway station is served by Merseyrail electric trains to Liverpool via Birkenhead, with services typically every 15 to 30 minutes. Bus services link the town with Birkenhead, Heswall and Chester.
