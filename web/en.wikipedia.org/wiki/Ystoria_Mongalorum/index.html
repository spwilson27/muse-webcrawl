Ystoria Mongalorum

The Ystoria Mongalorum ("History of the Mongols") is a report, in Latin, by the Franciscan friar Giovanni da Pian del Carpine (John of Plano Carpini) on his journey to the court of the Mongol Empire between 1245 and 1247. It is one of the earliest European accounts of the Mongols and the first systematic Western description of northern and Central Asia.

Carpine, a companion of Saint Francis of Assisi, was sent by Pope Innocent IV in 1245, in the aftermath of the Mongol invasions of Europe, as an envoy to the Great Khan. Travelling through Kiev and across the Eurasian steppe, he reached the Mongol capital region in Mongolia and witnessed the enthronement of Güyük Khan in 1246. He returned to Europe in 1247 bearing the Khan's famous letter to the Pope, which demanded the Pope's submission.

Contents

The work is organised into chapters describing the land of the Mongols, their climate, customs, religion, character, history (including the career of Genghis Khan), their methods of warfare, and advice on how the Mongols might be resisted militarily. A final chapter narrates Carpine's own journey. Two redactions of the text are known, the second somewhat expanded.

Significance

Carpine's report gave Latin Christendom its first reliable intelligence on Mongol society and military organisation. Along with the later account of William of Rubruck, it remained a principal European source on Asia until the age of Marco Polo. The narrative survives in several manuscripts and was partly absorbed into Vincent of Beauvais's Speculum Historiale.
