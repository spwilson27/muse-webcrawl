Prime triplet

In number theory, a prime triplet is a set of three prime numbers in which the smallest and largest differ by six. In other words, a prime triplet is a set {p, p + 2, p + 6} or {p, p + 4, p + 6} of primes. With the exceptions of (2, 3, 5) and (3, 5, 7), this is the closest possible grouping of three primes, since one of every three sequential odd numbers is a multiple of three, and hence not prime (except 3 itself).

Examples

The first prime triplets (of the form p, p + 2, p + 6) are (5, 7, 11), (11, 13, 17), (17, 19, 23), (41, 43, 47). Triplets of the form (p, p + 4, p + 6) include (7, 11, 13), (13, 17, 19), (37, 41, 43), (67, 71, 73).

Subtypes

A prime triplet contains a pair of twin primes (p and p + 2, or p + 4 and p + 6), a pair of cousin primes (p and p + 4, or p + 2 and p + 6), and a pair of sexy primes (p and p + 6).

Open questions

It is conjectured, as a special case of the prime k-tuple conjecture, that there are infinitely many prime triplets, but this remains unproven. Every prime triplet beyond (3, 5, 7) has the smallest member congruent to 5 modulo 6 in the first form, and skewes-type estimates suggest the count of triplets below x grows like a constant times x/(log x)³.
