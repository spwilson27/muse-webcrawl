Petah Tikva

Petah Tikva (Hebrew: פֶּתַח תִּקְוָה, "Opening of Hope") is a city in the Central District of Israel, about 10 km east of Tel Aviv, with a population of roughly a quarter of a million. Founded in 1878, it was one of the first modern Jewish agricultural settlements in Ottoman Palestine and is known as the "mother of the moshavot".

History

Settlers from Jerusalem purchased land near the Arab village of Mulabbis, enduring malaria from nearby swamps before establishing a viable citrus-growing community. The settlement grew with later waves of immigration, received city status in 1937, and absorbed surrounding neighbourhoods and former immigrant camps after 1948.

Economy

Today Petah Tikva is a major centre for high-tech, pharmaceuticals, and medical services; its industrial zones host companies such as Teva and its hospitals include the Rabin Medical Center and Schneider Children's Hospital. The city is served by the Tel Aviv Light Rail Red Line, opened in 2023.
