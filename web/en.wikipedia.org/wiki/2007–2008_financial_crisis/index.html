The 2007–2008 financial crisis, also known as the global financial crisis (GFC), was a severe worldwide financial crisis that followed the collapse of the United States housing bubble. Excessive risk-taking by banks, the proliferation of complex mortgage-backed securities, and failures in financial regulation combined to produce the worst economic downturn since the Great Depression.

Causes

During the early 2000s, low interest rates, lax lending standards and rising house prices fuelled a boom in subprime and adjustable-rate mortgages in the United States. These loans were packaged into mortgage-backed securities (MBS) and collateralised debt obligations (CDOs) sold worldwide, often with inflated credit ratings. When house prices fell from 2006 and mortgage defaults rose, the value of these securities collapsed, but their opacity made it impossible to know which institutions were exposed.

Crisis

Stress appeared in 2007 with the failure of two Bear Stearns hedge funds and the freezing of funds by BNP Paribas in August 2007. In March 2008 Bear Stearns was sold to JPMorgan Chase in a Federal Reserve-backed rescue. The crisis peaked in September 2008: Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac were placed into conservatorship, Lehman Brothers filed for bankruptcy on September 15 — the largest in US history — Merrill Lynch was sold to Bank of America, and insurer AIG was bailed out. Money market funds "broke the buck", interbank lending froze, and stock markets crashed worldwide.

Response and aftermath

Governments responded with bank recapitalisations, guarantees, near-zero interest rates and quantitative easing. In the United States, the $700 billion Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP) was authorised in October 2008. The crisis tipped the world economy into the Great Recession, with global trade contracting sharply and unemployment rising for years. Regulatory responses included the Dodd–Frank Act (2010) in the US and the Basel III capital standards internationally; in Europe the crisis fed into the subsequent eurozone sovereign debt crisis.
