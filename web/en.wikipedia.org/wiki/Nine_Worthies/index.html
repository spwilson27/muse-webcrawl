Nine Worthies

The Nine Worthies are nine historical, scriptural and legendary figures regarded in medieval literature as the perfect exemplars of chivalry. The canon was established by Jacques de Longuyon in Les Voeux du paon (1312).

The nine

They are grouped in three triads. The pagans: Hector, Alexander the Great and Julius Caesar. The Jews: Joshua, David and Judas Maccabeus. The Christians: King Arthur, Charlemagne and Godfrey of Bouillon.

In art and literature

The Worthies appear throughout late-medieval art — in tapestries, sculpture, castle decoration and playing cards — and are alluded to in Shakespeare's Love's Labour's Lost, where a pageant of the Worthies is staged. Later tradition added female counterparts, the Nine Worthy Women, with varying membership.
