Bob Dylan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Bob Dylan in 1965: wild hair, dark glasses, holding cue cards from the Subterranean Homesick Blues film clip]

Bob Dylan (legally Robert Dylan; born Robert Allen Zimmerman, 24 May 1941) is an American singer-songwriter. Generally regarded as one of the greatest songwriters of all time, Dylan has been a major figure in popular culture over his 60-year career. Much of his most celebrated work dates from the 1960s, when songs such as 'Blowin' in the Wind' and 'The Times They Are a-Changin'' became anthems of the civil rights and anti-war movements.

A folk prodigy from Minnesota who idolised Woody Guthrie, Dylan scandalised folk purists by 'going electric' at Newport in 1965 and made landmark albums Highway 61 Revisited (1965, with 'Like a Rolling Stone'), Blonde on Blonde (1966) and Blood on the Tracks (1975). He has toured almost continuously since 1988 on the 'Never Ending Tour', and in 2016 received the Nobel Prize in Literature 'for having created new poetic expressions within the great American song tradition'.
