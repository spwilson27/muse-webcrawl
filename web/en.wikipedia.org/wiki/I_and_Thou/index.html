I and Thou

Ich und Du
Author: Martin Buber
Published: 1923
Subject: Philosophy of dialogue

I and Thou (German: Ich und Du, 1923) is a book by the philosopher Martin Buber, his best-known work and a classic of existential and dialogical philosophy.

Content

Buber argues that human beings relate to the world through two fundamental attitudes, expressed by the word pairs I–Thou and I–It. In an I–Thou relation, the other is met as a subject and an end in itself, in genuine dialogue and mutuality; in an I–It relation, the other is treated as an object to be experienced, classified or used. Buber holds that a person becomes fully human through I–Thou encounters, and that God — the "eternal Thou" — can be encountered in, though never reduced to, such relations.

Influence

The book deeply influenced theology, psychology (including person-centred and Gestalt therapy), ethics and theories of education and communication.
