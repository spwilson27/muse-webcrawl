The Museum of London was a museum in the City of London documenting the history of the British capital from prehistoric times to the present. One of the largest urban history collections in the world, with over six million objects, it opened in 1976 at London Wall near the Barbican, formed by merging the collections of the Guildhall Museum and the London Museum at Kensington Palace. It closed in December 2022 ahead of its relocation and rebranding as the London Museum at Smithfield Market.

Collections and galleries

Its chronological galleries ran from the prehistoric Thames valley and Roman Londinium — including a celebrated reconstruction of Roman interiors — through medieval London, the Great Fire of 1666, and the expanding Georgian and Victorian metropolis. Star exhibits included the Lord Mayor's State Coach (1757), a recreated Victorian shopping street, the suffragette collection, and the enormous "Fatberg" sample from Whitechapel exhibited in 2018. The museum also curated the city's archaeology through its association with MOLA (Museum of London Archaeology), holding finds from countless excavations.

Move to Smithfield

In 2022 the museum closed its London Wall site after 46 years and began moving to the disused General Market buildings at West Smithfield, beside Farringdon station, under the new name London Museum. Its Docklands branch, the Museum of London Docklands in a former sugar warehouse at West India Quay, remains open and covers the history of the Thames and the port.
