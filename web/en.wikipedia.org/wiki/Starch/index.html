Starch

Starch
Chemical formula(C6H10O5)n
AppearanceWhite powder
ComponentsAmylose and amylopectin
SolubilityInsoluble in cold water; gelatinizes in hot water

Starch or amylum is a polymeric carbohydrate consisting of numerous glucose units joined by glycosidic bonds. This polysaccharide is produced by most green plants for energy storage. Worldwide, it is the most common carbohydrate in human diets, and is contained in large amounts in staple foods such as wheat, potatoes, maize (corn), rice, and cassava.

Structure

Starch consists of two types of molecules: the linear and helical amylose and the branched amylopectin. Plants store starch in organelles called amyloplasts as semi-crystalline granules. In the human digestive system, the enzyme amylase breaks starch down into sugars.

Uses

Pure starch is a white, tasteless, odorless powder used in food as a thickening and stabilizing agent (for example in sauces and puddings, often as corn starch) and processed into sugars such as glucose syrup. Industrially, starch is used in papermaking, adhesives, textiles, and bioplastics, and can be fermented into ethanol. When heated in water, starch granules swell and gelatinize; on cooling, retrogradation can produce resistant starch.
