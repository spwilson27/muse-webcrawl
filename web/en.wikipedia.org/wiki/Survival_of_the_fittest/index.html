Survival of the fittest

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

"Survival of the fittest" is a phrase describing the mechanism of natural selection, coined by the philosopher Herbert Spencer in his Principles of Biology (1864) after reading Charles Darwin's On the Origin of Species. Darwin adopted it in later editions of the Origin and in The Descent of Man, judging it sometimes more accurate than his own term.

In biology, "fitness" has a technical meaning: an organism's success in passing its genes to the next generation — reproductive success, not strength, speed or aggression. The phrase is thus easily misunderstood: the "fittest" may be the best camouflaged, the most cooperative or the most fecund, and a trait's fitness depends entirely on the environment. Biologists also note the apparent tautology ("survivors survive") is avoided because fitness is defined by heritable traits' expected reproductive contribution, testable in experiments.

Outside biology the phrase was bent to justify laissez-faire capitalism, colonialism and eugenics under the banner of "Social Darwinism" — applications Darwin's mechanism does not logically support and which remain politically charged. The underlying process it names, differential reproductive success, works alongside random genetic drift in shaping the adaptations of populations.
