Property insurance

Property insurance provides protection against most risks to property, such as fire, theft and some weather damage. This includes specialized forms of insurance such as fire insurance, flood insurance, earthquake insurance, home insurance, or boiler insurance. Property is insured in two main ways — open perils and named perils.

Coverage

Named perils policies cover only losses caused by the specific causes listed in the policy, such as fire, lightning, explosion, and theft. Open perils (or "all-risk") policies cover all causes of loss except those specifically excluded, typically including earthquake and flood, which are often sold as separate policies or endorsements. Policies pay either the replacement cost of the property or its actual cash value, which deducts depreciation.

Types

Common forms include homeowners insurance, renters insurance, commercial property insurance, and marine insurance. Property cover is frequently bundled with liability cover, and mortgage lenders generally require borrowers to carry it.
