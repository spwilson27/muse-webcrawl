Staatsgalerie Stuttgart

The Staatsgalerie Stuttgart (State Gallery) is an art museum in Stuttgart, Germany, opened in 1843. It houses one of Germany's most important art collections, ranging from medieval German painting to modern and contemporary art. The museum consists of the neoclassical Alte Staatsgalerie (Old State Gallery) and the postmodern Neue Staatsgalerie (New State Gallery) designed by James Stirling, one of the landmark museum buildings of the 20th century.

History

The gallery was founded in 1843 as the royal Museum of Fine Arts, installed in a neoclassical building by Gottlob Georg Barth. Its collection grew from the holdings of the dukes and kings of Württemberg together with major donations, notably the Italian paintings collected by Baron Giovanni Bonifacio de Lugo and the Barbini-Breganze collection. The building was heavily damaged in the Second World War and rebuilt in the 1950s.

Neue Staatsgalerie

By the 1970s the museum needed more space. A 1977 competition was won by the London architects James Stirling and Michael Wilford, whose New State Gallery opened in 1984. It is a celebrated work of postmodern architecture: a U-shaped gallery of travertine and sandstone bars wrapped around an open circular rotunda, threaded by a public footpath, enlivened by brightly coloured steel canopies, handrails and window frames that quote — and playfully subvert — classical and modernist models. A third building, the Steib annex for the graphic collection and special exhibitions, was added in 2002.

Collection

The collection spans seven centuries. Highlights include Old Master works by Jörg Breu, Hans Holbein the Elder, Rembrandt and Rubens; 19th-century painting from Caspar David Friedrich to the French impressionists; and a major modern collection with works by Picasso, Matisse, Kandinsky, Klee, Mondrian, Dalí and Joseph Beuys, including Matisse's celebrated Backs series and an important group of works by Oskar Schlemmer and the Stuttgart avant-garde.
