Mayotte (French: Mayotte; Shimaore: Maore) is an overseas department and region and single territorial collectivity of France. It is located in the northern part of the Mozambique Channel in the Indian Ocean off the coast of Southeastern Africa, between northwestern Madagascar and northeastern Mozambique. Mayotte consists of a main island, Grande-Terre (or Maore), a smaller island, Petite-Terre (or Pamanzi), as well as several islets around these two.

History

The islands were populated by Swahili-speaking peoples and later ruled by local sultanates. France purchased Mayotte from Sultan Andriantsoly in 1841. When the other Comoro islands voted for independence in 1974, Mayotte voted twice (1974 and 1976) to remain French. After a 2009 referendum, Mayotte became France's 101st department on 31 March 2011 and an outermost region of the European Union in 2014. The Union of the Comoros continues to claim sovereignty over the island.

Geography

Grande-Terre is geologically the oldest island in the Comoros archipelago. The island is surrounded by a coral barrier reef enclosing a lagoon of about 1,100 km², one of the world's largest, and notable for its marine biodiversity, including dugongs and nesting green turtles.

Demographics and economy

Mayotte has one of France's youngest and fastest-growing populations, driven by a high birth rate and immigration from the Comoros. Islam is the predominant religion. The economy is heavily supported by French public expenditure; exports include ylang-ylang, vanilla, coffee and copra. Despite rising incomes since departmentalisation, Mayotte remains the poorest French department, and unemployment and informal housing (bangas) are major challenges.
