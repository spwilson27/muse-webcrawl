Log–log plot

In science and engineering, a log–log graph or log–log plot is a two-dimensional graph of numerical data that uses logarithmic scales on both the horizontal and vertical axes. Power-law relationships of the form y = a·xk appear as straight lines on such a plot, because taking logarithms gives log y = log a + k·log x, with the slope equal to the exponent k.

A power law becomes a straight line on a log–log plot.

Uses

Log–log plots are used whenever quantities span many orders of magnitude or follow power laws: frequency distributions such as Zipf's law and Pareto distributions, fractal scaling, allometric scaling in biology, Bode plots in control engineering, and earthquake and network statistics. The slope of a straight segment estimates the power-law exponent, commonly by linear regression on the log-transformed data (though this method has known biases, and maximum-likelihood fitting is often preferred).

Relation to other plots

A semi-log plot uses a logarithmic scale on only one axis and straightens exponential relationships instead. Log–log plots were historically produced on specially printed log–log graph paper, with cycles of decades marked along each axis.
