The Books of Samuel (Hebrew: ספר שמואל, Sefer Shmuel) are two books of the Hebrew Bible and Christian Old Testament, originally a single work. They narrate the transition of Israel from the period of the judges to the monarchy: the career of the prophet Samuel, the reign of Saul, the first king, and the rise and reign of David.


Samuel anoints David

Content

1 Samuel

The book opens with the birth of Samuel to Hannah and his upbringing at Shiloh under Eli. Samuel becomes prophet and judge, and at the people's demand anoints Saul as king. Saul's disobedience leads God to reject him and send Samuel to anoint the shepherd David, whose defeat of Goliath launches his rise. The rest of the book follows Saul's jealous pursuit of David, who twice spares the king's life, until Saul and his son Jonathan die at Mount Gilboa fighting the Philistines.

2 Samuel

David is made king first of Judah, then of all Israel; he captures Jerusalem, brings up the Ark, and receives God's promise of an everlasting dynasty. The narrative then turns to his sins — the adultery with Bathsheba and the arranged death of her husband Uriah — and their consequences: the prophet Nathan's rebuke, the rape of Tamar, Absalom's rebellion and death, and further revolts and troubles, ending with David's census and plague. Appendices add the Song of David and his last words.

Composition

Traditionally attributed to Samuel, Nathan and Gad, the books are regarded by scholars as part of the Deuteronomistic history, compiled in the 7th–6th centuries BC from older sources including a "History of David's Rise" and a frank "Succession Narrative" often praised as among the finest historical writing of the ancient world. Textual witnesses (Septuagint, Dead Sea Scrolls) show the text remained fluid into the late Second Temple period.
