Friendly artificial intelligence

Friendly artificial intelligence (also friendly AI or FAI) is a hypothetical artificial general intelligence (AGI) that would have a positive rather than negative effect on humanity. The term was coined by Eliezer Yudkowsky of the Machine Intelligence Research Institute to discuss superintelligent artificial agents that reliably implement human values — the central problem of what is now called AI alignment.

Motivation

Proponents argue that a superintelligence optimizing a goal system that diverges even slightly from human values could pose an existential risk, since instrumental goals such as self-preservation and resource acquisition would be pursued regardless of the final goal. Friendliness research therefore focuses on how an AI's goals could be specified so that they remain stable and beneficial under self-improvement. Critics regard the framing as speculative or hold that alignment will be achieved incrementally through ordinary engineering and regulation.
