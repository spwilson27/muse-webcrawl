Women's lacrosse

Women's lacrosse (or girls' lacrosse) is the women's version of lacrosse, a team sport played with sticks and a small rubber ball. It differs from men's lacrosse in rules, equipment and permissible contact: body checking is limited and players wear less protective equipment, though eye protection is mandatory.

Rules

Field lacrosse is played with twelve players per side, including a goalkeeper. Players use the cradle, pass, and shoot into the opponent's goal. A 'free position' shot follows many fouls, and a restraining line limits how many players may enter the attacking zone.

Competition

The Women's Lacrosse World Cup has been held since 1982, with the United States and Australia the dominant nations. The sport is especially popular in US colleges under the NCAA, and professional leagues have included the UWLX, WPLL and Athletes Unlimited.
