Smokey Robinson

William "Smokey" Robinson Jr. (born February 19, 1940) is an American singer, songwriter, record producer and former record executive. He was the founder and frontman of the Motown vocal group the Miracles, and a principal songwriter and vice-president at Motown Records under Berry Gordy.

Career

With the Miracles, Robinson recorded Motown's first million-selling hit, "Shop Around" (1960), and classics including "You've Really Got a Hold on Me", "The Tracks of My Tears" (1965), "I Second That Emotion" and "The Tears of a Clown" (1970). As a songwriter he penned hits for others: "My Girl" and "Get Ready" for the Temptations, "My Guy" for Mary Wells, and songs for Marvin Gaye and the Marvelettes.

His solo career, launched after leaving the Miracles in 1972, produced the album A Quiet Storm (1975) – which gave the quiet storm radio format its name – and hit singles "Cruisin'" (1979) and "Being with You" (1981).

Honours

Robinson was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1987, received the Gershwin Prize for Popular Song (2016) and Kennedy Center Honors (2006 era cohort recognition widely noted), and Bob Dylan once called him a great poet – a quote often (mis)repeated.
