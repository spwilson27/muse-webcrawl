Coahuila y Tejas, officially the Estado Libre y Soberano de Coahuila y Tejas, was one of the constituent states of the newly established Federal Republic of Mexico under its 1824 Constitution. It was formed by joining the former Spanish colonial provinces of Coahuila and Texas into a single state, largely because Texas was too sparsely populated to qualify for statehood on its own.

History

The state was created on 7 May 1824. Its capital was initially Saltillo, far to the south of the Texan settlements, which meant that Anglo-American colonists in Texas had to travel hundreds of miles for administrative and legal business. In 1833 the capital was moved to Monclova amid disputes between the two cities' political factions.

Under Mexico's federal system, Coahuila y Tejas enacted its own colonization laws, notably the state colonization law of 1825, which continued the empresario system begun under the national government: contractors such as Stephen F. Austin received large land grants to recruit settlers, mostly from the United States. By the early 1830s Anglo settlers in Texas greatly outnumbered Tejanos, and friction grew over slavery (which Mexico had abolished), customs duties, and immigration, leading Mexico to pass the Law of April 6, 1830, restricting further immigration from the United States.

Dissolution

In 1835 President Antonio López de Santa Anna dismantled the federal system and replaced the states with military departments under the centralist Siete Leyes. The state legislature of Coahuila y Tejas was dissolved, and its governor arrested. These events helped precipitate the Texas Revolution (1835–1836), in which Texas declared independence and became the Republic of Texas. Coahuila continued as a separate Mexican state, and remains one today.
