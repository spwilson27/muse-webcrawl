Jaccard index

The Jaccard index, also known as the Jaccard similarity coefficient, is a statistic used for gauging the similarity and diversity of sample sets. It was developed by Grove Karl Gilbert in 1884 as his ratio of verification and was later developed by Paul Jaccard, who originally gave the French name coefficient de communauté. The measure is defined as the size of the intersection divided by the size of the union of two sets:

J(A, B) = |A ∩ B| / |A ∪ B|

Definition

The Jaccard index ranges from 0 to 1: it is 0 when the two sets share no elements and 1 when they are identical. The complementary measure, one minus the Jaccard index, is the Jaccard distance, which satisfies the properties of a metric and is used to quantify dissimilarity.

Applications

The index is widely used in ecology for comparing species composition between sites, in information retrieval and data mining for measuring the similarity of sets of words or shingles in documents, in machine learning for evaluating segmentation and classification, and in bioinformatics for comparing sets of genes or k-mers. Weighted and generalised versions exist for multiset and probability-vector data, and locality-sensitive hashing schemes such as MinHash estimate the Jaccard index efficiently for very large collections.
