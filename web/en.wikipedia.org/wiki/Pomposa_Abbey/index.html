Pomposa Abbey

Pomposa Abbey (Italian: Abbazia di Pomposa) is a Benedictine monastery near Codigoro in the province of Ferrara, Italy. One of the most important Romanesque abbeys in northern Italy, it was a celebrated centre of learning and culture in the Middle Ages, famous for its scriptorium and for its association with Guido d'Arezzo, the monk who is credited with inventing modern musical notation.

History

A church existed on the site by the seventh century, and a Benedictine community was established by the ninth century on what was then an island in the Po delta, surrounded by water and woodland. The abbey grew rich on gifts from emperors and nobles; in 1026 the church of Santa Maria was consecrated, and its magnificent atrium, inlaid floor mosaics, and 48-metre campanile (built in 1063) followed. At its height in the eleventh and twelfth centuries, Pomposa rivalled Monte Cassino and Nonantola as a centre of learning: Guido d'Arezzo worked there under Abbot Guido of Arezzo around 1025–1033, and the legal scholar and abbot's circle contributed to the revival of Roman law.

From the fourteenth century, malaria and the shifting course of the Po caused decline, compounded by the commendatory abbots who drew its revenues. The monks finally left in 1653, moving to Ferrara, and the abbey church became a parish church. Restorations in the nineteenth and twentieth centuries recovered much of the complex, which is now a national monument.

Art and architecture

The church preserves important cycles of fourteenth-century frescoes in the Bolognese-Riminese manner, including scenes from the Old and New Testaments and the Apocalypse, attributed to Vitale da Bologna and his workshop; the refectory has frescoes attributed to Pietro da Rimini, and the chapter house contains further trecento paintings. The atrium is decorated with terracotta and stone inlays, and the interior floor combines early medieval geometric inlay with later figural mosaics. The campanile, built in Lombard Romanesque style, increases the size of its windows with each of its nine storeys.
