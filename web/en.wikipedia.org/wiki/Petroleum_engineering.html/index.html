Petroleum engineering

Petroleum engineering is a field of engineering concerned with the activities related to the production of hydrocarbons — crude oil and natural gas. Exploration, by earth scientists, and petroleum engineering are the oil and gas industry's two main subsurface disciplines, which focus on maximizing the economic recovery of hydrocarbons from underground reservoirs.

Subdisciplines

  • Reservoir engineering — modeling reservoir fluid flow, estimating reserves, and planning field development and enhanced recovery.
  • Drilling engineering — designing and managing the drilling of wells, including casing, cementing, and well control.
  • Production engineering — managing the interface between the reservoir and the well and surface facilities, including artificial lift.
  • Petrophysics — measuring rock and fluid properties from well logs and core samples.

History and profession

The discipline grew with the commercial oil industry in the early 20th century; the first petroleum engineering degrees appeared around 1912–1915 in the United States. The Society of Petroleum Engineers is the main professional body. Petroleum engineers are usually among the highest-paid engineering graduates, though employment is cyclical with oil prices, and the field increasingly overlaps with carbon capture and storage and geothermal energy.
