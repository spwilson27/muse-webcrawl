Slim Harpo

Slim Harpo
Born: James Moore, 11 January 1924, Lobdell, Louisiana, US
Died: 31 January 1970, Baton Rouge, Louisiana
Genres: Swamp blues, Louisiana blues
Instruments: Vocals, harmonica, guitar

James Isaac Moore (January 11, 1924 – January 31, 1970), known professionally as Slim Harpo, was an American blues musician and a leading exponent of the swamp blues style. A master of the harmonica (the "harp" of his stage name, bestowed by producer Jay Miller), he recorded for Excello Records in Crowley, Louisiana, and scored national hits with "I'm a King Bee" (1957), "Rainin' in My Heart" (1961), and "Baby Scratch My Back" (1966), the last reaching number one on the Billboard R&B chart and number 16 on the pop chart.

His laid-back grooves, nasal vocals, and echo-laden harmonica made his records favourites of the 1960s British rhythm-and-blues bands: the Rolling Stones recorded "I'm a King Bee," and his songs were also covered by the Kinks, the Yardbirds, Van Morrison and Them, and later by many others. "Shake Your Hips" (1966) was covered by the Stones on Exile on Main St.

Just as he was preparing a European tour and a recording session with Canned Heat, Slim Harpo died suddenly of a heart attack in Baton Rouge in January 1970, aged 46. He was inducted posthumously into the Blues Hall of Fame, and "Baby Scratch My Back" and "I'm a King Bee" remain standards of the blues repertoire.
