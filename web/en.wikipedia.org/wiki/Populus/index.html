Populus


Quaking aspen, Populus tremuloides

Populus is a genus of 25–30 species of deciduous flowering plants in the family Salicaceae, native to most of the Northern Hemisphere. English names variously applied to different species include poplar, aspen, and cottonwood. The genus has a large genetic diversity, and can grow from 15–50 m (49–164 ft) tall, with trunks up to 2.5 m (8 ft 2 in) in diameter.

Description

Poplars are dioecious — male and female flowers are borne on separate trees — and are pollinated by wind, with flowers appearing in catkins before the leaves in spring. The seeds are tiny, carried on cottony tufts, and must germinate quickly on moist, bare soil. Leaves are alternate, simple, and often triangular or heart-shaped (deltoid) or, in aspens, nearly round with flattened stalks that make them tremble in the wind. Species such as quaking aspen (Populus tremuloides) spread clonally by root suckers, forming vast colonies such as Pando in Utah.

Uses

Fast-growing poplars are widely planted for timber, plywood, pallets, matches, and pulp, for windbreaks and soil stabilization, and increasingly for bioenergy plantations. The balsam poplars yield fragrant resins, and the black poplar and Lombardy poplar are common ornamental and landscape trees. Poplars are also model organisms in tree genomics: Populus trichocarpa was the first tree to have its genome fully sequenced.
