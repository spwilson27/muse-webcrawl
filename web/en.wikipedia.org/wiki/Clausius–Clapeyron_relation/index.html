Clausius–Clapeyron relation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Clausius–Clapeyron relation, named after Rudolf Clausius and Émile Clapeyron, expresses the slope of the coexistence curve between two phases of matter in terms of latent heat and the change in volume:

dP/dT = L / (T Δv)

where L is the specific latent heat, T the absolute temperature, and Δv the specific volume change on the phase transition.

Derivation

At coexistence, the Gibbs free energies of the two phases are equal; differentiating along the phase boundary and using dG = V dP − S dT yields the relation exactly, using the entropy of transition S2 − S1 = L/T.

Applications

  • Meteorology: saturation vapor pressure over water increases by roughly 7% per °C of warming — a key feedback in climate change.
  • Chemistry: estimating boiling points at altered pressures.
  • Materials science: predicting phase boundaries of alloys and solid-state transitions.

Anomalies, such as ice melting under pressure, follow from the sign of Δv: because ice is less dense than water, the melting curve has a negative slope.
