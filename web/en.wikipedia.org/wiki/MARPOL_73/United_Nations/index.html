United Nations

The United Nations (UN) is an intergovernmental organization established by the signing of the UN Charter on 26 June 1945 with the stated purposes of maintaining international peace and security, developing friendly relations among nations, achieving international cooperation, and being a centre for harmonizing the actions of nations. The UN is headquartered on international territory in New York City, and has other main offices in Geneva, Nairobi, Vienna, and The Hague, where the International Court of Justice is located.

The UN has 193 member states and two permanent observer states (the Holy See and Palestine). Its system includes six principal organs — the General Assembly, the Security Council, the Economic and Social Council, the Trusteeship Council, the International Court of Justice, and the UN Secretariat — as well as numerous specialized agencies, funds, and programmes, including the WHO, UNICEF, the World Food Programme, and the IMO (International Maritime Organization), whose MARPOL 73/78 convention on prevention of pollution from ships is one of the treaties administered within the UN system.

History

The UN replaced the League of Nations, founded after the First World War, which had failed to prevent the Second. The Charter was signed in San Francisco in June 1945 and came into force on 24 October 1945 after ratification by the five permanent members of the Security Council — China, France, the Soviet Union (now represented by Russia), the United Kingdom, and the United States — and a majority of other signatories.

Activities

The UN is the largest intergovernmental organization in the world. Its work spans peacekeeping operations, humanitarian aid and refugee protection, development goals (the Sustainable Development Goals adopted in 2015), international law, human rights monitoring through the Universal Declaration of Human Rights framework, and responses to global challenges including climate change.
