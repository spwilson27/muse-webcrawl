SOLAS

The International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea (SOLAS) is an international maritime treaty administered by the International Maritime Organization which sets minimum safety standards in the construction, equipment and operation of merchant ships. Together with MARPOL 73/78, it forms a pillar of the IMO regulatory regime.

SOLAS
Full name: International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea
Signed: 1 November 1974
In force: 25 May 1980
Administered by: International Maritime Organization

History

The first SOLAS convention was adopted in 1914 in response to the sinking of the Titanic. Successive versions followed, with the 1974 convention — kept current through a tacit-acceptance amendment procedure — remaining in force.

Provisions

SOLAS chapters cover construction and stability, fire protection, life-saving appliances, radiocommunications (including the GMDSS), safety of navigation, carriage of cargoes, and the ISM and ISPS codes on safe management and ship security.
