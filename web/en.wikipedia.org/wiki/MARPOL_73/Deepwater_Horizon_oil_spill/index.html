Deepwater Horizon oil spill

The Deepwater Horizon oil spill was an environmental disaster beginning 20 April 2010 in the Gulf of Mexico, at the BP-operated Macondo Prospect. It is considered the largest marine oil spill in the history of the petroleum industry and estimated to be 8 to 31 percent larger in volume than the previous largest, the Ixtoc I oil spill, also in the Gulf of Mexico.

Caused by the explosion and sinking of the Deepwater Horizon semi-submersible offshore drilling rig — which had been leased to BP — the explosion killed eleven workers and injured seventeen others; the rig sank two days later, on 22 April. Oil flowed from the seabed well for 87 days, until it was capped on 15 July 2010. The well was formally declared sealed on 19 September 2010. The total discharge of oil into the Gulf has been estimated at 3.19 million barrels (about 134 million US gallons), of which some 810,000 barrels were recovered or burned at the surface.

Environmental impact

Oil fouled hundreds of miles of beaches, marshes, and estuaries from Texas to Florida. Fisheries and tourism suffered major losses, and large numbers of marine mammals, sea turtles, birds, and fish were killed or harmed; the spill occurred during the breeding season of many species. Cleanup operations used skimmers, controlled burns, and nearly two million gallons of oil dispersants, including the controversial subsea injection of Corexit at the wellhead.

Legal consequences

BP established a $20 billion claims fund and, in settlements and penalties, ultimately bore costs exceeding $65 billion. In 2012 BP pleaded guilty to criminal charges including manslaughter and agreed to $4.5 billion in fines and penalties; in 2015 it reached an $18.7 billion settlement with the United States government and five Gulf states, the largest environmental damage settlement in US history. The disaster prompted major reforms in offshore drilling regulation and the breakup/creation of new regulatory agencies — and is regulated in part under pollution-prevention regimes such as MARPOL 73 conventions administered by the International Maritime Organization.
