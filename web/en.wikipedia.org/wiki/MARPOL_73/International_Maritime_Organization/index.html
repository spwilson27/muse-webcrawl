International Maritime Organization

The International Maritime Organization (IMO) is a specialised agency of the United Nations responsible for regulating international shipping. Under its MARPOL 73/78 convention, the IMO sets the global framework for preventing pollution from ships.

International Maritime Organization
Founded: 1948 (as IMCO); in force 1958
Headquarters: London, United Kingdom
Members: 175 states
Key convention: MARPOL 73/78

Role under MARPOL

The International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships (MARPOL) was adopted under IMO auspices in 1973 and modified by the 1978 Protocol. The IMO's Marine Environment Protection Committee administers the convention and its annexes covering oil, noxious liquids, harmful substances in packaged form, sewage, garbage and air pollution.

Structure

The IMO consists of an Assembly, a Council, and committees including the Maritime Safety Committee and the Marine Environment Protection Committee. Its headquarters are on the Thames Embankment in London.
