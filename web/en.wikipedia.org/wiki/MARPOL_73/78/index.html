MARPOL 73/78 (short for marine pollution) is the common name for the International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships, 1973, as modified by the Protocol of 1978. Administered by the International Maritime Organization, it is the principal international convention covering prevention of pollution of the marine environment by ships from operational or accidental causes.

History

The original convention was adopted in 1973 but did not enter into force; the 1978 Protocol, adopted after a series of tanker accidents including that of the Amoco Cadiz, absorbed the parent convention, and the combined instrument entered into force on 2 October 1983. It has been amended many times since, notably after the Exxon Valdez disaster, which prompted the requirement for double hulls on oil tankers.

Annexes

MARPOL's technical rules are set out in six annexes: Annex I (oil), Annex II (noxious liquid substances in bulk), Annex III (harmful substances in packaged form), Annex IV (sewage), Annex V (garbage) and Annex VI (air pollution from ships, including the 2020 global sulphur limit that cut the maximum sulphur content of marine fuel to 0.50%). States party enforce the rules through flag-state certification and port-state control inspections.
