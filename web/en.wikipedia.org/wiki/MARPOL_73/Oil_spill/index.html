Oil spill

An oil spill is the release of a liquid petroleum hydrocarbon into the environment, especially the marine ecosystem, due to human activity. Oil spills are a major form of pollution addressed by Annex I of the MARPOL 73/78 convention.

Oil spill
Type: Environmental pollution
Common sources: Tankers, offshore platforms, pipelines
Regulation: MARPOL Annex I
Notable incidents: Exxon Valdez (1989), Deepwater Horizon (2010)

Causes and scale

Spills may result from tanker collisions and groundings, blowouts at offshore wells, pipeline ruptures, or operational discharges such as ballast and bilge water. MARPOL limits operational oil discharges and requires double hulls for oil tankers.

Environmental effects

Oil coats birds and marine mammals, destroying insulation and buoyancy, and toxic components can persist in sediments for decades. Cleanup methods include booms, skimmers, dispersants, in-situ burning and bioremediation.
