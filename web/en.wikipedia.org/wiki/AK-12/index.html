AK-12

The AK-12 is a Russian assault rifle chambered in 5.45×39mm, designed by the Kalashnikov Concern and adopted as the standard service rifle of the Russian Armed Forces as part of the Ratnik soldier equipment program. It was derived from the AK-200 prototype series and entered service in 2018.

Design

The AK-12 retains the long-stroke gas piston system of the AK-74 but adds modernized ergonomics: a telescoping adjustable stock, Picatinny rails for optics, an ambidextrous-friendly fire selector with a two-round burst mode in early models, improved muzzle brake, and a free-floated barrel cover design. Variants include the AK-15 (7.62×39mm), the compact AK-19 (5.56×45mm NATO), and civilian semi-automatic versions (TR3).

Service

The rifle has seen wide use in the Russian invasion of Ukraine beginning in 2022, where captured and battlefield-recovered examples were extensively documented. Feedback from service led to design revisions, including removal of the two-round burst feature in later production models.
