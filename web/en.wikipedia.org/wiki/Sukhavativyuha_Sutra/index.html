The Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra ("Discourse on the Display of Sukhavati") is a Mahayana Buddhist sutra describing Sukhavati, the Pure Land of the Buddha Amitabha. It exists in two versions: the Longer Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra (often called the Infinite Life Sutra) and the Shorter Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra. Together with the Amitayus Sutra (Sutra on Contemplation of Amitayus), they form the three principal sutras of Pure Land Buddhism.

Contents

The Longer Sutra recounts how the bodhisattva Dharmakara, in a previous age, made forty-eight vows before the Buddha Lokesvararaja, resolving to create a perfect Buddha-land once he attained enlightenment. Having fulfilled the vows as Amitabha, his land Sukhavati is described in lavish detail: jeweled trees, lotus ponds, and beings who are reborn there by sincere faith and recitation of his name, free from suffering and guaranteed eventual Buddhahood. The sutra also includes the famous "three grades" of aspirants and the exclusion clause regarding those who commit the five grave offences and slander the Dharma, a passage debated for centuries by commentators such as Honen and Shinran in Japan.

History

The sutras were composed in India, probably in the first or second century CE, possibly in the northwest, and were translated into Chinese multiple times; the translation of the Longer Sutra attributed to Kang Sengkai (c. 252 CE) became standard, while Kumarajiva's translation of the Shorter Sutra (402 CE) remains the most recited version in East Asia.
