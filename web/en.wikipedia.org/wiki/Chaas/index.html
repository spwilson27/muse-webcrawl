Chaas

Chaas (also spelled chhas, chaach, or takra in some regions) is a traditional buttermilk-based drink from the Indian subcontinent, made by diluting curd (yoghurt) with water and churning out the butter. It is a staple beverage in Indian households, valued as a cooling drink in hot weather and as a digestive aid served with meals. Regional variations include the sweet lassi of Punjab and spiced salted versions common in Gujarat, Rajasthan and Maharashtra, where the drink is known variously as mattha, ghol or taak.

Preparation

Chaas is prepared by whisking or churning curd with water — often in a proportion of about one part curd to three or four parts water — until the fat separates as butter, which is removed; the remaining thin liquid is the buttermilk drink. It is seasoned with salt and commonly flavoured with roasted and ground cumin, black salt (kala namak), asafoetida, ginger, green chilli, and a tempering of mustard seeds and curry leaves fried in a little oil or ghee. Fresh coriander or mint leaves are often added as a garnish.

Variations

In Gujarat, chaas accompanies virtually every meal and is flavoured simply with salt, cumin and curry leaves. Rajasthani versions may be richer, while Maharashtrian taak often includes ginger and asafoetida. A popular modern variation adds finely chopped cucumber or bottle gourd. The sweetened, thicker relative of chaas is lassi, and the spiced South Indian equivalent is neer mor (also majjige in Kannada).

Cultural significance

In Ayurvedic tradition, buttermilk (takra) is regarded as a wholesome food said to support digestion, and chaas is routinely served after heavy or spicy meals. In rural areas it is traditionally offered to guests as a sign of hospitality. Industrially packaged chaas, sold by Indian dairy cooperatives such as Amul, is now widely available in tetra packs and bottles.
