Animal hoarding

SpecialtyPsychiatry
SymptomsKeeping far more animals than can be properly cared for, denial of their suffering
Related conditionsHoarding disorder, obsessive–compulsive disorder
[Image: Rescued cats being removed from a hoarding property by animal control]

Animal hoarding, sometimes called Noah syndrome, is the compulsive accumulation of a large number of animals — far beyond the person's ability to house, feed or provide veterinary care for them — combined with failure to recognise the animals' suffering or the deteriorating conditions. Hundreds of cases are reported in the United States each year, most often involving cats and dogs.

Characteristics

Unlike commercial breeders or shelters, hoarders typically deny that animals are malnourished, ill or living in unsanitary conditions, and resist giving them up. Homes often show severe sanitation hazards, including accumulated waste and carcasses. Research associates the behaviour with attachment disorders, trauma, and hoarding disorder, which the DSM-5 lists as a distinct diagnosis; animal hoarding is treated as a manifestation or special case of it in many studies.

Response

Intervention usually involves animal control agencies, courts and mental-health services. Prosecution alone has high recidivism; long-term monitoring, treatment and limits on animal ownership are recommended.
