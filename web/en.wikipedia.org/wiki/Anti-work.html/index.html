Anti-work

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Graffiti expressing the anti-work slogan 'Do not work'

Anti-work is a political and philosophical stance opposed to work as it is organised under modern economic systems, particularly the obligation to sell one's labour in wage employment for most of one's waking life. Anti-work thought does not necessarily reject productive activity or effort, but criticises compulsory, alienating and excessive labour, and advocates drastically reduced working time, automation of toil and alternatives such as universal basic income.

History

The idea has roots in Paul Lafargue's The Right to Be Lazy (1880), a refutation of the 19th-century glorification of labour, and in Bertrand Russell's essay In Praise of Idleness (1932). It was elaborated by the Situationists, by autonomist Marxists' "refusal of work", and by Bob Black's influential 1985 essay The Abolition of Work. The contemporary anti-work movement gained mass visibility through the Reddit community r/antiwork, which grew rapidly during the COVID-19 pandemic and the "Great Resignation" of 2021.
