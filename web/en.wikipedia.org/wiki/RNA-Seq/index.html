RNA-Seq

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

RNA-Seq (RNA sequencing) is a technique that uses next-generation sequencing to reveal the presence and quantity of RNA molecules in a biological sample, providing a snapshot of the transcriptome at a given moment. Since its emergence around 2008 it has largely replaced microarrays for gene-expression studies.

Method

A typical experiment isolates RNA, enriches for messenger RNA (often by poly-A selection) or depletes ribosomal RNA, fragments the RNA and converts it into a complementary DNA (cDNA) library with adaptors, which is then sequenced — typically tens of millions of short reads per sample. Computational pipelines align the reads to a reference genome or assemble them de novo, count reads per gene or transcript, and normalise the counts.

Applications

RNA-Seq measures differential gene expression between conditions with a wide dynamic range and no need for prior knowledge of sequences. It also detects alternative splicing, novel transcripts and long non-coding RNAs, allele-specific expression, RNA editing, and gene fusions important in cancer. Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), developed from 2009, applies the approach to individual cells and has transformed developmental biology and immunology by revealing cell types and states.

Analysis

Common tools include aligners such as STAR and HISAT2, quantification tools such as Salmon, and differential-expression packages such as DESeq2 and edgeR. Key challenges include batch effects, library-size normalisation, multi-mapping reads and the statistical treatment of count data.
