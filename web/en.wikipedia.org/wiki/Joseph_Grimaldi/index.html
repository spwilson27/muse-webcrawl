Joseph Grimaldi

Born18 December 1778, London, England
Died31 May 1837, Islington, London, England
OccupationActor, comedian, dancer, clown
Known forCreating the modern clown character "Joey"

Joseph Grimaldi (18 December 1778 – 31 May 1837) was an English actor, comedian and dancer who became the most popular English entertainer of the Regency era. In the harlequinades and pantomimes at Sadler's Wells and Covent Garden, he transformed the role of the Clown into the star of the show and created the white-faced, gaudily dressed figure — nicknamed "Joey" after him — that remains the archetype of the modern circus clown.

Early life and career

Grimaldi was born in Clare Market, London, the son of Joseph Giuseppe Grimaldi, an Italian dancer and dentist who was master of ballet at Drury Lane. He first appeared on stage at Sadler's Wells in 1780, before the age of two, and performed throughout his childhood under his father's harsh discipline. By the late 1790s he was the leading clown at Sadler's Wells.

Fame

His triumph came with the pantomime Harlequin and Mother Goose; or, The Golden Egg at Covent Garden in 1806, which ran for 92 nights and established him as London's favourite clown. Grimaldi's clown sang comic songs, performed acrobatic slapstick, ridiculed contemporary fashions and politics, and wore the red-spotted white face, mohawk tufts and outlandish costume that became standard. "Joey" became slang for a clown generally.

Later life

Decades of violent physical comedy wrecked Grimaldi's health, and he retired in 1823, impoverished by family misfortunes. He gave a farewell benefit performance in 1828, too infirm to stand through his songs. After his death in 1837, his memoirs were edited by Charles Dickens and published in 1838. Joseph Grimaldi Park in Islington contains his grave.
