Ryman Auditorium

The Ryman Auditorium is a 2,362-seat live-performance venue in Nashville, Tennessee, United States. Known as the "Mother Church of Country Music", it was home to the Grand Ole Opry from 1943 to 1974 and is a National Historic Landmark.

Location116 Rep. John Lewis Way N, Nashville, Tennessee
Opened1892
Capacity2,362
Former namesUnion Gospel Tabernacle

History

Riverboat captain Thomas G. Ryman built it as the Union Gospel Tabernacle after being converted at a revival in 1885. It opened in 1892 and after Ryman's death in 1904 was renamed for him. It hosted lectures, concerts, operas and political events, managed for decades by Lula C. Naff.

Grand Ole Opry

The Opry's radio broadcasts moved to the Ryman in 1943; its wooden pews, stained glass and superb acoustics made it iconic. Performers included Hank Williams, Patsy Cline, Johnny Cash and Dolly Parton. The Opry left for a new house in 1974 and the building narrowly escaped demolition.

Revival

After years of limited use, the Ryman was renovated and reopened in 1994 as a concert hall and museum. It now hosts country, rock, Americana and comedy, and was named a National Historic Landmark in 2001. A major expansion added a larger lobby in 2015 while preserving the original auditorium.
