Stirling numbers of the first kind

In mathematics, the Stirling numbers of the first kind count permutations according to their number of cycles. The unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind, written c(n,k) or [n choose k] in bracket notation, count permutations of n elements with exactly k disjoint cycles.

Definition and recurrence

They satisfy c(n+1,k) = n·c(n,k) + c(n,k−1), with c(0,0)=1. The signed Stirling numbers s(n,k) = (−1)^(n−k) c(n,k) appear as coefficients expanding the falling factorial x(x−1)...(x−n+1).

Applications

These numbers, introduced by James Stirling in 1730, connect factorials, permutations, and polynomial expansions, and appear throughout combinatorics and the analysis of algorithms (e.g. the expected number of cycles in a random permutation is the harmonic number H_n).
