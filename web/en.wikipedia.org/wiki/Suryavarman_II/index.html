Suryavarman II (died in or around 1150) was king of the Khmer Empire from 1113 to about 1145–1150. He is remembered above all as the builder of Angkor Wat, the largest religious monument in the world, and for reviving Khmer expansion with wars against Champa and Dai Viet.

Reign and wars

Suryavarman came to the throne as a young prince after defeating his great-uncle Dharanindravarman I, reuniting an empire that had been divided. He restored diplomatic relations with China, which recognised him in 1116 and 1120, and mounted repeated campaigns to the east: inscriptions claim victories over the Cham kingdom of Champa and expeditions against the Vietnamese state of Dai Viet, though these wars brought no lasting annexation and the final campaigns of the 1140s appear to have failed.

Angkor Wat

Suryavarman's supreme achievement is Angkor Wat, built in the early twelfth century as his state temple and probably intended as his funerary temple. Unusually for a Khmer temple, it is dedicated to the Hindu god Vishnu rather than Shiva, and it faces west, the direction of death. Its bas-reliefs include a celebrated portrait gallery of the king reviewing his army. His posthumous name, Paramavishnuloka — "he who has entered the heavenly world of Vishnu" — reflects this devotion.

Death and succession

The date and circumstances of Suryavarman's death are uncertain; he may have died during a campaign against Champa around 1145–1150. After a confused interregnum the throne passed to his cousin Dharanindravarman II. Angkor Wat itself survived the empire's later conversion to Buddhism and was never abandoned, becoming the symbol of Cambodia that appears on its national flag.
