Roman Scotland

Roman Scotland was the part of Britain north of the province of Britannia that, during the era of the Roman Empire, came at various times under Roman occupation and influence, roughly between AD 71 and 213. Scotland north of the Forth–Clyde isthmus was never permanently conquered, but Roman armies campaigned there repeatedly and at times occupied territory as far north as the Moray Firth.

Campaigns

The governor Gnaeus Julius Agricola advanced into Caledonia in AD 79–84, winning the Battle of Mons Graupius around AD 83 or 84, though the exact site remains uncertain. Later campaigns included those of the emperor Septimius Severus in 208–210, shortly before his death at York.

Frontier works

Rome's principal frontier installations in Scotland were Hadrian's Wall (begun c. 122) along the Tyne–Solway line and the Antonine Wall (begun c. 142), a turf rampart across the Central Belt that was occupied for only about two decades before abandonment. Forts, fortlets, and temporary marching camps recorded across the country include major sites at Trimontium, Cramond, and Inchtuthil, the latter a legionary fortress abandoned before completion.
