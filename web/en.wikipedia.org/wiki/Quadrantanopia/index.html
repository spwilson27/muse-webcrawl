Quadrantanopia

Quadrantanopia (also called quadrantanopsia) is a medical condition characterized by loss of vision in one quadrant of the visual field in both eyes. It results from lesions of the retrochiasmal visual pathways — the optic radiations or the occipital lobe — rather than from disease of the eye itself.

Causes and localization

A lesion of the temporal lobe affecting the inferior optic radiation (Meyer's loop) causes a contralateral superior quadrantanopia ("pie in the sky"), while a lesion of the parietal lobe affecting the superior optic radiation (Baumgarten loop) causes a contralateral inferior quadrantanopia ("pie on the floor"). Common causes include stroke, tumours and surgery. Occipital lesions tend to produce highly congruous defects.

Diagnosis

The defect is mapped by perimetry (visual field testing), and neuroimaging is used to find the underlying lesion. Quadrantanopia is related to but less extensive than hemianopsia, in which half the visual field is lost.
