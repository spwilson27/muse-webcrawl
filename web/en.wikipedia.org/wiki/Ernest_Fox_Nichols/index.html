Ernest Fox Nichols (1 June 1869 – 29 April 1924) was an American physicist and academic administrator. He is best known for his experimental measurement of radiation pressure, and he served as the tenth president of Dartmouth College and as president of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology.

Scientific career

Nichols studied at Kansas State Agricultural College (now Kansas State University) and took his doctorate at Cornell University in 1897, having also studied in Berlin. After posts at Colgate University and Dartmouth, he worked with Edward F. Hull at Dartmouth using a sensitive torsion balance of their own design — the Nichols radiometer — to measure the pressure exerted by light, publishing definitive results in 1901–1903 that confirmed Maxwell's theoretical prediction. From 1903 to 1909 he was professor of physics at Columbia University, where with Hull he refined the measurement further — work complementary to that of Pyotr Lebedev in Russia.

Administration

Nichols served as president of Dartmouth College from 1909 to 1916, resigning for health reasons. During the First World War he worked on research for the US Navy, and in 1921 he became president of MIT — the first person in that office with a background in pure rather than applied science — but poor health forced his resignation in 1922. He died in 1924 while addressing a meeting in Washington.
