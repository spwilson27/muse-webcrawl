Home of Franklin D. Roosevelt National Historic Site

Springwood, birthplace and home of Franklin D. Roosevelt

LocationHyde Park, Dutchess County, New York, US
AreaAbout 800 acres (324 ha) in the historic site
Established15 January 1944
Governing bodyNational Park Service

The Home of Franklin D. Roosevelt National Historic Site preserves the Springwood estate in Hyde Park, New York, United States. Springwood was the birthplace, lifelong home, and burial place of the 32nd president of the United States, Franklin D. Roosevelt. The National Historic Site was established in 1945, after Roosevelt deeded the estate to the American people (reserving a life interest for himself and his family) in 1943. The estate is administered by the National Park Service.

Springwood

The central element of the estate is the large, Colonial Revival main house, also known as the Franklin D. Roosevelt Home. The original house dated from around 1800 and was purchased by the president's father, James Roosevelt, in 1866. In 1915, Franklin Roosevelt, together with his mother Sara Delano Roosevelt, undertook a major remodelling designed by the New York firm Hoppin & Koen: the house was enlarged with fieldstone wings, a large portico and new service areas, giving it its present appearance. Roosevelt was born here in the second-floor tower bedroom in 1882 and returned throughout his life — he called it his "Summer White House" — making some 200 visits during his presidency.

Library, gravesite, and museum

On the grounds stands the Franklin D. Roosevelt Presidential Library and Museum, the first presidential library in the United States, designed by Roosevelt himself, built in 1939–1940, and dedicated in 1941; it continues to be operated by the National Archives alongside the historic site. In the rose garden near the house are the graves of Franklin and Eleanor Roosevelt, marked by a simple white marble monument.

The National Park Service site also includes trails through the estate's farm and forest lands and connects with the nearby Eleanor Roosevelt National Historic Site (Val-Kill) and Vanderbilt Mansion National Historic Site.
