Porcellio scaber

Porcellio scaber (otherwise known as the common rough woodlouse or simply rough woodlouse) is a species of woodlouse native to Europe but with a cosmopolitan distribution. It is one of the most common and widespread terrestrial isopods and a frequent inhabitant of gardens, leaf litter and compost heaps.

Description

Adults grow to about 11 mm long. The body is grey, flattened and oval, and — unlike pill woodlice such as Armadillidium — P. scaber cannot roll into a ball; instead it clings flat to surfaces or runs when disturbed. The upper surface is rough and tuberculate, giving the species its name.

Ecology

Like other woodlice it is a crustacean that breathes through gill-like pleopods and must avoid desiccation, foraging mostly at night. It feeds on decaying plant material, fungi and detritus, playing an important role in decomposition and nutrient cycling.

Physiology

The species is widely used as a laboratory model in ecotoxicology and physiology. Unusually for a crustacean, it can absorb water vapour directly from unsaturated air, and females carry developing eggs in a fluid-filled brood pouch (marsupium).
