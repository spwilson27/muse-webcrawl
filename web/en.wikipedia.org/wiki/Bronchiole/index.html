Bronchiole
System: respiratory system
Part of: lungs

The bronchioles are the smaller branches of the bronchial airways in the respiratory system. They begin where the bronchi divide, lack cartilage in their walls, and terminate in alveolar ducts leading to the alveoli, where gas exchange takes place.

Structure

Bronchioles are lined by simple cuboidal epithelium with club cells and are surrounded by smooth muscle, allowing their diameter to change and regulate airflow. Terminal bronchioles mark the end of the conducting zone; respiratory bronchioles begin the respiratory zone, with alveoli budding from their walls.

Clinical significance

Bronchioles are affected in asthma, in which their smooth muscle constricts, and in bronchiolitis, an inflammation common in infants, often caused by respiratory syncytial virus. Obliterative bronchiolitis is a rare, serious disease of small-airway scarring.
