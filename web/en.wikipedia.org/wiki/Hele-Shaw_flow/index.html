Hele-Shaw flow

Hele-Shaw flow is the Stokes flow of a viscous fluid between two close parallel plates — a configuration named after Henry Selby Hele-Shaw, who used it around 1898 to visualise streamlines around obstacles. The narrowness of the gap makes the depth-averaged velocity satisfy equations mathematically analogous to two-dimensional potential flow.

Properties and applications

Because the averaged flow obeys Laplace's equation, a Hele-Shaw cell can demonstrate potential-flow patterns experimentally. When a less viscous fluid is injected into a more viscous one in a Hele-Shaw cell, the interface becomes unstable and forms branching fingers — the Saffman–Taylor (viscous fingering) instability — a phenomenon important in oil recovery, pattern formation and moving-boundary problems in mathematics.
