Relativistic mechanics

Branch ofPhysics
Key conceptsFour-momentum, relativistic mass, Lorentz transformation
Based onSpecial relativity

In physics, relativistic mechanics refers to mechanics compatible with special relativity (SR) and general relativity (GR). It provides a non-quantum mechanical description of a system of particles, or of a fluid, in cases where the velocities of moving objects are comparable to the speed of light c.

Relativistic kinematics

In special relativity, the momentum of a particle is given by p = γmv, where γ = 1/√(1 − v²/c²) is the Lorentz factor. As the speed of a particle approaches the speed of light, its momentum grows without bound, so no finite force can accelerate a massive particle to light speed.

Energy

The total energy of a particle is E = γmc², which reduces at rest to the famous mass–energy equivalence E = mc². The kinetic energy is (γ − 1)mc², which for low speeds approaches the classical value ½mv².

Four-momentum

Energy and momentum combine into a single four-vector, the four-momentum, whose components transform linearly under Lorentz transformations. Its invariant magnitude yields the relation E² = (pc)² + (mc²)².
