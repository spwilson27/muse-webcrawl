Steeple

In architecture, a steeple is a tall tower on a building, topped by a spire and often incorporating a belfry and other components. Steeples are very common on Christian churches and cathedrals and the use of the term generally connotes a religious structure, though the term also appears in other contexts.

Structure

A steeple typically consists of the tower itself, a belfry housing bells, and a spire — a tapering conical or pyramidal structure on top. Steeples may be constructed of stone, brick or wood, and are often clad in slate, shingles, lead or copper. Clock faces are frequently incorporated into the tower.

Symbolism and history

Steeples developed from medieval church towers and spires, such as those of Gothic cathedrals. They serve as landmarks visible across a settlement, and symbolically direct attention heavenward. Lightning strikes and structural decay have destroyed many historic steeples, and their tall, narrow form makes them vulnerable in storms; a fallen steeple is sometimes rebuilt exactly, as with the spire of Notre-Dame de Paris after the 2019 fire.
