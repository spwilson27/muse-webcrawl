Selection differential

The selection differential, commonly denoted S, is a measure of the strength of selection in quantitative genetics and animal and plant breeding. It is defined as the difference between the mean value of a trait in the selected parents and the mean value of that trait in the whole population from which they were drawn: S = μselected − μpopulation.

Breeder's equation

The selection differential appears in the breeder's equation, R = h²S, which predicts the response to selection (R, the change in the trait mean in the offspring generation) from the selection differential and the narrow-sense heritability (h²) of the trait. A larger selection differential — for example, by selecting only the most extreme individuals as parents — produces a larger expected response, provided heritability is not zero.

Selection intensity

The standardized selection differential, S divided by the phenotypic standard deviation of the trait, is called the selection intensity (i). Selection intensity depends only on the proportion of the population selected, and tables of its values are used by breeders when planning selection programs.
