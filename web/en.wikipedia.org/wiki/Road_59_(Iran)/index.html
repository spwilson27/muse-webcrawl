Road 59, better known as the Chalous Road (also transliterated Chalus Road; Persian: جاده چالوس), is a mountain road in northern Iran connecting Karaj in Alborz Province with the city of Chalus on the Caspian Sea in Mazandaran Province. It is widely regarded as one of the most scenic — and most dangerous — roads in Iran, and is one of the country's busiest holiday routes, carrying Tehran residents to the Caspian resorts.

Route

From Karaj the road follows the Karaj River valley into the Alborz range, passing the Amir Kabir Dam (Karaj Dam) and climbing through a succession of tunnels, galleries and hairpin bends. Its highest section crosses the range near the Kandovan Tunnel, opened in 1938, at over 2,700 metres above sea level; from there it descends through the lush Hyrcanian forest valleys of the Caspian slope, past waterfalls and roadside restaurants, to emerge on the coastal plain at Chalus. The journey of roughly 160 km can take from three hours to a whole day at holiday peaks, when the road is operated one-way for long stretches.

History

Before the road, the route over the Alborz was a mule track. Construction of the modern road began under Reza Shah in 1931 and was completed in 1938, using large numbers of labourers paid, according to legend, by the length of tunnel drilled and the coins of the day. The Kandovan Tunnel alone cost the lives of many workers, whose memorial stands at its entrance.

Hazards

Avalanches, rockfalls and landslides regularly close the road in winter and spring; it has been the scene of numerous serious bus and car accidents, and traffic police close it in severe weather. A parallel modern freeway, the Tehran–Shomal Freeway, has been under construction for decades to relieve the route, with sections opened from 2020 onwards.
