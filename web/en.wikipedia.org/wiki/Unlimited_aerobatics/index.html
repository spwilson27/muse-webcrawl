Unlimited aerobatics

Unlimited aerobatics is the highest category of competition aerobatics, in which pilots fly sequences of demanding maneuvers in purpose-built aircraft without the restrictions applied at lower levels. Competitions are held under the rules of the Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI) and its aerobatics commission, CIVA, including the World Aerobatic Championships.

Aircraft and maneuvers

Unlimited aircraft — such as the Extra 300, Edge 540, and Sukhoi Su-26 family — are typically single-seat monoplanes with very high power-to-weight ratios, structural limits around ±10 g or more, and roll rates exceeding 400 degrees per second. Unlimited sequences include maneuvers forbidden at lower levels, such as gyroscopic "lomcovák" tumbles, multiple snap rolls, torque rolls, and vertical maneuvers with multiple rolls on up- and down-lines.

Competition format

Pilots fly a known compulsory sequence, free sequences of their own design, and unknown sequences handed out shortly before flight, all within a marked aerobatic box of 1,000 metres per side. Judges score each figure for precision and positioning using the Aresti system of notation. Unlimited is the level at which world champions are crowned and from which many top airshow and air-racing pilots come.
