Catadromous

Catadromous fish live primarily in fresh water as adults and migrate down to the sea to spawn — the opposite pattern to anadromous fish such as salmon. The term derives from the Greek kata (down) and dromos (running).

Examples

The classic examples are the freshwater eels of the genus Anguilla. The European eel (Anguilla anguilla) and American eel (A. rostrata) grow for years in rivers and lakes, then migrate thousands of kilometres to spawn in the Sargasso Sea, where they die; their leaf-like leptocephalus larvae drift back to the coasts and ascend rivers as elvers. Other catadromous or partly catadromous species include some mullets, the barramundi in parts of its range, and certain gobies and galaxiids whose larvae develop at sea.

Terminology

Animals that migrate between fresh and salt water are called diadromous. Within that group, anadromous species grow at sea and spawn in fresh water, catadromous species grow in fresh water and spawn at sea, and amphidromous species move between the two at a stage not directly tied to spawning.
