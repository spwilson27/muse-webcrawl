Nisan

Nisan (Hebrew: נִיסָן, Nīsān; from Akkadian nisānu) is the first month of the ecclesiastical year and the seventh month of the civil year (eighth in leap years) in the Hebrew calendar. It is a spring month of 30 days, roughly corresponding to March–April in the Gregorian calendar, and is called the month of Aviv ("spring", literally "ripening barley") in the Torah.

Biblical significance

Nisan is the month of the Exodus from Egypt: on the 14th of the month the Passover sacrifice was offered, and the festival of Passover (Pesach) begins on the 15th, lasting seven days in Israel and eight in the diaspora. The Torah designates the first of Nisan as the beginning of the year for counting the reigns of kings and the cycle of festivals. The Omer counting toward Shavuot begins on the second day of Passover.

Observances

During Nisan the blessing on flowering fruit trees (Birkat Ha'Ilanot) is recited, and Tachanun prayers are omitted for the whole month in honour of the festival and the dedication of the Tabernacle, whose twelve days of dedication offerings, described in Numbers 7, are traditionally read on the first twelve days of the month. The Fast of the Firstborn is held on the eve of Passover. In the Assyrian and Chaldean traditions, 1 Nisan marks Kha b-Nisan, the Assyrian New Year.

Events in Jewish history

Tradition associates Nisan with several events: the birth and death of the patriarchs Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob; the construction of the Tabernacle; and the birthday of Moses on 7 Nisan in some traditions. The Warsaw Ghetto Uprising began on the eve of Passover, 14 Nisan 5703 (19 April 1943).
