William Redington Hewlett (May 20, 1913 – January 12, 2001) was an American engineer and businessman who, with David Packard, co-founded the Hewlett-Packard Company (HP) in 1939. The partnership, begun in a Palo Alto garage with $538 in capital, became a founding enterprise of Silicon Valley and one of the largest technology companies in the world.

Early life and education

Hewlett was born in Ann Arbor, Michigan, where his father taught at the University of Michigan Medical School; the family moved to San Francisco when he was three. Despite dyslexia, which made reading difficult and pushed him toward mathematics and hands-on learning, he was admitted to Stanford University as a favor to his late father, a Stanford alumnus. There he earned a bachelor's degree in 1934, studied under radio pioneer Frederick Terman — who encouraged his partnership with classmate David Packard — and completed a master's degree in electrical engineering at MIT in 1936, followed by further study at Stanford.

Founding of HP

In 1939 Hewlett and Packard formalized their partnership with a coin toss to decide the order of their names. Their first product, the HP 200A audio oscillator — an electronic test instrument noted for its low cost and stability — was famously bought by Walt Disney Productions, which used the 200B model to test sound equipment for the film Fantasia. Hewlett's oscillator design, incorporating a small incandescent lamp as a temperature-dependent resistor in its feedback network, was the subject of his master's thesis work.

Career and management philosophy

Hewlett served as HP's president from 1964 and chief executive from 1969, stepping down in 1978 and remaining chairman until 1983. With Packard he developed "the HP Way," a management philosophy emphasizing trust in employees, decentralized divisions, open offices, and "management by walking around." During the Second World War he served in the Army Signal Corps and led an electronics inspection tour of Japanese industry after the war.

Philanthropy and legacy

With his wife Flora, Hewlett founded the William and Flora Hewlett Foundation in 1966, which became one of the largest American charitable foundations, supporting education, the environment, and the performing arts. He also gave extensively to Stanford and other institutions. Hewlett died in Palo Alto in 2001. The Palo Alto garage where HP began is preserved as a California historical landmark and is often called the "birthplace of Silicon Valley."
