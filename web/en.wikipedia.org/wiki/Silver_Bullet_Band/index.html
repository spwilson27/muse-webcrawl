The Silver Bullet Band is an American rock band formed in Detroit, Michigan, in 1974, best known as the backing band of singer-songwriter Bob Seger. Recording and touring as "Bob Seger & the Silver Bullet Band," the group was the vehicle for Seger's breakthrough to national stardom in the mid-1970s and for a string of hit albums and singles that made them one of the defining acts of heartland rock.

Bob Seger & the Silver Bullet Band on stage during their late-1970s peak.

History

Seger assembled the Silver Bullet Band in 1974 from Detroit-area musicians, including guitarist Drew Abbott, bassist Chris Campbell, keyboardist Robyn Robbins, drummer Charlie Allen Martin, and saxophonist Alto Reed, whose saxophone became a signature of the band's sound. The band's breakthrough came with the live album Live Bullet (1976), recorded at Detroit's Cobo Hall, followed by the studio album Night Moves (1976), whose title track became a major hit.

Subsequent albums with the Silver Bullet Band included Stranger in Town (1978), featuring "Still the Same" and "Old Time Rock and Roll," and Against the Wind (1980), which reached number one on the Billboard 200 and won a Grammy Award. Although Seger also recorded with the Muscle Shoals Rhythm Section during this period, the Silver Bullet Band remained his touring band and the core of his recorded identity.

Later years

The band continued to record and tour with Seger through the 1980s and 1990s, including the albums The Distance (1982), Like a Rock (1986), and The Fire Inside (1991). After a hiatus, Seger reunited with the Silver Bullet Band for tours in the 2000s and 2010s, with bassist Chris Campbell and saxophonist Alto Reed among its longest-serving members. Seger's farewell tour of 2018–19 brought the band's performing career to a close after more than four decades.
