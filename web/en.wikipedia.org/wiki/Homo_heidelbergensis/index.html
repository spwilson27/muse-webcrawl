Homo heidelbergensis

Homo heidelbergensis is an extinct species or subspecies of archaic human that lived in the Middle Pleistocene, roughly 700,000 to 200,000 years ago. It was named in 1908 from a massive jawbone found in a sand pit at Mauer, near Heidelberg, Germany.

Description

Members of the species were tall and robust, with large brow ridges and brains averaging about 1,200 cm³, close to modern humans. Fossils attributed to it come from Europe (Boxgrove, Atapuerca's Sima de los Huesos) and Africa (Kabwe cranium).

Significance

H. heidelbergensis is widely regarded as a common ancestor — or close relative of the ancestor — of Neanderthals, Denisovans and modern humans. Evidence includes wooden spears at Schöningen and controlled use of fire.
