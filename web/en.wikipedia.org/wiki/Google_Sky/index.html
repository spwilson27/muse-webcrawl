Google Sky

Google Sky is a feature of Google Earth and an online sky/outer space viewer based at Google Maps, devoted to viewing outer space. It was created on August 22, 2007, and allows users to pan and zoom across the cosmos using imagery from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey, the Hubble Space Telescope, and other sources.

Features

Google Sky includes layers showing constellations, planets in motion, galaxies and nebulae, historical star maps by Giovanni Maria Cassini, and imagery from the Digitized Sky Survey covering more than a million stars and some 200 million galaxies. Users can search for celestial objects by name or coordinates, view the sky in infrared and microwave overlays, and see the positions of the planets at past and future dates.

Related projects

The Sky mode was developed partly through Google's Virtual Telescope project and has been used in education and outreach alongside comparable tools such as Microsoft's WorldWide Telescope. A standalone web version, Google Maps Sky, and an Android app, Sky Map (donated by Google to open source), extended the same imagery to browsers and phones.
