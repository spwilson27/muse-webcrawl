Propyl gallate

Propyl gallate, or propyl 3,4,5-trihydroxybenzoate, is an ester of gallic acid and propanol used as an antioxidant food additive (E number E310). Since the 1940s it has been added to foods containing fats and oils — such as vegetable oils, chewing gum, processed potato products, and cosmetics — to prevent rancidity caused by the formation of peroxides during oxidation. It is a white to cream crystalline powder, slightly soluble in water and freely soluble in oils and alcohol.

Propyl gallate: the propyl ester of gallic acid

Properties and synthesis

Propyl gallate has the molecular formula C10H12O5 and a molar mass of about 212.2 g/mol. It is prepared by the esterification of gallic acid with propanol, or obtained from tannin-rich plant materials followed by transesterification. Like other gallate esters (octyl gallate, E311, and dodecyl gallate, E312), it works by donating hydrogen atoms to free radicals, interrupting the chain reactions of lipid autoxidation; it is often used in combination with BHA and BHT, with which it shows synergistic effects.

Regulation and safety

Propyl gallate is authorised as a food additive in the European Union and the United States within specified limits. Safety evaluations have periodically re-examined it: concerns have included its possible weak estrogenic (endocrine) activity and, in high-dose animal studies, effects that led regulators to set an acceptable daily intake. The European Food Safety Authority's 2014 re-evaluation established an ADI of 0.5 mg/kg body weight per day. Some manufacturers have nevertheless reformulated products to avoid gallates in response to consumer preference. Propyl gallate also finds use as a stabiliser in pharmaceuticals, adhesives, and lubricants, and as a fluorescence quencher in microscopy research.
