Mixed race

The term mixed race describes people whose ancestry comes from more than one racial or ethnic group as those groups are socially defined. Terminology varies by country: "multiracial" is common in the United States, "mixed heritage" or "dual heritage" in the United Kingdom, and historically specific terms — many now considered offensive — existed under colonial and segregationist systems.

By region

Censuses increasingly allow multiple categories: the US Census permitted marking more than one race from 2000, and the multiracial population grew rapidly in 2020 results. In Brazil, pardo is an official category; in South Africa, "Coloured" is a distinct legal and cultural identity. Mixed populations such as Métis in Canada, Anglo-Indians, and Eurasians have distinct histories.

History

Laws against interracial marriage (anti-miscegenation laws) existed in the US until Loving v. Virginia (1967) and in apartheid South Africa and Nazi Germany.
