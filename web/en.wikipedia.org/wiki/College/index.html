College

A college (Latin: collegium) is an educational institution or a constituent part of one. A college may be a degree-awarding tertiary educational institution, a part of a collegiate or federal university, an institution offering vocational education, a further education institution, or a secondary school.

Usage by country

United States

In the United States, "college" is often used synonymously with "university", though a college may also be a distinct undergraduate institution (such as a liberal arts college) or a constituent school within a university. Community colleges offer two-year degrees and vocational training.

United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom, colleges include the constituent colleges of Oxford and Cambridge, further education colleges, and sixth-form colleges attended by 16- to 19-year-olds. Some independent secondary schools also use "college" in their name.

Other countries

Usage varies widely: in Canada, colleges typically offer applied and vocational programmes distinct from universities; in Australia and New Zealand the word often refers to secondary schools; in France a collège is a lower secondary school; in India many teaching institutions are affiliated colleges of a university.

History

The collegiate model arose in medieval European universities, where colleges were endowed communities providing lodging, meals and teaching to scholars. The residential college system of Oxford and Cambridge became a model for institutions elsewhere, including the residential colleges of Yale and Harvard.
