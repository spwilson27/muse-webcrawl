Beauty and the Beast is a 1991 American animated musical romantic fantasy film produced by Walt Disney Feature Animation and released by Walt Disney Pictures. It is based on the 1756 fairy tale of the same name by Jeanne-Marie Leprince de Beaumont. The film was directed by Gary Trousdale and Kirk Wise, produced by Don Hahn, and features a screenplay by Linda Woolverton, with songs written by lyricist Howard Ashman and composer Alan Menken.

The film tells the story of a prince who is transformed into a Beast and his servants into household objects as punishment for his arrogance, and Belle, a young woman whom he imprisons in his castle. To break the curse, the Beast must learn to love Belle and earn her love in return before the last petal falls from an enchanted rose, or he will remain a Beast forever.

Plot

A selfish young prince is cursed by an enchantress disguised as a beggar woman: he is transformed into a hideous Beast, his servants into household objects, and the castle falls under a spell that can only be broken if he learns to love another and earn her love in return before the last petal of an enchanted rose falls on his twenty-first birthday.

Years later, in a nearby village, Belle, a book-loving young woman, dreams of adventure beyond her provincial life and fends off the advances of the arrogant hunter Gaston. Her father Maurice, an inventor, becomes lost in the forest and takes refuge in the Beast's castle, where the Beast imprisons him for trespassing. Belle offers herself in her father's place, and the Beast accepts.

With the help of the enchanted servants — Lumière the candelabra, Cogsworth the clock, Mrs. Potts the teapot and her son Chip — Belle gradually befriends the Beast, and the two bond over their shared love of reading. After the Beast saves Belle from wolves, their relationship deepens, culminating in a romantic ballroom dance. When the Beast learns that Maurice is ill, he releases Belle, giving her his magic mirror so she can remember him.

Gaston, humiliated by Belle's rejection, rallies the villagers to storm the castle and kill the Beast. During the ensuing battle, Gaston mortally wounds the Beast but falls to his own death. As the last petal falls, Belle professes her love for the dying Beast, breaking the spell: the Beast is restored to human form, the servants become human again, and the castle is transformed back into a shining palace.

Production

Walt Disney first attempted to adapt Beauty and the Beast into an animated film during the 1930s and 1950s, but the projects were abandoned. Following the success of The Little Mermaid (1989), Disney revived the idea, initially as a non-musical film under director Richard Purdum. Disney chairman Jeffrey Katzenberg ordered the film scrapped and restarted as a musical in the style of a Broadway show, mirroring The Little Mermaid. Lyricist Howard Ashman, who was terminally ill with AIDS-related complications during production, wrote the songs with Alan Menken; Ashman died eight months before the film's release, and the film is dedicated to him.

Music

The film's songs include "Belle", "Be Our Guest", "Gaston", "Something There", "The Mob Song", and the title song "Beauty and the Beast", performed in the film by Angela Lansbury and covered as a pop duet by Celine Dion and Peabo Bryson over the end credits. "Beauty and the Beast" won the Academy Award for Best Original Song, and the score won Best Original Score.

Release and reception

Beauty and the Beast premiered at the New York Film Festival on September 29, 1991, in an unfinished "work-in-progress" version, and was released theatrically on November 22, 1991. It was a major critical and commercial success, grossing over $331 million worldwide in its initial run and more than $440 million including reissues.

The film was the first animated film ever nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture, and remained the only one until the category was expanded in 2009. It won two Academy Awards and three Golden Globe Awards, including Best Picture – Musical or Comedy. In 2002, it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry by the Library of Congress as being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant".

Legacy

The film's success spawned a franchise including three direct-to-video sequels — Beauty and the Beast: The Enchanted Christmas (1997), Beauty and the Beast: Belle's Magical World (1998) and Belle's Tales of Friendship (1999) — a Broadway musical adaptation that ran from 1994 to 2007, and a live-action remake, Beauty and the Beast, released in 2017. The film is widely credited as a high point of the Disney Renaissance.
