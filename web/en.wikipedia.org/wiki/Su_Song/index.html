Su Song

Born1020, Tong'an, Fujian
Died1101
OccupationStatesman, polymath, engineer, astronomer
Known forKaifeng astronomical clock tower

Su Song (Chinese: 蘇頌; 1020–1101), courtesy name Zirong, was a Chinese polymath, statesman, and scholar-bureaucrat of the Song dynasty. His fields of work included astronomy, cartography, horology, pharmacology, mineralogy, zoology, botany, mechanical engineering, and civil service administration.

The clock tower

Su Song is best known for the astronomical clock tower he designed and had built in Kaifeng, the capital of Northern Song China, completed in 1092 (some accounts say 1094). The tower stood about 12 metres (39 ft) tall and was powered by a water wheel with an early escapement mechanism — using a chain drive (the "celestial ladder") that Su Song is credited with inventing. On top sat a rotating armillary sphere; inside, a celestial globe and mechanical mannequins struck bells, gongs, and drums to announce the time. Su documented the design in his treatise Xin Yi Xiang Fa Yao (New Design for an Armillary Sphere and Celestial Globe, 1092), which contains some of the earliest mechanical engineering drawings in Chinese history.

Other work

Su Song also produced a large celestial atlas of star maps — among the oldest printed star charts in existence — and a major pharmaceutical treatise, the Bencao Tujing (Illustrated Pharmacopoeia, 1061), with systematic descriptions of plants, animals, and minerals. His terrestrial maps, engraved on stone in 1137, display a grid scale and an accurate depiction of the Chinese coastline.

The Kaifeng clock tower was dismantled when the Jurchen Jin dynasty captured the city in 1127, and although an attempt was made to rebuild it in Beijing, it was never successfully reconstructed.
