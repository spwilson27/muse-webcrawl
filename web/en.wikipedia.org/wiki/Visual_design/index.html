Visual design

Visual design works with typography, colour, space and imagery

Visual design is the use of imagery, colour, form, typography and layout to enhance the usability of a product and improve its user's experience. Originating in graphic design and industrial design, the discipline is today most often associated with digital products — websites, applications and interfaces — where it forms the aesthetic and communicative layer of user experience (UX) design.

Elements and principles

Visual designers manipulate the classic elements of design — line, shape, colour, value, texture, typography and space — according to principles such as balance, contrast, emphasis, rhythm, proportion and unity. Gestalt principles of perception (proximity, similarity, closure, continuation and figure–ground) explain how viewers group elements and guide layout decisions. Consistent visual systems, expressed through style guides and design systems, keep large products coherent.

In digital products

Within software teams, visual design overlaps with user interface (UI) design: it determines the look and feel of screens, controls and icons, and the visual hierarchy that directs attention. It is distinct from interaction design (how controls behave) and from information architecture (how content is structured), though in practice one practitioner may cover several of these roles. Evaluation methods include aesthetic–usability research, which has found that users perceive attractive interfaces as more usable, as well as accessibility standards governing colour contrast and legibility.

History

The field descends from print graphic design, Swiss typography and the Bauhaus, was reshaped by desktop publishing in the 1980s and by the web in the 1990s, and matured with mobile interfaces, flat design and system-wide design languages such as Google's Material Design and Apple's Human Interface Guidelines.
