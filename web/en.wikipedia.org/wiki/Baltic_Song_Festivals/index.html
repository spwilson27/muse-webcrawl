Baltic Song Festivals

A massed choir at a Baltic song festival

The Baltic Song Festivals are massive choral celebrations held in Estonia, Latvia and Lithuania, among the largest amateur song and dance events in the world. They are recognised by UNESCO on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity (inscribed 2008, following a 2003 proclamation).

History

The tradition began during the 19th-century national awakenings: the first Estonian Song Festival was held in Tartu in 1869, the first Latvian Song Festival in Riga in 1873, and the first Lithuanian Song Day in Kaunas in 1924. Under Soviet rule the festivals were retained but ideologically controlled, yet they kept national culture alive; in the late 1980s mass singing at the festivals became the emblem of the "Singing Revolution" that helped restore Baltic independence in 1991. Modern editions — Estonia's Laulupidu, Latvia's Vispārējie latviešu Dziesmu un Deju svētki, and Lithuania's Dainų šventė — take place roughly every five years, drawing tens of thousands of singers and dancers in national dress, with massed choirs of 20,000–30,000 performers singing to audiences of 100,000 or more from purpose-built song festival stages and grounds.
