Olivier Messiaen

Olivier Eugène Prosper Charles Messiaen (10 December 1908 – 27 April 1992) was a French composer, organist, and ornithologist, one of the major composers of the 20th century. His music is rhythmically complex (he was interested in rhythms from ancient Greek and Hindu sources); harmonically and melodically it often uses modes of limited transposition, a technique he described in his treatise Technique de mon langage musical (1944).

Life and work

Messiaen entered the Paris Conservatoire at age eleven and in 1931 became organist of the Église de la Sainte-Trinité in Paris, a post he held for 61 years. While a prisoner of war in 1940–41, he composed the Quatuor pour la fin du temps (Quartet for the End of Time), premiered in Stalag VIII-A camp in Görlitz. After the war he taught at the Paris Conservatoire, where his analysis class influenced Pierre Boulez, Karlheinz Stockhausen and others.

Birdsong and faith

A devout Catholic, Messiaen wrote much music on religious themes, including the opera Saint François d'Assise and the vast orchestral work Turangalîla-Symphonie. He was also a serious ornithologist and considered birds the greatest musicians: he notated birdsong in the field and incorporated it into works such as Réveil des oiseaux, Oiseaux exotiques and the seven-volume Catalogue d'oiseaux for piano. He experienced synaesthesia, perceiving colours when he heard music, and sought to express dazzling colour in sound.
