Visual field test


Printout of a Humphrey visual field test

A visual field test is an eye examination that can detect dysfunction in central and peripheral vision which may be caused by various medical conditions such as glaucoma, stroke, pituitary disease, brain tumours or other neurological defects. The test maps the extent of what a person can see while looking straight ahead.

Methods

In confrontation testing, the examiner compares the patient's field with their own by presenting fingers or objects in each quadrant. Automated perimeters such as the Humphrey Field Analyzer present light stimuli of varying brightness at fixed positions, measuring sensitivity across the central 24 or 30 degrees. Goldmann perimetry moves a stimulus of fixed brightness to chart isopters kinetically.

Patterns of loss

Characteristic defects include the arcuate scotoma and nasal step of glaucoma, bitemporal hemianopia from lesions at the optic chiasm, and homonymous hemianopia from retrochiasmal damage in stroke.

Clinical use

Serial visual field testing is central to diagnosing and monitoring glaucoma progression and is also required in many jurisdictions for fitness-to-drive assessments.
