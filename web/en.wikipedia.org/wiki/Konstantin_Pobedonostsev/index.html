Konstantin Pobedonostsev

Konstantin Petrovich Pobedonostsev (Russian: Константин Петрович Победоносцев; 2 June 1827 – 23 March 1907) was a Russian jurist, statesman and adviser to three emperors. As Ober-Procurator of the Holy Synod from 1880 to 1905 he was the effective head of the Russian Orthodox Church, and as tutor and confidant of Alexander III and Nicholas II he became the leading ideologist of late-imperial autocracy and conservatism.

Career

Pobedonostsev was a professor of civil law at Moscow University and a respected legal scholar who took part in the judicial reforms of Alexander II. He tutored the future Alexander III in law and, after the assassination of Alexander II in 1881, drafted the manifesto reaffirming unshaken autocracy, helping to set aside the Loris-Melikov reform proposals. Under Alexander III he was a dominant figure in government, promoting Russification, Orthodox education through parish schools, strict censorship, and restrictions on religious minorities and on Jews.

Ideas

In his Moskovskii Sbornik (1896; translated as Reflections of a Russian Statesman) Pobedonostsev denounced parliamentary democracy, universal suffrage, trial by jury and a free press as dangerous falsehoods, and argued that Russia must be ruled by an autocrat bound to Orthodoxy and the people — a stance summarised in his description of parliamentarianism as "the great falsehood of our time". His influence waned after 1900, and he resigned in 1905 amid the revolutionary upheaval he had long predicted and feared.
