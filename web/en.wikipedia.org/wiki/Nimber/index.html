Nimber

In mathematics, the nimbers, also called Grundy numbers, are introduced in combinatorial game theory, where they are defined as the values of heaps in the game of Nim. The nimbers are the ordinal numbers endowed with nimber addition and nimber multiplication, which differ from ordinary ordinal addition and multiplication.

Definition

By the Sprague–Grundy theorem, every impartial game is equivalent to a Nim heap of a certain size, so the value of a position can be identified with a nimber. The nimber corresponding to a position is computed by the "mex" rule: the value of a position is the minimum excluded value (the smallest non-negative integer not among the values of the options). Nimber addition coincides with the bitwise exclusive or (XOR) of the ordinary binary representations, which is the rule used to solve Nim itself.

Properties

Nimber multiplication is defined recursively using the mex rule on products of smaller nimbers. Taken together, the nimbers form an algebraically closed field of characteristic 2; the finite nimbers below 22n form finite fields. The concept was developed in the work of Roland Sprague, Patrick Grundy, and later John Horton Conway in On Numbers and Games, where nimbers appear as a special case of surreal numbers.

Applications

Nimbers give a uniform method for analysing impartial combinatorial games such as Green Hackenbush, Treblecross, and turning-turtles games, reducing each position to a single number whose combination under the disjunctive sum is given by nimber addition.
