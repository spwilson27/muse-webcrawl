Haraam

Haraam (Arabic: حرام, ḥarām) is an Arabic term meaning "forbidden" or "prohibited" under Islamic law (Sharia). It describes acts, foods and behaviours that are prohibited by the Quran and the teachings of the Prophet Muhammad. Its opposite is halal (permissible).

Categories

Actions in Islamic jurisprudence are typically divided into five categories (al-aḥkām al-khamsa): obligatory (farḍ), recommended (mustaḥabb), neutral (mubāḥ), disliked (makrūh) and forbidden (ḥarām).

Examples

Commonly cited haram items include the consumption of pork and alcohol, gambling, usury (riba), and carrion or meat not slaughtered according to ritual requirements. The degree of emphasis varies among schools of Islamic law (madhhab).
