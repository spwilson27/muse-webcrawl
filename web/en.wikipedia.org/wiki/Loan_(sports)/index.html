Loan (sports)

In sports, a loan involves a particular player being able to temporarily play for a club other than the one to which they are currently contracted. Loan deals may last from a few weeks to a full season, sometimes persisting for multiple seasons at a time. A loan fee can be arranged by the club together with or in lieu of compensation such as wage cover.

Association football

Players may be loaned out to other clubs for several reasons. Most commonly, young prospects are loaned to clubs in lower divisions to gain first-team experience. The parent club may fully or partially pay the player's wages during the loan, or the borrowing club may contribute to the salary or pay a loan fee. Some clubs operate formal "farm" or feeder-club relationships, while in England the emergency loan system historically allowed Football League clubs to bring in players outside the transfer windows, a practice curtailed after 2016 by FIFA standardisation of transfer windows.

Loans may include an option to buy — a clause allowing the borrowing club to sign the player permanently for a pre-agreed fee — or an obligation to buy triggered by conditions such as appearances. Regulations in many leagues restrict the number of loanees a club may field, prevent a club from stockpiling borrowed players, and typically bar a loaned player from playing against his parent club (the "ineligibility" or "parent club" clause, standard in the English Premier League).

Reasons and criticism

  • Development of young players through regular competitive football
  • Reducing the wage bill of the parent club
  • Short-term cover for injuries or suspensions
  • Placing a player in the shop window ahead of a permanent sale

The loan system has been criticised where wealthy clubs accumulate dozens of young players — a "loan army" — with little intention of using them in their own first team. FIFA reforms phased in from 2022 limit the number of international loans of players aged 22 and over that a club may make and receive, in order to curb hoarding.

Other sports

Loans also occur in rugby league and rugby union, ice hockey (where players under contract can be assigned between affiliated clubs), and cricket, where counties may borrow players for short periods under ECB regulations.
