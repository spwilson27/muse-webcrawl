Two Generals' Problem

The Two Generals' Problem is a classic thought experiment in computer science and distributed systems illustrating the impossibility of reaching perfect agreement over an unreliable communication channel. It was formulated by E. A. Akkoyunlu and colleagues in 1975 and popularised in the context of computer networking.

Problem statement

Two generals command armies on opposite sides of an enemy camp. They can win only if they attack simultaneously, and they can communicate only by messengers who must cross the enemy camp and may be captured. General A sends a message proposing an attack time; but A cannot know whether it arrived. General B, even if he receives it and sends an acknowledgement, cannot know whether the acknowledgement arrived — and so on, in an infinite regress of confirmations. The problem proves that no finite exchange of messages can guarantee common knowledge of the plan.

Implications

The impossibility result underlies the design of communication protocols: systems such as TCP settle for practical confidence rather than certainty, using handshakes and retransmission. It is closely related to the Byzantine generals problem, which considers agreement in the presence of traitorous participants.
