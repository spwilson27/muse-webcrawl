Shear mapping

In plane geometry, a shear mapping is a linear map that displaces each point in a fixed direction by an amount proportional to its signed distance from a line parallel to that direction. Shearing distorts shapes: a rectangle becomes a parallelogram, and a square becomes a slanted parallelogram, while area is preserved.

Horizontal and vertical shear

A horizontal shear with factor m sends (x, y) to (x + my, y) and is represented by the matrix [[1, m], [0, 1]]. A vertical shear sends (x, y) to (x, y + mx) with matrix [[1, 0], [m, 1]]. Shear matrices have determinant 1, confirming that area (or volume in higher dimensions) is unchanged.

Applications

Shear mappings are used in computer graphics for oblique projections and text effects, in typography for faux italics, and in physics as Galilean transformations, which shear spacetime diagrams by mixing position with time.
