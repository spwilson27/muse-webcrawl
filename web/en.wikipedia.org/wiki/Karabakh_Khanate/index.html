Karabakh Khanate

The Karabakh Khanate was a khanate established in the mid-18th century in the Karabakh region of the South Caucasus, under nominal Persian suzerainty, following the collapse of Afsharid authority after the assassination of Nader Shah in 1747. It existed until its absorption into the Russian Empire in the early 19th century.

History

The khanate was founded by Panah Ali Khan of the Turkic Javanshir tribe, who built the fortress of Shusha, which became the capital. His son Ibrahim Khalil Khan expanded the khanate's territory and influence, manoeuvring between Persia, the Ottoman Empire, and Russia. By the Treaty of Kurakchay (1805), the khanate accepted Russian suzerainty, and after the Russo-Persian wars, the treaties of Gulistan (1813) and Turkmenchay (1828) confirmed Russian control. The khanate was formally abolished in 1822 and its territory incorporated into Russian administrative structures.

Society

The khanate's population was ethnically and religiously mixed, including Muslim Turkic and Kurdish groups, and a substantial Armenian population in the mountainous areas, governed partly through the Armenian melikdoms of Khamsa, which retained a degree of autonomy under the khans.
