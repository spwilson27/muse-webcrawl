Mimar Sinan (c. 1488/1490 – 17 July 1588), known as Sinan the Architect or Koca Mimar Sinan ("Sinan, the Great Architect"), was the chief Ottoman architect and civil engineer for sultans Suleiman the Magnificent, Selim II and Murad III. Over a career of some fifty years he was responsible for the design of more than three hundred major structures — mosques, palaces, bridges, aqueducts, caravanserais, baths and mausoleums — and is regarded as the defining figure of classical Ottoman architecture.

Early life

Sinan was born in the village of Ağırnas near Kayseri in central Anatolia, probably to a Christian family of Armenian or Greek background, and was taken into imperial service through the devşirme system. Trained at the palace school in Constantinople, he joined the Janissary corps and served in campaigns in Persia and the Balkans, gaining experience in military engineering — bridges, fortifications and siegecraft — that informed his later architecture. In 1538 he was appointed chief royal architect (mimarbaşı), a post he held until his death.

Major works

Sinan himself, in the autobiographies he dictated late in life, identified three masterworks marking his progress: the Şehzade Mosque in Istanbul (1548), his "apprenticeship" work; the Süleymaniye Mosque (1557), crowning Istanbul's skyline for Suleiman the Magnificent, his "journeyman" work; and the Selimiye Mosque in Edirne (1575), whose vast dome — slightly wider than that of Hagia Sophia — he called his "masterpiece". His bridges, including the Büyükçekmece Bridge, and aqueducts supplying Istanbul were engineering landmarks of the empire.

Legacy

Sinan's architecture fused Byzantine precedents, especially Hagia Sophia, with Ottoman requirements into a coherent classical style of domed, light-filled prayer halls buttressed with great elegance. He trained a school of architects; his pupil Sedefkâr Mehmed Ağa later built the Blue Mosque. Sinan is buried in a tomb of his own design near the Süleymaniye Mosque, and a crater on Mercury is named in his honour.
