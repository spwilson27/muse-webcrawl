Cretan music

The music of Crete (Greek: Κρητική μουσική), also called kritika, is a traditional form of Greek folk music prevalent on the island of Crete. It remains one of the most vibrant and recognisable regional traditions of Greece, centred on the Cretan lyra, a three-stringed bowed instrument, accompanied by the laouto (Cretan lute).

Instruments and dance

The lyra is held upright on the knee and played with a bow; its pear-shaped body and ringing tone define the sound of the island's music. Older ensembles used the violin, which remains common in eastern Crete, and instruments such as the askomandoura (a type of bagpipe) and the daouli drum. Cretan dances include the fast, leaping pentozali, the syrtos, the sousta and the maleviziotis, often danced in a circle with the lead dancer improvising athletic steps.

Mantinades

Singing in Crete is closely tied to the mantinada: rhyming fifteen-syllable couplets, improvised or traditional, sung to dance tunes or to the slow, free-rhythm rizitika songs of the mountain villages of western Crete, which are performed a cappella at feasts and commemorations.

History and musicians

Recorded widely from the early twentieth century, Cretan music was shaped by virtuoso lyra players and singers such as Kostas Mountakis, Thanasis Skordalos and Nikos Xilouris, whose recordings carried the tradition across Greece. Village feasts (panigiria) and the Hyakinthia festival in the mountains above Anogeia continue to sustain the tradition, which has also absorbed influences from Greek popular music and world music in recent decades.
