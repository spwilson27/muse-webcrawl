Protected areas or conservation areas are locations that receive protection because of their recognized natural, ecological or cultural values. Protected areas are those in which human presence or the exploitation of natural resources (such as firewood, non-timber forest products, water, etc.) is limited. The term encompasses marine protected areas and transboundary protected areas that span multiple countries.

Protected areas are essential for biodiversity conservation, often providing habitat and protection from hunting for threatened and endangered species. According to the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN), a protected area is "a clearly defined geographical space, recognised, dedicated and managed, through legal or other effective means, to achieve the long term conservation of nature with associated ecosystem services and cultural values."

IUCN categories

The IUCN classifies protected areas into six management categories:

  • Ia — Strict nature reserve: set aside to protect biodiversity, with human visitation strictly controlled.
  • Ib — Wilderness area: large unmodified areas retaining their natural character.
  • II — National park: large areas protecting ecological processes, with opportunities for recreation and education.
  • III — Natural monument or feature: areas protecting a specific natural monument.
  • IV — Habitat/species management area: areas managed to protect particular species or habitats.
  • V — Protected landscape/seascape: areas where the interaction of people and nature has produced a distinct character.
  • VI — Protected area with sustainable use of natural resources: large areas managed mainly for conservation with low-level, non-industrial use of resources.

History

Sacred groves and royal hunting reserves are ancient precursors of modern protected areas. Bogd Khan Uul in Mongolia, protected since 1778, is sometimes called the oldest protected area. The modern movement began in the nineteenth century with the establishment of reserves such as Yosemite (1864) and Yellowstone National Park (1872), the world's first national park. The IUCN was founded in 1948, and its World Commission on Protected Areas now maintains the World Database on Protected Areas.

Coverage and effectiveness

Under the 2011–2020 Aichi Biodiversity Targets, governments aimed to protect 17% of terrestrial and inland waters and 10% of marine areas by 2020; the Kunming–Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework of 2022 set a target of effectively conserving 30% of land and sea by 2030 ("30 by 30"). Research shows well-managed protected areas reduce habitat loss, though many suffer from underfunding and "paper park" status, in which legal protection exists without effective management.

Criticism

Protected areas have been criticized for displacing Indigenous peoples and local communities under the "fortress conservation" model. Modern approaches increasingly emphasize community-based conservation, Indigenous protected areas, and recognition of customary land tenure.
