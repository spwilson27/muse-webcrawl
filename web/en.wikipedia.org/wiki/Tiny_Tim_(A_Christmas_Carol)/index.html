Tiny Tim (A Christmas Carol)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Timothy "Tiny Tim" Cratchit is a fictional character from Charles Dickens's 1843 novella A Christmas Carol. The youngest son of Bob Cratchit, Ebenezer Scrooge's underpaid clerk, Tiny Tim is a frail, disabled child who walks with a crutch and wears iron braces, yet remains cheerful and kind-hearted.

Role in the story

Tiny Tim's gentle nature and his famous blessing, "God bless us, every one!", embody the novella's themes of charity and compassion. When the Ghost of Christmas Present shows Scrooge the Cratchit family's modest Christmas dinner, the boy's frailty moves him. The Ghost of Christmas Yet to Come reveals a possible future in which Tiny Tim has died — a vision that helps prompt Scrooge's moral transformation. After his change of heart, Scrooge raises Cratchit's salary and pays for medical care; the narrator reports that Tim did not die, and that Scrooge became "a second father" to him.

Interpretation

Scholars have debated the nature of Tim's illness, with suggestions including rickets, tuberculosis and renal tubular acidosis — conditions that were treatable or preventable but beyond a poor family's means, reinforcing Dickens's social criticism. Tiny Tim has become one of literature's most enduring symbols of the innocent child whose fate depends on the generosity of others, and the character has appeared in virtually every stage, film and television adaptation of the story.
