Politics of Cuba

Polity type: Unitary one-party socialist republic

Constitution: Constitution of Cuba (2019)

Head of state: President of Cuba

Head of government: Prime Minister of Cuba

The politics of Cuba take place within the framework of a unitary, one-party socialist republic in which the Communist Party of Cuba (PCC) is constitutionally recognised as the leading force of society and the state. Cuba has been governed by the PCC since the Cuban Revolution of 1959.

Constitutional framework

The current constitution was adopted in 2019, replacing the 1976 constitution. It reaffirmed the socialist character of the state and the leading role of the Communist Party, while also recognising private property in certain forms, introducing limits on presidential terms, and creating the office of Prime Minister separate from the President of the Republic.

Executive branch

The President of Cuba is head of state, elected by the National Assembly for a five-year term, renewable once. The President presides over the Council of State. The Prime Minister, appointed by the National Assembly on the proposal of the President, heads the Council of Ministers, which exercises executive and administrative functions. Miguel Díaz-Canel became president in 2018, succeeding Raúl Castro, and First Secretary of the Communist Party in 2021.

Legislature

Legislative power is vested in the National Assembly of People's Power, a unicameral body whose deputies are elected for five-year terms. The Assembly elects the Council of State from among its members to act on its behalf between sessions.

Elections

Deputies to the National Assembly and delegates to municipal assemblies are elected by direct vote. Candidates for the National Assembly are nominated by mass organisations and candidacy commissions rather than by the Communist Party directly, although the party's role in the process is significant. Municipal elections are competitive among multiple candidates; national elections have historically presented a single slate of candidates equal in number to the seats available.
