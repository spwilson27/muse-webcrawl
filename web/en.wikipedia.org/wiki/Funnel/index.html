Funnel

A funnel is a tube or pipe that is wide at the top and narrow at the bottom, used for guiding liquid or powder into a small opening. Funnels are usually made of stainless steel, aluminium, glass, or plastic. The material used in its construction should be sturdy enough to withstand the weight of the substance being transferred, and it should not react with the substance.

Construction

The typical funnel shape is a cone atop a narrow stem. Some funnels are constructed with a flexible tube or a mechanism that alters the shape, while others include filters, valves, or ribbed surfaces to speed the flow of liquid and reduce splashing.

Laboratory funnels

Chemical laboratories use several funnel types adapted to particular tasks: the Büchner funnel for vacuum filtration, the separatory funnel for separating immiscible liquids, the dropping funnel for the controlled addition of reagents, and the powder funnel with a wide stem. Filter paper is commonly folded into a cone and placed in a plain funnel for gravity filtration.

Other uses

Funnels are used in kitchens to decant liquids and fill jars, in automotive work to add oil and fuel, and in industry for charging hoppers. Large industrial funnels are termed hoppers. The word also serves as a metaphor in concepts such as the sales funnel and the funnel cloud of a developing tornado.
