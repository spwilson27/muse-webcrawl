The Suzuka International Racing Course (鈴鹿国際レーシングコース, Suzuka Kokusai Rēsingu Kōsu), commonly known as the Suzuka Circuit, is a 5.807 km (3.608 mi) motorsport race track located in Ino, Suzuka City, Mie Prefecture, Japan, and operated by Honda Mobilityland, a subsidiary of Honda Motor Co, Ltd. It has a capacity of 155,000 and is best known as the home of the Japanese Grand Prix in Formula One.

Design

The circuit was designed by Dutchman John "Hans" Hugenholtz and built in 1962 as a Honda test track. It is unusual among major circuits in having a figure-eight layout, in which the back straight crosses over the front section by means of a bridge. Famous corners include the S Curves, the Degner Curves, Spoon Curve, and the fast, sweeping 130R.

History

Suzuka first hosted the Japanese Grand Prix in 1987 and became the traditional season finale of Formula One in the late 1980s and 1990s, deciding several world championships there, including the famous collisions between Ayrton Senna and Alain Prost in 1989 and 1990. The event moved to Fuji Speedway for 2007–2008 before returning to Suzuka. The circuit also hosts the Suzuka 8 Hours motorcycle endurance race and rounds of Super GT and Super Formula.

Safety

The circuit was modified several times to improve safety, notably after the fatal accident of MotoGP rider Daijiro Kato in 2003, and the Casio Triangle chicane was reprofiled before the 2003 Formula One race.
