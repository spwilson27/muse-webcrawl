Battle of Lechfeld

The Battle of Lechfeld, also known as the Battle of Augsburg, was fought on 10 August 955 on the plain of the Lechfeld south of Augsburg in Bavaria between the East Frankish (German) kingdom under King Otto I and a Magyar (Hungarian) raiding army. Otto's victory ended half a century of Magyar incursions into western Europe and is regarded as a foundational moment in the formation of the German kingdom.

Background

Since their settlement in the Carpathian basin around 895, Magyar horse archers had raided as far as Italy, France and Spain, defeating East Frankish armies, notably at Pressburg (907). Otto I had already beaten them at Riade in 933, but in 954–55, exploiting a rebellion against Otto, the Magyars returned in force, ravaged Bavaria and besieged Augsburg, ably defended by Bishop Ulrich. Otto, his rebellion suppressed and his dukes reconciled, marched to relieve the city with an army drawn from nearly all the German stem duchies — a rare display of unity.

The battle

On 10 August 955, the feast of St. Lawrence (to whom Otto attributed the victory), the armies met on the Lechfeld. During the fighting a Magyar detachment crossed the Lech and fell on the German baggage and the Bohemian rearguard; Conrad the Red recovered the situation before being killed by an arrow in the throat. In the main engagement Otto's armoured lancers charged in close order through a storm of arrows — the accounts describe rain and the Magyars' traditional feigned flight failing on the confined ground between river and woods — and broke the Magyar army. The retreat became a slaughter at the rain-swollen Lech crossings; the captured chiefs Bulcsú and Lél were taken to Regensburg and hanged.

Aftermath

Lechfeld ended Magyar raiding against the West for good; Hungary subsequently consolidated as a settled Christian kingdom under Géza and Stephen I. In German memory — as reported by Widukind of Corvey — Otto's army hailed him on the field as imperator, and the victory underwrote his imperial coronation in Rome in 962, founding the Ottonian Holy Roman Empire.
