Melon de Bourgogne (also known simply as Melon) is a white grape variety used most famously to make the light, dry white wines of Muscadet in France's Loire Valley, near the city of Nantes.

History

As its name suggests, the grape originated in Burgundy, where it was once widely planted. In the eighteenth century Dutch wine merchants encouraged its planting around Nantes for distillation and dry white wine production. After a severe frost in 1709 devastated the region's vineyards, the cold-hardy Melon was extensively replanted and came to dominate the Pays Nantais.

Wines

Wines made from Melon de Bourgogne are typically light-bodied, high in acidity, and subtly flavoured, often aged sur lie (on the lees) to add texture. They are classic accompaniments to seafood. The grape is also grown in Oregon, where it is labelled simply as Melon.
