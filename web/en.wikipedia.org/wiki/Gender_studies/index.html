Gender studies

[Image placeholder: Gender studies classroom]

Gender studies is an interdisciplinary academic field devoted to analysing gender identity and gendered representation using the tools of the humanities and social sciences. It examines how gender intersects with class, race, ethnicity, sexuality, and location, and draws on literary theory, history, political science, sociology, anthropology, psychology, media studies, and law.

History

The field grew out of women's studies programmes founded in the late 1960s and 1970s (the first women's studies programme was established at San Diego State University in 1970) and broadened in the 1980s–90s to include masculinity studies and queer theory. Foundational texts include Simone de Beauvoir's The Second Sex (1949), Kate Millett's Sexual Politics (1970), and Judith Butler's Gender Trouble (1990), which introduced the idea of gender performativity. Kimberlé Crenshaw's concept of intersectionality (1989) became a central framework.

Criticism and politics

Gender studies has been a flashpoint in political debates about academia. Critics — from some academics, religious groups, and governments — have challenged its methods and premises; in 2018 Hungary removed accreditation from gender studies programmes, and similar restrictions have been debated elsewhere. Defenders respond that the field applies ordinary scholarly methods to questions of social organisation and that its findings inform public policy on health, education, and inequality.
