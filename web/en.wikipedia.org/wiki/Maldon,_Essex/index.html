Maldon (/ˈmɔːldən/) is a town and civil parish on the Blackwater Estuary in Essex, England. It is the seat of the Maldon District and a historic port known for its Thames sailing barges and for Maldon Sea Salt, harvested from the local salt marshes since Roman times.

History

Maldon was a Saxon stronghold and the site of the Battle of Maldon in 991, in which the Anglo-Saxon ealdorman Byrhtnoth was killed resisting a Viking invasion; the battle is commemorated in the Old English poem The Battle of Maldon and by a statue of Byrhtnoth on the Promenade. The town received a royal charter in the 12th century and prospered as a port. The Hythe was its historic quay, from which sailing barges traded along the east coast and to London.

Landmarks and culture

Landmarks include St Mary's Church, with its unusual triangular tower, the Moot Hall, and Plume Library, founded in 1704. Promenade Park, opened in 1895, hosts the annual Maldon Mud Race, a charity event in which competitors race across the mud of the River Blackwater at low tide. The Maldon district gives its name to a parliamentary constituency.
