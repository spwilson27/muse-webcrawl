Best linear unbiased estimator

[Image placeholder: BLUE estimator comparison diagram]

In statistics, a best linear unbiased estimator (BLUE) is an estimator that is linear in the observations, unbiased, and has the smallest variance among all linear unbiased estimators of the parameters of a linear model. The concept dates to work by Carl Friedrich Gauss and Andrey Markov.

Under the assumptions of the Gauss–Markov theorem — linearity, random sampling, no perfect multicollinearity, and spherical errors (homoscedasticity and no autocorrelation) — the ordinary least squares (OLS) estimator is BLUE. If errors are heteroscedastic or correlated, generalized least squares (GLS) provides the BLUE instead.

Definition

For the model y = Xβ + ε, an estimator β̃ = Cy is linear; it is unbiased if E[β̃] = β for all β, and "best" if Var(β̃) is minimal in the positive-semidefinite ordering among all such C. The Gauss–Markov theorem shows OLS attains this bound under spherical errors without requiring normally distributed disturbances.
