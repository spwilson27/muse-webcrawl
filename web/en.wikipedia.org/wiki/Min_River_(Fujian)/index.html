Min River (Fujian)

Min River
Chinese: 闽江 (Mǐnjiāng)
Length: 577 km
Basin: Fujian Province, China
Mouth: East China Sea near Fuzhou

The Min River (Chinese: 闽江; pinyin: Mǐnjiāng) is the largest river in Fujian Province in southeastern China. It flows for about 577 km (359 miles) from the mountains of northwestern Fujian southeastward to the East China Sea (Taiwan Strait) near the provincial capital, Fuzhou.

Course

The Min is formed by three main tributaries — the Jianxi, Futunxi and Shaxi — which converge near the city of Nanping. From there the river cuts through gorges in the coastal ranges before reaching the Fuzhou plain. Its drainage basin, roughly 60,000 km², covers about half of Fujian, and the river traditionally provided the chief artery of transport between the coast and the province's mountainous interior.

Significance

The Min gives Fujian its one-character abbreviation, Mǐn (闽). Navigation, hydroelectric dams (such as the Shuikou dam), sand extraction and seasonal flooding are enduring issues on the river; the lower reaches around Fuzhou are lined with the Mawei port district, historic centre of China's 19th-century naval shipyard.
