History of Paraguay

The history of Paraguay covers the Guaraní peoples, Spanish colonisation from 1537, the famous Jesuit reductions, independence in 1811, the isolationist dictatorship of José Gaspar Rodríguez de Francia, the catastrophic War of the Triple Alliance (1864–1870), the Chaco War with Bolivia, and the long dictatorship of Alfredo Stroessner (1954–1989).

Landlocked Paraguay lies between Argentina, Bolivia and Brazil.

Colonial period

Asunción was founded in 1537 and became a base for Spanish expeditions across the Río de la Plata region. From 1609 the Jesuits established mission towns (reducciones) among the Guaraní, shielding them from slave-raiders until the order's expulsion in 1767. Unlike most of Spanish America, Paraguay retained a strong Guaraní cultural and linguistic identity; Guaraní remains an official language spoken by most of the population.

Independence and Francia

Paraguay declared independence from Spain in May 1811. From 1814 until his death in 1840, José Gaspar Rodríguez de Francia ruled as dictator ("El Supremo"), isolating the country, breaking the power of the church and the Spanish elite, and pursuing economic self-sufficiency. His successor Carlos Antonio López (1844–1862) opened trade and modernised the army and infrastructure.

War of the Triple Alliance

Francisco Solano López took Paraguay into war against Brazil, Argentina and Uruguay in 1864. The War of the Triple Alliance ended in 1870 with Paraguay devastated: it lost territory and a large share of its population — estimates of deaths range widely, from around 200,000 to well over half the pre-war population. López died at Cerro Corá and the country was occupied until 1876.

20th century

Politics oscillated between Liberal and Colorado party governments. Paraguay defeated Bolivia in the Chaco War (1932–1935), gaining most of the disputed Gran Chaco. General Alfredo Stroessner seized power in 1954 and ruled for 35 years under the Colorado Party, a period of repression and of mega-projects such as the Itaipú Dam. He was overthrown in 1989 by Andrés Rodríguez. The first free presidential election was held in 1993. Fernando Lugo's election in 2008 ended 61 years of Colorado rule, though the party later returned to power.
