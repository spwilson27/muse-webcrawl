The Argentine National Observatory (Spanish: Observatorio Nacional Argentino), now the Astronomical Observatory of Córdoba, is an astronomical observatory founded in Córdoba, Argentina, in 1871 under President Domingo Faustino Sarmiento.

History

Sarmiento recruited the American astronomer Benjamin Gould as its first director. Gould and his assistants produced the Uranometría Argentina (1879), a catalogue and atlas of some 7,000 southern stars visible to the naked eye, and later the Córdoba Durchmusterung, a photographic and visual catalogue of hundreds of thousands of southern stars. The observatory also founded the Argentine national weather service in 1872.

Legacy

The observatory is now part of the National University of Córdoba and houses a museum of its historic instruments.
