Timeboxing

In time management, timeboxing allocates a fixed time period, called a timebox, within which planned activity takes place. It is used by several project management approaches, including agile project management, and for personal time management.

Principles

The essential characteristic of timeboxing is that the deadline is fixed and immovable: when the timebox expires, work on the activity stops and whatever has been accomplished is assessed. This contrasts with scope-driven planning, where the feature set is fixed and the time varies. Timeboxing motivates focusing on the most important content of a task.

In software development

Timeboxing is a core practice of agile frameworks such as Scrum, where iterations (sprints) are fixed-length timeboxes, and of methodologies such as DSDM, where timeboxes structure the whole project. The Pomodoro Technique applies short 25-minute timeboxes to individual work.

Benefits and criticism

Supporters argue that timeboxing combats Parkinson's law, reduces procrastination, and gives stakeholders predictable delivery rhythms. Critics note that rigid timeboxes can pressure teams into cutting quality or underestimating complex work.
