Irish stepdance

Irish stepdance is a style of performance dance originating in Ireland, characterised by a stiff upper body and quick, precise movements of the feet. It is performed solo or in groups (céilí dances) to traditional Irish music such as reels, jigs and hornpipes, and was popularised worldwide by stage shows such as Riverdance (1995) and Lord of the Dance.

Styles and footwear

Dancers perform in two kinds of shoe. Soft shoes (ghillies for women, reel shoes for men) are used for light dances — reels, light jigs, slip jigs and single jigs. Hard shoes, fitted with fibreglass tips and heels, produce percussive rhythms in heavy dances such as the heavy jig and hornpipe and in traditional set dances.

History and organisation

The form descends from the teaching of travelling dancing masters in 18th- and 19th-century Ireland, who codified technique and etiquette. In the 20th century the tradition was formalised by An Coimisiún Le Rincí Gaelacha (the Irish Dancing Commission, founded 1927), which certifies teachers and adjudicators and runs competitions (feiseanna) culminating in the World Championships, Oireachtas Rince na Cruinne, first held in 1970.

Costume

Competition costume is elaborate: solo dresses for girls are heavily embroidered with Celtic designs and worn with curled wigs, while boys wear tailored jackets or plain shirts with waistcoats. Rising costs and the pageantry of dress have attracted both devotion and criticism.
