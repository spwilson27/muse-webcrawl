The 1982 Lebanon War, called Operation Peace for Galilee by Israel, began on 6 June 1982 when the Israel Defense Forces invaded southern Lebanon. Israel's stated aim was to drive the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) away from its northern border, but the offensive expanded into a drive on Beirut, a confrontation with Syrian forces in the Bekaa Valley, and an attempt to reshape Lebanon's politics.

Background

Since its expulsion from Jordan in 1970–71, the PLO had built a state-within-a-state in southern Lebanon, launching cross-border attacks and shelling northern Israeli settlements; Israel had responded with raids, including the 1978 Litani Operation. In June 1982, the attempted assassination of Israel's ambassador to London, Shlomo Argov, by the Abu Nidal Organization provided the immediate casus belli. Prime Minister Menachem Begin's government, with Ariel Sharon as defence minister, authorised a broad invasion despite cabinet discussion of a more limited 40-kilometre incursion.

Course of the war

Israeli forces advanced rapidly up the coast, defeating PLO units and destroying much of Syria's surface-to-air missile network and air force in the Bekaa Valley in some of the largest jet battles since Korea. By mid-June the IDF had laid siege to West Beirut, subjecting PLO strongholds to weeks of bombardment. Under a US-brokered agreement negotiated by Philip Habib, PLO fighters evacuated Beirut by sea in late August 1982 under multinational supervision, and Yasser Arafat relocated his headquarters to Tunis.

Sabra and Shatila and aftermath

Following the assassination of President-elect Bashir Gemayel in September 1982, Israeli forces entered West Beirut, and Lebanese Phalangist militiamen massacred Palestinian and Lebanese civilians in the Sabra and Shatila refugee camps. Israel's Kahan Commission found Sharon bore personal responsibility, forcing his resignation as defence minister. Israeli troops withdrew in stages but maintained a "security zone" in southern Lebanon until 2000. The war reshaped the region: it midwifed the rise of Hezbollah, deepened Syrian influence in Lebanon, and cost Israel some 650 soldiers killed in the 1982 campaign and occupation's first phase, alongside many thousands of Lebanese and Palestinian dead.
