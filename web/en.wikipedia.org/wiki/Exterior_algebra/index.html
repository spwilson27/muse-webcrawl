Exterior algebra

The wedge product of two vectors represents an oriented area.

In mathematics, the exterior algebra (or Grassmann algebra, after Hermann Grassmann) of a vector space V is an algebra generated by a bilinear product, the exterior product or wedge product (written ∧), which is alternating: v ∧ v = 0 for every vector v, and consequently u ∧ v = −v ∧ u.

Elements of the exterior algebra are called multivectors. The wedge product of k vectors is a k-vector, which geometrically represents an oriented k-dimensional volume element: the wedge of two vectors represents the oriented area spanned by them (as a parallelogram), generalising the cross product of three-dimensional space.

Structure

The exterior algebra Λ(V) decomposes as a direct sum of graded pieces Λ0(V) (scalars), Λ1(V) (vectors), Λ2(V) (bivectors), and so on up to Λn(V), where n = dim V. If V has dimension n, the whole algebra has dimension 2n. It is characterised by a universal property: any alternating multilinear map out of V factors uniquely through the exterior algebra.

Applications

Exterior algebra is the algebraic home of differential forms: the exterior derivative and integration of forms on manifolds are built on it. It is also used in algebraic geometry, representation theory, and physics, where fermionic creation operators anticommute in the same way wedge products do.
