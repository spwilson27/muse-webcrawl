Unitary operator

In functional analysis, a unitary operator is a surjective bounded linear operator U : H → H on a Hilbert space that preserves the inner product: the equality ⟨Ux, Uy⟩ = ⟨x, y⟩ holds for all vectors x and y in the Hilbert space. Equivalently, U is unitary if its adjoint equals its inverse: U* U = UU* = I.

Unitary operators preserve lengths and angles, like rotations of a space.

Definition and properties

A unitary operator preserves norms, distances, and orthogonality, and it maps an orthonormal basis to an orthonormal basis. In finite dimensions, unitary operators are represented by unitary matrices. The eigenvalues of a unitary operator all lie on the unit circle in the complex plane, i.e. have modulus 1.

Examples

  • The identity operator and multiplication by a complex number of modulus 1.
  • Rotations and reflections (in the real case, orthogonal operators) in finite-dimensional space.
  • The Fourier transform on L2(ℝ) is a unitary operator.
  • Translation and multiplication operators on function spaces, suitably defined.
  • In quantum mechanics, the time-evolution operator is unitary; by Stone's theorem it is generated by a self-adjoint Hamiltonian.

Significance

Unitary operators describe symmetries and reversible dynamics. In quantum theory, Wigner's theorem states that symmetries of a quantum system are implemented by unitary (or antiunitary) operators. The spectral theorem for unitary operators generalizes the fact that unitary matrices are diagonalizable by a unitary basis of eigenvectors; for infinite-dimensional spaces the spectrum may be continuous and is described by a spectral measure on the unit circle.
