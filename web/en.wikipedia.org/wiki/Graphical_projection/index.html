Graphical projection

Common types of graphical projection

Graphical projection is a protocol, used in technical drawing and computer graphics, by which a three-dimensional object is projected onto a planar surface without the aid of mathematical calculation, in the way that is essential to engineering drawing. The projection is achieved by the use of imaginary "projectors"; the projected, mental image becomes the technician's vision and helps to convey information to the manufacturer.

Types

The two broad families are parallel projection, in which the projectors are parallel to one another, and perspective projection, in which they converge at a centre of projection so that objects appear smaller with distance, as in human vision. Parallel projections include the orthographic views used in engineering (front, top and side views, often combined in a multi-view drawing) and the axonometric projections such as isometric, dimetric and trimetric drawings.

Planar geometric projections

In computer graphics, graphical projections are formalised as projection transformations defined by matrices, mapping three-dimensional coordinates to a two-dimensional viewport. Perspective projection in particular underlies all modern 3D rendering pipelines.
