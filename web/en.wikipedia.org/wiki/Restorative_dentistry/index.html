Restorative dentistry

FieldDentistry
TreatmentsFillings, crowns, bridges, implants
GoalRestore form and function of teeth

A dental crown restoration

Restorative dentistry is the branch of dentistry concerned with the diagnosis and integrated management of diseases of the teeth and their supporting structures, and with restoring the dentition to functional and aesthetic requirements.

Treatments

Direct restorations, such as composite resin or amalgam fillings, are placed in a single visit. Indirect restorations — crowns, inlays, onlays, veneers, and bridges — are fabricated in a laboratory or by chair-side CAD/CAM milling and cemented in place. Missing teeth may be replaced with dentures or dental implants.

Scope

Restorative dentistry overlaps with endodontics (root canal treatment), periodontics (gum treatment), and prosthodontics. In the United Kingdom it is a recognised specialty, with consultants typically managing complex rehabilitation of worn, damaged, or missing teeth.
