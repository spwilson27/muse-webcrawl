Road transport in Australia

Road transport in Australia is a vital component of the country's freight and passenger movement, reflecting Australia's large distances, low population density and dispersed settlement. Australia has one of the highest rates of road use per capita in the world, and road freight — dominated by articulated trucks and the iconic multi-trailer road trains of the outback — carries the majority of non-bulk domestic freight by value and a large share by weight.

Network

Australia's road network totals roughly 880,000 km, most of it unsealed rural road. The National Highway network links the state and territory capitals and major regional centres; key routes include the Hume Highway (Sydney–Melbourne), the Pacific Highway (Sydney–Brisbane), the Stuart Highway (Adelaide–Darwin) and Highway 1, the roughly 14,500 km route that circumnavigates the continent and is among the longest national highways in the world. Traffic drives on the left.

Road trains

In remote areas, particularly the Northern Territory, northern Western Australia and outback Queensland and South Australia, trucks haul multiple trailers in combination. "Road trains" of two or three trailers — and historically more — may exceed 50 metres in length and are used to carry livestock, ore, fuel and general freight between remote communities and regional hubs. They are restricted to designated routes, largely away from major population centres.

Regulation and safety

Roads are primarily the responsibility of state and territory governments, with the Commonwealth funding nationally significant projects. Vehicle standards, licensing and road rules are harmonised to a large degree through national bodies and model laws, though speed limits and demerit-point schemes vary by state. Maximum speed limits are generally 100–110 km/h on rural highways; the Northern Territory permits 130 km/h on sections of major highways. Road safety campaigns have greatly reduced fatalities since the 1970s through compulsory seatbelts, random breath testing and speed enforcement.

Passenger transport

Private cars dominate passenger travel. Intercity coaches (such as Greyhound Australia) serve long-distance routes, and state capitals operate bus networks, but rail and air carry most intercapital passengers. The long distances make domestic aviation a major competitor to road travel between capitals.
