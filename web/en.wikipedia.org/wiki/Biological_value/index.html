Biological value

Whole egg is traditionally assigned a biological value of 100.

Biological value (BV) is a measure of the proportion of absorbed protein from a food which becomes incorporated into the proteins of the organism's body. It captures how readily the digested protein can be used in protein synthesis in the cells of the organism. In this analysis, protein is the only source of nitrogen in the diet.

Determination

BV is determined by nitrogen balance: subjects consume a protein-free diet to establish baseline nitrogen losses, then a diet containing the test protein, and the nitrogen retained is compared with the nitrogen absorbed. BV is expressed as a percentage of absorbed nitrogen that is retained. Whole egg protein, with an essential amino acid pattern closely matching human requirements, is used as a reference with a BV of 100; whey protein is often reported near or above this value, while wheat protein scores much lower (around 54–64) because it is limiting in lysine.

Advantages and disadvantages

BV reflects the amino acid composition of a food and actual nitrogen retention, but its measurement is slow, costly and influenced by the subject's energy intake and prior diet. Because BV ignores how the protein is affected by digestion, later scoring systems such as the protein digestibility-corrected amino acid score (PDCAAS) and the digestible indispensable amino acid score (DIAAS) were developed to improve on it.

Common values

Approximate values: whey protein ~104, whole egg 100, cow's milk ~91, egg white ~88, fish ~83, beef ~80, soy protein ~74, rice ~59, wheat ~54. Complementary proteins — combining foods with different limiting amino acids, such as grains with legumes — can raise the effective value of a mixed diet.
