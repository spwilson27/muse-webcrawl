David Ryan Adams (born November 5, 1974) is an American rock and country singer-songwriter. He first rose to prominence as the frontman of the alternative country band Whiskeytown, whose three albums helped define the alt-country movement of the 1990s. Since launching his solo career with Heartbreaker (2000), he has released a large and varied body of work, earning multiple Grammy Award nominations and a reputation as one of the most prolific songwriters of his generation.

Early life and Whiskeytown

Adams was raised in Jacksonville, North Carolina. He formed Whiskeytown in Raleigh in 1994 with Caitlin Cary and others; the band released Faithless Street (1995), Strangers Almanac (1997) and Pneumonia (recorded 1999, released 2001) before breaking up amid Adams's growing solo ambitions.

Solo career

His solo debut Heartbreaker (2000), featuring the songs "Oh My Sweet Carolina" and "Come Pick Me Up", drew wide critical acclaim. Gold (2001) brought his biggest commercial exposure with "New York, New York", whose video, filmed days before September 11, 2001, became an unlikely anthem for the city. A famously rapid release schedule followed, including Demolition (2002), Rock N Roll and Love Is Hell (2003/2004). With his backing band the Cardinals he released Cold Roses, Jacksonville City Nights and Easy Tiger. Later work includes the self-titled Ryan Adams (2014), a track-by-track cover of Taylor Swift's 1989 (2015), and Prisoner (2017).

Personal life

Adams was married to singer and actress Mandy Moore from 2009 to 2016. He has spoken publicly about struggling with substance abuse and with Ménière's disease, which contributed to a hiatus from touring in the late 2000s. In 2019, a New York Times report published allegations of emotional abuse and inappropriate communications from several women; Adams denied the criminal allegations and later issued a public apology. The FBI declined to open an investigation.
