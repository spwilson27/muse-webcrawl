Treasury of Atreus

The Treasury of Atreus, also called the Tomb of Agamemnon, is a large tholos (beehive) tomb on the Panagitsa Hill at Mycenae, Greece. Built around 1250 BC during the Late Bronze Age, it is the largest and finest surviving Mycenaean tholos tomb, remarkable for having the widest and highest corbelled dome in the world for more than a millennium.

Architecture

An open-air passage (dromos), about 36 metres long, leads through a façade once decorated with red and green stone half-columns to a doorway over 5 metres high, topped by a relieving triangle and an enormous lintel stone estimated at over 120 tonnes. Inside, the circular chamber measures some 14.5 metres across and 13.2 metres high, its walls stepped inward in corbelled courses of ashlar masonry. A smaller rectangular side chamber cut into the rock was presumably the actual burial chamber; the main dome may have served ceremonial purposes.

History

The tomb takes its traditional name from Atreus, legendary king of Mycenae and father of Agamemnon, though the association is mythical rather than historical. It was looted in antiquity—Heinrich Schliemann's contemporaries and earlier travellers, including Lord Elgin's agents, removed its sculptural decoration—and was cleared by the Greek Archaeological Service in the nineteenth century. It remains a highlight of the UNESCO World Heritage site of Mycenae.
