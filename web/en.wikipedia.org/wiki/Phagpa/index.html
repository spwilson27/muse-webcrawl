Drogön Chögyal Phagpa (Tibetan: འགྲོ་མགོན་ཆོས་རྒྱལ་འཕགས་པ་; 1235–1280), often known simply as Phagpa, was a Tibetan Buddhist monk of the Sakya school who became the spiritual advisor and Imperial Preceptor of Kublai Khan, founder of the Yuan dynasty of China. He devised the ʼPhags-pa script, which Kublai promulgated as the official script of the empire.

Early life

Phagpa was born in 1235 at Sakya in southwestern Tibet, nephew of Sakya Pandita, the leader of the Sakya school. As a boy he accompanied his uncle to the court of the Mongol prince Godan, where Sakya Pandita negotiated Tibet's submission to Mongol rule. After his uncle's death, Phagpa remained at the Mongol court and formed a close priest–patron relationship with Kublai.

Imperial Preceptor

In 1260 Kublai appointed Phagpa as guoshi ("State Preceptor"), and in 1264 he was placed in charge of the Bureau of Buddhist and Tibetan Affairs, effectively making him the ruler of Tibet under Mongol overlordship. Around 1269 he presented Kublai with the ʼPhags-pa script, a vertical alphabetic script derived from Tibetan writing intended to transcribe all languages of the empire, including Mongolian and Chinese. In 1270 he was elevated to Imperial Preceptor (dishi).

Later life and legacy

Phagpa spent his later years largely at Sakya, where he died in 1280, possibly by poisoning according to later accounts. His script fell out of general use after the fall of the Yuan dynasty in 1368, though it influenced later alphabets including, indirectly, the Korean Hangul according to one scholarly theory. Phagpa's role established the model of Tibetan Buddhist preceptors at the Mongol and later Manchu courts.
