Antichrist

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In Christian eschatology, the Antichrist is a person prophesied by the Bible to oppose Jesus Christ and substitute himself in Christ's place before the Second Coming. The term is found five times in the New Testament, solely in the First and Second Epistles of John, where it describes anyone who denies that Jesus is the Christ, as well as an expected future figure.

Biblical sources

Although the word appears only in John's epistles, Christian tradition has associated the Antichrist with several other biblical figures: the "man of lawlessness" of 2 Thessalonians, the Beast of the Book of Revelation, the "little horn" of Daniel, and the false prophets warned of in the Gospels. In Islamic eschatology, the corresponding figure is Al-Masih ad-Dajjal, opposed by the Mahdi and by the returning Jesus (Isa).

Historical interpretation

Through history, the label "Antichrist" has been applied polemically to emperors (such as Nero), popes (by Protestant reformers), reformers (by their opponents), Napoleon, and various twentieth-century dictators. In preterist interpretation, the prophecies were fulfilled in the first century; in historicist readings they unfold across church history; in futurist readings they concern a final world ruler before the end of the age.

In culture

The Antichrist is a recurring figure in literature and film, from medieval mystery plays and the frescoes of Luca Signorelli at Orvieto to modern works such as The Omen and the Left Behind novels.
