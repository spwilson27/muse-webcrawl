Cliché

A cliché is an expression, idea or element of a work that has become overused to the point of losing its original meaning or effect, even to the point of being trite or irritating. The term is frequently used in modern culture to describe an idea that might once have been meaningful or novel but has become banal through repetition.

Etymology

The word is borrowed from French, where cliché was printers' jargon for a stereotype plate — a block cast from a mould that could reproduce text or images repeatedly. The name was said to imitate the sound of the matrix striking molten metal. In nineteenth-century France the term passed from printing to photography and then to figurative use for a hackneyed phrase.

Use

Clichés may be true or not, and in literature and film the term covers worn plot devices, stock characters and predictable dialogue, as well as phrases such as "at the end of the day". Some commentators argue that clichés survive because they express shared truths efficiently, and that skilled writers can revive them by subversion.
