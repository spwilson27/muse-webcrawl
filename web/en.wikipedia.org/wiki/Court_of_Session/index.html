Court of Session


Parliament House, Edinburgh

The Court of Session is the supreme civil court of Scotland and constitutes part of the College of Justice; the High Court of Justiciary is Scotland's supreme criminal court. The Court of Session has sat at Parliament House in Edinburgh since 1638 and is both a court of first instance and a court of appeal for civil cases. The court was established in 1532 by an Act of the Parliament of Scotland, as the College of Justice, and was initially staffed by a mix of clerics and laymen.

Structure

The Court is divided into the Outer House, a court of first instance in which cases are heard by a single judge (a Lord Ordinary), and the Inner House, which hears appeals and is divided into divisions presided over by the Lord President and the Lord Justice Clerk. Judges of the Court of Session are known as Senators of the College of Justice and also sit as judges of the High Court of Justiciary.

History

Founded under James V, the College of Justice provided a permanent, professional judiciary replacing the earlier Session, a committee of the King's Council. The Articles of Union of 1707 expressly preserved the Court "in all time coming". Appeals from the Inner House lay historically to the House of Lords and now lie to the Supreme Court of the United Kingdom.
