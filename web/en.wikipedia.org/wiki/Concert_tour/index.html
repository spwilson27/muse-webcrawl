Concert tour

A concert tour (or simply tour) is a series of concerts by an artist or group of artists in different cities, countries or locations. Often concert tours are named to differentiate different tours by the same artist and to associate a specific tour with a particular album or product. Especially in the popular music world, such tours can become large-scale enterprises that last for several months or even years, are seen by hundreds of thousands or millions of people, and bring in millions of dollars in ticket revenue.

Large concert tours play stadiums and arenas across several continents over many months.

Overview

Tours are usually organised as a sequence of "legs", each covering a region or continent, with breaks between them. An artist promoting a new album will typically announce dates through promoters and ticketing agencies, travelling with a production that may include staging, lighting, video screens, pyrotechnics and a backing band. Supporting artists — opening acts — often perform earlier sets, sometimes joining tours to gain exposure to larger audiences.

Logistics

A touring production moves performers, crew and equipment between venues by tour bus, truck and air freight. Larger productions employ dozens or hundreds of staff, including tour managers, sound and lighting engineers, riggers, instrument technicians ("roadies"), security and caterers. Routing is planned to minimise travel distances and allow rest days, and venues are booked many months in advance. Residencies, in which an artist performs repeatedly at a single venue, are an alternative to continuous travel.

Economics

Since the decline of recorded-music revenues in the 2000s, touring has become the principal source of income for many popular musicians. Ticket prices, premium and VIP packages, and merchandise sales at venues all contribute to gross revenue, against which the costs of production, transport, promotion and venue hire are set. The highest-grossing tours of all time — each grossing well over half a billion US dollars — include farewell and reunion tours by established acts as well as record-breaking stadium tours by contemporary pop artists. Trade publications such as Billboard and Pollstar track tour grosses and publish annual rankings.

Types of tour

  • Promotional tour — supporting a new release, often the primary driver of a tour's theme and setlist.
  • Greatest hits tour — built around an artist's back catalogue rather than new material.
  • Farewell tour — billed as an artist's final tour, though some acts tour again afterwards.
  • Reunion tour — a re-formed group performing together after a split or hiatus.
  • Concert residency — a run of shows at one venue, sometimes counted alongside tours in commercial reporting.
