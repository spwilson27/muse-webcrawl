Subhash Ghisingh

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Born: 21 June 1936, Manju Tea Estate, Darjeeling
Died: 29 January 2015 (aged 78), New Delhi
Known for: Founder of the Gorkha National Liberation Front; leader of the Gorkhaland movement
Office: Chairman, Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council (1988–2001; 2005–2008 as caretaker)

Subhash Ghisingh (21 June 1936 – 29 January 2015) was an Indian politician and the founder of the Gorkha National Liberation Front (GNLF). He led the agitation in the 1980s for the creation of a separate state of Gorkhaland for the Nepali-speaking Gorkha people of the Darjeeling hills of West Bengal.

Early life

Ghisingh was born at Manju Tea Estate near Darjeeling. He served briefly in the Indian Army's Gorkha Regiment before leaving to work as a teacher, and he wrote poetry and plays in Nepali before entering politics.

Gorkhaland agitation

In 1980 Ghisingh founded the GNLF to press the demand for Gorkhaland. The movement escalated into a violent agitation between 1986 and 1988, marked by strikes, blockades of the hill districts, and clashes that cost many lives. The agitation ended in 1988 with a tripartite agreement between the central government, the West Bengal government, and the GNLF, creating the semi-autonomous Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council (DGHC).

Later career

Ghisingh chaired the DGHC from 1988, but his influence waned after 2001, when his close associate Bimal Gurung broke away. In 2007 Gurung founded the Gorkha Janmukti Morcha, which eclipsed the GNLF and pushed Ghisingh into political marginality; he left Darjeeling in 2008. He returned to the hills in 2011 but never regained his former position. He died in New Delhi in January 2015.

Legacy

Ghisingh is credited with bringing the Gorkha identity question to national prominence in India, though the creation of the DGHC fell short of the statehood the movement had demanded, and the Gorkhaland demand has been revived periodically by successor movements.
