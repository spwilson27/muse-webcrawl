Phish is an American rock band formed in Burlington, Vermont, in 1983. The band consists of guitarist and lead vocalist Trey Anastasio, bassist Mike Gordon, drummer Jon Fishman and keyboardist Page McConnell. Phish is famous for its extended improvisational live performances, eclectic repertoire blending rock, jazz, funk, bluegrass and progressive rock, and one of the most devoted fan followings in popular music — fans known as "Phans" who follow the band from show to show, in the tradition of the Grateful Dead.

History

Formed at the University of Vermont, Phish built its audience through relentless touring and by encouraging fans to tape and trade recordings of concerts, in which no two setlists are alike. Albums such as Junta (1989), A Picture of Nectar (1992) and Billy Breathes (1996) brought wider recognition, and the single "Free" and "Down with Disease" received radio airplay. The band is renowned for its large self-produced festivals, beginning with the Clifford Ball in 1996 and including the Big Cypress millennium concert (1999–2000), one of the longest single sets in rock history.

Phish took a hiatus from 2000 to 2002 and broke up in 2004, before reuniting in 2009. Since then the band has continued to tour successfully, including annual multi-night runs at Madison Square Garden culminating in New Year's Eve shows, and residencies such as the "Baker's Dozen" of 2017 — thirteen consecutive shows without repeating a song.
