Monbazillac AOC

Monbazillac is an Appellation d'Origine Contrôlée (AOC) for sweet white wine produced in the commune of Monbazillac and surrounding communes on the left bank of the Dordogne river, south of Bergerac in southwestern France. Like its more famous counterpart Sauternes, Monbazillac is made from grapes affected by noble rot (Botrytis cinerea), principally Sémillon, Sauvignon blanc, and Muscadelle.

History and production

Viticulture around Monbazillac dates to the Middle Ages, and its wines were exported extensively to the Netherlands in the 17th and 18th centuries. The AOC was established in 1936, making it one of France's older appellations. The vineyards, planted on north-facing slopes above the Dordogne valley, benefit from autumn mists that encourage botrytis; grapes are picked by hand in successive passes (tries), selecting only the rotted berries. The resulting wines are rich and honeyed, with flavours of apricot, peach, and acacia, and can age for many years.

The château and region

The 16th-century Château de Monbazillac, owned since 1960 by the local winegrowers' cooperative, overlooks the vineyards and is a landmark of the Bergerac wine region. Monbazillac is the largest sweet-wine appellation of the southwest, covering roughly 2,500 hectares.
