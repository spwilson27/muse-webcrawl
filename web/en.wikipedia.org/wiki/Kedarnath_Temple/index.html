Kedarnath Temple

The temple with the Kedarnath range behind it

Kedarnath Temple (Sanskrit: केदारनाथ मंदिर) is a Hindu temple dedicated to Shiva, located in the town of Kedarnath in Rudraprayag district, Uttarakhand, India, at an altitude of about 3,583 metres (11,755 ft) in the Garhwal Himalayas near the head of the Mandakini River. It is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas, the most sacred Shiva shrines, and one of the four sites of the Chota Char Dham pilgrimage circuit.

Legend and history

According to tradition, the temple was first established by the Pandavas of the Mahabharata, who sought Shiva's forgiveness after the Kurukshetra war; Shiva, eluding them in the form of a bull, is said to have left his hump at Kedarnath, with other parts of his body appearing at the four other Panch Kedar shrines. The present stone temple is attributed to the 8th-century philosopher Adi Shankaracharya, whose samadhi shrine lies behind the temple.

Architecture

Built of massive grey stone slabs in the North Indian (Nagara) style, the temple comprises a garbhagriha (sanctum) housing a conical rock formation worshipped as the lingam, an assembly hall, and a porch adorned with figures of deities and scenes from mythology. Its survival through centuries of heavy snowfall is considered remarkable.

Pilgrimage

Because of extreme weather, the temple is open only between late April/early May and autumn (around Bhai Dooj), closing for winter when the deity is carried down to Ukhimath. Pilgrims traditionally reached it by a 16-kilometre trek from Gaurikund. The temple survived the devastating June 2013 Uttarakhand floods — a huge boulder, now called Bhim Shila, lodged behind the temple and diverted the floodwaters — though the surrounding town was badly damaged. Reconstruction of the Kedarnath township followed in subsequent years.
