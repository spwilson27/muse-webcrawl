Nobel Prize in Physics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The gold Nobel Prize medal, showing Alfred Nobel's profile, on a dark background]

The Nobel Prize in Physics is an award presented yearly by the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences to scientists in physics, one of the five Nobel Prizes established by Alfred Nobel's 1895 will. It is widely regarded as the most prestigious award in the field.

The first prize, in 1901, went to Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen for the discovery of X-rays. Notable laureates include Marie Curie (1903, shared), Albert Einstein (1921, for the photoelectric effect), and the pioneers of quantum mechanics. John Bardeen is the only person to have won it twice (1956 and 1972).

Selection

Candidates are nominated by invited experts; a committee of the Academy evaluates them. The prize is presented in Stockholm on 10 December, the anniversary of Nobel's death.

Recent decades

Recent prizes have recognised exoplanet discoveries, black-hole research, gravitational waves (2017), machine learning foundations applied to neural networks, and attosecond physics, reflecting the field's widening frontiers.
