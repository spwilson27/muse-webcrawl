Boardwalk

A boardwalk is an elevated footpath, walkway, or causeway built with wooden planks, typically along a beach or waterfront. Boardwalks are often lined with shops, restaurants, amusement arcades and attractions, and are iconic features of many seaside resorts, particularly in the United States.

History

The first boardwalk in the United States was built in Atlantic City, New Jersey, in 1870, initially to keep sand out of hotel lobbies. It evolved into a major entertainment promenade. Coney Island's Riegelmann Boardwalk in New York, opened in 1923, is another famous example.

Notable boardwalks

Notable examples include the Atlantic City Boardwalk, the Santa Cruz Beach Boardwalk in California, the Kemah Boardwalk in Texas, and the boardwalks of Ocean City, Maryland and Wildwood, New Jersey. Modern boardwalks may be built of concrete or composite materials rather than wood. See also Promenade and Atlantic City.
