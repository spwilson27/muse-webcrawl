Lake Moeris

Lake Moeris
Location: Faiyum Depression, Egypt
Modern remnant: Lake Qarun (Birket Qarun)
Type: Ancient lake (freshwater, later saline)
Historical surface area: approx. 1,700 km² (New Kingdom era estimates)

Lake Moeris (from Ancient Egyptian Mer-wer, "the Great Lake") was an ancient lake in the Faiyum Depression southwest of the Nile Delta in Egypt. Much reduced today, its remnant is the saline Lake Qarun (Birket Qarun), lying some 20 metres below sea level.

History

In prehistory the Faiyum basin was filled by a much larger lake fed by the Nile. During the Middle Kingdom, especially under Amenemhat III of the 12th Dynasty, Egyptian engineers regulated the inflow through the Hawara channel (the Bahr Yusuf), reclaiming land and controlling floodwaters; the lake became a great reservoir and fishery. Herodotus described Lake Moeris as an artificial wonder with a circumference of 3,600 stades and two colossal statues standing in its midst — exaggerations, but reflecting its fame. Classical authors also associated it with the Labyrinth at Hawara.

Under the Ptolemies, further reclamation shrank the lake and created the fertile farmland of the Arsinoite nome, dotted with new towns such as Karanis and Soknopaiou Nesos. In Roman and later times reduced Nile inflow lowered the lake to roughly its modern level, and its water turned brackish and then saline.

Today

Modern Lake Qarun is about 40 km long and 230 km² in area, fed almost entirely by agricultural drainage. It remains an important fishery and a protected area for migratory birds, though rising salinity and pollution are continuing concerns.
