A scrum (short for scrummage) is a method of restarting play in rugby football that involves players packing closely together with their heads down and attempting to gain possession of the ball. A scrum is most commonly used in rugby union, where each side fields eight forwards in the formation; scrums also feature, in a largely uncontested form, in rugby league.

Formation (rugby union)

Each pack consists of a front row of two props and a hooker, a second row of two locks, and a back row of two flankers and a number eight. The packs bind together and engage on the referee's calls — since 2013, "crouch, bind, set" — to reduce the impact of engagement. The scrum-half of the team awarded the feed rolls the ball into the tunnel between the front rows; the hookers strike for it with their feet while the pack pushes, ideally driving the ball back to be collected by the number eight or scrum-half.

Offences and safety

Collapsing a scrum, engaging early, boring in, or deliberately wheeling beyond limits are penalised; straight feeding is required by law though variably enforced. Because of the enormous spinal forces in the front row, scrummaging is among the most technically coached and safety-regulated phases of the game, and uncontested scrums are ordered if a team lacks trained front-row replacements.

Rugby league

Rugby league scrums involve six forwards per side, are deliberately uncontested (the feeding side almost always wins the ball) and serve mainly to restart play and temporarily remove forwards from the defensive line; they were eliminated in some competitions (such as the NRL from 2021) in favour of a handover or play-the-ball.
