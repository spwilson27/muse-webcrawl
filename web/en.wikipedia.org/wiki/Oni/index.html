An oni (Japanese: 鬼) is a kind of yōkai — a demon, ogre, or troll in Japanese folklore. Oni are widely depicted with one or more horns growing from their heads, sharp claws, wild hair, and red, blue, or green skin, often wearing loincloths of tiger skin and carrying iron clubs called kanabō. The phrase oni ni kanabō — "an oni with an iron club" — means something invincible or unstoppable.

Origins

The word oni is thought to derive from on, the on'yomi reading of the character 隠, meaning "to hide", reflecting an early conception of spirits as invisible beings. Over time, under the influence of Chinese and Indian (via Buddhist) lore, oni took on their familiar ogre-like form, becoming associated with calamity, disease, and the punishments of the Buddhist hells, where oni serve as jailers of the damned.

In folklore and festivals

Oni feature in many famous tales, including Momotarō, in which the hero sails to Onigashima (Demon Island) to defeat a band of oni, and the legends of Shuten-dōji, the drunken oni chieftain of Mount Ōe. During the spring festival of Setsubun, people throw roasted soybeans out of their homes while chanting "Oni wa soto! Fuku wa uchi!" ("Oni out! Luck in!") to drive away evil spirits for the coming year.

Modern culture

Oni remain ubiquitous in Japanese art, theatre (including Noh and kabuki masks), manga, anime, and video games, where they are often softened into comic or even sympathetic figures while retaining their horns and clubs.
