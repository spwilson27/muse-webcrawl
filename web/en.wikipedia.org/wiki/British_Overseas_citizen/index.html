British Overseas citizen

Legal basisBritish Nationality Act 1981
Effective1 January 1983
Right of abode in UKNone by virtue of the status alone
TransmissibleGenerally no — a residual, diminishing class

A British Overseas citizen (BOC) is a holder of a class of British nationality created by the British Nationality Act 1981, which took effect on 1 January 1983. The status was granted mainly to Citizens of the United Kingdom and Colonies (CUKCs) who did not qualify either for British citizenship or for British Dependent Territories citizenship — typically people connected with former British colonies who were not settled in the UK or in a remaining colony.

Rights

British Overseas citizenship is largely a residual status. It does not carry the right of abode in the United Kingdom or in any British Overseas Territory. Holders may hold a British passport, receive consular assistance abroad, and vote in UK elections only if they also qualify on other grounds. Because the status generally cannot be transmitted to children, the number of British Overseas citizens declines over time; many holders, particularly ethnic Indians in East Africa and people of Malaysian birth, also hold another nationality.

Routes to British citizenship

Some British Overseas citizens may register as British citizens: notably those who hold no other nationality (to avoid statelessness), and those who have lived in the UK for a qualifying period and obtained settlement. The Nationality and Borders Act 2022 introduced further registration routes for certain people who missed out historically because of legislative unfairness or exceptional circumstances.

History

The 1981 Act replaced the single status of CUKC with three categories — British citizen, British Dependent Territories citizen (later British Overseas Territories citizen), and British Overseas citizen. The BOC category mostly covered CUKCs connected with colonies that had become independent Commonwealth countries, such as Kenya and Malaysia, whose holders had not kept a close connection with the UK itself.
