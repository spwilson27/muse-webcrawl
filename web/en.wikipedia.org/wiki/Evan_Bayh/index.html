Evan Bayh

Birch Evans "Evan" Bayh III (born December 26, 1955) is an American lawyer, lobbyist, and politician of the Democratic Party who served as the 46th governor of Indiana from 1989 to 1997 and as a United States senator from Indiana from 1999 to 2011. He is the son of former U.S. Senator Birch Bayh.

Early life and education

Bayh was born in Shirkieville, Indiana, the son of Marvella and Birch Bayh, who represented Indiana in the U.S. Senate from 1963 to 1981. He graduated from Indiana University Bloomington with a bachelor's degree in business economics in 1978 and earned a law degree from the University of Virginia School of Law in 1981. He then clerked for a federal judge and practised law in Indianapolis.

Secretary of State and Governor

Bayh entered electoral politics in 1986, winning election as Indiana's secretary of state. In 1988, at age 32, he was elected governor, becoming the youngest governor in the United States at the time, and he was re-elected in 1992 with the highest percentage of votes in a contested Indiana gubernatorial election to that date. As governor he emphasised fiscal conservatism, accumulating budget surpluses, cutting taxes, and promoting education reform and welfare-to-work programmes. He was a leading figure among the centrist "New Democrats" and chaired the Democratic Leadership Council.

United States Senate

Barred from a third consecutive term as governor, Bayh ran for the U.S. Senate in 1998 and won the seat his father had once held. He was re-elected comfortably in 2004. In the Senate he served on the Armed Services, Banking, and Intelligence committees, among others. He was mentioned as a possible presidential and vice-presidential candidate, briefly exploring a 2008 presidential run, and was reported to be a finalist on Barack Obama's shortlist for the 2008 vice-presidential nomination, which ultimately went to Joe Biden. In February 2010 Bayh announced he would not seek re-election, citing congressional dysfunction and partisan gridlock.

Later career

After leaving the Senate, Bayh worked as a partner at the law and lobbying firm McGuireWoods, a contributor to Fox News, and an adviser to private equity and corporate boards. In 2016 he attempted a political comeback, running again for his old Senate seat, but lost the general election to Republican Todd Young. His wife, Susan Bayh, a lawyer and corporate director, died of brain cancer in 2021.
