Dynamic covalent chemistry

Dynamic covalent chemistry (DCC) is a strategy in supramolecular and synthetic chemistry based on covalent bonds that can form, break and reform reversibly under thermodynamic control. Because the bonds are covalent, the resulting structures are robust; because they are reversible, mixtures can "proof-read" and select the most stable product from a dynamic combinatorial library.

Principles

In DCC, building blocks linked by reversible reactions exchange partners continuously until the system settles at its thermodynamic minimum. Adding a template — for example a guest molecule or a metal ion — can shift the equilibrium and amplify the best-binding product. Common reversible covalent reactions include imine formation and exchange, disulfide exchange, transesterification, boronate ester formation, alkene and alkyne metathesis, and reversible Diels–Alder reactions.

Applications

Dynamic combinatorial libraries are used to discover receptors, catalysts and drug candidates: the target itself selects its strongest binder from the equilibrating mixture. DCC also underpins self-healing polymers, adaptive materials, covalent organic frameworks and dynamic systems chemistry that mimics aspects of biological selection.
