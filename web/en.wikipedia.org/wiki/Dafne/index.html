Dafne

Dafne is an opera — generally regarded as the earliest surviving work of the genre — composed by Jacopo Peri with a libretto by Ottavio Rinuccini, first performed during the Carnival of 1598 (1597 old style) at the Palazzo Corsi in Florence.

The libretto, based on the myth of Daphne and Apollo from Ovid's Metamorphoses, survives, as do a few fragments of Peri's music; contributions were also made by Jacopo Corsi. Marco da Gagliano later set the same libretto in 1608.

Dafne emerged from the circle of the Florentine Camerata, whose experiments in recreating Greek drama through music gave rise to opera.
