Kwasi Alfred Addo Kwarteng (born 26 May 1975) is a British politician and historian who served as Chancellor of the Exchequer from September to October 2022 under Prime Minister Liz Truss. A member of the Conservative Party, he was previously Secretary of State for Business, Energy and Industrial Strategy (2021–2022) and Member of Parliament for Spelthorne from 2010 to 2024.

Early life and education

Kwarteng was born in Waltham Forest, London, to Ghanaian immigrant parents — his father an economist, his mother a barrister. He won scholarships to Colet Court and Eton College, then read history at Trinity College, Cambridge, where he took a double first and earned a PhD on the recoinage crisis of 1695–97. Before entering politics he worked as a financial analyst and wrote a column for the Daily Telegraph. His books include Ghosts of Empire (2011) and, with fellow MPs including Liz Truss, Britannia Unchained (2012), which advocated free-market reform.

Chancellorship and the mini-budget

Appointed Chancellor on 6 September 2022, Kwarteng presented a "Growth Plan" — the so-called mini-budget — on 23 September 2022, announcing large unfunded tax cuts, including abolition of the 45p top rate of income tax and cuts to stamp duty and planned corporation tax rises, without an accompanying forecast from the Office for Budget Responsibility. The announcement triggered a sharp fall in sterling to near parity with the US dollar, a surge in gilt yields, emergency Bank of England intervention in the bond market, and turmoil for pension funds using liability-driven investment strategies. Kwarteng reversed the top-rate cut and was dismissed by Truss after 38 days — the second-shortest tenure as Chancellor in modern British history. Truss resigned days later. Kwarteng announced in 2024 that he would not seek re-election.
