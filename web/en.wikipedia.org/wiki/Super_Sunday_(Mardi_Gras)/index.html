Super Sunday (Mardi Gras)

Super Sunday is a major annual gathering of the Mardi Gras Indian tribes of New Orleans, Louisiana, as well as of Social Aid and Pleasure Clubs. It is held on the Sunday closest to St. Joseph's Day (March 19), traditionally the third Sunday of March, and is regarded as the most important event of the year for the Black Masking Indian tradition outside Mardi Gras Day itself.

Tradition

The Mardi Gras Indians are African American carnival groups organized into "tribes," whose members hand-sew elaborate suits of beads, sequins, feathers, and rhinestones over the course of a year, embodying roles such as Big Chief, Spy Boy, Flag Boy, and Queen. The tradition honours both African heritage and the Native Americans who sheltered enslaved people and maroons in Louisiana bayous. On Super Sunday the tribes process through the city — the Uptown tribes gathering around A.L. Davis Park and the Downtown tribes around Bayou St. John — meeting in ritualized encounters of chant, dance, and display.

St. Joseph's Day

The timing links to St. Joseph's Day, March 19, a feast day deeply observed in New Orleans' historically Sicilian-influenced Catholic culture; by tradition, Mardi Gras Indians also mask on St. Joseph's night. Super Sunday therefore functions as a second, neighbourhood-centred carnival day after the parades of Mardi Gras season proper.

Recent history

The event was disrupted in 2005–2006 following Hurricane Katrina and cancelled or curtailed in 2020–2021 during the COVID-19 pandemic, resuming in full in 2022. Super Sunday remains central to New Orleans' second line and Black Masking culture, chronicled by institutions such as the Backstreet Cultural Museum.
