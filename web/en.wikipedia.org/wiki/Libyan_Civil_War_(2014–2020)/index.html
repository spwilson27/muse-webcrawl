The Libyan Civil War (2014–2020), often called the Second Libyan Civil War, was a multilateral armed conflict fought between rival Libyan governments and armed groups, with extensive foreign intervention. It began in 2014 as post-Gaddafi Libya split between the internationally contested government seated in Tripoli and the elected House of Representatives that fled to Tobruk, and it ended with a UN-brokered permanent ceasefire in October 2020.

Origins

After the 2011 war that overthrew Muammar Gaddafi, militias proliferated and the transitional authorities failed to build a national army. In May 2014, General Khalifa Haftar launched Operation Dignity against Islamist militias in Benghazi; amid disputes over the June 2014 elections, the losing factions formed the Libya Dawn coalition, seized Tripoli, and revived the old General National Congress, leaving Libya with two rival parliaments and governments.

Course of the war

Fighting centred on Benghazi, the Oil Crescent, Misrata, and Tripoli's airport. The Islamic State exploited the chaos to seize Derna and Sirte in 2015, holding Sirte until its defeat in December 2016. The UN-brokered Libyan Political Agreement of December 2015 created the Government of National Accord (GNA) under Fayez al-Sarraj, which arrived in Tripoli in March 2016 but never gained recognition from the Tobruk authorities. Haftar's Libyan National Army consolidated the east and south, then in April 2019 launched an offensive on Tripoli, which stalled in the suburbs.

Foreign intervention and end

Turkey intervened openly for the GNA from late 2019 under a security agreement, deploying drones, air defences, and Syrian auxiliaries; Russia's Wagner Group, Egypt, and the UAE supported Haftar. Turkish-backed GNA forces retook western coastal cities and Tarhuna in mid-2020, and the front stabilized along the Sirte–Jufra line. Military talks in Geneva produced a permanent ceasefire on 23 October 2020, followed by the Libyan Political Dialogue Forum's selection of a Government of National Unity in 2021.

Humanitarian impact

The war killed thousands of combatants and civilians, displaced hundreds of thousands, crashed oil production, and made Libya a principal route—and graveyard—for migrants crossing the Mediterranean.
