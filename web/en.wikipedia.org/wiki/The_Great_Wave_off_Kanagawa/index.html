The Great Wave off Kanagawa

The Great Wave off Kanagawa (Japanese: Kanagawa-oki Nami Ura, "Under the Wave off Kanagawa") is a woodblock print by the Japanese ukiyo-e artist Hokusai, created in late 1831 during the Edo period. It is the first print in Hokusai's series Thirty-six Views of Mount Fuji and his most famous work, widely considered one of the most recognisable pieces of Japanese art in the world.

Description

The print shows an enormous wave — its claw-like crest modelled with intense dynamism — threatening three wooden fishing boats in rough seas off the Kanagawa coast, with a snow-capped Mount Fuji small and serene in the background beneath the wave's curve. The composition uses the imported pigment Prussian blue together with traditional indigo, and the wave has been variously interpreted as a tsunami, a storm wave, or a rogue "okinami." Hokusai's signature appears in a cartouche at upper left.

Production and influence

Prints were produced in the thousands from carved cherry blocks; early impressions with crisp key-block lines and unfaded colour are the most prized. Copies are held by the Metropolitan Museum of Art, the British Museum, the Art Institute of Chicago and many other collections. The image influenced European art of the later nineteenth century — Debussy's La mer carried it on its 1905 score cover — and it remains globally ubiquitous on posters, clothing and merchandise, even appearing in emoji form (🌊).
