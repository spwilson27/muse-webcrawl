Borscht Belt

The Borscht Belt, also called the Jewish Alps, is a colloquial term for the mostly defunct summer resorts of the Catskill Mountains in parts of Sullivan and Ulster counties in upstate New York, United States. The resorts were a popular vacation spot for New York City Jews from the 1920s through the 1970s.

Borscht Belt
Region: Catskill Mountains, New York
Peak era: 1920s–1970s
Noted for: Resorts, stand-up comedy

History

Jewish families, many of them recent immigrants, began travelling to the Catskills in the early 20th century, partly because other resort areas excluded Jews. Hotels and bungalow colonies flourished, reaching some 500 resorts at their height. Famous establishments included Grossinger's, the Concord, Kutsher's, and the Nevele.

Entertainment

The Borscht Belt was a legendary training ground for American stand-up comedy. Performers such as Jerry Lewis, Sid Caesar, Milton Berle, Joan Rivers, and Rodney Dangerfield honed their acts in the resorts' nightclubs, where the resident social director (the tummler) kept guests entertained from morning to night.

Decline

From the 1960s, cheap air travel, air conditioning, and the opening of other destinations to Jewish vacationers eroded the resorts' clientele. Most closed by the 1980s and 1990s; the Concord closed in 1998 and Kutsher's, the last of the great hotels, in 2013. The era is remembered in films such as Dirty Dancing and the series The Marvelous Mrs. Maisel.
