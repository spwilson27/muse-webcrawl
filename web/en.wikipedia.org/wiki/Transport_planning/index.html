Transport planning

Transport planning, or transportation planning, is the process of defining future policies, goals, investments and spatial planning designs to prepare for future needs to move people and goods to destinations. As practiced today, it is a collaborative process that incorporates the input of many stakeholders including various government agencies, the public and private businesses. Transport planners apply a multi-modal and/or comprehensive approach to analyzing the wide range of alternatives and impacts on the transportation system to influence beneficial outcomes.

History

Modern transport planning emerged in the mid-twentieth century alongside mass motorisation and motorway construction. In the United States, the Federal-Aid Highway Act of 1962 required a "continuing, comprehensive and cooperative" planning process in metropolitan areas. Similar exercises in the United Kingdom, such as the Buchanan Report Traffic in Towns (1963), grappled with accommodating the car in historic cities. From the 1970s onwards, environmental concerns, the oil crises and criticism of road-building broadened the discipline towards public transport, land-use integration and, later, demand management and sustainability.

The planning process

Transport planning typically involves forecasting future travel demand, often using the classic four-step model: trip generation, trip distribution, mode choice and route assignment. Planners evaluate alternative networks and policies against objectives such as accessibility, economic efficiency, safety, equity and environmental impact. Contemporary practice increasingly uses activity-based models, scenario planning and appraisal frameworks that monetise benefits and costs.

Sustainable transport planning

Since the late twentieth century the field has shifted from a "predict and provide" approach centred on road capacity towards managing demand and promoting walking, cycling and public transport. Concepts such as transit-oriented development, complete streets and the 15-minute city reflect attempts to integrate transport with land-use planning to reduce car dependence and greenhouse gas emissions.
