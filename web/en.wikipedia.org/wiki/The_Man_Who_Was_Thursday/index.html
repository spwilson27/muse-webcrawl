The Man Who Was Thursday: A Nightmare (1908) is a metaphysical thriller by G. K. Chesterton. It has been described as one of the most influential early works of the spy and absurdist traditions, blending adventure, comedy and theological allegory.

Plot

Gabriel Syme, a poet recruited by Scotland Yard as a philosophical detective against anarchism, infiltrates the Central Anarchist Council by getting himself elected as the delegate "Thursday". To his horror he learns of a plot against the Tsar; to his bewilderment he discovers, one by one, that each of his fellow delegates — Gogol (Tuesday), the Marquis de St. Eustache (Friday), Dr Bull (Saturday), the Secretary (Monday) and the Professor (Wednesday) — is also an undercover policeman. The pursuit converges on the Council's terrifying, larger-than-life president, Sunday, whose nature forms the novel's closing mystery. Chesterton's subtitle and later comments frame the tale as an allegory of a world that appears governed by chaos and evil but is, at its heart, good.

Themes and influence

Written when anarchist bomb outrages were a live public fear, the novel plays with disguise, identity and the terror of conspiracy, but its real subject is theodicy: why a benevolent order should wear so frightening a face. The book influenced writers as varied as Jorge Luis Borges and Kingsley Amis, and it has been adapted for radio and the stage.
