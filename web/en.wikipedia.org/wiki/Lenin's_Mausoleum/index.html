Lenin's Mausoleum

LocationRed Square, Moscow, Russia
ArchitectAlexey Shchusev
Completed1930 (stone structure)
MaterialsRed granite, black labradorite, porphyry
ContentsEmbalmed body of Vladimir Lenin

Lenin's Mausoleum is the stepped pyramidal tomb on Red Square in Moscow that houses the embalmed body of Vladimir Lenin, founder of the Soviet Union, who died on 21 January 1924. Designed by Alexey Shchusev, the present stone mausoleum was completed in 1930, replacing two temporary wooden structures.

History

Against the wishes of Lenin's widow, the Soviet leadership decided to preserve his body, which was embalmed by specialists and placed on public display within days of his funeral. The granite mausoleum became the ceremonial centrepiece of Soviet state ritual: leaders reviewed parades from its rostrum. From 1953 to 1961 Joseph Stalin's body lay beside Lenin's, and the tomb was briefly renamed for both men, until Stalin was removed during de-Stalinisation and reburied by the Kremlin Wall.

Preservation and debate

A dedicated laboratory maintains the body through periodic re-embalming. Since the fall of the USSR in 1991, periodic public debate has continued over whether Lenin should be buried; the mausoleum remains open to visitors as a museum, guarded and maintained at state expense.
