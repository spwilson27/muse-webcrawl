Oberammergau

Oberammergau is a municipality in the district of Garmisch-Partenkirchen, in Bavaria, southern Germany. The village, with a population of about 5,000, lies in the Ammer valley beneath the Kofel mountain and is world-famous for its Passion Play, performed roughly every ten years since 1634.

Passion Play

According to tradition, in 1633, during the Thirty Years' War and an outbreak of plague, the villagers vowed that if God spared them they would perform a play depicting the Passion of Christ every ten years. Deaths ceased, and the first play was staged in 1634. The production has grown into an enormous undertaking involving more than 2,000 villagers — nearly half the population — as actors, singers, musicians, and technicians, with performances held in years ending in zero (most recently delayed from 2020 to 2022 by the COVID-19 pandemic).

Other features

Oberammergau is also known for its woodcarving tradition, its houses decorated with Lüftlmalerei frescoes of religious and fairytale scenes, and the nearby Linderhof Palace and Ettal Abbey. A NATO school has been based in the village since 1953.
