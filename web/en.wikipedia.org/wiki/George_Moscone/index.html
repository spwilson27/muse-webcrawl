George Moscone

George Richard Moscone
Born: November 24, 1929, San Francisco, California, U.S.
Died: November 27, 1978, San Francisco, California, U.S.
Office: 37th Mayor of San Francisco (1976–1978)

George Richard Moscone (November 24, 1929 – November 27, 1978) was an American attorney and Democratic politician who served as the 37th mayor of San Francisco from 1976 until his assassination in 1978, together with supervisor Harvey Milk.

Early life and career

Moscone was born in San Francisco's Marina District to Italian-American parents and attended the University of the Pacific on a basketball scholarship before studying law at the University of California, Hastings. After naval service he entered private practice and local politics, winning election to the San Francisco Board of Supervisors in 1963. In 1966 he moved to the California State Senate, where he rose to majority leader and championed liberal causes including consumer protection, school lunch funding and the decriminalisation of abortion and consensual sex acts.

Mayor of San Francisco

Moscone narrowly won the 1975 mayoral election against John Barbagelata. As mayor he appointed minorities, women and gay people to city boards in unprecedented numbers, appointed a liberal police chief, and blocked a proposed development at a former railyard in favour of what became the Yerba Buena Center and the convention centre later named for him, the Moscone Center.

Assassination

On November 27, 1978, former supervisor Dan White, who had resigned and then sought reappointment, entered City Hall through a window and shot and killed Moscone and Supervisor Harvey Milk. Board President Dianne Feinstein succeeded Moscone as mayor. White's conviction for voluntary manslaughter — following the notorious "Twinkie defense" — provoked the White Night riots of May 1979.
