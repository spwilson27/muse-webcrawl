Information (indictment)

An information is a formal criminal charge made by a prosecutor, without the involvement of a grand jury, stating that a person has committed a crime. It is one of the two principal charging instruments in Anglo-American criminal procedure, the other being the indictment, which is issued by a grand jury. After an information is filed, the accused is brought before the court for arraignment.

United States

The Fifth Amendment requires a grand jury indictment for federal felony prosecutions, but that requirement has never been held to apply to the states. About half the states allow prosecutors to charge felonies by information, usually after a preliminary hearing at which a judge finds probable cause. In federal court, an information is used for misdemeanors and for felonies where the defendant waives indictment. The information must state the offense, the statute violated, and the essential facts, so the accused can prepare a defense and plead double jeopardy against any later prosecution for the same offense.

Other jurisdictions

In England and Wales, informations were historically used to initiate proceedings, including in magistrates' courts, where the term long survived for the written allegation that began a summary case. Several Commonwealth jurisdictions retain informations for offences tried on indictment in superior courts, with procedural rules varying by country.
