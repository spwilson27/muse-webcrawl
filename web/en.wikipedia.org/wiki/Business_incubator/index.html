Business incubator

A business incubator is an organization designed to accelerate the growth and success of entrepreneurial companies through an array of business support resources and services, including physical space, mentoring, coaching, access to capital, and networking connections. Incubators differ from science and technology parks mainly in their focus on very early-stage firms and their limited duration of tenancy, after which companies "graduate".

History

The concept is usually traced to the Batavia Industrial Center in New York, opened in 1959 by Joseph Mancuso, who leased subdivided space in a warehouse to start-up businesses with shared services. Incubation spread in the United States during the 1980s with support from the Small Business Administration, and worldwide thereafter; the U.S.-based International Business Innovation Association (InBIA, formerly NBIA) was founded in 1985. Thousands of incubators now operate globally, many affiliated with universities or economic-development agencies.

Services

Typical services include affordable office or laboratory space, shared administrative and IT infrastructure, business planning and management training, introductions to investors and lenders, help with regulatory and intellectual-property issues, and mentoring by experienced entrepreneurs. Some incubators take equity in client companies; others charge rent and fees. Seed accelerators such as Y Combinator (founded 2005) are a related, shorter, cohort-based model sometimes distinguished from traditional incubators.

Types

Incubators may be categorized by sponsor (public, university, corporate, private) or by industry focus — technology, biotechnology, food, social enterprise, or mixed use. "Virtual incubators" provide services without requiring physical co-location.
