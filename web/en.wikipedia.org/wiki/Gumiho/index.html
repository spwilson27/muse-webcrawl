Gumiho

A gumiho (Korean: 구미호, literally "nine-tailed fox") is a creature that appears in the folktales and legends of Korea. It is a fox that has lived for a thousand years and has acquired magical powers, including the ability to transform into a beautiful woman who seduces men in order to eat their livers or hearts.

Characteristics

Unlike the largely benevolent Japanese kitsune or the ambivalent Chinese huli jing, the Korean gumiho is traditionally depicted as an evil being. In most tales it transforms into a beautiful maiden, often wearing traditional dress, and uses its yeouiju (fox bead) — a marble-like object said to contain knowledge and energy — in its deceptions. A gumiho is said to be able to become fully human if it abstains from eating human flesh for a thousand days, or, in other versions, if it marries without its true nature being discovered for a set period.

Origins and tales

Stories of nine-tailed foxes reached Korea from Chinese literature, where the nine-tailed fox of Mount Qingqiu appears in the Shanhaijing. Korean oral tales, recorded in Joseon-era collections, developed the figure into a distinctively sinister spirit haunting grave sites and lonely travellers. Common story motifs include a scholar discovering his wife's fox identity, or a gumiho seeking to steal a man's liver to complete its transformation.

In popular culture

The gumiho remains a popular figure in modern South Korean media, appearing in television dramas such as My Girlfriend Is a Gumiho (2010) and Tale of the Nine Tailed (2020), as well as films, webtoons, and video games, often in romanticised or sympathetic reinterpretations.
