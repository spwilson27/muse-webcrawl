Intramuros

Intramuros (Latin for "within the walls") is the historic walled area of Manila, the capital of the Philippines. Built by Spanish colonial authorities in the late sixteenth century, the 64-hectare fortified city on the south bank of the Pasig River was the seat of government of the Captaincy General of the Philippines for over three centuries. Today it is one of Manila's sixteen districts and its principal heritage area.

History

The site was the location of Maynila, a fortified Tagalog settlement ruled by Rajah Sulayman at the mouth of the Pasig River. Conquistador Miguel López de Legazpi founded Spanish Manila in 1571 after defeating Sulayman's forces. Construction of the stone defensive walls began around 1590 under Governor-General Santiago de Vera and was continued by his successors; the fortifications eventually included curtain walls, bastions, ravelins, a moat, and Fort Santiago at the river mouth.

Within the walls stood the Manila Cathedral, the San Agustin Church (completed in 1607 and the oldest surviving stone church in the Philippines), government palaces, convents, the University of Santo Tomas, and the houses of the Spanish elite. The non-Spanish population largely lived outside the walls in districts such as Binondo and Quiapo. The British briefly occupied Intramuros during the British occupation of Manila in 1762–1764.

Destruction and restoration

Intramuros was devastated during the Battle of Manila in 1945, when American artillery and Japanese resistance destroyed most of its buildings; only San Agustin Church survived intact. Post-war reconstruction was uneven, and squatter settlements filled much of the area. In 1979 the Philippine government created the Intramuros Administration to restore and redevelop the district. Rebuilt landmarks include the Manila Cathedral (rebuilt 1954–1958), the Casa Manila museum, and portions of the walls, which visitors can now walk; Fort Santiago contains the Rizal Shrine honouring national hero José Rizal, who was imprisoned there before his execution in 1896.

Landmarks

Notable sites include Fort Santiago, San Agustin Church and museum, Manila Cathedral, the Palacio del Gobernador, the Bahay Tsinoy museum of Chinese-Filipino history, and the Baluarte de San Diego. Horse-drawn kalesa carriages carry tourists along the cobbled streets, and the district hosts several universities and government offices.
