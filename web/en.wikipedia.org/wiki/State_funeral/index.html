State funeral

A state funeral is a public funeral ceremony, observing the strict rules of protocol, held to honour people of national significance. State funerals usually include much pomp and ceremony as well as religious overtones and distinctive elements of military tradition. They are held to involve a wider public in a national day of mourning, and often include a lying in state, a procession, and attendance by foreign dignitaries.

Practice by country

In the United Kingdom, state funerals are reserved principally for sovereigns; recent ones include those of Winston Churchill (1965), a rare non-royal exception, and Queen Elizabeth II (2022). In the United States, state funerals are held in Washington, D.C., for presidents and others designated by the president, following a military-coordinated plan; recent examples include those of Ronald Reagan (2004), Gerald Ford (2006–07), and George H. W. Bush (2018). Other countries, including France (funérailles nationales at the Panthéon and Invalides), India, and Japan, maintain their own traditions.

Elements

Common elements include a period of official mourning with flags at half-mast, the body lying in state for public viewing, a gun carriage or caisson procession, a funeral service in a national cathedral or equivalent, musical honours such as the "Last Post", and burial or interment at a place of national significance.
