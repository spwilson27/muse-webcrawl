Meudon (French pronunciation: [mø.dɔ̃]) is a commune in the southwestern suburbs of Paris, France, in the department of Hauts-de-Seine. It is located 9.1 km (5.7 mi) from the centre of Paris, on the left bank of the Seine, and is known for its wooded hills, its observatory and the former royal château.

History

The Château de Meudon, rebuilt for the Grand Dauphin, son of Louis XIV, and landscaped with terraces by André Le Nôtre, was largely destroyed by fire in 1795 and during the Franco-Prussian War in 1871; its Orangery and great terrace survive, offering views over Paris. Auguste Rodin lived and worked at the Villa des Brillants in Meudon from 1893 until his death in 1917, and is buried there beside Rose Beuret; the house is now the Musée Rodin de Meudon.

Science

Meudon is home to the Meudon campus of the Paris Observatory, founded in 1876 by Jules Janssen on the château grounds, known for solar astronomy and the Carte du Ciel. The commune also hosts laboratories of the CNRS and the French aerospace research centre ONERA, reflecting its long association with aeronautics — the first French military airship hangar (Hangar Y, 1879) still stands at nearby Chalais-Meudon.

Transport

Meudon is served by RER C stations (Meudon-Val-Fleury), Transilien lines L, N and U, and tramway T6 at Meudon-la-Forêt. The district of Meudon-la-Forêt was developed in the 1960s as a large housing estate.
