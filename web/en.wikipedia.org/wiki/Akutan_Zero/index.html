Akutan Zero

The Akutan Zero, also known as Koga's Zero, was a Mitsubishi A6M2 Model 21 Zero fighter of the Imperial Japanese Navy that crash-landed on Akutan Island, Alaska, in June 1942 during the Aleutian Islands campaign. It was recovered nearly intact by the United States Navy the following month — the first flyable example of Japan's premier fighter to fall into Allied hands — and its evaluation flight tests materially influenced American fighter tactics and design for the remainder of the Pacific War.

Koga's Zero flying in US markings during its 1942 evaluation.

Capture and evaluation

Pilot Petty Officer Tadayoshi Koga was killed when his damaged Zero flipped on soft ground after an emergency landing on 4 June 1942, following the attack on Dutch Harbor. A US search party found the aircraft five weeks later. Shipped to San Diego, it was repaired and first flown by American test pilots on 20 September 1942. Test pilot Edward R. Sanders flew 24 evaluation sorties, identifying serious weaknesses: the Zero's ailerons stiffened at high speed, it rolled poorly to the right, and its engine cut out under negative G.

Impact

The findings were rushed to front-line squadrons and confirmed the wisdom of energy tactics — high-speed diving attacks and the Thach Weave — over turning dogfights. Lessons from the Akutan Zero also fed into the design requirements that produced the Grumman F6F Hellcat, though historians debate the extent of the direct influence. The aircraft was destroyed in a training accident in February 1945. See also Mitsubishi A6M Zero and Aleutian Islands campaign.
