Calibri is a digital sans-serif typeface family designed by Dutch designer Lucas de Groot between 2002 and 2004 and released to the public by Microsoft with Windows Vista and Office 2007. It was designed as part of the ClearType Font Collection to render well on screens using Microsoft's ClearType subpixel technology, and features soft, rounded corners and a moderately condensed, humanist design.

As a default typeface

Calibri replaced Times New Roman as the default font in Microsoft Word and Arial in PowerPoint and Excel from Office 2007, and remained the default typeface across Microsoft Office and many Microsoft products for over fifteen years. Its ubiquity in documents, emails, and presentations made it one of the most-seen typefaces in the world. Users who lacked the font — it was proprietary — sometimes saw documents reflow in a substitute.

In 2021 Microsoft announced it would retire Calibri as the Office default and commissioned five candidate replacements; in 2023 it named the winner, Aptos (originally Bierstadt), which began rolling out as the new default.

The "Calibri controversy"

Calibri gained unusual public prominence in 2017 in Pakistan, during the Panama Papers investigation of Prime Minister Nawaz Sharif's family. A document presented as dating from 2006 was typeset in Calibri — which was not publicly released until 2007 — and font experts testified it could not have been produced at the claimed date using a publicly available version. The episode, dubbed "Fontgate", contributed to the Supreme Court case that disqualified Sharif from office, and the defence's argument rested on the font having existed in beta versions since 2004–2005.

Design characteristics

Calibri's letterforms have subtly rounded stems and corners, a large x-height, and gently curved terminals. The family includes regular, bold, italic, and bold italic, with extensive character sets covering Latin, Greek, and Cyrillic scripts.
