Barton Aqueduct

The Barton Aqueduct was a stone navigable aqueduct that carried the Bridgewater Canal over the River Irwell at Barton-upon-Irwell, near Manchester, England. Completed in 1761, it was one of the wonders of the early Industrial Revolution and the first navigable aqueduct in England.

Construction

The aqueduct was designed by engineer James Brindley for Francis Egerton, 3rd Duke of Bridgewater, whose canal brought coal from his mines at Worsley into Manchester. Critics predicted the stone arches could never support a water-filled trough, but the aqueduct stood for more than 130 years.

Replacement

When the much larger Manchester Ship Canal was built in the 1890s, the old aqueduct was demolished and replaced by the celebrated Barton Swing Aqueduct, a movable iron trough that swings open to let ships pass — still in operation today and a Scheduled Ancient Monument.
