The Bombing of Darwin, also known as the Battle of Darwin, on 19 February 1942 was the largest single attack ever mounted by a foreign power on Australia. On that day, 242 planes of the Imperial Japanese Navy Air Service attacked the ships in Darwin's harbour and the town's two airfields in an attempt to prevent the Allies from using them as bases to contest the invasions of Timor and Java.

Background

Darwin was an important naval base and staging point for Allied operations in the South West Pacific. After the attack on Pearl Harbor and the rapid Japanese advance through Southeast Asia, much of Darwin's civilian population was evacuated, but its military significance made it a priority target. The attacking force was built around the same First Air Fleet carriers — Akagi, Kaga, Hiryū and Sōryū — that had struck Pearl Harbor, commanded by Chūichi Nagumo, with air operations led by Mitsuo Fuchida.

The raids

The attack came in two waves. The first, beginning at about 9:58 a.m., comprised 81 Nakajima B5N "Kate" high-level bombers, 71 Aichi D3A "Val" dive bombers and an escort of 36 Mitsubishi A6M Zero fighters launched from the four carriers. A second wave in the late morning by 54 land-based Mitsubishi G3M and G4M bombers flying from Ambon and Kendari struck the RAAF base. A warning relayed from Bathurst Island was not acted upon, and ten USAAF P-40 Kittyhawk fighters were caught taking off or landing; most were shot down.

Aftermath

At least 235 people were killed, including the crew of the destroyer USS Peary and the passengers and crew of ships sunk in the harbour; the post office was destroyed with the loss of nine staff. Military control in Darwin briefly broke down, with widespread looting and desertion — the so-called "Darwin panic" — and an official inquiry under Justice Lowe criticised the town's preparedness and leadership. The raids were the first of almost 100 air attacks on northern Australia during 1942–43. A commemoration is held in Darwin every 19 February.
