Henry Augustus Rowland

Henry Augustus Rowland (November 27, 1848 – April 16, 1901) was an American physicist and the first president of the American Physical Society. As the founding professor of physics at Johns Hopkins University, he helped establish modern experimental physics research in the United States.

Work

Rowland is best known for the concave diffraction grating: by ruling fine lines onto a concave mirror, he combined dispersion and focusing in one element, greatly improving spectroscopic precision. His dividing engine produced the finest gratings of the era, and with them he produced a photographic atlas of the solar spectrum and a table of solar wavelengths that became the standard reference for decades. Earlier, in Berlin under Helmholtz, he demonstrated in 1876 that a moving electric charge produces a magnetic field (the "Rowland effect"). He also performed a celebrated redetermination of the mechanical equivalent of heat.

Honours

Rowland received the Rumford Prize and the Matteucci Medal, among many awards. The Henry Rowland House in Baltimore is a National Historic Landmark; the Henry Rowland crater on the Moon and the asteroid 10515 Old Joe are connected to his memory, and Johns Hopkins continues to honour him through the Rowland name in its physics department.
