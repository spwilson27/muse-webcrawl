Myron Samuel Scholes (born July 1, 1941) is a Canadian–American financial economist. Scholes is the Frank E. Buck Professor of Finance, Emeritus, at the Stanford Graduate School of Business, and a Nobel Prize winner. In 1997, Scholes — along with Robert C. Merton — was awarded the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences for a new method to determine the value of derivatives, the work embodied in the Black–Scholes model developed with Fischer Black.

Early life and education

Scholes was born in Timmins, Ontario, and grew up in Hamilton, where his family owned book and stationery stores. He earned a bachelor's degree in economics from McMaster University in 1962, then an MBA (1964) and a PhD (1969) from the University of Chicago Booth School of Business, where he worked with Eugene Fama and Merton Miller.

Career

Scholes taught at the MIT Sloan School of Management and the University of Chicago before joining Stanford in 1983. His 1973 paper with Fischer Black, "The Pricing of Options and Corporate Liabilities," provided the first widely used formula for pricing options.

Long-Term Capital Management

In 1994 Scholes co-founded the hedge fund Long-Term Capital Management (LTCM) with Merton and former Salomon Brothers trader John Meriwether. The fund achieved extraordinary early returns using mathematical models, but collapsed in 1998 amid the Russian financial crisis, requiring a $3.6 billion bailout organized by the Federal Reserve Bank of New York to prevent systemic disruption.

Later work

After LTCM, Scholes co-founded Oak Hill Capital's Platinum Grove Asset Management and remained active in research on liquidity, risk and tax planning.
