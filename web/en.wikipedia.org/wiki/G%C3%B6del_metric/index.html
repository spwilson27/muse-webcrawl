Gödel metric

The Gödel metric is an exact solution of Einstein's field equations of general relativity introduced by Kurt Gödel in 1949. It describes a rotating universe filled with dust (a pressureless perfect fluid) together with a negative cosmological constant.

Properties

The solution is homogeneous but not isotropic, and it is famous for containing closed timelike curves: world lines that loop back into their own past. Gödel presented it to show that general relativity does not by itself exclude time travel to the past.

Significance

The Gödel universe does not expand and does not resemble the observed universe, but it spurred decades of research into causality, cosmic rotation limits, and chronology protection in gravitational physics.
