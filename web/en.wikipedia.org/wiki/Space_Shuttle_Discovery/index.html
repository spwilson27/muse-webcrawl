Space Shuttle Discovery

Space Shuttle Discovery (Orbiter Vehicle Designation: OV-103) is a retired American Space Shuttle orbiter operated by NASA. The third of five operational orbiters to enter service, Discovery flew its first mission, STS-41-D, on 30 August 1984, and its last, STS-133, on 24 February 2011. It is the most-flown orbiter in history, completing 39 missions, spending 365 days in space, and travelling nearly 149 million miles.

Career highlights

Discovery flew both "Return to Flight" missions after the Challenger (STS-26, 1988) and Columbia (STS-114, 2005) disasters. In April 1990 it deployed the Hubble Space Telescope on STS-31, and it flew two of the later Hubble servicing missions. It carried John Glenn back to space at age 77 on STS-95 in 1998, docked with both the Mir space station (STS-91, 1998) and — 13 times — the International Space Station, and delivered major ISS components including the Harmony module and the Japanese Kibo laboratory elements.

Retirement

After its final landing on 9 March 2011, Discovery was retired to the Smithsonian Institution. On 17 April 2012 it was flown to Washington Dulles International Airport atop a Shuttle Carrier Aircraft and placed on display at the National Air and Space Museum's Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center in Chantilly, Virginia, where it replaced the prototype orbiter Enterprise.

The name Discovery honours historic exploration vessels including HMS Discovery, which accompanied James Cook on his third voyage, and Henry Hudson's ship Discovery.
