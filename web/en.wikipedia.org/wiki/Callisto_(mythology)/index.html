Callisto (mythology)

[Hero image placeholder: Callisto and the bear]

In Greek mythology, Callisto (Ancient Greek: Καλλιστώ, "most beautiful") was a nymph, or the daughter of King Lycaon of Arcadia, who was a follower of the goddess Artemis. Seduced by Zeus, she was transformed into a bear and later placed among the stars as the constellation Ursa Major (the Great Bear).

Myth

As retold in Ovid's Metamorphoses, Callisto had taken a vow of chastity in the service of Artemis. Zeus, desiring her, approached her in the form of Artemis herself and seduced or raped her. When her pregnancy was discovered as she bathed, Artemis expelled her from her company in anger. Later, the jealous Hera transformed Callisto into a bear. Her son by Zeus, Arcas, grew up a hunter and nearly killed his mother in the chase; Zeus intervened, placing Callisto in the sky as Ursa Major and Arcas as Ursa Minor (or, in some versions, Boötes). In another strand of the tale it is Artemis who shoots her, or Zeus who changes her to protect her.

Legacy

Callisto's story has been a popular subject in art, including paintings by Rubens and Titian ("Diana and Callisto"). The name was given to Callisto, the second-largest moon of Jupiter, discovered by Galileo in 1610, and to the asteroid 204 Kallisto.
