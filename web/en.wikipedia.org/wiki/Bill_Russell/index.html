William Felton Russell (February 12, 1934 – July 31, 2022) was an American professional basketball player who played as a center for the Boston Celtics of the National Basketball Association (NBA) from 1956 to 1969. Widely regarded as one of the greatest basketball players of all time, he won eleven NBA championships during his thirteen-year career — the most by any player in NBA history.

College career

Russell attended the University of San Francisco, where he led the Dons to consecutive NCAA championships in 1955 and 1956, anchoring a 55-game winning streak. He also won a gold medal with the United States national team at the 1956 Summer Olympics in Melbourne.

NBA career

Drafted by the St. Louis Hawks and immediately traded to Boston, Russell transformed the Celtics into a dynasty under coach Red Auerbach. A five-time NBA Most Valuable Player and twelve-time All-Star, Russell was renowned for his shot-blocking, rebounding, and defensive intelligence. He averaged 15.1 points and 22.5 rebounds per game for his career. In 1966 he succeeded Auerbach as player-coach, becoming the first Black head coach in NBA history, and led Boston to championships in 1968 and 1969.

Legacy

Russell was inducted into the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame in 1975, and the NBA Finals Most Valuable Player Award was renamed in his honor in 2009. A prominent civil rights activist, he received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2011.
