Daksha (Sanskrit: दक्ष, "able, dexterous") is a Hindu deity, one of the Prajapatis, the agents of creation and protectors of life in Hindu mythology. He is said to have sprung from the right thumb of the creator god Brahma, and is the father of many daughters who were married to various gods and sages, making him a patriarch of much of the Hindu pantheon.

The Daksha Yajna

Daksha is best known for the story of his great sacrifice (yajna), told in the Mahabharata and several Puranas. Daksha despised his son-in-law Shiva — an ascetic who frequented cremation grounds — and held a lavish sacrifice to which Shiva was not invited. Daksha's daughter Sati, Shiva's wife, attended uninvited, was publicly humiliated, and immolated herself in the sacrificial fire (the origin of the term sati in this mythic context). Enraged, Shiva created the fierce beings Virabhadra and Bhadrakali, who destroyed the sacrifice; Daksha was beheaded.

In the aftermath, Daksha was revived — at the intercession of the gods and, in many versions, forgiven by Shiva — but his head could not be found, so he was given the head of a goat. The yajna was then completed under Shiva's blessing. The myth explains, among other things, the exclusion and later reintegration of Shiva within orthodox Vedic sacrifice.

Family

By his wife Prasuti, Daksha fathered many daughters — 27 of whom were married to Chandra, the moon god, becoming the Nakshatra asterisms; others, including Aditi and Diti, became mothers of the devas and asuras respectively. Through these marriages Daksha became the grandfather of much of the divine world, earning the epithet "grandfather of beings".
