EGOT

The four awards of the EGOT: Emmy, Grammy, Oscar and Tony.

EGOT, an acronym for the Emmy, Grammy, Oscar and Tony Awards, is the designation given to people who have won all four of the major American entertainment awards. These awards honor excellence in television, recording/music, film and theatre respectively. Achieving the EGOT has been referred to as the "grand slam" of show business.

The term was coined by the actor Philip Michael Thomas in the mid-1980s, who expressed a desire to win all four awards; the acronym gained widespread popularity after being used in the television series 30 Rock, in which the character Tracy Jordan (played by Tracy Morgan) sets out to achieve the feat.

History

The four awards were established in different years: the Tony Awards in 1947, the Emmy Awards in 1949, the Grammy Awards in 1959, and the Academy Awards (Oscars) in 1929. The first person to complete the set was the composer Richard Rodgers in 1962, followed by the actress Helen Hayes in 1977 (who also achieved the "Triple Crown of Acting").

Variations

A related distinction is the Triple Crown of Acting, for winning an Oscar, Emmy and Tony in acting categories. Some performers hold honorary or non-competitive awards that complete the set; these are usually counted separately from competitive EGOT winners. Variants such as "PEGOT" add a Pulitzer Prize or Peabody Award to the four.
