Syria–Lebanon border clashes

The Syria–Lebanon border clashes refer to recurring armed incidents along the roughly 375-kilometre (233 mi) frontier between Syria and Lebanon, which intensified during and after the Syrian civil war. The border, never fully demarcated, runs through mountainous and smuggling-prone terrain, particularly near the Anti-Lebanon Mountains and the Bekaa Valley.

Background

Cross-border tensions arose from smuggling routes, the movement of fighters and weapons, and spillover of the Syrian conflict into Lebanon from 2011 onward, including shelling and raids around Arsal and the Qalamoun region. Hezbollah's intervention in Syria on the side of the Assad government, and the presence of Syrian rebel and jihadist groups near the border, further militarized the frontier.

Later clashes

Following the fall of the Assad regime in December 2024, the new Syrian authorities and the Lebanese Armed Forces confronted armed groups and smuggling networks along the border, with exchanges of fire reported in early 2025 around villages in the Hermel and Homs countryside, prompting both governments to coordinate on border security.
