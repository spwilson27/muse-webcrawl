Metabolic network

A metabolic network is the complete set of metabolic and physical processes that determine the physiological and biochemical properties of a cell. These networks comprise the chemical reactions of metabolism, the metabolic pathways, and the regulatory interactions that guide these reactions. Metabolic networks are commonly represented as graphs in which metabolites are nodes and the reactions (usually catalysed by enzymes) linking them are edges.

Representation of a genome-scale metabolic network

Reconstruction and modelling

With the sequencing of complete genomes, it became possible to reconstruct the network of biochemical reactions in many organisms, from bacteria to man. These reconstructions are assembled from genome annotations, biochemical databases such as KEGG and MetaCyc, and the literature, and can be converted into stoichiometric models amenable to constraint-based analysis. Techniques such as flux balance analysis predict growth rates and metabolic phenotypes by optimising an objective (for example biomass production) subject to mass-balance and capacity constraints, and have been applied to metabolic engineering and the study of disease.

Network properties

Analyses of metabolic networks have found scale-free features — a few highly connected hub metabolites such as ATP, NADH, and glutamate amid many sparsely connected compounds — as well as modularity corresponding roughly to classical pathways, and robustness arising from alternative routes through the network. These properties have been used to predict enzyme essentiality and to identify drug targets, especially in pathogens and cancer cells.
