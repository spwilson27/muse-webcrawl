Tart

A tart is a baked dish consisting of a filling over a pastry base with an open top not covered with pastry. The pastry is usually shortcrust, and the filling may be sweet or savoury, though modern tarts are generally fruit-based, sometimes filled with custard. Tarts are typically shallow, with firm, crumbly pastry, and are often baked in a fluted tin with a removable base; they are distinguished from pies, which usually have a pastry lid and deeper, softer crusts.

History

Tarts developed from medieval cookery, in which raised pastry cases ("coffins") held savoury fillings of meat and fruit; sweet fruit tarts became prominent in the 16th century as sugar prices fell. French pâtisserie refined the form, producing classics such as the tarte aux fruits, tarte au citron and the upside-down tarte Tatin, said to have been created by the Tatin sisters at Lamotte-Beuvron in the 1880s.

Varieties

Well-known examples include the French lemon tart and fruit tart with crème pâtissière; the English Bakewell tart (almond frangipane and jam), Manchester tart, gypsy tart and custard tart; the Portuguese pastel de nata; the Hong Kong egg tart; and savoury tarts such as quiche Lorraine and the Italian crostata's savoury counterpart, torta salata. Small individual tarts are called tartlets.
