Waziristan is a mountainous region in northwestern Pakistan, bordering Afghanistan, covering about 11,585 square kilometres. It comprises the districts of North Waziristan and South Waziristan, which until 2018 formed part of the Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA) before FATA's merger into the province of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. The region takes its name from the Wazir tribe of Pashtuns, who, together with the Mehsuds, are its dominant inhabitants. Its chief towns include Miranshah and Razmak in the north and Wana in the south.

History

Waziristan's fierce tribal independence made it a byword for resistance to outside rule. British forces mounted repeated expeditions into the region from the 19th century onward, and the interwar Waziristan campaign (1936–39) was among the largest British military efforts on the North-West Frontier, provoked in part by the resistance movement of the Pashtun leader known as the Faqir of Ipi. Under British and then Pakistani administration, the tribal agencies were governed indirectly through political agents and tribal elders under the Frontier Crimes Regulation.

War on Terror period

After 2001, Waziristan became a refuge for Taliban and al-Qaeda fighters fleeing Afghanistan, and a focus of US drone strikes and Pakistani military operations. The Pakistani Taliban (Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan) was formed in 2007 under Baitullah Mehsud, based in South Waziristan. Major operations, including Rah-e-Nijat (2009) and Zarb-e-Azb (2014), displaced hundreds of thousands of residents. The 2018 FATA merger extended Pakistan's constitution, courts, and regular policing to the region for the first time.

Geography and society

The terrain is a maze of barren ridges and seasonal streams (nullahs), rising to peaks above 3,000 metres near the Afghan border. Society follows Pashtunwali, the Pashtun code of honour, administered through jirgas (councils of elders). The region lies along the contested Durand Line, which many local Pashtuns do not recognise as a border.
