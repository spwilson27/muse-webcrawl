Joseph Paul DiMaggio (November 25, 1914 – March 8, 1999), nicknamed "Joltin' Joe", "The Yankee Clipper" and "Joe D.", was an American baseball center fielder who played his entire 13-year career in Major League Baseball for the New York Yankees. He is best remembered for his 56-game hitting streak (May 15 – July 16, 1941), a record widely considered unbreakable.

Career

The son of Sicilian immigrants, DiMaggio broke in with the Yankees in 1936 and helped them to four consecutive World Series titles. During his 1941 streak he hit .408; he finished his career with a .325 batting average, 361 home runs and 1,537 runs batted in, despite losing three prime seasons (1943–45) to service in the US Army Air Forces. The Yankees won nine World Series in his thirteen seasons. A three-time American League Most Valuable Player (1939, 1941, 1947), he retired after the 1951 season and was elected to the Baseball Hall of Fame in 1955.

Cultural figure

DiMaggio's brief marriage to Marilyn Monroe (1954) and his guarded dignity made him an enduring symbol of a bygone America: he is name-checked in Simon & Garfunkel's "Mrs. Robinson" ("Where have you gone, Joe DiMaggio?") and figures centrally in Hemingway's The Old Man and the Sea. Monroe's death in 1962 devastated him; for twenty years he had roses delivered to her crypt three times a week.
