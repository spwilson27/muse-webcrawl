Artery

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An artery is a blood vessel that carries blood away from the Heart. Most arteries carry oxygen-rich blood to the tissues; the exceptions are the pulmonary arteries, which carry oxygen-poor blood to the lungs, and the umbilical arteries in the fetus. Arteries have thick, elastic, muscular walls that withstand and smooth the high pressure generated by each heartbeat.

Structure

An artery wall has three layers: an inner endothelium (tunica intima), a middle layer of smooth muscle and elastic fibres (tunica media — especially thick in arteries), and an outer connective tissue layer (tunica adventitia). The largest artery, the aorta, is highly elastic; smaller arteries and arterioles adjust their diameter to control blood flow and Blood pressure.

Disease

Arteries are the site of atherosclerosis, in which fatty plaques narrow the lumen and can trigger clots causing heart attacks and strokes. Weakened walls can balloon into aneurysms. In contrast, veins return blood to the heart at low pressure and capillaries exchange materials with tissues.
