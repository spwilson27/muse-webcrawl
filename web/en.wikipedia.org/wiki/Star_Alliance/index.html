The Star Alliance is an airline alliance headquartered in Frankfurt, Germany. Founded on 14 May 1997, it was the world's first global airline alliance and remains one of the largest, alongside SkyTeam and Oneworld. Its founding members were United Airlines, Lufthansa, Air Canada, Scandinavian Airlines and Thai Airways.

Member airlines coordinate schedules, codeshare extensively, and offer reciprocal frequent-flyer benefits and lounge access. Passengers holding Star Alliance Gold status receive priority services across all member carriers. The alliance's slogan is "The way the Earth connects."

History

The alliance was announced on 14 May 1997 by five airlines from three continents. Air New Zealand, ANA and Ansett Australia joined in 1999. Membership expanded through the 2000s with carriers such as Singapore Airlines (2000), Asiana (2003), US Airways (2004), Air China and Turkish Airlines (2007–2008) and Egyptair. Scandinavian Airlines, a founding member, left the alliance in 2024 after its restructuring and joined SkyTeam.

Members

Full members include Aegean Airlines, Air Canada, Air China, Air India, Air New Zealand, ANA, Asiana Airlines, Austrian Airlines, Avianca, Brussels Airlines, Copa Airlines, Croatia Airlines, Egyptair, Ethiopian Airlines, EVA Air, LOT Polish Airlines, Lufthansa, Shenzhen Airlines, Singapore Airlines, South African Airways, SWISS, TAP Air Portugal, Thai Airways, Turkish Airlines and United Airlines. A "Connecting Partner" model, launched in 2015, extends the network through lower-cost and regional carriers such as Juneyao Airlines.

Services and benefits

  • Reciprocal mileage earning and redemption across members
  • Star Alliance Gold and Silver recognition tiers
  • Access to more than 1,000 airport lounges worldwide for eligible passengers
  • Co-location of members at major hub terminals, such as London Heathrow Terminal 2 ("the Queen's Terminal")
  • Round-the-world and regional airpass fares
