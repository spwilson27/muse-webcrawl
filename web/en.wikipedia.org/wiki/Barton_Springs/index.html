Barton Springs

Barton Springs Pool in Zilker Park, Austin.

Barton Springs is a group of natural springs in Zilker Park in Austin, Texas, fed by the Edwards Aquifer. The four springs — Main Barton Spring (Parthenia), Eliza (Zenobia) Spring, Old Mill (Sunken Garden) Spring and Upper Barton Spring — discharge on average tens of millions of gallons of water per day into Barton Creek, a tributary of the Colorado River.

Barton Springs Pool

The main spring fills Barton Springs Pool, a three-acre public swimming pool created by damming the creek in the 1920s. The pool maintains a near-constant temperature of about 68–70 °F (20–21 °C) year-round and is one of Austin's signature attractions, drawing roughly 800,000 visitors a year in recent decades. It is open daily for much of the year, with free early-morning "swim at your own risk" hours.

Ecology and conservation

The springs are habitat for the endangered Barton Springs salamander (Eurycea sosorum), listed under the US Endangered Species Act in 1997, and the Austin blind salamander, discovered in the springs system in 2001. Protecting the springs' water quality and recharge zone has been a defining issue of Austin environmental politics since the "Save Our Springs" movement and the citizen-initiated Save Our Springs ordinance of 1992.

History

The springs were long used by Tonkawa and other Indigenous peoples. They are named for William "Uncle Billy" Barton, who settled by them in 1837. In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries the springs were a popular resort; the surrounding land was acquired by Andrew Zilker and deeded to the city, forming the core of Zilker Park.
