Bronchiectasis

Bronchiectasis is a disease in which there is permanent abnormal dilation of parts of the airways of the lung (the bronchi). Symptoms typically include a chronic cough with production of mucus (sputum), shortness of breath, chest pain and coughing up blood. Recurrent chest infections are characteristic.

CT appearance of bronchiectasis, with dilated airways larger than their accompanying arteries.

Causes

Bronchiectasis results from a "vicious cycle" in which infection and inflammation damage the airway wall and impair mucus clearance, predisposing to further infection. Causes include severe or recurrent pneumonia, tuberculosis, cystic fibrosis, primary ciliary dyskinesia, immune deficiency and allergic bronchopulmonary aspergillosis; in many cases no cause is found. Diagnosis is by high-resolution CT scanning of the chest.

Treatment

There is no cure, but treatment aims to keep airways clear and control infection: daily airway-clearance physiotherapy, inhaled bronchodilators and saline, prompt antibiotics for exacerbations, long-term macrolide therapy in selected patients, and vaccination against influenza and pneumococcus. Surgery is occasionally used for localised disease complicated by severe bleeding.

History

The condition was described by René Laennec in 1819. It became less common in the developed world with antibiotics and immunisation, but prevalence is rising again, and it remains a major burden in poorer populations and in people with cystic fibrosis.
