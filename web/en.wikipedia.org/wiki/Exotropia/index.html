Exotropia

Exotropia is a form of strabismus in which one or both eyes turn outward, away from the nose. It is the opposite of esotropia (crossed eyes). Exotropia may be constant or intermittent; intermittent exotropia, in which the deviation appears mainly when viewing distant objects, when tired or daydreaming, is one of the most common forms of strabismus in children.

Signs and symptoms

Affected individuals may close one eye in bright sunlight — a characteristic sign — and may experience double vision (diplopia) when the deviation is manifest, though young children typically suppress the image from the deviating eye. Untreated, intermittent exotropia can progress in some patients to constant exotropia, with loss of binocular vision and stereopsis; unlike esotropia in childhood, it less commonly causes amblyopia because the deviation is usually intermittent and alternating.

Causes and classification

Exotropia is classified as congenital (infantile), presenting in the first year of life, or acquired. Acquired forms include intermittent exotropia, sensory exotropia (secondary to poor vision in one eye), consecutive exotropia (following surgery for esotropia), and forms associated with craniofacial anomalies or neurological conditions such as third cranial nerve palsy. The underlying mechanism involves an imbalance of the vergence system controlling eye alignment.

Treatment

Management may include glasses to correct refractive error, treatment of amblyopia, and observation for mild intermittent cases. Overminus lens therapy and part-time patching are sometimes used in children. When the deviation is frequent or constant, surgery on the extraocular muscles — commonly weakening the lateral rectus muscles (recession) — is performed to realign the eyes.
