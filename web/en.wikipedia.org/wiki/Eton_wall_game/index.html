Eton wall game

The Eton wall game is a game that originated at Eton College in England. It has similarities to both association football and rugby football, and is played on a strip of ground 5 metres wide and 110 metres long next to a slightly curved brick wall, built in 1717. The most important match of the year is played on St Andrew's Day, between the Collegers (King's Scholars) and the Oppidans.

Play

The game revolves around the bully, a formation resembling a scrum, which forms against the wall and can last for extended periods as the ball is worked up or down the field. Goals are extremely rare: a team scores a goal by kicking the ball through a designated door at one end, or a "shy" by raising the ball against the wall in the calx area at the other end and touching it. A goal is worth ten points and a shy one point. No goal was scored in the St Andrew's Day match between 1909 and 2009, when a Colleger ended the century-long drought.

Because the rules were never fully codified until relatively late and are largely transmitted by senior players, the wall game is famous for its impenetrability to spectators, who typically see long, static scrummages interspersed with sudden bursts of kicking and running.

Related games

Eton also gave its name to the Eton field game, a more conventional football code played away from the wall. Both are among the many public-school football variants from which the modern games of football and rugby evolved.
