Bania (caste)

Bania (also spelled Baniya, Vania, or Vaniya) is a broad occupational designation for the traditional merchant, trading, and moneylending castes of India. The word derives from the Sanskrit vaṇij, meaning "trader" or "merchant." Banias fall within the Vaishya varna, the third of the four classical varnas, and have historically dominated commerce, banking, and retail trade in much of northern and western India.

Communities

"Bania" is an umbrella term covering numerous endogamous sub-castes and communities, among them the Agrawals, Maheshwaris, Oswals, Khandelwals, Porwals, and Shrimalis of Rajasthan, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, and Uttar Pradesh. Many Bania families are Hindu, a substantial number are Jain, and some follow Sikhism or other faiths; Mahatma Gandhi, the most famous member of the Modh Bania community of Gujarat, often wrote about how his family's mercantile background shaped his views on trusteeship and non-possession.

History and role

Bania merchants financed Indian Ocean trade, operated the hundi bill-of-exchange networks, and served as bankers to Mughal, Maratha, and later British administrations. In the colonial period they were prominent both as collaborators with and competitors of European trading companies, and Bania capital underlay much of India's early industrial development, including the mills of the Birla and other great business houses. Today Bania communities remain heavily represented in Indian business and in the commercial diaspora of East Africa, Britain, and North America.
