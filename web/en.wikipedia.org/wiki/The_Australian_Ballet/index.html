The Australian Ballet

The Australian Ballet (TAB) is the largest classical ballet company in Australia. It was founded in 1962 by J. C. Williamson's impresario Dame Peggy van Praagh, with its first performance at Her Majesty's Theatre, Sydney, in November of that year. The company is based in Melbourne, with its primary performance venue the State Theatre of Arts Centre Melbourne, and it regularly performs at the Sydney Opera House and in other Australian and international venues.

History

The company grew out of the Borovansky Ballet, founded by Edouard Borovansky. Van Praagh directed the company until 1974 (with a break in the late 1960s), establishing a repertoire mixing classics such as Swan Lake and Giselle with new Australian works. Rudolf Nureyev's 1964 guest appearances and his film of Don Quixote (1973), made with the company, brought it international attention. Later artistic directors have included Maina Gielgud, Ross Stretton, David McAllister (2001–2020), and David Hallberg, who took up the post in 2021.

The Australian Ballet School, founded in 1964, is the company's associate school and the main feeder of dancers. The company tours internationally and has a repertoire including works by Graeme Murphy, Stephen Baynes, and other Australian choreographers, alongside the 19th-century classics.
