New York City Marathon

The New York City Marathon (currently branded the TCS New York City Marathon for sponsorship reasons) is an annual marathon held on the first Sunday of November in New York City. Organized by New York Road Runners, it is the largest marathon in the world, regularly drawing more than 50,000 finishers and an estimated one million spectators.

Course

The race first ran in 1970 entirely within Central Park, with 127 starters. Since 1976 the course has traversed all five boroughs: starting on Staten Island, crossing the Verrazzano-Narrows Bridge into Brooklyn, passing through Queens and over the Queensboro Bridge into Manhattan, briefly entering the Bronx, and returning down Fifth Avenue to finish in Central Park near Tavern on the Green.

History

Under race director Fred Lebow, the five-borough marathon became a civic celebration and a model for big-city races worldwide. Grete Waitz set a course standard by winning the women's race nine times between 1978 and 1988. The 2012 race was cancelled in the aftermath of Hurricane Sandy, and the 2020 race was cancelled because of the COVID-19 pandemic. It is one of the World Marathon Majors.
