Sodium hydride

Sodium hydride is the chemical compound with the empirical formula NaH. This alkali metal hydride is primarily used as a strong yet manageable base in organic synthesis. NaH is a saline (salt-like) hydride, composed of Na+ and H− ions, in contrast to molecular hydrides such as borane and methane.

Properties and handling

Sodium hydride is a grey-white crystalline solid that reacts violently with water, liberating hydrogen and sodium hydroxide. It is insoluble in organic solvents. Because the pure powder is pyrophoric, it is usually sold as a 60% dispersion in mineral oil, which is far easier and safer to handle. Ignition can occur on contact with moist air.

Applications

NaH is widely used to deprotonate alcohols, phenols, amides, thiols and weakly acidic C–H bonds, generating alkoxides and hydrogen gas; it is a standard reagent for Williamson ether synthesis, Claisen and Dieckmann condensations, and for drying solvents indirectly via preparation of alkoxides. It also serves in the production of sodium borohydride and as a reducing agent in some contexts, though its primary role is as a base since the hydride is much less nucleophilic in its ionic lattice.
