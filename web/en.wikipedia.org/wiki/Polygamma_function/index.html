Polygamma function

In mathematics, the polygamma function of order m is a meromorphic function on the complex plane defined as the (m+1)-th derivative of the logarithm of the gamma function:

ψ(m)(z) = dm+1/dzm+1 ln Γ(z) = dm/dzm ψ(z)

Thus ψ(0)(z) = ψ(z) = Γ′(z)/Γ(z) is the digamma function, and ψ(1)(z) is the trigamma function. Higher orders are sometimes called the tetragamma, pentagamma, and so on.

Series representation

The polygamma function has the series representation, valid for m ≥ 1 and z not a non-positive integer:

ψ(m)(z) = (−1)m+1 m! Σk=0∞ 1/(z + k)m+1

which relates it to the Hurwitz zeta function: ψ(m)(z) = (−1)m+1 m! ζ(m+1, z). It satisfies the recurrence relation ψ(m)(z+1) = ψ(m)(z) + (−1)m m! / zm+1, and a reflection formula in terms of cotangent derivatives.

Special values

At positive integers, the polygamma functions reduce to expressions involving the Riemann zeta function and the Euler–Mascheroni constant γ: ψ(n) = Hn−1 − γ for the digamma function (where H is a harmonic number), and ψ(m)(1) = (−1)m+1 m! ζ(m+1). Asymptotic expansions for large |z| are given in terms of Bernoulli numbers.
