Dione is a moon of Saturn. It was discovered in 1684 by Giovanni Domenico Cassini and named after the Titaness Dione of Greek mythology. With a diameter of about 1,123 km it is the 15th-largest moon in the Solar System, and it is composed mainly of water ice with a substantial rocky core.

Orbit and rotation

Dione orbits Saturn at a distance of about 377,400 km — roughly the distance from Earth to the Moon — completing one revolution in 2.737 days and rotating synchronously so that the same face always points toward Saturn. It shares its orbit with the small trojan moons Helene (at the L4 Lagrange point) and Polydeuces (at L5). Dione is also locked in a 1:2 orbital resonance with Enceladus, completing one orbit for every two of Enceladus.

Surface

The surface is heavily cratered, with bright, wispy lineaments — now understood to be the faces of ice cliffs and graben rather than cryovolcanic deposits — prominent on the trailing hemisphere. The leading hemisphere bears dark material thought to be deposited from Saturn's rings and other moons. Data from the Cassini spacecraft suggested Dione may harbour a subsurface ocean beneath its ice crust.

Exploration

Voyager 1 and 2 imaged Dione in 1980–81, and Cassini made five close flybys between 2005 and 2015, coming within about 100 km of the surface.
