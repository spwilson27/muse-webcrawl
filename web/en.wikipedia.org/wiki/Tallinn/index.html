Tallinn is the capital and most populous city of Estonia, situated on the shore of the Gulf of Finland in the Baltic Sea. Its UNESCO-listed Old Town is one of the best-preserved medieval cities in Europe: founded as a Danish stronghold in 1219 (the city's name is traditionally derived from taani linnus, "Danish castle"), it flourished as Reval, a Hanseatic trading port, with merchant houses, guild halls, the Gothic Town Hall (1404) and walls and towers — including "Kiek in de Kök" and Stout Margaret — that still ring the lower town beneath Toompea hill.

History

Tallinn passed among Danish, Teutonic, Swedish and Russian rulers, became the capital of independent Estonia in 1918, was occupied by the USSR and Nazi Germany in the Second World War (suffering a devastating Soviet bombing in March 1944), and spent 1944–91 as the capital of the Estonian SSR. The "Singing Revolution" of the late 1980s — mass song festivals at Tallinn's Song Festival Grounds — helped restore independence in 1991. Modern Tallinn brands itself on digital government: Estonia's e-ID, e-residency and online public services are showcased from Tallinn, and Skype was built here. The city balances a large Russian-speaking minority (roughly a third of residents) with Estonian nationhood, a tension sharpened since Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine; ferries link it daily across the gulf to Helsinki.
