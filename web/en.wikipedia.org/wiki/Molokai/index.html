Molokai (Hawaiian: Molokaʻi) is the fifth most populous of the eight main Hawaiian Islands and the fifth-largest in area. It lies in the Kaiwi Channel between Oahu and Maui. The island is administered as part of Maui County, except for the Kalaupapa Peninsula, which is separately administered as Kalawao County. Its principal town is Kaunakakai.

Geography

Molokai was formed by two shield volcanoes, West Molokai and the larger East Molokai, whose highest point is Kamakou at 1,512 metres. The northern coast of East Molokai features some of the highest sea cliffs in the world, dropping over 1,100 metres to the Pacific. Between the two mountains lies a flat agricultural plain once dominated by pineapple plantations.

History

The island was first settled by Polynesian voyagers, and was long famed as a centre of powerful kahuna (priests). In 1866, fearing the spread of leprosy (Hansen's disease), the Kingdom of Hawaii established an isolation settlement on the remote Kalaupapa Peninsula, to which more than 8,000 patients were exiled over the following century. The Belgian priest Father Damien (Saint Damien of Molokai) worked among the patients from 1873 until his own death from the disease in 1889, and Mother Marianne Cope later led the care there. Mandatory isolation ended in 1969; a few former patients still choose to live at Kalaupapa, now part of a National Historical Park.

Economy and culture

Molokai has the highest percentage of Native Hawaiian residents of the main islands and has repeatedly resisted large-scale tourism development. Agriculture, ranching, and small-scale tourism centred on ecotourism and cultural experiences form the economy.
