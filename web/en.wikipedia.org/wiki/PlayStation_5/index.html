The PlayStation 5 (PS5) is a home video game console developed by Sony Interactive Entertainment. It was announced in 2019 as the successor to the PlayStation 4 and launched on 12 November 2020 in Australia, Japan, New Zealand, North America, and South Korea, with a worldwide release a week later. Together with Microsoft's Xbox Series X and Series S, released the same month, it belongs to the ninth generation of consoles.

Hardware

The PS5 is based on a custom AMD system-on-a-chip with an eight-core Zen 2 CPU and an RDNA 2 GPU supporting hardware-accelerated ray tracing, paired with 16 GB of GDDR6 memory. Its defining feature is a custom solid-state drive engineered for extremely fast streaming of game data — 5.5 GB/s raw throughput — which dramatically reduced loading times and changed how games are designed. The console outputs up to 4K at 120 Hz and supports 3D audio through the Tempest engine. It launched in two models: one with an Ultra HD Blu-ray drive and a cheaper Digital Edition without one.

DualSense controller

The bundled DualSense controller replaced the DualShock line, adding haptic feedback driven by voice-coil actuators and adaptive triggers whose resistance can vary programmatically, alongside the established touchpad, motion sensing, and a built-in microphone.

Launch and revisions

Demand vastly outstripped supply for the console's first two years because of pandemic-era semiconductor shortages and scalping. The PS5 is backward compatible with the great majority of PlayStation 4 games. A slimmer redesign in 2023 made the disc drive detachable and optional, the PlayStation VR2 headset launched in February 2023, and a more powerful PlayStation 5 Pro was released in November 2024. Major titles include Astro's Playroom (bundled), Demon's Souls (2020), Ratchet & Clank: Rift Apart, God of War Ragnarök, Marvel's Spider-Man 2, and Astro Bot.
