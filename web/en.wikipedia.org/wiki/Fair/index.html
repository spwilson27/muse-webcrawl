Fair
TypesTrade fair, county fair, funfair, street fair, world's fair
OriginsMedieval chartered markets and religious festivals

A fair is a gathering of people for a variety of entertainment or commercial activities. Fairs are typically temporary, lasting from an afternoon to several weeks, and are held on a recurring basis. Historically, fairs were among the most important trading events in premodern economies, when merchants travelled long distances to buy and sell goods at chartered annual gatherings.

History

The word "fair" derives from the Latin feria, meaning a holiday or feast day. In medieval Europe, fairs grew up around religious festivals and were granted by royal charter, which conferred privileges such as protection for travelling merchants and the right to hold a special court, the court of piepowders, to settle disputes. The great Champagne fairs of 12th- and 13th-century France formed a cycle that linked the textile towns of Flanders with Italy and were central to medieval commerce.

Types of fair

Trade fairs (trade shows or expos) are exhibitions where companies in a specific industry demonstrate products and services. County and state fairs in North America showcase agriculture and livestock alongside competitions, food stalls and midway rides. A funfair is a travelling amusement show with rides and games of chance, while street fairs and village fêtes are smaller local celebrations. The largest international gatherings are the world's fairs (expos), held under the auspices of the Bureau International des Expositions.

Entertainment

Traditional fairground entertainment includes carousel and Ferris wheel rides, prize games, sideshows, livestock judging, baking and craft competitions, concerts and fireworks. Fair food — such as candy floss, corn dogs, funnel cakes and deep-fried novelties — has become a distinctive attraction of North American fairs.
