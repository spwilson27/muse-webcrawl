Maryland Campaign

Maryland Campaign
Part of the American Civil War
Date: September 4–20, 1862
Location: Maryland, Virginia
Result: Union strategic victory

The Maryland Campaign (or Antietam Campaign) occurred September 4–20, 1862, during the American Civil War. Confederate General Robert E. Lee's Army of Northern Virginia, after victories in the Northern Virginia Campaign, crossed the Potomac River into Maryland hoping to resupply, recruit Marylanders, influence Northern public opinion before the midterm elections, and win European recognition.

Background

After the Second Battle of Bull Run, Lee decided to take the war into enemy territory. His army was poorly supplied and he hoped to feed it from Maryland farms. A copy of Lee's Special Orders No. 191 fell into Union hands, giving Major General George B. McClellan crucial intelligence about Lee's dispersed forces.

Battles

The campaign included the capture of Harpers Ferry, the Battles of South Mountain (September 14), and the Battle of Antietam (September 17), the bloodiest single day in American history with about 22,700 casualties. The fighting ended with the Battle of Shepherdstown as Lee withdrew into Virginia.

Aftermath

Although tactically inconclusive, Antietam was a strategic Union victory. It halted Lee's invasion and gave President Abraham Lincoln the confidence to issue the Emancipation Proclamation, which discouraged foreign intervention on behalf of the Confederacy.
