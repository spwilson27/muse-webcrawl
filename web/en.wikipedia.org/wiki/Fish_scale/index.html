Fish scale

Fish scale

Anatomical systemIntegumentary system
MaterialDentine, enamel-like ganoine, bone, collagen
FunctionProtection, hydrodynamics, camouflage

A fish scale is a small rigid plate that grows out of the skin of a fish. The skin of most jawed fishes is covered with these protective scales, which can also provide effective camouflage through the use of reflection and colouration, as well as possible hydrodynamic advantages. The term scale derives from the Old French escale, meaning a shell pod or husk.

Scales vary enormously in size, shape, structure, and extent, ranging from strong and rigid armour plates in fishes such as shrimpfishes and boxfishes, to microscopic or absent in fishes such as eels and anglerfishes. The morphology of a scale can be used to identify the species of fish it came from, and growth rings (circuli and annuli) allow the age of individual fish to be estimated, a technique fundamental to fisheries science.

Types of scales

Placoid scales

Placoid scales, also called dermal denticles, are found on the skin of cartilaginous fishes such as sharks and rays. Structurally they resemble teeth, consisting of a central pulp cavity supplied with blood vessels, surrounded by a conical layer of dentine and capped with an enamel-like vitrodentine. They point backwards, reducing drag and giving shark skin its characteristic sandpaper-like texture.

Ganoid scales

Ganoid scales are found in gars, bichirs, and some other primitive ray-finned fishes. They are flat, diamond-shaped, and covered with a hard enamel-like layer called ganoine over a bony base, interlocking to form a tough armour.

Cycloid and ctenoid scales

Most bony fishes have either cycloid scales, which are smooth and round, or ctenoid scales, which bear tiny comb-like teeth (ctenii) along the rear edge. These thin, overlapping scales provide flexibility together with protection, and grow with the fish throughout its life.

Cosmoid scales

Cosmoid scales, with a complex layered structure including a spongy bone layer and cosmine, are characteristic of lobe-finned fishes; among living fish they are found only on coelacanths.

Development and growth

Scales develop from the dermis. In bony fishes, new circuli are added at the margin as the fish grows, so that the pattern of rings records seasonal growth much like the rings of a tree. Scales can be regenerated if lost, although regenerated scales lack the original ring pattern at their centre.

In human culture

Fish scales have been used historically in folk medicine, as a source of collagen and gelatin, in crafts and jewellery (including artificial mother-of-pearl), and the reflective crystals of guanine in some scales were once harvested for use in cosmetics and pearl essence.
