WordPress

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
WordPress
Released27 May 2003
DevelopersMatt Mullenweg, Mike Little; WordPress Foundation
Written inPHP
TypeContent management system / blogging platform
[Image: The WordPress logo: a white capital W in a circle on a blue-grey disc]

WordPress is a free and open-source content management system (CMS) written in PHP and paired with a MySQL or MariaDB database. Features include a plugin architecture and a template system, referred to within WordPress as 'Themes'. It is the most-used CMS in the world, powering — by most surveys — more than 40% of all websites.

WordPress began in 2003 when Matt Mullenweg and Mike Little forked the stalled b2/cafelog blogging software. It grew from a personal blogging tool into a general platform through its plugin ecosystem (tens of thousands of plugins, from the WooCommerce shop system to security and SEO suites), its themes market, and the hosted service WordPress.com run by Mullenweg's company Automattic, alongside the self-hosted software from WordPress.org.

Its dominance makes it a bellwether and a target: a large share of web compromises involve outdated WordPress sites, and its decisions (such as the 2018 Gutenberg block editor) shape web publishing generally. A public dispute in 2024 between Automattic and the hosting company WP Engine over trademarks and contributions drew unusual attention to the governance of a project on which so much of the web depends.
