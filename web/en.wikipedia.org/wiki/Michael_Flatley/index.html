Born: July 16, 1958, Chicago, Illinois, U.S.

Known for: Riverdance, Lord of the Dance, Feet of Flames, Celtic Tiger

Michael Ryan Flatley (born July 16, 1958) is an American dancer and choreographer of Irish descent, credited with reinventing traditional Irish dance by incorporating upper-body movement, contemporary rhythms, and theatrical spectacle. He rose to worldwide fame as the male lead in Riverdance and then as creator and star of Lord of the Dance, Feet of Flames, and Celtic Tiger.

Early life and training

Flatley was born in Chicago to Irish immigrant parents and began dance lessons at age 11 at the Dennehy School of Irish Dance, initially turned away for being "too old." At 17 he became the first American to win a World Irish Dance Championship title at the Oireachtas Rince na Cruinne, and he also won numerous amateur boxing titles and became an accomplished flautist. In 1989 he set a Guinness World Record for tapping speed at 28 taps per second, later raising it to 35 taps per second; his legs were famously insured for tens of millions of dollars.

Riverdance and Lord of the Dance

Flatley achieved international fame as choreographer and lead dancer of the seven-minute interval act performed at the 1994 Eurovision Song Contest in Dublin, which was expanded into the full-length show Riverdance in February 1995, with music by Bill Whelan. After a dispute with the show's producers, Flatley left and created Lord of the Dance, premiering in Dublin in 1996; it became one of the highest-grossing dance tours of all time. The expanded arena version, Feet of Flames, performed in Hyde Park, London in 1998, was billed as his farewell to that role, though he continued to tour in new productions including Celtic Tiger (2005) and a 3D film of Lord of the Dance (2011).

Legacy

Flatley's shows transformed Irish dance from a competitive, arms-at-the-sides tradition into a global commercial theatre form, touring to tens of millions of spectators. He officially retired from touring in 2016 after performing in Las Vegas, citing injuries.
