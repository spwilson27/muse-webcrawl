Byzantine architecture

Byzantine architecture is the building style of the Byzantine (Eastern Roman) Empire, spanning roughly the 4th to 15th centuries. It is characterised by massive domes carried on pendentives, rich mosaic decoration, brick and stone construction, and centrally planned churches, reaching its apex in Justinian I's Hagia Sophia in Constantinople (537 AD).

HallmarksDomes on pendentives, mosaics, cross-in-square plans

Development

Early Byzantine churches adapted Roman basilicas and rotundas. Anthemius of Tralles and Isidore of Miletus solved the problem of placing a round dome over a square bay with pendentives at Hagia Sophia. Later centuries favoured the cross-in-square church with a dome on a drum, exemplified by the Hosios Loukas and Daphni monasteries in Greece.

Influence

Byzantine forms shaped Orthodox church architecture in Greece, Serbia, Bulgaria, and Russia, and influenced Ottoman mosque architecture — Sinan's mosques respond directly to Hagia Sophia — as well as Romanesque and Renaissance building in Italy, notably St Mark's Basilica in Venice.
