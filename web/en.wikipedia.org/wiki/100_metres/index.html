The 100 metres, or 100-metre dash, is a sprint race in track and field competitions. The shortest common outdoor running distance, it is one of the most popular and prestigious events in athletics and has been contested at the Summer Olympics since 1896 for men and since 1928 for women.

The race

Runners start from blocks at the starter's command — "on your marks", "set", and the gun. Races are decided by photo finishes and fully automatic timing; a false start results in disqualification, and tailwinds greater than 2.0 m/s invalidate record times. Sprinters typically reach top speed near 60 metres, and average speeds exceed 35 km/h for elite men.

History

The event descends from the 100-yard dash and the ancient Greek stadion. Its champions, titled the fastest man or woman in the world, include Jesse Owens, Carl Lewis, Usain Bolt and Shelly-Ann Fraser-Pryce. See also 100 metres at the Olympics.
