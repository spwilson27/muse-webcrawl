Singidunum

Singidunum was an ancient Roman city at the confluence of the Sava and Danube rivers, on the site of modern Belgrade, the capital of Serbia. Its name is thought to derive from a Celtic predecessor settlement: the Scordisci tribe established a fortified town there in the 3rd century BC, and "Singidunum" is often interpreted as "fort of the Singi" or "round fort".

Roman period

The Romans conquered the area in the 1st century BC, and Singidunum grew into an important stronghold on the Danube frontier (the limes). From the end of the 1st century AD it was the base of the legion Legio IV Flavia Felix, and it stood on the military road linking the Danube forts with the Via Militaris running through the Balkans toward Constantinople. The city had a castrum (fortress) on the ridge where Belgrade Fortress (Kalemegdan) now stands, with the civilian town spreading down toward the rivers. Singidunum was the birthplace of the emperor Jovian (reigned 363–364), and the emperor Constantine is also sometimes associated with the region. It prospered as a trading and supply centre for the frontier army.

Late antiquity and after

As a frontier city Singidunum suffered repeated attacks: it was devastated by the Huns in 441, taken and retaken during the Gothic and Gepid wars, rebuilt by Justinian I in the 6th century, and finally overwhelmed by the Avars and Slavs in the early 7th century. On its ruins the Slavic and then medieval city of Belgrade ("white city") arose; its fortress preserves foundations and remains from the Roman and later periods, and Roman artefacts — altars, sarcophagi, coins and parts of the aqueduct — are held in Belgrade's museums.
