French Directory

The five Directors governed France from 1795 to 1799.

The Directory (French: Directoire) was the governing five-member committee of the French First Republic from 2 November 1795 until 9 November 1799, when it was overthrown by Napoleon Bonaparte in the coup of 18 Brumaire and replaced by the Consulate. It was established under the Constitution of the Year III following the Thermidorian Reaction against the excesses of the Reign of Terror.

Structure

Executive power was shared among five Directors chosen by the legislature, one retiring each year by lot. Legislative power rested with a bicameral body: the Council of Five Hundred, which proposed laws, and the Council of Ancients, which approved or rejected them. The franchise was restricted by property qualifications. The system was designed to prevent both monarchy and the dictatorship of a single assembly, but it produced chronic instability and deadlock.

History

The Directory faced opposition from royalists on the right and Jacobins on the left, financial crisis and inflation after the collapse of the assignats, corruption among officials and contractors, and continuing war against the First and Second Coalitions. It repeatedly annulled election results through coups — Fructidor (1797) against royalists, Floréal (1798) against Jacobins, and Prairial (1799). Meanwhile the army's prestige grew, especially through Bonaparte's Italian campaigns of 1796–97 and the Egyptian expedition of 1798.

Fall

Convinced the regime could not survive, Director Emmanuel Joseph Sieyès plotted a coup and enlisted Bonaparte, newly returned from Egypt. On 18 Brumaire (9 November 1799) the legislature was intimidated into dissolving the Directory and appointing three provisional consuls, ending the Directory after four turbulent years.
