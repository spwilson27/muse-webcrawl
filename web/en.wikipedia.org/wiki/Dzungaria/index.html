Dzungaria

Dzungaria (also spelled Junggar) is a geographical subregion in Northwest China corresponding to the northern half of Xinjiang, the Junggar Basin. It is bounded by the Altai Mountains to the north, the Tian Shan to the south, and extends west toward Lake Balkhash in Kazakhstan. The region takes its name from the Dzungar Mongols, the western (Oirat) Mongol confederation that ruled it in the 17th and 18th centuries.

Geography

The Junggar Basin is a large sedimentary basin containing the Gurbantünggüt Desert, China's second-largest desert, in its centre. Rivers from the surrounding mountains — notably the Irtysh, which flows north to the Arctic Ocean — water oasis towns around the basin margins. Major cities include Ürümqi (the capital of Xinjiang), Karamay, and Shihezi. The Dzungarian Gate, a mountain pass on the Kazakh border, has historically funneled invaders and trade between the Eurasian steppe and China.

History

The region was successively contested by the Xiongnu, Turkic khaganates, the Mongol Empire, and Chagatai Khanate. In the 17th century the Dzungar Khanate became the last great nomadic empire of Eurasia, clashing with Qing China and Russia. The Qianlong Emperor's campaigns of 1755–1759 destroyed the khanate; the Dzungar population was decimated by war, famine, and smallpox in what many historians describe as a genocide, and the emptied lands were repopulated by Uyghurs, Hui, Han, and other groups.

Economy

Dzungaria holds major oil fields (Karamay), coal deposits, and wind-power installations; Ürümqi is a hub of the Belt and Road Initiative's overland routes to Central Asia.
