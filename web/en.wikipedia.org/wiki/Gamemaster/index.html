Gamemaster


A gamemaster organizes, arbitrates, and narrates a role-playing game.

A gamemaster (also game master, GM) is a player in a multi-player game who acts as an organizer, officiant for questions regarding the rules, arbitrator, and moderator of gameplay. The role is most strongly associated with tabletop role-playing games, in which the gamemaster creates and presents the fictional world, portrays non-player characters, devises challenges and plots, and adjudicates the results of the other players' actions.

Terminology

Different games use different titles: Dungeon Master (DM) in Dungeons & Dragons, Keeper in Call of Cthulhu, Storyteller in White Wolf's systems, Referee in Traveller, and Marshal in Deadlands. The term "gamemaster" originated in play-by-mail gaming in the 1970s.

Duties

The gamemaster typically prepares adventures (or adapts published modules), controls all characters not run by the other players, describes the environment and consequences of actions, interprets and sometimes modifies the rules in play, and keeps the game moving and entertaining. Gamemasters may use a GM screen to hide dice rolls and notes. In online games the term extends to staff who administer servers and events, and in live-action role-playing to the organizers of play. Some modern role-playing games distribute or omit the gamemaster role entirely ("GM-less" games).
