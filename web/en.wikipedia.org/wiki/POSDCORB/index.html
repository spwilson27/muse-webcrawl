POSDCORB


Coined by: Luther Gulick (1937). Field: public administration / management theory.

POSDCORB is an acronym for the seven functions of executive management as formulated by Luther Gulick and Lyndall Urwick in their 1937 work Papers on the Science of Administration. It became a classic framework in public administration and management theory.

The seven functions

Planning — working out what needs to be done and how. Organizing — establishing the formal structure of authority and subdividing work. Staffing — recruiting and training personnel. Directing — making decisions and giving instructions. Coordinating — interrelating the parts of the work. Reporting — keeping superiors and subordinates informed through records and inspection. Budgeting — fiscal planning, accounting, and control.

Context and criticism

Gulick, a leading figure of the classical school of administration, developed POSDCORB while serving on the U.S. President's Committee on Administrative Management (the Brownlow Committee), whose work underpinned the reorganization of the American executive branch. Herbert Simon later criticized the classical "principles" behind such frameworks as mere "proverbs" — paired, mutually contradictory maxims lacking empirical grounding — though POSDCORB remains a staple of introductory administration courses for its tidy summary of managerial work.
