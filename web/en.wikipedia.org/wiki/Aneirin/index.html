Aneirin

Aneirin was an early medieval Brythonic poet, believed to have lived in the late 6th century in the Hen Ogledd, the Brittonic-speaking kingdoms of what is now southern Scotland and northern England. He is traditionally credited with Y Gododdin, one of the oldest surviving works of Welsh poetry.

FlourishedLate 6th century
LanguageOld Welsh / Brythonic
Notable workY Gododdin

Life

Little is known with certainty. Later Welsh tradition names him among the court poets of the north, alongside Taliesin. Some scholars have questioned whether a single historical Aneirin composed the whole of the work attributed to him.

Y Gododdin

The poem is a series of elegies (marwnadau) for the warriors of the Gododdin kingdom who fell at the Battle of Catraeth, conventionally dated around 600 AD, possibly at Catterick. It survives in the 13th-century Book of Aneirin (Llyfr Aneirin), held at Cardiff. The poem contains a possible early reference to King Arthur, debated among scholars.

Legacy

Aneirin is regarded, with Taliesin, as a founding father of Welsh literature.
