Curtis James Jackson III (born 6 July 1975), known professionally as 50 Cent, is an American rapper, actor, television producer and businessman. Raised in South Jamaica, Queens, New York, he became one of the best-selling rappers of the 2000s and later a successful television producer.

Career

Jackson survived being shot nine times in 2000, an attack that derailed his first record deal, and built his profile through mixtapes before being signed by Eminem and Dr. Dre. His major-label debut album, Get Rich or Die Tryin' (2003), powered by the single "In da Club", was a worldwide commercial success, as was its follow-up The Massacre (2005). He founded the G-Unit label and group, and starred in the semi-autobiographical film Get Rich or Die Tryin' (2005).

Business and television

Jackson's early equity stake in Vitamin Water brought him a widely reported windfall when the brand was sold to Coca-Cola in 2007. In the 2010s he moved into television as executive producer and co-star of the crime drama Power (2014–2020) and its spin-offs. He filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2015 and emerged from it in 2017, and continues to record, perform and produce.
