The International Bureau of Weights and Measures (French: Bureau international des poids et mesures, BIPM) is an intergovernmental organisation, created by the Metre Convention of 1875, through which member states act together on matters related to measurement science and measurement standards. It is based at the Pavillon de Breteuil in Saint-Cloud, near Paris, France, on a site granted international (extraterritorial) status.

The BIPM is the world hub of metrology. It coordinates the world-wide measurement system, ensures comparable and internationally accepted measurement results, maintains the international prototype standards historically kept, and operates the key comparison reference values that underpin the mutual recognition of national measurement standards. It works closely with the International Committee for Weights and Measures (CIPM) and the General Conference on Weights and Measures (CGPM), which together form the three organs created by the Metre Convention.

History

The BIPM was established by the Metre Convention signed in Paris on 20 May 1875 by representatives of seventeen nations. Its first tasks were to construct and preserve the new international prototypes of the metre and the kilogram, sanctioned by the first CGPM in 1889, and to carry out comparisons of national standards with the prototypes. The international prototype of the kilogram — a platinum–iridium cylinder known informally as "Le Grand K" — remained the definition of the kilogram until the 2019 revision of the SI, when the unit was redefined in terms of the Planck constant.

Role

Under the direction of the CIPM, the BIPM:

  • establishes fundamental standards and scales for the measurement of the principal physical quantities and maintains international prototypes;
  • carries out comparisons of national and international standards (key comparisons);
  • coordinates metrological activities among national metrology institutes and maintains the BIPM key comparison database (KCDB);
  • performs calibrations for member states and carries out scientific and technical work, including the computation of International Atomic Time (TAI) and Coordinated Universal Time (UTC) from atomic clocks worldwide;
  • provides the secretariat for the CGPM and CIPM and for their consultative committees.

Directors

Directors of the BIPM have included Ole Jacob Broch (1879–1889), J.-René Benoît (1889–1915), Charles Édouard Guillaume (1915–1936, Nobel laureate for the discovery of invar), Albert Pérard, Jean Terrien, Pierre Giacomo, Terry Quinn (1988–2003), Andrew Wallard (2004–2010), Michael Kühne (2011–2012) and Martin Milton (from 2013).
