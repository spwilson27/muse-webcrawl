Unicellular organism

A unicellular organism, also known as a single-celled organism, is an organism that consists of a single cell, unlike a multicellular organism that consists of multiple cells. Organisms fall into two general categories: prokaryotic organisms and eukaryotic organisms. Most prokaryotes are unicellular and are classified into bacteria and archaea. Many eukaryotes are multicellular, but some are unicellular, including protozoa, unicellular algae, and unicellular fungi.

Hypothetical life history

Primitive protocells are thought to have been the precursors of today's unicellular organisms. Although the origin of life is largely still unexplained, in the prevailing theory, the Miller–Urey experiment and similar experiments demonstrated that most amino acids, the chemical constituents of the proteins used in all living organisms, can be synthesized from inorganic compounds under conditions intended to replicate those of the early Earth.

Prokaryotes

Prokaryotes lack a membrane-bound nucleus and other membrane-bound organelles. Bacteria and archaea are the two domains of prokaryotes. They reproduce asexually, usually by binary fission. Some bacteria exchange genetic material through horizontal gene transfer mechanisms such as conjugation, transformation, and transduction.

Eukaryotes

Unicellular eukaryotes possess membrane-bound organelles such as a nucleus, mitochondria, and (in photosynthetic forms) chloroplasts. Examples include amoebae, paramecia, euglenoids, many algae such as diatoms and dinoflagellates, and yeasts. Some unicellular eukaryotes, such as Physarum polycephalum (a slime mold in its plasmodial stage), can grow to be quite large — several centimetres across — while remaining a single cell with many nuclei.

Survival and adaptation

Unicellular organisms inhabit nearly every environment on Earth, from deep-sea hydrothermal vents to polar ice. Some form dormant structures such as cysts or spores to survive adverse conditions. Because a single cell must carry out all life functions, many unicellular organisms have specialized structures for movement (flagella, cilia, pseudopods), feeding, and sensing their environment.
