The Census in Australia, officially the Census of Population and Housing, is the national census conducted every five years by the Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS). It is the largest statistical collection undertaken in Australia and provides a snapshot of the country's population, households, dwellings, income, education, religion, ancestry and languages, informing government funding, electoral boundaries and service planning.

History

The colonies conducted their own censuses from the 19th century; the first simultaneous census across the states was held in 1881, and the first national census after Federation took place in 1911. Early census records were destroyed after statistical tabulation, a practice changed so that, since 2001, respondents may opt in to having their identified census form retained by the National Archives and released after 99 years. The 1967 referendum altered the Constitution to allow Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people to be counted fully in the census, after decades of exclusion from official population figures.

Conduct

The census counts everyone present in Australia on census night, including visitors, and Australians overseas on government service. Questions cover age, sex, birthplace, languages spoken at home, ancestry, religion (the only optional question), marital status, education, occupation, income and housing. Since 2006 an online eCensus option has been offered; in 2016 the ABS website suffered a major outage on census night after distributed denial-of-service attacks, a widely reported failure that led to inquiries and reforms. The 2021 census achieved a high response rate despite COVID-19 lockdowns, recording a population of 25,422,788.

Content and debates

Changes to questions attract public debate: the 2021 census added topics such as long-term health conditions and Australian Defence Force service, while advocacy for questions on gender identity and sexual orientation influenced planning for the 2026 census. Data are released progressively from the year after collection and underpin per-capita grants to states and territories.
