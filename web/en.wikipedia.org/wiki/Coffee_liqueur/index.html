Coffee liqueur

A coffee liqueur is a liqueur flavoured with coffee. It typically combines coffee with a distilled spirit such as rum, vodka, or brandy, together with sugar or another sweetener. Coffee liqueurs are used as ingredients in cocktails and desserts, and are also drunk on their own, often over ice or with cream.

History

Coffee-based alcoholic drinks have a long history: coffee arrived in Europe in the 17th century and was soon combined with spirits. Commercial coffee liqueurs became popular in the 20th century. Kahlúa, created in Veracruz, Mexico in 1936, became the world's best-selling coffee liqueur, made from rum, sugar, and arabica coffee. Tia Maria, originally from Jamaica and dating to the mid-20th century, is another classic brand, made with rum and Blue Mountain coffee.

Uses

Coffee liqueur is an essential ingredient of the White Russian (with vodka and cream) and the Black Russian, and appears in the Espresso Martini, the B-52 layered shot, and the Mudslide. In the kitchen it flavours desserts such as tiramisu and is used in sauces, glazes, and ice creams. Regional varieties include the Spanish licor de café of Galicia and various homemade versions made by infusing spirits with coffee beans.

Production

Producers either infuse the base spirit with roasted coffee beans or grounds, or blend cold-brewed or distilled coffee extracts with the spirit and sweetener. Alcohol content typically ranges from about 15% to 30% ABV. Craft producers in recent years have released less sweet, single-origin coffee liqueurs.
