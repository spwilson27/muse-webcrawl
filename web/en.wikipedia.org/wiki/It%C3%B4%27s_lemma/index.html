Itô's lemma

FieldStochastic calculus
Named afterKiyosi Itô
Published1951
RelatedItô integral, stochastic differential equations

Itô's lemma is an identity used in Itô stochastic calculus to find the differential of a time-dependent function of a stochastic process. It serves as the stochastic calculus counterpart of the chain rule in ordinary calculus and can be heuristically derived by formally applying an ordinary Taylor expansion and using the rules of stochastic calculus.

Statement

For an Itô process dXt = μt dt + σt dBt, where Bt is a Wiener process, and a twice-differentiable function f(t, x), Itô's lemma states that f(t, Xt) is again an Itô process with differential df = (∂f/∂t + μ ∂f/∂x + ½σ² ∂²f/∂x²) dt + σ ∂f/∂x dBt. The extra second-order term ½σ² ∂²f/∂x² — absent from the ordinary chain rule — reflects the fact that Brownian motion has non-zero quadratic variation.

Applications

The lemma, introduced by the Japanese mathematician Kiyosi Itô in 1951, is one of the most widely used tools in mathematical finance. It underlies the derivation of the Black–Scholes equation for option pricing, the dynamics of geometric Brownian motion, and stochastic models of interest rates and volatility throughout quantitative finance.
