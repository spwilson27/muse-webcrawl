Magnesium sulfate

Magnesium sulfate or magnesium sulphate is a chemical compound, a salt with the formula MgSO4, consisting of magnesium cations Mg2+ and sulfate anions SO42−. It is a white crystalline solid, soluble in water but not in ethanol.

FormulaMgSO4
Molar mass120.366 g/mol (anhydrous)
AppearanceWhite crystalline solid
Solubility in water35.1 g/100 mL (20 °C, anhydrous)

Hydrates and Epsom salt

Magnesium sulfate occurs naturally as several hydrate minerals. The most common is the heptahydrate, MgSO4·7H2O, known as Epsom salt (or Epsom salts), named after a bitter saline spring at Epsom in Surrey, England. The monohydrate occurs as the mineral kieserite.

Uses

Medicine

Magnesium sulfate is used medically to treat and prevent low blood magnesium and seizures in women with eclampsia. It is also used in the management of certain heart arrhythmias, severe asthma attacks, and as a laxative. Epsom salt baths are a traditional remedy for muscle soreness, though evidence for systemic absorption is limited.

Agriculture

In gardening and agriculture, magnesium sulfate is used to correct magnesium deficiency in soils, since magnesium is an essential element in the chlorophyll molecule.

Other uses

It is used in beer brewing to adjust water chemistry, in tanning, in the preparation of tofu as a coagulant for soy milk, and as a drying agent in organic synthesis in its anhydrous form.
