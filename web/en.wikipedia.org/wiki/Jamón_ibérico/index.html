The Iberian pig

Jamón ibérico ("Iberian ham") is a variety of cured leg of pork produced from the black Iberian pig, a breed native to the southwestern Iberian Peninsula, raised largely free-range in the oak savannah known as the dehesa. The pig's genetics allow it to store fat within muscle tissue, giving the cured meat its characteristic marbling, deep red colour, and — in animals finished on acorns (bellotas) — a sweet, nutty flavour from oleic-acid-rich fat.

Production and curing

After slaughter, the hind legs are trimmed, salted, rested, washed, and then slowly dried and matured in curing houses (bodegas) where seasonal temperature and humidity changes develop flavour and texture over periods from one year to, for the finest hams, four or more. The front legs produce the cheaper paleta. The ham is sliced thin by hand with a long flexible knife (cuchillo jamonero) by cutters called cortadores.

Grades and labelling

Spanish law (2014 quality standard) grades jamón ibérico by breed purity and feeding, marked by coloured seals: black — 100% Iberian breed, acorn-fed free range (de bellota 100% ibérico, the supreme grade, sometimes called pata negra); red — crossbred (≥50% Iberian) acorn-fed; green — crossbred, pasture and feed finished outdoors (de cebo de campo); white — crossbred, intensively fed (de cebo). Only the black label corresponds to the legendary acorn ham.

Appellations

Four protected designations of origin govern jamón ibérico: Guijuelo (Salamanca), Dehesa de Extremadura, Jabugo (Huelva, famed for Joselito hams), and Los Pedroches (Córdoba). Portuguese production of the same breed and style is presunto ibérico / Barrancos ham.
