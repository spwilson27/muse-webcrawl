Cryptography

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Photograph of an Enigma cipher machine with its rotors visible]

Cryptography is the practice and study of techniques for secure communication in the presence of adversarial behaviour. Historically centred on ciphers that hide the meaning of messages, modern cryptography also covers authentication, digital signatures, key exchange and protocols underpinning computer and network security.

History

Classical ciphers include substitution systems such as the Caesar cipher and polyalphabetic systems such as the Vigenère cipher. In the 20th century, rotor machines such as the German Enigma were broken by Allied cryptanalysts including Polish mathematicians and the team at Bletchley Park. Public-key cryptography, introduced publicly in the 1970s (Diffie–Hellman key exchange and RSA), made secure communication possible between parties with no shared secret.

Modern cryptography

Modern systems combine symmetric encryption (e.g. AES), public-key methods, and cryptographic hash functions. Quantum computing threatens some widely used public-key schemes, motivating research into post-quantum cryptography.
