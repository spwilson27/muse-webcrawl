Webcomics (also known as online comics or Internet comics) are comics published on the internet. Many are exclusively published online, while others appear in print as well but maintain a web presence for marketing or commercial reasons.

History

The earliest comics distributed electronically appeared on CompuServe in the 1980s, such as Witches and Stitches (1985). With the World Wide Web came works like Doctor Fun (1993). The late 1990s and 2000s saw an explosion of strips — Penny Arcade, PvP, Megatokyo and xkcd — as cheap hosting and syndication portals such as Keenspot let creators bypass print gatekeepers entirely.

Formats

Webcomics range from traditional newspaper-style strips and full comic-book pages to experiments with the "infinite canvas" advocated Scott McCloud described. In South Korea, the vertical-scrolling webtoon format, optimised for smartphones, became dominant and spread worldwide through platforms such as Line Webtoon and Tapas.

Business models

Creators earn income through advertising, merchandise, print collections, subscriptions and crowdfunding platforms such as Patreon and Kickstarter. Andrew Hussie's Homestuck raised millions and became a major multimedia phenomenon.

Awards

Webcomics are recognised by the Eisner Awards' digital comic category, the Harvey Awards, and the Web Cartoonists' Choice Awards. Works such as Girl Genius and The Order of the Stick have won major honours.
