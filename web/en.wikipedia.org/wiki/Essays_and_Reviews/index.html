Essays and Reviews

Essays and Reviews is a collection of seven theological essays published in London in 1860, written by six Church of England clergymen and one layman. Applying historical and scientific criticism to the Bible and church doctrine, the volume provoked a major controversy in Victorian Anglicanism, selling some 22,000 copies in two years and, by one contemporary quip, "doing more to liberalise theology than any other book of its time".

Contents

The contributors were Frederick Temple (on the education of the world), Rowland Williams (on Bunsen's biblical research), Baden Powell (on the study of evidences of Christianity), Henry Bristow Wilson (on the national Church), C. W. Goodwin (on the Mosaic cosmogony), Mark Pattison (on the tendencies of religious thought 1688–1750) and Benjamin Jowett ("On the Interpretation of Scripture"). Jowett's essay, urging that the Bible be read "like any other book", attracted particular attention; Baden Powell's contribution openly accepted Darwinian evolution and denied miracles.

Controversy

Published only months after Darwin's On the Origin of Species, the book was condemned by bishops and in Convocation (1864). Williams and Wilson were prosecuted for heresy in the Court of Arches and suspended, but the judgment was overturned on appeal by the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council in 1864 — a decision lampooned as dismissing hell "with costs". The affair helped define the space for liberal, critical theology within the Church of England.
