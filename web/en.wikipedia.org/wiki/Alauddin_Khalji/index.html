Alauddin Khalji

Alauddin Khalji (Persian: علاء الدین خلجی; died 4 January 1316), born Ali Gurshasp, was a ruler of the Khalji dynasty that ruled the Delhi Sultanate in the Indian subcontinent. He reigned from 1296 to 1316 and was the most powerful ruler of his dynasty, expanding the sultanate deep into peninsular India and repeatedly repelling Mongol invasions from the Chagatai Khanate.

Rise to power

Ali Gurshasp was the nephew and son-in-law of Jalaluddin Khalji, founder of the dynasty. As governor of Kara he mounted a spectacular raid on Devagiri in the Deccan (1294/1296) that yielded enormous treasure. In 1296 he had his uncle murdered at Kara and proclaimed himself sultan at Delhi.

Conquests and Mongol wars

Alauddin conquered Gujarat (1299), Ranthambore (1301), Chittor (1303), Malwa (1305), and Jalore, and his general Malik Kafur led expeditions far into the Deccan against Devagiri, Warangal, the Hoysalas, and Madurai, bringing much of southern India into tributary status. Between 1297 and 1306 he defeated a series of large-scale Mongol invasions, fortifying Delhi with the new city of Siri and maintaining a large standing army.

Reforms

To sustain that army, Alauddin enacted famous economic measures: price controls on grain and other goods enforced through state granaries and market officials (the diwan-i-riyasat), measurement of cultivated land, a land revenue demand of half the produce in the core territories, and restrictions on the nobility intended to prevent rebellion. His market regulations were praised by the chronicler Ziauddin Barani, though modern historians debate their reach beyond Delhi.

His final years were marked by illness and the dominance of Malik Kafur. He died in January 1316; the dynasty fell four years later.
