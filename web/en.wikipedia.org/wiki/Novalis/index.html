Novalis

Novalis was the pen name of Georg Philipp Friedrich von Hardenberg (2 May 1772 – 25 March 1801), a German aristocrat, poet, author and philosopher of Early Romanticism. His unfinished novel Heinrich von Ofterdingen and his Hymns to the Night are among the defining works of German Romantic literature.

Life

Hardenberg was born at Schloss Oberwiederstedt in Saxony and studied law at Jena, Leipzig and Wittenberg, where he was influenced by Fichte's philosophy. He worked professionally as a mining engineer and salt-works administrator in Saxony. In 1795 he became engaged to the very young Sophie von Kühn, whose death from tuberculosis in 1797, aged fifteen, affected him profoundly and inspired the Hymns to the Night (published 1800). He later became engaged to Julie von Charpentier. Novalis himself died of tuberculosis in 1801, aged 28.

Works and thought

His writings, much of them published posthumously by his friends Friedrich Schlegel and Ludwig Tieck, include the fragment collections Pollen (Blüthenstaub), the political essay Faith and Love, and the unfinished Christendom or Europe. He developed the idea of "magic idealism" and the Romantic programme of "romanticising the world" — restoring wonder and unity to ordinary life. The blue flower (Blaue Blume) sought by Heinrich in his novel became the central symbol of Romantic longing.
