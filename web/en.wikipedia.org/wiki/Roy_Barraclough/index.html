Roy Barraclough

Roy Barraclough MBE (12 December 1935 – 1 June 2017) was an English comic actor best known for his long-running role as Alec Gilroy, the landlord of the Rovers Return, in the ITV soap opera Coronation Street. With Les Dawson he formed one half of the celebrated comedy double act of the gossiping women Cissie and Ada (Cissie Braithwaite and Ada Shufflebotham), who became a staple of British television comedy in the 1970s.

Early life and career

Barraclough was born in Hull and began his career in variety and repertory theatre, working as a holiday camp entertainer and in drama productions in the north of England. Television appearances followed in programmes such as Z-Cars, and he first appeared in Coronation Street in minor roles in the 1970s before the part for which he became famous.

Cissie and Ada

Alongside comedian Les Dawson, Barraclough created the characters of Cissie Braithwaite (Barraclough) and Ada Shufflebotham (Dawson), two elderly Lancashire women gossiping over the garden fence. The sketches, featuring exaggerated mouthing of unmentionable words and malapropisms, appeared on The Les Dawson Show and in stage tours and were widely voted among the best-loved double acts in British comedy. Barraclough revived Cissie in later years with other partners and in charity performances.

Coronation Street

Barraclough joined Coronation Street in 1986 as Alec Gilroy, a theatrical agent turned pub landlord, and remained a regular until 1992, returning from 1995 to 1998. Alec, sharp-tongued and money-minded, took over the tenancy of the Rovers Return and married Bet Lynch (Julie Goodyear), forming one of the soap's most memorable couples. The character departed in 1998 to run a bar in Brighton. Barraclough's many other credits included appearances in Last of the Summer Wine, Emmerdale, Funland and, in his final screen role in 2016, the revived Are You Being Served?. He was appointed MBE in 2006 for services to drama and charity in the North West.

Death

Barraclough died on 1 June 2017 at Willow Wood Hospice in Ashton-under-Lyne, aged 81, after a short illness. Tributes were paid by Coronation Street colleagues and by the production, which dedicated an episode to him.
