Lithuanian press ban

The Lithuanian press ban (Lithuanian: spaudos draudimas) was a prohibition imposed by the Russian Empire on publications in the Lithuanian language printed in the Latin alphabet. In force from 1865 to 1904, it was part of the Russification campaign launched after the January Uprising of 1863–64. Lithuanian was permitted only in Cyrillic characters, an imposition rejected by most Lithuanians.

Book smugglers

The ban provoked a celebrated resistance movement: books and newspapers were printed abroad, chiefly in Tilsit and other towns in East Prussia (Lithuania Minor), and smuggled across the border by knygnešiai — book smugglers, or "book carriers" — who faced exile to Siberia if caught. Figures such as Bishop Motiejus Valančius organised the smuggling networks, and Jurgis Bielinis became the most famous book carrier. Underground village schools (daraktorinės mokyklos) taught children to read the banned script.

End of the ban

Rather than eradicating Lithuanian print culture, the ban stimulated a national revival: circulation of illegal publications grew steadily, and standard Lithuanian was codified in works by Jonas Jablonskis. The ban was lifted on 7 May 1904, a date still commemorated in Lithuania as Press Restoration, Language and Book Day.
