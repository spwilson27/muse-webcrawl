Media of North Korea

The media of North Korea are among the most strictly controlled in the world. The constitution nominally provides for freedom of the press, but in practice all media are owned and controlled by the government of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea, and serve as outlets for state propaganda centred on the ruling Workers' Party and the Kim leadership.

Organisation

News is supplied by the Korean Central News Agency (KCNA). The principal newspaper is Rodong Sinmun, the organ of the Workers' Party. Television is dominated by Korean Central Television, and radio by the Korean Central Broadcasting Station. Journalists are expected to promote the party line, and foreign media access is extremely limited; radios and televisions sold in the country are fixed to domestic frequencies.

Internet and foreign media

Most citizens have no access to the global Internet, using instead the domestic Kwangmyong intranet. Despite controls, foreign media — South Korean dramas, films and broadcasts — circulate clandestinely on USB drives and via radio, a flow the authorities actively attempt to suppress.
