Thiên Mụ Pagoda

Thiên Mụ Pagoda
Location: Huế, Vietnam
Founded: 1601
Landmark: Phước Duyên tower (1844)

Thiên Mụ Pagoda (Vietnamese: Chùa Thiên Mụ, "Pagoda of the Celestial Lady") is a historic Buddhist temple in the city of Huế in Vietnam. Its iconic seven-storey Phước Duyên tower is regarded as the unofficial symbol of the city. The pagoda stands on Hà Khê hill on the northern bank of the Perfume River, about 5 km from the Imperial City of Huế.

History

The temple was founded in 1601 under the Nguyễn lord Nguyễn Hoàng, following a local legend of a mysterious old woman — the "Celestial Lady" (Thiên Mụ) — who appeared on the hill and prophesied that a lord would come and build a pagoda there. Successive Nguyễn emperors enlarged and renovated the complex; Emperor Thiệu Trị built the 21-metre octagonal Phước Duyên tower in 1844.

Features

Besides the tower, the complex includes halls housing a giant bronze bell cast in 1710 weighing over two tonnes, a pavilion with a large inscribed stele, temples, and gardens. A car displayed at the site — the Austin in which the monk Thích Quảng Đức travelled to Saigon before his self-immolation in 1963 — is preserved as a relic.

Significance

Thiên Mụ is one of the oldest and most revered pagodas in Vietnam and a major destination for pilgrims and visitors to Huế.
