Marid

A marid (Arabic: مارد mārid) is a type of powerful jinn in Islamic tradition and Arabic folklore. Marids are often described as the most powerful class of jinn, associated with the open waters of the seas and oceans, and characterized by their arrogance and pride. The word derives from the Arabic root meaning "rebellious", and in the Quran the term is used as an adjective for rebellious devils (shayatin).

In tradition

In the Quran, "marid" appears as an epithet for defiant demons, as in the verse concerning "every rebellious devil" (shaytan marid). Classical commentators and folklorists expanded the concept: in the hierarchy elaborated in works such as the Egyptian Book of Wonders, jinn were classified into types including the ghoul, the ifrit, the si'lat, and the marid, with the marid at the top in power. Amulets and magical operations intended to bind a marid were considered dangerous undertakings, as marids were unwilling servants and resentful of coercion.

In the One Thousand and One Nights

Marids feature prominently in the One Thousand and One Nights. In "The Fisherman and the Jinni", the fisherman releases a mighty marid from a sealed brass vessel, where it had been imprisoned by Solomon; the marid, after centuries of confinement, first vows to kill whoever frees it. Marids also appear in the tale of the City of Brass and in later stories, often as enormous beings of great strength that can grant wishes, transport people across great distances, or transform matter.

In popular culture

The marid appears in modern fantasy literature and games, usually as a water-associated genie of great power — for example in Jonathan Stroud's Bartimaeus trilogy, where marids rank among the highest classes of spirits, and in role-playing games such as Dungeons & Dragons, where marids are elemental genies of water.
