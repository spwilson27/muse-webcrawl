Karel Reisz

Karel Reisz (21 July 1926 – 25 November 2002) was a Czech-born British filmmaker who was one of the leading figures of the British Free Cinema movement and the British New Wave. He is best known for Saturday Night and Sunday Morning (1960) and The French Lieutenant's Woman (1981).

Early life

Reisz was born in Ostrava, Czechoslovakia, into a Jewish family. He arrived in Britain in 1938 on the Kindertransport after the Nazi occupation; his parents died in Auschwitz. He served in the Czechoslovak section of the RAF, studied at Emmanuel College, Cambridge, and briefly taught before turning to film criticism and documentary.

Career

With Lindsay Anderson, Tony Richardson and others, Reisz signed the Free Cinema manifesto in 1956. His documentaries Momma Don't Allow (1956, with Richardson) and We Are the Lambeth Boys (1959) preceded his landmark feature debut, Saturday Night and Sunday Morning, starring Albert Finney. Later films included Morgan – A Suitable Case for Treatment (1966), Isadora (1968), The Gambler (1974), The French Lieutenant's Woman (1981), Sweet Dreams (1985) and Everybody Wins (1990). He also co-wrote the influential book The Technique of Film Editing (1953).
