Bounding volume

In computer graphics and computational geometry, a bounding volume (or bounding region) for a set of objects is a closed region that completely contains the union of the objects in the set. Bounding volumes are used to improve the efficiency of geometrical operations, such as collision detection and visibility culling, by using simple volumes that are cheaper to test than the objects themselves.

Bounding volume
Field: Computer graphics
Types: Sphere, AABB, OBB, capsule
Uses: Collision detection, ray tracing, culling

Common types

The most common bounding volumes are the bounding sphere, the axis-aligned bounding box (AABB), the oriented bounding box (OBB), and convex hulls. Spheres are trivially cheap to test for overlap but fit shapes loosely; AABBs are fast to update and test but cannot rotate with an object; OBBs fit more tightly at the cost of slower intersection tests.

Hierarchies

A bounding volume hierarchy (BVH) nests objects in a tree of volumes, so that a ray or collider that misses a parent volume can skip an entire subtree. This makes ray tracing and physics simulation of complex scenes tractable, and BVHs are the standard acceleration structure in modern renderers and physics engines.

Trade-offs

Choosing a bounding volume balances tightness of fit against the cost of the intersection test and of updating the volume when objects move. Many systems combine types — for example, spheres at the top level of a hierarchy and boxes near the leaves.
