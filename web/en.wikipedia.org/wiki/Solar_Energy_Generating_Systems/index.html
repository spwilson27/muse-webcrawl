Solar Energy Generating Systems


Country: United States
Location: Mojave Desert, California
Status: Operational
Commission date: 1984–1990
Installed capacity: 354 MW

Solar Energy Generating Systems (SEGS) is a concentrated solar power plant in California, United States. With a combined capacity of 354 megawatts, the complex of nine separate plants was for many years the world's largest solar energy generating facility.

Overview

The SEGS plants are located at three sites in the Mojave Desert: Daggett, Kramer Junction, and Harper Lake. Built by Luz Industries between 1984 and 1990, the plants use parabolic trough collectors that focus sunlight onto receiver tubes carrying a synthetic oil heat transfer fluid. The heated oil generates steam in heat exchangers, driving conventional steam turbines. Natural gas boilers provide supplemental heat, supplying a small share of total output.

History

The first plant, SEGS I, began operation in 1984 with a capacity of 14 MW; the final and largest unit, SEGS IX at Harper Lake, entered service in 1990 at 80 MW. Luz Industries went bankrupt in 1991 as tax incentives lapsed, and the plants passed to new operators. SEGS I and II at Daggett were eventually retired and replaced by a photovoltaic facility, while the Kramer Junction and Harper Lake units continued operating.

Technology

Each plant fields thousands of parabolic trough mirrors arranged in loops. The plants pioneered commercial concentrating solar thermal technology and demonstrated large-scale thermal storage integration and hybrid gas backup at utility scale.
