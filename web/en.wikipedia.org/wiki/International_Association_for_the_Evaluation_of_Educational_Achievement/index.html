International Association for the Evaluation of Educational Achievement

The IEA conducts large-scale comparative studies of student achievement, including TIMSS and PIRLS.

The International Association for the Evaluation of Educational Achievement (IEA) is an independent, international cooperative of national research institutions and governmental research agencies. It conducts large-scale comparative studies of educational achievement and other aspects of education systems around the world.

History

The IEA was founded in 1958 at the UNESCO Institute for Education in Hamburg, Germany, by scholars who believed that systematically comparing educational outcomes across countries could reveal which school and policy factors were associated with student learning. Its first major undertaking, the First International Mathematics Study (1964), surveyed students in twelve countries and pioneered the methodology of international comparative assessment.

Studies

The IEA is best known for two cyclical studies:

  • TIMSS (Trends in International Mathematics and Science Study), conducted every four years since 1995, assessing mathematics and science achievement of fourth- and eighth-grade students.
  • PIRLS (Progress in International Reading Literacy Study), conducted every five years since 2001, assessing reading comprehension of fourth-grade students.

Other IEA studies have addressed civic and citizenship education (ICCS), computer and information literacy (ICILS), second languages, and teacher education. The IEA's international secretariat is based in Amsterdam, the Netherlands, and its Data Processing and Research Center is in Hamburg.

Influence

IEA study results are widely reported in the media and used by policymakers to benchmark national performance and inform education reform. The studies have also been subject to methodological debate, including questions about sampling, translation of test items, and the comparability of curricula across countries.
