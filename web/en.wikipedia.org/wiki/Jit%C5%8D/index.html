Jitō

Jitō (地頭, "land head") were medieval Japanese land stewards appointed by the shogunate to administer estates (shōen) and public lands. The office was created by Minamoto no Yoritomo after the Genpei War and became a cornerstone of the Kamakura shogunate's power.

History

From 1185, Yoritomo appointed jitō to estates across Japan, rewarding his retainers with rights to collect taxes and manage land in return for military service. Jitō worked alongside the estate proprietors in Kyoto, dividing income under the system known as shitaji chūbun in some cases.

Development

Over time many jitō encroached on proprietors' rights and became de facto local lords. During the Muromachi period the office evolved into the jitō-daimyo and contributed to the rise of the feudal landholding class that culminated in the Sengoku daimyo.
