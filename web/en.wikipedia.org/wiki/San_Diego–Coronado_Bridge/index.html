The San Diego–Coronado Bridge, commonly called the Coronado Bridge, is a prestressed concrete and steel girder bridge crossing San Diego Bay in California, linking San Diego with the city of Coronado on State Route 75.

Design

Opened in 1969, the bridge is 11,179 feet (3,407 m) long and rises to a maximum height of about 200 feet, allowing US Navy ships based at Naval Base San Diego to pass beneath. Its roadway curves at roughly 80 degrees rather than running straight, deliberately lengthening the span so its grade stays gentle. Thirty concrete pylons support the structure, painted in a distinctive blue.

History

The bridge replaced a ferry service that had operated since 1886. It was originally tolled; tolls were removed in 2002 after the construction bonds were paid off. The bridge is also noted, somberly, as a frequent site of suicide deaths, which has led to long-running proposals for a barrier.
