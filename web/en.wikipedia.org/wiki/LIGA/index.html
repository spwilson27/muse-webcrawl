LIGA

LIGA is a German acronym for Lithographie, Galvanoformung, Abformung (Lithography, electroplating, and molding), a fabrication technology for producing high-aspect-ratio microstructures. It was developed in the early 1980s at the Karlsruhe Nuclear Research Center in Germany, originally to manufacture nozzles for uranium enrichment.

Process

In the classic X-ray LIGA process, a thick layer of photoresist — typically poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA) — is exposed to collimated X-rays from a synchrotron source through a mask carrying the desired pattern. The exposed regions are dissolved, leaving a microstructure with nearly vertical sidewalls hundreds of micrometres tall and features measured in micrometres. The resist structure can then be filled by electroplating with metals such as nickel, copper, or gold, and the resulting metal part used directly or as a mold insert for mass replication by hot embossing or injection molding.

Variants and applications

Less expensive variants substitute ultraviolet lithography (UV-LIGA, often using SU-8 resist) for synchrotron X-rays, at some cost in aspect ratio. LIGA-fabricated parts are used in micro-optics, inertial sensors, microfluidics, inkjet nozzles, watch components, and micromotors.
