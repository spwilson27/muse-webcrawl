Conwy County Borough (Welsh: Bwrdeistref Sirol Conwy) is a county borough and principal area in north Wales. It is bordered by the principal areas of Denbighshire to the east, Gwynedd to the west and south, and the Irish Sea to the north. The county borough was formed on 1 April 1996 under the Local Government (Wales) Act 1994, by merging the boroughs of Aberconwy and Colwyn from the former counties of Gwynedd and Clwyd respectively.

Geography

Conwy County Borough covers an area of about 1,130 square kilometres. Its landscape ranges from the popular Victorian resort towns of the north coast — including Llandudno, Colwyn Bay, Conwy and Penmaenmawr — to the sparsely populated uplands of Snowdonia (Eryri) in the south, which include part of Snowdonia National Park. The River Conwy flows north through the borough to the sea at Conwy.

Places of interest

  • Conwy Castle and the town walls, a UNESCO World Heritage Site
  • Llandudno, the Great Orme and the Victorian pier
  • Bodnant Garden, near Tal-y-Cafn
  • The Sychnant Pass and Penmaenmawr
  • Betws-y-Coed and the Conwy Valley

Governance

The local authority is Conwy County Borough Council, which is based in Conwy. Elections are held every five years. The county borough also lies within the Clwyd West and Aberconwy parliamentary constituencies for Westminster, and the corresponding Senedd constituencies.

Welsh language

Conwy has one of the higher proportions of Welsh speakers among Welsh principal areas, with around a quarter to a third of residents able to speak Welsh, rising to well over half in some rural inland communities.
