Coma Supercluster

The Coma Supercluster (SCl 117) is a nearby supercluster of galaxies comprising the Coma Cluster (Abell 1656) and the Leo Cluster (Abell 1367). Located about 300 million light-years from Earth, it is in the constellation Coma Berenices and forms part of the CfA2 Great Wall, one of the largest known structures in the universe.

Structure

The supercluster is dominated by its two great clusters, Coma and Leo, connected by bridges and filaments of smaller galaxy groups. It was one of the first superclusters discovered and helped establish that galaxies are not distributed uniformly but organized into a cosmic web of clusters, filaments, and voids.

The Coma Cluster alone contains more than 1,000 identified galaxies in a region roughly 20 million light-years across. It was in Coma that astronomer Fritz Zwicky, in 1933, first inferred the existence of large amounts of unseen "dark matter" from the motions of its galaxies.

Observational history

Redshift surveys in the 1980s, particularly the CfA Redshift Survey, mapped the supercluster in three dimensions and revealed it as part of the Great Wall, a sheet-like structure hundreds of millions of light-years long first described by Margaret Geller and John Huchra in 1989.
