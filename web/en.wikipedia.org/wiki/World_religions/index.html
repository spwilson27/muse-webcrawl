World religions

World religions is a category used in religious studies for the largest and most internationally widespread religious traditions — typically Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, and Judaism, sometimes expanded to include Sikhism, Taoism, Jainism, Baháʼí, and others.

Largest: Christianity (~2.3 billion), Islam (~1.9 billion), Hinduism (~1.2 billion), Buddhism (~300+ million)

The concept

The paradigm emerged in 19th-century comparative religion and is criticised for privileging belief-centred, text-based traditions and Protestant assumptions. Scholars debate alternatives centred on practice and lived religion.

Demographics

Abrahamic religions account for over half of humanity; Dharmic religions most of the rest of adherents. The religiously unaffiliated are the third-largest grouping worldwide. See List of religious populations, Religion.
