Statutory license

A statutory license, also known as a compulsory license in some contexts, is a license created by law that allows a person to use copyrighted or patented material without negotiating permission from the rights holder, provided statutory conditions are met and required royalties are paid. Statutory licensing is established by a legislature to balance the interests of creators with broader public or industry needs.

Copyright examples

In United States copyright law, Section 115 of the Copyright Act provides a compulsory mechanical license allowing anyone to make and distribute recordings of a previously released musical work by paying a statutory royalty rate. Other statutory licenses cover cable retransmission of broadcast signals (Section 111), satellite retransmission (Sections 119 and 122), and certain digital audio transmissions and webcasting of sound recordings (Section 114), administered in part through royalty proceedings before the Copyright Royalty Board and collecting organisations such as SoundExchange.

Patent and other contexts

In patent law, compulsory licensing allows governments to authorize use of a patented invention without the patent holder's consent under certain conditions, a flexibility affirmed in the TRIPS Agreement and the 2001 Doha Declaration, notably for public health emergencies and access to medicines.
