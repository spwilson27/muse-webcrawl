Sierpiński number

For a Sierpiński number k, every value of k·2n + 1 is composite.

In number theory, a Sierpiński number is an odd natural number k such that every number of the form k·2n + 1 is composite for all natural numbers n. The concept is named after Wacław Sierpiński, who proved in 1960 that there are infinitely many such odd k. Numbers of the form k·2n + 1 with k odd and k < 2n are called Proth numbers; finding primes among them for a given k disqualifies k from being a Sierpiński number.

The Sierpiński problem

The Sierpiński problem asks: what is the smallest Sierpiński number? In 1962 John Selfridge proved that 78,557 is a Sierpiński number, using a covering set of congruences that forces a factor for every n. The covering set for 78,557 is {3, 5, 7, 13, 19, 37, 73}. It is conjectured that 78,557 is the smallest, but this requires eliminating every smaller candidate by exhibiting a prime k·2n + 1 for each.

Seventeen or Bust and PrimeGrid

The distributed computing project Seventeen or Bust, begun in 2002, set out to eliminate the last 17 candidates below 78,557; it later merged into the PrimeGrid project. Over the years most candidates fell as large Proth primes were discovered. Only a handful of candidates — including 21,181; 22,699; 24,729; 55,467; and 75,711 — have remained unresolved for long periods, each requiring ever larger searches as n grows.

Related concepts

The dual notion is a Riesel number, an odd k for which every k·2n − 1 is composite. Both problems illustrate how covering congruences can settle the primality of entire infinite families.
