Hollywood, Los Angeles

Hollywood is a neighbourhood and district in the central region of Los Angeles, California. Its name has come to be a shorthand reference for the American film industry and the people associated with it. Many notable film studios were once located there, and its landmarks include the Hollywood Sign, the Walk of Fame and the Dolby Theatre, home of the Academy Awards.

Hollywood
City: Los Angeles, California
Incorporated: 1903 (annexed to LA 1910)
Known for: US film industry, Hollywood Sign (1923)
Population: ~75,000–85,000 (estimates vary)

History

The name was applied by developer H.J. Whitley in the 1880s. Hollywood incorporated in 1903 and merged with Los Angeles in 1910 to access water. The Nestor Film Company opened the first studio in 1911; filmmakers were drawn by sunshine, varied scenery, cheap land and distance from Edison's patent enforcement. By the 1920s the major studios and theatre palaces on Hollywood Boulevard made it the world film capital.

Landmarks and industry

The Hollywood Sign (originally "Hollywoodland", 1923), Capitol Records Building, TCL Chinese Theatre (1927, famous for handprints), Hollywood Bowl, Griffith Observatory nearby, and the Walk of Fame (from 1960, 2,700+ stars) symbolise the district. Production largely moved to Burbank, Culver City and streaming-era facilities, but Paramount Pictures remains in Hollywood itself.

Neighbourhood

Modern Hollywood is ethnically diverse, includes Thai Town and Little Armenia, the Hollywood Forever Cemetery, and has undergone revitalisation and gentrification since the 2000s, alongside persistent homelessness and tourism pressures around Hollywood Boulevard.
