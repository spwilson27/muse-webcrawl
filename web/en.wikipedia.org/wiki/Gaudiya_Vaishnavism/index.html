Gaudiya Vaishnavism

Gaudiya Vaishnavism (also called Chaitanya Vaishnavism or Bengal Vaishnavism) is a Hindu devotional (bhakti) movement within Vaishnavism, inspired by Chaitanya Mahaprabhu (1486–1534) in the region of Gauda (present-day West Bengal and Bangladesh). Its followers worship Krishna as the supreme form of God (Svayam Bhagavan), together with his consort Radha, and regard Chaitanya himself as their combined incarnation.

Theology

Gaudiya theology, systematised by the Six Goswamis of Vrindavan and later teachers, is based chiefly on the Bhagavad Gita and Bhagavata Purana. Its philosophy is known as Achintya Bheda Abheda — "inconceivable simultaneous oneness and difference" between God and his energies. Devotion (bhakti) is held to be both the means and the goal of spiritual life, expressed above all through congregational chanting of the Hare Krishna maha-mantra (sankirtana), which Chaitanya taught as the prime practice for the present age (Kali Yuga).

History and spread

The tradition flourished in Bengal and Vrindavan, producing rich literatures in Sanskrit and Bengali. In the 19th and 20th centuries, teachers such as Bhaktivinoda Thakura and Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati revived and organised the movement, founding the Gaudiya Math. In 1965 Bhaktisiddhanta's disciple A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada travelled to the United States and founded the International Society for Krishna Consciousness (ISKCON, the "Hare Krishna movement") in 1966, carrying Gaudiya Vaishnavism worldwide. Its principal pilgrimage centres are Mayapur and Vrindavan.

Practices

Practices include chanting the maha-mantra on prayer beads (japa), congregational singing (kirtana), worship of temple deities, observance of festivals such as Janmashtami and Gaura Purnima, distribution of sanctified food (prasada), and scriptural study. Initiated devotees traditionally follow four regulative principles: no meat-eating, no intoxication, no gambling, and no illicit sex.
