Social accounting

Social accounting (also known as social and environmental accounting or corporate social reporting) is the process of communicating the social and environmental effects of an organisation's actions to stakeholders, extending conventional financial accounting beyond purely economic measures.

Scope

Social accounting considers impacts on employees, communities, consumers, and the natural environment. Frameworks such as the Global Reporting Initiative provide guidelines for sustainability reporting, and the "triple bottom line" concept — people, planet, profit — is a well-known formulation.

Criticism

Critics note that social accounting is largely voluntary and unaudited, which can allow selective disclosure or "greenwashing". Advocates argue it improves transparency and accountability for corporate behaviour.
