The Spanish Fighting Bull (Toro Bravo, "fierce bull"; Toro de lidia) is an Iberian cattle breed bred primarily for bullfighting. It is notable for its aggressiveness, muscular build, and instinctive charging behaviour, developed through centuries of selective breeding on extensive estates (dehesas) in Spain, Portugal, France, and Latin America.

Breeding

Fighting bulls are raised semi-wild with minimal human contact so that their natural aggression is preserved. Breeding stock is selected through tientas, in which cows are tested with a cape and pike to assess bravery (bravura), temperament, and stamina. Major breeding lines (encastes) descend from 18th- and 19th-century foundation herds, and famous modern ranches (ganaderías) include Miura, Victorino Martín, and Jandilla.

Characteristics

Bulls entering the ring weigh at least 460 kg in first-class plazas. The breed has a distinctive profile: a prominent hump of muscle over the shoulders, long upward-curved horns, and great variety of coat colours. Unlike most cattle breeds, its aggression is often inherited through the cow line, so cows are the principal focus of selection.

The corrida

The bullfight (corrida) in which the animals are used has become increasingly controversial on animal welfare grounds; it has been banned in Catalonia (later overturned on constitutional grounds) and in several Latin American jurisdictions, while remaining a protected cultural tradition in parts of Spain.
