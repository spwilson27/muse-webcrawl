Comprehensive Peace Accord

The Comprehensive Peace Accord was a peace agreement signed on 21 November 2006 between the Government of Nepal and the Communist Party of Nepal (Maoist), formally ending the Nepalese Civil War that had begun in 1996 and claimed more than 13,000 lives. The accord followed the 2006 democracy movement (Loktantra Andolan) that forced King Gyanendra to restore parliament.

Provisions

The agreement declared an end to the decade-long armed conflict, provided for the Maoists to join an interim government and interim legislature, and placed the arms and armies of both sides under United Nations monitoring (UNMIN). It committed the parties to elections for a Constituent Assembly, which would decide the future of the monarchy and draft a new constitution. Arms were stored in containers at cantonment sites, with Maoist combatants confined to camps.

Aftermath

Constituent Assembly elections were held in April 2008; the Maoists became the largest party, and the assembly abolished the monarchy, declaring Nepal a federal democratic republic in May 2008. The integration and rehabilitation of Maoist combatants remained contentious for years, with the process largely completed by 2013. A new constitution was promulgated in 2015.
