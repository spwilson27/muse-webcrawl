Philip IV (Spanish: Felipe IV; 8 April 1605 – 17 September 1665) was King of Spain from 1621 until his death, and (as Philip III) King of Portugal until 1640. His 44-year reign was the longest of any Spanish Habsburg monarch. Remembered as "the Planet King" for the empire over which the sun never set, he presided over both the high point of Spanish cultural achievement — the Spanish Golden Age — and the onset of Spain's political and military decline.

Reign

Philip governed through his favourite, the Count-Duke of Olivares, whose reforms sought to spread the costs of empire across Spain's kingdoms through the Union of Arms. The monarchy became embroiled in the Thirty Years' War and renewed war with the Dutch Republic, then with France from 1635. The strain provoked the Catalan Revolt and the Portuguese Restoration War, both beginning in 1640; Portugal's independence was ultimately lost. Olivares fell in 1643. The Peace of Westphalia (1648) recognised Dutch independence, and the Treaty of the Pyrenees (1659) with France, sealed by the marriage of Philip's daughter Maria Theresa to Louis XIV, confirmed France's ascendancy in Europe.

Court and culture

Philip was a discerning patron and collector of art. His court painter, Diego Velázquez, painted his most famous portraits and Las Meninas; Philip also patronised Rubens, Zurbarán, Lope de Vega and Calderón de la Barca, built the Buen Retiro Palace in Madrid, and assembled one of the greatest art collections in Europe, much of it now in the Prado. He wrote poetry and translated works of Guicciardini.

Family and succession

His first wife, Elisabeth of France, bore the heir Balthasar Charles, whose death in 1646 was a personal and dynastic blow. By his second wife, Mariana of Austria, Philip was the father of Charles II, the last Spanish Habsburg, whose disabilities and infertility led to the War of the Spanish Succession after his death.
