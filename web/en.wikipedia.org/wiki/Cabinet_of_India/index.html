Union Council of Ministers

The Union Council of Ministers, commonly called the Cabinet of India, is the principal executive organ of the Government of India. Chaired by the Prime Minister, it comprises cabinet ministers, ministers of state, and deputy ministers, appointed by the President on the Prime Minister's advice under Articles 74 and 75 of the Constitution.

Structure

Cabinet ministers head the major ministries — Home Affairs, Finance, Defence, External Affairs — and form the inner cabinet that takes key decisions. The council is collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha; its total strength, including the Prime Minister, may not exceed 15% of the Lok Sabha's membership since the 91st Amendment (2003).

Functions

The council directs policy, introduces government bills, and administers the Union through the civil services, coordinated by the Cabinet Secretariat. Compare the Cabinet of the United Kingdom, on whose Westminster model India's system is based.
