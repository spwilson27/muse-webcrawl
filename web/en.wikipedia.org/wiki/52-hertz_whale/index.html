The 52-hertz whale, also known popularly as 52 Blue, is an individual whale of an unidentified species that has been detected calling at the unusual frequency of 52 hertz. Most baleen whales call at far lower frequencies — blue and fin whales typically at 10 to 39 Hz — so this whale's call lies outside the usual vocal range of any known species. It has been tracked in the North Pacific since 1989 by hydrophone arrays originally built to detect submarines.

Characteristics

The call is a regular pattern of short pulses at 52 Hz, repeated in sequences resembling those of a blue whale, and its timing tracks a migration pattern similar to that of blue whales, moving between the Aleutian Islands, the Kodiak archipelago and the California coast. Only one such source has ever been detected at a time, suggesting a single individual. Researchers have proposed that the animal may be malformed, a hybrid of blue and fin whale, or simply an individual with an atypical voice.

Cultural impact

Because no other whale is known to call at its frequency, the animal has been dubbed "the loneliest whale in the world" in popular culture, inspiring songs, films and a 2021 documentary, The Loneliest Whale: The Search for 52. Scientists caution that the "loneliness" is anthropomorphic speculation: other whales may well hear the calls, and there is no evidence the whale is distressed. In 2021 a possible second 52-hertz source was reported off California, raising the possibility that more than one such whale exists.
