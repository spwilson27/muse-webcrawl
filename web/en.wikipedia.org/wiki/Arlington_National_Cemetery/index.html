Arlington National Cemetery is the largest cemetery in the United States National Cemetery System, located in Arlington County, Virginia, across the Potomac River from Washington, D.C. More than 400,000 people are buried in its 639 acres, principally U.S. service members, veterans, and their families.

History

The grounds were the estate of Mary Anna Custis Lee, wife of Confederate general Robert E. Lee. After the Lees abandoned Arlington House in 1861, the Union army occupied the property; in 1864, with Washington's cemeteries filling, Quartermaster General Montgomery Meigs ordered burials there — pointedly including graves near the house — and the first military burial took place on 13 May 1864. After the war the Lee family sued; the Supreme Court ruled the estate had been illegally seized, and it was then sold to the government by George Washington Custis Lee in 1883.

Notable sites

The cemetery contains the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier (1921), guarded continuously since 1937 by sentinels of the 3rd U.S. Infantry Regiment ("The Old Guard"); the graves of President John F. Kennedy, marked by an eternal flame, and of President William Howard Taft; the Memorial Amphitheater; the USS Maine mast memorial; and sections holding dead from every American war from the Revolution to the 21st century. The changing of the guard at the Tomb is one of the most recognisable military ceremonies in the United States.
