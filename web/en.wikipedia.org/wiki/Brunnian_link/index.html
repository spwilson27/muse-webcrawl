Brunnian link

In knot theory, a Brunnian link is a nontrivial link that becomes a set of unlinked, separate circles if any one of its components is removed. The property is named after the German mathematician Hermann Brunn, who wrote about such links in the nineteenth century.

Examples

The most famous example is the Borromean rings: three rings interlocked so that removing any single ring frees the other two completely. The standard twelve-crossing three-component link known as "Rubbers' rings" and various braid closures are also Brunnian. The simplest examples arise from the closure of Brunnian braids—pure braids that become trivial when any strand is deleted.

Properties and applications

Every proper sublink of a Brunnian link is trivial, yet the whole link is not, which makes Brunnian links useful test cases in topology, including Milnor's linking invariants, which can detect them. Outside pure mathematics, the Brunnian principle appears in puzzle designs, chain constructions that fail if one part is removed, and discussions of interdependence in systems.
