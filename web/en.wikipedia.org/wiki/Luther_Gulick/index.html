Luther Halsey Gulick Jr. (1892–1993) was an American expert on public administration and a key figure in the development of administration as an academic discipline. Born in Osaka to missionary parents and named after his father, the physician and physical-education pioneer Luther Gulick, he studied at Oberlin College and Columbia University, where he earned his PhD in 1920.

Career and ideas

Gulick directed the National Institute of Public Administration and its successor, the Institute of Public Administration in New York, for much of his career, and advised governments at city, state and federal level, including service connected with the Brownlow Committee that reshaped the Executive Office of the President of the United States under Franklin D. Roosevelt.

He is best remembered for the essay "Notes on the Theory of Organization" (1937), published in the Papers on the Science of Administration that he co-edited with Lyndall Urwick. In it he coined the acronym POSDCORB to summarise the functions of an executive: Planning, Organizing, Staffing, Directing, Coordinating, Reporting and Budgeting. The same essay discussed how work should be divided within organisations — by purpose, process, persons (clientele) and place — and the concept of span of control. His work belonged to the classical school of management theory associated with Henri Fayol, whose administrative functions influenced POSDCORB.
