Physical Web

The Physical Web was a Google project intended to enable quick, seamless interactions with physical objects and locations through web links. Rather than requiring a dedicated app for every smart object, beacons broadcast URLs using Bluetooth Low Energy — principally through the open Eddystone format — and nearby phones could list those URLs, making it possible to "walk up and use anything".

A beacon broadcasting a URL to nearby devices.

Operation

A beacon transmits a URL (Eddystone-URL frame) within a range of tens of metres. On Android, Chrome and Google Play Services scanned for these broadcasts and presented matching web pages, ranked by signal strength and vetted against spam and malware filters. Use cases included parking meters, vending machines, museum exhibits, transit stops showing schedules, and retail displays — all without installing an app or pairing.

History and shutdown

Google announced the Physical Web in 2014 and integrated discovery into Chrome in 2015–2016. Adoption was limited and user engagement low; Google deprecated Nearby Notifications in December 2018 because of spam, effectively ending the project's consumer-facing discovery mechanism, though the Eddystone beacon format and independent implementations remained available.
