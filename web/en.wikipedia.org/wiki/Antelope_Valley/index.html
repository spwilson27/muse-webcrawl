The Antelope Valley is a high desert valley in northern Los Angeles County and southern Kern County, California, forming the western tip of the Mojave Desert. Its principal cities are Lancaster and Palmdale. The valley takes its name from the pronghorn ("antelope") that grazed there in large herds until the 1880s.

Geography and climate

Bounded by the San Gabriel Mountains to the south and the Tehachapi Mountains to the northwest, the valley floor lies at roughly 2,300–3,000 feet elevation. The climate is arid, with hot summers, cold winters and strong winds — conditions that favour both wind energy generation and the spring wildflower blooms protected in the Antelope Valley California Poppy Reserve.

Economy

The valley is a centre of the aerospace industry: Edwards Air Force Base lies to the northeast, and Lockheed Martin's Skunk Works and Northrop Grumman facilities in Palmdale (Plant 42) have built aircraft including the U-2, B-2 Spirit and F-35 components. Historically the valley was known for alfalfa farming, supported by groundwater and the California Aqueduct, which skirts its southern edge. Affordable housing relative to coastal Los Angeles drove rapid suburban growth from the 1980s onward.

Communities

Besides Lancaster and Palmdale, communities include Quartz Hill, Rosamond, Littlerock, Lake Los Angeles and Mojave. The valley is served by the Antelope Valley Freeway (State Route 14) and Metrolink's Antelope Valley Line.
