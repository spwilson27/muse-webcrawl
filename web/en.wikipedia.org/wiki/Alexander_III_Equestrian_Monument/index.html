Alexander III Equestrian Monument

The Monument to Alexander III of Russia is a noted equestrian statue in Saint Petersburg, Russia, created by the Italian sculptor Paolo Troubetzkoy. Unveiled in 1909 on Znamenskaya Square in front of the Moscow railway station (today Ploshchad Vosstaniya), the massive bronze figure of the emperor on horseback became one of the most debated works of Russian monumental sculpture.

History

The monument was commissioned after the death of Alexander III in 1894 by his son and successor, Nicholas II. Troubetzkoy's unconventional, heavy-set portrayal of the emperor astride a stocky draught horse departed sharply from the heroic conventions of equestrian monuments and provoked strong reactions at the unveiling on 23 May 1909.

The statue was removed from its square in 1937 amid the reshaping of the city under Soviet rule and spent decades in storage. In 1994 it was re-erected in front of the Marble Palace, a branch of the Russian Museum, where it stands today on a much lower pedestal.

Description

The work depicts the emperor seated heavily in the saddle, hands on the reins, wearing a military greatcoat. Critics at the time mocked the horse for its squat, powerful build, while others praised the monument as a truthful portrait of an autocrat whose reign was marked by reaction and repression. The sculptor reportedly described his subject as "one brute seated upon another."
