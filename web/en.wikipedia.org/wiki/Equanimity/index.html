Equanimity

Equanimity is a state of psychological stability and composure which is undisturbed by the experience of or exposure to emotions, pain, or other phenomena that may cause the mind to lose its balance. The virtue and value of equanimity are extolled by a number of major religions and ancient philosophies.

In Buddhism

In Buddhism, upekkhā (Pali; Sanskrit: upekṣā) is one of the four immeasurables (brahmavihāras) and one of the seven factors of enlightenment. It is impartiality toward all beings, an even-mindedness in the face of gain and loss, praise and blame, pleasure and pain.

In other traditions

Stoicism prizes freedom from passion (apatheia) and tranquillity of mind; Hindu thought treats samatva, evenness of mind, as a mark of the wise person in the Bhagavad Gita. In modern psychology, equanimity is related to emotional regulation and is cultivated in mindfulness-based therapies.
