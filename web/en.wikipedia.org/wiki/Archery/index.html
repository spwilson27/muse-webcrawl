Archery

Archery is the sport, practice, or skill of using a bow to shoot arrows. The word comes from the Latin arcus, meaning bow. Historically, archery has been used for hunting and combat. In modern times, it is mainly a competitive sport and recreational activity, governed internationally by World Archery (formerly FITA). A person who practices archery is typically called an archer, bowman, or toxophilite.

History

The bow and arrow are among humanity's oldest projectile technologies — stone points from Sibudu Cave in South Africa suggest archery may be some 70,000 years old, and unambiguous evidence (the Holmegaard bows from Denmark) dates to about 9,000 years ago. Archery shaped warfare from Egyptian chariots and Assyrian armies to the English longbow at Agincourt (1415) and the mounted composite-bow archers of the Eurasian steppe. Firearms displaced the war bow by the 17th century, but archery survived as a sport: it appeared at the 1900 Olympics, was reintroduced permanently in 1972, and compound bows (patented in the 1960s) transformed hunting and target competition.

Modern sport

Competition formats include target archery (shooting at concentric-ring faces at set distances — 70 m for Olympic recurve), field archery over varied terrain, 3D archery at foam animal targets, and traditional disciplines such as English longbow, Turkish, Japanese (kyūdō), and Korean styles. Equipment divides broadly into recurve, compound, and traditional (longbow/barebow) categories, with arrows of carbon, aluminium, or wood matched to the bow's draw weight.
