Marine cloud brightening

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Marine cloud brightening (MCB), also known as marine cloud seeding or cloud albedo enhancement, is a proposed solar geoengineering technique. It would spray fine sea-salt aerosol particles into low marine stratocumulus clouds, increasing their droplet concentration and hence their albedo, reflecting more sunlight to space and cooling the planet — exploiting the Twomey effect.

Proposal

The idea builds on John Latham's work from 1990 and Stephen Salter's designs for unmanned, wind-powered spray vessels. Small droplets would be generated from seawater and lofted into the marine boundary layer. Modelling studies suggest that brightening clouds over a modest fraction of the oceans could offset a significant amount of greenhouse warming, though with risks of regional precipitation changes.

Experiments

Field trials have included the Australian Marine Cloud Brightening Project's small-scale tests over the Great Barrier Reef from 2020, and the University of Washington's Coastal Atmospheric Aerosol Research and Engagement (CAARE) experiment at Alameda, California, begun in 2024.

Governance

Like other geoengineering proposals, MCB raises questions about governance, termination effects and unequal regional impacts.
