Murti

In the Hindu tradition, a murti (Sanskrit: मूर्ति, mūrti, "form, embodiment") is a statue or image of a deity that serves as a focus of worship. After consecration through the prana pratishtha ritual, in which the deity is invoked to reside in the image, the murti is treated not as a mere representation but as a living presence of the divine, to be bathed, dressed, fed, and honored.

Significance

Murtis mediate darshan, the mutual seeing of deity and devotee that lies at the heart of Hindu temple worship. They are typically made according to iconographic prescriptions in texts such as the Shilpa Shastras, which specify proportions, postures (asana), gestures (mudra), attributes, and mounts (vahana) for each deity. Common materials include stone, metal alloys such as panchaloha, wood, and clay; temporary clay murtis, as used in festivals such as Ganesh Chaturthi and Durga Puja, are immersed in water at the festival's close.

Related traditions

The concept has parallels in Jain and some Buddhist practice, where consecrated images of Tirthankaras and Buddhas are likewise venerated. Within Hindu theology, schools differ on the status of the image, but for most practitioners murti worship is a central devotional practice in temples and home shrines alike.
