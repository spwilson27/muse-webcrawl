Circuit de la Sarthe

The Circuit des 24 Heures du Mans, also known as the Circuit de la Sarthe (after the 1906 French Grand Prix circuit), located in Le Mans, France, is a semi-permanent motorsport race course chiefly known as the venue for the 24 Hours of Le Mans endurance race, first held in 1923. At 13.626 km (8.467 miles), it is one of the longest racing circuits in the world, combining a permanent track section with closed public roads.

Layout

The circuit runs from the permanent Bugatti Circuit area onto public roads: the long Mulsanne Straight (Hunaudières), where prototypes once exceeded 400 km/h before chicanes were added in 1990, the sweeping Porsche Curves, Arnage, and the Ford Chicane before the start-finish straight. The Dunlop Bridge is one of motorsport's most recognisable landmarks. Because much of the course is public road, surface and conditions vary along its length.

History

The first 24 Hours of Le Mans was held on 26–27 May 1923. The circuit evolved through several configurations, shortened after the catastrophic 1955 Le Mans disaster, in which Pierre Levegh's car crashed into the crowd, killing Levegh and 83 spectators — the deadliest accident in motorsport history. Safety revisions, chicanes on the Mulsanne, and slower pit and spectator areas followed over subsequent decades.
