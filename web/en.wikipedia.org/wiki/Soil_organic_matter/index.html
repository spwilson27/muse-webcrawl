Soil organic matter

Soil organic matter (SOM) is the organic matter component of soil, consisting of plant and animal detritus at various stages of decomposition, cells and tissues of soil microbes, and substances that soil microbes synthesize. SOM provides numerous benefits to the physical and chemical properties of soil and its capacity to provide regulatory ecosystem services. SOM is especially critical for soil functions and soil quality.

Composition

The mass of SOM in most soils is between 1% and 6% of the topsoil mass. Its composition is conventionally described as a continuum: fresh litter and particulate organic matter; microbial biomass; and humus — the fraction of stable, decomposed organic material, itself subdivided into humic acids, fulvic acids, and humin. Soil organic carbon typically constitutes about 58% of SOM by mass.

Functions

  • Nutrition: SOM is the largest terrestrial reservoir of nitrogen and a major store of phosphorus and sulfur, releasing nutrients through mineralization.
  • Water dynamics: Organic matter increases water-holding capacity and infiltration.
  • Structure: It promotes aggregation, reducing erosion and compaction.
  • Carbon storage: Soils store more carbon than the atmosphere and vegetation combined, making SOM central to climate-change mitigation debates.

Factors affecting SOM

SOM levels are governed by the balance of inputs (residues, roots, manure) against decomposition, which is influenced by climate, soil texture and mineralogy, drainage, and management such as tillage, cover cropping, and fertilization. Intensive tillage generally accelerates SOM loss.
