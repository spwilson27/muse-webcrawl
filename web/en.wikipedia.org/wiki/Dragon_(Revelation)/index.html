Dragon (Revelation)

The dragon of the Book of Revelation is a figure in the final book of the New Testament, described most prominently in Revelation chapter 12 as "a great red dragon, with seven heads and ten horns, and on his heads seven diadems" (Revelation 12:3). The text explicitly identifies the dragon: "that ancient serpent, who is called the devil and Satan, the deceiver of the whole world" (Revelation 12:9).

The vision in Revelation 12

In the vision, the dragon stands before a woman clothed with the sun who is about to give birth, intending to devour her child (identified by most interpreters as the Messiah). The child is caught up to God, the woman flees into the wilderness, and war breaks out in heaven: Michael and his angels fight the dragon, who is thrown down to earth with his angels. Foiled, the dragon persecutes the woman and makes war on "the rest of her offspring."

Further appearances

In Revelation 13 the dragon gives authority to the Beast rising from the sea. In Revelation 20 an angel binds the dragon in the bottomless pit for a thousand years (the Millennium); after his brief release and final revolt he is thrown into the lake of fire.

Interpretation

Interpreters across Christian traditions read the dragon variously as Satan literally, as a symbol of the Roman Empire persecuting the early church, and — in historicist readings — as a cipher for successive world powers. The imagery draws on Old Testament chaos monsters such as Leviathan and Rahab, and parallels ancient Near Eastern combat myths in which a god defeats a sea serpent. The red dragon of Revelation became a standard subject of medieval illuminated manuscripts and apocalypse art.
