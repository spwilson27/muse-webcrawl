Gateshead is a town in Tyne and Wear, England, situated on the southern bank of the River Tyne opposite Newcastle upon Tyne, with which it is joined by seven bridges. Historically part of County Durham, it is the administrative centre of the Metropolitan Borough of Gateshead.

History

Gateshead grew as a coal-mining and industrial town during the Industrial Revolution, with ironworks, chemical works and engineering. The town's motto, "Caput inter nubila" ("a head among the clouds"), derives from its position on a hill above the river.

Landmarks

Modern Gateshead is known for major cultural and regeneration projects on its Quayside: the Sage Gateshead concert hall (now The Glasshouse International Centre for Music), the BALTIC Centre for Contemporary Art in a converted flour mill, and the Gateshead Millennium Bridge (2001), a tilting pedestrian and cycle bridge. Antony Gormley's Angel of the North sculpture (1998) stands on a hilltop at Low Eighton overlooking the A1. The MetroCentre, once Europe's largest shopping centre, lies in the west of the borough at Dunston.

Transport

Gateshead is served by the Tyne and Wear Metro, Gateshead Interchange, and the A1 and A184 roads.
