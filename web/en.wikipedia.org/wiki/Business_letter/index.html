Business letter

A business letter is a formal letter exchanged between businesses, or between a business and its customers, clients, suppliers, or other external parties. Business letters are used for purposes such as placing orders, requesting information, making complaints, applying for jobs (as cover letters), conveying legal or contractual notices, and building commercial relationships. Although much correspondence has moved to email, the conventions of the business letter remain the template for formal written communication in commerce.

Structure

A conventional business letter contains, in order:

  1. Letterhead or sender's address — the writer's name, organisation, and address.
  2. Date — the date of writing.
  3. Inside address — the recipient's name, title, and address.
  4. Reference or subject line — an optional line identifying the matter.
  5. Salutation — e.g. "Dear Ms Sharma:" (US style) or "Dear Sir or Madam".
  6. Body — an opening paragraph stating the purpose, middle paragraphs giving details, and a closing paragraph stating the desired action.
  7. Complimentary close — e.g. "Yours faithfully" (British) or "Sincerely" (American).
  8. Signature block — signature, typed name, and title.
  9. Enclosures and copies — notations such as "Encl." and "cc".

Formats

The main layout formats are block (all elements flush left), modified block (date and closing begin at the centre), and semi-block (paragraphs indented). Punctuation styles vary between "open" punctuation, which omits most marks after address lines and the salutation, and "closed" punctuation.

Types

Common types include enquiry letters, order letters, complaint and adjustment letters, sales letters, collection letters, resignation letters, recommendation letters, and cover letters accompanying job applications.
