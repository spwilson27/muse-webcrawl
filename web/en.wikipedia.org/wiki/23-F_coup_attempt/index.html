23-F coup attempt
Date23–24 February 1981
LocationMadrid and Valencia, Spain

The 23-F coup attempt (Spanish: Intento de golpe de Estado del 23-F), also known as El Tejerazo, was a failed coup d'état in Spain on 23 February 1981. Civil Guard lieutenant colonel Antonio Tejero seized the Congress of Deputies during the investiture vote of Leopoldo Calvo-Sotelo, while Captain General Jaime Milans del Bosch declared a state of emergency in Valencia and put tanks on the streets. The coup collapsed after King Juan Carlos I refused to support it and ordered the armed forces to defend the constitution in a televised address.

Background

Spain's transition to democracy after the death of Francisco Franco in 1975 was accompanied by economic crisis, ETA terrorism and military discontent. Prime Minister Adolfo Suárez resigned in January 1981, and the coup was timed for the second investiture vote of his successor, Leopoldo Calvo-Sotelo.

Events

At about 18:20 on 23 February, Tejero led some 200 Civil Guards into the Congress chamber, firing shots into the ceiling and holding the deputies hostage for some 18 hours. In Valencia, Milans del Bosch proclaimed a state of emergency. The plotters hoped the King would endorse a government of national salvation under General Alfonso Armada. Instead, in the early hours of 24 February, Juan Carlos appeared on television in uniform and ordered the military to uphold the constitutional order. The isolated plotters surrendered by midday.

Notably, only three men — Suárez, Santiago Carrillo and General Gutiérrez Mellado — refused to take cover during the shooting, an image that became emblematic of the young democracy's defence.

Aftermath

Tejero, Milans del Bosch and Armada were sentenced to 30 years' imprisonment for military rebellion at the Campamento trial in 1982. The failure of 23-F consolidated Spanish democracy and the authority of the Crown; the following year the Socialists under Felipe González won a landslide election. The newspaper El País, led by Juan Luis Cebrián, published special editions defending the constitution during the night of the coup.
