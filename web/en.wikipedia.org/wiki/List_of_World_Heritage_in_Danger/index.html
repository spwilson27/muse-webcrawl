The List of World Heritage in Danger is drawn up by UNESCO's World Heritage Committee under Article 11(4) of the World Heritage Convention to inform the international community of conditions threatening the characteristics for which a property was inscribed on the World Heritage List, and to encourage corrective action. Placement on the list is intended as a call for help rather than a sanction, but it can be a prelude to deletion: the Dresden Elbe Valley and Liverpool – Maritime Mercantile City both passed through the Danger List before being delisted in 2009 and 2021 respectively.

Grounds for listing

A site may be listed when threatened by "ascertained danger" — including armed conflict, natural disaster, serious deterioration or large-scale development already under way — or by "potential danger", such as planned projects, loss of legal protection or impending conflict. Listing unlocks priority emergency assistance from the World Heritage Fund and focused monitoring by the advisory bodies ICOMOS and IUCN.

Examples

Long-standing entries include the Old City of Jerusalem (listed 1982), all six Syrian sites (Aleppo, Palmyra, Damascus and others, listed amid the civil war in 2013), the sites of Yemen and Libya, the Everglades and other natural properties threatened by ecological change, the Historic Centre of Vienna (development pressure, 2017), and several Ukrainian sites — Kyiv's Saint-Sophia Cathedral and the historic centres of Lviv and Odesa — listed in 2023 following Russia's full-scale invasion. Sites can also recover and leave the list: the Belize Barrier Reef (2009–2018), the birthplace of Jesus in Bethlehem (2012–2019) and the Kathmandu Valley (2003–2007) are among the successful removals.
