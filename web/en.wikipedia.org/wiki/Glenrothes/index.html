Glenrothes

LocationFife, Scotland
Designated1948 (new town)
PopulationApproximately 39,000

Glenrothes is a town in Fife, Scotland, and the administrative capital of the Fife council area. Designated in 1948 as one of Scotland's first post-war new towns, it was originally intended to house miners working in the Fife coalfield.

History

When the Rothes colliery that was to anchor the town's economy failed because of flooding and geological problems, Glenrothes pivoted to manufacturing, becoming a centre for electronics firms in the "Silicon Glen" corridor. The town takes its name from the Earl of Rothes and the River Leven valley ("glen").

Features

Glenrothes is known for its extensive public art, including large concrete hippos placed around the town, and for its town-centre sculpture programme. It contains the Kingdom Shopping Centre and Fife House, the headquarters of Fife Council. The town is served by Glenrothes with Thornton railway station on the Fife Circle line.
