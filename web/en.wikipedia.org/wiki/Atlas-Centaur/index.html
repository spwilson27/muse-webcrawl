Atlas-Centaur

The Atlas-Centaur was a United States expendable launch vehicle derived from the SM-65 Atlas missile, topped with a Centaur upper stage powered by liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen — the first such high-energy stage to fly.

Development

Developed by General Dynamics (Convair) with the Centaur stage originating from a late-1950s NASA–Air Force program, the Atlas-Centaur first flew in 1962, with early failures before its first fully successful flight in 1963.

Operational history

Atlas-Centaur launched the Surveyor lunar landers, Mariner and Pioneer interplanetary probes, and numerous communications and military satellites from Cape Canaveral. The family evolved through several Atlas variants and remained in service, in later Atlas II and III forms, into the 2000s, while the Centaur stage continues to fly on Atlas V and Vulcan.
