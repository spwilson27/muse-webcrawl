Lorna Wing

Lorna Gladys Wing OBE FRCPsych (7 October 1928 – 6 June 2014) was an English psychiatrist. She was a pioneer in the field of developmental disorders, particularly autism spectrum disorders. Her work fundamentally changed the understanding of autism in the 1970s and 1980s, introducing the concept of a spectrum of autistic conditions and popularising the term Asperger syndrome.

Career

Wing trained in medicine at University College Hospital, London, qualifying in 1952, and specialised in psychiatry. Motivated in part by her own daughter Susie's autism, she turned to research into developmental disorders at the Institute of Psychiatry and the Medical Research Council's Social Psychiatry Unit.

With John Wing and others, she studied the prevalence of autism in Camberwell, London, and identified the "triad of impairments" in social interaction, communication and imagination. In a landmark 1981 paper she revived the work of Hans Asperger, coining the term "Asperger's syndrome" and bringing the condition to the attention of the English-speaking world. She proposed that autism be understood as a spectrum, the "autistic continuum", rather than a single narrowly defined condition.

Legacy

Wing co-founded the National Autistic Society in 1962 and helped establish its Lorna Wing Centre for Autism, which pioneered diagnostic methods for the condition. She was appointed OBE in 1994. Her concept of the autism spectrum underlies modern diagnostic practice, including the unified category of autism spectrum disorder in DSM-5.
