Hassan II (9 July 1929 – 23 July 1999) was King of Morocco from 1961 until his death in 1999. A member of the Alaouite dynasty, he succeeded his father, Mohammed V, and was in turn succeeded by his son, Mohammed VI.

Reign

Educated in law at the University of Bordeaux, Hassan served as crown prince and commander of the Royal Armed Forces before ascending the throne. His reign was marked by a strong, centralised monarchy. He survived two attempted military coups, in 1971 at Skhirat and in 1972, when his aircraft was attacked by Moroccan air force jets.

The period of harsh repression of political opponents during parts of his rule became known as the "Years of Lead". At the same time, Hassan II pursued economic liberalisation, oversaw the Green March of 1975 by which Morocco asserted its claim to Western Sahara, and played a prominent diplomatic role in the Arab world and in Arab–Israeli contacts. He commissioned the Hassan II Mosque in Casablanca, one of the largest mosques in the world, completed in 1993.

Death and succession

Hassan II died of a heart attack in Rabat on 23 July 1999 after a reign of more than 38 years. His funeral was attended by numerous heads of state. He was succeeded by his eldest son, Mohammed VI.
