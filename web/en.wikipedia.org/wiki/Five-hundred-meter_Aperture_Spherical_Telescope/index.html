Five-hundred-meter Aperture Spherical Telescope

The Five-hundred-meter Aperture Spherical Telescope (FAST; Chinese: 五百米口径球面射电望远镜), nicknamed Tianyan (“Eye of Heaven”), is a radio telescope in a natural karst depression in Pingtang County, Guizhou, China. It is the world’s second-largest single-dish radio telescope after Russia’s RATAN-600, and was the largest filled-aperture telescope when completed.

FAST
Location: Pingtang County, Guizhou, China
Completed: 2016
First light: 25 September 2016
Dish diameter: 500 m
Effective aperture: 300 m
Frequency: 70 MHz – 3 GHz

History

Proposed in the 1990s by astronomer Nan Rendong, its chief designer, construction began in 2011. The site in the Dawodang depression was chosen for its natural shape, radio quietness and drainage. Nearby villages were relocated to preserve radio silence. FAST saw first light in 2016, entered full operation in January 2020, and opened to international proposals in 2021.

Design

The reflector consists of about 4,450 triangular panels on a steel cable net. Actuators change its shape into a paraboloid aimed at the sky, using 300 metres of the dish at a time. A lightweight feed cabin, suspended on cables and positioned with millimetre accuracy, carries receivers covering a wide frequency range.

Science

FAST excels at pulsar discovery — it has found hundreds of new pulsars — as well as neutral hydrogen surveys, fast radio bursts, molecular spectroscopy and SETI searches. Its sensitivity surpasses the former Arecibo Observatory telescope.
