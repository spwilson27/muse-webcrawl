Galata Bridge

Galata Bridge (Turkish: Galata Köprüsü)
Location: Istanbul, Turkey
Crosses: Golden Horn
Length: 490 m (1,610 ft)
Current bridge opened: 1994

The Galata Bridge (Turkish: Galata Köprüsü) is a bridge that spans the Golden Horn in Istanbul, Turkey. From the end of the 19th century in particular, the bridge has featured in Turkish literature, theatre, poetry and novels, linking the old imperial centre of Eminönü with the districts of Karaköy and Beyoğlu (Galata) to the north.

History

Crossings at the site date to the early 19th century; the first modern bridge was built in 1845 under the patronage of Valide Sultan Bezmialem. Leonardo da Vinci had even proposed an unrealised single-span design for the Golden Horn in 1502–1503. Successive pontoon and iron bridges followed — the celebrated 1912 floating bridge stood until it was damaged by fire in 1992 and towed away. The present bridge, opened in December 1994, is a bascule bridge 490 metres long with a lower deck of restaurants and cafés.

Cultural life

The bridge is one of Istanbul's emblematic landmarks, famous for the rows of anglers fishing from its railings and for fish-sandwich (balık ekmek) boats moored at its Eminönü end.
