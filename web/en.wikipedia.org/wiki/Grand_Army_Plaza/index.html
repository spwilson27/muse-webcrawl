Grand Army Plaza

Grand Army Plaza, originally known as Prospect Park Plaza, is a large oval plaza at the main entrance of Prospect Park in Brooklyn, New York City. It was named in 1926 in honour of the Grand Army of the Republic, the organization of Union Army veterans.

Features

The plaza is dominated by the Soldiers' and Sailors' Arch, a triumphal arch dedicated in 1892 to the Union defenders, designed by John H. Duncan with sculptural groups by Frederick MacMonnies. The plaza also contains the Bailey Fountain, completed in 1932, and busts of notable figures. The central branch of the Brooklyn Public Library and the Brooklyn Museum stand nearby, at the junction of Flatbush Avenue and Eastern Parkway.

History

The plaza was designed by Frederick Law Olmsted and Calvert Vaux as the formal entrance to Prospect Park, part of their plan for a linked system of parks and parkways in Brooklyn. It forms the terminus of Eastern Parkway, modelled in part on Parisian boulevards. The plaza was designated a New York City scenic landmark in 1975.
