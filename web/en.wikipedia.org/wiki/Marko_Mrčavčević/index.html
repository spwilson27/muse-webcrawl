Prince Marko

Marko Mrnjavčević (c. 1335 – 17 May 1395) was the de jure King of Serbia from 1371 to 1395, while he was the de facto ruler of a territory in western Macedonia centered on the town of Prilep. He is known as Prince Marko (Serbian: Краљевић Марко, Kraljević Marko) and King Marko in South Slavic oral tradition, in which he became a major character and hero of epic poetry during the Ottoman occupation of the Balkans.

Historical Marko

Marko was the eldest son of Vukašin Mrnjavčević, who rose under Emperor Stefan Uroš V to become co-ruler and King of Serbia. After Vukašin was killed by the Ottomans at the Battle of Maritsa in 1371, Marko inherited the royal title but lacked the power of the great regional lords who partitioned the disintegrating Serbian Empire. Like several other Balkan rulers, he became an Ottoman vassal, obliged to pay tribute and provide troops to the sultan.

Marko ruled from Prilep in western Macedonia. His endowment, the Monastery of St. Archangels near Prilep (Markovi Kuli area), preserves his portrait as a donor. He died at the Battle of Rovine on 17 May 1395, fighting on the Ottoman side against the Wallachians — tradition records his words that he prayed God would help the Christians, even if he were the first to be killed.

Legendary Marko

In epic poetry sung across Serbia, North Macedonia, Bulgaria and neighbouring lands, Kraljević Marko is transformed into a superhuman hero: a defender of the oppressed, a trickster who outwits Ottoman authorities, and the companion of his talking, wine-drinking horse Šarac and his sister-by-oath, the vila Ravijojla. His legendary lifespan stretches over centuries, and poems such as "Marko and the Vila", "Marko Ploughs the Sultan's Roads" and "The Death of Marko" are among the most famous works of the South Slavic oral epic tradition, recorded by Vuk Karadžić and others.
