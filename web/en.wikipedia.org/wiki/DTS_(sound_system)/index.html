DTS, Inc. (originally Digital Theater Systems) is an American company specialising in multichannel audio technologies for film and video. Its DTS surround sound formats compete directly with Dolby formats in cinemas, home theatre, and consumer electronics. DTS debuted theatrically with Steven Spielberg's Jurassic Park in 1993, after Spielberg became an investor in the company, and its format distinguished itself by carrying audio on separate CD-ROM discs synchronised to the film by a timecode track printed on the film itself.

The DTS wordmark.

Formats

  • DTS (core) — a lossy 5.1-channel codec used on DVDs and some Blu-rays, typically encoded at up to 1,509 kbit/s, higher than its main rival's common DVD bitrates.
  • DTS-HD High Resolution Audio and DTS-HD Master Audio — extensions for Blu-ray; Master Audio is lossless and supports up to 7.1 channels at 24-bit/96 kHz and beyond.
  • DTS:X — an object-based immersive audio format (introduced 2015) competing with Dolby Atmos, allowing sounds to be placed in three-dimensional space independent of fixed channels.
  • DTS Virtual:X — virtual height/surround processing for soundbars and televisions.
  • DTS Play-Fi — a wireless multi-room audio platform.
  • DTS Headphone:X — binaural surround rendering for headphones.

History

The company was founded in 1990 by Terry Beard. Its cinema system launched in 1993 and expanded to hundreds of theatres worldwide. Consumer versions followed on LaserDisc, DVD and later Blu-ray and Ultra HD Blu-ray. DTS acquired SRS Labs in 2012, and in 2016 DTS was itself acquired by Tessera Technologies, which later became Xperi, under which the DTS brands continue.
