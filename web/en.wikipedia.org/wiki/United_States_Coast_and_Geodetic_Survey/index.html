United States Coast and Geodetic Survey

USC&GS
Founded: 1807 (as Survey of the Coast)
Abolished: 1970
Successor: National Ocean Survey / NOAA

The United States Coast and Geodetic Survey (USC&GS) was the first scientific agency of the United States Government. It was responsible for surveying and charting the nation's coastlines, conducting geodetic surveys, and producing nautical and aeronautical charts from 1807 until 1970, when its functions were transferred to the newly created National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA).

History

President Thomas Jefferson authorized a "Survey of the Coast" in 1807. Under Ferdinand Rudolph Hassler, its first superintendent, the Survey adopted rigorous geodetic methods. In 1878 it was renamed the Coast and Geodetic Survey to reflect its expanding interior geodetic work, including the transcontinental arc of triangulation.

Roles

The agency charted harbors and coasts, measured tides and currents, conducted gravity and magnetic surveys, and maintained triangulation networks that underpin American cartography. Its commissioned officer corps, one of the nation's uniformed services, survives today as the NOAA Commissioned Officer Corps.

World wars

Survey officers and ships served in both world wars, producing charts and conducting artillery and hydrographic surveys. In 1970 the agency's functions passed to the National Ocean Survey within NOAA.
