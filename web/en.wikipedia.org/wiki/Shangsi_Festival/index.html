Shangsi Festival

The Shangsi Festival (Chinese: 上巳节; pinyin: Shàngsì Jié), also known as the Double Third Festival, is a traditional Chinese festival celebrated on the third day of the third month of the Chinese lunar calendar. In ancient China it was one of the great spring festivals: a day of ritual purification by the water, outings to riverbanks, orchid baths, feasting, poetry, and courtship. Although its observance faded in much of China, the Double Third remains a major festival among several ethnic minority peoples, notably the Zhuang of Guangxi, for whom it is a public holiday centred on singing gatherings.

Origins and customs

The festival dates to the Zhou dynasty and was originally fixed to the first si (巳) day of the third month — hence "Shangsi," "upper si" — before being settled on the third day under the Han dynasty. Its core rite was fuxi (祓禊), the ritual washing away of ill fortune and disease at the river's edge. By the Wei and Jin dynasties, the day had become an occasion for elegant literati gatherings: scholars floated cups of wine down winding streams and composed poems before the cup reached them, on pain of drinking. The most famous such party was the Orchid Pavilion Gathering of 353 CE, where Wang Xizhi wrote the preface that became China's most celebrated piece of calligraphy.

In folk practice, young men and women met at riversides and markets, making the festival a traditional occasion of courtship and fertility; spring outings, picnics, and the wearing of orchids were typical. The festival also overlaps with later observances of the birthday of the Queen Mother of the West (Xi Wangmu) and, in some regions, merged with or yielded to the Qingming Festival.

Transmission abroad and modern observance

Shangsi influenced Japanese custom: Japan's Hinamatsuri (Doll Festival) on 3 March descends in part from the Chinese Double Third purification rites, as does the custom of nagashibina, floating dolls away on rivers. In contemporary China the festival survives most vigorously among the Zhuang, Li, Dong and Yao peoples; Zhuang celebrations feature antiphonal song fairs, five-coloured glutinous rice, and embroidered ball throwing, and Guangxi marks the Double Third with an official holiday.
