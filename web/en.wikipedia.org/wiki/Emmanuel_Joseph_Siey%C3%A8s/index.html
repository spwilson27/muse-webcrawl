Emmanuel Joseph Sieyès

Emmanuel Joseph Sieyès (3 May 1748 – 20 June 1836), usually known as the Abbé Sieyès, was a French Roman Catholic abbé, clergyman, and political writer who was one of the chief political theorists of the French Revolution. His 1789 pamphlet What Is the Third Estate? became the manifesto of the Revolution and helped transform the Estates-General into the National Assembly.

What Is the Third Estate?

Published in January 1789, Sieyès's pamphlet famously opened: "What is the Third Estate? Everything. What has it been until now in the political order? Nothing. What does it want to be? Something." It argued that the Third Estate—the commoners—constituted the true nation, and that sovereignty resided in the nation rather than in the crown or the privileged orders.

Revolutionary career

Sieyès was elected deputy for the Third Estate of Paris, helped draft the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen, and contributed to the Constitution of 1791. He survived the Terror, returned to prominence under the Directory, and, seeking a "sword" to carry out a constitutional coup, joined with Napoleon Bonaparte in the Coup of 18 Brumaire (1799), which brought the Consulate to power. Napoleon soon sidelined him, though he was made a count of the Empire. Exiled as a regicide after the Bourbon Restoration, he returned to France in 1830 and died in Paris in 1836.
