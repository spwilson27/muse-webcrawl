Elizabeth Woodville (c. 1437 – 8 June 1492), also called Dame Elizabeth Grey, was Queen of England as wife of King Edward IV from 1464 until his death in 1483. Her secret marriage to Edward, a commoner widow from a Lancastrian family, was a sensation of the Wars of the Roses.

Early life and marriage

Daughter of Richard Woodville, 1st Earl Rivers, and Jacquetta of Luxembourg, Elizabeth first married Sir John Grey, killed at the Second Battle of St Albans (1461). In May 1464 she secretly married Edward IV; the match and advancement of her many Woodville relatives alienated Richard Neville, Earl of Warwick.

Wars of the Roses

Elizabeth was crowned in 1465. During Edward's brief deposition (1470–71) she took sanctuary at Westminster Abbey, where Edward V was born. After Edward IV's death in 1483, her sons became the Princes in the Tower under Richard III, who declared her marriage invalid (Titulus Regius) and seized the throne.

Later life

Under Henry VII, who married her daughter Elizabeth of York, the attainder was reversed. Elizabeth retired to Bermondsey Abbey, dying there in 1492.

In culture

She is a central figure in Philippa Gregory's The White Queen and its television adaptation.
