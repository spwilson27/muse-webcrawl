Pieria (Greek: Πιερία) is one of the regional units of Greece, located in the southwestern part of the region of Central Macedonia. Its capital is the city of Katerini. Historically part of ancient Macedon, Pieria lies between Mount Olympus to the south and west and the Thermaic Gulf of the Aegean Sea to the east.

Geography and history

The regional unit covers 1,516 km², encompassing the Pierian Mountains, part of the Olympus massif — the highest mountain in Greece (2,918 m) and mythological home of the Olympian gods — and a fertile coastal plain. In Greek tradition Pieria was the home of the Muses, the "Pierides", and of Orpheus. Ancient sites include Dion, the sacred city of the Macedonians at the foot of Olympus, and Pydna, where the Romans defeated Perseus of Macedon in 168 BC, ending the Antigonid kingdom. The medieval castle of Platamon overlooks the coast.

Economy and municipalities

The economy is based on agriculture (kiwifruit, tobacco, cotton, fruit), tourism along the "Olympic Riviera" resorts such as Paralia, Leptokarya and Litochoro, and services in Katerini. Since the 2011 Kallikratis reform the regional unit comprises three municipalities: Katerini, Dion-Olympos and Pydna-Kolindros.
