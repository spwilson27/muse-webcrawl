Leszek Balcerowicz
Born: 19 January 1947, Lipno, Poland
Occupation: Economist, politician
Known for: Balcerowicz Plan; Governor of the National Bank of Poland (2001–2007)

Leszek Henryk Balcerowicz (born 19 January 1947) is a Polish economist, statesman and former professor at the Warsaw School of Economics. He served as Deputy Prime Minister and Minister of Finance in the first non-communist government of Poland (1989–1991, and again 1997–2000), and as Governor of the National Bank of Poland from 2001 to 2007.

The Balcerowicz Plan

Balcerowicz is best known as the architect of the "Balcerowicz Plan", the programme of shock-therapy economic reforms launched on 1 January 1990 that transformed Poland from a centrally planned economy to a market economy. The plan liberalised prices, opened the economy to foreign trade, made the złoty internally convertible, imposed tight monetary and fiscal policy to curb hyperinflation, and began privatisation of state enterprises. Although the transition caused a sharp short-term fall in output and high unemployment, Poland became the first post-communist country to return to growth, and the reforms are widely credited with laying the foundations of its later economic success.

Later career

Balcerowicz founded and led the Freedom Union party, served as a member of the Sejm, and has remained an influential liberal commentator and founder of the Civil Development Forum (FOR) think tank. In 2016 he briefly served as an adviser and representative of the President of Ukraine on economic reforms.
