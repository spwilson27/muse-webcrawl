Steering knuckle

In automotive suspension, a steering knuckle is that part which contains the wheel hub or spindle, and attaches to the suspension and steering components. It is variously called a steering knuckle, spindle, upright or hub, as well.

Function

The wheel and tyre assembly attach to the hub or spindle of the knuckle where the tyre/wheel rotates while being held in a stable plane of motion by the knuckle/suspension assembly. The knuckle pivots about the steering axis — defined by ball joints or a kingpin — so that the wheels can be steered, while simultaneously locating the wheel relative to the suspension links. It also provides mounting points for the brake caliper and, on driven axles, carries the drive shaft connection through the hub bearing.

In the attached photograph of a double-wishbone suspension, the knuckle is shown attached to the upper control arm at the top ball joint and the lower control arm at the bottom ball joint. The wheel assembly shown attaches to the knuckle at its centre point. Note the arm of the knuckle that juts outwards, which the steering mechanism attaches to in order to turn the knuckle and wheel assembly.

Construction

Knuckles are usually cast or forged from steel or cast iron, though weight-sensitive designs increasingly use cast aluminium. Because a failure of the knuckle can cause loss of steering, the component is designed with large safety margins and is subject to fatigue testing.
