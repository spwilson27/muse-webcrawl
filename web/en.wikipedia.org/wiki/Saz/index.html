Saz (bağlama)
ClassificationString instrument, necked lute
RegionTurkey, the Caucasus, the Balkans, Central Asia
RelatedBağlama, dutar, bouzouki

The saz or bağlama is a family of long-necked lutes used in Turkish folk music and in the music of the wider region including Azerbaijan, Armenia, the Balkans, and parts of Central Asia. It is the most common traditional instrument in Turkey and the instrument of the aşık (troubadour) tradition.

Description

The saz has a pear-shaped body carved from a single block of wood (traditionally mulberry) or built from ribs, a long neck with tied movable frets, and typically seven strings arranged in three courses (two, two, and three), played with a thin plectrum (tezene) or with the fingers in the şelpe style. Sizes range from the small cura through the standard short-necked and long-necked bağlama to the large divan saz and meydan sazı.

Cultural role

The bağlama is central to Alevi religious ceremonies (cem), accompanying sacred hymns (deyiş), and to the repertoire of folk poet-singers such as Âşık Veysel. Its movable frets allow performance of the microtonal makam system. An electrified version, the elektro bağlama, became prominent in Turkish popular and Anatolian rock music in the 20th century.
