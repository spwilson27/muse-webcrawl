University of Bristol

The University of Bristol is a public research university in Bristol, England. It received its royal charter in 1909, having developed from University College, Bristol, founded in 1876 — the first institution in England to admit women on an equal basis with men. It is a member of the Russell Group of research-intensive universities.

History

The university's foundation was supported by Bristol's merchant families, including the Wills tobacco family; the Wills Memorial Building (completed 1925), with its prominent tower, remains the university's centrepiece. Bristol physicists made major contributions in the 20th century: Cecil Powell won the Nobel Prize for work on the pion, and Paul Dirac, an alumnus, was a founder of quantum mechanics.

Academics

The university has around 30,000 students across six faculties: Arts, Engineering, Health Sciences, Life Sciences, Science, and Social Sciences and Law. It is consistently ranked among the top ten universities in the United Kingdom and is noted for research in areas including nanotechnology, epidemiology (the ALSPAC "Children of the 90s" cohort study), and aerospace engineering.
