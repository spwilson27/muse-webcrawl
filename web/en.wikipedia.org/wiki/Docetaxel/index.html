Docetaxel

Docetaxel, sold under the brand name Taxotere among others, is a chemotherapy medication used to treat a number of types of cancer, including breast cancer, head and neck cancer, stomach cancer, prostate cancer and non-small-cell lung cancer. It may be used alone or with other chemotherapy medication and is given by slow injection into a vein.

Docetaxel
Brand: Taxotere
Class: Taxane
Route: Intravenous
First approved: 1995

Medical uses

Docetaxel is a mainstay for metastatic and adjuvant breast cancer, castration-resistant prostate cancer (with prednisone), advanced gastric cancer, head and neck squamous cell carcinoma, and non-small-cell lung cancer.

Mechanism

Like paclitaxel, docetaxel binds to and stabilises microtubules, preventing their normal dynamic breakdown; cells cannot complete mitosis and undergo apoptosis. It is semi-synthesised from 10-deacetylbaccatin III, extracted from the needles of the European yew (Taxus baccata).

Side effects

Common side effects include hair loss, low blood cell counts (neutropenia), numbness, shortness of breath, nausea, vomiting and muscle pains. Fluid retention and allergic reactions led to routine premedication with corticosteroids. It is on the WHO List of Essential Medicines and available as a generic.
