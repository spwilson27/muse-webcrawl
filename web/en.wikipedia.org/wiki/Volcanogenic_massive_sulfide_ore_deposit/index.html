Volcanogenic massive sulfide ore deposit

Volcanogenic massive sulfide ore deposits, or VMS deposits, are a type of metal sulfide ore deposit, mainly copper–zinc, which are associated with and produced by volcanic-associated hydrothermal events in submarine environments. They form at or near the seafloor where hot, metal-bearing fluids discharge at "black smoker" vents.

Formation

Seawater circulating through hot volcanic rocks leaches metals and, upon venting at temperatures up to about 400 °C, precipitates mounds and chimneys of pyrite, chalcopyrite, sphalerite and galena, often with significant gold and silver. Beneath the mound a stockwork feeder zone develops in the altered footwall.

Classification and examples

VMS deposits are classified by host-rock composition (mafic, bimodal-mafic, siliciclastic-mafic, bimodal-felsic, siliciclastic-felsic types). Famous districts include the Iberian Pyrite Belt, the Abitibi greenstone belt of Canada (e.g., Kidd Creek and Noranda), Cyprus (the type source of copper's name), and the modern seafloor fields of the East Pacific Rise and mid-Atlantic ridges.
