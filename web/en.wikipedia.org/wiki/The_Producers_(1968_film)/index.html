Overview

The Producers is a 1967 American satirical black comedy film written and directed by Mel Brooks, released widely in 1968. It stars Zero Mostel as Max Bialystock, a failing Broadway producer, and Gene Wilder as Leo Bloom, a timid accountant who realizes that a producer could make more money with a deliberate flop than with a hit, by overselling shares in the production.

Bialystock and Bloom set out to stage the worst musical ever written: Springtime for Hitler, a gaudy tribute to Adolf Hitler written by the eccentric ex-Nazi Franz Liebkind (Kenneth Mars). Against all their intentions, the show becomes a runaway hit, leaving the conspirators facing ruin and prison.

Plot

Washed-up producer Max Bialystock raises money for his shows by romancing elderly wealthy women. Accountant Leo Bloom, auditing Bialystock's books, notices a discrepancy and idly observes that a flop could be more profitable than a success if the producer sold more than 100 percent of the show. The two men raise far more money than needed and search for the worst play ever written, settling on Springtime for Hitler.

They hire the worst director in New York, Roger De Bris, and cast the spaced-out hippie Lorenzo St. DuBois, known as L.S.D., as Hitler. The opening number shocks the audience into silence before they decide it is a satire, and the show becomes a smash. Liebkind, enraged at the mockery of Hitler, is injured; the producers' scheme collapses, and Bialystock, Bloom, and Liebkind end up in prison, where Bialystock promptly begins overselling shares in a prison production.

Reception and legacy

The film was initially controversial for its comic treatment of Hitler but grew into a beloved classic. Mel Brooks won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay. The film was later adapted by Brooks into a hit Broadway musical in 2001, which itself was adapted into a 2005 film. In 1996, The Producers was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry.
