Caesarea (Mazaca)

Caesarea Mazaca
Modern name: Kayseri
Region: Cappadocia, central Anatolia
Coordinates: 38.72° N, 35.48° E

Caesarea (Greek: Καισάρεια), also known historically as Mazaca, was the capital city of the Roman province of Cappadocia. It corresponds to the modern city of Kayseri in central Turkey.

History

The city, originally called Mazaca, was the residence of the kings of Cappadocia. It was renamed Eusebia under King Ariarathes V and then Caesarea in honour of Augustus (or Tiberius) around the turn of the era. The city stood on the northern slopes of Mount Argaeus (modern Erciyes Dağı) and controlled trade routes across Anatolia.

Roman and Byzantine periods

Under Roman rule Caesarea became an important administrative and commercial centre and a mint city. It suffered a devastating sack by the Sasanian king Shapur I in 260 AD. In the fourth century it became a leading centre of Christianity; its bishop Basil the Great made it famous for his theological writings and charitable institutions, including the Basileias hospital complex.

Later history

The city passed through Byzantine, Arab-contested, Seljuk and Ottoman hands, developing into the modern commercial city of Kayseri, which preserves Roman, Byzantine and Seljuk monuments alongside its modern districts.
