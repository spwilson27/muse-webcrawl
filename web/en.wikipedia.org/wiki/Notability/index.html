Notability

Notability is the property of being worthy of attention or notice. The term is used in several specialised senses, most prominently as the inclusion criterion for articles in Wikipedia, where a topic is presumed suitable for a standalone article if it has received significant coverage in reliable sources that are independent of the subject.

On Wikipedia

Under Wikipedia's general notability guideline, "significant coverage" means sources address the topic directly and in detail, and sources must be reliable and independent. Subject-specific guidelines supplement the general rule for areas such as academics, books, films, music, sports and web content. Articles whose subjects cannot demonstrate notability may be merged, redirected or deleted through processes including proposed deletion and Articles for Deletion discussions. The guideline is one of the site's most debated policies.

Other uses

In mathematics and science, notability may describe whether a result or person merits documentation; in law and journalism it relates to prominence and public interest. More generally, the word simply denotes fame or distinction.
