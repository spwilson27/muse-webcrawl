Applied Cryptography

Applied Cryptography, second edition (1996).

Applied Cryptography: Protocols, Algorithms, and Source Code in C is a book by cryptographer Bruce Schneier, first published in 1994 and in a second edition in 1996. It was one of the first books to make the algorithms and protocols of modern cryptography accessible to a general technical audience, and it became a standard reference for programmers implementing cryptographic systems.

Contents

The book is divided into broad sections covering cryptographic protocols (from simple authentication to key exchange, voting, and digital cash), cryptographic techniques, and detailed descriptions of algorithms including DES, other block ciphers, stream ciphers, one-way hash functions, and public-key systems such as RSA and Diffie–Hellman. Later chapters cover real-world implementations, the politics of cryptography — including the United States export controls of the era — and appendices containing source code in C for many of the algorithms described.

Impact

The first edition appeared while the US government still classified strong cryptography as a munition. A federal court case, Bernstein v. United States, involved the export status of the book's floppy-disk companion containing source code; the printed book itself could be exported freely under the First Amendment while the identical code in machine-readable form could not. Schneier later wrote that Applied Cryptography taught readers how cryptography works but not how to design secure systems, a gap he addressed in later works such as Practical Cryptography and Cryptography Engineering.
