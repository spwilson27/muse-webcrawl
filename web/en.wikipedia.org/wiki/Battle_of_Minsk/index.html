Battle of Białystok–Minsk

Date22 June – 9 July 1941

The Battle of Białystok–Minsk was a major German strategic operation conducted by Army Group Centre during Operation Barbarossa, the opening phase of the German invasion of the Soviet Union in the summer of 1941. The battle ended with the encirclement and destruction of much of the Soviet Western Front in the Białystok and Minsk pockets.

Background

On 22 June 1941 Germany launched Operation Barbarossa. In the centre, Field Marshal Fedor von Bock's Army Group Centre, spearheaded by the panzer groups of Heinz Guderian and Hermann Hoth, faced General Dmitry Pavlov's Western Front, which was deployed far forward in the Białystok salient — a vulnerable position that invited a double envelopment.

The encirclement

The panzer groups broke through on both flanks of the salient and drove deep, meeting near Minsk on 27–28 June and trapping the Soviet 3rd, 4th, 10th and parts of the 13th Armies in two pockets around Białystok and Minsk. Minsk itself fell on 28 June. Soviet counterattacks, ordered from Moscow despite the hopeless situation, failed to break the ring, and German infantry armies methodically reduced the pockets by 9 July.

Aftermath

The Germans claimed over 300,000 prisoners, along with thousands of tanks and guns destroyed or captured — one of the largest encirclements in military history. Pavlov and several of his senior officers were recalled to Moscow, tried and executed. Yet the time spent reducing the pockets, and the survival of substantial Soviet forces further east, contributed to the delays that preceded the subsequent Battle of Smolensk and the eventual failure to take Moscow before winter.
