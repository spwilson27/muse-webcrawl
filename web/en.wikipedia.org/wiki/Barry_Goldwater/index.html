Barry Morris Goldwater (2 January 1909 – 29 May 1998) was an American politician and major general in the Air Force Reserve. He represented Arizona in the United States Senate for five terms and was the Republican Party's nominee for president in the 1964 election, in which he lost in a landslide to Lyndon B. Johnson. Often described as the father of the modern American conservative movement, Goldwater championed limited government, anti-communism and free enterprise, and his campaign helped realign American politics.

Early life and Senate career

Born in Phoenix when Arizona was still a territory, Goldwater was heir to Goldwater's, the state's leading department store, and served as a pilot in the Second World War, ferrying aircraft overseas. Elected to the Phoenix city council in 1949, he won a Senate seat in 1952 by defeating Majority Leader Ernest McFarland. In the Senate he became the leading voice of the Republican right, and his manifesto The Conscience of a Conservative (1960) sold millions of copies and galvanised a generation of activists.

1964 presidential campaign

Goldwater secured the 1964 Republican nomination over the party's moderate establishment, declaring in his acceptance speech that "extremism in the defense of liberty is no vice" and "moderation in the pursuit of justice is no virtue". His opposition to the Civil Rights Act of 1964 on constitutional grounds, and Johnson campaign advertising that portrayed him as reckless on nuclear war — most famously the "Daisy" advertisement — contributed to one of the largest defeats in American electoral history. He carried only Arizona and five states of the Deep South, yet the campaign built the activist and donor networks that later powered Ronald Reagan's rise.

Later life

Returned to the Senate in 1969, Goldwater served until 1987, chairing the Armed Services Committee and co-authoring the Goldwater–Nichols Act of 1986, a sweeping reorganisation of the military command structure. In his later years he broke with the religious right, supporting abortion rights and the right of gays to serve in the military. He died in 1998 at the age of 89.
