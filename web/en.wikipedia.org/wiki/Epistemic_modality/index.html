Epistemic modality

Epistemic modality is a sub-type of linguistic modality that concerns a speaker's judgment about the truth or likelihood of a proposition. In English it is expressed by modal auxiliaries such as must, might, may, and could, by adverbs such as probably and possibly, and by phrases such as "I think that…".

Examples

In "He must be home by now", must expresses a strong inference from evidence rather than an obligation; this is the epistemic reading, contrasted with the deontic (obligation) reading of "You must be home by ten". Weaker commitment is expressed by "He might/may/could be home". Many languages grammaticalise epistemic distinctions through evidential markers that indicate the source of the speaker's information.

Analysis

In formal semantics, following work by Angelika Kratzer, epistemic modals are analysed as quantifiers over possible worlds restricted by a conversational background consisting of what is known (the epistemic modal base) and an ordering source reflecting stereotypical expectations. Epistemic modality is studied in linguistics, philosophy of language, and epistemology, where it relates to graded belief and the semantics of probability statements.
