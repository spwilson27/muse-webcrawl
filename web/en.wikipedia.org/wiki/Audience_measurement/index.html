Audience measurement

Audience measurement measures how many people are in an audience, usually in relation to radio listenership and television viewership, but also in relation to newspaper and magazine readership and, increasingly, web traffic. Sometimes, the term is used in reference to practices that help broadcasters and advertisers determine who is listening, rather than how many people are listening.

Audience measurement figures are the currency of the broadcast advertising market: broadcasters sell advertising time based on the size and demographic composition of their audiences, and advertisers use the data to plan campaigns and evaluate their effectiveness.

Methods

Diaries

The diary method was one of the earliest forms of measurement. Panel households recorded their listening or viewing in paper diaries, typically for one week at a time. Diary methods remain in use for radio measurement in some markets, but are subject to errors of memory and compliance.

Meters

Electronic meters attached to television sets record when the set is on and what channel is tuned. The Portable People Meter (PPM), developed by Arbitron (now part of Nielsen Audio), is a pager-like device worn by panel members that detects inaudible codes embedded in audio broadcasts, allowing passive measurement of radio and television exposure. In the United States, Nielsen ratings are produced using panel data from meters installed in sample households.

Server and digital measurement

For online media, measurement relies on server logs, cookies, tags embedded in web pages and apps, and panel-based services such as those from comScore, which estimate unique visitors, page views, and time spent. Connected televisions and set-top box return-path data have added large-scale census-like datasets to television measurement.

Measures

  • Rating – the percentage of a defined population (or of households with television) tuned to a programme.
  • Share – the percentage of households actually using television (or radio) at the time that are tuned to a programme.
  • Reach (cume) – the number or percentage of different people exposed at least once over a period.
  • Average minute audience – the average number of people viewing during a typical minute.
  • Gross rating points (GRP) – the sum of ratings achieved by an advertising schedule.

Providers

Major audience measurement organisations include Nielsen (United States and many international markets), Kantar Media, BARB (United Kingdom), OzTAM (Australia), and GfK. In many countries measurement is commissioned by joint industry committees representing broadcasters, advertisers, and agencies, to ensure the data are accepted as a common trading currency.

Criticism

Audience measurement has been criticised for small sample sizes in panel methods, under-representation of some demographic groups, and difficulties in capturing out-of-home, time-shifted, and cross-platform viewing. The fragmentation of media consumption across streaming services has driven efforts toward cross-media measurement combining panel and big-data sources.
