The Blackpool Dance Festival is a ballroom dancing competition held every year in the Empress Ballroom at the Winter Gardens in Blackpool, England. First held in 1920, it is widely considered the most prestigious event in the world of competitive ballroom dancing — often described as its unofficial world championship — and runs for eight days each May.

History

Early festivals in the 1920s were held at the Tower Ballroom; the event took its modern form after moving to the Empress Ballroom. The festival grew from a mainly British competition into a global one, especially after dance couples from Japan, China, and Eastern Europe began to dominate entries in the late twentieth century. Alongside the main festival, Blackpool hosts a Junior Dance Festival and, since the 1990s, events for juveniles and seniors.

Events

The festival's core competitions are the British Open Championships in professional, amateur, and pro-am categories for both Ballroom (International Standard) and Latin American styles, judged by panels of former champions. Thousands of competitors from more than fifty countries typically take part, accompanied by exhibitions, lectures, and a large trade fair for dancewear and shoes.
