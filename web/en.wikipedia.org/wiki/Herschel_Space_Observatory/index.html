Herschel Space Observatory

The Herschel Space Observatory was a space observatory built and operated by the European Space Agency (ESA). It was active from 2009 to 2013 and was, at the time of its launch, the largest infrared telescope ever put into orbit, with a primary mirror 3.5 metres (11.5 ft) in diameter. It was named after Sir William Herschel, the discoverer of the infrared spectrum and of the planet Uranus.

Mission

Herschel was launched on 14 May 2009 aboard an Ariane 5 rocket from the Guiana Space Centre, together with the Planck spacecraft. It operated from a halo orbit around the second Lagrangian point (L2), 1.5 million kilometres from Earth. Its instruments — the PACS and SPIRE cameras and the HIFI heterodyne spectrometer — were cooled by more than 2,000 litres of superfluid helium; the mission ended on 29 April 2013 when the helium coolant was exhausted, and the spacecraft was formally switched off on 17 June 2013.

Science results

Herschel observed the coldest and dustiest objects in the universe, studying the formation of stars and galaxies, the chemistry of interstellar space, and the composition of comets and planetary atmospheres. Its notable results include the discovery of water vapour in unexpected places, detailed measurements of star-forming clouds in the Milky Way, evidence that comet Hartley 2 contains water with a composition similar to Earth's oceans, and observations of the aftermath of galaxies colliding in the early universe.
