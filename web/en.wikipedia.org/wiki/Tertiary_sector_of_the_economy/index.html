Tertiary sector of the economy

The tertiary sector of the economy, generally known as the service sector, is the third of the three economic sectors in the three-sector model (also known as the economic cycle). The others are the primary sector (raw materials) and the secondary sector (manufacturing). The tertiary sector consists of the provision of services instead of end products. Services include activities such as retail, transport, distribution, food services, banking and finance, insurance, healthcare, education, entertainment, media, tourism, and professional and public services.

Characteristics

Services are intangible, are produced and consumed simultaneously, and cannot be stored or transported in the way goods can. Because service output is difficult to measure and productivity gains are slower than in manufacturing, economists have debated the sector's long-run effects on growth — a phenomenon known as Baumol's cost disease.

Size and development

The share of the tertiary sector rises with economic development: in most advanced economies services account for 70–80% of GDP and employment, and globally the sector contributes the largest share of output. Economists sometimes subdivide services further into a quaternary sector (information and knowledge-based services) and a quinary sector (high-level decision-making and public services).
