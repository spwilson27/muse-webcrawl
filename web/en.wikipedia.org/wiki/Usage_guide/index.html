Usage guide

A usage guide is a reference work that gives guidance on the use of a language: its grammar, vocabulary, pronunciation, punctuation, and style. Usage guides range from comprehensive manuals of style to short dictionaries of disputed points of usage, and they are consulted by writers, editors, and students who want to know which forms are standard, acceptable, or controversial.

History

The tradition of English usage guidance is often traced to Robert Lowth's A Short Introduction to English Grammar (1762) and Lindley Murray's bestselling grammar of 1795. In the twentieth century the best-known works included H. W. Fowler's A Dictionary of Modern English Usage (1926), Ernest Gowers's Plain Words (1948), and in the United States, Wilson Follett's Modern American Usage (1966) and Bryan A. Garner's Garner's Modern English Usage (first published 1998).

Prescription and description

Usage guides are a natural battleground for the tension between prescriptivism and descriptivism. Some guides chiefly lay down rules for "correct" usage; others, such as Merriam-Webster's Dictionary of English Usage (1989), survey the historical evidence and report how educated writers actually use contested forms. Modern guides typically combine evidence from corpora with judgments about register, clarity, and audience.

Manuals of style

Related works, often grouped with usage guides, include manuals of style such as The Chicago Manual of Style (first published 1906), the Associated Press Stylebook, and the MLA Handbook, which concentrate on citation, typography, and the mechanics of publishing as well as on points of usage.
