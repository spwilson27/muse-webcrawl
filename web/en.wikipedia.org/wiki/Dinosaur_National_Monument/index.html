Dinosaur National Monument

LocationUtah and Colorado, United States
Area210,844 acres (853 km²)
EstablishedOctober 4, 1915
Governing bodyNational Park Service

Dinosaur National Monument is a United States national monument straddling the border of Colorado and Utah at the confluence of the Green and Yampa rivers. It is famous for the Carnegie Dinosaur Quarry, a cliff face containing some 1,500 dinosaur fossils embedded in rock, and for its dramatic desert canyons.

Palaeontology

Palaeontologist Earl Douglass of the Carnegie Museum discovered the quarry in 1909 while searching for dinosaur fossils in the Jurassic Morrison Formation. The quarry yielded specimens of Allosaurus, Apatosaurus, Camarasaurus, Diplodocus and Stegosaurus, among others. Today the Quarry Exhibit Hall encloses the fossil-bearing wall, allowing visitors to view the bones in place.

History

President Woodrow Wilson proclaimed the original 80-acre quarry area a national monument in 1915. In 1938 the monument was expanded greatly to include the canyon country of the Green and Yampa rivers. A proposed Echo Park Dam inside the monument in the 1950s was defeated by a landmark conservation campaign, an important early victory for the modern environmental movement.

Geography and recreation

Beyond the quarry, the monument protects over 800 palaeontological sites, Fremont culture petroglyphs, and popular white-water rafting stretches of the Green and Yampa rivers amid the Uinta Mountains' eastern foothills.
