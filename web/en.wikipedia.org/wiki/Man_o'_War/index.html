Man o' War (March 29, 1917 – November 1, 1947) was an American Thoroughbred racehorse widely regarded as one of the greatest racehorses of all time. Affectionately known as "Big Red", he won 20 of his 21 races, set multiple world records, and during his two seasons of racing (1919–1920) did much to revive public interest in the sport in the United States.

Racing career

Bred by August Belmont Jr. and sold as a yearling to Samuel D. Riddle for $5,000, Man o' War was trained by Louis Feustel. He won nine of ten starts as a two-year-old; his only defeat came in the Sanford Memorial Stakes, when he was caught at the start and beaten by a horse aptly named Upset. At three he won the Preakness and Belmont Stakes (Riddle did not enter him in the Kentucky Derby), and victories in the Travers, St. Leger (American), and Jockey Club Gold Cup, frequently by enormous margins and under heavy imposts. His career closed with a celebrated match race victory over the Canadian Triple Crown winner Sir Barton at Kenilworth Park in October 1920.

Stud career and legacy

At stud at Faraway Farm in Kentucky, Man o' War sired numerous champions, most famously War Admiral, winner of the American Triple Crown in 1937, and was the grandsire of Seabiscuit through Hard Tack. He died in 1947 and was originally buried at Faraway Farm; his remains were later moved to the Kentucky Horse Park, where his grave is marked by a statue. In The Blood-Horse magazine's ranking of the top 100 American racehorses of the twentieth century, Man o' War was placed first.
