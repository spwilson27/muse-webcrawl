Eighteenth Amendment to the Constitution of Pakistan

The Eighteenth Amendment to the Constitution of Pakistan (Urdu: آئین پاکستان میں اٹھارویں ترمیم) was adopted by the National Assembly of Pakistan on 8 April 2010 and by the Senate on 15 April 2010; it was signed into law by President Asif Ali Zardari on 19 April 2010. The amendment significantly reshaped Pakistan's constitutional order, restoring the parliamentary character of the 1973 Constitution and reversing changes introduced under military rulers.

Main provisions

  • It removed the President's power to dissolve the National Assembly unilaterally (Article 58(2)(b)), a power repeatedly used to dismiss elected governments.
  • It devolved substantial powers to the provinces, abolishing the Concurrent Legislative List and transferring subjects such as education and health to provincial control.
  • It renamed the North-West Frontier Province as Khyber Pakhtunkhwa.
  • It revised the appointment process for senior judges through a Judicial Commission and a parliamentary committee.
  • It made the Council of Common Interests and the National Economic Council more representative of the provinces, and strengthened provincial shares in federal resources.

Background and reception

The amendment was drafted by a bipartisan Parliamentary Committee on Constitutional Reforms and passed unanimously, an unusual consensus in Pakistani politics. It is widely regarded as a landmark in the country's constitutional development for advancing provincial autonomy and parliamentary sovereignty, though debates continue over the devolution of subjects such as higher education and the balance between federation and provinces.
