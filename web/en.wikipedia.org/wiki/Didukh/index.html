Didukh

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A didukh sheaf in a Ukrainian home]

A didukh (Ukrainian: дідух, from дід, "grandfather") is a Ukrainian Christmas decoration made from a sheaf of wheat, oats, rye, or flax, traditionally prepared from the first or last sheaf of the harvest. It symbolises the spirits of the family's ancestors, the bounty of the harvest, and the unity of the family across generations, and occupies a place of honour in the home during the Christmas season.

Custom

On Christmas Eve (Sviatyi Vechir), the head of the household ceremonially carries the didukh into the house and sets it in the pokutia, the icon corner, where it remains through the holidays near the table set with the twelve meatless dishes of the Holy Supper. Straw from the sheaf may be spread beneath the tablecloth and on the floor to recall the stable at Bethlehem and to invite ancestral spirits. Hay or straw from the didukh is afterwards fed to livestock or saved for its supposed healing and protective powers.

Construction and symbolism

A didukh is bound from dried stalks into one or several upright bundles, often with three tiers said to represent the past, present, and future of the family, or the unity of heaven, earth, and the underworld in pre-Christian Slavic cosmology. Modern didukhy range from simple sheaves to elaborate woven "trees" decorated with ribbons, dried flowers, and berries; very large public didukhy are erected in Ukrainian cities at Christmas.

History

The custom is rooted in pre-Christian agrarian rites honouring ancestors and ensuring fertility, later absorbed into Ukrainian folk Christianity. Suppressed along with other religious observances under Soviet rule, the didukh revived after Ukrainian independence in 1991 and has become a prominent emblem of Ukrainian Christmas, including in the diaspora.
