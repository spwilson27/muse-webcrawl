Festa della Repubblica

Festa della Repubblica (Italian, "Festival of the Republic") is the Italian National Day and Republic Day, observed annually on 2 June. It commemorates the referendum held on 2 June 1946, in which the Italian people voted to abolish the monarchy and establish a republic following the end of World War II and the fall of Fascism.

History

On 2–3 June 1946, Italians went to the polls in the first truly universal suffrage election in the country's history — women voted nationally for the first time. Voters chose between a republic and the monarchy, and simultaneously elected a Constituent Assembly to draft a new constitution. The official results, announced on 18 June 1946, gave the republic about 54.3% of the vote. King Umberto II left Italy, and Alcide De Gasperi became head of state in the interim. The Constitution of the Italian Republic came into force on 1 January 1948.

Celebrations

The centrepiece of the celebrations is a large military parade in Rome, held along the Via dei Fori Imperiali and attended by the President of the Italian Republic. The Frecce Tricolori aerobatic team of the Italian Air Force flies over the capital, trailing smoke in the green, white and red of the Italian flag. Ceremonies are also held in cities across Italy and at Italian embassies worldwide.
