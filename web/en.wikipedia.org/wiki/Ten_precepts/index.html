Ten precepts

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The ten precepts (Pali: dasasikkhāpada; Sanskrit: daśaśikṣāpada) refer to the ten training rules observed by Buddhist samaneras (novice monks) and samaneris (novice nuns). They are also undertaken by devout lay practitioners on observance days (uposatha) and during periods of intensive meditation, extending the basic five precepts.

The precepts

#PaliEnglish
1Pāṇātipātā veramaṇīRefraining from taking life
2Adinnādānā veramaṇīRefraining from taking what is not given
3Abrahmacariyā veramaṇīRefraining from all sexual activity (complete celibacy)
4Musāvādā veramaṇīRefraining from false speech
5Surāmerayamajjapamādaṭṭhānā veramaṇīRefraining from intoxicants
6Vikālabhojanā veramaṇīRefraining from eating at the wrong time (after noon)
7Naccagītavāditavisūkadassanā veramaṇīRefraining from dancing, singing, music and entertainments
8Mālāgandhavilepanadhāraṇamaṇḍanavibhūsanaṭṭhānā veramaṇīRefraining from garlands, perfumes, cosmetics and adornments
9Uccāsayanamahāsayanā veramaṇīRefraining from high and luxurious beds
10Jātarūparajatapaṭiggahaṇā veramaṇīRefraining from accepting gold and silver (money)

Practice

Novices undertake the ten precepts upon ordination and observe them until full ordination as a bhikkhu or bhikkhuni, when they become subject to the full monastic code (Vinaya): 227 rules for monks in the Theravada tradition. In Mahayana traditions, novices observe a similar set of ten precepts derived from the Brahmajāla Sūtra and other texts.

Lay followers may observe the eight precepts — a subset in which the seventh and eighth precepts are combined and the third is understood as complete celibacy — on full-moon observance days or during retreats.
