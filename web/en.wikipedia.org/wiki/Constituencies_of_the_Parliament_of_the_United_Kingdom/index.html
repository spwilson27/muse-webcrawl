Constituencies of the Parliament of the United Kingdom

Westminster constituencies following the 2023 boundary review.

The Parliament of the United Kingdom currently has 650 constituencies, each electing one Member of Parliament (MP) to the House of Commons by the first-past-the-post system. Constituencies are divided between county constituencies and borough constituencies (burgh constituencies in Scotland), a distinction that affects election expense limits and the type of returning officer.

Boundaries are reviewed periodically by four independent boundary commissions, one for each country of the United Kingdom. The 2023 Periodic Review of Westminster constituencies, implemented for the 2024 general election, kept the total at 650 seats but redrew most boundaries so that electorates, with a few protected exceptions such as island seats, fall between 69,724 and 77,062 electors. England has 543 constituencies, Scotland 57, Wales 32 and Northern Ireland 18.

History

Before the Reform Act 1832, representation was distorted by "rotten boroughs" with tiny electorates. Successive reforms in 1832, 1867, 1884–85, 1918 and 1948 redistributed seats and standardised the franchise, and standing boundary commissions were established in the 1940s to keep constituency sizes roughly equal.
