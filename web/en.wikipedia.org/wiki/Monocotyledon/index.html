Monocotyledons
CladeAngiosperms
Species~60,000–70,000

Monocotyledons, commonly referred to as monocots, are a major group of flowering plants (angiosperms) whose seeds typically contain only one embryonic leaf, or cotyledon. They form a natural (monophyletic) group in modern APG classification.

IMAGE: A young wheat plant, a typical monocot, showing parallel-veined leaves and fibrous roots

Characteristics

  • Single cotyledon in the seed
  • Leaf veins usually parallel
  • Flower parts typically in multiples of three
  • Vascular bundles scattered through the stem (atactostele), generally lacking true secondary growth
  • Fibrous (adventitious) root systems

Economic importance

Monocots include the world's staple cereals — rice, wheat, maize, barley, sorghum — as well as sugarcane, bananas, palms, bamboo, onions and orchids. Grasses alone dominate vast biomes and underpin global food supply.
