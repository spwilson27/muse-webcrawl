Scissor doors

TypeAutomobile door hinge design
First usedAlfa Romeo Carabo concept (1968)
Popularised byLamborghini Countach (1974)
Other namesLambo doors, vertical doors

Scissor doors (also called Lambo doors, jack-knife doors, beetle-wing doors or vertical doors) are automobile doors that rotate vertically at a fixed hinge at the front of the door, rather than outward as with a conventional door. The hinge is arranged near the end of the windscreen, and the door opens upward, pivoting slightly outward as it rises.

History

The design first appeared on the 1968 Alfa Romeo Carabo concept car designed by Marcello Gandini at Bertone. Gandini applied it to production with the Lamborghini Countach (1974): the mid-engined car's width and poor rear visibility made the upward-opening doors practical as well as dramatic, allowing the driver to open the door and lean out while reversing. Scissor doors became a signature of Lamborghini's V12 flagship models, including the Diablo, Murciélago and Aventador.

Advantages and disadvantages

Scissor doors can be opened in tight parking spaces and let occupants enter where a wide conventional door could not swing. They are, however, more expensive and complex to engineer, require strong hinges and gas struts, can be difficult to open if the car rolls onto its roof, and are poorly sealed against weather in some designs. Several later Lamborghini models (such as the Huracán and Gallardo) reverted to conventional doors, reserving scissor doors for the flagship line.

Other applications

Scissor or scissor-like doors have appeared on other production cars and concepts, including the Toyota Sera, the Autozam AZ-1 (gullwing), various McLaren models (technically "dihedral" doors), and numerous aftermarket conversions, which popularised the term "Lambo doors" in car modification culture.
