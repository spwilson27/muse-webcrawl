Seattle is a seaport city on the West Coast of the United States, the seat of King County, Washington, and the largest city in the Pacific Northwest. It sits on an isthmus between Puget Sound and Lake Washington, about 160 km south of the Canadian border. The metropolitan area of about four million is one of the fastest-growing in the U.S. and a major gateway for trade with Asia.

History

The area was inhabited for at least 4,000 years by peoples including the Duwamish, whose leader Chief Si'ahl (Seattle) gave the city its name. American settlers arrived in 1851; logging built the town, and the Klondike Gold Rush of 1897 made Seattle the outfitting port for Alaska — a link still visible in its ties to Alaska. Boeing, founded in Seattle in 1916, anchored its industry through both world wars. The 1962 World's Fair left the Space Needle and monorail. From the 1980s the region became a software and e-commerce powerhouse — Microsoft (founded in Albuquerque, based in nearby Redmond) and Amazon (founded in Seattle in 1994) — while grunge music (Nirvana, Pearl Jam) and Starbucks coffee gave it global cultural brands. The 1999 WTO protests ("Battle in Seattle") and the tech boom's housing pressures are the modern counterpoints to its progressive image.
