Ashoka (non-profit organization)

Ashoka
Founded: 1980
Founder: Bill Drayton
Headquarters: Arlington, Virginia, U.S.
Focus: Social entrepreneurship

Ashoka (formerly branded Ashoka: Innovators for the Public) is an international non-profit organization founded in 1980 by Bill Drayton that promotes social entrepreneurship by identifying and supporting leading social entrepreneurs through its Ashoka Fellows programme. It is headquartered in Arlington, Virginia, and operates in dozens of countries around the world.

History

Drayton, a former McKinsey consultant and assistant administrator of the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency, founded Ashoka in 1980, naming it after Ashoka, the 3rd-century BCE Indian emperor regarded as a pioneer of social and economic development. The first Ashoka Fellows were elected in India in 1981. Drayton is credited with popularising the term "social entrepreneur" for individuals who combine entrepreneurial methods with social goals.

Ashoka Fellows

Ashoka Fellows are elected through a rigorous selection process based on criteria including a new idea, creativity, entrepreneurial quality, the social impact of the idea, and ethical fibre. Fellows receive a living-cost stipend, typically for three years, along with access to a global network of peers and professional support, so that they can devote themselves full-time to launching their ventures. More than 4,000 Fellows have been elected in over 90 countries. Well-known Fellows include Nobel laureate Muhammad Yunus of Grameen Bank, Kailash Satyarthi, and Wendy Kopp of Teach For America.

Programmes

Beyond the fellowship, Ashoka runs programmes such as Ashoka U, which promotes social innovation in universities, Changemakers, an open platform for social innovation competitions and communities, and initiatives supporting young changemakers. Its stated vision is an "Everyone a Changemaker" world.
