Jukebox musical

A jukebox musical is a stage musical or musical film whose score consists largely of previously released popular songs, rather than music written specifically for the production — taking its name from the jukebox, a machine that plays selected recordings for coin payment.

Types

Two broad forms predominate. Biographical jukebox musicals tell the life story of a performer or group using their own catalogue, such as Jersey Boys (The Four Seasons), Beautiful: The Carole King Musical, and Ain't Too Proud (The Temptations). Fictional jukebox musicals weave a new plot around existing hits, most famously Mamma Mia! (1999), built on the songs of ABBA, which became one of the highest-grossing musicals of all time.

History

Precursors include revue-style shows and films such as Singin' in the Rain. The modern boom began with Buddy – The Buddy Holly Story (1989) and accelerated after Mamma Mia!, We Will Rock You (Queen), and Motown: The Musical. Later successes include & Juliet, which reimagines Shakespeare with the pop catalogue of Max Martin, and Moulin Rouge! The Musical.

Reception

Critics often dismiss jukebox musicals as commercially calculated and dramatically thin, while producers value their built-in audience recognition. Many have nevertheless won Tony and Olivier Awards and achieved long international runs.
