Tatarstan

The Republic of Tatarstan is a federal subject (republic) of Russia, located in the Volga Federal District at the confluence of the Volga and Kama rivers in Eastern Europe. Its capital and largest city is Kazan, a major cultural and economic centre. The republic is home to the Volga Tatars, a Turkic and predominantly Muslim people, alongside a large ethnic Russian population, and is one of Russia's most economically developed regions, with significant oil production, petrochemicals, and manufacturing.

History

The territory corresponds roughly to Volga Bulgaria, a medieval state that adopted Islam in 922 and was destroyed by the Mongol invasions in the 1230s. It then formed part of the Golden Horde and, after its fragmentation, of the Khanate of Kazan (1438–1552), which was conquered by Ivan the Terrible of Russia in 1552. Under the Russian Empire and the Soviet Union the region was administered as part of Kazan Governorate and, from 1920, as the Tatar Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic.

In August 1990 Tatarstan declared state sovereignty, and in 1992 held a referendum endorsing a sovereign status and adopted its own constitution. A 1994 bilateral treaty with Moscow granted the republic extensive autonomy, making Tatarstan a prominent model of asymmetric federalism in the 1990s; its special powers were progressively curtailed under Vladimir Putin's centralisation, and the power-sharing treaty lapsed in 2017. In 2023 the republic's head formally changed title from President to Rais following federal requirements.

Geography and demographics

Tatarstan lies on the East European Plain where the Kama joins the Volga; the Kuybyshev Reservoir occupies part of its west. Ethnic Tatars form a slight majority (about 53%), followed by Russians (about 40%), with Chuvash and other minorities. Islam and Russian Orthodoxy are the principal religions, and Tatar (a Kipchak Turkic language) and Russian share official status.

Economy

The republic is a major oil producer (Tatneft) with large petrochemical complexes at Nizhnekamsk, truck manufacturing by Kamaz at Naberezhnye Chelny, and helicopter and aircraft plants in Kazan. Kazan hosted the 2013 Summer Universiade and matches of the 2018 FIFA World Cup.
