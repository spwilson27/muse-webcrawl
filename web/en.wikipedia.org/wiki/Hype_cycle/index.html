Hype cycle

The hype cycle is a graphical representation of the maturity and adoption of technologies and applications, developed and popularized by the research firm Gartner. It depicts a common pattern in how public expectations for a new technology evolve over time, from initial enthusiasm through disillusionment to a realistic understanding of its value.

The five phases

The cycle comprises five phases. (1) The innovation trigger: a breakthrough or product launch generates media interest. (2) The peak of inflated expectations: publicity and early successes (and failures) drive expectations beyond what the technology can deliver. (3) The trough of disillusionment: interest wanes as experiments fail and producers are shaken out. (4) The slope of enlightenment: second- and third-generation products emerge and real benefits become understood. (5) The plateau of productivity: mainstream adoption takes off as the technology's value is proven. Gartner publishes hype-cycle reports for many domains, plotting individual technologies along the curve with estimates of time to maturity.

Criticism

Analysts and academics have criticized the hype cycle as unscientific — not all technologies follow the pattern, the phases lack objective measures, and the model offers little predictive precision — though it remains widely used as a heuristic in technology management and marketing.
