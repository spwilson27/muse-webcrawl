Scotch bonnet (also known as Bonney peppers, or Caribbean red peppers) is a variety of chili pepper named for its resemblance to a tam o' shanter (a traditional Scottish bonnet). It is a cultivar of Capsicum chinense, closely related to the habanero, and is ubiquitous in West African and Caribbean cuisines.

Scotch bonnet peppers in yellow, orange and red.

Culinary use

Scotch bonnets are essential to Jamaican jerk seasoning, hot sauces, and dishes such as peppered shrimp, escovitch fish and curried goat. Their flavour is often described as fruity and slightly sweet beneath intense heat rated at roughly 100,000 to 350,000 Scoville heat units — comparable to a habanero. The peppers ripen through green to yellow, orange and finally red.

Names

In Jamaica they are simply "Scotch bonnet peppers"; in Guyana a related round variety is called the ball-of-fire pepper, and in other islands they may be known as Bahama Mama or Jamaican Hot.
