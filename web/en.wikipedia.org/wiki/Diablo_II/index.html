Diablo II is an action role-playing video game developed by Blizzard North and published by Blizzard Entertainment in 2000. It is the sequel to the 1997 game Diablo and was followed by an expansion pack, Lord of Destruction (2001), and a remaster, Diablo II: Resurrected (2021).

Gameplay

Players choose from five character classes — Amazon, Necromancer, Barbarian, Sorceress and Paladin — with the expansion adding the Druid and Assassin. Progressing through four acts of procedurally generated levels, the player battles hordes of monsters, gathers randomly generated magical items, and allocates attribute and skill points to build increasingly specialised characters. Finishing the game on Normal difficulty unlocks Nightmare, and then Hell, with stronger monsters and better loot.

The pursuit of Diablo, Lord of Terror, takes the player from the Rogue Encampment through the deserts of Lut Gholein and the jungles of Kurast to the Pandemonium Fortress and Hell itself. Online play over Battle.net, including ladder seasons and the permadeath Hardcore mode, kept the game popular for decades.

Reception and legacy

Diablo II sold over a million copies in its first two weeks and won numerous game-of-the-year awards. Its loot-driven design, skill trees and dark atmosphere made it one of the most influential action RPGs ever made, shaping later titles such as Path of Exile.
