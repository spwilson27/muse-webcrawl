L'Anse aux Meadows

L'Anse aux Meadows
Location: Newfoundland, Canada
Founded: c. 1000 CE by Norse explorers
UNESCO World Heritage Site: 1978

L'Anse aux Meadows is an archaeological site on the northern tip of Newfoundland, Canada, containing the remains of a Norse settlement dating to around 1000 CE — the only confirmed site of pre-Columbian trans-oceanic contact between Europe and the Americas.

Discovery

Norwegian explorer Helge Ingstad and archaeologist Anne Stine Ingstad identified the site in 1960, guided by local resident George Decker. Excavations revealed eight turf-and-timber buildings, a forge, and Norse artefacts including a ringed pin and spindle whorl.

Significance

The settlement, possibly the Straumfjǫrðr or a gateway camp linked to the Vinland of the Icelandic sagas, housed perhaps 70–90 people briefly. Tree-ring analysis published in 2021 dated Norse wood-cutting at the site precisely to 1021 CE. See Vinland and Leif Erikson.
