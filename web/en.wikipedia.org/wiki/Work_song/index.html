Work song

A work song is a piece of music closely connected to a form of work, either sung while conducting a task — often to co-ordinate timing — or a song linked to a task which might be a connected narrative, description, or protest song.

Examples: Sea shanties, field hollers, waulking songs, chain-gang songs

Functions and types

Work songs synchronise group effort (hauling, rowing, threshing), set pace, relieve boredom, and express grievance. Traditions include African American field and prison work songs that fed into blues, sailors' sea shanties, Scottish waulking songs, and military cadence calls.

Recordings

Alan Lomax recorded prison work songs in the American South, preserving a tradition that mechanisation largely ended. See Folk music.
