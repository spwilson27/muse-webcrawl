West End of London

The West End of London (commonly referred to as the West End) is a district of Central London, west of the City of London and north of the River Thames, in which many of the city's major tourist attractions, shops, businesses, government buildings and entertainment venues, including West End theatres, are concentrated.

The term was first used in the early 19th century to describe fashionable areas to the west of Charing Cross. The West End covers parts of the boroughs of Westminster and Camden.

History

While the City of London is the historic and financial centre, the West End developed as the fashionable centre for shopping and entertainment from the 17th century onwards, when wealthy residents moved west to escape the smoke of the industrial city and to be close to the royal court at Westminster. Areas such as Mayfair, St James's and Marylebone became home to the aristocracy.

During the 19th century the construction of theatres, music halls, department stores and hotels transformed Shaftesbury Avenue, the Strand and Oxford Street into a major entertainment and retail district.

Districts and streets

Major areas include Mayfair, Soho, Covent Garden, Fitzrovia, Marylebone, Bloomsbury, Knightsbridge and Kensington. Famous streets include Oxford Street, Regent Street, Bond Street, Piccadilly, Shaftesbury Avenue and the Strand.

Theatre

"West End theatre" is the mainstream professional theatre staged in the large theatres in and near the West End. Along with New York City's Broadway theatre, West End theatre is usually considered to represent the highest level of commercial theatre in the English-speaking world.
