Deucalion

In Greek mythology, Deucalion (/djuːˈkeɪliən/; Greek: Δευκαλίων) was the son of the Titan Prometheus. He is known from the myth of the great flood sent by Zeus, in which he and his wife Pyrrha were the only survivors and became the ancestors of the Hellenic peoples — a story closely paralleling the flood myths of Noah and of the Mesopotamian tradition.

The flood

Warned by Prometheus of Zeus's plan to destroy the corrupt Bronze Age race of men, Deucalion built a chest (larnax) and stocked it with provisions. When the rains came and flooded most of Greece, Deucalion and Pyrrha drifted for nine days and nights before landing on Mount Parnassus. There Deucalion made sacrifices to Zeus Phyxius ("the god of escape").

Repopulating the earth

Consulting the oracle of Themis on how to restore mankind, the couple were told to "throw the bones of their mother behind them". Understanding "mother" to mean Mother Earth (Gaia) and her bones the stones of the ground, they cast stones over their shoulders: those thrown by Deucalion became men, and those thrown by Pyrrha became women.

Offspring

Deucalion and Pyrrha's children included Hellen, the legendary ancestor of the Hellenes (Greeks), Amphictyon, and Protogeneia. The myth is recounted by Apollodorus, Ovid in the Metamorphoses, and Hyginus, among others.
