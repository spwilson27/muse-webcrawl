Avalokiteśvara

Avalokiteśvara
Sanskritअवलोकितेश्वर ("Lord who gazes down")
TraditionMahāyāna Buddhism
AttributeCompassion (karuṇā)
East Asian formsGuanyin (China), Kannon (Japan), Gwanse-eum (Korea)
MantraOṃ maṇi padme hūṃ

Avalokiteśvara is a bodhisattva who embodies the compassion of all Buddhas. One of the most widely revered bodhisattvas in Mahāyāna Buddhism, Avalokiteśvara is associated with the mantra Oṃ maṇi padme hūṃ and is the central figure of the "Universal Gateway" chapter of the Lotus Sutra.

Forms and iconography

Avalokiteśvara is depicted in many forms, including the thousand-armed, eleven-headed Ekādaśamukha, symbolising the bodhisattva's capacity to perceive and respond to suffering everywhere. In Tibet the bodhisattva is known as Spyan ras gzigs (Chenrezig) and is regarded as the patron deity of Tibet; the Dalai Lamas are considered his emanations.

In East Asia

In China, Avalokiteśvara became the goddess Guanyin, typically depicted in female form, and in Japan as Kannon. The bodhisattva's cult spread throughout East and Southeast Asia, and Guanyin remains among the most beloved figures in Chinese folk religion, Buddhism and Taoism alike.
