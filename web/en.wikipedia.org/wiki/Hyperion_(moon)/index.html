Hyperion (moon)

Hyperion, also known as Saturn VII, is a moon of Saturn discovered by William Cranch Bond, his son George Phillips Bond and William Lassell in 1848. It is distinguished by its irregular shape, its chaotic rotation, and its unexplained sponge-like appearance.

Physical characteristics

Hyperion is one of the largest highly irregular bodies in the Solar System, measuring roughly 360 × 266 × 205 km, with a low density of about 0.54 g/cm³ suggesting it is mostly water ice with a porous, rubble-like interior. Its surface is covered with deep, sharp-edged craters that give it a sponge-like look; dark material at the crater bottoms is thought to come from the moon Phoebe.

Chaotic rotation

Unlike most moons, Hyperion is not tidally locked. Its strongly non-spherical shape and its eccentric orbit, together with a 3:4 orbital resonance with the large moon Titan, make its rotation chaotic: its orientation tumbles unpredictably and cannot be forecast over long periods. It was the first known example of chaotic rotation in the Solar System.

Exploration

Hyperion was imaged by Voyager 2 in 1981 and in detail by the Cassini spacecraft, whose close flyby in September 2005 provided the striking sponge-like portraits and evidence of hydrocarbons on the surface.
