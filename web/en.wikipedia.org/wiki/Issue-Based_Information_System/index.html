Issue-Based Information System

The Issue-Based Information System (IBIS) is an argumentation-based approach to clarifying wicked problems — complex, ill-defined design and planning problems. It was developed by Horst Rittel and colleagues in the 1970s, building on the work of Werner Kunz.

Structure

IBIS represents a discussion as a network of three node types: issues (questions to be resolved), positions (proposed answers), and arguments (statements supporting or objecting to a position). Issues can generalise, replace or be raised by other elements, forming a directed graph of the deliberation.

Applications

The notation underlies design rationale capture in software engineering and tools such as gIBIS and Compendium. It is related to other argumentation schemes including Toulmin's model and Questions, Options and Criteria (QOC).

References: Kunz & Rittel (1970); Conklin, Dialog Mapping.
