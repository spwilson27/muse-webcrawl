Y-chromosomal Adam

In human genetics, the Y-chromosomal most recent common ancestor (Y-MRCA, informally known as Y-chromosomal Adam) is the patrilineal most recent common ancestor from whom all currently living humans are descended through the male line. Because the Y chromosome is passed largely unchanged from father to son, all living men's Y chromosomes coalesce back to this single ancestral chromosome.

Definition

The Y-chromosomal Adam is defined retrospectively: he is the most recent man from whom all living men descend in an unbroken male line. He was not the only man alive in his time, nor the first anatomically modern human; other men of his generation have descendants alive today, but their direct father-to-son lines have since died out. The identity of the Y-MRCA also shifts forward in time whenever an entire patrilineal branch goes extinct.

Age estimates

Estimates of when Y-chromosomal Adam lived have changed substantially with new data and methods. Studies in the 2000s commonly suggested around 60,000–142,000 years ago. The 2013 discovery of the very divergent A00 haplogroup in an African American man, and its presence in the Mbo people of Cameroon, pushed estimates back dramatically, to roughly 200,000–338,000 years ago in some analyses, overlapping the age range estimated for Mitochondrial Eve, the analogous matrilineal ancestor traced through mitochondrial DNA. Later large-scale sequencing studies have generally placed the Y-MRCA in the range of about 200,000 to 300,000 years ago, in Africa, before the major Out-of-Africa dispersals.

Relationship to Mitochondrial Eve

The biblical names are coincidental metaphors: Y-chromosomal Adam and Mitochondrial Eve need not have lived at the same time or place, never met, and were each one member of a population of thousands. Their lineages simply happen to be the ones that survive unbroken through one sex.
