Slender-billed vulture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The slender-billed vulture (Gyps tenuirostris) is an Old World vulture native to sub-Saharan regions of Southeast Asia and the Indian subcontinent. It is listed as critically endangered on the IUCN Red List, having suffered a catastrophic population collapse in the late twentieth and early twenty-first centuries.

Taxonomy

The slender-billed vulture was long treated as a subspecies of the Indian vulture (Gyps indicus) but is now recognised as a full species. It is distinguished by its slimmer build, darker coloration and finer, less heavily feathered neck and bill.

Distribution and habitat

Its range extends from northern India and Nepal through Myanmar, Thailand, Laos, Cambodia and Vietnam, where it inhabits open country, savanna and areas near human habitation where livestock carcasses are available.

Decline

Like other Gyps vultures in Asia, its decline was caused primarily by poisoning from diclofenac, a veterinary anti-inflammatory drug administered to cattle; vultures feeding on treated carcasses suffer kidney failure and visceral gout. Conservation measures include bans on veterinary diclofenac, vulture restaurants, captive breeding and reintroduction programmes.
