Pine Flat Dam

Pine Flat Dam, Fresno County, California

Pine Flat Dam is a concrete gravity dam on the Kings River in the foothills of the Sierra Nevada, about 28 miles (45 km) east of Fresno, California. Completed in 1954 by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers, it stands 440 feet (134 m) high and creates Pine Flat Lake, a reservoir with a capacity of about 1,000,000 acre-feet (1.2 km³) of water.

Purposes

The dam's primary authorised purpose is flood control for the Kings River basin and the city of Fresno and surrounding farmland in the San Joaquin Valley. Its reservoir also stores irrigation water for distribution through the Kings River system, supports recreation on Pine Flat Lake, and generates hydroelectric power: the Jeff L. Taylor Pine Flat Power Plant, completed in the 1980s by the Kings River Conservation District, has a capacity of about 165 megawatts.

History

Proposals to dam the Kings River date to the early twentieth century. Federal construction began in 1947 amid debate between the Army Corps of Engineers and the Bureau of Reclamation over which agency should build and operate the project. The dam was finished in 1954. Because flood control was its primary purpose, the reservoir is drawn down seasonally to reserve space for winter and spring runoff from Sierra snowmelt.

Fish and wildlife

Regulated releases from Pine Flat Dam sustain a trout fishery in the lower Kings River below the dam, one of the notable tailwater fisheries in California.
