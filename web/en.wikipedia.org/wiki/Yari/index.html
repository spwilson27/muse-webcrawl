Yari

The yari (槍) is a traditional Japanese spear, typically consisting of a straight blade mounted on a long wooden shaft. Blades were forged by swordsmiths using the same techniques as Japanese swords, with a tang (nakago) running deep into the shaft, and could range from a few centimetres to nearly a metre in length; total weapon length commonly ran from about 2 to more than 6 metres for the longest infantry pikes.

Types

Yari are classified by blade shape. The plain straight sugu-yari was the most common. Cross-bladed forms include the jūmonji-yari, with two lugs curved upward like a magatama-edged cross, and the kama-yari, with a sickle-like side blade. The omi-yari is an exceptionally long-bladed (and long-shafted) type associated with massed ashigaru tactics under Oda Nobunaga, whose pike units used spears of up to about 5.6 metres. Short or unusual forms include the triangular-section sankaku-yari and the one-handed te-yari used indoors by guards.

Use

The spear enjoyed periods of supremacy in Japanese warfare: it eclipsed the bow and sword as the primary weapon of foot soldiers during the Sengoku period's massed battles, though it had been a mainstay of mounted and foot combat since the introduction of Chinese-style spears in the early historic period. The art of spear fighting, sōjutsu, was taught in numerous classical schools (koryū), many of which survive today. Famous spearmen, such as Honda Tadakatsu with his famed spear Tonbokiri ("Dragonfly Cutter"), are fixtures of Japanese historical tradition.
