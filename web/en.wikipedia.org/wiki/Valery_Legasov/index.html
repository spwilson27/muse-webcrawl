Valery Legasov

Valery Alekseyevich Legasov (1 September 1936 – 27 April 1988) was a Soviet inorganic chemist and professor at Moscow State University. He is best known as the chief scientific investigator of the Chernobyl disaster of 26 April 1986 and for his attempts to press for openness and reactor-safety reform afterward.

Chernobyl

Legasov was appointed to the government commission investigating the accident and spent months at the site, directing scientific aspects of the response, including the dropping of boron, sand, and lead on the exposed core. He presented the Soviet report to the International Atomic Energy Agency in Vienna in August 1986, which was praised abroad for its candour, though at home he became disillusioned as his warnings about the design flaws of the RBMK reactor and the culture of secrecy were resisted.

Death and legacy

Having recorded his frustrations in tape-recorded memoirs, Legasov died by suicide on 27 April 1988, shortly after the second anniversary of the disaster. He was posthumously awarded the title Hero of the Russian Federation in 1996. His tapes informed the BBC docudrama and the 2019 HBO/Sky miniseries Chernobyl, in which he was played by Jared Harris.
