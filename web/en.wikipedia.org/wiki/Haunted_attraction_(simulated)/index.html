Haunted attraction (simulated)

A haunted attraction is a form of live entertainment — most commonly a haunted house, haunted hayride, or scare maze — that simulates the experience of visiting a location inhabited by ghosts, monsters, and other frightening figures. Actors in costume and make-up, animatronics, elaborate sets, special effects, and sound design are used to startle and thrill paying guests. Though some operate year-round, most open for the autumn Halloween season.

History

Simulated haunts descend from nineteenth-century illusion shows, Madame Tussaud's Chamber of Horrors, and early twentieth-century "ghost houses" at fairs and in works such as the Paris Théâtre du Grand-Guignol. In the United States, home-made haunted houses became a Halloween tradition in the 1930s as a wholesome alternative to pranks. Charity organisations — notably the Jaycees — turned haunted houses into major fund-raisers in the 1960s and 1970s, and commercial professional haunts followed, beginning with ventures such as the Haunted Vine in California. Theme parks built large-scale seasonal events, including Knott's Scary Farm (from 1973) and Universal's Halloween Horror Nights (from 1991).

Types and industry

Attractions include walk-through haunted houses, outdoor scream parks combining several haunts, haunted hayrides, corn mazes, and extreme "full contact" or immersive experiences requiring waivers. The modern industry in the United States comprises thousands of attractions, from small charity haunts to multimillion-dollar productions employing hundreds of "scareactors." Operators gather at trade shows such as TransWorld's Halloween & Attractions Show, and industry groups publish safety codes covering fire exits, actor conduct, and crowd control — a regulatory area reshaped after the deadly 1984 Haunted Castle fire at Six Flags Great Adventure.

In popular culture

Beyond Halloween season, simulated haunts appear in Japan as obakeyashiki, popular in summer, the traditional season for ghost stories. The genre's folk cousin is the putatively "real" haunted house tour, offered at sites claiming actual paranormal activity.
