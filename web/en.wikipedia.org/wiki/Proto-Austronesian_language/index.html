Proto-Austronesian (commonly abbreviated as PAN or PAn) is the reconstructed ancestor of the Austronesian languages, one of the major language families of the world. It is believed to have been spoken in Taiwan roughly 5,000–6,000 years ago, before the Austronesian peoples began their seaborne expansion across Island Southeast Asia, the Pacific, and as far west as Madagascar.

Homeland

The great diversity of Austronesian languages in Taiwan — where most of the family's primary subgroups are found among the indigenous Formosan languages — is the principal linguistic evidence that Taiwan was the Austronesian homeland. The "Out of Taiwan" model, supported by archaeology, holds that speakers of Proto-Austronesian or its immediate descendants began migrating to the northern Philippines around 2000 BCE, from where the family spread rapidly.

Phonology

Proto-Austronesian is reconstructed with a modest consonant inventory. Robert Blust's widely used reconstruction distinguishes a series of consonants including *p, *t, *k, *q, *b, *d, *g, sibilants and affricates such as *C and *z, nasals *m, *n, *ŋ, liquids *l, *r, *R, and glides *w, *y, as well as the glottal stop *ʔ and *h. Four vowels are reconstructed: *i, *ə, *a, *u. Syllables were typically of the form (C)V(C), and penultimate stress is commonly assumed.

Grammar

Proto-Austronesian is reconstructed with the famous Austronesian alignment (or "focus") system, in which the verb is morphologically marked to indicate the semantic role of the privileged noun phrase in the clause — actor voice, patient voice, locative voice and circumstantial/benefactive voice. Verb-initial word order (VSO or VOS) is generally reconstructed for the proto-language.
