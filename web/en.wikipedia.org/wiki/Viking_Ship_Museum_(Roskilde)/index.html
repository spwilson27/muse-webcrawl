Viking Ship Museum (Roskilde)

The Viking Ship Museum (Danish: Vikingeskibsmuseet) in Roskilde, Denmark, is a museum dedicated to ships, seafaring, and boatbuilding culture of the Viking Age and the Middle Ages. It is built around the five Skuldelev ships, Viking vessels that were deliberately sunk around 1070 in Roskilde Fjord to block a navigation channel, and excavated in 1962.

The Skuldelev ships

The five wrecks represent the main Viking ship types: two warships (a large longship, Skuldelev 2, and a smaller one, Skuldelev 5), two cargo vessels (a large ocean-going knarr, Skuldelev 1, and a smaller coastal trader, Skuldelev 3), and a fishing boat (Skuldelev 6). The original fragments are displayed in the Viking Ship Hall, opened in 1969, which stands at the edge of the fjord.

Reconstructions and Museum Island

Since the 1980s the museum has built full-scale reconstructions of the five ships using traditional tools and methods. The most famous, Sea Stallion from Glendalough (a reconstruction of Skuldelev 2), sailed to Dublin in 2007 and back in 2008. In 1997 the museum expanded to the Museum Island, with a boatyard where traditional wooden boats are built, and an archaeological workshop. Visitors can sail in replica Viking boats on the fjord during the summer season.
