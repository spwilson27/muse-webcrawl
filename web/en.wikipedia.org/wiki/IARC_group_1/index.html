IARC group 1

Agents classified as carcinogenic to humans

The IARC hazard classification scheme

IARC group 1 is the highest category in the classification of the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC), part of the World Health Organization. Agents in group 1 are classified as carcinogenic to humans, based on sufficient evidence of carcinogenicity in humans, or in some cases sufficient evidence in animals together with strong evidence of a relevant mechanism in humans.

Meaning of the classification

The IARC groups express the strength of the scientific evidence that an agent can cause cancer (a hazard assessment), not the level of risk at any given exposure. Consequently group 1 contains both extremely dangerous exposures and comparatively common ones: entries include tobacco smoke, asbestos, plutonium, benzene, alcoholic beverages, solar radiation (ultraviolet), processed meat, and the bacterium Helicobacter pylori, among more than a hundred agents, mixtures and exposure circumstances.

The IARC groups

The other categories are group 2A (probably carcinogenic), group 2B (possibly carcinogenic), and group 3 (not classifiable as to its carcinogenicity to humans). A former group 4, "probably not carcinogenic", was retired after only one agent was ever placed in it.
