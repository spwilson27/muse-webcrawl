Lawrence Colburn

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lawrence M. Colburn (born July 6, 1949) is a former United States Army soldier who, as a young door gunner in Vietnam, intervened with helicopter pilot Hugh Thompson Jr. and crew chief Glenn Andreotta to stop the My Lai massacre on March 16, 1968.

My Lai

Colburn was serving as a door gunner on Thompson's OH-23 Raven helicopter, on a reconnaissance mission over the village of Sơn Mỹ. Seeing American troops killing unarmed Vietnamese civilians, Thompson landed between advancing U.S. soldiers and villagers, and Colburn and Andreotta manned their weapons covering Thompson while he confronted the ground troops and arranged for the evacuation of wounded civilians. Colburn later testified about the massacre and, three decades later, publicly supported the prosecution of those responsible.

Recognition

In 1998, thirty years after the event, Colburn, Thompson, and (posthumously) Andreotta were awarded the Soldier's Medal, the U.S. Army's highest award for heroism not involving conflict with an enemy. Colburn afterwards worked to preserve the memory of the intervention, speaking to military and school audiences about moral courage and the duty to disobey unlawful orders. He appeared in documentaries and at the My Lai memorial in Vietnam.
