Tuva (Russian: Тува́), officially the Republic of Tuva, is a republic of Russia. It is situated in the extreme south of Siberia, bordering Mongolia to the south, and lies at the geographic centre of Asia. Its capital is the city of Kyzyl. The population is predominantly ethnically Tuvan, a Turkic people known internationally for the throat-singing tradition called khöömei.

History

Tuva was part of the Mongol Empire and later ruled by Qing-dynasty China as part of Outer Mongolia. After the fall of the Qing in 1911, it became a Russian protectorate (Uryankhay Krai). The Tuvan People's Republic existed as a partially recognised Soviet satellite state from 1921 to 1944, when it was annexed by the USSR. The American physicist Richard Feynman famously developed a fascination with Tuva (then unreachable for most Westerners), inspiring the book Tuva or Bust! by Ralph Leighton.

Geography and culture

The republic is a mountain-rimmed basin drained by the upper Yenisei River. Traditional Tuvan culture is nomadic-herding based, with Buddhism (in its Tibetan form) and shamanism as the principal religions. Groups such as Huun-Huur-Tu and the Alash Ensemble have popularised Tuvan throat singing worldwide.
