Band of Brothers is a 2001 American war drama miniseries based on historian Stephen E. Ambrose's 1992 non-fiction book of the same name. It was created by Steven Spielberg and Tom Hanks, who also served as executive producers and had collaborated on the 1998 World War II film Saving Private Ryan.

The series dramatizes the history of "Easy" Company, 2nd Battalion, 506th Parachute Infantry Regiment of the 101st Airborne Division, from jump training in the United States through its participation in major actions in Europe until Japan's capitulation and the end of World War II in 1945.

Episodes

The ten episodes include "Currahee", "Day of Days", "Carentan", "Replacements", "Crossroads", "Bastogne", "The Breaking Point", "The Last Patrol", "Why We Fight" and "Points". Each episode opens with excerpts from interviews with surviving veterans of Easy Company.

Production

The series was filmed in England, principally at Hatfield Aerodrome in Hertfordshire, with a budget reported at around $125 million, making it one of the most expensive television productions of its time. Spielberg and Hanks also produced the companion series The Pacific (2010) and Masters of the Air (2024).

Reception

The series was a major critical success, winning the Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Miniseries and the Golden Globe Award for Best Miniseries or Television Film, among many other honours.
