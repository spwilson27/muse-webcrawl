Saint Marinus

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[hero image: Saint Marinus]
Marinus, founder of San Marino

Saint Marinus (Italian: San Marino; traditionally 3 September 257 – 3 September 301) was a stonemason and Christian deacon credited with founding the settlement that became the Republic of San Marino — regarded as the world's oldest surviving sovereign state and constitutional republic. His feast day, 3 September, is San Marino's national day and marks the traditional date of the republic's foundation in 301.

Life and legend

According to tradition, Marinus was born on the island of Rab (Arbe) in the Adriatic, off the coast of modern Croatia, and worked as a stonemason in Rimini. Fleeing the Diocletian persecution of Christians around 301, he withdrew as a hermit to Monte Titano, where he built a small church and gathered a community. Tradition holds that the wealthy Roman matron Felicissima, owner of the mountain, donated the land to the community. On his deathbed Marinus is said to have uttered the words "Relinquo vos liberos ab utroque homine" ("I leave you free from both men") — a reference to his community's independence from both emperor and pope.

San Marino

The community Marinus founded evolved over centuries into the Republic of San Marino, an enclave entirely surrounded by Italy. Its claims to continuity from 301 make it, by tradition, the oldest republic in the world. Marinus appears on Sammarinese coinage and stamps, and the Basilica of San Marino in the capital houses relics attributed to him, including part of his skull. He is the patron saint of San Marino, of bachelors, and of deacons.
