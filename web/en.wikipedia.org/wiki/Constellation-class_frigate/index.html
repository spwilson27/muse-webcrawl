Constellation-class frigate

An artist's rendering of the future USS Constellation (FFG-62).

The Constellation class is a class of guided-missile frigates being built for the United States Navy under the FFG(X) programme. The design is based on the Italian–French FREMM multipurpose frigate, modified for U.S. Navy requirements. In April 2020 Fincantieri Marinette Marine won the contract to build the lead ship at its shipyard in Marinette, Wisconsin.

The lead ship, USS Constellation (FFG-62), is named for one of the U.S. Navy's original six frigates. The ships displace about 7,300 tons, are roughly 151 metres long, and are designed for anti-air, anti-surface and anti-submarine warfare in both blue-water and littoral environments. They are armed with a 57 mm gun, a 32-cell Mark 41 vertical launching system, Naval Strike Missiles, a Rolling Airframe Missile launcher, and carry an MH-60R helicopter. Sensors include the AN/SPY-6(V)3 radar and the Aegis Baseline 10 combat system.

Construction of USS Constellation began in 2022, though design changes and workforce challenges delayed delivery beyond the original 2026 target. The Navy planned a class of at least 20 ships, with later vessels including Congress (FFG-63) and Chesapeake (FFG-64).
