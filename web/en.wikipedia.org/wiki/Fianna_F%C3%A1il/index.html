Fianna Fáil – The Republican Party (Irish: Fianna Fáil – An Páirtí Poblachtánach) is a conservative political party in Ireland. Founded in 1926 by Éamon de Valera and other opponents of the Anglo-Irish Treaty who had left Sinn Féin, it has historically been one of the two dominant parties of Irish politics, alongside Fine Gael.

History

De Valera founded Fianna Fáil after splitting from Sinn Féin over the question of taking seats in Dáil Éireann, which the abstentionists refused to do while an oath of allegiance to the British Crown was required. The party entered the Dáil in 1927 and first came to power in 1932, beginning a period of dominance in which it governed for most of the following eight decades, under leaders including Seán Lemass, Jack Lynch, Charles Haughey, Albert Reynolds, and Bertie Ahern.

Decline and coalition era

Fianna Fáil suffered a dramatic collapse in the 2011 general election following the Irish financial crisis, falling to third place. Under Micheál Martin it recovered, supporting a Fine Gael minority government from 2016, and in 2020 entered a historic coalition government with Fine Gael and the Green Party, with Martin serving as Taoiseach. Its name is often translated as "Soldiers of Destiny" or "Warriors of Ireland".
