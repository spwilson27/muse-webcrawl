Mexican art

Mexican art encompasses the visual arts produced in Mexico from the ancient Mesoamerican civilisations to the present day. It is noted for the great pre-Hispanic traditions of the Olmec, Maya and Mexica (Aztec), for the hybrid colonial art of New Spain, and for the world-famous muralist movement of the twentieth century.

Pre-Hispanic art

The Olmec produced colossal basalt heads and jade carvings; Teotihuacan covered its pyramids with murals; Maya art reached high levels in stelae, lintels, ceramics and the murals of Bonampak; and the Mexica carved monumental sculpture such as the Sun Stone (Calendar Stone) and the Coatlicue statue. Featherwork, goldwork and codices were also major media.

Colonial period

After the Spanish conquest, art fused European and indigenous traditions. Churches in Mexican Baroque and Churrigueresque styles were decorated with altarpieces, while casta paintings depicted the racial mixtures of colonial society. Feather mosaics, inlaid enconchado panels and the paintings of Miguel Cabrera are distinctive products of New Spain.

Nineteenth century

The Academy of San Carlos promoted European academic styles, while landscape painter José María Velasco became famous for his views of the Valley of Mexico. Folk traditions — pottery, textiles, retablos and ex-votos — flourished outside the academy.

Mexican muralism

After the Mexican Revolution, the state commissioned monumental public murals celebrating history and social ideals. The leaders, "Los Tres Grandes", were Diego Rivera, José Clemente Orozco and David Alfaro Siqueiros. Alongside them, Frida Kahlo developed her intense, autobiographical painting, and Rufino Tamayo pursued a more universal modernism. The mid-century Generación de la Ruptura reacted against muralist nationalism.

Contemporary art

Contemporary Mexican artists such as Gabriel Orozco, Francis Alÿs (based in Mexico) and Teresa Margolles work internationally in conceptual and installation art, while Mexico City has become a major centre for galleries and art fairs.
