Cholistan Desert

Cholistan Desert
Location: Punjab, Pakistan
Area: about 26,300 km²
Climate: arid subtropical
Also known as: Rohi

The Cholistan Desert (Urdu: صحرائے چولستان), also locally known as Rohi, is a desert region in the southwestern part of Punjab province of Pakistan that stretches into the Thar Desert of India. It covers an area of about 26,300 square kilometres and extends into the Indian states of Rajasthan and Gujarat.

Geography

The word Cholistan is derived from the Turkic word chol, meaning "desert", and the Persian suffix -istan, meaning "land of". The desert is divided into two parts: the Lesser Cholistan in the north and west, with relatively fertile alluvial flats, and the Greater Cholistan in the south, a barren area of sand dunes rising up to 150 metres in height.

History

The desert was once watered by the Hakra River, along whose banks flourished settlements of the Indus Valley Civilisation. More than 400 archaeological sites have been recorded along the dry river course. The imposing Derawar Fort, a massive square fortress with bastions up to 30 metres high, is the best surviving monument of the region.

Life and culture

The inhabitants of Cholistan traditionally lead a semi-nomadic life, moving in search of water and fodder for their cattle. The region is known for its camel breeding, folk music, and crafts, and hosts the annual Cholistan Jeep Rally around Derawar Fort.
