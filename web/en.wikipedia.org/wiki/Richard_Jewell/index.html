Richard Jewell

Richard Allensworth Jewell (born Richard White; December 17, 1962 – August 29, 2007) was an American security guard and police officer famous for his role in the events surrounding the Centennial Olympic Park bombing at the 1996 Summer Olympics in Atlanta, Georgia. Jewell discovered a backpack containing three pipe bombs, alerted police and helped evacuate the area, saving many lives. He was initially hailed as a hero, but the FBI then investigated him as a suspect and media coverage widely implied his guilt before he was exonerated.

The bombing

Working as a security guard for the AT&T pavilion, Jewell spotted a suspicious unattended backpack under a bench in the early hours of July 27, 1996. A bomb squad confirmed it contained explosives; Jewell helped clear the area before the device exploded, killing one person and injuring 111. Without the evacuation, casualties would have been far worse.

Investigation and exoneration

Days later, news reports revealed that the FBI was treating Jewell as a possible "lone bomber," and he endured 88 days of intense media scrutiny and surveillance. In October 1996 the Department of Justice formally cleared him. The real bomber, Eric Rudolph, was arrested in 2003 and pleaded guilty in 2005. Jewell settled libel claims against several media outlets; the Atlanta Journal-Constitution litigated and prevailed on the grounds its reporting was substantially true at the time.

Later life

Jewell worked in law enforcement in Georgia and died of complications from diabetes and kidney failure at age 44. His story was dramatised in Clint Eastwood's 2019 film Richard Jewell.
