Epoprostenol

Trade namesFlolan, Veletri
ClassProstacyclin (PGI2) analogue
RoutesIntravenous infusion; inhaled
Elimination half-life~6 minutes

Epoprostenol is a synthetic analogue of prostacyclin (PGI2). Sold under the brand names Flolan and Veletri, it is a potent vasodilator and inhibitor of platelet aggregation. It is used to treat pulmonary arterial hypertension (PAH), where long-term continuous intravenous infusion improves exercise capacity, symptoms and survival in severe disease.

Medical uses

Epoprostenol is indicated for the long-term treatment of pulmonary arterial hypertension (WHO Group I) in patients with NYHA Class III–IV symptoms. Because it has a very short half-life (about six minutes), it must be given by continuous intravenous infusion through a permanent central venous catheter using an ambulatory infusion pump. Abrupt interruption of the infusion can cause rebound pulmonary hypertension and can be fatal, so patients carry backup pumps and medication. Inhaled epoprostenol is used off-label in critical care as a selective pulmonary vasodilator for refractory hypoxemia and right ventricular failure.

Adverse effects

Common side effects include flushing, headache, jaw pain, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, muscle and joint aches, anxiety and flu-like symptoms; many relate to dose and to prostacyclin's vasodilatory action. Serious risks are chiefly those of the delivery system: catheter-related bloodstream infection and sepsis, and pump malfunction causing overdose or abrupt interruption. High-output cardiac failure can occur with chronic high doses.

Pharmacology

Epoprostenol is identical to endogenous prostacyclin. It binds the prostacyclin (IP) receptor on vascular smooth muscle and platelets, activating adenylate cyclase, raising cyclic AMP, and thereby producing vasodilation of pulmonary and systemic arterial beds and inhibiting platelet aggregation. It is rapidly hydrolyzed in blood to 6-keto-PGF1α. The original formulation (Flolan) required reconstitution with a specific diluent and cooling during administration; a later room-temperature-stable formulation (Veletri) simplified use.

History

Prostacyclin was identified in 1976 by a team including Salvador Moncada and John Vane, work contributing to Vane's 1982 Nobel Prize. Synthetic epoprostenol was approved for PAH in the United States in 1995, and a landmark randomized trial published in 1996 showed a survival benefit in primary pulmonary hypertension — the first therapy to do so.
