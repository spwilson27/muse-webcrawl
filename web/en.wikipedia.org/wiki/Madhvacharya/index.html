Madhvacharya

Madhvacharya
Born1238 (or 1199), Pajaka, near Udupi, Karnataka, India
Died1317
PhilosophyDvaita Vedanta (dualism)
HonoursFounder of Dvaita; chief proponent of Tattvavada

Madhvacharya (1238–1317), also known as Purna Prajna and Ananda Tirtha, was an Indian philosopher and theologian, the chief proponent of the Dvaita (dualism) school of Vedanta. He taught that the individual soul (Atman) and God (Brahman, identified with Vishnu) are eternally and irreducibly distinct, and that the world is real — positions opposed to the monism of Shankara's Advaita.

Life

Tradition places Madhva's birth at Pajaka village near Udupi in coastal Karnataka. He took sannyasa as a young man, studied under Achyutapreksha, and travelled widely in India debating rival schools. At Udupi he founded the Krishna Matha, installing an image of Krishna, and established the Ashta Mathas — eight monasteries whose swamis worship at the temple in rotation. He is traditionally held to be an incarnation of the wind god Vayu.

Philosophy

Madhva's system, called Tattvavada ("realistic viewpoint"), rests on five eternal differences (pancha-bheda): between God and soul, God and matter, soul and soul, soul and matter, and one material thing and another. Vishnu alone is independent (svatantra); all else is dependent (paratantra). Liberation comes through devotion (bhakti) and divine grace. Souls are intrinsically graded (taratamya), a doctrine that drew criticism from other schools.

Works

Madhva is credited with 37 works in Sanskrit, including commentaries on the Brahma Sutras, the principal Upanishads and the Bhagavad Gita, and the summary treatise Tattva Sankhyana.
