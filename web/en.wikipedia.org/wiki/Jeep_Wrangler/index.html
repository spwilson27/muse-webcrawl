The Jeep Wrangler is a series of compact and mid-size off-road sport utility vehicles manufactured by Jeep since 1986. The Wrangler is the direct descendant of the Second World War Willys MB and the civilian Jeep CJ models, and is recognisable by its boxy body, removable doors and roof, fold-down windscreen, solid axles and body-on-frame construction.

Design

The Wrangler retains the classic Jeep formula: a separate ladder frame, live front and rear axles, a two-speed transfer case and low-range gearing for off-road driving. Trail-rated Rubicon models add locking differentials, an electronically disconnecting front sway bar and lower crawl ratios. The vehicle's doors, roof and windscreen can be removed, a feature carried over from its military ancestors.

The related Jeep Gladiator pickup, introduced for 2020, shares the JL platform and front-end styling.

Reception

The Wrangler is one of the most iconic American vehicles, with an extensive aftermarket and owner culture (including the informal "Jeep wave" between drivers). It consistently posts strong resale values, and the JL generation has been among Jeep's best-selling models.
