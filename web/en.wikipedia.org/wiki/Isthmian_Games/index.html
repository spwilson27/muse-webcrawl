Isthmian Games

The Isthmian Games (Ancient Greek: Ἴσθμια) were one of the Panhellenic Games of Ancient Greece, held near the Isthmus of Corinth in honour of the sea god Poseidon. Together with the Olympic, Pythian, and Nemean Games, they formed the circuit (periodos) of premier athletic festivals.

History

Tradition held that the games were founded as funeral games for the drowned child-hero Melicertes, whose body was carried ashore by a dolphin. They were reorganised around 580 BC and held every two years, in the spring of the second and fourth years of each Olympiad, at the sanctuary of Poseidon at Isthmia. The prize was originally a wreath of celery, later of pine.

Events and later history

Events included foot races, wrestling, boxing, the pankration, the pentathlon, chariot racing, and contests in music and poetry. In 196 BC the Roman general Titus Quinctius Flamininus used the games to proclaim the freedom of the Greeks from Macedonian rule. The games continued under Roman patronage until the late 4th century AD.
