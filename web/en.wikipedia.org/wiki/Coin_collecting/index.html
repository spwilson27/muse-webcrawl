Coin collecting

Coin collecting is the collecting of coins or other forms of minted legal tender. Coins of interest to collectors often include those that circulated for only a brief time, coins with mint errors, and especially beautiful or historically significant pieces. Coin collecting can be distinguished from numismatics — the systematic study of currency — although the two overlap considerably.

History

The hobby has ancient roots: coins were hoarded in antiquity, and by the Renaissance rulers and scholars assembled systematic cabinets of ancient coins; the Italian poet Petrarch is often cited as an early collector-scholar. Coin collecting became a popular pastime in the nineteenth century with the spread of standardised national coinages, and organised clubs such as the American Numismatic Association (founded 1891) helped popularise it.

Motivations and approaches

Collectors may assemble coins by country, period, denomination, metal or theme, or complete "type sets" of one example of each design. Others focus on ancient coins, commemorative issues, bullion coins or error coins. Condition — assessed on standard grading scales — strongly affects value, as do rarity and demand.

Market

Coins are bought and sold through dealers, auctions, coin shows and online marketplaces. Third-party grading and encapsulation services authenticate and grade coins. Rare, high-grade pieces can command very high prices: famous rarities include the 1933 United States double eagle and the 1804 silver dollar.

Care and storage

Collectors store coins in albums, holders or inert plastic capsules, and are advised to handle coins by their edges and avoid cleaning, which can damage surfaces and reduce value.
