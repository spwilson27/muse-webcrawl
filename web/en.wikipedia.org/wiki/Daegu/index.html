Daegu (Korean: 대구, pronounced [tɛɡu]), formerly spelled Taegu and officially known as Daegu Metropolitan City, is a city in southeastern South Korea. It is the third-largest urban agglomeration in South Korea after Seoul and Busan, and the fourth-largest city overall, with a population of over 2.3 million residents as of 2023.

Daegu sits in a basin surrounded by low mountains, in the valley of the Geumho River, a tributary of the Nakdong River. Because of this topography the city is known for being one of the hottest in South Korea during the summer, earning it the nickname "Daefrica" (a portmanteau of Daegu and Africa). The city has long been a centre of the Korean textile industry and hosts the Daegu International Airport.

History

Archaeological evidence shows the Daegu area has been settled since prehistoric times, and it became an important market town under the Silla kingdom. During the Joseon dynasty, Daegu was the administrative capital of Gyeongsang Province and hosted the famous Seomun Market, one of the three great markets of the era. During the Korean War the city stood just inside the Pusan Perimeter and was the site of fierce fighting in 1950, though it was never captured by North Korean forces.

In 2003 a fire set by an arsonist on a Daegu subway train killed nearly 200 people, one of the worst disasters in modern South Korean history. The city hosted the 2011 World Championships in Athletics and the 2002 FIFA World Cup.

Geography and climate

Daegu is located at the southeastern end of the Korean Peninsula. Surrounding peaks include Mount Palgong (1,193 m) to the north and Mount Biseul to the south. The city has a humid subtropical climate; summers are hot and humid, with temperatures regularly exceeding 35 °C, while winters are cold and dry.

Economy

Daegu's economy was traditionally dominated by textiles, and the city remains a fashion and fabric centre, hosting the annual Daegu International Textile Fair. Machinery, metal processing and medical industries (the city promotes itself as a "Medicity") have grown in importance.

Culture

The city is known for its yangnyeom (seasoned) dishes, the Daegu Yangnyeongsi herbal medicine market, and the annual Daegu Chimac Festival celebrating chicken and beer. Major attractions include the Donghwasa temple on Mount Palgong, the Daegu National Museum, and the modern art venue Daegu Art Museum.
