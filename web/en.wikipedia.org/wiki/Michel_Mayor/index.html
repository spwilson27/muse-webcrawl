Michel Mayor

Michel Mayor
Born12 January 1942, Lausanne, Switzerland
Known forFirst exoplanet around a Sun-like star (51 Pegasi b, 1995, with Didier Queloz)
AwardsNobel Prize in Physics (2019)
[Image: Photograph of Michel Mayor at the La Silla Observatory in Chile]
Mayor at La Silla.

Michel Gustave Édouard Mayor (born 12 January 1942) is a Swiss astrophysicist and professor emeritus at the University of Geneva's Department of Astronomy. With Didier Queloz he discovered 51 Pegasi b in 1995 — the first Exoplanet found orbiting a Sun-like star — a discovery that transformed astronomy by proving planetary systems are common and often unlike our own. He shared half of the 2019 Nobel Prize in Physics with Queloz.

Mayor's discovery relied on the ELODIE spectrograph he led at Haute-Provence Observatory, measuring stellar radial velocities to ~13 m/s: 51 Pegasi's wobble revealed a Jupiter-mass planet in a 4.2-day orbit — a 'hot Jupiter' no formation theory had predicted. He later led the HARPS spectrograph consortium, which found hundreds of planets including super-Earths in habitable zones.

Impact

Before 1995, planets were known only around our Sun (and, from 1992, around a Pulsar). Mayor and Queloz's result detonated the field: within a generation, over 5,000 exoplanets were catalogued by radial velocity, transit (Kepler) and imaging, and the 2019 Nobel — awarded with cosmologist James Peebles — symbolised exoplanet science joining cosmology as Nobel-grade astronomy.
