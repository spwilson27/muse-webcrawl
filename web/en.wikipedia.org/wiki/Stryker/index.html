Stryker

The Stryker is a family of eight-wheeled armoured fighting vehicles derived from the Canadian LAV III, itself based on the Swiss Mowag Piranha. It is produced by General Dynamics Land Systems and entered service with the United States Army in 2002 as the core vehicle of the Army's Stryker brigade combat teams.

Design and variants

The Stryker was designed to be rapidly deployable — transportable in a C-130 cargo aircraft — while providing more firepower and protection than light infantry vehicles. The family includes the Infantry Carrier Vehicle (ICV), the Mobile Gun System with a 105 mm cannon, reconnaissance, mortar carrier, command, engineer, medical evacuation, anti-tank guided missile, and a nuclear-biological-chemical reconnaissance variant. Vehicles can be fitted with slat armour or explosive reactive armour for additional protection against rocket-propelled grenades.

Service

Strykers were deployed extensively in Iraq and Afghanistan. Their road speed and quiet operation were praised, while their vulnerability to improvised explosive devices prompted upgrades, including double-V hull versions. The name honours two unrelated Medal of Honor recipients surnamed Stryker.
