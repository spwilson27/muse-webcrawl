Non-negative matrix factorization

Non-negative matrix factorization (NMF or NNMF) is a group of algorithms in multivariate analysis and linear algebra in which a matrix V is factorised into (usually) two matrices W and H, with the property that all three matrices have no negative elements: V ≈ WH. The non-negativity makes the resulting representation easier to interpret, because data is modelled as purely additive combinations of parts.

History

The method traces to a 1994 paper by Paatero and Tapper on "positive matrix factorization" in environmental science. It was popularised in machine learning by Daniel Lee and H. Sebastian Seung, whose 1999 Nature paper showed that NMF learns a parts-based representation of faces — basis images resembling eyes, noses and mouths — unlike the holistic eigenfaces of principal component analysis, and whose 2001 paper gave simple multiplicative update rules for minimising the reconstruction error or divergence.

Algorithms

Finding an exact factorisation is NP-hard in general, so NMF is computed approximately, typically by alternating optimisation: fixing one factor and solving a non-negative least-squares problem for the other, or by Lee and Seung's multiplicative updates, which are a rescaled gradient descent with guaranteed non-increasing cost. The rank (number of components) must be chosen in advance and is usually far smaller than the matrix dimensions, giving a compressed, low-rank approximation.

Applications

NMF is used in text mining and topic modelling (documents as additive mixtures of topics), recommender systems, image and audio source separation, bioinformatics (for example extracting mutational signatures from cancer genomes and clustering gene expression), and chemometrics.
