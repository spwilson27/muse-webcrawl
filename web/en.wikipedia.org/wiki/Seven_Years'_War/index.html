The Seven Years' War (1756–1763) was a global conflict fought between major European powers and their allies across five continents. It pitted Great Britain, Prussia, and their allies against France, Austria, Russia, Spain, and Sweden. Often described as the first "world war", it decided the colonial balance in North America and India and confirmed Prussia's status as a great power in Europe.

Background

The war grew out of the unresolved rivalries of the War of the Austrian Succession (1740–1748) and the Anglo-French colonial competition overseas. The "Diplomatic Revolution" of 1756 realigned the old alliances: Austria allied with France to recover Silesia from Prussia, while Britain allied with Prussia to protect Hanover.

Course of the war

In Europe, Frederick the Great of Prussia invaded Saxony in August 1756 and fought a series of celebrated battles, including Rossbach and Leuthen (1757) and Kunersdorf (1759), where he was heavily defeated. The death of Tsarina Elizabeth of Russia in 1762 and the accession of the Prussophile Peter III — the "Miracle of the House of Brandenburg" — saved Prussia from collapse. In North America (where the conflict is known as the French and Indian War), British forces under James Wolfe captured Quebec in 1759, and Montreal fell in 1760. In India, Robert Clive's victory at Plassey (1757) laid the foundations of British rule in Bengal.

Peace settlements

The Treaty of Paris (1763) transferred Canada and French claims east of the Mississippi to Britain, while Louisiana went to Spain. The Treaty of Hubertusburg restored the pre-war status quo in Europe, leaving Silesia in Prussian hands. France's defeat effectively ended its position as a major colonial power in the Americas.

Legacy

The war's enormous cost pushed Britain to tax its American colonies, helping to spark the American Revolution. It also left Prussia entrenched as a great power and began Britain's ascendancy as the dominant global colonial and naval empire.
