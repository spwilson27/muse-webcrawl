Cremona

Country: Italy
Region: Lombardy
Province: Cremona
Population: approx. 72,000
Known for: Violin making (Stradivari, Amati, Guarneri), Torrazzo bell tower

Cremona (Italian: [kreˈmoːna]) is a city and comune in northern Italy, situated in Lombardy on the left bank of the Po River in the middle of the Po Valley. It is the capital of the province of Cremona and the seat of the local government. The city is celebrated worldwide as the home of the great violin-making families — the Amati, Guarneri and Stradivari — whose instruments of the sixteenth to eighteenth centuries remain unsurpassed.

History

Cremona was founded as a Roman colony in 218 BC, among the first north of the Po, and later became a prosperous medieval free commune. It was fought over by the Visconti and Sforza of Milan, held by Venice, Spain and Austria in turn, and joined the Kingdom of Italy in 1859. The Romanesque cathedral, begun in 1107, and the adjacent Torrazzo — at 112.7 metres one of the tallest brick bell towers in the world — dominate the historic centre.

Violin making

Andrea Amati established the Cremonese school of lutherie in the mid-sixteenth century, inventing the modern violin family. Antonio Stradivari (1644–1737), Cremona's most famous craftsman, made over a thousand stringed instruments. The traditional craftsmanship of violin making in Cremona was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2012, and the city's Museo del Violino displays masterpieces of the craft.
