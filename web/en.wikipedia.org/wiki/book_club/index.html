Book club

A book club is a reading group, usually consisting of a number of people who read and talk about books based on a topic or an agreed-upon reading list. It is common for book club members to meet in a regularly scheduled way (such as monthly) and discuss the same book they have all read. The term can also refer to a commercial scheme in which subscribers receive and pay for books by mail, such as the Book of the Month Club.

Reading groups

Discussion-based reading groups have existed in various forms since at least the nineteenth century, when literary societies and women's reading circles flourished. The modern book club typically chooses a title in advance, members read it independently, and then the group meets in a home, library, café or online to discuss it — often with the help of discussion questions supplied by publishers.

Public libraries and bookstores frequently sponsor or host clubs, and many publishers provide "reading group guides" bound into paperback editions. Celebrity-run clubs have a large effect on sales: Oprah's Book Club, launched in 1996, reliably turned its selections into bestsellers, and later television and online clubs, such as Reese Witherspoon's and various social-media communities (for example "BookTok" on TikTok), have continued the pattern.

Commercial book clubs

Mail-order book clubs became a major channel for book sales in the twentieth century. Founded in 1926, the Book of the Month Club in the United States sent a monthly selection chosen by a panel of judges, with members able to substitute alternates; the model was widely imitated, for example by the Literary Guild and many specialised clubs for science fiction, history or cooking. Such clubs declined with the rise of discount bookselling and e-commerce.
