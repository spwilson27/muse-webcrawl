Las Campanas Observatory (LCO) is an astronomical observatory in the Atacama Desert of northern Chile, owned and operated by the Carnegie Institution for Science. Established in 1969, it sits at an altitude of about 2,380 metres and is prized for its dark skies, dry air and excellent seeing.

Telescopes

The observatory's principal instruments are the twin 6.5-metre Magellan Telescopes, named Baade and Clay, which saw first light in 2000 and 2002. Other telescopes include the 2.5-metre du Pont telescope and the 1-metre Swope telescope, the latter long used by the Carnegie Supernova Project. The site has been selected for the future Giant Magellan Telescope, a 24.5-metre-equivalent segmented telescope under construction by an international consortium.

Discoveries

Las Campanas instruments have contributed to supernova cosmology, exoplanet research, and studies of distant galaxies. The Swope telescope was used by the Carnegie Supernova Project, whose observations of Type Ia supernovae helped refine the cosmic distance scale.
