Marjoram (Origanum majorana) is a cold-sensitive perennial herb in the mint family, Lamiaceae, grown for its sweet, citrus- and pine-flavoured leaves. Native to Cyprus, the Mediterranean and western Asia, it is closely related to oregano (Origanum vulgare) but milder and sweeter in flavour.

Culinary use

Marjoram is used fresh or dried in Mediterranean and Middle Eastern cooking — in sauces, soups, dressings and meat dishes — and is an ingredient of blends such as herbes de Provence and za'atar. In German cooking it is the traditional seasoning for goose and many sausages, hence its German name Wurstkraut ("sausage herb").

Cultivation

In cold climates it is treated as an annual. It prefers full sun and well-drained soil, and is propagated from seed, cuttings or division. Its essential oil contains terpinen-4-ol, sabinene hydrate and other aromatic compounds.
