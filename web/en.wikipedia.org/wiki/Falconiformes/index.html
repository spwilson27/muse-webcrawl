Falconiformes

The order Falconiformes is represented by the extant family Falconidae and several extinct families. Traditionally, the order has included all the diurnal birds of prey, but modern molecular phylogenies show that falcons are more closely related to parrots and passerines than to hawks and eagles, which now occupy the order Accipitriformes.

FamilyFalconidae (living)
SpeciesAbout 65 (falcons and caracaras)

Characteristics

Falcons are built for speed: long, pointed wings, streamlined bodies, and a notched "tomial tooth" on the upper mandible used to dispatch prey. The peregrine falcon is the fastest animal on Earth in its hunting stoop, exceeding 320 km/h (200 mph). Unlike hawks, falcons kill primarily with the beak rather than the talons.

Classification

The family Falconidae contains true falcons and kestrels (Falco), caracaras, falconets, and laughing falcons. DNA studies in the 2000s overturned the long-standing assumption of a close relationship with Accipitridae, reshaping raptor taxonomy.
