The Gordie Howe International Bridge is a cable-stayed international bridge across the Detroit River, connecting Detroit, Michigan, in the United States with Windsor, Ontario, in Canada. Owned by the Government of Canada and the State of Michigan and delivered by the Windsor–Detroit Bridge Authority, it provides a second modern crossing at the busiest commercial border point between the two countries, supplementing the privately owned Ambassador Bridge (1929).

Project

The six-lane bridge is being built by the Bridging North America consortium under a public–private partnership announced in 2018, with a project cost of about C$5.7 billion (later revised to C$6.4 billion). Its main span of 853 metres is the longest cable-stayed span in North America, supported by two A-shaped towers about 220 metres tall. The project includes large ports of entry on both sides, direct connections to Interstate 75 in Michigan and Highway 401 in Ontario via the Herb Gray Parkway, and a multi-use path for pedestrians and cyclists — the first pedestrian crossing of the Detroit River.

History

Plans for a new publicly owned crossing dated to the early 2000s, driven by congestion, redundancy concerns and opposition from the Ambassador Bridge's owners, who litigated against the project for years. Advance works began in 2015; full construction started in October 2018. The bridge deck was connected in mid-2024, and opening was targeted for fall 2025, later expected in early 2026. The bridge is named for Gordie Howe, the Canadian hockey legend who played most of his career with the Detroit Red Wings.
