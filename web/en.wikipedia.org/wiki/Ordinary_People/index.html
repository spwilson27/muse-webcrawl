Ordinary People

Ordinary People is a 1976 novel by American author Judith Guest, and the title of its highly acclaimed 1980 film adaptation, which marked the directorial debut of Robert Redford. The story portrays the disintegration of an affluent suburban family in Lake Forest, Illinois, after one son dies in a boating accident and the surviving son attempts suicide.

The novel

Guest's first novel, rejected by several publishers before Viking published it in 1976, alternates perspectives between Conrad Jarrett, the younger son recovering from a suicide attempt, and his father Calvin. Conrad's older brother Buck drowned when their sailboat capsized in a storm; the cold, perfectionist mother, Beth, withdraws emotionally, while Conrad slowly rebuilds his life with the help of his psychiatrist, Dr. Berger.

The film

The 1980 film, directed by Robert Redford and written by Alvin Sargent, starred Donald Sutherland (Calvin), Mary Tyler Moore in a dramatic turn as Beth, Timothy Hutton as Conrad, and Judd Hirsch as Dr. Berger. It won four Academy Awards, including Best Picture, Best Director, Best Adapted Screenplay, and Best Supporting Actor for Hutton, who at 20 was then the youngest winner in that category. Its win for Best Picture over Raging Bull remains a subject of critical debate.

Themes

Both novel and film are noted for their realistic treatment of grief, survivor's guilt, family denial, and the value of psychotherapy, and for helping bring depictions of therapy and depression into mainstream American culture.
