Yemeni civil war (2014–present)

Began: September 2014
Location: Yemen
Status: Ongoing, with periods of truce

The Yemeni civil war is an ongoing multilateral civil war that began in late 2014. It follows the Houthi takeover in Yemen, in which the Ansar Allah (Houthi) movement seized the capital Sanaa and ousted the internationally recognised government.

Background

After the 2011 Yemeni revolution, President Ali Abdullah Saleh was replaced by Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi. Transition faltered amid economic crisis, separatism in the south, and Houthi grievances in the north.

Escalation

In March 2015 a Saudi-led coalition intervened militarily in support of the recognised government. The war fragmented further, with the Southern Transitional Council, local forces, and jihadist groups controlling territory.

Humanitarian crisis

The United Nations has described Yemen's situation as one of the world's worst humanitarian crises, with widespread food insecurity, displacement, cholera, and damage to infrastructure. A UN-brokered truce in 2022 reduced fighting, though a lasting settlement remained elusive.
