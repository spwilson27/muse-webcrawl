Mount Greylock is the highest point in the U.S. state of Massachusetts, at 3,489 feet (1,063 metres) above sea level. Located in the northwest corner of the state in Berkshire County, within the Taconic Mountains, the mountain forms the centrepiece of the Mount Greylock State Reservation, Massachusetts' first state park, established in 1898.

Geography and geology

Greylock's summit ridge stretches across the towns of Adams, North Adams, Williamstown, Cheshire, Lanesborough and New Ashford. The mountain is composed largely of ancient metamorphic rock — schist and marble — thrust up during the Taconic orogeny. Its upper slopes support the only taiga-boreal forest in Massachusetts, with stands of red spruce and balsam fir more typical of northern New England and Canada.

Summit features

The summit is crowned by the 93-foot Veterans War Memorial Tower, a granite lighthouse-like shaft built in 1932 to honour the state's war dead, whose beacon was designed to be visible from great distances. Nearby stands Bascom Lodge, a rustic stone-and-wood lodge built by the Civilian Conservation Corps in the 1930s. On a clear day, views from the summit extend up to 90 miles, taking in parts of five states. An auto road, laid out in the early 20th century, climbs to the top; the Appalachian Trail also crosses the summit.

Cultural associations

The mountain has inspired generations of American writers. Nathaniel Hawthorne, Henry David Thoreau — who climbed it in 1844 and wrote of the night he spent on its summit — and Herman Melville all knew Greylock; from his home in Pittsfield, Melville looked out at the snow-covered ridge, which he compared to a great white whale, an image often linked to Moby-Dick. The name Greylock is said to honour a Missisquoi chief of the 18th century, also remembered in local tradition as Grey Lock.
