Shimazaki Tōson

Born25 March 1872, Magome, Japan
Died22 August 1943, Ōiso, Japan
OccupationPoet, novelist
Notable worksThe Broken Commandment; Before the Dawn

Shimazaki Tōson (1872–1943) was a Japanese author, first celebrated as a Romantic poet and later as a leading figure of Japanese naturalist fiction. Born Shimazaki Haruki in Magome, a post town on the Nakasendō highway, he is best known for the novel The Broken Commandment (1906) and the historical epic Before the Dawn (1929–1935).

Poetry

Tōson's early collection Wakanashū (1897) helped establish modern Japanese lyric poetry in colloquial-influenced verse. He taught at schools in Sendai and Komoro, experiences that later fed into his fiction.

Fiction

The Broken Commandment tells of a schoolteacher from the outcaste burakumin community who hides his origins, and is regarded as a landmark of socially engaged Japanese literature. The Family (1910–11) drew on his own relatives. His final masterpiece, Before the Dawn, chronicles the Meiji Restoration era through the life of his father in the Kiso valley. Tōson spent several years in France during the First World War and served as the first president of the Japanese PEN Club.
