Date of Easter

The date of Easter, the Christian feast of the Resurrection, is determined as the first Sunday after the first full moon (the Paschal full moon) occurring on or after the March equinox. This computus makes Easter a moveable feast falling between 22 March and 25 April in Western Christianity.


Determined by: Computus (lunisolar calculation)
Range (Western): 22 March – 25 April

Computus

The Council of Nicaea (325) standardised an independent calculation tied to the Jewish Passover's lunar calendar but computed by Christians themselves. Tables using the 19-year Metonic cycle and epacts were developed by Dionysius Exiguus and refined in the Gregorian calendar reform of 1582.

East–West differences

Eastern Orthodox churches use the Julian calendar's equinox and require Easter to follow Passover, so Orthodox Easter often falls later, between 4 April and 8 May Gregorian. Reform proposals, such as fixing Easter to a set Sunday in April, have been discussed ecumenically but not adopted.
