Men's rights movement

The men's rights movement addresses issues its supporters see as disadvantages faced by men

The men's rights movement (MRM) is a branch of the men's movement. The MRM in particular is composed of a variety of groups and individuals (men's rights activists, or MRAs) who focus on general social issues and specific government services which they say discriminate against men, or in some cases disadvantage and oppress them.

Issues

Common topics discussed within the men's rights movement include family law, such as child custody, adoption and child support; reproduction; suicide among men; domestic violence against men; false accusations of rape; military conscription; education; circumcision; and health policies. Men's rights activists also point to disparities in criminal sentencing, workplace deaths, and homelessness statistics.

History

The men's rights movement emerged in the 1970s out of the broader men's liberation movement, in part as a reaction to second-wave feminism. Groups such as the National Coalition for Men (founded 1977) campaigned on family law issues. The movement grew with books such as Warren Farrell's The Myth of Male Power (1993), and expanded considerably with internet activism in the 2000s and 2010s through blogs, forums, and websites.

Criticism

The movement has been widely criticized by feminists and scholars, who argue that it misrepresents gender inequality and, in some of its online forms, promotes misogyny and harassment. The Southern Poverty Law Center has described parts of the online men's rights milieu as promoting hatred of women, while men's rights advocates respond that they seek equal treatment and that criticism conflates extremist voices with the movement's legitimate concerns.
