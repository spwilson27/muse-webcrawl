Andrzej Witold Wajda (Polish: [ˈandʐɛj ˈvajda]; 6 March 1926 – 9 October 2016) was a Polish film and theatre director, widely regarded as Poland's greatest filmmaker and one of the most important directors in the history of European cinema. Recipient of an Honorary Oscar and the Palme d'Or, he became the chronicler of Poland's twentieth-century history, from the Second World War through the Solidarity movement.

Life and early career

Wajda was born in Suwałki, the son of an infantry officer who was murdered by the Soviet NKVD in the Katyń massacre of 1940 — an event Wajda filmed seventy years later in Katyń (2007). As a teenager he served in the Polish Home Army. After the war he studied painting in Kraków before enrolling at the Łódź Film School. His war trilogy — A Generation (1955), Kanał (1957, Special Jury Prize at Cannes), and Ashes and Diamonds (1958) — made his international reputation with their searing, symbol-laden portraits of young Poles destroyed by war and its political aftermath.

The cinema of moral anxiety

In the 1970s Wajda led the "cinema of moral anxiety", films probing the corruption and compromises of communist Poland. Man of Marble (1977) dissected the Stalinist cult of a model bricklayer; its sequel Man of Iron (1981), made amid the Gdańsk strikes and featuring Lech Wałęsa as himself, won the Palme d'Or at Cannes and became the filmic emblem of Solidarity. After martial law was imposed, Wajda worked partly abroad, directing Danton (1983) in France. He also directed theatre in Poland and internationally, and headed the Polish Filmmakers Association.

Later years

After 1989 Wajda served briefly as a senator in the first post-communist parliament. His later films include Korczak (1990), the epic Pan Tadeusz (1999), Katyń (2007, Oscar-nominated), Wałęsa: Man of Hope (2013), and his final film Afterimage (2016), about the avant-garde painter Władysław Strzemiński. Four of his films were nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film, and in 2000 he received an Honorary Academy Award for lifetime achievement. He founded a film school in Warsaw bearing his name and died in October 2016, aged 90.
