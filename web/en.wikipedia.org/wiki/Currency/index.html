Currency

[Image: a selection of international banknotes and coins — dollars, euros, yen and pounds]

A currency is a system of Money in common use within a country or monetary union — the US dollar, euro, yen, pound sterling and Chinese renminbi being the largest — serving as a medium of exchange, unit of account and store of value. Currencies are issued by central banks (notes) and treasuries (coin) and today consist overwhelmingly of bank deposits rather than cash.

Exchange and regimes

Most major currencies are fiat money — valuable by law and trust rather than backed by gold, a system general since the collapse of Bretton Woods in 1971–73 — and trade on the foreign-exchange market, the world's largest financial market, at floating exchange rates. Other regimes fix (peg) their rate to a reserve currency or basket, join a union (the euro, used by 20 EU states), or adopt another country's currency outright (dollarisation). Reserve currencies, above all the dollar, dominate trade invoicing and central-bank reserves. Digital forms — central bank digital currencies and private cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin — are recent additions to the monetary landscape.
