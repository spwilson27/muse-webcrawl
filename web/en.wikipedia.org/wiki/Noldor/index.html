Noldor

The Noldor (Quenya: "those with knowledge") are a fictional people in the legendarium of J. R. R. Tolkien. They are one of the three kindreds of Elves — with the Vanyar and the Teleri — who undertook the Great Journey from Cuiviénen to Valinor in the Years of the Trees, and they are the most prominent Elves in The Silmarillion.

Characteristics

The Noldor are renowned above all Elves for their skill in crafts, especially smithwork, gem-cutting, and the making of beautiful things. They invented writing (the Tengwar of Rúmil and later Fëanor), created the palantíri, and were the most learned of the Elven kindreds in lore and languages. Their kings included Finwë, the first High King, and his sons Fëanor, Fingolfin, and Finarfin.

History

In Valinor, Fëanor, the greatest of the Noldor, created the Silmarils, jewels containing the light of the Two Trees. When Morgoth destroyed the Trees and stole the Silmarils, Fëanor swore a terrible oath to recover them and led most of the Noldor into exile, in defiance of the Valar. The exile involved the First Kinslaying at Alqualondë, where the Noldor slew Teleri Elves to seize their ships. The exiles crossed to Middle-earth and fought the long Wars of Beleriand against Morgoth through the First Age — wars that ended in their near ruin and the sinking of Beleriand in the War of Wrath.

In the Second Age, survivors such as Galadriel and Gil-galad founded realms including Lindon and Eregion, whose smiths forged the Rings of Power with the help of the disguised Sauron. By the Third Age the Noldor were few in Middle-earth, lingering in Rivendell and Lothlórien, and most eventually departed over the Sea.

Language

The Noldor spoke the Quenya dialect known as Noldorin Quenya, which changed after the exile, partly through contact with Sindarin; in Middle-earth, Sindarin became the everyday tongue of the Exiles, with Quenya retained as a language of ceremony and lore.
