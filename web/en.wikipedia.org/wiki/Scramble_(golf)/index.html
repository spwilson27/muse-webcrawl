Scramble (golf)

A scramble is a format in the sport of golf in which a team of players all tee off, choose which shot is best, and from that spot all players play their next shots, repeating the process until the ball is holed. The format is extremely popular in charity, corporate and casual amateur events because it keeps play moving, lets weaker golfers contribute, and produces low team scores.

Rules of play

Each member of a team — usually two or four players — hits a tee shot. The team selects the best drive, marks its position, and every player then plays their second shot from within a prescribed distance of that spot (commonly one club-length, no nearer the hole, and often not improving the lie between rough and fairway). The process repeats for each subsequent shot, including putts, until the ball is holed, at which point the score for the hole is recorded as a single team score.

Local rules vary: events commonly require each team to use a minimum number of tee shots from every player (to prevent one strong player from dominating), and many use handicaps — often a percentage (e.g. 25% of the combined course handicaps of a four-person team) — to level the field.

Variations

  • Texas scramble — a variant, popular in the UK, which specifies a minimum number of drives per player (typically four) and applies prescribed handicap allowances.
  • Florida scramble (step aside) — the player whose shot is chosen steps aside and does not play the next shot.
  • Ambrose — the Australian name and handicap-based version of the format.
  • Shamble — teams select the best drive, after which each player plays their own ball to the hole, with the team's best score counting.
  • Bramble — a related format combining a best-drive selection with individual play thereafter.

Strategic considerations

Strategy in a scramble favours aggressive play: a team can attempt risky shots knowing that a safe shot by a teammate provides a fallback. Order of play matters — steadier players typically play first to secure a ball in play, freeing stronger players to attack — and putting is crucial, as the first putter reveals the line for the rest of the team. Because all players putt from the same spot, team scores well under par are routine, with winning four-person scramble gross scores frequently in the high 50s or low 60s.
