The period of a repeating event is the duration of time of one cycle in that event. It is the reciprocal of the frequency: the number of cycles that occur per unit time. The period is usually denoted by the symbol T and measured in seconds.

T = 1 / f

Oscillations and waves

For an oscillating system such as a pendulum or a mass on a spring, the period is the time required to return to the same position moving in the same direction. For a simple pendulum of length L swinging through small angles in a gravitational field g, the period is approximately

T ≈ 2π √(L/g)

For a travelling wave, the period is the time between successive crests passing a fixed point. Together with the wavelength λ and the wave speed v, it satisfies v = λ / T = λf.

Orbital period

In astronomy, the orbital period is the time a given astronomical object takes to complete one orbit around another object. For planets in the Solar System it is usually measured relative to the Sun; Earth's orbital period — the sidereal year — is about 365.256 days. Kepler's third law relates the orbital period of a planet to the semi-major axis of its orbit.

Units

The SI unit for period is the second. Depending on the phenomenon, periods may range from fractions of a nanosecond (for high-frequency electromagnetic waves) to billions of years (for stars orbiting the centre of a galaxy).
