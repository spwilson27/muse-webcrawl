Square (algebra)

In mathematics, a square is the result of multiplying a number by itself. The verb "to square" is used to denote this operation. Squaring is the same as raising to the power 2, and is denoted by a superscript 2; for instance, the square of 3 may be written as 32, which is the number 9.

In cases where the exponent is not a printable superscript, the notations x*2 or x^^2 are used, particularly in computer programming. The adjective which corresponds to squaring is quadratic.

Properties

The squaring function is an even function: (−x)2 = x2. For real numbers it is non-negative, taking the value zero only at zero itself. The square of an integer is called a square number or a perfect square: 0, 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, and so on.

Squaring interacts with addition through the well-known identities:

  • (a + b)2 = a2 + 2ab + b2
  • (a − b)2 = a2 − 2ab + b2
  • a2 − b2 = (a − b)(a + b), the difference of squares.

In geometry

The name comes from geometry: the area of a square with side length x is x2. More generally, the area of any shape scales as the square of its linear dimensions. The Pythagorean theorem relates the squares of the sides of a right triangle: a2 + b2 = c2.

Applications

Squares appear throughout algebra and analysis: in the quadratic formula, in completing the square, in the definition of variance and standard deviation in statistics, in the Euclidean distance formula, and in physics, where quantities such as kinetic energy depend on the square of velocity. In the complex numbers, the square of the imaginary unit is −1: i2 = −1.
