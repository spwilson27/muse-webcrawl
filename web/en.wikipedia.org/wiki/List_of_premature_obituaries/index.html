List of premature obituaries

A premature obituary is a published report of a person's death before the person has actually died. Causes include mistaken identity, hoaxes, confusion with a namesake, and news outlets accidentally publishing pre-written obituaries kept on file. The most famous remark on the phenomenon is Mark Twain's: "The report of my death was an exaggeration" (often quoted as "greatly exaggerated").

Notable premature obituaries

PersonYearCircumstances
Alfred Nobel1888A French newspaper, confusing Nobel with his brother Ludvig, called him a "merchant of death"; the obituary is said to have inspired the Nobel Prizes.
Mark Twain1897Reports of his death arose while his cousin was ill; Twain responded with the famous quip.
Bertrand Russell1948Reported dead in a plane crash in Norway; he survived, having been in the smoking section that stayed afloat — he quipped he owed his life to smoking.
Ernest Hemingway1954Reported killed in African plane crashes; he read his obituaries while recovering.
Pope John Paul II1982 and laterSeveral outlets prematurely reported his death more than once, including after the 1981 assassination attempt confusion.
Bob Hope1998, 2003A pre-written Associated Press obituary was accidentally posted online in 1998; US Representative Bob Stump announced his "death" in Congress in 2003.
Britney Spears2001A radio hoax claimed she had died in a car crash with Justin Timberlake.
Nelson Mandela2013 (earlier false reports)Several social-media hoaxes announced his death before his actual death in December 2013.
Muhammad Ali2016Some outlets published reports ahead of the official announcement during his final hospitalisation.
Kirk Douglas2014People magazine accidentally published his pre-written obituary, marked "DO NOT PUB", six years early.
Queen Elizabeth II / others in the "London Bridge" leaksvariousAccidental tweets and template publications have occasionally announced royal deaths early.
Alice Cooper1970sA music magazine published his obituary believing reports of his death by alcohol poisoning; Cooper joked he could "prove" it wrong on stage.
Marcus Garvey1940Read obituaries describing him as "broke, alone and unpopular" after a stroke; he suffered a second, fatal stroke shortly afterwards.
Paul McCartney1969Subject of the "Paul is dead" conspiracy theory rather than a true obituary.
Steve Jobs2008Bloomberg accidentally published his obituary template; in 2011 his real death was briefly announced early by some outlets.
Dick Cheney2013CNN's website accidentally displayed a template obituary page combining elements written for other figures.

Causes

News organisations keep advance obituaries for prominent people; accidents happen when templates are published by mistake, when a death hoax spreads on social media, or when two people share a name. Pre-written obituaries of living people have leaked from the Associated Press, Bloomberg, CNN and the New York Times, among others.
