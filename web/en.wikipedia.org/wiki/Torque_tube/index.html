Torque tube

A torque tube is a drivetrain arrangement in an automobile in which the propeller shaft runs inside a rigid hollow tube that connects the transmission at the front of the vehicle to the differential at the rear axle. The tube resists the torque reaction of the driven axle and locates the axle fore-and-aft, so the rear suspension does not need separate links to absorb drive and braking torque.

Function

In an open-shaft (Hotchkiss) drive, axle torque reaction is taken by the leaf springs, which can wind up under load. In a torque tube system the enclosed shaft usually has a single universal joint at the front, and the tube itself pivots about a ball joint at the transmission, forcing the axle to move in a fixed arc as the suspension travels. Because the tube takes the torque reaction, softer springs can be used for a better ride.

Applications

Torque tubes were used by many early manufacturers — notably Ford on the Model T and Model A, and Buick, which retained the layout into the 1960s. The concept survives in modern transaxle cars such as the Chevrolet Corvette (from the C5 generation onward) and the Porsche 944 and 928, where a rigid torque tube connects the front engine to a rear-mounted transaxle, improving weight distribution.
