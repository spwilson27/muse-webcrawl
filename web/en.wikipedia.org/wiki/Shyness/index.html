Shyness

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Shyness (also called diffidence) is the feeling of apprehension, lack of comfort, or awkwardness especially when a person is around other people. This commonly occurs in new situations or with unfamiliar people; a shy person may simply opt to avoid these situations. Although shyness can be a characteristic of people who have low self-esteem, the primary defining characteristic of shyness is a fear of what other people will think of a person's behavior. This fear of negative reactions — such as being laughed at, humiliated, patronized, or rejected — can cause the person to be withdrawn.

Shyness can be a personality trait or an aspect of certain psychological conditions. Stronger forms of shyness are sometimes referred to as social anxiety or social phobia when they become disabling. Shyness is distinct from introversion: introverts may enjoy solitude and low-stimulation environments by preference, whereas shy people may desire social interaction but fear it.

Origins

The origins of shyness are not well understood, but research suggests both genetic and environmental contributions. Some infants are born with a temperamental predisposition toward behavioral inhibition, reacting strongly to novelty. Parenting styles, early social experiences, and cultural norms also shape how shyness develops and how it is valued: in some cultures shyness is viewed positively, as a sign of modesty and reticence, while in others it is treated as a social handicap.

Characteristics

Shy individuals may experience physiological arousal in social situations — blushing, increased heart rate, sweating, and trembling — together with cognitive symptoms such as excessive self-consciousness, negative self-evaluation, and worry about others' judgments. Behaviorally, shyness can manifest as quietness, avoidance of eye contact, and difficulty initiating conversation. Shyness is not necessarily constant: many people are shy only in particular settings, such as public speaking or meeting strangers.

Treatment

For mild shyness, gradual exposure to social situations, social-skills training, and building self-confidence are commonly recommended. When shyness reaches the level of social anxiety disorder, treatments include cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT), which targets distorted beliefs about social judgment, and in some cases medication such as selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs).
