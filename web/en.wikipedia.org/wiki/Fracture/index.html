Fracture

Fracture is the separation of an object or material into two or more pieces under the action of stress. The fracture of a solid usually occurs due to the development of certain displacement discontinuity surfaces within the solid. In engineering, fracture is a major concern in the design of structures, and the field of fracture mechanics studies how cracks form and propagate.

Types

Ductile fracture occurs after extensive plastic deformation and is characterized by slow crack propagation and a rough, fibrous fracture surface with necking. Brittle fracture occurs with little or no prior deformation, rapid crack propagation, and relatively flat fracture surfaces, often along crystallographic cleavage planes.

Fracture strength and toughness

The fracture strength is the stress at which a specimen fails via fracture. Fracture toughness measures a material's resistance to crack propagation and is a central property in damage-tolerant design. Temperature, loading rate and environment strongly influence whether a metal fails in a ductile or brittle manner; many steels show a ductile-to-brittle transition temperature.

Fracture mechanics

Founded on the work of A. A. Griffith in the 1920s, fracture mechanics relates applied stress, crack size and material toughness through quantities such as the stress intensity factor K. It underpins safety assessment in aerospace, pressure vessels, pipelines and bridges.

Bone fracture

In medicine, a fracture commonly refers to a broken bone, classified as open or closed, complete or incomplete, and by pattern such as transverse, spiral or comminuted.
