Chen Jingrun

Chen Jingrun (Chinese: 陳景潤; 22 May 1933 – 19 March 1996) was a Chinese mathematician who made significant contributions to number theory, in particular to the study of the Goldbach conjecture.

Life

Chen was born in Fuzhou, Fujian province, the third son of a postal worker. He graduated from Xiamen University in 1953 and worked as a teacher before being noticed by the mathematician Hua Luogeng, who arranged for him to join the Institute of Mathematics of the Chinese Academy of Sciences in Beijing.

During the Cultural Revolution Chen was persecuted and his work was interrupted, but he continued his research in secret, working in a small room by lamplight. His health suffered permanently from these years of hardship.

Chen's theorem

In 1966 Chen announced, and in 1973 published the full proof of, the result now known as Chen's theorem: every sufficiently large even number can be written as the sum of a prime and a number that is either prime or the product of two primes (a semiprime). In notation, every large even integer is of the form p + P2. This remains the closest approach to the Goldbach conjecture for even numbers obtained by sieve methods, building on the earlier work of Viggo Brun and others.

Related concepts bearing his name include the Chen prime — a prime p such that p + 2 is either prime or semiprime — and Chen's work on the twin prime problem.

Legacy

Chen became a national hero in China after the writer Xu Chi published a popular biographical reportage, "Goldbach's Conjecture" (1978), portraying him as an emblem of scientific dedication. The asteroid 7681 Chenjingrun is named in his honour, and a statue of Chen stands at Xiamen University. In 1999 China Post issued a postage stamp commemorating his theorem.
