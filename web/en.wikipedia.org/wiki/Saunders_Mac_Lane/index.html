Saunders Mac Lane

Saunders Mac Lane (born Leslie Saunders MacLane, 4 August 1909 – 14 April 2005) was an American mathematician who, with Samuel Eilenberg, co-founded category theory. Born in Norwich, Connecticut, he studied at Yale and the University of Chicago before earning his doctorate in Göttingen in 1934 under Hermann Weyl and Paul Bernays, completing his thesis in mathematical logic shortly before the Nazi purges drove many mathematicians from Germany.

Career

After posts at Harvard, Mac Lane spent most of his career at the University of Chicago, where he chaired the mathematics department and helped build it into a leading research centre. During the Second World War he worked on applied mathematics for the war effort, later chronicled in his memoir A Mathematical Autobiography (2005). He served as president of the American Mathematical Society and the Mathematical Association of America, and received the National Medal of Science in 1989.

Work

With Samuel Eilenberg, Mac Lane introduced the concepts of category, functor, and natural transformation in a 1945 paper, "General Theory of Natural Equivalences", originally motivated by problems in algebraic topology and group cohomology. His book Categories for the Working Mathematician (1971) became the standard reference for the subject. Earlier, with Garrett Birkhoff, he wrote A Survey of Modern Algebra (1941), an influential textbook that reshaped undergraduate algebra teaching. Mac Lane also wrote extensively on the philosophy of mathematics, arguing in Mathematics: Form and Function (1986) that mathematical structures arise from human activities and their formal patterns.
