United World Colleges (UWC) is an international educational movement founded in 1962 by German educator Kurt Hahn. Its colleges bring together students aged roughly 16 to 19 from many countries and backgrounds, selected on merit and potential, to study for the International Baccalaureate Diploma in a deliberately diverse residential community.

History

The first college, Atlantic College in Wales, opened in 1962 in the aftermath of the Second World War and during the Cold War, with the aim of promoting international understanding through education. The movement grew to include schools on every inhabited continent, including UWC Adriatic in Italy, Li Po Chun UWC in Hong Kong, and UWC-USA in New Mexico.

Philosophy

UWC's mission states that it "makes education a force to unite people, nations and cultures for peace and a sustainable future." Selection is handled by national committees in more than 150 countries, and many students attend on scholarships.

Notable alumni

Alumni include King Willem-Alexander of the Netherlands and numerous figures in politics, science, and the arts.
