Declaration and forfeiture

A captain weighing a declaration: ending the innings voluntarily to leave time to bowl the opposition out.

The declaration and forfeiture are provisions of the Laws of Cricket that allow the batting side in a two-innings match to end its innings before all ten wickets have fallen, or to forgo an innings entirely. They exist because, in the longer forms of the game, a team must not only score more runs than its opponents but also dismiss them twice within the allotted time; a captain who bats too long risks the match being drawn.

The Laws

Under Law 15 of the Laws of Cricket (2017 Code), the captain of the batting side may declare the innings closed at any time when the ball is dead, regardless of the score. The same law permits a captain to forfeit either innings before its commencement — a provision introduced in the 2000 Code, partly in response to match situations in which teams had wanted to skip an innings to engineer a result.

Strategy

Declarations are a central tactical device in first-class cricket and Test matches. A captain typically balances the value of additional runs against the time needed to take ten wickets, factoring in the pitch, the weather forecast and the target that the opposition would have to chase. Bold declarations that leave a gettable target are celebrated — teams sometimes declare behind or set sporting targets to entice opponents into a run chase — while premature declarations that end in defeat draw heavy criticism. Declarations are rare in limited-overs cricket, where innings are capped by overs and the option is generally irrelevant.

History

Declarations were not permitted until 1889, when captains were first allowed to declare, initially only late in a match; the restriction was progressively relaxed during the twentieth century. The forfeiture provision gained attention in January 2000, when England and South Africa at Centurion agreed, after rain, that South Africa would forfeit its second innings and England its first, setting up a one-innings finish — a match later revealed to have been influenced by the bookmaker-related corruption of Hansie Cronje, which led to forfeiture in such circumstances being treated with caution.
