Local regression

Local regression or local polynomial regression is a non-parametric method of regression analysis that fits a smooth curve to a scatter plot by fitting many simple models locally rather than one global model. Its best-known form is LOESS (locally estimated scatterplot smoothing) and its variant LOWESS (locally weighted scatterplot smoothing), developed by William S. Cleveland and colleagues.

Method

For each target point x₀, the method selects a neighbourhood of nearby observations — the proportion of the data used is called the span or bandwidth — weights them by a kernel function (commonly the tricube weight) that decreases with distance, and fits a low-degree polynomial (usually linear or quadratic) by weighted least squares. The fitted value at x₀ becomes the smoothed estimate, and the process is repeated across the range of the data. Robust iterations reweight points by their residuals to reduce the influence of outliers.

Properties

Because no global functional form is assumed, local regression can reveal complex structure in data. The span controls smoothness: small spans follow the data closely but are noisy, while large spans approach a global polynomial fit. The method is computationally intensive for large datasets and does not produce a simple equation, but it is widely used for exploratory graphics, including in statistical software such as R, where loess is a standard function.
