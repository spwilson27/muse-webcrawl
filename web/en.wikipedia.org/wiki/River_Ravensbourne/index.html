The River Ravensbourne is a tributary of the River Thames in south-east London, England. It is 11 miles (17.4 km) long and flows from its source at Caesar's Well in Keston, near Bromley, through the London boroughs of Bromley, Lewisham, and Greenwich before joining the Thames at Deptford Creek. Unlike many of London's streams, much of the Ravensbourne still flows above ground, and its corridor forms a chain of parks and green spaces.

Course

Rising at Keston, the river flows north through Hayes and Bromley, where it passes Church House Gardens, then through Beckenham (Kelsey Park), under Catford Bridge at Catford, and through Ladywell Fields and Brookmill Park. At Lewisham it is joined by its principal tributary, the River Quaggy, which flows from Chislehurst via Chinbrook Meadows and Manor Park. Below the confluence the Ravensbourne runs through Deptford, becoming tidal as Deptford Creek before reaching the Thames at Greenwich Reach.

History

The river powered numerous mills from the medieval period onward, including corn mills and, in the 17th and 18th centuries, mills associated with the armaments and metal trades around Deptford and Lewisham. Local tradition connects the river's name with ravens and with a legend that Julius Caesar camped by its source—hence "Caesar's Well"—during the Roman invasion of Britain. In the 20th century, severe flooding, notably in 1968, led to extensive channel engineering, but recent decades have seen restoration projects that re-naturalise banks, remove concrete channels in parks such as Ladywell Fields and Chinbrook Meadows (on the Quaggy), and restore floodplain wetlands that slow floodwater.

Ecology and recreation

The river supports fish including chub, dace, and sticklebacks, and kingfishers, herons, and little egrets are seen along its banks. The Waterlink Way, a walking and cycling route, follows the Ravensbourne and Pool River from Sydenham to the Thames. Restoration of the river's open sections has made it a model for urban river recovery in London.
