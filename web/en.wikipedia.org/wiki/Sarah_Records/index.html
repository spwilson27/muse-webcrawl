Sarah Records

Sarah Records was an independent record label based in Bristol, England, active from 1987 to 1995. Founded by Clare Wadd and Matt Haynes, veterans of the fanzine scene, the label became one of the defining imprints of British indie pop and the "twee" aesthetic, releasing a numbered series of 7-inch singles alongside albums, fanzines and famously polemical press advertisements.

History

Wadd and Haynes began the label after producing fanzines such as Are You Scared To Get Happy? and Kvatch. Sarah releases were numbered (Sarah 1 to Sarah 100), and the catalogue included the Orchids, the Field Mice, Heavenly, the Wake, Blueboy, St Christopher, Another Sunny Day, the Sea Urchins (whose "Pristine Christine" was Sarah 001) and Talulah Gosh connections through Amelia Fletcher's Heavenly.

Ethos and design

The label championed a defiantly non-macho, anti-rockist pop sensibility, cheap pricing, and strikingly consistent graphic design. Its advertisements in the music press attacked sexism and the commercial music industry. The label ended, as long announced, with its hundredth release in 1995, marked by a farewell celebration in Bristol.

Legacy

Sarah's influence on indie pop, twee and later DIY labels has been widely recognised; the 2014 documentary My Secret World: The Story of Sarah Records and Clare Wadd's writings have chronicled its history. Haynes later co-founded the Shinkansen label.
