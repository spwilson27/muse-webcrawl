COP26

The 2021 United Nations Climate Change Conference, more commonly referred to as COP26, was the 26th UN Climate Change conference, held at the SEC Centre in Glasgow, Scotland, from 31 October to 13 November 2021. Postponed by a year because of the COVID-19 pandemic, it was presided over by Alok Sharma and attended by around 120 world leaders and nearly 40,000 registered participants.

Glasgow Climate Pact

The conference concluded with the Glasgow Climate Pact, under which countries reaffirmed the goal of limiting warming to 1.5 °C, agreed to revisit and strengthen their 2030 nationally determined contributions in 2022, and — in a first for a COP decision — explicitly called for the "phase-down of unabated coal power" and phase-out of inefficient fossil fuel subsidies, language weakened from "phase-out" at the insistence of India and China in the final hours.

Other outcomes

More than 100 countries pledged to halt and reverse deforestation by 2030; over 100 joined the Global Methane Pledge to cut methane emissions 30% by 2030; India announced a net-zero target of 2070, and rules for international carbon markets (Article 6) were finally completed. Finance for adaptation and the unresolved issue of "loss and damage" compensation remained contentious.

Reception

Reaction was mixed: the pact kept the 1.5 °C goal nominally alive, but analyses found the pledges still implied warming of around 2.4 °C, and activists such as Greta Thunberg dismissed the summit as "blah, blah, blah".
