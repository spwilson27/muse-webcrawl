Interregnum (England)

The Interregnum was the period between the execution of Charles I on 30 January 1649 and the arrival of Charles II in London on 29 May 1660, when England, Scotland and Ireland were governed as a republic.

Course

After the English Civil War, the Rump Parliament declared a Commonwealth. Oliver Cromwell conquered Ireland and Scotland, dissolved the Rump (1653), and ruled as Lord Protector under the Instrument of Government, with the Major-Generals experiment and the Western Design against Spain. After Oliver's death in 1658, his son Richard's Protectorate collapsed; General Monck's march on London enabled the Restoration of Charles II in 1660.

Legacy

The era saw religious toleration experiments, the readmission of Jews, naval expansion and the First Anglo-Dutch War, and left enduring constitutional caution against military rule.
