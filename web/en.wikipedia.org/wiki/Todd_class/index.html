Todd class

Todd class
Field: Algebraic geometry, algebraic topology
Named after: J. A. Todd

In mathematics, the Todd class is a certain construction now considered a part of the theory of characteristic classes in algebraic topology and algebraic geometry. Introduced by J. A. Todd in 1937, it is defined for a vector bundle (typically the tangent bundle of a complex manifold) in terms of its Chern classes through a power series involving Bernoulli numbers.

Definition

If a bundle has formal Chern roots xi, its Todd class is the product over i of xi/(1 − e−xi). Expanded in terms of Chern classes, it begins 1 + (1/2)c1 + (1/12)(c12 + c2) + …, the higher terms involving Bernoulli numbers.

Role

The Todd class is central to the Hirzebruch–Riemann–Roch theorem, which expresses the holomorphic Euler characteristic of a coherent sheaf on a smooth projective variety as an integral over the variety of its Chern character times the Todd class of the tangent bundle. Its genus, the Todd genus, equals 1 on complex projective spaces and is related to arithmetic genera.
