Ferrero Rocher (Italian pronunciation: [ferˈrɛːro roʃˈʃe]) is a chocolate and hazelnut confection produced by the Italian chocolatier Ferrero. Created by Michele Ferrero and introduced in Italy in 1979 and across Europe in 1982, the spherical pralines are individually wrapped in gold-coloured foil and are among the best-selling boxed chocolates in the world.

Composition

Each Ferrero Rocher consists of a whole roasted hazelnut at its centre, surrounded by a hazelnut cream similar to gianduja, enclosed in a thin wafer shell and coated in milk chocolate studded with chopped hazelnuts. The name derives from the Rocher de Massabielle, the grotto in Lourdes where the Virgin Mary is said to have appeared; rocher is French for "rock" or "boulder".

Production and sales

The chocolates are machine-made through a process long kept secret at Ferrero's Alba plant and factories worldwide. Ferrero Rocher is sold in more than 40 countries and is particularly associated with festive gifting seasons such as Christmas. Advertising campaigns, including the famous "Ambassador's reception" commercials, positioned the product as an affordable luxury.

Related products

The Ferrero range also includes Raffaello, a coconut–almond variant without chocolate, and Ferrero Rondnoir, a dark chocolate version. Seasonal and limited editions appear regularly.
