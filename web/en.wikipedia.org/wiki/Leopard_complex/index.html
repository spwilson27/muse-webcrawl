Leopard complex

Leopard complex
Other names: LP, leopard spotting
Gene: TRPM1, chromosome 1 (ECA1)
Inheritance: Incomplete dominant
Breeds: Appaloosa, Knabstrupper, Noriker, others

The leopard complex is a group of genetically related coat patterns in horses, characterised by white patterning that can range from minimal white markings to a full "leopard" pattern of dark spots over a white base. These patterns are most closely associated with the Appaloosa breed, but also occur in the Knabstrupper, Noriker and other breeds.

Genetics

Leopard complex patterns are produced by an incompletely dominant allele, designated LP, of the TRPM1 gene on horse chromosome 1. Horses homozygous for LP (LP/LP) typically show few or no leopard spots but display characteristic traits, and are affected by congenital stationary night blindness (CSNB), a non-progressive vision disorder linked to the same gene. Heterozygous horses (LP/lp) show more extensive spotting and do not have night blindness.

Patterns and characteristics

Expression of the pattern is highly variable and is modified by other genes, including the pattern-1 (PATN1) modifier that controls the amount of white. Recognised patterns include the blanket, leopard, snowcap, frost and varnish roan. Horses with the complex also commonly display mottled skin, striped hooves and visible white sclera around the eye.
