4AD is a British record label owned by the Beggars Group. Founded in London in 1980 by Ivo Watts-Russell and Peter Kent with funding from Beggars Banquet, it became one of the most influential independent labels of the 1980s and 1990s, known for an atmospheric roster and for the distinctive sleeve art of designer Vaughan Oliver and his studio 23 Envelope (later v23).

History

Originally named Axis, the label changed its name to 4AD — from "1980 Forward" — after its first releases. Early signings included Bauhaus, The Birthday Party and Modern English, whose "I Melt with You" became an unlikely American hit. Under Watts-Russell the label cultivated a dreamlike aesthetic through the Cocteau Twins, Dead Can Dance, This Mortal Coil (his own studio project) and the Colourbox/M/A/R/R/S collaboration, whose "Pump Up the Volume" (1987) was a UK number one.

Later years

A licensing deal with Warner Bros. in the US helped break the Pixies and The Breeders internationally. Watts-Russell sold his share in 1999. In the 2010s and 2020s the label enjoyed renewed success with artists such as The National, Grimes, Bon Iver-related projects and Big Thief.
