Flutter-tonguing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Flutter-tonguing is a wind instrument tonguing technique in which performers roll (or "flutter") their tongues to produce a characteristic rough, trembling or growling sound. It is performed either by rolling the tip of the tongue (as in a Spanish rolled "r") or, for players unable to do so, by a uvular roll at the back of the throat, similar to gargling.

Use in music

The technique is used on flute, recorder, trumpet, trombone, saxophone and other winds, and is notated with tremolo slashes through the note stem or the abbreviation "flt." (German scores often say Flatterzunge). Richard Strauss used it notably in Don Quixote and Ein Heldenleben, and Mahler and later composers employed it for dramatic or pastoral effects — Strauss's flutter-tongued brass imitate bleating sheep in Don Quixote. In jazz and popular music, growling on saxophone and brass often combines flutter-tonguing with humming or singing into the instrument.
