Edward White Benson

Edward White Benson (14 July 1829 – 11 October 1896) was an English churchman who served as Archbishop of Canterbury from 1883 until his death. Before that he was the founding headmaster of Wellington College and the first Bishop of Truro, where he was responsible for the building of Truro Cathedral.

Early life and education

Benson was born in Lombard Street, Birmingham, the son of a chemical manufacturer. He was educated at King Edward's School, Birmingham, and at Trinity College, Cambridge, where he graduated with first-class honours in 1852 and was elected a fellow. He was ordained deacon in 1853 and priest in 1857.

Career

After a period as a master at Rugby School, Benson became the first headmaster of Wellington College in 1859, transforming the new school into one of the great public schools of England. In 1877 he was appointed the first bishop of the newly created diocese of Truro in Cornwall. There he promoted the building of Truro Cathedral, whose foundation stones were laid in 1880.

On the death of Archibald Campbell Tait, Benson was chosen by Queen Victoria, on the advice of William Ewart Gladstone, to succeed him as Archbishop of Canterbury in 1883. As archbishop he was a central figure in the life of the Church of England and the wider Anglican Communion, presiding over the Lambeth Conference of 1888. He devised the service of Nine Lessons and Carols, first used at Truro on Christmas Eve 1880, which later became famous through its use at King's College, Cambridge.

Family and death

Benson married Mary Sidgwick in 1859. Their children included the novelists E. F. Benson, A. C. Benson and Robert Hugh Benson, and the Egyptologist Margaret Benson. He died suddenly on 11 October 1896 while attending church at Hawarden in Wales, and was buried in Canterbury Cathedral.
