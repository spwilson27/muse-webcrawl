Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem

The Cauchy–Kovalevskaya theorem is the main local existence and uniqueness theorem for analytic partial differential equations. It guarantees that an initial value problem for a system of PDEs with analytic coefficients and analytic initial data has a unique analytic solution in a neighbourhood of the initial surface, provided the surface is non-characteristic.

FieldPartial differential equations
Named afterAugustin-Louis Cauchy, Sofia Kovalevskaya
Key datesCauchy 1842; Kovalevskaya 1875

History

Cauchy proved special cases in 1842 by his method of majorants. Sofia Kovalevskaya gave the general theorem in her 1875 doctoral work, putting the result in the standard form taught today; her work also uncovered the famous counterexample showing the heat equation with analytic data need have no analytic solution, because its initial surface is characteristic.

Method and scope

The proof reduces the problem to a system in normal (Kovalevskaya) form and constructs the solution as a power series, bounding it by an explicitly solvable majorant problem. The theorem is purely local and confined to the analytic category: it does not address global existence or continuous dependence in the sense of Hadamard well-posedness for general smooth data.
