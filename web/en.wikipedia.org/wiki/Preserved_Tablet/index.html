Preserved Tablet

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Preserved Tablet (Arabic: اللوح المحفوظ, al-Lawḥ al-Maḥfūẓ) is a concept in Islam: the heavenly tablet upon which God has inscribed all that has happened and will happen, including the archetype of the Quran. The term appears in the Quran itself (85:22), which describes the scripture as being "in a Preserved Tablet".

In Islamic theology

Classical commentators understood the Tablet to record divine decree (qadar): every event, and the destinies of all creatures, written before creation. It is closely associated with the Pen (al-Qalam), which in tradition was the first thing created and commanded to write all that would occur until the Day of Judgement. Belief in the Tablet is connected to debates within Islamic theology over free will and predestination: schools such as the Ash'aris affirmed the Tablet's record while holding humans responsible for their acts, whereas the Mu'tazila emphasised human freedom.

Related concepts

The Mother of the Book (Umm al-Kitāb) is a related Quranic expression for the celestial source of revelation. Parallels have been drawn with the heavenly tablets or books of life in earlier Near Eastern and biblical traditions.
