Difficulty scaleInternational Scale of River Difficulty (Class I–VI)

Whitewater kayaking is the sport of paddling a kayak on a moving body of water, typically a whitewater river. The sport combines paddling technique with river-reading skills and ranges in difficulty from gentle Class I riffles to extreme Class V rapids and waterfalls. It is a distinct discipline from sea kayaking and canoe slalom, though its equipment and techniques overlap with whitewater canoeing.

Techniques and equipment

Whitewater kayaks are short, manoeuvrable boats, usually made of rotomoulded polyethylene, with designs divided into river runners, creek boats (for steep low-volume streams), and playboats (for freestyle wave and hole tricks). Paddlers wear a helmet, buoyancy aid (PFD), and spraydeck to seal the cockpit. Core skills include the Eskimo roll, eddy turns, ferry glides, boofing, and reading features such as holes, wave trains, and eddies.

Disciplines

Sub-disciplines include river running, creeking, freestyle (playboating), squirt boating, and extreme racing, with events such as the Green River Narrows Race. Freestyle and slalom-derived competitions are run under international bodies, and extreme kayaking races are held on steep rivers worldwide.

Safety

Whitewater kayaking carries risks including drowning, entrapment, and strainers (submerged obstacles). Safety practice emphasises proper training, paddling in groups, scouting rapids, throw-bag rescue skills, and wearing appropriate protective equipment.
