List of massacres of Indigenous Australians

This is a list of massacres of Indigenous Australians during the period of British colonisation, roughly 1788 to the 1930s. A massacre is generally defined here, following the University of Newcastle's Colonial Frontier Massacres in Australia, 1788–1930 project, as the deliberate killing of six or more undefended people in a single operation. The project, led by historian Lyndall Ryan, has verified more than 400 massacre sites across the continent, and estimates that over 10,000 Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander people were killed in frontier massacres, alongside around 160–170 colonists killed in retaliatory or resistance attacks. Many events were never officially recorded, and estimates of deaths at individual sites vary widely between colonial records and Aboriginal oral histories.

Queensland

Queensland's frontier was among the most violent, with the paramilitary Native Police conducting "dispersals" across the colony from 1849 into the early 1900s. Documented massacres include those at Battle Mountain (1884, Kalkatungu resistance under Kajabbi ended), Skull Hole (Bladensburg, 1877), and numerous sites along the Palmer, Dawson and Herbert river districts. The reprisals after the Cullin-la-ringo killings (1861) and after the wreck of the Maria (1872) each killed many Aboriginal people.

Tasmania

The Black War of the 1820s–early 1830s devastated Tasmania's Aboriginal population. Events include the Cape Grim massacre (1828, ~30 people killed by shepherds) and Risdon Cove (1804, an early clash whose death toll of 3–50 is disputed). Martial law was declared in 1828, and the remaining Aboriginal people were exiled to Flinders Island in the 1830s.

Western Australia

Events include the Pinjarra massacre (1834), the Flying Foam massacre (1868, Dampier Archipelago), the Forrest River massacre (1926, investigated by the Wood royal commission), the Laverton incident (1910), and many Kimberley massacres during the pastoral expansion of the 1880s–1920s, including killings around Bedford Downs (1924).

Northern Territory and South Australia

The Coniston massacre (1928) was the last large documented frontier massacre, in which Constable George Murray's parties killed at least 31 Warlpiri, Anmatyerre and Kaytetye people after the death of dingo trapper Frederick Brooks. In South Australia, the Rufus River massacre (1841) killed at least 30 Maraura people, and the Avenue Range station massacre (1848) and Waterloo Bay (Elliston) killings (1849) are documented in the south-east and Eyre Peninsula.

Historiography

The scale of frontier killing was long minimised in Australian historical writing; Keith Windschuttle's The Fabrication of Aboriginal History (2002) provoked the "history wars" debate, while historians including Henry Reynolds and Lyndall Ryan have used archival and oral evidence to document the violence. The University of Newcastle massacre map (first released 2017, expanded in stages to 2022) is the most comprehensive public record.
