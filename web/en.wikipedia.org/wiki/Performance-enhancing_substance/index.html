Performance-enhancing substance

Performance-enhancing substances, also known as performance-enhancing drugs (PEDs), are substances used to improve any form of activity performance in humans. In sport, their use — commonly called doping — is prohibited by governing bodies including the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA), which maintains a list of banned substances and methods.

Types

  • Anabolic steroids — synthetic derivatives of testosterone that promote muscle growth and strength.
  • Stimulants — such as amphetamines and ephedrine, which increase alertness and reduce fatigue.
  • Erythropoietin (EPO) — stimulates red blood cell production, increasing oxygen delivery to muscles.
  • Human growth hormone — used to promote tissue growth and recovery.
  • Blood doping — transfusion or manipulation of blood components to boost aerobic capacity.
  • Beta blockers — steady the hands and lower heart rate in precision sports such as shooting and archery.

History and regulation

The use of performance aids dates to ancient athletic competition, but systematic anti-doping rules began in the 20th century. The International Olympic Committee established a prohibited list in the 1960s, and WADA was founded in 1999. High-profile cases, from the 1988 Olympic 100 metres final to the BALCO scandal and revelations about systematic state-sponsored doping, have shaped testing regimes that include in- and out-of-competition testing and athlete biological passports.

Health risks

PED use is associated with cardiovascular disease, liver and kidney damage, hormonal disruption, infertility and psychiatric effects, and athletes have died in connection with doping practices.
