John R. Brinkley

John Romulus Brinkley
Born: July 8, 1885, Beta, North Carolina, U.S.
Died: May 26, 1942 (aged 56), San Antonio, Texas, U.S.
Occupation: Physician (credentials disputed), broadcaster
Known for: Goat-gland transplants; radio station KFKB

John Romulus Brinkley (July 8, 1885 – May 26, 1942) was an American physician who became famous — and wealthy — as a medical charlatan. He claimed that transplanting goat testicles into men could restore virility and cure numerous ailments, and he promoted his procedures through one of the most powerful radio stations in the United States.

Goat-gland practice

Brinkley obtained a medical diploma from an unaccredited school and settled in Milford, Kansas. From around 1918 he performed operations implanting goat glands into patients, charging large fees. Despite the absence of scientific support and reports of infections and deaths, his clinic thrived on the back of relentless advertising.

Radio career

In 1923 Brinkley founded radio station KFKB in Milford, one of the most powerful stations in the country, broadcasting medical advice (including his "Medical Question Box", in which he diagnosed listeners' ailments by letter and prescribed his own medicines), country music, and populist entertainment. KFKB helped popularise country and roots music across the Midwest and made Brinkley a national figure. His broadcasting also made him a pioneer — and a cautionary tale — of radio advertising and border radio: after US authorities revoked KFKB's licence in 1930, he moved across the Mexican border and built the powerful "border blaster" station XER (later XERA) in Villa Acuña, broadcasting back into the United States.

Political campaigns and downfall

Brinkley ran for Governor of Kansas three times in the early 1930s as an independent, coming close to winning in 1930 through an extensive write-in campaign. His medical credentials were attacked by the American Medical Association and journalist Morris Fishbein, and a libel suit Brinkley brought against Fishbein ended in a verdict that effectively branded him a quack. Tax claims and malpractice suits mounted; he died in 1942 with his fortune largely dissipated.

Legacy

Brinkley is remembered as a landmark figure in the history of medical quackery, broadcasting regulation, and media-driven populism.
