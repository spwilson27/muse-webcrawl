William Madison Wood


Born 1858, Edgartown, Massachusetts, U.S.
Died February 2, 1926 (aged 67–68), Daytona Beach, Florida
Occupation President of the American Woolen Company

William Madison Wood (1858 – February 2, 1926) was an American textile industrialist who built the American Woolen Company into the largest woolen manufacturer in the United States.

Early life

Wood was born on Martha's Vineyard, Massachusetts, the son of a whaling family of Portuguese descent. His father's death left the family poor, and Wood went to work in a cotton mill in New Bedford as a boy. He rose through office work at a bank and then in the woolen trade.

American Woolen Company

In 1899 Wood organised the American Woolen Company as a consolidation of mills, headquartered in Lawrence, Massachusetts, and served as its president. The company became central to the great Lawrence textile strike of 1912 (the "Bread and Roses" strike), when a wage cut imposed after a reduction in working hours provoked a walkout of some 20,000 workers; Wood initially resisted concessions but the strike ended with raises for workers across the industry.

Later life and death

Wood resigned from the presidency in 1924 amid declining business. He built the estate Arden in Andover, Massachusetts. Suffering from ill health, he died by suicide in Florida in 1926.
