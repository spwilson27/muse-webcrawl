Edvard Beneš (28 May 1884 – 3 September 1948) was a Czech politician and statesman who served as President of Czechoslovakia from 1935 to 1938 and again from 1940 (in exile) to 1948. A close associate of Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk, he helped found independent Czechoslovakia in 1918 and served as its foreign minister from 1918 to 1935.

Presidency and exile

Beneš resigned after the Munich Agreement of 1938 and led the Czechoslovak government-in-exile in London during the Second World War. Returning to Prague in 1945, he issued the Beneš decrees, which provided for the transfer of the German and Hungarian populations and the confiscation of their property.

Final years

He resigned in June 1948 following the Communist coup of February that year and died a few months later at Sezimovo Ústí.
