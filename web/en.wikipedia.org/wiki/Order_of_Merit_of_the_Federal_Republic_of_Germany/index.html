The Order of Merit of the Federal Republic of Germany (German: Verdienstorden der Bundesrepublik Deutschland), commonly called the Bundesverdienstkreuz (Federal Cross of Merit), is the only federal decoration of Germany. It was instituted on 7 September 1951 by Federal President Theodor Heuss and is awarded for achievements that serve the Federal Republic of Germany — in political, economic, social, and intellectual fields, and for services in the charitable sphere.

Classes

The order has several classes, ascending from the Medal of Merit and Cross of Merit on Ribbon, through Cross of Merit 1st Class, Commander's Cross, Grand Cross of Merit, Grand Cross of Merit with Star, Grand Cross of Merit with Star and Sash, up to the Grand Cross, and a Special Class of the Grand Cross reserved for heads of state. The insignia is a red-enamelled cross pattée with a gold disc bearing the federal eagle.

Awarding

The Federal President confers the order, generally on the proposal of state governments, federal ministers, or other bodies; most awards are presented by state or local officials. More than 250,000 awards have been made since its foundation. Recipients must be at least 40 years old for the initial award of most classes, and the proportion of women among recipients is monitored to reflect their share of service.
