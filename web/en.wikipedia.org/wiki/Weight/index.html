In science and engineering, the weight of an object is a quantity associated with the gravitational force acting on the object. Some standard textbooks define weight as a vector quantity, the gravitational force acting on the object. Others define weight as a scalar quantity, the magnitude of the gravity force. Still others define it as the magnitude of the reaction force exerted on a body by mechanisms that counteract the effects of gravity: the weight is the quantity that is measured by, for example, a spring scale.

The unit of measurement for weight is that of force, which in the International System of Units (SI) is the newton. For example, an object with a mass of one kilogram has a weight of about 9.8 newtons on the surface of the Earth, and about one-sixth as much on the Moon.

History

Discussion of the concepts of heaviness (weight) and lightness (levity) date back to the ancient Greek philosophers. These were typically viewed as inherent properties of objects. Plato described weight as the natural tendency of objects to seek their kin. To Aristotle, weight and levity represented the tendency to restore the natural order of the basic elements: air, earth, fire and water.

Definitions

Gravity definition

The most common definition of weight found in introductory physics textbooks defines weight as the force exerted on a body by gravity. This is often expressed in the formula W = mg, where W is the weight, m the mass of the object, and g the gravitational acceleration.

Operational definition

In the operational definition, the weight of an object at rest on the surface of the Earth is equal to the magnitude of the force exerted by the object on its support (or the force measured by a scale). Under this definition, an object in free fall has zero weight, even though the gravitational force on it is unchanged.

Mass versus weight

Mass is an intrinsic property of matter, measured in kilograms, whereas weight depends on the local gravitational field. The same object has the same mass everywhere but a different weight on different planets. In everyday commerce, however, the terms are often used interchangeably, as in "net weight" of packaged goods.

Units

The SI unit of weight (force) is the newton. In the imperial system the pound-force is used; the pound is legally defined as a unit of mass (0.45359237 kg), and the corresponding force unit is the poundal or pound-force depending on the system.
