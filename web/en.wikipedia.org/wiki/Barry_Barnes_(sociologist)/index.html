Barry Barnes (sociologist)


Barry Barnes

Barry Barnes (born 1943) is a British sociologist of science, long associated with the University of Edinburgh, where he was a central figure in the "Edinburgh school" of the sociology of scientific knowledge (SSK). With David Bloor he formulated the strong programme, which proposed that true and false beliefs alike should be explained by the same kinds of social causes.

Work

Barnes's Scientific Knowledge and Sociological Theory (1974) was among the first systematic attempts to give a sociological account of the content of natural science, drawing on the later philosophy of Wittgenstein and Kuhn's account of paradigms. In Interests and the Growth of Knowledge (1977) he argued that the development of scientific theories could be understood in terms of the interests of the social groups carrying them.

Later work turned to the concepts of power and social theory more generally. In The Nature of Power (1988) he treated power as a matter of the distribution of knowledge within a society, and in The Elements of Social Theory (1995) he presented an account of social life as sustained by mutually susceptible, self-referential agents. With John Henry he wrote Scientific Knowledge: A Sociological Analysis (1996). Barnes spent the later part of his career at the University of Exeter, where he was Professor of Sociology and associated with the EGENIS research centre on the social study of genomics.
