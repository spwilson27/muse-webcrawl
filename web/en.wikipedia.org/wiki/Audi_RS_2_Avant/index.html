The Audi RS 2 Avant is a high-performance mid-size sports estate car (station wagon / shooting brake) manufactured from March 1994 to July 1995. Collaboratively designed as a joint venture between Audi AG and Porsche and based on Audi's 80 Avant — the platform for the Audi S2 — it was Audi's first "RS" vehicle, and the first of their high-performance Avants (Audi's estate car / station wagon terminology). A sedan version was planned but never produced beyond a prototype.

Overview

Although most of the car's underpinnings were manufactured by Audi, assembly was handled by Porsche at their Rossle-Bau plant in Zuffenhausen, Germany, which had become available after discontinuation of the Mercedes-Benz 500E that Porsche had manufactured there under contract. The Rossle-Bau plant was also used for the famous Porsche 959.

Porsche substantially modified the 2.2-litre five-cylinder turbo engine with a larger KKK turbocharger, bigger intercooler, higher-flow injectors, a new camshaft and a low-pressure exhaust, raising output to 315 PS (232 kW). Coupled with a six-speed manual gearbox and quattro all-wheel drive, the RS 2 accelerates from 0–100 km/h in about 5.4 seconds and reaches about 262 km/h (163 mph). Porsche-sourced brakes, wheels and mirrors underline the co-development.

Legacy

Only 2,891 examples were built, and none were officially sold in the United States when new. The RS 2 founded the line of Audi RS performance models and the fast-wagon formula later carried by the RS 4 and RS 6 Avant; original examples are now collectible classics.
