Pyran

Pyran
FormulaC5H6O
TypeHeterocyclic organic compound
Isomers2H-pyran and 4H-pyran

In chemistry, a pyran is a six-membered heterocyclic, non-aromatic ring consisting of five carbon atoms and one oxygen atom, containing two double bonds. The molecular formula is C5H6O. There are two isomers: 2H-pyran, with a saturated carbon at position 2, and 4H-pyran, with the saturated carbon at position 4.

Occurrence and significance

Although the parent pyrans are of limited importance themselves, the pyran ring is a structural feature of many natural products. Pyranose sugars — including glucose — take their name from their resemblance to pyran, and benzopyran structures occur in flavonoids and other compounds.

Related compounds

The pyrylium cation, in which the oxygen bears a positive charge, is aromatic and is a useful synthetic intermediate. Tetrahydropyran (THP) is a common protecting group for alcohols in organic synthesis.
