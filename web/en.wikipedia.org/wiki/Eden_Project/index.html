Eden Project

LocationNear St Austell, Cornwall, England
Opened17 March 2001
FounderTim Smit
ArchitectNicholas Grimshaw

The Eden Project is a visitor attraction and educational charity in Cornwall, England, consisting of huge artificial biomes housing thousands of plant species from many climates of the world. The project is located in a reclaimed china clay pit a few miles from St Austell and opened to the public in March 2001.

Biomes

The two largest enclosures are made of adjoining geodesic domes covered with hexagonal panels of ETFE foil. The Rainforest Biome, the largest indoor rainforest in the world, maintains a tropical climate with plants including banana, coffee, rubber and bamboo, along with a canopy walkway. The Mediterranean Biome reproduces the warm temperate climates of the Mediterranean basin, South Africa, California and Western Australia, with olives, vines and citrus.

The outdoor gardens cover the surrounding pit with plantings representing temperate regions, along with sculptures and exhibits on sustainability. The Core, an education centre opened in 2005, houses exhibitions and a large seed-like sculpture carved from a single block of granite.

History

The project was conceived by Tim Smit, who had previously restored the Lost Gardens of Heligan. Funding came in part from the Millennium Commission. The site was a working clay pit, and construction required extensive landforming and drainage. Since opening, Eden has attracted tens of millions of visitors and hosts concerts known as the Eden Sessions.
