Argos, Peloponnese

Argos
Country: Greece
Region: Peloponnese
Regional unit: Argolis
Population: approx. 22,000 (town)

Argos is a city in Argolis, in the Peloponnese region of Greece, and one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, with a history of settlement stretching back some 7,000 years. It lies on the western edge of the Argolic plain, beneath the Larissa hill, crowned by a medieval castle.

Ancient history

In Greek mythology Argos was linked with the watchman Argus Panoptes and the kings of the Argolid. The city was a major Mycenaean centre and later a powerful Dorian city-state, rival to Sparta for dominance of the Peloponnese — a rivalry decided in Sparta's favour at the Battle of Sepeia (494 BC). Argos remained largely neutral or allied with Athens in the Peloponnesian War and was a significant democracy in the classical period.

Archaeology

Excavations have revealed the ancient agora, a large theatre cut into the hillside seating some 20,000 spectators, Roman baths, and sanctuaries including the nearby Heraion, the great sanctuary of Hera shared by the Argive cities. Successive layers from the Neolithic through Byzantine periods illustrate the city's long continuity.

Modern Argos

Today Argos is a market town serving the citrus-growing Argolic plain and forms part of the municipality of Argos-Mykines. Its archaeological museum displays finds from the region, including a famous Geometric-period krater.
