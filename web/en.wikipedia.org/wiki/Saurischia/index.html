Saurischia

Saurischia (from Greek sauros, "lizard", and ischion, "hip joint") is one of the two traditional orders of dinosaurs, distinguished by a lizard-like pelvic structure in which the pubis bone points forward. The order was named by Harry Govier Seeley in 1888, who divided dinosaurs into Saurischia and Ornithischia on the basis of hip anatomy.

Saurischia
Temporal range: Late Triassic – Present (birds)
Subgroups: Theropoda, Sauropodomorpha
Named by: Harry Seeley, 1888

Subgroups

Saurischia comprises two great lineages. The Theropoda — mostly bipedal, largely carnivorous dinosaurs such as Tyrannosaurus, Allosaurus, and Velociraptor — include the ancestors of birds, so birds themselves are living saurischians. The Sauropodomorpha include the "prosauropods" and the giant long-necked quadrupedal herbivores of the Sauropoda, such as Diplodocus, Brachiosaurus, and the titanosaurs, the largest land animals known.

Classification debate

Although Seeley's division was standard for over a century, a 2017 phylogenetic study by Matthew Baron and colleagues proposed rearranging dinosaur relationships — grouping theropods with ornithischians in a clade called Ornithoscelida and placing sauropodomorphs with the early herrerasaurids in a redefined Saurischia. The proposal remains debated among palaeontologists. Ironically, the "bird-hipped" ornithischians are not the ancestors of birds; birds evolved from "lizard-hipped" theropods and secondarily evolved a backward-pointing pubis similar to that of ornithischians.
