Mercaz HaRav (Hebrew: מרכז הרב, "The Rav's Center") is a national-religious yeshiva in Jerusalem, founded in 1924 by Rabbi Abraham Isaac Kook, the first Ashkenazi Chief Rabbi of British Mandatory Palestine. It is considered the flagship yeshiva of the Religious Zionist movement and the most influential of its institutions, having educated many of the movement's rabbis and leaders.

History and ideology

Rav Kook envisioned a central yeshiva ("Merkaz HaRav" — the central yeshiva of the Rav) that would combine traditional Talmud study with his universalist religious-Zionist philosophy, seeing the return to the Land of Israel as part of a redemptive process. After his death in 1935, his son Rabbi Zvi Yehuda Kook led the yeshiva for nearly five decades, shaping generations of students. Under Zvi Yehuda, Mercaz HaRav became the spiritual engine of the settlement movement after the 1967 Six-Day War; its students and graduates founded Gush Emunim and many West Bank settlements.

Study and influence

The yeshiva, located in the Kiryat Moshe neighborhood, enrolls several hundred students. Its alumni include chief rabbis, heads of yeshivas, members of the Knesset, and senior officers. In March 2008, a Palestinian gunman attacked the yeshiva's library, murdering eight students — an event widely known as the Mercaz HaRav massacre.
