Augustus III (Polish: August III Sas; 17 October 1696 – 5 October 1763) was King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania from 1734 until 1763, as well as Elector of Saxony in the Holy Roman Empire as Frederick Augustus II from 1733 until 1763. He was the only legitimate son of Augustus II the Strong and succeeded his father in both Saxony and, after a contested election and war, in Poland.

Accession and the War of the Polish Succession

On his father's death in 1733, Augustus was elected king by a minority of the Polish nobility, supported by Russia and Austria, while the majority elected Stanisław Leszczyński, backed by France. The resulting War of the Polish Succession (1733–1735) ended with Augustus recognised as king; Leszczyński was compensated with the Duchy of Lorraine.

Reign

Augustus showed little interest in the affairs of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, preferring to reside in Dresden and to devote himself to art, opera, and hunting. Government in Poland was largely left to his minister Heinrich von Brühl. The Commonwealth's political paralysis deepened; most sejms of his reign were broken by the liberum veto, and Poland's neighbours, particularly Russia, increasingly treated it as a protectorate. His reign did see cultural flourishing at the Saxon court in Warsaw and Dresden, and the completion of works such as the Zwinger palace complex.

Seven Years' War

During the Seven Years' War, Prussian forces under Frederick the Great invaded and occupied Saxony in 1756, forcing Augustus to flee to Warsaw, where he remained until the Peace of Hubertusburg (1763) restored his electorate. He died later that year in Dresden.

Family

In 1719 Augustus married Archduchess Maria Josepha of Austria, daughter of Emperor Joseph I. They had fourteen children, including Frederick Christian, who briefly succeeded him in Saxony, and Maria Josepha, who married the Dauphin Louis of France and was the mother of Louis XVI.
