Mozilla is a free software community founded in 1998 by members of Netscape, and the name of the not-for-profit Mozilla Foundation and its taxable subsidiary, the Mozilla Corporation, which together develop the Firefox web browser and champion an open, accessible internet. The name was originally the codename of the Netscape Navigator browser — a portmanteau of "Mosaic" and "Godzilla". Mozilla's products and advocacy are presented at mozilla.org.

History

In January 1998 Netscape, losing the browser wars to Microsoft's Internet Explorer, released its browser source code as the open-source Mozilla project. After a long rewrite, the Mozilla Suite appeared in 2002; the standalone browser became Firefox (2004), which revived browser competition and reached several hundred million users at its peak before Google's Chrome (2008) overtook it. In 2003 AOL, which had bought Netscape, endowed the new Mozilla Foundation with $2 million to continue the project. The Foundation's revenue has come largely from search-engine placement deals — chiefly with Google, whose payments have also drawn antitrust attention because of their effect on the browser market.

Software

Mozilla's best-known products are the Firefox browser (built on the Gecko engine), the Thunderbird email client, Firefox for Android and iOS, the Mozilla VPN, and the Pocket read-it-later service, which was shut down in 2025. Mozilla also maintains the Rust programming language's early history (Rust began as a Mozilla project) and the MDN Web Docs developer documentation site.

Organisation

The Mozilla Foundation, a California non-profit, owns the Mozilla Corporation, which employs the staff who develop Firefox. Mozilla publishes the Mozilla Manifesto, ten principles stating that the internet is a global public resource that must remain open and accessible.
