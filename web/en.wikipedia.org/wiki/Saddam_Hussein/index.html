Overview

Saddam Hussein (1937–2006) was an Iraqi politician and dictator who served as president of Iraq from 1979 until his overthrow in the US-led invasion of 2003. Rising through the Ba'ath Party — after the 1959 assassination attempt on Prime Minister Qasim, exile, and the coups of 1963 and 1968 — he became the regime's strongman under President al-Bakr before taking the presidency himself.

Rule and wars

Saddam centralised a police state around his Tikriti clan, nationalised oil wealth into development and patronage, and launched the Iran–Iraq War (1980–88), in which chemical weapons were used against Iranian troops and Kurdish civilians (Halabja, 1988; the Anfal campaign killed tens of thousands of Kurds). His invasion of Kuwait in 1990 triggered the Gulf War (1991), after which sanctions and weapons inspections shadowed Iraq through the 1990s. The 2003 invasion, justified publicly by claims of weapons of mass destruction that proved unfounded, toppled him; he was captured in December 2003, tried by an Iraqi tribunal for the Dujail massacre, and hanged on 30 December 2006.
