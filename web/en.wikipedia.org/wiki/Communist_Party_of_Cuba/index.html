The Communist Party of Cuba (Spanish: Partido Comunista de Cuba, PCC) is the sole legal political party and the ruling party of the Republic of Cuba. Founded on 3 October 1965 through the merger of the 26th of July Movement, the Popular Socialist Party, and the Revolutionary Directorate of 13 March, it is organised on Marxist–Leninist principles. Article 5 of the Cuban constitution describes the party as the "superior leading political force of society and the State."

History

An earlier Communist Party of Cuba had been founded in 1925 and later became the Popular Socialist Party; it initially opposed Fidel Castro's guerrilla movement but joined the revolutionary coalition after 1958. Following the triumph of the Cuban Revolution in 1959, the revolutionary organisations were progressively unified, first as the Integrated Revolutionary Organisations, then the United Party of the Socialist Revolution, and finally as the PCC in 1965. The party's First Congress was not held until 1975, when its central institutions were formalised. Fidel Castro served as First Secretary from 1965 until 2011, when he was succeeded by his brother Raúl Castro. At the Eighth Congress in April 2021, Raúl Castro stepped down and Miguel Díaz-Canel, also President of Cuba, became First Secretary — the first person outside the Castro family to lead the party.

Organisation

The party's highest body is its Congress, held roughly every five years, which elects a Central Committee; the Political Bureau (Buró Político) and Secretariat direct work between congresses. The party claims several hundred thousand members drawn from workplaces and communities, with candidacy requiring workplace nomination and vetting. Its daily newspaper is Granma, named after the yacht that carried the revolutionary expeditionaries to Cuba in 1956. Affiliated mass organisations include the Young Communist League (UJC), the Federation of Cuban Women, and the Central Union of Cuban Workers.

Role in the state

While formally distinct from the government, the PCC directs state policy through its leading cadres; the National Assembly of People's Power is elected from candidate lists in which party members predominate. The 2019 constitution retained the party's leading role and the irreversibility of socialism, while recognising limited private property and market mechanisms in the economy.
