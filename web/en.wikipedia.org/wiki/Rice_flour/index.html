Rice flour

TypeFlour
Main ingredientMilled rice (white or brown)
EnergyApprox. 366 kcal per 100 g
GlutenNone

Rice flour (also rice powder) is a form of flour made from finely milled rice. It is distinct from rice starch, which is usually produced by steeping rice in lye. Rice flour is a common substitute for wheat flour and is naturally gluten-free, making it suitable for people with coeliac disease or gluten sensitivity, though it behaves very differently in baking because it lacks gluten's elasticity.

Types

Rice flour may be made from white rice or brown rice, and from long-grain or short-grain varieties. Glutinous rice flour — despite the name, also gluten-free — is milled from sticky (glutinous) rice and produces a chewy, elastic texture; it is the basis of Japanese mochi (as mochiko) and many Southeast Asian sweets. In Thailand, a distinction is made between ordinary rice flour (paeng khao chao) and glutinous rice flour (paeng khao niao). Stone-milled and industrially milled flours vary in particle size, which affects water absorption.

Culinary uses

Rice flour is a staple across Asian cuisines: it is used for rice noodles and rice paper in Vietnam and China, idli and dosa batters in India, tteok rice cakes in Korea, and bibingka and puto in the Philippines. It is also used as a thickening agent for sauces and soups, in batters for frying (where it gives a crisp crust), and in gluten-free breads and cakes, usually blended with other flours and starches plus binding agents such as xanthan gum.

Other uses

Rice flour has been used historically in cosmetics as a face powder, and finely milled rice powder appears in some traditional skin-care preparations.
