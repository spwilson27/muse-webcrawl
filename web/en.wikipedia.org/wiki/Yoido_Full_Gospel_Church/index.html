Yoido Full Gospel Church

Location: Yeouido, Seoul, South Korea
Founded: 1958
Founders: David Yonggi Cho, Choi Ja-shil
Denomination: Assemblies of God (Pentecostal)
Membership: ~800,000 (claimed)

Yoido Full Gospel Church is a Pentecostal Christian megachurch affiliated with the Assemblies of God, located on Yeouido Island in Seoul, South Korea. Founded in 1958 by David Yonggi Cho and his mother-in-law Choi Ja-shil, it grew from a tent church of five members into what has been described as the world's largest single congregation, with a claimed membership of around 800,000 and Sunday services held in multiple languages throughout the day.

History

Cho, a convert from Buddhism who credited his recovery from tuberculosis to divine healing, began holding services in a tent in a poor district of Seoul after the Korean War. The church's emphasis on the "threefold blessing" — spiritual salvation, prosperity, and health, drawn from 3 John 2 — resonated strongly in a rapidly urbanising, impoverished nation. In 1973 the congregation moved to its present site on Yeouido, then a largely empty island in the Han River, and the main sanctuary was expanded repeatedly as membership soared.

Cell groups and influence

The church pioneered the home cell-group system, dividing the enormous congregation into small neighbourhood groups led largely by women — a structure credited with sustaining growth and later widely imitated by megachurches worldwide. Yoido's Church Growth International, founded by Cho in 1976, promoted its model globally. The church also operates a prayer mountain retreat centre, a university, newspapers, and broadcasting ministries.

Controversies

In 2014 Cho was convicted of embezzlement and breach of trust involving church funds, receiving a suspended prison sentence, an episode that damaged the church's reputation. Cho retired as senior pastor in 2008, succeeded by Young-hoon Lee, but remained pastor emeritus until his death in 2021.
