Fumarate

Fumarate is the dianion (conjugate base) of fumaric acid, the trans isomer of butenedioic acid, with the formula C₄H₂O₄²⁻. Salts and esters of fumaric acid are likewise known as fumarates.

Role in metabolism

Fumarate is a key intermediate in the citric acid cycle (Krebs cycle), where it is formed from succinate by the enzyme succinate dehydrogenase and is then hydrated to malate by fumarase. It is also produced in the urea cycle and in purine nucleotide synthesis, linking nitrogen metabolism to energy metabolism. Mutations in the fumarase gene cause fumarase deficiency and predispose to hereditary leiomyomatosis and renal cell cancer; in such tumours, accumulated fumarate acts as an oncometabolite by inhibiting dioxygenase enzymes and stabilising HIF.

Uses

Fumaric acid and its salts are used as food acidulants (E297 and related numbers), and fumarate esters such as dimethyl fumarate are used pharmaceutically, notably in the treatment of psoriasis and relapsing multiple sclerosis. Ferrous fumarate is an iron supplement.
