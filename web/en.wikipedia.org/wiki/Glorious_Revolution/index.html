Glorious Revolution

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Glorious Revolution was the deposition of James II and VII and the accession of his daughter Mary II and her husband William III of Orange as joint monarchs of England, Scotland and Ireland in 1688–89. Facing a Catholic king who suspended laws, maintained a standing army and produced a Catholic heir, seven English notables (the “Immortal Seven”) invited William to intervene; he landed at Torbay in November 1688 with a Dutch army, James's support collapsed, and James fled to France.

A Convention Parliament declared that James had abdicated and settled the crown on William and Mary, enacting the Bill of Rights (1689) — asserting free elections, frequent parliaments, freedom of speech in Parliament, and the illegality of suspending laws, a standing army in peacetime and excessive bail — and the Toleration Act for Protestant dissenters. In Scotland the Claim of Right made parallel assertions; Jacobite resistance in Scotland and war in Ireland (the Williamite War, including the Siege of Derry and the Battle of the Boyne, 1690) were bloodier than the largely unresisted English transfer, and Jacobite risings continued until 1746.

Significance

The Revolution established parliamentary supremacy as the working constitution of Britain: no later monarch attempted to govern against Parliament. Whig historiography celebrated it as the founding of English liberty — hence “Glorious” and “bloodless” (in England); modern historians qualify both adjectives, stressing foreign invasion, popular anti-Catholicism and the Irish and Scottish wars. Its principles influenced the American revolutionaries and the United States Bill of Rights.
