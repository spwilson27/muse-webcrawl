René Thom (1923–2002)

René Frédéric Thom (2 September 1923 – 25 October 2002) was a French mathematician who received the Fields Medal in 1958 for his work in topology, particularly for creating the field of cobordism theory. He later became famous for catastrophe theory, a theory which he developed in the late 1960s as a mathematical model for sudden qualitative changes in natural and social processes.

Work

Thom's early research concerned characteristic classes and cobordism: his 1954 thesis characterized the cobordism groups using what are now called Thom spaces, and he proved that cobordism classes could be computed from them. He also contributed to singularity theory and transversality, the Thom transversality theorem being a fundamental tool in differential topology.

Catastrophe theory

In Stabilité structurelle et morphogenèse (1972; in English, Structural Stability and Morphogenesis), Thom proposed that sudden discontinuous changes ("catastrophes") in systems governed by smooth underlying dynamics could be classified: for systems depending on no more than four control parameters, there are exactly seven elementary catastrophes (fold, cusp, swallowtail, butterfly, and the elliptic, hyperbolic and parabolic umbilics). Christopher Zeeman and others popularized applications in biology, linguistics and the social sciences, though some proposed applications were criticized as lacking empirical rigor — a controversy Thom himself addressed by emphasizing the theory's qualitative, topological character.

Life

Thom was born in Montbéliard and educated at the École Normale Supérieure. He taught at Grenoble and Strasbourg before joining the Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques (IHÉS) at Bures-sur-Yvette in 1964, where he remained for the rest of his career. He died in Bures-sur-Yvette in 2002.
