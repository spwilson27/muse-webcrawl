Last Judgement

The Last Judgement (also spelled Last Judgment) is the concept, found in the Abrahamic religions and in the Frashokereti of Zoroastrianism, of a final divine assessment of humanity at the end of the world, in which the deeds of all people — living and dead — are judged, and the righteous and the wicked receive their respective rewards and punishments. In Christianity the doctrine is associated with the Second Coming of Jesus Christ; in Islam it is known as Yawm al-Qiyāmah (the Day of Resurrection) or Yawm ad-Dīn (the Day of Judgement).

A medieval representation of the Last Judgement.

In Christianity

The Christian doctrine of the Last Judgement holds that at the end of time, following the resurrection of the dead, Christ will return in glory to judge all nations. The Gospel of Matthew describes the Son of Man separating people "as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats" (Matthew 25:31–46), with eternal life granted to the righteous and eternal punishment to the wicked. The Book of Revelation likewise depicts a great white throne and the dead being judged "according to what they had done" (Revelation 20:11–15). The Apostles' Creed affirms that Christ "will come again to judge the living and the dead."

The subject was one of the most frequently depicted scenes in medieval and Renaissance art, commonly placed on the tympanum of church portals — as at Autun and Conques — and most famously in Michelangelo's fresco, The Last Judgment, in the Sistine Chapel (1541), as well as in Giotto's earlier fresco in the Scrovegni Chapel in Padua.

In Islam

In Islam, the Day of Judgement (Yawm al-Qiyāmah) is one of the central tenets of faith. Muslims believe that when the world ends, all human beings will be resurrected and assembled before God (Allah), who will weigh their deeds on a scale (mīzān). Those whose good deeds outweigh their bad will enter Paradise (Jannah), while the wicked will be consigned to Hell (Jahannam). The Qur'an describes the day vividly, and the Prophet Muhammad is regarded as an intercessor on behalf of his community.

In Judaism

While Judaism places its main emphasis on judgement during the annual festivals of Rosh Hashanah and Yom Kippur, Jewish tradition also contains an eschatological expectation of a final judgement associated with the coming of the Messiah, the resurrection of the dead, and the world to come (Olam Ha-Ba), drawing on passages in the Book of Daniel (Daniel 12:2).

In Zoroastrianism

The Zoroastrian doctrine of Frashokereti describes a final renovation of the world in which the dead are resurrected, a river of molten metal purifies all humanity, and evil (Angra Mainyu) is finally defeated. Some scholars regard this as an early influence on later Abrahamic eschatology.
