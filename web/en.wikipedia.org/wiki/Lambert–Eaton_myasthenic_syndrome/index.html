Lambert–Eaton myasthenic syndrome (LEMS, also called Lambert–Eaton syndrome) is a rare autoimmune disorder of the neuromuscular junction characterised by muscle weakness of the limbs. It is caused by antibodies directed against presynaptic voltage-gated calcium channels, which impair the release of acetylcholine from motor nerve terminals.

Signs and symptoms

Patients typically present with slowly progressive weakness of the proximal muscles, especially of the legs, which characteristically improves briefly with exercise (post-exercise facilitation) — the opposite of the fatigable weakness of myasthenia gravis. Deep tendon reflexes are reduced or absent but may normalise after exercise. Autonomic symptoms are common and often prominent: dry mouth, constipation, erectile dysfunction, dry eyes and impaired sweating. Bulbar and respiratory involvement is usually milder than in myasthenia gravis.

Causes

In roughly 60 percent of cases, LEMS is a paraneoplastic syndrome associated with small-cell lung carcinoma; the tumour expresses calcium channels that trigger the autoimmune response. In the remaining cases no tumour is found and the condition is considered a primary autoimmune disorder, often associated with HLA-B8 and other autoimmune diseases. The condition was described by Edward Lambert and Lee Eaton in the 1950s.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis rests on the clinical picture, electromyography (which shows a characteristic incremental response of compound muscle action potentials to rapid repetitive stimulation), and detection of antibodies against P/Q-type voltage-gated calcium channels. Because of the strong cancer association, patients undergo screening for malignancy, typically with CT or PET imaging.

Treatment

Treatment of an underlying tumour is the first priority and often improves the neurological symptoms. Symptomatic therapy uses 3,4-diaminopyridine (amifampridine), which blocks presynaptic potassium channels and prolongs calcium channel opening. Immunosuppressive agents, plasma exchange and intravenous immunoglobulin are used in more severe or refractory cases.
