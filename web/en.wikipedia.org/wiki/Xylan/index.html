Xylan

TypeHemicellulose polysaccharide
MonomerD-xylose
OccurrencePlant cell walls, especially hardwoods and grasses

Xylan is a group of hemicelluloses found in plant cell walls, second in abundance only to cellulose among plant polysaccharides. It consists mainly of a backbone of β-(1→4)-linked D-xylose units, substituted with side chains of arabinose, glucuronic acid, and acetyl groups depending on the species.

Occurrence and structure

Xylans make up roughly 20–35% of hardwood dry mass and a large share of cereal straws and grasses. Hardwood xylans are typically O-acetyl-4-O-methylglucuronoxylans, while grass xylans are arabinoxylans bearing arabinofuranose side chains. In the cell wall, xylan coats and tethers cellulose microfibrils, contributing to wall strength.

Uses

Xylan is hydrolysed by xylanase enzymes, used industrially in pulp bleaching, animal feed, and biofuel production from lignocellulosic biomass. Its sugar, xylose, can be converted to the sweetener xylitol.
