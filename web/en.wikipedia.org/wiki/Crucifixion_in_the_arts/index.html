Crucifixion in the arts

The Crucifixion of Jesus is one of the most frequently depicted subjects in Western art, appearing in painting, sculpture, manuscript illumination, stained glass and other media from late antiquity to the present day. Depictions typically show Jesus nailed to the cross, often flanked by the Virgin Mary and Saint John the Evangelist, with a titulus above the cross reading INRI.

Development

Explicit depictions of the Crucifixion were rare before the 5th century; early Christian art preferred symbolic representations such as the cross or the Chi Rho. Byzantine art presented a triumphant, serene Christ (Christus triumphans), while medieval Western art increasingly emphasised suffering (Christus patiens), notably in the work of Giotto and his successors. Northern Renaissance artists such as Matthias Grünewald, whose Isenheim Altarpiece presents an agonisingly contorted Christ, carried this pathos to an extreme.

Later treatments

Renaissance and Baroque painters including Masaccio, Michelangelo, Tintoretto, Rubens and Rembrandt produced celebrated Crucifixions. The subject continued into modern art, in works by artists such as Salvador Dalí, whose Christ of Saint John of the Cross (1951) shows the crucified figure from above, and in Francis Bacon's fragmented triptychs.
