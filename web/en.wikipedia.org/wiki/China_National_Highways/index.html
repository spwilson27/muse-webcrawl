China National Highways

The China National Highways (Chinese: 国道; pinyin: Guódào) are a nationwide network of trunk roads across mainland China, designated with the prefix G followed by a three-digit number. Together with the separate National Expressway network, they form the principal long-distance road system of the country, distinct from provincial (S), county (X) and other local roads.

Numbering system

National highways are organized in series by number:

  • G1xx — routes radiating from Beijing (e.g., G101 to Shenyang, G110 to Yinchuan).
  • G2xx — north–south routes (e.g., G205, G207).
  • G3xx — east–west routes, including the network's longest road, G318 from Shanghai to the Nepalese border (5,476 km).
  • G5xx–G6xx — regional and inter-provincial routes added by the 2013 expansion plan.

Notable routes include G312 (Shanghai to the Kazakhstan border), G314 (Urumqi to the Khunjerab Pass and Pakistan), and G219, which runs for over 10,000 km along China's western and southern frontiers following the 2013 renumbering.

History

The modern numbering scheme dates from a 1981 State Council plan that designated a trunk network of national highways, consolidated in the 1990s by the "five verticals and seven horizontals" trunk-road programme. A 2013 national plan greatly expanded the designated network, extending national highways to all counties and adding many new routes. Most national highways are ordinary (non-expressway) roads of varying grade, historically tolled in sections; long-distance traffic increasingly uses the parallel expressway system, while national highways remain essential for regional access and non-motorized and agricultural traffic.
