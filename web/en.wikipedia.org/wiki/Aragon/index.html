Aragon (Spanish and Aragonese: Aragón; Catalan: Aragó) is an autonomous community in northeastern Spain, coextensive with the medieval Kingdom of Aragon. It comprises the provinces of Huesca, Zaragoza and Teruel, and is bordered by France to the north across the Pyrenees. Its capital and largest city is Zaragoza, home to about half the region's population.

Geography

Aragon stretches from the high Pyrenees — including the range's highest peak, Aneto (3,404 m) — through the broad valley of the River Ebro to the Iberian System mountains in the south. The climate ranges from alpine in the north to semi-arid steppe in the central depression (Los Monegros). Ordesa y Monte Perdido National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, protects some of the finest Pyrenean scenery. Much of the interior is very sparsely populated; the province of Teruel's depopulation inspired the protest movement and political party "Teruel Existe" ("Teruel Exists").

History

The Kingdom of Aragon emerged in the 11th century from a Frankish frontier county and expanded during the Reconquista, taking Zaragoza in 1118. In 1137 the marriage of Petronilla of Aragon to Count Ramon Berenguer IV of Barcelona created the Crown of Aragon, a Mediterranean power that came to rule Catalonia, Valencia, the Balearic Islands, Sicily, Sardinia and Naples. The dynastic union of Ferdinand II of Aragon with Isabella I of Castile (1469) laid the foundation of modern Spain, though Aragon retained its laws (fueros) until the Nueva Planta decrees of the early 18th century. During the Spanish Civil War the region was divided and saw the battle of Teruel and short-lived anarchist collectives and the Regional Defence Council of Aragon.

Culture

Aragon's heritage includes Mudéjar architecture (a UNESCO listing), the paintings of Goya, born at Fuendetodos, and the jota, the region's traditional music and dance. The Aragonese language survives in northern valleys, and Catalan is spoken in the eastern strip (La Franja). Zaragoza's Fiestas del Pilar, centred on the Basilica of Our Lady of the Pillar, are among Spain's major festivals.
