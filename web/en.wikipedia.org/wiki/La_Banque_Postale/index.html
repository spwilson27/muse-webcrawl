La Banque Postale

La Banque Postale is a French bank, a subsidiary of La Poste, France's national postal service. It was established on 1 January 2006, when it took over the financial services previously offered directly by La Poste. Headquartered in Paris, it serves some 20 million customers and is notable for offering banking services through France's network of roughly 17,000 post offices and contact points, the largest physical banking network in the country.

La Banque Postale services are offered through the La Poste network

History

Postal financial services in France date to the 19th century: postal savings accounts (the Livret A, distributed through post offices from 1881) and postal checking accounts (comptes chèques postaux, created in 1918). For much of the 20th century these services were run directly by the postal administration and were restricted in scope — notably, La Poste could not grant most consumer or mortgage loans. The 2005 law reforming postal activities authorized the creation of a full-service bank, and La Banque Postale began operations in 2006, progressively expanding into mortgages, consumer credit, insurance (through ventures and its subsidiary CNP Assurances, in which it took a majority stake in 2020), asset management, and corporate banking.

Activities and mission

Under French law, La Banque Postale carries public-service missions of banking accessibility: it must open a Livret A account for anyone who asks, and the Livret A distributed through its network finances social housing. The bank has positioned itself as a "citizen bank" emphasizing sustainable finance; it was among the first major banks to commit to exiting oil and gas financing by 2030. With total assets in the hundreds of billions of euros, it ranks among France's largest retail banks.
