Compulsive buying disorder

Compulsive buying disorder (CBD), also called oniomania (from Greek ōnios "for sale" and mania), is a pattern of chronic, repetitive purchasing that becomes difficult to stop and results in harmful consequences, such as debt, damaged relationships, and emotional distress. It is characterised by preoccupation with shopping, irresistible urges to buy, and buying items that are not needed or used.

History and classification

The condition was described clinically by Emil Kraepelin and Eugen Bleuler in the early twentieth century. Its classification remains debated: it has been regarded as an impulse-control disorder, an obsessive–compulsive spectrum condition, or a behavioural addiction. It is not listed as a distinct diagnosis in the DSM-5.

Characteristics

Sufferers often experience euphoria or relief while buying, followed by guilt or remorse. Many items purchased are never used, given away, or returned. Compulsive buying frequently co-occurs with mood disorders, anxiety, eating disorders, hoarding, and substance use disorders. Estimates suggest it affects a few percent of the population, with women over-represented in clinical samples.

Treatment

Cognitive behavioural therapy, including group therapy focused on the disorder, has shown benefit. Antidepressants, particularly SSRIs, have been studied with mixed results. Financial counselling and self-help groups such as Debtors Anonymous may also help.
