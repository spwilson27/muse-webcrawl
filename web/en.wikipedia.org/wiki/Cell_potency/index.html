Cell potency

The hierarchy of cell potency during development.

FieldDevelopmental biology, stem cell biology
LevelsTotipotent, pluripotent, multipotent, oligopotent, unipotent

Cell potency is a cell's ability to differentiate into other cell types. The more cell types a cell can differentiate into, the greater its potency. Potency is also described as the gene activation potential within a cell, and a continuum of potency begins with the fully potent zygote and narrows as cells become committed to specialised functions during development.

Totipotency

A totipotent cell can give rise to all cell types of the body plus the extraembryonic tissues such as the placenta. In mammals, only the zygote and the blastomeres of the very early embryo are totipotent. Totipotent cells can, in principle, generate a complete viable organism.

Pluripotency

Pluripotent cells can form all cell types of the three germ layers — ectoderm, mesoderm, and endoderm — but not the extraembryonic tissues. Embryonic stem cells derived from the inner cell mass of the blastocyst are the classic example. Induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), produced by reprogramming adult cells with factors such as Oct4, Sox2, Klf4, and c-Myc, share these properties and were first reported by Shinya Yamanaka's group in 2006.

Multipotency, oligopotency and unipotency

Multipotent cells differentiate into a limited family of related cell types: haematopoietic stem cells produce all blood cell lineages, and mesenchymal stem cells produce bone, cartilage, and fat. Oligopotent cells can form a few cell types, such as lymphoid progenitors, and unipotent cells produce only one cell type while retaining self-renewal, as with muscle satellite cells. Terminally differentiated cells are sometimes said to have no potency, although experimental reprogramming shows this state can be reversed.

Assays

Potency is tested by transplanting cells and observing their contributions, by teratoma formation for pluripotent cells, and increasingly by molecular signatures of gene expression and chromatin state.
