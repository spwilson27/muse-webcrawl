Mumbai Suburban Railway

The Mumbai Suburban Railway (colloquially called "local trains" or simply "locals") is the public transport railway system of Mumbai, Maharashtra, India, and one of the busiest commuter rail systems in the world. It is operated jointly by the Western Railway and Central Railway zones of Indian Railways, over a network of more than 400 kilometres serving well over 100 stations, and carries on the order of 7–8 million passengers on a typical weekday.

Lines

  • Western Line — from Churchgate north through Dadar and Borivali to Dahanu Road.
  • Central Line — from Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj Terminus (CSMT) to Kalyan, with branches to Kasara and Khopoli.
  • Harbour Line — from CSMT to Panvel and Goregaon/Andheri, serving the eastern docklands and Navi Mumbai.
  • Trans-Harbour Line — linking Thane with Vashi and Panvel in Navi Mumbai.
  • Port Line and the newer Uran corridor extend services within Navi Mumbai.

History

The first suburban services ran on the line between Bombay and Thane, opened on 16 April 1853 — the first railway in India. The suburban network electrified beginning in 1925 (initially at 1.5 kV DC, later converted to 25 kV AC, a conversion completed in the 2010s). Dedicated suburban corridors with electric multiple-unit (EMU) rakes grew with the city through the 20th century.

Operations and issues

Services run roughly from 4 a.m. to 1 a.m., with trains every few minutes at peak times on main corridors; rakes usually have 12 or 15 coaches, including ladies' and first-class compartments. The system is notorious for extreme crowding at peak hours — with more than a dozen passengers per square metre in the busiest trains ("super dense crush load") — and for fatalities among passengers falling from open doorways or crossing tracks, long-standing issues addressed through infrastructure upgrades, additional lines, air-conditioned EMUs (introduced in 2017), and the Mumbai Urban Transport Projects funded with the World Bank.
