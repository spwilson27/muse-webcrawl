The June solstice is the solstice on Earth that occurs annually between 20 and 22 June, most often on 20 or 21 June. At this moment the Sun reaches its most northerly declination of the year, +23.44°, appearing directly overhead at noon along the Tropic of Cancer. In the Northern Hemisphere the June solstice is the summer solstice, marking the longest day and shortest night of the year; in the Southern Hemisphere it is the winter solstice, with the shortest day and longest night. Astronomy

A solstice occurs because Earth's axis is tilted about 23.44° relative to the plane of its orbit. At the June solstice the North Pole is inclined most directly toward the Sun. The Sun's declination reaches its maximum northern value and momentarily appears to stand still (Latin solstitium, "the Sun stands still") before reversing toward the south. Within the Arctic Circle the Sun remains above the horizon for a full 24 hours — the "midnight sun" — while within the Antarctic Circle it does not rise at all.

Although the June solstice brings the greatest amount of daylight in the Northern Hemisphere, it is not typically the day of earliest sunrise or latest sunset, because of the equation of time and the geometry of Earth's elliptical orbit. Seasonal lag also means the warmest weather usually follows the solstice by several weeks.

Timing

The June solstice usually falls on 20 or 21 June. In 2024 and 2025 it occurred on 20 June; it will fall on 21 June in 2026. Because the tropical year is not exactly 365.25 days, the solstice instant drifts by roughly six hours each year and is reset by leap years; over centuries it migrated earlier in the calendar under the Julian calendar, one of the discrepancies addressed by the Gregorian reform.

Cultural significance

The summer solstice has been celebrated since prehistory. Monuments such as Stonehenge are aligned to the solstice sunrise, and solstice gatherings continue there today. Midsummer festivals around 24 June remain major celebrations in Scandinavia, the Baltic states, and elsewhere in northern Europe. In various Indigenous, Neopagan, and other traditions the solstice marks a peak of the ritual year; the United Nations recognises 21 June as the International Day of the Celebration of the Solstice, and the same date is observed as International Yoga Day.
