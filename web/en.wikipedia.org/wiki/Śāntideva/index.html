Śāntideva

Flourished8th century CE
RegionIndia (Nalanda)
SchoolMahayana, Madhyamaka
Main workBodhicaryāvatāra

Śāntideva (Sanskrit: शान्तिदेव; c. 685 – c. 763) was an 8th-century Indian Buddhist monk and scholar of the Mahayana tradition, associated with the monastic university of Nalanda. He is celebrated as the author of the Bodhicaryāvatāra ("A Guide to the Bodhisattva's Way of Life"), one of the most beloved works of Buddhist literature.

Life

Traditional biographies, such as those of Butön and Tāranātha, relate that Śāntideva was born a prince but renounced the throne after a vision of Mañjuśrī. At Nalanda he was outwardly idle — nicknamed "Bhusuku", one who only eats, sleeps, and defecates — but secretly practised meditation. Challenged by fellow monks to recite a text before the assembly, he is said to have delivered the Bodhicaryāvatāra and, during the chapter on wisdom, to have levitated into the sky, his voice continuing after his body vanished.

Works

The Bodhicaryāvatāra, in ten chapters of Sanskrit verse, guides the reader through the cultivation of bodhicitta, the perfections (pāramitās) of generosity, ethics, patience, effort, and meditation, culminating in a famed chapter on wisdom and a closing dedication of merit. His other surviving work, the Śikṣāsamuccaya ("Compendium of Training"), assembles scriptural passages on Mahayana practice. His verses on exchanging self and other remain a touchstone of Tibetan Buddhist lojong teaching, and the 14th Dalai Lama frequently cites him as a source of inspiration.
