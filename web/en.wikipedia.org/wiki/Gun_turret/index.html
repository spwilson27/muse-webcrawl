Gun turret

A gun turret is a mounting platform from which weapons can be fired that protects the crew or mechanism of a ranged weapon and at the same time lets the weapon be aimed and fired in some degree of azimuth and elevation. Gun turrets are found on warships, armoured vehicles such as tanks, and military aircraft, as well as on fortifications.

Naval turrets

The revolving armoured turret was developed in the mid-19th century — Ericsson's USS Monitor (1862) is the famous early example — and became the standard mounting for heavy naval guns. By the dreadnought era, turrets carried two, three, or four guns of up to 18 inches, rotated by hydraulic or electric machinery, with shells and charges supplied from magazines below via hoists; the armoured barbette protected the turret's working chamber.

Land and air turrets

Tanks carry their main armament in a rotating turret housing the gun, crew and sights. Aircraft of the Second World War used powered manned turrets with machine guns for defence, a role later largely taken over by missiles; remote weapon stations are the modern descendant of the turret concept.

Design elements

A turret typically includes the weapon, recoil system, sights and fire control, crew positions, armour protection, and a turret ring or race on which it traverses.
