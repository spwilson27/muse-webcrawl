Jerome David Kern (January 27, 1885 – November 11, 1945) was an American composer of musical theatre and popular music. One of the most important American theatre composers of the early 20th century, he wrote more than 700 songs used in over 100 stage works, including such classics as "Ol' Man River", "Smoke Gets in Your Eyes", "The Way You Look Tonight" and "All the Things You Are".

Career

Kern began his career interpolating songs into Broadway shows and writing for revues. His watershed success came with the Princess Theatre musicals of 1915–1918, written with Guy Bolton and P. G. Wodehouse, which replaced European operetta with intimate, naturalistic American musical comedy.

His masterpiece, Show Boat (1927), written with lyricist Oscar Hammerstein II from Edna Ferber's novel, revolutionised the musical by integrating song, character and serious subject matter — including race — into a unified drama. Later successes included Sweet Adeline (1929), The Cat and the Fiddle (1931), Music in the Air (1932) and Roberta (1933).

Film work and later years

In Hollywood, Kern wrote for films including Swing Time (1936), whose "The Way You Look Tonight" won the Academy Award for Best Original Song, and Cover Girl (1944), with "Long Ago (and Far Away)". He won a second Oscar for "The Last Time I Saw Paris" (1941). Kern died in New York in 1945 after collapsing on Park Avenue; a biographical film, Till the Clouds Roll By, appeared in 1946.

Legacy

Kern's melodic sophistication and harmonic invention laid the groundwork for the Great American Songbook and influenced George Gershwin, Richard Rodgers and Cole Porter. He was among the inaugural inductees into the Songwriters Hall of Fame.
