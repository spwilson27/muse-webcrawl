Edward Balliol (c. 1283 – 1364) was a claimant to the throne of Scotland during the Second War of Scottish Independence. The eldest son of King John Balliol — who had been deposed by Edward I of England in 1296 — he invaded Scotland with English backing in 1332 at the head of the "Disinherited", Anglo-Scottish lords who had lost their lands under Robert the Bruce.

Career

Balliol's force landed in Fife and crushed the Scots at the Battle of Dupplin Moor (August 1332); he was crowned at Scone that September. Within months he was ambushed at Annan and fled to England in disorder, but returned with Edward III's full support, defeating the Scots at Halidon Hill (July 1333) and recovering Edinburgh. In return he ceded the Lothians and Berwickshire to England and did homage for his kingdom. Scottish resistance under the guardians of the young David II never accepted him; he controlled little beyond the south, and his authority steadily ebbed as David II's party regained ground with French assistance. After David's capture at Neville's Cross (1346) Balliol made a final demonstration in Scotland, but in 1356 he formally surrendered his claim to Edward III in exchange for a pension. He died childless at Wheatley, near Doncaster, in 1364.
