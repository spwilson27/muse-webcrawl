Wedding reception

A wedding reception is a party usually held after the completion of a marriage ceremony as hospitality for those who have attended the wedding, hence the name reception: the couple "receive" society, in the form of family and friends, for the first time as a married couple. Hosts provide their guests with food and drink and a chance to socialize, and receptions are held in venues ranging from banquet halls and hotels to homes, gardens, and community halls.

Common elements

In Western traditions, a reception commonly includes a receiving line or grand entrance, a formal meal or buffet, toasts — traditionally by the best man, maid of honor, and parents — and the ceremonial cutting of a tiered wedding cake. Dancing is central in many cultures, often beginning with the couple's first dance, followed by parent dances. Other widespread customs include the bouquet toss and garter toss, speeches, and a farewell send-off of the couple.

Around the world

Chinese wedding banquets may feature many courses served to large numbers of guests, often in a restaurant, with the couple toasting each table. Indian wedding celebrations frequently include multi-day events with music and dance performances by relatives. In Japan, a formal reception (hiroen) emphasizes speeches, costume changes by the bride, and carefully staged presentations. Across cultures, the reception functions both as celebration and as public recognition of the marriage, and its cost and scale vary enormously; in the United States the average reception accounts for the largest share of total wedding spending.
