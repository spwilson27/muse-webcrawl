London Beer Flood

The London Beer Flood was an accident at Meux & Co's Horse Shoe Brewery in London on 17 October 1814. A huge wooden vat of fermenting porter burst, releasing a wave of beer that destroyed other vats and flooded the surrounding slum housing of the St Giles rookery. Eight people were killed.

The accident

The brewery's largest vat stood about 6.7 metres (22 ft) tall and held over 3,500 barrels of porter, held together by enormous iron hoops. At around 4:30 p.m. one hoop snapped; about an hour later the vat burst. The force of some 570 tonnes of beer demolished the brewery's rear wall and ruptured other vats, sending a wave of beer 4.6 metres (15 ft) high into New Street and George Street.

Victims

The wave destroyed two houses and the wall of the Tavistock Arms pub. Eight people, mostly women and children living in cellar dwellings and in a room where a wake was being held, were drowned or killed by debris and falling masonry. Among them were Eleanor Cooper, a 14-year-old servant at the pub, crushed by the falling wall.

Aftermath

An inquest returned verdicts of accidental death, and the brewery successfully argued the flood was an act of God, avoiding compensation claims; it was even granted a rebate of excise duty on the lost beer. The incident nonetheless encouraged the brewing industry's transition from wooden vats to lined concrete vessels. The Horse Shoe Brewery was demolished in 1922; the Dominion Theatre now occupies part of the site.
