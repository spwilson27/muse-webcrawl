The Copa Libertadores de América, known for sponsorship reasons as the CONMEBOL Libertadores, is an annual international club football competition organised by CONMEBOL since 1960. It is the highest level of club competition in South American football, comparable to Europe's UEFA Champions League, and one of the most watched club tournaments in the world. The name honours the Libertadores, the leaders of the Spanish American wars of independence.

The Copa Libertadores trophy, first contested in 1960.

History

The first edition, in 1960, was won by Peñarol of Uruguay. Argentine clubs dominated the early decades, with Independiente winning a record seven titles, including four consecutively (1972–1975) — their nickname Rey de Copas ("King of Cups") derives from this era. Boca Juniors won six titles, and Brazilian clubs became increasingly dominant from the 1990s onwards. Mexican clubs participated as invitees from 1998 to 2016.

Since 2017 the tournament has run across most of the calendar year, and from 2019 the final has been played as a single match at a pre-selected neutral venue — a change made after crowd trouble forced the 2018 second leg between Boca Juniors and River Plate to be moved to Madrid. The 2024 final in Buenos Aires was won by Botafogo, their first title.

Format

The competition begins with three qualifying phases, followed by a group stage of 32 teams in eight groups, then two-legged knockout rounds (round of 16, quarter-finals, semi-finals) and a single-match final. The champion qualifies for the FIFA Club World Cup (and its expanded successor), the Recopa Sudamericana against the Copa Sudamericana champion, and the following season's group stage.

Records

Independiente lead the honours list with seven titles, followed by Boca Juniors (six) and Peñarol (five). Ecuadorian striker Alberto Spencer remains the all-time top scorer with 54 goals. The tournament's winners' base receives a replica-plaque addition on the trophy for each champion.
