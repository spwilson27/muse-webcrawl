Witt group
Algebraic structure in quadratic form theory

In mathematics, a Witt group of a field, named after Ernst Witt, is an abelian group whose elements are represented by symmetric bilinear forms over the field, modulo a certain equivalence relation. Witt groups are fundamental invariants in the algebraic theory of quadratic forms.

Definition

Two symmetric bilinear forms are Witt equivalent if their orthogonal difference is a hyperbolic form. The set of equivalence classes forms a group under orthogonal direct sum, with the zero element represented by hyperbolic forms. The Witt group of a field F is denoted W(F).

Properties

For the real numbers, the Witt group is infinite cyclic, detected by the signature. For finite fields and number fields, Witt groups have been completely classified. The Witt ring adds a multiplication given by the tensor product of forms. Related constructions include Witt vectors and the Witt groups of schemes in algebraic geometry.
