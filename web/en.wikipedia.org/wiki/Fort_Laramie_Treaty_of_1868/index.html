Fort Laramie Treaty of 1868

SignedApril 29 – November 6, 1868
LocationFort Laramie, Wyoming Territory
PartiesUnited States; Lakota, Dakota, Nakota, Arapaho

The Fort Laramie Treaty of 1868 was an agreement between the United States and the Oglala, Miniconjou, and Brulé bands of the Lakota people, along with the Yanktonai Dakota and Arapaho Nation, signed in 1868 at Fort Laramie in the Wyoming Territory. It established the Great Sioux Reservation, which included the Black Hills, and set aside additional lands as unceded Indian territory.

Background

The treaty followed Red Cloud's War (1866–1868), in which Lakota and allied forces fought the U.S. Army over the Bozeman Trail. The United States agreed to abandon its forts along the trail, and the Powder River Country was recognized as unceded Indian territory.

Terms

The treaty guaranteed the Lakota ownership of the Black Hills, hunting rights in large areas outside the reservation, and required that further land cessions obtain the consent of three-fourths of adult male tribal members. It also provided for schools, farming assistance, and annuities.

Aftermath

After gold was discovered in the Black Hills, the United States seized them in 1877 in violation of the treaty. In United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians (1980), the Supreme Court ruled the taking illegal and awarded compensation, which the Sioux have refused to accept, insisting on the return of the land.
