Sword

[Image placeholder: sword]

A sword is an edged, bladed weapon intended for manual cutting or thrusting. Its blade, longer than a knife or dagger, is attached to a hilt and can be straight or curved, with one or two cutting edges. Swords have been used since the Bronze Age in warfare, duelling, ceremony, and as status symbols, and the word continues to be applied to many bladed weapons of history and myth.

Anatomy

A sword consists of the blade and the hilt. The blade includes the edge, the point, the fuller (a groove that lightens the blade), and the tang, which extends into the grip. The hilt comprises the guard, grip, and pommel, which counterbalances the blade and can be used to strike.

History

The first swords appeared in the Bronze Age, evolving from daggers around 1700 BC; iron and later steel swords — such as the Roman gladius, the medieval arming sword and longsword, the Japanese katana, and the smallsword of the early modern period — dominated personal weaponry for millennia. Cavalry sabres and naval cutlasses remained in military use into the nineteenth century.

Use today

Swords survive in ceremonial roles, martial arts such as fencing, kendo, and historical European martial arts, and as prized collectibles. Typologies such as Ewart Oakeshott's classify medieval blades by form and function.
