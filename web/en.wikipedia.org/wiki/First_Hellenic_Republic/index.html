First Hellenic Republic

The First Hellenic Republic (Greek: Αʹ Ελληνική Δημοκρατία) is the conventional name for the provisional Greek state during the Greek War of Independence against the Ottoman Empire, from the First National Assembly at Epidaurus in 1822 until the establishment of the Kingdom of Greece in 1832. It is called the "first" republic by analogy with the Second (1924–1935) and Third (1974–present) Hellenic Republics, though the term was not used by contemporaries.

Provisional governments

The First National Assembly at Epidaurus (December 1821 – January 1822) adopted Greece's first constitution and proclaimed independence. Subsequent national assemblies at Astros (1823) and Troezen (1827) revised the constitutional order, but the revolutionary government was repeatedly crippled by factionalism and two civil wars (1823–1825) among military chieftains, island shipowners and politicians. An Egyptian army under Ibrahim Pasha, sent to aid the Ottomans, devastated the Peloponnese from 1825, and the revolution seemed close to collapse when the Great Powers intervened, destroying the Ottoman-Egyptian fleet at the Battle of Navarino in October 1827.

Kapodistrias

The Third National Assembly at Troezen elected Ioannis Kapodistrias, a former foreign minister of Russia, as the first Governor (Kyvernitis) of Greece for a seven-year term. Arriving in January 1828, Kapodistrias built a centralised administration, founded schools, an orphanage, a military academy and the first national bank, introduced the phoenix as currency, and promoted the cultivation of the potato and quarantine measures against plague. His authoritarian style made powerful enemies, and he was assassinated in Nafplio on 9 October 1831 by members of the Mavromichalis clan of Mani.

End of the republic

After Kapodistrias's assassination, Greece relapsed into disorder under his brother Augustinos and then a series of governing committees. The London Conference of 1832 settled Greece as an independent monarchy under the Bavarian prince Otto, who arrived in 1833 as the first King of Greece, ending the republican experiment.
