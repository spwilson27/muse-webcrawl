Multimedia Messaging Service

Multimedia Messaging Service (MMS) is a standard way to send messages that include multimedia content to and from mobile phones over a cellular network. It extends the core SMS (Short Message Service) capability, which transmits only text, allowing users to send and receive messages containing images, audio, video, and rich text.

Technical details

MMS was developed by the 3GPP and 3GPP2 standards bodies and the Open Mobile Alliance. Unlike SMS, which travels over signalling channels, an MMS message is sent as data: the sending phone uploads the message to the carrier's Multimedia Messaging Service Center (MMSC) over an HTTP-based interface using a mobile data connection, and the recipient is notified by a special SMS that triggers retrieval of the content. Because phones differ in screen size, codecs, and maximum message size (historically often limited to a few hundred kilobytes), MMSCs perform content adaptation, transcoding media to formats the receiving handset supports.

History and usage

The first MMS services were launched in the early 2000s, beginning in Europe and Asia around 2002, as camera phones spread. MMS saw use for picture messaging, mobile commerce, and content delivery, and business-to-consumer uses such as news alerts and advertising. Adoption was hampered by interoperability problems between carriers and handsets, configuration difficulties, and per-message charges.

Decline

With the growth of smartphones and mobile internet, person-to-person multimedia messaging largely moved to over-the-top applications such as WhatsApp, iMessage, and social media platforms, which offer higher-quality media and group messaging without per-message fees. MMS remains in use for group texting between incompatible platforms and as a fallback, and is being succeeded by Rich Communication Services (RCS) in many markets.
