Mustafa Kamil Pasha

Mustafa Kamil Pasha (Arabic: مصطفى كامل; 14 August 1874 – 10 February 1908) was an Egyptian lawyer, journalist and nationalist activist who founded the National Party (al-Hizb al-Watani) and campaigned for the end of the British occupation of Egypt, which had begun in 1882. A gifted orator, he is regarded as one of the founders of modern Egyptian nationalism.

Life and career

Born in Cairo, Kamil studied law in Egypt and at the University of Toulouse in France. In 1893 he began writing for the newspaper Al-Mu'ayyad and in 1900 founded the daily Al-Liwa ("The Banner"), which became the principal organ of the nationalist movement; he later launched French- and English-language sister papers, L'Étendard Égyptien and The Egyptian Standard. He enjoyed the support of Khedive Abbas II and sought French backing against Britain.

The Dinshaway incident of 1906 — in which British officers clashed with villagers at Dinshaway and the ensuing tribunal imposed harsh sentences, including public executions — gave Kamil his most powerful cause: his articles and speeches publicised the affair across Europe and helped force the resignation of the British agent-general, Lord Cromer, in 1907. Kamil formally founded the National Party in December 1907, shortly before his death in February 1908 at the age of 33. His funeral was an enormous public demonstration.

Legacy

Kamil's mausoleum in Cairo, built in neo-Pharaonic style between 1947 and 1949, now houses the Mustafa Kamil Museum. His speeches and writings, including his book The Egyptian Question, remain landmarks of nationalist literature, and the slogan attributed to him — that if he were not Egyptian he would wish to be Egyptian — is still widely quoted.
