Tiltrotor

A tiltrotor is an aircraft that generates lift and propulsion by way of one or more powered rotors (proprotors) mounted on rotating shafts at the ends of fixed wings. Almost all tiltrotors use a transverse-rotor design. In vertical flight the rotors point upward like a helicopter's; in forward flight they tilt forward to act as propellers, combining vertical take-off and landing with the speed and range of a fixed-wing aircraft.

History

The concept dates to early 20th-century patents, but practical machines came with experiments such as the Transcendental Model 1-G of the 1950s and the Bell XV-3 and XV-15 research aircraft. The XV-15's success led directly to the Bell Boeing V-22 Osprey, which entered service with the United States Marine Corps in 2007 as the first production tiltrotor.

Examples

Operational and developmental tiltrotors include the V-22 Osprey, the civilian AgustaWestland AW609, and the Bell V-280 Valor, selected by the US Army as its future long-range assault aircraft. Tiltrotor designs trade mechanical complexity and cost against their unique combination of helicopter versatility and turboprop speed.
