Milü
Chinese: 密率 ("close ratio")
Value: 355/113 ≈ 3.14159292
Attributed to: Zu Chongzhi (5th century CE)

Milü (Chinese: 密率; pinyin: mìlǜ), also known as Zulü after its discoverer, is the rational approximation 355/113 to the value of π (pi), accurate to six decimal places: 355/113 = 3.14159292035398…, while π = 3.14159265358979….

History

The fraction was found by the Chinese mathematician and astronomer Zu Chongzhi (429–500 CE), who also gave the cruder "approximate ratio" (yuelü) 22/7. Zu bounded π between 3.1415926 and 3.1415927, a precision unmatched anywhere for nearly a thousand years; his original derivation is lost, though it is thought to have extended Liu Hui's polygon method to a polygon of 24,576 sides. In the West the same fraction was rediscovered by Adriaan Metius in the late 16th century, and it appears as a convergent of the continued fraction of π, which explains its exceptional accuracy for such a small denominator.
