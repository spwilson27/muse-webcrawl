Tabloid (paper size)

Tabloid is a North American paper size of 11 × 17 inches (279 × 432 mm), also designated ANSI B and sometimes called "ledger" (when oriented 17 × 11 for landscape use). The name derives from the tabloid newspaper format, which is roughly this size, although newspaper tabloid pages vary by publication and country.

Two letter-size sheets placed side by side make one tabloid sheet, so tabloid printing is convenient for newsletters, posters, spreadsheets, and folded booklets.

Newspapers

Tabloid newspapers traditionally measure about 17 inches long by 11 inches wide (in North America), though many British and European tabloids are somewhat larger. The compact format, associated with popular and sensational journalism since the early 20th century, gave "tabloid" its figurative meaning. In the 2000s many broadsheet newspapers, including The Times and The Guardian in the United Kingdom, switched to tabloid or similar compact formats for convenience.
