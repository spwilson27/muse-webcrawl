Spectre (DC Comics character)

The Spectre is the name given to several fictional antiheroes published by DC Comics. He is the embodiment of the Wrath of God, a cosmic entity bonded to the soul of a deceased human to carry out divine vengeance on Earth. The character first appeared in More Fun Comics #52 (February 1940), created by Jerry Siegel and Bernard Baily.

The Spectre, the spirit of God's vengeance.

Jim Corrigan

The original host was Jim Corrigan, a hard-boiled police detective murdered by mobsters and stuffed into a cement-filled barrel. Refused entry to the afterlife, his spirit was bound by "the Voice" to fight evil on Earth as the ghostly Spectre. Corrigan was a founding member of the Justice Society of America. His near-omnipotent powers were progressively curtailed by later writers, who established that the human host restrains the Spectre's capacity for indiscriminate vengeance.

Hal Jordan and Crispus Allen

After Corrigan's soul attained peace in The Spectre vol. 3 (written by John Ostrander and drawn by Tom Mandrake, a critically acclaimed 1990s run), the Spectre Force passed to fallen Green Lantern Hal Jordan following the 1999 storyline Day of Judgment, reframing him as a spirit of redemption. Jordan's tenure ended with his resurrection in Green Lantern: Rebirth (2004). In Gotham Central and Infinite Crisis, the host became murdered Gotham detective Crispus Allen.

Powers and concept

The Spectre is among the most powerful beings in the DC Universe — capable of reality warping, matter manipulation, invulnerability and enormous size — limited chiefly by the judgement of the Presence and the moral restraint of his host. Unbonded, the Spectre Force becomes unstable and increasingly vengeful, a theme explored in Day of Vengeance (2005), where, manipulated by Eclipso, he waged war on magic itself.

In other media

The Spectre has appeared in animated form (Batman: The Brave and the Bold, voiced by Mark Hamill; the 2010 DC Showcase short) and in live action in the Arrowverse crossover Crisis on Infinite Earths, where Stephen Lobo played Jim Corrigan and Oliver Queen assumed the Spectre mantle.
