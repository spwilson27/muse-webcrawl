The Government of the French Community (French: Gouvernement de la Communauté française) is the executive body of the French Community of Belgium, one of the country's three linguistic communities, representing French speakers in Wallonia and Brussels. Since 2011 the community has officially styled itself the "Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles" (Wallonia-Brussels Federation), although the constitutional name remains the French Community.

Competences

The community is responsible for person-related and cultural matters for French speakers: education (from nursery to university), culture and audiovisual media — including the public broadcaster RTBF — sport, youth policy, scientific research related to its competences, and parts of health and social assistance such as preventive medicine and child welfare through the Office de la naissance et de l'enfance (ONE).

Composition

The government is appointed by the Parliament of the French Community, whose 94 members are drawn from the Walloon Parliament and the French-speaking group of the Brussels Parliament. It is led by a Minister-President and typically includes ministers for education, higher education, culture, and childhood. In Brussels, community competences are partly exercised in cooperation with the French Community Commission (COCOF).

History

The community institutions emerged from Belgium's state reforms: cultural communities were created in 1970, given executives and councils in 1980, and received the crucial education competence from the state reform of 1988, making education — its largest budget item — a community responsibility.
