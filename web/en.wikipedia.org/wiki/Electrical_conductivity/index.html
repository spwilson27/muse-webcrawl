Electrical conductivity

Electrical conductivity (often represented by σ, sigma) is a measure of a material's ability to conduct electric current. It is the reciprocal of electrical resistivity and is measured in siemens per metre (S/m). Conductivity depends on the density of charge carriers, their charge, and their mobility.

Mechanisms

In metals, conduction is by electrons in a partially filled band; conductivity falls with temperature as lattice vibrations scatter the electrons. In electrolytes and plasmas, ions carry the current. In semiconductors, conductivity is intermediate and rises strongly with temperature and doping; in superconductors it is effectively infinite below a critical temperature.

Applications

Conductivity measurement is used to assess water purity and salinity, to locate ores in geophysics, and in materials characterisation. The International Annealed Copper Standard (IACS) expresses the conductivity of conductor grades as a percentage of that of pure annealed copper.
