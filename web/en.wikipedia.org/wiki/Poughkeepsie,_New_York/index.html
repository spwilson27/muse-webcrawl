Poughkeepsie, New York

Main Street in downtown Poughkeepsie.

Poughkeepsie is a city in and the county seat of Dutchess County, New York, United States. It is situated on the east bank of the Hudson River, about 75 miles (120 km) north of New York City and 70 miles south of Albany, and is part of the Hudson Valley region. The city had a population of 31,577 at the 2020 United States census. It is legally independent of the surrounding town of Poughkeepsie, with which it shares its name; both derive from a Wappinger phrase meaning "the reed-covered lodge by the little water place".

History

English colonist Robert Sanders and Dutch-born miller Myndert Harmense Van Den Bogaerdt bought land from the native people in 1686, and the settlement grew around its mill, courthouse and river landing. Poughkeepsie became the capital of New York for a period after the British burned Kingston in 1777, and in July 1788 the state's convention ratified the United States Constitution at the courthouse on Market Street. The village was incorporated in 1799 and chartered as a city in 1854.

Nineteenth-century Poughkeepsie was a centre for brewing, carpet and shoe manufacture, and whale-ship outfitting. Smith Brothers cough drops were first made there, and Matthew Vassar founded the brewery that funded Vassar College. The Poughkeepsie Railroad Bridge, completed in 1889, was for a time the longest bridge in the world. In the twentieth century the city declined as manufacturing shifted, while IBM's large plant in the town provided regional employment; the city has since pursued downtown and waterfront redevelopment centred on the Walkway over the Hudson, opened on the old railroad bridge in 2009.

Geography and demographics

The city covers about 5.7 square miles at the confluence of Fall Kill Creek with the Hudson. It is served by US Route 9, US 44/NY 55 via the Mid-Hudson Bridge, and the Poughkeepsie train station, a stop for Metro-North's Hudson Line and Amtrak. The city is racially diverse, with a substantial African American and growing Hispanic population, and includes neighbourhoods shaped by urban renewal in the 1960s and 1970s.

Education and culture

Vassar College lies just outside the city in the town, and Marist College and Dutchess Community College are nearby. Cultural institutions include the Bardavon 1869 Opera House, the Mid-Hudson Civic Center (Majed J. Nesheiwat Convention Center), and the Frances Lehman Loeb Art Center at Vassar.
