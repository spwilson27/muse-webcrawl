SpaceX Dragon

Dragon is a family of SpaceX spacecraft. The original Dragon (Dragon 1), flown from 2010 to 2020, was a partially reusable uncrewed cargo capsule developed under NASA's COTS programme to resupply the International Space Station after the Space Shuttle's retirement. In May 2012 a Dragon became the first commercial spacecraft to rendezvous and berth with the ISS — the moment commercial spaceflight stopped being a promise.

Dragon 1 flew 22 missions (one lost in the 2015 CRS-7 launch failure), returning significant science cargo to splashdown — a capability only it and later its successor possessed among freighters. The design's blunt capsule, Draco thrusters and trunk formed the basis for Crew Dragon, which added crew systems, abort engines and docking.

First flight

Dragon's very first flight in December 2010 — a COTS demonstration — carried a whimsical secret cargo revealed only after splashdown: a wheel of cheese, in homage to a Monty Python sketch. The capsule splashed down precisely on target, and SpaceX founder Elon Musk noted that had it been crewed, the ride would have been survivable — the era of commercial human spaceflight, previewed in cheese.
