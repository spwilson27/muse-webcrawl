Urban Indian

Urban Indian centres sustain tribal cultures in metropolitan areas

Urban Indians are Native Americans who live in urban areas of the United States. Today the majority of Native Americans — roughly seven in ten by recent census counts, depending on definitions used — live in cities and suburbs rather than on reservations or in rural tribal areas, a demographic transformation driven substantially by federal Indian policy in the mid-20th century.

History

Although Native people have always traded, worked, and lived in cities, large-scale urban migration followed the Indian Relocation Act of 1956 and the broader federal termination policy, which encouraged (and often pressured) Native people to leave reservations for designated cities such as Chicago, Denver, Los Angeles, Minneapolis, Cleveland, and Oakland, with promises of jobs and housing assistance that were frequently inadequate. Many relocatees returned to their tribal communities, but many others stayed, forming lasting urban Indian communities.

Community and identity

Urban Indian life is sustained by intertribal institutions: Indian centers, health clinics (funded in part under the Indian Health Care Improvement Act), churches, powwow circuits, and organizations addressing housing, employment, and education. Urban Native identity is often intertribal in expression, while individuals maintain ties — through travel, remittances, ceremony, and tribal enrollment — to their specific tribal nations. Scholars note the tension between the US government's historic expectation that urbanization meant assimilation and the reality that urban Indians built distinctly Indigenous urban cultures.

Challenges

Urban Indians may face a "invisibility" in public policy: federal trust responsibilities and services have historically been organized around reservations, and urban populations can be underserved by both tribal and municipal programmes. Urban Indian health organizations serve a large and growing population with a fraction of the Indian Health Service budget.
