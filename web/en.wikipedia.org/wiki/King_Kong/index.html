King Kong

King Kong is a fictional giant ape monster, resembling an enormous gorilla, who has appeared in various media since 1933. He was created by American filmmaker Merian C. Cooper and first appeared in the novelisation of the 1933 film from RKO Pictures, before becoming the star of that landmark movie, released the same year.

The 1933 film

In the original film, filmmaker Carl Denham leads an expedition to the remote Skull Island, where the giant ape Kong is worshipped as a god. Kong becomes infatuated with actress Ann Darrow, is captured, and is brought to New York City to be exhibited. He escapes, climbs the Empire State Building with Ann, and is shot down by naval aircraft — prompting Denham's famous epitaph: "'Twas beauty killed the beast." The film's stop-motion effects by Willis O'Brien and score by Max Steiner were revolutionary.

Legacy and remakes

Kong has become one of the world's most recognisable popular culture icons. The character has been remade in 1976 (produced by Dino De Laurentiis), in Peter Jackson's 2005 film, and in Kong: Skull Island (2017), part of Legendary's MonsterVerse, which led to crossovers with Godzilla in Godzilla vs. Kong (2021) and Godzilla x Kong: The New Empire (2024). The 1933 film was selected for preservation in the US National Film Registry in 1991.
