Andal (Tamil: ஆண்டாள், Āṇṭāḷ), also known as Godadevi or Kodhai, was the only female saint among the twelve Alvars, the Tamil poet-saints of South India devoted to the god Vishnu. She is traditionally dated to the eighth century CE and is revered in Sri Vaishnavism as an avatar of the goddess Bhudevi, the earth consort of Vishnu.

Life

According to tradition, Andal was found as an infant under a tulsi plant in the temple garden at Srivilliputhur by the Alvar saint Periyalvar (Vishnuchitta), who raised her as his own daughter. She grew up immersed in devotion to Vishnu in his form as Ranganatha of Srirangam. She is said to have secretly worn the flower garlands prepared for the temple deity before they were offered — when Periyalvar discovered this and rebuked her, Vishnu is said to have appeared in a dream declaring that he preferred the garlands she had worn, earning her the title "she who wore and gave" (Sudikkodutha Nacchiyar). Refusing earthly marriage, she vowed to wed the deity himself, and tradition holds that she was taken in procession to Srirangam, where she merged with the image of Ranganatha.

Works

Andal composed two celebrated works in Tamil, both sung during the winter month of Margazhi. The Tiruppavai, thirty verses in which girls undertake a vow at dawn to win the Lord, is among the most beloved texts of Tamil Hinduism. The Nachiyar Tirumoli, 143 verses, expresses her longing for Vishnu in the language of bridal mysticism, including a famous dream sequence describing her wedding to the god. Her poetry is included in the Nalayira Divya Prabandham, the Alvar canon.

Legacy

The Srivilliputhur Andal Temple is a major pilgrimage centre, and the festival of Adi Pooram celebrates her birth. Her image — often shown with a parrot on her hand and a distinctive side-knotted hairstyle — is carried in procession in Vaishnava temples across Tamil Nadu.
