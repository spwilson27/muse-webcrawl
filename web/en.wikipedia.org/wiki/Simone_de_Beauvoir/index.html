Simone Lucie Ernestine Marie Bertrand de Beauvoir (1908–1986) was a French existentialist philosopher, novelist and feminist thinker. Her The Second Sex (1949) — a vast philosophical, historical and literary analysis of women's oppression — founded modern feminism with its claim that woman is made, not born, and cast as man's 'Other'. With Jean-Paul Sartre she formed the most famous intellectual partnership of the century.

Thought

In The Ethics of Ambiguity (1947) Beauvoir developed an existentialist ethics of freedom that wills the freedom of others. The Second Sex applied existential categories — transcendence versus immanence — to women's situation across biology, history, myth and lived experience, arguing for women's economic independence and self-definition. Her novels, including The Mandarins (Prix Goncourt, 1954), and her memoirs document French intellectual life; she campaigned for abortion rights, signing the Manifesto of the 343 in 1971.
