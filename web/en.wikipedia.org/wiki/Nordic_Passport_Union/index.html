Nordic Passport Union

The Nordic Passport Union is an arrangement that allows citizens of the Nordic countries — Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway and Sweden — to travel freely between them without a passport or other travel documents, and to live and work in another Nordic country without residence or work permits. Established by agreements in the 1950s, it is one of the oldest passport-free zones in the world.

History

Passport requirements between the Nordic countries were abolished in stages: first among Denmark, Norway and Sweden in 1952, with Finland and Iceland joining by 1957. A common labour market agreement followed in 1954. Since 2001 the Nordic EU/EEA members have also participated in the Schengen Area, but the Nordic union remains broader for Nordic citizens — who may, for example, prove identity with ordinary ID rather than a passport — and it covers Greenland and the Faroe Islands, which are outside Schengen.
