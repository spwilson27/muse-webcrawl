Permanent residency in Canada

Permanent residency in Canada is a status granting a person who is not a Canadian citizen the right to live and work in Canada without time limit on their stay. A permanent resident (PR) must live in Canada for at least two years within a five-year period (730 days) to maintain the status, and may apply for Canadian citizenship after meeting further residence and other requirements.

Rights and limitations

Permanent residents have the right to live, work or study anywhere in Canada, to receive most social benefits including health care coverage, and to protection under Canadian law and the Charter of Rights and Freedoms. They cannot vote or run for political office, and some high-security jobs are restricted. Status can be lost through serious criminality or failure to meet the residency obligation.

Routes to permanent residence

Most new permanent residents are admitted through economic programmes, including the Express Entry system (federal skilled worker, Canadian experience class and federal skilled trades streams) and provincial nominee programmes, or through family sponsorship of spouses, children and parents. Refugees and protected persons, and humanitarian and compassionate applicants, form other streams. Successful applicants are issued a Confirmation of Permanent Residence and, on entry, a permanent resident card used as proof of status for travel back to Canada.

History

The status replaced the older concept of "landed immigrant" under successive immigration acts; the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act (2002) introduced the permanent resident card and the current residency obligation framework.
