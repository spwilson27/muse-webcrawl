The Cessna 150, one of the most popular flight trainers ever built.

The Cessna 150 is a two-seat tricycle-gear general aviation aircraft designed for flight training, touring and personal use. Produced from 1959 to 1977, it is the fifth most produced civil airplane ever, with 23,949 built in the United States and France (by Reims Aviation as the F150/FA150).

Design and development

Developed from the taildragger Cessna 140, the 150 features a tricycle landing gear and a 100 hp Continental O-200 engine. Successive model years added refinements such as a swept tail fin (1966), a rear window, and improved interiors. Variants included the Commuter with upgraded equipment and the Aerobat, strengthened for limited aerobatics. The 150 was succeeded in 1978 by the Cessna 152, powered by a Lycoming engine.

Specifications

Typical figures: crew one, capacity one passenger; length 7.28 m; wingspan 10.2 m; maximum takeoff weight 726 kg; cruise speed about 152 km/h (82 knots); range about 680 km; service ceiling 4,300 m (14,000 ft).
