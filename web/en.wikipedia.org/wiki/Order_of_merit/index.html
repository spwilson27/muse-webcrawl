Order of merit

Order of merit

Type: Order of chivalry / merit

Awarded for: Distinguished service to the state or nation

An order of merit is an order of honour awarded by a sovereign, head of state, or other authority to recognise distinguished service or achievement, most commonly in the military, science, arts, literature, or public service. The term is used both as a general category of national honours and as the proper name of several specific orders. Orders of merit developed as states expanded their honours systems beyond the older dynastic and chivalric orders, whose membership was often confined to the nobility.

History

The model is often traced to the Prussian Pour le Mérite, founded by Frederick II of Prussia in 1740 and later given an eminent civil class by Alexander von Humboldt's prompting in 1842. Napoleon Bonaparte's Légion d'honneur (1802) set the pattern for modern national orders of merit open to citizens of all ranks according to achievement. During the 19th and 20th centuries most states established one or more orders of merit with graded classes — typically grand cross, grand officer, commander, officer, and knight or member.

Notable orders of merit

  • The Order of Merit of the United Kingdom and Commonwealth realms, founded by Edward VII in 1902, limited to 24 living members appointed personally by the sovereign.
  • The French Ordre national du Mérite, founded in 1963 by Charles de Gaulle as the second national order after the Legion of Honour.
  • The Order of Merit of the Federal Republic of Germany (Bundesverdienstkreuz), the only federal decoration of Germany, founded in 1951.
  • The Italian Order of Merit of the Italian Republic (1951), the senior order of Italy.
  • National orders of merit in countries such as Poland, Ukraine, Chile, Paraguay and many others.

Structure

Most orders of merit are divided into classes distinguished by the size and manner of wearing the insignia: a badge on a sash for the highest class, a neck badge for commanders, and a breast badge or medal for lower classes. Appointment is usually in the gift of the head of state, often on ministerial advice, and the order carries an official order of precedence (an order of wear) relative to a country's other decorations.
