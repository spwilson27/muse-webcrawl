Lattice (group)

In geometry and group theory, a lattice in Rn is a subgroup of the additive group Rn that is isomorphic to the additive group Zn and spans Rn as a real vector space. Equivalently, a lattice is the set of all integer linear combinations of a basis of Rn: given linearly independent vectors b1, …, bn, the lattice they generate is { x1b1 + … + xnbn : all xi ∈ Z }. Lattices are discrete subgroups of Euclidean space with periodic structure, and they are the mathematical model for the arrangement of atoms in a crystal. The study of lattices connects geometry, number theory, and algebra, and in recent decades has become central to cryptography.

A lattice in the plane R2.

Definitions and basic properties

A lattice is determined by a basis, but the basis is not unique: any two bases of the same lattice are related by a unimodular matrix (an integer matrix with determinant ±1). The fundamental parallelepiped of a basis tiles space by lattice translations, and its volume — the determinant (or covolume) of the lattice — is independent of the choice of basis. Important invariants and problems include the length of the shortest non-zero vector (the first successive minimum), the covering radius, and the packing density of spheres centred at lattice points. Finding short or close vectors in high-dimensional lattices (the shortest vector problem, SVP, and closest vector problem, CVP) is computationally hard, a fact exploited in lattice-based cryptography.

Examples

  • The integer lattice Zn, the cubic grid.
  • The two-dimensional hexagonal (triangular) lattice, which gives the densest circle packing in the plane.
  • The face-centred cubic and body-centred cubic lattices, which occur in metals.
  • The root lattices of Lie theory, such as An, Dn, and the exceptional E8 lattice — the densest sphere packing in eight dimensions.
  • The Leech lattice in 24 dimensions, which has remarkable symmetry (its automorphism group relates to the sporadic simple groups) and gives the densest sphere packing in its dimension.
  • In crystallography, the 14 Bravais lattices classify all lattice types in three dimensions by symmetry.

Applications

Lattices underlie the mathematics of crystals and quasicrystals, error-correcting codes (via constructions from codes to lattices), and the geometry of numbers pioneered by Hermann Minkowski, whose theorem on convex bodies guarantees short lattice vectors. In the twenty-first century, the apparent hardness of lattice problems — which, unlike factoring and discrete logarithms, are not known to be vulnerable to quantum algorithms — has made lattices the leading foundation for post-quantum cryptography; NIST standardised lattice-based schemes (such as ML-KEM, derived from Kyber) for this purpose.

Related senses

"Lattice" in mathematics also names an unrelated structure in order theory (see lattice (order)), and in group theory the term is used for discrete subgroups of locally compact groups more generally, of which the Euclidean lattices described here are the prototype.
