Lacerta


The constellation Lacerta

Lacerta is one of the 88 modern constellations, a small, faint grouping of stars in the northern sky. Its name is Latin for "lizard". The constellation was created by the Polish astronomer Johannes Hevelius in 1687, who originally named it Stellio, after a type of lizard with star-like spots. Lacerta lies between the constellations Cygnus, Cassiopeia, Cepheus, Pegasus and Andromeda, and contains no bright stars; its brightest member, Alpha Lacertae, is a white main-sequence star of magnitude 3.8.

Notable features

Because it lies in the plane of the Milky Way, Lacerta contains several open clusters, including NGC 7243. The constellation became notable in the twentieth century for BL Lacertae, an object first catalogued as a variable star but later recognised as the prototype of the BL Lac objects — a class of active galactic nuclei whose jets are pointed almost directly at Earth. Lacerta also contains the binary star system and planet host EV Lacertae, a red dwarf famous for powerful stellar flares.

Mythology

Lacerta has no associated classical mythology, being a modern creation; Hevelius placed the little lizard among the older figures of the sky, and alternative names such as "the sceptre and hand of justice", proposed in France, never gained acceptance.
