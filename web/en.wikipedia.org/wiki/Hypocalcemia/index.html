Hypocalcemia

Hypocalcemia is a low level of calcium in the blood serum, conventionally a corrected total calcium below 2.1 mmol/L (8.5 mg/dL) or an ionised calcium below 1.1 mmol/L. It most commonly results from hypoparathyroidism (often after thyroid or parathyroid surgery) and vitamin D deficiency, and also occurs in chronic kidney disease, acute pancreatitis and critical illness.

Chvostek's and Trousseau's signs reflect neuromuscular irritability in hypocalcemia.

Signs and symptoms

Low ionised calcium increases neuronal excitability. Symptoms include perioral and fingertip tingling, muscle cramps, carpopedal spasm and tetany; the classic signs are Chvostek's sign (facial twitching on tapping the facial nerve) and Trousseau's sign (carpal spasm on inflating a blood-pressure cuff). Severe hypocalcemia can cause seizures, laryngospasm, QT prolongation and heart failure. Chronic hypocalcemia may cause cataracts, dry skin and basal ganglia calcification.

Management

Symptomatic or severe hypocalcemia is treated with intravenous calcium gluconate, with attention to magnesium (hypomagnesaemia both causes and accompanies it). Chronic management uses oral calcium and vitamin D analogues such as calcitriol or alfacalcidol, and recombinant parathyroid hormone in selected cases of hypoparathyroidism.
