Brixton

Brixton is a district in South London, England, within the London Borough of Lambeth. It lies about 3.8 miles (6.1 km) south of Charing Cross. Brixton is known as a major centre of London's African-Caribbean community and for its markets, music venues and nightlife.

History

The name is recorded as Brixistane in 1062, meaning "stone of a Saxon named Brihtsige". The area remained rural until the early nineteenth century, when the opening of Vauxhall Bridge in 1816 spurred development. Brixton grew rapidly as a middle-class suburb after the railway arrived in the 1860s, and Electric Avenue, built in the 1880s, was one of the first shopping streets in Britain lit by electricity.

After the Second World War, Brixton became a principal settlement for the Windrush generation of Caribbean migrants, many of whom found lodging there from 1948 onwards. The district subsequently became the heart of Black British culture, home to the Black Cultural Archives and a celebrated scene of sound systems, markets and clubs.

1981 riots

In April 1981 Brixton saw serious rioting following tensions between the Metropolitan Police and the local community, aggravated by the controversial "sus" stop-and-search laws and the police operation Swamp 81. The Scarman Report that followed became a landmark in British race relations and policing policy. Further disturbances occurred in 1985 and 1995.

Culture and landmarks

Landmarks include the Brixton Academy (now the O2 Academy Brixton), one of London's leading live music venues; the Ritzy Cinema; Brixton Market and Brixton Village, known for Afro-Caribbean food and independent shops; and the David Bowie memorial mural, Bowie having been born at 40 Stansfield Road. The Brixton pound, a local currency launched in 2009, featured Bowie on its ten-pound note.
