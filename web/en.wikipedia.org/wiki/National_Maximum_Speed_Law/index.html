National Maximum Speed Law

The National Maximum Speed Law (NMSL) was a provision of the Emergency Highway Energy Conservation Act of 1974 that prohibited speed limits higher than 55 miles per hour (89 km/h) in the United States. Enacted in response to the 1973 oil crisis, it required states to lower their maximum speed limits to 55 mph or lose federal highway funding.

Background

President Richard Nixon signed the law on 2 January 1974. Although intended primarily to conserve fuel by reducing highway driving speeds, the law also coincided with a notable decline in traffic fatalities, which supporters attributed to lower speeds. Compliance was imperfect; studies in the 1980s found that a large share of freeway traffic exceeded the limit.

Amendments and repeal

In 1987, Congress permitted states to raise limits to 65 mph on rural Interstate highways, and in 1988 this was extended to other qualifying rural roads. The National Highway Designation Act of 1995, signed by President Bill Clinton, fully repealed the NMSL effective 8 December 1995, returning speed limit authority to the states. Many states promptly raised limits to 70 or 75 mph, and some later went higher.

Legacy

The "double nickel" limit left a lasting mark on American culture, inspiring songs such as Sammy Hagar's "I Can't Drive 55". Debates over the law's safety benefits and fuel savings continue to inform discussions of speed limits and energy policy.
