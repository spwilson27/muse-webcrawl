The Kettle Moraine is a large band of glacial landforms in southeastern Wisconsin, United States, running roughly 120 miles from Walworth County in the south to Kewaunee County in the north-east. It was formed during the last Ice Age, where two lobes of the Laurentide Ice Sheet — the Green Bay lobe and the Lake Michigan lobe — met and deposited interlobate moraine. The region is named for its abundance of kettles, depressions formed when buried blocks of glacial ice melted and the sediment above them collapsed.

Geology

Alongside kettles, the moraine displays classic glacial features: kames (conical hills of sand and gravel), eskers (winding ridges deposited by subglacial streams), drumlins and numerous small kettle lakes. The deposits were laid down as the ice retreated roughly 15,000–20,000 years ago during the Wisconsin glaciation.

Recreation

The Kettle Moraine State Forest protects large parts of the formation in its Northern Unit (near Campbellsport) and Southern Unit (near Eagle), together with the smaller Pike Lake and Lapham Peak units. The Ice Age National Scenic Trail threads through the moraine for over 100 miles, and the area is popular for hiking, camping, cross-country skiing, mountain biking and wildlife watching. A glacial interpretive centre, the Henry S. Reuss Ice Age Visitor Center, stands in the Northern Unit.
