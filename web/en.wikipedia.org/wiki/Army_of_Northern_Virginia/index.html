The Army of Northern Virginia was the primary military force of the Confederate States of America in the Eastern Theater of the American Civil War. It was also the primary command structure for the Department of Northern Virginia. It was most often opposed by the Union Army of the Potomac.

Origins

The name Army of Northern Virginia was first used in the autumn of 1861 for the force defending northern Virginia, descended from the Army of the Potomac (Confederate) and the Army of the Shenandoah. Its early commanders included P. G. T. Beauregard and Joseph E. Johnston, under whom it fought at the First Battle of Bull Run in July 1861 and in the Peninsula Campaign of 1862.

Under Robert E. Lee

When Johnston was wounded at the Battle of Seven Pines on 1 June 1862, command passed to Robert E. Lee, who led the army for the rest of the war. Under Lee and corps commanders such as James Longstreet, Stonewall Jackson and J. E. B. Stuart, the army won celebrated victories at the Seven Days Battles, Second Bull Run, Fredericksburg and Chancellorsville, but was defeated at Antietam in 1862 and decisively at Gettysburg in July 1863. From 1864 Ulysses S. Grant's Overland Campaign and the Siege of Petersburg ground the army down through attrition.

Surrender at Appomattox

After the fall of Petersburg and Richmond in April 1865, Lee's attempted retreat westward was cut off, and on 9 April 1865 he surrendered the Army of Northern Virginia — by then reduced to roughly 28,000 men — to Grant at Appomattox Court House. Terms allowed officers and men to return home on parole, and the surrender effectively ended the Civil War.
