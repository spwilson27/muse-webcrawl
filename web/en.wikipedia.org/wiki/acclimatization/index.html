Acclimatization

Also called: Acclimation

Field: Physiology, evolutionary biology

Acclimatization or acclimation is the process by which an individual organism adjusts its physiology, morphology or behaviour to changes in its environment — such as temperature, altitude, humidity or photoperiod — over days to weeks, so as to maintain performance despite the change. Unlike adaptation, it is reversible and occurs within a single lifetime.

Altitude acclimatization

At high altitude, the body responds to lower oxygen availability with increased breathing and heart rate, followed over weeks by increased production of red blood cells under the influence of erythropoietin. Ascending too quickly can cause acute mountain sickness, high-altitude pulmonary edema or cerebral edema.

Heat acclimatization

Repeated heat exposure leads to earlier and more profuse sweating, expanded plasma volume and reduced electrolyte loss, markedly improving tolerance of exercise in hot conditions over roughly one to two weeks.
