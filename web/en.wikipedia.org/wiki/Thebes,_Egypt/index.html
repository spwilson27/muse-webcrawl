Thebes (Egyptian: Waset), known to the ancient Greeks as Thebai, was an ancient Egyptian city on the east bank of the Nile, about 800 km south of the Mediterranean, at the site of modern Luxor. It was the capital of Egypt during parts of the Middle Kingdom and most of the New Kingdom.

History

Thebes rose with the 11th Dynasty (Mentuhotep II, who reunified Egypt c. 2055 BC) and reached its zenith in the New Kingdom (c. 1550–1070 BC), when its god Amun, joined with Ra as Amun-Ra, became the national deity and its temples accumulated enormous wealth. The Achaemenid, Assyrian (sacked by Ashurbanipal in 663 BC) and later invasions diminished it; a Roman-era decline followed. Homer called it "hundred-gated Thebes".

Archaeology

On the east bank stand the Karnak and Luxor temple complexes, among the largest religious structures ever built. Across the river, the west bank necropolis includes the Valley of the Kings (with Tutankhamun's tomb), the Valley of the Queens, Deir el-Bahari (Hatshepsut's mortuary temple) and the Colossi of Memnon. The region figures in the era of Tefnakht and the Kushite pharaohs who ruled from the south.
