Atkins diet

The Atkins diet is a low-carbohydrate diet program devised by cardiologist Robert C. Atkins and popularised by his 1972 book Dr. Atkins' Diet Revolution. It restricts carbohydrate intake, particularly sugar and refined starches, while allowing liberal consumption of protein and fat.

Phases

The diet is structured in phases: an initial Induction phase limiting carbohydrate to about 20 grams per day, followed by Balancing, Pre-Maintenance and Maintenance phases in which carbohydrates are gradually reintroduced. The diet relies on the metabolic state of ketosis during its strictest phase.

Reception

The Atkins diet was hugely popular in the early 2000s and is credited with popularising low-carbohydrate eating. Its long-term health effects have been debated; studies generally show short-term weight loss comparable to other diets, with concerns raised about high intakes of saturated fat.
