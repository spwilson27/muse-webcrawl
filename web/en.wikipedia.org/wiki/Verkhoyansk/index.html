Verkhoyansk (Russian: Верхоянск; Yakut: Үөһээ Дьааҥы, Üöhee Caañı) is a town in the Sakha Republic, Russia, located on the Yana River in the Arctic Circle region of northeastern Siberia, near the coldest permanently inhabited places on Earth. With a population of roughly a thousand, it is among the smallest settlements in Russia holding town status.

History

The settlement was founded in 1638 as a Cossack wintering post (zimovye) during the Russian expansion into Yakutia. It later served as a place of political exile under both the Tsarist empire and the Soviet Union; the Polish writer Wacław Sieroszewski was exiled here in the 1880s and wrote ethnographic studies of the Yakut people during his stay. Verkhoyansk was granted town status in 1817.

Climate

Verkhoyansk has an extreme subarctic climate and, together with Oymyakon, is famed as one of the two "Poles of Cold" of the Northern Hemisphere. A temperature of −67.7 °C (−89.9 °F) was recorded here in February 1892, among the lowest ever measured at a permanently inhabited place. Winters are long, dark and bitterly cold, with January averages around −45 °C, while brief summers can be surprisingly warm, occasionally exceeding 30 °C; the town has recorded one of the greatest temperature ranges on Earth.

Economy and transport

The local economy rests on reindeer herding, horse breeding, fur trapping and river transport. Verkhoyansk has a small river port on the Yana, and a winter road (zimnik) connects it seasonally to other settlements; there is no year-round road link to Yakutsk.
