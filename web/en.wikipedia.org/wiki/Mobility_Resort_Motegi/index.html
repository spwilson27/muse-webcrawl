Mobility Resort Motegi, formerly known as Twin Ring Motegi, is a motorsport and leisure complex in Motegi, Tochigi Prefecture, Japan. Built by Honda and opened in 1997, the facility is notable for combining two separate major racing circuits: a 2.493 km (1.549 mi) superspeedway oval and a 4.801 km (2.983 mi) road course, which share pit and paddock facilities but can operate simultaneously.

History

Honda constructed the circuit as part of its effort to promote motorsport in Japan and to bring American-style oval racing, in particular IndyCar, to a Japanese audience. The oval hosted CART and later IndyCar Series races from 1998 until 2011, when earthquake damage and scheduling issues ended the event. The road course has hosted the Japanese motorcycle Grand Prix in most years since 2000.

In 2022 the facility was renamed Mobility Resort Motegi as part of a broader rebranding emphasising its non-racing attractions.

Facilities

Beyond the two circuits, the complex contains the Honda Collection Hall, a museum displaying historic Honda racing machines and production vehicles, plus karting tracks, off-road courses, campsites and nature activities aimed at families. The site lies in a wooded valley near the town of Motegi, roughly 100 km north of Tokyo.

The road course features 14 turns and is used for Super GT, Super Formula and numerous national series, while the oval is unique in Japan as a purpose-built American-style superspeedway.

Events

The headline annual event is the Japanese round of the MotoGP World Championship. Domestic series including Super GT and Super Formula race on the road course, and the venue also hosts historic festivals, driving schools and manufacturer events throughout the year.
