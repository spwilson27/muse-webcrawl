Life and war service

James Ramon Jones (6 November 1921 – 9 May 1977) was an American novelist whose work anatomised the United States Army in the Second World War from the enlisted man's point of view. Jones enlisted in 1939 and served with the 25th Infantry Division in Hawaii — he was present at Pearl Harbor on 7 December 1941 — and at Guadalcanal, where he was wounded and decorated with the Bronze Star and Purple Heart. His wartime experience supplied the substance of all his major fiction.

From Here to Eternity

His first published novel, From Here to Eternity (1951) — the story of Private Robert E. Lee Prewitt, a bugler and boxer destroyed by the peacetime army in Hawaii in 1941 — won the National Book Award and became a classic of American war literature despite heavy editorial bowdlerisation, restored only in the 2011 unexpurgated edition. The 1953 film adaptation, starring Burt Lancaster, Montgomery Clift, and Frank Sinatra, won eight Academy Awards including Best Picture.

The war trilogy

Jones conceived his three great war novels as a trilogy tracing the army through the war: From Here to Eternity (the peacetime army), The Thin Red Line (1962; combat at Guadalcanal, filmed in 1964 and again, to acclaim, by Terrence Malick in 1998), and Whistle (published posthumously, 1978; stateside wounded). His other books include Some Came Running (1957), the controversial The Pistol (1959), and the documentary WW II (1975). He spent much of his later life in Paris and died in New York in 1977.
