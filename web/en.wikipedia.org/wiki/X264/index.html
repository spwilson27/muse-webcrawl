x264

x264 is a free and open-source software library and application for encoding video streams into the H.264/MPEG-4 AVC compression format, released under the GNU General Public License. It was developed by the VideoLAN project and a community of contributors, and is widely regarded as one of the most feature-complete and highest-quality H.264 encoders, used by Internet video services, broadcasters and in Blu-ray disc authoring.

Features

x264 implements a large portion of the H.264 standard, including multiple reference frames, variable block-size motion compensation, B-frames (including their use as references), CABAC and CAVLC entropy coding, adaptive quantisation, and psychovisual optimisations such as psy-RD and adaptive quantisation strength adjustments. It supports 8-bit and 10-bit colour depth, interlaced coding (MBAFF), and multithreaded encoding. Presets (from "ultrafast" to "placebo") trade encoding speed against compression efficiency.

History

The project was started in 2003 by Laurent Aimar; subsequent lead developers included Loren Merritt and Jason Garrett-Glaser. In 2010 x264 was the first free encoder to implement the full Blu-ray specification encoding profiles, and commercial licensing was offered through x264 LLC for users who could not comply with the GPL.
