The British Rail Class 313 is a class of dual-voltage electric multiple unit passenger trains built by British Rail Engineering Limited (BREL) at York between 1976 and 1977. A total of 64 three-car units were constructed. They were designed for the Great Northern suburban electrification and were notable for their ability to operate both from 25 kV AC overhead lines and from 750 V DC third rail, allowing them to run through the Northern City Line tunnels between Drayton Park and Moorgate, where overhead equipment could not initially be used.

Service history

The units entered service on Great Northern services from Moorgate and King's Cross to Welwyn Garden City and Hertford North. After privatisation they passed to the WAGN franchise and later First Capital Connect and Govia Thameslink Railway's Great Northern operation. Some units transferred to London Overground services on the North London and East London-derived routes, and a fleet moved to Southern for Coastway services between Brighton, Portsmouth and Southampton from 2010.

Withdrawal

The Class 313s were among the oldest electric multiple units in service on the national network. Great Northern withdrew its last units in 2019, replacing them with Class 717 trains, and Southern withdrew its fleet in 2023, replaced by Class 377 units — ending almost 47 years of Class 313 operation. Several vehicles have been preserved or repurposed, and one unit was converted as the Class 316 test train for ETCS signalling trials.
