Texcoco (officially Texcoco de Mora) is a city and municipality in the State of Mexico, Mexico, located about 25 kilometres (16 mi) northeast of Mexico City in the Valley of Mexico. It stands on the eastern shore of the former Lake Texcoco, much of which was drained in the colonial and modern eras.

History

Before the Spanish conquest, Texcoco was one of the great city-states of the Aztec world and the second city of the Aztec Empire as a member of the Triple Alliance with Tenochtitlan and Tlacopan. Under its tlatoani Nezahualcoyotl (reigned 1431–1472), a poet-king and engineer, Texcoco became a centre of law, poetry, and learning, and its engineers built dikes and aqueducts for Tenochtitlan. After the conquest it was an early centre of Franciscan evangelization, and friar Pedro de Gante founded a school there.

Modern era

Today Texcoco is a growing commuter and agricultural city, home to the Chapingo Autonomous University, a leading agricultural university. The dried lakebed east of the city was the site of a controversial new Mexico City airport project, begun in 2015 and cancelled in 2018; part of the area has since been developed as the Lake Texcoco Ecological Park.
