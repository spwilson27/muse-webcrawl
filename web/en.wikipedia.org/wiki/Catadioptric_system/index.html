Catadioptric system

A catadioptric optical system is one that uses both refraction (dioptrics, with lenses) and reflection (catoptrics, with mirrors) to form an image. Such systems, and the telescopes built from them, achieve long focal lengths in short tubes and correct the aberrations of a simple mirror.

Types

The Schmidt–Cassegrain uses an aspheric corrector plate at the front of the tube and a convex secondary mirror; it became the most popular amateur telescope design of the late 20th century. The Maksutov–Cassegrain uses a thick meniscus corrector lens. Other designs include the Schmidt camera, the Argunov and Klevtsov systems, and catadioptric camera lenses (mirror lenses), which fold a long telephoto focal length into a compact, if fixed-aperture, package.

Advantages and drawbacks

Combining mirrors and correctors suppresses coma and spherical aberration over a wide field, and the folded path makes the instrument portable. Drawbacks include the central obstruction from the secondary mirror, which reduces contrast, and, for closed tubes, thermal equilibration time.
