K. Kelappan

Koyapalli Kelappan Nair (24 August 1889 – 7 October 1971), popularly known as K. Kelappan and sometimes called the "Gandhi of Kerala", was an Indian politician, independence activist, educationist and journalist. He was a leading figure of the Indian National Congress in the Malabar region and the founding president of the Nair Service Society's work in Malabar, as well as a prominent social reformer who campaigned against untouchability.

Early life

Kelappan was born in the village of Muchukunnu near Calicut (Kozhikode) in present-day Kerala. He studied in Calicut and Madras and began his career as a teacher. Inspired by Mahatma Gandhi, he abandoned his studies and career to join the non-cooperation movement in the early 1920s, thereafter devoting his life to public work.

Freedom struggle and reform work

Kelappan was one of the leaders of the Vaikom Satyagraha (1924–25), the movement for the right of lower-caste Hindus to use the roads around the Vaikom temple in Travancore. In 1930 he led the salt satyagraha march from Kozhikode to Payyanur in Malabar, modelled on Gandhi's Dandi March. In 1932 he undertook a fast in support of temple entry for all Hindus connected with the Guruvayur Satyagraha, which drew national attention to the cause; the campaign contributed momentum toward the Travancore Temple Entry Proclamation of 1936.

Kelappan served as president of the Kerala Pradesh Congress Committee and was active in constructive work — khadi, village industries, Harijan welfare and basic education. He edited and was associated with the newspaper Mathrubhumi in its early years. After independence he distanced himself from party politics, worked with the Sarvodaya movement, and led campaigns including the movement against the proposed liquor policy and for the development of north Kerala. He died on 7 October 1971.
