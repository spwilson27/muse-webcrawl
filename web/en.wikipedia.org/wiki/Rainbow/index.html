Rainbow

Rainbow
CauseRefraction, dispersion and reflection of sunlight by raindrops
Angular radius (primary bow)42° around the antisolar point
VariantsPrimary, secondary, supernumerary, fog bow, moonbow, circumhorizontal arc

A rainbow is a meteorological phenomenon caused by the reflection, refraction, and dispersion of sunlight in water droplets suspended in the air, resulting in a spectrum of light appearing in the sky. It appears as a multicoloured circular arc centred on the antisolar point — the point directly opposite the Sun in the observer's sky — and is visible when the Sun is behind the observer and rain is falling ahead.

Optics

Sunlight entering a raindrop is refracted at the surface, internally reflected once (for the primary bow), and refracted again as it exits; because the refractive index of water varies slightly with wavelength, the colours separate — red on the outer edge, violet on the inner. A second reflection produces the fainter secondary bow with reversed colours, and interference can create delicate supernumerary arcs just inside the primary bow. Because each observer sees light scattered only from drops along a specific 42° cone, every person sees their own personal rainbow.

Culture

Rainbows have long carried symbolic meaning — from the biblical covenant of Noah to the rainbow flag of the LGBTQ+ movement and the rainbow of Irish legend. The seven-colour scheme of popular accounts comes from Isaac Newton's sevenfold division (red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet), which he made partly to parallel the seven notes of the musical scale.
