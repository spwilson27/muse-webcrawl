Sir Philip Anthony Hopkins (born 1937) is a Welsh actor regarded as one of the greatest actors of his generation, known for his performances on stage and screen.

Career

Trained at RADA and mentored early by Laurence Olivier at the National Theatre, Hopkins gained fame in film with The Lion in Winter (1968) and The Elephant Man (1980). His portrayal of Hannibal Lecter in The Silence of the Lambs (1991) won him the Academy Award for Best Actor and became an iconic screen role.

Later work

Hopkins won a second Best Actor Oscar for The Father (2020), becoming the oldest winner in the category. His other notable films include Howards End, The Remains of the Day, Nixon, Amistad, and the Thor series as Odin. He was knighted in 1993.
