Power trio


A classic three-piece rock line-up

A power trio is a rock and roll band format having a lineup of electric guitar, bass guitar and drums, leaving out the second rhythm guitar or keyboard instrument (such as the Hammond organ) that are used in other four-piece rock bands.

History

The format emerged in the mid-1960s with blues-rock groups such as Cream, whose guitarist Eric Clapton, bassist Jack Bruce and drummer Ginger Baker each took prominent, virtuosic roles. The Jimi Hendrix Experience and Blue Cheer further popularised the stripped-down line-up, which allowed each musician maximum room for improvisation and volume.

Characteristics

Because only one harmonic instrument is present, the guitarist in a power trio typically combines rhythm and lead playing, while the bassist often adopts a more melodic, busy style to fill out the sound. Notable power trios include Rush, Motörhead, ZZ Top, The Police, Nirvana (in its core form) and Primus.
