Rumba

Rumba, also spelled rhumba, is a term for a family of percussive rhythms, songs, and dances that originated in Cuba, as well as a style of ballroom dance and music that developed in the United States and Europe from Cuban son music in the early twentieth century.

Cuban rumba

In Cuba, rumba is a secular genre of music and dance of African origin, developed in the port cities of Havana and Matanzas among Afro-Cuban communities. It is performed with cajón boxes, conga drums (quinto, conga, and tumba), claves, and call-and-response singing. Its three main traditional forms are the slow yambú, the couples dance guaguancó, and the fast, competitive columbia danced solo by men. In 2016, Cuban rumba was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Ballroom rumba

The ballroom rumba, standardized in the 1930s and 1940s, is a slow, romantic couples dance in 4/4 time, derived largely from the Cuban son and bolero-son rather than from folkloric rumba. It is one of the five dances of the International Latin ballroom program. During the 1930s rumba craze, the dance became widely popular in the United States and Europe, popularized by bandleaders such as Xavier Cugat.
