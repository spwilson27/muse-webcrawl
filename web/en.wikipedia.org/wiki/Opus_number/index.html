Opus number

In music, the opus number (abbreviated Op.) is the work number assigned to a musical composition, or to a set of compositions, to indicate the order in which it was published. Opus numbers usually roughly track the order in which compositions were completed, but the assignment was historically inconsistent, especially during the 18th and early 19th centuries.

Usage

The Latin word opus means "work". Publishers began assigning opus numbers systematically around the turn of the 19th century; Beethoven's works, for example, were mostly published with opus numbers during his lifetime, and the numbering is fairly reliable for his career. Works published posthumously may be marked "Op. posth." Works without opus numbers are often catalogued with other systems, such as "WoO" (Werke ohne Opuszahl) for Beethoven, "BWV" for Bach, "K." for Mozart, "D" for Schubert, and "Hob." for Haydn.

Modern practice

Since about 1900, opus numbers have fallen out of common use: unique titles and composition dates are usually preferred to identify works, and numberings within genres (e.g., "Symphony No. 5") serve a similar purpose.
