Easter Bunny

The Easter Bunny (also called the Easter Rabbit or Easter Hare) is a folkloric figure and symbol of Easter, depicted as a rabbit — sometimes dressed with clothes — bringing Easter eggs. Originating among German Lutherans, the "Easter Hare" (Osterhase) originally played the role of a judge, evaluating whether children were good or disobedient in behaviour, bearing coloured eggs and sometimes candy or toys for well-behaved children.

Origins and customs

The custom was first mentioned in Georg Franck von Franckenau's De ovis paschalibus ("About Easter eggs") in 1682. German immigrants brought the tradition to Pennsylvania in the 18th century, where it spread across the United States. Children often leave out carrots for the bunny, build nests for it to lay its eggs in, and take part in egg hunts for the eggs it has hidden.

Hares and rabbits have long been symbols of fertility and rebirth in spring festivals, and the egg is likewise an ancient symbol of new life, making both natural emblems of the Easter season.
