Soo Locks
Location: Sault Ste. Marie, Michigan, USA
Waterway: St. Marys River
Operator: U.S. Army Corps of Engineers
First opened: 1855 (state locks)

The Soo Locks (sometimes spelled Sault Locks) are a set of parallel ship locks on the St. Marys River at Sault Ste. Marie, Michigan, enabling vessels to travel between Lake Superior and the lower Great Lakes. They bypass the rapids where the river drops about 21 feet (6.4 m).

History

The first lock on the American side, the State Lock, was completed in 1855, allowing iron ore and copper from the Lake Superior region to reach industrial markets. Successively larger locks followed, including the Poe Lock (rebuilt 1968) and the MacArthur Lock (1943). The locks are considered one of the world's busiest and most strategically important shipping passages; a large share of the United States' iron ore and taconite passes through them.

Operation

The locks operate toll-free and are maintained by the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers. They close during winter, typically from late January to late March, when ice covers the upper lakes. A Canadian lock on the Ontario side serves mainly recreational and tour boats. Construction of a long-planned new large lock, begun in recent years, is intended to provide redundancy for the single Poe Lock capable of handling the largest "footers" (1,000-foot vessels).
