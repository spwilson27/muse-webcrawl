Abū Jaʿfar Muḥammad ibn Jarīr ibn Yazīd al-Ṭabarī (839–923) was a Persian historian and scholar from Amol, Tabaristan, and one of the most prolific and influential authors of the Islamic Golden Age. He is best known for two monumental works: the History of the Prophets and Kings (Tarikh al-Rusul wa al-Muluk), a universal history from Creation to 915 CE, and his Quran commentary, the Tafsir al-Tabari, foundational for later exegesis.

Life

A prodigy who reportedly memorised the Quran by seven, al-Tabari studied in Rayy, Baghdad, Syria and Egypt before settling in Baghdad, where he taught and wrote for the rest of his life, declining official posts. Towards the end of his life he faced hostility from Hanbali partisans over doctrinal disputes, and he died in 923.

Works

The History assembles earlier reports with their chains of transmission (isnad), covering biblical and Persian antiquity, the life of Muhammad, the caliphates, and events down to his own century; its English translation runs to 39 volumes. The Tafsir likewise gathers transmitted interpretations verse by verse. Al-Tabari also wrote extensively on law, founding a school (the Jaririyya) that survived a few centuries, and was credited by biographers with writing dozens of pages daily for forty years.
