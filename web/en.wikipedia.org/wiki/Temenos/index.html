Temenos

A temenos (Greek: τέμενος, plural temene) is a piece of land cut off and assigned as an official domain, or a sacred precinct dedicated to a god. The word derives from the verb temnō ("I cut"). In ancient Greece, the temenos was the walled or marked-off enclosure surrounding a temple or shrine, within which sacred law applied and profane activities were excluded.

Greek sanctuaries

Major sanctuaries such as Olympia, Delphi and Delos were organised as temene containing the temple, altars, treasuries, stoas and votive monuments. Boundary stones (horoi) marked the precinct, and entering in a state of ritual impurity was forbidden. The concept has precedents in Near Eastern sacred enclosures, such as the temple precincts of Mesopotamia and Egypt.

Other uses

In Homer, temenos could also denote a royal estate granted to a king or hero. Carl Jung later borrowed the term in analytical psychology for a protected, sacred inner space in which psychological work and individuation can occur — a usage echoed by the artist and writer circles that adopted the word, including the journal Temenos founded by Kathleen Raine.
