Entr'acte

Entr'acte
LiterallyFrench: "between the acts"
ContextsTheatre, opera, ballet, musical theatre
RelatedInterlude, intermezzo, overture

An entr'acte (French for "between the acts") is a piece of music performed between the acts of a play, opera or musical, during the intermission. In theatre practice the term also covers short entertainments — songs, dances or comic turns — offered while scenery was changed or the audience took refreshments.

History

In 17th- and 18th-century theatre, entr'acte entertainments were substantial attractions in their own right and were advertised on playbills. In opera, orchestral interludes between acts became integral to the drama, as in the intermezzi of verismo operas or the entr'acte music of Bizet's Carmen.

In musical theatre

Broadway and West End musicals traditionally open their second act with an entr'acte — an orchestral medley of the show's tunes played as the audience returns to its seats, a practice formalised in the era of Rodgers and Hammerstein.
