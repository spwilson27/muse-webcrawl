United Airlines Flight 93

United Airlines Flight 93 was a domestic scheduled passenger flight that was hijacked by four al-Qaeda terrorists on 11 September 2001, as part of the September 11 attacks. The Boeing 757-222, flying from Newark International Airport in New Jersey to San Francisco International Airport, was taken over about 46 minutes after takeoff. Unlike the other three hijacked aircraft that day, Flight 93 did not reach its intended target — believed by investigators to be the United States Capitol or the White House in Washington, D.C. — because passengers and crew, having learned by phone of the other attacks, fought back against the hijackers. The aircraft crashed into a field near Shanksville, Pennsylvania, killing all 44 people on board.

The hijacking

Flight 93 departed Newark at 8:42 a.m., 25 minutes late, with 37 passengers (including the four hijackers) and a crew of seven. At 9:28 a.m., as the aircraft cruised over Ohio, the hijackers stormed the cockpit, killing or incapacitating the pilots. The lead hijacker, Ziad Jarrah, took the controls and turned the plane southeast toward Washington.

Passenger revolt

Passengers and flight attendants phoning family members and emergency services learned that airliners had been flown into the World Trade Center and the Pentagon. Realising their flight was part of the same plot, the passengers voted to resist. At about 9:57 a.m. they rushed the cockpit. Cockpit voice recordings captured sounds of the struggle; the hijackers rocked the aircraft and finally pitched it into a steep dive. At 10:03 a.m. Flight 93 crashed inverted at 563 mph into a reclaimed coal mine field near Shanksville, in Somerset County, Pennsylvania, about 20 minutes' flying time from Washington, D.C.

Aftermath and memorial

The 9/11 Commission concluded that the passengers' actions saved the intended target and many lives. The phrase "Let's roll", spoken by passenger Todd Beamer as overheard by a phone operator, became a national rallying cry. The crash site is preserved as the Flight 93 National Memorial, administered by the National Park Service; its features include the Wall of Names, a memorial plaza, and the 93-foot Tower of Voices, dedicated in 2018. The story was dramatised in the 2006 film United 93.
