The Kurdistan Regional Government (KRG; Kurdish: حکومەتی هەرێمی کوردستان) is the official governing body of the Kurdistan Region, the autonomous federal region of Iraq comprising the governorates of Erbil, Sulaymaniyah, Dohuk, and Halabja. Recognized by Iraq's 2005 constitution, the KRG exercises broad self-government, including its own parliament, ministries, and security forces, the Peshmerga.

History

Effective Kurdish self-rule began after the 1991 Gulf War, when Allied no-fly zones protected the region from Saddam Hussein's forces and Kurdish parties held elections in 1992. Rivalry between the Kurdistan Democratic Party (KDP) and the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan (PUK) erupted into civil war from 1994 to 1998, after which the two parties maintained separate administrations until a unified KRG cabinet was formed in 2006. The 2003 Iraq War and the subsequent constitution formalized the Kurdistan Region's federal status.

Government and politics

The KRG is headed by a president, while executive authority rests chiefly with the prime minister and cabinet. The Kurdistan Region Parliament, seated in Erbil, has 100 seats (reduced from 111), including quota seats for Turkmen, Christian, and Armenian components. The KDP and PUK remain the dominant parties and have shared power in coalition cabinets.

Economy and foreign relations

The region's economy depends heavily on oil exported via pipeline through Turkey, alongside agriculture, construction, and trade. The KRG maintains representative offices abroad and hosts numerous consulates in Erbil. Its relations with Baghdad are frequently strained by disputes over oil revenue, budget transfers, and the status of disputed territories such as Kirkuk.

2017 referendum

In September 2017 the KRG held an independence referendum in which over 90 percent of voters backed secession. Baghdad, neighboring states, and Western governments rejected the result; Iraqi federal forces retook Kirkuk and other disputed areas the following month, and the KRG's president Masoud Barzani stepped down, resetting relations with the federal government.
