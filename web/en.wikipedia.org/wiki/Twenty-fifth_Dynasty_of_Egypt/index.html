Twenty-fifth Dynasty of Egypt

The Twenty-fifth Dynasty of Egypt (c. 747–656 BC), also known as the Nubian Dynasty or the Kushite Empire, was a line of pharaohs who originated in the Kingdom of Kush, in what is now northern Sudan and southern Egypt. The Kushite kings conquered Egypt and ruled a unified empire stretching from Khartoum to the Mediterranean during the Third Intermediate Period.

Rise

The Kushite kingdom centered on Napata had long-standing religious ties to the cult of Amun at Jebel Barkal. King Kashta extended Kushite influence into Upper Egypt, and his son Piye (Piankhy) campaigned north around 727 BC, defeating a coalition of Delta rulers and recording his victory on the famous Victory Stela. Piye's successor Shabaka consolidated Kushite rule over the whole of Egypt.

Rule and culture

The Kushite pharaohs presented themselves as restorers of Egypt's ancient traditions: they revived pyramid burial (at el-Kurru and Nuri in Nubia), archaic art styles, and the office of God's Wife of Amun at Thebes, held by royal women such as Amenirdis I. Shebitku and Taharqa — the best-known king of the dynasty, mentioned in the Hebrew Bible — undertook major building at Karnak and elsewhere.

Conflict with Assyria and fall

Taharqa's involvement in the Levant brought Egypt into conflict with the Neo-Assyrian Empire. Esarhaddon invaded in 671 BC and captured Memphis; Ashurbanipal later drove Taharqa's successor Tantamani south, and Assyrian forces sacked Thebes in 663 BC. The Assyrian-backed Saite princes of the Twenty-sixth Dynasty ended Kushite rule in Egypt by about 656 BC, though the Kingdom of Kush endured in Nubia for another thousand years.
