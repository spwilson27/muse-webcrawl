Agroforestry

Agroforestry (or agro-sylviculture) is a land use management system that integrates trees with crops or livestock on the same plot of land. It combines agricultural and forestry technologies to create more diverse, productive, profitable, healthy, and sustainable land-use systems than monoculture farming. The World Agroforestry Centre (now part of CIFOR-ICRAF), founded in 1978 in Nairobi, Kenya, is the leading international research body for the discipline.

Systems

Major categories include alley cropping, in which crops grow between rows of trees; silvopasture, combining trees with grazing livestock; forest farming, cultivating high-value crops such as mushrooms or medicinal plants under a forest canopy; windbreaks and shelterbelts that protect fields and farmsteads; and riparian buffer strips that filter runoff along waterways. In the tropics, multistorey home gardens and shaded coffee and cacao plantations are widespread forms.

Benefits

Tree roots cycle nutrients from deep soil layers, leguminous trees fix atmospheric nitrogen, and leaf litter builds organic matter, reducing erosion and fertiliser needs. Agroforestry plots store more carbon than cropland alone, support greater biodiversity, and spread farmers' economic risk across timber, fruit, fodder, and annual crops. Challenges include the long wait for tree products to mature, competition between trees and crops for light and water, and land-tenure arrangements that discourage planting trees.
