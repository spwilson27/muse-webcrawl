1931 Hawke's Bay earthquake

The 1931 Hawke's Bay earthquake, also known as the Napier earthquake, struck New Zealand at 10:47 a.m. local time on 3 February 1931, measuring 7.8 on the Richter scale (surface-wave magnitude 7.7).

Damage and casualties

The earthquake devastated the Hawke's Bay region, including the towns of Napier and Hastings, killing 256 people—remaining New Zealand's deadliest natural disaster. Fires following the quake destroyed much of Napier's business district. The seabed was uplifted, draining the Ahuriri Lagoon and creating about 40 km² of new land.

Rebuilding

Napier was rebuilt in the then-fashionable Art Deco style, making it a noted architectural destination. The disaster led to New Zealand's first building codes for earthquake resistance.
