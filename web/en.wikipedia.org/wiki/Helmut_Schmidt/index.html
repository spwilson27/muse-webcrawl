Helmut Schmidt

Helmut Heinrich Waldemar Schmidt (23 December 1918 – 10 November 2015) was a German politician and member of the Social Democratic Party of Germany (SPD) who served as Chancellor of West Germany from 1974 to 1982. A pragmatic manager — his nickname in the press was "Macher" (doer) — Schmidt steered the Federal Republic through the oil crises, the terrorism of the Red Army Faction, and the opening phase of the Euromissile crisis, while helping to create the European Monetary System and the G7 summits. Before becoming Chancellor he had won national fame directing the response to the Hamburg flood of 1962 and served as minister of defence and of finance.

Early life

Schmidt was born in Hamburg, the son of schoolteachers; his paternal grandfather was Jewish, a fact concealed by the family in the Nazi era. He served as an anti-aircraft officer on the Eastern Front, was captured by the British in 1945, studied economics at Hamburg, joined the SPD in 1946, and entered the Bundestag in 1953. As Hamburg's senator (minister) for the interior he took command of the rescue effort during the catastrophic North Sea flood of February 1962, which killed 315 people in the city, cementing his reputation for decisive crisis management.

Minister and Chancellor

In the SPD–FDP governments of Willy Brandt, Schmidt served successively as minister of defence (1969–1972), minister of economics and finance, and minister of finance (1972–1974). When Brandt resigned after the Guillaume espionage affair, Schmidt became Chancellor on 16 May 1974. His chancellorship faced the aftermath of the 1973 oil shock and recession, the "German Autumn" of 1977 — when the RAF murdered industrialist Hanns Martin Schleyer and hijacked the Lufthansa airliner Landshut, which Schmidt ordered stormed at Mogadishu — and stagflation. Internationally he co-founded the G7 (first summit at Rambouillet, 1975), created the European Monetary System with French president Valéry Giscard d'Estaing (1979), and drove NATO's 1979 dual-track decision on intermediate-range missiles, which split his own party. When the FDP switched to the CDU in 1982, Schmidt lost a constructive vote of no confidence to Helmut Kohl on 1 October 1982 — the only such successful vote in the Federal Republic's history, succeeding where Rainer Barzel had failed in 1972.

Later life

Schmidt left the Bundestag in 1986 and became co-editor of the weekly Die Zeit, writing widely read books on global affairs and remaining Germany's most respected elder statesman until his death in Hamburg in 2015, aged 96. He received a Staatsakt (state ceremony) in Hamburg, attended by leaders from across Europe.
