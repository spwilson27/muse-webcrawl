Harvey Bernard Milk (May 22, 1930 – November 27, 1978) was an American politician and the first openly gay man to be elected to public office in California, as a member of the San Francisco Board of Supervisors. A leading figure in the gay rights movement, Milk became an icon and martyr for the LGBT community following his assassination in 1978.

Early life

Milk was born in Woodmere, New York, to a Lithuanian Jewish family. He graduated from New York State College for Teachers at Albany in 1951 and served as an officer in the United States Navy during the Korean War. After leaving the Navy he worked as a schoolteacher, an insurance analyst, and on Wall Street. In the late 1960s he became involved in countercultural circles and worked on the Broadway production of Hair.

San Francisco and Castro Street

In 1972 Milk moved to San Francisco and settled in the Castro District, then becoming the centre of a growing gay community. He opened a camera shop, Castro Camera, which became a neighbourhood hub. Frustrated by government indifference, Milk ran three unsuccessful campaigns for office before winning election to the Board of Supervisors in 1977, following the city's switch to district elections. Known as the "Mayor of Castro Street," he built coalitions across unions, seniors, and ethnic communities.

Time in office

During his eleven months as supervisor, Milk sponsored and passed a landmark gay rights ordinance banning discrimination in employment, housing, and public accommodation. He also led the successful campaign against the Briggs Initiative (Proposition 6), which would have banned gay teachers in California schools. His "Hope Speech," urging young LGBT people to come out, became one of the most famous speeches of the movement.

Assassination and legacy

On November 27, 1978, Milk and Mayor George Moscone were shot and killed in City Hall by Dan White, a former supervisor. White's conviction on the lesser charge of voluntary manslaughter sparked the White Night riots of May 1979. Milk was posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2009; his life was portrayed by Sean Penn in the Oscar-winning film Milk (2008). The U.S. Navy named a replenishment oiler, USNS Harvey Milk, after him in 2016.
