An anemometer is an instrument that measures wind speed; some designs also measure wind pressure or direction. It is a standard instrument at weather stations and is also used in aviation, sailing, HVAC engineering and wind-energy assessment. The name derives from the Greek anemos, "wind".

Types

Cup anemometers

The most familiar type, invented in 1846 by John Thomas Romney Robinson of Armagh Observatory, consists of three or four hemispherical cups mounted on horizontal arms rotating about a vertical axis. The difference in drag between the hollow and convex sides of the cups makes the assembly spin at a rate proportional to wind speed, counted mechanically or electrically. Robinson wrongly assumed the cups moved at one third of the wind speed; later calibration corrected the factor, which varies with design.

Other designs

Vane (propeller) anemometers combine a fan-like propeller with a tail vane that keeps it facing the wind. Hot-wire anemometers infer speed from the cooling of an electrically heated fine wire, responding quickly to gusts and turbulence. Ultrasonic anemometers measure the difference in transit time of sound pulses between transducer pairs and have no moving parts. Pitot-tube and pressure-plate instruments measure wind pressure, while laser Doppler and sonic-resonator designs serve research and specialist applications.

Use and standards

The World Meteorological Organization standard requires wind measurements at a height of 10 metres over open ground. Airspeed indicators on aircraft use pitot-static systems calibrated as anemometers. Handheld anemometers are common among sailors, drone operators, crane operators and outdoor-event organisers for safety monitoring.
