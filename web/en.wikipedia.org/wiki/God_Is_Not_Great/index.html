AuthorChristopher Hitchens
Published2007
SubjectAtheism; criticism of religion

God Is Not Great: How Religion Poisons Everything is a 2007 book by the British-American author and journalist Christopher Hitchens, in which he argues that organised religion is violent, irrational and intolerant, allied to racism and tribalism, hostile to free inquiry, and contemptuous of women and children. Its title inverts the Islamic phrase Allahu Akbar ("God is great"). The book became an international bestseller and, together with works by Richard Dawkins, Sam Harris and Daniel Dennett, was a central text of the "New Atheism" movement.

Contents

Hitchens contends that religion is man-made, that its scriptures are inconsistent and historically unreliable, and that supernatural belief has repeatedly obstructed scientific progress and medical advances. He examines the major world faiths — including Christianity, Islam, Judaism, Hinduism and Buddhism — and argues case by case that religious institutions have supported war, censorship, slavery and the abuse of children. He also discusses his own experience of religion from childhood, criticises the veneration of figures such as Mother Teresa, and argues that secular humanism provides a sufficient basis for ethics.

Reception

The book was shortlisted for a National Book Award and sold in large numbers, but it also drew substantial criticism. Theologians and religious commentators accused Hitchens of selective history and rhetorical excess, and several book-length responses were published. Debates between Hitchens and religious opponents became a notable part of the book's public life until his death in 2011.
