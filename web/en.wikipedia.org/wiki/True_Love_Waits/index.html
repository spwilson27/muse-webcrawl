True Love Waits

True Love Waits is an international Christian abstinence program, founded in 1993 by the Southern Baptist Convention's Sunday School Board (now Lifeway Christian Resources), that promotes sexual abstinence before marriage among adolescents and young adults. Participants sign pledge cards committing to abstinence, often in conjunction with purity rings and related ceremonies.

History

The campaign was conceived by youth ministry worker Jimmy Hester and launched at a 1993 conference in Nashville. In July 1994 the movement famously displayed 210,000 signed pledge cards on the National Mall in Washington, D.C., an event that brought national media attention. At its peak, organizers claimed more than two million pledges worldwide, and the program spread through affiliated campaigns in Africa, Asia and Latin America.

Criticism and research

Sociological studies of virginity pledging, including the well-known analyses by Peter Bearman and Hannah Brückner of the National Longitudinal Study of Adolescent Health, found that pledging could delay first intercourse modestly in some social contexts but that pledgers who did become sexually active were less likely to use contraception. Critics such as journalist Sarah Posner and former supporters have argued that abstinence-only programs are ineffective and can spread misinformation; defenders maintain the campaign reinforced parental and church teaching about sexuality.
