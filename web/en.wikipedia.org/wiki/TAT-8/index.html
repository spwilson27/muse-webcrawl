TAT-8

Type: Submarine fiber-optic cable

Route: United States – United Kingdom / France

In service: 1988

Retired: 2002

Capacity: 280 Mbit/s (TASI-equipped, ~40,000 calls)

TAT-8 was the eighth transatlantic communications cable and the first transatlantic fiber-optic cable. Entering service in 1988, it linked Tuckerton, New Jersey in the United States with Widemouth Bay in England and Penmarch in France. It was developed by a consortium led by AT&T, France Télécom, and British Telecom at a cost of about US$335 million.

Technology

Unlike the coaxial cables that preceded it, TAT-8 carried signals as pulses of light through optical fibers, using repeaters spaced roughly 50–70 km apart. The system operated at 280 Mbit/s; with digital circuit multiplication equipment (time-assignment speech interpolation, TASI), it could carry the equivalent of some 40,000 simultaneous telephone calls — far more than the last coaxial transatlantic cable, TAT-7.

Significance

TAT-8 demonstrated that fiber optics could cross an ocean reliably, and its capacity helped fuel the rapid growth of international data traffic, including the early Internet. When the cable failed in 1991, the resulting disruption drew wide attention to the importance of undersea cables. The system was retired in 2002 as successive generations of fiber cables (TAT-12/13 and beyond) brought vastly greater capacity.
