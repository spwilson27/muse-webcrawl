National Incident Management System

The National Incident Management System (NIMS) is a standardised approach to incident management in the United States, developed by the Department of Homeland Security and released in 2004. It provides a common framework for federal, state, local, tribal and private-sector responders to work together during emergencies.

Components

NIMS builds on the Incident Command System (ICS) and includes resource management, command and coordination structures such as Emergency Operations Centers, and communications and information management based on interoperability and common terminology.

Adoption

Adoption of NIMS was made a condition of federal preparedness funding after the September 11 attacks highlighted problems in multi-agency coordination.
