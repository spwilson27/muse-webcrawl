Shen Kuo

Born: 1031, Hangzhou, Song China
Died: 1095 (aged 63–64), Zhenjiang
Known for: Dream Pool Essays; magnetic declination; polymathic science

Shen Kuo (Chinese: 沈括; 1031–1095), courtesy name Cunzhong, was a Chinese polymath of the Song dynasty — scientist, engineer, astronomer, geologist, diplomat, general, finance minister and poet. His Dream Pool Essays (夢溪筆談, Mengxi Bitan, c. 1088) is one of the most remarkable scientific works of the pre-modern world.

Contributions

Among many firsts, Shen was the first to describe the magnetic needle compass for navigation and to discover magnetic declination — that the needle points somewhat east of true north — centuries before Europe. He described Bi Sheng's movable type printing, improved the armillary sphere, gnomon and water clock, formulated a theory of land formation from marine fossils and erosion (recognizing that fossil shells in mountains implied ancient seas and a changing climate), hypothesized that the sun and moon are spherical and explained eclipses and rainbows, and advanced mathematics including summation of higher series and spherical trigonometry. His relief-map and canal engineering (including his survey work using raised dikes and a new type of sluice) complemented an official career that included directing the Bureau of Astronomy, negotiating with the Liao dynasty, and military command on the western frontier.

Later life

Implicated in factional politics, Shen was demoted and spent his final years in semi-retirement at his Dream Brook estate near Zhenjiang, where he completed the Essays. Joseph Needham regarded him as one of the greatest scientific minds in Chinese history.
