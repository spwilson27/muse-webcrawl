Union Cycliste Internationale

The Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI; French for "International Cycling Union") is the world governing body for sports cycling and oversees international competitive cycling events. Founded on 14 April 1900 in Paris by the national federations of Belgium, France, Italy, Switzerland, and the United States, it is headquartered at the World Cycling Centre in Aigle, Switzerland, and is recognised by the International Olympic Committee.

Role

The UCI issues licences, writes the rules of competition, maintains world rankings and calendars—including the UCI WorldTour for elite men's road racing and the Women's WorldTour—and organises the annual World Championships in its disciplines. It is responsible for anti-doping in cycling (administered with the International Testing Agency since 2021) and for technical regulations, famously including the 6.8 kg minimum bicycle weight and rules on frame shapes and rider positions.

History

The UCI replaced the earlier International Cycling Association in 1900. For much of the 20th century amateur and professional racing were administered by separate sub-federations (FIAC and FICP) under the UCI umbrella; they were merged into the UCI proper in 1992. The organisation has faced persistent controversy over doping, including its handling of the Lance Armstrong era, which led to the USADA sanctions of 2012 and later governance reforms.

Rainbow jersey

World champions in UCI disciplines wear the distinctive rainbow jersey—white with green, yellow, black, red and blue bands—while competing in that discipline for the following year.
