Treaty of Brest-Litovsk

The Treaty of Brest-Litovsk was a peace treaty signed on 3 March 1918 at Brest-Litovsk (now Brest, Belarus) between the Bolshevik government of Soviet Russia and the Central Powers—Germany, Austria-Hungary, Bulgaria, and the Ottoman Empire—ending Russia's participation in the First World War.

Negotiations

After the October Revolution, Russia sued for peace. Leon Trotsky, leading the Soviet delegation after Adolph Joffe, pursued a policy of "neither war nor peace," walking out in February 1918. Germany responded with Operation Faustschlag, an advance that met little resistance, forcing the Bolsheviks led by Lenin to accept far harsher terms than those first offered. Lenin argued that preserving the revolution outweighed territorial losses, over bitter opposition from the Left Socialist Revolutionaries and the "Left Communists" around Bukharin.

Terms

Russia ceded vast territories: the Baltic states to Germany, Kars and Batum to the Ottoman Empire, and recognized the independence of Ukraine, with which the Central Powers had signed a separate "Bread Peace" on 9 February 1918. In all, Russia lost roughly a third of its population, half its industry, and most of its coal mines.

Aftermath

The treaty freed German divisions for the 1918 Spring Offensive in the west but was annulled by the Armistice of 11 November 1918 and formally by the Treaty of Versailles. Soviet Russia annulled it on 13 November 1918 as German power collapsed.
