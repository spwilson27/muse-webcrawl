The Invincibles (football)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Arsenal's 2003–04 "Invincibles" squad with the Premier League trophy]

The Invincibles is a nickname given to football teams that complete a league season undefeated. In English football the term is most famously associated with two sides: Preston North End of 1888–89 and Arsenal of 2003–04.

Preston North End, 1888–89

In the inaugural season of the Football League, Preston North End won the championship without losing a match (18 wins, 4 draws) and also won the FA Cup without conceding a goal, completing the first "Double". Managed by William Sudell, the team included many Scottish professionals.

Arsenal, 2003–04

Arsène Wenger's Arsenal won the 2003–04 Premier League with 26 wins and 12 draws, the only unbeaten league campaign in the Premier League era. Key players included Thierry Henry, Patrick Vieira, Robert Pires, and Sol Campbell. The run eventually extended to a record 49 league matches unbeaten before defeat at Manchester United in October 2004. The Premier League awarded Arsenal a special gold replica trophy to mark the achievement.

Other Invincibles

Unbeaten league seasons have also been achieved by clubs including Celtic (2016–17), Juventus (2011–12), AC Milan (1991–92), Ajax (1994–95), and Bayer Leverkusen (2023–24).
