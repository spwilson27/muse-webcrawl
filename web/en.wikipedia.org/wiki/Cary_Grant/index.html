Cary Grant

Cary Grant (born Archibald Alec Leach; 18 January 1904 – 29 November 1986) was an English-American actor. Known for his debonair demeanour, light-hearted approach to acting, and sense of comic timing, he was one of classic Hollywood's definitive leading men.

Early life

Grant was born in Horfield, Bristol, to a troubled family. He joined the Bob Pender stage troupe as a stilt walker and toured the United States, deciding to stay there and pursue a career on stage and in vaudeville.

Career

After signing with Paramount, he adopted the name Cary Grant. He rose to fame in screwball comedies such as The Awful Truth (1937), Bringing Up Baby (1938), His Girl Friday (1940) and The Philadelphia Story (1940). He became a favourite of director Alfred Hitchcock, starring in Suspicion (1941), Notorious (1946), To Catch a Thief (1955) and North by Northwest (1959).

Later life and legacy

Grant retired from acting in 1966 after Walk, Don't Run. He received an Honorary Academy Award in 1970 and the Kennedy Center Honors in 1981. The American Film Institute ranked him second on its list of the greatest male stars of Golden Age Hollywood, behind Humphrey Bogart.
