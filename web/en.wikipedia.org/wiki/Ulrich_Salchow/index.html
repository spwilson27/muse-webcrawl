Ulrich Salchow

Karl Emil Julius Ulrich Salchow (7 August 1877 – 19 April 1949) was a Swedish figure skater, who dominated the sport in the first decade of the 20th century. He won the first Olympic title in men's figure skating, at the 1908 Summer Olympics in London, and was World Champion a record ten times (1901–1905 and 1907–1911). The Salchow jump, which he invented in 1909, is named after him and remains a standard element in figure skating.

Born: 7 August 1877, Copenhagen, Denmark
Died: 19 April 1949, Stockholm, Sweden
Country: Sweden
Titles: Olympic champion 1908; 10× World champion; 9× European champion

Career

Born in Copenhagen to Swedish parents and raised in Stockholm, Salchow won his first Swedish and Nordic titles as a teenager. His decade of world dominance was built on mastery of compulsory figures and athletic free skating. At the 1908 Olympics — where figure skating debuted as a summer sport — he won gold ahead of fellow Swedes Richard Johansson and Per Thorén. His reign included a famous controversy at the 1906 European Championships and a disputed 1910 World title won over Gilbert Fuchs-era competition; altogether he also took nine European titles.

The Salchow jump

In 1909 Salchow introduced the jump that bears his name: taking off from the back inside edge of one skate and landing on the back outside edge of the opposite foot, rotating in the air. Singles, doubles, triples and quadruples are performed today; the quadruple Salchow is a staple of elite men's skating.

Later life

After retiring, Salchow worked as a sports administrator and journalist, serving as president of the International Skating Union from 1925 to 1937 and helping to organize speed skating in Sweden. He also competed in sailing. He died in Stockholm in 1949 and was inducted into the World Figure Skating Hall of Fame in 1976.
