Communist Party of Malaya

The Communist Party of Malaya (CPM; Malay: Parti Komunis Malaya), also known as the Malayan Communist Party (MCP), was a political party and resistance movement in Malaya and Singapore. Founded in 1930, it led the Malayan Peoples' Anti-Japanese Army during the Second World War, then waged the guerrilla war of the Malayan Emergency (1948–1960) against British colonial rule, and a second, lower-level insurgency until signing a peace agreement with the governments of Malaysia and Thailand in 1989.

History

The party emerged in 1930 from earlier communist organisations sponsored by the Comintern, drawing much of its membership from the ethnic Chinese community of Malaya and Singapore. It organised labour unions and strikes during the Great Depression and built a clandestine network despite colonial suppression.

Japanese occupation

After Japan invaded Malaya in December 1941, the CPM formed the Malayan Peoples' Anti-Japanese Army (MPAJA), the largest armed resistance force in the peninsula. It cooperated with the British Force 136, receiving training and supplies, and emerged from the war with considerable prestige and stockpiled weapons.

The Malayan Emergency

Post-war disputes over demobilisation rewards and labour unrest pushed the party towards armed struggle. In June 1948, after the killing of European planters at Sungai Siput, the British declared a state of emergency and banned the party. The CPM's armed wing, later called the Malayan National Liberation Army, fought from jungle bases while the authorities resettled rural Chinese villagers into guarded "New Villages" to cut off supplies. The Emergency was declared over in 1960, though most fighting had ended years earlier.

Final years

Under Chin Peng, the party maintained camps along the Thai border and fought a second insurgency from 1968, before internal splits and changing regional politics weakened it. On 2 December 1989 the CPM signed the Hat Yai Peace Agreement with Malaysia and Thailand, formally ending its armed struggle after more than four decades.
