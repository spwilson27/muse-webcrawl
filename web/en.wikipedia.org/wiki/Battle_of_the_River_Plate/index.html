The Battle of the River Plate was a naval battle in the South Atlantic on 13 December 1939, the first naval battle of the Second World War. The German heavy cruiser Admiral Graf Spee, a commerce raider, was engaged by a Royal Navy squadron under Commodore Henry Harwood composed of the cruisers HMS Exeter, HMS Ajax and the New Zealand cruiser HMNZS Achilles.

The battle

Harwood, anticipating that the raider would strike the busy shipping lanes off the River Plate estuary, concentrated his force there. On the morning of 13 December the squadrons met. Harwood split his force to divide the German ship's fire. Exeter was heavily damaged and forced to withdraw to the Falkland Islands, and Ajax and Achilles were also hit, but Graf Spee sustained damage that her captain, Hans Langsdorff, judged serious enough to put into the neutral port of Montevideo, Uruguay, for repairs.

Montevideo and scuttling

Under international law the ship was limited to 72 hours. British diplomacy and a deception campaign convinced Langsdorff that a greatly superior force, including the carrier Ark Royal and the battlecruiser Renown, awaited him offshore — in fact reinforcements were still days away. On 17 December Langsdorff sailed Graf Spee out of Montevideo and scuttled her in the estuary before a huge crowd of spectators. He shot himself in Buenos Aires two days later.

Aftermath

The battle boosted Allied morale early in the war and removed a dangerous raider that had sunk nine merchant ships. The engagement was dramatised in the 1956 film The Battle of the River Plate.
