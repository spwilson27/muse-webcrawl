General Post Office

The General Post Office (GPO) was the state postal system and telecommunications carrier of the United Kingdom for most of its history. Established in 1660 under Charles II, it held a monopoly over postal services and later operated the national telephone network, the Post Office Savings Bank, and Girobank.

History

The GPO was created by the Post Office Act of 1660, establishing a general post office for England and Wales; services were extended across Great Britain and Ireland and eventually the British Empire. In 1840 it introduced the Uniform Penny Post and the world's first adhesive postage stamp, the Penny Black, following reforms by Rowland Hill.

Telecommunications

The GPO took over private telegraph companies in 1870 and built the state telephone network after nationalizing the National Telephone Company in 1912. In wartime it ran vital research establishments, including the Post Office Research Station at Dollis Hill, where engineers built the Colossus codebreaking computers for Bletchley Park.

Dissolution

The Post Office Act 1969 converted the GPO from a government department into a public corporation, the Post Office. Telecommunications were split off as British Telecommunications in 1981, an early step toward the privatization of British Telecom in 1984. The name "General Post Office" also survives as the title of landmark post office buildings in cities such as Dublin, Sydney and Hong Kong.
