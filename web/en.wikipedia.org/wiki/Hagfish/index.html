Hagfish

Scientific classification
Class: Myxini
Order: Myxiniformes
Family: Myxinidae
Species: ~76 known

Hagfish are eel-shaped, slime-producing marine animals in the class Myxini, among the only known living animals with a skull but no vertebral column. Together with lampreys they are jawless fish (cyclostomes).

Anatomy and slime

Hagfish have cartilaginous skulls, barbels around the mouth, no jaws or paired fins, and degenerate eyes. When threatened, glands along the body expel proteins that expand into vast quantities of slime, clogging predators' gills; hagfish can tie themselves in knots to scrape slime off.

Feeding and ecology

They burrow in mud on the deep sea floor, feed on invertebrates, and scavenge dead and dying fish, entering carcasses and absorbing nutrients sometimes through the skin.

Evolution and humans

Fossils show hagfish have changed little for ~300 million years. They are fished for food in Korea and for their skin, sold as 'eel leather'.
