Dievturi

The Dievturi are adherents of Dievturība, a modern Baltic neopagan movement that claims to be a reconstruction of the ethnic religion of the Latvians before Christianisation in the 13th century. The name derives from Latvian Dievs ("God") and turēt ("to hold" or "uphold"), so a Dievturis is literally a "God-upholder".

History

Dievturība was founded in the 1920s by the Latvian writer and artist Ernests Brastiņš, who drew on Latvian folklore — above all the dainas, short folk songs rich in mythological imagery — to construct a Latvian national faith. The movement was suppressed during the Soviet occupation of Latvia, when many of its leaders were deported or imprisoned, but survived in exile among Latvian émigré communities in the West. After Latvia regained independence in 1991, Dievturība revived at home and was registered as a religious organisation.

Beliefs and practice

Dievturi theology centres on Dievs, the sky god of Latvian mythology, within a wider pantheon and cosmology drawn from folklore, including the goddess of fate Laima and the sun goddess Saule. Practice emphasises seasonal festivals (such as Jāņi, the summer solstice celebration), folk song, traditional costume, harmonious living in accordance with nature, and life-cycle ceremonies conducted in the Latvian language. The movement also stresses Latvian cultural identity and ethics derived from the dainas.

Organisation

The central organisation is the Latvijas Dievturu sadraudze (Latvian Dievturi Fellowship), led by a council; local congregations hold services, festivals and ceremonies. Dievturība is generally classed within the broader family of Baltic and European ethnic-religion revivals, alongside Lithuanian Romuva, with which it shares roots in folklore-based reconstruction.
