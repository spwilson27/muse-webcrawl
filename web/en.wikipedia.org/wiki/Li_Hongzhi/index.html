Li Hongzhi

BornMay 13, 1951 (claimed), Gongzhuling, Jilin, China
NationalityChinese (resident in the United States)
Known forFounder of Falun Gong

Li Hongzhi (Chinese: 李洪志; born 1951) is a Chinese religious leader and the founder of Falun Gong (Falun Dafa), a spiritual practice combining slow-moving qigong exercises with moral teachings centred on the principles of truthfulness, compassion, and forbearance (真、善、忍). He introduced the practice publicly in Changchun in May 1992, and by the end of the decade it claimed tens of millions of practitioners in China.

Biography

Little of Li's early life is independently documented; official Chinese biographies and those issued by Falun Gong differ sharply, including over his birth date — followers celebrate May 13, 1951, while government records have listed July 7, 1952. Before 1992 he worked in Changchun, reportedly including a post with a grain and oil company's security office. Falun Gong sources describe his training under Buddhist and Daoist masters from childhood; Chinese state media have disputed these accounts.

Falun Gong and exile

Li's lectures spread rapidly through China in the 1990s, filling stadiums, and were compiled in the book Zhuan Falun (1995), the movement's central text. He left China in the mid-1990s and settled in the United States, becoming a permanent resident in 1998. On July 20, 1999, the Chinese Communist Party banned Falun Gong and began a wide-ranging campaign of suppression; Li has since lived in the United States, reportedly at the movement's Dragon Springs compound in New York State, and communicates with followers mainly through published writings and conference talks.

Teachings and controversies

Li teaches that practitioners cultivate a rotating falun ("law wheel") in the abdomen and that moral elevation, rather than the exercises alone, is the essence of practice; his writings also contain controversial statements on modern science, race, homosexuality, and medicine. The Chinese government designates Falun Gong an "evil cult" and accuses Li of fraud and responsibility for deaths of followers who refused medical care; Falun Gong and human rights organizations document arrests, imprisonment, and alleged forced organ harvesting of practitioners. Media ventures associated with his followers, including The Epoch Times and Shen Yun Performing Arts, have made Li's movement a significant presence in Western politics as well.
