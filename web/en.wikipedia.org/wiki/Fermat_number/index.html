Fermat number

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Fermat number, named after Pierre de Fermat, is a positive integer of the form Fn = 22n + 1, where n is a non-negative integer. The first few Fermat numbers are 3, 5, 17, 257 and 65537 — all of them prime, which led Fermat to conjecture that all such numbers are prime.

Fermat primes

A Fermat number that is prime is called a Fermat prime. Only the first five (F0 to F4) are known to be prime. In 1732 Leonhard Euler showed that F5 = 232 + 1 = 641 × 6,700,417, disproving Fermat's conjecture. No further Fermat primes are known, and it is an open problem whether any exist beyond F4; many larger Fermat numbers have been shown composite, though the status of some remains unknown.

Properties

Distinct Fermat numbers are pairwise coprime, which yields a proof that there are infinitely many primes (Goldbach). Their product satisfies F0F1···Fn−1 = Fn − 2. Prime divisors of Fn are of the form k·2n+2 + 1 (for n ≥ 2), a result that guides factorisation searches.

Constructible polygons

Gauss showed that a regular polygon with p sides is constructible with compass and straightedge if and only if p is a Fermat prime (combined with powers of 2). Thus the constructible odd-sided polygons depend on Fermat primes: the pentagon (5), the 17-gon, 257-gon and 65537-gon are constructible; the heptagon (7) is not.
