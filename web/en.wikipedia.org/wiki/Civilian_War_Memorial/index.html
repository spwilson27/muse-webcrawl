Civilian War Memorial

The Civilian War Memorial is a monument in Singapore's War Memorial Park, on Beach Road, dedicated to the civilian victims of the Japanese occupation of Singapore during the Second World War. It is one of the city's most recognisable landmarks.

Design and history

The memorial consists of four identical tapering white pillars, each about 67 metres (220 feet) tall, rising from a broad base — a form so distinctive that Singaporeans nicknamed it the "chopsticks" memorial. The four pillars symbolise the four main ethnic groups of Singapore — Chinese, Malay, Indian and Eurasian — suffering together. Beneath the structure lie remains of victims exhumed from mass graves discovered after the war, including those of the Sook Ching massacre of 1942, in which tens of thousands of Chinese civilians were killed.

The memorial was championed by Singapore's first prime minister, Lee Kuan Yew, and was unveiled by him on 15 February 1967, the 25th anniversary of the fall of Singapore. A memorial service is held at the site every year on 15 February, now part of Singapore's Total Defence Day commemorations. The memorial was gazetted as a national monument in 2013.
