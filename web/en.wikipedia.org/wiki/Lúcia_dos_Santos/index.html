Lúcia dos Santos

Sister Lúcia of Jesus
Born: 28 March 1907, Aljustrel, Portugal
Died: 13 February 2005 (aged 97), Coimbra, Portugal
Known for: Our Lady of Fátima visions

Lúcia de Jesus Rosa dos Santos (28 March 1907 – 13 February 2005), known as Sister Lúcia of Jesus, was a Portuguese Catholic Discalced Carmelite nun. With her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto, she reported witnessing apparitions of the Virgin Mary, venerated as Our Lady of Fátima, at Cova da Iria in Fátima, Portugal, in 1917.

Fátima apparitions

Between May and October 1917, the three children reported six apparitions of the Virgin Mary, culminating in the "Miracle of the Sun" of 13 October 1917, witnessed by a crowd of tens of thousands. The children said they were entrusted with three secrets; Francisco and Jacinta died in the 1918–20 influenza pandemic and were canonized in 2017.

Life as a nun

Lúcia entered religious life in 1925, first with the Sisters of St. Dorothy in Spain, then as a Carmelite at Coimbra in 1948. She wrote several memoirs recording the apparitions and the three secrets; the Third Secret was revealed by the Vatican in 2000. She reported further private revelations, including devotion to the Immaculate Heart of Mary and the First Saturdays devotion.

Death and beatification process

Sister Lúcia died in 2005, shortly before her 98th birthday. Her cause for beatification was opened in 2008; in June 2023 Pope Francis recognized her heroic virtues, declaring her Venerable.
