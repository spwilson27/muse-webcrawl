Counter-Strike

Series of multiplayer tactical first-person shooters by Valve.

Counter-Strike (CS) is a series of multiplayer tactical first-person shooter video games in which teams of terrorists battle counter-terrorists to perpetrate or prevent an act of terror, typically planting or defusing a bomb, or guarding or rescuing hostages. The series began as a modification of Half-Life and has become one of the most influential and widely played esports titles in the world.

History

The original Counter-Strike was developed by Minh Le and Jess Cliffe as a mod for Valve's Half-Life, released in 1999. Valve acquired the rights, hired the creators, and released a retail version in 2000. Subsequent entries include Counter-Strike: Condition Zero (2004), Counter-Strike: Source (2004), Counter-Strike: Global Offensive (2012), and Counter-Strike 2 (2023), which replaced Global Offensive on the Source 2 engine.

Gameplay

Matches are played in rounds between two teams of five. In the principal bomb-defusal mode, the terrorist team must plant a C4 explosive at one of two bombsites and defend it until detonation, while the counter-terrorists attempt to prevent the plant or defuse the bomb. Players earn in-game money for kills and objectives, which they spend on weapons and equipment in subsequent rounds, creating a distinctive team economy. Eliminated players wait until the next round to respawn.

Esports and culture

Counter-Strike has been a cornerstone of professional esports since the early 2000s, with Valve-sponsored Major championships drawing stadium crowds and large online audiences. Maps such as Dust II, Inferno, Mirage, and Nuke have become iconic. The games also support a large economy of cosmetic weapon finishes ("skins") traded on the Steam marketplace.
