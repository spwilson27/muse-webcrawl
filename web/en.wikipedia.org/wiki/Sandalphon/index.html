Sandalphon

Sandalphon (Hebrew: סַנְדַּלְפוֹן Sanḏalfōn; Greek: Σανδαλφών) is an archangel in Jewish and Christian writings, particularly in the mystical literature of Kabbalah and in the apocryphal Book of Enoch traditions. Sandalphon is frequently paired with Metatron, sometimes described as his brother or counterpart, and the two are linked in tradition with the prophets Elijah and Enoch respectively.

Role and attributes

In rabbinic and kabbalistic sources Sandalphon is the angel who weaves the prayers of Israel into garlands or crowns for God, and who stands at the threshold between the earthly realm and the heavens. He is described as standing "on the earth" while his head reaches the celestial throne, a measure of immense stature. Traditions variously assign him dominion over music and song in heaven, guardianship of unborn children and embryos, and the role of determining the sex of a child. In some texts Sandalphon battles the demonic prince Samael and assists the archangel Michael.

Origins

The name is usually derived from the Greek synadelphos, "co-brother" or "fellow-brother", reflecting Sandalphon's relationship to Metatron. Some scholars connect the figure with Elijah, who in Jewish tradition did not die but ascended to heaven. Sandalphon appears in the Talmud (Hagigah 13b, where "Master of the Blue" passages are debated), in medieval liturgical poetry, and in works of practical Kabbalah.

In popular culture

Sandalphon appears in Milton's era of angelology literature, in modern fantasy novels, video games such as the Shin Megami Tensei series, and the television series Supernatural-adjacent angel lore; the archangel also inspired the name of musical projects and compositions.
