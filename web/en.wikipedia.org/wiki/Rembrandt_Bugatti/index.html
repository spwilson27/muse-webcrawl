Rembrandt Bugatti (16 October 1884 – 8 January 1916) was an Italian sculptor, known primarily for his dramatic bronze sculptures of wildlife subjects. Born in Milan into an artistic family — his father, Carlo Bugatti, was a celebrated furniture and jewellery designer, and his elder brother Ettore Bugatti founded the Bugatti automobile company — he was named after the Dutch painter Rembrandt van Rijn.

Career

Bugatti worked mainly in Paris and Antwerp, modelling animals from life at the Jardin des Plantes menagerie and the Antwerp Zoo. His bronzes, cast by the Hébrard foundry using the lost-wax process, capture animals with unusual naturalism and psychological intensity. He produced over 300 works, including elephants, panthers, hippopotamuses, and sacred baboons. The famous "Dancing Elephant" mascot of the Bugatti Type 41 "Royale" automobile was adapted from his sculpture.

Death and legacy

Depressed by the outbreak of the First World War, the destruction of the Antwerp Zoo animals he had modelled, and financial difficulties, Bugatti took his own life in Paris in January 1916, aged 31. His works now command high prices and are held by museums including the Musée d'Orsay.
