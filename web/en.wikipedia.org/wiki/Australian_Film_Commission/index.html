Australian Film Commission

Agency overview
Formed1975
Dissolved2008
Superseding agencyScreen Australia
JurisdictionCommonwealth of Australia

The Australian Film Commission (AFC) was an Australian government agency with a mandate to promote the creation and distribution of Australian screen content, for both domestic and international audiences. It was established in 1975 as part of the federal government's film support framework and operated for more than three decades.

Functions

The AFC funded the development and production of Australian film and television, supported marketing and festival participation, conducted industry research and statistics, and promoted Australia as a production location. It also maintained programs for Indigenous screen content and for the preservation and accessibility of Australian screen heritage through commissioning and collections work.

Merger into Screen Australia

On 1 July 2008, the AFC merged with Film Australia and the Film Finance Corporation Australia to form Screen Australia, a single federal agency for screen content support, under the Screen Australia Act 2008.

History

The AFC was created during the revival of Australian cinema in the 1970s — the "Australian New Wave" era of films such as Picnic at Hanging Rock and Mad Max — and its funding and marketing roles helped Australian features and documentaries reach international screens for a generation.
