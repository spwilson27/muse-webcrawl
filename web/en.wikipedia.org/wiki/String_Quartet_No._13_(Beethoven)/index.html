String Quartet No. 13 (Beethoven)

The String Quartet No. 13 in B-flat major, Op. 130, by Ludwig van Beethoven was completed in 1825 and premiered in March 1826 by the Schuppanzigh Quartet. It is one of the "late quartets", a set of works from Beethoven's final years regarded among the greatest compositions in Western classical music.

Movements

The quartet has six movements: an opening Adagio ma non troppo – Allegro; a fleeting Presto; an Andante con moto; an Alla danza tedesca; a lyrical Cavatina (marked beklemmt, "oppressed", in its middle section); and a finale. The Cavatina famously moved the composer himself to tears.

The Große Fuge

The original finale was the monumental Große Fuge (Great Fugue), a massive and dissonant movement that baffled its first audiences. At his publisher's urging, Beethoven agreed to replace it with a new, lighter Allegro finale — the last music he completed — and the Große Fuge was published separately as Op. 133. Modern performances frequently restore the fugue as the finale, sometimes playing the replacement as an encore.

Legacy

Together with Opp. 131 and 132, the Op. 130 quartet represents the summit of Beethoven's late style. A recording of the Cavatina was included on the Voyager Golden Record.
