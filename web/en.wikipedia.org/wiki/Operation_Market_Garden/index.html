Operation Market Garden was an Allied military operation during the Second World War, fought in the German-occupied Netherlands from 17 to 25 September 1944. Conceived by Field Marshal Bernard Montgomery, it was the largest airborne operation of the war: three and a half airborne divisions were dropped behind German lines to seize a series of bridges over the Maas, Waal and Lower Rhine, while British XXX Corps advanced overland to relieve them, opening a corridor into the Ruhr and northern Germany.

Course of the battle

The airborne landings ("Market") initially went well: American paratroopers took the bridges at Eindhoven and Nijmegen, and British XXX Corps ("Garden") advanced up a single highway — later nicknamed "Hell's Highway" — under constant attack. At Arnhem, however, the British 1st Airborne Division landed too far from its objective, encountered unexpectedly strong SS panzer forces refitting nearby, and held only the northern end of the road bridge. Cut off and outgunned, the division was largely destroyed; only about 2,000 of some 10,000 men were withdrawn across the Rhine on the night of 25–26 September.

Aftermath

The operation liberated much of the southern Netherlands, including Eindhoven and Nijmegen, but failed in its central aim of establishing a bridgehead over the Rhine. The northern Netherlands remained occupied through the "Hunger Winter" of 1944–45, and the Rhine was not crossed in force until March 1945. The battle was popularised by Cornelius Ryan's book A Bridge Too Far (1974) and the 1977 film of the same name.
