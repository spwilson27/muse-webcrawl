Operation Torch was the Allied invasion of French North Africa during the Second World War. Beginning on 8 November 1942, American and British forces landed at multiple points in Morocco and Algeria, then controlled by Vichy France. It was the first major operation in which American troops took part in the European–North African theatre, and the largest amphibious operation yet attempted at that point in the war.

Background

United States leaders had favoured an early cross-Channel attack, but British planners argued for action in the Mediterranean periphery. Torch was the compromise: an invasion of North Africa that would clear the southern shore of the Mediterranean, relieve pressure on Malta and Egypt, and gain experience for inexperienced American forces. The Allies hoped French forces would not seriously resist; secret contacts with French officers, including General Charles Mast in Algiers, aimed to secure cooperation.

The landings

Three task forces landed simultaneously. The Western Task Force under George S. Patton, sailing directly from the United States, landed near Casablanca in Morocco. The Center Task Force landed at Oran and the Eastern Task Force, a mixed Anglo-American force, at Algiers in Algeria. Resistance varied: Algiers fell quickly with the help of a locally staged coup, while fighting at Oran and around Casablanca, including a naval battle with French warships, lasted several days. A ceasefire negotiated with Admiral François Darlan, the senior Vichy commander who happened to be in Algiers, ended organised French resistance by 11 November (16 November in some accounts of the final arrangements), and Darlan ordered French forces in North Africa to cooperate with the Allies.

Aftermath

The landings caught German forces in the region off balance, but Germany responded by occupying Vichy France (Case Anton) and rushing troops into Tunisia, leading to the Tunisian campaign of 1942–43. Torch gave Allied forces a base from which to attack Axis positions from the west while the British Eighth Army advanced from Egypt, eventually trapping the Axis forces in Tunisia, which surrendered in May 1943.
