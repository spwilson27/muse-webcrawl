Aflatoxin

Aflatoxins are poisonous carcinogens and mutagens produced by certain moulds, primarily Aspergillus flavus and Aspergillus parasiticus, which grow in soil, decaying vegetation, and stored grains and nuts. Contamination is a major food-safety problem, especially in warm, humid climates and where grain is poorly stored.

Types and toxicity

The major aflatoxins are designated B1, B2, G1, and G2. Aflatoxin B1 is among the most potent naturally occurring liver carcinogens known; acute exposure can cause aflatoxicosis, with liver damage and death, while chronic exposure is linked to liver cancer and childhood stunting. Aflatoxin M1, a metabolite, appears in the milk of animals fed contaminated feed.

Regulation and control

Food regulators set maximum permitted levels — for example 20 parts per billion in the United States for most foods. Control relies on rapid drying and proper storage of crops, sorting out mouldy produce, and biocontrol using non-toxigenic strains of the fungus.
