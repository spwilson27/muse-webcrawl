Smallpox

Smallpox

Smallpox was an infectious disease caused by the variola virus, characterised by fever and a distinctive progressive skin rash with raised blistering pustules. It killed an estimated 300–500 million people in the 20th century alone, and survivors were often left scarred or blind. Following an intensive global vaccination campaign led by the World Health Organization, smallpox was declared eradicated in 1980 — the first disease, and so far one of only two (with cattle plague rinderpest), to be wiped out worldwide.

The vaccine, developed from Edward Jenner's 1796 experiments using cowpox, was the first vaccine ever created. The last naturally occurring case was in Somalia in 1977 (Ali Maow Maalin). Official stocks of the virus remain only at two high-security laboratories — in the United States (CDC, Atlanta) and Russia (Koltsovo) — and whether to destroy them remains debated.

History

Smallpox scars are found on Egyptian mummies; the disease shaped history, killing European monarchs and devastating indigenous populations of the Americas after 1492. Variolation — deliberate inoculation with smallpox matter — preceded vaccination in China, India and the Ottoman world, and was popularised in Britain by Lady Mary Wortley Montagu.
