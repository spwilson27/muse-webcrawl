Beneficiary

A beneficiary (also, in trust law, cestui que trust) in the broadest sense is a natural person or other legal entity who receives money or other benefits from a benefactor. The term is used in several areas of law and finance, most prominently in trusts, wills and estates, insurance, and retirement accounts.

Trusts

In trust law, the beneficiary is the person or class of persons for whose benefit a trustee holds and administers trust property. Beneficiaries hold the equitable interest in the trust assets, while the trustee holds legal title. Beneficiaries generally have rights to enforce the trust, to receive information and accounts, and to income or capital as the trust instrument provides. Trusts may name primary and contingent (remainderman) beneficiaries, and charitable trusts benefit charitable purposes or the public rather than identifiable individuals.

Wills, insurance, and accounts

Under a will, a beneficiary is anyone who receives property from an estate. In insurance, a policyholder designates a beneficiary to receive the proceeds of a life insurance policy or annuity on the insured's death; beneficiary designations on policies, pensions, and payable-on-death accounts typically override the terms of a will. In government and social programmes, "beneficiary" also denotes a person entitled to payments, such as a recipient of Social Security or Medicare benefits in the United States.

Third-party beneficiaries

In contract law, a third-party beneficiary is a person who is not a party to a contract but is intended to benefit from it, and who may in many jurisdictions enforce it — for example, where two parties contract for payment to be made to a third person.
