Data warehouse

In computing, a data warehouse (DW or DWH), also known as an enterprise data warehouse (EDW), is a system used for reporting and data analysis and is a core component of business intelligence. Data warehouses are central repositories of integrated data from one or more disparate sources, storing current and historical data in one place, optimized for analytical queries rather than transaction processing.

Definition and approaches

Bill Inmon defined a data warehouse as "subject-oriented, integrated, time-variant, and non-volatile." Inmon's approach builds a centralized, normalized enterprise store from which departmental data marts are derived. Ralph Kimball's competing approach builds the warehouse as a union of dimensional data marts, modelling data in star schemas of fact and dimension tables. Data is loaded through ETL (extract, transform, load) processes.

Architecture

A typical warehouse separates the operational (OLTP) systems from analytical (OLAP) workloads, stores data in columnar or dimensional structures, and supports aggregates, cubes, and history tracking through slowly changing dimensions. Cloud data warehouses such as Snowflake, Amazon Redshift, Google BigQuery, and Azure Synapse separated storage from compute and popularized ELT pipelines. Related concepts include the data lake, which stores raw data in native format, and the "lakehouse," which combines both approaches.
