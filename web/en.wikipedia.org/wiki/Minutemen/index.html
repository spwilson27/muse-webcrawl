Minutemen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Lexington Minuteman statue (1900) honours the militia who fought on 19 April 1775.

Minutemen were civilian colonists in British America who independently organised into well-prepared militia units, trained in weapons and tactics, and pledged to respond to military threats "at a minute's notice". They played a crucial role in the early stages of the American Revolutionary War, fighting at the Battles of Lexington and Concord in April 1775.

Origins

The concept of a rapidly mobilising militia element existed in Massachusetts from the seventeenth century, when a portion of each town's militia company was designated for quick response. In the autumn of 1774, as relations with Britain deteriorated after the Massachusetts Government Act, the Massachusetts Provincial Congress formally recommended that militia companies elect officers and form minuteman units — roughly a quarter to a third of the militia — who drilled more frequently and were paid for their readiness. Other New England colonies adopted similar arrangements.

Lexington and Concord

The minutemen are most famous for their stand on 19 April 1775, when British regulars marched from Boston to seize colonial military stores at Concord. Warned by riders including Paul Revere, militia and minuteman companies assembled at Lexington Green, where the "shot heard round the world" opened the war, and at Concord's North Bridge, where colonists returned British fire in force. Minutemen then harassed the British column throughout its retreat to Boston, firing from behind walls and trees — a day's fighting that cost the British some 273 casualties and launched the war and the Siege of Boston.

Legacy

The minuteman became an enduring American symbol of the citizen-soldier. The name was later adopted by the United States' intercontinental ballistic missile family (the Minuteman ICBMs), by numerous civic and military organisations, and — controversially — by various modern activist groups. Daniel Chester French's famous statue The Minute Man (1875) at Concord, depicting a farmer leaving his plough for his musket, and the Lexington statue, are iconic commemorations; Ralph Waldo Emerson's "Concord Hymn" supplied the phrase "the shot heard round the world".
