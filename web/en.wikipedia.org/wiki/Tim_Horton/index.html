Tim Horton

Miles Gilbert "Tim" Horton (January 12, 1930 – February 21, 1974) was a Canadian ice hockey defenceman who played 24 seasons in the National Hockey League, principally for the Toronto Maple Leafs. He is also remembered as the co-founder of the Tim Hortons restaurant chain, which grew into one of Canada's most recognisable businesses. Horton was inducted into the Hockey Hall of Fame in 1977.

Hockey career

Born in Cochrane, Ontario, Horton grew up in mining country and joined the St. Michael's Majors of the Ontario Hockey Association before turning professional with the Pittsburgh Hornets of the AHL. He became a regular on the Maple Leafs' blue line in the 1952–53 season and was the anchor of Toronto's defence through the 1950s and its Stanley Cup dynasty of the 1960s, winning four championships (1962, 1963, 1964, 1967). Renowned for his exceptional strength — he popularised the "bear hug" tactic against opposing forwards — and his durability, he was named to six NHL All-Star teams. At age 41 he was claimed by the New York Rangers, then played for the Pittsburgh Penguins before finishing his career with the Buffalo Sabres under his old coach Punch Imlach. At the time of his death he had played 1,446 regular-season NHL games.

Business career

In 1964 Horton and businessman Jim Charade opened the first Tim Horton Donut Drive-In in Hamilton, Ontario. After Charade's departure, Horton partnered with Ron Joyce, who expanded the chain aggressively through franchising. By 1974 there were roughly forty stores in Ontario and beyond.

Death

In the early hours of February 21, 1974, driving home to Buffalo from a game in Toronto, Horton lost control of his De Tomaso Pantera sports car on the Queen Elizabeth Way near St. Catharines and was killed in the crash; he was 44. His widow sold her shares in the restaurant company to Joyce the following year. The chain kept his name, and "Tim Hortons" became a fixture of Canadian daily life.
