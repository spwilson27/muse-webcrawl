James Hamilton, 2nd Earl of Arran (c. 1516 – 22 January 1575), was a Scottish nobleman and regent who governed Scotland during the minority of Mary, Queen of Scots, from 1543 to 1554. For his services in arranging the marriage of the young queen to the French Dauphin, he was made Duke of Châtellerault in the peerage of France in 1548/49.

Regency

As the closest adult heir to the throne through his descent from James II, Arran became regent after the death of James V in December 1542. Initially he favoured an English alliance and Protestant reforms — the proposed Treaty of Greenwich (1543) would have married Mary to the future Edward VI of England — but under pressure from Cardinal Beaton and the queen dowager Mary of Guise he repudiated the treaty, prompting Henry VIII's invasions known as the Rough Wooing. After the Scots' catastrophic defeat at Pinkie Cleugh (1547), the infant queen was sent to France and betrothed to the Dauphin Francis. Arran's regency was marked by weak control over feuding nobles, and in 1554 he surrendered the regency to Mary of Guise, receiving money and French honours in return.

Later life

During the Scottish Reformation crisis of 1559–1560, Arran and his son James (the third earl) joined the Protestant Lords of the Congregation against the regent's French troops. The younger Arran was briefly proposed as a husband for Elizabeth I of England. After Mary's return to Scotland the Hamiltons' ambitions continued to complicate politics; the third earl's madness left the family diminished. Châtellerault supported Mary in the civil war after her deposition, was forfeited in 1571, and died at Hamilton in 1575, having been restored in France under the pacification. Historians have generally judged him indecisive and self-interested, a verdict summarised by David Hume's description of him as weak in judgment.
