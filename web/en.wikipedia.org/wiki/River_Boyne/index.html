The River Boyne (Irish: An Bhóinn or Abhainn na Bóinne) is a river in Leinster, Ireland, about 112 kilometres (70 mi) long. It rises at Trinity Well, Newberry Hall, near Carbury in County Kildare, and flows north-east through County Meath past Trim, Navan, and Slane before entering the Irish Sea between Mornington and Baltray, south of Drogheda in County Louth. Despite its modest length, the Boyne drains a rich agricultural catchment of about 2,695 square kilometres and is one of Ireland's most historically significant rivers.

History

The Boyne Valley is famous for the prehistoric passage tombs of Brú na Bóinne—Newgrange, Knowth, and Dowth—which date to around 3200 BC and form a UNESCO World Heritage Site. In Irish mythology the river is associated with the goddess Boann, from whom it takes its name. The river gives its name to the Battle of the Boyne (1690), fought near Drogheda between the forces of William III and the deposed James II, a decisive event in Irish and British history still commemorated annually. The Boyne Navigation, a canal system paralleling the lower river, was constructed in the 18th and 19th centuries to link Drogheda with Navan.

Wildlife and use

The Boyne is a noted salmon and trout fishery, and its estuary is an important habitat for migratory birds. It supplies drinking water and receives treated wastewater from towns along its course. Tributaries include the Blackwater, Deel, and Mattock rivers.
