John Fraser Muth (September 27, 1930 – October 23, 2005) was an American economist. He is widely considered the originator of the rational expectations revolution in economics, principally through his 1961 paper "Rational Expectations and the Theory of Price Movements", published in Econometrica.

Life and career

Muth studied at the Carnegie Institute of Technology in Pittsburgh, earning his undergraduate degree and his PhD (1956) there; he was one of the first recipients of a doctorate from its Graduate School of Industrial Administration and an early winner of the Alexander Henderson Award for excellence in economic theory. He taught at Carnegie Tech and later at Indiana University, where he spent the remainder of his career.

Rational expectations

In his 1961 paper Muth proposed that agents' expectations, "since they are informed predictions of future events, are essentially the same as the predictions of the relevant economic theory" — that is, expectations are formed using all available information and the actual structure of the economy, so that forecast errors are on average zero and uncorrelated with known information. The idea lay comparatively neglected for a decade, then became the foundation of the macroeconomic revolution led by Robert Lucas Jr., Thomas Sargent and others in the 1970s, reshaping monetary economics, econometrics and the theory of economic policy.

Other work

Muth also contributed to operations research and production planning, including work on optimal inventory and production smoothing — the context in which his study of expectations formation in a market with price "cobweb" dynamics first arose.
