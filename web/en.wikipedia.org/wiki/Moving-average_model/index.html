Moving-average model

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A realization of an MA(2) process.

In time series analysis, the moving-average model (MA model), also known as the moving-average process, is a common approach for modeling univariate time series. The moving-average model specifies that the output variable depends linearly on the current and various past values of a stochastic (imperfectly predictable) term — often interpreted as shocks or innovations.

Definition

The notation MA(q) refers to the moving-average model of order q:

Xt = μ + εt + θ1εt−1 + … + θqεt−q

where μ is the mean of the series, θ1, …, θq are parameters of the model, and εt, εt−1, … are white-noise error terms. Despite the name, the model is not a weighted moving average of past observations but a regression on past forecast errors. An MA model is always stationary; it is called invertible if it can be equivalently expressed as a convergent autoregressive representation, which allows shocks to be recovered from observed data.

Use

Moving-average models form one half of the ARMA and ARIMA models developed in the Box–Jenkins methodology; the MA structure captures short-lived shocks whose effects die out after q periods, and its autocorrelation function cuts off abruptly after lag q — a diagnostic used to identify model order. Estimation is typically performed by maximum likelihood (often via the Kalman filter) or conditional least squares.
