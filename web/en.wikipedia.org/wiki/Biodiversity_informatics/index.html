Biodiversity informatics is the application of information technology to the management, presentation, discovery and analysis of biodiversity data — information about species, their names, occurrences, distributions and traits. The field emerged as natural history collections and taxonomic literature began to be digitised at scale in the 1990s and 2000s.

Scope

The discipline covers several families of data: taxonomic name and checklist data (catalogues of species and their classification); specimen and occurrence data from museum collections and field observations; ecological and trait data; and molecular sequence data. Major aggregators include the Global Biodiversity Information Facility (GBIF), which publishes billions of occurrence records, the Catalogue of Life for accepted species names, and citizen-science platforms such as iNaturalist whose records flow into research databases.

Standards

Biodiversity Information Standards (TDWG) maintains community data standards, of which Darwin Core is the most widely used: a vocabulary of terms (such as scientificName, decimalLatitude, eventDate) for sharing occurrence data. Persistent identifiers, data cleaning, georeferencing of historical locality descriptions, and linking names across databases remain central technical challenges.

Applications

Biodiversity informatics underpins species distribution modelling, conservation planning and Red List assessments, invasive species early warning, and global-change biology, as well as digit-on-demand access to centuries of taxonomic literature through the Biodiversity Heritage Library.
