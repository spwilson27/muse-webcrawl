Red Cloud (Lakota: Maȟpíya Lúta; 1822 – December 10, 1909) was a leader of the Oglala Lakota and one of the most prominent Native American leaders of the nineteenth century. He led his people in Red Cloud's War (1866–1868) against the United States Army over control of the Powder River Country, achieving one of the few clear Native victories in the wars of the northern Plains: the United States abandoned its forts along the Bozeman Trail and recognised Lakota control of the contested hunting grounds in the Treaty of Fort Laramie (1868).

Early life

Red Cloud was born in 1822 near the forks of the Platte River, in present-day Nebraska. His father, Lone Man, was a Brulé Lakota; his mother, Walks As She Thinks, was Oglala, and according to Lakota matrilineal custom he was raised among her people, under the tutelage of his uncle, the Oglala chief Smoke. He distinguished himself as a young warrior in fights against the Pawnee, Crow, and other rivals of the Lakota.

Red Cloud's War

In the mid-1860s the Bozeman Trail, a route to the Montana goldfields, cut through the Lakota's prime buffalo-hunting grounds in the Powder River Country. When the U.S. Army began building forts along the trail in 1866, Red Cloud organised a coalition of Lakota, Northern Cheyenne, and Arapaho warriors to resist. The campaign culminated in the Fetterman Fight of December 21, 1866, in which a detachment of 81 soldiers under Captain William J. Fetterman was destroyed near Fort Phil Kearny — then the army's worst defeat on the northern Plains. Two years of pressure forced the government to negotiate, and by the 1868 Treaty of Fort Laramie the United States abandoned the Bozeman Trail forts, which the Lakota promptly burned.

Later life

After 1868 Red Cloud pursued negotiation rather than war. He travelled to Washington, D.C., in 1870 and met President Ulysses S. Grant, impressing eastern audiences with his oratory. He settled at the Red Cloud Agency in Nebraska and later at Pine Ridge in South Dakota, where he spent decades contesting government policy — opposing the seizure of the Black Hills, the allotment of tribal land, and corrupt agency administration — while urging his people to adapt to reservation life. He outlived most of the famous Plains leaders, dying at Pine Ridge in 1909. The town of Red Cloud, Nebraska, and Red Cloud Indian School on the Pine Ridge Reservation are named for him.
