Quinic acid is a cyclitol carboxylic acid, a cyclic polyol, obtained from cinchona bark, coffee beans, and other plant products. Chemically it is (1S,3R,4S,5R)-1,3,4,5-tetrahydroxycyclohexane-1-carboxylic acid. It is one of the principal acids contributing to the perceived acidity of coffee, and it is formed from chlorogenic acids during roasting.

Structure of quinic acid, a hydroxycyclohexane carboxylic acid.

Occurrence and biosynthesis

Quinic acid occurs widely in plants, including in Eucalyptus globulus leaves, cranberries, and the bark of cinchona trees (the source of quinine, from which the name derives). It is an intermediate of the shikimate pathway, lying on a side branch from dehydroquinate, and serves as a storage and transport form feeding into the biosynthesis of aromatic compounds such as chlorogenic acids and lignin.

Uses

Quinic acid is used as an astringent and as a starting material (chiral synthon) in organic synthesis, notably in routes to the antiviral drug oseltamivir developed as alternatives to shikimic acid feedstocks.
