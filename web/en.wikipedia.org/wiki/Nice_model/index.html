Nice model

The Nice model (pronounced "niece") is a scenario for the early evolution of the Solar System, proposed in 2005 by Rodney Gomes, Hal Levison, Alessandro Morbidelli and Kleomenis Tsiganis, working at the Observatoire de la Côte d'Azur in Nice, France. It holds that the giant planets formed in a compact configuration and later migrated to their present orbits after the dispersal of the protoplanetary gas disc.

The scenario

In the model, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune began much closer to the Sun, surrounded by a massive disc of planetesimals. Slow scattering of planetesimals drove the planets apart until, hundreds of millions of years later, Jupiter and Saturn crossed their 1:2 orbital resonance. The resulting instability flung Uranus and Neptune outward into the planetesimal disc, scattering most of it — sending a shower of comets and asteroids into the inner Solar System identified with the Late Heavy Bombardment of the Moon about 4.1 to 3.8 billion years ago — and leaving the Kuiper belt with its observed structure.

Later developments

Subsequent work, including the "jumping Jupiter" variant and the Nice 2 model, suggests an additional fifth giant planet may have been ejected. The scenario also motivates the Grand Tack hypothesis for the formation of the terrestrial planets.
