Jesus Christ Superstar is a sung-through rock opera with music by Andrew Lloyd Webber and lyrics by Tim Rice. Loosely based on the Gospels' accounts of the Passion, the work interprets the psychology of Jesus and Judas Iscariot — much of the plot centred on Judas, who is depicted as a tragic figure dissatisfied with the direction in which Jesus steers his disciples — and features contemporary attitudes and sensibilities.

History

The work began as a concept album in 1970, with Murray Head as Judas, Ian Gillan as Jesus and Yvonne Elliman as Mary Magdalene; its single "Superstar" and the song "I Don't Know How to Love Him" became hits. The Broadway production opened in October 1971, directed by Tom O'Horgan and starring Jeff Fenholt and Ben Vereen, and the London production, running from 1972 to 1980, was at the time the longest-running West End musical. A film adaptation directed by Norman Jewison followed in 1973, shot on location in Israel.

Reception and controversy

The show's rock score, its portrayal of Judas as sympathetic, and its ending at the crucifixion — with no resurrection — provoked protests from some religious groups, while other critics praised its boldness. It has been revived many times; notable productions include the 2012 Broadway revival, the 2016 Regent's Park Open Air Theatre production, and NBC's 2018 live television concert starring John Legend.

Musical numbers

Famous songs include "Heaven on Their Minds", "What's the Buzz?", "Everything's Alright", "This Jesus Must Die", "Hosanna", "I Don't Know How to Love Him", "Gethsemane (I Only Want to Say)", "The Last Supper", "Superstar" and "John Nineteen: Forty-One".
