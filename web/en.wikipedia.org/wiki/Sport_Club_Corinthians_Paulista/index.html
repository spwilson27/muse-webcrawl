Sport Club Corinthians Paulista

Corinthians
Full name: Sport Club Corinthians Paulista
Founded: 1 September 1910
Ground: Neo Química Arena (Arena Corinthians), São Paulo
Nickname: Timão

Sport Club Corinthians Paulista is a Brazilian sports club based in São Paulo, best known for its professional football team. Founded in 1910 by working-class railway workers inspired by the touring English side Corinthian F.C., it is one of Brazil's most popular and successful clubs.

History

Corinthians won its first São Paulo state championship in 1914 and became a symbol of the working class. The club endured a 23-year title drought before winning the 1977 Paulista championship. In the early 1980s, players led by Sócrates and Wladimir ran the club under the "Corinthian Democracy" movement, a famous experiment in self-management during Brazil's military dictatorship.

Honours

Corinthians has won the Brazilian Série A seven times, the Copa do Brasil several times, and in 2012 won the Copa Libertadores and the FIFA Club World Cup, defeating Chelsea in the final. It also won the inaugural FIFA Club World Championship in 2000. Its main rivals are Palmeiras (Derby Paulista), São Paulo, and Santos. The club moved into the Arena Corinthians — which hosted the opening match of the 2014 FIFA World Cup — in 2014.
