The 1966 NFL season was the 47th regular season of the National Football League. It was a landmark season: the NFL and the rival American Football League agreed in June 1966 to merge, and their respective champions met for the first time in the AFL–NFL World Championship Game — later known as Super Bowl I.

The Packers defeated the Cowboys in the 1966 NFL Championship Game in Dallas.

Season summary

The league expanded to fifteen teams with the addition of the Atlanta Falcons. In the Eastern Conference, the Dallas Cowboys, led by coach Tom Landry and quarterback Don Meredith, won their first conference title. In the Western Conference, Vince Lombardi's Green Bay Packers finished 12–2 behind quarterback Bart Starr, who was named the league's most valuable player.

NFL Championship Game

On January 1, 1967, the Packers defeated the Cowboys 34–27 at the Cotton Bowl in Dallas to win the NFL championship. The game ended with a famous goal-line stand, Don Chandler's extra point having earlier been blocked to keep Dallas within reach. Two weeks later, on January 15, 1967, Green Bay beat the AFL champion Kansas City Chiefs 35–10 in the first AFL–NFL World Championship Game at the Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum.

Merger agreement

The merger agreement of June 8, 1966 ended the costly bidding war for players between the two leagues. It provided for a common draft, interleague play beginning in 1970, and the annual championship game between the league champions that became the Super Bowl.
