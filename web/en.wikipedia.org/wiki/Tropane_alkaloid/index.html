Tropane alkaloid

The tropane bicyclic skeleton shared by the group

Tropane alkaloids are a class of bicyclic alkaloids and secondary metabolites that contain a tropane ring in their chemical structure. They occur naturally in many members of the plant family Solanaceae (nightshades) and in some other families, and have powerful effects on the nervous system.

Main compounds

The best-known tropane alkaloids include:

  • Atropine, found in deadly nightshade (Atropa belladonna) and related plants, used in medicine as a muscarinic antagonist.
  • Hyoscyamine and scopolamine (hyoscine), found in henbane, datura and mandrake, noted for anticholinergic and sedative effects.
  • Cocaine, from the coca plant (Erythroxylum coca), a stimulant rather than a typical anticholinergic member of the family.

Pharmacology

Most tropane alkaloids act as competitive antagonists of muscarinic acetylcholine receptors, producing anticholinergic effects: dilated pupils, dry mouth, rapid heartbeat and, at higher doses, delirium and hallucinations. Plants containing them have a long history in medicine, ritual and poisoning. Synthetic tropane derivatives are used as bronchodilators and anti-motion-sickness drugs.
