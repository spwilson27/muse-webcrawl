The City of Brimbank is a local government area in the western suburbs of Melbourne, Victoria, Australia, covering about 123 square kilometres. It was formed in 1994 from the merger of the City of Keilor and the City of Sunshine. Brimbank is one of the most culturally diverse municipalities in Australia and one of the largest in Victoria by population, with roughly 200,000 residents.

Geography and suburbs

Brimbank extends from Keilor and Taylors Lakes in the north to Sunshine and Deer Park in the south. Major suburbs include St Albans, Sydenham, Albanvale, Kings Park, Delahey, Cairnlea, and Albion. The municipality takes in parts of the Maribyrnong River valley and Brimbank Park, a large regional park along the river in Keilor after which the city is named — "Brimbank" derives from the horseshoe shape ("brim of the bank") of the river bend there.

Demographics

Brimbank is notable for its multicultural population: a large proportion of residents were born overseas, with major communities from Vietnam, Malta, Italy, the Philippines, Greece, India, and Sudan, among many others. Dozens of languages are spoken in its homes, and centres such as St Albans and Sunshine are known for their Vietnamese and multicultural shopping districts.

Transport and industry

The area is served by the Sunbury railway line and major arterials including the Western Ring Road and Calder Freeway. Sunshine is a designated major activity centre and one of Melbourne's principal western employment hubs, with significant manufacturing, logistics, and health precincts.
