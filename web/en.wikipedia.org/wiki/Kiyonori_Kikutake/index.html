Kiyonori Kikutake (菊竹 清訓, Kikutake Kiyonori; April 1, 1928 – December 26, 2011) was a Japanese architect known as one of the founders of the Metabolist movement, which proposed cities and buildings as evolving, organic systems whose parts could be replaced and renewed over time.

Career

Kikutake graduated from Waseda University in 1950. His own residence, the Sky House (1958) in Tokyo, became an early manifesto of Metabolist ideas: a single open space raised on four concrete piers, designed so that rooms — including a children's room and kitchen units — could be added, moved, or removed as the family's needs changed. At the World Design Conference in Tokyo in 1960 he presented his "Marine City" project, proposing floating platforms as a response to Japan's land scarcity, and he contributed to the Metabolist manifesto Metabolism: The Proposals for New Urbanism alongside Kisho Kurokawa, Fumihiko Maki, and others, under the influence of Kenzo Tange.

Major works

His built works include the Izumo Taisha administrative building (1963), the Hotel Tokoen (1964) with its exposed megastructure frame, the Expo Tower for Expo '70 in Osaka, the National Theatre of Tanzania, the Matsumi Tower, the Edo-Tokyo Museum (1993) — a vast raised structure housing a full-scale replica of the Nihonbashi bridge — and the Kyushu National Museum (2005).
