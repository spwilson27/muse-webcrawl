Institution of Mechanical Engineers

The Institution of Mechanical Engineers (IMechE) is an independent professional association and learned society headquartered in London, United Kingdom, representing mechanical engineers. Founded in 1847 in Birmingham, it has over 115,000 members in some 140 countries and is licensed by the Engineering Council to assess candidates for registration as Chartered Engineers (CEng), Incorporated Engineers (IEng) and Engineering Technicians (EngTech). Its headquarters since 1899 have been at One Birdcage Walk, Westminster, London.

History

The Institution was founded on 27 January 1847 at the Queen's Hotel, Birmingham, following a meeting of engineers many of whom had worked on the expanding railway network; the locomotive pioneer George Stephenson became its first president. It was created partly because the older Institution of Civil Engineers was felt not to serve the new discipline of mechanical engineering. Membership required a published technical paper — a tradition continued in its Proceedings, first issued in 1847. The Institution received a Royal Charter in 1930 and moved with the profession through the steam, automotive, aerospace and energy eras.

Activities

IMechE accredits university engineering degree programmes, sets professional standards, publishes journals (the multi-part Proceedings of the IMechE) and technical magazines, and runs conferences, seminars and codes and standards work. It promotes engineering to the public and policymakers through reports and the annual Formula Student competition at Silverstone, in which university teams design and race single-seat cars. Other notable outreach includes the Bloodhound land-speed record project partnership and annual parliamentary reports on topics such as energy storage and transport.

Membership grades

Grades include Student, Affiliate, Associate (AMIMechE), Member (MIMechE) and Fellow (FIMechE). Academically and professionally qualified members may register with the Engineering Council as EngTech, IEng or CEng, the last being the senior grade requiring demonstrated competence in developing and leading engineering solutions.

Presidents

Past presidents include George Stephenson (1847–48), Robert Stephenson, Sir Charles Parsons (inventor of the steam turbine), Frederick Lanchester, Sir Frank Whittle (jet engine pioneer) and, in 1998, Pamela Liversidge, the institution's first female president.
