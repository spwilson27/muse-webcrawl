Potential evaporation

Potential evaporation (PE) or potential evapotranspiration (PET) is the amount of evaporation that would occur if a sufficient water source were available. It represents the evaporative demand of the atmosphere for a given surface and climate.

Estimation

Methods of estimating potential evaporation include the Thornthwaite method, based mainly on temperature; the Penman and Penman–Monteith equations, which use radiation, temperature, humidity and wind; and direct measurement with evaporation pans and lysimeters. The FAO adopted the Penman–Monteith equation as the standard reference (ET0).

Applications

Potential evapotranspiration is used in hydrology, irrigation scheduling, drought indices such as the aridity index, and climate classification.
