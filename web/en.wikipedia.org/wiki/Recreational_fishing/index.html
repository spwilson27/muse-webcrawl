Recreational fishing

Image placeholder: recreational anglers on a lake.

Recreational fishing, also called sport fishing or game fishing, is fishing done primarily for pleasure or competition, as opposed to commercial fishing, which is done for profit, or artisanal fishing, which is done for subsistence. Recreational fishing is one of the most popular outdoor leisure activities in many countries, involving tens of millions of participants worldwide.

History

Fishing for pleasure has ancient roots, but it developed into a widespread pastime in the modern era with the growth of leisure time and tourism. Treatises such as Izaak Walton's The Compleat Angler (1653) helped popularise angling as a contemplative recreation in England. In the nineteenth and twentieth centuries, organised clubs, competitions and specialised tackle industries emerged.

Methods

Common recreational methods include angling with rod and line, fly fishing, lure fishing, bait fishing, trolling, ice fishing and spear fishing. Anglers target species such as bass, trout, salmon, pike and various saltwater game fish. Many jurisdictions regulate recreational fishing through licences, seasons, size limits and catch limits, and catch-and-release practice is widely promoted for conservation.

Economic and social significance

Recreational fishing supports substantial spending on tackle, boats, travel and guiding services, and contributes to rural and coastal economies. It also raises management questions about fish stocks, habitat and access rights, which are addressed by fisheries agencies in most developed countries.
