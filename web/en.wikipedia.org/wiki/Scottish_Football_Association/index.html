The Scottish Football Association (Scots: Scots Fitbaw Association; also known as the Scottish FA or SFA) is the governing body of association football in Scotland. Founded in 1873, it is the second oldest national football association in the world, after England's Football Association. The SFA is a member of FIFA and UEFA and sits on the International Football Association Board, which determines the Laws of the Game.

History

The SFA was formed on 13 March 1873 by representatives of eight clubs, following Scotland's participation in the first official international match against England in 1872. It organised the Scottish Cup from 1873–74, the oldest national trophy in world football. The association briefly withdrew from FIFA in the 1920s along with the other Home Nations over disputes concerning amateurism and payments, rejoining permanently in 1946.

Responsibilities

The SFA administers the Scotland men's and women's national teams, the Scottish Cup and Scottish Youth Cup, referee development and appointments, coach education, and the national performance programme based at the Oriam national sports centre. Professional league football is run separately by the Scottish Professional Football League (formed in 2013 from the SPL and SFL), and the SFA cooperates with the SPFL and regional and junior associations. The association is based at Hampden Park, the national stadium, where the Scottish Football Museum is also housed.
