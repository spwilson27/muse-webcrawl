S-type asteroid

S-type asteroids are asteroids with a spectral type characterised by a moderately red slope and absorption features indicating a composition of silicate ("stony") material mixed with metallic nickel–iron. They are the second most common spectral type after carbonaceous C-types and comprise about 17 percent of known asteroids. S-types dominate the inner main asteroid belt, within about 2.2–3 AU of the Sun.

Characteristics

S-type spectra show a steep red slope at wavelengths shorter than about 0.7 micrometres and moderate absorption bands near 1 and 2 micrometres caused by olivine and pyroxene. Their densities and spectra indicate bodies made of magnesium silicates with some free metal, resembling ordinary chondrite meteorites, the most common meteorites found on Earth. For decades astronomers debated the mismatch between S-type spectra and the more neutral spectra of ordinary chondrites; space weathering—the darkening and reddening of surfaces by the solar wind and micrometeorite impacts—is now understood to explain it, as confirmed by the Hayabusa mission's sample return from the S-type asteroid 25143 Itokawa, whose grains proved to be ordinary-chondrite-like.

Subtypes

In the Tholen classification, the S class subdivides in modern schemes (Bus, Bus–DeMeo) into subtypes such as S, Sa, Sq, Sr and Sv, reflecting varying olivine–pyroxene ratios and degrees of space weathering; Sq-types are transitional between fresh Q-type spectra and weathered S-types. Asteroids such as 4 Vesta, with basaltic surfaces, are classified separately as V-types.

Exploration

S-type asteroids have been visited by several spacecraft: NEAR Shoemaker orbited and landed on 433 Eros in 2000–2001; JAXA's Hayabusa returned samples from Itokawa in 2010; ESA's and other flybys have imaged further examples. Their accessibility and metal–silicate composition make S-types targets of interest both for science and for proposed asteroid resource utilisation.
