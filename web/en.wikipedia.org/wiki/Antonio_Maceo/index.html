Antonio Maceo Grajales (14 June 1845 – 7 December 1896) was a Cuban military leader of the wars of independence against Spain and one of the national heroes of Cuba. Second-in-command of the Cuban Liberation Army, he was renowned for his tactical brilliance, physical endurance—he survived more than twenty wounds—and uncompromising commitment to complete independence and the abolition of slavery, earning him the nickname the Bronze Titan.

Major General Antonio Maceo

Ten Years' War

Born near Santiago de Cuba to a free family of mixed African and Spanish descent, Maceo enlisted as a private in the Liberation Army at the outbreak of the Ten Years' War in 1868 and rose rapidly to general through daring guerrilla operations. When most rebel leaders accepted the Pact of Zanjón with Spain in 1878, Maceo rejected it in the Protest of Baraguá, refusing any peace that did not grant full independence and abolish slavery—one of the most celebrated episodes in Cuban history. Forced into exile soon afterward, he spent years abroad attempting to organize new expeditions.

War of Independence

In the renewed war of 1895, organized by José Martí, Maceo landed in eastern Cuba and, under commander-in-chief Máximo Gómez, led the invasion of western Cuba: a rapid campaign across the entire island that reached Pinar del Río, the westernmost province, in January 1896, defeating Spanish attempts to contain the rebels. His forces tied down large numbers of Spanish troops under Captain General Valeriano Weyler. On 7 December 1896 Maceo was killed in a skirmish at Punta Brava, near Havana, along with Panchito Gómez Toro, the son of Máximo Gómez.

Legacy

Maceo is venerated in Cuba as a symbol of resistance and racial equality; his remains rest in the Cacahual monument near the site of his death, and the airport of Santiago de Cuba bears his name. His mother, Mariana Grajales, who sent her sons to fight for independence, is likewise honored as a heroine of the nation.
