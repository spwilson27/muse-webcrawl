Peggy Gale Fleming (born July 27, 1948) is an American former figure skater. She is the 1968 Olympic champion in ladies' singles and a three-time World champion (1966–1968). She won five consecutive U.S. national titles from 1964 to 1968.

Early life

Fleming was born in San Jose, California, and began skating at age nine. Her family moved to Los Angeles, where she trained with William Kipp. In 1961, when she was twelve, her coach was killed in the crash of Sabena Flight 548, which also killed the entire U.S. figure skating team en route to the World Championships in Prague. Fleming then trained under Carlo Fassi.

Career

Fleming won her first U.S. senior national title in 1964 at age fifteen and placed sixth at the 1964 Winter Olympics in Innsbruck. She won the World Championships in 1966, 1967, and 1968. Her signature performance came at the 1968 Winter Olympics in Grenoble, France, where she won the only gold medal for the United States at those Games, skating in a chartreuse dress sewn by her mother.

Her elegant, balletic style — a contrast to the athletic European school — helped revive American figure skating after the 1961 disaster and made her one of the most popular athletes of her era. She was voted the Associated Press Female Athlete of the Year in 1967.

Later life

After turning professional in 1968, Fleming starred in television specials and toured with ice shows. She worked for many years as a commentator for ABC Sports. A breast cancer survivor (diagnosed in 1998), she became an advocate for cancer awareness. With her husband, dermatologist Greg Jenkins, she founded Fleming Jenkins Vineyards & Winery in California. She was inducted into the World Figure Skating Hall of Fame in 1986.
