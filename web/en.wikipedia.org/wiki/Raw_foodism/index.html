Raw foodism


Raw food diets emphasise uncooked fruits, vegetables, nuts, and seeds.

Raw foodism, also known as rawism or a raw food diet, is the dietary practice of eating only or mostly food that is uncooked and unprocessed. Depending on the philosophy, raw food diets may include raw fruits, vegetables, nuts, seeds, sprouted grains and legumes, and sometimes raw eggs, unpasteurized dairy, and raw or dried meat and fish.

Principles

Adherents typically define "raw" as food not heated above about 40–48 °C (104–120 °F), believing that cooking destroys enzymes and nutrients. Preparation techniques include juicing, blending, soaking, sprouting, and dehydrating.

History

Modern raw foodism traces to the 19th- and early 20th-century natural hygiene movement and figures such as Maximilian Bircher-Benner and Ann Wigmore. It gained popularity in alternative-health circles in the late 20th century.

Health considerations

Health authorities note risks including nutrient deficiencies (notably vitamin B12, vitamin D, calcium, and protein), foodborne illness from raw animal products, and low energy density. Some studies associate plant-rich diets with health benefits, but long-term strict raw diets require careful planning.
