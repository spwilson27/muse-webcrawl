Economy of Japan

[Image: the Marunouchi business district and Tokyo Station, centre of Japan's economy]

The economy of Japan is the world's third- or fourth-largest by nominal GDP (approximately US$4 trillion in recent years, behind the US, China and roughly level with Germany), highly developed and export-oriented, famous for automobiles, electronics, machinery and robotics. The yen is a major reserve Currency; Tokyo hosts one of the largest stock exchanges.

From miracle to stagnation and reform

Post-war Japan achieved the 'Japanese economic miracle' — near-double-digit growth to 1973 through export-led industrial policy (MITI), high savings and the main-bank/keiretsu corporate system. An asset bubble's collapse in 1990–91 opened the 'Lost Decades' of slow growth, mild deflation and rising public debt (gross debt the highest in the developed world, above 250% of GDP), against rapid population ageing and shrinkage. The Bank of Japan pioneered zero rates and quantitative easing long before 2008; 'Abenomics' (from 2012) combined monetary easing, fiscal stimulus and structural reform. Inflation and wage growth finally returned in the 2020s, and the Nikkei 225 regained its 1989 peak only in 2024 — a record 34-year round trip.
