German–Soviet Frontier Treaty

Signed28 September 1939
LocationMoscow
SignatoriesNazi Germany, Soviet Union

The German–Soviet Frontier Treaty, signed on 28 September 1939, was the second supplementary protocol of the Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact. It redrew the spheres of influence of Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union in Eastern Europe following their joint invasion of Poland.

Terms

The treaty transferred Lithuania to the Soviet sphere in exchange for a shift of the demarcation line in Poland eastward, giving Germany a larger share of central Poland, including the Lublin and parts of the Warsaw voivodeships. It confirmed the partition of Poland along the Pisa, Narew, Vistula, and San rivers.

Aftermath

The arrangements were accompanied by population exchanges of ethnic Germans and Ukrainians/Belarusians between the two zones. The treaty remained in force until Germany invaded the Soviet Union in June 1941.
