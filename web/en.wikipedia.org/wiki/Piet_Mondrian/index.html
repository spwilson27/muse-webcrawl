Piet Mondrian

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
Broadway Boogie Woogie (1942–43): a grid of yellow lines interrupted by small red, blue and white blocks, pulsing like city lights

Pieter Cornelis Mondriaan (7 March 1872 – 1 February 1944), known after 1906 as Piet Mondrian, was a Dutch painter and art theorist who is regarded as one of the greatest artists of the 20th century and a founder of abstract art. He is known for being one of the pioneers of 20th-century abstract art, as he changed his artistic direction from figurative painting to an increasingly abstract style, until he reached a point where his artistic vocabulary was reduced to simple geometric elements.

Co-founding the De Stijl movement (1917) with Theo van Doesburg, Mondrian developed 'neoplasticism': compositions of black lines and rectangles of primary colour on white. Later works such as Broadway Boogie Woogie (1942–43), inspired by New York and jazz, replaced black lines with coloured bands. His grid compositions profoundly influenced architecture, design and fashion.
