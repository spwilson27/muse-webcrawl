Status of forces agreement

A status of forces agreement (SOFA) is an agreement between a host country and a foreign nation stationing military forces in that country. SOFAs are often included, along with other types of military agreements, as part of a comprehensive security arrangement. They typically define the legal status of the foreign personnel and property in the host country.

Contents

A typical SOFA addresses criminal jurisdiction (which country prosecutes offences by service members), civil claims for damage caused by the visiting force, the wearing of uniforms, carrying of weapons, tax and customs treatment, entry and exit of personnel, and the use of facilities. Jurisdiction provisions are often the most politically sensitive, particularly where alleged crimes by foreign troops against local citizens occur.

Examples

The United States maintains SOFAs with many countries hosting American forces, including Japan, South Korea, Germany (under the NATO SOFA supplementary agreements), and formerly Iraq, where disagreement over legal immunity contributed to the withdrawal of U.S. forces in 2011. NATO's Status of Forces Agreement of 1951 provides the multilateral framework among alliance members.
