La damnation de Faust (English: The Damnation of Faust), Op. 24, is a work for orchestra, chorus and solo voices by the French composer Hector Berlioz. He called it a "légende dramatique" (dramatic legend). The French libretto, by Berlioz himself and Almire Gandonnière, is largely based on Gérard de Nerval's translation of Goethe's Faust, Part One.

Composition and premiere

Berlioz had been captivated by Goethe's Faust since reading it in 1828, and first set texts from it in his Huit scènes de Faust (1829). He composed La damnation de Faust largely while on tour in central Europe in 1845–46, adding the famous Rákóczi March for a concert in Hungary. The first performance, at the Opéra-Comique in Paris on 6 December 1846, was a commercial failure, but the work was later recognized as one of Berlioz's masterpieces and entered the international repertoire, frequently performed in concert and sometimes staged as an opera.

Structure

The work is in four parts, following Faust's pact with Méphistophélès, his love for Marguerite, and her eventual redemption while Faust is carried to hell. Celebrated excerpts include the Hungarian (Rákóczi) March, the Ballet of the Sylphs, the Dance of the Will-o'-the-Wisps, and Marguerite's ballad "D'amour l'ardente flamme."
