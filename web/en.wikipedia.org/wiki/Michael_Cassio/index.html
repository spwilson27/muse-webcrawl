Michael Cassio

Michael Cassio is a fictional character in William Shakespeare's tragedy Othello (c. 1603). He is Othello's lieutenant — his second in command — and becomes an unwitting instrument in Iago's plot to destroy Othello.

Role in the play

Cassio is a young Florentine soldier and mathematician, promoted by Othello to the lieutenancy over the more experienced Iago. Iago, resentful at being passed over, exploits Cassio's courteous manners and his weakness for drink: he gets Cassio drunk on watch in Cyprus, provokes a brawl, and so engineers Cassio's dismissal. Advised by Iago to plead for reinstatement through Desdemona, Cassio's innocent visits to her are twisted by Iago into supposed evidence of an affair. The lost handkerchief, passed through Cassio's mistress Bianca, deepens Othello's conviction. Cassio is wounded in an ambush by Iago and Roderigo but survives; at the play's end, after the truth is revealed, he is left in authority in Cyprus:

"For Michael Cassio, I dare be sworn I think that he is honest."

Character

Cassio is often read as a figure of courtesy, education and reputation-consciousness — his lament "Reputation, reputation, reputation! O, I have lost my reputation!" is one of the play's famous speeches. Critics contrast his bookish soldiering and gallantry with Iago's cynical professionalism and Othello's heroic self-image; his genuine honesty makes him the perfect screen onto which Iago projects false meanings.
