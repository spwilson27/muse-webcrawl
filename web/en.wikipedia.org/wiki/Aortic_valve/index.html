Aortic valve

Aortic valve
SystemCardiovascular
LocationBetween the left ventricle and the aorta
StructureUsually three semilunar cusps (left, right, non-coronary)
FunctionPrevents backflow of blood into the left ventricle

The aortic valve is a valve in the heart of humans and other animals, located between the left ventricle and the aorta. It is one of the four heart valves and one of the two semilunar valves, the other being the pulmonary valve. The aortic valve normally has three cusps or leaflets; in about 1–2% of the population it has only two, a congenital variant called a bicuspid aortic valve. When the left ventricle contracts, pressure forces the valve open so blood flows into the aorta; when the ventricle relaxes, the valve snaps shut, and the cusps' pockets (the sinuses of Valsalva) help feed the coronary arteries, whose openings lie just above the valve.

Diseases

The two principal disorders are aortic stenosis, in which the valve narrows — most commonly through age-related calcification — obstructing outflow, and aortic regurgitation (insufficiency), in which it leaks, allowing blood to flow back into the ventricle. Both place strain on the heart and can progress to heart failure; severe disease is treated by surgical aortic valve replacement or by transcatheter aortic valve replacement (TAVR), a less invasive catheter-based procedure developed in the 2000s. Infective endocarditis can also damage the valve.
