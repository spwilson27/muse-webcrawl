Northrop Grumman EA-6B Prowler

The Northrop Grumman (formerly Grumman) EA-6B Prowler is a twin-engine, four-seat, mid-wing electronic-warfare aircraft derived from the A-6 Intruder airframe. Operated by the United States Navy and Marine Corps, it was designed to jam enemy radar and communications and to attack radar installations with anti-radiation missiles.

Design

The EA-6B carried its AN/ALQ-99 jamming system in underwing pods and a distinctive pod on top of the vertical fin. The four-person crew — a pilot and three electronic countermeasures officers — managed receivers and jammers covering multiple radar bands. Later versions could fire the AGM-88 HARM anti-radiation missile, giving the Prowler a lethal suppression-of-enemy-air-defences (SEAD) role.

Service history

The Prowler entered service in time for the Vietnam War and went on to support virtually every US combat operation for four decades, including strikes on Libya in 1986, the Gulf War, operations over the Balkans, and the Iraq and Afghanistan wars — the US military did not fly strike missions in contested airspace without Prowler (or successor) jamming support. The Navy replaced it with the EA-18G Growler, retiring its last Prowlers in 2015; the Marine Corps flew the type until 2019.
