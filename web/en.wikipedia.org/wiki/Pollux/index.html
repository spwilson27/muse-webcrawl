Pollux is the brightest star in the constellation of Gemini. It has the Bayer designation β Geminorum, which is Latinised to Beta Geminorum and abbreviated Beta Gem or β Gem. Despite its "beta" designation, it is brighter than Castor (Alpha Geminorum). Pollux is an orange-hued evolved giant star about 33.8 light-years from the Sun, making it the closest giant star to the Solar System.

Properties

Pollux is a red clump star of spectral type K0 III, with about twice the mass of the Sun and roughly nine times its radius. Having exhausted the hydrogen in its core, it now fuses helium. Its apparent magnitude of 1.14 makes it the eighteenth-brightest star in the night sky. In 2006, the existence of an extrasolar planet orbiting Pollux was confirmed. The planet, Pollux b (also designated Thestias), is a gas giant with a mass at least 2.3 times that of Jupiter and an orbital period of about 590 days.

Nomenclature

The name Pollux refers to the mythological twin brother of Castor, the Dioscuri of Greek and Roman mythology. In 2016 the International Astronomical Union's Working Group on Star Names approved the name Pollux for this star, and the planet's name Thestias—after the mother of Leda in some traditions—was chosen in a public naming campaign.
