Hrant Dink (Armenian: Հրանտ Տինք; 15 September 1954 – 19 January 2007) was a Turkish-Armenian intellectual, editor-in-chief of Agos, journalist and columnist. As editor-in-chief of the bilingual Turkish-Armenian newspaper Agos, Dink was a prominent member of the Armenian minority in Turkey best known for advocating Turkish–Armenian reconciliation and human and minority rights in Turkey.

He was often critical both of Turkey's denial of the Armenian genocide and of the Armenian diaspora's campaign for its international recognition. Dink was prosecuted three times for denigrating Turkishness, while receiving numerous death threats from Turkish nationalists.

Dink was assassinated in Istanbul on 19 January 2007 by Ogün Samast, a 17-year-old ultra-nationalist, in front of the Agos office. His funeral was attended by an estimated 200,000 mourners in Istanbul, who chanted "We are all Hrant Dink" and "We are all Armenians".

Early life

Dink was born in Malatya, the eldest of three sons of Sarkis Dink, a tailor from Sivas, and Gülvart Dink from Kangal. His family moved to Istanbul in 1961; after his parents separated, he was raised largely in Armenian orphanages and boarding schools, including the Gedikpaşa Armenian Orphanage and the Tıbrevank boarding school in Üsküdar. He later studied zoology at Istanbul University and, after leaving that program, philosophy.

Career

Dink worked in publishing and printing before helping to found Agos ("Furrow" in Armenian) in 1996 as an outlet for Istanbul's Armenian community, serving as its editor-in-chief until his death. He also wrote a column for the daily BirGün and contributed to Zaman. In his writing he questioned official Turkish narratives of 1915 and argued that recognition and dialogue, rather than denial or diaspora resolutions, offered a path to reconciliation.

Prosecutions

Dink was charged under Article 301 of the Turkish Penal Code for "denigrating Turkishness" following a series of articles published in Agos in 2004 on the identity of Turkish Armenians, including a passage about "the poisoned blood" of Turks which he insisted had been taken out of context — he argued he had written of the perception of Turks among Armenians. In October 2005 he received a six-month suspended sentence. He appealed to the European Court of Human Rights.

Assassination

On 19 January 2007 Dink was shot dead outside the Agos office in Şişli, Istanbul. The gunman, 17-year-old Ogün Samast from Trabzon, was captured in Samsun within days. Photographs showing Samast posing with gendarmerie officers beside a Turkish flag provoked public outrage and allegations of security-force complicity. In 2010 the European Court of Human Rights ruled that Turkey had failed to protect Dink's life and violated his freedom of expression. In 2011 Samast was sentenced to 22 years and 10 months in prison; the investigation into wider official negligence continued for years afterwards.

Legacy

The Hrant Dink Foundation, established by his family in 2007, promotes dialogue, human rights and his unfinished project of Turkish–Armenian understanding. The annual International Hrant Dink Award honors people who work toward a world free of discrimination, racism and violence.
