History of Serbia

The history of Serbia spans from prehistoric settlements and Roman provinces through medieval Serbian kingdoms and empire, five centuries of Ottoman rule in parts of its territory, the 19th-century principality and kingdom, Yugoslavia, and the modern Republic of Serbia.

Serbia

Antiquity and Middle Ages

Vinča culture flourished in prehistory; Romans ruled the region (Singidunum = Belgrade; emperors including Constantine were born in present-day Serbia). Slavs settled in the 6th–7th centuries. The Nemanjić dynasty (from 1166) unified Serbian lands; St Sava secured autocephaly of the Serbian Orthodox Church (1219), Stefan Prvovenčani was crowned king (1217), and Stefan Dušan created a large Balkan empire and law code (1349). After his death the empire fragmented; Prince Lazar died at Kosovo Polje (1389), and the Despotate fell to the Ottomans in 1459.

Ottoman and Habsburg rule

Serbia was an Ottoman frontier, with great migrations of Serbs into Habsburg Vojvodina (1690, 1737–39). Uprisings led by Karađorđe (1804) and Miloš Obrenović (1815) won autonomy and then independence at the Congress of Berlin (1878).

Kingdom and Yugoslavia

Serbia expanded in the Balkan Wars (1912–13), suffered huge losses in World War I after the Sarajevo assassination triggered the war, and in 1918 entered the Kingdom of Serbs, Croats and Slovenes (Yugoslavia). In World War II it was occupied by Germany; Tito's Partisans liberated the country and socialist Yugoslavia followed. Under Slobodan Milošević, the Yugoslav wars of the 1990s, sanctions and NATO bombing (1999) over Kosovo devastated Serbia; Milošević fell in 2000. Montenegro left the union in 2006 and Kosovo unilaterally declared independence in 2008, which Serbia does not recognise. Serbia is now an EU candidate country.
