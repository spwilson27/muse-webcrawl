Lion

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The lion (Panthera leo) is a large cat of the genus Panthera, native to Africa and (as a small relict population) India's Gir Forest. Muscular, deep-chested and — in males — maned, lions are the most social of the big cats, living in prides of related females, their cubs and a coalition of males. They are apex predators of the savanna, hunting mostly large ungulates cooperatively at night.

Lions carry an outsized cultural weight — 'king of beasts', heraldic emblem, the cowardly Lion of Oz — and figure in human art back to cave paintings. Their roar can be heard kilometres away. Numbers have fallen sharply: roughly 20,000–25,000 remain in the wild, about half the population of the early 1990s, and the species is listed Vulnerable, with West African lions Critically Endangered.

Society and hunting

Pride females do most hunting and raise cubs communally; males defend territory and cubs from rival coalitions — infanticide follows takeovers. Lions scavenge substantially, stealing kills from hyenas as often as the reverse.

Conservation

Habitat fragmentation, prey decline, conflict with livestock farmers and trophy hunting are the main threats. Asiatic lions, a single population of several hundred, face the acute risk of one epidemic or fire; relocation within India has long been debated.
