Craigellachie, British Columbia


The Last Spike site

Craigellachie is a locality in British Columbia, located along the Trans-Canada Highway between Sicamous and Revelstoke, near Eagle Pass in the Monashee Mountains. It is famous as the site where the Last Spike of the Canadian Pacific Railway was driven on 7 November 1885, completing Canada's first transcontinental railway.

History

The place was named after the village of Craigellachie in Moray, Scotland, the ancestral home of George Stephen, 1st Baron Mount Stephen, the first president of the Canadian Pacific Railway. At a small ceremony, CPR director Donald Smith drove a plain iron spike — not a golden one, as the first spike bent and had to be replaced — into the last tie. The event is commemorated in the well-known photograph of Smith raising the hammer, and it symbolised the fulfilment of the promise of a transcontinental railway made to British Columbia on joining Confederation in 1871.

Today

A cairn, interpretive displays, and a gift shop mark the site, which is a popular stop on the Trans-Canada Highway; the railway line remains a busy Canadian Pacific main line.
