Yoga pants

Type: Athletic clothing
Material: Stretch fabrics with nylon, polyester and elastane (spandex)
Introduced: 1990s–2000s
Related: Leggings, athleisure

Yoga pants are form-fitting, stretchable trousers designed for practicing yoga and other physical exercise, typically made from blends of nylon, polyester, cotton and elastane. Originating as specialist practice wear in the late 1990s, they became one of the defining garments of the athleisure trend, worn widely as everyday casual clothing far beyond the yoga studio.

History

The modern yoga pant was popularised by the Canadian retailer Lululemon Athletica, founded in 1998, whose black bootcut pants made from a proprietary nylon–Lycra blend ("Luon") sold at premium prices and helped turn yoga clothing into a major market. A key enabling technology was the development of new stretch fabrics in the 1990s that combined opacity, compression, and moisture management. Competing brands — including Athleta, Nike, and Adidas — expanded the category into capris, leggings, and flared "yoga jeans" for casual wear.

Cultural impact and controversy

By the 2010s, sales of yoga pants in the United States surpassed those of denim jeans in some markets, and the garment became a flashpoint in school and workplace dress-code debates. In 2013 Lululemon recalled a line of pants found to be unintentionally transparent, an episode that briefly hit the company's share price. A 2016 letter to the New York Times criticising women over 30 for wearing yoga pants in public provoked a tongue-in-cheek protest parade in Rhode Island and renewed public debate. Critics also raise concerns about the environmental impact of synthetic microfibre shedding from stretch garments.

Design

Yoga pants are characterised by an elastic or drawstring waistband, four-way stretch, gusseted construction for freedom of movement, and in studio styles, a slight flare at the hem. Compression variants are marketed for muscle support, while high-waisted styles became dominant in the late 2010s.
