Yacht club

A yacht club is a recreational boating or yachting club, typically centred on a marina or harbour, whose members own or sail yachts and other pleasure craft. Yacht clubs organise racing, cruising, and social events, and provide facilities such as moorings, clubhouses, and training.

History

The earliest known yacht club is generally considered to be the Neva Yacht Club, founded in St. Petersburg, Russia, in 1718 by Peter the Great. The Royal Cork Yacht Club in Ireland, founded in 1720, is the oldest still in existence. Yacht clubs spread through Britain, continental Europe, and North America in the 19th century; the New York Yacht Club, founded in 1844, became famous for its defence of the America's Cup, which it held from 1851 to 1983.

Organisation and customs

Yacht clubs traditionally elect officers with naval-style titles such as commodore, vice commodore, and rear commodore. Most have a unique burgee — a triangular flag flown by members' boats. Clubs may be private and membership-based, or municipal and open to the public. Many run junior sailing programmes and are affiliated with national bodies such as US Sailing or the Royal Yachting Association.
