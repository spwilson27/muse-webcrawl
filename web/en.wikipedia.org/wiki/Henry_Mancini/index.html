Henry Mancini

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Henry Nicola Mancini (born Enrico Nicola Mancini; April 16, 1924 – June 14, 1994) was an American composer, conductor, arranger, pianist and flutist, widely regarded as one of the greatest composers in the history of film. He won four Academy Awards, a Golden Globe, and twenty Grammy Awards, plus a posthumous Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award in 1995.

Career

Born in Cleveland and raised in Pennsylvania, Mancini studied at the Juilliard School before wartime service and a stint as pianist-arranger for the Glenn Miller Orchestra. Joining Universal Pictures in 1952, he contributed to films such as The Glenn Miller Story and Creature from the Black Lagoon. His jazzy score for Blake Edwards's television series Peter Gunn (1958) made his reputation.

Famous works

Mancini's celebrated collaborations with Blake Edwards include Breakfast at Tiffany's (1961), whose song "Moon River" won an Academy Award, Days of Wine and Roses (1962), The Pink Panther (1963), with its slinky saxophone theme, and Victor/Victoria (1982). Other famous songs include "Dear Heart" and "The Sweetheart Tree".

Death and legacy

Mancini died of pancreatic cancer in Los Angeles in 1994. His sophisticated, jazz-inflected orchestral sound remains instantly recognisable, and his melodies are standards of the American songbook.
