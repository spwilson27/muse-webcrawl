Christianshavn

Christianshavn is a neighbourhood of Copenhagen, Denmark, built on an artificial island in the harbour between Zealand and Amager. Founded in the early 17th century by King Christian IV as a fortified merchant town inspired by Dutch canal cities, it is known for its canals, colourful houses, and the spiral-towered Church of Our Saviour.

History

Construction began in 1617–1619 as part of Christian IV's expansion of Copenhagen's fortifications. The quarter's canals and bastions followed Amsterdam's model, and Dutch-style gabled houses still line Christianshavns Kanal. In the 19th–20th centuries it was a working-class district; the old naval and industrial sites have since been redeveloped into housing and institutions.

Landmarks

The Church of Our Saviour (1696) has an external spiral staircase winding around its spire, offering panoramic views. Christianshavn also contains the freetown of Christiania, founded in 1971 on a former military area, the Danish Architecture Centre at BLOX nearby, and several historic warehouses converted to flats and hotels.
