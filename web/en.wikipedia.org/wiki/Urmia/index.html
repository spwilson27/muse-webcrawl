Urmia (Persian: ارومیه; Azerbaijani: اورمو, Urmu; Kurdish: Ûrmiye) is the largest city in West Azerbaijan Province of Iran and the capital of Urmia County. It lies in the Azerbaijan region in northwestern Iran, on the fertile Urmia Plain near the western shore of Lake Urmia, close to the borders with Turkey and Iraq. The city sits at an elevation of about 1,330 metres and is the tenth most populous city in Iran.

History

The Urmia Plain has been inhabited since antiquity and lay within the orbit of Urartu and later the Medes and Persians. Urmia was a significant center of the region known as Azerbaijan (Atropatene) and passed through Arab, Seljuk, Mongol, Safavid, and Ottoman–Persian frontier rule; it was repeatedly fought over by Persian and Ottoman armies. In the 19th and early 20th centuries the city had a large Christian population of Assyrians (Church of the East and Chaldean Catholics) and Armenians, and American missionaries founded schools and one of the first medical colleges in Persia there. During the First World War, the region suffered occupation, famine, and massacres of Christians in 1915–1918 that decimated the Assyrian community. Urmia was the birthplace of a notable episode of the war: the Assyrian exodus of 1918.

Demographics and culture

Today the population is predominantly Azerbaijani Turkic-speaking, with Kurdish, Persian, and small Armenian and Assyrian minorities. The city's culture mixes Azerbaijani, Kurdish, and Persian traditions; it is known for its grapes and wine-making heritage from before the Islamic era, its bazaar, and landmarks including the Jameh Mosque of Urmia and the three-domed Seh Gonbad tower.

Geography and Lake Urmia

East of the city lies Lake Urmia, once one of the world's largest salt lakes, which shrank dramatically from the 1990s due to drought, damming, and agricultural water use, prompting a major national restoration programme. The lake's partial recovery, aided by water transfers and wet years, remains central to the region's environment and economy. The climate is cold semi-arid with warm summers and snowy winters.

Economy and transport

The surrounding plain produces grapes, apples, and other fruit, and the city processes agricultural goods and manufactures carpets and food products. Urmia has an international airport and road links to Tabriz, Mahabad, and the Turkish border crossings.
