DFB-Pokal

The DFB-Pokal (German: [deːʔɛfˈbeː poˈkaːl]), also known as the German Cup in English, is a German knockout football cup competition held annually by the German Football Association (DFB). Sixty-four teams participate, including all clubs from the Bundesliga and the 2. Bundesliga, together with the top four finishers of the 3. Liga and regional cup winners. It is considered the second-most important club title in German football after the Bundesliga championship.

History

The competition was founded in 1935 as the Tschammer-Pokal, named after Reichssportführer Hans von Tschammer und Osten; the first final was won by 1. FC Nürnberg. It was revived in West Germany in 1952–53 under the DFB-Pokal name, with Rot-Weiss Essen the first post-war winner. Since 1985 the final has traditionally been held at the Olympiastadion in Berlin. The women's competition is the DFB-Pokal Frauen, founded in 1981.

Format

The cup is played as a single-elimination tournament over six rounds. Lower-division teams host matches against higher-division opponents in the early rounds. Drawn games were historically decided by replays in later rounds, but are now settled by extra time and penalty shoot-outs. The winner qualifies for the UEFA Europa League and plays the Bundesliga champion in the DFL-Supercup.

Records

Bayern Munich is the most successful club in the competition, with 20 titles, well ahead of Werder Bremen with six. Bayern also won three consecutive cups from 2013 to 2016-era runs and holds the record for the biggest final victory.
