Memphis International Airport (IATA: MEM) is a civil–military airport located about 7 miles (11 km) southeast of downtown Memphis, Tennessee, United States. It is the home of the FedEx Express global hub — the FedEx World Hub, often called the "SuperHub" — and for decades was the busiest cargo airport in the world; it remains the busiest in North America and among the top two worldwide by cargo throughput.

History

The airport opened in 1929 as Memphis Municipal Airport. Federal Express began operations there in 1973, choosing Memphis for its central location, good weather, and room to expand. The airport was a passenger hub for Republic Airlines and then Northwest Airlines, and briefly for Delta after their merger, until Delta ended hub operations in 2013. Passenger traffic is now dominated by low-cost and legacy carriers on point-to-point routes.

FedEx World Hub

The FedEx hub occupies most of the airport's north side and processes millions of packages per night sort. It employs tens of thousands of people, making it one of the largest employers in Tennessee. The airport's cargo pre-eminence passed to Hong Kong International in 2010; Memphis regained the world cargo lead briefly in 2020 before Hong Kong retook it.

Facilities

The passenger terminal's three concourses were consolidated into a modernised single concourse (Concourse B), reopened in 2022. The airfield has four runways, the longest being 11,120 feet (3,389 m). The Tennessee Air National Guard's 164th Airlift Wing is based on the field.
