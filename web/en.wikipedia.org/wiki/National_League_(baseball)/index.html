The National League of Professional Baseball Clubs, known simply as the National League (NL), is the world's oldest surviving professional sports league. Founded on February 2, 1876, in New York City, it is one of the two leagues constituting Major League Baseball in the United States and Canada, alongside the American League.

Composition

The NL currently consists of 15 teams divided into three divisions of five: the East, Central, and West. Its champion plays the American League champion in the World Series. Two of its charter-era clubs survive: the Atlanta Braves franchise (founded as the Boston Red Stockings) and the Chicago Cubs (founded as the Chicago White Stockings).

History

The National League was founded by William Hulbert to replace the loosely organized National Association with a stricter structure of schedules, contracts, and standards. For most of its history the league's pitchers batted for themselves, unlike the American League, which adopted the designated hitter in 1973; the NL permanently adopted the designated hitter rule in 2022. The league's champion receives the Warren C. Giles Trophy.
