Beirut (Arabic: بيروت, Bayrūt) is the capital and largest city of Lebanon. Situated on a peninsula on the Mediterranean coast, it is Lebanon's chief port and main commercial and cultural centre. Inhabited for more than 5,000 years, it is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world.

History

Known in antiquity as Berytus, the city was a Phoenician settlement that became a Roman colony under Augustus and famous for its law school, one of the empire's leading centres of jurisprudence. It passed through Byzantine, Arab, Crusader, Mamluk and Ottoman rule, growing in the 19th century into a major trading port.

Under the French Mandate and after independence in 1943, Beirut flourished as a banking, publishing and tourism hub — the "Paris of the Middle East". The Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990) devastated the city, which was divided by the Green Line. Post-war reconstruction, led by the Solidere company, rebuilt the central district. The city suffered further blows with the 2006 Lebanon War and the catastrophic port explosion of 4 August 2020, which killed more than 200 people and damaged much of the eastern city.

Geography and neighbourhoods

Beirut occupies a triangular peninsula jutting into the Mediterranean, backed by Mount Lebanon. Distinctive districts include the rebuilt Downtown, Gemmayzeh and Mar Mikhael (known for nightlife and heritage houses), Achrafieh, Hamra (a commercial and university quarter near the American University of Beirut), the seafront Corniche, and the southern suburbs (Dahieh). The Pigeon Rocks (Raouché) are a landmark off the western shore.

Culture and economy

Beirut is the Arab world's publishing capital, home to major universities (AUB, Saint Joseph University), newspapers, and a celebrated food and nightlife scene. Its economy centres on services, banking, trade and tourism, though it has been severely affected by Lebanon's financial crisis since 2019.
