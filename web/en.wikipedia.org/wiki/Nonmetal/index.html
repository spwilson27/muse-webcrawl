A nonmetal is a chemical element that lacks metallic character: nonmetals are generally poor conductors of heat and electricity, are brittle when solid, and gain or share electrons in reactions, often forming acidic oxides. The nonmetals are hydrogen, the noble gases, the halogens, and C, N, O, P, S and Se.

Although fewer than a quarter of the elements are nonmetals, they dominate living matter (C, H, N, O, P, S) and the atmosphere. Their chemistry is built on covalent bonding and discrete molecules rather than metallic lattices.

Properties

Nonmetals span gases (O₂, N₂, Cl₂), one liquid (Br₂) and solids of very different character — diamond (hardest natural material) and graphite (soft lubricant) are both carbon. Ionisation energies and electronegativities are high, rising across the table toward fluorine.

Hydrogen's oddity

Hydrogen sits atop group 1 but is a nonmetal gas; it can lose its electron like an alkali metal or gain one like a halogen, and is sometimes placed in a group of its own.
