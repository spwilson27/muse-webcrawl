Three Character Classic

The Three Character Classic (Chinese: 三字經; pinyin: Sānzìjīng) is one of the classical Chinese texts for children, written in rhymed couplets of three characters each. Together with the Hundred Family Surnames and the Thousand Character Classic, it formed the traditional "San Bai Qian" trio of primers by which Chinese children learned characters and Confucian values for centuries.

Content

The text is traditionally attributed to the Song-dynasty scholar Wang Yinglin (1223–1296), though its authorship and revisions are debated. It opens with the celebrated lines "People at birth are naturally good" (人之初，性本善), echoing the philosophy of Mencius, and proceeds through the importance of education, filial piety, numbers, the seasons, geography, Chinese history dynasties, and the Confucian canon, concluding with exemplars of diligent study and an exhortation to the student. Its short, rhythmic lines were designed to be chanted and memorised.

Legacy

The Three Character Classic was used in elementary education in China well into the twentieth century and has seen modern revivals; expanded and revised versions appeared across the centuries, including some adapting the history sections to later dynasties. It was translated into other languages, including a Manchu version and early English translations, and in 1990 UNESCO included it in a world children's moral-education collection. Versions exist in the traditions of Korea and Vietnam as well.
