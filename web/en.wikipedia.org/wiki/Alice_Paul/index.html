Alice Paul

Alice Stokes Paul (11 January 1885 – 9 July 1977) was an American Quaker, suffragist and women's rights activist. A leader of the campaign for the Nineteenth Amendment, she later authored the proposed Equal Rights Amendment.

Born11 January 1885, Mount Laurel, New Jersey
Died9 July 1977, Moorestown, New Jersey
EducationSwarthmore College, University of Pennsylvania (PhD)
Known forSilent Sentinels, National Woman's Party, ERA

Suffrage campaign

After working with the militant British suffragettes of Emmeline Pankhurst — and enduring imprisonment and force-feeding — Paul returned to the United States and organised the 1913 suffrage parade in Washington, D.C. She founded the National Woman's Party and in 1917 led the Silent Sentinels, the first group to picket the White House; arrested picketers were abused in the "Night of Terror" at Occoquan Workhouse, generating public sympathy.

Equal Rights Amendment

After the Nineteenth Amendment was ratified in 1920, Paul earned law degrees and in 1923 drafted the Equal Rights Amendment, introduced in Congress that year. She campaigned for it for the rest of her life; it passed Congress in 1972 but fell short of state ratification by its deadline. Paul also helped secure a reference to women in the UN Charter and the 1964 Civil Rights Act's sex provision.
