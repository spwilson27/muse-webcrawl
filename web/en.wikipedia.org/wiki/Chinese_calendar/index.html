Chinese calendar

Chinese calendar
Traditional Chinese: 農曆 / 曆法
Type: Lunisolar calendar
Used in: China, Taiwan, Vietnam (variant), Korea (historically), diaspora communities
Epoch: Traditionally Yellow Emperor era or Han dynasty Taichu reform (104 BCE)

The Chinese calendar is a lunisolar calendar that incorporates elements of both lunar and solar calendars. It has been used for millennia to set festivals, choose auspicious dates, and regulate agricultural activity. Months begin with the new moon, and a leap month is inserted roughly seven times in nineteen years to keep the calendar in step with the solar year and the seasons.

Structure

Each month has 29 or 30 days. A normal year has 12 months (353–355 days) and a leap year has 13 months (383–385 days). The year is also divided into 24 solar terms (jiéqì), such as Lichun (Beginning of Spring) and the winter solstice, which track the Sun's position along the ecliptic and guide farming.

Sexagenary cycle

Years, months, days and hours are named using the 60-term sexagenary cycle, combining the ten Heavenly Stems with the twelve Earthly Branches. The twelve branches are also associated with the twelve animals of the Chinese zodiac — Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog and Pig. See Chinese zodiac.

History

Oracle-bone inscriptions from the Shang dynasty already record a lunisolar system. The Taichu calendar of 104 BCE under Emperor Wu of Han fixed the first month and the placement of leap months. Successive dynasties issued their own reforms; the Shixian calendar of the late Ming, prepared with Jesuit astronomers, remained in use into the Qing. The Republic of China adopted the Gregorian calendar for official purposes in 1912, but the traditional calendar continues for festivals.

Festivals

Chinese New Year (Spring Festival) falls on the first day of the first lunar month; the Mid-Autumn Festival falls on the 15th day of the 8th month; the Dragon Boat Festival is on the 5th day of the 5th month. Related systems include the Vietnamese calendar and Korean calendar.
