Ogg is a free, open container format maintained by the Xiph.Org Foundation, designed for efficiently streaming and storing multimedia. The name derives from "ogging", jargon from the computer game Netrek. Ogg files commonly contain audio encoded with the Vorbis codec (hence "Ogg Vorbis"), video with Theora, or audio with the newer Opus codec.

Structure and codecs

The format organises data into pages, each with a "capture pattern" allowing players to resynchronise within damaged streams. Codecs most associated with Ogg include Vorbis and Opus (audio), FLAC (lossless audio), Theora and Daala (video), and Speex (speech). The related .opus files use Ogg encapsulation specifically for Opus audio.

Adoption

Ogg formats are supported natively in Firefox and Chromium-based browsers and are the default audio format for Wikipedia media. Because Xiph placed the format in the public domain without patent restrictions, it has been widely used in open-source projects and video games.
