Azes I (Greek: Ἄζης Ázēs, Kharoshthi: Aya) was an Indo-Scythian ruler who completed the domination of Scythians in northern India, generally dated to the period around 57–35 BCE. He is best known for founding an era named after him, the "Azes era," which may coincide with the Vikrama era traditionally beginning in 57 BCE.

Reign

Azes inherited and expanded the Indo-Scythian kingdom established by Maues, ruling over the Punjab and the northwestern regions of the Indian subcontinent. His coinage, issued in silver and copper and struck in both Greek and Kharoshthi legends, is abundant in Gandhara and Taxila. On many of his coins the king appears mounted on horseback, a typical feature of Indo-Scythian money.

The Azes era

Azes is credited with establishing an era that commenced in 57 BCE. This "Azes era" is widely believed to be identical with the Vikrama Samvat, one of the two principal Hindu calendars still in use. Inscriptions from Gandhara dated in this era provide an important chronological framework for the history of early South Asian art and Buddhism.

Coinage

The coins of Azes I commonly show the king on horseback holding a whip on the obverse, with deities such as Zeus, Heracles, or Tyche on the reverse. Bilingual legends name him "King of Kings Azes" in Greek and Kharoshthi. Some issues were struck jointly with Azilises, who later succeeded him.
