The Hardanger Bridge (Norwegian: Hardangerbrua) is a suspension bridge across the Eidfjord branch of Hardangerfjord in Vestland county, Norway. It connects the municipalities of Ulvik and Ullensvang and replaced the ferry between Bruravik and Brimnes. When it opened in 2013 it had the longest main span (1,310 metres) of any bridge in Norway and was among the ten longest suspension spans in the world.

Construction

Construction began in February 2009. The bridge has two traffic lanes and a combined pedestrian and cycle path. The two main cables rest on towers 186 and 200 metres high; the steel deck, at 55 metres above the fjord, was assembled from prefabricated sections lifted from barges. Tunnels on both sides — the Vallavik Tunnel to the north and a shorter tunnel toward Eidfjord — connect the bridge directly into the mountainsides.

Impact

The bridge is a key link on the shortest road route between Oslo and Bergen via National Road 13 and Road 7, cutting journey times for the Hardanger region and for traffic between western Norway and the capital. It is tolled, with toll collection projected to repay part of the roughly NOK 2.3 billion construction cost.
