Hội An

Hội An is a city in Quảng Nam Province on the central coast of Vietnam, with a population of roughly 120,000. Once a major Southeast Asian trading port of the Champa kingdom and later of Nguyễn-lord Vietnam, it preserves an exceptionally well-kept old town of wooden shophouses, assembly halls and temples reflecting Vietnamese, Chinese, Japanese and European influences. Hội An Ancient Town was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1999.

History

From the 15th to the 19th century Hội An (known in the West as Faifo) was one of the busiest ports in Southeast Asia, visited by ships from China, Japan, India and Europe trading in silk, ceramics, spices and sandalwood. Japanese and Chinese merchant communities settled in distinct quarters; the Japanese Covered Bridge, built around 1593, remains the town's emblem. As the Thu Bồn River silted up and Đà Nẵng grew in importance under French rule, Hội An declined, which spared its old town from wartime destruction and modern redevelopment.

Attractions

Highlights include the Japanese Covered Bridge, the Fujian and Cantonese assembly halls, old merchant houses such as Tan Ky, tailor shops famous for rapid bespoke clothing, and the monthly lantern festival held on the full moon, when the old town is lit by silk lanterns. The nearby Champa sanctuary of Mỹ Sơn and the beaches of Cù Lao Chàm are popular excursions.
