Permeance

Permeance, in general, is the degree to which a material or structure admits the flow of matter or energy. The term is used in several fields with related but distinct technical meanings, most prominently in electromagnetism and in building science.

Magnetic permeance

In electromagnetism, permeance is the reciprocal of reluctance: it measures how readily a magnetic circuit conducts magnetic flux. It is defined as the ratio of magnetic flux to magnetomotive force, ℙ = Φ / F, and is measured in webers per ampere-turn, equivalently in henries. For a uniform core, permeance equals permeability times cross-sectional area divided by length. In Hopkinson's law, the magnetic analogue of Ohm's law, flux equals magnetomotive force times permeance.

Vapour permeance

In building physics and materials science, permeance is the rate at which water vapour passes through a material under a given vapour-pressure difference, measured in perms (in US customary units, grains per hour per square foot per inch of mercury). Materials are classed as vapour impermeable, semi-impermeable, semi-permeable, or permeable according to their permeance, a property critical to moisture control in wall and roof assemblies. Permeance is the product of a material's intrinsic permeability and its thickness relationships — permeability being the thickness-independent material property.
