Colimit

In category theory, a branch of mathematics, the abstract notion of a colimit generalises constructions such as disjoint unions, direct sums, free products, pushouts, quotients and localisations. A colimit is the dual notion of a limit: it is defined by reversing all arrows in the definition of a limit.

Definition

The colimit of a diagram F : J → C is an object colim F together with morphisms from each F(j) into it (a cocone) that is universal: any other cocone factors uniquely through it. When it exists, the colimit is unique up to unique isomorphism.

Examples

Initial objects, coproducts, coequalisers and pushouts are all special cases of colimits. In the category of sets, the colimit of a diagram is a quotient of the disjoint union of the sets involved; in topology, quotient spaces and gluing constructions arise as colimits.

Properties

Colimits are preserved by left adjoint functors, and a category is cocomplete if all small colimits exist; the categories of sets, groups, rings and topological spaces are all cocomplete.
