Eight Precepts

[Hero image placeholder: lay practitioners observing Uposatha]

The Eight Precepts (Sanskrit: aṣṭāṅga-śīla; Pali: aṭṭhaṅga-sīla) are a list of precepts in Buddhism that are observed by lay devotees on observance days (Uposatha) and festivals. They include general precepts such as refraining from killing and stealing, and more specifically, refraining from eating after noon, entertainments, using perfumes, and sleeping in luxurious beds. The eight precepts are similar to the ten precepts observed by novice monks and nuns, except that the seventh and eighth precepts are combined, the ninth precept (on adornment) is absent, and the tenth precept (on handling money) is excluded.

The precepts

  1. Refraining from killing living beings (pāṇātipātā veramaṇī)
  2. Refraining from taking what is not given (stealing)
  3. Refraining from all sexual activity (complete celibacy)
  4. Refraining from false and harmful speech
  5. Refraining from intoxicants that cause heedlessness
  6. Refraining from eating at the wrong time (after noon until dawn)
  7. Refraining from dancing, singing, music, entertainments, garlands, perfumes, and cosmetics
  8. Refraining from using high and luxurious beds and seats

Practice

The eight precepts expand on the basic five precepts observed by lay Buddhists in daily life. They are traditionally undertaken on Uposatha days — the new moon, full moon, and quarter-moon days — and during retreats and festivals such as Vesak. In Thailand and other Theravada countries, devotees observing the eight precepts often wear white clothing and stay overnight at temples. In Mahayana traditions, the precepts are observed during the six fasting days of the month and on retreats.

The practice is intended to train laypeople in renunciation and meditation, bringing their conduct closer to that of monastics and supporting deeper practice on observance days.
