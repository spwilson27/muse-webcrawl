Christianization of Scandinavia

The Christianization of Scandinavia was the long process, roughly from the 8th to the 12th century, by which Denmark, Norway, and Sweden (and their settlements in Iceland and elsewhere) converted from Norse paganism to Christianity. It proceeded unevenly through missionary activity, royal decree, trade contacts, and gradual syncretism.

Missions and kings

The Frankish monk Ansgar ("Apostle of the North") made missions to Denmark and Sweden in the 820s–850s. Denmark's King Harald Bluetooth proclaimed his realm Christian around 965, as the Jelling runestones boast. Norway's conversion was pressed by Olaf Tryggvason and Olaf II (Saint Olaf) in the late 10th and early 11th centuries, sometimes forcibly. Iceland adopted Christianity peacefully by decision of the Althing in 999/1000.

Completion

Sweden converted latest; pagan worship at Uppsala persisted into the late 11th century, and the archbishopric of Uppsala was established only in 1164 (Lund 1103, Nidaros 1152). Older customs — Yule, burial practices, amulets combining Thor's hammer and the cross — blended into Scandinavian folk Christianity for centuries.
