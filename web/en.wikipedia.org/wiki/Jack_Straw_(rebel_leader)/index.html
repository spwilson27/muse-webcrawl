Jack Straw (rebel leader)

Jack Straw was one of the leaders of the Peasants' Revolt of 1381 in England, alongside Wat Tyler and the priest John Ball. Little is reliably known about him, and some historians have questioned whether he was a distinct historical person or a pseudonym or alias of Tyler himself.

Role in the revolt

The revolt was provoked by a poll tax, labour laws restricting wages after the Black Death, and resentment of serfdom. Rebels from Kent and Essex marched on London in June 1381, opening prisons and attacking the Savoy Palace and the Temple. Chroniclers such as Froissart name Jack Straw as a leader of the Essex contingent.

Death and legend

After Tyler was killed at Smithfield, the revolt collapsed and its leaders were hunted down. According to some chronicles, Straw was captured and executed in London, making a confession — probably fabricated — that the rebels intended to kill the king and abolish the existing order. His name survived in English folklore and in the later idiom of the "man of straw."
