Leicestershire
Ceremonial county in the East Midlands, England
County town: Leicester
Population: approx. 1.1 million (with Leicester and Rutland in some counts)

Leicestershire is a ceremonial county in the East Midlands of England. It is named after its county town and largest settlement, Leicester, and also contains the towns of Loughborough, Hinckley, Melton Mowbray, Coalville, Ashby-de-la-Zouch and Market Harborough, and the city-status borough of Leicester itself, which is administered separately as a unitary authority.

Geography

The county is largely lowland, drained by the River Soar, a tributary of the Trent. Charnwood Forest in the north-west is an area of rugged, ancient volcanic hills and contains Bardon Hill, at 278 metres the highest point in the county. The National Forest straddles the north-west of the county, overlapping into Derbyshire and Staffordshire.

History

Leicester was the Roman town of Ratae Corieltauvorum and a major centre of the Danish Danelaw. The county is famously associated with King Richard III, who was killed at the Battle of Bosworth Field in 1485, fought near Market Bosworth; his remains were rediscovered beneath a Leicester car park in 2012 and reinterred in Leicester Cathedral in 2015. In the 18th and 19th centuries the county industrialised around hosiery, footwear, coal mining and engineering.

Economy and culture

Traditional industries included knitwear and shoe manufacture, while modern Leicestershire has strengths in food production, logistics and higher education, with universities at Leicester and Loughborough. The county is the home of Stilton and Red Leicester cheeses and of the Melton Mowbray pork pie. Leicestershire County Cricket Club plays at Grace Road, and the county's fox-hunting heritage gave its name to the Melton hunt country.
