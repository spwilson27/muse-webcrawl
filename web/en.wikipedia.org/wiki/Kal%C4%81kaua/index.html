Kalākaua (David Laʻamea Kamananakapu Mahinulani Naloiaehuokalani Lumialani Kalākaua; 16 November 1836 – 20 January 1891), sometimes called the Merrie Monarch, was the last king and penultimate monarch of the Kingdom of Hawaii, reigning from 12 February 1874 until his death in 1891.

Reign

Elected king by the legislature after the death of Lunalilo, who left no heir, Kalākaua negotiated the Reciprocity Treaty of 1875 with the United States, boosting Hawaii's sugar economy. He built ʻIolani Palace, revived Hawaiian culture including hula and surfing, and in 1881 became the first reigning monarch to circumnavigate the globe. In 1887 he was forced to sign the so-called Bayonet Constitution, which sharply limited royal power and disenfranchised many Native Hawaiians.

Legacy

Kalākaua died in San Francisco in 1891 and was succeeded by his sister Liliʻuokalani. The Merrie Monarch Festival in Hilo, the world's premier hula competition, honours his revival of Hawaiian culture.
