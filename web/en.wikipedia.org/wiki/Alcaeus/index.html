Alcaeus of Mytilene (c. 625/620 – c. 580 BC) was a lyric poet from the Greek island of Lesbos, credited as the inventor of the Alcaic stanza. A contemporary and countryman of Sappho, he was numbered among the canonical nine lyric poets of ancient Greece. His poems survive only in fragments quoted by later authors and recovered from Egyptian papyri.

Alcaeus, aristocrat, exile and inventor of the Alcaic stanza.

Life

Alcaeus was born into the aristocracy of Mytilene during a period of bitter factional struggle against a succession of tyrants — Melanchrus, Myrsilus and finally Pittacus. He fought against Athens at Sigeum, where he famously lost his shield, and spent periods of exile in Egypt and Thrace. His songs are inseparable from this political warfare: they attack his enemies, celebrate his drinking companions, and lament exile.

Poetry

Alcaeus composed hymns to the gods, love songs, and skolia (drinking songs), but his most influential invention was the allegory of the "ship of state" — the city as a storm-tossed vessel — later taken up by Horace in his ode O navis referent. Horace adapted the Alcaic stanza into Latin, where it became one of the great metres of Roman lyric. Alcaeus also sang of Lesbia-like figures and of wine as solace, themes echoed by later sympotic poets.
