Angular acceleration
Common symbolsα
SI unitrad/s² (radians per second squared)
DefinitionRate of change of angular velocity

In physics, angular acceleration (symbol α, alpha) is the time rate of change of angular velocity. It describes how quickly a rotating object's spin speeds up or slows down, and in three dimensions also how its axis of rotation changes direction. The SI unit is the radian per second squared (rad/s²).

Definition

Angular acceleration is defined as α = dω/dt, where ω is the angular velocity vector, or equivalently as the second derivative of the angular position θ: α = d²θ/dt². For a particle moving in a circle, its tangential acceleration is at = α × r, the cross product of angular acceleration with the radius vector. In two-dimensional rotation about a fixed axis, α is simply a signed scalar: positive when the rotation speeds up in the chosen positive sense (or slows down against it), negative otherwise.

Dynamics

By the rotational analogue of Newton's second law, a net torque τ applied to a rigid body produces angular acceleration according to τ = Iα, where I is the body's moment of inertia about the rotation axis. For motion in three dimensions the relation takes the tensor form given by Euler's equations of rigid body dynamics. Angular acceleration can be measured with gyroscopes and derived from tachometer and encoder data, and it is a central quantity in the design of engines, turbines, flywheels, spacecraft attitude systems and centrifuges.

Special cases

Uniform circular motion has zero angular acceleration even when angular velocity is constant in direction; motion with constant angular acceleration is described by kinematic equations exactly analogous to the SUVAT equations of linear motion.
