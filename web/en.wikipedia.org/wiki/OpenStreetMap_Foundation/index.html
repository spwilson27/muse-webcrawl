The OpenStreetMap Foundation (OSMF) is a non-profit organisation whose aim is to support and enable the development of freely reusable geospatial data. It is the legal and fiscal home of the OpenStreetMap project, which creates and provides free geographic data such as street maps to anyone who wants them.

Role

The Foundation does not own the map data contributed by the community — contributors retain their rights and license their contributions under the Open Database License (ODbL). Instead, the OSMF holds the project's servers, trademarks, and infrastructure, maintains the openstreetmap.org website and API, and organises the annual State of the Map conference. Its affairs are managed by an elected board of directors, and working groups handle areas such as licensing, data quality disputes (the Data Working Group), communications, and engineering operations.

History

OpenStreetMap was founded by Steve Coast in 2004. The Foundation was registered in England and Wales on 22 August 2006 to provide a formal legal structure for the growing project. In 2012 the project's data licence changed from Creative Commons BY-SA to the Open Database License after a lengthy community process administered by the Foundation. Over time, local chapters in various countries have become formally affiliated with the OSMF.

Funding

The Foundation is funded by individual membership fees, corporate memberships, donations, and conference income. Corporate members include major technology and logistics companies that rely on OpenStreetMap data.
