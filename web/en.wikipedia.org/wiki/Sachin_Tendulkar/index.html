Sachin Tendulkar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Sachin Tendulkar in his India blue one-day kit, raising his bat to acknowledge the crowd after a century]

Sachin Ramesh Tendulkar (born 24 April 1973) is an Indian former international cricketer who captained the Indian national team. Widely regarded as one of the greatest batsmen in cricket history, he is the highest run-scorer of all time in international cricket, the only player to score 100 international centuries, and was part of India's 2011 World Cup-winning team. He received the Bharat Ratna, India's highest civilian award, in 2014.

Career

Tendulkar debuted for India aged 16 against Pakistan in 1989 and played for 24 years, retiring in 2013 after his 200th Test match. He scored 15,921 Test runs and 18,426 ODI runs, both records, and was the first batsman to score a double century in a One Day International (2010).

Legacy

Nicknamed the 'Master Blaster' and 'Little Master', Tendulkar's technique, longevity and conduct made him a unifying national figure in India. He later served as a Rajya Sabha MP and mentor in the Indian Premier League.
