Sailor suit
Type: Uniform / garment style
Origin: Royal Navy, 19th century
Features: Flap collar, neckerchief, bell-bottoms

A sailor suit is a uniform traditionally worn by enlisted seamen in a navy or other governmental sea services, distinguished by its flap collar (or "tar flap"), neckerchief and bell-bottom trousers. Later it was adapted as a popular style of children's clothing and, in Japan, as a school uniform for girls (sērā fuku).

History

The style derives from the standardised uniforms adopted by the Royal Navy in the mid-19th century. The broad square collar is often said to have protected the uniform from tarred or powdered hair, and the neckerchief doubled as a sweat rag or bandage. Children's versions became fashionable after 1846, when the young Prince Albert Edward was painted wearing a miniature naval uniform.

School uniform

In Japan, the sailor suit was introduced as a girls' school uniform in the 1920s, modelled on European naval dress, and remains iconic in Japanese popular culture.
