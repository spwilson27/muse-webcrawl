Sporting art

Sporting art is a genre of art depicting sports, originally and most typically the field sports of hunting, shooting, fishing, and horse racing, together with the animals involved — horses, hounds, game birds, and fish. The genre flourished in Britain from the late seventeenth century, closely tied to country-house culture and the landowning patrons who commissioned portraits of their horses, dogs, and estates.

History

Early sporting pictures were largely horse portraits and hunting scenes. George Stubbs (1724–1806) brought anatomical precision and artistic distinction to equine painting, and his works remain the benchmark of the genre. In the nineteenth century John Frederick Herring Sr. painted celebrated racing and coaching scenes, while firms such as Ackermann's mass-produced sporting prints for a wider market. In America, artists including A. F. Tait produced hunting and frontier sporting images for Currier and Ives, and in the twentieth century Sir Alfred Munnings became the best-known painter of racing and hunting subjects.

Themes and collecting

Typical subjects include race meetings, steeplechases, fox hunting, grouse shooting, fly fishing, and individual champion animals. Sporting art remains a recognised collecting field with dedicated auctions and institutions such as the National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame in Saratoga Springs and the collections at the Virginia Museum of Fine Arts.
