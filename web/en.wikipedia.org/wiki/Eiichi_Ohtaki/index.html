Eiichi Ohtaki

Eiichi Ohtaki (大瀧 詠一, Ōtaki Eiichi, 28 July 1948 – 30 December 2013) was a Japanese musician, songwriter and record producer. He first gained prominence as a member of the rock band Happy End in the early 1970s, one of the most influential Japanese-language rock acts, and went on to a celebrated solo career. He is best known for his 1981 album A Long Vacation, a landmark of Japanese city pop that has sold over a million copies.

Career

Born in Esashi, Iwate Prefecture, Ohtaki formed Happy End in 1969 with Haruomi Hosono, Shigeru Suzuki and Takashi Matsumoto. The band's albums Happy End (1970), Kazemachi Roman (1971) and HAPPY END (1973) helped establish rock sung in Japanese. After the band's breakup, Ohtaki pursued solo work and production, founding the Niagara label. His solo releases included Songs (1975) and Niagara Moon (1975), drawing heavily on American pop of the 1950s and 1960s — Phil Spector, the Beach Boys and doo-wop were lifelong influences.

A Long Vacation

Released in March 1981, A Long Vacation became one of the best-selling Japanese albums of its era, winning Album of the Year at the 23rd Japan Record Awards. Its sunlit, Wall-of-Sound-inflected production and songs such as "Kimi wa Tennenshoku" defined the sound of city pop and have been repeatedly reissued. Ohtaki largely withdrew from regular album releases after Each Time (1984), working as a producer, songwriter and radio personality, and curating reissues of his Niagara catalogue.

Death and legacy

Ohtaki died on 30 December 2013 after collapsing at his home in Tokyo, aged 65. Posthumous releases and the global city pop revival of the 2010s brought renewed attention to his work, and A Long Vacation remains a fixture of lists of the greatest Japanese albums.
