Arnold Weinstock

Arnold Weinstock, Baron Weinstock (29 July 1924 – 23 July 2002) was an English industrialist who built the General Electric Company (GEC) into one of Britain's largest electronics and defence conglomerates during his tenure as managing director from 1963 to 1996.

Career

Born in Stoke Newington, London, to Polish-Jewish immigrant parents, Weinstock studied statistics at the London School of Economics and worked in naval intelligence during the Second World War. Joining Radio and Allied Industries, he engineered its merger into GEC and then transformed the company through takeovers of AEI and English Electric.

Style and legacy

Weinstock was famous for tight financial control, detailed monthly cash reporting and a lean head office, accumulating a cash mountain that critics said reflected under-investment. He was knighted in 1970 and made a life peer in 1980. After his retirement GEC's successor Marconi collapsed in the dot-com era.
