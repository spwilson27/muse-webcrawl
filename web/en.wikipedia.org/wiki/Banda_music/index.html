Banda music

Banda is a traditional Mexican musical genre and ensemble form, most strongly associated with the Pacific-coast state of Sinaloa. A banda ensemble is a large brass-and-woodwind band built around clarinets, trumpets, trombones (typically valve trombones), alto horns, a sousaphone-style tuba (charcheta rhythm section), and the tambora, a large bass drum with an attached cymbal that gives the genre its name and its distinctive driving beat.

History

Banda developed in the late nineteenth century, when European military and municipal brass bands — introduced during the French intervention and the Porfirian era — took root in rural northwestern Mexico. German immigrant communities in Sinaloa and neighboring states contributed polkas, waltzes, and marches, which local musicians fused with Mexican son and ranchera traditions. By the mid-twentieth century, banda had become the sound of Sinaloa's celebrations, and the pioneering Banda El Recodo, founded in 1938 by Cruz Lizárraga, carried the style to national and international audiences.

Instrumentation and style

A typical banda numbers between fifteen and twenty musicians: three or four clarinets, three trumpets, three valve trombones, two alto horns, a tuba, tarolas (snare drums), and the tambora. The repertoire includes rancheras, corridos, cumbias, boleros, sones, and dance numbers such as the quebradita that swept Mexican-American dance halls in the early 1990s. Vocal banda — with one or more singers out front — became increasingly dominant from the 1990s onward.

Modern era

In the 2010s, acts such as Banda MS and Banda Los Recoditos brought banda to arenas across the United States, and collaborations with pop and regional Mexican stars pushed the genre onto global streaming charts. Banda remains a cornerstone of música regional mexicana alongside mariachi, norteño, and corridos tumbados.
