Rowing

Rowing is the act of propelling a boat through the water using oars, in which the rower sits facing the stern and the oars act as levers pivoting on oarlocks. The word also names the sport of crew, in which athletes race in long, narrow shells with sliding seats.

Forms

Rowing takes many forms: recreational rowing in wherries and dinghies, traditional fixed-seat rowing (such as Cornish pilot gig racing), ocean rowing across seas and oceans, and competitive sweep rowing and sculling on flat water. In sweep rowing each rower uses one oar; in sculling, two smaller oars.

History

Oared vessels date from ancient Egypt, Greece and Rome, whose warships and merchant galleys depended on rowers. Rowing as a sport developed in 18th- and 19th-century England, with the Oxford–Cambridge Boat Race (from 1829) and Henley Royal Regatta (from 1839) among its landmark events. It became one of the original modern Olympic sports.
