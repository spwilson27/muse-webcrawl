Irregular verb

An irregular verb, in the context of linguistics, is a verb whose conjugation follows a different pattern than that of regular verbs in the same language. A verb whose conjugation follows a different pattern is called a defective verb if its conjugation does not form all its forms.

In English, irregular verbs typically change their past tense and past participle forms in ways not covered by the standard "-ed" rule. Examples include sing, sang, sung, go, went, gone, and be, was/were, been.

English irregular verbs

English has roughly 200 irregular verbs, though many are archaic or rarely used. The irregular forms descend largely from Old English strong verbs, which used vowel changes (ablaut) inherited from Proto-Indo-European.

In other languages

Many languages have irregular verbs. In French, common irregular verbs include être (to be), avoir (to have), aller (to go), and faire (to do/make). In German, strong verbs change their stem vowel in the past tense. In Spanish, verbs such as ser, ir, tener, and estar are highly irregular.
