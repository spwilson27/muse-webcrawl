Burton upon Trent

Burton upon Trent, also known as Burton-on-Trent or simply Burton, is a market town in the borough of East Staffordshire in Staffordshire, England, close to the border with Derbyshire. In 2021, it had a population of 76,270. The demonym for residents of the town is Burtonian. Burton is located on the River Trent and is historically known for brewing; the town was home to numerous breweries at its peak, including Bass, Ind Coope, and Marston's.

History

Burton grew up around Burton Abbey, founded in 1003 by the thegn Wulfric Spott. The town received a market charter in 1200. A battle was fought at Burton Bridge in 1322 during the Despenser War, when Edward II defeated the Earl of Lancaster. The abbey was dissolved in 1539, and the town later became known for its woollen and leather industries before brewing came to dominate.

Brewing

Burton became the pre-eminent brewing town of Britain in the 19th century, thanks to the qualities of its water, rich in dissolved salts from gypsum-bearing strata, which suited pale ale production — a process known as "Burtonisation". At its height in the 1880s there were more than 30 breweries in the town. Burton ale, and later pale ales such as Bass Pale Ale and India pale ale shipped to India, were exported worldwide. The National Brewery Centre (formerly the Bass Museum) recorded the industry's history.

Geography

The town lies on both banks of the River Trent, about 28 miles (45 km) east of Stafford and 11 miles (18 km) southwest of Derby. The River Dove forms part of the boundary nearby. Washlands adjacent to the Trent flood regularly in winter.

Transport

Burton-on-Trent railway station is served by CrossCountry trains between Birmingham, Derby, and Nottingham. The A38 dual carriageway bypasses the town, and the A511 and A444 provide links to Leicester and Nuneaton.
