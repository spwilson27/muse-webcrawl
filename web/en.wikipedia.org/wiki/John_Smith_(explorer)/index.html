John Smith (explorer)

Captain John Smith (c. 1580 – 21 June 1631) was an English soldier, explorer, colonial governor, admiral of New England, and author. He played an important role in the establishment of Jamestown, the first permanent English settlement in North America, led expeditions exploring Chesapeake Bay, and is associated with the story of Pocahontas.

Early adventures

Smith served as a mercenary in Europe and against the Ottoman Turks in Hungary, was captured and enslaved, escaped, and travelled widely before joining the Virginia Company's expedition that founded Jamestown in 1607.

Virginia

Captured by Powhatan's people in December 1607, Smith later wrote that Pocahontas, daughter of Chief Powhatan, saved his life — an account historians debate. As president of the council (1608–09) he enforced discipline ("he that will not worke shall not eate"), traded for food, and explored and mapped Chesapeake Bay. Injured by a gunpowder explosion, he returned to England in 1609.

New England and writings

In 1614 he explored and named New England, promoting colonisation in works such as A Description of New England (1616), New Englands Trials, and his Generall Historie of Virginia (1624). He died in London in 1631.
