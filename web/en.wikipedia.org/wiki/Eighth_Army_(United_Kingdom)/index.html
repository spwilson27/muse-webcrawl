Eighth Army (United Kingdom)

Eighth Army armour in North Africa

The Eighth Army was a field army formation of the British Army during the Second World War, famous for its role in the North African campaign against Erwin Rommel's Afrika Korps. A multinational force of British, Commonwealth, Polish, Free French and other troops, it later fought through Sicily and Italy.

North Africa

Formed in September 1941 from the Western Desert Force, the Eighth Army launched Operation Crusader to relieve Tobruk under General Alan Cunningham, then Neil Ritchie. After the defeat at Gazala and the fall of Tobruk in 1942, General Claude Auchinleck halted Rommel at the First Battle of El Alamein. In August 1942 Bernard Montgomery took command, winning the defensive battle of Alam el Halfa and then, in October–November 1942, the decisive Second Battle of El Alamein. The army advanced 2,000 miles across Libya into Tunisia, fighting at the Mareth Line and Wadi Akarit, before the Axis surrender in Africa in May 1943.

Italy

The Eighth Army landed in Sicily in July 1943 and on the Italian mainland at Reggio and Taranto that September, then fought up Italy's Adriatic coast through the sangro, Ortona, the Gothic Line, and the final spring 1945 offensive that ended the campaign. Its veterans — who called themselves the "Desert Rats" legacy aside, many wore the crusader shield insignia — were celebrated in Britain as one of the most famous formations of the war.
