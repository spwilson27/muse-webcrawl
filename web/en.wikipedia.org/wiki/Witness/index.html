Witness

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A witness is a person who, being present, sees an event or has knowledge relevant to a legal proceeding and gives testimony (evidence) about it, usually under oath or affirmation and subject to cross-examination. Witnesses are classified as lay (fact) witnesses, who testify to what they perceived, and expert witnesses, who may give opinion evidence within their expertise (forensic science, medicine, valuation and the like).

Rules and protections

Competence (capacity to understand and communicate) and compellability (being legally obliged to testify, usually under subpoena) are governed by evidence law, with privileges excusing spouses, lawyers' clients, and those claiming self-incrimination protection. False testimony is the crime of perjury. Because identification and memory evidence is fallible — eyewitness misidentification figures in most DNA-exoneration cases — courts admit psychological research and give cautionary jury instructions. Intimidation of witnesses is criminalised and countered by protection programmes (the US federal WITSEC relocated thousands of witnesses), anonymity orders and special measures (screens, video links) for vulnerable witnesses such as children and complainants in sexual cases.
