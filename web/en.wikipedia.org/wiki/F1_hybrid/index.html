F1 hybrid

DefinitionFirst filial generation of offspring from two genetically distinct, usually inbred, parents
Key propertyUniformity and hybrid vigour (heterosis)
Used inAgriculture, horticulture, animal breeding
NotationF1 = first filial generation; F2 = its selfed/crossed offspring

An F1 hybrid (also written F1 hybrid) is the first filial generation of offspring of distinctly different parental types, usually produced by crossing two highly inbred lines. F1 hybrids are widely used in agriculture and horticulture because they are genetically uniform and frequently display hybrid vigour, or heterosis — growth, size or yield exceeding that of either parent.

Production

To produce F1 seed, breeders maintain two parent lines, each inbred over many generations until it is nearly homozygous. Crossing the two lines gives seed in which every plant carries one set of alleles from each parent, making the crop highly uniform in height, maturity and fruit characteristics. In crops such as maize, preventing self-pollination of the female parent (historically by detasseling, now often by male sterility) is essential to guarantee the cross. In many vegetables, including tomatoes, brassicas and carrots, F1 seed is produced by hand pollination or by using self-incompatibility systems.

Advantages and disadvantages

Uniformity simplifies mechanised harvesting and marketing, and heterosis can raise yields substantially — the adoption of hybrid maize in the United States in the 1930s–1950s is credited with a large share of the crop's yield gains, following the work of George Harrison Shull and Edward Murray East. However, seed saved from an F1 crop (the F2 generation) segregates genetically, producing variable and often inferior plants, so farmers typically buy fresh F1 seed each season. This dependence on seed companies, and the cost of producing hybrid seed, are recurring points of debate in agricultural policy.

In animal breeding

The term is also used for first-generation crosses between distinct breeds or species — for example, F1 cattle crosses bred for vigour and maternal traits, and aquarium fish or laboratory animals produced from two inbred strains, valued because every individual is genetically identical (though heterozygous).
