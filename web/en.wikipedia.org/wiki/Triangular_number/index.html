A triangular number or triangle number counts the number of objects that can form an equilateral triangle, in the same way that square numbers count objects that form a square. The nth triangular number is the number of dots in a triangular arrangement with n dots on each side, and equals the sum of the first n natural numbers:

Tn = 1 + 2 + … + n = n(n + 1) / 2

The first few triangular numbers are 0, 1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 21, 28, 36, 45, 55. According to legend, the young Carl Friedrich Gauss rediscovered the formula at school when asked to sum the numbers from 1 to 100, quickly obtaining 5050 by pairing terms.

Properties

The sum of two consecutive triangular numbers is a perfect square: Tn−1 + Tn = n². Every positive integer is the sum of at most three triangular numbers — a result conjectured by Fermat and proved by Gauss in 1796, who recorded in his diary "ΕΥΡΗΚΑ! num = Δ + Δ + Δ". The square of the nth triangular number equals the sum of the first n cubes (Nicomachus's theorem). A number T is triangular if and only if 8T + 1 is a perfect square.

Applications

Triangular numbers count handshakes among a group of people, the pairs in round-robin tournaments, and the pins in ten-pin bowling (T4 = 10). They appear as the third diagonal of Pascal's triangle and as binomial coefficients: Tn = C(n + 1, 2).
