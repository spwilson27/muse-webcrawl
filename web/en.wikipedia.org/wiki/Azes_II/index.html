Azes II was an Indo-Scythian king who ruled in north-western India, in the region of Gandhara and the Punjab, roughly in the late 1st century BCE. He is known principally from his extensive coinage, struck in silver and copper with Greek and Kharoshthi legends.

Coinage

His coins commonly depict the king on horseback with a spear, accompanied by Greek inscriptions naming him "the great king, king of kings". Many of his issues were later overstruck or imitated, making the chronology of his reign debated among numismatists.

Legacy

Azes II is often associated in popular tradition with one of the Three Wise Men, though there is no historical evidence for this. His decline marks the waning of Indo-Scythian power before the rise of the Indo-Parthians under Gondophares.
