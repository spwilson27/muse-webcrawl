Pratapgad

Pratapgad (Marathi: प्रतापगड, literally "Fort of Valour") is a mountain fort in the Satara district of Maharashtra, India, near the hill station of Mahabaleshwar. It is famous as the site of the Battle of Pratapgad in 1659, in which the Maratha leader Shivaji defeated the forces of the Bijapur sultanate under Afzal Khan, a turning point in the rise of the Maratha kingdom.

History

Shivaji commissioned the fort's construction in 1656, recognising the strategic value of its position commanding the Par pass and the route between the Konkan coast and the Deccan plateau. Built under the supervision of Moropant Pingale, the fort consists of an upper and lower fortification on a spur of the Sahyadri range, with steep cliffs protecting most approaches.

Battle of Pratapgad

In 1659 the Bijapur court sent Afzal Khan, a veteran general, to crush Shivaji. After negotiations, the two leaders met at the foot of the fort on 10 November 1659. According to Maratha accounts, Afzal Khan attempted to kill Shivaji during an embrace; Shivaji, prepared for treachery, killed Khan with a concealed tiger-claw weapon (wagh nakh). Leaderless, the Bijapuri army was routed by Maratha forces concealed in the surrounding jungle. The victory brought Shivaji substantial territory, artillery, and prestige, and is commemorated by a tomb and memorial at the site.

Today

The fort is a popular tourist destination, reached by road from Mahabaleshwar. It contains a temple of Bhavani, temples and memorials associated with Shivaji, and a large statue of Shivaji unveiled in 1957. See also Maratha Empire.
