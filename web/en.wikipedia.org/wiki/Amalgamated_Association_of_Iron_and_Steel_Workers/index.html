Amalgamated Association of Iron and Steel Workers

The Amalgamated Association of Iron and Steel Workers was an American labour union formed in 1876 by merging several unions of ironworkers. It became one of the strongest craft unions in the iron and steel industry and a significant affiliate of the American Federation of Labor.

Growth and conflict

The Amalgamated represented skilled puddlers, heaters, rollers and other workers whose knowledge was difficult to replace. It negotiated wage scales tied to the price of iron and steel products. Its most famous confrontation was the 1892 Homestead strike against Carnegie Steel, where a lockout, Pinkerton agents and state troops broke the union's power at the Homestead works.

Decline and legacy

After Homestead and later defeats in steel organising, the union weakened as large integrated steel companies resisted recognition. Surviving locals later fed into twentieth-century steelworker organising, including the Steel Workers Organizing Committee and the United Steelworkers. The Amalgamated's history illustrates both the strength and limits of craft unionism in heavy industry.
