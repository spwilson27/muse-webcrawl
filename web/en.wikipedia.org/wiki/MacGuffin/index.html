MacGuffin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In fiction, a MacGuffin (sometimes McGuffin) is an object, device, or event that is necessary to the plot and the motivation of the characters, but insignificant, unimportant, or irrelevant in itself. The term was originated by Angus MacPhail, a screenwriter who worked with Alfred Hitchcock, and popularized by Hitchcock himself, who explained it with a joke about a device for trapping lions in the Scottish Highlands — "but there are no lions in the Scottish Highlands"; exactly: no MacGuffin.

History and use

Hitchcock used MacGuffins extensively — the uranium in Notorious, the "government secrets" in North by Northwest — while insisting the audience cared only that the characters cared. The concept recurs across genres: the One Ring's cousins include the briefcase in Pulp Fiction, the Maltese Falcon in the film of the same name, the Death Star plans in Star Wars, and the Ark of the Covenant in Raiders of the Lost Ark. George Lucas distinguished a MacGuffin from a mere plot device by arguing the audience must also care about it. The term is sometimes used pejoratively for thin or arbitrary motivation.
