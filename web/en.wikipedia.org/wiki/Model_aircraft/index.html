Model aircraft

A model aircraft is a small unmanned aircraft, built either as a flying replica of a full-size design or as a non-flying display model. Model aircraft are typically divided into static models, built for display, and flying models, used for sport, competition, and research.

Flying models

  • Free flight models fly without any control after launch, powered by rubber bands, small engines, or electric motors, or soar as gliders.
  • Control line models are flown in a circle, tethered to the pilot by two thin wires that operate the elevator.
  • Radio-controlled (RC) models are steered by radio signals and may be powered by electric motors, glow-fuel engines, or miniature turbines.

Static models

Static scale models are usually built from plastic kits, wood, or metal, and are finished to replicate real aircraft in fine detail. Popular scales include 1:72, 1:48, and 1:32.

History and organizations

Model aviation predates full-size powered flight — experimenters such as Alphonse Pénaud flew rubber-powered models in the 1870s — and the hobby expanded greatly in the twentieth century. National bodies such as the Academy of Model Aeronautics in the United States and the international FAI organize competitions and safety codes.
