Magic in Harry Potter

CreatorJ. K. Rowling
Source worksHarry Potter novels (1997–2007)
Key elementsWands, spells, potions, magical creatures, Hogwarts

In J. K. Rowling's Harry Potter series, magic is an innate supernatural ability possessed by witches and wizards, inherited largely genetically and concealed from the non-magical world ("Muggles") under the International Statute of Secrecy. Children born to magical families normally display accidental magic in childhood and are trained from age eleven at schools such as Hogwarts School of Witchcraft and Wizardry. Rare non-magical children of magical parents are called Squibs, while magical children of Muggle parents ("Muggle-borns") are a recurring political flashpoint in the series.

Casting and wands

Most directed magic is channelled through a wand, an instrument of wood with a core of magical substance (such as phoenix feather, unicorn hair or dragon heartstring) that "chooses the wizard". Spells are typically performed with an incantation — often derived from Latin, such as Wingardium Leviosa for levitation or Expecto Patronum for the Patronus Charm — together with prescribed wand movements, though advanced practitioners can cast non-verbally and a few can perform wandless magic. Underage magic outside school is detected and restricted by the Ministry of Magic through "the Trace".

Branches of magic

Hogwarts subjects map the discipline: Transfiguration, Charms, Potions, Defence Against the Dark Arts, Herbology, Astronomy, Divination, Care of Magical Creatures and others. Powerful branches include Apparition (instantaneous travel), Legilimency and Occlumency (mind-reading and its defence), and the Dark Arts, whose gravest expressions are the three Unforgivable Curses — Imperius, Cruciatus and the Killing Curse Avada Kedavra — whose use earns a life sentence in Azkaban prison. Gamp's Law of Elemental Transfiguration sets limits on conjuring, notably that food cannot be created from nothing.

Magical world

The magical community sustains its own government (the Ministry of Magic), economy (the goblin-run bank Gringotts, using Galleons, Sickles and Knuts), sport (Quidditch, played on broomsticks), creatures from house-elves to dragons, and objects such as the Marauder's Map, the Invisibility Cloak and the Horcruxes in which Lord Voldemort hides fragments of his soul — the central devices of the series' plot.
