Robert Brown (botanist, born 1773)

Born21 December 1773, Montrose, Scotland
Died10 June 1858, London, England
Known forBrownian motion; Australian botany; cell nucleus

Robert Brown FRS FRSE (21 December 1773 – 10 June 1858) was a Scottish botanist and palaeobotanist, "the father of Australian botany" and one of the leading naturalists of his age. As naturalist aboard HMS Investigator under Matthew Flinders (1801–1805), he collected and later described some 3,400 plant species from Australia, about 2,000 of them new to science.

Brownian motion and the nucleus

In 1827, observing pollen grains of Clarkia pulchella suspended in water under a microscope, Brown described the ceaseless jittering movement now called Brownian motion, a phenomenon later explained by Einstein and Perrin as evidence for atoms. In 1831 he drew attention to the structure present in plant cells that he named the "nucleus".

Career

Brown worked for decades at the British Museum, where he built the botanical collections, and described many plant families and genera in his Prodromus Florae Novae Hollandiae (1810). He was librarian to Sir Joseph Banks and inherited responsibility for Banks's herbarium and library.
