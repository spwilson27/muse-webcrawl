Springer Science+Business Media

Founded: 1842 (Berlin)
Founder: Julius Springer
Headquarters: Berlin / Heidelberg
Successor: Springer Nature (2015)

Springer Science+Business Media, commonly known simply as Springer, was a German multinational publishing company of scientific, technical, and medical books and journals. Founded in Berlin in 1842 by bookseller Julius Springer, it grew into one of the world's largest academic publishers, issuing thousands of journals and book titles and hosting the SpringerLink platform. In 2015 Springer merged with Nature Publishing Group and other Macmillan science holdings to form Springer Nature.

History

Julius Springer opened a bookshop and publishing house in Berlin in 1842. Under his descendants the firm became a leading publisher of engineering, mathematics, and medicine; its distinctive yellow covers and the "springer" knight chess-piece logo became hallmarks of scientific monographs. After the Second World War the company expanded from Heidelberg and opened offices in New York (1964) and worldwide. The twentieth century saw acquisitions including J. F. Bergmann, Steinkopff, and the Dutch publisher Kluwer Academic Publishers (merged in 2004), which greatly enlarged its journal list. Financial investors Candover and Cinven bought the group in 2003, and EQT and GIC acquired it in 2009 before its combination into Springer Nature.

Publishing programme

Springer published more than 2,000 journals and many thousands of books annually, including the famous Lecture Notes in Mathematics and Lecture Notes in Computer Science series, major reference works, and the computer-science and mathematics backlists of Kluwer. Imprints under the Springer umbrella included Springer Spektrum, Springer Gabler, and BioMed Central, an open-access publisher acquired in 2008.
