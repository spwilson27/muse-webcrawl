Egbert of Wessex

Egbert

Reign: 802 – 839

Predecessor: Beorhtric

Successor: Æthelwulf

Died: 839

House: House of Wessex

Egbert (also spelled Ecgberht; c. 770/775 – 839) was King of Wessex from 802 until his death in 839. His father was Ealhmund of Kent. In the 780s Egbert was forced into exile at the court of Charlemagne by Offa of Mercia and Beorhtric of Wessex, but on Beorhtric's death in 802 he returned and took the throne.

Reign

Little is known of the first two decades of Egbert's reign, during which he appears to have maintained Wessex's independence against the Mercian supremacy. In 825 he decisively defeated Beornwulf of Mercia at the Battle of Ellandun, a turning point that ended Mercian dominance of southern England: Kent, Surrey, Sussex and Essex submitted to Wessex, and the East Angles requested Egbert's protection. In 829 he conquered Mercia itself and received the submission of the Northumbrians, and the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle lists him as the eighth bretwalda (overlord of Britain).

Later years

Mercia regained its independence in 830, but Egbert retained control of the south-east. In 836 he was defeated by the Danes at Carhampton, though in 838 he defeated a combined force of Vikings and Cornish at the Battle of Hingston Down. His son Æthelwulf, who governed Kent as sub-king, succeeded him on his death in 839. Egbert was buried at Winchester.
