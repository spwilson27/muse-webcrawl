Lake Biwa

LocationShiga Prefecture, Japan
Surface areaApprox. 670 km²
Max. depthApprox. 104 m
AgeOver 4 million years (ancient lake)
OutflowSeta River (Yodo River system)

Lake Biwa (琵琶湖, Biwa-ko) is the largest freshwater lake in Japan, located entirely within Shiga Prefecture northeast of Kyoto. Named for its resemblance in shape to the biwa lute, it is one of the world's roughly twenty ancient lakes, with a basin more than four million years old.

Ecology

Because of its great age, the lake hosts an unusually rich and endemic fauna: dozens of species and subspecies — including the Biwa trout and several gobies and catfishes — are found nowhere else. It supports a long tradition of fisheries, including ayu fishing with trained cormorants and the winter trapping of funa, and is a vital wintering site for migratory birds, protected under the Ramsar Convention since 1993.

Importance

Lake Biwa supplies drinking water to some 14 million people in the Kansai region via the Seta and Yodo rivers. Historically it formed part of the transport network linking Kyoto to the Sea of Japan, and its shores are dotted with celebrated sites: the castles of Hikone and Nagahama, the temple island of Chikubu, the floating torii of Shirahige shrine, and Enryaku-ji on Mount Hiei. The "Eight Views of Ōmi" celebrated these shores in Japanese and Chinese art for centuries. Water quality declined with eutrophication in the 1970s, prompting strict prefectural controls that have since improved the lake.
