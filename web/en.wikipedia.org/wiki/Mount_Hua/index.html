Mount Hua

Mount Hua (Huashan)
Elevation2,154.9 m (South Peak)
LocationHuayin, Shaanxi, China
SignificanceWestern of the Five Great Mountains; sacred Taoist mountain

Mount Hua (华山, Huàshān) is a mountain in Shaanxi Province, China, near the city of Huayin about 120 kilometres east of Xi'an. It is the westernmost of China's Five Great Mountains and has long been one of the most sacred Taoist mountains, dotted with temples and hermitages. Mount Hua is famed for its sheer granite peaks and precipitous, vertigo-inducing footpaths, and is often described as one of the most dangerous tourist hikes in the world.

Geography

The mountain has five principal peaks — North, South (the highest, at 2,154.9 metres), East, West, and Middle — connected by cliff-edge trails. The ascent historically began at the Jade Spring Temple at the mountain's base and climbed through the narrow defile of Huashan Gorge. Famous features include the Thousand-Foot Precipice, the Hundred-Foot Crevice, and the Plank Walk in the Sky, a path of wooden boards bolted to a sheer cliff face on South Peak, where visitors clip into safety harnesses above a drop of hundreds of metres.

History and religion

Religious activity on the mountain dates back at least to the Han dynasty, and it became an important centre of Taoist practice; the sage Chen Tuan is said to have lived and died in seclusion here in the 10th century. Temples such as the Xiyue Temple at the foot of the mountain served imperial sacrifices to the god of Mount Hua. In popular culture the mountain gives its name to the Mount Hua Sect of wuxia fiction, notably in Jin Yong's novels.
