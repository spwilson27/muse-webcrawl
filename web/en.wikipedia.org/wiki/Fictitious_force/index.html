Fictitious force

A fictitious force is a force that appears to act on a mass whose motion is described using a non-inertial frame of reference, such as a rotating reference frame or one accelerating in a straight line. It is not the result of any physical interaction between objects, but arises from the acceleration a of the non-inertial frame itself. As stated by Iro: "This additional force, due to the relative motion of two reference frames, is called a pseudo-force."

When the equations of motion are written in a non-inertial frame, extra terms appear that can be interpreted as forces. The total fictitious force on an object of mass m in a frame whose origin accelerates with A and which rotates with angular velocity Ω is:

Ffic = −mA − m dΩ/dt × r − m Ω × (Ω × r) − 2m Ω × vrot

Background

The surface of the Earth is a rotating reference frame. To solve classical mechanics problems correctly using an Earth-based reference frame, the fictitious forces of centrifugal force, Coriolis force and Euler force must be introduced. Fictitious forces do not arise from interactions with other bodies and have no reaction counterpart, so they appear to violate Newton's third law; this is one criterion by which they can be recognised.

Detection of a non-inertial frame

Observers inside a closed box moving with constant acceleration cannot distinguish their motion from a gravitational field using any local experiment in classical mechanics — a fact connected with Einstein's equivalence principle. However, rotation can be detected absolutely: a Foucault pendulum, a gyroscope, or the flattening (equatorial bulge) of a rotating body all reveal rotation without reference to the outside world.

Examples

Acceleration in a straight line

In an accelerating car, passengers feel pressed back into their seats. In the car's frame this is a fictitious force acting toward the rear, balancing the forward frictional force from the seat so that the passenger remains at rest in that frame.

Circular motion

In a rotating frame, the centrifugal force points radially outward and the Coriolis force acts on objects moving within the frame, deflecting them perpendicular to their velocity. The Euler force appears when the rotation rate itself changes.
