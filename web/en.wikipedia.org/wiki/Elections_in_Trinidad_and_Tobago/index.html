Elections in Trinidad and Tobago are held at the national level for the House of Representatives and locally for regional corporations and the Tobago House of Assembly. The twin-island republic is a parliamentary democracy on the Westminster model, with the prime minister drawn from the majority in the House.

General elections

The 41 members of the House of Representatives are elected by first-past-the-post in single-member constituencies — 39 in Trinidad and 2 in Tobago — for terms of up to five years. The Senate is appointed. The president, head of state, is elected by an electoral college of both houses of Parliament. Elections are administered by the Elections and Boundaries Commission.

Political history

Universal adult suffrage was introduced in 1946. The People's National Movement (PNM), founded by Eric Williams, dominated politics after independence from Britain in 1962 and has alternated in government with opposition alliances including the United National Congress (UNC) and the National Alliance for Reconstruction, which won a landslide in 1986. Voting patterns have historically shown ethnic dimensions, with the PNM drawing mainly Afro-Trinidadian support and the UNC mainly Indo-Trinidadian support, though both parties campaign nationally.

Tobago

Tobago elects its own House of Assembly, responsible for many internal affairs of the island, in separate elections held every four years.
