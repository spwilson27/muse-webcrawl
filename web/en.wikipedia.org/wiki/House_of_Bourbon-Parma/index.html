House of Bourbon-Parma

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The House of Bourbon-Parma (Italian: Casa di Borbone-Parma) is an Italian branch of the House of Bourbon, itself a cadet branch of the French Capetian dynasty. It ruled the Duchy of Parma and Piacenza from 1748 until the duchy's annexation into the Kingdom of Italy in 1860, with interruptions during the Napoleonic era.

Origins

The line descends from Philip, Duke of Parma (1720–1765), third son of Philip V of Spain and Elisabeth Farnese. Under the Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle (1748), Philip received the duchies of Parma, Piacenza, and Guastalla, founding the Bourbon-Parma dynasty. His descendants ruled a small but culturally notable Italian court through the 18th century.

19th century

After the Napoleonic Wars, the duchy was assigned for life to Napoleon's widow, Marie Louise of Austria, and the Bourbon-Parma line temporarily held the Duchy of Lucca before returning to Parma in 1847. Duke Robert I, who came to the throne as a child in 1854, was the last ruling duke; he was deposed in 1859–1860 as Parma joined the new Italian kingdom.

Later history

Members of the family retained prominence in European royalty. Robert I's daughter Zita became the last Empress of Austria as wife of Charles I. Another branch of the family reigned in Luxembourg descending from Prince Felix, consort of Grand Duchess Charlotte. The current head of the house is Carlos, Duke of Parma (born 1970), a member of the Dutch royal family by birth, who also represents a Carlist claim to the Spanish throne.
