M. S. Golwalkar

Madhav Sadashiv Golwalkar (19 February 1906 – 5 June 1973), popularly known as Guruji, was an Indian politician and the second Sarsanghchalak (chief) of the Rashtriya Swayamsevak Sangh (RSS), the Hindu nationalist volunteer organisation, a position he held for 33 years, from 1940 until his death in 1973. Under his leadership the RSS grew into one of the largest volunteer organisations in the world, and Golwalkar became one of the most influential — and controversial — thinkers of the Hindutva movement.

Early life

Golwalkar was born in Ramtek, near Nagpur, in a Marathi-speaking family. He studied at Banaras Hindu University, earning a master's degree in zoology, and taught there for a time — the source of the honorific "Guruji" ("teacher") by which his followers knew him. Drawn first to the Ramakrishna Mission and a period of spiritual seeking in Bengal, he returned to Nagpur, took a law degree, and came under the influence of RSS founder K. B. Hedgewar, who designated him as his successor shortly before his death in 1940.

Leadership of the RSS

As Sarsanghchalak, Golwalkar steered the RSS away from direct participation in the freedom struggle and towards long-term cultural and organisational work, expanding the network of local branches (shakhas) across India. After the assassination of Mahatma Gandhi in 1948 by Nathuram Godse, a former RSS member, the organisation was banned and Golwalkar imprisoned; the ban was lifted in 1949 after the RSS adopted a formal constitution committing it to cultural work and loyalty to the Indian constitution. Golwalkar subsequently oversaw the creation of a family of affiliated organisations (the Sangh Parivar), including the Vishva Hindu Parishad (1964).

Thought and writings

Golwalkar's ideas are principally set out in We, or Our Nationhood Defined (1939) and the posthumous compilation Bunch of Thoughts (1966). He defined the Indian nation in cultural terms, identifying nationhood with a shared Hindu culture, and argued that the unity of India rests on this cultural foundation. His views on minorities, caste and the nation have been a continuing subject of scholarly and political controversy; critics describe them as exclusionary, while admirers regard him as a nation-builder and a saintly, selfless organiser.

Death and legacy

Golwalkar died of cancer in Nagpur on 5 June 1973. He remains the longest-serving chief of the RSS and a formative figure in the development of Hindu nationalism; India's government issued a commemorative postage stamp in his honour in 1999.
