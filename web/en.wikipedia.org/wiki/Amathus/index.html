Amathus (Ancient Greek: Ἀμαθοῦς, Amathous) was an ancient royal city of Cyprus, on the island's southern coast about 10 kilometres east of modern Limassol. One of the ten city-kingdoms of Cyprus, Amathus was inhabited from the early Iron Age and remained an important port and cult centre into the Roman and early Byzantine periods before being abandoned in about the 7th century CE. Its acropolis was crowned by a famous sanctuary of Aphrodite, whom the Greeks honoured with the cult title Amathusia.

Unlike most Cypriot kingdoms, Amathus retained an indigenous, non-Greek character: its people, often identified with the "Eteocypriots", left inscriptions in an undeciphered language written in the Cypriot syllabary alongside Greek, and the city famously resisted Hellenization — it fought against the pro-Greek Onesilus of Salamis during the Ionian Revolt (499 BCE).

Mythology and cult

Greek myth made Amathus a foundation of Amathus, son of Aërias, and connected it with the cult of Aphrodite and Adonis; Plutarch relates a version of the Ariadne myth set here. The great temple on the acropolis, rebuilt in Roman times, housed one of the island's principal Aphrodite cults, and a colossal stone vase carved from a single block — one of the largest known from antiquity, now in the Louvre — stood in the sanctuary.

History

The city came under Assyrian, Egyptian and Persian domination in turn, paid tribute to Alexander the Great, and passed to the Ptolemies, under whom it declined relative to other ports, and then to Rome. It prospered again in the Roman period, when the acropolis temple was rebuilt in the 1st century CE, but Arab raids in the 7th century ended its occupation; the site's stones were later quarried, some reportedly for the Suez Canal works and the harbour of Limassol.

Archaeology

Excavations by a French mission since the 1970s have uncovered the agora, baths, the palace complex on the acropolis, the harbour works (now submerged), and extensive necropolises. Amathus is one of the richest archaeological sites of Cyprus, notable for its bilingual Eteocypriot-Greek inscriptions, key evidence for the island's pre-Greek language.
