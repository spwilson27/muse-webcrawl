Literary genre

A literary genre is a category of literature defined by shared style, form, or subject-matter conventions. Genre classification goes back to Aristotle's Poetics, which distinguished epic, lyric and drama and, within drama, tragedy and comedy. Genres are not fixed: they evolve, subdivide and hybridise as writers and audiences develop new expectations.

Major forms

  • Poetry — including epic, lyric, narrative and dramatic verse.
  • Prose fiction — the novel, novella and short story.
  • Drama — tragedy, comedy, history, tragicomedy.
  • Nonfiction — biography, memoir, essay, history, journalism.

Genres of fiction

Modern publishing divides fiction into genre categories such as mystery and crime, science fiction, fantasy, romance, horror, thriller, western, and historical fiction, alongside "literary fiction" marketed as a category of its own. Each genre carries conventions of plot, setting and tone — the locked-room puzzle, the quest narrative, the haunted house — which writers may follow, subvert or combine, producing subgenres such as hard science fiction, cozy mystery, epic fantasy and domestic noir.

Function

Genres serve as a compact between writer and reader, guiding expectations, shelving and marketing. Literary theorists note that genres are institutions as much as forms: they shape how works are reviewed, taught, awarded and remembered.
