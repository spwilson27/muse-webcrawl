Richard Mather

Richard Mather (1596 – 22 April 1669) was a New England Puritan minister, a leader of the first generation of Massachusetts Bay colonists, and patriarch of the Mather dynasty that included his son Increase Mather and grandson Cotton Mather.

Life in England

Born in Lowton, Lancashire, Mather studied at Brasenose College, Oxford, but left without a degree. He became a schoolmaster and minister at Toxteth, Liverpool, where his Puritan preaching led to suspension by church authorities in the 1630s under Archbishop Laud's policies.

New England

Mather emigrated in 1635 and became teacher of the church at Dorchester, Massachusetts, serving there for the rest of his life. He was a principal author of the Bay Psalm Book (1640), the first book printed in British North America, and helped draft the Cambridge Platform (1648) defining Congregational church government.

Half-Way Covenant

Mather was a leading advocate of the Half-Way Covenant (1662), which allowed children of baptized but non-communicant church members to be baptized, a controversial attempt to preserve church membership across generations. He died in Dorchester in 1669.
