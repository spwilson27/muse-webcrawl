Honeysuckle

Honeysuckles are arching shrubs or twining vines in the genus Lonicera of the family Caprifoliaceae, native to northern latitudes in North America and Eurasia. About 180 species are known. The genus is named after the German botanist Adam Lonicer.

Description

Most species are climbers or shrubs with opposite, simple oval leaves and sweet-scented, bilaterally symmetrical tubular flowers that yield sweet nectar — the source of the name "honeysuckle". Flowers are often white, yellow, pink or red, and are pollinated by bees, hummingbirds and hawk-moths. The fruit is a berry, red, blue or black depending on species.

Cultivation and invasiveness

Several species, such as common honeysuckle (L. periclymenum) and Japanese honeysuckle (L. japonica), are widely grown as garden ornamentals. Some introduced species — including Japanese honeysuckle in North America — have become invasive weeds, smothering native vegetation.

Uses

Honeysuckle flowers are used in traditional Chinese medicine (jīn yín huā). Caution is warranted: the berries of many species are mildly poisonous to humans.
