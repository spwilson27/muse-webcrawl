Assisted reproductive technology

Assisted reproductive technology (ART) includes medical procedures used primarily to address infertility. This subject involves procedures such as in vitro fertilisation (IVF), intracytoplasmic sperm injection (ICSI), cryopreservation of gametes or embryos, and the use of fertility medication. When used to address infertility, ART may also be referred to as fertility treatment.

ART primarily belongs to the field of reproductive endocrinology and infertility. Some forms of ART may also be used for fertile couples for genetic reasons, such as preimplantation genetic diagnosis. ART may also be used in surrogacy arrangements, although not all surrogacy arrangements involve ART.

Procedures

Fertility medication

The main use of fertility medication is the treatment of female infertility, in which cases they are broadly referred to as fertility drugs; however, they may also be used in some forms of male infertility. Fertility medication generally stimulates follicular development of the ovary, and may be combined with other fertility techniques.

In vitro fertilisation

In vitro fertilisation is the technique of letting fertilisation of the male and female gametes (sperm and egg) occur outside the female body. Techniques usually used in IVF include transvaginal ovum retrieval, transferring the mixture of sperm and eggs to a laboratory dish for fertilisation, and embryo transfer into the woman's uterus two to five days later.

Intracytoplasmic sperm injection

ICSI is a procedure in which a single sperm is injected directly into an egg. It is used when the quality or quantity of a man's sperm is poor. Babies conceived by ICSI have been monitored over a long period, and method does not seem to cause any specific disorders.

Cryopreservation

Cryopreservation is a technique used to preserve embryos, sperm, or eggs at very low temperatures, typically using liquid nitrogen. It allows gametes and embryos to be stored for later use in ART cycles.

Usage rates

The global usage of ART has increased steadily since the birth of the first IVF baby, Louise Brown, in 1978. Usage rates vary considerably between countries, influenced by cost, insurance coverage, cultural factors, and the availability of clinics. In some countries, such as Denmark and Belgium, a relatively high proportion of births result from ART.

Risks

ART carries some risks, including multiple pregnancy, ovarian hyperstimulation syndrome, and a small increased risk of certain congenital conditions, although the absolute risks remain low. Children born after ART are generally healthy, and long-term follow-up studies are ongoing.
