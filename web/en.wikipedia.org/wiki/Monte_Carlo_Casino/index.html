The Monte Carlo Casino (French: Casino de Monte-Carlo) is a gambling and entertainment complex in Monte Carlo, Monaco. It includes the casino proper, the Opéra de Monte-Carlo (housed in the Salle Garnier), and the offices of Les Ballets de Monte-Carlo. It is owned and operated by the Société des Bains de Mer, in which the government of Monaco and the ruling House of Grimaldi hold a majority interest. Citizens of Monaco are forbidden to enter the casino's gaming rooms.

History

Faced with the loss of revenue after Menton and Roquebrune broke away in 1848, the House of Grimaldi turned to gambling as a source of income. Princess Caroline persuaded her son Charles III to develop a spa and casino district on the Spélugues plateau, which was renamed Monte Carlo ("Mount Charles") in 1866. After early failures, the concession passed in 1863 to François Blanc, the successful operator of the Bad Homburg casino, who founded the Société des Bains de Mer and built the casino, luxury hotels, and a railway connection that made Monte Carlo the pre-eminent resort casino of Belle Époque Europe. The casino's profits enabled Monaco to abolish direct taxation in 1869 — a fiscal signature the principality retains.

Architecture

The present building was designed in 1878–79 by Charles Garnier, architect of the Palais Garnier in Paris, in an exuberant Beaux-Arts/Belle Époque style, with an atrium paved in marble and surrounded by onyx columns. Garnier also built the adjoining opera house, the Salle Garnier, inaugurated in 1879 with a performance by Sarah Bernhardt, and later additions included the Salle des Amériques and further gaming salons.

Famous events and culture

The casino's roulette wheels inspired the 1892 music-hall song "The Man Who Broke the Bank at Monte Carlo," after Charles Wells repeatedly "broke the bank" (exhausted the table's reserve) in 1891. In 1913 the "Monte Carlo fallacy" got its name when a roulette ball landed on black 26 times in a row and gamblers lost millions betting on red. The casino and its square have been featured in numerous films, including James Bond pictures Never Say Never Again and GoldenEye, and the surrounding streets form part of the Circuit de Monaco for the Monaco Grand Prix.

Gaming

The casino offers roulette, Trente et Quarante, blackjack, baccarat (punto banco), craps, poker, and slot machines, with more exclusive games in private salons (the salles privées). A formal dress code historically applied in the main rooms has been relaxed in recent decades.
