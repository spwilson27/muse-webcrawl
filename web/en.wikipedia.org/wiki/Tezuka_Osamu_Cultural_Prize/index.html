Tezuka Osamu Cultural Prize

The Tezuka Osamu Cultural Prize (手塚治虫文化賞, Tezuka Osamu Bunka-shō) is a yearly Japanese manga prize awarded to manga artists or their works that follow the Osamu Tezuka manga approach. It was established in 1997 by the Asahi Shimbun newspaper, one of Japan's major dailies, which continues to sponsor and organise the award.

The prize honours manga in the spirit of Osamu Tezuka, the "God of Manga".

Categories

The prize is divided into several categories. The Grand Prize (マンガ大賞) goes to the best overall work of the year. Other categories have included a Prize for Excellence, an Award for a New Creator (later the New Creator Prize), a Short Story Prize, and special prizes awarded at the judges' discretion. The categories and their names have been revised over the years; the current structure centres on the Grand Prize, the New Creator Prize, the Short Story Prize, and occasional Special Prizes.

Notable winners

The first Grand Prize, in 1997, went to Fujiko F. Fujio for Doraemon. Other Grand Prize winners include Naoki Urasawa (Monster, and later Pluto), Moto Hagio, Takehiko Inoue (Vagabond), Taiyō Matsumoto, and Moyoco Anno. The prize is regarded as one of the most prestigious awards in Japanese comics, frequently recognising ambitious, literary, or historically significant works alongside popular successes.

Selection

Winners are chosen by a committee of manga critics, scholars, booksellers, and other specialists, with nominations drawn from manga published in the preceding year. The awards are announced each spring, traditionally around April.
