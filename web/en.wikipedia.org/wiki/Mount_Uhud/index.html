Mount Uhud

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mount Uhud, Medina, Saudi Arabia.

Mount Uhud (Arabic: جَبَل أُحُد, Jabal Uḥud) is a mountain north of Medina, in the Hejazi region of Saudi Arabia. It is 1,077 m (3,533 ft) high and about 7.5 km (4.7 mi) long, and is the largest mountain in Medina.

Battle of Uhud

The mountain gave its name to the Battle of Uhud, fought on 19 March 625 CE (3 AH) between the early Muslim community of Medina under the Prophet Muhammad and a force from Mecca led by Abu Sufyan ibn Harb. The Muslims initially gained the advantage, but archers stationed on a hill (Jabal al-Rumah, "Archers' Hill") left their position against orders to gather spoils, allowing Meccan cavalry under Khalid ibn al-Walid to attack from the rear. Some seventy Muslims were killed, among them Muhammad's uncle Hamza ibn Abd al-Muttalib. The fallen are buried in the Uhud Martyrs' Cemetery at the mountain's foot, which Muslim pilgrims visit during Hajj and Umrah.

Significance

A well-known hadith reports Muhammad saying, "Uhud is a mountain that loves us and that we love." The mountain is a prominent landmark of Medina and a major destination for religious visitors to the city.
