East Berlin was the eastern part of Berlin, Germany, from 1949 until 1990. It consisted of the Soviet sector of Berlin established in 1945 and served as the capital of the German Democratic Republic (GDR, East Germany). The Western Allies never formally recognised it as the GDR's capital, regarding Berlin as a single city under four-power occupation, while the GDR officially styled it "Berlin, Capital of the GDR".

History

After the Second World War, Berlin was divided into four occupation sectors. Political conflict led to the Berlin Blockade of 1948–49 and to the founding of two German states in 1949. East Berlin became the seat of the GDR government, including the Volkskammer and the Council of State, housed along Unter den Linden and around Alexanderplatz. On 17 June 1953 a workers' uprising that began in East Berlin spread across the GDR and was suppressed by Soviet troops.

On 13 August 1961 the GDR sealed the sector border and built the Berlin Wall, which physically divided the city until 9 November 1989. East Berlin was rebuilt as a showcase socialist capital: monumental projects included the Stalinallee (later Karl-Marx-Allee), the Palace of the Republic and the 368-metre Fernsehturm (TV tower, 1969) at Alexanderplatz. The Peaceful Revolution of 1989 led to the fall of the Wall, free elections in March 1990, and German reunification on 3 October 1990, when East and West Berlin merged into a single city and Berlin again became Germany's capital.

Districts

At reunification East Berlin comprised the boroughs of Mitte, Prenzlauer Berg, Friedrichshain, Pankow, Weißensee, Lichtenberg, Marzahn, Hellersdorf, Hohenschönhausen, Köpenick and Treptow. Landmarks surviving from the period include the East Side Gallery section of the Wall and the Soviet War Memorial in Treptower Park.
