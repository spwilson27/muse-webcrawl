Joseph Heinrich Beuys (12 May 1921 – 23 January 1986) was a German artist, teacher, performance artist, and cultural theorist. He is widely regarded as one of the most influential artists of the second half of the twentieth century, known for his concept of "social sculpture" — the idea that society itself is a work of art that everyone can shape — and for his signature materials of felt and fat.

Early life and the war myth

Beuys was born in Krefeld and grew up in Kleve, near the Dutch border. In 1941 he volunteered for the Luftwaffe, serving as a radio operator and rear gunner in dive-bombers on the Eastern Front. He was wounded several times and survived a crash in Crimea in 1944. Around this event Beuys later constructed a founding myth: that after his plane crashed he was rescued by nomadic Tartars who wrapped him in fat and felt to keep him alive. Historians have questioned the story, but it became the symbolic source of his characteristic materials and of his shamanic public persona — the felt hat and fishing vest he wore throughout his career.

Actions and performances

After studying sculpture at the Kunstakademie Düsseldorf, where he became professor in 1961, Beuys developed the staged performance he called "Actions". In How to Explain Pictures to a Dead Hare (1965), his head covered in honey and gold leaf, he carried a dead hare around a gallery, whispering explanations of the pictures to it. For I Like America and America Likes Me (1974) he spent three days in a New York gallery sharing the space with a live coyote. These ritualised works, like his "multiples" and blackboard lectures, were designed to provoke discussion about creativity, healing and social change.

Teaching and politics

Beuys's professorship ended in dismissal in 1972 after he occupied the academy secretariat in protest at admission limits; a court later ruled the dismissal unlawful. He co-founded the Free International University for Creativity and Interdisciplinary Research and was involved in founding the German Green Party. His project 7000 Oaks, begun at documenta 7 in 1982, saw seven thousand oak trees planted across Kassel, each paired with a basalt stone.

Legacy

Beuys's slogan "everyone is an artist" extended art into politics, ecology and economics. Loved and derided in his lifetime, he permanently widened the definition of what art could be and influenced generations of performance and installation artists.
