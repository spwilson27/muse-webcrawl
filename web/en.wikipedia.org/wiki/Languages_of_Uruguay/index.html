Languages of Uruguay

The languages of Uruguay are dominated by Spanish, the de facto national language spoken by nearly the entire population of about 3.4 million. Uruguay has no constitutionally designated official language, but Spanish is used in government, education, and media. The local variety, Rioplatense Spanish, is known for its use of voseo and Lunfardo slang.

Other languages

Along the Brazilian border, a Spanish–Portuguese mixed variety called Portuñol (in its Uruguayan form, fronteiriço) is widely spoken. Uruguayan Sign Language (LSU) is legally recognized. Historically, Italian immigration left a mark on the local dialect, and indigenous languages such as Charrúa are extinct, surviving mainly in place names.
