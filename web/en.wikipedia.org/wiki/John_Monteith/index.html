John Monteith

John Lennox Monteith CBE FRS (3 September 1929 – 20 July 2012) was a British environmental physicist and academic who made fundamental contributions to the study of the interactions between crops, the environment and the atmosphere. He is particularly associated with the Penman–Monteith equation, widely used to estimate evapotranspiration.

Career

Monteith worked at Rothamsted Experimental Station before moving to the University of Nottingham, where he became professor of environmental physics. His research applied the principles of physics to agricultural and ecological systems, treating crops as physical systems exchanging energy, water and carbon dioxide with their surroundings.

Penman–Monteith equation

Building on the earlier work of Howard Penman, Monteith incorporated surface (stomatal) resistance into the combination equation for evaporation, producing the Penman–Monteith equation. The United Nations Food and Agriculture Organization adopted the FAO-56 form of the equation as the standard method for computing reference evapotranspiration for irrigation scheduling.

Publications

With Mike Unsworth, Monteith co-authored the textbook Principles of Environmental Physics, a standard work in agricultural meteorology and environmental science.
