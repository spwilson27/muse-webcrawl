The North American Aviation P-51 Mustang is an American long-range, single-seat fighter and fighter-bomber used during World War II and the Korean War, among other conflicts. The Mustang was designed in 1940 by a team headed by J.H. Kindelberger of North American Aviation (NAA) in response to a requirement of the British Purchasing Commission. The prototype NA-73X airframe was rolled out in just 102 days.

Development

The early Mustang, powered by an Allison V-1710 engine, performed well at low altitude but was limited at high altitude. Replacing the Allison with a Rolls-Royce Merlin — built under licence in the United States as the Packard V-1650 — transformed the aircraft. The resulting P-51B/C and definitive P-51D, with a bubble canopy and six .50 calibre guns, combined speed, manoeuvrability and exceptional range.

Operational history

From late 1943, Mustangs escorted USAAF heavy bombers on daylight raids deep into Germany, defeating the Luftwaffe's fighter force and helping secure Allied air superiority before D-Day. Mustang pilots were credited with destroying thousands of enemy aircraft. The type later served in Korea as the F-51, and with many air forces into the 1980s; it remains iconic on the warbird and air-racing circuit.

Variants

  • P-51A — Allison-engined early model.
  • P-51B/C — first Merlin-engined models.
  • P-51D — most numerous variant, with teardrop canopy.
  • P-51H — lightweight, faster late-war model.
