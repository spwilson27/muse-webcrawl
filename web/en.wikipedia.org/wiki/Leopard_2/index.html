The Leopard 2 is a third-generation German main battle tank developed by Krauss-Maffei in the 1970s and first entering service with the West German Bundeswehr in 1979. It succeeded the Leopard 1 and became one of the most widely exported Western tanks, serving with more than twenty countries.

Development and design

Development began after the cancellation of the joint US–German MBT-70 programme. The Leopard 2 combines heavy composite armour, a powerful MTU diesel engine giving speeds around 68 km/h, and the Rheinmetall 120 mm smoothbore gun — the L/44 version of which also arms the American M1 Abrams. Successive upgrades produced the widely used 2A4, the wedge-armoured 2A5, the long-gun 2A6, the urban-optimised 2A7, and further variants including the 2A8.

Service

Dutch, Danish and Canadian Leopard 2s saw combat in Afghanistan, and Turkish tanks in Syria. In 2023, after months of negotiation, Germany and partner nations supplied Leopard 2s to Ukraine for use against the Russian invasion, marking the type's first large-scale employment in a European land war. Over 3,600 Leopard 2s have been built.
