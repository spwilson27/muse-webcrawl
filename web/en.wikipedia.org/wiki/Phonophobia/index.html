Phonophobia

Phonophobia, also called ligyrophobia or sonophobia, is a fear of, or aversion to, loud sounds — a type of specific phobia. The feared sounds may include sudden explosive noises such as thunder, balloons popping, fireworks, or alarms. Phonophobia should be distinguished from hyperacusis, an abnormal sensitivity in which ordinary sounds are perceived as uncomfortably loud, and from misophonia, in which particular (often quiet, repetitive) sounds provoke strong negative reactions.

Features

People with phonophobia anticipate feared sounds with anxiety and may avoid places or events where such sounds are likely — thunderstorms, concerts, celebrations with fireworks, or children's parties. The response can include startle, panic symptoms, and, in children, crying and attempts to escape. The condition is particularly common in children and may be associated with migraine, in which sensitivity to sound is also a frequent accompanying symptom (in clinical neurology, "phonophobia" is used in this broader sense of sound sensitivity during a migraine attack).

Causes and treatment

Specific phobias are thought to arise from a combination of temperament, learning experiences (for example, a frightening experience with a sudden loud noise), and, in some cases, general anxiety. Sound sensitivity can also accompany autism spectrum conditions and certain ear disorders. Treatment for phobic phonophobia usually follows the standard approach for specific phobias: graduated exposure and cognitive behavioural therapy aimed at reducing avoidance and catastrophic appraisals of sound. Where the complaint is primarily physiological sound intolerance, audiological assessment is used to distinguish hyperacusis and related disorders.
