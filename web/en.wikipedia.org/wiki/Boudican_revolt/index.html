Boudican revolt

Boudican revolt
Date: AD 60–61
Location: Roman Britain
Leaders: Boudica vs Suetonius Paulinus
Result: Roman victory

The Boudican revolt was an uprising against Roman occupation of Britain in AD 60–61 led by Boudica, queen of the Iceni tribe, joined by the Trinovantes and others.

Causes

After King Prasutagus died, Romans seized Iceni lands, flogged Boudica and assaulted her daughters, provoking revolt while governor Suetonius Paulinus campaigned in Wales.

Course

Rebels destroyed Camulodunum (Colchester), Londinium (London) and Verulamium (St Albans), killing an estimated 70,000–80,000 people, per Tacitus and Cassius Dio. Suetonius defeated the much larger British force at the Battle of Watling Street. Boudica died shortly after, by suicide or illness.

Aftermath

Roman rule was consolidated; a more conciliatory administration followed.
