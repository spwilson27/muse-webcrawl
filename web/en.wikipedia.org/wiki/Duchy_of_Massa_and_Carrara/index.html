Duchy of Massa and Carrara

Duchy of Massa and Carrara (Ducato di Massa e Carrara)
Existed: 1473–1829 (principality from 1568)
Capital: Massa
Ruling house: Cybo-Malaspina

The Duchy of Massa and Carrara was a small Italian state in northern Tuscany, encompassing the territories of Massa and Carrara, famed worldwide for the white marble quarried in the Apuan Alps above Carrara.

History

The state arose from the marquisate of Massa, ruled by the Malaspina family, and passed through marriage to the Genoese Cybo family, creating the Cybo-Malaspina dynasty. In 1568 Alberico I Cybo-Malaspina obtained the princely title from Emperor Maximilian II. The principality was raised to a duchy in 1664.

The last Cybo-Malaspina ruler, Maria Teresa Cybo-Malaspina, married Ercole III d'Este of Modena, linking the duchy to the House of Este. After the Napoleonic upheavals — during which the territory was annexed to the Cisalpine Republic and then the Kingdom of Italy — the Congress of Vienna restored it to Maria Beatrice d'Este. On her death in 1829 the duchy was absorbed permanently into the Duchy of Modena and Reggio.

Economy

The duchy's wealth rested on Carrara marble, exported since Roman times and used by sculptors from Michelangelo to the present day, alongside olive oil, wine and coastal trade.
