Edward Whymper

Born27 April 1840, Lambeth, London, England
Died16 September 1911 (aged 71), Chamonix, France
OccupationMountaineer, engraver, author
Known forFirst ascent of the Matterhorn (1865) and Chimborazo (1880)

Edward Whymper FRGS (27 April 1840 – 16 September 1911) was an English mountaineer, explorer, illustrator and author. He is best known for the first ascent of the Matterhorn in 1865, an expedition on whose descent four of the seven-man party were killed.

The Matterhorn

A wood engraver sent to the Alps in 1860 to make sketches, Whymper became obsessed with the then-unclimbed Matterhorn. After eight failed attempts, mostly from the Italian side, he joined a party including Lord Francis Douglas, Douglas Hadow, Charles Hudson and the guides Michel Croz and Peter Taugwalder father and son. On 14 July 1865 they reached the summit via the Hörnli ridge, beating an Italian party. On the descent, Hadow slipped, knocking Croz off his feet and pulling Hudson and Douglas with him; the rope broke, and the four fell to their deaths on the Matterhorn Glacier. Whymper and the Taugwalders survived. The disaster marked the end of the golden age of alpinism and prompted debate about the sport's dangers.

Later explorations

Whymper explored Greenland (1867, 1872), and in Ecuador in 1879–80 made the first ascent of Chimborazo (twice) and several other Andean peaks, making pioneering studies of altitude sickness. He later worked in the Canadian Rockies. His classic book Scrambles Amongst the Alps (1871), illustrated with his own engravings, remains a mountaineering landmark. He died in Chamonix in 1911 and is buried there.
