New Hampshire presidential primary

The New Hampshire presidential primary is the first primary election in the United States presidential nominating process, held in the state of New Hampshire every four years since 1920 and traditionally the first contest in the nation since 1952. Following the Iowa caucuses, it is the second event of the season, and its small scale encourages retail politics — candidates campaign in diners, town halls and voters' homes.

Significance

Because it is first, the primary receives saturation media coverage, and a strong showing can transform an obscure candidate into a frontrunner, while a weak result can end campaigns. Memorable instances include Dwight Eisenhower's 1952 victory while serving abroad, Lyndon Johnson's 1968 write-in win that helped push him out of the race, and Bill Clinton's 1992 second-place "Comeback Kid" finish.

Rules and defence of first place

New Hampshire state law requires its primary to be scheduled at least seven days before any "similar election" in another state, and secretaries of state have repeatedly moved the date earlier to protect its status. The tiny community of Dixville Notch famously votes just after midnight. Recent cycles have seen the Democratic Party reorganize its calendar, complicating the primary's traditional position.
