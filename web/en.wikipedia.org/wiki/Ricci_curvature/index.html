Ricci curvature

In differential geometry, the Ricci curvature tensor, named after Gregorio Ricci-Curbastro, represents the amount by which the volume of a narrow cone of geodesics on a Riemannian manifold deviates from that of a standard cone in Euclidean space. It is obtained as a trace of the full Riemann curvature tensor.

Definition

For a Riemannian manifold with Riemann curvature tensor R, the Ricci tensor is defined by contracting two of its indices: Ric(X, Y) = trace(Z ↦ R(Z, X)Y). Its further trace gives the scalar curvature. A manifold of constant Ricci curvature proportional to the metric is called an Einstein manifold.

Applications

Ricci curvature plays a central role in general relativity through the Einstein field equations, which relate the Ricci tensor and scalar curvature to the stress–energy content of spacetime. It is also central to the Ricci flow, Richard Hamilton's deformation of a metric by its Ricci curvature, which Grigori Perelman used to prove the Poincaré conjecture.
