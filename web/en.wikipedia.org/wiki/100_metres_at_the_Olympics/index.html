The 100 metres at the Summer Olympics has been contested since the first modern Games in Athens 1896 for men and since Amsterdam 1928 for women. It is widely regarded as the blue-riband event of the athletics programme, its winner styled the fastest person on Earth.

History

Thomas Burke won the inaugural men's final in 12.0 seconds. Famous champions include Harold Abrahams (1924), Jesse Owens (1936), Wilma Rudolph (1960), Carl Lewis (1984, 1988), and Usain Bolt, who won three consecutive titles from 2008 to 2016 — a feat matched in the women's event only in part by repeat winners such as Shelly-Ann Fraser-Pryce (2008, 2012) and Elaine Thompson-Herah (2016, 2020).

Notable finals

The 1988 men's final in Seoul, won by Ben Johnson before his disqualification for doping, is remembered as the "dirtiest race in history". Bolt's 9.69 at Beijing 2008, run while easing up in celebration, announced a new era of sprinting.
