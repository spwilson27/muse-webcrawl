Sheffield Wednesday Football Club is a professional association football club based in Sheffield, South Yorkshire, England. Founded in 1867 as The Wednesday — taking its name from the day of the week on which the founding cricket club played its matches — it is one of the oldest football clubs in the world and one of the oldest professional clubs. The club plays at Hillsborough Stadium and wears blue and white striped shirts; its nickname is "the Owls," after the Owlerton district where Hillsborough stands.

Hillsborough Stadium, Wednesday's home since 1899.

History

The Wednesday Cricket Club, formed in 1820, established a football section in 1867; the two split in 1882 and the football club turned professional in 1887. Wednesday joined the Football League in 1892 and won the FA Cup in 1896 and 1907. League championships followed in 1902–03 and 1903–04 under Arthur Dickinson, and again in 1928–29 and 1929–30 under Bob Brown. A third FA Cup was won in 1935.

The club spent much of the post-war period in the top two divisions. In 1991, managed by Ron Atkinson, Wednesday beat Manchester United 1–0 in the League Cup final — the last major trophy won by a club outside the top flight at the time — and finished third in the First Division in 1992. Financial problems after relegation from the Premier League in 2000 led to spells in the third tier; the club won the famous 2023 League One play-offs under Darren Moore, overturning a 4–0 semi-final first-leg deficit against Peterborough United, before returning to the Championship.

Hillsborough disaster

On 15 April 1989, during an FA Cup semi-final against Liverpool at Hillsborough, a crush on the Leppings Lane terrace killed 97 Liverpool supporters. It remains the worst disaster in British sporting history and led to the Taylor Report and the conversion of top-flight grounds to all-seater stadiums.

Rivalries and honours

Wednesday's main rivalry is with Sheffield United in the Steel City derby. Major honours: League champions 1902–03, 1903–04, 1928–29, 1929–30; FA Cup winners 1896, 1907, 1935; League Cup winners 1991.
