Caribbean Hindustani

Caribbean Hindustani is an Indo-Aryan language variety spoken by Indo-Caribbeans and the Indo-Caribbean diaspora. It is based mainly on the Bhojpuri and Awadhi dialects of northern India, brought to the Caribbean by indentured labourers from the 19th century onward.

History

Between 1838 and 1917, hundreds of thousands of Indians arrived in British Guiana, Trinidad, Jamaica, and other colonies — and in Dutch Suriname and French Guadeloupe and Martinique — under the indenture system. Their regional dialects levelled into a Caribbean koine, absorbing vocabulary from English, Dutch, French creoles, and Caribbean English Creole.

Varieties and status

The Surinamese variety, called Sarnami, remains the most widely spoken and has a presence in media and literature. In Guyana and Trinidad, Caribbean Hindustani survives mainly among older speakers and in religious and musical contexts, including chutney music, having been largely displaced by English-based creoles and standard English.
