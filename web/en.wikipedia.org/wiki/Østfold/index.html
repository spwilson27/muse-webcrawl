Østfold is a county in south-eastern Norway, bordering Akershus and Innlandet in Norway and Västra Götaland County in Sweden, with a coastline on the Oslofjord. Its administrative centre is Sarpsborg, while its largest city is Fredrikstad. From 2020 to 2023 Østfold was merged into Viken county; the merger was reversed on 1 January 2024, restoring Østfold as a separate county.

Geography

Østfold is a low-lying county of farmland, forests, and lakes, cut by the Glomma, Norway's longest river, which flows through Sarpsborg — where the Sarpefossen waterfall powered historic industry — to the sea at Fredrikstad. The Hvaler archipelago and the Ytre Hvaler National Park lie off its coast. The county includes the fortress town of Fredrikstad (founded 1567), Halden with the Fredriksten fortress, Moss, and Askim.

History

The area is rich in prehistory: the rock carvings at Solberg and elsewhere in Østfold are among Scandinavia's finest Bronze Age petroglyphs, and the Tune and Gokstad ship burials are associated with the region's Viking Age. In the Middle Ages the area was known as Vingulmark and later Borgarsyssel, named for Borg (Sarpsborg), founded by Saint Olaf in 1016. Timber floating, sawmills, and brickworks along the Glomma made Østfold an early industrial region; Fredrikstad and Halden were repeatedly contested in wars with Sweden.

Economy and transport

Today Østfold's economy combines manufacturing, agriculture, energy, and services, with many residents commuting to Oslo. The E6 motorway and the Østfold Line railway run through the county toward Gothenburg and Stockholm.
