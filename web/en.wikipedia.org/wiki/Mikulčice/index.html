Mikulčice is a municipality and village in the Hodonín District of the South Moravian Region of the Czech Republic, lying near the Morava River and the border with Slovakia. The modern village is best known internationally for the major early medieval archaeological site of Mikulčice-Valy, one of the principal centres of Great Moravia in the 9th century.

The Valy stronghold

The fortified settlement at Valy, excavated from 1954 onward (notably under Josef Poulík), covered roughly 10 hectares within its ramparts, with extensive suburbs on both banks of the Morava. Archaeologists uncovered the foundations of at least twelve stone churches — the largest concentration in the Slavic world of the period — including a three-aisled basilica about 35 metres long, as well as a princely palace, workshops, and cemeteries containing richly furnished graves with swords, spurs, gilded ornaments and jewellery in the "Blatnica–Mikulčice style".

The site's scale, with an estimated population in the low thousands, has led many scholars to identify it as a seat of the Mojmirid rulers of Great Moravia and possibly the unnamed capital of the Moravian realm; some have also connected it with the mission of Saints Cyril and Methodius, who arrived in Moravia in 863. The stronghold declined and was abandoned after the collapse of Great Moravia in the early 10th century, under pressure from Magyar incursions.

Modern village

Today's Mikulčice is an agricultural village with a population of around 1,900, known for wine-growing in the Slovácko region. The archaeological park at Mikulčice-Valy, with a visitor centre and exhibitions, is a national cultural monument and forms part of a cross-border archaeological park together with the Slovak site of Kopčany, where the early medieval Church of St Margaret of Antioch still stands.
