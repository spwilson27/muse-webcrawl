Redmond Barry

Sir Redmond Barry, KCMG (7 June 1813 – 23 November 1880) was an Irish-born judge in the Australian colony of Victoria. He was a foundational figure in Melbourne's cultural institutions, instrumental in establishing the University of Melbourne and the Melbourne Public Library (now the State Library Victoria), and presided as judge at the final trial of the bushranger Ned Kelly, whom he sentenced to death.

Life

Barry was educated at Trinity College Dublin and called to the Irish Bar in 1838. He emigrated to Australia in 1839, first to Sydney, then to Melbourne. Appointed the first Solicitor-General of the newly separated colony of Victoria in 1851, he was elevated that year to the Supreme Court bench, where he served for nearly three decades.

Cultural legacy

Barry served as the first Chancellor of the University of Melbourne from 1853 until his death, and chaired the trustees of the Melbourne Public Library, the first free public library in Australia. Buildings named for him include the Redmond Barry Building at the University of Melbourne, and his statue stands outside the State Library Victoria.

Ned Kelly trial

In October 1880 Barry presided over the trial of Ned Kelly for the murder of Constable Thomas Lonigan. During sentencing, a famous exchange — partly legend — had Kelly predict that he would "see [Barry] there" in the afterlife; Barry died twelve days after Kelly's execution on 11 November 1880, a coincidence that entered Australian folklore.
