Mizuho Fukushima (born December 24, 1955) is a Japanese politician and lawyer. She has served as leader of the Social Democratic Party (SDP) and as a member of the House of Councillors, the upper house of the National Diet, since 1998.

Career

Fukushima graduated from the University of Tokyo and worked as a lawyer, taking up cases involving women's rights and citizens' movements before entering politics. She became leader of the SDP in 2003, succeeding Takako Doi. In 2009, when the SDP joined the coalition government of Yukio Hatoyama, she entered the cabinet as Minister of State for Consumer Affairs and Food Safety, and for social affairs and gender equality.

In May 2010 she was dismissed from the cabinet after refusing to sign a cabinet decision relocating the US Marine Corps Air Station Futenma within Okinawa, prompting the SDP to leave the coalition. She stepped down as party leader in 2013 but returned to lead the party again in 2020. She is known for her advocacy of separate surnames for married couples, opposition to nuclear power, and defence of Japan's pacifist constitution.
