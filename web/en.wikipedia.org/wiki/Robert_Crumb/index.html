Robert Crumb

Robert Dennis Crumb
Born: 30 August 1943, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania
Occupation: Cartoonist
Known for: Zap Comix, Fritz the Cat, Mr. Natural, Keep On Truckin'

Robert Dennis Crumb (born August 30, 1943) is an American cartoonist who is widely regarded as one of the most influential figures in underground comix. His work often satirises contemporary American culture. Crumb was a founder of the underground comix movement, launching Zap Comix in 1968.

Career

After work for greeting cards and Help! magazine, Crumb moved to San Francisco in 1967, where characters such as Mr. Natural, Fritz the Cat and Angelfood McSpade, and the "Keep On Truckin'" strip made him a counterculture icon. Fritz the Cat was adapted into a 1972 animated film by Ralph Bakshi, which Crumb disliked. He later collaborated with Harvey Pekar on American Splendor and published an acclaimed illustrated Book of Genesis (2009).

Reception

Crumb's confessional, often sexually explicit work has been both celebrated for its honesty and draughtsmanship and criticised for depictions of women and race. Terry Zwigoff's documentary Crumb (1994) brought him wider recognition. He lives in France and has been inducted into the Will Eisner Award Hall of Fame.
