Foraker Act

Enacted: 12 April 1900
Sponsored by: Senator Joseph B. Foraker
Territory: Puerto Rico
Superseded by: Jones–Shafroth Act (1917)

The Foraker Act, officially the Organic Act of 1900, is a United States federal law that established civilian government on the island of Puerto Rico, which Spain had ceded to the United States in the Treaty of Paris at the end of the Spanish–American War. It was sponsored by Senator Joseph B. Foraker of Ohio and signed into law on 12 April 1900.

Provisions

The act created a government structure for Puerto Rico consisting of a governor appointed by the President of the United States, an Executive Council (the upper house of the legislature, also largely appointed), and a House of Delegates of 35 members elected by island voters. It also established a judicial system and a non-voting Resident Commissioner to represent Puerto Rico in the United States Congress.

The act defined the inhabitants of Puerto Rico as "citizens of Puerto Rico" entitled to the protection of the United States, but it did not grant them United States citizenship. It also imposed tariff and trade provisions and declared Spanish-era law to remain in force where not inconsistent with the act.

Legacy

The Foraker Act governed Puerto Rico until 1917, when the Jones–Shafroth Act conferred US citizenship on Puerto Ricans and expanded elected self-government. The political status questions the Foraker Act left open remain central to Puerto Rican politics.
