Forest Preserve (New York)

The Forest Preserve of New York State consists of state-owned land within the Adirondack Park and the Catskill Park that is protected by Article XIV of the New York State Constitution, the "Forever Wild" clause. The preserve totals nearly three million acres (about 1.2 million hectares).

History

New York began acquiring Adirondack and Catskill lands in 1885, partly from tax-delinquent properties, in response to concerns about deforestation, watershed protection for the state's canals and waterways, and forest fires. The 1894 constitutional convention added the Forever Wild provision, which requires that the lands "shall be forever kept as wild forest lands" and forbids the sale or removal of timber.

Management

The preserve is managed by the New York State Department of Environmental Conservation, with land-use planning by the Adirondack Park Agency for the Adirondack portion. Lands are classified into categories such as Wilderness, Primitive, Canoe Area, Wild Forest and Intensive Use, which determine permitted recreation. The preserve is intermingled with private land inside the park boundaries.
