Caloosahatchee River

Caloosahatchee River
Location: Florida, United States
Length: about 67 mi (108 km)
Source: Lake Okeechobee (via canal)
Mouth: San Carlos Bay, Gulf of Mexico

The Caloosahatchee River is a river on the southwestern Gulf coast of Florida in the United States, approximately 67 miles (108 km) long. It drains rural areas on the northern edge of the Everglades, east of Fort Myers. Together with the St. Lucie Canal on the Atlantic side, it forms the Okeechobee Waterway, a cross-Florida inland route linking Lake Okeechobee to both coasts.

Course

The river flows westward from Lake Okeechobee through a lock at Ortona and a series of water-control structures past LaBelle and Fort Myers to its mouth at San Carlos Bay near Sanibel Island. Much of its channel was straightened and deepened by canal works beginning in the late 19th century, when Hamilton Disston's dredging connected it to Lake Okeechobee.

Ecology and water management

Releases of nutrient-laden lake water down the Caloosahatchee have been blamed for harmful algal blooms and damage to the estuary's oyster beds and seagrasses, while insufficient freshwater in dry periods raises salinity. Balancing flood control, water supply, and estuary health is a central issue in Everglades restoration planning.
