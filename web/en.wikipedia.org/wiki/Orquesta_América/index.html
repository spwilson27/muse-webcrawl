Orquesta América

Origin: Havana, Cuba
Founded: 1942
Genres: Danzón, chachachá, mambo
Founder: Ninón Mondéjar

Orquesta América is a Cuban charanga orchestra founded in Havana in 1942 by singer Ninón Mondéjar. Playing in the French-derived charanga format — European strings and French flute over an Afro-Cuban rhythm section — the orchestra became one of the defining ensembles of the danzón and is credited with launching the chachachá, the last of the great Cuban popular dance rhythms to emerge before the mambo era gave way to salsa.

History

During the late 1940s Orquesta América was one of Havana's most popular dance orchestras, with a string section led by violinists such as Enrique Jorrín. Around 1951 Jorrín, experimenting with the final section (montuno) of the danzón, introduced a syncopated, strongly dancer-oriented beat in pieces such as "La engañadora" (recorded 1953). The new rhythm — dancers' feet scraped the floor in a shuffling "cha-cha-chá" that gave the genre its onomatopoeic name — spread rapidly through Havana's dance halls and, via recordings and tours, to Mexico and the United States.

Musical style

As a charanga francesa, the orchestra combines violins playing syncopated rhythmic figures (guajeos), a wooden French flute improvising ornate solos, piano, bass, timbales, güiro, and conga drums. Its repertoire centred on the danzón — Cuba's national dance, structured in contrasting sections — and on the nuevo ritmo danzones from which chachachá crystallised as an independent genre.

Legacy

After Jorrín left in 1954 to form his own orchestra, Orquesta América continued under Mondéjar's direction. The band's early chachachá recordings remain reference points for the genre, and charangas in Cuba, including later ensembles such as Orquesta Aragón, carried its format forward. A successor group, Orquesta América del 55, was formed by former members in the mid-1950s.
