Vijaydurg Fort

Vijaydurg Fort (Marathi: विजयदुर्ग) is a sea fort located at Vijaydurg, in the Sindhudurg district of Maharashtra, India, on the bank of the Vaghotan Creek where it meets the Arabian Sea. It is considered the oldest and one of the strongest marine forts on the Konkan coast, and is sometimes called the "Fort Victory".

History

The fort was originally built by Raja Bhoja II of the Shilahara dynasty in 1193 and was then known as Gheriya. It later passed among the Bijapur Sultanate and others before being captured by Chhatrapati Shivaji in 1653, who renamed it Vijaydurg ("Victory Fort") and greatly strengthened it, tripling its area and adding triple-layered walls and numerous bastions.

Under the Maratha admiral Kanhoji Angre in the early 18th century, Vijaydurg became the principal base of the Maratha navy and resisted repeated attempts by the British, Portuguese, and Dutch to take it. The fort finally fell in 1756 to a combined British and Peshwa force under Admiral Watson and was ceded to the British in 1757; it remained in their hands until Indian independence.

A notable feature is an underwater wall, about 250 metres long and built in the creek, which could rip the hulls of enemy ships approaching at high tide. The fort covers around 17 acres and its walls rise about 30 feet.

Present day

The fort is a protected monument of the Archaeological Survey of India and a tourist attraction. In 2025, it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the serial nomination "Maratha Military Landscapes of India".
