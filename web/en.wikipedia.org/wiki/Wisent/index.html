Wisent

The wisent (Bison bonasus), also called the European bison or zubr, is a European species of bison and one of the two extant bison species, alongside the American bison. It is among the heaviest surviving wild land animals in Europe, with bulls weighing up to about 1,000 kg. Wisents are woodland animals, feeding on grasses, shoots, bark, and leaves.

Extinction in the wild and reintroduction

Hunted for centuries, the wisent was extinct in the wild by 1927, when the last wild animal in the Caucasus was killed. The species survived only in zoos, where 54 animals remained. A carefully managed breeding program based on twelve founders reintroduced wisents to the wild, beginning in Poland's Białowieża Forest in 1952. Free-ranging herds now live in Poland, Belarus, Romania, Slovakia, the Carpathians, and elsewhere in Europe, and the population numbers in the thousands.

Taxonomy and description

Two subspecies are recognized: the lowland wisent (B. b. bonasus) and the lowland–Caucasian line. Compared with the American bison, the wisent is taller and less massively built in the forequarters, with a less shaggy coat. Wisents live in mixed herds of females and young led by an older cow, while adult bulls are largely solitary outside the rutting season.

Cultural significance

The wisent is the national animal of both Poland and Belarus and a symbol of European wildlife conservation; the Polish Żubrówka vodka is named after it.
