Famines
A famine is a widespread scarcity of food caused by war, crop failure, population imbalance or government policy.

This is a list of famines worldwide, arranged chronologically. A famine is a widespread scarcity of food, caused by several possible factors, including war, natural disasters, crop failure, widespread poverty, an economic catastrophe or government policies. This phenomenon is usually accompanied by regional malnutrition, starvation, epidemics and increased mortality.

Notable famines are listed below with estimates of excess deaths. Figures for historical famines are approximate and often contested by historians and demographers.

Famine scales

Since 2004, famine declarations have used the Integrated Food Security Phase Classification (IPC), under which Phase 5 (Catastrophe/Famine) is declared when at least 20 per cent of households face extreme food gaps, acute malnutrition exceeds 30 per cent, and deaths exceed two per 10,000 people per day.
