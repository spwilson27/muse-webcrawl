Open rotor

An open rotor, also called a propfan or unducted fan, is a type of aircraft engine combining features of turboprops and turbofans: a gas turbine drives one or two rows of highly swept propeller blades that are not enclosed in a duct. The concept promises very high propulsive efficiency and large fuel savings at speeds and altitudes typical of jet airliners.

History

Propfans were studied intensively in the 1970s and 1980s in response to fuel price shocks; the GE36 unducted fan flew on a Boeing 727 and an MD-80 testbed, and the Progress D-27 powered the Antonov An-70. Falling fuel prices and noise and certification concerns shelved the designs. Interest revived in the 21st century: Safran tested an open rotor in 2017, and in 2021 CFM International launched the RISE programme, targeting around 20% lower fuel burn with an open fan architecture planned for the mid-2030s.

Challenges

Key difficulties include blade noise in the cabin and community, blade-off containment without a casing, and gearbox or pitch-control complexity for contra-rotating designs.
