Rope dart
Chinese繩標 (shéng biāo)
OriginChina
TypeFlexible weapon
ComponentsMetal dart head, rope or chain (c. 3–5 m), ring and tassels

The rope dart (Chinese: 繩標; pinyin: shéng biāo) is a Chinese weapon consisting of a heavy metal dart attached to a long rope or chain, usually between three and five metres in length, with a ring and sometimes brightly coloured tassels or flags near the head. It belongs to the family of flexible weapons in Chinese martial arts and is closely related to the meteor hammer, from which it differs in having a pointed, piercing head rather than a blunt weight.

Use

The practitioner swings the dart in circles using the whole body, wraps the rope around the neck, arms, torso, legs or feet, and shoots the dart out at a target before retracting it by the cord. Techniques include thrusting, wrapping and binding, sweeping, and spinning defences, and the tassels help the user judge the dart's position and speed. Because the weapon's trajectory is difficult to read and it can strike around guards, the rope dart was traditionally considered a concealed or secondary weapon; its mastery is regarded as one of the more demanding skills in wushu, requiring years of practice to avoid injury from the returning head.

History and modern practice

Flexible weapons of this type have a long history in China, with the rope dart's development usually placed in the Tang-to-Ming period alongside the meteor hammer and flying claw. Today the rope dart is taught in traditional kung fu schools and performed in modern wushu demonstrations; a soft, padded practice version — and a glowing or fire version — is also popular in flow-arts and circus spinning communities worldwide.
