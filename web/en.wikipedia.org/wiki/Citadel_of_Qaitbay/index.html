Citadel of Qaitbay

The Citadel of Qaitbay (or Qaitbey Citadel; Arabic: قلعة قايتباي) is a 15th-century defensive fortress located on the Mediterranean seacoast in Alexandria, Egypt. It was built between 1477 and 1479 CE (882–884 AH) by the Mamluk Sultan Al-Ashraf Sayf al-Din Qa'it Bay on the northern tip of the former island of Pharos, at the entrance to Alexandria's Eastern Harbour — the site of the legendary Lighthouse of Alexandria, which had been ruined by earthquakes.

History

The Pharos lighthouse, one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, was badly damaged by earthquakes in the 10th and 14th centuries and finally destroyed by the quake of 1303. Visiting the site in 1477, Sultan Qaitbay ordered a fortress built on its foundations, reusing some of the lighthouse's fallen stone; pink granite columns and blocks from the ancient structure can still be seen in and around the citadel, and large red granite blocks lie in the sea nearby.

The citadel guarded Alexandria against the growing Ottoman threat. It resisted during the Ottoman conquest of Egypt in 1517 but declined in importance thereafter. It was bombarded and partly damaged by the British fleet during the bombardment of Alexandria in 1882, in the course of the Anglo-Egyptian War. In the 20th century the Egyptian Supreme Council of Antiquities carried out extensive restorations, and the citadel became a museum site; the Egyptian Naval Museum was formerly housed in it.

Architecture

The fortress is a compact Mamluk military complex protected by sea on three sides. A square keep — three storeys high, with corner towers — rises in the centre of an inner courtyard, surrounded by defensive walls and a larger outer enclosure. The keep's first floor contains a mosque, notable as among the oldest surviving parts of the citadel, along with magazines and cisterns; the upper floors housed the garrison and the commander's quarters. The main entrance bears the foundation inscription of Qaitbay.
