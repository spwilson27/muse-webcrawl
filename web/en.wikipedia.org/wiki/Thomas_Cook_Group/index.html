Thomas Cook Group

Thomas Cook Group plc
Founded: 1841 (as Thomas Cook); group formed 2007
Defunct: 23 September 2019
Headquarters: Peterborough, England
Industry: Travel and tourism

The Thomas Cook Group plc was a British global travel company that operated tour operations, travel agencies, and airlines. Formed in 2007 by the merger of Thomas Cook AG with MyTravel Group, it traced its origins to Thomas Cook, who founded his travel business in 1841 and is considered the inventor of modern mass tourism.

History

Thomas Cook began by organising railway excursions in the English Midlands, starting with a temperance trip from Leicester to Loughborough in 1841. The business grew into a worldwide travel agency famous for its traveller's cheques, timetables, and package holidays. In the 20th century it passed through ownership by the British government and various consortia; the German C&N Touristik (later Thomas Cook AG) acquired it in 2001, and the 2007 merger with MyTravel created the listed Thomas Cook Group.

Collapse

Burdened by debt of around £1.7 billion, weakening demand, and competition from online rivals, the group entered compulsory liquidation on 23 September 2019 after rescue talks failed. The collapse stranded some 600,000 travellers abroad, including about 150,000 from the UK, prompting the largest peacetime repatriation in British history (Operation Matterhorn, run by the Civil Aviation Authority). Around 21,000 employees lost their jobs worldwide. The brand was later bought by Fosun International and relaunched as an online travel agency.
