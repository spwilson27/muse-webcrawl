Lake Tappan

Lake Tappan is a reservoir on the Hackensack River in Bergen County, New Jersey, and Rockland County, New York, in the north-eastern United States. It was created in 1966–67 by damming the river at River Vale, New Jersey, and takes its name from the Tappan tribe of Native Americans who once lived in the area.

Description

The lake covers about 1,255 acres and holds roughly 12.5 billion gallons of water at full capacity. It is one of four reservoirs in the Hackensack River watershed operated for water supply, alongside the Oradell Reservoir, Lake DeForest, and Woodcliff Lake. The reservoir supplies drinking water to communities in northern New Jersey and is managed by Veolia (formerly Suez Water / United Water).

Surroundings

Lake Tappan borders the municipalities of River Vale, Old Tappan, and Harrington Park in New Jersey, and Orangetown in New York; the borough of Oradell lies just downstream along the Oradell Reservoir. Fishing and boating are restricted to protect water quality.
