Music tracker

A music tracker is a type of music sequencer software for creating music, most commonly associated with the demoscene and video game music of the late 1980s and 1990s. Trackers present music as a vertically scrolling grid: columns represent channels, and rows represent time steps, into which the composer enters note pitches, instrument numbers, volume, and effect commands in a compact alphanumeric notation.

The pattern editor of a typical tracker program.

History

The first widely used tracker was Ultimate Soundtracker (1987) for the Commodore Amiga, written by Karsten Obarski. It introduced the module file format (MOD): a self-contained file storing digitised instrument samples plus the pattern data needed to play them, fitted to the Amiga's four-channel Paula sound chip. Successors such as NoiseTracker and ProTracker standardised the format and spread tracker culture through the demoscene.

On the PC, Scream Tracker and later FastTracker II (with the XM format) and Impulse Tracker (IT format) expanded channel counts and effects. Popular later software includes ModPlug Tracker/OpenMPT, Milkytracker, Renoise (a modern commercial DAW-style tracker), and the chiptune-focused FamiTracker and Furnace, used to compose for retro hardware sound chips.

How it works

A song is assembled from patterns — typically 64 rows each — played in an order list. Samples are replayed at varying pitches to form melodies; effect letters control vibrato, portamento, arpeggio, sample offset, and retriggering. Because the sound is synthesised from short samples at playback time, module files are far smaller than recorded audio, an advantage that made trackers ideal for games and floppy-disk demos.

Culture and use

Tracker music ("mods") formed a pillar of the demoscene, with competitions at demo parties and distribution via BBSes and the internet. Trackers were used commercially for the music of games such as Unreal and Deus Ex (by Alexander Brandon and Michiel van den Bos). The format persists in chiptune, electronic music production, and retro game development.
