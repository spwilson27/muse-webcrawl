European Court of Human Rights

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The European Court of Human Rights (ECtHR), also known as the Strasbourg Court, is an international court of the Council of Europe which interprets the European Convention on Human Rights. The court hears applications alleging that a contracting state has breached Convention rights, brought by individuals (after exhausting domestic remedies), groups and other states; its judgments bind the respondent states, whose execution is supervised by the Committee of Ministers.

Work and influence

Sitting with one judge elected per state party, in chambers of seven and a Grand Chamber of seventeen for the gravest questions, the Court has decided tens of thousands of cases — on torture and police abuse, fair-trial delay, free expression and press freedom, privacy and data, religious dress, prisoners' votes, expulsion and asylum (the Soering and Chahal lines), and the conduct of armed conflict and occupation (Chechnya, Iraq, and the interstate cases arising from Russia's wars against Georgia and Ukraine). Pilot judgments address structural violations; interim measures (Rule 39) halt removals and executions de facto across Europe. Backlogs (historically tens of thousands of pending applications despite single-judge filtering since Protocol 14) and uneven state compliance are chronic; yet the Court remains the institution most responsible for a common European rights floor and the template — alongside the Inter-American Court — for regional rights adjudication worldwide.
