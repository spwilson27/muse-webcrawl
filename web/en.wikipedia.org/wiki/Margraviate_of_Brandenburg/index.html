The Margraviate of Brandenburg (German: Markgrafschaft Brandenburg) was a major principality of the Holy Roman Empire from 1157 to 1806. It was one of the seven original electorates, whose margraves held the dignity of prince-elector. Brandenburg became the core territory of the rising power of the House of Hohenzollern and, through the personal union with the Duchy of Prussia, the foundation of the Kingdom of Prussia and ultimately of the German Empire.

Origins

The territory east of the Elbe had been inhabited by Slavic Wends before German eastward expansion (the Ostsiedlung). In 1157 the Ascanian margrave Albert the Bear recovered the stronghold of Brandenburg from the Slavic prince Jaxa of Köpenick and assumed the title Margrave of Brandenburg, an event conventionally taken as the founding of the mark. His successors extended the territory eastward to the Oder, founding towns including Berlin-Cölln and Frankfurt an der Oder.

From the Ascanians to the Hohenzollerns

When the Ascanian line died out in 1320, Brandenburg passed through periods of Wittelsbach and Luxembourg rule, marked by disorder and the episode of the False Waldemar. In 1415 Emperor Sigismund granted the margraviate to Frederick of Nuremberg, founder of the Brandenburg line of the House of Hohenzollern, who ruled as Elector Frederick I. The Golden Bull of 1356 had already confirmed Brandenburg's status as an electorate.

Rise of Brandenburg-Prussia

The electors gradually consolidated their lands, acquired Cleves, Mark and Ravensberg in the west (1614) and the Duchy of Prussia in personal union (1618). The Great Elector, Frederick William (r. 1640–1688), rebuilt the war-ravaged territory after the Thirty Years' War, created a standing army and welcomed Huguenot refugees under the Edict of Potsdam (1685). In 1701 his son Frederick crowned himself King in Prussia, and Brandenburg thereafter formed the central province of the Prussian monarchy, even though the margraviate formally endured until the Empire's dissolution in 1806.

Legacy

The modern German state of Brandenburg, re-established in 1990, takes its name and much of its territory from the historic margraviate.
