Nesoenas is a genus of pigeons and doves (family Columbidae) native to islands of the western Indian Ocean. The genus was erected by the Italian zoologist Tommaso Salvadori in 1893. Its members are closely related to the turtle doves of the genus Streptopelia, from which they were split on the basis of morphological and molecular evidence.

Species

The genus contains the following species:

  • Malagasy turtle dove (Nesoenas picturatus) — Madagascar and neighbouring islands including the Seychelles, Comoros and Mauritius.
  • Pink pigeon (Nesoenas mayeri) — endemic to Mauritius; once critically endangered, it has been brought back from the brink of extinction by intensive conservation by the Durrell Wildlife Conservation Trust and the Mauritian Wildlife Foundation.
  • Rodrigues dove (Nesoenas rodericanus) — extinct; known from subfossil bones and a brief account from Rodrigues Island.
  • The Réunion pink pigeon (Nesoenas duboisi), extinct since about 1700, is sometimes included.

Taxonomy and evolution

DNA studies indicate that Nesoenas diverged early among the columbid lineages of the Mascarene and Malagasy region; the genus represents an old island radiation. The extinct species were lost following human colonisation of the Mascarene Islands, chiefly through hunting and predation by introduced mammals.
