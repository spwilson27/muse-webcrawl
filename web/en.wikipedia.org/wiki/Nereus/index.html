Nereus

In Greek mythology, Nereus (Ancient Greek: Νηρεύς) was the eldest son of Pontus (the Sea) and Gaia (the Earth), and with the Oceanid Doris he fathered the fifty Nereids, sea nymphs who attended Poseidon. Nereus lived in the Aegean Sea and was known as the "Old Man of the Sea."

Character

Unlike many sea deities, Nereus was depicted as wise, truthful and benevolent, incapable of deception. Heracles wrestled him — he was a shape-shifter who turned into various creatures and elements — to learn the way to the garden of the Hesperides. His truthful, prophetic counsel also appears in accounts of the Trojan War's origins, in which he foresaw the ruin Paris would bring.

Family

His daughters the Nereids included Thetis (mother of Achilles), Amphitrite (wife of Poseidon) and Galatea. The asteroid 4660 Nereus and various sea creatures are named after him.
