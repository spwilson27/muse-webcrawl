Ayyanar

Region: Tamil Nadu, India
Role: Guardian deity of villages
Attributes: Terracotta horses, sword, attendants

Ayyanar is a Hindu deity worshipped primarily in Tamil Nadu, India, as a guardian of villages and their boundaries. His shrines are usually located at village outskirts, near water bodies, and are famous for rows of large, brightly painted terracotta horses offered by devotees.

Worship

Ayyanar is regarded as a protective god who patrols the village at night on horseback, guarding against evil and disease. He is attended by subordinate deities such as Karuppu Sami. Festivals involve processions, offerings and the dedication of clay horses, sometimes life-size or larger.

Iconography and mythology

The god is depicted as a youthful, moustached figure, often with a sword or whip. In some traditions he is identified as a son of Shiva and Mohini, linking him to Ayyappa, though the village cult is distinct and largely non-Brahmanical in practice.
