Pullback bundle


Construction of the pullback bundle f*E over a base B′.

In mathematics, a pullback bundle or induced bundle is the fiber bundle that is induced by a map of its base-space. Given a fiber bundle π : E → B and a continuous map f : B′ → B, one can define a pullback of E by f, denoted f*E, which is a fiber bundle over B′ whose fiber over a point b′ in B′ is the same as the fiber over f(b′). Thus f*E fits into a commutative diagram known as a pullback square.

Definition

Formally, the total space of the pullback bundle is defined as the subset of the Cartesian product B′ × E consisting of pairs (b′, e) such that f(b′) = π(e). The projection π′ : f*E → B′ sends (b′, e) to b′, and the canonical map f̄ : f*E → E sends (b′, e) to e. The subspace topology makes f*E into a fiber bundle over B′ with the same typical fiber as E.

Properties

Pullback is a functorial operation: pulling back along the identity map returns the original bundle, and pulling back along a composition of maps is the composition of the pullbacks. Homotopic maps induce isomorphic pullback bundles when the bundles are numerable, a fact that underlies the classification of vector bundles by homotopy classes of maps into Grassmannians. The pullback of a vector bundle, principal bundle, or fibration inherits the corresponding structure. Dual notions include the pushforward of tangent vectors along a smooth map.

Examples

The restriction of a bundle to a subspace of its base is a pullback along the inclusion map. Pulling back the tangent bundle of a manifold along an immersion gives a bundle whose sections relate to the differential of the map. The normal bundle of an immersed submanifold can be characterized through such pullback constructions.
