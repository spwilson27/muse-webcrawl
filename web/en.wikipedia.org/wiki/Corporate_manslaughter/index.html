Corporate manslaughter

[Image placeholder: corporate headquarters]

Corporate manslaughter is a crime in several jurisdictions in which the actus reus of manslaughter is committed by a corporation or other organisation. The offence typically applies where a person is killed as a result of a gross breach of a relevant duty of care owed by the organisation to the deceased, and the way in which the organisation's activities were managed or organised was a substantial element in the breach.

The doctrine developed because of the difficulty of prosecuting large companies for manslaughter under the common law "identification principle", which required a "directing mind" of the company — such as a director — to be personally guilty of the offence before the company itself could be liable. A series of disasters, including the sinking of the Herald of Free Enterprise ferry in 1987 and the Southall and Ladbroke Grove rail crashes, exposed the practical limits of that approach and led to statutory reform.

United Kingdom

In England and Wales and Northern Ireland, the Corporate Manslaughter and Corporate Homicide Act 2007 created the offence of corporate manslaughter, which came into force on 6 April 2008. In Scotland the equivalent offence is called corporate homicide. The offence is committed if the way in which an organisation's activities are managed or organised causes a person's death and amounts to a gross breach of a relevant duty of care owed by the organisation to the deceased. A substantial part of the breach must involve the failure of senior management to organise or manage the activities safely. The penalty is an unlimited fine, and courts may also impose remedial orders and publicity orders requiring the organisation to publicise its conviction.

The first conviction under the Act was of Cotswold Geotechnical (Holdings) Ltd in 2011, following the death of a geologist in a collapsed trial pit. Later prosecutions have included cases arising from deaths in construction, manufacturing, agriculture and care settings.

Other jurisdictions

Canada amended its Criminal Code in 2004 (the "Westray Bill", named after the 1992 Westray Mine disaster in Nova Scotia) to make it easier to prosecute organisations for criminal negligence causing death. In Australia, industrial manslaughter offences have been enacted in several states and territories, beginning with the Australian Capital Territory in 2003 and later including Queensland, Victoria, Western Australia and the Northern Territory.
