James Stanhope, 1st Earl Stanhope

Born: 1673, Paris, France

Died: 5 February 1721 (aged 47–48), London, England

Title: 1st Earl Stanhope

Known for: British general; Secretary of State; commander in Spain during the War of the Spanish Succession

James Stanhope, 1st Earl Stanhope (1673 – 5 February 1721) was a British Army officer and statesman. He was commander of British forces in the War of the Spanish Succession and afterward served as Secretary of State and First Lord of the Treasury, becoming a dominant figure in the British government under George I.

Military career

The eldest son of Alexander Stanhope (1638–1707), Stanhope attended Eton College and Trinity College, Oxford, before joining the army. He fought in Flanders during the Nine Years' War and in Spain during the War of the Spanish Succession, winning victories at the Battle of Almenar and the Battle of Zaragoza in 1710, where he captured the Spanish Bourbon army at Saragossa. However, later that year he was forced to surrender after the Battle of Brihuega and remained a prisoner in Spain until 1712.

Political career

Returning to England, Stanhope became a leading Whig politician and was appointed Secretary of State for the Southern Department in 1714 under George I. He was instrumental in suppressing the Jacobite rising of 1715 and negotiated the Quadruple Alliance of 1718. Created Earl Stanhope in 1718, he served as the effective chief minister of Britain until his death in 1721 during a parliamentary debate over the South Sea Bubble.
