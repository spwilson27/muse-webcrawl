The Twelfth United States Army Group was the largest and most powerful command ever fielded solely by the United States Army. Activated in London on 14 July 1944 and commanded throughout by General Omar N. Bradley, it eventually comprised four field armies and over 1.3 million soldiers fighting in France and Germany in 1944–45.

Composition

At various times the Army Group controlled the First Army (Hodges), Third Army (Patton), Ninth Army (Simpson), and Fifteenth Army (Gerow). On becoming operational in France on 1 August 1944, it directed the breakout from Normandy (Operation Cobra) alongside the British 21st Army Group.

Operations

Twelfth Army Group formations closed the Falaise Pocket, liberated Paris (with French forces), and raced across France to the German border. In December 1944 it bore the brunt of the Ardennes offensive, the Battle of the Bulge. In 1945 its armies crossed the Rhine at Remagen, encircled the Ruhr Pocket, and advanced to the Elbe, where they met Soviet forces. The Army Group was inactivated at Wiesbaden on 12 July 1945.
