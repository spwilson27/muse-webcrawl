4K resolution refers to a horizontal display resolution of approximately 4,000 pixels. Two standard variants dominate: 3840 × 2160, known as 4K UHD (ultra-high-definition) and used in television and consumer media, and 4096 × 2160, the DCI 4K standard used in digital cinema projection.

History and adoption

The term emerged from digital cinema, where the DCI specification standardised 4096-pixel-wide projection in the 2000s. Consumer adoption followed in the 2010s as LCD and OLED panels, HDMI 2.0 connections, and streaming services made UHD televisions mainstream; by the late 2010s 4K sets accounted for the majority of large-screen TV sales in many markets. Streaming platforms, the Ultra HD Blu-ray format (launched 2016), game consoles and broadcasters adopted 2160p delivery, typically paired with high dynamic range (HDR) formats such as HDR10 and Dolby Vision.

Comparison

4K UHD contains four times the pixels of 1080p Full HD (1920 × 1080). At typical living-room viewing distances the benefit over 1080p is most visible on large screens. 8K UHD (7680 × 4320) quadruples 4K in turn but remains a niche consumer format.
