The Pavlov Hills (Czech: Pavlovské vrchy), often known by their local name Pálava, are a range of limestone hills in the South Moravian Region of the Czech Republic, near the town of Mikulov and the border with Austria. The hills form the westernmost outcrop of the Carpathian mountain system and rise steeply from the lowlands of the Dyje (Thaya) river.

Geography

The range is a line of white Jurassic limestone klippes running roughly north–south for about 12 kilometres. The highest summit is Děvín (550 m), topped by a television transmitter and the ruins of Děvičky castle; other landmarks include the castle ruins of Sirotčí hrádek and the hill of Stolová hora near the village of Pavlov, from which the hills take their name.

Nature and protection

The warm, dry climate of the hills supports rare steppe and rock-steppe vegetation and many warmth-loving plant and insect species found nowhere else in the Czech Republic. The area was declared a protected landscape area (CHKO Pálava) in 1976 and is also a UNESCO biosphere reserve. The slopes around Mikulov are planted with vineyards and form part of the Mikulov wine sub-region.

Archaeology

The foothills are world-famous for their Upper Palaeolithic (Gravettian) sites, including Dolní Věstonice and Pavlov, which have yielded some of the earliest known ceramic figures — including the Venus of Dolní Věstonice — mammoth-bone dwellings, and burials dating back roughly 26,000–30,000 years.
