Muscle dysmorphia

Muscle dysmorphia is a subtype of body dysmorphic disorder in which a person becomes pathologically preoccupied with the belief that their body is too small or insufficiently muscular, despite being of normal build or even very muscular. It is sometimes informally called "bigorexia".

The condition, described in research by Harrison Pope and colleagues in the 1990s, primarily affects males engaged in weightlifting and bodybuilding. It can lead to compulsive exercise, rigid diets, social impairment and misuse of anabolic steroids and other appearance- and performance-enhancing drugs.

Treatment

Treatment follows approaches used for body dysmorphic disorder, including cognitive behavioural therapy and selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors, though affected individuals often resist the idea that their physique is normal.
