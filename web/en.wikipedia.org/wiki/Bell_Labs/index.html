Bell Labs

Bell Labs (formerly Bell Telephone Laboratories) is an American industrial research and scientific development company, long headquartered at Murray Hill, New Jersey. Founded in 1925 as the research arm of AT&T and Western Electric, it is widely regarded as one of the most productive research institutions of the twentieth century. Its researchers are credited with the transistor, the laser, the photovoltaic cell, the charge-coupled device, information theory, the Unix operating system and the C programming language, among many other inventions. Bell Labs scientists have won nine Nobel Prizes. Since 2016 it has been owned by Nokia.

History

Bell Telephone Laboratories was formed in 1925 from the engineering department of Western Electric and part of AT&T's research staff. Under presidents including Mervin Kelly, the Murray Hill campus (opened 1942) gathered physicists, chemists, mathematicians and engineers in deliberately collaborative surroundings. The 1947 invention of the transistor by Bardeen, Brattain and Shockley launched the semiconductor age; Claude Shannon's 1948 paper founded information theory. Other landmarks include the first communications satellites (Telstar), the discovery of the cosmic microwave background by Penzias and Wilson (1965), Unix (1969) and C (1972) by Thompson, Ritchie and colleagues, and the CCD (1969).

Later years

After the 1984 breakup of AT&T, Bell Labs went with the equipment business into Lucent Technologies (1996), then Alcatel-Lucent (2006), and was acquired by Nokia in 2016, which retained the Bell Labs name for its research organisation. Though reduced from its peak of tens of thousands of staff, it continues research in networking and communications.
