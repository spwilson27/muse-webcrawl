Parmigianino

Parmigianino
Full name: Girolamo Francesco Maria Mazzola
Born: 11 January 1503, Parma, Italy
Died: 24 August 1540 (aged 37), Casalmaggiore
Movement: Italian Renaissance, Mannerism
Known works: Self-portrait in a Convex Mirror, Madonna with the Long Neck, The Vision of Saint Jerome

Girolamo Francesco Maria Mazzola (11 January 1503 – 24 August 1540), better known as Parmigianino ("the little one from Parma"), was an Italian Renaissance painter and printmaker, a leading figure of early Mannerism. His work is celebrated for its refined elegance, elongated figures, and sophisticated, deliberately artificial grace — what contemporaries called grazia.

Life and career

Born in Parma, Parmigianino was apprenticed to his uncles, the painters Michele and Pier Ilario Mazzola, and was deeply influenced by Correggio, then working in Parma. His earliest mature works include the frescoes of the Story of Diana and Actaeon at Fontanellato (c. 1523). At 21 he painted the Self-portrait in a Convex Mirror (c. 1524), executed on a convex wooden panel to mimic a barber's mirror, and took it to Rome to present to Pope Clement VII.

In Rome he painted The Vision of Saint Jerome (1527), but the Sack of Rome that year drove him to Bologna, where he painted the Madonna di Santa Margherita. Returning to Parma in 1530, he began his most famous altarpiece, the Madonna with the Long Neck (1534–1540, Uffizi), left unfinished at his death, and received the commission for frescoes in Santa Maria della Steccata, which he worked on slowly amid a growing obsession with alchemy — Vasari claimed he neglected painting for alchemical experiments and was briefly imprisoned for breach of contract.

Legacy

Parmigianino was also a significant printmaker, among the first Italian painters to practise etching extensively. His drawings and prints circulated widely, spreading Mannerist style across Europe. He died of a fever at Casalmaggiore in 1540, aged 37.
