A touring bicycle is a bicycle designed or modified to handle bicycle touring: long-distance, self-supported travel carrying luggage. Compared with a racing bicycle, a tourer is built for durability, comfort over long days, and the ability to carry heavy loads without the handling becoming unstable, and to be repairable far from bike shops.

Design

Typical features include a longer wheelbase and relaxed geometry for stability under load; a strong frame, traditionally of Reynolds or Columbus steel tubing (valued because it can be welded for repair anywhere in the world, though aluminium and titanium are also used); clearance for wider tyres and mudguards; mounting eyelets for front and rear racks, multiple bottle cages, and sometimes dynamo lighting. Gearing is low — triple chainsets or wide-range doubles with large rear cassettes — so the rider can climb hills carrying camping gear. Wheels use many spokes (often 36 or more) on sturdy rims, and components are chosen for reliability rather than light weight: bar-end or down-tube shifters, cantilever or disc brakes, and dynamo hubs are common choices.

Variations

Related designs include lightweight "credit-card" tourers for supported or inn-to-inn riding, expedition bicycles with oversized tubing and 26-inch wheels for remote regions, recumbent tourers, folding tourers for travel by air or train, and tandem tourers. Modern bikepacking machines blur the line, using frame bags on rigid mountain-bike-like frames for off-road routes where a traditional tourer would struggle.
