Kbal Spean

Kbal Spean
Location: Siem Reap Province, Cambodia
Period: 11th–13th centuries
Known as: River of a Thousand Lingas

Kbal Spean is an Angkorian archaeological site in the Kulen Hills of Cambodia, known as the "River of a Thousand Lingas". The bed and banks of the Stung Kbal Spean river are carved with lingas, yonis and images of Hindu gods.

Carvings

The carvings, dating mainly to the reigns of Suryavarman I and Udayadityavarman II in the 11th and 12th centuries, were intended to sanctify the water flowing over them toward the temples and reservoirs of Angkor. Scenes include Vishnu reclining on the serpent Ananta, and Shiva with Uma.

Access

The site lies north-east of Angkor and is reached by a jungle path of about 1.5 km from the road. It was brought to wider attention in the late 1960s after being shown to the ethnologist Jean Boulbet.
