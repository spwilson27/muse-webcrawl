Coldstream

Coldstream (Scots: Cauldstream) is a town and civil parish in the Scottish Borders area of Scotland, lying on the north bank of the River Tweed, directly on the border with England. It gives its name to the Coldstream Guards, a regiment of the British Army.

History

Coldstream was a strategically important border crossing, repeatedly contested during the Anglo-Scottish wars; Edward I's army crossed here in 1296. In 1650 George Monck raised the regiment that became the Coldstream Guards in the town, and in 1660 marched from Coldstream to London in the march that led to the Restoration of Charles II.

Coldstream Guards

The regiment, founded in 1650, is the oldest continuously serving regular regiment in the British Army. The town retains close ties with it, and guardsmen are granted the freedom of Coldstream.
