Chilblains

Chilblains, also known as pernio and perniosis, are a medical condition in which damage occurs to the capillaries of the skin after exposure to cold, damp, non-freezing conditions. They present as itchy, red or purple, sometimes swollen and tender patches, most often on the toes, fingers, ears, and nose.

Signs and symptoms

Chilblains typically develop hours after exposure to cold. The affected areas become red or violaceous, swollen, and intensely itchy; burning sensations, blistering, and in severe cases ulceration may occur. Symptoms usually resolve within a few weeks if further cold exposure is avoided. The condition is related to an abnormal vascular response to cold and is distinct from frostbite, in which tissue actually freezes.

Causes and risk factors

Risk factors include poor circulation, being underweight, wearing tight footwear in cold weather, and living in damp housing. Chilblains are more common in women. Similar lesions ("COVID toes") were widely reported during the COVID-19 pandemic. Persistent or atypical pernio can be associated with connective tissue diseases such as lupus (chilblain lupus).

Treatment and prevention

Management centres on keeping the affected parts warm and dry, avoiding rapid rewarming, and not scratching. Calcium channel blockers such as nifedipine are sometimes used for severe or recurrent cases. Prevention is by adequate warm clothing and heating during cold weather.
