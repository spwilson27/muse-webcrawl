Alfonso VI (c. 1040/1041 – 1 July 1109), nicknamed the Brave (Spanish: el Bravo) or the Valiant, was king of León (1065–72), and of Castile and Galicia (1072–1109). After the death in battle of his brother Sancho II, Alfonso succeeded to the three kingdoms left by their father, Ferdinand I.

He is best remembered for conquering Toledo in 1085, the first great offensive success of the Christian Reconquista, and for his lifelong rivalry with Rodrigo Díaz de Vivar, El Cid. His reign also saw the dramatic reversal at the Battle of Sagrajas (1086) against the Almoravids of Yusuf ibn Tashufin, which checked Christian expansion for a generation.

Early life and succession

Alfonso was the second son of Ferdinand I and Sancha of León. On his father's death in 1065 the kingdoms were divided: Sancho received Castile, Alfonso León, and García Galicia. War among the brothers followed; after Sancho defeated and exiled Alfonso in 1072, Alfonso took refuge in Muslim Toledo. Sancho's assassination later that year allowed Alfonso to return and claim all three kingdoms, though legend holds that El Cid made him swear at Burgos that he had no part in his brother's death.

Conquest of Toledo

In 1085 Alfonso captured Toledo, the ancient Visigothic capital, after a prolonged siege and negotiated surrender. The acquisition of the Tagus valley extended Christian rule deep into the peninsula and alarmed the Muslim taifa kingdoms, which invited the Almoravids from North Africa to intervene. Alfonso's defeat at Sagrajas in 1086 began a long defensive struggle. Toledo itself, defended by El Cid's successors and later by Alfonso's forces, was never retaken.

Later reign and death

The last decade of Alfonso's reign brought further defeats, including Consuegra (1097) and the death of his only son, Sancho Alfónsez, at the Battle of Uclés in 1108. Alfonso died at Toledo on 1 July 1109 and was succeeded by his daughter Urraca.

Marriages and children

Alfonso married several times, most notably Agnes of Aquitaine and Constance of Burgundy. His children included Urraca, queen regnant after him; Elvira, who married Raymond IV of Toulouse; Teresa, countess of Portugal; and Sancho, his short-lived heir.
