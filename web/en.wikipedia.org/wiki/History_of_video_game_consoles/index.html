History of video game consoles

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The history of video game consoles is conventionally divided into generations, beginning with the first home console, the Magnavox Odyssey (1972), and continuing through today's systems from Nintendo, Sony, and Microsoft.

First generations and the crash

The Odyssey, designed by Ralph Baer, played simple games using plastic overlays on a television screen. Atari's home version of Pong (1975) and the cartridge-based Fairchild Channel F (1976) and Atari 2600 (1977) defined the second generation. The North American video game crash of 1983, caused by market saturation and poor-quality software, nearly destroyed the industry.

Nintendo, Sega, and 3D

Nintendo revived the market with the Nintendo Entertainment System (1985), strict licensing, and hits such as Super Mario Bros. The 16-bit contest between the Super NES and Sega Genesis gave way to CD-based 3D systems: Sony's PlayStation (1994) outsold the Nintendo 64 and Sega Saturn and established Sony as a dominant force.

Modern era

The PlayStation 2 (2000) remains the best-selling console of all time. Microsoft entered with the Xbox (2001) and pioneered online console gaming with Xbox Live; Nintendo's Wii (2006) expanded the audience with motion controls. Later generations — PlayStation 4 and Xbox One (2013), the hybrid Nintendo Switch (2017), and PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S (2020) — emphasized digital distribution, services, and high-fidelity graphics. Handheld systems, from the Game Boy (1989) onward, form a parallel lineage now largely merged into hybrid designs.
