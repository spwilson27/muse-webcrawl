The Zimbabwe African National Union – Patriotic Front (ZANU–PF) is a political party that has ruled Zimbabwe since independence in 1980.

History

ZANU split from Joshua Nkomo's ZAPU in 1963 under Ndabaningi Sithole and Robert Mugabe. Its armed wing ZANLA fought in the Rhodesian Bush War mainly from Mozambique. ZANU won the 1980 elections; Mugabe became prime minister, then president. After the Gukurahundi conflict, ZANU and ZAPU merged in the 1987 Unity Accord, forming ZANU–PF.

Rule

Under Mugabe until his removal in the 2017 military intervention, and Emmerson Mnangagwa thereafter, the party oversaw land reform from 2000, economic crisis, hyperinflation and disputed elections against the MDC opposition, while retaining power. See Economy of Zimbabwe.
