Emily Eavis (born 1979) is a British festival organiser, best known as the co-organiser of the Glastonbury Festival of Contemporary Performing Arts, which she runs alongside her father, the festival's founder Michael Eavis, at Worthy Farm in Pilton, Somerset.

Early life

Eavis grew up on Worthy Farm, where the festival has been held since 1970. She trained as a teacher and worked in schools before joining the festival organisation full-time. Her earliest musical memory of the festival is associated with her mother Jean, in whose memory she later established the Park Stage area.

Role at Glastonbury

Eavis books many of the festival's headline acts and oversees its day-to-day organisation. She is credited with modernising the festival's line-ups and championing greater gender balance among headline performers, and with initiatives such as the 2019 ban on single-use plastic bottles on site. She famously persuaded acts including Beyoncé (2011) and Stormzy (2019) to headline the Pyramid Stage, and organised the festival's 50th anniversary edition, delayed by the COVID-19 pandemic to 2022.

She is married to Nick Dewey, a music manager, and lives near the farm.
