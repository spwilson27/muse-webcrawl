Edward Palmer Thompson (3 February 1924 – 28 August 1993) was an English historian, writer and socialist activist. He is probably best known for his historical work on the British radical movements in the late 18th and early 19th centuries, in particular his book The Making of the English Working Class (1963), a landmark of social history.

Life and politics

Thompson served as a tank officer in the Second World War in Italy; his elder brother Frank, a SOE officer, was killed in Bulgaria in 1944. He joined the Communist Party of Great Britain during the war but left in 1956, after the Soviet invasion of Hungary, and co-founded the New Reasoner and later the New Left Review, becoming a leading figure of the British New Left. From 1965 he taught at the University of Warwick, resigning in 1971 after conflicts over university governance recounted in Warwick University Ltd (1970). In the 1980s he was a prominent campaigner for nuclear disarmament with European Nuclear Disarmament (END).

Historical work

The Making of the English Working Class argued that class is a historical relationship, not a static category, and sought to rescue the "poor stockinger, the Luddite cropper" and other ordinary people "from the enormous condescension of posterity". His essay "Time, Work-Discipline, and Industrial Capitalism" (1967) is a classic of the field. Other works include Whigs and Hunters (1975), on the Black Act, and the long polemic The Poverty of Theory (1978) against Althusserian Marxism.
