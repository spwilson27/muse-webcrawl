St. Bernard (dog breed)

OriginSwitzerland / Italy
Height70–90 cm (28–35 in)
Weight64–120 kg (140–264 lb)
CoatShort or long; red and white, mahogany brindle
Life span8–10 years

The St. Bernard is a giant breed of working dog originating in the Swiss and Italian Alps. Recognised by major kennel clubs in the working or utility group, the breed standard describes a powerful, proportionately tall, strong and muscular dog, with a massive head and a friendly, calm expression.

Breed standard

The breed exists in two coat varieties: the original short-haired (smooth) type and a long-haired type that arose from crosses with the Newfoundland in the 19th century. Acceptable colours are white with red, or red with white markings; a dark shadowing on the head and ears is typical. The tail is long, thick, and carried low.

Temperament and care

St. Bernards are noted for their gentle, patient, and tolerant nature and are generally good family dogs, but they require early socialisation and training because of their size. Like other giant breeds they are prone to hip and elbow dysplasia, certain heart conditions, and gastric dilatation-volvulus (bloat), and they mature slowly, reaching full size at around two to three years of age. They drool considerably and shed heavily, particularly in spring and autumn.

Recognition

The breed was formally recognised in Switzerland in 1887 with the founding of the Swiss St. Bernard Club, and Henry Schumacher of Holligen is credited with establishing the first stud book. The breed remains the national dog of Switzerland and one of the most recognisable breeds worldwide, popularised in films such as Beethoven.
