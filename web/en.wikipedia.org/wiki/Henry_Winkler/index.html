Henry Franklin Winkler (born October 30, 1945) is an American actor, producer, director and author. He achieved stardom as Arthur "Fonzie" Fonzarelli on the sitcom Happy Days (1974–1984), and decades later won an Emmy Award for his role as acting teacher Gene Cousineau on the HBO series Barry (2018–2023).

Early life

Winkler was born in Manhattan to German Jewish immigrants who had fled Nazi Germany shortly before the Second World War. Struggling with undiagnosed dyslexia, he performed poorly at school but found his footing in acting, earning an MFA from the Yale School of Drama in 1970. After small stage and screen roles he was cast in the 1974 film The Lords of Flatbush.

Happy Days and the Fonz

Premiering in 1974, Happy Days made Winkler's leather-jacketed greaser Fonzie a cultural phenomenon — his catchphrase "Ayyy", thumbs-up gesture and jukebox-bumping cool defined the show's popularity, and he won two Golden Globe Awards. Fearing typecasting, he turned down high-profile roles (including Danny Zuko in Grease) and moved into producing, co-founding Winkler-Rich Productions, whose credits include the series MacGyver.

Later career and writing

Winkler enjoyed a broad career revival: films such as Scream (1996), The Waterboy (1998) and Click (2006); television roles in Arrested Development, Parks and Recreation and Children's Hospital; and an Emmy-winning turn in Barry (2018), his first Emmy after nearly five decades in the business. With Lin Oliver he co-authored the bestselling Hank Zipzer children's books — inspired by his own dyslexia — and further series for young readers. He was appointed an honorary OBE in 2011 for his work with British children with learning difficulties, and published the memoir Being Henry: The Fonz... and Beyond in 2023.
