IBM System

IBM System is a naming convention used by International Business Machines (IBM) for several of its computer product lines. Over the decades, IBM has marketed families of mainframe, midrange, and server computers under names beginning with "System", including the System/360, System/370, System/390, System i, System p, System x, and System z.

System/360

Announced on April 7, 1964, the System/360 was the first family of computers designed to cover both commercial and scientific applications and a complete range of sizes. The "360" referred to the 360 degrees of a compass, symbolizing the full circle of applications. A key innovation was that customers could purchase a smaller system and later upgrade to a larger one while retaining their software — a departure from IBM's earlier practice of incompatible product lines.

Successors

The System/360 was followed by the System/370 in 1970, the System/390 in 1990, and eventually the System z line of mainframes, which evolved into the modern IBM Z family. Parallel naming was used for midrange and UNIX systems: the AS/400 became the System i, and the RS/6000 line became the System p; both were unified under the IBM Power Systems brand in 2008. IBM's x86 servers were sold as System x before the line was sold to Lenovo in 2014.

Significance

The System families established the concept of a compatible computer family and dominated the mainframe and enterprise computing markets for decades. The instruction set architecture introduced with System/360 remains, in evolved form, in use in IBM Z mainframes today.
