Edfu (Arabic: إدفو) is an Egyptian city on the west bank of the Nile between Luxor (Esna) and Aswan, with a population of roughly 100,000 or more. It is famous for the Temple of Horus, the best-preserved ancient temple in Egypt.

Temple of Edfu

The Ptolemaic Temple of Horus at Edfu was built between 237 and 57 BC, under the Ptolemaic dynasty, on the site of an earlier New Kingdom temple. Dedicated to the falcon god Horus, it is one of the largest temples in Egypt and by far the most complete: its pylons, courts, halls, sanctuary and surrounding inscriptions survive almost intact, having been buried under sand and Nile silt until excavated by Auguste Mariette in the 1860s. The temple walls carry extensive hieroglyphic texts, including the myth of the conflict between Horus and Seth and the inscriptions known as the Edfu Texts, a major source for Egyptian religion, ritual and geography.

History

Edfu was the capital of the second nome of Upper Egypt and a cult centre of Horus from early times; in the Old Kingdom it was a staging point for expeditions into Nubia and the Eastern Desert. Under the Greeks it was known as Apollonopolis Magna, identifying Horus with Apollo. The ancient tell of Edfu preserves settlement remains from the Old Kingdom through the Roman and early Islamic periods, including mastaba tombs and, nearby, small step pyramids of the late 3rd dynasty.

In 2005, remains of the ancient city were threatened by expanding modern settlement, and protective measures and new excavations were undertaken. Today the temple is a standard stop on Nile cruises between Luxor and Aswan, and tourism, agriculture (notably sugar cane) and a sugar factory underpin the modern economy.
