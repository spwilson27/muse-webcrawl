What Is a Nation?

What Is a Nation? (French: Qu'est-ce qu'une nation?) is a lecture delivered by the French historian and philosopher Ernest Renan at the Sorbonne on 11 March 1882. It is one of the most famous and influential texts on the concept of nationalism and the definition of a nation.

Content

Renan argued against defining a nation by race, language, religion, geography, or shared interests alone. Instead, he defined the nation as a "spiritual principle" founded on two things: a rich legacy of shared memories, and the present-day consent — the desire to continue living together. He famously called the nation a "daily plebiscite" (plébiscite de tous les jours).

Legacy

The lecture is often cited as an early statement of civic nationalism, in contrast to ethnic nationalism, although Renan's own views were more complex and included essentialist elements. The text remains a standard reference in nationalism studies.
