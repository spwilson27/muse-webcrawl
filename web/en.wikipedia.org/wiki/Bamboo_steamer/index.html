Bamboo steamer

TypeKitchenware
Place of originChina
Main usesSteaming dim sum, dumplings, buns, vegetables

A bamboo steamer is a type of kitchen utensil originating in China, consisting of one or more round, shallow baskets woven from bamboo that can be stacked on top of each other over a pot or wok of boiling water. Steam rises through the slatted bases of the baskets, cooking the food inside, while a woven bamboo lid traps the steam. Bamboo steamers are strongly associated with dim sum service and with the cooking of dumplings, steamed buns, and other dishes in Cantonese and wider Chinese cuisine.

Design

A bamboo steamer is built from strips of bamboo woven into a flat, slatted base surrounded by a circular rim. Because bamboo absorbs some of the condensation produced during steaming, food is less likely to become soggy than in a metal steamer, and the material does not conduct as much heat to the food resting above the water line. Baskets typically range from about 10 cm to over 30 cm in diameter, and several may be stacked so that different dishes cook simultaneously. The steamer sits in a wok or on a ring above a pot of boiling water.

Use

Food is usually placed on a liner — such as parchment paper, cabbage leaves, or a perforated steamer mat — to prevent sticking. Small items typical of dim sum, including har gow and siu mai, are served directly in the small steamer baskets in which they were cooked. Bamboo steamers are also used for reheating bread and for proofing dough in some households.
