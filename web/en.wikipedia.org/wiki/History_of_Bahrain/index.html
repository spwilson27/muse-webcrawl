History of Bahrain

The history of Bahrain spans some five thousand years, from the ancient civilisation of Dilmun — one of the most important trading centres of the Bronze Age world — to the modern island kingdom in the Persian Gulf. Bahrain's strategic location and its sweet-water springs made it a hub of commerce linking Mesopotamia with the Indus Valley.

Ancient Dilmun

From roughly 3000 BCE, Bahrain was the centre of Dilmun, a trading entrepôt mentioned in Sumerian, Akkadian and Assyrian texts as a source of copper, pearls, dates and timber, and in Sumerian myth as a pure and holy land, the setting of the creation myth of Enki. Hundreds of thousands of burial mounds survive from the Dilmun era. The island later passed through Babylonian, Persian, Greek (as Tylos) and Parthian spheres of influence.

Islamic period

Bahrain — a name that in early Islamic sources applied to a wider region of eastern Arabia — accepted Islam in 629 CE. The islands were ruled successively under the Umayyad and Abbasid caliphates, enjoyed autonomy under the Qarmatians in the 10th century, and later came under the Uyunids, Usfurids and Jabrids. The Portuguese seized Bahrain in 1521 and held it until 1602, when it came under Safavid Persian control.

Al Khalifa rule and the British protectorate

In 1783, the Al Khalifa family, of the Utbah tribal confederation, conquered Bahrain from the Persians; the family has ruled ever since. Treaties with Britain in the 19th century culminated in Bahrain becoming a British protectorate in 1861, reinforced by agreements in 1880 and 1892. Oil was discovered in 1932 — the first strike on the Arab side of the Gulf — transforming the economy and society. Bahrain's pearling industry, once dominant, collapsed with the advent of Japanese cultured pearls and the Great Depression.

Independence and modern era

Bahrain declared independence on 15 August 1971 after Britain announced its withdrawal from the Gulf; Sheikh Isa bin Salman Al Khalifa became the first emir. Iran's historical claims to the island had been withdrawn after a 1970 UN-sponsored survey found Bahrainis favoured independence. In 2002, under King Hamad bin Isa Al Khalifa, Bahrain became a kingdom with an elected lower house of parliament, though the appointed upper house and the ruling family's dominance have remained contested. The 2011 Bahraini uprising, part of the Arab Spring, was suppressed with the aid of Saudi-led Gulf forces, and tensions between the Sunni monarchy and the Shia majority continue to shape the country's politics.
