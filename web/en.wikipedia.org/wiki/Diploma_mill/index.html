Diploma mill

A diploma mill (also known as a degree mill) is a company or organisation that claims to be a higher education institution but provides illegitimate academic degrees and diplomas for a fee. The degrees may be fabricated, misrepresented, based on "life experience", or granted without any study at all. Diploma mills are distinct from legitimate unaccredited institutions in that they are intentionally fraudulent, often claiming accreditation from fake accrediting bodies ("accreditation mills") that they operate themselves.

Characteristics

Common warning signs include degrees awarded after days or weeks rather than years, credit granted wholesale for "life experience", flat per-degree pricing, absence of qualified faculty or coursework, institutions with no physical campus or only a mail drop, and names deliberately similar to those of prestigious universities. Many mills operate online and across jurisdictions, exploiting the fact that the words "university" and "accreditation" are unregulated in some countries.

Legal status and enforcement

The legality of diploma mills varies by jurisdiction. In the United States, several states — including Oregon, Texas and Washington — publish lists of unapproved institutions and restrict the use of their credentials for employment. The federal government has prosecuted large operations; the Saint Regis University network, run from Liberia and shut down in 2005, led to convictions, and a 2008 investigation found federal employees holding degrees from mills. In Pakistan, the Axact company was exposed in 2015 as running a global network of hundreds of fake online universities. Degrees from mills have been implicated in scandals involving public officials, teachers and job applicants in many countries.

Use by credential seekers

Buyers of mill degrees are sometimes deceived consumers, but often knowing customers seeking to embellish résumés. Presenting a mill degree as a genuine qualification can constitute fraud and has led to dismissals, prosecutions and, in fields such as medicine and engineering, risks to public safety.
