Mike Harrington

Michael Harrington is an American software engineer and businessman, best known as a co-founder of Valve Corporation, the video game developer and digital distribution company behind Half-Life and the Steam platform.

Career

Harrington worked at Microsoft during the 1980s and 1990s, where he contributed to projects including work on the Windows NT operating system and co-founded the team behind Microsoft Research's early consumer efforts, working alongside Gabe Newell. In 1996, Harrington and Newell left Microsoft and used their own money to found Valve in Kirkland, Washington.

At Valve, Harrington was closely involved in the creation of the company's first game, Half-Life (1998), which became a landmark first-person shooter, and he helped personally finance and oversee much of the young company's development. Following the success of Half-Life, Harrington left Valve in 2000 to take an extended sabbatical and travel with his wife. He later co-founded Picnik, an online photo-editing service acquired by Google in 2010, and subsequently worked on other ventures including the Catapult/Amplion companies.
