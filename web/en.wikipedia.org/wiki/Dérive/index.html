Dérive

A dérive (French for 'drift') is a revolutionary strategy originally put forward in 1956 by Guy Debord, a member at the time of the Letterist International. In a dérive, persons drop their usual motives for movement and let themselves be drawn by the attractions of the terrain and the encounters they find there.

Dérive
Origin: Letterist International, Situationist International
Key figure: Guy Debord

Theory

Debord defined the dérive as a mode of experimental behaviour linked to urban society: a technique of rapid passage through varied ambiances, part of psychogeography.

Practice

Participants wander in small groups for hours or days, observing architecture and ambience, sometimes producing psychogeographic maps such as Debord's The Naked City (1957).
