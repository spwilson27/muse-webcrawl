The Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF) is a Kurdish-led multi-ethnic alliance of militias in north and east Syria, formed in October 2015 during the Syrian civil war. Its core is the Kurdish People's Protection Units (YPG), joined by Arab, Assyrian/Syriac, Armenian, and other groups organized partly through the Syrian Arab Coalition. The SDF serves as the armed force associated with the Autonomous Administration of North and East Syria (Rojava).

War against ISIL

Backed by United States airpower, advisers, and equipment, the SDF became the principal ground partner of the anti-ISIL coalition. It captured Manbij (2016), Raqqa (2017) — the Islamic State's de facto capital — and finally Baghuz in March 2019, ending ISIL's territorial caliphate at the cost of some 11,000 SDF fighters killed.

Tensions and later developments

Turkey regards the YPG as an extension of the PKK and launched operations against SDF-held areas (Afrin 2018; northeast Syria 2019). After the fall of the Assad regime in December 2024, the SDF entered negotiations with the new Syrian government over integrating its forces and administration into the Syrian state, concluding an agreement in March 2025, even as clashes with Turkish-backed factions continued.
