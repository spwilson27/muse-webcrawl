CHARMM

Chemistry at Harvard Macromolecular Mechanics (CHARMM) is the name of a widely used set of force fields for molecular dynamics, and the name for the molecular dynamics simulation and analysis computer software package associated with them. The CHARMM Development Project involves a network of developers in the United States and around the world working with Martin Karplus and his group at Harvard to develop and maintain the CHARMM program.

History

The CHARMM program originated in Karplus's group at Harvard University in the 1970s, building on earlier work on the conformational energy of peptides and proteins. Bernard Brooks played a central role in the program's long-term development. It became one of the foundational tools of computational chemistry, used to study the structure, dynamics, and function of proteins, nucleic acids, lipids, and small molecules.

Force fields

The CHARMM force fields provide parameters for proteins, nucleic acids (DNA and RNA), lipids, carbohydrates, and small organic molecules, including the CHARMM General Force Field (CGenFF) for drug-like molecules. Together with AMBER and GROMOS, CHARMM is one of the three major families of biomolecular force fields. Academic versions of the software are available at a cost to academic groups, and the force fields are also implemented in other packages such as GROMACS, NAMD, and LAMMPS.
