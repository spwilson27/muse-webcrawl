Potato bread

Potato bread is a form of bread in which potato — mashed, grated, or as flour — supplements or partly replaces regular wheat flour. The potato adds moisture and softness, producing a tender crumb and a loaf that stays fresh longer than plain wheat bread.

Varieties

In Ireland, potato bread includes potato farls (also called fadge), flat griddle-cooked quarters of dough that form part of an Ulster fry. German Kartoffelbrot is a wheat or rye bread enriched with cooked potato. In Peru, pan de papa is a soft roll made with potato flour. Lefse, the Norwegian flatbread, is made from potatoes, flour, butter, and milk. Commercial potato bread and potato rolls are popular in the United States for sandwiches and hamburgers.

History

Potato breads developed where potatoes were abundant and wheat expensive or scarce, notably in Ireland, Germany, and Eastern Europe in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Bakers found that potato extended flour supplies while improving texture.

Preparation

Typical recipes combine mashed or riced cooked potato with flour, water or milk, yeast or baking soda, salt, and fat, then knead, proof, and bake like ordinary bread dough. The starch in the potato helps retain moisture, softening the crumb.
