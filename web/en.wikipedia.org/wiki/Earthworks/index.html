Earthworks

Earthworks are artificial changes in land level, typically made from piles of soil and rock. The term is used in archaeology for prehistoric monuments and in engineering for construction works.

This is a summary article; see also Earthwork (engineering).

Archaeology

Archaeological earthworks include burial mounds (barrows), hillforts, henges, causewayed enclosures, Roman roads and deserted medieval villages. Famous examples include the Serpent Mound in Ohio, built by Indigenous peoples of the Americas, Offa's Dyke on the Welsh border, and the Nazca Lines in Peru. Survey methods include aerial photography, LiDAR and geophysics, which reveal features levelled by ploughing.

Engineering

In civil engineering, earthworks involve excavation, haulage and compaction of soil and rock for roads, railways, dams, canals and foundations — see the engineering article for cut and fill, embankments and retaining structures.

Art

Earthworks also denotes large-scale land art of the 1960s–70s, such as Robert Smithson's Spiral Jetty.
