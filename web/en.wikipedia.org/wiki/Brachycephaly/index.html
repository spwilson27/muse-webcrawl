Brachycephaly

Brachycephaly
Cephalic index: 80 or above
Skull shape: short and broad
Skull shapes classified by cephalic index

Brachycephaly (from the Greek brachys, "short", and kephale, "head") is a type of cephalic disorder in which the skull is disproportionately short and wide. It is classified by a cephalic index — the ratio of the maximum width of the head to its maximum length, multiplied by 100 — of 80 or above. Heads with an index below 75 are termed dolichocephalic (long-headed), and those between 75 and 80 are mesocephalic.

Causes

Brachycephaly can be a normal population trait, a result of deliberate cranial modification, or the consequence of a developmental condition. Positional (deformational) brachycephaly occurs in infants who spend prolonged periods lying on their backs, a practice encouraged since the early 1990s to reduce the risk of sudden infant death syndrome; the resulting flattening of the occiput usually resolves as the child grows and spends more time upright. Syndromic brachycephaly may occur in conditions such as Down syndrome and in craniosynostosis, where premature fusion of the coronal sutures restricts front-to-back growth of the skull.

In animals

The term is also applied to domestic animals, most famously brachycephalic dog breeds such as the Bulldog, Pug, Boxer and Shih Tzu, whose shortened muzzles result from selective breeding. Extreme brachycephaly in dogs is associated with brachycephalic obstructive airway syndrome (BOAS), in which stenotic nares, an elongated soft palate and other features obstruct breathing.
