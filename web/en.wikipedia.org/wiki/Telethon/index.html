Telethon

A telethon (a portmanteau of "television" and "marathon") is a televised fundraising event that lasts many hours or even days, broadcast to raise money for a charitable, political, or other cause. It typically mixes entertainment performances with appeals, phone banks of volunteers taking pledges, and running on-screen donation totals.

History

The format was pioneered in the United States in 1949 by Milton Berle. The most famous was the Jerry Lewis MDA Labor Day Telethon, held annually from 1966 to 2010 in support of the Muscular Dystrophy Association. Other notable examples include the French Téléthon for muscular dystrophy research, the United Kingdom's Comic Relief and Children in Need appeals, and Mexico's Teletón.

Modern forms

With the decline of long-form broadcast marathons, the format has migrated online to livestreamed charity marathons on platforms such as Twitch and YouTube, including speedrunning events like Games Done Quick.
