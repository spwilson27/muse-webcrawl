The Pine Ridge Indian Reservation, in southwestern South Dakota, is the homeland of the Oglala Lakota.

The Oglala Lakota (Lakota: Oglála Lakȟóta) are one of the seven subtribes of the Lakota people, who, along with the Dakota and Nakota, form the Oceti Sakowin (Seven Council Fires), historically called the Sioux. The Oglala are the largest Lakota division; today most are citizens of the federally recognised Oglala Sioux Tribe, whose reservation is the Pine Ridge Indian Reservation in southwestern South Dakota.

History

The name Oglala is often translated as "those who scatter their own" or "dust scatterers," said to derive from a historic division within the tribe. By the early 19th century the Oglala were a powerful equestrian people of the northern Plains, ranging over the Black Hills (Paha Sapa) and the Powder River country. Famous leaders include Red Cloud, who waged Red Cloud's War (1866–1868) and secured the Treaty of Fort Laramie (1868), the war leader Crazy Horse, and American Horse.

Wars and Wounded Knee

The Great Sioux War of 1876–77, the US seizure of the Black Hills, and confinement to reservations followed. On 29 December 1890, US cavalry killed some 250–300 Lakota — mostly Miniconjou, with Hunkpapa among them — at Wounded Knee Creek on Pine Ridge, the last major armed conflict of the Indian Wars. In 1973, Oglala activists and the American Indian Movement occupied Wounded Knee for 71 days to protest tribal government corruption and federal treaty violations.

Modern era

Pine Ridge, among the largest reservations in the United States, struggles with poverty and unemployment while sustaining a strong Lakota cultural and linguistic revival, including language-immersion schools. The Black Hills land claim remains unresolved: in United States v. Sioux Nation of Indians (1980) the Supreme Court awarded compensation the Sioux have refused, maintaining that the Black Hills are not for sale.
