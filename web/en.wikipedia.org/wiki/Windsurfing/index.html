Windsurfing

Windsurfing
Water sport combining surfing and sailing
Equipment: board, sail, mast, boom
Olympic class since 1984

Windsurfing is a water sport that combines elements of surfing and sailing. It consists of a board usually 2 to 2.5 metres (6 ft 7 in to 8 ft 2 in) long, with displacements typically between 45 and 150 litres (9.9 and 33.0 imp gal; 12 and 40 US gal), powered by wind acting on a single sail. The sail area generally ranges from 2.5 to 12 square metres (27 to 129 sq ft) depending on the conditions, the skill of the sailor, the type of windsurfing being undertaken and the weight of the person windsurfing.

History

Windsurfing can be traced back to the 1960s, when Newman Darby of Pennsylvania developed a hand-held sail and rig mounted on a universal joint, which he called the "sailboard". In California, Jim Drake and Hoyle Schweitzer developed the sport further, founding the company Windsurfer International and popularising the sport worldwide through the 1970s. Windsurfing became an Olympic sport at the 1984 Los Angeles Games.

Equipment

The basic equipment consists of a board, a mast, a boom and a sail, collectively called the rig. Boards are classified by volume and intended use, from large beginner boards with daggerboards to small high-wind wave boards. Modern sails are made of monofilm and dacron, and masts are typically carbon fibre or aluminium.

Techniques and disciplines

Disciplines include freeride, freestyle, slalom racing, speed sailing, wave riding and formula racing. Advanced techniques include planing, carve gybing, waterstarting and aerial manoeuvres. Windsurfers are capable of exceeding 50 knots (93 km/h; 58 mph) in speed sailing events.
