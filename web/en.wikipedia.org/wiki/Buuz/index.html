Buuz

Course: Main dish
Place of origin: Mongolia
Main ingredients: Wheat dough, minced mutton or beef, onion, garlic

Buuz (Mongolian: бууз) are a type of Mongolian steamed dumpling filled with meat. They are one of the most characteristic dishes of Mongolian cuisine and are eaten in large quantities, especially during the Tsagaan Sar (Lunar New Year) celebrations.

Preparation

The filling consists of minced mutton or beef mixed with onion, garlic, salt and sometimes caraway seeds. The meat is enclosed in a small round of wheat dough, pinched closed at the top, and steamed. A distinctive feature of buuz is the small opening traditionally left at the top of the dumpling.

Cultural significance

During Tsagaan Sar, Mongolian families may prepare hundreds or thousands of buuz in advance, freezing them outdoors in the winter cold. Guests are expected to eat generously, and the dumplings symbolise prosperity for the coming year. A closely related fried variant is known as khuushuur.
