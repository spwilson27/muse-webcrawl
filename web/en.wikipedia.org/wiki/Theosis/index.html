Theosis

Theosis
Greekθέωσις (théōsis), "deification"
TraditionEastern Orthodox, Eastern Catholic
Related conceptsHesychasm, essence–energies distinction, divinization
Key proponentsAthanasius of Alexandria, Gregory Palamas, Maximus the Confessor

Theosis (Greek: θέωσις), also called deification or divinization, is a central doctrine of Eastern Orthodox theology: the transformative process by which a Christian becomes partaker of the divine nature (cf. 2 Peter 1:4) and is made like God through grace. In the famous formulation of Athanasius of Alexandria, "He was made man, that we men might be made gods" — deification is understood as the very purpose of the Christian life and of salvation itself.

Biblical and patristic roots

The doctrine draws on scriptural language of participation in the divine nature and on the Fathers of the Church. Irenaeus of Lyons, Clement of Alexandria, Athanasius, the Cappadocian Fathers, and Maximus the Confessor all taught that human beings are called to share, by grace, in what God is by nature — while never becoming God in essence. Deification thus preserves the distinction between Creator and creature even at the height of union.

Essence and energies

In the fourteenth century the hesychast controversy led Gregory Palamas to articulate the distinction between God's unknowable essence and his uncreated energies — the divine life and glory in which believers genuinely participate, as in the light of the Transfiguration on Mount Tabor. Palamas's theology was affirmed by councils at Constantinople (1341–1351) and became normative in Orthodoxy. Theosis is attained through the sacramental life of the Church, prayer (especially the hesychast Jesus Prayer), ascetic struggle, and the purification–illumination–deification stages of the spiritual life described by the monastic tradition.

In other traditions

Cognate ideas appear in Roman Catholic theology as "divinization," in Wesleyan teaching on Christian perfection, and in some Anglican and Lutheran writers, though the term theosis itself is characteristic of the Christian East. Modern Orthodox theologians such as Vladimir Lossky have presented theosis as the key to Orthodox soteriology.
