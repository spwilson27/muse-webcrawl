Joseph Rochefort

Joseph John Rochefort (May 12, 1900 - July 20, 1976) was an American naval officer and cryptanalyst. He headed Station HYPO, the U.S. Navy's codebreaking unit at Pearl Harbor, whose work on the Japanese naval code JN-25 provided the intelligence that made possible the American victory at the Battle of Midway in 1942.

Early career

Rochefort enlisted in the Navy in 1918 and was commissioned through the Stevens Institute of Technology. A gifted linguist, he was selected for Japanese language training in Tokyo and developed a rare combination of operational and cryptanalytic expertise, serving tours in naval intelligence and codebreaking in Washington and at sea.

Station HYPO and Midway

In 1941 Rochefort took command of the Combat Intelligence Unit (Station HYPO) in Hawaii. After the attack on Pearl Harbor, his team worked around the clock on JN-25. In the spring of 1942 HYPO deduced that the Japanese target designated "AF" was Midway Atoll; Rochefort famously confirmed the identification by having Midway broadcast an unencrypted report of a water shortage, which Japanese radio traffic then echoed as "AF is short of water." Admiral Nimitz trusted Rochefort's assessment over doubts in Washington and positioned his carriers accordingly, winning the decisive battle of June 1942.

Later career and recognition

Despite his success, Rochefort was transferred later in 1942 amid bureaucratic rivalry with Washington's OP-20-G, and he never again held a cryptanalytic post; he commanded a floating dry dock for the remainder of the war and retired in 1947. His role at Midway was recognized only long after the war: he was posthumously awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1986 and inducted into the NSA's Cryptologic Hall of Honor.
