Urnes Stave Church

Urnes Stave Church

Location: Ornes, Lustrafjord, Norway

Built: c. 1130

Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site (1979)

Urnes Stave Church (Norwegian: Urnes stavkyrkje) is a stave church at the farm of Ornes on the Lustrafjord in Vestland county, Norway. Built around 1130, it is the oldest surviving stave church in the country and has been inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List since 1979.

Architecture

The church blends Christian architecture with earlier Viking-age ornament. It gives its name to the "Urnes style" of animal carving, characterised by slender, elegantly interlaced beasts. Carved panels and a doorway from an earlier church on the site, dating to the mid-11th century, are reused in the north wall. Inside, distinctive carved capitals and a series of 17th-century furnishings survive.

History

Archaeology shows at least two earlier churches stood on the site. The present church was built as a private chapel for a powerful local family. It is now owned by the Society for the Preservation of Ancient Norwegian Monuments (Fortidsminneforeningen) and functions as a museum, with occasional religious services.

The carved north portal, with its intertwining dragon-like animals grappling with snakes, is frequently interpreted as a depiction of the struggle between good and evil, possibly alluding to Ragnarök seen through Christian imagery.
