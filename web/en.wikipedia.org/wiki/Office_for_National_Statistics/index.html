The Office for National Statistics (ONS) is the executive office of the UK Statistics Authority and the largest independent producer of official statistics in the United Kingdom. It is responsible for collecting and publishing statistics on the economy, population, and society of the UK, and for conducting the decennial census in England and Wales.

History

The ONS was formed on 1 April 1996 by the merger of the Central Statistical Office and the Office of Population Censuses and Surveys. Since 2008 it has operated as the executive arm of the UK Statistics Authority, a non-ministerial body reporting directly to Parliament, created to promote and safeguard the independence of official statistics. Its main office is in Newport, South Wales, with other sites including Titchfield in Hampshire.

Work

Key ONS outputs include the consumer prices index (CPI) and retail prices index (RPI) measures of inflation, labour market statistics such as unemployment and earnings, national accounts and GDP estimates, population estimates and migration statistics, and data on births, deaths, and marriages. It conducted the 2021 census of England and Wales, the first primarily online census.

The National Statistician

The ONS is led by the National Statistician, who is also chief executive of the UK Statistics Authority and head of the Government Statistical Service. Holders of the post have included Jil Matheson and Sir Ian Diamond.
