G20

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Group of Twenty (G20) is an intergovernmental forum comprising 19 sovereign countries, the European Union and (since 2023) the African Union. Its members account for roughly 85% of global GDP, 75% of international trade and two-thirds of the world's population. The G20 was founded in 1999, after the Asian financial crisis, as a meeting of finance ministers and central-bank governors, and was elevated to leaders' summits in 2008 during the global financial crisis, when it coordinated stimulus and financial regulation and declared itself the premier forum for international economic cooperation.

Work and tensions

Rotating presidencies host annual summits and ministerial tracks on finance, trade, health, climate and development; achievements include crisis coordination (2009), tax cooperation (the global minimum corporate tax), debt initiatives and the AU's admission under India's 2023 presidency (New Delhi). Critics cite communiqués' vagueness and implementation gaps, and since 2022 the forum has been strained by the presence of both Russia and its Western critics — managed, in successive presidencies, through negotiated declarations of varying specificity.
