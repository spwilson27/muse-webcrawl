World Car Free Day, celebrated on 22 September, is an annual event in which motorists are encouraged to give up their cars for a day and use public transport, cycling or walking instead. Cities and towns around the world close streets to motor traffic, hold events highlighting the benefits of car-free travel, and promote alternatives to the private automobile. The event is the culmination of European Mobility Week, held each year from 16 to 22 September.

History

Car-free days were organized in individual cities from the 1970s onward, often in response to the oil crises. In 1994, a keynote speech by Eric Britton at a conference in Toledo, Spain, called for a structured, pan-European approach. National car-free days were held in Iceland, the United Kingdom and France in the late 1990s, and in 2000 the European Commission established the day as a European initiative; the same year the event went global with the World Carfree Network. Bogotá, Colombia, held one of the largest early events, closing the entire city to cars, and Jakarta, Indonesia, later adopted a weekly car-free day on main avenues.

Aims and effects

Supporters argue that the day demonstrates how cities can look and feel with fewer cars, reduces air and noise pollution, and encourages investment in public transport and cycling infrastructure. Studies of individual events have measured substantial short-term reductions in air pollution on closed streets. Critics note that a single day has limited long-term effect unless it leads to permanent changes in transport policy.
