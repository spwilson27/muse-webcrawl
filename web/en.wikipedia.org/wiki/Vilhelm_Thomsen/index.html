Vilhelm Ludwig Peter Thomsen (25 January 1842 – 12 May 1927) was a Danish linguist and Turkologist. He is best remembered for deciphering the Old Turkic Orkhon inscriptions in 1893, the oldest known texts in a Turkic language, a breakthrough that opened the early history of the Turkic peoples.

Life and career

Thomsen studied at the University of Copenhagen, where he developed extraordinary facility in languages — Finno-Ugric, Turkic, Slavic, and many others — and became professor of comparative linguistics there in 1887. His early work on the contacts between Finnic and Baltic and Germanic languages (Berøringer mellem de finske og de baltiske Sprog, 1890) remains a classic of contact linguistics. He was president of the Royal Danish Academy of Sciences and Letters (from 1902) and received numerous international honours.

Decipherment of the Orkhon inscriptions

The great runic-like stone inscriptions of the Orkhon Valley in Mongolia, found in 1889 and published by expeditions under Vasily Radlov, were written in an unknown script. Working from photographs and squeezes, Thomsen identified the language as Turkic and, by recognising recurrent words such as täŋri ("heaven/God") and personal names, announced the decipherment to the Royal Danish Academy on 15 December 1893. Crucially, he established that the texts — the Kül Tigin and Bilge Khagan stelae of the Second Turkic Khaganate — dated to the 730s CE, centuries older than any Turkic text previously known. His full edition, Inscriptions de l'Orkhon déchiffrées, appeared in 1896. A friendly rivalry with Radlov, who published his own readings shortly after, marked the birth of Old Turkic studies.

Other work

Thomsen contributed to Indo-European and Finno-Ugric comparative grammar, to the study of runes, and late in life to work connected with the decipherment environment around Etruscan, though the Orkhon achievement dominates his legacy. His collected minor writings were published in the three-volume Samlede Afhandlinger (1919–1931).
