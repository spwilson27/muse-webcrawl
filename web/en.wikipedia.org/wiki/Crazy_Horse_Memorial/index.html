The Crazy Horse Memorial is a mountain monument under construction on privately held land in the Black Hills, in Custer County, South Dakota, United States. When completed, it will depict the Oglala Lakota warrior Crazy Horse riding a horse and pointing to his tribal land. The memorial was commissioned by Lakota Chief Henry Standing Bear, who invited sculptor Korczak Ziolkowski — who had worked briefly on Mount Rushmore — to create it.

Construction

Work began with the first blast on June 3, 1948. Ziolkowski worked on the mountain largely with his family until his death in 1982; his wife Ruth and their descendants have continued the project through the nonprofit Crazy Horse Memorial Foundation. The sculpted face of Crazy Horse, 27 metres (87 ft 6 in) high, was completed and dedicated in 1998. Work has since focused on the horse's head and the warrior's outstretched arm.

If completed as planned, the monument will be the world's second-tallest statue, at about 172 metres (563 ft) high and 195 metres (641 ft) long. The project has been financed entirely by admission fees and donations, having declined federal funding. The memorial complex includes the Indian Museum of North America and a Native American cultural centre.

Controversy

The memorial is controversial among some Native American groups: the Black Hills are sacred to the Lakota and were taken in violation of the Treaty of Fort Laramie, and some descendants of Crazy Horse object that he never consented to being depicted and opposed photography. Defenders, including the Ziolkowski family and Standing Bear's descendants, argue the memorial honours Native American heritage and provides education and employment.
