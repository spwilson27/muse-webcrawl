Entheogen

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An entheogen is a psychoactive substance, typically of plant origin, that is ingested to produce a nonordinary state of consciousness for religious or spiritual purposes. The term was coined in 1979 by a group of ethnobotanists and scholars of mythology, including Carl A. P. Ruck, Jonathan Ott, R. Gordon Wasson, and Jeremy Bigwood, who sought a word free of the pejorative connotations of "hallucinogen" and "psychedelic".

The word derives from the Ancient Greek entheos (ἔνθεος), meaning "full of god, inspired, possessed", and genesthai, "to come into being". An entheogen is thus a substance that "generates the divine within".

Historical use

Entheogens have been used in religious and shamanic contexts for thousands of years. Well-documented examples include the use of peyote cactus by Indigenous peoples of North America and the Native American Church, ayahuasca brews in Amazonian traditions and in the Santo Daime and União do Vegetal churches of Brazil, psilocybin mushrooms in Mesoamerican ritual, iboga in the Bwiti religion of Central Africa, and the fly agaric mushroom (Amanita muscaria) in Siberian shamanism.

Scholars have also proposed entheogenic interpretations of substances in the ancient world, including the kykeon consumed during the Eleusinian Mysteries of ancient Greece and soma in the Rigveda, though the identities of these preparations remain debated.

Legal status

The legal status of entheogens varies widely by country and substance. Many are controlled substances under international drug conventions, while some jurisdictions recognize exemptions for bona fide religious use. In the United States, the Supreme Court case Gonzales v. O Centro Espírita Beneficente União do Vegetal (2006) affirmed a religious-use exemption for ayahuasca (hoasca) tea under the Religious Freedom Restoration Act.
