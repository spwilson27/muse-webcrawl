11 Downing Street

11 Downing Street in London is the official residence of the Chancellor of the Exchequer, the United Kingdom's finance minister. It stands beside the better-known 10 Downing Street, the residence of the Prime Minister, in the City of Westminster. The building has been the Chancellor's official home since 1828.

History

The houses of Downing Street were built in the 1680s by Sir George Downing on the site of the former Hampden House. Number 11 was occupied in the early nineteenth century by various ministers before becoming the Chancellor of the Exchequer's residence in 1828. The interiors of Nos. 10 and 11 have long been interconnected, and the buildings behind their modest brick facades are far larger than they appear, having been extensively remodelled over the centuries, notably under Sir John Soane and Raymond Erith.

Use

Although Number 11 is the Chancellor's official residence, the living accommodation above the offices is sometimes used by the Prime Minister instead; in 1997, Tony Blair and Gordon Brown famously swapped flats because the accommodation above Number 11 is larger and suited Blair's family better. The Chancellor traditionally poses outside the door of Number 11 displaying the Budget box on Budget day.
