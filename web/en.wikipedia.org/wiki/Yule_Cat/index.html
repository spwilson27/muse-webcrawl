Yule Cat

The Yule Cat (Icelandic: Jólakötturinn, also Jólaköttur) is a huge and vicious cat from Icelandic folklore that is said to lurk in the snowy countryside around Christmastime and eat people who do not receive new clothes to wear before Christmas Eve — in some versions, eating their food or the people themselves if they have not earned new garments through hard work.

History

The threat of the Yule Cat was used by farmers as an incentive for their workers to finish processing the autumn wool before Christmas. Those who participated in the work would receive new clothes, but those who did not would be left without — and would thus be preyed upon by the cat. The tradition connects with the Icelandic custom by which everyone, however poor, received at least one new item of clothing and new shoes at Christmas; those who did not were said to "go to the Christmas cat".

Although often claimed to be ancient, written references to the Yule Cat date mainly from the nineteenth century. The creature is associated with other figures of the Icelandic Christmas season — the ogress Grýla, her husband Leppalúði, and their thirteen sons, the Yule Lads — and is said to be Grýla's pet.

In culture

The poet Jóhannes úr Kötlum wrote a famous poem, "Jólakötturinn", about the cat, which was later set to music and popularised by the singer Björk in a 1987 recording ("Jólakötturinn", on the single "Gilsbakki"). The Yule Cat appears in modern Icelandic Christmas celebrations, decorative displays and international coverage of unusual Christmas traditions.
