Died21 September 1936 (aged 69), Châteaumeillant, France

Paul Jules Antoine Meillet (11 November 1866 – 21 September 1936) was a French linguist, one of the most influential figures in historical linguistics and the study of the Indo-European languages in the first half of the twentieth century. He taught at the Collège de France and trained a generation of linguists that included Émile Benveniste, Georges Dumézil, and André Martinet.

Life and career

Meillet studied at the Sorbonne, where he was influenced by Ferdinand de Saussure and Michel Bréal. He travelled to Vienna and the Caucasus to study Armenian. From 1897 he directed studies in Armenian philology at the École Pratique des Hautes Études, and in 1906 he succeeded Saussure's teacher Bréal at the Collège de France, holding the chair of comparative grammar until his death.

He edited the journal Bulletin de la Société de Linguistique de Paris for many years and was a central figure in French academic linguistics.

Work

Meillet's most famous works include the Introduction à l'étude comparative des langues indo-européennes (1903), a standard textbook revised through many editions; the two-volume Caractères généraux des langues germaniques (1917); Les langues dans l'Europe nouvelle (1918), on the languages of post-war Europe; and Le slave commun (1924), a landmark reconstruction of Common Slavic.

His name is attached to Meillet's law, describing the loss of mobile accent in certain Balto-Slavic forms. He also gave the classic formulation of grammaticalization, defining it as "the attribution of a grammatical character to a previously autonomous word."

Rejecting purely mechanical views of sound change, Meillet insisted that language change must be understood in its social context — a perspective summarised in his dictum that languages change because societies change. He nonetheless maintained the rigour of the comparative method developed by the Neogrammarians.
