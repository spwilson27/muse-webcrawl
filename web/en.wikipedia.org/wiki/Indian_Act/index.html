Indian Act

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Indian Act is a Canadian federal statute governing matters pertaining to registered Indians and reserves.

The Indian Act (French: Loi sur les Indiens) is a Canadian Act of Parliament that concerns registered Indians, their bands, and the system of Indian reserves. First passed in 1876 as The Indian Act, 1876, it consolidated earlier colonial legislation on the status and management of Indigenous peoples. The Minister of Crown–Indigenous Relations administers much of the Act.

Scope

The Act defines who is a "status Indian" for federal purposes, governs band membership and elections, reserve land management, wills and estates, and the tax-exemption provisions of section 87. It historically imposed sweeping controls: compulsory enfranchisement, bans on the potlatch and Sun Dance, restrictions on leaving reserves, and the framework for the residential school system. Most coercive provisions were repealed in the 1951 revision, and the 1985 Bill C-31 removed the rule stripping status from women who married non-status men, though second-generation cut-off rules remain contested.

Criticism and reform efforts

First Nations leaders have long denounced the Act as paternalistic and colonial; the government's 1969 White Paper proposing its repeal was withdrawn after fierce opposition, as many feared losing treaty rights and status protections. Successive governments have pursued incremental change and self-government agreements outside the Act, while the statute itself remains in force and is sometimes defended as a shield of constitutionally recognised rights.
