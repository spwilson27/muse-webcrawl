Mariner program

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mariner program
OperatorNASA / JPL
Flights1962–1975 (10 spacecraft)
TargetsVenus, Mars, Mercury
First successMariner 2 (Venus, 1962)
A Mariner probe.

The Mariner program was NASA's first series of interplanetary probes, built by the Jet Propulsion Laboratory between 1962 and 1975, which made the first successful flybys of Venus, Mars and Mercury and returned humanity's first close-up photographs of other planets. Ten spacecraft flew (Mariner 1 was lost), mapping the era when the solar system changed from points of light to worlds.

Mariner 2 (1962) confirmed Venus's furnace-hot surface; Mariner 4 (1965) showed a cratered, apparently dead Mars; Mariner 9 (1971) became the first spacecraft to orbit another planet, mapping Mars's volcanoes and the canyon system later named Valles Marineris for it; Mariner 10 (1974–75) used the first Gravity assist to reach Mercury.

Legacy

The Mariner buses taught JPL deep-space navigation, telecommunications and imaging — the foundation on which Voyager, built from Mariner heritage, and all later planetary missions stand. Mariner 9's dust-storm wait and global map remain the template for orbiters' patience and ambition.
