Mzilikazi

Mzilikazi Khumalo (c. 1790 – 9 September 1868) was a Southern African king who founded the Matabele (Ndebele) kingdom in what is now southwestern Zimbabwe. Originally a lieutenant of Shaka Zulu, he broke away during the Mfecane and led his people, the Khumalo, on a long migration north.

Bornc. 1790, near Mkuze, Zululand
Died9 September 1868, Ingama, Matabeleland
SuccessorLobengula

Migration

Around 1823 Mzilikazi quarrelled with Shaka and fled west across the Drakensberg, conquering Sotho-Tswana peoples in the Transvaal and ruling from near modern Pretoria. Defeated by Voortrekkers at Vegkop (1836) and Mosega (1837), he crossed the Limpopo and established Matabeleland, with his capital at Bulawayo.

Kingdom

He organised the Ndebele state on Zulu military lines, incorporating Shona and other peoples. He maintained relations — sometimes tense — with missionaries such as Robert Moffat and traders. He died in 1868 and, after a succession dispute, was succeeded by his son Lobengula, who later signed the Rudd Concession.
