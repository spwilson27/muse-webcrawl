Gerlachov (Hungarian: Gerlachfalva, German: Gerlsdorf) is a village and municipality in Poprad District in the Prešov Region of northern Slovakia, situated at the foot of the High Tatra mountains. It lies below Gerlachovský štít, the highest peak of the Tatras and of Slovakia (2,655 m), from which both the village and the peak take their names.

History

The village was first mentioned in historical records in 1326. It was founded by German gold prospectors and named after a locator (founder) called Gerlach; it was one of the German (Zipser) settlements of the Spiš region. Mining and later agriculture, forestry and shepherding shaped the local economy. The village belonged to the Kingdom of Hungary until 1918, then to Czechoslovakia, and since 1993 to Slovakia. The historic Evangelical (Lutheran) church, built in the 14th century in early Gothic style and later rebuilt, preserves medieval wall paintings.

Tourism

Today Gerlachov serves as a base for tourism and mountaineering in the central High Tatras, with hotels, pensions, and a ski slope. It forms part of the network of Tatra settlements together with nearby Tatranská Polianka, Nová Polianka, and Vyšné Hágy. Hiking routes lead from the village toward the Velická Valley and the ascent of Gerlachovský štít, which by regulation may be climbed only with a certified mountain guide.
