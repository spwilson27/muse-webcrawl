Michael Somare

Born: Michael Thomas Somare, 9 April 1936, Rabaul, Territory of New Guinea

Died: 25/26 February 2021 (aged 84), Port Moresby

Prime Minister of Papua New Guinea: 1975–1980; 1982–1985; 2002–2011

Party: Pangu Party (co-founder); National Alliance Party

Sir Michael Thomas Somare (9 April 1936 – 25/26 February 2021) was a Papua New Guinean politician widely regarded as the founding father of the nation. He led Papua New Guinea to self-government in 1973 and independence from Australia on 16 September 1975, serving as its first Prime Minister, and held the office for a total of about 17 years across three periods — longer than any other Papua New Guinean. He was universally known in the country by the Tok Pisin title "Sana", and styled the "Grand Chief".

Early life and political beginnings

Somare was born in Rabaul and grew up at Karau village in East Sepik Province. His father was among the first Papua New Guinean policemen. He worked as a teacher and radio broadcaster in Wewak, where in 1967 he co-founded the Pangu Pati (Papua and Niugini Union Party) to press for self-government. He was elected to the House of Assembly in 1968, became Chief Minister after the 1972 elections, and negotiated the transition to independence, overseeing the drafting of the constitution.

Prime ministerial career

Somare served as prime minister from 1975 to 1980 and again from 1982 to 1985, before returning to power in 2002 and winning re-election in 2007. His 2002–2011 government was notable for stability and for presiding over economic growth driven by mining and the Papua New Guinea LNG project. In 2011, while Somare was receiving medical treatment in Singapore and incapacitated, parliament elected Peter O'Neill as prime minister, sparking the 2011–12 constitutional crisis, in which the Supreme Court ruled Somare remained the legitimate prime minister. Somare chose not to press the confrontation, and O'Neill's position was confirmed at the 2012 election.

Later life and legacy

Somare retired from politics in 2017 after serving as governor of East Sepik. He died of pancreatic cancer in Port Moresby in February 2021, and was given a state funeral and a period of national mourning; he was buried at Kreer Heights in Wewak. He was appointed a Knight Grand Cross of the Order of St Michael and St George (GCMG) and was a member of the Order of the Companions of Honour. Papua New Guinea's reverence for Somare as the father of independence is reflected in monuments, the Sir Michael Somare Stadium, and his place in the national motto of unity in diversity.
