Bimini is the westernmost district of the Bahamas, a chain of islands roughly 50 miles (80 km) east of Miami, Florida. The largest islands are North Bimini and South Bimini, and the district capital is Alice Town on North Bimini. Bimini is the Bahamian island group closest to the United States mainland.

History

The islands were inhabited by the Lucayan people before European contact. In Lucayan, Bimini is thought to mean "two islands". During Prohibition in the United States, Bimini became a hub for rum-running. Author Ernest Hemingway lived on Bimini from 1935 to 1937, wrote parts of To Have and Have Not there, and popularised the island as a destination for big-game sport fishing, particularly for Atlantic blue marlin and tuna.

Attractions and folklore

Bimini is famous for the "Bimini Road", an underwater rock formation off North Bimini that some have claimed to be a road or wall of the lost city of Atlantis; geologists generally regard it as naturally fractured beachrock. The islands are also associated in folklore with the Fountain of Youth, which Juan Ponce de León was said to be seeking in the region. Other draws include sport fishing tournaments, diving with sharks and wild dolphins, and the resort development on North Bimini.

Geography

The islands sit on the edge of the Great Bahama Bank where the Gulf Stream passes close offshore, making the surrounding waters rich in marine life. South Bimini hosts an airport and a small nature trail; Cat Cay and Gun Cay lie to the south of the main islands.
