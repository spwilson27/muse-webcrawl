Sergei Yesenin

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sergei Aleksandrovich Yesenin (Russian: Сергей Александрович Есенин; 3 October [O.S. 21 September] 1895 – 28 December 1925) was a Russian lyric poet, one of the most popular and celebrated figures in Russian literature. Raised in a peasant family in the village of Konstantinovo, Ryazan Governorate, he became the leading voice of the "peasant poets" and later a founder of the Imaginist movement, famous for his tender, musical verse about rural Russia, nature, love and loss.

Life

Yesenin moved to Moscow in 1912 and then to Petrograd in 1915, where Alexander Blok and the peasant-poet circle welcomed him; his first collection, Radunitsa (1916), brought him fame. He initially celebrated the 1917 Revolution (as in the poem Inonia) but grew disillusioned, mourning the vanishing wooden Russia of his childhood in poems like "I Am the Last Poet of the Village" and "The Stern October Has Deceived Me".

In 1921 he co-founded the Imaginist group with Anatoly Marienhof. From 1922 to 1923 he was married to the American dancer Isadora Duncan and toured Europe and the United States with her, a chaotic period marked by heavy drinking. His final masterpieces include the confessional cycle Moscow of the Taverns, the narrative poem Anna Snegina, "The Black Man", and the farewell poem written — according to legend, in his own blood — "Goodbye, my friend, goodbye" ("До свиданья, друг мой, до свиданья").

Death and legacy

Yesenin died in a room of the Angleterre Hotel in Leningrad on 28 December 1925; the official finding was suicide by hanging, though conspiracy theories alleging murder by the secret police have circulated ever since without conclusive evidence. Despite periodic official disfavour in the Soviet era, his poems were memorised and beloved by generations of Russians, and many have been set to music as popular songs.
