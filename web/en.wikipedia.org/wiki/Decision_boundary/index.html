Decision boundary

FieldStatistical classification, machine learning
DefinitionSurface dividing the feature space between predicted classes

In a statistical classification problem, a decision boundary is a hypersurface that partitions the underlying feature space into regions, on each side of which the classifier assigns a different class label. If the boundary is a hyperplane, the classifier is a linear classifier; more flexible models produce curved or highly irregular boundaries.

Examples

Linear discriminant analysis and logistic regression produce linear decision boundaries, support vector machines choose the hyperplane with maximum margin between classes, and neural networks and decision-tree ensembles can learn complex nonlinear boundaries. In the classical two-class problem with Gaussian class distributions, the optimal (Bayes) decision boundary can be derived from the class densities.

Role in learning theory

The flexibility of a model's decision boundary relates to the bias–variance trade-off: overly complex boundaries may overfit training data, while overly simple ones underfit. Empirically mapping a classifier's boundary is also a way to visualise and audit model behaviour in low-dimensional problems.
