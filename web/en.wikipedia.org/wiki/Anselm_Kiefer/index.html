Anselm Kiefer

Anselm Kiefer (born 8 March 1945) is a German painter and sculptor. His monumental works confront German history, the Holocaust, myth and memory, often incorporating materials such as straw, ash, clay, lead and shellac. He is associated with Neo-Expressionism.

Born8 March 1945, Donaueschingen, Germany
MovementNeo-Expressionism
Known forPainting, installation, sculpture

Career

Born in the final months of the Second World War, Kiefer studied with Joseph Beuys in Düsseldorf. His early "Occupations" photographs (1969), performing the Nazi salute in Europe, provoked controversy. Through the 1980s his vast, scarred landscapes and architectural interiors made him internationally prominent, including at the 1980 Venice Biennale.

Later work

Since 1993 he has lived in France, creating studio complexes at Barjac and Croissy-Beaubourg with towers, tunnels and installations. Themes expanded to Kabbalah, alchemy, Norse and Egyptian myth, and poets such as Paul Celan. Major retrospectives include the Royal Academy, London (2014), and his permanent installation honours Maurice Genevoix at the Panthéon, Paris (2020).
