Decentralized application

A decentralized application (dApp or DApp) is a computer application that runs on a decentralized computing system, most often a blockchain. Unlike traditional applications, dApps operate on a peer-to-peer network rather than a single central server, and their back-end logic typically runs in smart contracts, with records of operation stored immutably on the chain.

Characteristics

A dApp generally has open-source code, operates autonomously with no entity controlling a majority of its tokens, stores data cryptographically on a public blockchain, and uses cryptographic tokens for access or to reward network participants. Because the rules are encoded in smart contracts, dApps can run exactly as programmed without any possibility of downtime, censorship or third-party interference — the pitch made for Ethereum, the leading dApp platform, at its 2015 launch.

Applications

  • Decentralized finance (DeFi) — exchanges such as Uniswap, lending protocols such as Aave, and stablecoins such as Dai.
  • Games and collectibles — from CryptoKitties (2017) onward, with non-fungible tokens (NFTs) representing in-game assets.
  • Governance — decentralized autonomous organizations (DAOs) coordinating by token voting.

Criticism

Critics point to poor user experience, scalability limits, high transaction fees, smart-contract exploits, and the frequent concentration of control in foundations, front-end operators or large token holders — questioning how decentralized many dApps really are.
