Dogwood

Dogwood is a common name for plants of the genus Cornus, a group of about 30–60 species of woody plants in the family Cornaceae, native throughout much of temperate and boreal Eurasia and North America, with a few species in tropical Africa and South America.

Description

Dogwoods include deciduous trees and shrubs as well as a few herbaceous subshrubs. Most are grown as ornamentals for their showy petal-like bracts surrounding small flower clusters — as in the flowering dogwood (Cornus florida) and Pacific dogwood (Cornus nuttallii) — for colourful stems in winter, as in the red-osier dogwood, or for autumn foliage and fruit. The hard, dense wood was historically used for shuttle-making and tool handles.

Cultivation

Dogwoods generally prefer moist, acidic, well-drained soil and partial shade. They are subject to diseases such as dogwood anthracnose.
