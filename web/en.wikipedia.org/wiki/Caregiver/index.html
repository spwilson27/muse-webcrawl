Caregiver

A caregiver or informal caregiver is a person who regularly looks after a child, or a sick, elderly, or disabled person. Caregivers may be family members, friends, or paid professionals such as home health aides. A caregiver helps with the activities of daily living, which can include bathing, dressing, feeding, mobility assistance, medication management, and household tasks.

Types

The majority of care is provided informally by spouses, adult children and other relatives, often called family caregivers. Paid caregivers include personal care aides, home health aides, and nurses. Live-in caregivers reside in the home of the person receiving care.

Health effects

Providing care can be emotionally and physically demanding. Research has documented elevated rates of depression, anxiety and physical illness among heavily burdened carers — a phenomenon discussed under caregiver stress. Support services include respite care, support groups, counselling and, in some jurisdictions, carer's allowances or paid leave.

Epidemiology

As populations age, the number of people requiring long-term care is growing in most developed countries, and the demand for both informal and formal caregivers is rising correspondingly. Family caregivers provide the bulk of long-term care in the United States and many other countries, with the economic value of their unpaid work estimated in the hundreds of billions of dollars per year.
