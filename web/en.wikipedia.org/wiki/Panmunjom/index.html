Panmunjom

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Panmunjom (Korean: 판문점) is a village in the Korean Demilitarized Zone (DMZ), best known as the site of the negotiations that ended the fighting of the Korean War and of the Joint Security Area (JSA), where North and South Korean forces stand in close proximity. The original village, a small farming community, lay just south of the present JSA.

The armistice negotiations

Armistice talks between the United Nations Command and the Korean People's Army/Chinese People's Volunteers began at Kaesong in July 1951 and moved to Panmunjom that autumn, where talks continued for two years in tents and temporary structures. On 27 July 1953, the Korean Armistice Agreement was signed at Panmunjom by representatives of the UN Command, North Korea, and China — South Korea declined to sign — establishing the Military Demarcation Line and the DMZ. The modest building erected for the signing, by North Korean accounts in a matter of hours, is preserved as the North Korean Peace Museum.

The Joint Security Area

The JSA, often called the "Truce Village," contains the iconic row of blue conference huts straddling the demarcation line, where the Military Armistice Commission met and where tourists (on supervised tours) may briefly step across the line inside the central hut. Nearby landmarks include the Bridge of No Return, used for prisoner exchanges in 1953; Freedom House and the Peace House on the southern side; and Panmungak on the northern side. The area saw violent incidents, most notoriously the 1976 axe murder of two U.S. officers during a tree-pruning operation, and the 2017 defection of a North Korean soldier who crossed under fire.

In 2018, Panmunjom hosted the inter-Korean summit between Moon Jae-in and Kim Jong Un, who stepped across the line together; the resulting Panmunjom Declaration pledged work toward peace and denuclearization. A trilateral meeting with U.S. President Donald Trump followed at the site in June 2019.
