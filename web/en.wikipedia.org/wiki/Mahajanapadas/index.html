Mahājanapadas

Periodc. 600 – c. 300 BCE
RegionIndo-Gangetic Plain, northern India
Succeeded byMagadha Empire; Maurya Empire

The Mahājanapadas (Sanskrit: "great realms") were sixteen kingdoms and aristocratic republics that existed in ancient India from the sixth to fourth centuries BCE, during the second urbanisation of northern India after the decline of the Indus Valley Civilisation. Ancient Buddhist texts such as the Anguttara Nikaya list sixteen states: Kasi, Kosala, Anga, Magadha, Vajji, Malla, Chedi, Vatsa, Kuru, Panchala, Matsya, Surasena, Assaka, Avanti, Gandhara, and Kamboja.

Organisation

Most Mahājanapadas were monarchies ruled by a king advised by councils, but the Vajji confederacy and the Mallas were gaṇa-saṅgha — oligarchic republics governed by assemblies of clan chiefs. The era saw the growth of fortified cities, punch-marked coinage, standing armies, and trade along the northern and southern routes (Uttarapatha and Dakshinapatha). It was also the age of the Buddha and Mahavira, whose teachings spread through these states.

Decline

Through conquest and diplomacy, the eastern kingdom of Magadha under the Haryanka, Shishunaga, and Nanda dynasties gradually absorbed or subordinated its rivals, and by the late fourth century BCE the remaining Mahājanapadas had been incorporated into a single imperial system that paved the way for the Maurya Empire.
