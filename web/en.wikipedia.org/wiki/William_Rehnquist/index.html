William Rehnquist


Born October 1, 1924, Milwaukee, Wisconsin, U.S.
Died September 3, 2005 (aged 80), Arlington, Virginia
Office Chief Justice of the United States (1986–2005); Associate Justice (1972–1986)

William Hubbs Rehnquist (October 1, 1924 – September 3, 2005) was an American lawyer and jurist who served on the U.S. Supreme Court for 33 years, first as an Associate Justice from 1972 to 1986 and then as the 16th Chief Justice from 1986 until his death in 2005.

Early life and career

Rehnquist grew up in Wisconsin, served in the Army Air Forces during the Second World War, and graduated first in his class at Stanford Law School, where he clerked for Justice Robert H. Jackson. He practised law in Phoenix, Arizona, became active in Republican politics, and joined the Nixon administration as Assistant Attorney General for the Office of Legal Counsel in 1969.

Supreme Court

As an associate justice, Rehnquist was the Court's most consistent conservative, often dissenting alone in his early years in favour of states' rights and a narrower reading of the Fourteenth Amendment. As Chief Justice he presided over a Court that limited congressional power under the Commerce Clause in cases such as United States v. Lopez (1995) and United States v. Morrison (2000), a development known as the federalism revolution. He wrote the majority opinion in Bush v. Gore (2000) and presided over the impeachment trial of President Bill Clinton in 1999.

Legacy

Rehnquist died in office in 2005 and was succeeded by John Roberts. He authored several popular books on Supreme Court history, including The Supreme Court and Centennial Crisis.
