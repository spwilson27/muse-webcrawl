Military history of Australia

The military history of Australia spans the pre-colonial warfare of Indigenous Australians, the frontier conflicts of the colonial era, and the operations of Australian forces from Federation in 1901 to the present. More than 100,000 Australians have died in war service, and Australian forces have served in most major conflicts of the twentieth and twenty-first centuries alongside allies including Britain, New Zealand, and the United States.

Colonial era and Federation

British forces garrisoned the Australian colonies from 1788, and colonial volunteers later served overseas, including in New Zealand, the Sudan, South Africa during the Boer War, and China during the Boxer Rebellion. Frontier conflict between settlers and Aboriginal peoples continued for well over a century. After Federation, the new Commonwealth created national naval and military forces.

First and Second World Wars

In the First World War, the First Australian Imperial Force served at Gallipoli in 1915 — a campaign central to Australian national commemoration on Anzac Day — and on the Western Front, while light horsemen fought in the Middle East. In the Second World War, Australian forces fought in the Mediterranean and North Africa, including at Tobruk and El Alamein, and then in the Pacific, notably on the Kokoda Track in New Guinea; Australia itself came under air attack, including the bombing of Darwin in 1942.

Post-war era

Australian forces subsequently served in the Korean War, the Malayan Emergency, the Indonesia–Malaysia confrontation, and the Vietnam War, in which more than 60,000 Australians served and conscription proved deeply divisive. Since the Cold War, Australians have deployed on peacekeeping missions and to the Gulf War, East Timor, Afghanistan, and Iraq. The modern Australian Defence Force comprises the Royal Australian Navy, the Australian Army, and the Royal Australian Air Force.
