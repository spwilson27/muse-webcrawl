Ministry of Railways (India)

The Ministry of Railways is a ministry in the Government of India responsible for the country's rail transport system. It administers Indian Railways, one of the world's largest railway networks, through the Railway Board, headquartered at Rail Bhavan in New Delhi.

Organisation

The ministry is headed by the Minister of Railways, a cabinet-rank minister, assisted by ministers of state. The Railway Board, chaired by the Chairman and Chief Executive Officer, is the apex administrative body for Indian Railways, which operates more than 68,000 kilometres of track, runs tens of thousands of passenger and freight trains daily, and employs well over a million people, making it one of the largest employers in the world.

History

Railways in India began in 1853 with the first line between Bombay and Thane. After independence, the fragmented colonial-era companies were nationalized and unified. Uniquely among Indian ministries, the railways long presented a separate Railway Budget to Parliament — a practice dating to the 1920s — until it was merged with the general Union Budget in 2017.

Functions and initiatives

The ministry oversees operations, infrastructure expansion, electrification, safety, and modernization programs, including high-speed and semi-high-speed services such as the Vande Bharat Express, dedicated freight corridors, and station redevelopment schemes. It also controls public sector undertakings such as IRCTC, RailTel and CONCOR.
