Dyson sphere

A Dyson sphere is a hypothetical megastructure that encompasses a star and captures a large percentage of its solar power output. The concept is a thought experiment that attempts to imagine how a sufficiently advanced civilisation could meet its energy demands, and it serves as a target in the search for extraterrestrial intelligence: a Dyson sphere would radiate waste heat in the mid-infrared, potentially detectable across interstellar distances.

Origin

The concept was proposed by physicist Freeman Dyson in his 1960 paper "Search for Artificial Stellar Sources of Infrared Radiation" in Science. Dyson was inspired in part by Olaf Stapledon's 1937 novel Star Maker. Dyson himself envisaged not a solid shell but a loose collection of independently orbiting solar collectors and habitats — now often distinguished as a Dyson swarm.

Variants

  • Dyson swarm — large numbers of independent satellites in stable orbits; regarded as the most feasible form.
  • Dyson bubble — a swarm of statites held in place by radiation pressure rather than orbital motion.
  • Dyson shell — a uniform solid shell around the star; mechanically implausible with known materials and popularised mainly by science fiction.
  • Dyson ring and net — intermediate configurations.

Searches

Infrared surveys using IRAS, WISE and other telescopes have searched for Dyson-sphere signatures without confirmed results. The unusual dimming of Tabby's Star (KIC 8462852) briefly revived public interest, though natural explanations (comet and dust clouds) are now favoured.
