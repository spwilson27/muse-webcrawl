The Westerwald (German pronunciation: [ˈvɛstɐˌvalt], literally "Western forest") is a low mountain range on the right bank of the Rhine in western Germany, spanning parts of Rhineland-Palatinate, Hesse, and North Rhine-Westphalia. It lies between the Rhine, the Lahn, the Dill, and the Sieg rivers, and forms part of the Rhenish Massif. Its highest point is the Fuchskaute at 657.3 metres (2,156 ft).

Geography and geology

The Westerwald is an uplifted, eroded part of the Rhenish Slate Mountains, built largely of Devonian slate, greywacke, and basalt from Tertiary volcanic activity. The basalt cones and the Siebengebirge at its northwestern edge reflect this volcanic origin. The region is traditionally divided into the Upper Westerwald around the Fuchskaute plateau, the Lower Westerwald toward the Rhine, and the Vorderwesterwald along the Rhine hills.

Economy and culture

Historically the region was known for iron-ore mining, slate and basalt quarrying, and for the salt-glazed stoneware pottery of the Kannenbäckerland ("jug-bakers' land") around Höhr-Grenzhausen, exported worldwide for centuries. Today the economy rests on medium-sized manufacturing, logistics along the A3 motorway between Cologne and Frankfurt, and tourism centred on hiking, the Westerwaldsteig trail, and its lakes and half-timbered towns such as Hachenburg, Altenkirchen, Montabaur, and Dillenburg.

The region is celebrated in the folk song "Westerwaldlied" ("Oh du schöner Westerwald"), composed in the 1930s.
