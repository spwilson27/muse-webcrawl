My Little Pony: Friendship Is Magic fandom

The My Little Pony: Friendship Is Magic fandom is the community of fans of the 2010 animated television series created by Lauren Faust for Hasbro. Unusually for a show aimed at young girls, it attracted a large following of adult fans, predominantly male, who call themselves "bronies" (a portmanteau of "bro" and "pony"); female fans are sometimes called "pegasisters". The fandom emerged on the imageboard 4chan in late 2010 and grew into a substantial creative subculture producing fan art, fan fiction, music, videos, games and conventions.

Origins

Interest began when the show premiered in October 2010 on The Hub. Discussions on 4chan's comics and cartoons board, initially ironic, gave way to genuine appreciation of the show's writing, animation and humour, and fans began organising on dedicated sites such as Equestria Daily (founded January 2011) and Ponychan. The phenomenon attracted wide media coverage, with commentators noting the sincerity and productivity of the community as well as the surprise of its demographic.

Activities and culture

Bronies have produced a vast body of fan works, including the long-running fan fiction Fallout: Equestria, fan-made episodes and games, and remixes of the show's music; musicians such as "Eurobeat Brony" gained followings through fan songs. Conventions included BronyCon in Baltimore, which peaked at over 10,000 attendees before holding its final edition in 2019, as well as GalaCon in Germany and many regional events. The fandom has also been noted for charitable fundraising, including support for humanitarian and community causes through organisations such as Bronies for Good.

Response

The show's creators and voice cast embraced the adult fandom, incorporating nods to it in later seasons and in the character "Derpy Hooves". Hasbro accommodated the audience with merchandise aimed at older collectors. Academics have studied the fandom as a case of participatory culture and shifting norms of masculinity; large-scale fan surveys (the "State of the Herd" reports) documented its demographics. Although activity declined after the series ended in 2019, the community persists around the franchise's subsequent generations.
