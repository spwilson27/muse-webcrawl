Utrecht

Utrecht is the fourth-largest city of the Netherlands, as well as the capital and the most populous city of the province of Utrecht. Located in the eastern corner of the Randstad conurbation, it is a major transport and university hub, centred on the medieval old town with its canals and the iconic Dom Tower, the tallest church tower in the Netherlands.

History

Utrecht began as the Roman fortress of Traiectum, built around AD 47 on the frontier of the empire along the Rhine. In the early Middle Ages it became the seat of Saint Willibrord, the "Apostle to the Frisians", and grew into one of the most important cities of the northern Netherlands, governed for centuries by its prince-bishops.

In 1579 the Union of Utrecht was signed in the city, laying the foundation of the Dutch Republic. The Treaties of Utrecht (1713) ended the War of the Spanish Succession and redrew the map of Europe and its colonial empires.

Geography and transport

The city is split by canals, notably the Oudegracht with its distinctive wharves and wharf cellars. Utrecht Centraal is the largest railway station in the Netherlands, reflecting the city's position at the centre of the national rail network; Utrecht is also famous for its extensive cycling infrastructure.

Culture and education

Utrecht University, founded in 1636, is one of the largest universities in the Netherlands. The city is home to the Centraal Museum, the Rietveld Schröder House (a UNESCO World Heritage Site designed by Gerrit Rietveld), TivoliVredenburg concert hall, and the football club FC Utrecht.
