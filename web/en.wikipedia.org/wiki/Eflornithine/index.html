Eflornithine

Chemical structure of eflornithine (DFMO)

Eflornithine, sold under brand names including Vaniqa and Ornidyl, is a medication used to treat African trypanosomiasis (sleeping sickness) and excessive facial hair in women. It is a suicide inhibitor of ornithine decarboxylase and is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical uses

For sleeping sickness caused by Trypanosoma brucei gambiense, eflornithine is given intravenously, often in combination with nifurtimox (NECT), and is effective in the second, neurological stage of the disease. Its resurrection of patients from coma earned it the nickname "the resurrection drug." As a cream, it slows the growth of unwanted facial hair in women by inhibiting the enzyme needed for hair production in the follicle.

History

Eflornithine (α-difluoromethylornithine, DFMO) was developed in the 1970s as a potential cancer drug. Production for sleeping sickness was halted in 1995 for commercial reasons and resumed in 2001 after the drug found a market as a hair-removal cream and an agreement between the manufacturer and the WHO — a case often cited in debates over neglected tropical diseases. Since 2023, trials have also shown that high-dose eflornithine prolongs survival in children with high-risk neuroblastoma, leading to regulatory approval for that use.

Side effects

Intravenous use can cause anemia, low white-cell counts, seizures and hearing effects; the topical cream mainly causes local skin irritation. Cancer-treatment use is also being investigated in prevention of colorectal and skin cancers.
