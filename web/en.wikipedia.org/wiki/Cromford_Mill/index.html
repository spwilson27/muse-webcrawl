Cromford Mill

Location: Cromford, Derbyshire, England

Built: 1771 (first mill); expanded 1776 and later

Founder: Richard Arkwright

Status: Museum; part of Derwent Valley Mills UNESCO World Heritage Site

Cromford Mill is a former cotton-spinning mill in Cromford, Derbyshire, England, built by Richard Arkwright in 1771. It was the first successful water-powered cotton spinning mill in the world and the archetype of the factory system that powered the Industrial Revolution; Arkwright's mills at Cromford pioneered round-the-clock shift working, machine-paced production, and the factory village.

History

Arkwright chose Cromford for the reliable flow of water from the Bonsall Brook and the Cromford Sough, a lead-mine drainage channel that never froze and rarely dried up. His water frame, patented in 1769, produced a strong cotton thread suitable for warp, enabling all-cotton cloth for the first time. A second mill followed in 1776, and the complex grew to employ hundreds, many of them children, working 12-hour shifts day and night.

To house the workforce, Arkwright built rows of stone cottages, including North Street (1771–76), among the earliest purpose-built industrial housing in Britain. The Arkwright Society acquired the site in 1979 and restored the derelict buildings; Cromford Mills now hosts a visitor centre, exhibitions, shops and workspaces.

World Heritage status

In 2001 the mill became part of the Derwent Valley Mills UNESCO World Heritage Site, which stretches 24 km along the Derwent valley and includes the mills at Derby, Belper, Milford and Darley Abbey, recognising the valley as the birthplace of the factory system.
