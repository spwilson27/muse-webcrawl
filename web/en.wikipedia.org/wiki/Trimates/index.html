Trimates

The Trimates, sometimes called "Leakey's Angels," were three women primatologists chosen in the 1960s and 1970s by the palaeoanthropologist Louis Leakey to conduct long-term field studies of great apes in their natural habitats: Jane Goodall, who studied chimpanzees at Gombe, Tanzania; Dian Fossey, who studied mountain gorillas in Rwanda; and Birutė Galdikas, who studied orangutans in Indonesian Borneo.

Background

Leakey believed that long-term observation of living great apes could illuminate the behaviour of early human ancestors, and he thought women might prove especially patient and perceptive field observers. Each of the three established a research station that continues today and devoted decades to habituating and recording individual apes.

Legacy

The Trimates transformed primatology and public understanding of apes: Goodall documented tool-making and warfare among chimpanzees, Fossey's work — and her 1985 murder — drew worldwide attention to gorilla conservation, and Galdikas founded Orangutan Foundation International and campaigned for rainforest protection. All three became leading conservation advocates, and their studies are among the longest-running animal behaviour research programmes ever undertaken.
