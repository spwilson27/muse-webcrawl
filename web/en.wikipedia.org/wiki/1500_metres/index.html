1500 metres

The 1500 metres is a middle-distance track event in athletics, contested at the Olympic Games since 1896 for men and 1972 for women. Run over three and three-quarter laps of a standard 400 m track, it is the metric counterpart of the mile and is sometimes called the "metric mile".

Race characteristics

The 1500 m blends aerobic endurance with tactical speed. Championship races are frequently slow and tactical, decided by a finishing kick over the last 300–400 metres, while paced invitational races produce faster times. Runners start from a curved waterfall start and may break for the inside lane immediately.

Records and champions

The men's world record is 3:26.00, set by Hicham El Guerrouj of Morocco in Rome in 1998. The women's world record is held by Faith Kipyegon of Kenya, who ran 3:49.04 in Paris in 2024. Famous champions include Sebastian Coe, Noureddine Morceli, El Guerrouj and Kipyegon, who won three consecutive Olympic titles.
