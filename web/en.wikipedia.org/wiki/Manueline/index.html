The Manueline style (Portuguese: estilo manuelino), also known as Portuguese late Gothic, is the sumptuous, composite architectural style developed in Portugal during the reign of King Manuel I (1495–1521), at the height of the Portuguese Age of Discovery.

Characteristics

Manueline ornament fuses late Gothic structure with motifs drawn from the sea and the voyages: twisted ropes, coral, shells, anchors, armillary spheres, the cross of the Order of Christ, and exotic flora and fauna from newly encountered lands. Richly carved portals, windows and cloisters burst with this nautical and naturalistic decoration, reflecting the wealth flowing into Lisbon from the spice trade.

Notable examples

Masterpieces of the style include the Jerónimos Monastery and the Tower of Belém in Lisbon, the chapter-house window of the Convent of Christ in Tomar — one of the most exuberant carved compositions in European art — and the Royal Cloister of Batalha Monastery.
