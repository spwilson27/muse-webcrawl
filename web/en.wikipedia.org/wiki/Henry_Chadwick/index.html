Henry Chadwick (5 October 1820 – 20 April 1908) was an English-American sportswriter, baseball statistician and historian, often called the "Father of Baseball" for his early reporting on and promotion of the game, and for developing many of the statistics and tabular representations that remain in use today.

Career

Born in Exeter, England, Chadwick emigrated to Brooklyn in 1837. A cricket reporter, he encountered baseball in the 1850s and became its foremost chronicler, editing the Beadle's Dime Base-Ball Player guides and writing for New York newspapers. He devised the box score (adapted from cricket scorecards), the batting average, and the scoring shorthand that uses "K" for strikeout.

Legacy

Chadwick was a member of the rules committees of the early associations and campaigned for reforms to the game. He was inducted into the Baseball Hall of Fame in 1938, one of the first writers so honoured, and the Hall's annual award for baseball research writing bears his name.
