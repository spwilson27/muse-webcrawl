The Exorcism of Emily Rose is a 2005 American legal drama horror film directed by Scott Derrickson and written by Derrickson and Paul Harris Boardman. The story, inspired by the real-life case of Anneliese Michel, is presented largely in flashback during the negligent-homicide trial of a Catholic priest.

Plot

Father Richard Moore is charged after 19-year-old college student Emily Rose dies following an exorcism he performed. Defense attorney Erin Bruner argues Emily was possessed, while the prosecution maintains she suffered from epilepsy and psychosis and died because medical treatment was stopped. Witnesses, taped recordings of the exorcism, and Emily's letter — in which she accepts her suffering as a sign to others — structure the testimony. The jury convicts Moore but recommends time served, and he is released; Bruner declines a partnership and visits Emily's grave, marked with a verse from Philippians.

Reception

The film grossed about $144 million worldwide against a modest budget. Critics were mixed but praised Jennifer Carpenter's physically intense performance as Emily, for which she won an MTV Movie Award. The film is frequently cited as an unusual hybrid of courtroom drama and horror, and it renewed public interest in the Anneliese Michel case, whose exorcism in 1975–76 in Germany led to the conviction of her parents and the priests involved.
