PricewaterhouseCoopers, doing business as PwC, is a multinational professional services network of firms operating as partnerships under the PwC brand. It is one of the "Big Four" accounting firms, alongside Deloitte, EY, and KPMG, and is among the largest professional services networks in the world by revenue and headcount.

History

The firm in its present form was created in 1998 by the merger of Price Waterhouse and Coopers & Lybrand, whose histories date to the nineteenth century: Samuel Lowell Price founded an accountancy practice in London in 1849, and Coopers & Lybrand traced its origins to firms founded in 1854 and 1898. In 2010 the firm shortened its trading name to PwC.

Operations

PwC firms operate in more than 150 countries and employ well over 300,000 people. Its service lines include audit and assurance, tax and legal services, deals, and consulting. As a network, PwC is structured as locally owned and managed member firms that agree to common standards, coordinated by PricewaterhouseCoopers International Limited.

Controversies

Like its peers, PwC has faced regulatory scrutiny over audit failures, including its role as auditor in cases such as the collapse of British outsourcing firm Carillion's audit alongside competitors, the Satyam scandal in India, and a 2023 Australian scandal involving the misuse of confidential government tax-plan information, which led to leadership changes and divestment of its Australian government consulting business.
