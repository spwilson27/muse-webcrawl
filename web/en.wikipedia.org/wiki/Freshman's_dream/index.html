Freshman's dream

The freshman's dream is a name given to the erroneous identity (x + y)p = xp + yp, where p is a real number. The name derives from the fact that students beginning algebra commonly make this mistake when first learning to expand powers of sums. The identity is false in general over the real numbers — for example, (1 + 1)2 = 4, not 1 + 1 = 2 — but it holds in special circumstances that make it a useful teaching example.

When the identity holds

When p is a prime number and x and y are members of a commutative ring whose characteristic is p, the freshman's dream turns out to be correct. This follows from the binomial theorem: in the expansion of (x + y)p, every intermediate binomial coefficient is divisible by p, and hence vanishes in a ring of characteristic p, leaving only xp + yp. The resulting map is known as the Frobenius endomorphism, a cornerstone of algebra in positive characteristic.

Related results

A similar error occurs with other operations that do not distribute over addition, such as square roots and logarithms: students may wrongly write √(x + y) = √x + √y. More generally, the "dream" generalises to sums of more than two terms in characteristic p, where (x1 + ... + xn)p equals the sum of the individual p-th powers. The name "freshman's dream" is also applied to the related identity for p-th powers of matrices in suitable settings.
