Theme Building

LocationLos Angeles International Airport (LAX), California
Completed1961
StyleGoogie / Mid-century modern, Populuxe
ArchitectsPereira & Luckman; Paul R. Williams; Welton Becket (design team)

The Theme Building is a landmark structure at Los Angeles International Airport (LAX), completed in 1961. Its two white parabolic arches, rising 135 feet and supporting a disc-shaped restaurant building, make it look like a flying saucer on four legs, and it is one of the most famous examples of Googie and mid-century modern architecture.

Design and history

The building was designed for the airport's expansion for the jet age by a team led by Pereira & Luckman with Paul R. Williams and Welton Becket, its space-age form expressing Los Angeles's optimism about air travel. Original plans imagined the arches supporting a glass dome linking all terminals; that scheme was never built. The disc housed a restaurant — for many years the Encounter Restaurant, refurbished in 1997 with a retro-futuristic interior by Disney Imagineering — and an observation deck. It was designated a Los Angeles Historic-Cultural Monument in 1993.

Appearances

Because it is instantly recognisable, the Theme Building appears in numerous films, television programmes and video games set in Los Angeles. Structural work in 2010 repaired stucco on the arches; the restaurant has opened and closed several times, but the building remains the visual symbol of LAX.
