Acoustic reflex

The acoustic reflex (also known as the stapedius reflex) is an involuntary contraction of the stapedius muscle in the middle ear in response to loud sound stimulation. When present, the reflex stiffens the ossicular chain, reducing the transmission of low-frequency sound energy to the cochlea by up to about 20 decibels.

Reflex arc

Sound signals travel from the cochlea through the vestibulocochlear nerve to the cochlear nucleus and superior olivary complex of the brainstem; motor output returns via the facial nerve to the stapedius on both sides, so stimulating one ear normally triggers contraction in both ears. The reflex threshold in healthy ears is typically 70–100 dB above hearing threshold.

Clinical measurement

Acoustic reflex thresholds are measured during tympanometry in audiology. Absent or elevated reflexes can indicate middle-ear disease, sensorineural hearing loss, retrocochlear pathology such as vestibular schwannoma, or facial nerve lesions.
