Charles James Fox (24 January 1749 – 13 September 1806) was a prominent British Whig statesman whose parliamentary career spanned 38 years of the late 18th and early 19th centuries. He was a celebrated orator, a rival of William Pitt the Younger, and a leader of the opposition to the policies of King George III and Lord North during the American War of Independence.

Early life

Fox was the third son of Henry Fox, 1st Baron Holland, a leading Whig politician. Privately educated and then sent to Eton and Hertford College, Oxford, he entered Parliament for Midhurst in 1768 at the age of nineteen, a year below the legal minimum, without challenge.

Political career

Fox opposed the government's coercive policy toward the American colonies and became a leading voice for conciliation. In 1782 he became Foreign Secretary in the Rockingham ministry and negotiated preliminaries of peace with the United States. In 1783 he formed the Fox–North coalition with his former enemy Lord North, which introduced Fox's East India Bill; its defeat in the House of Lords brought down the government and installed Pitt the Younger.

For most of the rest of his career Fox led the Whig opposition, defending civil liberties during the repression that accompanied the French Revolutionary Wars and championing the abolition of the slave trade. He briefly returned to office as Foreign Secretary in the "Ministry of All the Talents" in 1806, the year of his death.
