RAI — Radiotelevisione italiana is Italy's national public broadcasting company, owned by the Italian Ministry of Economy and Finance. It operates numerous television channels and radio stations, funded by a licence fee (the canone) supplemented by advertising, and is one of the largest broadcasters in Europe.

History

Italian broadcasting began with the Unione Radiofonica Italiana in 1924, succeeded by the fascist-era EIAR and, after the Second World War, by RAI — Radio Audizioni Italiane. Regular television broadcasts began on 3 January 1954. For two decades RAI held a monopoly, structured after 1975 reforms into networks associated with the major political parties — a practice known as lottizzazione. Private competition arrived with Silvio Berlusconi's Mediaset in the 1980s, creating a RAI–Mediaset duopoly that long dominated Italian television.

Services

RAI's flagship channels are Rai 1 (general entertainment), Rai 2 and Rai 3 (the latter with strong regional programming), alongside news, sport, film, children's and cultural channels, the streaming platform RaiPlay, and radio networks Rai Radio 1, 2 and 3. RAI is a founding member of the European Broadcasting Union and organises Italy's participation in the Eurovision Song Contest; it founded the Sanremo Music Festival, its most famous entertainment institution.

Criticism

RAI has recurrently been criticised for political interference in appointments and news coverage, an issue raised by international press-freedom observers whenever governing parties tighten control of its board.
