Deva, Romania

Deva (Romanian pronunciation: [ˈdeva]; Hungarian: Déva, German: Diemrich) is a city in Romania, in the historical region of Transylvania, on the left bank of the river Mureș. It is the capital of Hunedoara County and had a population of about 53,000 at the 2021 census. The city is dominated by its medieval fortress, built on a steep volcanic hill in the middle of the town.

History

The name Deva is thought to derive from the Dacian word dava, meaning fortress, and the area of the nearby Sarmizegetusa region was a heartland of the ancient Dacian kingdom. Documentary evidence of the settlement dates from 1269, when the "royal castle of Deva" was mentioned. The fortress was repeatedly rebuilt and played a role in the defence of Transylvania; John Hunyadi and later Transylvanian princes held it, and in 1551 it was briefly occupied by the Ottomans. In 1849, during the Hungarian war of independence, an explosion of the fortress's powder magazine destroyed much of the citadel, leaving the picturesque ruins seen today. Deva developed as an administrative and commercial centre and grew substantially during Romania's industrialisation in the twentieth century.

Landmarks and sport

The fortress ruins, reached by an inclined lift or on foot, are the city's symbol and a protected monument within a nature reserve. Below the hill stand the Magna Curia palace, now the Museum of Dacian and Roman Civilisation, and the old town centre. Deva is also famous in Romania as the home of the national gymnastics training centre where Nadia Comăneci trained under Béla Károlyi in the 1970s, establishing the city as a cradle of Romanian gymnastics.
