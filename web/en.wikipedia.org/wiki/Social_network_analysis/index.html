Social network analysis (SNA) is the process of investigating social structures through the use of networks and graph theory. It characterises networked structures in terms of nodes (individual actors, people, or things within the network) and the ties, edges, or links (relationships or interactions) that connect them. Rather than treating individuals as independent units, SNA assumes that how actors behave depends substantially on their position within a web of relationships.

History

The field descends from Jacob Moreno's sociometry in the 1930s, the Manchester School of anthropology, and structural sociology at Harvard in the 1970s. Mark Granovetter's 1973 "strength of weak ties" paper and his work on embeddedness, Ronald Burt's theory of structural holes, and Barry Wellman's community studies established SNA as a distinct approach within sociology, before it spread to organisational studies, public health, criminology, and computer science.

Measures

Common measures include degree centrality (number of direct ties), betweenness centrality (how often a node lies on shortest paths between others), closeness centrality, and eigenvector centrality (connection to other well-connected nodes). Network-level measures include density, centralisation, and modularity; community detection algorithms identify clusters of densely connected nodes. Ties may be directed or undirected, weighted or binary, and relationships can be multiplex — several types of tie at once.

Data collection

Data are gathered through surveys (for example, name-generator questions), observations, archival records, and, since the 2000s, digital trace data from online platforms, e-mail, and mobile phones. Analysts must define network boundaries, handle missing data, and address the ethics of mapping people's relationships.

Applications

SNA is used to study the spread of disease and behaviour, organisational communication and innovation, political influence, terrorist and criminal networks, and online communities. Visualisation software such as Gephi and analytic libraries such as igraph and NetworkX are standard tools.
