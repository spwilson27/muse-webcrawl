Mexican Texas is the historiographical name used to refer to the era of Texan history between 1821 and 1836, when Texas was governed as part of Mexico. The era began with Mexico's War of Independence, which secured its freedom from Spain, and ended with the Texas Revolution, in which Texas declared its independence and established the Republic of Texas.

Immigration

To develop the sparsely populated frontier, Mexico adopted colonization laws and granted contracts to empresarios such as Stephen F. Austin, who brought hundreds of Anglo-American families — the Old Three Hundred — to settle in Texas. By 1830, Anglo settlers outnumbered Tejanos, prompting Mexico to pass the Law of April 6, 1830, which banned further immigration from the United States and suspended empresario contracts.

Tensions and revolution

Disputes over customs duties, slavery (which Mexico had abolished), and centralist reforms under President Antonio López de Santa Anna escalated into open conflict in 1835. Following battles at Gonzales, the Alamo, and San Jacinto, Texas secured de facto independence in 1836, though Mexico did not formally recognize it until after the Mexican–American War.
