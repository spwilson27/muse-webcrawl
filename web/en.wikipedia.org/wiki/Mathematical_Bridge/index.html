Mathematical Bridge

Mathematical Bridge
Crosses: River Cam
Locale: Cambridge, England
Design: Tangent and radial trussing wooden bridge
Designer: William Etheridge
Built: 1749 (original); rebuilt 1866 and 1905

The Mathematical Bridge is a wooden footbridge in the southwest of central Cambridge, England. It bridges the River Cam between Queens' College and the President's Lodge, and connects the two parts of Queens' College across the river. Its official name is simply the Wooden Bridge or Queens' Bridge.

History and design

The bridge was designed by William Etheridge (1709–1776) and built by James Essex the Elder in 1749. It has been rebuilt on two occasions, in 1866 and in 1905, but has kept the same overall design. The bridge is built of straight timbers arranged in a tangent and radial trussing pattern, which produces its characteristic curved appearance: the members form tangents to the arc of the bridge, held together with radial pieces.

Legend

Popular myth holds that the bridge was designed and built by Sir Isaac Newton without the use of nuts or bolts, and that students, attempting to take it apart to understand it, could not reassemble it without bolts. In fact, Newton had died in 1727, more than two decades before the bridge was built, and the original structure did use bolts and iron fixings.
