Jean-François Lyotard

Jean-François Lyotard (10 August 1924 – 21 April 1998) was a French philosopher, sociologist, and literary theorist best known for articulating the concept of postmodernism in his 1979 work The Postmodern Condition.

Born10 August 1924, Versailles, France
Died21 April 1998, Paris, France
Era20th-century philosophy

Work

Lyotard famously defined the postmodern as "incredulity toward metanarratives," arguing that grand legitimating stories of progress and emancipation had lost credibility. In The Differend (1983) he explored conflicts between incommensurable language games, and in Libidinal Economy (1974) he developed a controversial critique of Marxism and structuralism.

Influence

His writings shaped debates in aesthetics, ethics, and politics, and he taught at the University of Paris VIII (Vincennes) and Emory University.
