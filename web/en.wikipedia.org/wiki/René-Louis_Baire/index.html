René-Louis Baire

René-Louis Baire (French: [ʁəne lwi bɛʁ]; 21 January 1874 – 5 July 1932) was a French mathematician best known for his Baire category theorem, which he proved in his dissertation of 1899. His work laid a foundation for general topology and functional analysis.

Biography

Baire was born in Paris, the son of a tailor. He won a scholarship to attend the Lycée Lakanal and later studied at the École Normale Supérieure, where he was a student of Charles Hermite and came to know Émile Borel and Henri Lebesgue. His doctoral thesis, Sur les fonctions de variables réelles ("On the functions of real variables"), defended in 1899, introduced the hierarchy of functions now called Baire classes and proved the category theorem for complete metric spaces and for the real line.

Career

After teaching at lycées in Troyes, Bar-le-Duc and Nancy, Baire held posts at the University of Montpellier, where he became professor of analysis, and then at the University of Dijon. His health was fragile throughout his life; plagued by a chronic digestive illness, he took long leaves of absence in Italy and later at Lausanne in Switzerland, where he taught for a time. He was elected to the French Academy of Sciences in 1922. His disputes with Lebesgue over the priority and significance of certain results marked his later years. Baire died in Chambéry in 1932.

Contributions

The Baire category theorem states that a complete metric space cannot be expressed as a countable union of nowhere dense sets. Metric spaces in which the theorem's conclusion holds are called Baire spaces. Baire's classification of discontinuous functions into classes (Baire class 0 being the continuous functions, and so on through the transfinite hierarchy) became a cornerstone of descriptive set theory. The Baire measure and the notion of a set of the first category ("meagre set") also trace back to his work.
