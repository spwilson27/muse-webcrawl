Saadiyat Island (Arabic for "Island of Happiness") is a natural island and tourism-cultural project off the coast of Abu Dhabi in the United Arab Emirates. Developed from the 2000s, it is divided into districts devoted to culture, beaches, marinas, nature reserves and lagoons, and is planned to house some 145,000 residents.

Cultural District

The island's centerpiece is the Saadiyat Cultural District, a cluster of museums by Pritzker Prize-winning architects: the Louvre Abu Dhabi by Jean Nouvel, opened in 2017 beneath a vast perforated dome; the Zayed National Museum by Foster + Partners; the Guggenheim Abu Dhabi by Frank Gehry; the Natural History Museum Abu Dhabi; and teamLab Phenomena Abu Dhabi. Their scale and budgets made the project one of the most ambitious cultural developments of its era.

Other developments

The island hosts the campus of New York University Abu Dhabi, branches of Cranleigh and Berklee College of Music, the Abrahamic Family House (a complex of a mosque, church and synagogue opened in 2023), luxury beach resorts, the Saadiyat Beach Golf Club designed by Gary Player, and protected nesting beaches for hawksbill turtles.

Controversy

Construction on Saadiyat drew sustained international criticism over the treatment of migrant workers, including recruitment fees, withheld passports and poor housing, documented by Human Rights Watch and others from 2009. Artists organized boycotts, and the Guggenheim faced protests. Abu Dhabi introduced labour reforms and monitoring in response, though campaigners continued to dispute their effectiveness.
