James Francis Dorsey (February 29, 1904 – June 12, 1957) was an American jazz clarinetist, saxophonist, composer, and big band leader. He was famed for his technical brilliance on the alto saxophone and clarinet, and he led one of the most successful big bands of the swing era. He was the older brother of bandleader Tommy Dorsey.

Career

The Dorsey brothers, sons of a coal miner and music teacher in Shenandoah, Pennsylvania, played together in the 1920s in bands including the Scranton Sirens and Paul Whiteman's orchestra, and co-led the Dorsey Brothers Orchestra from 1934. A quarrel on stage in 1935 split the brothers; Jimmy took over the band, which became the Jimmy Dorsey Orchestra.

His orchestra scored major hits in the early 1940s, notably with singers Bob Eberly and Helen O'Connell on "Green Eyes," "Amapola," "Tangerine," and "Brazil (Aquarela do Brasil)." His virtuoso alto features, such as "Contrasts" (his theme) and the clarinet showcase on standards, set a technical standard for saxophonists.

Later years

In 1953 the brothers reconciled and merged bands; after Tommy's sudden death in November 1956, Jimmy led the orchestra until his own death from lung cancer six months later. His final record, "So Rare," released in 1957, reached No. 2 and became a posthumous gold record.
