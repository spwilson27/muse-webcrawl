Identity theory of mind

The identity theory of mind holds that states and processes of the mind are identical to states and processes of the brain. It is a form of physicalism in the philosophy of mind, first articulated in the late 1950s by U. T. Place and Herbert Feigl, and developed by J. J. C. Smart.

Overview

According to the theory, mental states such as pain are not correlated with, but literally identical to, brain states — for example, pain is the firing of certain nerve fibres. These identities are contingent, discovered empirically, much as science discovered that water is H2O or that lightning is electrical discharge.

Criticism

The multiple realizability argument, advanced by Hilary Putnam, objects that the same mental state may be realised by very different physical systems, undermining type-identity. This critique helped motivate functionalism as a successor view.
