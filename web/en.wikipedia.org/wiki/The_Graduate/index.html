The Graduate is a 1967 American romantic comedy-drama film directed by Mike Nichols and written by Buck Henry and Calder Willingham, based on Charles Webb's 1963 novel. It stars Dustin Hoffman as Benjamin Braddock, a recent college graduate with no direction, who is seduced by Mrs. Robinson (Anne Bancroft), the wife of his father's business partner, and later falls in love with her daughter Elaine (Katharine Ross).

Plot

Benjamin returns home to Pasadena after graduating college and drifts through the summer, rebuffing advice — famously the one-word career counsel "plastics." He begins an affair with Mrs. Robinson but is pushed by his parents into dating Elaine. When Elaine learns of the affair she leaves for Berkeley; Benjamin follows, is rebuffed, but persists. In the climax, he races to Santa Barbara to stop Elaine's wedding, fighting off guests with a cross and escaping with her on a bus, where their elation fades into ambiguity in the celebrated final shot.

Legacy

The film was a massive commercial success and won Nichols the Academy Award for Best Director. Its use of Simon & Garfunkel songs, including "Mrs. Robinson" and "The Sound of Silence," helped popularize soundtrack-driven filmmaking. It captured the generational discontent of the 1960s and is preserved in the United States National Film Registry.
