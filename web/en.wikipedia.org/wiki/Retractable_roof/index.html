Retractable roof

A retractable roof is a roof system designed to slide or fold open so that the interior of a building — most often a sports stadium — can be open to the outdoors. Retractable roofs allow venues in cold, hot, or rainy climates to host events in the open air in good weather while protecting spectators and playing surfaces from bad weather when closed.

A stadium retractable roof in the process of opening

History

Small retractable covers date to antiquity — the Roman velarium shaded the Colosseum — but the modern stadium roof began with projects such as the Pittsburgh Civic Arena (1961), whose dome could open. Large operable roofs became prominent with Toronto's SkyDome (now Rogers Centre, 1989), the first fully retractable motorised stadium roof, and spread widely in North American baseball and football stadiums from the 1990s onward, including Chase Field, Daikin Park, and loanDepot Park.

Mechanisms

Roof panels typically travel on rails driven by electric motors; some designs slide panels over one another, others pivot or fan them, and some — notably Wimbledon's Centre Court roof (2009) and No. 1 Court roof — use trusses carrying a folding fabric membrane. Opening or closing a large stadium roof typically takes 10–25 minutes. In tennis, play can continue under a closed roof with artificial lighting, transforming an outdoor tournament into an indoor one.

Notable examples

  • Rogers Centre, Toronto
  • Millennium Stadium (Principality Stadium), Cardiff
  • Johan Cruyff Arena, Amsterdam
  • Tokyo Dome uses an air-supported membrane rather than a sliding roof
  • Mercedes-Benz Stadium, Atlanta, with pinwheel-shaped panels
