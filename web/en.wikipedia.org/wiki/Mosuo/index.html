The Mosuo (Chinese: 摩梭; pinyin: Mósuō) are a small ethnic group living in Yunnan and Sichuan provinces of China, close to the border with Tibet, principally around Lugu Lake in the Himalayan foothills. They are famous for their matrilineal social organisation and the practice of "walking marriages", and are often described — with some simplification — as one of the world's last matrilineal societies.

Society and family

Traditional Mosuo households are headed by a senior woman (the ah mi), and property and family names pass through the female line. Children belong to their mother's household and are raised there, often with maternal uncles fulfilling many day-to-day responsibilities associated elsewhere with fathers.

Walking marriage

In the custom known as tisese, or "walking marriage", partners do not live together: a man visits a woman's room at night and returns to his own maternal home before dawn. Such unions are based on mutual affection, may be long-lasting or brief, and carry no formal economic obligations between the partners.

Religion and modern change

Mosuo religious life combines the indigenous Daba priesthood with Tibetan Buddhism. Since the 1990s, tourism around Lugu Lake and integration into the wider Chinese economy have brought rapid change, though many Mosuo communities actively preserve their language and customs.
