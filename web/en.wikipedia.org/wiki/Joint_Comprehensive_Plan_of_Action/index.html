Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action

Drafted14 July 2015, Vienna, Austria
Effective16 January 2016 (Implementation Day)
PartiesIran; the P5+1 (China, France, Germany, Russia, United Kingdom, United States) and the European Union
SubjectNuclear programme of Iran

The Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA), commonly known as the Iran nuclear deal, is an agreement reached in Vienna on 14 July 2015 between Iran and the P5+1 group of world powers, under which Iran agreed to limit its nuclear programme in exchange for relief from economic sanctions.

Terms

Iran agreed to reduce its stockpile of enriched uranium by about 98 per cent, cap enrichment at 3.67 per cent for 15 years, operate a reduced number of first-generation centrifuges at Natanz, convert the Fordow facility to a research centre, redesign the Arak heavy-water reactor so it could not produce weapons-grade plutonium, and accept an expanded International Atomic Energy Agency inspection regime. In return, the United Nations, the European Union and the United States lifted nuclear-related sanctions once the IAEA verified implementation in January 2016.

United States withdrawal

On 8 May 2018, President Donald Trump announced the withdrawal of the United States from the agreement and the reimposition of sanctions, despite IAEA reports that Iran was complying with its terms. The remaining parties sought to preserve the deal, but from 2019 Iran progressively reduced its compliance, enriching uranium to higher levels and expanding its stockpile.

Aftermath

Diplomatic efforts to restore the agreement, including indirect talks in Vienna in 2021–2022, failed to produce a revival. The JCPOA's remaining restrictions have continued to lapse under its sunset provisions, and its future remains a central issue in Middle East diplomacy.
