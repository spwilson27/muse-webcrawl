Charles McArthur Ghankay Taylor (born 28 January 1948) is a former Liberian politician and convicted war criminal who served as the 22nd president of Liberia from 2 August 1997 until his resignation on 11 August 2003. As leader of the National Patriotic Front of Liberia (NPFL), he initiated the First Liberian Civil War in December 1989, and his forces' support for the Revolutionary United Front in neighbouring Sierra Leone made him a central figure in that country's civil war as well.

Early life and Doe government

Taylor was born in Arthington, Montserrado County, to an Americo-Liberian father and a Gola mother. He studied economics at Bentley College in Massachusetts, graduating in 1977. Returning to Liberia after Samuel Doe's 1980 coup, he headed the General Services Agency but fled in 1983 after being accused of embezzlement. Arrested in the United States in 1984, he escaped from a Massachusetts detention facility in 1985 and later surfaced in Libya, where his NPFL fighters trained.

Civil war and presidency

On 24 December 1989 Taylor's NPFL invaded Liberia from Côte d'Ivoire, igniting a civil war that killed more than 200,000 people and displaced much of the population. After years of factional fighting, Taylor won the 1997 general election with about 75 percent of the vote, in a ballot widely seen as held under intimidation. His presidency was marked by corruption, repression, and continued regional interference; a second civil war erupted in 1999 as the LURD rebel movement advanced on Monrovia.

Resignation, trial and conviction

Indicted by the Special Court for Sierra Leone in 2003, Taylor resigned on 11 August 2003 and went into exile in Nigeria. In March 2006 he was arrested while attempting to flee Nigeria and transferred to The Hague, where his trial opened in June 2007. On 26 April 2012 he was convicted on 11 counts of aiding and abetting war crimes and crimes against humanity — the first former head of state convicted by an international court since the Nuremberg trials — and sentenced to 50 years' imprisonment, which he is serving in the United Kingdom.
