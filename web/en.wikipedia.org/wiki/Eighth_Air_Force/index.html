Eighth Air Force

B-17s of the "Mighty Eighth" over occupied Europe

The Eighth Air Force (Air Forces Strategic) is a numbered air force of the United States Air Force, headquartered at Barksdale Air Force Base, Louisiana. Activated in 1942, it became famous in the Second World War as the "Mighty Eighth," the American heavy-bomber force that carried out the daylight strategic bombing campaign against Nazi Germany from bases in England. Today it is a component of Air Force Global Strike Command, responsible for the Air Force's bomber and reconnaissance forces.

Second World War

Activated in January 1942 and deployed to England under commanders including Carl Spaatz, Ira Eaker and, from January 1944, Jimmy Doolittle, the Eighth grew into the largest air armada of the war, flying B-17 and B-24 bombers escorted by P-51 Mustangs. It flew more than 600,000 bomber sorties, dropped some 700,000 tons of bombs, and suffered more than 26,000 airmen killed — more than the entire U.S. Marine Corps in the war — in campaigns from the costly 1943 Schweinfurt–Regensburg missions to the oil and transportation plans that crippled the German war economy in 1944–45.

Cold War and after

Postwar, the Eighth became a core Strategic Air Command force of nuclear bombers, served in Korea and Vietnam (including Operation Linebacker II), and after SAC's disbandment in 1992 passed through Air Combat Command to Global Strike Command. Its current forces include B-52H and B-2 bomber wings and the RC-135 reconnaissance fleet.
