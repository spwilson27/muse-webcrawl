Video on demand

Video on demand (VOD) is a media distribution system that allows users to access videos whenever they choose, rather than at a scheduled broadcast time. Users can typically pause, rewind, fast-forward, and resume content, streaming it over the internet or downloading it to a device such as a computer, set-top box, smart TV, or smartphone.

Early VOD experiments were conducted by cable companies in the 1990s, limited by bandwidth and server costs. The spread of broadband internet in the 2000s made large-scale streaming practical: Netflix launched its streaming service in 2007, and YouTube, Amazon Prime Video, Hulu, and later Disney+ and many others transformed television and film distribution. The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated adoption, and major film studios began releasing films simultaneously in cinemas and on streaming platforms.

Business models

  • SVOD (subscription video on demand) — a recurring fee for access to a library, e.g. Netflix
  • TVOD (transactional video on demand) — pay-per-view rental or purchase of individual titles
  • AVOD (advertising-supported video on demand) — free access funded by advertisements, e.g. YouTube and some tiers of major services
  • PVOD (premium video on demand) — higher-priced early access to new releases
  • NVOD (near video on demand) — scheduled staggered start times, used by some cable and satellite pay-per-view services

Downloadable content is delivered either by streaming with adaptive bitrate protocols (such as HLS and MPEG-DASH) that adjust quality to connection speed, or by progressive download. Digital rights management (DRM) systems are widely used to enforce licensing terms.
