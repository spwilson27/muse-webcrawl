Hilderic (c. 460s – 533) was the penultimate king of the Vandals and Alans in North Africa, ruling from 523 to 530. The son of King Huneric and the Roman princess Eudocia, he was therefore a grandson of both the Vandal king Genseric and the Roman emperor Valentinian III — a lineage reflected in his notably pro-Roman policies.

Reign

Unlike most Vandal kings, who were Arians and periodically persecuted the Nicene (Catholic) Church in Africa, Hilderic — influenced by his Roman mother — granted religious freedom to Catholics, allowed their bishops to return from exile, and permitted the election of a new Catholic bishop of Carthage. He also sought close relations with the Byzantine Empire under Justinian I. His leniency and his abandonment of traditional Vandal military values made him unpopular with the Vandal nobility, and a military defeat against Moorish rebels gave his cousin Gelimer the pretext to depose and imprison him in 530.

Death and aftermath

Hilderic's deposition provided Justinian with a casus belli for the Vandalic War (533–534). As Byzantine forces under Belisarius advanced, Gelimer had Hilderic and his close relatives executed. The Vandal kingdom was annexed to the Byzantine Empire shortly afterwards.
