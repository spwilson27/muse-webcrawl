Mogontiacum

Mogontiacum was the Roman name for the city that became modern Mainz, in Germany. Founded as a Roman military camp by the general Drusus around 13/12 BCE at the confluence of the Rhine and Main rivers, it became one of the most important Roman bases on the Rhine frontier and the capital of the province of Germania Superior.

History

The legionary fortress at Mogontiacum, guarded for long periods by two legions, anchored Rome's presence on the upper Rhine. A civilian settlement (canabae and vicus) grew around the camp, and the city received monumental buildings including a theatre — the largest north of the Alps — temples, and an aqueduct. The name is thought to derive from the Celtic god Mogon. The funerary monument known as the Drususstein commemorates Drusus, who died in 9 BCE.

Mogontiacum suffered during the invasions of the third and fourth centuries, including Alamanni attacks, but remained occupied into the early medieval period, when it developed into the Frankish and ecclesiastical city of Mainz.

Remains

Visible Roman remains in Mainz include the Jupiter Column, parts of the aqueduct (Römersteine), the Roman theatre excavated near the main station, the Drususstein, and the sanctuary of Isis and Magna Mater; the Romano-Germanic Central Museum in the city preserves many finds.
