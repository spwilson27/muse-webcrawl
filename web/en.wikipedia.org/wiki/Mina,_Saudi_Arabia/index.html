Mina, Saudi Arabia

Location: Makkah Province, Saudi Arabia
Known for: Hajj rituals — the "City of Tents"
Key sites: Jamarat Bridge; Masjid al-Khayf

Mina (Arabic: مِنَىٰ) is a valley and neighborhood located about five kilometres east of the city of Mecca in western Saudi Arabia. It is best known for its role in the Hajj pilgrimage, during which it hosts millions of pilgrims in a vast encampment of fireproof tents — often described as the largest tent city in the world, with on the order of 100,000 air-conditioned tents.

Role in the Hajj

Pilgrims spend several nights in Mina during the Hajj. Here they perform the ritual of the Stoning of the Devil (ramy al-jamarāt), throwing pebbles at three pillars (jamarāt) that represent Satan, at the site now served by the multi-level Jamarat Bridge. Islamic tradition associates Mina with the story of Abraham (Ibrahim), who is said to have been tempted by Satan here and to have driven him away by throwing stones, and with the place of the intended sacrifice of his son.

Safety

The concentration of enormous crowds in Mina has historically led to deadly crowd incidents, most catastrophically the stampede of 24 September 2015, in which at least several hundred — by some estimates more than two thousand — pilgrims died. Saudi authorities have since invested heavily in crowd management, one-way pedestrian flows, and expanded infrastructure.
