Liopleurodon

Temporal range: Middle to Late Jurassic, ~166–155 Ma

Family: Pliosauridae

Genus: Liopleurodon Sauvage, 1873

Liopleurodon (meaning "smooth-sided teeth") is an extinct genus of large, carnivorous marine reptiles belonging to the Pliosauroidea, a clade of short-necked plesiosaurs. The genus contains species that lived from the Callovian stage of the Middle Jurassic to the Kimmeridgian stage of the Late Jurassic Period, roughly 166 to 155 million years ago. It was a vast apex predator of the Middle to Late Jurassic seas that covered Europe.

Two species are widely recognised: Liopleurodon ferox, the type species known from England and France, and Liopleurodon pachydeirus. Fossil remains have also been reported from Germany and Russia.

Description

Liopleurodon was a large pliosaur with four powerful flippers, a relatively short neck, and a long, robust skull armed with large conical teeth that were smooth on one side — the feature that gave the genus its name. Early popular estimates greatly exaggerated its size; modern studies based on verified specimens indicate that typical adults were about 5–7 metres (16–23 ft) long, with very large individuals perhaps reaching around 10 metres. Its strong, paddle-like limbs suggest it was a fast, powerful swimmer, likely using alternating strokes of its flippers.

Palaeobiology

As an apex predator, Liopleurodon probably preyed on fish, squid-like cephalopods, ichthyosaurs, and other marine reptiles. Its large nostrils and well-developed sense of smell may have helped it locate prey in murky water. Bite marks on plesiosaur fossils and stomach contents including fish bones support this interpretation. Like other plesiosaurs, it likely gave birth to live young rather than laying eggs on land.

In popular culture

Liopleurodon achieved widespread fame through the BBC television series Walking with Dinosaurs (1999), which depicted it at an exaggerated length of 25 metres; the animal also became an internet meme through the viral animated short Charlie the Unicorn.
