Engine displacement

Engine displacement is the measure of the cylinder volume swept by all of the pistons of a piston engine, excluding the combustion chambers. It is commonly used as an expression of an engine's size, and by extension as a loose indicator of the power an engine might be capable of producing and the amount of fuel it should be expected to consume.

Calculation

Displacement is calculated from the bore (cylinder diameter), stroke (distance the piston travels), and number of cylinders:

displacement = π × (bore/2)² × stroke × (number of cylinders)

For example, an engine with a bore of 86 mm, a stroke of 86 mm and four cylinders displaces about 1,998 cm³, marketed as a "2.0-litre" engine.

Units and regulation

Displacement is usually expressed in litres (L), cubic centimetres (cc or cm³), or cubic inches (CID) in the United States. Many jurisdictions base vehicle taxation, licence classes and insurance categories on displacement thresholds, which has historically influenced engine design — encouraging, for example, the development of small turbocharged engines that deliver the performance of larger engines with a lower nominal displacement. Motorcycles are frequently classified by displacement classes such as 125 cc, 500 cc and 1000 cc.
