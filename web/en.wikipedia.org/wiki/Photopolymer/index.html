Photopolymer

A photopolymer is a polymer that changes its properties when exposed to light, most importantly by curing — hardening through polymerisation or crosslinking — in the illuminated regions. Photopolymers typically consist of monomers or oligomers combined with a photoinitiator that absorbs light (usually ultraviolet) and generates reactive species that start the polymerisation.

Applications

Photopolymer printing plates are widely used in flexography: a relief plate is produced by exposing a photopolymer sheet through a negative, so that the image areas harden and the unexposed areas are washed away. In dentistry, light-cured composite resins are hardened with a blue curing light. Photopolymer resins are the feedstock of stereolithography and related resin 3D printing processes, in which a part is built layer by layer by selective curing. Other uses include photoresists in microfabrication, UV-cured coatings, inks and adhesives, and aspherical optical elements and holograms.

Mechanism

Most photopolymers cure by free-radical polymerisation of acrylate or methacrylate groups; a second class uses cationic polymerisation of epoxies or vinyl ethers, which is less sensitive to oxygen inhibition. Polymerisation converts a liquid or soft solid into a crosslinked network, changing its solubility, hardness, and mechanical properties. Oxygen in the air can inhibit surface curing of radical systems, producing a tacky layer that is exploited deliberately in some continuous 3D printing processes.
