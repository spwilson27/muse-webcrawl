Escondida is a copper mine complex in the Atacama Desert of northern Chile, about 170 km southeast of Antofagasta at an elevation of roughly 3,100 metres. Its name means "hidden" in Spanish, referring to the main orebody being buried beneath hundreds of metres of barren rock. Escondida is the world's largest copper-producing mine, in peak years accounting for around 5% of global copper output and a significant share of Chilean GDP.

History

The porphyry copper deposit was discovered in 1981 after exploration along the West Fissure fault system that hosts many of Chile's great mines. Development by BHP and partners proceeded through the 1980s, and production began in late 1990. Successive expansions added concentrator capacity, a cathode plant using bioleaching, the Escondida Norte pit, and seawater desalination plants at the port of Coloso to relieve pressure on Atacama water supplies.

Labour disputes

The mine has been the site of major strikes, notably in 2006 and a 44-day strike in 2017 — the longest in Chilean mining history at that time — which affected global copper prices and the Chilean economy.
