Risshun (立春) is the first of the twenty-four solar terms (sekki) of the traditional East Asian calendar, marking the beginning of spring. It falls on or about 4 February, when the Sun reaches the celestial longitude of 315°. In Japan the day before Risshun is Setsubun, celebrated with the bean-throwing ritual mamemaki to drive away evil spirits.

Calendar significance

In the traditional lunisolar calendar, Risshun was treated as the start of the year for some astrological and fortune-telling purposes, including the counting of age and the beginning of the cycle of the Chinese zodiac in Japanese onmyōdō practice. The old Japanese calendar also counted the 88th night (Hachijūhachiya), the 210th day (Nihyakutōka) and the 220th day from Risshun as significant markers for farmers, the latter two warning of the typhoon season.

Because Risshun is fixed astronomically, the traditional New Year (lunar new year) was arranged to fall near it, and the term corresponds to the Chinese Lìchūn, around which spring festival customs developed.

Seasonal associations

Though February is still mid-winter in most of Japan, Risshun is associated with the first signs of spring: plum blossom, milder winds said to melt the ice of the mountains, and the Seven Herbs and spring greetings in formal correspondence, where the phrase "early spring" refers to the period after Risshun. In haiku, Risshun is a spring season word (kigo).
