VISION 2020

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


VISION 2020: The Right to Sight was a global initiative to eliminate avoidable blindness.

VISION 2020: The Right to Sight was a global initiative launched in 1999 by the World Health Organization (WHO) together with the International Agency for the Prevention of Blindness (IAPB) and other partners, with the aim of eliminating avoidable blindness by the year 2020.

Background

At the time of the launch, an estimated 45 million people worldwide were blind and a further 135 million had low vision, with the great majority of cases either preventable or treatable. The initiative sought to intensify and accelerate prevention of blindness activities and promote the development of comprehensive eye care systems integrated into national health systems.

Priority conditions

VISION 2020 focused on the conditions causing the greatest burden of avoidable vision loss, including cataract, refractive errors, trachoma, onchocerciasis (river blindness), and childhood blindness caused by vitamin A deficiency and other conditions. Strategies included disease control, human resource development, and strengthening infrastructure and technology.

Progress and legacy

Although global blindness was not eliminated by 2020, the initiative was credited with building eye-care capacity, expanding cataract surgery rates, and helping reduce the number of people blind from infectious causes. Its work was carried forward by the IAPB's subsequent "2030 In Sight" strategy and by World Health Assembly resolutions on integrated people-centred eye care.
