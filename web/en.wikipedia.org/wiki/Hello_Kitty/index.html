Hello Kitty (Japanese: ハロー・キティ, Harō Kiti), also known by her real name Kitty White, is a fictional character created by Yuko Shimizu, designed by Yuko Yamaguchi, and owned by the Japanese company Sanrio. Sanrio depicts Hello Kitty as a British anthropomorphic white cat with a red bow and no visible mouth, though her personification as a young girl in London is also presented in her backstory. According to her backstory, she lives in a London suburb with her parents and twin sister Mimmy.

History

Sanrio, founded in 1960, built its business on selling character goods decorated with cute designs. Hello Kitty was created in 1974 and first appeared on a vinyl coin purse sold in 1975. The character became a hit in Japan and, from the 1990s, grew into a global phenomenon with an extensive merchandising empire — stationery, school supplies, clothing, accessories, electronics, and collaborations with luxury brands. Sanrio deliberately left her mouthless so that, as Yamaguchi put it, people could project their own feelings onto her.

Character profile

Kitty White is said to be a young girl living in a London suburb with her mother Mary, father George, and twin sister Mimmy. She is five apples tall and weighs three apples; her blood type is A; she loves baking cookies and playing the piano, and aspires to be a pianist or a poet. Her best friend is her sister Mimmy, who wears a yellow bow over her right ear (Kitty's red bow is over her left). Her first animated series, Hello Kitty's Furry Tale Theater, aired in 1987.

Cultural impact

Hello Kitty is one of the highest-grossing media franchises of all time, a global symbol of Japanese kawaii (cute) culture, and twice appointed a tourism ambassador for Japan. In 2014 Sanrio clarified, to widespread surprise, that Hello Kitty is not literally a cat but a little girl — an official personification in keeping with the character's storytelling.
