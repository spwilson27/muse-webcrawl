Nayarit, officially the Free and Sovereign State of Nayarit (Spanish: Estado Libre y Soberano de Nayarit), is one of the 31 states of Mexico. It is divided into 20 municipalities and its capital city is Tepic. It is located in western Mexico, bordered by the states of Sinaloa to the northwest, Durango to the north, Zacatecas to the northeast and Jalisco to the south, and by the Pacific Ocean to the west.

Geography

Nayarit covers 27,857 square kilometres of terrain that descends from the Sierra Madre Occidental in the east through river valleys to a fertile coastal plain. The Río Grande de Santiago and the San Pedro Mezquital are its principal rivers. Off the coast lie the Islas Marías, a small archipelago long used as a penal colony. The climate ranges from tropical on the coast to temperate in the highlands.

History

The region was home to the Cora, Huichol (Wixárika), and Tepehuano peoples, many of whom resisted Spanish conquest; the Sierra del Nayar remained effectively independent until 1722. During the colonial period the territory formed part of Nueva Galicia, and after independence it belonged to Jalisco. Nayarit was separated as the federal Territory of Tepic in 1884 and admitted as a state on 26 January 1917, taking its name from Nayar, an early Cora leader.

Economy and tourism

Agriculture is central to the economy — tobacco, sugarcane, mangoes, avocados, coffee and beans are important crops — and fishing and shrimp farming are significant along the coast. The southern Pacific coast, branded the Riviera Nayarit, contains resort centres such as Nuevo Vallarta, Bucerías, Sayulita and Punta Mita, and tourism is a major source of income. The port of San Blas, founded in the colonial era as a Pacific naval base, is another historic attraction.
