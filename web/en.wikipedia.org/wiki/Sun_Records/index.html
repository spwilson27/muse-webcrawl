Sun Records is an American independent record label founded by producer Sam Phillips in Memphis, Tennessee on March 27, 1952. It is widely considered one of the most important record labels in American music history: Sun was the first label to record Elvis Presley, Roy Orbison, Johnny Cash, Carl Perkins and Jerry Lee Lewis, and played a central role in the emergence of rock and roll and rockabilly in the 1950s.

History

Phillips had opened the Memphis Recording Service at 706 Union Avenue in January 1950, recording blues and R&B artists including B.B. King, Howlin' Wolf, Junior Parker and Ike Turner, whose "Rocket 88" (1951, released on Chess) is often cited as the first rock and roll record. Licensing recordings to Chess and RPM, Phillips launched Sun in 1952 to record local talent directly; its early releases were mostly blues, R&B and country.

Phillips famously sought "a white man who had the Negro sound and the Negro feel" who could reach the teenage market. In July 1954 Elvis Presley, Scotty Moore and Bill Black recorded a fast version of Arthur Crudup's "That's All Right" at Sun — an experiment Phillips recognised as the breakthrough he wanted. Presley's Sun singles fused country and blues and ignited rockabilly, but in November 1955 Phillips sold Presley's contract to RCA Victor for $35,000 to keep the label solvent.

The Million Dollar Quartet and later years

On 4 December 1956 Presley, Cash, Perkins and Jerry Lee Lewis jammed together at the studio — the celebrated "Million Dollar Quartet" session. Sun scored major hits with Perkins's "Blue Suede Shoes" (1956), Jerry Lee Lewis's "Whole Lotta Shakin' Goin' On" and "Great Balls of Fire" (1957), Johnny Cash's "I Walk the Line" (1956), and records by Roy Orbison and Charlie Rich. By the early 1960s the hits waned; Phillips opened a larger studio and invested in radio stations. He sold Sun in 1969 to Shelby Singleton, whose Sun Entertainment Corporation has since managed and reissued the catalogue.

Legacy

Sun Studio remains a working studio and tourist attraction in Memphis, designated a National Historic Landmark in 2003. The label's yellow-and-brown rooster logo and its slapback-echo sound are icons of the rock and roll era.
