Full breakfast

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A full English breakfast, with fried eggs, bacon, sausages, beans and toast.

A full breakfast is a substantial cooked breakfast meal, typically featuring eggs, meat, and additional savoury items, served as the day's first meal. The best-known form is the full English breakfast, but equivalent traditions exist across the British Isles and in former British colonies, each with its own characteristic items.

Full English breakfast

A typical full English includes:

  • Back bacon and pork sausages (often Cumberland or Lincolnshire)
  • Fried, poached or scrambled eggs
  • Fried or grilled tomatoes, mushrooms and black pudding
  • Baked beans and fried bread or toast
  • Hash browns (a later addition, popular from the late 20th century)

Tea or coffee is the usual drink. The meal evolved from the hearty country-house breakfasts of Victorian Britain, spread through hotels and cafés, and by the mid-20th century was a standard offering of British transport cafés and bed-and-breakfasts.

Regional variants

  • Full Scottish breakfast — adds Lorne sausage (square sausage), potato scone (tattie scone) and, sometimes, haggis and oatcakes.
  • Full Welsh breakfast — adds cockles, laverbread (seaweed purée) and Welsh rarebit.
  • Full Irish breakfast — adds white pudding, soda bread and potato bread; often served with tea.
  • Ulster fry — the Northern Irish version, served with potato bread and soda farls, and traditionally all fried in one pan.

Outside the British Isles

In Australia and New Zealand, café versions are often called a "big breakfast" and include similar items with local additions such as avocado. In the United States, comparable plates — eggs, bacon or sausage, hash browns and toast or pancakes — are simply the classic diner breakfast. Health criticism of the traditional full breakfast, especially for its saturated fat and sodium content, has led to lighter variants, but it remains a popular weekend and holiday meal.
