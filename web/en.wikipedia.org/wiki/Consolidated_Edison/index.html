Consolidated Edison

The Consolidated Edison Building at Irving Place, New York City.

Consolidated Edison, Inc., commonly known as Con Edison or ConEd, is one of the largest investor-owned energy companies in the United States, headquartered in New York City. Its principal subsidiary, Consolidated Edison Company of New York, provides electric service to most of New York City and Westchester County, natural gas to parts of Manhattan, the Bronx, Queens and Westchester, and steam service in Manhattan through the New York City steam system, the largest district steam system in the world.

Con Edison traces its history to the New York Gas Light Company, founded in 1823. In 1882 Thomas Edison's Edison Illuminating Company began supplying electricity from the Pearl Street Station, the first commercial central power plant in the United States. A series of mergers combined the city's gas and electric companies, and the firm took the name Consolidated Edison in 1936.

The company serves about 3.6 million electric customers and 1.1 million gas customers, and operates roughly 94,000 miles of underground cable — the largest underground electrical system in the world. Con Edison is listed on the New York Stock Exchange (ticker: ED) and has paid dividends for decades as part of the S&P 500 index.
