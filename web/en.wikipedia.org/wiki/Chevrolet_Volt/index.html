The Chevrolet Volt is a plug-in hybrid and extended-range electric car manufactured by General Motors and marketed in various markets also as the Holden Volt in Australia and New Zealand, and as the Buick Velite 5 in China, and with a different fascia as the Vauxhall Ampera in the United Kingdom and as the Opel Ampera in the rest of Europe. Sales of the 2011 Volt began in the United States in mid-December 2010, making it one of the first mass-produced plug-in hybrids offered by a major manufacturer.

Powertrain

The Volt uses GM's Voltec powertrain: an electric motor drives the wheels, powered primarily by a lithium-ion battery pack (16 kWh in early models). When the battery reaches a minimum state of charge, a small petrol engine acts as a generator to sustain driving, giving a total range of roughly 380 miles. GM described the car as an "extended-range electric vehicle" because the engine does not normally drive the wheels directly, though in some high-speed modes a mechanical link improves efficiency.

Generations

The first generation (2011–2015) offered an EPA-rated 35–38 miles of all-electric range. The second generation (2016–2019), built on the D2XX platform, brought a larger 18.4 kWh battery, 53 miles of electric range, a lighter 1.5-litre range-extender engine, seating for five and substantially redesigned styling. Production ended in February 2019 as GM shifted focus to fully electric vehicles such as the Chevrolet Bolt EV.

Reception

The Volt won numerous awards, including the 2011 North American Car of the Year, and was for several years the best-selling plug-in vehicle in the United States. It is credited with popularising the plug-in hybrid concept and influencing subsequent vehicles across the industry.
