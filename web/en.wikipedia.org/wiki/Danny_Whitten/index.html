Danny Whitten

Danny Ray Whitten (May 8, 1943 – November 18, 1972) was an American guitarist and songwriter, best known for his work with Neil Young and the band Crazy Horse, and for writing the song "I Don't Want to Talk About It", a hit for Rod Stewart and Rita Coolidge. Whitten's struggles with heroin addiction led to his dismissal from Young's touring band in 1972; he died of a drug overdose the same night he was sent home.

Early career

Whitten grew up in Los Angeles and fronted Danny & the Memories, a doo-wop group that evolved into the Rockets, a psychedelic rock band whose self-titled 1968 album featured Whitten alongside future Crazy Horse members Billy Talbot and Ralph Molina, and violinist Bobby Notkoff.

Crazy Horse and Neil Young

In 1969 the Rockets' rhythm section and Whitten joined Neil Young as backing band Crazy Horse for Everybody Knows This Is Nowhere, featuring "Cinnamon Girl", "Down by the River" and "Cowgirl in the Sand". Whitten's harmony singing and dual guitar interplay with Young became central to the band's sound. Crazy Horse's self-titled 1971 debut album included Whitten's "I Don't Want to Talk About It".

Death

Whitten's heroin addiction worsened during rehearsals for Young's 1972–73 Time Fades Away tour. Unable to perform reliably, he was fired on November 18, 1972, and given money for a flight back to Los Angeles. That night he died of an overdose of alcohol and Valium. Young later wrote "The Needle and the Damage Done" about the devastation of heroin, and the album Tonight's the Night (recorded 1973, released 1975) was largely a requiem for Whitten and roadie Bruce Berry.

Legacy

Recordings of Whitten's unreleased solo material and his work with Crazy Horse appeared posthumously. His guitar-driven interplay with Young remains a template for American rock bands.
