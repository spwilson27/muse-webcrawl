Tom Landry

Tom Landry
Born: 11 September 1924, Mission, Texas
Died: 12 February 2000, Dallas, Texas
Head coach: Dallas Cowboys (1960–1988)

Thomas Wade Landry (September 11, 1924 – February 12, 2000) was an American professional football coach, player, and World War II veteran. He was the original head coach of the Dallas Cowboys, leading the team for 29 seasons from its founding in 1960 until 1988.

Coaching career

Landry built the Cowboys into "America's Team", winning two Super Bowls (VI and XII), five NFC championships, and recording twenty consecutive winning seasons (1966–1985), an NFL record. His 270 career wins rank among the most in league history. He was inducted into the Pro Football Hall of Fame in 1990.

Innovations

Landry was one of the game's great innovators. As defensive coordinator of the New York Giants in the 1950s he developed the 4–3 defence; in Dallas he refined the "flex defence", revived the shotgun formation as a regular offensive weapon, and popularized situational substitutions and computer-assisted scouting. Stoic on the sideline and famed for his fedora, he presented an image of calm discipline.

Early life

Born in Mission, Texas, Landry flew B-17 bombing missions over Europe with the Eighth Air Force during World War II, then played defensive back and punter for the New York Yankees of the AAFC and the New York Giants of the NFL.
