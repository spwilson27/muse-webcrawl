Corvallis is a city in and the county seat of Benton County in western Oregon, United States. Situated in the Willamette Valley on the west bank of the Willamette River, some 84 miles (135 km) south of Portland, its population was about 59,922 at the 2020 census. The name derives from the Latin phrase cor vallis, meaning “heart of the valley”.

History

The area was home to Kalapuya peoples before settlement. Joseph C. Avery arrived in 1845 and claimed land at the confluence of the Marys River and the Willamette; the town was first called Marysville, then renamed Corvallis in 1853 to avoid confusion with Marysville, California. Corvallis briefly served as territorial capital of Oregon in 1855. The Oregon Legislative Assembly chartered a college there in 1868, which became Oregon State University.

Economy and education

Oregon State University, with about 30,000 students, is the city's largest employer and shapes its economy and culture; the university's sports teams are the Oregon State Beavers. A major Hewlett-Packard campus, historically associated with inkjet printer development, has been another significant employer. Corvallis frequently ranks highly in quality-of-life and education measures and has one of the highest shares of residents with advanced degrees in the United States. Good Samaritan Regional Medical Center and research laboratories, including an EPA laboratory, also contribute to the economy.

Geography

Corvallis sits at the foot of the Oregon Coast Range, with Mary's Peak, the highest point in the Coast Range at 4,101 feet, visible to the west. The climate is Mediterranean-influenced, with wet winters and dry summers.
