Haemangioma

Haemangioma
Specialty: Oncology, dermatology
Symptoms: Red or purple raised lesion
Types: Infantile, congenital
Treatment: Often none; propranolol, laser, surgery

A haemangioma (British spelling; American: hemangioma) is a benign vascular tumour or vascular malformation consisting of an abnormal build-up of blood vessels in the skin or internal organs. Haemangiomas may occur anywhere on the body, most commonly on the face, scalp, chest or back.

Types

The most common type is the infantile haemangioma, often called a strawberry mark, which appears shortly after birth, grows rapidly for several months, and then slowly involutes over years. Congenital haemangiomas are fully formed at birth. Other types include cavernous haemangioma, capillary haemangioma, and tufted angioma.

Signs and symptoms

Haemangiomas are typically painless red to blue-purple lesions. Most infantile haemangiomas are harmless and resolve without treatment, but lesions near the eye, airway or lip, and very large lesions, may cause functional problems, ulceration or bleeding.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually clinical, supplemented by ultrasound or MRI for deeper lesions. Treatment, when needed, may include oral propranolol — now first-line for problematic infantile haemangiomas — topical timolol, laser therapy, corticosteroids, or surgical excision.
