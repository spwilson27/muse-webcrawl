G7

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Group of Seven (G7) is an intergovernmental political and economic forum consisting of Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, the United Kingdom and the United States of America, with the European Union participating as a non-enumerated member. Originating in finance-ministers' meetings in 1973 and leaders' summits from 1975 (Rambouillet), the group coordinated the economic policies of the largest advanced democracies through the Cold War and after.

History and role

Russia joined in the 1990s, creating the G8, but was suspended in 2014 following the annexation of Crimea and never readmitted, restoring the G7 format. Summits — rotating among members — set agendas on macroeconomic coordination, trade, development, health and security; the group's share of world GDP has declined with the rise of China and other emerging economies (still roughly two-fifths nominally), prompting the parallel G20's elevation after 2008 and debates about the G7's representativeness. Recent summits have concentrated on support for Ukraine, economic security and supply chains, climate finance, and coordination of sanctions and export controls.
