Automobilwerk Eisenach

Automobilwerk Eisenach (AWE, "Eisenach Automobile Works") was an automobile manufacturer in Eisenach, in the German state of Thuringia. Founded in 1896 as the Fahrzeugfabrik Eisenach, the plant built cars under several names and regimes across more than a century, and is best known as the producer of the Wartburg car in the German Democratic Republic (East Germany).

History

The Fahrzeugfabrik Eisenach was founded in 1896 by Heinrich Ehrhardt. Its first car, the Wartburg, appeared in 1898, borrowing the name of the nearby Wartburg Castle. In 1904 the Dixi brand was adopted, and in 1928 the factory was acquired by BMW, which used it to enter car production; the BMW 3/15 was built there.

After the Second World War Eisenach lay in the Soviet occupation zone. The plant was nationalised and produced cars badged EMW (Eisenacher Motorenwerk), continuing pre-war BMW-derived designs. In 1953 the works was renamed Automobilwerk Eisenach, and in 1956 launched the revived Wartburg marque with the Wartburg 311, a three-cylinder two-stroke saloon. Later models included the Wartburg 353 (1966) and its successor, the four-stroke Wartburg 1.3 (1988), built with a Volkswagen-derived engine.

Closure

Following German reunification in 1990, the ageing Wartburg could not compete with Western products. Production ended in April 1991 and the company was wound up by the Treuhandanstalt. Much of the workforce was taken on by a new Opel plant opened in Eisenach in 1992. Part of the old factory survives as the automobile welt eisenach museum.
