Proxima Centauri

Proxima Centauri
Type: Red dwarf (M5.5 Ve)
Distance: 4.246 light-years
Constellation: Centaurus
Discovered: 1915, Robert Innes
Planets: Proxima b, c (disputed), d

Proxima Centauri is a small, low-mass star located 4.2465 light-years (1.3020 pc) away from the Sun in the southern constellation of Centaurus. It is the closest known star to the Sun. Its Latin name means "nearest [star] of Centaurus". It was discovered in 1915 by Robert Innes.

Characteristics

Proxima is a red dwarf with about 12% of the Sun's mass, about 14% of its radius and a surface temperature near 3,000 K, shining with a tiny fraction of the Sun's luminosity, much of it infrared. It is a flare star with strong magnetic activity. It is gravitationally bound to the Alpha Centauri binary at a distance of about 0.2 light-years, orbiting with a period of roughly 550,000 years.

Planets

Proxima Centauri b, announced in 2016, is an Earth-mass planet in the habitable zone with an 11.2-day orbit, though intense stellar flares may threaten any atmosphere. A smaller planet, Proxima d, orbits every 5.1 days; the claimed cold super-Earth Proxima c remains disputed. The star is the target concept for interstellar probe proposals such as Breakthrough Starshot.
