Law of Spain

The law of Spain is the legal system of the Kingdom of Spain, a civil law jurisdiction rooted in Roman law and codified most importantly in the Spanish Constitution of 1978 and the Civil Code of 1889. Spain's system is influenced by the Napoleonic Code and by the country's own medieval compilations, such as the Siete Partidas.

The Palacio de las Cortes, seat of the Spanish legislature.

Sources and hierarchy

The hierarchy of norms places the Constitution at the apex, followed by organic laws (which regulate fundamental rights and require an absolute majority in the Congress of Deputies), ordinary laws, and regulations. Custom and general principles of law serve as supplementary sources. The law of the European Union also applies directly in Spain as a member state.

Territorial organisation

Spain's autonomous communities possess their own legislative powers, and several — including Catalonia, the Basque Country, Navarre, Galicia, Aragon, and the Balearic Islands — maintain historic regional civil laws (derechos forales) alongside the common Civil Code. The Constitutional Court resolves conflicts of competence and protects fundamental rights.
