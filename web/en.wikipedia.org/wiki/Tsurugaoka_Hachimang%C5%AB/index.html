Tsurugaoka Hachimangū

Tsurugaoka Hachimangū (鶴岡八幡宮) is the most important Shinto shrine in Kamakura, Kanagawa Prefecture, Japan, dedicated to Hachiman, the tutelary god of the Minamoto clan and of samurai. Founded by Minamoto no Yoriyoshi in 1063 near Yuigahama, the shrine was moved to its present site and greatly expanded by Minamoto no Yoritomo, founder of the Kamakura shogunate, in 1180.

Grounds and events

The shrine is approached by the long Dankazura raised walkway from the sea at Yuigahama, lined with cherry trees. Its grounds include the main hall (hongū) atop a grand stairway, the Wakamiya shrine, lotus ponds, and a museum. A famous giant ginkgo tree beside the stairs, said to have hidden the assassin of the shōgun Minamoto no Sanetomo in 1219, stood until it was toppled by a storm in 2010. Major festivals include the Reitaisai in September, featuring yabusame horseback archery.
