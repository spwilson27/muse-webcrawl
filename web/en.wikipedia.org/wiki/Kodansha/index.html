Kodansha Ltd. (株式会社講談社, Kabushiki-gaisha Kōdansha) is a Japanese privately held publishing company headquartered in Bunkyō, Tokyo. Founded in 1909 by Seiji Noma, it is one of Japan's largest publishers and the country's biggest manga publisher, long rivalled by Shueisha and Shogakukan.

History

Noma founded the company as the Dai-Nippon Yūbenkai ("Great Japan Oratorical Society") in 1909, publishing the speech magazine Yūben; it took the name Kodansha — from kōdan, traditional storytelling — in 1911. Magazines such as Kingu (1925) made it a mass-market giant before the Second World War. After the war the company rebuilt and expanded strongly into manga, children's books and general publishing.

Manga and magazines

Kodansha publishes many of Japan's best-known manga magazines, including Weekly Shōnen Magazine, Monthly Shōnen Magazine, the Bessatsu Shōnen Magazine, Nakayoshi (shōjo), Morning and Afternoon (seinen), and Weekly Young Magazine. Its manga catalogue includes Akira, Sailor Moon, Attack on Titan, Fairy Tail, The Seven Deadly Sins, Cardcaptor Sakura and Ghost in the Shell. Non-manga periodicals include the women's magazine with and the news weekly Shūkan Gendai. The company presents the annual Kodansha Manga Award. Its English-language arm, Kodansha USA, publishes manga in North America, and Kodansha also holds a major stake in the anime studio sector through affiliates.
