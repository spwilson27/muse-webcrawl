Detective Comics is an American comic book series published by DC Comics since 1937. It is best known for introducing the superhero Batman in issue #27 (cover-dated May 1939). The publisher's name, DC Comics, is itself derived from the initials of Detective Comics. Alongside Action Comics, it is one of the longest continuously published comic books in the United States, reaching its 1,000th issue in 2019.

History

Launched by entrepreneur Major Malcolm Wheeler-Nicholson's National Allied Publications, Detective Comics was originally an anthology of hard-boiled detective stories. Issue #1 (March 1937) featured characters such as Slam Bradley (created by Jerry Siegel and Joe Shuster). The series was the last publication founder Wheeler-Nicholson produced before DC Comics was established to finance it.

Issue #27 introduced "the Bat-Man" by Bob Kane and Bill Finger, who quickly became the series' cover feature and permanent lead. The following years debuted much of Batman's supporting world, including Robin (#38), and villains and allies who later headlined backup features. Over the decades the title hosted characters including Batgirl, Manhunter (in Archie Goodwin and Walt Simonson's acclaimed 1970s run), Elongated Man, and Batwoman.

Numbering and relaunches

In 2011, DC relaunched the title with a new #1 as part of The New 52. In 2016, with the DC Rebirth initiative, the series resumed its original numbering with issue #934, counting the New 52 issues into the total. Issue #1000 (March 2019) was published as an oversized anniversary special shortly before Batman's 80th anniversary.
