Four Heavenly Kings

The Four Heavenly Kings (Sanskrit: Caturmahārāja; Chinese: 四大天王, Sìdà Tiānwáng; Japanese: Shitennō) are four guardian deities of Buddhism, each watching over one cardinal direction of the world from Mount Sumeru. They are the protectors of the Dharma and are commonly enshrined in a Hall of Heavenly Kings near the entrance of East Asian Buddhist temples.

The four kings

  • Vaiśravaṇa (Duōwén, "He Who Hears Everything"), guardian of the north, commander of the four; he carries a pagoda or umbrella and is associated with wealth.
  • Virūḍhaka (Zēngzhǎng, "He Who Causes Growth"), guardian of the south, who carries a sword.
  • Dhṛtarāṣṭra (Chíguó, "He Who Upholds the Realm"), guardian of the east, who carries a pipa (lute) in Chinese iconography.
  • Virūpākṣa (Guǎngmù, "He Who Sees All"), guardian of the west, often shown with a serpent or pearl.

In the sutras the kings report the moral state of the world to Śakra (Indra) and defend believers against evil beings; they rule over yakshas, gandharvas, kumbhandas, and nagas respectively. In Japan, as the Shitennō, they famously guard the temple Shitennō-ji in Osaka, traditionally founded by Prince Shōtoku.
