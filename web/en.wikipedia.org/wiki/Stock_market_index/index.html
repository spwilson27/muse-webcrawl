In finance, a stock index, or stock market index, is an index that measures the performance of a stock market, or of a subset of a stock market. It helps investors compare current stock price levels with past prices to calculate market performance. Indices are typically computed from the prices of selected stocks, weighted either by price or by market capitalisation.

Types of indices

Broad market indices, such as the S&P 500 in the United States, aim to represent the performance of an entire market. Other indices track specific sectors (technology, energy, healthcare), company sizes (large-cap, small-cap), regions, or investment styles such as value and growth. Global indices, such as the MSCI World Index, aggregate markets across many countries.

Weighting methods

Most modern indices are weighted by market capitalisation, so larger companies have a greater effect on the index's movement. The Dow Jones Industrial Average is a notable price-weighted exception, in which higher-priced stocks carry more weight regardless of company size. Equal-weighted indices give every constituent the same influence.

Index funds

Indices underpin index funds and exchange-traded funds (ETFs), which allow investors to buy a diversified portfolio tracking an entire index at low cost. The first index fund for individual investors was launched by John Bogle's Vanguard in 1976, tracking the S&P 500.
