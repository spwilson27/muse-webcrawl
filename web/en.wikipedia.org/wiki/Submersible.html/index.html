Submersible

A submersible is a small watercraft designed to operate underwater. The term submersible is often used to differentiate from other underwater vessels known as submarines, in that a submarine is a fully autonomous craft capable of renewing its own power and breathing air, whereas a submersible is usually supported by a nearby surface vessel, platform, shore team or sometimes a larger submarine.

Types

Submersibles include manned research craft, remotely operated vehicles (ROVs), and autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs). Deep-submergence vehicles (DSVs) such as the bathyscaphe Trieste — which reached the bottom of the Challenger Deep in 1960 — and the US Navy's Alvin (DSV-2), used since 1964 for oceanographic research and famous for exploring the wreck of the Titanic, carry small crews in a spherical pressure hull, typically of steel or titanium, surrounded by syntactic foam for buoyancy.

Operation

Manned submersibles usually have limited endurance (hours to a day or so), rely on batteries, and are transported to and from the dive site by a mother ship. They are equipped with viewports, external lights, cameras, manipulator arms and sampling baskets. Unmanned tethered ROVs are common in the offshore oil and gas industry and in science.

Safety

The safety of private and tourist submersibles drew international attention after the Titan submersible, operated by OceanGate, imploded in June 2023 while diving to the wreck of the Titanic, killing all five people aboard. The incident spurred debate about certification and regulation of experimental submersibles.
