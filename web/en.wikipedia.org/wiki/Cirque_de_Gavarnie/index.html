Cirque de Gavarnie

The Cirque de Gavarnie is a cirque in the central Pyrenees, in southwestern France, close to the border of Spain. It is within the commune of Gavarnie in the département of Hautes-Pyrénées, and the Pyrenees National Park. The cirque is 800 metres wide (on the deepest point) and about 3,000 metres wide at the top. It is a classic example of a glacial cirque, carved by ice into the limestone mountains.

Description

The major features of the cirque are the Gavarnie Falls (French: Grande Cascade de Gavarnie), one of the tallest waterfalls in Europe, with an overall drop of 422 metres (1,385 ft), and the Brèche de Roland, a dramatic natural gap in the ridge above. The cirque forms part of the Pyrénées – Mont Perdu World Heritage Site, inscribed by UNESCO in 1997.

The writer Victor Hugo famously described the cirque as "an impossible and astonishing object" and compared it to a colosseum built by nature. The site is a popular destination for hikers approaching from the village of Gavarnie.
