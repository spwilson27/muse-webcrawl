Philippe François Marie Leclerc de Hauteclocque (22 November 1902 – 28 November 1947), known simply as General Leclerc, was a French army officer and hero of the Free French Forces in the Second World War. Operating under the nom de guerre "Leclerc" (adopted to protect his family in occupied France), he became one of France's most celebrated commanders.

Second World War

After the fall of France in 1940, Leclerc escaped to London and joined Charles de Gaulle. Sent to French Equatorial Africa, he rallied Chad to the Free French cause, then led a small column on an audacious march across the Sahara to capture the Kufra oasis in Libya in 1941, where he swore the Oath of Kufra — not to lay down arms until the French flag flew over Strasbourg cathedral. His forces fought across North Africa and joined the Eighth Army in Tunisia.

As commander of the 2nd Armoured Division (2e DB), Leclerc landed in Normandy in August 1944 and led the liberation of Paris on 25 August 1944, accepting the surrender of the German garrison. His division went on to liberate Strasbourg in November 1944, fulfilling the Kufra oath, and finished the war in Bavaria. In 1945 he represented France at the Japanese surrender aboard USS Missouri. He was killed in an air crash in Algeria in 1947 and was posthumously raised to the dignity of Marshal of France in 1952. The French Army's Leclerc main battle tank is named in his honour.
