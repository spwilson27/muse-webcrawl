Convergence (media)

Media convergence
Concept byHenry Jenkins (2006)
DimensionsTechnological, economic, social, cultural, global

Convergence in media refers to the merging of previously distinct media technologies, industries, and audiences, driven by digitisation and the internet. Scholar Henry Jenkins popularised the term in Convergence Culture (2006), describing "the flow of content across multiple media platforms, the cooperation between multiple media industries, and the migratory behavior of media audiences."

One device, many media: the smartphone as convergence symbol.

Forms

Jenkins and others distinguish technological convergence (devices merge), economic convergence (media conglomerates merge), and cultural convergence (audiences remix and circulate content).

Technological convergence

The smartphone is the standard example: it absorbs the telephone, camera, music player, television, and newspaper into a single networked device.

Participatory culture

Convergence empowers fans and users to annotate, remix, and redistribute media ("spreadable media"), blurring the line between producers and consumers.

Criticism

Critics note convergence also concentrates corporate power, erodes local journalism, and raises bias and ownership concerns.
