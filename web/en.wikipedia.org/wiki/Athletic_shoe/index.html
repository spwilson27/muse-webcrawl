Athletic shoe

An athletic shoe is a generic name for a shoe designed for sporting activities. Originally used mainly for sports, they are now also widely used as everyday footwear. Athletic shoes are designed with flexibility, cushioning and stability suited to particular activities, from running shoes with foam midsoles, to court shoes with lateral support, to cleats for field sports.

History

The first rubber-soled sports shoes ("plimsolls") appeared in the 19th century. Companies such as Converse (founded 1908), the Dassler brothers' firms Adidas and Puma, and later Nike (as Blue Ribbon Sports, 1964) drove technological development through the 20th century. Air cushioning (Nike Air, 1978), gel systems and carbon-fibre racing plates followed.

Types

Major categories include running shoes (road, trail and racing), basketball shoes, tennis/court shoes, soccer cleats, cross-training shoes and specialty footwear for weightlifting, cycling and climbing. Modern "super shoes" with resilient foams and stiff plates have sharply reduced elite marathon times since 2016.

Market and culture

The global athletic footwear market is worth well over US$100 billion annually. Sneakers are also a major fashion and collectible market, with limited-edition releases resold at high premiums — the "sneakerhead" culture.
