Idealism

Idealism is the family of metaphysical views asserting that reality is fundamentally mental, immaterial, or otherwise dependent on mind. George Berkeley's subjective idealism holds that physical objects are bundles of ideas whose being is to be perceived; Immanuel Kant's transcendental idealism holds that space, time and the categories are forms our mind imposes on an unknowable thing-in-itself; Hegel's absolute idealism sees reality as the self-unfolding of Spirit or the Absolute Idea.

Influence

Idealism dominated 19th-century philosophy in Germany and Britain (F. H. Bradley, T. H. Green) before the analytic revolt of Moore and Russell. Its motifs persist in debates about consciousness, constructivism and the interpretation of quantum mechanics, and parallels are drawn (controversially) with Indian philosophies such as Advaita Vedanta and Yogacara Buddhism.
