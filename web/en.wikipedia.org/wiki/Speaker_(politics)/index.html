Speaker (politics)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The speaker of a deliberative assembly, especially a legislature, is its presiding officer and, in many systems, its political leader. Speakers chair debates, maintain order and discipline, rule on procedure, represent the chamber institutionally, and — depending on the system — range from fiercely impartial referees to powerful partisan leaders.

Models

The British Speaker of the House of Commons renounces party affiliation on election, seeks re-election as "the Speaker" by convention unopposed by major parties, and rules the chamber without voting except to break ties (by convention, in favour of further debate or the status quo). The Speaker of the US House of Representatives is the opposite pole: leader of the majority party, the chamber's chief strategist and second in the presidential line of succession after the vice president. Other systems (Canada, Australia, India, most European parliaments where the office is "president" of the chamber) fall between — formally impartial chairs who remain party figures. Selecting and removing speakers (as in the US House's October 2023 removal of its Speaker) tests each chamber's constitutional machinery.
