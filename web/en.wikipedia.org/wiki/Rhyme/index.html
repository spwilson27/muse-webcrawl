Rhyme

Rhyme
DefinitionCorrespondence of sound between words, especially at the ends of lines of poetry
TypesPerfect, slant (half-rhyme), eye rhyme, internal rhyme, masculine, feminine
Used inPoetry, song, rap, nursery rhymes

A rhyme is a repetition of similar sounds (usually the exact same phonemes) in the final stressed syllables and any following syllables of two or more words. Most often, this kind of perfect rhyming is used at the ends of lines in poems and songs. More broadly, a rhyme may also variously refer to other types of similar sounds near the ends of two or more words. Rhyme partly serves a mnemonic function—the conclusion of a phrase can be anticipated from its corresponding rhyme—and partly an aesthetic one, marking form and closure.

Types of rhyme

  • Perfect rhyme — the stressed vowel and all following sounds are identical (e.g. "moon" / "June").
  • Masculine rhyme — rhyme on a single final stressed syllable; feminine rhyme — rhyme on two syllables, stressed then unstressed ("mother" / "brother").
  • Slant rhyme (half-rhyme, near-rhyme) — approximate correspondence of sounds; favoured by poets such as Emily Dickinson and Wilfred Owen (pararhymes).
  • Eye rhyme — words that look alike but sound different ("love" / "move").
  • Internal rhyme — rhyming words within a single line, common in rap and in poets such as Poe.
  • Identical rhyme — use of the same word twice, sometimes regarded as a fault in English verse but a feature in French.

Rhyme schemes

Patterns of end rhyme are described with letters: ABAB (alternate rhyme), AABB (couplets), ABBA (enclosed rhyme), and so on. Fixed forms prescribe particular schemes—the sonnet (Petrarchan ABBAABBA CDECDE, Shakespearean ABAB CDCD EFEF GG), the ballade, the villanelle with its refrains, and the terza rima (ABA BCB) of Dante's Divine Comedy.

History

Rhyme is ancient and near-universal: it structures classical Chinese poetry (the Shijing), Arabic qasidas, and Sanskrit verse. Classical Greek and Latin poetry generally relied on metre rather than rhyme; rhymed verse became dominant in medieval Europe through Latin hymns, troubadour lyric, and the Romance vernaculars, displacing the alliterative metre of early Germanic poetry. Twentieth-century free verse downplayed rhyme, but rhyme remains central to song lyrics, children's verse, and hip-hop, where multisyllabic rhymes are a virtuoso technique.
