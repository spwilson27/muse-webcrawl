Great Swamp Fight

The Great Swamp Fight (also written Great Swamp Massacre) was a battle fought on 19 December 1675 during King Philip's War, in which a combined force of colonial militia from the Massachusetts Bay, Plymouth, and Connecticut colonies attacked a fortified Narragansett village in the Great Swamp, near present-day South Kingstown, Rhode Island.

Background

At the outset of King Philip's War, the Narragansett people under their sachem Canonchet had attempted to remain neutral, though they harboured refugees from other tribes attacked by the English. Colonial leaders, fearing that the Narragansett would join Metacomet (King Philip) in the spring, resolved on a pre-emptive winter strike against the tribe's fortified winter camp.

The battle

A force of roughly 1,000 colonial soldiers and about 150 allied Native scouts, commanded overall by Plymouth governor Josiah Winslow, marched through a snowstorm and located the Narragansett fort — a palisaded village of some 500 dwellings on raised ground surrounded by swamp. After hard fighting at the single entrance, the colonists forced their way in; the village was set on fire, and large numbers of Narragansett, including women, children, and the elderly, were killed. Estimates of Narragansett dead range from about 600 to over 1,000; colonial losses were around 70 killed and 150 wounded.

Aftermath

The attack destroyed Narragansett power and drove survivors into alliance with Metacomet, extending and intensifying the war. Canonchet was captured and executed in April 1676. A granite monument erected in 1906 marks the presumed site of the fort in the Great Swamp Wildlife Management Area.
