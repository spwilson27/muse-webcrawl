The Two Cultures

"The Two Cultures" is the first part of an influential 1959 Rede Lecture by British scientist and novelist C. P. Snow, published as The Two Cultures and the Scientific Revolution. Snow argued that modern intellectual life had split into two mutually uncomprehending cultures — the sciences and the humanities — and that this breakdown, perpetuated by the British education system, was a major hindrance to solving the world's problems.

The Two Cultures
AuthorC. P. Snow
Delivered7 May 1959 (Rede Lecture, Cambridge)
SubjectScience–humanities divide in education

Thesis

Snow famously wrote that a scholar who could not describe the Second Law of Thermodynamics was displaying the equivalent of never having read a work of Shakespeare. The two groups, he said, "had almost ceased to communicate at all," with literary intellectuals dismissive of science and scientists ignorant of literature — a gulf he considered dangerous when scientific expertise was needed to address poverty and industrialisation in the developing world.

Reception

The lecture provoked a furious response from the literary critic F. R. Leavis, whose 1962 lecture attacked Snow's standing as both novelist and thinker. The phrase "two cultures" nonetheless entered general usage, and the debate continues in discussions of STEM versus humanities education, the "science wars" of the 1990s, and proposals for a "third culture" of thinkers who bridge the divide.
