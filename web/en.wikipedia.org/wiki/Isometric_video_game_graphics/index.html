Isometric video game graphics

Isometric video game graphics are graphics generated using a form of parallel projection in which the three coordinate axes appear equally foreshortened, giving a three-dimensional appearance to two-dimensional games. Common in video games from the 1980s and 1990s — especially strategy games and role-playing games — the technique lets designers depict height and depth on hardware incapable of true 3D rendering.

A typical isometric game view.

History

Early examples include the arcade games Q*bert (1982) and Zaxxon (1982). The perspective became a staple of titles such as SimCity 2000, Diablo, Age of Empires, Fallout and Final Fantasy Tactics. Technically, most "isometric" games use a dimetric projection with a 2:1 pixel ratio rather than true isometric angles.

Characteristics

Isometric graphics preserve object size regardless of position (no perspective foreshortening) and are usually implemented with tiles and sprites. The style has seen revivals in modern independent games, valuing its clarity for tactics and city-building.
