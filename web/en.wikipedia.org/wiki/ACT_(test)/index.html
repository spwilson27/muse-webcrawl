The ACT (originally an abbreviation of American College Testing) is a standardized test used for college admissions in the United States. It is administered by ACT, Inc., a nonprofit organization based in Iowa City, Iowa. The test was introduced in November 1959 by University of Iowa professor Everett Franklin Lindquist as a competitor to the SAT.

Structure

The ACT consists of four multiple-choice subject tests: English, mathematics, reading, and science. An optional writing test measures essay composition skills. Each subject test is scored on a scale of 1 to 36, and the composite score is the average of the four subject scores, rounded to the nearest whole number. The test takes about three hours, or three and a half hours with the writing section.

Usage

Colleges and universities use ACT scores, along with high school grades and other materials, in admissions decisions. All four-year colleges in the United States accept the ACT. Many states also use the ACT as part of statewide school accountability testing for high school students. In 2024 the ACT was taken by roughly 1.4 million students.

History

Lindquist envisioned a test more closely tied to high school curricula than the aptitude-oriented SAT of the era. The first ACT was administered to about 75,000 students in 1959. Over the decades the test has been revised several times; in 1989 the four subject tests took their modern form. In the 2020s ACT, Inc. announced an "enhanced" ACT with fewer questions and optional science and writing sections.
