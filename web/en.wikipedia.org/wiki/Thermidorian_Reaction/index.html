Thermidorian Reaction
Date: 27 July 1794 (9 Thermidor, Year II)
Place: Paris, France
Result: Fall of Robespierre; end of the Reign of Terror

The Thermidorian Reaction (French: Réaction thermidorienne) is a common term for the coup d'état of 9 Thermidor Year II (27 July 1794) in which the members of the National Convention overthrew Maximilien Robespierre and the revolutionary government he dominated, ending the most radical phase of the French Revolution known as the Reign of Terror.

Background

By the summer of 1794, the Committee of Public Safety under Robespierre had overseen mass executions during the Terror. Military victories on France's frontiers reduced the justification for emergency terror, while the Law of 22 Prairial, which accelerated revolutionary tribunals, alienated many deputies who feared for their own lives.

The coup

On 9 Thermidor, Robespierre was denounced in the Convention, denied a hearing, and arrested together with his allies, including Louis Antoine de Saint-Just. After a confused night in which the Paris Commune attempted a rescue, Robespierre and 21 of his supporters were guillotined on 28 July 1794.

Aftermath

The Thermidorians dismantled the machinery of the Terror: the Law of 22 Prairial was repealed, political prisoners were released, the Jacobin Club was closed, and economic controls were abandoned. The period that followed saw a conservative reaction, the White Terror against former terrorists, and eventually the establishment of the Directory in 1795.
