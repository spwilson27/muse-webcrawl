Balipratipada, also called Bali Padva or Padva, is a Hindu festival celebrated on the fourth day of Diwali, the first day (pratipada) of the bright half of the month of Kartika. It commemorates the annual return to earth of the benevolent asura king Bali, granted by the god Vishnu in his Vamana avatar, and marks the beginning of the new year in some regions, notably for the Marwari and Gujarati trading communities.

Legend

According to the Puranas, the virtuous demon-king Bali had conquered the three worlds. Vishnu, born as the dwarf Vamana, asked Bali for three paces of land and, granted the boon, grew to cosmic size, measuring heaven and earth in two strides. Bali offered his own head for the third step and was pushed down to the netherworld of Sutala, which Vishnu granted him to rule, with permission to visit his subjects on earth once a year — the visit celebrated as Balipratipada (and in Kerala as Onam).

Celebrations

Devotees draw images of Bali and his wife Vindhyavali with rice flour and colours, offer food, and light lamps. In Maharashtra the day is associated with gifts between husbands and wives (Padva) and with Vikram Samvat new-year greetings in Gujarat, where it is called Bestu Varas. Govardhan Puja is performed on the same day in many regions, honouring Krishna's lifting of Mount Govardhan.
