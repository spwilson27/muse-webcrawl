Diss track

Diss track
Stylistic originsHip hop, battle rap
Also calledDiss song
Notable examples"Hit 'Em Up" (2Pac); "Ether" (Nas); "No Vaseline" (Ice Cube); "Not Like Us" (Kendrick Lamar)

A diss track, or diss song, is a song whose primary purpose is to verbally attack someone else, usually another artist. The practice is a central tradition of hip hop culture, growing out of battle rap and the competitive boasts of early MCs, and diss tracks are typically released in response to an existing feud or to a previous diss track, generating back-and-forth exchanges.

History

Precursors include the verbal duelling of the dozens and earlier rivalries in other genres, but the modern diss track crystallised in hip hop in the 1980s with exchanges such as the Roxanne Wars and LL Cool J's battles with Kool Moe Dee. The 1990s East Coast–West Coast rivalry produced landmark tracks including 2Pac's "Hit 'Em Up" and The Notorious B.I.G.'s "Who Shot Ya?" In the 2000s, Nas's "Ether" and Jay-Z's "Takeover" became canonical, and the 2010s and 2020s saw high-profile feuds (Drake and Meek Mill; Drake and Pusha T; Drake and Kendrick Lamar) play out largely through diss releases, with Lamar's "Not Like Us" (2024) becoming a commercial phenomenon.

Characteristics

Diss tracks range from playful competitive jabs to deeply personal attacks on an opponent's career, abilities, appearance, and private life. They are closely analysed by fans for indirect references ("subliminals"), and a well-received diss can significantly damage or revive an artist's reputation.
