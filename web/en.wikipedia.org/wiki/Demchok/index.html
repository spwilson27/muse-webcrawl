Demchok

The Indus valley near Demchok, on the disputed India–China boundary

Demchok is a village and disputed border region in the Ladakh region of the Himalayas, situated on the bank of the Indus River near the boundary between India and China. The area is claimed by both countries and lies close to the Line of Actual Control.

Geography and history

The Indian-administered village of Demchok lies in Leh district, opposite the Chinese-administered settlement of Dêmqog in Tibet. Historically the area was a trading point on routes between Ladakh and Tibet, and the 1684 Treaty of Tingmosgang between Ladakh and Tibet is sometimes cited in boundary discussions. The Sino-Indian boundary dispute in this sector centers on differing interpretations of where the boundary runs along the Indus and its tributary streams.

Border tensions

Demchok has periodically been a flashpoint in India–China border standoffs, including confrontations in 2014 and during the 2020–2022 Ladakh crisis, when troops and construction activity in the area featured in bilateral military talks. Nomadic grazing by local Changpa herders has also been affected by restrictions along the contested boundary.
