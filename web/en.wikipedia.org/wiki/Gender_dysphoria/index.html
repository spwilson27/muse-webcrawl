Gender dysphoria

Gender dysphoria (GD) is the distress a person experiences due to a mismatch between their gender identity — their personal sense of their own gender — and their sex assigned at birth. The term replaced "gender identity disorder" in the DSM-5 in 2013, in part to remove the connotation that the identity itself is disordered; the diagnosis applies to the distress rather than the identity.

Signs and symptoms

In the DSM-5, gender dysphoria in adolescents and adults is characterized by a marked incongruence between one's experienced/expressed gender and assigned gender of at least six months' duration, manifested by at least two specified criteria, together with clinically significant distress or impairment. In children, criteria include a strong desire to be of the other gender or insistence that one is another gender, strong preferences for cross-gender roles, toys, and playmates, and a strong dislike of one's sexual anatomy.

Causes

The causes of gender identity and dysphoria are not fully understood. Research implicates a combination of genetic, hormonal, and neurobiological factors; twin studies suggest a heritable component. No single factor has been identified, and the American Psychiatric Association states that gender dysphoria is not itself a mental disorder but that associated distress may require support.

Diagnosis and classification

The DSM-5 contains separate criteria sets for children and for adolescents and adults. The ICD-11, adopted by the World Health Organization in 2019, reclassified the related condition as "gender incongruence" and moved it out of the chapter on mental disorders into a chapter on conditions related to sexual health.

Treatment

Treatment is individualized and may include psychotherapy, social transition, and, for adolescents meeting criteria, puberty blockers; for older adolescents and adults, gender-affirming hormone therapy and surgeries are options under standards such as those of WPATH. The goal of treatment is relief of distress and improvement in quality of life rather than changing the person's gender identity.
