Chitosan

Chitosan is a linear polysaccharide produced commercially by the deacetylation of chitin, the structural material in the exoskeletons of shrimp, crabs, and other crustaceans. It is composed of randomly distributed β-(1→4)-linked D-glucosamine and N-acetyl-D-glucosamine units. Chitosan is notable for being biodegradable, biocompatible, and soluble in acidic aqueous solutions, unlike chitin.

Production and properties

Commercial chitosan is made by treating crustacean shells with concentrated sodium hydroxide. Its degree of deacetylation and molecular weight determine its solubility and viscosity. In acid solution its amine groups become protonated, making chitosan a rare naturally occurring cationic polymer.

Uses

Chitosan is used in agriculture as a seed treatment and biopesticide, in winemaking as a fining agent, in water treatment as a flocculant and chelator of heavy metals, and in medicine in haemostatic wound dressings (such as chitosan-based bandages used by military medics). It is also marketed as a dietary supplement, although evidence that it causes meaningful fat malabsorption or weight loss is weak.
