51 Pegasi b

51 Pegasi b, formally named Dimidium and formerly nicknamed Bellerophon, is an exoplanet orbiting the Sun-like star 51 Pegasi, about 50 light-years away in Pegasus. Announced in 1995 by Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz, it was the first exoplanet confirmed around a main-sequence star.


Discovered: 1995, Mayor & Queloz
Method: radial velocity
Orbital period: 4.23 days
Mass: ≥0.46 Jupiter masses

Discovery and properties

Detected by the radial-velocity (Doppler) method at Haute-Provence Observatory, the planet is a 'hot Jupiter' — a gas giant of at least half Jupiter's mass orbiting in just 4.2 days, far closer than expected, challenging planet-formation theories and prompting migration models. Its equilibrium temperature is about 1,250 K.

Legacy

The discovery launched modern exoplanet science; Mayor and Queloz shared half of the 2019 Nobel Prize in Physics. In 2015 its reflected light was directly detected, and the IAU named it Dimidium ('half', for its mass).
