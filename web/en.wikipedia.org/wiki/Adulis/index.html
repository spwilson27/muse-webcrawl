Adulis

Adulis
Location: Near Zula, Eritrea, Red Sea coast
Period: c. 1st century BC – 8th century AD
Associated with: Kingdom of Aksum

Adulis (Ge'ez: ኣዱሊስ) was an ancient city and port in the Horn of Africa, located on the western shore of the Red Sea in present-day Eritrea, near the modern town of Zula. It was the chief port of the Kingdom of Aksum and a major emporium linking Roman, Egyptian, Arabian, and Indian trade.

History

Mentioned in the Periplus of the Erythraean Sea (1st century AD) and by Pliny the Elder, Adulis exported ivory, tortoiseshell, rhinoceros horn, gold, and slaves, and imported wine, olive oil, cloth, and metalware. Christianity reached Aksum partly through this port. The city flourished until about the 7th–8th centuries, when shifts in trade and possibly environmental changes led to its decline.

Archaeology

Excavations from the 19th century onward, including by the British Museum (1868) and Italian teams, revealed churches, large buildings, coins, and imported pottery. The site is among Eritrea's most important archaeological locations.
