Lambda Boötis star

Lambda Boötis stars are a class of chemically peculiar stars, constituting roughly 2% of A-type stars within their temperature range. They are named after the prototype, Lambda Boötis in the constellation Boötes. They show a distinctive abundance pattern: light elements such as carbon, nitrogen, oxygen and sulfur have roughly solar abundances, while heavier elements such as iron are strongly underabundant.

Characteristics

Lambda Boötis stars are Population I, early A-type (roughly A0–F0) main-sequence or pre-main-sequence stars. Their spectra show weak metallic lines — particularly the Mg II line at 4481 Å — despite hydrogen lines indicating a normal A-type temperature. Surface abundances of iron-peak elements can be depleted by up to a factor of about 100 relative to the Sun.

Explanation

The leading explanation involves accretion of metal-depleted gas from the interstellar medium or a circumstellar shell: volatile elements are accreted while refractory elements remain locked in dust grains that are blown away by radiation pressure. Diffusion and mass loss may also contribute. Many Lambda Boötis stars show infrared excesses consistent with circumstellar material.

Classification

The class was recognized in the 1940s by W. W. Morgan and collaborators. Membership is defined spectroscopically, and modern surveys, including space-based asteroseismology, have refined lists of bona fide members.
