Ragamuffin War

The Ragamuffin War (Portuguese: Guerra dos Farrapos or Revolução Farroupilha) was a Republican uprising in the province of Rio Grande do Sul, in southern Brazil, that began on 20 September 1835 and lasted until 1 March 1845. It was the longest and one of the largest armed rebellions of Brazil's imperial regency period. The rebels were called farrapos ("ragamuffins") because of the ragged clothing of their troops.

Causes

The revolt was driven by economic grievances: gaúcho ranchers producing charque (salted, dried beef) were undercut by cheaper imports from Uruguay and Argentina, favoured by the tariff policies of the imperial government in Rio de Janeiro. Liberal political ideas, resentment of centralization, and the influence of Italian revolutionary exiles added ideological fuel.

Course of the war

Led by Bento Gonçalves da Silva and Antônio de Sousa Neto, the rebels proclaimed the separatist Riograndense Republic (Piratini Republic) in 1836. Giuseppe Garibaldi and other Italian volunteers fought for the rebels, and in 1839 they briefly proclaimed the Juliana Republic in neighbouring Santa Catarina. Imperial forces under Luís Alves de Lima e Silva, later Duke of Caxias, gradually contained the rebellion.

Peace

The Treaty of Ponche Verde (1 March 1845) ended the conflict with unusually generous terms for the defeated: amnesty for the rebels, incorporation of farroupilha officers into the imperial army, freedom for enslaved men who had fought for the republic, and a protective tariff on imported charque. The war remains central to gaúcho identity, and 20 September is a state holiday in Rio Grande do Sul.
