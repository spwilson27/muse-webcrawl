Northwest Indian War

"The Treaty of Greenville" — depiction of the 1795 negotiations.

The Northwest Indian War (1785–1795), also known as the Ohio War or Little Turtle's War, was a war fought between the United States and a confederation of Native American tribes, with support from the British, for control of the Northwest Territory.

Background

Under the Treaty of Paris (1783), Britain ceded the territory north of the Ohio River to the United States, but the Western Confederacy of Native nations rejected American claims. Encroachment by settlers led to escalating violence along the frontier.

Course of the war

Early American expeditions ended in disaster: Harmar's defeat (1790) and St. Clair's defeat (1791), the worst defeat ever suffered by the U.S. Army at Native hands. President George Washington then appointed General Anthony Wayne, whose Legion of the United States defeated the Confederacy at the Battle of Fallen Timbers in 1794. The war ended with the Treaty of Greenville (1795), by which the tribes ceded much of present-day Ohio.
