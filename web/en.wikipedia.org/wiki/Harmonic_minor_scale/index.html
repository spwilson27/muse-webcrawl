Harmonic minor scale

Pitch classes7 per octave

The harmonic minor scale is a seven-note musical scale derived from the natural minor scale by raising the seventh degree by a semitone. In A minor it runs A–B–C–D–E–F–G♯. The alteration arose in tonal harmony practice: raising the subtonic to a leading tone creates a major dominant chord (V) and an authentic cadence in minor keys, which the natural minor's minor v chord cannot provide. It is called "harmonic" minor because it was developed for harmony rather than melody.

Structure

Its interval sequence — whole, half, whole, whole, half, augmented second, half — contains a distinctive augmented second between the sixth and raised seventh degrees (F to G♯ in A minor). This gap gives the scale an exotic color often loosely associated with Middle Eastern or Eastern European idioms, and it produces characteristic harmonies: a major tonic-major-seventh sonority set, a diminished seventh chord on the leading tone, and an augmented III chord. Because the augmented second is awkward to sing, composers of melodic lines often smooth it by raising the sixth degree as well, giving the melodic minor scale.

Usage

The harmonic minor is foundational in Baroque and Classical minor-key harmony and remains a staple in jazz improvisation (over dominant chords in minor), in flamenco and metal guitar styles, and in film scoring, where its augmented second signals drama or menace.
