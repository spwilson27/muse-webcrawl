Bidding

Bidding is the act of offering a price for a good, service or asset in a competitive process, most characteristically an auction, in which the item is sold to the highest bidder; or, in procurement, the submission of a price at which a supplier offers to perform work, with the contract commonly awarded to the lowest qualified bidder. Bidding also occurs in financial markets, where the bid is the highest price a buyer is willing to pay for a security, and in games such as bridge and other trick-taking card games, where players bid on contracts.

In auctions

In an open ascending (English) auction, bids rise by set increments until no higher bid is offered; in a Dutch auction the price descends until a bidder accepts. Sealed-bid formats — first-price and Vickrey (second-price) — require bidders to submit bids without knowing others'. Sellers may set a reserve price below which the item will not sell. Online marketplaces such as eBay use proxy bidding, in which the system automatically raises a bidder's offer to the minimum needed to stay ahead, up to their maximum. Phenomena such as bid sniping, shill bidding and the winner's curse are widely studied in auction theory.

In procurement and markets

Governments and firms solicit competitive bids (tenders) for construction, supply and services; bid documents specify quantities and terms, and anti-collusion rules target bid rigging, an illegal practice in most jurisdictions. In stock markets the bid–ask spread between the best bid and best offer measures liquidity. In contract bridge, bidding is a structured auction in which partnerships exchange information about their hands to select the final contract.
