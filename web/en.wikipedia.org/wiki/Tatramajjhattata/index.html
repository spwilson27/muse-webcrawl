Tatramajjhattatā

Palitatramajjhattatā
Translation"Equipoise", " neutrality of mind"
TypeMental factor (cetasika)
TraditionTheravāda Buddhism (Abhidhamma)

Tatramajjhattatā (Pali) is a Buddhist term that is translated as "equipoise", "neutrality of mind", etc. In the Theravāda tradition, it is defined as a mental attitude of balance, detachment and impartiality. It is one of the nineteen "beautiful mental factors" (sobhana cetasikas) and is grouped among the six pairs of mental factors that bring tranquillity and balance to consciousness and its factors.

Definition

The Visuddhimagga and Abhidhamma commentaries describe tatramajjhattatā as the quality that keeps consciousness and the mental factors balanced, preventing excess and deficiency, much as a charioteer watches two evenly running horses with impartial attention. Bhikkhu Bodhi explains that it "has the characteristic of conveying consciousness and the mental factors evenly. Its function is to prevent deficiency and excess, or to prevent partiality. It is manifested as neutrality."

Relation to equanimity

Although tatramajjhattatā is often rendered as equanimity, commentators distinguish it from upekkhā in its broader sense as the fourth divine abiding (brahmavihāra). As a universal beautiful factor it accompanies every wholesome consciousness, quietly balancing the associated mental factors, whereas the equanimity of the divine abidings is cultivated deliberately toward beings. It is listed among the ten perfections' supporting qualities and is associated with the balanced temperament praised in meditation manuals.
