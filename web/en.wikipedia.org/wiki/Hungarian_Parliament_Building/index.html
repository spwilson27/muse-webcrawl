Hungarian Parliament Building

The Hungarian Parliament Building (Hungarian: Országház, literally "House of the Country") is the seat of the National Assembly of Hungary, located on Kossuth Lajos Square in Budapest, on the eastern bank of the Danube. It is one of Europe's oldest legislative buildings and a notable landmark of Hungary, and is currently the largest building in the country.

History

An international competition was held in 1880 to design a permanent home for the Hungarian parliament. The winning design, by architect Imre Steindl, was inspired by the Palace of Westminster in London. Construction began in 1885 and the building was inaugurated on the 1,000th anniversary of Hungary's founding in 1896, though it was not fully completed until 1904. Steindl went blind before its completion and died in 1902. During construction, roughly 100,000 people were involved, and about 40 kilograms of 22-23 karat gold were used for decoration.

Architecture

The building is built in the Gothic Revival style, with a symmetrical façade and a central dome combining Gothic and Renaissance Revival elements. It is 268 metres (879 ft) long and 123 metres (404 ft) wide, and its dome rises to 96 metres (315 ft) — a height referencing the year 896, the traditional date of the Hungarian conquest. The building has 691 rooms and about 20 kilometres of stairs. The exterior is decorated with statues of Hungarian rulers, Transylvanian leaders, and famous military figures.

The Holy Crown

Since 2000, the central hall beneath the dome has displayed the Holy Crown of Hungary, together with the coronation sceptre, orb, and coronation sword. The crown jewels are guarded around the clock by the Hungarian Defence Forces.
