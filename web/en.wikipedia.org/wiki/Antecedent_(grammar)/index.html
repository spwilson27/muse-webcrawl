Antecedent (grammar)

Antecedent
FieldGrammar, linguistics, syntax
Related conceptsPronoun, anaphora, reference, binding
ExampleIn "Anna lost her keys", Anna is the antecedent of her

In grammar and linguistics, an antecedent is an expression that gives its meaning to a pro-form — typically a pronoun, pro-adverb or pro-verb. The pro-form takes its meaning from its antecedent: in the sentence "Anna lost her keys", the proper noun Anna is the antecedent of the pronoun her. The relationship between a pro-form and its antecedent is an instance of anaphora.

Antecedents usually precede their pro-forms, which is the origin of the word (from Latin ante-, "before", and cedere, "to go"). When the dependent expression comes first, as in "When he arrived, John sat down", the relationship is sometimes called cataphora, and the noun is a postcedent.

Properties

An antecedent is normally a noun phrase, but it can also be a clause, a verb phrase, or even an implied referent recoverable from context. Pronouns typically agree with their antecedents in person, number and, in many languages, gender. A pronoun may also lack an explicit antecedent altogether — as with impersonal it in "It is raining" — in which case it is called exophoric or a dummy pronoun.

Ambiguity

When a sentence contains more than one candidate antecedent, the reference of the pronoun can be ambiguous: "The lawyer told the witness that he would be recalled" leaves open whether he refers to the lawyer or the witness. Style guides in technical and legal writing recommend restructuring such sentences, and the problem of pronoun resolution is a well-known challenge in natural language processing.

Binding theory

In generative grammar, the distribution of anaphors (reflexives and reciprocals), pronouns and referring expressions is analysed under binding theory, introduced in Chomsky's government and binding framework. Principle A requires an anaphor to be bound by a c-commanding antecedent within a local domain, which explains why "John admires himself" is grammatical while "Himself admires John" is not.
