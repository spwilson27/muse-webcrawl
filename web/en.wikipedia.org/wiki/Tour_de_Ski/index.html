The Tour de Ski is an annual cross-country skiing stage race held in Central Europe over the Christmas and New Year period as part of the FIS Cross-Country World Cup. Modelled on cycling's Tour de France, it was first held in the 2006–07 season and typically consists of six to eight stages — sprints, interval-start races, mass starts, and pursuits — in Germany, Switzerland, and Italy over about nine days, with rest days between stages.

Format

The overall standings are determined by cumulative time, with time bonuses awarded for sprint results and intermediate points. The defining feature is the final stage, the Final Climb at Val di Fiemme in Italy: a pursuit (now run in mass-start form in recent editions) finishing with the ascent of the Alpe Cermis alpine ski slope, with average gradients around 12 percent and ramps approaching 28 percent. The first skier across the summit line wins the Tour.

History and records

The inaugural Tours were won by Tobias Angerer and Virpi Kuitunen in 2007. Switzerland's Dario Cologna and Norway's Therese Johaug are among the most successful racers; Johannes Høsflot Klæbo has won multiple editions, and Justyna Kowalczyk of Poland won four consecutive women's titles (2010–2013). The Tour carries heavy World Cup points weight and is, with the World Championships and Olympics, among the most prestigious prizes in the sport, though its demanding schedule means some athletes skip it in championship years.
