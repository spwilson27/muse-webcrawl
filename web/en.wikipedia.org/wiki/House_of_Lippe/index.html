House of Lippe

The House of Lippe is a German noble family that ruled the Lordship, County and later Principality of Lippe in present-day North Rhine-Westphalia, with its residence at Detmold, until 1918. The family is first documented in 1123 with the brothers Bernhard and Herman de Lippia, who took their name from the River Lippe and founded the town of Lippstadt.

History

Bernhard II of Lippe (c. 1140–1196) was a notable warrior and church founder. The lordship became a county in 1528/29 under Simon V, and Lippe adopted the Reformed faith in the early 17th century under Simon VI. In 1789 Leopold I was raised to Imperial Prince. Cadet branches included Lippe-Brake, Lippe-Alverdissen and Schaumburg-Lippe, the latter ruling its own small principality at Bückeburg until 1918. A famous succession dispute between the Lippe-Detmold and Schaumburg-Lippe lines over the regency was settled in 1897 in favour of the Lippe-Biesterfeld line.

Notable members

Prince Bernhard of Lippe-Biesterfeld (1911–2004) married Crown Princess Juliana of the Netherlands and became Prince Consort when she ascended the Dutch throne in 1948. The last reigning Prince of Lippe, Leopold IV, abdicated on 12 November 1918. The head of the house since 2015 is Stephan, Prince of Lippe, who resides at Detmold Castle.
