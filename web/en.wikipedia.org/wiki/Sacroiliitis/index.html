Sacroiliitis

Sacroiliitis is inflammation within the sacroiliac joint, the joint connecting the sacrum at the base of the spine to the ilium of the pelvis. It is a common cause of buttock and lower back pain and is a hallmark feature of the spondyloarthropathies, most notably ankylosing spondylitis.

The sacroiliac joints connect the sacrum to the iliac bones of the pelvis.

Signs and symptoms

People with sacroiliitis commonly report pain in the buttocks and lower back, sometimes radiating down the back of the thigh. The pain is typically worse after prolonged sitting or standing, when climbing stairs, and in the early morning, and it often improves with activity. Stiffness of the lower back and hips is frequent, particularly after periods of rest.

Causes

Sacroiliitis may arise from several conditions. Inflammatory causes include the spondyloarthropathies — ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, reactive arthritis and arthritis associated with inflammatory bowel disease. Other causes include infection of the joint (septic sacroiliitis), trauma, degenerative osteoarthritis, and the mechanical stresses of pregnancy, which relax the ligaments supporting the joint.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis begins with a physical examination using provocative manoeuvres such as the FABER (Patrick's) test, distraction, compression and thigh thrust tests that stress the sacroiliac joint. Plain radiographs may show erosions, sclerosis or eventual fusion of the joint. Magnetic resonance imaging is the most sensitive technique for detecting early inflammation, revealing bone marrow oedema before structural damage appears.

Treatment

Treatment depends on the underlying cause. First-line therapy for inflammatory sacroiliitis is nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) combined with physical therapy aimed at maintaining mobility and posture. When disease remains active, biologic agents such as TNF inhibitors or IL-17 inhibitors may be used. Infective sacroiliitis requires antibiotics, and occasionally surgery.
