The Eighty Years' War or Dutch Revolt (Dutch: Tachtigjarige Oorlog) was the revolt of the Seventeen Provinces of the Habsburg Netherlands against Philip II of Spain, lasting from 1568 (or the iconoclasm of 1566) to 1648.

Causes and early war

Resentment at Spanish centralisation, taxation and persecution of Protestants erupted in the Beeldenstorm of 1566. The Duke of Alba's repression and the Council of Troubles radicalised resistance under William the Silent, Prince of Orange. Sea Beggars captured Brielle in 1572; sieges at Haarlem, Alkmaar and Leiden followed, and the Spanish Fury at Antwerp (1576) united provinces in the Pacification of Ghent.

Division and Dutch Republic

The Union of Arras (1579) reconciled southern Catholic provinces with Spain, while the northern Union of Utrecht formed the core of the Dutch Republic, which abjured Philip in 1581. Under Maurice of Nassau and Johan van Oldenbarnevelt the Republic professionalised its army and thrived commercially during the Twelve Years' Truce (1609–1621).

End

War resumed alongside the Thirty Years' War. The Peace of Münster (1648), part of the Peace of Westphalia, recognised Dutch independence. The war helped launch the Dutch Golden Age and colonial expansion by the Dutch East India Company.
