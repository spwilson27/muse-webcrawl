Reception (gridiron football)

In gridiron football, a reception is a completed catch of a forward pass by an eligible receiver. When a receiver catches the ball in the field of play, the reception is credited to him along with the yardage gained; receptions are a standard statistic for wide receivers, tight ends and running backs.

Requirements

For a catch to count as a reception, the receiver must gain control of the ball in bounds. Under NFL rules, a player who goes to the ground while making a catch must maintain control of the ball through the ground, and a receiver coming down in bounds must have both feet — or another part of the body other than the hands — touch the ground in bounds. In NCAA football, only one foot is required to touch in bounds. If the ball is not controlled, the pass is ruled incomplete; if it is caught and then lost, it may be a fumble.

Records

The NFL record for career receptions is held by Jerry Rice, with 1,549 catches. The single-season record is held by Michael Thomas, who caught 149 passes in 2019. Receptions are tallied together with receiving yards and receiving touchdowns in standard box scores and fantasy football scoring.
