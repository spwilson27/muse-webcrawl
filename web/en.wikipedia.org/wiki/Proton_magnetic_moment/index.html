Proton magnetic moment

The proton magnetic moment is the magnetic dipole moment of the proton, symbol μp. Its recommended value is approximately 1.41060679736(45) × 10−26 joules per tesla, or 2.79284734462 nuclear magnetons. Protons are spin-1/2 particles, and the magnetic moment arises from the proton's spin and internal charge structure.

History

The proton's magnetic moment was measured in 1933 by Otto Stern and co-workers using molecular-beam methods. For a point-like Dirac particle the moment would be exactly one nuclear magneton, so the measured value of about 2.79 revealed that the proton has internal structure — an anomalous magnetic moment later explained by its composition from quarks and gluons. This was one of the first indications that the proton is not an elementary particle.

Measurement

Modern measurements determine the proton magnetic moment through the ratio of the proton's Larmor precession frequency to its cyclotron frequency in a Penning trap, and by nuclear magnetic resonance in water samples, which underlies the calibration of magnetic field measurements and the gyromagnetic ratio used in MRI.
