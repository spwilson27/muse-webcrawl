ArguedOctober 12, 1977
DecidedJune 28, 1978

HoldingRace may be considered as one factor among many in university admissions, but specific racial quotas are impermissible.

Regents of the University of California v. Bakke, 438 U.S. 265 (1978), was a landmark decision by the Supreme Court of the United States that upheld affirmative action, allowing race to be one of several factors in college admission policy. However, the Court struck down specific racial quotas, holding that the University of California's special admissions program for its medical school violated the Equal Protection Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment and Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964.

Background

The University of California, Davis School of Medicine reserved 16 of the 100 places in each entering class for members of certain minority groups under a special admissions program. Allan Bakke, a white applicant in his thirties and a former Marine Corps officer and NASA engineer, was rejected in both 1973 and 1974, although his grades and test scores were higher than those of a number of applicants admitted under the special program. Bakke sued, claiming he was the victim of unlawful racial discrimination.

Decision

The Court was deeply divided and issued multiple opinions; there was no single majority opinion. Justice Lewis F. Powell Jr.'s opinion proved decisive. Powell concluded that the Davis quota system was unconstitutional and that Bakke must be admitted, but he rejected the argument that race could never be considered in admissions. He identified the attainment of a diverse student body as a compelling state interest, and pointed to Harvard College's admissions program — which treated race as one "plus" factor among many in an individualized review — as a permissible model.

Two blocs of four justices formed shifting majorities: one bloc, joining Powell on the quota question, would have barred consideration of race; another bloc, joining Powell on the diversity question, would have upheld more extensive remedial programs.

Legacy

Bakke became the controlling framework for affirmative action in higher education for 45 years. Its diversity rationale was reaffirmed in Grutter v. Bollinger (2003). In 2023, in Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard and the companion UNC case, the Supreme Court effectively overruled Grutter and ended race-conscious admissions at American colleges and universities. Bakke himself graduated from the Davis medical school and pursued a career as an anesthesiologist, largely avoiding public comment on the case.
