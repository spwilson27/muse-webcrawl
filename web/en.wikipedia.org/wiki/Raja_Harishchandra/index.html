Raja Harishchandra

Raja Harishchandra (transl. King Harishchandra) is a 1913 Indian silent film directed and produced by Dadasaheb Phalke. It is widely considered the first full-length Indian feature film, marking the birth of the Indian film industry. The film is based on the legend of King Harishchandra, the mythical king famed for never telling a lie and enduring great suffering to keep his word.

Production

Phalke, inspired after watching a film about the life of Christ in Bombay in 1910, mortgaged his possessions to finance the picture. Because acting was considered disreputable for women, all female roles were played by men, including Anna Salunke as Queen Taramati. D. D. Dabke played the title role. The film was shot in Bombay and processed by Phalke himself, and ran about 3,700 feet (four reels), with intertitles in Hindi and English.

Release and legacy

The film premiered on 21 April 1913 at Bombay's Olympia Theatre and opened to the public on 3 May 1913 at the Coronation Cinematograph, where it was a commercial success. Only the first and last reels of the original survive; Phalke remade the film in 1917. India celebrates 3 May 1913 as the birthday of Indian cinema, and the Dadasaheb Phalke Award, the country's highest film honour, is named after its director.
