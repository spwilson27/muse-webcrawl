NoSQL

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Well-known NoSQL systems: MongoDB, Cassandra, Redis

NoSQL (originally referring to "non-SQL" or "non-relational") is an approach to database design that focuses on providing a mechanism for storage and retrieval of data that is modeled in means other than the tabular relations used in relational databases. Often backronymed as "Not only SQL", NoSQL systems are used for very large, distributed, or flexible-schema datasets where the relational model's guarantees are relaxed in favour of scalability and performance.

History

The term was coined by Carlo Strozzi in 1998 for his lightweight, open-source relational database that did not use SQL, and was reintroduced in 2009 by Eric Evans for an event about distributed non-relational stores inspired by Google's BigTable (2006) and Amazon's Dynamo (2007). Systems such as Cassandra, HBase, MongoDB, CouchDB, and Redis spread rapidly with web-scale applications and the big data era.

Types

NoSQL databases are commonly grouped by data model: document stores (MongoDB, Couchbase) keep semi-structured JSON-like documents; key-value stores (Redis, DynamoDB, Riak) map keys to values for very fast lookups; wide-column stores (Cassandra, HBase) organize data into column families across many servers; and graph databases (Neo4j) store nodes and relationships for connected data. Search engines such as Elasticsearch and time-series and vector databases are sometimes included in the umbrella.

Characteristics and trade-offs

Most NoSQL systems scale horizontally across commodity servers, use dynamic or schema-free data models, and sacrifice some of the ACID guarantees of relational databases in favour of eventual consistency and availability — a trade-off described by the CAP theorem and summarized by the "BASE" acronym (Basically Available, Soft state, Eventual consistency). Critics note the loss of declarative querying, joins, and data independence; in practice, modern architectures are often polyglot, combining relational and NoSQL stores, and many relational systems have added JSON and horizontal-scaling features (a trend sometimes called NewSQL), narrowing the gap.
