Darwin, Northern Territory

Darwin is the capital city of the Northern Territory, Australia. Located on the Timor Sea at the Top End, it is the largest city in the sparsely populated territory and a major port and gateway to Asia. The Larrakia people are the traditional owners of the Darwin area.

History

Surveyor George Goyder founded the settlement in 1869, named Palmerston after British Prime Minister Lord Palmerston. The port was named Port Darwin in 1839 by John Lort Stokes aboard HMS Beagle, honouring Charles Darwin. It was renamed Darwin in 1911. The city was bombed by Japan in February 1942—the largest single attack on Australia—and largely destroyed by Cyclone Tracy on Christmas Day 1974, after which it was rebuilt.

Geography and climate

Darwin has a tropical savanna climate with distinct wet (November–April, monsoonal rain, cyclones) and dry seasons, with average highs around 30–33°C year-round. It lies on a peninsula on Darwin Harbour, near Charles Darwin National Park and the mangrove-lined inlets.

Economy and culture

Government, defence, mining, tourism and port trade dominate. Attractions include Mindil Beach markets, Crocosaurus Cove, the Museum and Art Gallery of the Northern Territory, and proximity to Litchfield and Kakadu National Parks. The population is notably multicultural and has one of Australia's highest proportions of Aboriginal residents for a capital city.
