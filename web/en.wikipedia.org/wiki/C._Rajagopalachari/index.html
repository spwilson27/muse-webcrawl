C. Rajagopalachari

Chakravarti Rajagopalachari (10 December 1878 – 25 December 1972), popularly known as Rajaji, was an Indian statesman, writer, lawyer and independence activist. He was the last Governor-General of India (1948–1950) and the only Indian to hold that office, before it was abolished when India became a republic. A close associate of Mahatma Gandhi, he served as Premier of the Madras Presidency and later as Chief Minister of Madras State.

Career

Rajagopalachari joined the Indian National Congress and participated in the Non-Cooperation Movement and the Salt Satyagraha, leading the march from Trichinopoly to Vedaranyam in 1930. As Premier of Madras (1937–1939) he introduced prohibition and temple entry reforms. He controversially advocateded a negotiated settlement with the Muslim League (the "Rajaji formula" of 1944). After independence he served as Governor of West Bengal, then Governor-General (1948–1950), and briefly as Union Home Minister.

Later life

Becoming disillusioned with the Congress party's socialist direction, Rajagopalachari founded the free-market Swatantra Party in 1959, which became the principal opposition in several states. He was also a noted writer, producing celebrated English retellings of the Mahabharata and Ramayana, and received the Bharat Ratna in 1954.
