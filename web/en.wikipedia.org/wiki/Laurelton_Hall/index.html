Laurelton Hall

Laurelton Hall was the estate of Louis Comfort Tiffany in Oyster Bay (Laurel Hollow), Long Island, New York. Completed around 1905 to Tiffany's own designs, the 84-room mansion was a total work of art showcasing his glass, mosaics, and landscape design, and housed much of his personal collection.

Design

The Art Nouveau house featured an entrance court with a fountain, exotic plantings, and stained-glass installations integrated with nature themes — wisteria, daffodils and magnolias rendered in glass. Tiffany used the estate for entertaining and for displaying works from Tiffany Studios, including the famous peacock mosaic and favrile glass pieces.

Art foundation and fire

In 1918 Tiffany established the Louis Comfort Tiffany Foundation, turning Laurelton Hall into a residential school for young artists. After his death in 1933 the estate's contents dwindled; the main house was gutted by fire in 1957 and the remains were later demolished. Surviving elements — including the entrance loggia — were salvaged by Hugh and Jeannette McKean of the Charles Hosmer Morse Museum in Winter Park, Florida, which now displays the largest Laurelton Hall collection, including the restored Daffodil Terrace.
