Aldwych is a closed station on the London Underground, located on the corner of Surrey Street and the Strand in central London. It was the terminus of a short branch of the Piccadilly line from Holborn. Opened in 1907 under the name Strand, it was renamed Aldwych in 1915 and closed in 1994. It is distinct from the nearby Aldwych Theatre.

History

The station was the surviving fragment of a planned merger route between the Great Northern & Strand Railway and the Brompton & Piccadilly Circus Railway. It opened with two platforms, but the shuttle service to Holborn was never busy: one platform and tunnel were taken out of use as early as 1917, and the branch settled into one of the Underground's quietest services, worked by a single short train. Plans to extend it south under the Thames to Waterloo were never carried out.

Closure

By the 1990s the lifts required costly replacement that could not be justified by the station's roughly 600 passengers a day. The branch closed on 30 September 1994. Since then, Aldwych has been retained as a remarkably complete time capsule of an Edwardian tube station — including original tiling and posters — and is regularly used as a filming location (including for scenes in V for Vendetta and various period dramas standing in for wartime stations), for emergency-services training, and for occasional public tours run by the London Transport Museum.
