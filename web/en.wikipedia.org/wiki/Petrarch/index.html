Francesco Petrarca (1304–1374), known as Petrarch, was an Italian scholar and poet of early Renaissance humanism, often called a founder of humanism. His rediscovery and imitation of classical Latin authors such as Cicero helped launch the Renaissance revival of antiquity.

Works

Petrarch is best known for the Canzoniere, 366 poems — mostly sonnets — addressed to his idealised beloved Laura, whom he claimed to have first seen in Avignon in 1327; the 'Petrarchan sonnet' form influenced European poetry for centuries. He also wrote the Latin epic Africa, letters, and an account of climbing Mont Ventoux that is often read as a landmark of Renaissance self-awareness.
