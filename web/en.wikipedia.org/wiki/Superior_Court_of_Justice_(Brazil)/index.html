The Superior Court of Justice (Portuguese: Superior Tribunal de Justiça, STJ) is the highest appellate court in Brazil for non-constitutional questions of federal law. Created by the 1988 Constitution and installed on 7 April 1989, it sits in Brasília and is often described as the "citizens' court" (Tribunal da Cidadania).

Headquarters of the Superior Court of Justice in Brasília

Jurisdiction

The STJ hears appeals from state courts and federal regional courts involving the interpretation of federal statutes, through the recurso especial (special appeal). It also has original jurisdiction over certain cases, including conflicts of competence between courts, habeas corpus in defined circumstances, and actions involving state governors. Constitutional questions go instead to the Supreme Federal Court (STF).

Composition

The court consists of 33 ministers appointed by the President of Brazil from three lists drawn up by the court itself: one-third from federal appellate judges, one-third from state appellate judges, and one-third, in equal parts, from lawyers and members of the public prosecution service. Ministers must be between 35 and 70 years old and retire compulsorily at 75.

Repetitive appeals

To manage a very large caseload, the STJ decides leading cases under a "repetitive appeals" procedure, whose rulings guide lower courts on identical questions of law across thousands of stayed cases.
