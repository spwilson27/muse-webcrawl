The 1986 FIFA World Cup Final was played on 29 June 1986 at the Estadio Azteca in Mexico City between Argentina and West Germany. Argentina won 3–2 to claim their second World Cup title. The tournament and its final are inseparably associated with Argentina's captain Diego Maradona, whose performances — including the "Hand of God" goal and the "Goal of the Century" against England in the quarter-finals — made 1986 his defining tournament.

Diego Maradona lifts the trophy after Argentina's 3–2 victory over West Germany.

Match summary

Argentina led 2–0 through goals by defender José Luis Brown in the 23rd minute and Jorge Valdano in the 55th. West Germany, coached by Franz Beckenbauer, rallied late: Karl-Heinz Rummenigge pulled a goal back in the 74th minute and substitute Rudi Völler equalised in the 81st. Three minutes later, Maradona threaded a pass to Jorge Burruchaga, who ran on to score the winning goal. Argentina, coached by Carlos Bilardo, thus emulated their 1978 triumph.

Aftermath

Maradona won the Golden Ball as the tournament's outstanding player, finishing with five goals and five assists. The final was the last match of the 1986 tournament, which had been awarded to Mexico after Colombia withdrew as hosts for economic reasons. Argentina and West Germany met again in the 1990 final, which the Germans won.
