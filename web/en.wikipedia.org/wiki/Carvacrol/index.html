Carvacrol

Carvacrol
IUPAC name2-methyl-5-(propan-2-yl)phenol
FormulaC10H14O
Molar mass150.22 g/mol
AppearanceColourless to pale yellow liquid
Found inOregano, thyme, summer savory essential oils

Carvacrol, or cymophenol, is a monoterpenoid phenol with the formula C10H14O. It is a principal component of the essential oils of oregano, thyme and summer savory, and gives oregano its characteristic warm, pungent aroma and flavour. It is isomeric with thymol.

Occurrence and properties

Carvacrol makes up a large fraction — in some chemotypes the majority — of oregano oil (Origanum vulgare), and is also abundant in Thymus species and wild bergamot. It is a colourless to pale-yellow liquid with a boiling point around 237 °C, sparingly soluble in water but freely soluble in alcohol and ether.

Biological activity and uses

Carvacrol shows antibacterial, antifungal and insecticidal activity in laboratory studies, disrupting bacterial cell membranes, and it is used as a flavouring and fragrance ingredient and investigated as a feed additive and natural preservative. It activates the TRPV3 and TRPA1 ion channels associated with warmth and pungency sensations. Despite its wide use in food flavouring at low levels, the concentrated oil is an irritant, and claims of therapeutic benefit in humans remain largely unproven.
