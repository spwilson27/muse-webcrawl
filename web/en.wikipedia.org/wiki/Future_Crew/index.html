Future Crew (FC) was a Finnish demogroup that became one of the most influential groups of the PC demoscene in the early 1990s. Founded in 1986, initially on other platforms, the group rose to prominence on the IBM PC with a series of technically and artistically accomplished demos.

History

Future Crew's breakthrough came with the demo Unreal, released at The Party in 1992, where it won the demo competition. Its follow-up Panic! also proved highly influential. The group's best-known production, Second Reality, won first place in the demo competition at Assembly 1993 in Helsinki and is frequently cited as one of the greatest PC demos of all time, combining smooth real-time effects such as texture mapping and vector graphics with a polished soundtrack.

Members used handles including Psi, Trug, Wildfire, Chaos, Marvel, Pixel, Orc, and the musicians Skaven (Peter Hajba) and Purple Motion (Jonne Valtonen), whose tracked music in the Scream Tracker format became classics of the scene. Future Crew also produced tools used by other groups, including the Scream Tracker music editor, which helped popularise PC demo music.

Legacy

The group gradually became inactive after 1994–95 as members moved into the games and software industries; members went on to work at companies such as Remedy Entertainment, where the Future Crew heritage fed into games like Max Payne. The Finnish demoscene tradition itself was later inscribed on UNESCO's national list of intangible cultural heritage, with Future Crew among its emblematic groups.
