Pakistan national cricket team

The Pakistan men's national cricket team, nicknamed the Shaheens and the Men in Green, represents Pakistan in men's international cricket. It is administered by the Pakistan Cricket Board (PCB). Pakistan has been a full member of the International Cricket Council since 1952.

History

Pakistan played its first Test match in October 1952 against India in Delhi, and won its first Test on the subsequent tour at Lucknow. Captained over the years by figures such as Abdul Hafeez Kardar, Imran Khan, Javed Miandad, Wasim Akram, Inzamam-ul-Haq and Misbah-ul-Haq, the team developed a reputation for brilliant but unpredictable cricket and for producing outstanding fast bowlers and wrist spinners.

Tournament success

Pakistan won the Cricket World Cup in 1992 under Imran Khan, defeating England in the final at Melbourne. The team also won the ICC World Twenty20 in 2009 and the ICC Champions Trophy in 2017, beating India in the final at The Oval. Pakistan reached the top of the ICC Test rankings in 2016 under Misbah-ul-Haq.

Home cricket

Following the 2009 attack on the Sri Lanka team bus in Lahore, international teams declined to tour Pakistan for a decade, and Pakistan played its "home" series mainly in the United Arab Emirates. International cricket gradually returned from 2015 onwards, with the Pakistan Super League and visiting Test teams restoring regular home fixtures.
