ELAS

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Active: 1942–1945

Country: Greece

Allegiance: National Liberation Front (EAM)

Type: Guerrilla army

Size: c. 50,000 (1944)

Commander: Aris Velouchiotis (chief kapetanios); Stefanos Sarafis (military commander)

The Greek People's Liberation Army or ELAS (Greek: Ελληνικός Λαϊκός Απελευθερωτικός Στρατός, Ellinikós Laïkós Apeleftherotikós Stratós) was the military arm of the National Liberation Front (EAM), the largest resistance organisation in Axis-occupied Greece during the Second World War. EAM and ELAS were dominated by the Communist Party of Greece (KKE).

History

ELAS was founded in 1942 and grew from small guerrilla bands in the mountains of central Greece into a force that by 1944 numbered tens of thousands and controlled much of the countryside — the self-governing territory known as "Free Greece", with its own administration, schools and courts. It fought the German, Italian and Bulgarian occupiers, but also clashed with rival resistance groups, notably EDES, in a first round of civil conflict in 1943–44.

Dekemvriana and disbandment

After liberation in October 1944, tensions between EAM and the British-backed government erupted in the Dekemvriana fighting in Athens in December 1944. Defeated there, ELAS accepted demobilisation under the Treaty of Varkiza in February 1945 and surrendered its weapons. Many of its veterans were subsequently persecuted, and former ELAS fighters formed the core of the Democratic Army of Greece in the full-scale Greek Civil War of 1946–49.

Legacy

ELAS remains a contested subject in Greek memory: celebrated by the left as the backbone of national resistance, and criticised by opponents for violence against rival Greeks and civilians during the occupation and after.
