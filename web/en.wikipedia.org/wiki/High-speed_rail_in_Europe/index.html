High-speed rail in Europe


TGV Duplex in France

High-speed rail in Europe comprises an expanding network of lines and services operating at speeds of 200 km/h and above, coordinated increasingly at the European Union level through interoperability standards (TSI) and the common ERTMS signalling system. Europe pioneered high-speed rail outside Japan with Italy's Direttissima (1977) and France's TGV Sud-Est (1981).

National networks

France built the archetypal network of dedicated lines (lignes à grande vitesse) radiating from Paris, operated by SNCF's TGV and Ouigo services. Germany developed the ICE network with a mix of new and upgraded lines serving dispersed centres. Spain built Europe's largest dedicated network by route length, with AVE services operated by Renfe (and, increasingly, competitors such as Ouigo España and Iryo) radiating from Madrid. Italy operates high-speed services (Frecciarossa, Frecciargento, and the private operator Italo) on north–south corridors.

Cross-border services

International services include Eurostar (London–Paris/Brussels/Amsterdam via the Channel Tunnel), Thalys (now part of the Eurostar brand), TGV Lyria (France–Switzerland), and cross-border ICE and Railjet services. The EU's Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T) programme supports new corridors, including Rail Baltica and the Lyon–Turin base tunnel.
