Ethnic groups in China

China is a multi-ethnic state. The People's Republic of China officially recognizes 56 ethnic groups (minzu), of which the Han Chinese form the overwhelming majority, accounting for roughly 91 percent of the population. The other 55 groups are officially termed ethnic minorities.

Recognition

In the 1950s, an Ethnic Classification Project examined hundreds of self-identified groups and consolidated them into the recognized list, which was finalized at 56 with the addition of the Jino in 1979. Recognized minority groups include the Zhuang (the largest minority), Hui, Manchu, Uyghurs, Miao, Yi, Tujia, Tibetans, and Mongols.

Policy

Minority groups are concentrated in border and western regions, many of which are organized as autonomous regions, prefectures, and counties, including the five provincial-level autonomous regions: Inner Mongolia, Xinjiang, Tibet (Xizang), Guangxi, and Ningxia. Official policy provides for representation in government, use of minority languages, and certain exemptions and preferences, while unrecognized groups and sub-groups continue to exist outside the official framework.

Languages and culture

The ethnic groups of China speak languages from several families, including Sino-Tibetan, Altaic (Turkic and Mongolic), Tai–Kadai, Hmong–Mien, and Austroasiatic, and practice religions including Buddhism, Islam, Taoism, Christianity, and various indigenous traditions.
