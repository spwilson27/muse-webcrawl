Gannets and boobies (family Sulidae) are plunge-diving seabirds.

The Sulidae are a family of seabirds comprising the gannets and boobies. It includes about ten living species in three genera: Morus (the gannets), Sula (the boobies), and Papasula (Abbott's booby). Sulids are medium-large coastal and oceanic birds found in temperate and tropical seas worldwide.

Characteristics

Sulids are streamlined birds, roughly 60–85 cm long, with long pointed wings, long wedge-shaped tails, stout conical bills without external nostrils, and totipalmate feet (all four toes joined by webbing). Their plumage is mainly white with black wingtips in the gannets, while boobies show greater variety, including the bright blue feet of the blue-footed booby, used in courtship displays.

Behaviour

Sulids are spectacular plunge-divers: gannets may dive from heights of 30 metres or more, folding their wings and striking the water at speeds approaching 100 km/h to catch fish and squid; reinforced skulls and air sacs under the skin cushion the impact. They breed in dense colonies on cliffs and islands, laying one to three chalky blue eggs in nests of seaweed and debris, and incubate with their large webbed feet. Many species are famous for elaborate courtship rituals, such as the sky-pointing and foot-flaunting displays of boobies.

Species

Familiar members include the northern gannet (Morus bassanus), Australasian gannet, Cape gannet, blue-footed booby (Sula nebouxii), red-footed booby, brown booby, masked booby, Nazca booby, Peruvian booby, and the endangered Abbott's booby, which breeds only on Christmas Island.
