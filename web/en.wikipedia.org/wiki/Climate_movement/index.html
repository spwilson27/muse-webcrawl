Climate movement

The climate movement is a global social movement focused on pressuring governments and industry to take actions to address the causes and impacts of climate change. It is closely related to the environmental movement, but has grown dramatically in prominence since the late 2010s, driven by youth-led activism, extreme weather events, and increasingly stark scientific warnings.

History

Climate activism developed alongside scientific understanding of global warming in the 1980s and 1990s, with organizations such as Greenpeace and Friends of the Earth campaigning at international climate conferences. The movement expanded with mass mobilizations such as the People's Climate March of 2014 in New York City.

School strikes and Fridays for Future

In August 2018, Swedish school student Greta Thunberg began striking outside the Swedish parliament, inspiring the global Fridays for Future school strike movement. In September 2019, millions of people participated in global climate strikes coordinated with a United Nations climate summit. Other prominent groups include Extinction Rebellion, the Sunrise Movement, and 350.org.

Strategies

The movement employs protest marches, civil disobedience, litigation against governments and fossil fuel companies, divestment campaigns targeting the fossil fuel industry, and advocacy for policies such as the Green New Deal.
