Rayon

Rayon, also called viscose, is a semi-synthetic fibre made from natural sources of regenerated cellulose, such as wood and related agricultural products. It has the same molecular structure as cellulose. Many types and grades of viscose fibres and films exist; some imitate the feel and texture of natural fibres such as silk, wool, cotton and linen.

History

The first commercial production was by the Comte Hilaire de Chardonnet in France in the 1890s, making rayon the first manufactured fibre. The viscose process — dissolving cellulose pulp with caustic soda and carbon disulfide to form viscose, then regenerating it in an acid bath — was patented by Cross, Bevan and Beadle in 1892 and became the dominant method. The fibre was marketed as "artificial silk" until the name rayon was adopted in 1924.

Properties and uses

Rayon is soft, absorbent and drapes well, but loses strength when wet and wrinkles easily. It is used in clothing, furnishings, tyre cord, and medical products. Related regenerated-cellulose fibres include modal and lyocell (Tencel), the latter made by a lower-impact solvent process.
