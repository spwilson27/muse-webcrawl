The Bronze Star Medal (BSM) is a United States Armed Forces decoration awarded to members of the United States Armed Forces for either heroic achievement, heroic service, meritorious achievement, or meritorious service in a combat zone. When the medal is awarded by the Army, Air Force, or Space Force for acts of valour in combat, the "V" device is authorized for wear. The medal is sometimes unofficially called the Bronze Star.

History

The medal was established by Executive Order 9419 on 4 February 1944, signed by President Franklin D. Roosevelt, and made retroactive to 7 December 1941. The idea came from Colonel Russell P. "Red" Reeder, who suggested an award recognising ground troops comparable to the Air Medal. General George C. Marshall championed the proposal, intending it to boost the morale of infantry and other ground forces. The medal's design, by Bailey Banks & Biddle, is a bronze star 1½ inches across with a raised red enamel centre star on the obverse.

Criteria and devices

The Bronze Star may be awarded for meritorious service in a combat zone without the "V" device, or for specific acts of heroism under fire with the "V" device for valour. Additional awards are denoted by oak leaf clusters (Army, Air Force) or 5/16-inch stars (Navy, Marine Corps, Coast Guard). Foreign soldiers and, in some circumstances, civilians serving with the armed forces are also eligible.
