Kékfrankos

SpeciesVitis vinifera
OriginCentral Europe (Austria–Hungary region)
Notable regionsSopron, Eger, Szekszárd (Hungary); Burgenland (Austria)
Also calledBlaufränkisch, Lemberger, Frankovka

Kékfrankos is the Hungarian name for the dark-skinned wine grape variety known in German as Blaufränkisch. It is one of the most widely planted red grape varieties in Hungary and a signature grape of Central European red wines.

Characteristics

The variety buds early and ripens late, producing deeply coloured wines with firm acidity, moderate tannins, and flavours of dark cherry, blackberry, and spice. It is valued for its ability to express terroir and for its freshness compared with many international red varieties.

Wine regions

In Hungary, Kékfrankos is prominent in Sopron — which calls itself the "capital of Kékfrankos" — as well as in Eger, where it is a key component of Egri Bikavér ("Bull's Blood"), and in Szekszárd and Villány. Across the border in Austria's Burgenland it is the leading red variety, and it is also grown in Germany (as Lemberger), Slovakia, Croatia, and elsewhere in Central and Eastern Europe.
