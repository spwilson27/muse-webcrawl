Data degradation

Data degradation is the gradual corruption of computer data due to the accumulation of non-critical failures in data storage devices. The phenomenon is also known as data decay, data rot or bit rot. Over time, storage media physically decay — magnetic domains lose alignment, optical discs oxidise, and flash memory cells leak charge — causing individual bits to flip or become unreadable.

Causes

Degradation arises from the limited retention lifetimes of storage media, environmental factors such as heat, humidity and radiation, cosmic rays causing soft errors in memory, and the slow deterioration of media such as magnetic tape, CD-R discs and early solid-state drives. Studies of large storage systems have found silent data corruption to be more common than drive manufacturers' specifications suggest.

Protection

Filesystems such as ZFS and Btrfs defend against degradation by checksumming data blocks and, when redundant copies exist, automatically repairing corrupted blocks — a process called scrubbing. Other defences include error-correcting codes, RAID arrays, regular backups, and periodic copying of data to fresh media, a strategy emphasised by digital preservation initiatives.
