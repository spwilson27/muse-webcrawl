Zechariah (Greek: Ζαχαρίας; Hebrew: זְכַרְיָה, "Yahweh remembers") is a figure in the New Testament, a priest in the Temple in Jerusalem and the father of John the Baptist. His story is told in the first chapter of the Gospel of Luke. In Islam he is honoured as the prophet Zakariya, guardian of Mary.

The annunciation

According to Luke, Zechariah belonged to the priestly division of Abijah and was married to Elizabeth, a descendant of Aaron. Both were righteous but childless and elderly. While Zechariah was burning incense in the Temple sanctuary — chosen by lot for this duty — the angel Gabriel appeared and announced that Elizabeth would bear a son, to be named John, who would prepare the way for the Lord. When Zechariah asked how this could be, Gabriel struck him mute for his disbelief, and he remained unable to speak until the child's birth.

The Benedictus

After John was born and circumcised, Zechariah confirmed the name in writing, whereupon his speech returned, and he uttered the hymn known as the Benedictus ("Blessed be the Lord God of Israel"), one of the canticles of Luke's infancy narrative and part of the daily Morning Prayer in many Christian traditions.

In later tradition

Traditions beyond the New Testament, including the second-century Protoevangelium of James, expanded Zechariah's story, in some accounts making him a martyr slain in the Temple. The Tomb of Zechariah in the Kidron Valley in Jerusalem is named for him, though it is a funerary monument of the Second Temple period unrelated to the historical priest. In the Qur'an, Zakariya prays for a son, receives the good news of Yahya (John), and is entrusted with the care of Maryam (Mary) in her sanctuary.
