Balcones Canyonlands

Balcones Canyonlands (image placeholder)

The Balcones Canyonlands are a region of rugged canyon country along the Balcones Fault Zone in Central Texas, at the eastern edge of the Edwards Plateau. The area is characterised by limestone canyons, juniper and oak woodlands, and clear spring-fed streams.

Conservation

The region is protected in part by the Balcones Canyonlands National Wildlife Refuge, established in 1992 in Travis, Burnet, and Williamson counties northwest of Austin, and by the Balcones Canyonlands Preserve, a system of protected lands managed by the City of Austin and Travis County under a regional habitat conservation plan — one of the first of its kind in the United States.

Wildlife

The canyonlands provide habitat for two endangered songbirds, the golden-cheeked warbler, which nests only in Central Texas, and the black-capped vireo, together with numerous rare plant species and karst invertebrates found in area caves.
