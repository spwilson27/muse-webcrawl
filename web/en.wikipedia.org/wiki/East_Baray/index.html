East Baray

East Baray
Location: Angkor, Cambodia
Built: 10th century, under Yasovarman I / Rajendravarman
Dimensions: c. 7.5 × 1.8 km

The East Baray (Khmer: បារាយណ៍ខាងកើត) is a now-dry baray, or artificial reservoir, at Angkor in Cambodia, east of Angkor Thom. Measuring roughly 7.5 by 1.8 kilometres, it was among the largest Khmer reservoirs.

History

Construction is attributed to Yasovarman I around 900 CE and completed or modified under Rajendravarman II. Fed by the Siem Reap River diverted into it, the baray stored monsoon water for irrigation and symbolised the cosmic ocean, with the East Mebon temple on an island at its centre. Inscriptions describe its embankments and sluices.

Present state

The reservoir has long been dry and partly cultivated and settled, though its embankments remain visible from the air. Together with the West Baray it illustrates Khmer hydraulic engineering — debated by scholars as to irrigation versus ritual primacy. See also Angkor and West Baray.
