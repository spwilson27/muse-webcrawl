Battle of Lepanto

The Battle of Lepanto was a naval engagement on 7 October 1571 in the Gulf of Patras, off Lepanto (Naupaktos), Greece. A fleet of the Christian Holy League, led by Venice, Spain and the Papal States under Don John of Austria, decisively defeated the Ottoman fleet — one of the largest naval battles in history and the last major battle fought by rowing vessels.

Background

The Ottomans had invaded Venetian Cyprus in 1570; Famagusta fell in August 1571 with atrocities against its defenders. Pope Pius V assembled the Holy League to relieve Cyprus and challenge Ottoman sea power.

Battle

The League's six Venetian galleasses, stationed ahead of the line, disrupted the Ottoman formation with heavy cannon fire. In the centre Don John's flagship Real grappled Ali Pasha's Sultana; Ali was killed and his flagship taken. On the Christian left Venier prevailed; on the right Doria's manoeuvring opened a gap exploited by Uluç Ali, who captured the Maltese flagship but escaped with a remnant — the only Ottoman squadron to survive largely intact. Some 12,000 Christian galley slaves were freed.

Aftermath

The Ottomans rebuilt a fleet within a year and retained Cyprus, but lost thousands of experienced sailors and archers who could not be replaced so quickly. Lepanto became a symbol of Christian resistance, celebrated in art and literature; Miguel de Cervantes, wounded there, called it the most glorious occasion of the age.
