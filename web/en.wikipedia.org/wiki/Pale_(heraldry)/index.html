Pale (heraldry)

In heraldry and vexillology, a pale is a charge consisting of a band running vertically down the centre of a shield. A pale is one of the ordinaries in heraldry, along with the chief, bend, fess, and others. The pale takes its name from the Latin palus, a stake or palisade post.

Characteristics

By default, a pale occupies about one-third of the width of the shield, though this may be adjusted, particularly when other charges accompany it. A paler, narrower ordinary is called an endorse, and a still narrower one a riband in some contexts; a field divided by multiple vertical stripes is described as paly. A pale that does not reach the top or bottom edges of the shield is said to be couped. Charges placed vertically upon or alongside one another are said to be in pale.

Derived forms

The pale gives rise to diminutives and variants including the pallet (half the width of a pale) and the endorse (half the width of a pallet). In the flag of Canada, the central white square bearing the maple leaf is a famous modern example of a "Canadian pale", a term coined in heraldry for a pale occupying half the flag, designed to give the central device adequate space.

Examples

Pales appear in the arms of many cities, families, and institutions; the arms of Aragon bear four red pallets on gold, and numerous civic arms in Europe and elsewhere employ a single central pale, often charged with additional devices.
