The Kurdistan Democratic Party (Kurdish: پارتی دیموکراتی کوردستان, romanized: Partî Dîmukratî Kurdistan; abbreviated KDP) is the dominant political party in the Kurdistan Region of Iraq and one of the two historic ruling parties of Iraqi Kurdistan, alongside the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan (PUK). It has been led by the Barzani family since its founding.

History

The KDP was founded in 1946 in Mahabad, Iran, during the short-lived Kurdish Republic of Mahabad, with Mustafa Barzani — leader of the Barzan tribe and commander of the republic's forces — elected president in absentia while on campaign. After the republic's collapse, Barzani fled to the Soviet Union, returning to Iraq only after the 1958 revolution. The party led the Kurdish armed struggle (the Aylul revolt) against Baghdad from 1961; the movement collapsed in 1975 when Iran withdrew support under the Algiers Agreement with Saddam Hussein's Iraq. In 1975 Jalal Talabani split to found the PUK, beginning a decades-long KDP–PUK rivalry that erupted into civil war in 1994–1997.

In the Kurdistan Region

After the 1991 uprising and the establishment of the no-fly zone, the KDP governed the northern (Erbil and Duhok) part of the region. Since the 2003 invasion of Iraq, the KDP has been the largest party in the Kurdistan Region's parliament; Nechirvan Barzani has served as the region's prime minister and (from 2019) president, and Masrour Barzani became prime minister in 2019. Masoud Barzani served as president of the Kurdistan Region from 2005 to 2017, resigning after the controversial 2017 independence referendum and Baghdad's retaking of Kirkuk. The KDP maintains its own Peshmerga forces and close relations with Turkey, in contrast to the PUK's ties with Iran.
