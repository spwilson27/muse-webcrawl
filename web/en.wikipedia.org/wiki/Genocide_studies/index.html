Genocide studies

Genocide studies is an academic field that studies the causes, processes, prevention and consequences of genocide and mass atrocity. It grew out of the work of Raphael Lemkin, who coined the word "genocide" in 1944, and developed as a distinct interdisciplinary field from the 1970s onward.

Scope

Drawing on history, sociology, political science, law and psychology, scholars such as Leo Kuper, Helen Fein, Israel Charny, Barbara Harff and Gregory Stanton have modelled risk factors and stages of genocide, comparing cases including the Armenian genocide, the Holocaust, Cambodia, Rwanda and Bosnia. Major institutions include the International Association of Genocide Scholars (founded 1994) and dedicated journals and university centres. Recurring debates concern the definition of genocide, the role of intent, and whether colonial and settler-colonial violence falls within the field.
