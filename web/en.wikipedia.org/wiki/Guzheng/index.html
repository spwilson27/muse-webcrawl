Guzheng

ClassificationString instrument, plucked zither
OriginChina, by the Warring States period
Strings21 (modern standard; historically 13–25)
RelatedSe, koto, gayageum, đàn tranh

The guzheng (Chinese: 古箏, "ancient zheng"), or simply zheng, is a Chinese plucked zither with a history of more than 2,500 years. It is the ancestor of several East and Southeast Asian zithers, including the Japanese koto and Korean gayageum.

Construction and technique

The modern guzheng is about 163 cm long with 21 strings stretched over individual movable bridges on a hollow paulownia soundboard, tuned pentatonically. Players wear finger picks on the right hand to pluck and strum, while the left hand presses strings on the other side of the bridges to produce vibrato, pitch bends and ornaments — techniques essential to its expressive, flowing style.

Repertoire

Traditional regional schools (Henan, Shandong, Chaozhou, Hakka, Zhejiang) each developed characteristic pieces such as "High Mountain and Flowing Water" and "Fishing Junks at Sunset". In the 20th century the instrument was modernised with steel-nylon strings and virtuoso concert repertoire, and it remains a staple of Chinese orchestras and solo performance.
