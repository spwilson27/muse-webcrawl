Red team

A red team is a group that pretends to be an enemy, attempts to enter a physical or digital system, and attempts to improve an organization's effectiveness by challenging its assumptions and security posture. Red teaming originated in military wargaming, where an opposing force (OPFOR) playing the adversary was often designated the "red" force, a convention inherited from Cold War exercises in which friendly forces were blue and enemy forces red.

Applications

Cybersecurity

In cybersecurity, a red team performs adversarial simulation: emulating the tactics, techniques and procedures of real attackers to test detection and response. Unlike a penetration test, which typically seeks to enumerate vulnerabilities, a red team engagement is goal-oriented — for example, reaching a critical database or the domain controller — and tests people and processes as well as technology. The defending security operations team is the blue team; a combined exercise is a purple team engagement.

Military and intelligence

Armed forces use red teams and red cells to role-play adversaries in exercises, and intelligence agencies use them to challenge analytic assumptions and guard against groupthink and mirror-imaging.

Physical security

Red teams also test physical defences — locks, guards, badge controls and access procedures — by attempting covert entry into facilities.
