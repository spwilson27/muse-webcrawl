Tarn (river)

The Tarn (Occitan: Tarn) is a river in southern France, about 380 kilometres (240 mi) long. It rises on Mont Lozère in the Cévennes and flows generally westward to join the Garonne near Moissac, giving its name to the Tarn and Tarn-et-Garonne departments.

Course

From its source in Lozère, the Tarn passes Florac and cuts the celebrated Gorges du Tarn through the limestone causses, a canyon up to 500 metres deep popular with canoeists. It continues through Millau — where the Millau Viaduct, one of the world's tallest bridges, spans its valley — then Albi, Gaillac, and Montauban.

Features

The upper river is swift and clear, the lower navigable in stretches. The Tarn is subject to violent autumn floods fed by Cévenol storms, most famously the catastrophic flood of March 1930 in Montauban and Moissac. Its valley is known for Albi's red-brick cathedral, the vineyards of Gaillac, and the medieval bastide towns along its banks.
