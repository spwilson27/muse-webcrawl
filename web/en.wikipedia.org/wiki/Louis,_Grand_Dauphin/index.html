Louis, Dauphin of France (1 November 1661 – 14 April 1711), commonly known as le Grand Dauphin, was the eldest son and heir apparent of King Louis XIV and his spouse, Maria Theresa of Spain. As the heir apparent to the French throne, he was styled Dauphin. He became known as the "Grand Dauphin" after the birth of his own son, Louis, Duke of Burgundy, the "Petit Dauphin". As he predeceased his father, he never became king.

Life

Louis was born at the Château de Fontainebleau. His education was overseen by the stern Bishop Bossuet, who composed the Discours sur l'histoire universelle for his instruction. Though intelligent and a keen patron of the arts and hunting, the Grand Dauphin was overshadowed throughout his life by his father's dominant personality and was given few real responsibilities.

Military career

He saw military service in the Nine Years' War, commanding on the Rhine in 1688 and capturing Philippsburg, and served in Flanders in the 1690s. His conduct was creditable, though never given independent strategic control.

Family and death

He married Maria Anna Victoria of Bavaria in 1680; they had three sons, including Philip, Duke of Anjou, who in 1700 became Philip V of Spain — the Grand Dauphin thus became, through his son, the ancestor of the Spanish Bourbon line. Louis died of smallpox at Meudon on 14 April 1711, four years before his father; the succession crises that followed his death left his young great-grandson as the eventual Louis XV.
