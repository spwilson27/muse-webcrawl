Aniseikonia

Aniseikonia is an ocular condition in which the two eyes perceive images of different sizes. It most commonly arises when the refractive powers of the two eyes differ substantially (anisometropia), particularly when the difference is corrected with spectacles, whose lenses magnify or minify the retinal image according to their power and distance from the eye. Differences can also arise from retinal disease, after cataract surgery with an intraocular lens in one eye, or from differences in the axial length of the eyes.

Symptoms and effects

Even modest image-size differences of a few percent can impair binocular fusion and stereopsis. Symptoms include headaches, eye strain (asthenopia), dizziness, disorientation, distorted spatial perception, and reading difficulty. Larger differences may cause suppression of one eye's image or double vision.

Measurement and treatment

Aniseikonia is measured with instruments such as the eikonometer or with software-based tests comparing perceived sizes of targets seen by each eye. Treatment aims to equalise retinal image sizes: contact lenses, which sit on the eye, cause less image-size change than spectacles; specially designed "iseikonic" spectacle lenses alter magnification through changes in lens thickness, curvature, and vertex distance; and refractive surgery or appropriate choice of intraocular lens can reduce optical causes. Retinal causes may require treatment of the underlying disease.
