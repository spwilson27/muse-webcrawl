Akatsuki (spacecraft)

Akatsuki (あかつき, "Dawn"), also known as the Venus Climate Orbiter (VCO) and Planet-C, is a Japanese space probe operated by JAXA to study the atmosphere of Venus. Launched on 20 May 2010 aboard an H-IIA rocket, it was the first Japanese spacecraft to orbit another planet.

Failed insertion and recovery

Akatsuki's first orbit-insertion attempt on 7 December 2010 failed when its main engine shut down prematurely, and the probe flew past Venus into a heliocentric orbit. Mission controllers used the small attitude-control thrusters to adjust its trajectory over five years, and a second insertion attempt on 7 December 2015 succeeded, placing Akatsuki in a large elliptical orbit of about 14 days.

Science

From orbit, the spacecraft's cameras image Venus in ultraviolet and infrared to track cloud motions, measure atmospheric super-rotation, and search for lightning and volcanic activity. Among its notable findings was a huge stationary gravity wave in the Venusian atmosphere, reported in 2017, thought to be generated by air flowing over a mountainous region of the surface.
