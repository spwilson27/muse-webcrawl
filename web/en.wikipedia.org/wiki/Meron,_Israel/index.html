Meron (Hebrew: מֵירוֹן, Meiron) is a moshav in northern Israel. Located in the Upper Galilee, at the foot of Mount Meron, it falls under the jurisdiction of Merom HaGalil Regional Council. The moshav was founded in 1949 by former Haganah soldiers on the site associated with the ancient Jewish town of Meron.

The tomb and Lag BaOmer

Meron is best known for the tomb of Rabbi Shimon bar Yochai, the second-century sage traditionally credited with authoring the Zohar, the foundational work of Kabbalah. The tomb is one of Judaism's holiest pilgrimage sites. On Lag BaOmer, the anniversary of Bar Yochai's death and the traditional date of the end of a plague among Rabbi Akiva's students, hundreds of thousands of pilgrims gather at Meron for bonfires, dancing and prayer — historically the largest annual gathering in Israel.

2021 crowd crush

On the night of 29–30 April 2021, a crowd crush on a slippery, overcrowded walkway at the Lag BaOmer celebrations killed 45 people and injured about 150, the deadliest civilian disaster in Israel's history. A state commission of inquiry found systemic failures in the site's management and safety arrangements, and the event led to permanent reforms in how the pilgrimage is organised, including limits on attendance.
