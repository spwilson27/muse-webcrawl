Speed camera

A speed camera is a system used in traffic enforcement that measures the speed of passing vehicles and photographically records those exceeding a set limit, so that a penalty notice can be issued to the registered owner or driver. Speed cameras are a form of automated traffic enforcement.

Technology

Fixed cameras commonly measure speed using radar (Doppler effect), lidar, piezoelectric sensors or inductive loops buried in the road, or pairs of sensors that time a vehicle's passage. Average-speed systems identify vehicles by automatic number-plate recognition (ANPR) at two points and compute speed over the intervening distance. Mobile units operate from tripods or police vehicles, and red-light cameras share much of the same photographic technology. Images typically capture the vehicle and its licence plate, with speed, date, and time recorded for evidential use.

History and use

Speed-camera devices trace back to the Dutch company Gatsometer, founded by rally driver Maurice Gatsonides in the 1950s–60s. Large-scale deployment began in the 1990s, notably in the United Kingdom, France, Australia, and the Netherlands. Studies and official evaluations generally find that cameras reduce speeds and injury collisions near treated sites, though debates persist about revenue motives, camera placement and signage, driver "kangaroo" braking, privacy, and the legality of automated citations in some jurisdictions, especially in the United States, where several states restrict their use.
