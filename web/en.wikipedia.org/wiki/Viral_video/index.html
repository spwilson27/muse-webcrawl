Viral video

A viral video is a video that becomes popular through a viral process of internet sharing, typically through video sharing websites such as YouTube, social media, and email. The term plays on the analogy of a virus spreading from person to person; a viral video may gain millions of views within days of posting.

Viral videos spread rapidly through social sharing on mobile devices.

History

Before YouTube, humorous clips such as "Dancing Baby" (1996) circulated by email and early websites. After YouTube's founding in 2005, videos like "Evolution of Dance" (2006) and "Charlie Bit My Finger" (2007) reached tens of millions. Music videos became the most-viewed category: Psy's "Gangnam Style" (2012) was the first video to reach one billion views, and "Baby Shark Dance" became the first to pass ten billion. The rise of TikTok in the late 2010s shifted virality toward very short, algorithmically promoted clips.

Characteristics and impact

Viral videos are often humorous, surprising, or emotionally striking, and are frequently amateur productions, though advertisers and political campaigns deliberately engineer "viral marketing." Virality can make instant celebrities of ordinary people, launch music careers, spread misinformation, or capture events of public significance—citizen footage of protests and disasters among them. Recommendation algorithms, rather than purely person-to-person sharing, increasingly determine what goes viral.
