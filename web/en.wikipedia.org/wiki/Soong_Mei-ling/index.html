Soong Mei-ling (traditional Chinese: 宋美齡; March 5, 1898 – October 23, 2003), also known as Madame Chiang Kai-shek, was a Chinese political figure who was First Lady of the Republic of China as the wife of Generalissimo and President Chiang Kai-shek. One of the famous Soong sisters, she played a major role in Chinese diplomacy, especially in relations with the United States.

Early life and education

Mei-ling was born in Shanghai into the wealthy family of Charlie Soong, a Methodist missionary and businessman. Educated partly in the United States from childhood, she graduated from Wellesley College in Massachusetts in 1917 with honours in English literature and philosophy. Her fluent, American-accented English made her an exceptional representative of China abroad.

Marriage and political career

She married Chiang Kai-shek in 1927. As First Lady she served as Chiang's interpreter, secretary, and adviser, and founded the New Life Movement promoting Confucian and civic virtues. During the Second Sino-Japanese War she worked to rally international support for China, organising relief for war orphans.

In February 1943 she addressed both houses of the United States Congress — among the first women and the first Chinese national to do so — appealing for American support against Japan. Her charm offensive made her a celebrity in the United States, and she appeared repeatedly on the cover of Time magazine alongside her husband.

Later life

After the Nationalist defeat in the Chinese Civil War, she moved with Chiang to Taiwan, where she remained influential in the Republic of China's diplomacy. Following Chiang's death in 1975 she emigrated to the United States, living in New York. She died in 2003 at the age of 105, having been a figure of public life across three centuries.
