Brännvin

Brännvin is the Swedish term for liquor distilled from potatoes or grain, literally meaning "burnt wine" (bränt vin). The term ties into a long tradition of home distilling in Sweden and neighboring Nordic countries. Brännvin is closely related to the Scandinavian drink akvavit and to vodka, and it is often flavored with herbs and spices such as caraway, dill, or fennel.

History

Distillation was introduced to Sweden in the late 15th century, and brännvin was initially used for medicinal purposes and in the production of gunpowder. During the 18th and 19th centuries, home distilling became widespread, leading to recurring government attempts to regulate or ban it. The Swedish state eventually took control of alcohol production and sales, with well-known brännvin brands produced under state supervision.

Types

Unflavored brännvin is sometimes called renat, while flavored varieties include the various kryddat brännvin (spiced brännvin) traditions served at midsummer, crayfish parties, and Christmas tables. The alcohol content of Swedish brännvin is usually between 30% and 45% ABV.
