ANFO

ANFO
Composition: ~94% ammonium nitrate, ~6% fuel oil
Type: Blasting agent
Detonation velocity: about 3,200 m/s (confined)

ANFO (ammonium nitrate/fuel oil) is a widely used bulk industrial explosive mixture, consisting of about 94 percent porous prilled ammonium nitrate acting as the oxidiser, and about 6 percent fuel oil acting as fuel.

Properties and use

ANFO is inexpensive, stable and safe to handle because it is classified as a blasting agent: it is insensitive to a standard detonator cap and requires a booster charge to detonate. It accounts for a large share of the explosives used in coal mining, quarrying and construction in North America. Its main limitations are a lack of water resistance and low density, addressed by packaged emulsion blends such as heavy ANFO.

History and misuse

Porous fertiliser-grade prills suited to absorbing fuel oil became common in the 1950s, and ANFO displaced dynamite in many applications. Its components' availability has also led to criminal misuse in improvised explosive devices, prompting regulation of ammonium nitrate sales in many countries. See also Ammonium nitrate.
