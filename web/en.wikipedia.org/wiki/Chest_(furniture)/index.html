Chest (furniture)

A carved oak chest with a hinged lid (image placeholder)

A chest (also called a coffer or kist) is a form of furniture typically of rectangular structure with four walls and a removable or hinged lid, used for storage, usually of personal items such as clothing, linens, documents, or valuables. Chests are among the oldest forms of furniture, known from ancient Egypt onwards.

History

Medieval chests were often the principal piece of furniture in a household, serving simultaneously as storage, seat, table, and travelling container. Construction evolved from dug-out logs to planked ("six-board") chests joined with nails, and later to framed-and-panelled designs. Decorative variants include the carved cassone of Renaissance Italy, the painted bridal or dower chests of northern Europe, and the cedar "hope chest" tradition in North America.

Types

Related forms include the chest of drawers (where drawers replace the single cavity), the blanket chest, the campaign chest designed for military travel, the strongbox or safe chest reinforced with iron, and the tool chest. In nautical contexts, the sea chest held a sailor's belongings and served as a seat.
