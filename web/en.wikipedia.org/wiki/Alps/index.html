The Alps are one of the highest and most extensive mountain ranges in Europe, stretching about 1,200 km (750 mi) across eight countries — France, Switzerland, Italy, Austria, Germany, Slovenia, Liechtenstein and Monaco — and crowned by Mont Blanc (4,808 m).

Formed by the collision of the African and Eurasian plates over tens of millions of years, the Alps are the source of great rivers (Rhine, Rhône, Po, Danube tributaries), a climatic divide, and a cradle of mountaineering (golden age ascents of the 1850s–60s, including the Matterhorn, 1865). Tourism, hydroelectric power, dairy farming and transport passes/tunnels (Gotthard, Brenner, Mont Blanc) define the modern Alpine economy; glaciers are retreating rapidly under climate change.
