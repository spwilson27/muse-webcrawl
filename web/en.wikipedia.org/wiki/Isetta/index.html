Isetta

Isetta
Manufacturer: Iso Autoveicoli S.p.A.; BMW (under license)
Production: 1953–1962
Class: Microcar / city car
Body style: 1-door hatchback (front door)
Engine: 236–298 cc single-cylinder

The Isetta is an Italian-designed microcar built under license in a number of different countries, including Argentina, Spain, Belgium, France, Brazil, Germany, and the United Kingdom. Because of its egg shape and bubble-like windows, it became known as a "bubble car," a name also given to similar vehicles.

History

The car originated with the Italian firm Iso Autoveicoli, whose owner Renzo Rivolta wanted to build a small, economical car positioned between a motorcycle and a conventional automobile. The Iso Isetta debuted at the Turin Motor Show in 1953. Its most distinctive feature was its single door at the front of the car, to which the steering wheel and instrument panel were attached, swinging out with the door.

BMW Isetta

In 1955 BMW acquired a license to build the car, re-engineering it around a BMW motorcycle engine. The BMW Isetta became the best-selling single-cylinder car in the world, with 161,728 units sold. Its low price and fuel economy made it ideal transport in post-war Germany, and it is widely credited with helping to save BMW from financial collapse in the 1950s. BMW produced the Isetta 250 and the larger Isetta 300, as well as the four-wheeled derivative, the BMW 600.

Design

The Isetta seats two (three at a squeeze) on a bench seat, accessed through the front door. Rear wheels were set close together, giving many versions only three wheels — in some markets allowing registration as a motorcycle, driveable with a motorcycle license. Top speed was about 85 km/h (53 mph).

Legacy

The Isetta remains an icon of 1950s microcar design and has influenced modern city-car concepts, including electric revivals such as the Microlino.
