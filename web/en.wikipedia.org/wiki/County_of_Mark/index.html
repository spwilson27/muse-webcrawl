County of Mark

The County of Mark (German: Grafschaft Mark) was a historical territory of the Holy Roman Empire in the Ruhr valley and Sauerland region of present-day North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany. It existed from the 12th century until 1807.

History

The counts of Mark took their name from the village of Mark near Hamm. The county passed by inheritance to the dukes of Cleves and later to Brandenburg-Prussia, becoming an early Prussian possession in western Germany. Its territory included Hamm, Unna, Soest, Lüdenscheid and Altena, whose castle was a seat of the counts. During the Napoleonic Wars the county was dissolved into the Grand Duchy of Berg.
