Auger electron spectroscopy
AcronymAES
ClassificationSurface analysis

Auger electron spectroscopy (AES) is an analytical technique used to study surfaces and materials. It relies on the Auger effect: an inner-shell ionised atom relaxes by emitting an electron whose kinetic energy is characteristic of the elements present.

Principle

A focused electron beam (typically 3–30 keV) ionises surface atoms. Auger electrons, emitted from the top few nanometres, are energy-analysed, giving elemental composition and some chemical-state information.

Applications

AES is used in semiconductor, metallurgy and thin-film research for surface composition, contamination and depth profiling combined with sputtering. See X-ray fluorescence and XPS.
