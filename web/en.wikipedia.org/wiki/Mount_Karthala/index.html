Mount Karthala or simply Karthala is an active shield volcano on the island of Grande Comore (Ngazidja) and the highest point of the Comoros archipelago in the Indian Ocean, at 2,361 metres. It is one of the largest active shield volcanoes in the world, with a summit caldera about 3 by 4 kilometres across, and it dominates the southern half of the island, lying close to the capital, Moroni.

Eruptions

Karthala has erupted more than twenty times since the 19th century. Eruptions are typically Strombolian and confined to the summit caldera, but activity in 1977 sent lava down to the sea, and an eruption in April 2005 — the first since 1991 — displaced tens of thousands of people from villages around the volcano and covered much of the island in ash. Further eruptions occurred in late 2005 and May 2006, contaminating water supplies: rainwater harvested in cisterns is the island's main water source, and volcanic ash rendered much of it temporarily undrinkable.

Environment

The volcano's forested slopes host a distinctive montane ecosystem with endemic species, including the Karthala scops owl and several endemic birds and plants; the upper mountain supports the largest remaining tract of natural forest in the Comoros. Deforestation and the pressure of a growing population threaten its catchments, which are vital to Grande Comore's water supply.
