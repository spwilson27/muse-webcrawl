Fellowship of the Ring

The Fellowship of the Ring, also called the Company of the Ring, is the group of nine companions formed in J. R. R. Tolkien's The Lord of the Rings to carry the One Ring to Mordor and destroy it in the fires of Mount Doom. It lends its name to the first volume of the novel, The Fellowship of the Ring (1954).

Formation

At the Council of Elrond in Rivendell, representatives of the Free Peoples resolved that the Ring must be destroyed. Elrond appointed nine walkers to set against the nine Nazgûl: Frodo Baggins, the Ring-bearer, with his servant Samwise Gamgee and fellow hobbits Meriadoc Brandybuck and Peregrin Took; the wizard Gandalf the Grey; Aragorn, heir of Isildur; Boromir of Gondor; the Elf Legolas; and the Dwarf Gimli.

Journey and breaking

The Company travelled south from Rivendell, failed to cross the Misty Mountains at the Redhorn Pass in snow, and passed instead through Moria, where Gandalf fell fighting a Balrog. After resting in Lothlórien, they journeyed down the Anduin to the Falls of Rauros. There the Fellowship broke: Boromir, tempted by the Ring, tried to take it; Frodo and Sam departed alone for Mordor, Merry and Pippin were captured by Orcs, and Aragorn, Legolas and Gimli pursued the captors — the events that open The Two Towers. Boromir died defending the hobbits and was given a funeral boat on the river.
