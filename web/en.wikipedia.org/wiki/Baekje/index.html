Baekje

Baekje (Korean: 백제) was a Korean kingdom located in southwestern Korea from 18 BC to AD 660. It was one of the Three Kingdoms of Korea, together with Goguryeo and Silla. At its peak in the 4th century it controlled most of the western Korean peninsula and parts of Manchuria and maintained maritime trade with China and Japan.

History

Tradition credits Onjo, a son of Goguryeo's founder Jumong, with founding Baekje at Wiryeseong (present-day Seoul area). Under King Geunchogo (r. 346–375) the kingdom reached its greatest extent. In 475 Goguryeo captured the capital and the court moved to Ungjin (Gongju); in 538 King Seong moved it again to Sabi (Buyeo) and renamed the state Nambuyeo. Baekje played a key role in transmitting Buddhism, writing, and technology to early Japan, with which it maintained close ties to Yamato.

Fall

Weakened by rivalry with Silla, Baekje fell in 660 to a combined Silla–Tang China alliance; King Uija surrendered after the fall of Sabi. A restoration movement led by Gwisil Boksin and the monk Dochim, aided by Yamato Japan, was defeated at the Battle of Baekgang in 663.

Culture

Baekje is noted for refined, warm craftsmanship: gilt-bronze incense burners, the Mireuksa and Jeongnimsa pagodas, and royal tombs at Songsan-ri including that of King Muryeong. The Baekje Historic Areas were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2015. See also history of Korea.
