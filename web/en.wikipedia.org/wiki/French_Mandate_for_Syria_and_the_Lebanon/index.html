The Mandate for Syria and the Lebanon was a League of Nations Class A mandate entrusted to France after the First World War, covering territory formerly part of the Ottoman Empire. Allocated to France at the San Remo conference in 1920 and entering into force in 1923, it lasted until Syrian and Lebanese independence in the 1940s.

Establishment

French forces under General Henri Gouraud defeated the short-lived Arab Kingdom of Syria of Faisal I at the Battle of Maysalun in July 1920. France then divided the mandate into states, including Greater Lebanon, the State of Damascus, the State of Aleppo, the Alawite State, and the Druze state of Jabal Druze — a partition widely seen as a policy of divide and rule.

Resistance and revolt

The Great Syrian Revolt of 1925–1927, beginning among the Druze under Sultan al-Atrash, spread across Syria and saw French forces bombard Damascus. Thereafter France alternated between negotiated treaties and direct rule; a 1936 treaty promising independence was never ratified in Paris.

Independence

During the Second World War the Levant was taken by British and Free French forces in 1941. Lebanon proclaimed independence in 1943 and Syria in 1944; after the Levant Crisis of 1945, the last French troops withdrew in April 1946, ending the mandate. The Sanjak of Alexandretta had earlier been transferred to Turkey in 1939 as Hatay Province.
