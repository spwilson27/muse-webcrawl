The Los Angeles Kings are a professional ice hockey team based in Los Angeles. The Kings compete in the National Hockey League (NHL) as a member of the Pacific Division of the Western Conference. The team founded in 1967 played its home games at the Forum in Inglewood until 1999, when it moved to the Staples Center (now Crypto.com Arena) in downtown Los Angeles.

History

The Kings were one of six teams added in the NHL's 1967 expansion. The franchise first reached the Stanley Cup Final in 1993, led by Wayne Gretzky — acquired in 1988 in a blockbuster trade from Edmonton — but lost to the Montreal Canadiens. After years of modest success, the Kings won their first Stanley Cup in 2012, defeating the New Jersey Devils as the eighth seed, and won again in 2014, defeating the New York Rangers in a final famous for multiple overtime games.

Identity

The team's colours are black, silver and white, an identity adopted in 1988 to coincide with Gretzky's arrival. Notable players in franchise history include Gretzky, Marcel Dionne, Luc Robitaille, Rob Blake, Dustin Brown, Anze Kopitar, Drew Doughty and goaltender Jonathan Quick.

Rivalries

The Kings' chief rivals include the Anaheim Ducks, their opponents in the cross-town "Freeway Face-Off," and the San Jose Sharks.
