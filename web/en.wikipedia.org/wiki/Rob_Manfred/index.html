Robert D. Manfred Jr. (born September 28, 1958) is an American lawyer and business executive who has served as the tenth Commissioner of Baseball since January 25, 2015. He previously worked as a labour lawyer and, from 1998, as Major League Baseball's executive vice president for labour relations, leading negotiations on several collective bargaining agreements before becoming MLB's chief operating officer in 2013.

Tenure as commissioner

Manfred's tenure has included the introduction of pace-of-play measures, most notably the pitch clock in 2023, expanded playoffs, and the handling of the Houston Astros sign-stealing scandal, in which he granted players immunity and described the World Series trophy controversially as "a piece of metal". He oversaw the league during the 2020 COVID-shortened season, the 2021–22 lockout, the integration of Negro leagues statistics into the major-league record, and the Athletics' relocation from Oakland to Las Vegas via Sacramento. He has announced he intends to retire when his term ends in January 2029.
