United States Army enlisted rank insignia

Summary chart of current United States Army enlisted insignia.

The enlisted rank insignia of the United States Army identify the pay grade and seniority of soldiers below the rank of commissioned officer. Junior enlisted soldiers advance from Private (E-1) through Specialist/Corporal (E-4); non-commissioned officers (NCOs) begin at Sergeant (E-5) and senior NCOs continue to Sergeant Major and Command Sergeant Major (E-9).

History

Chevron-based insignia date to the early 19th century, when chevrons were awarded for long service and adopted to show rank. The modern system developed through the world wars; the "upside-down" chevron orientation worn today was standardised in 1902 for most grades. Specialist grades once ran parallel to NCO ranks (technicians and specialists up to E-9 existed in the mid-20th century) but were progressively reduced, leaving a single Specialist grade at E-4 since 1985. Insignia are worn in gold-on-blue on the Army Service Uniform and in subdued forms on combat uniforms.
