Bảo Đại (born Nguyễn Phúc Vĩnh Thụy, 22 October 1913 – 30 July 1997) was the thirteenth and last emperor of the Nguyễn dynasty of Vietnam, reigning from 1926 until his abdication in 1945. He later served as chief of state of the State of Vietnam from 1949 to 1955.

Reign

Educated in France at the Lycée Condorcet and the Paris Institute of Political Studies, Vĩnh Thụy succeeded his father Khải Định in 1926 and took the era name Bảo Đại ("keeper of greatness"). Returning to Vietnam in 1932, he announced reforms — including a chamber of representatives and abolition of some court rituals and of the mandarinate examinations — but real power remained with the French protectorate administration in Annam. In March 1945, after the Japanese coup de force against the French, he proclaimed the Empire of Vietnam independent under Japanese sponsorship, with Trần Trọng Kim as prime minister.

Abdication and later life

Under pressure from the Viet Minh during the August Revolution, Bảo Đại abdicated on 25 August 1945, famously remarking that he would rather be a citizen of a free country than king of an enslaved one, and briefly served as "supreme counsellor" to Ho Chi Minh's Democratic Republic before going into exile. In 1949 the French brought him back as chief of state of the anti-communist State of Vietnam within the French Union — the "Bảo Đại solution" — but he spent much of his tenure in France, and in 1955 his prime minister Ngô Đình Diệm deposed him in a referendum widely regarded as fraudulent and proclaimed the Republic of Vietnam. Bảo Đại lived the rest of his life in exile on the French Riviera and in Paris, where he died in 1997 and was buried at Passy Cemetery.
