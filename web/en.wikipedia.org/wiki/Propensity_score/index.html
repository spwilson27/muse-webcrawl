Propensity score

In statistics, the propensity score is the probability of a unit (e.g., a person) being assigned to a particular treatment given a set of observed covariates. Propensity scores are used to reduce bias in estimating treatment effects from observational data, where random assignment is not possible.

Method

Introduced by Paul Rosenbaum and Donald Rubin in 1983, the propensity score is typically estimated with logistic regression of treatment assignment on observed confounders. Conditional on the propensity score, the distribution of those covariates is balanced between treated and untreated groups, mimicking aspects of a randomized experiment.

Uses

Propensity scores can be used for matching treated to untreated units with similar scores, stratification, covariate adjustment, or inverse probability weighting. The approach relies on the assumption of no unmeasured confounding; it cannot balance variables that were not observed.
