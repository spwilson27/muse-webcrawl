Public inquiry

A public inquiry is an official review of events or actions ordered by a government body, typically in response to a matter of public concern such as a disaster, scandal or systemic failure. In the United Kingdom, statutory inquiries are held under the Inquiries Act 2005.

Process

An inquiry is usually chaired by a judge or senior figure, takes evidence in public hearings, and can compel witnesses and documents if statutory. It publishes findings and recommendations, though these are generally not legally binding on the government.

Examples

Notable British inquiries include the Bloody Sunday (Saville) Inquiry, the Leveson Inquiry into press ethics, the Grenfell Tower Inquiry and the UK Covid-19 Inquiry. Similar mechanisms exist in Ireland, Canada, Australia and other Commonwealth countries.
