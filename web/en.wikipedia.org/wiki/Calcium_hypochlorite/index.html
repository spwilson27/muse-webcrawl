Calcium hypochlorite is an inorganic compound with formula Ca(ClO)2, also written as Ca(OCl)2. It is a white solid, although commercial samples appear yellow. It strongly smells of chlorine, owing to its slow decomposition in moist air. This compound is relatively stable and has greater available chlorine than sodium hypochlorite, and it is widely used for water treatment and as a bleaching agent or disinfectant — most familiarly as "pool chlorine".

Production

Calcium hypochlorite is produced industrially by treating slaked lime (calcium hydroxide) with chlorine gas. The mixed salt Ca(OCl)2 can be prepared in various grades with available chlorine contents typically around 65–70% for the high-test product (HTH).

Uses

The compound is used to disinfect drinking water and swimming pool water, to sanitise public restrooms and food preparation areas, and in organic chemistry as an oxidising agent. It was historically used in bleaching powder together with calcium chloride and calcium hydroxide.

Safety

Calcium hypochlorite is a strong oxidiser. It reacts with many organic compounds and must never be mixed with acids (which release chlorine gas) or ammonia. Fires involving large quantities can be intensified by the oxygen the compound releases on heating.
