The Battle of the Frontiers (French: Bataille des Frontières; German: Grenzschlachten) was a series of battles fought along the eastern frontier of France and in southern Belgium shortly after the outbreak of the First World War. Between 14 and 24 August 1914 the French armies, attacking according to Plan XVII into Lorraine and the Ardennes, suffered a series of defeats at the hands of the German armies, while the German right wing swept through Belgium toward Paris.

French infantry in August 1914, at the opening of the First World War.

Course of the battles

The battles comprised the offensives in Lorraine (Morhange and Sarrebourg, 14–20 August), the Battle of the Ardennes (21–23 August), the Battle of Charleroi on the Sambre (21–23 August), and the Battle of Mons (23 August), where the British Expeditionary Force first engaged the Germans. French doctrine emphasised the offensive — the offensive à outrance — and infantry advancing in dense order against entrenched positions supported by artillery and machine guns suffered catastrophic losses.

Casualties and consequences

22 August 1914, the day of the Ardennes and Charleroi fighting, became the deadliest day in French military history, with some 27,000 French soldiers killed. Total French casualties for August 1914 exceeded 300,000. General Joffre dismissed numerous generals and began transferring forces westward; the Allied armies retreated until they turned and stopped the German advance at the First Battle of the Marne in September.
