Yamnaya culture

The Yamnaya culture (from Russian Yamnaya, "relating to pits"; also Pit Grave culture) was a late Copper Age to early Bronze Age archaeological culture of the Pontic–Caspian steppe, dating to roughly 3300–2600 BC.

Yamnaya culture
Period: c. 3300–2600 BC
Region: Pontic–Caspian steppe
Type site: Mikhailovka, Ukraine
Burials: Kurgan pit graves

Characteristics

The Yamnaya were nomadic pastoralists herding cattle and sheep, using wheeled wagons, with some agriculture. They buried their dead in pit graves under kurgan mounds, often with ochre, in a supine position with bent knees.

Origins and genetics

Ancient DNA shows Yamnaya ancestry derives from Eastern Hunter-Gatherers and Caucasus Hunter-Gatherers. Their expansion westward contributed greatly to the Corded Ware culture and to the ancestry of modern Europeans, and eastward to the Afanasievo culture.

Indo-European languages

Under the Kurgan hypothesis, the Yamnaya are identified with late Proto-Indo-Europeans; their migrations are proposed to have spread Indo-European languages across Europe and parts of Asia.
