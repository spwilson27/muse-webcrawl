Laurens Hammond (1895–1973)

Laurens Hammond (January 11, 1895 – July 3, 1973) was an American engineer and inventor whose inventions include the Hammond organ, the Hammond clock, and the first polyphonic electronic musical instrument, the Novachord. Despite his central place in the history of electronic music, Hammond was not himself a musician and could not play the organ he created.

Early life and career

Hammond was born in Evanston, Illinois, and showed an early talent for mechanics; he had an early automobile-related patent while still a teenager. After serving in France during the First World War, he worked as chief engineer for the Gray Motor Company in Detroit before becoming an independent inventor. His development of a silent synchronous electric motor led to the founding of the Hammond Clock Company in 1928.

The Hammond organ

When clock sales slumped during the Great Depression, Hammond applied his synchronous motor to driving tone wheels in an electric organ, which he patented in 1934. The Hammond organ, beginning with the Model A of 1935, used rotating tonewheels and additive synthesis with drawbars, and became enormously successful in churches, jazz, gospel, and later rock music. The B-3 model, paired with the Leslie speaker, became a classic instrument. Hammond also invented the Novachord (1938), an early polyphonic synthesiser, and the Solovox.

Later life

Hammond retired as president of his company in 1955 and as a director in 1960. He held more than 110 patents over his lifetime. He died in Cornwall, Connecticut, in 1973.
