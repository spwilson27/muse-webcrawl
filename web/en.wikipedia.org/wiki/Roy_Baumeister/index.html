Roy Baumeister

Roy F. Baumeister (born May 16, 1953) is an American social psychologist known for his work on the self, self-control, belongingness, and free will. He is among the most cited psychologists of his generation and has held positions at Case Western Reserve University, Florida State University, and the University of Queensland.

Education and career

Baumeister was born in Cleveland, Ohio. He earned his bachelor's degree from Princeton University and his PhD in psychology from Princeton in 1978, working with Edward E. Jones. After a postdoctoral period at the University of California, Berkeley, he joined Case Western Reserve University, where he spent much of his early career, and later moved to Florida State University as the Francis Eppes Eminent Scholar. In 2016 he moved to the University of Queensland in Australia. He has received major honours including the William James Fellow Award and election to the American Academy of Arts and Sciences.

Research

Baumeister's research spans several influential topics:

Need to belong. With Mark Leary, he argued in a landmark 1995 review that the need to belong is a fundamental human motivation, driving much cognition, emotion, and behaviour, and that social exclusion has wide-ranging harmful effects.

Self-control and ego depletion. Baumeister proposed that self-control operates like a muscle that can fatigue — the "ego depletion" effect — and with John Tierney wrote the bestseller Willpower: Rediscovering the Greatest Human Strength (2011). Large replication attempts later produced mixed and contested results for ego depletion, a debate in which Baumeister has remained an active participant, and the episode became a prominent case in psychology's replication crisis.

Negativity bias. In "Bad Is Stronger Than Good" (2001), Baumeister and colleagues reviewed evidence that negative events, emotions, and information generally have greater psychological impact than positive ones.

Other work includes research on self-esteem (concluding that high self-esteem does not straightforwardly cause better performance or interpersonal success), sexual economics theory, the cultural animal view of human nature in The Cultural Animal (2005), and free will, which he has defended in a naturalistic form in Free Will and Consciousness. His book Is There Anything Good About Men? (2010) argued controversially that gender differences reflect complementary cultural specialisations.

Selected books

Baumeister's books include Meanings of Life (1991), Evil: Inside Human Violence and Cruelty (1997, with Aaron Beck), The Social Dimension of Sex (2001, with Jennifer Wotman), The Cultural Animal (2005), Willpower (2011, with John Tierney), and The Power of Bad (2019, with John Tierney).
