Whitehead manifold

In mathematics, the Whitehead manifold is an open 3-manifold that is contractible but not homeomorphic to three-dimensional Euclidean space R3. Constructed by J. H. C. Whitehead in 1935, it was the first example of such a space and resolved a mistaken claim in Poincaré's work on the classification of 3-manifolds.

Construction

Start with a solid torus T1 embedded in a larger solid torus T2 in the pattern of the two components of the Whitehead link: the inner torus winds twice around inside the outer one, linked so that it is null-homotopic in the complement yet geometrically ensnared. Repeat the construction indefinitely, producing an increasing sequence of solid tori T1 ⊂ T2 ⊂ T3 ⊂ ⋯. The union of all of them is the Whitehead manifold.

Properties

Every loop in the Whitehead manifold can be contracted to a point — it is contractible and simply connected, like R3 — but the manifold is not "simply connected at infinity," and this failure distinguishes it from Euclidean space. Whitehead himself famously announced a proof of the Poincaré conjecture based on the assumption that every contractible open 3-manifold is R3, then found the mistake and produced this counterexample instead.

The Whitehead manifold times R is homeomorphic to R4, a curiosity much studied in exotic smoothness theory. Its existence illustrates the subtlety of three-dimensional topology that later work — up to Perelman's proof of the Poincaré conjecture — had to overcome.
