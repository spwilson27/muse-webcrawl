Bank Al-Maghrib (Arabic: بنك المغرب) is the central bank of the Kingdom of Morocco. Founded in 1959, it succeeded the former Banque d'État du Maroc, established under the Act of Algeciras in 1907. It is responsible for issuing the Moroccan dirham, conducting monetary policy, supervising the banking system, and managing the country's foreign exchange reserves.

Functions

The bank sets the key policy interest rate and works to maintain price stability. A 2019 statute strengthened its independence and broadened its mandate to include financial stability and the promotion of financial inclusion. Bank Al-Maghrib supervises credit institutions, oversees payment systems, and prints banknotes and mints coins at its Dar As-Sikkah facility. It has been gradually moving the dirham toward a more flexible exchange-rate regime, widening the currency's fluctuation band in reforms begun in 2018 and 2020.

Governance and museums

The bank is headed by a governor titled Wali, appointed by the King; Abdellatif Jouahri has held the post since 2003 and is among the world's longest-serving central bank governors. Bank Al-Maghrib operates regional branches and agencies across Morocco and maintains the Bank Al-Maghrib Museum in Rabat, which displays Moroccan currency and numismatic collections.
