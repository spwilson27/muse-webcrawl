Medical license

A medical license is an occupational license that permits a person to legally practice medicine. In most countries, a person must have a medical license bestowed either by a specified government-approved professional association or a government agency before they can practice medicine. Licenses are not granted automatically to all people with medical degrees. A medical school graduate must receive a license to practice medicine to be legally called a physician.

United States

In the United States, medical licenses are usually granted by individual states. Only those with medical degrees from schools listed in the World Directory of Medical Schools are permitted to apply for medical licensure. Board certification is a separate process. Requirements vary, but generally include graduation from an approved medical school, completion of residency training, and passing the United States Medical Licensing Examination (USMLE) for MDs and DOs, or the COMLEX-USA for osteopathic physicians.

The Federation of State Medical Boards represents state boards and publishes disciplinary data. Licensure is typically renewed every few years, contingent on continuing medical education and good standing. Since the early 2020s, the Interstate Medical Licensure Compact has allowed physicians to obtain licenses in multiple member states through an expedited process.

United Kingdom

In the United Kingdom, doctors must register with the General Medical Council (GMC) and hold a licence to practise. Registration requires an acceptable primary medical qualification and, for many international graduates, passing the Professional and Linguistic Assessments Board (PLAB) test or an equivalent route. The licence must be revalidated periodically, usually every five years, based on annual appraisals.

Canada

Canadian physicians are licensed by provincial and territorial medical regulatory authorities. Most require the Licentiate of the Medical Council of Canada (LMCC), granted after passing the Medical Council of Canada Qualifying Examination, in addition to postgraduate training.

History

Regulation of medical practice dates to antiquity; formal licensing spread in the 19th century, for example with the UK Medical Act 1858, which established the General Medical Council and an official register of qualified practitioners.
