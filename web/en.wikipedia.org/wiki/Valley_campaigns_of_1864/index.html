Valley campaigns of 1864

The Valley campaigns of 1864 were a series of operations during the American Civil War in the Shenandoah Valley of Virginia, running roughly from May to October 1864. They began with Confederate Lieutenant General Jubal Early's offensive down the Valley and his raid to the outskirts of Washington, D.C., and ended with Union Major General Philip Sheridan's victories and devastation of the Valley.

Background

In mid-1864, Robert E. Lee sent Early's Second Corps to clear the Valley of Union forces and, if possible, threaten Washington to draw Union troops away from the siege of Petersburg. Early defeated Union forces in the Lynchburg campaign and won the Battle of Monocacy on 9 July, though the delay there allowed Washington's defences to be reinforced before he reached Fort Stevens.

Sheridan's campaign

Ulysses S. Grant placed Sheridan in command of the Army of the Shenandoah with orders to pursue Early and deny the Valley to the Confederacy. Sheridan defeated Early at the Third Battle of Winchester (19 September), Fisher's Hill (22 September), and, after a surprise Confederate attack, at Cedar Creek (19 October). His troops then systematically destroyed barns, mills, crops and railroads in "The Burning", crippling the Valley's ability to supply Confederate armies.

Consequences

The Union victories, arriving alongside the fall of Atlanta, boosted Northern morale and helped Abraham Lincoln win re-election in November 1864. The Shenandoah was effectively removed as a Confederate supply base and invasion route for the remainder of the war.
