Senchas Mar

The Senchas Már (Old Irish for "great tradition" or "great antiquity") is the largest and best-known compilation of early Irish law (Brehon law). It was assembled, probably in the 7th or 8th century AD, in the northern half of Ireland, and consists of some 47–59 separate legal tracts covering a wide range of topics, preceded by a famous pseudo-historical introduction.

Contents

The tracts of the Senchas Már deal with such subjects as distraint (the legal seizure of property), the law of kinship and land, contracts, marriage and divorce, fosterage, sick-maintenance, bee-keeping, and the laws governing the various ranks of society and their honour-prices. Only about one-third of the original text survives intact; much of it is known through fragments accompanied by later glosses and commentary added by medieval jurists.

The introduction

The introduction presents a legendary account of the codification of Irish law: the pagan laws of Ireland were supposedly recited to Saint Patrick by the poet Dubthach maccu Lugair and the judge Morann, and were revised so that nothing in them conflicted with Christian teaching, with the resulting body of law being attributed to the authority of Patrick. Modern scholars regard this as a legitimizing fiction created by the jurists and churchmen who compiled the collection.

Significance

Together with other collections, the Senchas Már is the principal source for knowledge of the native legal system that operated in Gaelic Ireland, in whole or part, until the collapse of the Gaelic order in the 17th century.
