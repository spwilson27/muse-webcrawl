The Cotswold Way is a 102-mile (164 km) long-distance footpath in England, running the length of the Cotswold Hills from Chipping Campden in the north to the city of Bath in the south. The trail follows the western escarpment of the Cotswolds, an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty, for much of its course, offering wide views over the Severn Vale towards the Malvern Hills and Wales.

Route

From Chipping Campden the path climbs to Dover's Hill, passes Broadway Tower, and runs through Winchcombe — near Sudeley Castle and the Neolithic long barrow of Belas Knap — before ascending Cleeve Hill, the highest point in the Cotswolds at 330 m. It continues past Cheltenham and the Devil's Chimney on Leckhampton Hill, over Crickley Hill and Painswick, to Cooper's Hill (famous for cheese-rolling), then south via the Tyndale Monument at North Nibley, Wotton-under-Edge and Dyrham Park to finish at Bath Abbey.

History

The route was proposed by Gloucestershire rambler Tony Drake and others in the 1950s, opened as a recreational path in 1970, and was formally designated a National Trail in 2007. Waymarking uses the National Trail acorn symbol. Most walkers complete it in 7–10 days; an annual ultramarathon covers the whole route in a single day.
