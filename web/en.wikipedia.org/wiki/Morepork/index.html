Morepork

The morepork (Ninox novaeseelandiae), also called the ruru, is a small brown owl found in New Zealand, Norfolk Island and formerly Lord Howe Island. The Māori name ruru and the English name morepork are both imitative of its distinctive two-pitched call.

The morepork is New Zealand's only surviving native owl. It is about 26–29 cm long, with a dark brown plumage speckled with cream, a short tail and relatively long legs. It inhabits forests and well-vegetated urban areas, roosting by day in dense foliage.

A nocturnal hunter, the morepork feeds mainly on large insects such as wētā, moths and beetles, as well as small birds, rats and mice, which it catches with silent flight and powerful talons. It nests in tree hollows, epiphytes or cavities, where the female incubates two or three eggs.

Related populations in Australia and New Guinea were formerly treated as the same species (the boobook), but taxonomic treatments now usually separate the southern boobook (Ninox boobook). The extinct Laughing Owl was a different, larger New Zealand species. The morepork appears in Māori tradition as a watchful guardian, and its call is famously rendered "ruru" or "more-pork".
