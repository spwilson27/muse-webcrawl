Paul Farmer

Paul Edward Farmer (26 October 1959 – 21 February 2022) was an American medical anthropologist and physician. He co-founded Partners In Health (PIH) and dedicated his career to delivering high-quality health care to poor communities, demonstrating that diseases such as multidrug-resistant tuberculosis and HIV could be treated effectively in low-resource settings.

Life and work

Farmer grew up in Florida and studied at Duke University before earning his MD and a PhD in anthropology from Harvard. Beginning in the 1980s in Cange, Haiti — where PIH's sister organisation Zanmi Lasante built a clinic, hospital and community-health-worker system — he developed a model of care based on accompaniment and on treating the social as well as medical roots of disease. PIH expanded to Peru, Russia, Rwanda, Lesotho, Malawi and elsewhere, and Farmer held chairs at Harvard Medical School and Brigham and Women's Hospital and served as a UN special adviser.

Books and legacy

His books include AIDS and Accusation (1992), Infections and Inequalities (1999) and Pathologies of Power (2003); Tracy Kidder's Mountains Beyond Mountains (2003) popularised his life. Farmer responded to the 2010 Haiti earthquake and the West African Ebola epidemic, criticising weak global health systems. He died in his sleep in Butaro, Rwanda, in February 2022, aged 62.
