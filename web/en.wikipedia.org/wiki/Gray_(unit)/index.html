Gray (unit)

The gray (symbol: Gy) is the unit of ionizing radiation dose in the International System of Units (SI), defined as the absorption of one joule of radiation energy per kilogram of matter. It is used as a measure of absorbed dose, specific energy (imparted), and kerma.

The unit was named in honour of Louis Harold Gray, a British physicist who pioneered the study of the effects of radiation on living tissue. It was adopted as part of the SI system in 1975, replacing the older unit, the rad (1 Gy = 100 rad).

Usage

The gray is used in radiation therapy, where tumours are typically treated with total doses of tens of grays delivered in fractions, and in radiation protection and dosimetry. The absorbed dose is distinguished from the equivalent dose, measured in sieverts, which weights the absorbed dose by the biological effectiveness of the radiation type.

A whole-body absorbed dose of about 5 Gy is usually fatal to humans without medical treatment. By contrast, a chest CT scan delivers only a few milligrays to the organs examined. See also sievert, absorbed dose, and dosimetry.
