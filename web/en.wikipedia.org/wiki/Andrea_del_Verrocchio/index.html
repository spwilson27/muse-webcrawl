Andrea del Verrocchio (c. 1435 – 1488), born Andrea di Michele di Francesco de' Cioni, was an Italian sculptor, painter and goldsmith of the Florentine Renaissance. Trained as a goldsmith, he took his name from his master, the goldsmith Giuliano Verrocchi. From his large and influential workshop came some of the finest sculpture of the quattrocento — and the young Leonardo da Vinci, who served his apprenticeship there.

Works

His bronze David (c. 1473–1475, Bargello), a lithe, self-assured youth, contrasts famously with the Davids of Donatello and Michelangelo. The painting The Baptism of Christ (c. 1472–1475, Uffizi) was largely executed by his workshop; according to Vasari, the angel painted by the apprentice Leonardo was so superior that Verrocchio abandoned painting. His last masterpiece is the bronze equestrian monument to the condottiere Bartolomeo Colleoni in Venice, modelled by Verrocchio and cast after his death by Alessandro Leopardi; it stands beside the church of Santi Giovanni e Paolo.

Workshop and influence

The Verrocchio workshop was a training ground for a generation of Renaissance artists including Perugino, Ghirlandaio, Lorenzo di Credi and Leonardo, and its blend of goldsmith precision, anatomical study and naturalistic detail shaped the development of Florentine art.
