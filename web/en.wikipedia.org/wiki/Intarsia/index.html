Intarsia

Intarsia is a form of wood inlaying, similar to marquetry, in which pieces of wood of different colours and grains are fitted together to form a picture or pattern, set into a solid background. The term also describes a knitting technique that produces patterns with blocks of colour worked from separate balls of yarn.

Woodworking

The craft flourished in Renaissance Italy, where intarsia panels decorated choir stalls, studioli — famously those of Federico da Montefeltro in Urbino and Gubbio — and church furniture with still lifes, landscapes and perspective city views. German and other northern workshops developed their own schools. Unlike marquetry, intarsia pieces are typically thicker and sculpted to give a three-dimensional effect.

Knitting

In knitting, intarsia creates large areas of contrasting colour; yarns are not carried across the back of the work, so each area is knitted with its own supply, producing a single-thickness fabric.
