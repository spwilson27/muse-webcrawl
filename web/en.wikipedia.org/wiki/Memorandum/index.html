Memorandum

A memorandum (plural: memoranda or memorandums), commonly abbreviated to memo, is a written message used in business and organisational communication. The word derives from the Latin memorandum, "that which is to be remembered". Traditionally, a memo is an internal document used to communicate policies, procedures, decisions or short reports within an organisation, as opposed to a letter, which is used for external correspondence.

Format

A typical memo begins with a heading block listing the recipient ("To:"), the sender ("From:"), the date, and the subject ("Re:"). The body is usually concise and direct, often organised with headings and lists. Unlike letters, memos normally have no salutation or signature block, though the sender may initial the heading.

Types

Special forms include the memorandum of understanding (MOU), which records an agreement between parties without necessarily being legally binding, and the memorandum of association, a founding document of a company in many legal systems. In government, memoranda may carry legal or policy weight — for example, presidential memoranda in the United States.
