Francesco Schettino

Francesco Schettino (born 14 November 1960) is an Italian former naval officer who was the captain of the cruise ship Costa Concordia when it struck rocks and capsized off Isola del Giglio on 13 January 2012, killing 32 people.

The disaster

Schettino ordered a sail-by salute deviation from the planned course that brought the ship onto rocks, tearing a 53-metre gash in the hull. He was heavily criticised for leaving the ship while passengers were still aboard; recorded exchanges with Coast Guard Captain Gregorio De Falco, who ordered him back on board, became internationally notorious.

Trial and imprisonment

In 2015 an Italian court convicted Schettino of multiple manslaughter, causing a shipwreck and abandoning ship, sentencing him to 16 years in prison. The conviction was upheld on appeal and by Italy's Court of Cassation in 2017, when he began serving his sentence in Rome's Rebibbia prison. Recordings, survivor testimony and his defence that a successful manoeuvre saved lives were central to the proceedings.
