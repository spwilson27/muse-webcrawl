Jacob Kettler (German: Jakob von Kettler; 28 October 1610 – 1 January 1682) was Duke of Courland and Semigallia from 1642 to 1682. Under his rule the small Baltic duchy, a vassal of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, reached the peak of its prosperity, building one of Europe's largest merchant fleets and briefly establishing overseas colonies in the Caribbean and West Africa.

Early life and accession

Jacob was born in Goldingen (present-day Kuldīga, Latvia), the son of Duke Wilhelm Kettler and Princess Sophie of Prussia, and was a godson of James I of England. Educated at Rostock and Leipzig, he travelled widely in western Europe and studied the commercial policies of the Dutch Republic. He became co-ruler with his uncle Friedrich in 1638 and sole duke in 1642.

Economic policy

Kettler pursued mercantilist policies modelled on the Netherlands. He developed ironworks, shipyards at Ventspils (Windau) and Liepāja, glassworks, saltpetre and textile manufactures, and built a merchant fleet that contemporary accounts numbered among the largest in Europe. Courland's ships traded as far as the Mediterranean, and the duchy exported grain, timber, flax and manufactured goods across the continent.

Colonies

Kettler sought overseas possessions to match his commercial ambitions. Courland established a colony on Tobago in the Caribbean (New Courland, held intermittently from 1654) and a trading fort, Jacobsfort, on James Island in the Gambia River in West Africa. The colonies were chronically under-resourced and were lost to stronger rivals, but they make Courland the smallest European state to have attempted colonisation in the Americas.

War and later reign

During the Second Northern War, Swedish forces invaded Courland in 1658 and captured Kettler, holding him prisoner until the Treaty of Oliva in 1660. The duchy's economy was badly damaged, and though Kettler rebuilt much of his fleet and industry, Courland never fully recovered its former position. He died at Mitau (Jelgava) on 1 January 1682 and was succeeded by his son Frederick Casimir.
