Mitsuyo Maeda

Mitsuyo Maeda (1878–1941), known by the nickname Count Koma (Conde Koma in Portuguese), was a Japanese judoka and prizefighter who became a foundational figure in the development of Brazilian jiu-jitsu. A student of Jigoro Kano at the Kodokan, he travelled the world from 1904 giving exhibitions and taking challenge matches against wrestlers and fighters of many styles.

Travels and challenge matches

Maeda toured the United States, Cuba, Mexico, Europe, and Central and South America, fighting hundreds of matches and reportedly never losing a judo contest. In Spain he acquired the ring name "Conde Koma". Along the way he adapted his techniques to the no-holds-barred format of prize fighting, emphasising groundwork (newaza) in ways that diverged from sport judo.

Brazil

Maeda settled in Belém, Brazil, around 1914–1915, where he taught judo. Among his students were Carlos Gracie and Luiz França. Carlos Gracie and his brothers, notably Hélio Gracie, developed the art into Brazilian jiu-jitsu, while França's lineage led through Oswaldo Fadda to another branch of the art. Maeda also helped establish Japanese immigrant communities in Brazil and was posthumously promoted to 7th dan.

Legacy

Maeda is considered the father of Brazilian jiu-jitsu and an early pioneer of mixed martial arts-style challenge fighting, bridging Kodokan judo and modern grappling arts.
