European Article Number

Image: EAN-13 barcode (placeholder)

The European Article Number (EAN) is a standard describing a barcode symbology and numbering system used in global trade to identify a specific retail product type, in a specific packaging configuration, from a specific manufacturer. The most common form, EAN-13, encodes thirteen digits and is now formally part of the GS1 system's Global Trade Item Number (GTIN).

Structure

An EAN-13 number consists of a GS1 country/company prefix, an item reference assigned by the manufacturer, and a final check digit computed by a modulo-10 algorithm. The closely related EAN-8 is used on small packages where a full-length barcode would not fit. Although developed in Europe in the 1970s as a superset of the American Universal Product Code (UPC), EAN barcodes are now used worldwide, and since 2005 the standards have been harmonised under GS1 so that EAN and UPC codes are mutually compatible.

Uses

EAN barcodes appear on nearly all consumer goods sold at retail. Books use the "Bookland" EAN derived from the ISBN, and periodicals use a variant based on the ISSN.
