PEST analysis

PEST analysis (sometimes expanded as PESTLE or other variants) is a framework used in strategic management and marketing to analyse the macro-environmental factors that can affect an organisation, market or project. The acronym stands for Political, Economic, Social and Technological factors. By systematically scanning these external influences — which the organisation cannot control but must respond to — planners can identify opportunities and threats to feed into wider strategic analysis, commonly alongside a SWOT analysis (where PEST findings typically supply the "opportunities" and "threats") and industry analysis such as Porter's five forces.

The four factors

FactorTypical considerations
PoliticalGovernment policy and stability, taxation, trade regulation and tariffs, labour and environmental law, political risk, corruption, industry regulation
EconomicGrowth rates, inflation, interest and exchange rates, unemployment, disposable income, commodity prices, access to finance
SocialDemographics, population growth and ageing, culture and lifestyle trends, consumer attitudes, education levels, health consciousness, social mobility
TechnologicalResearch and development, automation, innovation and disruptive technologies, technology transfer, infrastructure, intellectual property

Variants

The framework is frequently extended to capture additional factors. PESTLE (or PESTEL) adds Legal and Environmental factors; other versions include STEEPLE (adding Ethics), DESTEP (Demographic, Ecological) and PESTLIED (adding International and Demographic). The choice of variant depends on the industry: environmental and legal factors are critical in, for example, energy, pharmaceuticals and food production.

History

The framework traces to Francis J. Aguilar, a Harvard Business School professor, whose 1967 book Scanning the Business Environment surveyed "ETPS" factors — Economic, Technical, Political and Social. The mnemonic was later reordered and popularised as PEST in management textbooks and consulting practice.

Use and limitations

A PEST analysis is normally performed as a structured brainstorming and research exercise, listing current and anticipated developments under each heading and assessing their likely impact and time horizon. It is most valuable when entering a new market, launching a product, or reviewing strategy in a changing environment. Critics note that PEST by itself merely lists factors: without prioritisation, evidence and linkage to decisions it degenerates into a box-ticking exercise; the categories overlap (a tax change is both political and economic); and the pace of change can outrun a static analysis. Used properly, it complements internal analyses such as SWOT and value-chain analysis.
