Teclu burner

TypeLaboratory gas burner
InventorNicolae Teclu
Year1892
RelatedBunsen burner, Meker burner

The Teclu burner is a laboratory gas burner invented by the Romanian chemist Nicolae Teclu in 1892. It is an improvement on the Bunsen burner, offering finer control of the air supply and a hotter, better-defined flame.

Design

Like the Bunsen burner, the Teclu burner consists of a vertical metal tube mounted on a heavy base, with a gas inlet near the bottom connected to a fuel gas supply. Its distinguishing feature is a cone-shaped (disc) regulator at the base of the barrel: a threaded conical plug that can be raised or lowered to vary the size of the annular air opening. Turning the cone adjusts the air-to-gas ratio continuously, allowing the flame to be set anywhere from a cool, luminous yellow flame to a hot, nearly invisible blue flame.

The barrel is also typically wider at the base and slightly tapered, which improves the mixing of gas and air (the Venturi effect) compared with the straight tube and rotating collar of the Bunsen design.

Use

Teclu burners are used for heating, sterilization, and flame tests in chemistry laboratories. With natural gas the flame can reach temperatures around 1,500 °C, sufficient for bending glass tubing and for many inorganic analyses. The fine air adjustment makes the burner popular in laboratories where reproducible flame conditions matter.
