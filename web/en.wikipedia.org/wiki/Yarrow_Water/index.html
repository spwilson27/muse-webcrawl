Yarrow Water

The Yarrow Water is a river in the Scottish Borders region of Scotland. It rises in the Moffat Hills near the Yarrow reservoir area and flows generally north-eastward through the Yarrow Valley, past the village of Yarrow, to join the Ettrick Water near the ancient tower of Newark Castle, on its way to the River Tweed at Selkirk.

Course

The river's principal tributaries include streams descending from the hills around St Mary's Loch, the largest natural loch in the Borders, which lies close to the head of the valley; the Yarrow flows from the eastern end of St Mary's Loch. The valley is traversed by the A708 road between Selkirk and Moffat.

Literary associations

The Yarrow valley is celebrated in Scots ballad and poetry. William Wordsworth wrote several poems on Yarrow ("Yarrow Unvisited", "Yarrow Visited" and "Yarrow Revisited"), and the river features in the border ballad "The Dowie Dens of Yarrow". Sir Walter Scott, who lived at nearby Abbotsford and served as Sheriff of Selkirkshire, drew on the valley's history and landscape in his writings. The area was also home to James Hogg, the "Ettrick Shepherd".
