Poomsae

Poomsae (also spelled pumsae or poomse; Korean: 품새) is a defined pattern of defence-and-attack motions in taekwondo, comparable to kata in karate. Practitioners perform a set sequence of blocks, strikes, kicks, and stances against imaginary opponents, and poomsae performance is both a requirement for belt promotion and a competitive discipline in its own right, governed internationally by World Taekwondo (WT) and the Kukkiwon.

The patterns

The standard Kukkiwon curriculum comprises eight colour-belt forms, the Taegeuk poomsae (Taegeuk Il Jang through Pal Jang), which replaced the older Palgwae forms, and a series of black-belt (dan) forms:

  • Koryo — named for the Koryo (Goryeo) dynasty (1st dan);
  • Keumgang — "diamond," symbolising hardness and the Keumgang Mountains (2nd dan);
  • Taebaek — named for the mountain associated with the mythical founder Dangun (3rd dan);
  • Pyongwon — "plain," and Sipjin — "ten," symbolising longevity (4th–5th dan);
  • Jitae — "earth," Cheonkwon — "sky," Hansu — "water," and Ilyeo — "oneness" (higher dan grades).

Each form embodies a concept from Korean philosophy or history and is drawn from the trigram symbolism of the I Ching, also visible in the Taegeukgi, the South Korean flag.

Competition

Recognised poomsae is a medal event at the World Taekwondo Poomsae Championships (held since 2006), the Asian Games (2018), and multi-sport events, and freestyle poomsae — choreographed routines with music, acrobatics, and breaking techniques — has been added as a separate category. Athletes are scored on accuracy of technique and on presentation, including speed, power, rhythm, and expression of energy. Poomsae was demonstrated in the context of taekwondo's Olympic programme but, unlike sparring (kyorugi), has not been contested at the Olympic Games.

History

Modern poomsae were systematised in the 1970s by the Korea Taekwondo Association and the Kukkiwon as part of the unification of the Korean kwans. Earlier Korean forms derived heavily from karate kata; the Taegeuk and yudanja series were created to give taekwondo a distinctive Korean forms syllabus. The ITF (International Taekwon-do Federation) uses a different set of patterns, the tul, devised by Choi Hong-hi.
