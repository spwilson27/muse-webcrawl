Mamak stall

Mamak stalls are indoor and open-air food establishments particularly found in Southeast Asia, especially in Malaysia and Singapore, that serve a type of Indian Muslim cuisine unique to the region. The word mamak comes from the Tamil term for maternal uncle; in Malaysia and Singapore it is traditionally used as an honorific for Tamil Muslims, who operate many of these stalls and restaurants.

Characteristics

Mamak stalls are often open 24 hours a day, offering an inexpensive, informal gathering place where people of all communities socialize, eat, and watch televised football. Many are roadside stalls with simple tables and plastic chairs, though some have grown into large restaurant chains such as Murni and Pelita in Malaysia. A hallmark is the self-service buffet spread of rice and curries (nasi kandar), and the theatrical pulling of tea — teh tarik — poured back and forth between vessels to cool and froth it, alongside roti canai flatbread flipped and stretched on a hot griddle.

Typical dishes

  • Roti canai (and variants: roti telur, roti tissue, murtabak)
  • Nasi kandar and nasi lemak
  • Mee goreng mamak (spicy fried noodles)
  • Pasembur (Indian rojak)
  • Teh tarik and other pulled drinks
  • Tandoori chicken and naan

Cultural significance

Cheap, halal, and always open, mamak stalls are a fixture of Malaysian social life, serving students, night-shift workers, and football fans; the phrase "lepak at the mamak" captures their role as a third place in Malaysian cities.
