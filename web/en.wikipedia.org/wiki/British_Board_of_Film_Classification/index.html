The British Board of Film Classification (BBFC) is a non-governmental organisation, founded by the film industry in 1912, responsible for the national classification and censorship of films exhibited in cinemas and of video works released on physical media (and, by agreement, some streaming content) in the United Kingdom. It was formerly the British Board of Film Censors.

Classification categories

The current cinema and video categories are:

  • U – Universal, suitable for all (ages 4+).
  • PG – Parental Guidance; some scenes may be unsuitable for young children.
  • 12A (cinema) / 12 (video) – suitable only for ages 12 and over; under-12s may attend 12A films with an adult.
  • 15 – suitable only for ages 15 and over.
  • 18 – adults only.
  • R18 – restricted to licensed premises/adult outlets, used for explicit sexual works.

Each classification is accompanied by consumer advice ("insight") describing content such as violence, language or sexual references, based on published BBFC guidelines that are revised following large-scale public consultations.

History and role

Founded in 1912, the Board began classifying films in 1913 with U and A categories; the X certificate (later 18) arrived in 1951. Although the BBFC's cinema decisions can technically be overridden by local licensing authorities, in practice its certificates are adopted nationwide. Under the Video Recordings Act 1984 the BBFC became the statutory authority for video classification, and it has also classified video games in the past (now handled mainly by PEGI). The Board funds itself through fees charged per minute of content submitted.
