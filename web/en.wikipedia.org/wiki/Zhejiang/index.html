Zhejiang is an eastern coastal province of the People's Republic of China. Its capital and largest city is Hangzhou, and other notable cities include Ningbo and Wenzhou. Zhejiang is bordered by Jiangsu and Shanghai to the north, Anhui to the northwest, Jiangxi to the west, and Fujian to the south; to the east lies the East China Sea. The province's name derives from the Zhe River, the former name of the Qiantang River that flows through Hangzhou.

One of China's smallest provinces by area, Zhejiang is mountainous in its south and west, with plains and a deeply indented coastline in the north and east, including thousands of offshore islands such as the Zhoushan archipelago. The Grand Canal terminates at Hangzhou, and the province is crossed by the Qiantang River, famous for the world's largest tidal bore.

History

The region was home to the Neolithic Hemudu and Liangzhu cultures; the Liangzhu archaeological ruins near Hangzhou are a UNESCO World Heritage Site recognised for evidence of early rice cultivation and jade working. During the Spring and Autumn period the area was contested by the states of Wu and Yue; the story of King Goujian of Yue is a famous episode of the era. Hangzhou became the capital of the Southern Song dynasty (as Lin'an) in the 12th century and was described by Marco Polo as one of the world's greatest cities. Ningbo was one of the treaty ports opened after the First Opium War.

Economy

Zhejiang is one of China's most prosperous provinces, known for private enterprise, light manufacturing, and trade. Ningbo-Zhoushan is among the world's busiest ports by cargo tonnage. The province is famous for Longjing (Dragon Well) tea grown around West Lake, for silk production, and in recent decades for e-commerce and technology: Alibaba Group is headquartered in Hangzhou. Wenzhou became a byword for entrepreneurial private business, and Yiwu hosts the world's largest small-commodities wholesale market.

Culture and sights

West Lake in Hangzhou, celebrated in Chinese poetry and painting, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and the province's best-known landscape. Other landmarks include the Buddhist island of Putuoshan, the water towns of Wuzhen and Xitang, and the ancient city of Shaoxing, home of the writer Lu Xun. The population speaks mainly Wu Chinese varieties, and Zhejiang cuisine (Zhe cuisine) is counted among the great traditions of Chinese cooking, known for fresh, delicate flavours.
