David John Teece (born September 2, 1948) is a New Zealand-born American organizational economist and the Thomas W. Tusher Professor Emeritus of Global Business at the Haas School of Business of the University of California, Berkeley. He is the originator of the dynamic capabilities framework in strategic management, and of influential work on how firms profit from technological innovation. Teece is among the most highly cited scholars in business and management.

Education and career

Teece was raised in Blenheim, New Zealand, and earned bachelor's and master's degrees in economics from the University of Canterbury before completing his PhD at the Wharton School of the University of Pennsylvania, specialising in industrial economics. He taught at Stanford Graduate School of Business from 1975 to 1982, then joined UC Berkeley, where he directed the Haas School's Institute for Business Innovation and founded its Tusher Center for the Management of Intellectual Capital. He also co-founded the consulting firm Berkeley Research Group.

Research

Profiting from innovation

In his 1986 paper "Profiting from Technological Innovation", Teece argued that whether an innovating firm or imitators capture the economic value of an innovation depends on the appropriability regime (the nature and strength of intellectual property protection), the emergence of a dominant design, and ownership of complementary assets such as manufacturing, distribution, and service capabilities. The framework explained why firms such as EMI (with the CT scanner) failed to profit from their own inventions while others captured the value.

Dynamic capabilities

With Gary Pisano and Amy Shuen (1997), Teece developed the concept of dynamic capabilities: the firm's ability to integrate, build, and reconfigure internal and external competences to address rapidly changing environments. In later work he decomposed these capabilities into sensing opportunities and threats, seizing opportunities through investment and business model design, and transforming the organization as conditions evolve. The framework has become one of the most influential in strategic management research.

Other work

Teece has also written extensively on the theory of the multiproduct firm, technology transfer, antitrust and competition policy, intellectual property economics, and the management of innovation ecosystems. He was appointed a Companion of the New Zealand Order of Merit and has received multiple honorary doctorates for his contributions to management scholarship.
