Rufous-necked wryneck

Scientific nameJynx ruficollis
FamilyPicidae (woodpeckers)
RangeSub-Saharan Africa
HabitatSavanna and open woodland
ConservationLeast Concern (IUCN)

The rufous-necked wryneck (Jynx ruficollis), also known as the red-throated wryneck, is a species of bird in the woodpecker family Picidae. It is found across sub-Saharan Africa and is the counterpart of the Eurasian wryneck, from which it differs in its rufous throat and upper breast.

Description

Like other wrynecks, it has intricately mottled grey-brown plumage providing excellent camouflage against bark, and a remarkable ability to twist its neck — a snake-like display used to deter predators at the nest. It feeds mainly on ants, flicking out its long sticky tongue.

Habitat and breeding

The species inhabits savanna and open woodland, nesting in holes in trees, including old barbet and woodpecker holes, and sometimes in nest boxes. Several subspecies are recognised across its range, from Ethiopia and East Africa to Angola and South Africa. Its call is a repeated, penetrating series of notes, quite unlike its drumming woodpecker relatives — wrynecks do not drum.
