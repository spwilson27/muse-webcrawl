Brasidas (Greek: Βρασίδας, died 422 BC) was a Spartan officer during the first decade of the Peloponnesian War. Energetic, eloquent and unusually willing to champion diplomacy alongside force, he was judged by Thucydides the ablest Spartan commander of the war's early years. His brilliant campaign in Thrace seized Athens' key colony of Amphipolis, and his death there — in the same battle that killed his Athenian opponent Cleon — cleared the way for the Peace of Nicias.

Early career

Brasidas first distinguished himself in 431 BC by relieving Methone from an Athenian attack with a dash through the enemy lines, the first Spartan to be publicly praised that war. As eponymous ephor in 431/0 and later as an adviser in the campaigning around Pylos (425 BC), where he was wounded storming the Athenian position, he gained a reputation for daring that set him apart from the cautious Spartan norm.

Thrace campaign

In 424 BC Brasidas marched a small force — 700 helot hoplites and 1,000 Peloponnesian mercenaries — through hostile Thessaly to Macedonia and the Chalcidice. Combining speed with conciliatory speeches promising liberation, he won over Acanthus, Stagira and, crucially, Amphipolis, the Athenian colony guarding the Strymon crossings and the timber and gold of Mount Pangaeum. The historian Thucydides, then an Athenian general in the area, arrived too late to save Amphipolis and was exiled for the failure.

Death and honours

In 422 BC the Athenian demagogue Cleon led an expedition to recover the city. In the Battle of Amphipolis, Brasidas' sally from the gates routed the Athenians and Cleon was killed in the pursuit; Brasidas himself was carried from the field mortally wounded. The Amphipolitans buried him with heroic honours in their agora, adopted him as founder in place of the Athenian Hagnon, and celebrated games and sacrifices to him annually.
