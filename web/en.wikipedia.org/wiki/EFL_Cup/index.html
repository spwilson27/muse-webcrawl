EFL Cup

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The EFL Cup trophy.

The EFL Cup (historically and often simply the League Cup) is an annual knockout football competition in men's English football, organised by the English Football League (EFL). It is open to all 92 clubs in the Premier League and the EFL, making it one of the two major domestic cup competitions in England alongside the FA Cup. Since 2017 it has been sponsored by the energy drink Carabao and is known as the Carabao Cup.

History

The competition was founded in 1960 by Football League secretary Alan Hardaker, in part to give clubs a new source of revenue as floodlights made midweek evening football practical, and in part to strengthen the League's position in negotiations over the restructuring of English football. The first final, in 1961, was won by Aston Villa over two legs. Early finals were played home-and-away; from 1967 the final became a single match at Wembley Stadium. Liverpool are the most successful club in the competition's history, with ten titles.

Format

The cup is played over seven rounds in a straight knockout format. Clubs in European competition receive byes to the third round. Semi-finals are played over two legs; all other rounds, including the final — traditionally held at Wembley in late February or March — are single matches. For much of its history drawn matches went to replays, but ties are now settled on the night, by penalty shoot-out if necessary. The winners qualify for the UEFA Conference League play-off round, unless they have already qualified for Europe by league position.

Status

Although sometimes ranked below the FA Cup and league in prestige — and used by larger clubs to field young and fringe players — the League Cup provides smaller clubs with lucrative ties against top-flight opposition and a realistic route to a major trophy and European football. Its future has been debated amid fixture congestion, particularly since the expansion of European club competitions.
