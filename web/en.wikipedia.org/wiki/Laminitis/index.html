Laminitis

Laminitis is a disease that affects the feet of ungulates and is found mostly in horses, ponies, and cattle. It is characterized by damage to the laminae, the sensitive tissue that binds the hoof wall to the pedal bone (coffin bone) inside the hoof. In severe cases the pedal bone can rotate or sink within the hoof capsule, a condition commonly called founder.

Causes

Laminitis can be triggered by excessive intake of soluble carbohydrates (grain overload or lush spring pasture), endotoxemia following colic or colitis, severe systemic illness, retained placenta, and excessive concussive stress on hard surfaces ("road founder"). Obesity and metabolic disorders such as equine metabolic syndrome and pituitary pars intermedia dysfunction are major risk factors.

Signs and treatment

Affected horses show reluctance to move, a characteristic rocking-back stance to shift weight off the front feet, heat in the hooves, and a bounding digital pulse. Treatment focuses on removing the cause, cryotherapy (icing the feet) in the acute phase, anti-inflammatory medication, and therapeutic shoeing to support the pedal bone.
