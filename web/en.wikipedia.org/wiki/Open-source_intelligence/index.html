Open-source intelligence

Open-source intelligence (OSINT) is the collection and analysis of data gathered from open sources — publicly available information — to produce actionable intelligence. Sources include news media, public government data, commercial data, academic publications, social media, satellite imagery and the wider internet.

Practice

OSINT is one of the classic intelligence disciplines alongside HUMINT, SIGINT and IMINT. The growth of social media and commercial satellite imagery has transformed it: investigative collectives such as Bellingcat used geolocation, shadow analysis and image verification to investigate events including the downing of flight MH17 and conflicts in Syria and Ukraine. Intelligence agencies and journalists alike now treat open sources as a first resort rather than a supplement.

Tools and ethics

Practitioners use search operators, metadata analysis, reverse image search and archiving tools. Ethical and legal questions arise around privacy, doxxing and the safety of sources, and professional frameworks stress verification and minimising harm.
