Neidan, or internal alchemy (Chinese: 內丹術, nèidānshù), is an array of esoteric doctrines and physical, mental, and spiritual practices that Taoist initiates use to prolong life and create an immortal spiritual body that would survive after death. Also known as Jindan ("golden elixir"), inner alchemy combines theories derived from external alchemy (waidan), correlative cosmology (including the Five Phases), the emblems of the I Ching, and medical theory, with techniques of Taoist meditation, daoyin gymnastics, and sexual hygiene.

Practice

In neidan, the human body becomes a cauldron (ding) in which the Three Treasures — jing (essence), qi (vital energy) and shen (spirit) — are cultivated for the purpose of improving physical, emotional and mental health, and ultimately returning to the primordial unity of the Tao, becoming an immortal. Stages are often described as refining jing into qi, qi into shen, and shen into emptiness. The process is mapped symbolically using the trigrams of the I Ching, yin-yang, and the "inner landscape" of the body depicted in charts such as the Neijing Tu.

History

Neidan developed from the Tang dynasty onward and flourished in the Song, with lineages such as Zhong-Lü and the Southern School founded by Zhang Boduan, whose Wuzhen pian (1075) is a classic of the tradition. It became central to organized Taoism, including the Quanzhen School, and remains influential in qigong and Chinese medicine.
