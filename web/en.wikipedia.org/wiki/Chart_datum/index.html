Chart datum

Diagram of tidal datums, including chart datum and mean high water.

Chart datum is the level of water from which depths shown on a nautical chart are measured. Depths on the chart (soundings) and the drying heights of features that uncover at low tide are given relative to this plane, and the predicted heights of tide published in tide tables are measured above the same datum, so that a mariner can add the height of tide to a charted depth to obtain the actual depth of water at any time.

Definitions

Chart datum
International standard: Lowest Astronomical Tide (LAT)
Used for: Soundings, drying heights, tide predictions
Related levels: MSL, MHW, MHWS, HAT

Modern practice recommended by the International Hydrographic Organization is to adopt approximately the Lowest Astronomical Tide (LAT) — the lowest tide level that can be predicted under average meteorological conditions and any combination of astronomical conditions — as chart datum. Because LAT is so low, the actual depth of water will rarely be less than the charted depth, giving mariners a conservative safety margin. Earlier datums, still found on older charts, include mean low water springs and the Indian spring low water mark.

Variations by country

Countries adopt datums suited to their waters: the United States uses Mean Lower Low Water on its coasts; the United Kingdom and much of Europe use LAT; land-locked or non-tidal waters may use a low-water level of a river or lake, or mean sea level. Heights of bridges, lights and landmarks above water, by contrast, are usually measured from a high-water datum such as mean high water springs, so that clearances are also conservative.

Relation to land surveying

Chart datum is a tidal level and generally differs from the vertical datums used on land, such as those based on mean sea level at a reference tide gauge (for example Ordnance Datum Newlyn in Great Britain). The offset between chart datum and the national levelling datum at each port is published in tide tables and is important for coastal engineering and for combining hydrographic and topographic data.
