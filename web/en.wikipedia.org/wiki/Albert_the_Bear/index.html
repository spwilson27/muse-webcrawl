Albert the Bear

Albert I (German: Albrecht I; c. 1100 – 18 November 1170), commonly known as Albert the Bear (German: Albrecht der Bär), was a Saxon nobleman of the House of Ascania who became the first Margrave of Brandenburg. A leading figure in the German eastward expansion (Ostsiedlung), he inherited the Saxon Northern March in 1134, recovered Brandenburg from Slavic control by inheritance treaty and conquest, and in 1157 formally took the title Margrave of Brandenburg — a date traditionally regarded as the founding of the Margraviate that later grew into Prussia and, ultimately, Germany.

Life

Albert was the son of Otto the Rich, Count of Ballenstedt. Granted the Northern March by Emperor Lothair III in 1134, he secured Brandenburg through a succession arrangement with the Christian Slav prince Pribislav-Henry of the Hevelli, whose lands he inherited on Pribislav's death in 1150; a Slav uprising under Jaxa of Köpenick was defeated in 1157, and Albert styled himself Margrave of Brandenburg from that year. He encouraged settlement by German, Flemish and Dutch colonists in the reclaimed territories, founded towns and monasteries, and also ruled as Duke of Saxony from 1138 to 1142, when he lost the duchy to Henry the Lion, his lifelong rival — though in 1158 he famously fell out with Henry over their competing expansion. His descendants, the Ascanians, ruled Brandenburg until 1320. Berlin's coat of arms and the city's founding mythology are closely associated with his name.
