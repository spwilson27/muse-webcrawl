Data recovery

In computing, data recovery is a process of retrieving inaccessible, lost, corrupted, damaged or formatted data from secondary storage, removable media or files, when the data stored in them cannot be accessed in a standard way. Recovery may be needed because of physical damage to the storage device, logical damage to the filesystem, accidental deletion, or file corruption.

Scenarios

The most common scenario involves an operating system failure, malfunctioning storage device, or accidental deletion, where the data itself remains intact on the medium until overwritten. Physical damage — head crashes, motor failure, scratched optical surfaces — may require specialist work in a cleanroom, replacing components such as read/write heads or controller boards. Logical damage is addressed with software that rebuilds filesystem structures or carves files from raw disk images by recognising file signatures.

Techniques

Recovery specialists first image the failing device sector-by-sector using hardware write-blockers to avoid further damage, then work on the image. Remote data recovery over a network is possible for logical failures. Data may also be recoverable from backup media, disk arrays, or residual traces such as journal files and unallocated space, which is why overwriting is emphasised in secure deletion and computer forensics.
