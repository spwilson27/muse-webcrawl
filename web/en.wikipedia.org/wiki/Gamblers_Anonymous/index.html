Gamblers Anonymous

Gamblers Anonymous (GA) is an international fellowship of people who have a compulsive gambling problem. It is a twelve-step program modelled on Alcoholics Anonymous, in which members meet regularly to help one another recover from gambling addiction through mutual support and adherence to the Twelve Steps and Twelve Traditions.

History

Gamblers Anonymous was founded in Los Angeles, California, in 1957 by Jim W., a recovering alcoholic who applied the AA program to gambling. The first group meeting was held on 13 September 1957. The fellowship grew slowly at first, boosted by favourable publicity in the early 1960s, and today holds meetings in many countries around the world.

Program

The only requirement for membership is a desire to stop gambling. GA defines gambling as any betting or wagering on an uncertain outcome. Members are encouraged to abstain completely, work the Twelve Steps, attend meetings regularly, and use the telephone list to contact other members between meetings. A distinctive part of GA practice is the "pressure relief" meeting, in which a new member's financial situation is reviewed with experienced members and a realistic plan for paying debts is drawn up — without loans or bailouts from the group.

To help people judge whether they have a problem, GA publishes "20 Questions" (e.g., "Did gambling affect your reputation?", "Have you ever gambled to escape worry or trouble?"), noting that most compulsive gamblers will answer yes to at least seven.

Gam-Anon

Gam-Anon is the affiliated fellowship for family and friends of compulsive gamblers, analogous to Al-Anon for relatives of alcoholics. GA also maintains a hotline, literature, and the "combo book" containing its recovery and unity programs.
