Development bank

A development bank is a financial institution that provides risk capital for economic development projects on a non-commercial basis. Development banks are often government-owned or chartered and supply long-term finance for infrastructure, industry, agriculture, and small businesses where private capital is scarce or unwilling to bear the risk.

Types

Multilateral development banks, such as the World Bank, Asian Development Bank, African Development Bank, Inter-American Development Bank, and the newer Asian Infrastructure Investment Bank and New Development Bank, are owned by member governments and lend across countries. National development banks, such as Germany's KfW, Brazil's BNDES, the China Development Bank, and the Development Bank of Japan, operate within a single country.

Functions

Beyond lending, development banks provide technical assistance, project preparation, guarantees, and equity investment, and they aim to mobilize ("crowd in") private finance. Critics note risks of crowding out private lenders, political direction of credit, and support for environmentally harmful projects; supporters emphasize countercyclical lending and the financing of long-horizon public goods.
