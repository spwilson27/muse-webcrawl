SCORM

SCORM (Sharable Content Object Reference Model) is a collection of standards and specifications for web-based electronic educational technology (also called e-learning). It defines communications between client side content and a host system, commonly supported by a learning management system (LMS). SCORM also defines how content may be packaged into a transferable ZIP file called a "Package Interchange Format".

SCORM is a specification of the Advanced Distributed Learning (ADL) Initiative from the Office of the United States Secretary of Defense. SCORM 2004 introduced a complex idea called Sequencing, a set of rules that specify the order in which a learner may experience content objects. The standard uses XML, and it is based on the work of AICC, IMS Global, IEEE, and Ariadne.

History

The ADL Initiative was established in 1997 to standardize and modernize training and education management and delivery. SCORM 1.0 was released in January 2001, followed quickly by SCORM 1.1 and SCORM 1.2 (October 2001), the latter becoming the most widely adopted version in practice. SCORM 2004 appeared in January 2004, adding sequencing and navigation rules adapted from the IMS Simple Sequencing specification.

Components

The SCORM standard is divided into three main parts: the Content Aggregation Model (CAM), which describes how content is packaged; the Run-Time Environment (RTE), which describes the JavaScript API and data model used by content to communicate with the LMS; and Sequencing and Navigation (SN), added in SCORM 2004, which controls the order of presentation of content objects.

Compliant software

Many learning management systems, including Moodle, Sakai, and numerous commercial platforms, support SCORM packages. Content authoring tools such as Articulate Storyline, Adobe Captivate, and iSpring export SCORM-compliant courses.

Successor efforts

The Experience API (xAPI, also known as Tin Can API), released in 2013, was developed as a more flexible successor capable of tracking learning experiences outside an LMS, including offline and mobile learning.
