Vietnamese (tiếng Việt) is an Austroasiatic language spoken mainly in Vietnam, where it is the national and official language, and by large diaspora communities in the United States, France, Australia, Canada, and elsewhere. It is the most widely spoken Austroasiatic language, with over 95 million speakers. Vietnamese is an analytic, tonal language whose modern written form uses a Latin-based alphabet rich in diacritics.

Classification and history

Vietnamese belongs to the Vietic branch of the Austroasiatic family, most closely related to Mường. Over a millennium of Chinese rule and cultural influence, it absorbed enormous Sino-Vietnamese vocabulary — by some estimates 30–60% of the lexicon, especially in formal registers — while retaining a Vietic grammatical core. Older written Vietnamese used Chinese characters (chữ Hán) and a demotic script of adapted characters (chữ Nôm). The modern alphabet, chữ Quốc ngữ, was developed by Portuguese and French missionaries in the 17th century — notably Alexandre de Rhodes, whose 1651 dictionary codified it — and was promoted under French colonial rule and after independence, achieving near-universal use by the mid-20th century.

Phonology

Vietnamese syllables consist of an initial consonant, a vowel or diphthong (with possible glide), an optional final consonant or glide, and a tone. The northern (Hanoi) standard distinguishes six tones:

Southern dialects merge the hỏi and ngã tones. Regional dialects also differ in consonant mergers and vowel systems; the three broad dialect regions are northern, central, and southern.

Grammar

Vietnamese is an isolating (analytic) language: words do not inflect, and grammatical relations are shown by word order (subject–verb–object) and particles. Classifiers accompany nouns when counted or specified (e.g., cái, con, quyển for books). Aspect and modality are marked by particles such as đã (past/perfective), đang (progressive), and sẽ (future). Pronouns and address terms are famously elaborate, encoding kinship-style relations of age and status between speakers.

Vocabulary

Beyond Sino-Vietnamese layers and native Vietic vocabulary, Vietnamese has borrowed from French (e.g., ga "station", from gare; phô mai "cheese", from fromage) and, more recently, from English, especially in technology and popular culture.
