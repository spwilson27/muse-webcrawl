Guayana Esequiba

Guayana Esequiba, also known as the Essequibo region, is a territory of about 159,500 square kilometres west of the Essequibo River in South America. It is administered by Guyana but claimed by Venezuela, which considers it part of its national territory — a dispute that makes up one of the longest-running territorial controversies in the Americas.

The dispute has its roots in colonial-era boundaries between Spanish and Dutch/British possessions and in the 1899 Paris Arbitral Award, which assigned the region to British Guiana. Venezuela later repudiated that award as unfair, and the 1966 Geneva Agreement committed both sides to a peaceful settlement. The case has been before the International Court of Justice, with Guyana asking the court to confirm the 1899 boundary.

Interest in the region intensified after major offshore oil discoveries in Guyanese waters from 2015 onward, and Venezuela held a consultative referendum on the claim in December 2023, raising regional tensions.
