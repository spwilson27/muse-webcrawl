Loot box

Loot box
TypeVideo game monetisation mechanic
MechanismVirtual container yielding randomised in-game items
PredecessorsGacha games, in-game card packs
RegulationBanned or restricted as gambling in some jurisdictions (e.g. Belgium)

In video games, a loot box is a consumable virtual item which can be redeemed to receive a randomised selection of further virtual items, ranging from simple cosmetic customisation options to powerful equipment. Loot boxes are a form of in-game monetisation: players may buy them with real money or earn them through play, and they became widespread in the 2010s in titles such as Overwatch, FIFA Ultimate Team, Counter-Strike, and Star Wars Battlefront II.

History

The model descends from Japanese gacha capsule-toy games and from randomised card packs in digital card games. Western adoption accelerated with free-to-play and live-service games after about 2010.

Controversy and regulation

Loot boxes have been criticised for structural similarities to gambling, particularly where items have real-money value, and for their appeal to minors. Belgium's Gaming Commission ruled in 2018 that certain loot boxes constituted illegal gambling, leading publishers to remove them there; other countries, including China, require disclosure of item drop probabilities, and several legislatures have debated or enacted restrictions. The controversy peaked in 2017 around Star Wars Battlefront II, whose planned loot-box system was widely protested and revised before launch.
