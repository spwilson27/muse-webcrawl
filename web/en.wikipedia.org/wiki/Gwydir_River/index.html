The Gwydir River, a major perennial river of the Barwon catchment within the Murray–Darling basin, is located in the Northern Tablelands, North West Slopes, and Orana districts of New South Wales, Australia. The river rises near Uralla on the Northern Tablelands and flows generally west and northwest for about 488 kilometres, through the towns of Bingara, Gravesend, and Moree, before joining the Barwon River near Collarenebri.

Course and features

From its source at an elevation of around 1,000 metres, the Gwydir descends through rugged gorge country east of Bingara, where it is impounded by Copeton Dam, a major irrigation and water supply storage completed in 1976. Below the dam the river emerges onto the plains, where its flow is heavily regulated for irrigation — the Gwydir valley is one of Australia's most important cotton-growing regions, centred on Moree.

Downstream of Moree the river divides into multiple channels, including the Mehi River and the Lower Gwydir Watercourse, which feed the Gwydir Wetlands near the river's end. These wetlands, including the Gingham and Lower Gwydir watercourses, are listed as nationally important and support large colonial waterbird breeding events in wet years.

History and name

The river takes its name from a local Aboriginal word. The explorer Allan Cunningham crossed and named the river in 1827. The valley is the traditional land of the Kamilaroi (Gamilaraay) people, and the colonial-era Myall Creek massacre of 1838 occurred in the Gwydir catchment near Bingara.

Environmental issues

Water extraction for irrigation has dramatically reduced flows to the terminal wetlands, an issue at the centre of continuing debates over water management in the Murray–Darling Basin. Environmental water allocations under the Basin Plan aim to restore some flows to the Gwydir Wetlands.
