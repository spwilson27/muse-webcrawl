Prior authorization

Prior authorization (also called preauthorization or precertification) is a process used by health insurance companies in the United States in which a physician or other healthcare provider must obtain approval from the patient's insurer before a specific medication, procedure, service or item of durable medical equipment is provided, in order for the insurer to pay for it.

Process

The provider submits clinical information — diagnosis, medical history, prior treatments ("step therapy") — to the insurer or its pharmacy benefit manager. The request is reviewed, sometimes by a clinician in the relevant specialty and sometimes through automated criteria, and is approved, denied, or approved in modified form. Denials may be appealed, including through "peer-to-peer" reviews between the treating physician and the insurer's medical reviewer.

Criticism and reform

Physician surveys by the American Medical Association have repeatedly found that prior authorization delays care and adds substantial administrative burden to practices; patients sometimes abandon treatment after denials or delays. Insurers argue the process controls costs and prevents inappropriate care. In recent years, US regulators have adopted rules requiring faster decisions, electronic automation of the process, and public reporting of denial rates, and some large insurers have announced voluntary reductions in the number of services subject to prior authorization.
