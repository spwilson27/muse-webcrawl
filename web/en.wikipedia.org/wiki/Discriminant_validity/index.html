Discriminant validity

In psychology and the social sciences, discriminant validity tests whether concepts or measurements that are not supposed to be related are actually unrelated. It is a component of construct validity, introduced by Campbell and Fiske in 1959 through the multitrait-multimethod matrix.

Concept

A measure demonstrates discriminant validity when it correlates only weakly with measures of theoretically distinct constructs, and more strongly with measures of the same construct (convergent validity). For example, a test of numerical ability should not strongly measure verbal knowledge if the two are regarded as different abilities.

Assessment

Common criteria for evaluating discriminant validity include:

  • The Fornell–Larcker criterion — the square root of the average variance extracted (AVE) for each construct should exceed its correlations with other constructs.
  • The heterotrait–monotrait ratio (HTMT) of correlations, which should fall below thresholds such as 0.85 or 0.90.
  • Examining cross-loadings in factor analysis.
