Henry Purcell (10 September 1659 – 21 November 1695) was an English composer of the Baroque era. Considered one of the greatest English composers of all time, he blended Italian and French stylistic elements with a native English tradition, producing a body of work of more than 100 individual pieces — operas, semi-operas, anthems, odes, songs, and instrumental music — in a career of barely two decades.

Life

Purcell was born in Westminster into a family of court musicians; his father and uncle both sang in the Chapel Royal of Charles II. As a boy he was a chorister of the Chapel Royal under Captain Henry Cooke and was taught by Pelham Humfrey and John Blow. At twenty he succeeded Blow as organist of Westminster Abbey, and he later became organist of the Chapel Royal as well, holding royal appointments under Charles II, James II, and William and Mary.

Works

Purcell's only true opera, Dido and Aeneas (c. 1688, libretto by Nahum Tate), is a miniature masterpiece built around Dido's ground-bass lament "When I am laid in earth." For the public theatre he wrote "semi-operas" — plays with elaborate musical masques — including Dioclesian (1690), King Arthur (1691, with John Dryden) and The Fairy-Queen (1692), adapted from Shakespeare's A Midsummer Night's Dream.

For the court he composed a long series of welcome songs and birthday odes, among them Come, ye Sons of Art (1694) for Queen Mary. His church music includes the Funeral Sentences and the music for the funeral of Queen Mary (1695) — performed again at his own funeral months later. His instrumental legacy includes the trio sonatas, fantasias for viols, keyboard suites, and the famous Chaconne in G minor.

Death and legacy

Purcell died in November 1695, aged 36, and was buried in Westminster Abbey beside the organ. His widow Frances published a selection of his songs as Orpheus Britannicus (1698–1702), the title by which posterity knew him: the "British Orpheus." His music influenced composers from Handel to Britten, who based his The Young Person's Guide to the Orchestra on a theme by Purcell.
