Samuel Pepys
Born: 23 February 1633, London, England
Died: 26 May 1703 (aged 70), Clapham, Surrey
Occupation: Naval administrator, Member of Parliament
Known for: Diary of Samuel Pepys

Samuel Pepys (23 February 1633 – 26 May 1703) was an English diarist and naval administrator. He served as administrator of the Royal Navy and Member of Parliament and is most famous for the diary he kept for a decade while still a young man. Pepys recorded his daily life in remarkable detail for ten years, from 1660 to 1669, and the diary — first published in the 19th century — became one of the most important primary sources of the English Restoration period, providing an eyewitness account of the Second Anglo-Dutch War, the Great Plague of London, and the Great Fire of London.

Early life and career

Pepys was born in Salisbury Court, Fleet Street, London, the fifth of eleven children of a tailor. He was educated at St Paul's School and Magdalene College, Cambridge. Through the patronage of his cousin Edward Montagu, Earl of Sandwich, he entered the naval administration, accompanying the fleet that brought Charles II back from exile in 1660. He rose to become Chief Secretary to the Admiralty under both Charles II and James II, professionalised the navy's administration and officer corps, and served as President of the Royal Society from 1684 to 1686. He was briefly imprisoned in the Tower of London in 1679 on suspicion of treason, and again after the Glorious Revolution.

The diary

Pepys began his diary on 1 January 1660 and wrote almost daily for nine and a half years, in shorthand, until failing eyesight led him to stop on 31 May 1669. He recorded his work, meals, music, amours, and jealousies with equal candour, alongside great public events: he watched the coronation of Charles II, lived through the plague year of 1665, and described the Great Fire of 1666, famously burying his wine and Parmesan cheese in the garden to save them. The diary runs to well over a million words and was written in Thomas Shelton's shorthand system; it was deciphered and first published in abridged form in 1825. The complete text appeared in the definitive Latham and Matthews edition of 1970–83.

Legacy

Pepys bequeathed his library of some 3,000 volumes to Magdalene College, Cambridge, where it is still kept in its original presses, the diary among them. The Pepys Club and a large literature attest to his enduring popularity; Robert Louis Stevenson called him a "genius of the commonplace" perfected.
