Homonationalism

Homonationalism is a term coined by the scholar Jasbir K. Puar in her 2007 book Terrorist Assemblages: Homonationalism in Queer Times to describe the alignment of some LGBT people and gay-rights discourse with nationalist and state ideologies, particularly in the United States after the September 11 attacks. Puar argues that the partial inclusion of (some) homosexual subjects into the nation — through military service, marriage and consumer citizenship — has been accompanied by the portrayal of Muslim and other racialised populations as exceptionally homophobic and sexually repressive, thereby legitimating Islamophobia, xenophobic immigration politics and the "war on terror".

Concept

Drawing on biopolitics and intersectionality, Puar contrasts homonationalism with earlier queer critiques of the nation-state: rather than sexuality simply being repressed by nationalism, forms of homosexuality deemed respectable and patriotic can be folded into it, while other queer and racialised bodies are marked for surveillance or death. Related concepts include "pinkwashing", the promotion of a state or corporation's LGBT-friendly image to deflect criticism of other policies, and "homonormativity" (Lisa Duggan), the assimilation of gay politics into neoliberal norms of domesticity and consumption.

Applications and criticism

The framework has been widely applied in queer studies, migration studies and political commentary — for example to debates in Europe over immigration and Islam, where support for gay rights is invoked by some right-wing parties, and to criticism of Israel's self-presentation as gay-friendly. Critics have questioned the concept's breadth, its focus on the United States, and whether it undervalues the material gains of LGBT legal equality; Puar has defended it as an analytic of how inclusion and exclusion operate together rather than a judgement on individual LGBT people.
