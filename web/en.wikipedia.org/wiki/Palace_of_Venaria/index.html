The Palace of Venaria (Italian: Reggia di Venaria Reale) is a former royal residence and gardens located in Venaria Reale, near Turin in the Piedmont region of northern Italy. It is the largest of the Residences of the Royal House of Savoy and one of the largest royal palaces in the world, comparable in scale to Versailles and Caserta. It was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1997.

History

Duke Charles Emmanuel II commissioned the palace in 1658 as a base for hunting in the heaths north of Turin; the architect Amedeo di Castellamonte designed a palace joined to a planned town and a circular temple. The complex was repeatedly enlarged: Michelangelo Garove built the long gallery wing, Filippo Juvarra added the Great Gallery (Galleria Grande), the Chapel of St. Hubert and the Juvarra stables in the 1710s–1720s, and Benedetto Alfieri continued work later in the century.

After the Napoleonic era the palace became an army barracks and training ground; the gardens were destroyed for a parade ground, and the buildings decayed through the twentieth century. A vast restoration project, funded by the European Union, the Italian state and the Piedmont region, began in 1999; the palace and its reconstructed gardens reopened to the public in October 2007.

The palace today

The palace now hosts exhibitions, concerts and displays on Savoy court life, and its 80 hectares of gardens, restored with contemporary installations, draw large numbers of visitors. The Galleria Grande, with its chequerboard floor and stucco decoration, is regarded as a masterpiece of Italian Baroque architecture.
