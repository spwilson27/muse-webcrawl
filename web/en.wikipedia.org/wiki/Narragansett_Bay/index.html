Narragansett Bay is a bay and estuary on the north side of Rhode Island Sound, covering about 380 km² (147 sq mi), of which 312 km² (120 sq mi) lie in Rhode Island. It is New England's largest estuary and forms much of the coastline and identity of Rhode Island — the "Ocean State". The bay takes its name from the Narragansett people, the Native American tribe that historically lived around its shores.

Geography

The bay is a drowned river valley (ria) formed after the last Ice Age. Three main passages — West Passage, East Passage and the Sakonnet River — separate large islands including Aquidneck Island (site of Newport, Middletown and Portsmouth), Conanicut Island (Jamestown) and Prudence Island. At its head the Providence River reaches the port city of Providence; the Taunton River enters from Massachusetts through Mount Hope Bay.

History

The Italian explorer Giovanni da Verrazzano visited the bay in 1524. Colonial ports on the bay — Providence, Newport, Bristol and Fall River — grew wealthy on maritime trade, shipping and, in the 19th century, textile manufacturing. Newport became a major sailing centre and long hosted the America's Cup (1930–1983).

Ecology and economy

The bay supports shellfisheries (quahogs, soft-shell clams, oysters), finfish and important eelgrass and salt-marsh habitat. Once severely polluted by sewage and industrial waste, water quality has improved markedly since the 1970s through wastewater treatment upgrades, though nutrient pollution and hypoxia events remain concerns managed by the Narragansett Bay Commission and state agencies. Tourism, boating and the marine trades are major economic drivers.
