Dan role

The dan (旦) is one of the principal role types (hangdang) in Chinese opera, most prominently in Peking opera. The dan is the female role, traditionally played by male performers, and is subdivided into several subtypes distinguished by the character's age, status and temperament.

Subtypes

  • Qingyi (青衣) — the virtuous, demure leading lady, emphasising singing and graceful restraint.
  • Huadan (花旦) — the vivacious, flirtatious young woman, emphasising gesture and speech.
  • Daoma dan (刀馬旦) — the female warrior, skilled with weapons and sometimes horseback scenes.
  • Laodan (老旦) — elderly women, played with a natural voice and stooped bearing.
  • Wudan (武旦) — acrobatic fighting women.
  • Huashan (花衫) — a composite type combining features of qingyi, huadan and daoma dan, developed by Wang Yaoqing and made famous by Mei Lanfang.

History

Because women were barred from the stage for much of imperial Chinese history, dan roles were performed by men, who developed an elaborate stylised femininity of falsetto singing, delicate hand gestures and bound-foot-like steps. Mei Lanfang (1894–1961) became the most celebrated dan performer of the 20th century and helped introduce Peking opera to the world. Since the mid-20th century the roles have increasingly been performed by women.
