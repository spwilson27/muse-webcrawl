Discogs (short for discographies) is a database of information about audio recordings, including commercial releases, promotional releases, and bootleg or off-label releases, from which a significant amount of data is user-generated and archived from independent sources. Its servers, based in the United States, are owned by Zink Media, LLC. The site is widely used by record collectors and sellers as one of the largest online resources for music releases and as an online marketplace for buying and selling music.

History

Discogs was started in 2000 by Kevin Lewandowski, a programmer who had worked for Intel. Lewandowski originally intended to build a database of electronic music for his personal website, and the site's focus gradually broadened to cover all genres and formats of recorded music. In 2004 the site received a large influx of contributions after its data was highlighted elsewhere, and in 2007 it launched a marketplace allowing users to buy and sell records, CDs, cassettes, and other physical media directly through the platform.

Over the 2010s Discogs expanded into related projects, including sister databases for books (Bookogs), films (Filmogs), posters (Posterogs), and gear (Gearogs), most of which were later discontinued to refocus on the core music database and marketplace. The company also operated VinylHub, a database of record stores worldwide.

Database

The Discogs database is built almost entirely from user contributions. Registered users submit new releases and edit existing entries, with changes subject to a voting and review system. Entries are organised into artists, labels, releases, and "master" releases that group different versions of the same title. As of the mid-2020s the database contained well over 17 million releases and millions of artist and label entries, making it one of the largest music databases in existence.

Marketplace

The Discogs Marketplace allows sellers to list items from the database for sale, with Discogs charging a commission on completed sales. Because listings are tied to specific database entries (down to individual pressings and variants), the marketplace became a standard reference for the pricing of collectible vinyl records. Discogs also publishes price-history data drawn from completed sales, which collectors use to estimate the market value of recordings.
