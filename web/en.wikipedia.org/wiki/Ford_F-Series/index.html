Ford F-Series

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Ford F-Series is a series of pickup trucks produced by the Ford Motor Company since 1948. The best-known model, the F-150, has been the best-selling vehicle in the United States for decades, and the F-Series as a whole is among the most commercially successful vehicle lines in history — and a mainstay of Ford's profits.

History

The first F-Series ("Bonus-Built" trucks) of 1948–1952 replaced Ford's car-based pickups with a dedicated truck range, from the half-ton F-1 to heavy-duty F-8. Successive generations added the F-100 name (1953), four-wheel drive, the twin-I-beam front suspension, and in 1975 the F-150 designation, which soon became the volume leader. The range expanded into the Super Duty line (F-250 and above), separated as heavier chassis-cab and work trucks from 1999.

Recent generations

The thirteenth-generation F-150 (2015) attracted wide attention for its aluminium-alloy body, cutting weight by up to about 320 kg. The fourteenth generation (2021) added hybrid "PowerBoost" versions, and the all-electric F-150 Lightning was launched in 2022. Engines have ranged from straight-sixes and small-block V8s to EcoBoost turbocharged V6s and diesel options.

Variants and culture

Performance variants include the off-road F-150 Raptor and the earlier SVT Lightning. The F-Series underpins SUVs historically (Bronco, Expedition) and is deeply embedded in American work and rural culture; cumulative production runs to tens of millions of trucks across more than fourteen generations.
