Lockheed Martin F-35 Lightning II

F-35 Lightning II
Type: Stealth multirole fighter
Manufacturer: Lockheed Martin
First flight: 15 December 2006
Introduced: 2015 (F-35B, USMC)

The Lockheed Martin F-35 Lightning II is a family of American single-seat, single-engine, stealth multirole combat aircraft designed for air superiority and strike missions, with advanced sensor fusion and network-enabled operations. It is built in three main variants: the F-35A conventional takeoff and landing version; the F-35B short takeoff and vertical landing (STOVL) version; and the F-35C carrier-based version.

Development

The F-35 grew out of the Joint Strike Fighter programme of the 1990s. Lockheed Martin's X-35 design won the competition against Boeing's X-32 in 2001. The F-35 first flew in December 2006. Development was marked by cost growth, delays, and technical challenges, and the programme became the most expensive military weapons system in history, while also producing the most widely exported fifth-generation fighter.

Design

The aircraft combines a low-observable airframe with the powerful Pratt & Whitney F135 engine, an internal weapons bay, and an extensive sensor suite including the AN/APG-81 AESA radar, electro-optical targeting system, and distributed aperture system feeding a helmet-mounted display. The F-35B uses a shaft-driven lift fan for vertical lift.

Operators and service

The F-35 entered United States Marine Corps service in 2015, Air Force service in 2016, and Navy service in 2019, and has been ordered by numerous allied nations including the United Kingdom, Italy, Australia, Japan, Israel, and others. It has seen combat use by Israel and the United States and allies.
