Ptolemy V Epiphanes (Greek: Πτολεμαῖος Ἐπιφανής, "Ptolemy the Manifest"; 210–180 BC) was king of the Ptolemaic Kingdom in Egypt from 204 to 180 BC. He inherited the throne at the age of five after the death of his father Ptolemy IV, and regents ruled in his name for much of his early reign.

Reign

His reign was marked by the loss of most of Egypt's overseas possessions in the Fifth Syrian War against the Seleucid king Antiochus III, who seized Coele-Syria, and by internal revolts in Upper Egypt led by native pharaohs Hugronaphor and Ankhmakis. The famous Rosetta Stone decree was issued in his name in 196 BC, granting privileges to the Egyptian priesthood.

Marriage and death

In about 194 BC he married Cleopatra I, daughter of Antiochus III, as part of a peace settlement. He died suddenly in 180 BC, possibly poisoned, and was succeeded by his young son Ptolemy VI.
