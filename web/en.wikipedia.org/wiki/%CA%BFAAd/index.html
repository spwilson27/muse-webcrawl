ʻAAd

ʻAAd (Arabic: عاد) were an ancient Arabian tribe mentioned in the Quran, associated with the prophet Hud, whom they are said to have rejected. According to Islamic tradition they inhabited a region of southern Arabia, often linked with the sands of al-Ahqaf between present-day Yemen and Oman, and were destroyed by a violent wind for their disobedience.

In the Quran

The ʻĀd are named in numerous surahs, including Al-A'raf, Hud, Al-Ahqaf and Al-Fajr, where "Iram of the Pillars" (Iram dhāt al-ʿimād) is connected with them. The narratives present them as a powerful, tall and prosperous people whose arrogance led to their punishment: after rejecting Hud's call to worship God alone, a black cloud brought a devastating windstorm lasting seven nights and eight days.

Historical interpretation

Scholars treat the story as part of the Arabian prophetic tradition concerning vanished peoples (al-ʿArab al-bāʾida). Some researchers have linked Iram to the lost city of Ubar in Oman, though the identification remains conjectural.
