Eurovision Song Contest 1993

The Eurovision Song Contest 1993 was the 38th edition of the Eurovision Song Contest. It took place in Millstreet, Ireland, following the country's victory at the 1992 contest with the song "Why Me?" performed by Linda Martin. Organised by the European Broadcasting Union (EBU) and host broadcaster Raidió Teilifís Éireann (RTÉ), the contest was held at the Green Glens Arena on Saturday 15 May 1993 and was hosted by Irish television and radio presenter Fionnuala Sweeney.

Background

Millstreet, a small town in County Cork with a population of around 1,500, became the smallest town ever to host the contest at the time. The choice of venue was made after the owner of the Green Glens Arena, local entrepreneur Noel Duggan, offered the equestrian centre to RTÉ free of charge. The event brought significant attention and investment to the town.

Participating countries

Twenty-five countries participated in the contest. With the dissolution of Yugoslavia and the Soviet Union, several new countries sought to enter. Bosnia and Herzegovina, Croatia and Slovenia made their debuts as independent nations, having previously participated as part of Yugoslavia. To accommodate the growing number of interested countries, a pre-qualification round, Kvalifikacija za Millstreet, was held in Ljubljana, Slovenia, on 3 April 1993, in which seven countries competed for three places in the final. Estonia, Hungary, Romania and Slovakia were eliminated in this round.

Results

The winner was Ireland with the song "In Your Eyes", performed by Niamh Kavanagh and written by Jimmy Walsh. This was Ireland's fifth victory in the contest and the second of three consecutive Irish wins in the 1990s. The United Kingdom finished second with Sonia's "Better the Devil You Know", just one point behind after a tense voting sequence decided by the final jury from Malta.

Voting

Each country awarded points to their top ten songs: 12 points to their favourite, then 10, 8, and 7 down to 1. The juries were linked by satellite for the first time in some cases, and the final result remained in doubt until the last set of votes. Ireland received the maximum 12 points from several juries, and the contest is remembered as one of the closest and most exciting voting sequences of the era.

Legacy

The 1993 contest marked the beginning of the relegation system, introduced to manage the growing number of participating countries; the lowest-placed countries would sit out the following year's contest. It also cemented Ireland's dominance of the contest in the 1990s.
