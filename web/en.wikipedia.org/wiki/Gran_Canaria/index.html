Gran Canaria is the third-largest island in the Canary Islands, an autonomous community of Spain in the Atlantic Ocean off northwestern Africa. Its capital, Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, is the largest city in the Canaries and a major Atlantic port, co-capital of the autonomous community. The island is often called a "miniature continent" for its sharply contrasting climates and landscapes, from humid laurel forests to the arid dunes of Maspalomas.

History

The island was inhabited by the Canarii, an indigenous people related to the Berbers of North Africa, before Castilian conquest between 1478 and 1483 under Juan Rejón and Pedro de Vera. Las Palmas was founded in 1478 and served as a stopover for Christopher Columbus in 1492. Sugar, then wine and cochineal, drove the colonial economy.

Geography and climate

Gran Canaria is roughly circular, about 50 km across, with a central volcanic massif crowned by Pico de las Nieves (1,949 m). Trade winds leave the north lush and the south dry and sunny, supporting large beach resorts at Playa del Inglés and Maspalomas. Much of the island is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve (2005). The Risco Caído sacred mountains were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage site in 2019.

Economy

Tourism dominates, drawing several million visitors yearly, mainly to the southern resorts. Agriculture—tomatoes, bananas—has declined but persists. The Port of Las Palmas (Puerto de la Luz) is one of Spain's busiest, a bunkering hub for Atlantic shipping.
