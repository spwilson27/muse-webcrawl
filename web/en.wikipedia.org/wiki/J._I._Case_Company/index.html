J. I. Case Company

The J. I. Case Company was an American manufacturer of agricultural and construction equipment founded in 1842 by Jerome Increase Case in Racine, Wisconsin. It became famous for its threshing machines and steam traction engines and was for many years one of the largest builders of farm machinery in the world.

History

Case began by improving a crude thresher design and moved his business to Racine in 1844. The company expanded into steam-powered threshers and by the early 20th century dominated the traction-engine market. It entered the tractor business in the 1890s with an experimental gasoline tractor and added automobiles for a time in the 1910s and 1920s.

Successors

In 1967 the Kern County Land Company, and with it Case, was acquired by Tenneco. Case merged with International Harvester's agricultural division in 1985 to form Case IH, and in 1999 Case Corporation combined with New Holland to become CNH Industrial. The Case IH and Case Construction brands continue today.
