Implantation (human embryo)

Implantation
Timing6–12 days after fertilisation
LocationEndometrium of the uterus, usually posterior wall
StagesApposition, adhesion, invasion
Key hormoneHuman chorionic gonadotropin (hCG)

In humans, implantation is the stage of pregnancy at which the embryo adheres to the wall of the uterus. At this stage of prenatal development the conceptus is a blastocyst. It is by this adhesion that the embryo receives oxygen and nutrients from the mother to be able to grow. Successful implantation is a critical milestone: a large proportion of embryos fail to implant, and failure results in the loss of the pregnancy, often before it is detected.

Timeline

Following fertilisation in the fallopian tube, the zygote undergoes cleavage and reaches the uterus as a morula, then develops into a blastocyst. The blastocyst "hatches" from the zona pellucida around day 5–6 and implants between days 6 and 12 after ovulation, most commonly on days 8–10.

Stages

Implantation proceeds through apposition (loose contact between the blastocyst and the endometrium), adhesion (firm attachment mediated by adhesion molecules such as integrins and selectins), and invasion, in which the syncytiotrophoblast penetrates the endometrial epithelium and stroma, remodels the maternal spiral arteries and establishes the early placenta.

Window of receptivity

The endometrium is receptive only during a limited "window of implantation", prepared by progesterone after oestrogen priming. Pinopodes, cytokine signalling (including leukaemia inhibitory factor) and immune modulation at the maternal–fetal interface all contribute to successful attachment. The implanted embryo secretes hCG, which rescues the corpus luteum and maintains progesterone production; hCG is the hormone detected by pregnancy tests.

Clinical significance

Implantation outside the uterus produces an ectopic pregnancy, most often in the fallopian tube. Implantation failure and defective placentation are implicated in miscarriage, pre-eclampsia and intrauterine growth restriction. In assisted reproduction, embryo transfer aims to place the embryo in the uterus during the receptive window.
