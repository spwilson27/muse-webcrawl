Consolato del Mare

The Consolato del Mare (Catalan: Llibre del Consolat de Mar, "Book of the Consulate of the Sea") is a medieval compilation of maritime law that governed trade and navigation across the Mediterranean for centuries. Originating in Barcelona, it was first printed in Catalan in 1484 and was translated into many European languages.

Origins

The work grew out of the customs administered by the sea consulates (consolats de mar) established in Catalan and Valencian ports, building on earlier sources such as the Ordinances of Barcelona of 1258 and the maritime usages of the western Mediterranean. Its chapters regulate the duties of shipowners, masters, merchants, and crews; charter parties; jettison and general average; salvage; piracy and prizes; and the jurisdiction of the consular courts, which decided commercial disputes swiftly by merchant judges.

Influence

Adopted or imitated in ports from Valencia and Mallorca to Sicily, Genoa, and beyond, the Consolato became the nearest thing to a common maritime law of the Mediterranean and influenced later codifications such as the French Ordonnance de la marine of 1681. It remained in force in parts of Spain into the nineteenth century.
