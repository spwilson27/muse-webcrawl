Ernst Haeckel

Ernst Heinrich Philipp August Haeckel
Born: 16 February 1834, Potsdam, Prussia
Died: 9 August 1919 (aged 85), Jena, Germany
Fields: Zoology, natural history, philosophy
Known for: Recapitulation theory; coining "ecology"; Kunstformen der Natur

Ernst Heinrich Philipp August Haeckel (16 February 1834 – 9 August 1919) was a German zoologist, naturalist, philosopher, physician, professor, marine biologist, and artist who discovered, described and named thousands of new species, mapped a genealogical tree relating all life forms, and coined many terms in biology, including ecology, phylum, phylogeny, and ontogeny. He was the leading populariser of Darwinism in Germany.

Work

Haeckel's monographs on the Radiolaria, sponges, and jellyfish combined meticulous taxonomy with celebrated artwork. His Kunstformen der Natur ("Art Forms in Nature", 1904) collected one hundred prints of organisms, especially marine invertebrates, and strongly influenced Art Nouveau design. In Generelle Morphologie (1866) he coined the term Oecologie. His bestselling Die Welträthsel ("The Riddle of the Universe", 1899) promoted a monistic philosophy uniting science and religion in nature.

Recapitulation and controversy

Haeckel is associated with the biogenetic law — "ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny" — the idea that embryonic development replays evolutionary history, illustrated by his famous (and later criticised, and by him admitted to be partly schematic) embryo drawings. Modern biology has rejected the doctrine in its strong form, though a "phylotypic stage" of development remains of interest in evolutionary developmental biology. His later racial theories and hierarchical rankings of human groups have been condemned and are part of the contested prehistory of scientific racism, and historians debate the extent to which his monist movement influenced later ideologies.
