Battle of the Downs

The Battle of the Downs was a naval battle fought on 21 October 1639 (New Style) in the Downs anchorage off the English coast near Deal, Kent, during the Eighty Years' War. A Dutch fleet under Lieutenant-Admiral Maarten Tromp destroyed a large Spanish fleet under Admiral Antonio de Oquendo that was carrying reinforcements and money to the Spanish Netherlands.

Background

Spain, unable to supply Flanders by the overland "Spanish Road" alone, sent a great armada of some 77 ships and 24,000 men north in 1639. Tromp intercepted it in the Channel in September and, in the first recorded use of line-ahead tactics, mauled the Spanish fleet, which took refuge in the neutral English anchorage of the Downs. Charles I of England, sympathetic to Spain, failed to protect it, while the Dutch fleet grew to well over a hundred ships blockading the anchorage.

The battle

On 21 October Tromp attacked in force, sending fireships among the anchored Spanish vessels. Many Spanish ships cut their cables and ran aground on the English shore; others were captured or burned. Only about a dozen escaped to Dunkirk. Spanish losses were estimated at 40 or more ships and thousands of men.

Aftermath

The defeat broke Spanish sea power in northern waters, crippled the Army of Flanders' supply line, and confirmed the Dutch Republic as the leading naval power in Europe. It also humiliated England, whose neutrality had been violated with impunity — an early sign of English naval decline before the Commonwealth's revival.
