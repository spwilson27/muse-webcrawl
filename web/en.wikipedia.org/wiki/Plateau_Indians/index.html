Plateau Indians

The Plateau Indians are the Indigenous peoples of the Columbia Plateau and surrounding Interior Plateau region of the northwestern United States and southwestern Canada, between the Cascade Range and the Rocky Mountains. Peoples of the region include the Nez Perce, Umatilla, Yakama, Spokane, Coeur d'Alene, Okanagan, Ktunaxa, and Interior Salish groups, among others.

Culture

Traditional Plateau culture centred on the great salmon fisheries of the Columbia River and its tributaries — Celilo Falls was a major trading and fishing centre for thousands of years — supplemented by camas and other roots, berries, and hunting of deer, elk, and (after the adoption of the horse in the 18th century) bison on the Plains fringe. Winter dwellings included semi-subterranean pit houses and longhouses covered with tule mats; summer shelters were lighter mat lodges or tipis. Trade networks exchanged dried salmon, roots, and hides widely.

History

The Lewis and Clark expedition crossed the Plateau in 1805–1806. Mid-19th-century treaties confined most Plateau peoples to reservations, and fishing rights at usual and accustomed places have remained a central legal and cultural issue, particularly as dams flooded sites such as Celilo Falls in 1957.
