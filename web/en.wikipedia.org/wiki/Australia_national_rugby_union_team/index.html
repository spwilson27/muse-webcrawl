The Australia men's national rugby union team, nicknamed the Wallabies, represents Australia in men's international rugby union. Governed by Rugby Australia, the team has competed in every Rugby World Cup, winning the tournament twice — in 1991 and 1999 — and finishing as runners-up in 2003 and 2015. Australia contests the annual Rugby Championship with New Zealand, South Africa and Argentina, and the Bledisloe Cup with New Zealand.

The Wallabies in green and gold.

History

Australia's first international was played against a touring British Isles side in Sydney in 1899, which Australia won 13–3. The 1908 touring team, gold medallists at the London Olympics, acquired the Wallabies name on that tour. Post-war highlights include the 1984 Grand Slam tour of Britain and Ireland, in which Australia defeated all four home nations, and the golden era under coaches Rod Macqueen and captains Nick Farr-Jones and John Eales, yielding the 1991 and 1999 World Cups — Eales's 1999 side remains the only team to win the cup on northern-hemisphere soil in that era and made Australia the first two-time winners. The 2003 home World Cup ended in an extra-time final loss to England. Results fluctuated in the professional era, with Tri Nations/Rugby Championship titles in 2000, 2001, 2011 and 2015.

Records and players

Record cap-holder James Slipper passed George Gregan's mark of 139 caps; other notable Wallabies include David Campese, Mark Ella, John Eales, George Gregan, Stephen Larkham, Michael Lynagh — Australia's record points scorer for many years — and Michael Hooper, the most-capped captain. Traditional rivals include the All Blacks (Bledisloe Cup, contested since the 1930s), England (Cook Cup) and the Springboks (Mandela Challenge Plate).

Kit and identity

The Wallabies play in gold jerseys with green trim, a colour scheme used since 1929; the wallaby — a small macropod — was chosen as an emblem in part because earlier suggested animals were taken by other codes or states.
