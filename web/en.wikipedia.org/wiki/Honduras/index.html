Honduras, officially the Republic of Honduras, is a country in Central America bordered by Guatemala to the west, El Salvador to the southwest and Nicaragua to the southeast, with coasts on both the Caribbean Sea and (via the Gulf of Fonseca) the Pacific. Its name ("depths") is traditionally credited to Columbus, who in 1502 reportedly thanked God for escaping the deep waters off its cape. The mountainous interior held the Maya city of Copán, a jewel of Classic-period art, while the north coast became the original "banana republic" — a term coined (by O. Henry, who lived in Honduras) for economies dominated by foreign fruit companies such as United Fruit and Standard Fruit.

Modern history

Honduras spent much of the 20th century under military or military-backed rule and served in the 1980s as the principal US base for the Contra war against Nicaragua's Sandinista government — "the USS Honduras". Civilian politics resumed in the 1990s; Hurricane Mitch (1998) killed thousands and wrecked the economy. In 2009 a military coup removed President Manuel Zelaya, isolating Honduras internationally and polarising it for a decade; subsequent years brought contested elections, high homicide rates (San Pedro Sula was once ranked the world's most violent city), and large migrant caravans heading north from 2018. In 2022 Xiomara Castro, Zelaya's wife, became Honduras's first female president on an anti-corruption platform. Coffee, bananas, palm oil and maquila textiles anchor the economy; the Bay Islands (Roatán, Útila) draw divers to the Mesoamerican reef, second in size only to Australia's Great Barrier Reef.
