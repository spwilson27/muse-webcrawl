Calcium is the chemical element with the symbol Ca and atomic number 20. It is a greyish alkaline earth metal and the fifth most abundant element in Earth's crust, chiefly as limestone, chalk and marble (calcium carbonate).

Calcium is the most abundant metal in the human body: calcium phosphate builds bones and teeth, and calcium ions control muscle contraction, nerve signals and blood clotting. Calcium compounds — lime, cement, plaster — are foundations of construction.

History

Lime (calcium oxide) has been used since antiquity, but the metal itself was first isolated by Humphry Davy in 1808 by electrolysis of a lime and mercury oxide mixture. The name derives from the Latin calx, lime.

Chemistry

Calcium reacts steadily with cold water to give calcium hydroxide ('limewater'), which turns milky in the classic test for carbon dioxide. Its flame colour is brick-red.
