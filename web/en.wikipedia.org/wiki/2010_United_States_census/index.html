The 2010 United States census was the twenty-third decennial census of the United States, conducted by the Census Bureau with Census Day on April 1, 2010. It counted 308,745,538 residents, a 9.7 percent increase over the 281.4 million counted in 2000, and its results governed the apportionment of seats in the House of Representatives and the distribution of federal funds for the following decade.

Conduct

The 2010 census returned to a short-form-only design: all households received a ten-question form covering name, sex, age, race, Hispanic origin, household relationship, and housing tenure. The long-form sample of earlier censuses had been replaced by the continuous American Community Survey. The count cost about US$13 billion and was followed by non-response follow-up visits to tens of millions of households. The slogan was "It's In Our Hands."

Apportionment and findings

Apportionment results, released in December 2010, shifted twelve House seats among eighteen states, reflecting continued growth in the South and West: Texas gained four seats, Florida two, while New York and Ohio each lost two. The results documented a diversifying country — the Hispanic population grew 43 percent to 50.5 million — and suburban and Sun Belt metropolitan growth outpacing the Northeast and Midwest.

State rankings

Nevada was the fastest-growing state (up 35 percent), while Michigan was the only state to lose population. Utah, Texas, Colorado and others grew rapidly; the national center of population moved to near Plato, Missouri.
