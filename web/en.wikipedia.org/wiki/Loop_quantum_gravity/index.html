Loop quantum gravity

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Illustration of a spin network: a graph of nodes and links representing quantum geometry]
A spin network: spacetime built from discrete quantum geometry.

Loop quantum gravity (LQG) is a theory of quantum gravity that incorporates the general principles of quantum mechanics and General relativity without requiring extra dimensions or unification with the other forces. In LQG, as in general relativity, spacetime is a dynamical entity; quantising it yields a discrete structure: areas and volumes have quantised spectra, and the geometry of space is described by spin networks — graphs whose edges carry representations of SU(2).

The approach grew from Abhay Ashtekar's reformulation of general relativity (1986) and the loop representation developed with Carlo Rovelli and Lee Smolin in the late 1980s and 1990s. Its characteristic prediction is that space is granular at the Planck scale (~10⁻³⁵ m), with a minimum nonzero area of order the Planck area. Loop quantum cosmology applies the theory to the early universe, replacing the Big Bang singularity with a 'Big Bounce'.

Comparison with string theory

LQG and String theory represent contrasting strategies: background-independent quantisation of geometry alone (LQG, mathematically conservative about inputs) versus a unified framework on a fixed background requiring extra dimensions and supersymmetry (strings). Neither has experimental confirmation. LQG's concrete achievements include a derivation of black-hole entropy counting reproducing the Bekenstein–Hawking formula, and finite, singularity-free cosmological models.
