Dōgen

Dōgen Zenji
Born: 1200, Kyoto, Japan
Died: 1253
School: Sōtō Zen
Major work: Shōbōgenzō

Dōgen Zenji (1200–1253), also known as Eihei Dōgen, was a Japanese Buddhist priest, writer and philosopher who founded the Sōtō school of Zen in Japan. He is regarded as one of Japan's greatest religious thinkers.

Life

Orphaned young and ordained on Mount Hiei, Dōgen travelled to China in 1223, studying Caodong (Sōtō) Chan under Rujing, from whom he received dharma transmission. Returning in 1227, he taught in Kyoto, then moved to remote Echizen Province, founding Eihei-ji in 1244.

Thought and works

His masterwork, the Shōbōgenzō (Treasury of the True Dharma Eye), explores time, being (uji, being-time), and practice-enlightenment unity. He emphasised shikantaza — just sitting zazen — as itself enlightenment, not a means to it, and wrote instructions including the Fukan zazengi and Tenzo kyōkun. See also Zen and Sōtō.
