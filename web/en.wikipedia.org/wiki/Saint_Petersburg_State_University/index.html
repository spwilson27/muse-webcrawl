Saint Petersburg State University

Saint Petersburg State University (SPbSU; Russian: Санкт-Петербургский государственный университет) is a public research university in Saint Petersburg, Russia. Founded in 1724 by a decree of Peter the Great, it is among the oldest and largest universities in Russia and was long known as Leningrad State University during the Soviet era.

History

The university was established in 1724 alongside the Saint Petersburg Academy of Sciences. Its early professors included the mathematician Leonhard Euler, and Mikhail Lomonosov studied and taught there. Closed in the early nineteenth century, it was refounded in 1819 as Saint Petersburg University and grew into one of the Russian Empire's leading centres of learning. In the Soviet period it bore the names of Leningrad and of Andrei Zhdanov. It regained its historic name in 1991.

Academics

SPbSU comprises more than twenty faculties and institutes spanning mathematics, physics, chemistry, biology, medicine, law, economics, history, philology and international relations, with major facilities both on Vasilievsky Island in central Saint Petersburg and at a modern campus in Petergof. It receives special status under Russian law, alongside Moscow State University, including the right to set its own educational standards and award its own diplomas.

Notable people

Alumni and faculty include nine Nobel laureates, among them the physiologist Ivan Pavlov and the economist Leonid Kantorovich; the mathematicians Pafnuty Chebyshev and Grigori Perelman; the chemist Dmitri Mendeleev, who spent much of his career at the university; and the Russian president Vladimir Putin and former president Dmitry Medvedev, both graduates of its law faculty.
