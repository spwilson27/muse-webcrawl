Harry Nyquist

Harry Nyquist (February 7, 1889 – April 4, 1976) was a Swedish-American physicist and electrical engineer who made fundamental contributions to information theory and telecommunications during his career at Bell Telephone Laboratories.

Early life and career

Nyquist was born in Nilsby, Sweden, and emigrated to the United States in 1907. He studied electrical engineering at the University of North Dakota and received a PhD in physics from Yale University in 1917. He then joined AT&T's research division, which became Bell Labs, where he remained until his retirement in 1954.

Contributions

In 1924 and 1928 Nyquist published landmark papers analysing telegraph speed and the transmission of information, deriving the maximum signalling rate over a channel of given bandwidth — the foundation of the Nyquist rate and, with the later work of Claude Shannon, the sampling theorem: a band-limited signal can be perfectly reconstructed if sampled at more than twice its highest frequency.

In 1928 he explained the thermal noise in conductors first measured by John B. Johnson, giving the Johnson–Nyquist noise formula relating noise power to temperature and resistance. In 1932 he published the Nyquist stability criterion, a graphical method (the Nyquist plot) for determining the stability of feedback amplifiers, which remains a standard tool of control theory.

Honours

Nyquist received the IEEE Medal of Honor in 1960. He died in Harlingen, Texas, in 1976.
