Polycarp

Polycarp of Smyrna (Greek: Πολύκαρπος; c. AD 69 – c. 155) was a Christian bishop of Smyrna in Asia Minor and one of the Apostolic Fathers. According to tradition he had been a disciple of the Apostle John, making him one of the last links between the apostolic age and the second-century Church.

Life and writings

Irenaeus of Lyons, who had heard Polycarp in his youth, describes him as a teacher who recounted what he had learned from the apostles. Polycarp travelled to Rome around 154 to discuss the date of Easter with Pope Anicetus; the two disagreed but parted in peace. His surviving work is the Letter to the Philippians, a short exhortation quoting many New Testament writings and urging righteousness, humility and endurance.

Martyrdom

The Martyrdom of Polycarp, an account written by his church, is among the earliest surviving martyrdom narratives. Arrested during a persecution in Smyrna, the aged bishop refused to curse Christ — "Eighty and six years I have served him, and he has done me no wrong" — and was burned at the stake and then stabbed, traditionally dated to 155 or 156. He is venerated as a saint in the Roman Catholic, Eastern Orthodox and other churches; his feast day is 23 February. See also Apostolic Fathers.
