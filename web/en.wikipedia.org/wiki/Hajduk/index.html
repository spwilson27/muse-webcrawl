Hajduk

Hajduk
Region: Balkans, Central and Eastern Europe
Era: 16th–19th centuries
Role: Irregular fighter, outlaw, folk hero

A hajduk (plural hajduks, hajduci) is a type of irregular infantry and outlaw found in the Balkans and parts of Central and Eastern Europe from the 16th to the 19th century. Living in bands in mountains and forests, hajduks fought against the Ottoman Empire and other authorities, and are celebrated in epic poetry and folklore as freedom fighters and protectors of the poor.

Etymology and distribution

The word derives from Hungarian hajdú, originally a cattle drover. Related traditions include the Serbian and Bulgarian haiduk, Romanian haiduc, Croatian hajduk, and the Ukrainian haidamaka. Famous hajduks include Baba Novac, Ivo Senković, and, in Romania, Iancu Jianu and Pintea the Brave.

Legacy

Hajduks remain potent national symbols. The Croatian football club HNK Hajduk Split, founded in 1911, takes its name from them, as do clubs in Serbia and elsewhere.
