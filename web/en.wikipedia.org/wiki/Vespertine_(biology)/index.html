Vespertine (biology)

In biology, vespertine describes behaviour or activity that occurs in the evening, around dusk. It is one of several temporal activity patterns, contrasted with diurnal (daytime), nocturnal (night), matutinal (morning) and crepuscular (twilight) activity.

In animals

Vespertine animals become active in the early evening. Many bats leave their roosts at dusk to feed, and various insects, birds and mammals show peak activity in the first hours after sunset, when temperatures fall and some predators are less active. The vesper bats (family Vespertilionidae) take their name from this habit.

In plants

Vespertine flowers open or release scent in the evening to attract dusk-flying pollinators such as hawk-moths; examples include evening primroses and night-scented stock.
