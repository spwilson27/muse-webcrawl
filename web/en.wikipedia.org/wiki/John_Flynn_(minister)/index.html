John Flynn OBE (25 November 1880 – 5 May 1951) was an Australian Presbyterian minister who founded the Australian Inland Mission (AIM) and the world's first air ambulance service, which became the Royal Flying Doctor Service. He is commemorated on the Australian twenty-dollar note.

Early life and inland mission

Flynn was born at Moliagul, Victoria, worked as a teacher, and was ordained in 1911. Appointed to the Smith of Dunesk Mission in the northern Flinders Ranges, he travelled the outback and in 1912 became the first superintendent of the new Australian Inland Mission, establishing nursing hostels ("bush hospitals") in remote settlements and publishing the magazine The Inlander.

The Flying Doctor idea

Convinced that aviation and radio could conquer the isolation of the bush — an idea sharpened by the death of outback stockman Jimmy Darcy in 1917 and by a 1917 letter from young airman Clifford Peel — Flynn campaigned for a decade. In 1928 the AIM Aerial Medical Service made its first flight from Cloncurry, Queensland, in a de Havilland DH.50 leased from the young airline Qantas. Alfred Traeger's pedal radio made two-way communication possible from isolated homesteads.

Legacy

The service, renamed the Flying Doctor Service in 1942 and "Royal" in 1955, grew into one of the world's largest aeromedical organisations. Flynn died in Sydney in 1951; his ashes rest beneath the John Flynn memorial near Mount Isa, and the Flynn Memorial Church in Alice Springs opened the year of his death.
