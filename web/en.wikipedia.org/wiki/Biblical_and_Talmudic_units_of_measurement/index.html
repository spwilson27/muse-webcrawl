Biblical and Talmudic units of measurement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Biblical and Talmudic units of measurement were used primarily by ancient Israelites and appear frequently in the Hebrew Bible and in later rabbinic writings such as the Mishnah and Talmud. These systems are not fully known, and their values must be inferred from texts and archaeology, so modern equivalents are approximate and sometimes disputed — a matter of practical importance in modern Jewish law (halakha), where many obligations are defined by these measures.

Length and distance

Length units were mostly derived from the human body:

  • Etzba (fingerbreadth) — about 2 cm
  • Tefach (handbreadth) — 4 fingerbreadths, about 8–9.6 cm
  • Zeret (span) — about 3 handbreadths
  • Amah (cubit) — 6 handbreadths, roughly 46–58 cm depending on the authority
  • Kaneh (reed/rod) — 6 cubits

Distances include the mil (related to the Roman mile, about 1–1.2 km) and the parsa (Persian parasang), and the "Sabbath limit" (techum) of 2,000 cubits.

Volume and capacity

Dry and liquid measures are based on units such as the log (about 0.3–0.5 litres), the hin (12 logs), the se'ah, the ephah and bath (both roughly 22–40 litres), and the kor/homer (10 ephahs). Small volume units used in halakha include the k'zayit ("like an olive") and k'beitza ("like an egg"), which define minimum quantities for blessings and ritual obligations.

Weight and coinage

The basic weight was the shekel (about 11–14 g), with the mina (50 or 60 shekels) and the talent (kikar, 3,000 shekels, roughly 34 kg). Many of these "weights" also served as units of money before coinage, and coins such as the zuz/dinar and perutah appear in Talmudic sources.

Area and time

Land was measured by the area a pair of oxen could plough in a day (beit se'ah corresponds to a volume-derived seeding measure), and time was divided into seasonal hours (sha'ah zmanit, one twelfth of daylight), with fixed points such as dawn (alot hashachar) and nightfall (tzeit hakochavim) governing the timing of prayers and fasts.
