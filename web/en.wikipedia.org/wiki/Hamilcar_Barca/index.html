Hamilcar Barca (c. 275 – 228 BC) was a Carthaginian general and statesman, leader of the Barcid family, and father of Hannibal, Hasdrubal and Mago. He was a commander during the First Punic War (264–241 BC) and the subsequent Mercenary War (240–238 BC), and he led the Carthaginian conquest of much of coastal Iberia from 237 BC until his death in battle in 228 BC.

First Punic War

Hamilcar took command of Carthaginian forces in Sicily in 247 BC. Operating from strongholds at Heirkte and Eryx, he waged a highly effective guerrilla campaign against the Romans, raiding the Italian coast and never losing a major engagement. Despite his successes, Carthage's defeat at the Battle of the Aegates Islands in 241 BC forced the city to sue for peace, and Hamilcar negotiated the surrender terms.

Mercenary War

Returning to Africa, Hamilcar became entangled in the revolt of Carthage's unpaid mercenaries. After initial failures by other commanders, he was given full command and crushed the rebellion with notable severity, defeating the rebels at the Battle of the Bagradas River and the Battle of Leptis.

Conquest of Iberia

In 237 BC Hamilcar landed at Gades (modern Cádiz) and began the subjugation of southern and eastern Iberia, securing silver mines and new sources of revenue and manpower to compensate for the loss of Sicily and Sardinia. According to tradition, he made his young son Hannibal swear an oath never to be a friend of Rome. Hamilcar died in 228 BC, reportedly drowning in a river while retreating from an ambush by Iberian forces, having laid the foundations of the Barcid empire in Spain that his sons would inherit.
