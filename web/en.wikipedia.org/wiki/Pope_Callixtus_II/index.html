Pope Callixtus II (c. 1065 – 13 December 1124), born Guy of Burgundy, was head of the Catholic Church and ruler of the Papal States from 1 February 1119 to his death in 1124. A son of Count William I of Burgundy and brother of Raymond of Burgundy (father of Alfonso VII of León and Castile), he served for three decades as Archbishop of Vienne before his election. His pontificate is chiefly remembered for concluding the Investiture Controversy with the Holy Roman Emperor Henry V through the Concordat of Worms (1122), and for convoking the First Lateran Council (1123).

Early life and election

Guy was a member of one of Europe's most powerful aristocratic families, the House of Ivrea, counts of Burgundy. Appointed Archbishop of Vienne in 1088, he was a leading supporter of the Gregorian reform and an opponent of lay investiture. When Pope Gelasius II died at Cluny in January 1119 while fleeing imperial pressure, the cardinals present elected Guy in his place on 1 February 1119; he was crowned at Vienne a week later and took the name Callixtus II.

Concordat of Worms

Callixtus first pursued negotiation with Emperor Henry V; a meeting at Mouzon in 1119 collapsed, and the pope excommunicated the emperor at the Council of Reims. Diplomacy eventually prevailed. On 23 September 1122 the two parties signed the agreement known as the Concordat of Worms (Pactum Calixtinum). The emperor renounced investiture with ring and staff — the symbols of spiritual office — and granted free canonical election of bishops, while the pope conceded that elections in Germany could take place in the emperor's presence and that the emperor would invest bishops with their temporal regalia by the sceptre. The settlement ended half a century of conflict between empire and papacy.

First Lateran Council

In March 1123 Callixtus presided over the First Council of the Lateran, regarded in the Catholic tradition as the ninth ecumenical council. Its canons ratified the Concordat of Worms, condemned simony and lay investiture, forbade clerical marriage, and addressed the inheritance of church property.

Other acts

Callixtus strengthened ties with his family's Iberian kingdoms and confirmed the privileges of the see of Santiago de Compostela, whose archbishop Diego Gelmírez was his ally; Compostela tradition later credited him (probably wrongly) with the pilgrimage guide known as the Codex Calixtinus. In 1120 he issued the bull Sicut Judaeis, the foundational papal statement forbidding forced conversion of Jews and protecting their persons, property and festivals — a bull frequently reissued by later popes. He also moved against the antipope Gregory VIII, who was captured at Sutri in 1121 and imprisoned. Callixtus died in Rome on 13 December 1124 and was succeeded by Honorius II.
