F. Augustus Heinze

Born5 December 1869, Brooklyn, New York
Died4 November 1914, Saratoga, New York
OccupationMining magnate
Known forWar of the Copper Kings; Panic of 1907

Fritz Augustus Heinze (December 5, 1869 – November 4, 1914) was one of the three "Copper Kings" of Butte, Montana, along with William A. Clark and Marcus Daly. He was a central figure in the failed attempt to corner the copper market that helped trigger the Panic of 1907.

Early life

Born in Brooklyn to German immigrant parents, Heinze studied mining engineering and travelled west, arriving in Butte in 1889 as a mining engineer. He quickly recognised the richness of the Butte copper district and began acquiring claims.

Copper wars

Heinze fought prolonged legal battles against the Amalgamated Copper Company (later Anaconda), exploiting Montana's apex law and a sympathetic local judiciary. In 1906 he sold his Butte holdings to Amalgamated for a reported $10.5 million and moved to New York to pursue finance with his brothers.

Panic of 1907

In October 1907, Heinze's brothers Otto and Arthur attempted to corner the stock of United Copper. The corner failed, ruining the Heinzes and the banks associated with them, including the Knickerbocker Trust, whose collapse spread the panic through the American financial system.

Death

Heinze died of cirrhosis of the liver in 1914, aged 44, his fortune largely dissipated.
