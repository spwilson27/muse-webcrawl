Motorola 68000

The Motorola 68000 ("sixty-eight-thousand"; also called the m68k) is a 16/32-bit microprocessor introduced by Motorola in 1979. It became the first member of the 68000 family, widely used in personal computers, workstations, and embedded systems during the 1980s and 1990s.

Architecture

The 68000 has a programmer's model with eight 32-bit data registers and eight address registers, a 24-bit external address bus (16 MB address space), and a 16-bit data bus. Its instruction set is notably orthogonal and regular, making it a favorite in computer-science teaching. Internally it was implemented with 16-bit components, hence the "16/32-bit" description. Later family members — the 68010, 68020, 68030, 68040, and 68060 — added full 32-bit buses, virtual memory support, caches, and on-chip floating-point and memory-management units.

Uses

The 68000 powered the original Apple Macintosh, the Commodore Amiga, the Atari ST, the Sega Genesis/Mega Drive, early Sun and Apollo workstations, and the first LaserWriter printers. Variants (ColdFire and DragonBall) continued in embedded applications for decades.
