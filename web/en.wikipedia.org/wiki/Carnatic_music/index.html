Carnatic music (Sanskrit: Karṇāṭaka saṃgīta) is a system of music commonly associated with South India, including the states of Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Karnataka, Kerala, and Tamil Nadu. Together with Hindustani music of the north, it is one of the two main subgenres of Indian classical music. Carnatic music is largely devotional in character, with most compositions addressed to Hindu deities.

Structure

Like all Indian classical music, Carnatic music is built on raga (melodic framework) and tala (rhythmic cycle). Its theoretical system uses 72 melakarta (parent) ragas from which derivative janya ragas are drawn. Melodies are richly ornamented with gamaka (oscillations and slides), and improvisation — including alapana (unmetered exposition), neraval, and kalpanaswaram — is central to performance.

Compositions and composers

The principal compositional form is the kriti, a structured devotional song. The "Trinity" of Carnatic music — Tyagaraja (1767–1847), Muthuswami Dikshitar (1775–1835), and Syama Sastri (1762–1827) — composed hundreds of kritis in Telugu and Sanskrit that form the core of the concert repertoire. Earlier figures include Purandara Dasa (1484–1564), honoured as the "father" (pitamaha) of Carnatic music for systematising its teaching.

Performance

A concert (kutcheri) typically features a vocalist accompanied by violin, the double-headed drum mridangam, and percussion such as the ghatam (clay pot) and kanjira. The annual December music season in Chennai is the tradition's largest festival.
