Godfrey of Bouillon

Bornc. 1060, Boulogne, France
Died18 July 1100, Jerusalem
TitleAdvocate of the Holy Sepulchre; Lord of Bouillon; Duke of Lower Lorraine

Godfrey of Bouillon (French: Godefroy de Bouillon; c. 1060 – 18 July 1100) was a Frankish knight and nobleman who was one of the leaders of the First Crusade and the first ruler of the Kingdom of Jerusalem, although he refused the title of king and instead took the title Advocatus Sancti Sepulchri ("Advocate of the Holy Sepulchre").

Godfrey was the second son of Eustace II, Count of Boulogne, and Ida of Lorraine. He inherited the lordship of Bouillon and later became Duke of Lower Lorraine. To fund his expedition to the Holy Land, he sold or mortgaged much of his land, including the castle of Bouillon.

First Crusade

Godfrey's contingent was among the first to reach Constantinople, and he played a major role throughout the crusade: at the sieges of Nicaea and Antioch, and in the decisive Battle of Dorylaeum. When Jerusalem fell to the crusaders on 15 July 1099, Godfrey was among the first to enter the city, and a week later he was elected its ruler. He secured the kingdom's borders with a victory over the Fatimids at the Battle of Ascalon in August 1099.

Death and legacy

Godfrey died in July 1100, less than a year after the capture of Jerusalem, and was succeeded by his brother Baldwin, who accepted the title of King of Jerusalem. In later medieval tradition Godfrey became one of the Nine Worthies, paragons of chivalry, and a hero of epic poetry, prominently in Torquato Tasso's Jerusalem Delivered.
