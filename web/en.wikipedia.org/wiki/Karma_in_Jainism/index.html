Karma in Jainism

Karma in Jainism is the principle of cause and effect, by which the actions of a soul (jiva) attract subtle particles of matter (pudgala) that bind to it and obstruct its innate qualities of omniscience, bliss and energy. Unlike in many other traditions, Jain karma is conceived as actual material substance that adheres to the soul and determines its rebirths.

Mechanism

Activity of body, speech and mind (yoga) causes an influx of karmic matter (asrava); passions such as anger, pride, deceit and greed make the soul "sticky" so that karma binds (bandha). Jain texts classify karma into eight types: knowledge-obscuring, perception-obscuring, deluding, feeling-producing, lifespan-determining, body-determining, status-determining and obstructive.

Liberation

Stooping the influx (samvara) through right conduct and shedding bound karma (nirjara) through austerity gradually purify the soul. When all karma is destroyed, the soul attains moksha, rising to the top of the universe in its omniscient, liberated state. The doctrine is closely tied to ahimsa: harming any living being binds heavy karma.
