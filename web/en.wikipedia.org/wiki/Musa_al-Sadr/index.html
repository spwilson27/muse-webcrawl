Musa al-Sadr

Sayyid Musa al-Sadr
Born: 4 June 1928, Qom, Iran
Disappeared: 31 August 1978, Libya
Known for: Founder of the Amal Movement; leader of Lebanon's Shia community

Musa al-Sadr (4 June 1928 – disappeared 31 August 1978) was an Iranian-born Lebanese Shia cleric and political leader who transformed Lebanon's historically marginalized Shia community into a major political force. He is commonly given the title Imam by his followers.

Life and activism

Born in Qom into a distinguished clerical family tracing descent from Jabal Amel in Lebanon, al-Sadr studied in Qom and Najaf before settling in Tyre in 1959. Charismatic and reformist, he founded schools, charities, and vocational institutes for the poor of the south, established the Supreme Islamic Shia Council (1969), and in 1974 launched the Movement of the Deprived, whose armed wing, Amal, was formed as the Lebanese Civil War began. He advocated interfaith dialogue and famously preached in churches as well as mosques.

Disappearance

In August 1978 al-Sadr travelled to Libya for celebrations marking Muammar Gaddafi's rise to power. He was last seen on 31 August 1978; Libya claimed he left for Italy, but he never arrived. His fate remains unknown and a matter of enduring controversy: Lebanese investigations and his followers maintain he was imprisoned or killed on Gaddafi's orders, and the case remains officially open in Lebanon.
