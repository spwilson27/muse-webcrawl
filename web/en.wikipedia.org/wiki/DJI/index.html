DJI

SZ DJI Technology Co., Ltd., trading as DJI, is a Chinese technology company headquartered in Shenzhen, Guangdong. Founded in 2006 by Frank Wang (Wang Tao), DJI is the world's largest manufacturer of commercial unmanned aerial vehicles (drones), with a dominant share of the global consumer drone market. Its name derives from Dà-Jiāng Innovations.

Products

DJI's product lines include the Phantom series, which popularized ready-to-fly camera drones from 2013; the folding Mavic series; the compact Mini series; the professional Inspire series; and the Avata cinewhoop FPV drones. The company also makes the Osmo line of handheld gimbals and action cameras, Ronin camera stabilizers for cinema, flight controllers and propulsion systems, and enterprise and agricultural aircraft such as the Agras spraying drones. In 2015 DJI acquired a majority stake in the Swedish camera maker Hasselblad.

History and market position

Wang founded the company in his dormitory room at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology, initially selling flight controllers for model helicopters before moving to Shenzhen. DJI grew into one of China's most valuable technology startups, employing tens of thousands of people, and its drones became standard tools in filmmaking, agriculture, surveying, public safety, and warfare.

Controversies and restrictions

DJI has faced scrutiny in the United States over data-security and geopolitical concerns: the US Department of Commerce placed it on the Entity List in 2020, the Department of Defense designated it a "Chinese military company" in 2022, and US legislation has restricted federal use of its drones and debated broader import limits.
