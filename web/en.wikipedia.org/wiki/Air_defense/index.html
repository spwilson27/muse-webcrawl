Air defense

Air defense (also written air defence) encompasses all measures designed to protect territory, forces, and assets from air and missile attack, and the systems that carry them out. It includes detection and tracking by radar and other sensors, command and control, and weapons — fighter aircraft, surface-to-air missiles (SAMs), anti-aircraft artillery, and electronic warfare — coordinated to identify, intercept, and destroy hostile aircraft, cruise missiles, ballistic missiles, and drones.

History

Organized air defence emerged in the First World War with anti-aircraft guns and observers against airships and bombers. The Second World War saw integrated systems such as Britain's Dowding system, combining radar, plotting rooms, and fighter control during the Battle of Britain, and Germany's Kammhuber Line against night bombing. In the Cold War, SAMs became prominent — Soviet S-75 missiles shot down a US U-2 in 1960 — and integrated air defence systems (IADS) layered long-, medium-, and short-range weapons.

Modern systems

Contemporary air defence is organized in layers: long-range systems such as the Russian S-400 and American Patriot and THAAD (the latter two also providing missile defence), medium-range systems like NASAMS and IRIS-T SLM, short-range systems such as Pantsir and Avenger, and man-portable missiles (MANPADS) and guns for point defence. The proliferation of cheap drones and loitering munitions, prominent in the Russo-Ukrainian War, has driven development of electronic jamming, guns, and directed-energy weapons as low-cost countermeasures.

Suppression

Attacking forces attempt to degrade such networks through SEAD/DEAD (suppression/destruction of enemy air defences) using anti-radiation missiles, decoys, and stealth aircraft.
