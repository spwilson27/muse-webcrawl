South Lebanon Army

The South Lebanon Army (SLA; French: Armée du Liban Sud) was a Lebanese militia active from 1977 to 2000, aligned with Israel during the Lebanese Civil War and the South Lebanon conflict. It was led first by Major Saad Haddad and, after his death in 1984, by Antoine Lahad.

History

The SLA grew out of the Army of Free Lebanon, a breakaway faction of the Lebanese Army formed in 1976–77. With Israeli arms, funding, and training, it controlled the "security zone" Israel maintained in southern Lebanon after its invasions of 1978 and 1982, administering the area, running the Khiam detention centre, and broadcasting from "Voice of Hope" radio and television.

Collapse

Following Israel's decision to withdraw to the Blue Line under UN Security Council Resolution 425, the SLA collapsed in May 2000 as Hezbollah and local residents advanced. Its fighters and their families fled into Israel or surrendered; several thousand were given refuge in Israel, while others faced trial in Lebanon, mostly receiving light sentences. Lahad died in Paris in 2015.
