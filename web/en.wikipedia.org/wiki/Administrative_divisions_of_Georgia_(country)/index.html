Administrative divisions of Georgia (country)

The administrative divisions of Georgia consist of a hierarchy of autonomous republics, regions (mkhare), and municipalities. Georgia is a unitary state whose subdivisions are defined by its constitution and organic law. The country includes two autonomous republics, Abkhazia and Adjara, whose status differs: Abkhazia is controlled by a separatist government recognized by few states, while Adjara functions within Georgia's constitutional framework.

Regions

Outside the autonomous republics and the capital Tbilisi, Georgia is divided into nine regions (mkharebi): Guria, Imereti, Kakheti, Kvemo Kartli, Mtskheta-Mtianeti, Racha-Lechkhumi and Kvemo Svaneti, Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti, Samtskhe-Javakheti, and Shida Kartli. Regions are headed by state commissioners (governors) appointed by the central government; their role is mainly coordinative, as most local governance is exercised by municipalities.

Municipalities

Municipalities (munitsipaliteti) form the self-governing tier, each with an elected council (sakrebulo) and mayor. The capital, Tbilisi, has a special status and its own municipal government subdivided into districts.
