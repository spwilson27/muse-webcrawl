Lord of the Dance (hymn)

"Lord of the Dance" is a hymn written in 1963 by the English songwriter Sydney Carter. It tells the story of Jesus of Nazareth in the first person, presenting his life, death and resurrection as a dance in which he invites all people to join. The hymn is set to the American Shaker tune "Simple Gifts", composed in 1848 by Joseph Brackett.

Background

Carter explained that the imagery of the dance drew partly on the apocryphal image of Jesus singing and leading a dance, and partly on the Hindu god Shiva as Nataraja, the lord of the dance. The words follow the pattern of a traditional English carol, "Tomorrow Shall Be My Dancing Day", which likewise presents Christ's life as a dance.

Lyrics

The first verse begins:

I danced in the morning when the world was begun,
And I danced in the moon and the stars and the sun,
And I came down from heaven and I danced on the earth,
At Bethlehem I had my birth.

The chorus runs: "Dance, then, wherever you may be, / I am the Lord of the Dance, said he, / And I'll lead you all, wherever you may be, / And I'll lead you all in the dance, said he."

Reception

The hymn quickly became one of the most popular modern hymns in the English-speaking world and is included in many hymnals. Carter's use of the Shaker melody "Simple Gifts" — the same tune Aaron Copland used in Appalachian Spring — helped make the song widely known. The Irish dance show Lord of the Dance, created by Michael Flatley, shares the title but is not directly derived from the hymn. See also Simple Gifts.
