Master of Arts (Oxford, Cambridge, and Dublin)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In the universities of Oxford, Cambridge and Dublin, the degree of Master of Arts (MA) is an academic rank — rather than a postgraduate qualification — conferred on holders of the Bachelor of Arts (BA) after the passage of a period of years, without further study or examination. At Oxford the status is available 21 terms (about seven years) after matriculation; at Cambridge, graduates may supplicate for the MA six years after the end of their first term of residence; Dublin (Trinity College) follows a similar pattern. The MA confers membership of the university's governing convocation or senate and certain precedence and voting rights.

Historical origin

In the medieval university, the MA was a real teaching qualification: bachelors spent several further years studying and "incepting" as masters, whereupon they were required to lecture. Over the centuries the exercises decayed, first to formalities and then to mere lapse of time and payment of a fee, and by the nineteenth century the degree had become an automatic promotion of seniority. Attempts to abolish or reform it have repeatedly failed, partly because of the rights attached to it — at Oxford, MAs (as members of Congregation) vote on university legislation; at Cambridge the Regent House is the governing body.

Status and recognition

Holders style themselves MA (Oxon), MA (Cantab) or MA (Dubl), and the degree traditionally confers social precedence equivalent to a master's degree. Because no additional study is involved, it is not generally regarded by other institutions as equivalent to an earned postgraduate MA; universities and employers occasionally warn against representing it as such. Holders of MAs from these universities who take an earned master's elsewhere cannot count the same study twice.

Similar degrees

The ancient universities of Scotland award the MA as a first degree in humanities subjects, which is a different arrangement again. Cambridge and Oxford also confer particular master's degrees by incorporation to holders of equivalent degrees from the sister university or Dublin.
