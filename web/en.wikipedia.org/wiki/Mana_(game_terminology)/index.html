Mana (game terminology)

Mana is an attribute assigned to characters in role-playing and video games that indicates their power to use special abilities or "spells". Mana is usually measured in mana points (MP). When a character uses an ability that costs mana, their mana points decrease by a specific amount. As a character progresses in level, their amount of mana typically increases. When a character's mana falls to zero, they often cannot use most abilities until some of their mana is recovered.

History

The term "mana" derives from the Austronesian concept of mana, a spiritual energy and life force found in many Pacific cultures, popularised in the West by anthropologists in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The concept was adapted for fantasy fiction by authors such as Larry Niven, whose 1969 story "Not Long Before the End" and subsequent novel The Magic Goes Away (1978) treated magic as consuming a depletable natural resource called mana. The idea was taken up by game designers during the 1970s and 1980s as tabletop role-playing games sought alternatives to the "Vancian" spell-slot system of Dungeons & Dragons.

In video games, mana became a standard resource in titles such as Dungeon Master (1987), the Final Fantasy series, Diablo (1996), in which mana is represented by a blue orb, and countless later role-playing games. Related concepts include "energy", "rage", "stamina", and other ability-resource systems in modern games.

Mechanics

Mana is most often consumed by casting spells or activating special abilities, and may be restored by resting, levelling up, or drinking a mana potion. Many games feature passive mana regeneration, items or skills that increase a character's maximum mana, or armour that reduces the mana cost of spells. Spellcasters such as wizards, priests, and mages tend to rely on mana, while martial classes such as warriors use alternative resources or none at all.
