Bungy jumping

Bungy jumping, also spelled bungee jumping, is an activity that involves a person jumping from a great height while connected to a large elastic cord. The launching pad is usually erected on a tall structure such as a building or crane, a bridge across a deep ravine, or on a natural geographic feature such as a cliff. It is also possible to jump from a type of aircraft that has the ability to hover above the ground, such as a hot-air balloon or helicopter.

History

The modern sport was inspired by the "land diving" ritual of young men on Pentecost Island in Vanuatu. The first modern jumps were made on 1 April 1979 from the Clifton Suspension Bridge in Bristol, England, by members of the Oxford University Dangerous Sports Club. New Zealander A. J. Hackett popularised and commercialised the activity, opening the world's first permanent commercial site at the Kawarau Gorge Suspension Bridge near Queenstown in 1988.

Equipment and safety

The elastic cord is a braided shock cord made of many latex strands. Jumpers are fitted with an ankle harness, and sometimes a body harness, and cord length is matched to the jumper's weight so that the cord stretches without the jumper striking the ground or water below. Commercial operations are governed by codes of practice in most countries where the sport is offered.
