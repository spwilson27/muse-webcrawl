László Lovász

László Lovász (born 9 March 1948) is a Hungarian mathematician best known for his work in combinatorics, graph theory, and theoretical computer science. A child prodigy inspired by Paul Erdős, he won gold medals at the International Mathematical Olympiads in the 1960s.

Work

Lovász's contributions include the Lovász local lemma in probability, the LLL lattice basis reduction algorithm (with Arjen and Hendrik Lenstra), the ellipsoid method applied to combinatorial optimisation, the theory of graph limits (graphons), and work on perfect graphs and the Shannon capacity of graphs (the Lovász number). He held positions at Eötvös Loránd University in Budapest and at Yale University, and worked at Microsoft Research. He served as president of the International Mathematical Union (2007–2010) and of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences (2014–2020).

Awards

He shared the Abel Prize in 2021 with Avi Wigderson "for their foundational contributions to theoretical computer science and discrete mathematics". He has also received the Wolf Prize, the Knuth Prize, the Gödel Prize, and a Kyoto Prize.
