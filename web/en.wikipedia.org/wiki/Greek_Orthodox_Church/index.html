The term Greek Orthodox Church has several related uses. In its widest sense it denotes the body of Eastern Orthodox churches that historically used Koine Greek in their liturgy and are in full communion with the Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople. More specifically, it commonly refers to the autocephalous Church of Greece and the Church of Cyprus, and to Greek-speaking patriarchates such as those of Alexandria, Antioch and Jerusalem.

History

Christianity spread early in the Greek-speaking world: the Apostle Paul preached in Corinth, Thessalonica and Athens in the 1st century AD. After the East–West Schism of 1054, the Greek-speaking churches of the Byzantine Empire formed the core of Eastern Orthodoxy. During the centuries of Ottoman rule, the Ecumenical Patriarch exercised wide civil as well as religious authority over Orthodox Christians through the millet system.

The Church of Greece declared its autocephaly in 1833, following Greek independence, and this was recognised by the Ecumenical Patriarchate in the Tomos of 1850. The Church of Cyprus, one of the most ancient autocephalous churches, traces its independence to the Council of Ephesus in 431.

Organisation

The Church of Greece is governed by a Holy Synod of metropolitans chaired by the Archbishop of Athens and All Greece. The New Lands dioceses of northern Greece remain nominally under the spiritual jurisdiction of the Ecumenical Patriarchate. Monasteries on Mount Athos form a self-governing monastic community under the Patriarchate's spiritual jurisdiction.

Beliefs and worship

Greek Orthodox doctrine is that of Eastern Orthodoxy generally: Scripture and Holy Tradition, the seven sacraments, veneration of icons, and the Divine Liturgy, usually that of St John Chrysostom, celebrated in Koine Greek (and, in the diaspora, often partly in local languages). Orthodoxy is the prevailing religion of Greece, recognised in the Greek constitution as the country's "prevailing religion".
