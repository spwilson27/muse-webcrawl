Reinforcement learning from human feedback

RLHF
FieldMachine learning, AI alignment
Notable usesInstructGPT, ChatGPT, Claude

Reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF) is a technique to train a machine learning model — especially a large language model — using direct feedback from humans, in order to align the model's behaviour with human preferences and values. It became widely known as the key method behind the alignment of models such as OpenAI's InstructGPT and ChatGPT.

Method

The standard RLHF pipeline for language models has three stages. First, a pretrained model is fine-tuned with supervised learning on human-written demonstrations. Second, human annotators rank or compare multiple model outputs for the same prompt, and these comparisons train a reward model that predicts human preferences. Third, the language model is optimised against the reward model using a reinforcement learning algorithm, commonly proximal policy optimization (PPO), with a KL penalty to prevent the policy from drifting too far from the supervised model.

History and variants

The approach grew out of work on learning from human preferences in robotics and simulated control (e.g. Christiano et al., 2017) and was applied to language tasks such as summarisation before InstructGPT (2022) demonstrated broad instruction-following gains. Limitations include reward hacking, annotator bias and cost, motivating variants such as reinforcement learning from AI feedback (RLAIF) and direct preference optimization (DPO), which skips the explicit reward model.
