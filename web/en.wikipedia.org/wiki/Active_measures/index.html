Active measures (Russian: активные мероприятия, aktivnye meropriyatiya) is a term for the political warfare conducted by the security services of the Soviet Union and later Russia, aimed at influencing world events and the policies of other countries. Unlike passive espionage, active measures seek to shape events themselves through disinformation, propaganda, forgery, front organisations, and the manipulation of media, political movements and individuals abroad.

Forged documents attributed to official sources were a classic tool of Soviet active measures.

History

The practice dates to the 1920s under the Cheka and was expanded by the KGB's Service A. Notable Cold War examples include Operation INFEKTION, which spread the false claim that the United States created HIV/AIDS; forged documents designed to discredit Western leaders; and support for front organisations and "agents of influence" in foreign media and politics. Archival evidence, including the Mitrokhin Archive, documented the scale of these operations.

Modern usage

Analysts describe contemporary Russian information operations — state media, social-media manipulation and election-interference campaigns documented in the 2010s — as descendants of Soviet active measures, adapted to the internet age. The concept is related to, but broader than, "hybrid warfare" and reflexive control.
