The White Devil is a revenge tragedy by English playwright John Webster, first performed and published in 1612. Loosely based on the real Italian scandal of Vittoria Accoramboni, murdered in Padua in 1585, the play is regarded—together with Webster's The Duchess of Malfi—as a masterpiece of Jacobean tragedy.

Plot

Count Bracciano, aided by the scheming Flamineo, murders his wife Isabella and Vittoria's husband Camillo so that he may marry Vittoria Accoramboni. Vittoria is tried for murder and adultery in a famous arraignment scene and is sentenced to a house of convertites. Cardinal Monticelso, later elected Pope Paul IV, engineers revenge: Bracciano is poisoned by means of his helmet, and the disguised revenger Lodovico and his accomplices murder Vittoria, Flamineo, and their mother Cornelia. The play ends with the young Giovanni observing the carnage and resolving to let these deeds teach him "miserable events".

Reception

Webster noted in his preface that the play failed with its first audience at the Red Bull Theatre, performed in winter before an unreceptive crowd. Its reputation grew steadily; modern critics praise its poetry, its sardonic moral vision, and its powerful female protagonist, and it is regularly revived.
