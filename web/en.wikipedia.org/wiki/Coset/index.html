Coset

Coset
FieldAbstract algebra (group theory)
NotationgH (left coset), Hg (right coset)
Key resultLagrange's theorem

In mathematics, specifically group theory, a coset is a subset formed by multiplying all elements of a subgroup by a fixed element of the group. If H is a subgroup of a group G and g is an element of G, then gH = { gh : h ∈ H } is a left coset of H, and Hg = { hg : h ∈ H } is a right coset. Cosets are among the most fundamental constructions in group theory.

Properties

Two left cosets of H are either identical or disjoint, so the left cosets partition the group G. Every coset has the same number of elements as H itself. Consequently the order of H divides the order of G — this is Lagrange's theorem. The number of distinct left cosets (equal to the number of distinct right cosets) is called the index of H in G, written [G : H].

Examples

In the additive group of integers, the cosets of the subgroup 3ℤ of multiples of three are the residue classes 3ℤ, 1 + 3ℤ and 2 + 3ℤ — the basis of modular arithmetic. In a permutation group, cosets of a point stabiliser correspond to the images of that point.

Quotient groups and normal subgroups

If H is a normal subgroup, its left and right cosets coincide, and the set of cosets itself forms a group under the operation gH · kH = (gk)H: the quotient group G/H. For a general subgroup, the coset space G/H carries a natural action of G, central to the study of group actions, homogeneous spaces and, in geometry, spaces such as spheres viewed as coset spaces of rotation groups.
