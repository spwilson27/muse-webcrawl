Legal rights

Legal rights are laws that protect people, recognised and enforced by a legal system. They are distinguished from natural rights, which are held to exist independently of government recognition, though the two categories often overlap.

Concepts

Legal rights may be constitutional, statutory, or derived from common law and precedent. Examples include the right to a fair trial, freedom of expression as protected by a constitution, property rights, and contractual rights. In Wesley Hohfeld's influential analysis, rights involve correlative duties and can be analysed as claims, liberties, powers, and immunities.

Legal vs natural rights

Thinkers in the natural law tradition, such as John Locke, argued that certain rights precede government. Legal positivists such as Jeremy Bentham and John Austin held that rights are creations of law. Modern human rights law combines both views: international treaties declare universal rights and require states to protect them in domestic law.
