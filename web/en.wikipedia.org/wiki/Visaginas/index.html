Visaginas

Visaginas is a city in northeastern Lithuania, in Utena County, near the borders with Latvia and Belarus. It is best known as the town built to house workers of the Ignalina Nuclear Power Plant.

History

The settlement was founded in 1975 as Sniečkus, named after Antanas Sniečkus, the long-time leader of the Lithuanian Communist Party. It was constructed from scratch on the shore of Lake Visaginas to accommodate workers constructing and operating the Ignalina plant, whose two RBMK reactors came online in 1983 and 1987. After Lithuanian independence the town was renamed Visaginas in 1992 and granted city rights in 1995.

Demographics

Visaginas is notable for its ethnically diverse, largely Russian-speaking population, a legacy of Soviet-era migration of plant workers from across the USSR. Ethnic Russians, Lithuanians, Belarusians, Ukrainians and Poles all form significant communities.

Ignalina Nuclear Power Plant

Both reactors were shut down — in 2004 and 2009 — as a condition of Lithuania's accession to the European Union, transforming the town's economy and prompting programmes for social and economic conversion, including development around Lake Visaginas as a recreational area.
