Galaxy merger

TypeAstronomical interaction
TimescaleHundreds of millions of years
ExamplesAntennae Galaxies; Mice Galaxies; Arp 220

A galaxy merger occurs when two or more galaxies collide and coalesce under their mutual gravity. Although stars rarely collide directly because of the vast distances between them, the gravitational interaction distorts the galaxies, draws out tidal tails and bridges, and funnels gas toward the centres.

Effects

Mergers commonly trigger intense bursts of star formation ("starbursts") and can feed central supermassive black holes, producing active galactic nuclei. Over time the merged remnant often relaxes into an elliptical galaxy. Major mergers involve galaxies of comparable mass, while minor mergers involve a large galaxy absorbing a much smaller companion.

Examples and the Milky Way

Famous interacting systems include the Antennae Galaxies (NGC 4038/4039) and the Mice Galaxies (NGC 4676). The Milky Way has consumed several dwarf galaxies in the past and is expected to merge with the Andromeda Galaxy in roughly 4–5 billion years.
