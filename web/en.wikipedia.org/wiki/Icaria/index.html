Icaria, also spelled Ikaria (Greek: Ικαρία), is a Greek island in the Aegean Sea, southwest of Samos. Named after Icarus of Greek mythology, who is said to have fallen into the sea nearby, it is noted for the longevity of its inhabitants and is classified as a "Blue Zone" — a region where people live exceptionally long lives.

Icaria's mountainous, green interior drops steeply to the Aegean.

Geography

The island is elongated, roughly 40 km long, and dominated by the Atheras mountain range. Its main ports are Agios Kirykos in the southeast and Evdilos in the north. Icaria is administratively a regional unit of the North Aegean region, comprising the island itself and the small Fourni archipelago.

History

Inhabited since prehistory and colonised by Milesians in antiquity, Icaria passed through Byzantine, Genoese, and Ottoman rule before joining Greece in 1912 — briefly declaring itself the independent Free State of Icaria during the First Balkan War under Dr. Ioannis Malachias. In the mid-20th century the Greek government exiled thousands of communists to the island, an experience that left a lasting mark on local political culture.

Longevity

Studies popularised by Dan Buettner found Icarians reach age 90 at unusually high rates with low dementia, attributing this to a Mediterranean diet, herbal teas, daily movement in steep terrain, social life centred on village festivals (panigyria), and a relaxed attitude to time.
