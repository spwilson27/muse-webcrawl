The Deutsches Eck ("German Corner") is a headland in Koblenz, Germany, where the Moselle river joins the Rhine. It is named after a nearby commandery of the Teutonic Order (Deutscher Orden). A monumental equestrian statue of Kaiser Wilhelm I, the first German Emperor, dominates the site and makes it one of Germany's best-known landmarks and symbols of German unity.

History

The site takes its name from the Teutonic Knights, who established a commandery (the Deutschherrenhaus) nearby around 1216, at the invitation of the Archbishop of Trier. The artificial headland itself was created during works to make the Moselle navigable in the late 19th century.

The Wilhelm I monument

After Wilhelm I died in 1888, the government of the Rhine Province resolved to build a grand monument to him. Designed by architect Bruno Schmitz and sculptor Emil Hundrieser, the 37-metre-high monument with its 14-metre equestrian statue was inaugurated on 31 August 1897 in the presence of Kaiser Wilhelm II. The statue was destroyed by American artillery fire in March 1945; the empty pedestal later served as a memorial to German unity, flying the flags of the German states. A replica statue, funded by a local publishing couple, was re-erected on 2 September 1993.

Today

The Deutsches Eck is a major tourist attraction and a venue for open-air concerts and events. Flagpoles along the promontory display the flags of the German federal states and of Europe.
