The New York Times Book Review (NYTBR) is a weekly paper-magazine supplement to The New York Times in which current non-fiction and fiction books are reviewed. First published on October 10, 1896, it is one of the most influential and widely read book-review publications in the United States, with offices located near Times Square in Manhattan, New York City.

Overview

The Book Review appears in the Sunday edition of the Times and is also sold separately. Each week the editorial office receives a large volume of books — historically hundreds of titles per week — from which a comparatively small selection is chosen for review. Reviews are commissioned from freelance critics, writers and subject specialists; the section also publishes author interviews, essays on literary topics, a column on new releases, and letters from readers. The current version of the magazine is edited by Gilbert Cruz, who became editor in 2022, succeeding Pamela Paul.

Bestseller lists

The Book Review is responsible for compiling the New York Times Best Seller lists, first published in 1931, which rank the week's best-selling books in categories including hardcover fiction, hardcover nonfiction, paperback, children's and graphic books. Placement on the list is widely used in book marketing, and its methodology — based on sales reports from a confidential sample of retailers — has periodically been the subject of dispute and litigation by authors who felt excluded.

Notable features

Annual features of the Book Review include the "10 Best Books" list and the larger selection of "Notable Books" of the year. The long-running "By the Book" column interviews writers about their reading habits. Since 2004 the Book Review has also produced a companion podcast, The Book Review Podcast. The section's reviews are archived online, with an archive of reviews extending back to 1851 through the Times' broader books coverage.

Influence

A review in the Book Review has traditionally been regarded as one of the most consequential in American publishing, capable of significantly affecting a book's sales and an author's reputation. The publication's critics and judgments have at times been controversial, and its perceived literary tastes — sometimes characterised as middlebrow — have been debated within the literary world, but it remains the most prominent general-interest book review venue in the United States.
