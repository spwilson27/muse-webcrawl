Leeds is a city in West Yorkshire, England, on the River Aire. It is the largest city in Yorkshire and the principal centre of the Leeds City Region. Historically part of the West Riding of Yorkshire, Leeds grew from a manorial borough in the 13th century into one of Britain's great industrial cities through the woollen cloth trade.

History

Leeds was recorded as "Ledes" in early medieval sources and in the Domesday Book. Granted a market charter in 1207, it became a hub of the West Riding woollen industry, with cloth halls — including the White Cloth Hall of 1775 — handling trade for the region. Canals (the Leeds and Liverpool Canal, completed 1816) and railways powered further growth in textiles, engineering and ready-made clothing; firms such as Marks & Spencer (founded as a Leeds market stall in 1884) and Burton originated here.

Economy

After the decline of manufacturing, Leeds reinvented itself as the North of England's leading financial and legal centre, with the largest financial services sector outside London, alongside digital, healthcare and retail strengths. Landmarks include the Royal Armouries Museum, Leeds Kirkgate Market — one of Europe's largest indoor markets — the Victorian Quarter arcades, and Millennium Square.

Culture and education

The city is home to the Universities of Leeds (founded 1904), Leeds Beckett and Leeds Trinity, and to cultural institutions including Opera North, Northern Ballet and the Leeds Playhouse. The Leeds Festival and the annual Light Night are major events. In sport, Leeds United football club plays at Elland Road, Leeds Rhinos contest rugby league at Headingley, and Yorkshire County Cricket Club is also based at Headingley.
