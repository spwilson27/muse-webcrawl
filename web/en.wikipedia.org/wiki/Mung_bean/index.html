Mung bean

Scientific nameVigna radiata
FamilyFabaceae
OriginSouth Asia

The mung bean (Vigna radiata) is a legume cultivated for its small, green seeds. Native to the Indian subcontinent, it is widely grown across South, East, and Southeast Asia, principally in India, Myanmar, China, and Thailand.

Cultivation

Mung bean is a warm-season annual with a short growing season of about 60–90 days. It is often grown in rotation with cereals; like other legumes, it fixes atmospheric nitrogen in symbiosis with root bacteria, improving soil fertility. The seeds are harvested from dry pods or picked green.

Culinary uses

Whole or split and hulled (as yellow mung dal), the beans are cooked in dals, soups, and curries. Sprouted mung beans are a familiar vegetable in East and Southeast Asian cooking. Ground mung bean starch makes glass noodles (cellophane noodles) and the Filipino and Chinese dessert fillings; mung bean paste sweetens mooncakes and other pastries, and the beans are the base of the Vietnamese dessert chè and the Korean pancake bindae-tteok.

Nutrition

Mung beans are rich in protein, folate, fibre, and several minerals, and are easily digested compared with many other pulses.
