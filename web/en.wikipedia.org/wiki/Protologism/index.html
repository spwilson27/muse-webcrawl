Protologism

Protologism
FieldLexicology, word formation
Term introducedMikhail Epstein, early 2000s
RelationPrecedes neologism; precedes nonce word in uptake

A protologism is a newly coined word or expression that has been invented but has not yet been accepted into mainstream language. The term was coined by the scholar Mikhail Epstein to fill a lexical gap: while a neologism is a new word that has begun to spread, a protologism exists only at the moment of its invention — it is a word "in the first stage of its life", proposed by its coiner but not yet used by others.

Distinction from related terms

A nonce word is created for a single occasion and may never be reused; a protologism, by contrast, is consciously offered for general adoption, for instance to name a new phenomenon or to enrich the language. If a protologism is picked up by a speech community and gains currency, it graduates to the status of a neologism, and eventually may become an ordinary dictionary headword. Most protologisms never progress beyond their inventor.

Examples and practice

Epstein himself proposed many English protologisms, such as "linguicide" and "vocabularity", to demonstrate how deliberate word-coining can expand expressive resources. Deliberate coinage is common in science and technology, where terms like "quark" began as one person's proposal, and in internet culture, where coined words can spread rapidly or vanish. Lexicographers generally wait for evidence of independent use before treating a coinage as established.

Self-reference

Fittingly, the word "protologism" itself began as a protologism and has been discussed mainly in the literature on word formation; whether it has fully entered the general vocabulary remains debatable.
