Ayub Khan

Muhammad Ayub Khan (14 May 1907 – 19 April 1974) was a Pakistani military officer and statesman who served as the second president of Pakistan from 1958 to 1969. A field marshal of the Pakistan Army, he seized power in the 1958 Pakistani coup d'état, becoming the country's first military ruler.

Early life and military career

Ayub Khan was born in Rehana village near Haripur in the North-West Frontier Province of British India. He was educated at Aligarh Muslim University and the Royal Military College, Sandhurst, and was commissioned into the British Indian Army in 1928. He served in the Second World War and, after the Partition of India in 1947, rose rapidly in the new Pakistan Army, becoming its first native commander-in-chief in 1951.

Presidency

In October 1958 Ayub abrogated the constitution and imposed martial law, soon assuming the presidency. His rule brought political stability and rapid economic growth — the 1960s were later called Pakistan's "decade of development" — along with land reforms, the construction of the new capital Islamabad, and the system of "Basic Democracies". Pakistan fought the Indo-Pakistani War of 1965 during his tenure, which ended in a stalemate and the Tashkent Declaration.

Growing inequality, discontent in East Pakistan, and mass protests in 1968–69 forced Ayub to resign in March 1969, handing power to General Yahya Khan. He died in 1974.
