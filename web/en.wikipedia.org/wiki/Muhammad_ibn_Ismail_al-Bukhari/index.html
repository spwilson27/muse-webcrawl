Muhammad ibn Isma'il al-Bukhari (Arabic: محمد بن إسماعيل البخاري; 21 July 810 – 1 September 870), commonly known as Imam al-Bukhari, was a Persian-born Islamic scholar from Bukhara in Central Asia. He is best known as the compiler of Sahih al-Bukhari, regarded by Sunni Muslims as the most authentic collection of hadith — the reports of the sayings and actions of the Prophet Muhammad.

Life and travels

Al-Bukhari lost his father as a child and was raised by his mother. Tradition recounts that he memorised vast numbers of hadith from a young age. At sixteen he made the pilgrimage to Mecca and remained in the Hijaz studying; thereafter he spent decades travelling through the Islamic world — to Nishapur, Baghdad, Damascus, Cairo, and many other cities — learning hadith from more than a thousand teachers and collecting reports.

Sahih al-Bukhari

Over about sixteen years, al-Bukhari distilled his enormous collection — tradition puts the number of reports he examined in the hundreds of thousands — into the Sahih, applying strict criteria for the reliability of each chain of transmission (isnad) and for the text of each report. The work contains roughly 7,500 hadith including repetitions (about 2,600 without repetition), arranged by legal and theological topic. He also wrote works of hadith criticism and biography, notably Al-Tarikh al-Kabir, and the devotional collection Al-Adab al-Mufrad.

Al-Bukhari died in exile in Khartank, near Samarkand, in 870, after a dispute with the governor of Bukhara. His tomb there remains a major pilgrimage site.
