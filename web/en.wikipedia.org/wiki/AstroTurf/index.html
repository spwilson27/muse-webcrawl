AstroTurf

Product typeArtificial turf
Introduced1965 (as ChemGrass)
First major useHouston Astrodome, 1966

AstroTurf is an American subsidiary that produces artificial turf for playing surfaces in sports. The original AstroTurf product was a short-pile synthetic carpet invented in 1965 by Monsanto employees. It was first installed at scale in the Houston Astrodome in 1966, after natural grass failed under the domed roof, giving the product its name.

History

Developed as ChemGrass by Monsanto and first tested at Moses Brown School in Rhode Island, the surface was renamed AstroTurf after its Astrodome debut. It spread widely in indoor and multi-purpose stadiums in the 1970s and 1980s in American football, baseball, and association football.

Characteristics and criticism

Early AstroTurf was hard, abrasive, and hot, and was blamed for increased injuries such as “turf toe” and skin burns. Later generations of artificial turf, including competitors such as FieldTurf, used longer fibres with sand and rubber infill to mimic grass more closely. The word astroturfing later entered politics to mean artificially manufactured grassroots campaigns — a metaphor derived from the fake grass.
