Kraków Cloth Hall

Kraków Cloth Hall (Sukiennice)
LocationMain Market Square, Kraków, Poland
Polish nameSukiennice
Built13th–14th centuries; rebuilt in Renaissance style 1555–1559
Now housesMarket stalls (ground floor); Gallery of 19th-Century Polish Art (upper floor); Rynek Underground museum (below)

The Kraków Cloth Hall (Polish: Sukiennice) is a Renaissance market hall standing in the centre of the Main Market Square in the Old Town of Kraków, Poland. One of the city's most recognisable landmarks, it was for centuries a major hub of international trade in cloth, lead, salt, and spices, and remains a working market today.

History

A cloth hall stood on the square from the 13th century, when Kraków received its city charter in 1257. The Gothic hall built under Casimir III the Great burned in 1555 and was rebuilt in Renaissance style with an ornate parapet decorated with masks, arcaded loggias, and side annexes. Merchant stalls lined its long central passage, and the hall hosted royal balls and festivities—most famously a ball for the wedding of Bona Sforza. In the 1870s the building was renovated to a design by Tomasz Pryliński; the upper floor then became home to the first branch of the National Museum.

Today

The ground floor still houses rows of stalls selling amber, folk crafts, and souvenirs. Upstairs, the Gallery of 19th-Century Polish Art displays works by Jan Matejko, Józef Chełmoński, and others. Since 2010 the Rynek Underground museum, excavated beneath the square, has presented medieval Kraków around the hall's foundations. The Cloth Hall forms part of the Kraków Old Town UNESCO World Heritage Site, inscribed in 1978.
