Sturmgeschütz III

The Sturmgeschütz III (StuG III) was an assault gun developed by Nazi Germany during the Second World War. Built on the chassis of the Panzer III tank, it was the most-produced German fully tracked armoured fighting vehicle of the war, with around 10,000 examples manufactured, and it served in infantry-support, anti-tank and defensive roles on all fronts.

Development

The StuG concept originated in 1936 from a proposal by Colonel Erich von Manstein for armoured assault artillery to support infantry attacks on fortified positions. Daimler-Benz developed the vehicle on the Panzer III chassis, mounting the gun in a low, turretless casemate superstructure. Early models carried the short-barrelled 7.5 cm StuK 37 L/24 howitzer.

Combat history

StuG IIIs first saw action in the Battle of France in 1940. From 1942 the long-barrelled StuK 40 gun made the vehicle an effective tank destroyer, and it became a mainstay of German defensive operations, particularly on the Eastern Front. Its low profile made it easy to conceal in ambush positions. After the war, captured and surplus vehicles served in several armies, including those of Finland, Syria and Spain.
