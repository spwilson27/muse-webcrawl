The Louisville Cardinals football team represents the University of Louisville in the sport of American football. The Cardinals compete in the Football Bowl Subdivision (FBS) of the NCAA and the Atlantic Coast Conference, which they joined in 2014.

History

Football at Louisville dates to 1912. The program rose to national prominence under coach Howard Schnellenberger in the late 1980s and early 1990s, culminating in a victory in the 1991 Fiesta Bowl over Alabama. Under John L. Smith and Bobby Petrino, the Cardinals became known for explosive offenses. The program's signature moment came in the 2013 Sugar Bowl, when Louisville upset Florida 33–23 behind quarterback Teddy Bridgewater. Quarterback Lamar Jackson won the Heisman Trophy in 2016, the first in school history.

Stadium

The Cardinals play at L&N Federal Credit Union Stadium, an on-campus venue that opened in 1998 as Papa John's Cardinal Stadium and has been expanded to a capacity of over 60,000.

Rivalries

Louisville's principal rival is the Kentucky Wildcats, contested annually for the Governor's Cup. The Cardinals also have a longstanding rivalry with Cincinnati for the Keg of Nails.
