Jimi Hendrix

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Jimi Hendrix at Woodstock 1969: white fringed shirt, red bandana, playing a white Stratocaster upside-down through a wall of Marshall amplifiers]

James Marshall 'Jimi' Hendrix (born Johnny Allen Hendrix; 27 November 1942 – 18 September 1970) was an American guitarist, songwriter and singer. Although his mainstream career spanned only four years, he is widely regarded as the greatest and one of the most influential electric guitarists in the history of popular music, and one of the most celebrated musicians of the 20th century. The Rock and Roll Hall of Fame describes him as 'arguably the greatest instrumentalist in the history of rock music'.

A Seattle-born sideman for the Isley Brothers and Little Richard, Hendrix exploded in London in 1966 with the Jimi Hendrix Experience: Are You Experienced (1967), 'Purple Haze', 'The Wind Cries Mary' and a version of 'All Along the Watchtower' that Dylan himself adopted. His feedback, wah-wah and studio experimentation rewired the guitar's possibilities; his Woodstock performance of 'The Star-Spangled Banner' (1969) became an era's emblem. He died in London at 27 after an overdose of sleeping pills.
