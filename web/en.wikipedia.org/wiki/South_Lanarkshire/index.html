South Lanarkshire (Scots: Sooth Lanrikshire; Scottish Gaelic: Siorrachd Lannraig a Deas) is one of 32 unitary council areas of Scotland. It borders the city of Glasgow to the south-east and includes towns such as Hamilton (the administrative centre), East Kilbride, Rutherglen, Cambuslang, Lanark, and Larkhall. The area covers much of the historic county of Lanarkshire outside Glasgow.

Geography

The council area stretches from the urban fringe of Glasgow down the Clyde Valley to rural uplands in the south. The River Clyde flows through it, passing the Falls of Clyde near Lanark, a notable scenic and nature reserve. New Lanark, a restored 18th-century mill village, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

South Lanarkshire Council was created in 1996 from the districts of Hamilton, East Kilbride, Clydesdale, and parts of Glasgow district. East Kilbride, designated in 1947, was Scotland's first new town and remains one of its largest towns.
