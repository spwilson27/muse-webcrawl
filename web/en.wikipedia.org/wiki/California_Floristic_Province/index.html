California Floristic Province

The California Floristic Province (CFP) is a floristic province with a Mediterranean-type climate located on the Pacific Coast of North America, with a distinctive flora similar to other regions with a winter rainfall and summer drought climate like the Mediterranean Basin. This biodiversity hotspot is known for being home to the endemic giant sequoia and coast redwood trees, as well as a host of other endemic plant species.

Geography

The province covers most of the U.S. state of California, extends into southwestern Oregon, a portion of western Nevada in the Sierra Nevada, and part of northern Baja California in Mexico. It is bounded by the Pacific Ocean to the west, the Sierra Nevada and Cascade ranges to the east, and transverse and peninsular ranges to the south. The region includes a wide variety of habitats, from coastal sage scrub and chaparral to oak woodlands, grasslands, and mixed evergreen and conifer forests.

Biodiversity

The California Floristic Province is recognized as a world biodiversity hotspot by Conservation International. It contains more than 3,400 terrestrial plant species, of which over 2,100 are endemic, found nowhere else on Earth. Notable endemics include the giant sequoia (Sequoiadendron giganteum), coast redwood (Sequoia sempervirens), and numerous species of manzanita, ceanothus, and wildflowers such as the California poppy, the state flower. The province has lost a large fraction of its original habitat to agriculture, urbanization, and altered fire regimes, making conservation of the remaining area a priority.
