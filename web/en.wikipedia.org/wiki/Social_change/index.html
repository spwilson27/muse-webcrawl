Social change

Social change is the alteration of the social order of a society — changes in institutions, social structures, cultural norms, values and behaviours over time. It is a central subject in sociology, studied from the classical theories of Karl Marx, Max Weber and Émile Durkheim to modern analyses of globalisation and digital technology.

Causes

Classic theories emphasise: conflict between classes and interests (Marx); rationalisation and changing values (Weber's Protestant ethic thesis); technological innovation; demographic shifts such as urbanisation and ageing; environmental pressures; and collective action by social movements. William Ogburn proposed the concept of "cultural lag", in which material culture changes faster than the norms that govern it.

Forms

Change may be gradual and evolutionary, or abrupt and revolutionary. It can be intended (reform, revolution, policy) or unintended (the side-effects of industrialisation or new media). Modernisation theory, dependency theory and world-systems analysis offer competing accounts of change in developing societies.

Contemporary drivers

Digital communication, climate change, demographic transition and shifting gender and family norms are among the most studied drivers of social change in the 21st century.
