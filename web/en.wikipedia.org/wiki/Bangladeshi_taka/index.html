Bangladeshi taka

The taka (Bengali: টাকা, sign: ৳, code: BDT) is the currency of Bangladesh. In Unicode, it is encoded at U+09F3 ৳. Issuance of banknotes ৳10 and larger is controlled by Bangladesh Bank, the central bank; the ৳1, ৳2 and ৳5 notes and all coins are the responsibility of the Ministry of Finance. The most commonly used symbol for the taka is "৳". It is divided into 100 poisha, though poisha coins are now rare.

History

Following independence in 1971, Bangladesh initially continued to use the Pakistani rupee, overprinted with "Bangladesh". The taka was formally introduced on 4 March 1972 at par with the rupee. The word taka derives from the Sanskrit tanka, historically used for stamped coinage across South Asia; in Bengal the term had long been used generically for money.

Coins

Coins have been issued in denominations from 1 poisha to ৳5. Smaller poisha denominations have effectively disappeared from circulation due to inflation, and current circulation coins are chiefly ৳1, ৳2 and ৳5.

Banknotes

Banknotes circulate in denominations of ৳2, ৳5, ৳10, ৳20, ৳50, ৳100, ৳200, ৳500 and ৳1,000. Notes characteristically feature the portrait of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, the founding leader of Bangladesh, and images such as the National Parliament House, mosques, and wildlife including the Bengal tiger. A ৳200 note was issued in 2020 to mark the birth centenary of Sheikh Mujibur Rahman.
