Cairns (/kænz/; locally /ˈkeənz/) is a city in the Cairns Region, Queensland, Australia, on the tropical north-east coast of far north Queensland. Founded in 1876 and named in honour of the state governor William Cairns, it served as a port for the Hodgkinson goldfields; the city now has a population of roughly 155,000 and is the 14th-largest city in Australia.

Geography and climate

Cairns lies on Trinity Bay, hemmed in by the rainforest-clad mountains of the Great Dividing Range, about 1,700 km north of Brisbane. It has a tropical monsoon climate with a hot, humid wet season from November to May — including the risk of tropical cyclones — and a mild, dry winter. Before European settlement the area was inhabited for tens of thousands of years by the Yidinji and Djabugay Aboriginal peoples.

Economy and tourism

Tourism is the city's dominant industry: Cairns is the principal gateway to two World Heritage areas, the Great Barrier Reef and the Wet Tropics rainforests, with reef boats departing daily from the marina and the Kuranda Scenic Railway and Skyrail climbing into the ranges. Cairns Airport is Australia's seventh-busiest. Sugar cane, bananas and other tropical agriculture, fishing and a naval base (HMAS Cairns) also contribute. During the Second World War the city was a staging base for the Battle of the Coral Sea.
