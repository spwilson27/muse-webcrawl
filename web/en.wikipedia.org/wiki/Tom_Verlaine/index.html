Tom Verlaine (born Thomas Joseph Miller; December 13, 1949 – January 28, 2023) was an American singer, songwriter and guitarist, best known as the frontman of the rock band Television. Verlaine's poetic lyrics and intricate, intertwining guitar work with Richard Lloyd were central to the band's influential 1977 debut album Marquee Moon, widely regarded as one of the greatest rock albums of all time.

Early life

Miller was born in Morristown, New Jersey, and grew up in Wilmington, Delaware. He took his stage name from the French symbolist poet Paul Verlaine. At school he befriended Richard Hell, with whom he later formed the Neon Boys and then Television.

Television

Television formed in New York City in 1973 and became one of the foundational acts of the CBGB scene, alongside acts such as the Ramones, Patti Smith and Talking Heads. The band released Marquee Moon (1977) and Adventure (1978) before splitting; they later reunited for a self-titled third album in 1992 and toured intermittently thereafter.

Solo career

Verlaine released a series of solo albums beginning with Tom Verlaine (1979), including Dreamtime (1981), Words from the Front (1982) and Flash Light (1987). His solo work continued his distinctive guitar style, characterised by vibrato, harmonics and jazz-influenced improvisation.

Legacy

Verlaine is frequently cited among the most influential guitarists in alternative rock; Rolling Stone ranked him among the greatest guitarists of all time. He died in New York City on 28 January 2023, aged 73.
