Mencey

Mencey was the title of the kings or chiefs of the Guanches, the indigenous people of Tenerife in the Canary Islands, before the Spanish conquest of the island in the 1490s. Each mencey ruled a territory called a menceyato. At the time of the conquest Tenerife was divided into nine menceyatos, a division traditionally attributed to the partition of the island among the nine sons of the last unified ruler, Tinerfe.

The nine menceyatos

The nine kingdoms were Taoro, Güímar, Abona, Adeje, Daute, Icod, Tacoronte, Tegueste and Anaga. Taoro, ruled by the mencey Bencomo, was the largest and most powerful. When the Castilian forces of Alonso Fernández de Lugo invaded in 1494, the menceyes were divided: those of the north (Taoro, Tacoronte, Tegueste, Icod and Daute) formed a war league, while the southern kingdoms submitted to or allied with the Spanish. The northern alliance won a famous victory at the First Battle of Acentejo (1494) but was defeated at Aguere and the Second Battle of Acentejo (1495), after which Tenerife was incorporated into the Crown of Castile.

Legacy

The menceyes remain symbols of Canarian identity. Bronze statues of the nine menceyes stand beside the Basilica of Candelaria, and their names — Bencomo, Bentor, Pelicar, and others — survive in place names, schools and cultural associations across Tenerife.
