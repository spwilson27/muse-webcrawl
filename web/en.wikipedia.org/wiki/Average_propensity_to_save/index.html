Average propensity to save

Average propensity to save
FormulaAPS = Saving ÷ Income (S/Y)
Related conceptsAPC, MPS, MPC
IdentityAPC + APS = 1

In Keynesian economics, the average propensity to save (APS), also known as the saving ratio, is the proportion of income which is saved, rather than spent on goods and services. Put otherwise, it is the ratio of total savings to total disposable income, APS = S/Y.

Properties

Because income is either consumed or saved, the average propensity to save and the average propensity to consume (APC) sum to one: APC + APS = 1. As income rises, the APS generally increases, since consumption needs can be met while a larger share of additional income is saved; at very low incomes the APS can be negative, when households dissave by borrowing or drawing down assets. The marginal propensity to save (MPS) is the corresponding ratio for changes: MPS = ΔS/ΔY, and MPC + MPS = 1.
