The qilin (Chinese: 麒麟; pinyin: qílín) is a legendary hooved chimerical creature that appears in Chinese mythology, and is said to appear with the imminent arrival or passing of a sage or illustrious ruler. Qilin are a specific type of the lin mythological family of one-horned beasts. The qilin also appears in the mythologies of other East Asian cultures, including Japanese and Korean mythology, where it is known as the kirin and girin respectively.

Description

The qilin may be depicted with a wide range of features, but commonly it has Chinese dragon-like features: a head with antlers, eyes with thick eyelashes, a mane that always flows upward, and a beard. Its body is fully or partially scaled and often shaped like an ox, deer, or horse. It is usually shown with cloven hooves. In modern depictions, it often resembles a European unicorn, though in Chinese mythology the qilin's horn is not magical, and the creature is associated with water, earth, wood, metal, and fire — the five elements.

According to tradition, the qilin is so gentle that it does not walk on living grass or eat living vegetation, and it is able to walk on water. It is a peaceful creature whose appearance is considered a good omen, foretelling serenity and prosperity. It is said to live for a thousand years and appears at the birth or death of great sages — one is famously said to have appeared at the birth of Confucius.

History

References to the qilin appear in Chinese texts from as early as the 5th century BCE. In the Spring and Autumn Annals, traditionally attributed to Confucius, the capture of a lin in 481 BCE is recorded as the chronicle's final event. During the Ming dynasty, the qilin became associated with the giraffe after Zheng He's voyages brought giraffes back to China from East Africa; the association is still reflected in the Chinese word for giraffe, which in Korean and Japanese retains the name kirin.

In other cultures

  • Japan: The kirin is regarded as one of the holiest animals, ranking above the phoenix (hōō) and the dragon (ryū). It appears on the label of Kirin beer.
  • Korea: The girin appears in Korean folklore and is traditionally depicted similarly to its Chinese counterpart.
  • Vietnam: The kỳ lân is one of the four sacred creatures (tứ linh), alongside the dragon, phoenix, and turtle.
