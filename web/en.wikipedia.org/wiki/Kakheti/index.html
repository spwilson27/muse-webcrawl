Kakheti (Georgian: კახეთი) is a region (mkhare) in eastern Georgia, formed in the 1990s from the historical province of Kakheti and the small mountain province of Tusheti. Its capital and largest city is Telavi. Kakheti is Georgia's principal wine-producing region, home to the fertile Alazani River valley and some of the oldest continuously practiced winemaking traditions in the world.

Geography

The region lies between the Greater Caucasus range to the north and the Gombori ridge to the southwest, and borders Russia (Dagestan and Chechnya), Azerbaijan, and the Georgian regions of Kartli and Mtskheta-Mtianeti. The Alazani River flows southeast through its heartland toward the Mingachevir Reservoir in Azerbaijan. The far northeastern district of Tusheti, accessible only by a high seasonal pass, is a protected landscape of medieval stone tower villages.

History

Kakheti was an independent feudal kingdom from the late 15th century, with its capital first at Gremi and later at Telavi, though it was repeatedly devastated by invasions from Safavid Persia in the 17th century under Shah Abbas I. In 1762 it was united with neighbouring Kartli under King Erekle II, and in 1801 the combined kingdom was annexed by the Russian Empire.

Wine

Georgia is often called the "cradle of wine", with evidence of winemaking going back some 8,000 years, and Kakheti produces the majority of Georgian wine. The traditional method ferments and ages wine in large clay vessels called qvevri buried in the ground, a technique inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list in 2013. Famous appellations include Tsinandali, Kindzmarauli, Saperavi-based reds and amber (orange) wines made with extended skin contact.

Sights

Major attractions include the David Gareja cave monastery complex on the Azerbaijani border, the Nekresi and Ikalto monasteries, the cathedral of Alaverdi, the walled hill town of Sighnaghi, and the estate of Prince Alexander Chavchavadze at Tsinandali.
