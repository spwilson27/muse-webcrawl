Forensic linguistics

Forensic linguistics is the application of linguistic knowledge and methods to the law, legal proceedings and criminal investigations. The term was popularised by Jan Svartvik's 1968 study The Evans Statements, which re-examined statements attributed to Timothy Evans.

Areas of work

Forensic linguists work on authorship attribution — comparing disputed texts such as ransom notes, threatening messages or suicide notes with a suspect's known writing; on analysing the language of police interviews, confessions and courtroom interaction; and on disputes over the meaning of contracts, warnings, trademarks and legislation. Voice identification and transcription of disputed recordings are sometimes included but are more often classed under forensic phonetics.

Notable cases

Linguistic analysis contributed to the identification of the Unabomber, Theodore Kaczynski, after his brother recognised phrases in the published manifesto. In England, Derek Bentley's conviction was re-examined partly in light of linguistic analysis of his alleged statement. Forensic linguists stress that authorship conclusions are probabilistic and should be presented to courts with care.
