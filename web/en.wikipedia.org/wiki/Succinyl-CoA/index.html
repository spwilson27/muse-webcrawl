Succinyl-CoA

FormulaC25H40N7O19P3S
Molar mass867.61 g·mol−1
RoleCitric acid cycle intermediate

Succinyl-coenzyme A, commonly succinyl-CoA, is a high-energy thioester compound that occupies a central position in cellular metabolism. It consists of succinic acid linked through a thioester bond to coenzyme A, and it participates both in energy-yielding catabolism and in biosynthetic pathways.

Role in the citric acid cycle

Succinyl-CoA is formed in the mitochondrial matrix by the oxidative decarboxylation of alpha-ketoglutarate, catalysed by the alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase complex — a reaction that reduces NAD+ to NADH and releases carbon dioxide. It is then converted to succinate by succinyl-CoA synthetase (succinate thiokinase). The energy of the thioester bond drives the phosphorylation of GDP to GTP (or ADP to ATP in some tissues), the only substrate-level phosphorylation step of the citric acid cycle.

Other sources and uses

Succinyl-CoA is also generated during the catabolism of odd-chain fatty acids (via propionyl-CoA and methylmalonyl-CoA) and of the branched-chain amino acids valine and isoleucine and the amino acid methionine; inherited defects of methylmalonyl-CoA mutase cause methylmalonic acidemia. As a biosynthetic precursor, succinyl-CoA condenses with glycine in the first, rate-limiting step of haem synthesis, catalysed by ALA synthase, and it donates succinyl groups for protein succinylation, a post-translational modification.
