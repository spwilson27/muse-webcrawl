In mathematics, the general linear group of degree n, written GL(n, F), is the set of all invertible n × n matrices with entries in a field F, together with the operation of matrix multiplication. It is equivalently the group of automorphisms (invertible linear transformations) of the vector space Fn. A matrix belongs to the group precisely when its determinant is nonzero.

Properties

GL(n, F) is a group that is non-abelian for n ≥ 2. Its subgroup of matrices with determinant 1 is the special linear group SL(n, F). Other important subgroups include the orthogonal, unitary and symplectic groups (over ℝ and ℂ), which preserve bilinear forms.

Over the real and complex numbers

Over ℝ or ℂ, GL(n) is a Lie group of dimension n2, and an open subset of matrix space; it has two connected components over ℝ, distinguished by the sign of the determinant. It plays a foundational role in representation theory: a representation of a group is precisely a homomorphism into some GL(n), and linear groups provide the basic examples throughout algebra, geometry and physics.
