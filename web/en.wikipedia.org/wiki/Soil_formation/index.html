Soil formation

Soil formation, also called pedogenesis, is the process by which soil is created from parent rock material through weathering and the action of living organisms, climate, and topography over time. It is a slow process: the formation of a few centimetres of topsoil can take hundreds to thousands of years.

Factors

The Swiss-Russian scientist Vasily Dokuchaev identified soil as an independent natural body formed by interacting factors, formalised by Hans Jenny in 1941 as the CLORPT equation: climate, organisms, relief (topography), parent material, and time. Climate drives weathering rates and vegetation; organisms mix and enrich the soil with organic matter; topography affects drainage and erosion; parent material determines mineral composition; and time allows horizons to develop.

Processes

Pedogenesis involves additions (leaf litter, dust), losses (leaching, erosion), translocations (clay and iron moving between horizons), and transformations (weathering of minerals, humification). These processes produce the characteristic layered soil profile: the organic O horizon, mineral A horizon (topsoil), B horizon (subsoil), C horizon (weathered parent material), and underlying bedrock.
