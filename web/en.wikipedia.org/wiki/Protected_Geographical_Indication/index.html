Protected Geographical Indication

Protected Geographical Indication (PGI) is one of the three European Union schemes of geographical indications and traditional specialities, together with Protected Designation of Origin (PDO) and Traditional Speciality Guaranteed (TSG). The scheme protects the names of agricultural products and foodstuffs whose qualities or reputation are attributable to the region where they are produced.

Criteria

To qualify as a PGI, a product must be produced, processed or prepared in a defined geographical area, and a specific quality, reputation or other characteristic must be attributable to that area. Unlike PDO status, which requires all stages of production to occur in the region and all qualities to derive from it, PGI requires only one stage of production in the area and a looser link to place.

Examples

Well-known PGI products include Welsh lamb, Melton Mowbray pork pies, Arbroath smokies, Toscano olive oil, and the black pudding of Stornoway. Thousands of names are registered in the EU's eAmbrosia database, and the system has been partly retained in the United Kingdom after Brexit under a domestic GI scheme.

Legal basis and international recognition

The system was introduced in 1992 and is currently governed by EU Regulation No 1151/2012 on quality schemes for agricultural products and foodstuffs. Geographical indications are also protected under the WTO's TRIPS Agreement and bilateral trade deals, which extend EU PGI names into many third countries.
