Viral phenomenon

A viral phenomenon is an object, pattern or piece of content that spreads rapidly from person to person, by analogy with the spread of a biological virus. In the internet age the term most often refers to images, videos, phrases or challenges that are shared exponentially across social media — content said to have "gone viral".

Background

The concept predates the web: Richard Dawkins coined the term meme in 1976 for units of culture that propagate by imitation, and chain letters, urban legends and catchphrases spread virally through older media. Online, the dynamics changed in scale and speed: platform algorithms that reward engagement, frictionless sharing buttons, and vast follower networks can carry a post from obscurity to tens of millions of views within hours. The term "viral marketing" emerged in the 1990s for campaigns designed to exploit this spreading behaviour.

Notable examples

Widely cited viral phenomena include the "Dancing baby" animation of the 1990s, the Star Wars Kid, "Charlie Bit My Finger", the ALS Ice Bucket Challenge (which raised substantial funds for charity in 2014), the dress colour debate of 2015, and countless TikTok dances and sounds. News events, political messages and misinformation can spread by the same mechanics, which has made virality a subject of research in network science, marketing and public health communication.
