Pale of Settlement

The Pale of Settlement was a western region of the Russian Empire with varying borders between 1791 and 1917 in which permanent residency by Jews was allowed and beyond which Jewish residency was generally prohibited. It comprised much of present-day Belarus, Lithuania, Moldova, Ukraine, Poland, and parts of western Russia.

History

The Pale originated with decrees of Catherine the Great, beginning in 1791, as the empire absorbed large Jewish populations through the Partitions of Poland. Within the Pale, Jews were largely confined to towns and shtetls and excluded from many occupations; the May Laws of 1882 tightened restrictions further, barring new settlement in rural areas.

Life and emigration

By the late 19th century some five million Jews — the great majority of Russia's Jewish population — lived in the Pale, where poverty, quotas, and periodic pogroms drove mass emigration, especially to the United States. The Pale was formally abolished by the Provisional Government after the February Revolution of 1917.
