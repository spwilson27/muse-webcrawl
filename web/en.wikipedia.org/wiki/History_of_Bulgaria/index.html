History of Bulgaria

The history of Bulgaria spans from the Thracian civilisations of antiquity through two medieval Bulgarian empires, nearly five centuries of Ottoman rule, and the modern Bulgarian state founded in 1878. Bulgaria is one of Europe's oldest continuously existing states, having kept its name since the First Bulgarian Empire of the 7th century.

Antiquity and the First Empire

The lands of Bulgaria were inhabited by Thracians, famed for goldsmiths' work such as the Panagyurishte treasure, and later incorporated into the Roman and Byzantine worlds. In 681 the Bulgar khan Asparuh established the First Bulgarian Empire south of the Danube. Under Boris I the Bulgarians adopted Orthodox Christianity (864) and the Slavonic liturgy; his son Simeon I (893–927) presided over a "Golden Age" of Slavic culture and an empire stretching across much of the Balkans. Byzantine emperor Basil II — the "Bulgar Slayer" — conquered the empire by 1018.

Second Empire and Ottoman rule

The brothers Asen and Peter restored Bulgarian independence in 1185, founding the Second Empire with its capital at Tarnovo; Ivan Asen II brought it to its greatest extent in the 13th century. Ottoman armies conquered Bulgaria by 1396, and Ottoman rule lasted almost 500 years. The Bulgarian National Revival of the 18th–19th centuries, the struggle for an independent church (the Bulgarian Exarchate, 1870), and the April Uprising of 1876 preceded liberation.

Modern Bulgaria

The Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78 led to the Treaty of San Stefano and the more limited Treaty of Berlin, which created an autonomous Principality of Bulgaria; full independence was declared in 1908. Bulgaria fought in the Balkan Wars and both World Wars, generally aligned with Germany, emerging in 1944 as a Soviet-aligned People's Republic under communist rule until 1989. Since then Bulgaria has become a parliamentary republic, joining NATO in 2004 and the European Union in 2007.
