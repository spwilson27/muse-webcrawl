His Majesty's Airship R100 was a privately designed and built British passenger-carrying rigid airship made as part of the Imperial Airship Scheme, a competition to develop a commercial airship service for use on British Empire routes. It was built by the Airship Guarantee Company, a subsidiary of the armaments firm Vickers, at Howden in Yorkshire. Designed by Barnes Wallis, it made its first flight in December 1929.

Design and development

The R100 programme ran in parallel with the government-built R101 at Cardington; the two ships were informally known as the "capitalist" and "socialist" airships. R100 used a duralumin frame with a streamlined hull and six Rolls-Royce Condor petrol engines, carrying passengers in a three-deck interior with a dining saloon and observation promenade.

Transatlantic flight

In July–August 1930, R100 flew from Cardington to Canada and back, mooring at St-Hubert, Quebec, near Montreal, in just under 79 hours — a successful demonstration of the transatlantic route. After the crash of R101 in October 1930, however, the Imperial Airship Scheme was abandoned, and R100 was sold for scrap in December 1931, being flattened with steamrollers and broken up.

Specifications

R100 was about 219 metres (719 ft) long with a gas volume of roughly 146,000 cubic metres (5.16 million cubic feet) of hydrogen, giving a gross lift of about 156 tonnes. It cruised at around 70 mph with a maximum speed of about 81 mph.
