Studentenverbindung

A Studentenverbindung (plural: Studentenverbindungen) is a student fraternity found primarily in Germany, Austria, Switzerland and neighbouring countries with German academic traditions. Often translated loosely as "student association" or "fraternity", these organisations are run by students and alumni, are usually single-sex (traditionally male), and are characterised by lifelong membership (Lebensbund), distinctive colours worn as ribbon and cap (Couleur), and their own houses near the university.

Types

  • Corps — the oldest and most tradition-conscious, dating to the late 18th and early 19th centuries; typically fencing (duelling) fraternities.
  • Burschenschaften — founded from 1815 with originally liberal and national ideals, beginning with the Urburschenschaft in Jena.
  • Landsmannschaften and Turnerschaften — regional and gymnastic associations.
  • Catholic fraternities (CV, KV and others) — confessionally bound and non-duelling.
  • Wingolf and other Christian associations; musical, technical and hunting associations; and, since the late 20th century, a small number of women's associations (Damenverbindungen).

Customs

Traditional features include the Kneipe (formal drinking ceremony), the wearing of Couleur, a hierarchy of Fux (pledge), Bursche (full member) and Alter Herr (alumnus), and the principle that alumni finance and govern the corporation's house. Fencing fraternities (schlagende Verbindungen) practise academic fencing, the Mensur, with sharp blades; duelling scars (Schmisse) were historically a mark of honour.

History and criticism

Studentenverbindungen played a significant role in 19th-century German nationalism and liberalism, were dissolved or co-ordinated under National Socialism, and were re-established in West Germany and Austria after 1945; they were banned in the former East Germany. Today they encompass a small minority of students and are sometimes controversial, particularly Burschenschaften criticised for nationalist or far-right tendencies, while defenders emphasise tradition, networks and housing provision.
