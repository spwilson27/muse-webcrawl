The Fairchild Republic A-10 Thunderbolt II is a single-seat, twin-turbofan, straight-wing attack aircraft developed for the United States Air Force. Nicknamed the "Warthog", it was designed from the outset for close air support of ground forces and for destroying tanks and armoured vehicles.

Design

The A-10 is built around the enormous GAU-8 Avenger 30 mm rotary cannon, whose recoil is comparable to the thrust of one of its engines and which can fire depleted-uranium armour-piercing shells at up to 3,900 rounds per minute. The pilot sits in a titanium armoured "bathtub", critical systems are redundant, and the aircraft can survive heavy damage, fly at low speed and low altitude, and operate from austere forward airstrips. Its twin engines are mounted high at the rear to reduce vulnerability to ground fire.

Service

The A-10 entered service in 1977 and first saw major combat in the 1991 Gulf War, destroying large numbers of Iraqi tanks and vehicles. It subsequently served in the Balkans, Afghanistan, Iraq and against Islamic State targets, earning an enduring reputation with ground troops. Despite repeated Air Force proposals to retire the fleet in favour of multirole fighters, Congress long blocked full retirement; a phased drawdown of the roughly 280-aircraft fleet began in the 2020s following upgrades including new wings.
