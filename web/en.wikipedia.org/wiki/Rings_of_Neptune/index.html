Rings of Neptune

The planet Neptune has a system of five known rings, named in order of increasing distance from the planet: Galle, LeVerrier, Lassell, Arago and Adams. The rings are very faint and dark, composed of dust-sized particles, and contain a much higher proportion of dust than the rings of Uranus.

Discovery

Stellar occultations observed from Earth in the 1980s gave intermittent indications of ring material, but the puzzling pattern — signals appearing on only one side of the planet — was explained when NASA's Voyager 2 spacecraft imaged the system in 1989 and found that the outermost Adams ring contains several bright arcs: partial ring segments in which the material is locally concentrated.

The arcs

The Adams ring's arcs, named Fraternité, Égalité, Liberté and Courage, are unique in the Solar System. Their confinement may be maintained by resonances with Neptune's moon Galatea, which orbits just inside the ring, though observations have shown the arcs shifting and even fading over time, indicating the system is dynamically evolving. The rings are thought to be relatively young and possibly formed from the debris of a shattered moon.
