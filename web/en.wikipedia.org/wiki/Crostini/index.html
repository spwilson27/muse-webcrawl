Crostini (meaning "little crusts" in Italian; singular crostino) are an Italian appetizer consisting of small slices of grilled or toasted bread and toppings. The toppings may include a variety of different cheeses, meats, vegetables, and condiments, or may be presented more simply with olive oil and herbs or a sauce.

Crostini are typically made using French or Italian ciabatta bread. Along with bruschetta — a larger, heartier preparation — crostini have been part of Italian peasant cuisine since the Middle Ages, when meals were eaten off slices of bread rather than plates.

History

In medieval Tuscany, farmers and workers ate their meals atop slices of bread, which served as an edible plate and were eaten at the end. The most emblematic Tuscan preparation is crostini neri ("black crostini"), topped with a pâté of chicken livers, often with capers and anchovy, splashed with Vin Santo. The dish remains the classic opening of a Tuscan festive meal and appears at weddings and holidays throughout the region.

Preparation and toppings

Day-old bread is sliced about a centimetre thick, brushed with olive oil, and grilled or baked until crisp at the edges. Toppings are effectively unlimited:

  • Chicken liver pâté (crostini neri / crostini toscani)
  • Fresh tomato and basil, or tomato and garlic
  • White beans (fagioli) puréed with sage and olive oil
  • Mushrooms sautéed with garlic and parsley
  • Cheeses such as pecorino, goat cheese, or gorgonzola with honey or fig jam
  • Cured meats, smoked salmon, or olive tapenade

Outside Italy, crostini broadly denotes any small toast with a topping, used as a canapé base at parties.

Crostini vs. bruschetta

The two are often confused outside Italy. Crostini are made from small, thin slices of fine-textured bread (like a baguette), are crisper, and carry finely prepared toppings; bruschetta uses large, thick slices of rustic country bread, rubbed with garlic and dressed more robustly — classically with tomato, or simply oil and salt. Bigger still are crostoni, which approach a meal in themselves.
