States and union territories of India

India is a federal union comprising 28 states and 8 union territories, for a total of 36 entities. The states and union territories are further subdivided into districts and smaller administrative divisions. The states have their own elected governments, while union territories are administered by the central government through appointed administrators or lieutenant governors; Delhi and Puducherry have their own legislatures and chief ministers.

History

At independence in 1947, British India was partitioned between the Dominions of India and Pakistan, and more than 500 princely states were integrated into the union. The Constitution of 1950 organised India into Part A, B, C and D states. The States Reorganisation Act of 1956 redrew boundaries largely along linguistic lines, a principle that continued to shape later state formation: Maharashtra and Gujarat (1960), Punjab and Haryana (1966), and the north-eastern states among them. Chhattisgarh, Uttarakhand and Jharkhand were created in 2000, and Telangana was separated from Andhra Pradesh in 2014. In 2019 the state of Jammu and Kashmir was reorganised into the union territories of Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh; in 2020 Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu were merged into a single union territory.

Government

Each state has a governor appointed by the President of India, a chief minister heading the council of ministers, and a legislature (unicameral or bicameral). Subjects are divided between the Union List, State List and Concurrent List of the Seventh Schedule of the Constitution. Uttar Pradesh is the most populous state, with a population comparable to a large country, while Sikkim is the least populous; Rajasthan is the largest by area and Goa the smallest.
