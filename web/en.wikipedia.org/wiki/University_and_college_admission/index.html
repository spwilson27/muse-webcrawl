University and college admission

University admission or college admission is the process through which students enter tertiary education at universities and colleges. Systems vary widely from country to country, and sometimes from institution to institution.

Models

  • Centralised examination systems: admission is determined mainly by national examinations, such as China's gaokao, South Korea's College Scholastic Ability Test, or India's JEE and NEET.
  • Centralised application services: applicants apply through a national clearinghouse, such as UCAS in the United Kingdom or the CAO in Ireland, often on the basis of school-leaving examinations (A-levels, Leaving Certificate).
  • Holistic institutional admission: common in the United States, where individual colleges weigh grades, standardised test scores (where required), essays, recommendations, and extracurricular activities.
  • Open admission: many community colleges and some national systems admit any holder of a secondary school diploma.

Issues

Debates in admissions policy include the role of standardised testing, affirmative action and legacy preferences, the cost and commercialisation of application coaching and test preparation, and the transparency of selection criteria. In 2023 the US Supreme Court ended race-conscious admissions at American colleges in Students for Fair Admissions v. Harvard.
