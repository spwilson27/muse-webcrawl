The blue-and-yellow macaw (Ara ararauna), also known as the blue-and-gold macaw, is a large South American parrot with a mostly blue dorsum, light yellow/orange venter, and gradient hues of green forehead colouring. It is a member of the large group of neotropical parrots known as macaws.

Blue-and-yellow macaw
Scientific name: Ara ararauna
Family: Psittacidae
Length: 76–86 cm
Conservation status: Least Concern

Description

These birds can reach a length of 76 to 86 cm and weigh 0.9 to 1.5 kg, making them some of the larger members of their family. The upperparts are a vivid blue, the underparts golden yellow, the forehead greenish, and the bare facial skin white with rows of small black feathers. The powerful black beak is adapted for cracking hard nuts and seeds.

Distribution and behaviour

The species occurs from Panama and Colombia south through Brazil, Bolivia and Paraguay, inhabiting forests, woodland and savannah. It was extirpated from Trinidad by the 1960s but later reintroduced. Blue-and-yellow macaws are popular in aviculture because of their striking appearance and talking ability, though their size and vocalisations make them demanding pets.
