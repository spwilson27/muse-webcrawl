Nuno Tristão

Nuno Tristão (died 1446) was a fifteenth-century Portuguese explorer and slave raider, one of the first Europeans to explore the coast of West Africa south of the Sahara under the patronage of Prince Henry the Navigator.


Died: 1446, off the Geba River (present-day Guinea-Bissau)
Nationality: Portuguese
Occupation: Explorer, navigator

Voyages

Tristão was a household knight of Prince Henry. In 1441 Henry sent him south in a lateen-rigged caravel; Tristão reached the region of Cabo Branco (Cap Blanc) and returned with captives taken near Arguin — among the first enslaved Africans brought to Portugal by sea — helping to inaugurate the Portuguese Atlantic slave trade. On later voyages he pushed the known coast farther south: in 1443 he reached the island of Arguim, and in 1445 he explored the coast as far as the area of present-day Guinea, giving his name to the Rio Nuno (the Nunez River) in modern Guinea.

Death

In 1446 Tristão sailed up the Geba River in present-day Guinea-Bissau. According to the chronicler Gomes Eanes de Zurara, his landing party was attacked by local inhabitants with poisoned arrows; Tristão and most of his men were killed, and only a few survivors brought the ship back to Portugal. Despite the disaster, his voyages extended European knowledge of the West African coast hundreds of kilometres beyond the Sahara and set the pattern for subsequent expeditions sponsored by Henry the Navigator.
