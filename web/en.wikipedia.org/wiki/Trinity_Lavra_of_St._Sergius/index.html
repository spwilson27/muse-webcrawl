Trinity Lavra of St. Sergius

The Trinity Lavra of St. Sergius (Russian: Троице-Сергиева лавра) is the most important Russian Orthodox monastery and the spiritual centre of the Russian Church. It stands in the town of Sergiyev Posad, about 70 kilometres northeast of Moscow, and was founded in 1337 by St. Sergius of Radonezh, Russia's most revered monastic saint. A lavra is a monastery of the highest rank; the Trinity Lavra is one of only two lavras in Russia, the other being the Alexander Nevsky Lavra in St. Petersburg.

History

Sergius settled in the forest with his brother Stephen and built a small wooden church of the Holy Trinity; disciples gathered, and the monastery grew rich on princely donations. It blessed Dmitry Donskoy before the Battle of Kulikovo (1380), was burned by the Tatars in 1408 and rebuilt in stone, and withstood a famous sixteen-month Polish siege in 1608–10 during the Time of Troubles. The relics of St. Sergius rest in the Trinity Cathedral (1422–23), frescoed by Andrei Rublev and Daniil Cherny; Rublev's icon of the Trinity was painted for the cathedral. The monastery's white walls, blue-and-gold domed Assumption Cathedral (modelled on the Kremlin's), and 88-metre five-tiered bell tower accumulated over the following centuries. Suppressed after the Revolution, it was partly returned to the Church in 1946 and houses the Moscow Theological Academy. UNESCO lists the ensemble as a World Heritage Site.

Significance

The Lavra draws pilgrims and tourists throughout the year and remains the seat of major Church ceremonies; the Patriarch of Moscow frequently officiates there.
