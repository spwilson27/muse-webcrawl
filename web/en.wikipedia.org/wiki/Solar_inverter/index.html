Solar inverter


Function: Converts DC from photovoltaic panels to AC
Types: String, central, microinverter
Efficiency: Typically 95–99%

A solar inverter or photovoltaic (PV) inverter is a type of power inverter that converts the variable direct current (DC) output of a photovoltaic solar panel into a utility-frequency alternating current (AC) that can be fed into a commercial electrical grid or used by a local, off-grid electrical network. It is a critical balance-of-system component in a photovoltaic system.

Types

String inverters connect a series "string" of panels to a single inverter and serve most residential and commercial systems. Central inverters handle hundreds of kilowatts in utility-scale plants. Microinverters are mounted on each individual panel, converting DC to AC at the module and allowing panel-level monitoring and optimisation; power optimisers offer a hybrid approach, conditioning DC at the panel before a central inverter. Battery-based and hybrid inverters integrate energy storage and islanding functions.

Functions

Modern solar inverters perform maximum power point tracking (MPPT) to extract the most power from the array, anti-islanding protection to shut down during grid outages, grid support functions such as voltage and frequency regulation, and data monitoring. Conversion efficiencies typically range from 95% to over 99%.
