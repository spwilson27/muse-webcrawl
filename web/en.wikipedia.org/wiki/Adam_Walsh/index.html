Adam Walsh (1974–1981), whose abduction and murder galvanised the American missing-children movement.

Adam John Walsh (14 November 1974 – 27 July 1981) was an American boy who was abducted from a Sears department store in Hollywood, Florida, on 27 July 1981 and later found murdered. His death prompted national attention to missing children in the United States.

Abduction and investigation

Adam disappeared after being left briefly near a video-game display while his mother shopped nearby. His severed head was found two weeks later in a drainage canal near Vero Beach, Florida; his body was never recovered. The case remained officially unsolved for decades, with drifter Ottis Toole a long-standing suspect who confessed and later recanted. In December 2008, Hollywood police announced that the investigation concluded Toole was responsible and closed the case.

Legacy

Adam's father, John Walsh, became a prominent victims' advocate, co-founding the National Center for Missing & Exploited Children (1984) and hosting the television programme America's Most Wanted. The case contributed to the Missing Children's Assistance Act of 1984, and in 2006 the federal Adam Walsh Child Protection and Safety Act — which created a national sex-offender registry framework — was named in his memory. The "Code Adam" missing-child procedure used by retailers is also named for him.
