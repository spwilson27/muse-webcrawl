City council (Israel)

A city council (Hebrew: עירייה, iriya) is the official designation of a city municipality in Israel. City council status may be granted by the Minister of the Interior to a municipality, typically a local council, whose population exceeds 20,000 and whose character is urban, as opposed to rural.

Role and structure

Israeli city councils are responsible for local services including education, welfare, water and sanitation, road maintenance, parks, cultural activities and local planning and building permits. They are headed by a mayor, who is directly elected by residents, alongside a council elected on party lists in municipal elections held every five years.

Below city councils in the Israeli system of local government are local councils, which govern smaller towns, and regional councils, which group together rural communities such as kibbutzim and moshavim. Municipal status and boundaries are supervised by the Ministry of the Interior, which also approves budgets and can intervene in council administration.

History

The framework derives from the Municipal Corporations Ordinance of the British Mandate period, retained and amended after the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948. Major city councils include those of Jerusalem, Tel Aviv-Yafo, Haifa and Beersheba.
