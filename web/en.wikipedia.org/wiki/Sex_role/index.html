Sex role

A sex role, also known as a gender role, is a social role encompassing a range of behaviours and attitudes that are generally considered acceptable, appropriate, or desirable for a person based on that person's sex. Sex roles are culturally specific, vary across societies and historical periods, and are learned through socialisation by family, peers, education, media, and religion.

Concepts and theories

Sociologists distinguish between roles associated with males and females in a given culture, including expectations about occupations, domestic responsibilities, dress, speech, and emotional expression. Functionalist theorists such as Talcott Parsons described complementary "instrumental" and "expressive" roles, while later scholars criticised such models for naturalising inequality. Social role theory (Alice Eagly) explains perceived sex differences as arising from the distribution of men and women into different social roles, and socialisation theories describe how children internalise gendered expectations.

Variation and change

Although some expectations recur across cultures, the specific content of sex roles differs widely; some societies recognise additional gender categories. Over the 20th and 21st centuries, women's increased participation in education and paid work, legal reforms, and feminist movements have substantially transformed sex roles in many countries, while debates continue over biological versus social explanations of role differences and over the persistence of stereotypes.

Measurement

Psychologists have devised instruments such as the Bem Sex-Role Inventory (1974) to assess masculinity and femininity as independent dimensions, a framework that also introduced the concept of psychological androgyny.
