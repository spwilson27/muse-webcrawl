Dawit Isaak (born 27 October 1964) is an Eritrean-born Swedish journalist, author and playwright who has been held in prison in Eritrea since 2001 without trial and is considered a prisoner of conscience by Amnesty International. He is one of the longest-detained journalists in the world.

Biography

Isaak fled Eritrea's war of independence and settled in Sweden in 1987, becoming a Swedish citizen. After Eritrean independence he returned and co-founded Setit, the country's first independent newspaper. In September 2001, after Setit published letters by reformist officials (the "G-15") calling for democratic change, the government shut down the independent press and arrested journalists and politicians; Isaak was arrested on 23 September 2001.

Imprisonment and campaign

Isaak has never been charged or tried, and his whereabouts and health have long been concealed, fuelling recurring rumours of his death. Sweden and the European Union have pursued his release through "quiet diplomacy", a strategy criticised by campaigners and his family. He has received numerous awards in absentia, including the Norwegian PEN's Ossietzky Prize and UNESCO's Guillermo Cano World Press Freedom Prize (2017). The "Free Dawit" campaign maintains vigils outside the Eritrean embassy in Stockholm.
