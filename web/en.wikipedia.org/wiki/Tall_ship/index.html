Tall ship

A tall ship is a large, traditionally rigged sailing vessel. Popular modern tall ship rigs include topsail schooners, brigantines, brigs, and barques. The term was popularised by the Tall Ships' Races and is often used loosely for any large sailing vessel, though organisations such as Sail Training International apply a more specific definition based on rig and size: Class A vessels are square-rigged ships and other vessels over 40 metres in length.

History

Tall ships in the modern sense descend from the great sailing ships of the 18th and 19th centuries — naval frigates, clippers, and merchant barques — that carried world trade before steam. Although commercial sail declined after 1900, large square-riggers survived into the 20th century in the grain and nitrate trades, and many navies retained sail-training ships for cadet instruction.

Sail training

Today most tall ships are operated by sail-training organisations, navies, and charter companies. Crews of trainees, many of them young people with no prior experience, take part in all aspects of running the vessel, including climbing the rigging to set and furl sails. Regular international gatherings and races — notably the Tall Ships' Races, first held in 1956 — draw fleets of these vessels to host ports around the world.

Terminology

Classical rigs are distinguished by mast number and sail plan: a ship proper (full-rigged ship) has three or more masts, all square-rigged; a barque has three or more masts with fore-and-aft sails on the aftermost; a brig has two square-rigged masts; and schooners are primarily fore-and-aft rigged. Notable modern examples include the German Gorch Fock, the Polish Dar Młodzieży, the Norwegian Statsraad Lehmkuhl, and the American barque USCGC Eagle.
