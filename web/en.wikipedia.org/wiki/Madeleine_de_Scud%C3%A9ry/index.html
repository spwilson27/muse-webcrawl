Madeleine de Scudéry

Madeleine de Scudéry (15 November 1607 – 2 June 1701), often known simply as Mademoiselle de Scudéry, was a French writer and salonnière. She authored vast multi-volume romances such as Artamène, ou le Grand Cyrus (1649–1653) and Clélie (1654–1660), among the longest novels ever written, published under her brother Georges's name.

Life and work

Scudéry hosted one of the most famous salons of Paris, the samedis of the Hôtel de Rambouillet circle, where the culture of the Précieuses flourished. Clélie contains the famous Carte de Tendre ("Map of Tenderness"), an allegorical geography of courtship that codified the era's ideas of love and gallantry.

Legacy

Though later mocked by Molière in Les Précieuses ridicules, Scudéry is now recognised as a major figure of early French prose fiction and of women's literary history.
