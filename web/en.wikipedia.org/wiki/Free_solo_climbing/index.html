Free solo climbing

Free solo climbing, or free soloing, is a form of rock climbing in which the climber ascends without ropes, harnesses, or any protective equipment, relying entirely on their physical strength and technique. A fall is almost always fatal, making it the most dangerous form of climbing. It differs from free climbing, in which ropes are used for protection though not for upward progress.

History

Early ascents were made by climbers such as Paul Preuss in the Alps before the First World War. The discipline gained prominence in the 1970s with climbers including John Bachar and Peter Croft, whose ropeless ascents in Yosemite National Park set new standards. In the 1990s and 2000s Derek Hersey, Michael Reardon, and others pushed the practice, while many prominent soloists have died in falls.

Notable ascents

On 3 June 2017, Alex Honnold completed the first free solo ascent of El Capitan in Yosemite, climbing the 900-metre Freerider route in 3 hours 56 minutes. The ascent is widely considered one of the greatest athletic achievements ever recorded and was documented in the Academy Award–winning film Free Solo (2018).

Ethics and reception

Free soloing is controversial within the climbing community: admirers praise its purity and mental discipline, while critics argue it glorifies unacceptable risk. Practitioners typically rehearse routes extensively with ropes before attempting them unroped.
