Korean Martyrs

Jeoldusan ("Beheading Mountain") in Seoul, site of executions during the 1866 persecution

The Korean Martyrs were Korean and foreign Catholics killed during the persecutions of Christianity in Joseon-dynasty Korea between 1791 and 1888. The most famous group, the 103 Martyrs, were canonised by Pope John Paul II in Seoul on 6 May 1984 — the first canonisation ceremony held outside the Vatican in modern times — and are commemorated on 20 September. A further 124 martyrs were beatified by Pope Francis in 2014.

Background

Catholicism entered Korea unusually: not through missionaries but through books brought from Beijing by Korean envoys and scholars in the late eighteenth century. Yi Seung-hun was baptised in Beijing in 1784 and returned to baptise others, founding a lay-led church. Because Catholic teaching rejected Confucian ancestral rites (jesa), the Joseon court treated the faith as a subversive heterodoxy, and waves of persecution followed: the Sinyu persecution (1801), the Gihae persecution (1839), the Byeong-o persecution (1846), and the great Byeong-in persecution (1866), which killed some 8,000–10,000 Korean Catholics and nine French missionaries, and helped trigger the French expedition to Korea that year.

The 103 saints

The canonised group includes Andrew Kim Taegon, the first Korean-born Catholic priest, executed in 1846 at age 25; Paul Chong Hasang, the lay catechist who defended the faith in a famous memorial to the king and was martyred in 1839; the French bishops Laurent Imbert and Siméon-François Berneux; ten-year-old and elderly laypeople; court ladies, farmers, and translators. They represent the martyrs of the 1839, 1846, and 1866 persecutions. Their relics are venerated at shrines including Jeoldusan, Saenamteo, and Jungdong in Daejeon, and the Korean Catholic Church — grown from this persecuted community — counts the martyrs as its founding witnesses.
