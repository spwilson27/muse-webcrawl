Livestreaming

Livestreaming, also known as live-streaming or streaming live, is the real-time delivery of media over the internet — most commonly video and audio — as it is captured, so that audiences watch or listen as events happen rather than downloading a finished recording. Livestreaming spans sports and news broadcasts, video game streaming, live commerce, concerts, religious services, webinars and the everyday broadcasts of social-media creators.

History

Streaming media experiments date to the early 1990s — the band Severe Tire Damage performed the first live streamed concert in 1993 — and RealNetworks' RealAudio (1995) and RealVideo made live internet broadcasting practical, though limited by dial-up speeds. Broadband in the 2000s enabled Ustream (2007) and Justin.tv (2007), which popularised personal "lifecasting" and live gaming. Justin.tv's gaming section split off as Twitch in 2011 and was bought by Amazon in 2014 for about $970 million. YouTube, Facebook, Instagram and TikTok later built live features into mass-market platforms, and the COVID-19 pandemic from 2020 dramatically expanded livestreaming for work, education, worship and commerce.

Forms

  • Video game streaming — streamers play games live for audiences, with chat interaction, subscriptions and donations; major platforms include Twitch and YouTube Gaming, and esports tournaments draw audiences in the tens of millions.
  • Live commerce — hosts sell products in interactive shopping broadcasts, a huge market in China (Taobao Live, Douyin) that has spread worldwide.
  • Social livestreaming — individuals broadcast from phones to followers, alone or with guests.
  • Broadcast streaming — television, sports and news delivered live over the internet (OTT services).
  • Live audio and podcasting, including social audio rooms.

Technology

A livestream is captured by camera and microphone, encoded (commonly H.264 or H.265 video with AAC audio) and pushed to a distribution server, traditionally via the RTMP protocol, then delivered to viewers at scale through content delivery networks using adaptive-bitrate protocols such as HLS and MPEG-DASH. Latencies range from under a second (WebRTC-based services) to 30 seconds or more for large HLS broadcasts.

Issues

Livestreaming raises distinctive problems: harmful content is hard to moderate before it is seen (extremist attacks have been livestreamed), streamers have faced harassment and "swatting", and platforms struggle with copyrighted music and broadcast rights in real-time audio and video.
