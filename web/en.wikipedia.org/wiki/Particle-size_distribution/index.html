Particle-size distribution

The particle-size distribution (PSD) of a powder, granular material or dispersed particles is a list of values or a mathematical function that defines the relative amount, typically by mass or volume, of particles present according to size. PSD is a critical quality attribute in industries from pharmaceuticals and cement to soils and sediments.

Measurement

Common techniques include sieve analysis, laser diffraction, dynamic light scattering, sedimentation and image analysis. Results are often reported as percentiles such as D10, D50 (the median) and D90 — the sizes below which 10%, 50% and 90% of the material's volume lies — and summarised by the span.

Models

Many natural and manufactured distributions are approximated by the log-normal distribution; others used include the Rosin–Rammler (Weibull) distribution for crushed materials and the normal distribution. The distribution affects flow, packing, dissolution rate, reactivity and optical properties of the material.
