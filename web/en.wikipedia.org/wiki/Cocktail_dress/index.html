Cocktail dress

[Image placeholder: A classic black cocktail dress]

A cocktail dress is a dress suitable for a semi-formal occasion, sometimes called cocktail hour. Historically the length of a cocktail dress is long, often touching the ankle. When it is about five centimetres (two inches) above the ankle it is called a tea length, and when it is almost touching the ankle it is called ballerina length, although a modern cocktail dress may be shorter.

History

The cocktail dress emerged in the 1920s, when American women began attending semi-formal evening gatherings at which cocktails were served. Christian Dior gave the name "cocktail dress" to early evening attire in the late 1940s. The little black dress, popularised by Coco Chanel, became a canonical example of the style.

Etiquette

At a cocktail event, dresses are typically shorter and less formal than evening gowns, giving rise to the dress code known as cocktail attire. Lengths range from mini to tea length; fabrics such as silk, satin and chiffon are common, often with embellishments.
