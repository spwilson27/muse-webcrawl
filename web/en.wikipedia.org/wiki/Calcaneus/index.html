In humans and many other primates, the calcaneus (from the Latin calcaneus or calcaneum, meaning heel) or heel bone is a bone of the tarsus of the foot which constitutes the heel. In some other animals it is the point of the hock.

Structure

The calcaneus is the largest of the tarsal bones and the largest bone of the foot. It is roughly cuboidal in shape and projects posteriorly to form the heel. The talus bone, which supports the leg, rests above it, and the cuboid bone sits in front of it. On its posterior surface is the calcaneal tuberosity, to which the Achilles tendon (tendo calcaneus) attaches; this is the largest and strongest tendon in the human body. The sustentaculum tali is a medial projection that supports the head of the talus.

Function

The calcaneus transmits the weight of the body to the ground and acts as a short lever for the calf muscles (gastrocnemius and soleus) that insert onto its posterior surface via the Achilles tendon, enabling plantarflexion of the foot during walking, running and jumping.

Clinical significance

Calcaneal fractures often result from falls from a height and may be associated with spinal injuries. Plantar fasciitis and heel spurs are common causes of heel pain. Retrocalcaneal bursitis can occur where the Achilles tendon passes over the bone.
