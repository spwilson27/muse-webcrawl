Bangor Cathedral (Welsh: Eglwys Gadeiriol Bangor) is the cathedral church of the Anglican Diocese of Bangor in the city of Bangor, Gwynedd, north-west Wales. Dedicated to Saint Deiniol, who founded a monastery on the site around 530 AD, it stands on one of the oldest ecclesiastical sites in continuous use in Britain, although the present building is largely the result of medieval rebuilding and a major Victorian restoration.

History

Deiniol's monastery, a clas enclosed by a fence (the name Bangor is often explained as "the high enclosure" or wattle fence), was an early centre of Welsh Christianity. The site suffered Viking attacks and was burned during the conflicts of the 11th and 13th centuries, including destruction by King John's forces in 1211. The cruciform stone cathedral was rebuilt in the 13th and 14th centuries; the nave arcades date from the later 14th century, and the western tower was completed in the early 16th century, though left with a modest low spire.

Architecture

The building was extensively restored by Sir George Gilbert Scott between 1870 and 1880, who rebuilt the crossing tower area and reroofed the church; his intended tall spire was never built because of structural concerns and cost. The cathedral is cruciform with an aisled nave, transepts, and a chancel. Notable features include the Mostyn Christ, a late-15th-century wooden figure of Christ, and the Anian Pontifical, a 13th-century illuminated bishop's service book. The cathedral has a bilingual tradition of worship in Welsh and English.
