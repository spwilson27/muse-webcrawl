4-H is a network of youth organisations in the United States whose mission is engaging young people in positive youth development through adult mentorship and hands-on learning. The name represents four aspects of development: head, heart, hands and health. It is administered by the National Institute of Food and Agriculture of the US Department of Agriculture, with local delivery through land-grant university extension services.

History

4-H grew out of early-1900s agricultural clubs in which rural youth grew demonstration crops — famously tomato and corn clubs organised by educators such as Seaman Knapp and O.H. Benson. The Smith-Lever Act of 1914 created the Cooperative Extension System and nationalised the programme. The four-leaf clover emblem, with an H on each leaf, was adopted in the 1910s.

Programmes

Members aged 5–19 pursue projects in agriculture and animal science, STEM, healthy living, citizenship and leadership, exhibiting livestock and projects at county and state fairs. The 4-H pledge, recited at meetings, commits the member's head to clearer thinking, heart to greater loyalty, hands to larger service and health to better living. Alumni number roughly 25 million in the US, and similar programmes operate in more than 50 countries.
