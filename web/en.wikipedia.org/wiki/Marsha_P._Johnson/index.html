Marsha P. Johnson (August 24, 1945 – July 6, 1992) was an American gay liberation activist and self-identified drag queen, and one of the most prominent figures of the LGBTQ rights movement in New York City. Known as an outspoken advocate for homeless LGBTQ youth, people affected by HIV/AIDS, and gay and transgender rights, Johnson was a central presence in the gay rights movement that followed the 1969 Stonewall uprising. The "P" in her name stood for "Pay It No Mind," her response when asked about her gender.

Early life

Johnson was born in Elizabeth, New Jersey, one of seven children in a working-class African American family. After graduating from high school in 1963, she moved to New York City with little money and settled in Greenwich Village, where she became part of the street and performance scene, working as a waitress and performing as a drag queen.

Stonewall and activism

Johnson was present at the Stonewall Inn on the night of the June 1969 police raid that sparked several nights of demonstrations — a foundational moment of the modern gay rights movement. She subsequently joined the Gay Liberation Front and the Gay Activists Alliance, marching in the first Christopher Street Liberation Day parade in 1970.

With her close friend Sylvia Rivera, Johnson founded STAR (Street Transvestite Action Revolutionaries) in 1970, an organisation advocating for homeless trans and gender-nonconforming youth; STAR House provided shelter for street youth in New York. Johnson also performed with the drag performance troupe Hot Peaches and with the Angels of Light, and modelled for Andy Warhol's "Ladies and Gentlemen" series of screenprints in 1975.

Death and legacy

Johnson's body was found in the Hudson River on July 6, 1992, shortly after that year's Pride march. Police initially ruled the death a suicide; friends and activists campaigned for years for a proper investigation, and in 2012 the case was reclassified as an undetermined drowning and reopened. Her life has been the subject of documentaries including Pay It No Mind: The Life and Times of Marsha P. Johnson (2012) and The Death and Life of Marsha P. Johnson (2017). In 2019 New York announced a monument honouring Johnson and Rivera near the Stonewall Inn, and East River State Park in Brooklyn was renamed the Marsha P. Johnson State Park in 2020.
