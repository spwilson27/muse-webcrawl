Thomas Greenway (25 March 1838 – 30 October 1908) was a Canadian politician who served as the seventh premier of Manitoba from 1888 to 1900. A Liberal, he led the province's first avowedly party government and is best remembered for his role in the Manitoba Schools Question.

Premiership

Born in Cornwall and raised in Canada West, Greenway moved to Manitoba in 1878 and was first elected to the provincial assembly in 1879. As premier he promoted immigration, railway development, and economy in government. In 1890 his government abolished public funding for Catholic separate schools and ended official bilingualism, measures aimed at the French-speaking Catholic minority that provoked a decade-long national crisis, federal litigation, and eventually the Laurier–Greenway compromise of 1896 permitting some religious instruction and bilingual teaching in public schools.

Later career

Defeated provincially, Greenway sat in the House of Commons from 1904 until his appointment to the Board of Railway Commissioners in 1908; he died in Ottawa the same year.
