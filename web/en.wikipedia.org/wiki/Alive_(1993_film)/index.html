Alive is a 1993 American biographical survival drama film directed by Frank Marshall, based on Piers Paul Read's 1974 non-fiction book Alive: The Story of the Andes Survivors. It dramatises the true story of a Uruguayan rugby team whose plane crashed in the Andes mountains in 1972 and whose survivors endured seventy-two days in extreme conditions.

Plot

On 13 October 1972, Uruguayan Air Force Flight 571, carrying the Old Christians Club rugby team to a match in Chile, crashes in the remote Andes. Of the forty-five people on board, twenty-nine survive the crash; avalanches and exposure reduce them further. When they learn by radio that the search has been called off, and with no food, the survivors take the agonising collective decision to eat the bodies of the dead. Finally Nando Parrado and Roberto Canessa trek for ten days over the mountains into Chile and raise the alarm; the remaining sixteen survivors are rescued on 22–23 December 1972.

Production and reception

The film was shot in the Purcell Mountains of British Columbia, Canada. Ethan Hawke stars as Nando Parrado, with John Malkovich providing a framing narration as an older Carlitos Páez. Survivors advised the production. The film received mixed reviews — praised for its harrowing survival scenes and restrained treatment of cannibalism, criticised for emotional distance and for casting English-speaking actors — and performed modestly at the box office. The story was retold in the Spanish film Society of the Snow (2023).
