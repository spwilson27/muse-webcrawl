Heinrich Schütz

Heinrich Schütz (c. 8 October 1585 – 6 November 1672) was a German early Baroque composer, generally regarded as the most important German composer before Johann Sebastian Bach and one of the most important composers of the seventeenth century. He spent most of his career as Kapellmeister at the Saxon court in Dresden.

Life

Born in Köstritz, Schütz was discovered by Landgrave Moritz of Hesse-Kassel, who funded his education and sent him to Venice to study with Giovanni Gabrieli. After a second Italian trip in 1628, when he likely met Monteverdi, he brought the Italian concertato style to Germany. The Thirty Years' War devastated his Dresden chapel, and he spent periods working in Copenhagen before returning to Dresden, where he died at 87.

Works

Almost all of his surviving output is sacred vocal music, including the Psalmen Davids (1619), Symphoniae Sacrae, Musikalische Exequien (1636), the Schwanengesang and three Passions. His opera Dafne (1627), the first German opera, is lost. His works are catalogued with SWV numbers.

Legacy

Schütz fused Venetian polychoral and concertato techniques with German counterpoint and a profound sensitivity to the Lutheran text, shaping German music for a century.
