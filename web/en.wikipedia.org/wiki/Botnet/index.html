Botnet

A botnet is a network of computers and other internet-connected devices infected with malicious software ("bots" or "zombies") and controlled remotely by an attacker, the bot herder, usually without the owners' knowledge. Botnets are used to send spam, launch distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks, steal credentials and data, commit click fraud, mine cryptocurrency and distribute further malware.

[ Image: Diagram of a command-and-control server directing a network of infected zombie computers ]

Architecture

Early botnets used centralised command-and-control (C&C) servers, frequently reached through IRC channels or HTTP. Because taking down the server disables the botnet, operators moved to peer-to-peer designs and to fast-flux DNS and domain-generation algorithms, in which bots compute large numbers of rendezvous domains daily and the operator need register only a few. Infection typically spreads through phishing, malicious downloads, exploit of unpatched software and default passwords on Internet of Things devices.

Notable botnets

Major examples include Conficker (2008), which infected millions of Windows machines; Zeus, a credential-stealing toolkit; Mirai (2016), which conscripted insecure IoT devices and produced record DDoS attacks including one on the DNS provider Dyn; and Emotet, a modular botnet disrupted by an international operation in 2021. The largest botnets have numbered in the millions of devices.

Countermeasures

Defences include patching, anti-malware tools, network filtering and sinkholing of C&C domains, together with coordinated law-enforcement takedowns that seize servers and domains. Botnet operation is a criminal offence in most jurisdictions, under laws such as the U.S. Computer Fraud and Abuse Act.
