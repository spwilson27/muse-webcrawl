A close-up or closeup in filmmaking, television production, still photography, and the comic strip medium is a type of shot that tightly frames a person or object. Close-ups are one of the standard shots used regularly with medium and long shots. They display the most detail but do not include the broader scene.

Usage

Close-ups are used to emphasise emotion and reaction: a character's face fills the screen so the audience registers subtle expressions. Directors such as Sergio Leone made the extreme close-up — eyes or hands filling the frame — a signature device in films like The Good, the Bad and the Ugly. In documentary and news, close-ups create intimacy and focus attention on key details.

History

Early cinema mostly used full-length framing borrowed from theatre. Pioneers including D. W. Griffith helped establish the close-up as a storytelling tool in the 1910s, despite studio fears that audiences would be confused by "partial" bodies.

Related shot types

  • Extreme close-up (ECU): frames only part of the face or a small detail.
  • Medium close-up: frames from the chest or waist up.
  • Insert: a close-up of an object, often cutting away from the main action.
