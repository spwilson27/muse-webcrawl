Independence Day (United States)

Independence Day, commonly known as the Fourth of July, is a federal holiday in the United States commemorating the ratification of the Declaration of Independence by the Second Continental Congress on July 4, 1776, thereby establishing the United States of America as an independent nation separate from the British Empire.

The Continental Congress had voted in favor of independence two days earlier, on July 2, by passing the Lee Resolution, and it adopted the Declaration of Independence — drafted primarily by Thomas Jefferson — on July 4. John Adams famously predicted that July 2 would be celebrated as the great anniversary festival, but the date printed on the Declaration itself, July 4, became the day Americans marked.

Observance

Independence Day is commonly associated with fireworks, parades, barbecues, carnivals, fairs, picnics, concerts, baseball games, family reunions, political speeches, and ceremonies. Public displays of the American flag are ubiquitous, and many communities hold morning parades and evening fireworks spectaculars. The most prominent celebration in the nation's capital features a concert on the West Lawn of the U.S. Capitol and fireworks over the National Mall.

History

During the American Revolution, the legal separation of the American colonies from Great Britain in 1776 occurred on July 2, when the Second Continental Congress voted to approve a resolution of independence proposed by Richard Henry Lee. After voting for independence, Congress turned its attention to the Declaration of Independence, a statement explaining the decision, prepared by a Committee of Five, whose principal author was Thomas Jefferson. Congress adopted it on July 4.

The first celebrations included mock funerals for King George III, bonfires, and the ringing of bells. Philadelphia held the first annual commemoration of independence on July 4, 1777, while Congress was still occupied with the war. Massachusetts became the first state to recognize July 4 as a state celebration in 1781. In 1870, the U.S. Congress made Independence Day an unpaid holiday for federal employees, and in 1938 Congress changed it to a paid federal holiday.

Customs

Many citizens display the flag outside their homes or buildings. Fireworks are often accompanied by patriotic songs such as "The Star-Spangled Banner", "God Bless America", "America the Beautiful", and "Yankee Doodle". Major fireworks displays are held in cities such as New York, Boston, Chicago, and San Diego. The Nathan's Hot Dog Eating Contest in Coney Island, held annually since 1972, is another famous Fourth of July tradition broadcast on national television.
