Regional anaesthesia

Regional anaesthesia is the use of local anaesthetics to block pain in a large part of the body, such as a limb or the lower half of the body. Unlike general anaesthesia, the patient remains conscious, or may be lightly sedated, during the procedure.

Techniques

Regional techniques are broadly divided into central (neuraxial) blocks and peripheral nerve blocks. Neuraxial techniques include spinal anaesthesia and epidural anaesthesia, in which local anaesthetic is injected near the spinal cord. Peripheral blocks target specific nerves or plexuses, such as the brachial plexus, and are increasingly performed under ultrasound guidance.

Advantages and risks

Regional anaesthesia can reduce the need for systemic opioids, lower the incidence of postoperative nausea, and provide prolonged postoperative pain relief. Risks include local anaesthetic toxicity, nerve injury, infection, bleeding, and — for neuraxial blocks — hypotension and post-dural-puncture headache.
