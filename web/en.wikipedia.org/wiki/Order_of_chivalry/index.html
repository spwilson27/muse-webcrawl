Order of chivalry

An order of chivalry, order of knighthood, or equestrian order is an order of knights, typically founded during or inspired by the original Catholic military orders of the Crusades (c. 1099–1291) and paired with medieval concepts of chivalry. It is a society of knights usually under the leadership of a sovereign or head of state, membership of which confers honour, insignia, and precedence.

History

The earliest orders were the military religious orders founded in the Holy Land, such as the Knights Templar (1119) and the Knights Hospitaller, whose members took monastic vows and fought to protect pilgrims. In imitation, European monarchs founded secular courtly orders from the fourteenth century onward: Edward III's Order of the Garter (1348), Philip the Good's Order of the Golden Fleece (1430), and others that bound leading nobles to the sovereign in loyalty and ceremony.

Modern orders

As monarchy evolved, orders of chivalry became orders of merit, awarded for civil and military distinction rather than martial service. In the United Kingdom, appointments to orders such as the Order of the Bath, the Order of St Michael and St George, and the Royal Victorian Order form the core of the honours system. Dynastic orders remain in the gift of royal houses, while republics typically maintain orders of merit with analogous grades, crosses, and ribbons. Admission is usually by nomination and investiture, and membership is often limited in number within each grade.

Structure and insignia

Orders are typically headed by a sovereign and administered by a grand master and officers such as a chancellor and a heraldic authority. Grades commonly run from grand cross or knight grand cross down through commander to knight or companion. Insignia include a badge (often worn on a riband or neck ribbon), a breast star, a collar for great occasions, and a distinctive mantle and chapel for the most prestigious orders, such as St George's Chapel, Windsor, for the Order of the Garter.
