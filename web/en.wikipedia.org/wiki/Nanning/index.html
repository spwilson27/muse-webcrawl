Nanning is the capital and largest city of the Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region in southern China. Known as the "Green City" for its lush subtropical vegetation, it stands on the Yong River, a tributary system of the Pearl River, at a crossroads between China and Southeast Asia. The prefecture-level city administers a large area of districts and counties with a total population of roughly 8.7 million.

History

The area has been administered from a town on the site since the Jin dynasty, and the name Nanning, meaning "southern tranquillity", dates to the Yuan dynasty. The city opened as a trading port in the early twentieth century and became the capital of Guangxi in the Republican era. It grew rapidly after 1949 and, from 2004, became the permanent host of the China–ASEAN Expo, reinforcing its role as China's gateway to the Association of Southeast Asian Nations.

Geography and economy

Nanning lies in a hilly basin just north of the Tropic of Cancer, with a humid subtropical climate and abundant rainfall. A large share of its population is Zhuang, China's largest ethnic minority group, alongside Han Chinese. The economy is based on food processing, sugar refining, machinery, electronics and services linked to regional trade. The city is served by Nanning Wuxu International Airport, a growing metro system opened in 2016, and high-speed rail lines to Guangzhou, Kunming and the Vietnamese border. Qingxiushan (Qingxiu Mountain) scenic area, within the city, is a popular park built around a forested hill and pagoda.
