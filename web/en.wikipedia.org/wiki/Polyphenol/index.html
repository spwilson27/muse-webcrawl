Polyphenol

Polyphenols are a large family of naturally occurring organic compounds characterised by multiple phenol units — aromatic rings bearing hydroxyl groups. Thousands have been identified in plants, where they serve in defence against ultraviolet radiation and pathogens and contribute colour, flavour, and astringency to foods such as tea, wine, coffee, cocoa, olive oil, fruits, and vegetables.

Flavonoids are the largest class of dietary polyphenols.

Classification

Major classes include flavonoids (such as flavonols, flavones, anthocyanins, and the catechins of tea), phenolic acids, stilbenes (including resveratrol of grapes and wine), and lignans. Tannins are larger polymeric polyphenols responsible for the astringency of wine and unripe fruit.

Diet and health research

Polyphenols are abundant in plant-based diets and have antioxidant activity in vitro. Research has investigated possible roles in cardiovascular and cognitive health, but evidence for specific health benefits in humans remains inconclusive: most dietary polyphenols are poorly absorbed and are extensively metabolised by the liver and gut microbiota, which complicates extrapolation from test-tube studies. Regulatory bodies have authorised few health claims for them.
