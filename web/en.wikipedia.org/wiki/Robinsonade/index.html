Robinsonade

Robinsonade
Stylistic originsAdventure fiction, castaway narrative
Cultural origins18th-century England and Germany
Originating workRobinson Crusoe by Daniel Defoe (1719)
Typical featuresShipwreck, desert island, self-sufficiency, improvised technology, eventual rescue or homecoming

A Robinsonade is a literary genre, or a work in that genre, that takes its name and pattern from Daniel Defoe's Robinson Crusoe (1719). A typical Robinsonade tells of a protagonist — often shipwrecked, sometimes deliberately marooned or stranded in space — who must survive alone (or in a small group) in an isolated place, improvising shelter, food and tools from whatever is at hand, and who is ultimately rescued or finds a way home.

History of the term

The word Robinsonade was coined in German literary discourse — the novelist Johann Gottfried Schnabel used it in the preface to his Insel Felsenburg (1731) — and German children's literature in particular developed the form: Joachim Heinrich Campe's Robinson der Jüngere (1779) recast Crusoe for young readers across Europe.

Characteristics

Robinsonades characteristically dwell on the practical reconstruction of civilisation in miniature: salvaging a wreck, building a dwelling, domesticating animals, keeping a journal and calendar. Many carry didactic or religious purposes, using isolation to explore self-reliance, providence, colonialism or human ingenuity. The genre overlaps with utopian fiction, survival stories and, in the twentieth century, science fiction about astronauts stranded on other worlds.

Notable examples

  • The Swiss Family Robinson (1812) by Johann David Wyss — a family survives on a lush island.
  • The Coral Island (1858) by R. M. Ballantyne — three boys in the Pacific.
  • The Mysterious Island (1874) by Jules Verne — castaways engineer their way to comfort.
  • Lord of the Flies (1954) by William Golding — a dark inversion of the form.
  • Life of Pi (2001) by Yann Martel — a boy survives on a lifeboat with a Bengal tiger.
  • The Martian (2011) by Andy Weir — the Robinsonade transferred to Mars.
