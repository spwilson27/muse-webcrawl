The Imjin War (Korean: 임진왜란, "Japanese disturbance of the Imjin year") was a pair of Japanese invasions of Korea in 1592–1598 launched by Toyotomi Hideyoshi with the ambition of conquering Ming China. Korea, aided by Ming China, ultimately repelled the invasions, but the war devastated the peninsula, killing perhaps a million Koreans and destroying land, records and cultural treasures on a massive scale.

First invasion (1592)

Japanese armies landed at Busan in May 1592 and, equipped with arquebuses and battle-hardened by the Sengoku wars, overwhelmed Korean forces and captured Seoul within weeks; King Seonjo fled north. The invasion stalled, however, through guerrilla resistance by "righteous armies" (uibyeong), the Korean navy's destruction of Japanese supply fleets under Admiral Yi Sun-sin — victor at Hansan Island in 1592 using the famed turtle ships — and the arrival of a large Ming relief army, which retook Pyongyang in 1593 and fought the Japanese to a standstill around Seoul.

Second invasion and end (1597–1598)

Peace talks collapsed and Hideyoshi invaded again in 1597. This time Korean and Ming defences were better prepared; Yi Sun-sin, restored to command after court intrigue, destroyed a Japanese fleet at Myeongnyang in October 1597 with only 13 ships. Hideyoshi's death in September 1598 ended the enterprise: the Japanese evacuated, suffering a final naval defeat at Noryang in December 1598, where Yi Sun-sin was killed.

Consequences

The war ruined Korean agriculture and population for a generation, carried off tens of thousands of Korean captives to Japan — among them potters who transformed Japanese ceramics — and looted cultural property (the Mimizuka "ear mound" in Kyoto marks the fate of Korean dead). Ming military expenditure weakened the dynasty, indirectly aiding the rise of the Manchus, while Korea commemorates Yi Sun-sin as its greatest national hero.
