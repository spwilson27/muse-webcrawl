Operation Polo

Operation Polo was a military operation in September 1948 in which the Indian Armed Forces invaded the princely state of Hyderabad, overthrowing its Nizam. Officially termed a "police action", the five-day campaign ended Hyderabad's attempt to remain independent after the partition of India and integrated the state into the Indian Union.

Background

Hyderabad, the largest and richest of the princely states, was a landlocked, predominantly Hindu-populated state ruled by the Muslim Nizam, Mir Osman Ali Khan. After British rule ended in August 1947, the Nizam declined to accede to either India or Pakistan, signing only a Standstill Agreement with India in November 1947. Negotiations for accession failed amid worsening communal tension, the activities of the Razakar paramilitaries led by Kasim Razvi, and reports of violence against the Hindu population, while India's Home Minister, Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, pressed for a settlement of the anomaly of an independent enclave in the heart of India.

The operation

On 13 September 1948 Indian forces under Major General J. N. Chaudhuri advanced from several directions. The main thrust from the west, along the Solapur–Hyderabad road, overcame resistance at Naldurg and Tulwapur; other columns advanced from Vijayawada, Raichur and Berar. The Hyderabad State Forces, numbering about 22,000 regulars with Razakar auxiliaries, were outmatched in the air and in armour. On 17 September the Nizam ordered a ceasefire; Hyderabad's army formally surrendered on 18 September and Indian troops entered Hyderabad city.

Aftermath

The Nizam acceded to India and was retained as Rajpramukh (governor) of the new Hyderabad State until its reorganisation in 1956. Indian casualties were light — about 32 killed — while Hyderabad's forces lost some 800 dead. The suppressed Sunderlal Committee report later estimated that 27,000–40,000 civilians, mostly Muslims, were killed in communal violence during and after the operation, a figure long contested. Operation Polo is remembered in India as the completion of national integration and in Hyderabad with more ambivalence.
