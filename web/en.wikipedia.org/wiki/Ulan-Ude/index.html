Ulan-Ude

Ulan-Ude is the capital city of the Republic of Buryatia, Russia, located about 100 kilometres southeast of Lake Baikal on the Trans-Siberian Railway. Founded by Cossacks in 1666 as the wintering settlement of Udinskoye, it has a population of roughly 437,000.

History and sights

The town grew as a trade centre between Russia, Mongolia and China and was renamed Verkhneudinsk, then Ulan-Ude ("red Uda" in Buryat) in 1934. Its main square is dominated by an enormous sculpted head of Vladimir Lenin, erected in 1971 and among the largest depictions of Lenin's head in the world. The nearby Ivolginsky Datsan is the centre of Buddhism in Russia, and the city has a significant Buryat Mongol cultural heritage, alongside communities of Old Believers in the surrounding region.

Transport

Ulan-Ude is a junction where the Trans-Mongolian Railway branches south from the Trans-Siberian line toward Ulaanbaatar and Beijing.
