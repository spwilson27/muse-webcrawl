Koreatown (Korean: 코리아타운) is a neighbourhood in central Los Angeles, California, centred near Wilshire Boulevard and Western Avenue. It is one of the most densely populated districts in Los Angeles County and home to the largest concentration of Koreans outside Korea, although the present-day population is majority Latino and highly diverse.

History

The area was developed in the early 20th century along the Wilshire corridor, with landmarks such as the Ambassador Hotel (1921) and the Art Deco Wiltern Theatre. Korean immigration after the 1965 Immigration Act transformed the declining district: Korean businesses, churches, banks and media clustered along Olympic Boulevard and Western Avenue in the 1970s and 1980s.

Koreatown suffered extensive damage during the 1992 Los Angeles riots (called Saigu, "4-29", in the Korean American community), when many Korean-owned shops were looted or burned. The neighbourhood rebuilt and thereafter saw large-scale mixed-use development; the city formally recognised Koreatown's boundaries in 2010.

Culture and economy

Koreatown is famous for its 24-hour nightlife, Korean barbecue restaurants, karaoke (noraebang), spas (jjimjilbang) and shopping malls such as Koreatown Plaza and the Madang complex. The Wilshire/Western station connects it to the Metro D Line. Annual events include the Korean Festival held each autumn. Despite its name, Korean Americans now form a minority of residents, alongside large Mexican, Salvadoran, Guatemalan and Bangladeshi communities.
