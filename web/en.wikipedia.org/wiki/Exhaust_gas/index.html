Exhaust gas

Exhaust gas or flue gas is emitted as a result of the combustion of fuels such as natural gas, petrol (gasoline), diesel fuel, fuel oil, biodiesel blends, or coal. It is discharged into the atmosphere through an exhaust pipe, flue gas stack, or propelling nozzle. Exhaust gas is a major component of emissions from motor vehicles and stationary engines, and several of its constituents are harmful air pollutants and greenhouse gases.

Composition

The bulk of combustion exhaust consists of nitrogen (from intake air), carbon dioxide and water vapour. The harmful minority components include:

  • Carbon monoxide (CO) — a toxic gas from incomplete combustion.
  • Nitrogen oxides (NOx) — formed at high combustion temperatures; contribute to smog, acid rain and respiratory disease.
  • Hydrocarbons / volatile organic compounds — unburned fuel, some carcinogenic (e.g. benzene).
  • Particulate matter — soot and ash, especially from diesel engines; fine particles penetrate deep into the lungs.
  • Sulfur oxides (SOx) — from sulfur in fuels, reduced by low-sulfur fuel standards.
  • Trace metals and, historically, lead compounds from leaded petrol.

Carbon dioxide, while not directly toxic at ambient levels, is the principal long-lived greenhouse gas from fossil-fuel combustion.

Sources

The largest sources are road vehicles, ships, aircraft, power stations, industrial furnaces and domestic heating. Diesel exhaust, classified by the WHO's International Agency for Research on Cancer as carcinogenic to humans (Group 1), is a particular focus of regulation.

Control and regulation

Emission standards such as the European emission standards (Euro 1–6/VI) and US EPA tiers limit pollutants per kilometre driven or per unit of output. Control technologies include catalytic converters, diesel particulate filters, selective catalytic reduction (SCR) using urea to cut NOx, exhaust gas recirculation (EGR) to lower combustion temperatures, and cleaner fuels. Inspection and maintenance programmes and on-board diagnostics (OBD) enforce compliance in use. Despite large per-vehicle improvements, growth in vehicle numbers and size has kept total emissions significant, motivating a transition to electric vehicles, which produce no tailpipe exhaust.
