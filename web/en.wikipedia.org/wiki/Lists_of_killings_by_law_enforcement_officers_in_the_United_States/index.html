Lists of killings by law enforcement officers in the United States

This article indexes Wikipedia's lists, organised by month and year, of people killed by law enforcement officers in the United States. The lists were created because the United States government long kept no comprehensive count of such deaths; official FBI statistics relied on voluntary reporting by police agencies and captured only a fraction of incidents.

Background

Journalistic and academic databases — including The Washington Post's database of fatal police shootings (begun in 2015), The Guardian's "The Counted" project (2015–2016), and the Mapping Police Violence project — have generally counted roughly 1,000 or more fatal police shootings per year, in addition to deaths by other means such as restraint, Tasers and vehicle pursuits. The FBI launched a National Use-of-Force Data Collection in 2019, still based on voluntary agency participation.

Structure of the lists

The Wikipedia lists are divided by year and month (for example, "List of killings by law enforcement officers in the United States, January 2024") and tabulate date, the deceased's name, age, race, state, and a summary of the circumstances as reported. Entries include both justified and disputed shootings, off-duty incidents, and deaths in custody where documented.

Debate

Scholars and journalists have debated the completeness and framing of crowd-sourced lists, while noting they provided an essential public record in the absence of official data. The topic gained prominence after the 2014 shooting of Michael Brown in Ferguson, Missouri, and again in 2020 following the murder of George Floyd.
