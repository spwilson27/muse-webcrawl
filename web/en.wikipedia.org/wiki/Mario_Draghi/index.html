Mario Draghi (born 3 September 1947) is an Italian economist, banker and statesman who served as Prime Minister of Italy from February 2021 to October 2022 and as President of the European Central Bank from 2011 to 2019. At the ECB he is widely credited with preserving the euro during the European debt crisis, above all through his pledge in July 2012 to do "whatever it takes" to save the single currency.

Early career

Draghi studied economics at the Sapienza University of Rome and earned a PhD from MIT in 1976, where his supervisors included Franco Modigliani and Robert Solow. After academic posts in Italy and a period as an executive director of the World Bank, he became Director General of the Italian Treasury in 1991, a post he held for a decade, overseeing extensive privatisations and the fiscal consolidation that enabled Italy to join the euro. He then worked as a vice-chairman of Goldman Sachs International in London from 2002 to 2005. As Governor of the Bank of Italy from 2006, he also chaired the Financial Stability Forum (later the Financial Stability Board) during the global financial crisis.

President of the European Central Bank

Draghi succeeded Jean-Claude Trichet as ECB President on 1 November 2011, in the depths of the eurozone sovereign debt crisis. In a speech in London on 26 July 2012 he declared: "Within our mandate, the ECB is ready to do whatever it takes to preserve the euro. And believe me, it will be enough." Bond yields in the periphery fell sharply even before the resulting Outright Monetary Transactions programme was announced; the programme itself was never activated. Draghi later introduced negative deposit rates (2014) and large-scale quantitative easing (2015). He was succeeded by Christine Lagarde on 1 November 2019.

Prime Minister of Italy

After the collapse of Giuseppe Conte's government, President Sergio Mattarella asked Draghi to form a government of national unity, which took office on 13 February 2021 with support from nearly all parties in parliament. His government ran the COVID-19 vaccination campaign and negotiated Italy's National Recovery and Resilience Plan under the EU's post-pandemic recovery fund, and it strongly supported Ukraine after Russia's invasion in 2022. In July 2022 the withdrawal of support by coalition partners brought the government down despite Draghi's broad public popularity; he was succeeded by Giorgia Meloni in October 2022. In September 2024 he presented a major report for the European Commission, The Future of European Competitiveness, arguing that the EU needed investment on the order of €800 billion per year to close its productivity and technology gap with the United States and China.
