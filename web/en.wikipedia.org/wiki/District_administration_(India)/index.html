District administration (India)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A district collectorate building in India

District administration in India refers to the management of government affairs within a district, the principal unit of territorial administration in the country. The system has its origins in the British Raj, when the district collector (also known as the district magistrate or deputy commissioner) was made responsible for revenue collection, law and order, and general administration within a district.

The district collector

The district collector is an officer of the Indian Administrative Service (IAS) who is the chief executive and administrative head of the district. The collector's duties typically include revenue administration, maintenance of law and order (as district magistrate), conduct of elections, disaster management, and supervision of development programmes. The collector is assisted by additional district magistrates, sub-divisional officers, and tehsildars.

Structure

Districts are generally subdivided into sub-divisions (headed by a sub-divisional magistrate) and further into tehsils or talukas, which consist of groups of villages. Policing in the district is headed by a superintendent of police, who works in coordination with the district magistrate on matters of public order. Elected local bodies, such as the zila panchayat under the Panchayati Raj system, form the democratic tier of rural local government.
