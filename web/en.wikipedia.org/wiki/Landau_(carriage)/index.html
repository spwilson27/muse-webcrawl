Landau (carriage)

A landau is a coachbuilding term for a type of four-wheeled horse-drawn carriage with a convertible folding top. It was named after the German city of Landau, where such carriages were first produced in the 18th century. The landau's defining feature is its soft leather top, divided into two sections, which can be folded down or left up.

Description

The landau was a luxury carriage, typically drawn by a pair or four horses and driven by a coachman. Its folding head made it suitable for ceremonial use in good weather, and it became associated with royalty and heads of state. The British Royal Mews still maintains state landaus used on ceremonial occasions.

Automotive use

The name survived into the automobile era: a "landau" described a car with a folding rear roof section, and later the term "landau bar" referred to the decorative S-shaped bar on the C-pillar of hearses and luxury cars, evoking the folding mechanism of the original carriage.
