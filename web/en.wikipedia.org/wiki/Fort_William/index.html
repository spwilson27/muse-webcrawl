Overview

Fort William is a town in Lochaber in the Scottish Highlands, on the shores of Loch Linnhe, near the southern end of the Great Glen and in the shadow of Ben Nevis, the highest mountain in the British Isles (1,345 m). The town grew around a Cromwellian fort (1654) rebuilt and named for William III; the Jacobite sieges of 1746 failed to take it. The original fort is largely gone, its site by the station.

Today

Fort William is a hub for hillwalking and mountain biking (the Nevis Range gondola and World Cup downhill track), the start/finish of the West Highland Way and the Great Glen Way, the terminus of the Caledonian Canal at Corpach, and the southern end of the Jacobite steam train line to Mallaig over the Glenfinnan Viaduct, famed from the Harry Potter films. Rain is a defining feature — it is among Britain's wettest towns.
