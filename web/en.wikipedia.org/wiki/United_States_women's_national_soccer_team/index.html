United States women's national soccer team

The United States women's national soccer team (USWNT) represents the United States in international women's soccer. The team is governed by the United States Soccer Federation and competes in CONCACAF. It is the most successful team in the history of the FIFA Women's World Cup, having won the tournament four times (1991, 1999, 2015, and 2019).

History

The team played its first international match in 1985 in Italy. It won the inaugural Women's World Cup in China in 1991, defeating Norway in the final. The 1999 World Cup, hosted by the United States, was a landmark for women's sport; the final against China at the Rose Bowl drew over 90,000 spectators and ended with a penalty shoot-out decided by Brandi Chastain's famous winning kick.

The USWNT has also won four Olympic gold medals (1996, 2004, 2008, 2012) and numerous CONCACAF championships. Notable players across its history include Mia Hamm, Abby Wambach, Carli Lloyd, Alex Morgan, and Megan Rapinoe.

Equal pay

Members of the team pursued a lengthy legal and public campaign for equal pay with the men's national team. In 2022 the players settled their equal pay lawsuit against the federation, and new collective bargaining agreements guaranteed identical economic terms, including World Cup prize money pooling, for the men's and women's teams.
