Glyph

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Glyph
DefinitionA specific graphical representation of a character or grapheme
Related conceptsCharacter, grapheme, typeface, font

A glyph is a particular design, in a typographical font, of a character or graphic symbol. It is the specific shape that represents an abstract character in a given typeface: for example, the letters a and ɑ are two different glyphs (allographs) of the same lowercase letter "a".

In typography

In modern typography and computing, a character is an abstract unit of text, while a glyph is a concrete visual form. A typeface (font) is a collection of glyphs. One character may correspond to several glyphs — contextual forms in Arabic, ligatures such as "fi", and variant forms in different fonts — and one glyph may represent a sequence of characters. In Unicode-based systems, characters are encoded as code points, and a font-rendering engine selects glyphs, potentially composing them through OpenType features.

Historical use

The word comes from the Greek glyphē, "carving". It is used for carved symbols in archaeology — notably Maya glyphs and Egyptian hieroglyphs — and for the marks in scripts such as cuneiform. In these contexts a glyph is any purposeful mark forming part of a writing system.

Distinctions

  • A grapheme is the smallest functional unit of a writing system.
  • A character is an abstract unit encoded in a character set.
  • A glyph is the rendered shape chosen by a font.
