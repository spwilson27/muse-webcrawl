The Hopi language (Hopi: Hopilàvayi) is a Uto-Aztecan language spoken by the Hopi people of northeastern Arizona. It is the only language in its branch of Northern Uto-Aztecan, its closest relatives being the Numic, Takic and Tubatulabal languages. Hopi is spoken in the villages on and below the three Hopi mesas, which fall into three main dialect groups.

Phonology and grammar

Hopi has a modest consonant inventory including a series of plain and labialised velar stops, a distinction between falling and level tone in some dialects (notably First Mesa), and vowel length contrasts; the Third Mesa dialect famously lacks the labialised consonants /kʷ/ series and tonal contrasts of the east. The language is agglutinative and strongly suffixing, with complex verb morphology encoding aspect, plurality and evidential distinctions. Noun plurality is expressed by reduplication for many nouns, and Hopi distinguishes animate and inanimate classes. Most Hopi sentences place the verb last, though word order is flexible.

The Whorf controversy

Hopi achieved unusual fame in linguistics through the work of Benjamin Lee Whorf, who used it as his principal example for linguistic relativity, claiming that Hopi lacks words, grammatical forms or expressions referring directly to time, and that its speakers therefore conceptualise time in a fundamentally "timeless" way. Later field research, especially Ekkehart Malotki's Hopi Time (1983), documented extensive Hopi vocabulary and grammar for temporal relations and sharply criticised Whorf's analysis; the debate remains a standard case study in discussions of the Sapir–Whorf hypothesis.

Vitality and revitalisation

Surveys in the 1990s found Hopi still widely spoken by adults, though child acquisition had declined unevenly among villages. The Hopi Tribe has supported language preservation through a standardised orthography (developed with linguists and codified in the 1998 Hopìikwa Lavàytutuveni dictionary), school immersion programmes, and the Hopi Language Program office. Hopi is also notable for its early and extensive documentation, from 19th-century vocabularies to modern grammars and text collections.
