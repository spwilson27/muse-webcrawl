A spa is a location where mineral-rich spring water (and sometimes seawater) is used to give medicinal baths. The term also denotes resorts and commercial establishments offering health and wellness treatments — massages, facials, hydrotherapy, saunas and steam rooms. The word derives from the town of Spa, Belgium, whose curative springs were famed across Europe from the 16th century; a popular folk etymology derives it from the Latin sanitas per aquam, "health through water".

History

Therapeutic bathing is ancient: Bronze Age springs were venerated in Britain and Gaul, the Romans built thermae throughout their empire (Aquae Sulis at Bath, England, among them), and bathing cultures flourished independently in Japan (onsen), the Islamic world (hammam) and Finland (sauna). In early modern Europe, physicians prescribed "taking the waters" at resorts such as Spa, Bath, Vichy, Karlsbad and Baden-Baden, which became fashionable social centres. The 19th century added hydropathic establishments; the late 20th century saw the modern wellness industry grow into a global business.

Types and treatments

Day spas offer treatments without overnight stays; destination spas combine lodging with programmes of fitness and diet; medical spas provide clinically supervised procedures; and traditional thermal spa towns — eleven of which were inscribed in 2021 as the UNESCO World Heritage "Great Spa Towns of Europe" — centre on natural springs. Scientific evidence for many spa treatments is modest, though warm-water therapy and massage show benefits for some musculoskeletal conditions.
