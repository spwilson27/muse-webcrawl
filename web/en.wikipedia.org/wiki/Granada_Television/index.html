Granada Television was the ITV franchise holder for North West England from 1956, and one of the most influential of the British regional television companies. Based at Granada Studios on Quay Street, Manchester — the first purpose-built television studios in Britain — Granada combined a powerful regional identity with an outsized record of acclaimed network drama, current affairs and entertainment.

History

The franchise was founded by Sidney Bernstein, of the Granada cinema chain, who chose Manchester as his base (partly, he said, because of its rain — judging that northerners would watch more television) and conceived the region's self-image as "Granadaland". Broadcasting began on 3 May 1956. At first Granada covered the whole of northern England on weekdays, sharing with ABC at weekends; in the 1968 franchise round the region was split, Yorkshire Television taking the east, and Granada retaining the North West seven days a week.

Under Sidney Bernstein and later his successors Denis Forman and David Plowright, Granada built a reputation for serious journalism and gritty realism. The company survived the 1991 franchise auction, expanded through the 1990s by acquiring LWT, Yorkshire-Tyne Tees and Border Television, and in February 2004 merged with Carlton Communications to form ITV plc, ending its independent existence. The Granada name survives in the regional service ITV Granada and in the production label's legacy.

Programmes

  • Coronation Street (1960– ) — the world's longest-running television soap opera, created by Tony Warren and still filmed in Manchester.
  • World in Action (1963–1998) — hard-hitting investigative current affairs.
  • The Royle Family, Cracker, Cold Feet and Prime Suspect (the latter for the network) — landmark drama and comedy.
  • Brideshead Revisited (1981) and The Jewel in the Crown (1984) — lavish literary adaptations.
  • Seven Up! and the Up documentary series (1964–2019), following a group of children through their lives.
  • University Challenge (1962–1987 on ITV) and The Krypton Factor.

Studios

Granada Studios on Quay Street were a Manchester landmark, their red "GRANADA TV" sign visible across the city centre; the Coronation Street set and the Studios Tour (1988–1999) drew millions of visitors. Production moved to MediaCityUK in Salford from 2013, and the Quay Street site was subsequently redeveloped, with parts of the original building retained.
