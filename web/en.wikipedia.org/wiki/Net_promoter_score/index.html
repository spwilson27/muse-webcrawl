Net promoter score

Net promoter score (NPS) is a market research metric that is based on a single survey question asking respondents to rate the likelihood that they would recommend a company, product, or a service to a friend or colleague. It is a proprietary instrument developed by Fred Reichheld, who owns the registered NPS trademark in conjunction with Bain & Company and Satmetrix.

Respondents are grouped as detractors (0–6), passives (7–8), and promoters (9–10).

Method

Respondents answer the question "How likely is it that you would recommend [company/product/service] to a friend or colleague?" on an 11-point scale from 0 (not at all likely) to 10 (extremely likely). Based on their rating, customers fall into three categories:

  • Promoters (score 9–10) are loyal enthusiasts who will keep buying and refer others.
  • Passives (score 7–8) are satisfied but unenthusiastic customers.
  • Detractors (score 0–6) are unhappy customers who can damage the brand through negative word of mouth.

The NPS is calculated as the percentage of promoters minus the percentage of detractors, giving a score between −100 and +100.

History and adoption

Reichheld introduced the metric in his 2003 Harvard Business Review article "The One Number You Need to Grow", and expanded on it in the book The Ultimate Question (2006). NPS is widely used by Fortune 500 companies and other organizations as a measure of customer loyalty and a predictor of business growth.

Criticism

Academic researchers have questioned whether NPS is a better predictor of growth than other customer satisfaction measures, criticized the loss of information in collapsing an 11-point scale into three groups, and noted that the claimed superiority of the metric has not always been replicated in independent studies.
