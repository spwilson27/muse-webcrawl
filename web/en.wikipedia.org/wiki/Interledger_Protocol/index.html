Interledger Protocol

The Interledger Protocol (ILP) is an open protocol suite for sending payments across different payment networks or ledgers. Like the Internet Protocol, Interledger uses connectors — analogous to routers — to forward packets of value between independent ledgers, which may include banks, mobile money systems, and cryptocurrency networks.

Design

Interledger is designed so that no single ledger, currency, or central operator is required. Payments are split into small packets, each secured by cryptographic conditions: a payment completes end-to-end only when the recipient produces a fulfillment that propagates back along the chain of connectors, allowing each hop to settle atomically. Its STREAM protocol multiplexes payments and data, and ILPv4 is defined in IETF-style specifications maintained by the Interledger community.

History

The protocol was proposed in 2015 by Stefan Thomas and Evan Schwartz of Ripple, and development moved to a W3C community group and later the Interledger Foundation. Implementations include Rafiki, an open-source reference implementation, and it underpins the Web Monetization standard proposal.

Applications

Interledger is used for cross-border payments, streaming micropayments for web content, and linking otherwise incompatible payment systems, particularly in financial-inclusion projects in emerging markets.
