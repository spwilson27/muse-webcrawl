The Venus of Dolní Věstonice

Dolní Věstonice

Dolní Věstonice (German: Unterwisternitz) is a municipality and village in Břeclav District in the South Moravian Region of the Czech Republic, with a population of about 300. It lies on the northern slopes of the Pavlov Hills, near the Dyje (Thaya) river and the Nové Mlýny reservoirs.

Archaeological site

The village is internationally famous for the Upper Palaeolithic archaeological site of Dolní Věstonice, one of the most important Gravettian sites in Europe, excavated from the 1920s onwards, notably under Karel Absolon and Bohuslav Klíma. The site, dating to roughly 29,000–25,000 years ago, yielded mammoth-bone dwellings, burials, and some of the earliest known ceramic artefacts.

Most famous is the Venus of Dolní Věstonice, an 11.1 cm nude female figurine fired from clay around 29,000–25,000 BCE, among the oldest known ceramic objects in the world. Other finds include portrait-like carved heads in ivory, a triple burial, and evidence of early textile impressions in clay. The site's museum, Archeopark Pavlov, opened nearby in 2016.
