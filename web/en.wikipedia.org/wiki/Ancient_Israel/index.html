Ancient Israel refers to the Israelite kingdoms and society that existed in the southern Levant during the Iron Age, from roughly the 12th century BC until the Babylonian conquest of Jerusalem in 586 BC. The term covers the united monarchy described in the Hebrew Bible, and the historically attested kingdoms of Israel in the north and Judah in the south.

Origins

The earliest known reference to "Israel" outside the Bible is the Egyptian Merneptah Stele of about 1208 BC, which names Israel as a people in Canaan. Archaeologists generally see the early Israelites as emerging from the indigenous population of the Canaanite hill country, where hundreds of small villages appeared in the Iron I period (c. 1200–1000 BC). These settlers shared a material culture marked by four-room houses, collared-rim storage jars and an apparent avoidance of pork.

The monarchies

According to the biblical account, the tribes were united under Saul, David and Solomon in the 10th century BC before splitting into two kingdoms. The northern kingdom of Israel, with capitals at Shechem, Tirzah and then Samaria, was the larger and wealthier state; it fell to the Neo-Assyrian Empire in 722 BC. The southern kingdom of Judah survived as an Assyrian, then Babylonian, vassal until Nebuchadnezzar II destroyed Jerusalem and its Temple in 586 BC and exiled much of its population.

Religion and society

Israelite religion centred on the worship of Yahweh, increasingly conceived as the sole god, with the Jerusalem Temple as his principal sanctuary. The period saw the composition of much of the Hebrew Bible — legal codes, royal annals, prophecy and poetry — which was edited into its received form during and after the exile.

Aftermath

Following the Persian conquest of Babylon, exiles were permitted to return and rebuild the Temple (completed 515 BC), establishing the province of Yehud from which Second Temple Judaism developed.
