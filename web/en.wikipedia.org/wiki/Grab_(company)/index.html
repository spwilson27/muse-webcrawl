Grab Holdings Limited is a Singaporean multinational technology company and the developer of the Grab "super-app" for ride-hailing, food and parcel delivery, and digital financial services. It operates across eight Southeast Asian countries — Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand and Vietnam — and is widely described as the region's first "decacorn".

History

Grab was founded in Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, in 2012 by Harvard Business School classmates Anthony Tan and Tan Hooi Ling, launching as MyTeksi, a taxi-booking app intended to improve the safety and reliability of Malaysian taxis. It rebranded as GrabTaxi in 2013 and moved its headquarters to Singapore in 2014, expanding into motorcycle taxis (GrabBike), private-car hailing (GrabCar), and deliveries (GrabExpress).

In March 2018 Grab acquired Uber's Southeast Asian operations in exchange for Uber taking a 27.5% stake in Grab — a landmark deal that ended a costly regional price war and attracted regulatory scrutiny over competition in Singapore and the Philippines. Grab unified its services into a single super-app in 2018 and built out GrabFood (food delivery) and the GrabPay mobile wallet, later adding lending, insurance and digital banking ventures, including GXS Bank in Singapore and GX Bank in Malaysia.

Grab went public on the Nasdaq in December 2021 by merging with the special-purpose acquisition company Altimeter Growth Corp., in what was then the largest-ever SPAC merger, valuing the company at roughly US$40 billion. SoftBank and Uber are among its major shareholders.

Services

  • Mobility — ride-hailing across taxis, private cars, motorcycles and premium vehicles.
  • Deliveries — GrabFood, GrabMart and GrabExpress last-mile parcel services.
  • Financial services — GrabPay e-wallet, GrabFin lending and insurance, and digital banks (GXS, GX Bank, and a stake in Indonesia's Superbank).
  • Enterprise — GrabForBusiness, advertising, merchant tools and maps/GrabMaps.

Criticism and regulation

Grab has faced competition fines following the Uber merger, disputes over driver classification and earnings, and regulatory caps on commissions in several markets. It has also been involved in debates over gig-worker protection legislation in Malaysia, Indonesia and Singapore.
