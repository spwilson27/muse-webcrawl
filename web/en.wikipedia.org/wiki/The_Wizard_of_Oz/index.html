The Wizard of Oz

The Wizard of Oz is a 1939 American musical fantasy film produced by Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer, based on L. Frank Baum's 1900 children's novel The Wonderful Wizard of Oz. Starring Judy Garland as Dorothy Gale, it is widely regarded as one of the greatest films of all time and is among the most watched movies in history.

Plot

Kansas farm girl Dorothy, swept to the magical Land of Oz by a tornado, follows the Yellow Brick Road to the Emerald City to ask the Wizard to send her home — "There's no place like home." She is joined by a Scarecrow who wants brains, a Tin Woodman who wants a heart, and a Cowardly Lion. After confronting the Wicked Witch of the West and exposing the Wizard as an ordinary (if resourceful) man behind a curtain, Dorothy learns that her silver shoes — ruby slippers in the film — could have taken her home all along, and Glinda the Good Witch helps her return to Kansas.

Production and legacy

Directed principally by Victor Fleming, the film made innovative use of Technicolor, contrasting sepia-toned Kansas with the vivid colours of Oz. Its songs by Harold Arlen and Yip Harburg include "Over the Rainbow," which won the Academy Award for Best Original Song and was named the greatest movie song of all time by the American Film Institute. Initially only a modest box-office success, the film became a cultural institution through annual television broadcasts beginning in the 1950s. In 1989 it was among the first films selected for the U.S. National Film Registry, and UNESCO's Memory of the World register includes it. Its phrases and images remain deeply embedded in popular culture.
