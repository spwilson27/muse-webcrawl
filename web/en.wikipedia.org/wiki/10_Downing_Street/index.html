10 Downing Street in London is the official residence and office of the Prime Minister of the United Kingdom. Together with the adjoining Cabinet Office at 70 Whitehall, it forms the headquarters of His Majesty's Government. Colloquially known as Number 10, the building is over 300 years old and contains approximately 100 rooms, including the Cabinet Room, state dining rooms, and the prime minister's private flat.

History

The street was built in the 1680s by Sir George Downing on the site of the medieval Cockpit. George II offered the house to Robert Walpole in 1732; Walpole accepted it not as a personal gift but as the residence of the First Lord of the Treasury — the office now invariably held by the prime minister — and moved in in 1735. Architect William Kent joined No. 10 to a larger house behind it, creating the complex that survives today.

The building

The black front door, with its lion-and-unicorn fanlight and polished number "10", is one of the most photographed in Britain; the original door is preserved in the Churchill Museum. The building's modest facade conceals an extensive interior rebuilt and strengthened several times, notably in 1958–1963 and during the Second World War. A resident cat, the Chief Mouser to the Cabinet Office, is a longstanding tradition of the office.
