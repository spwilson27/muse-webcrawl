Libertarianism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Libertarianism is a political philosophy that takes liberty to be the primary political value. Libertarians seek to maximise autonomy and political freedom, emphasising free association, freedom of choice, individualism and voluntary association. In the usage dominant in the United States, libertarianism advocates civil liberties, natural law, free-market capitalism and a major reduction — or, in some variants, abolition — of the modern welfare state.

Principles

Core doctrines include self-ownership, strong private property rights, and the non-aggression principle: no one may initiate force or fraud against another. Robert Nozick's Anarchy, State, and Utopia (1974) defended a "night-watchman" minimal state limited to courts, police and defence ("minarchism"); thinkers such as Ayn Rand and Murray Rothbard developed objectivist and anarcho-capitalist variants. Libertarians typically support free markets, free speech, drug decriminalisation, open immigration, gun rights and non-interventionist foreign policy, and oppose conscription, most taxation and economic regulation.

History and usage

The word "libertarian" was first used politically by European anarchists (Joseph Déjacque, 1857); in much of Europe it still connotes left-libertarian and socialist traditions — "libertarian socialism" — whereas the American movement, organised from the 1970s around the Libertarian Party (founded 1971) and think tanks, identifies it with propertarian, market liberalism. The tension between these usages is a standard caveat in the literature.

Criticism

Critics argue that formal liberty without resources is hollow, that property rights themselves rest on state coercion, and that libertarian premises can entrench private power. Defenders reply that dispersed, voluntary arrangements outperform centralised authority on both moral and practical grounds, and that libertarian ideas have influenced real policy debates — drug liberalisation, school choice, deregulation and civil-liberties litigation.
