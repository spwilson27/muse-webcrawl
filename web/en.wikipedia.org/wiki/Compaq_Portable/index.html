The Compaq Portable was one of the earliest commercially successful portable computers and the first product of Compaq Computer Corporation. Announced in November 1982 and shipped in March 1983, it was the first legally reverse-engineered, IBM PC-compatible computer.

Design

The machine was housed in a rugged "luggable" case the size of a portable sewing machine, with a built-in 9-inch monochrome CRT display, detachable keyboard, 128 KB of RAM (expandable), and dual 5¼-inch floppy disk drives (later models offered a hard disk).

Significance

Because Compaq clean-room reverse-engineered the IBM BIOS, the Portable ran virtually all IBM PC software. It sold over 50,000 units in its first year and established Compaq as a major competitor to IBM.
