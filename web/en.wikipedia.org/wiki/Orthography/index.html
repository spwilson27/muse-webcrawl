Orthography

An orthography is a set of conventions for writing a language, including norms of spelling, hyphenation, capitalisation, word breaks, emphasis, and punctuation. Most transnational languages in the modern period have a system of writing, and for most such languages a standard orthography has been developed, often based on a standard variety of the language.

The English word orthography dates from the 15th century. It comes from the French orthographie, from Latin orthographia, which derives from the Greek ὀρθός (orthós, "correct") and γράφειν (gráphein, "to write").

Definition and etymology

Orthography is largely concerned with matters of spelling, and in particular the relationship between phonemes and graphemes in a language. Other elements that may be considered part of orthography include hyphenation, capitalisation, word breaks, emphasis, and punctuation. Orthography thus describes or defines the set of symbols used in writing a language and the rules regarding how to write these symbols.

Most natural languages developed as oral languages, and a writing system has usually been crafted or adapted afterwards as an adjunct to the spoken language. Even today, there exist many languages with no writing system. When a language develops a writing system, it is common for a range of cultural, political and individual factors to contribute to inconsistencies and exceptions in the spelling, so that over time an orthography may come to reflect the history of the language more than its present-day phonology.

Typology

Writing systems may be classified according to the primary unit they represent: phonemes (alphabets), syllables (syllabaries), or morphemes and words (logographies). The degree to which an orthography corresponds to the spoken language varies: "shallow" orthographies, such as those of Finnish, Spanish or Italian, have a near one-to-one correspondence between letters and sounds, whereas "deep" orthographies, such as English and French, preserve many historical spellings that diverge from modern pronunciation.

Orthographic reform

Orthographies are sometimes deliberately reformed by governments or language academies. Examples include the German orthography reform of 1996, the Portuguese Language Orthographic Agreement of 1990, and various spelling reforms of Dutch, Russian, and Turkish. Reform proposals for English spelling, such as those championed by Noah Webster in the United States, have succeeded only partially; Webster's reforms account for many of the systematic differences between American and British spelling, such as color versus colour.
