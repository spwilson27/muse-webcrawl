McDonnell Douglas F-15E Strike Eagle

F-15E Strike Eagle
Role: Multirole strike fighter
Manufacturer: McDonnell Douglas / Boeing Defense, Space & Security
First flight: 11 December 1986
Introduction: April 1988
Primary user: United States Air Force
Developed from: F-15 Eagle

The McDonnell Douglas (now Boeing) F-15E Strike Eagle is an American all-weather multirole strike fighter derived from the McDonnell Douglas F-15 Eagle. The F-15E was designed in the 1980s for long-range, high-speed interdiction without relying on escort or electronic-warfare aircraft, and it has flown in operations over Iraq, Afghanistan, Libya and Syria.

Design

Unlike the single-seat air-superiority F-15C, the Strike Eagle has a two-seat cockpit: a pilot in front and a weapon systems officer behind, managing the APG-70 (later APG-82 AESA) radar, LANTIRN or Sniper targeting pods, and precision weapons. Conformal fuel tanks fitted along the intakes carry additional fuel and sensor/weapons stations while adding little drag. The rear fuselage was strengthened for the strike role, and the aircraft can carry up to about 23,000 lb (10,400 kg) of stores, including laser- and GPS-guided bombs, while retaining the full air-to-air armament of the Eagle.

Variants and operators

Export derivatives include the F-15I Ra'am for Israel, the F-15S and F-15SA for Saudi Arabia, the F-15K Slam Eagle for South Korea, the F-15SG for Singapore and the F-15QA for Qatar. The F-15EX Eagle II, a single-seat derivative based on the QA, entered US service in the 2020s to complement the existing fleet.

Operational history

The F-15E made its combat debut in Operation Desert Storm in 1991, hunting Scud missile launchers and striking targets deep in Iraq with laser-guided bombs. It has since been a mainstay of US strike operations, including enforcing no-fly zones, Operation Allied Force in 1999, and campaigns in Afghanistan, Iraq and Syria.
