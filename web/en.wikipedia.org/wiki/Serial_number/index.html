Serial number

A serial number (also known as a S/N or serial no.) is a unique identifier assigned incrementally or sequentially to an item, to uniquely identify it. Serial numbers need not be strictly numerical. They may contain letters and other typographical symbols, or may consist entirely of a character string.

Applications

Serial numbers are used to identify individual manufactured units of a product, allowing manufacturers to track production batches, detect counterfeit goods, and manage warranty claims. Common applications include automobiles, where the vehicle identification number (VIN) encodes the manufacturer, model, year, and a unique production sequence; firearms; electronics; certificates and banknotes.

History

The practice of numbering units dates to early industrial mass production. With the growth of assembly-line manufacturing in the early twentieth century, serial numbering became essential for quality control and recall management. Modern systems often combine serial numbers with barcodes and RFID tags.

Serial number schemes

A serial number may be a simple sequential counter, or it may embed information such as the date of manufacture, factory location, or product variant. Some manufacturers encode check digits to detect transcription errors. In cryptographic and software contexts, serial numbers are issued as license keys whose validity can be verified algorithmically.
