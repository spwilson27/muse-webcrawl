Scattered disc

The scattered disc (or scattered disk) is a distant circumstellar disc region of the Solar System populated by icy small bodies known as scattered-disc objects (SDOs). It extends from the outer edge of the Kuiper belt at roughly 35 astronomical units out to 100 AU and beyond, with orbits that are highly eccentric, steeply inclined, and unstable on gigayear timescales.

Formation

Unlike classical Kuiper belt objects, which formed roughly in place, scattered-disc objects are thought to be former Kuiper belt bodies that were gravitationally "scattered" into their present orbits by close encounters with Neptune during the planet's outward migration in the early Solar System. Their perihelia remain near Neptune's orbit (around 30–38 AU), so they continue to interact with the giant planets and slowly leak inward to become centaurs and eventually short-period comets.

Members

The largest known scattered-disc object is the dwarf planet Eris, whose discovery in 2005 — initially announced as the "tenth planet" — prompted the reclassification of Pluto. Other prominent members include Gonggong and the extremely distant detached objects sometimes grouped with the disc, such as Sedna. The total population of SDOs larger than 100 km is estimated in the tens of thousands, though only a few hundred have been observed because of their great distances and slow motion.
