Quarter panel

A quarter panel (British English: rear wing) is the body panel (exterior surface) of an automobile between a rear door (or side of a two-door model) and the trunk (boot), surrounding the wheel well for the rear wheel. In similar usage, a front quarter panel is the corresponding panel between a front door and the hood (bonnet), enclosing the front wheel well; on most modern cars these are simply called fenders.

Characteristics

Quarter panels are typically made of sheet metal or, on some vehicles, fiberglass or carbon fiber. In unibody construction the rear quarter panel is often welded to the body shell, making replacement a major repair involving cutting and welding, whereas front fenders are usually bolted on.

The visible "shoulder" of the rear quarter panel is a significant styling feature; prominent rear quarter panels and the integrated rear haunches are hallmarks of many sports and muscle cars.

Repair

Damage to quarter panels is among the most expensive body repairs because of their structural integration and large surface area. Minor dents may be repaired with paintless dent removal; severe damage usually requires sectioning and replacement.
