Cleobis and Biton

Cleobis and Biton (Greek: Κλέοβις καὶ Βίτων) are figures in Greek mythology, the twin sons of Cydippe, a priestess of Hera at Argos. Their story is told by Herodotus in his Histories, in the speech of Solon to Croesus on the question of who is the happiest of men.

The story

When the oxen needed to draw their mother's cart to the festival of Hera failed to arrive, Cleobis and Biton yoked themselves to the cart and pulled it some 45 stadia (about 8 km) to the temple. Cydippe prayed to Hera to grant her sons the greatest blessing possible for mortals. After feasting, the young men lay down to sleep in the temple and never awoke. Herodotus presents their peaceful death at the height of their honour as proof that it is better to die than to live in misery.

Statues

Two archaic kouros statues found at Delphi, inscribed as the work of Polymedes of Argos and identified as Cleobis and Biton, date to around 600–580 BC and are now in the Delphi Archaeological Museum.
