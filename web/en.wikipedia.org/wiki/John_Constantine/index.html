John Constantine is a fictional character appearing in American comic books published by DC Comics. An occult detective, con man and working-class magician from Liverpool, England, he first appeared in The Saga of the Swamp Thing #37 (1985), created by writer Alan Moore with artists Stephen R. Bissette, Rick Veitch and John Totleben. He became the protagonist of the long-running series Hellblazer (1988–2013), the flagship title of DC's Vertigo imprint, and later joined the mainstream DC Universe in series such as Justice League Dark.

Creation and characterisation

Moore created Constantine as a supporting character for Swamp Thing, wanting a magician who resembled the musician Sting — a resemblance the artists, fans of the Police, gladly honoured. Costumed in a rumpled trench coat and perpetually smoking Silk Cut cigarettes, Constantine is a cynical anti-hero who defeats demons and angels alike less by raw power than by bluff, bargaining and sacrifice of others: a recurring pattern of the series is that his friends die while he survives, haunted by their ghosts.

Publication history

Hellblazer launched in 1988 with Jamie Delano as writer, followed by notable runs from Garth Ennis, Warren Ellis, Brian Azzarello, Mike Carey and Peter Milligan. Ennis's "Dangerous Habits" storyline, in which Constantine cheats the Devil to cure his lung cancer, is among the most acclaimed. Unusually, the character aged in real time during the Vertigo run. In 2011 he was folded into the main DC continuity as leader of Justice League Dark, and has since headlined several relaunched series.

Fictional biography

Born in Liverpool in 1953, Constantine's mother died in childbirth, and he was raised — and blamed — by his father. His early dabbling in magic ended in disaster at Newcastle in 1978, when a botched summoning left a young girl, Astra, damned to Hell — the failure that haunts him throughout the series. Based largely in London and travelling widely, he meddles in conflicts between Heaven and Hell while maintaining his hard-drinking, chain-smoking lifestyle.

In other media

Constantine was played by Keanu Reeves in the film Constantine (2005) and by Matt Ryan in the NBC television series Constantine (2014–15), a role Ryan reprised in the Arrowverse and in animated films. The character has also appeared in numerous animated series and video games.
