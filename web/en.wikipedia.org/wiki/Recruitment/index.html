Recruitment

Recruitment is the overall process of identifying, sourcing, screening, shortlisting, and interviewing candidates for jobs within an organisation, in order to attract and select suitably qualified people for vacant positions. It is a core function of human resource management and may be carried out by in-house recruiters, hiring managers, employment agencies, or specialist recruitment firms. The term also applies to enlistment in the armed forces, recruitment of research participants, and the enlistment of members by organisations generally.

Recruitment matches candidates to roles through sourcing, screening, and selection.

Process

A typical recruitment cycle begins with workforce planning and a job analysis that produces a job description and person specification. Candidates are then sourced — through job advertisements, job boards, social and professional networks such as LinkedIn, employee referrals, careers fairs, or direct approaches ("headhunting") for senior roles. Applications are screened against the requirements, shortlisted candidates are assessed through interviews, tests, work samples, or assessment centres, and references and background checks precede a formal offer and onboarding.

Methods and technology

Modern recruitment relies heavily on applicant tracking systems (ATS) that parse résumés and manage candidate pipelines, on online job platforms, and increasingly on automated screening and AI-assisted tools — which raise questions of bias and fairness that regulators have begun to address. Recruitment process outsourcing (RPO) transfers some or all of the function to external providers. Metrics such as time-to-fill, cost-per-hire, and quality-of-hire are used to evaluate effectiveness.

Internal and external recruitment

Internal recruitment fills vacancies by promotion or transfer, rewarding existing staff and preserving institutional knowledge; external recruitment brings in new skills and perspectives. Employers weigh these benefits alongside legal obligations: in most jurisdictions recruitment must comply with anti-discrimination and equal-opportunity law governing advertisements, selection criteria, and interview practice.
