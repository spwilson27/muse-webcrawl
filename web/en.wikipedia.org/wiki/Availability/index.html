Availability

In reliability engineering, availability is the probability that a system or component is functioning correctly at a given point in time, or the proportion of time over an interval during which it is in a functioning condition. It is one of the principal dependability attributes of computing and industrial systems, alongside reliability, maintainability and safety.

Definition

Steady-state availability is commonly expressed as A = MTBF / (MTBF + MTTR), where MTBF is the mean time between failures and MTTR is the mean time to repair. Availability therefore improves either by failing less often or by being repaired more quickly. In service-level agreements for computing services, availability is often quoted as a percentage of "nines": 99.9% availability ("three nines") permits about 8.8 hours of downtime per year, while 99.999% ("five nines") permits only about five minutes.

Related concepts

Availability differs from reliability, which is the probability of functioning without failure over a stated period; a system may be highly available despite frequent failures if repairs are very fast. High-availability design employs redundancy, automatic failover and clustering to eliminate single points of failure. In information security, availability forms one leg of the CIA triad, denoting that data and services are accessible to authorised users when needed.
