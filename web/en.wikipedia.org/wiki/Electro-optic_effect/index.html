Electro-optic effect

The electro-optic effect is the change in the optical properties of a material — principally its refractive index — in response to an applied electric field. It is exploited in electro-optic modulators that control the intensity, phase or polarisation of light in telecommunications, laser systems and signal processing.

Types

In the Pockels effect (linear electro-optic effect), the change in refractive index is proportional to the applied field; it occurs only in crystals lacking a centre of symmetry, such as lithium niobate, potassium dihydrogen phosphate (KDP) and gallium arsenide. In the Kerr effect (quadratic electro-optic effect), the index change is proportional to the square of the field and occurs in all materials, though it is generally weaker. Related phenomena include the Franz–Keldysh effect in semiconductors, in which a field changes absorption near the band edge.

Applications

Pockels cells use the linear effect as fast optical shutters and Q-switches in lasers, and as voltage-controlled phase and amplitude modulators. Integrated lithium-niobate and silicon-photonic modulators based on the electro-optic effect are core components of fibre-optic communications, converting electrical data signals onto optical carriers at tens of gigabaud.
