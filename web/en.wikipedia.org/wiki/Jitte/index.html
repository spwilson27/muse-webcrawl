Jitte

A jitte with its characteristic single side prong (kagi).

The jitte or jutte (Japanese: 十手, "ten hands") is a Japanese special weapon that was used by police in Edo-period Japan. It is a short iron or steel baton with a single prong, called a kagi, projecting from the shaft just above the handle.

Description and use

A jitte typically measures around 30 to 60 centimetres in length. The kagi allowed the user to catch and hold an opponent's sword blade — in some accounts even to snap it — as well as to hook clothing or limbs. The blunt shaft could be used to strike, thrust at vulnerable points such as the eyes or throat, and to apply locks and pressure. Because it was an edgeless weapon, it was suited to arresting samurai, whom commoner and low-ranking police officials were expected to take alive where possible.

History

Jitte were carried by Edo-period police officers such as the doshin and okappiki, and the weapon became an emblem of their office, sometimes worn as a badge of authority. Schools of juttejutsu developed within several martial arts traditions, and the weapon remains in the curriculum of some classical bujutsu and Kobudo styles, as well as in modern Okinawan kobudo, where a paired form also exists.
