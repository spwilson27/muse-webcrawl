Goat-antelope
FamilyBovidae
SubfamilyCaprinae (in part)
ExamplesSerow, goral, chamois, mountain goat, muskox, takin

Goat-antelopes are sure-footed, medium-sized bovids that share characteristics of both goats and antelopes. The term is informal and is generally applied to members of the bovid subfamily Caprinae that are not true goats or sheep — notably the serows and gorals of Asia (tribe Rupicaprini/Nemorhaedini), the chamois of Europe, the mountain goat of North America, and sometimes the muskox and takin.

Characteristics

Goat-antelopes are typically stocky, with short legs, dense coats, and relatively short, sharp horns in both sexes. Most are specialised for steep, rocky, mountainous terrain, with rubbery-edged hooves and powerful hindquarters that make them outstanding climbers. Unlike true antelopes of open plains, they favour cliffs and broken ground, escaping predators by climbing.

Representative species

  • Chamois (Rupicapra rupicapra) — European Alps and Pyrenees.
  • Mountain goat (Oreamnos americanus) — high mountains of western North America.
  • Gorals (Naemorhedus spp.) — Himalayas and East Asia.
  • Serows (Capricornis spp.) — forested mountains of Asia.
  • Takin (Budorcas taxicolor) — eastern Himalayas; sometimes called the gnu goat.

Conservation

Several goat-antelopes are threatened by hunting and habitat loss; gorals and serows are protected across much of their range, and the Arabian tahr — a related caprine — is endangered.
