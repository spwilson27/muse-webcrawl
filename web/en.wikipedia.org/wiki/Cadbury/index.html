Cadbury

Cadbury, formerly Cadbury's and Cadbury Schweppes, is a British multinational confectionery company founded in Birmingham in 1824 by John Cadbury. Known for Dairy Milk chocolate, Creme Eggs, and Roses, it is owned by Mondelez International since a controversial 2010 takeover by Kraft Foods.

History

John Cadbury, a Quaker, sold tea, coffee, and drinking chocolate before manufacturing cocoa. His sons Richard and George built the model village of Bournville from 1879, pioneering good factory conditions. Cadbury launched Dairy Milk in 1905 and merged with Fry's in 1919 and Schweppes in 1969. After demerging its drinks business in 2008, Cadbury was acquired by Kraft in 2010 for £11.5 billion amid public debate in Britain.

Products and culture

Iconic products include the Cadbury Creme Egg (a seasonal Easter staple), Flake, Crunchie, and Dairy Milk, marketed with the famous "glass and a half" symbol. In the US, Cadbury products are manufactured under licence by Hershey.
