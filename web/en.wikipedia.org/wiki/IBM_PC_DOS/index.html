IBM PC DOS (Personal Computer Disk Operating System), marketed as PC DOS, is a discontinued disk operating system for the IBM Personal Computer and compatibles. It was IBM's version of DOS, jointly developed with Microsoft, which sold a near-identical product as MS-DOS.

History

PC DOS 1.0 shipped with the original IBM PC in August 1981, adapted from 86-DOS purchased by Microsoft from Seattle Computer Products. Successive versions added subdirectories and hard-disk support (2.0, 1983), support for the PC/AT and 1.2 MB floppies (3.0), and networking. IBM and Microsoft's joint development ended around DOS 5.0; IBM thereafter developed PC DOS independently, releasing PC DOS 6.1 (1993), 6.3, the feature-rich PC DOS 7.0 (1995, including Stacker compression and Rexx), and PC DOS 2000, a Year-2000-fixed version of 7.0. The retail product was withdrawn in the early 2000s, though it survived in embedded and OEM uses.

Relationship to MS-DOS

For most versions PC DOS and MS-DOS were functionally identical, differing mainly in branding, bundled utilities and which OEM supplied them; files such as IBMBIO.COM and IBMDOS.COM correspond to MS-DOS's IO.SYS and MSDOS.SYS.
