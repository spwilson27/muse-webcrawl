The National Transportation Safety Board (NTSB) is an independent U.S. federal agency responsible for investigating civil transportation accidents. It determines the probable cause of major accidents in aviation and other modes of transport and issues safety recommendations aimed at preventing future accidents.

Jurisdiction

The NTSB investigates every civil aviation accident in the United States, as well as significant accidents involving railroads, highways, marine vessels, pipelines and hazardous materials. Its "Go Team" of investigators deploys rapidly to major accident sites, and its laboratory in Washington reads cockpit voice and flight data recorders.

History

The board was established in 1967, taking over accident investigation duties from the Civil Aeronautics Board, and became fully independent of the Department of Transportation under the Independent Safety Board Act of 1974. Board members are appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate.

Publications

Alongside accident reports, the NTSB publishes an annual Most Wanted List of transportation safety improvements and special studies on recurring issues such as runway incursions, fatigue and distracted driving.
