Arizona

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Arizona
Statehood: 14 February 1912 (48th state)
Capital: Phoenix
Population: about 7.4 million

Arizona is a state in the Southwestern United States, bordering Mexico to the south and sharing the Four Corners with Utah, Colorado and New Mexico. It is the sixth-largest state by area and is famous for its desert landscapes, the Sonoran Desert's saguaro cacti, and the Grand Canyon in its northwest.

Phoenix, the capital, is the fifth-most-populous city in the US and the centre of a metropolitan area of about five million; Tucson is the second city and home to the University of Arizona. Indigenous peoples — including the Navajo Nation, the largest Native American reservation in the country — have deep histories here; Spanish explorers arrived in the 1540s, and the territory passed from Mexico to the United States after the Mexican–American War (1848) and the Gadsden Purchase (1853).

Arizona's economy spans copper mining (it leads US production), electronics and aerospace, tourism and retirement migration. Its climate ranges from scorching low desert — Phoenix regularly exceeds 43 °C in summer — to snowy ponderosa forests around Flagstaff. Water, supplied precariously by the Colorado River, is the state's defining political issue.
