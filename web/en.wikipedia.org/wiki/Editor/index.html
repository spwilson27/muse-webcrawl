Editor

An editor is a person who selects, prepares and revises material for publication in books, newspapers, magazines, websites, films and other media. Editors work at various stages of the production process: acquiring manuscripts, shaping their structure and content, correcting language and style, and overseeing the work of authors, journalists and other contributors.

Types of editors

In publishing, an editor-in-chief leads a publication and has final responsibility for its content and policies. Managing editors oversee day-to-day operations. A copy editor (or sub-editor in British usage) checks text for grammar, spelling, punctuation, factual consistency and conformity to a house style. A commissioning editor seeks out and acquires new books or articles, while developmental editors work with authors on the structure and substance of a work. In film and video, an editor assembles recorded footage into a finished production.

History

The role of the editor grew with the expansion of printing and periodical publishing in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Editors of newspapers and magazines became influential public figures, and in the twentieth century professional editing became central to book publishing houses, scholarly journals and broadcast media. Digital publishing and collaborative platforms such as Wikipedia extended the title to volunteers and professionals who create and curate online content.
