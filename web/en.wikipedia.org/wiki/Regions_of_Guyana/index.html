Regions of Guyana

Number: 10 administrative regions
Most populous: Demerara-Mahaica (Region 4)
Largest by area: Upper Takutu-Upper Essequibo (Region 9)
Each named for: Rivers and geography

Guyana is divided into ten administrative regions, each administered by a Regional Democratic Council. The regions are generally named after the rivers and physical features within them and range from the densely populated Atlantic coast, where most Guyanese live, to the vast forested and savanna interior.

The ten regions

The regions are: 1 Barima-Waini; 2 Pomeroon-Supenaam; 3 Essequibo Islands-West Demerara; 4 Demerara-Mahaica; 5 Mahaica-Berbice; 6 East Berbice-Corentyne; 7 Cuyuni-Mazaruni; 8 Potaro-Siparuni; 9 Upper Takutu-Upper Essequibo; and 10 Upper Demerara-Berbice. Demerara-Mahaica contains the capital, Georgetown, and holds the largest share of the population, while the interior regions are extensive but sparsely inhabited.

Administration

Each region is governed by an elected Regional Democratic Council led by a regional chairman, working alongside central government ministries. The regional system was established in 1980 under President Forbes Burnham as part of a decentralisation programme.

Territorial note

Guyana's claimed territory includes the Essequibo region west of the Essequibo River, which is administered by Guyana but claimed by Venezuela; regions 1, 7 and 8 fall wholly or partly within the disputed area.
