Stone's theorem

In functional analysis, Stone's theorem on one-parameter unitary groups is a fundamental result about unitary operators on a Hilbert space, proved by Marshall Harvey Stone in the early 1930s. The theorem establishes a one-to-one correspondence between self-adjoint operators and strongly continuous one-parameter unitary groups.

Statement

Let {U(t)}t ∈ R be a strongly continuous one-parameter unitary group on a Hilbert space H. Then there exists a unique self-adjoint operator A such that

U(t) = eitA   for all t ∈ R.

Conversely, every self-adjoint operator arises in this way from a unique such group. The operator A is called the infinitesimal generator of the group.

Applications

Stone's theorem is fundamental in quantum mechanics, where the time evolution of a closed quantum system is described by a one-parameter unitary group, and the theorem identifies its generator with the Hamiltonian (energy) observable of the system. It also plays a central role in the theory of partial differential equations and the Hille–Yosida theorem extends the idea to contraction semigroups.
