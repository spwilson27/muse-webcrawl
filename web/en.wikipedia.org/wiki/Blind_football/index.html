Blind football, also known as football 5-a-side, is an adaptation of association football for players who are blind or visually impaired. Governed internationally by the International Blind Sports Federation (IBSA), it has been a Paralympic sport since the 2004 Games in Athens. Outfield players are completely blind (B1 classification) and wear eyeshades to ensure fairness; the goalkeeper may be sighted or partially sighted.

Rules

Matches are played on a pitch 40 by 20 metres with side kickboards that keep the ball in play and serve as an orientation aid. The ball contains a noise-making system of small bells or bearings so players can locate it by sound, and spectators must remain silent during play. A sighted guide behind the opponent's goal gives instructions during attacks, and the goalkeeper directs the defence. Players must shout "voy!" (Spanish for "I go") when challenging for the ball to reduce collisions. Games consist of two halves of 20 minutes (formerly 25) with a running clock at elite level. There is no offside rule.

History

Football for the blind developed in schools for blind children in Spain in the 1920s and in Brazil from the 1960s, where it became a mass sport. Brazil has dominated international competition, winning the Paralympic tournament at every Games from 2004 to 2020, before Argentina — long the other powerhouse — triumphed at Paris 2024. National and continental championships spread the game through Europe, Asia, and Africa; a separate partially sighted (B2/B3) version of futsal is also played internationally.

Major competitions

The IBSA Blind Football World Championships are held every four years; Brazil and Argentina have won all editions between them. Blind football is a highlight of the Paralympic programme, drawing large crowds despite the requirement of silence during play.
