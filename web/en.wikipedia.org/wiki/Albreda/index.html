Albreda

The Gambia riverfront at Albreda

Albreda is a historic town in the Gambia, in the North Bank Division, located on the northern bank of the Gambia River near its mouth. In the late 17th and 18th centuries it was a French trading post, and together with neighbouring Juffureh and nearby Kunta Kinteh Island (formerly James Island) it forms part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site commemorating the history of West African trade and the transatlantic slave trade.

History

The French established a trading post at Albreda in 1681, having obtained permission from the Niumi king, and it served as France's foothold on the river in rivalry with the English, who occupied James Island (now Kunta Kinteh Island) a short distance offshore. Trade at Albreda involved gum arabic, gold, ivory, hides — and enslaved people, shipped across the Atlantic. The post passed between French and British hands several times; after the Treaty of Versailles of 1783 the French retained Albreda while Britain held the river, but France finally ceded the settlement to Britain in 1857.

Heritage

Today a small museum in a former colonial building (the Maurel Frères building) interprets the history of the slave trade, and Albreda, with Juffureh — associated with the story of Kunta Kinte in Alex Haley's Roots — is a destination for heritage tourism, particularly for visitors from the African diaspora. Ferries and boats connect Albreda with Banjul across the river mouth.
