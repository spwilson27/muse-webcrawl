Phoropter

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A patient positioned at a phoropter.

A phoropter or refractor is an ophthalmic testing device. It is commonly used by eye care professionals during an eye examination, and contains different lenses used for refraction of the eye during sight testing, to measure an individual's refractive error and determine their eyeglass prescription.

The phoropter is positioned in front of the patient's eyes, who looks through it at an eye chart. The examiner changes lenses and other settings while asking the patient for subjective feedback on which settings give the best vision — the familiar question "which is better, one or two?"

History

The trademarked name Phoropter originates with a device patented in the early twentieth century; Henry DeZeng patented an early version in 1909, and the American Optical Company developed and marketed the instruments that became standard in examination lanes. The name combines phoro-, referring to phoria (the relative position of the eyes), with -opter.

Manual phoropters house a large battery of spherical and cylindrical lenses that can be dialled in rapidly, along with prisms, cross cylinders (such as the Jackson cross cylinder), and occluders. Modern digital refractors motorise the lens changes and can be driven from a computer, importing starting data automatically from an autorefractor or lensmeter.

Use

Typical measurements made with the phoropter include sphere, cylinder power and axis for each eye, near and distance visual acuity, phoria and vergence ranges, and accommodative function. Subjective refraction with a phoropter remains a standard method of determining spectacle prescriptions, though automated refraction is increasingly used to obtain an objective starting point.
