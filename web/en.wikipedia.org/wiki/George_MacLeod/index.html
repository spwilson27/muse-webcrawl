George MacLeod

George Fielden MacLeod, Baron MacLeod of Fuinary (17 June 1895 – 27 June 1991) was a Scottish soldier, clergyman and founder of the Iona Community.

Born: Glasgow, Scotland
Died: Glasgow
Known for: Founding the Iona Community (1938)

George Fielden MacLeod, Baron MacLeod of Fuinary, MC & Bar, was a Scottish clergyman of the Church of Scotland, a decorated First World War soldier, and the founder of the ecumenical Iona Community. He served as Moderator of the General Assembly of the Church of Scotland in 1957–58.

Early life and war service

MacLeod was born into a prominent Glasgow family and educated at Winchester and Oriel College, Oxford. In the First World War he served with the Argyll and Sutherland Highlanders, winning the Military Cross and Bar and reaching the rank of captain. The war profoundly shaped his later determination to reconnect the church with working people.

Ministry

After ordination he became minister of St Cuthbert's, Edinburgh, and then of Govan Old Parish Church in a poor shipbuilding district of Glasgow during the Depression. Disturbed by the gulf between the church and the unemployed men of Govan, he conceived a scheme in which ministers and craftsmen would together rebuild the ruined cloister of Iona Abbey, training young clergy alongside unemployed workers.

Iona Community

The project began in 1938 and became the Iona Community, an ecumenical Christian community devoted to peace, social justice, and the renewal of worship and common life. MacLeod led the rebuilding of the abbey's domestic quarters and the community grew into an international movement with a publishing house (Wild Goose Publications) and centres on Iona and in Glasgow.

Later life

A passionate campaigner against nuclear weapons and for Scottish and social causes, MacLeod was made a life peer in 1967 as Baron MacLeod of Fuinary — taking the title from a small Highland settlement — the first Church of Scotland minister in the House of Lords. He received the Templeton Prize in 1989 and died in 1991, aged 96.
