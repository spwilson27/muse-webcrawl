Acrylonitrile

Acrylonitrile is an organic compound with the formula CH2CHCN and the structure H2C=CH−C≡N. It is a colorless, volatile liquid with a pungent odor, although commercial samples may appear yellowish due to impurities. Acrylonitrile is an important monomer for the manufacture of useful plastics and is a member of a family of chemicals called nitriles.

Molar mass53.06 g/mol
AppearanceColorless liquid
Boiling point77 °C (171 °F)
SolubilityModerately soluble in water

Production

Acrylonitrile is produced industrially by the Sohio process, the catalytic ammoxidation of propylene with ammonia and oxygen over bismuth phosphomolybdate catalysts at around 400–510 °C. Roughly five million tonnes are manufactured each year. Acetonitrile and hydrogen cyanide are significant byproducts.

Uses

The principal use of acrylonitrile is as a monomer in the polymerization of:

  • Acrylic fibers, such as those used in clothing and carpets
  • Acrylonitrile butadiene styrene (ABS) and styrene-acrylonitrile (SAN) plastics
  • Nitrile rubber (NBR), used in gloves, hoses, and seals
  • Barrier resins and other copolymers

It is also an intermediate in the industrial synthesis of acrylamide and adiponitrile.

Safety

Acrylonitrile is flammable and toxic. It is metabolized to cyanide in the body, and chronic exposure has been associated with cancer; it is classified as a possible human carcinogen. Large accidental releases are hazardous to aquatic life. The compound has also been detected in the atmosphere of Titan, Saturn's largest moon, and in interstellar space.
