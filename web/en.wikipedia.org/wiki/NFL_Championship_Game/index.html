NFL Championship Game

The NFL Championship Game was the annual championship game of the National Football League (NFL) from 1933 to 1969. Before 1933, the league champion was determined by standings. The game was superseded by the Super Bowl following the NFL's merger with the American Football League (AFL); the final NFL Championship Game was played on 4 January 1970.

History

The first championship game, in 1933, saw the Chicago Bears defeat the New York Giants 23–21. The game produced some of the most famous contests in early professional football, including the 1940 title game in which the Bears routed Washington 73–0 — still the most lopsided result in NFL history — the 1958 championship, in which the Baltimore Colts beat the New York Giants 23–17 in sudden-death overtime, widely called "The Greatest Game Ever Played", and the 1967 "Ice Bowl", in which Green Bay defeated Dallas in sub-zero temperatures in Bart Starr's famous quarterback sneak.

Champions

The Green Bay Packers under Vince Lombardi dominated the 1960s, winning five championships including the final two NFL title games before the merger era. Other multiple winners included the Chicago Bears, New York Giants, Cleveland Browns and Baltimore Colts. Winners of the NFL Championship Game in 1966–1969 went on to represent the league in the first four Super Bowls against the AFL champions.

Legacy

Although replaced by the Super Bowl as the league's showcase, the NFL Championship Game era established professional football's postseason tradition, and its winners are recognised in the league's official championship history.
