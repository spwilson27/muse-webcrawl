Mark Van Doren (June 13, 1894 – December 10, 1972) was an American poet, writer and critic, winner of the Pulitzer Prize for Poetry in 1940 for his Collected Poems 1922–1938. He was a professor of English at Columbia University from 1920 to 1959 and one of the most celebrated teachers of his generation, noted for his Socratic lecturing style and advocacy of liberal education.

Life and career

Van Doren was born in Hope, Illinois, the younger brother of the writer and historian Carl Van Doren. He studied at the University of Illinois and Columbia University, where he earned his PhD in 1920 and joined the faculty. He also served as literary editor of The Nation in the 1930s and hosted the CBS radio programme Invitation to Learning.

Works

Van Doren published more than twenty volumes of poetry as well as plays, novels, short stories, and critical studies, including Shakespeare (1939), a book of lucid single-chapter essays on each play, and a study of John Dryden. His Liberal Education (1943) argued for a curriculum centred on the great books and the cultivation of judgment. His son Charles Van Doren achieved notoriety in the 1950s quiz show scandals.

Teaching legacy

Generations of Columbia students — including Lionel Trilling, Allen Ginsberg, Jack Kerouac and Thomas Merton — recalled Van Doren's lectures as formative. Columbia's undergraduate award for teaching, the Mark Van Doren Award, is named in his honour.
