Sudbury Neutrino Observatory

Sudbury Neutrino Observatory (SNO)
LocationVale Creighton Mine, Sudbury, Ontario, Canada
Depth2,100 m underground
Operated1999–2006

The Sudbury Neutrino Observatory (SNO) was a neutrino observatory located 2,100 metres underground in Ontario, Canada. It detected solar neutrinos through their interactions with a large tank of heavy water.

Results

SNO demonstrated in 2001–2002 that electron neutrinos produced in the Sun oscillate into muon and tau neutrinos, solving the solar neutrino problem and proving neutrinos have mass. Director Arthur B. McDonald shared the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physics for this discovery.

Detector

The detector held 1,000 tonnes of heavy water in a 12-metre acrylic sphere surrounded by about 9,600 photomultiplier tubes, shielded by ordinary water in a barrel-shaped cavity. Depth shielded it from cosmic rays.

Successor

The facility was expanded into SNOLAB, and the SNO detector was refitted as SNO+ using liquid scintillator. See neutrino oscillation and solar neutrino problem.
