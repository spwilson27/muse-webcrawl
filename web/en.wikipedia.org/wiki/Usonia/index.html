Usonia

Usonia

Coined by: Frank Lloyd Wright (attributed)

Meaning: The United States; a proposed name and adjective

Also: Usonian houses

Usonia is a term associated with the architect Frank Lloyd Wright, who used it to refer to the United States and its distinctive democratic culture, freeing it from associations with the two American continents. The adjective "Usonian" is best known from Wright's Usonian houses — modest, affordable, organically designed homes built from the 1930s.

Origin

Wright attributed the word to the writer James Duff Law, who in 1903 proposed "Usona" for the country and "Usonian" for its people, deriving it from "United States of North America". Wright adopted and popularised the term in his writings and in his vision for decentralised American living, embodied in his Broadacre City concept.

Usonian houses

The first Usonian house is generally considered to be the Herbert and Katherine Jacobs House (1936–37) in Madison, Wisconsin. Features included open plans, carports (a word Wright coined), radiant floor heating, natural materials and strong integration with the landscape. About sixty Usonian houses were built, and the type influenced post-war American ranch-house design.
