TFT LCD

TFT LCD
TypeFlat-panel display (liquid-crystal, active-matrix)
Key technologyThin-film transistors (amorphous silicon, LTPS, oxide/IGZO)
Panel typesTN, IPS, VA
ApplicationsMonitors, televisions, phones, laptops, instruments

A TFT LCD (thin-film-transistor liquid-crystal display) is a type of liquid-crystal display that uses thin-film-transistor technology to improve image quality such as contrast and addressability. In a TFT display each pixel is controlled by one or more microscopic transistors fabricated on the glass substrate, making it an active-matrix display; this allows pixels to hold their state between refreshes, giving much better images than older passive-matrix LCDs.

Construction

The panel sandwiches a layer of liquid crystals between two polarising filters and a backlight (now almost always LED). Each subpixel has its own transistor and storage capacitor; row and column driver circuits scan the matrix. The liquid-crystal layer types define panel families: twisted nematic (TN) panels are cheap and fast but have narrow viewing angles; in-plane switching (IPS) and vertical alignment (VA) panels offer better colour and viewing angles at higher cost. Transistors are most often amorphous silicon, with low-temperature polysilicon (LTPS) and oxide semiconductors (IGZO) used where higher pixel densities or lower power are needed.

History and use

Active-matrix LCDs were demonstrated in the 1970s–80s (notably at Westinghouse and in Japanese industry) and became dominant in laptops in the 1990s and in televisions and monitors in the 2000s, displacing CRTs. Although OLED displays compete in premium phones and TVs, TFT LCDs remain the most widely used display technology by volume.
