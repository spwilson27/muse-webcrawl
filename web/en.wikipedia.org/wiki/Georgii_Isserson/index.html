Georgii Isserson

Born 16 May 1898, Kaunas (Kovno), Russian Empire
Died 27 April 1976 (aged 77)
Nationality Russian / Soviet
Occupation Military theorist, Red Army officer
Rank Kombrig (brigade commander)

Georgii Samoilovich Isserson (Russian: Георгий Самойлович Иссерсон; 16 May 1898 – 27 April 1976) was a Soviet military theorist and Red Army officer, one of the principal developers, together with Mikhail Tukhachevsky, Vladimir Triandafillov, and Nikolai Varfolomeev, of the Soviet theory of deep operations (glubokaya operatsiya) in the interwar period.

Career

Isserson served as an officer in the Imperial Russian Army in World War I and joined the Red Army during the Civil War. A gifted staff officer and graduate of the Frunze Military Academy, he taught operational art at the Academy of the General Staff and commanded formations, rising to the rank of kombrig. His writings, notably The Evolution of Operational Art (1932; 2nd ed. 1937) and New Forms of Struggle (1940), analyzed how mechanization, aviation, and mass armies had destroyed the linear warfare of 1914–1918 and argued for operations conducted simultaneously through the entire depth of the enemy's deployment — successive deep thrusts by echeloned shock armies supported by aircraft and airborne forces.

Arrest and later life

Isserson survived the Great Purge of the Red Army officer corps that killed Tukhachevsky and Triandafillov's colleagues in 1937–1938, but in June 1941, on the eve of the German invasion, he was arrested on fabricated charges, possibly connected with his analysis of German operational methods. He spent some fourteen years in camps and exile in Kazakhstan before being released and rehabilitated in the mid-1950s. His final work, a memoir and history of the deep operations theory, was published only in heavily edited form; the full manuscript appeared after his death. Isserson's writings influenced later Western studies of Soviet operational art, including NATO's reassessment of maneuver warfare in the 1980s.
