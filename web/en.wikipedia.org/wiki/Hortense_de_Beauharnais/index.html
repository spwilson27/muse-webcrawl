Hortense Eugénie Cécile Bonaparte (née de Beauharnais; 10 April 1783 – 5 October 1837) was the daughter of Joséphine de Beauharnais and the stepdaughter of Napoleon Bonaparte. Through her marriage to Napoleon's brother Louis Bonaparte she became Queen of Holland (1806–1810), and she was the mother of Napoleon III, the first president and last monarch of France.

Early life

Hortense was born in Paris to Alexandre de Beauharnais and Joséphine Tascher de la Pagerie. Her father was guillotined during the Revolution; her mother remarried Napoleon in 1796. Hortense was educated at Madame Campan's school at Saint-Germain-en-Laye, where she developed the musical talent for which she was later known.

Queen of Holland

In 1802, at Napoleon's urging, she married his brother Louis. The marriage was unhappy, though it produced three sons. When Louis became King of Holland in 1806 Hortense reigned as queen consort, residing mainly at The Hague and later, increasingly, in Paris. Louis abdicated in 1810 and the couple separated permanently.

Later life

After the fall of Napoleon, Hortense was exiled from France and eventually settled at Arenenberg Castle on Lake Constance in Switzerland, where she raised her youngest son, Louis-Napoleon, the future Napoleon III. She composed songs — the romance Partant pour la Syrie became an unofficial anthem of the Second Empire — and wrote memoirs. She died at Arenenberg in 1837.
