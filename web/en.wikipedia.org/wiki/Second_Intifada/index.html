The Second Intifada (Arabic: الانتفاضة الثانية, al-Intifāḍa aṯ-Ṯāniya), also known as the Al-Aqsa Intifada, was a major Palestinian uprising against Israeli occupation from September 2000. It followed the collapse of the Camp David Summit and Ariel Sharon's visit to the Temple Mount/Haram al-Sharif on 28 September 2000, which Palestinians viewed as provocative; rioting the following day escalated into a prolonged conflict of far greater intensity and militarisation than the First Intifada (1987–1993). The causes remain contested: Palestinians cite the failure of the Oslo peace process, continued settlement expansion and occupation; Israel views the uprising as pre-planned terrorism by the Palestinian leadership.

Course of the conflict

The uprising featured Palestinian suicide bombings and shootings against Israeli civilians and soldiers by groups including Hamas, Islamic Jihad, the al-Aqsa Martyrs' Brigades and the PFLP, and Israeli military incursions, targeted killings, curfews and reoccupation of West Bank cities. Major episodes included the 2002 Israeli Operation Defensive Shield following the Passover massacre in Netanya, the siege of the Church of the Nativity in Bethlehem, and heavy fighting in Jenin and Nablus. In response Israel began constructing the West Bank separation barrier in 2002. Israeli prime minister Ariel Sharon announced unilateral disengagement from the Gaza Strip in 2004.

End and aftermath

Violence declined after the Sharm el-Sheikh summit of February 2005, where Sharon and Palestinian president Mahmoud Abbas (who succeeded Yasser Arafat after his death in November 2004) declared a truce; Israel completed its withdrawal from Gaza in September 2005. Roughly 3,000 Palestinians and 1,000 Israelis were killed. The intifada devastated the Palestinian economy, destroyed much of the Oslo framework, and hardened public opinion on both sides against a negotiated settlement.
