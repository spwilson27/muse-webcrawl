Aqualung is the fourth studio album by the British rock band Jethro Tull, released on 19 March 1971. It is widely regarded as the band's landmark work and one of the defining albums of progressive rock. The album's first side presents character studies of society's outcasts — including the title character, a homeless man named in Ian Anderson's lyrics after the wheezing sound of his breathing — while the second side ("My God") critiques organised religion and the concept of God. Anderson has resisted describing it as a concept album, calling it instead a collection of songs with recurring themes.

The album's cover painting by Burton Silverman portrays the down-and-out title character.

Recording and music

Aqualung was recorded at Island Studios in London, with Anderson producing alongside Terry Ellis and John Evan contributing piano and organ arrangements. The music alternates heavy electric passages — Martin Barre's guitar on the title track and "Locomotive Breath" — with delicate acoustic pieces such as "Cheap Day Return", "Wond'ring Aloud", and "Mother Goose". The title track's distinctive guitar riff and the travelling-rhythm drive of "Locomotive Breath" became staples of album-oriented rock radio. Photographs by Anderson's then-wife Jennie inspired the title character's portrait, and Burton Silverman's watercolour cover art depicted him.

Reception and legacy

The album reached No. 4 in the UK and No. 7 on the US Billboard 200, and has sold more than seven million copies worldwide, making it Jethro Tull's best-selling album. Contemporary reviews were mixed — some critics found the religious themes heavy-handed — but retrospective assessments rank it among the greatest rock albums; it was remixed by Steven Wilson for a celebrated 40th-anniversary edition in 2011, and its songs remain fixtures of the band's and Anderson's live repertoire. In 2022 Anderson and the band released the sequels The Zealot Gene, an album distinct from the original but thematically linked; Anderson has also toured a theatrical presentation of the Aqualung story.
