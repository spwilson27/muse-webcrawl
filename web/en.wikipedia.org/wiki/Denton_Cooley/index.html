Denton Cooley

Denton Arthur Cooley (August 22, 1920 – November 18, 2016) was an American cardiothoracic surgeon famous for performing the first implantation of a total artificial heart in a human and for pioneering techniques in cardiac surgery, including repairs of aortic aneurysms and congenital heart defects. He founded the Texas Heart Institute in Houston in 1962.

Career

Born in Houston, Cooley studied at the University of Texas and Johns Hopkins University School of Medicine, and worked in London with Russell Brock. In Houston he collaborated — and later famously feuded — with Michael DeBakey at Baylor College of Medicine, developing open-heart surgery using the heart–lung machine; he championed the use of blood-free (dextrose) prime for the pump, greatly simplifying open-heart operations. His surgical teams performed enormous numbers of operations, building Houston into a world centre of cardiovascular surgery.

Artificial heart

On 4 April 1969, Cooley implanted a pneumatic total artificial heart, designed by Domingo Liotta, into Haskell Karp, a patient whose own heart could not be repaired; the device sustained Karp for 65 hours until a donor heart was transplanted, though the patient died 32 hours later. The episode intensified his rivalry with DeBakey, whose laboratory had funded the device's development. Cooley received the National Medal of Technology in 1984 and the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1998.
