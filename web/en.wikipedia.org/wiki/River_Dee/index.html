River Dee is the name of several rivers in the British Isles. The name derives from the Brittonic Deva, meaning "goddess", reflecting the sacred character attributed to these rivers in pre-Christian times. The best-known rivers of the name are in Scotland and Wales.

Rivers named Dee

River Dee, Aberdeenshire

The River Dee in Aberdeenshire, Scotland, flows 140 km (87 mi) from its source in the Cairngorms — among the highest river sources in the United Kingdom — eastwards through Royal Deeside, passing Braemar, Ballater, Banchory and the Balmoral Estate, to enter the North Sea at Aberdeen. It is famed as one of Scotland's great salmon rivers.

River Dee, Galloway

The River Dee in Dumfries and Galloway rises near Loch Dee and flows south through the Glenkens, past Threave Castle, to Kirkcudbright Bay on the Solway Firth. Together with its tributary the Water of Ken it forms part of the Galloway hydro-electric scheme.

River Dee, Wales

The River Dee (Welsh: Afon Dyfrdwy) rises in Bala Lake (Llyn Tegid) in Snowdonia and flows about 110 km (70 mi) through Llangollen, where it is crossed by the Pontcysyllte Aqueduct, and through Chester, before entering the Irish Sea via its estuary between Wales and the Wirral Peninsula in England.

River Dee, Cumbria

A smaller River Dee in Cumbria flows from the slopes of Dent Fell and joins the River Rawthey near Sedbergh, in the Yorkshire Dales National Park.

Etymology

The shared name derives from the Celtic root dēvā, "goddess". Roman settlements on these rivers bore related names: the legionary fortress at Chester was Deva Victrix, and Aberdeen's name records its position at the mouth of the Dee and the Don.
