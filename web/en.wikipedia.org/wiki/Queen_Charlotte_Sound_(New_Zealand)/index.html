Queen Charlotte Sound / Tōtaranui

Location: Marlborough Sounds, South Island, New Zealand
Coordinates: approx. 41.2° S, 174.3° E
River sources: various small streams
Ocean/sea sources: Cook Strait
Towns: Picton

Queen Charlotte Sound / Tōtaranui is the easternmost of the main sounds of the Marlborough Sounds, at the northern end of the South Island of New Zealand. It is a drowned river valley system of sheltered inlets, bays, and bush-covered peninsulas opening into Cook Strait.

The town of Picton, the South Island terminal for the Interislander ferries from Wellington, stands at the head of the sound. The sound is a popular destination for boating, kayaking, and walking; the Queen Charlotte Track runs some 70 kilometres along its ridges and shorelines from Ship Cove to Anakiwa.

Geography

Queen Charlotte Sound is roughly 35 kilometres long. Its many arms include Grove Arm, Endeavour Inlet, and Resolution Bay. Islands in the sound include Motuara Island, Long Island, and Blumine Island, several of which are predator-free wildlife sanctuaries used for the conservation of native birds such as the little spotted kiwi, rowi, and South Island saddleback.

History

The Māori name Tōtaranui refers to the great tōtara trees once common in the area. The sound was a significant place of Māori settlement and travel for centuries. In 1770 Captain James Cook anchored in Ship Cove, which became his favoured New Zealand base during his voyages in the 1770s; he named the sound for Queen Charlotte, wife of King George III. The cliffs of nearby Motuara Island were where Cook formally took possession of the South Island for Britain.

In the nineteenth century the sound hosted whaling stations, notably at Te Awaiti, one of the earliest European settlements in New Zealand. Today the economy of the area centres on tourism, mussel and salmon aquaculture, and the ferry trade through Picton.
