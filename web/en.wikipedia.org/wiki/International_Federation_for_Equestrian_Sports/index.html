The International Federation for Equestrian Sports (French: Fédération Équestre Internationale, FEI) is the international governing body of equestrian sports. It is based in Lausanne, Switzerland, and was formed in 1921 by representatives of Belgium, Denmark, France, Italy, Japan, Norway, Sweden and the United States. The FEI is recognised by the International Olympic Committee as the sole authority for international equestrian competition.

Disciplines

The FEI governs eight disciplines: jumping (show jumping), dressage, eventing, driving, endurance, vaulting, reining and para-equestrian (para dressage and para driving). Jumping, dressage and eventing are the Olympic disciplines; para dressage is part of the Paralympic Games. The federation sets the rules for international competition, including horse welfare standards, anti-doping rules for both horses and riders, and the registration of horses and athletes.

Events

Major FEI events include the FEI World Equestrian Games (held every four years from 1990 to 2018), the World Cup series in jumping and dressage, the Nations Cup series, and world championships in each discipline. The FEI also oversees equestrian competition at the Olympic and Paralympic Games in conjunction with the organisers.

Horse welfare

The FEI's code of conduct for the welfare of the horse states that horses must be treated humanely and that welfare must take precedence over competitive or commercial interests. The federation has faced scrutiny over practices in endurance racing, where horse deaths and doping cases in the 2010s led to sanctions and rule reforms, and over the use of controversial training methods in dressage. Its Clean Sport programme applies anti-doping and controlled medication rules to equine athletes.

Presidents

Notable presidents have included Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh (1964–1986), Anne, Princess Royal (1986–1994), and Infanta Pilar of Spain (1994–2006), followed by Princess Haya bint Hussein of Jordan (2006–2014) and Ingmar De Vos of Belgium, elected in 2014.
