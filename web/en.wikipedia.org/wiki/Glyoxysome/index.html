Glyoxysome

Glyoxysome
TypeSpecialised peroxisome
Found inGerminating plant seeds; some fungi and protists
Key enzymesIsocitrate lyase, malate synthase, catalase

Glyoxysomes are specialised peroxisomes found in the fat-storing tissues of germinating plant seeds (such as the endosperm or cotyledons of castor bean, sunflower and other oil seeds) and also in filamentous fungi. They contain the enzymes of the glyoxylate cycle, which allows the seedling to convert stored lipids into sugars for growth before photosynthesis begins.

Function

Fatty acids released from stored triglycerides are broken down by β-oxidation in the glyoxysome to acetyl-CoA. Two unique enzymes of the glyoxylate cycle, isocitrate lyase and malate synthase, bypass the CO2-releasing steps of the citric acid cycle, so that acetyl-CoA is converted net into succinate and then, via the mitochondrion and cytosol, into glucose by gluconeogenesis. Like other peroxisomes, glyoxysomes contain catalase to detoxify the hydrogen peroxide produced by oxidases. As the seedling becomes photosynthetic, the glyoxysomes of leaf cells are converted into ordinary leaf peroxisomes involved in photorespiration.

Discovery

Glyoxysomes were identified in the 1960s in the work of Harry Beevers and colleagues on castor bean endosperm, who demonstrated the glyoxylate cycle enzymes in a distinct organelle fraction.
