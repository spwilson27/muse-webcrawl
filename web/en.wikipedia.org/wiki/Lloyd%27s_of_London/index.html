Lloyd's of London

Lloyd's
Type: Insurance market
Founded: 1688 (Lloyd's Coffee House)
Headquarters: Lloyd's building, Lime Street, London
Industry: Insurance and reinsurance

Lloyd's of London, generally known simply as Lloyd's, is an insurance and reinsurance market located in London, England. Unlike most of its competitors, it is not an insurance company; rather, Lloyd's is a corporate body governed by the Lloyd's Act 1871 and subsequent acts of Parliament, in which independent syndicates underwrite insurance as specialty insurers.

Structure

Risk is underwritten by "members" — both corporate investors and, historically, individual "Names" with unlimited liability — who group into syndicates managed by managing agents. Brokers bring risks to the market, negotiating face-to-face with underwriters seated at "boxes" in the Underwriting Room. Lloyd's is especially known for unusual and large risks: marine, aviation, energy, catastrophe reinsurance and even celebrity body parts.

History

The market grew from Lloyd's Coffee House, opened by Edward Lloyd in Tower Street around 1688, where shipowners, merchants and underwriters gathered for shipping news; the famous Lutine Bell, recovered from the wreck of HMS Lutine, hangs in the Underwriting Room. Lloyd's survived a crisis in the late 1980s and early 1990s, when asbestos and pollution losses nearly bankrupted many Names, leading to the Reconstruction and Renewal settlement and the admission of corporate capital. Since 1986 the market has occupied the high-tech Lloyd's building on Lime Street, designed by Richard Rogers.
