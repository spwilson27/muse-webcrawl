The Shins is an American indie rock band formed in Albuquerque, New Mexico, in 1996. The band is the project of singer, songwriter and multi-instrumentalist James Mercer, its founder and only constant member. Based in Portland, Oregon, since the early 2000s, the Shins became one of the defining acts of modern indie rock with albums including Oh, Inverted World (2001), Chutes Too Narrow (2003) and Wincing the Night Away (2007), characterised by Mercer's melodic, cryptically worded songs and intricate pop arrangements.

History

Mercer formed the Shins in 1996 as a side project to his band Flake Music, with Jesse Sandoval, Marty Crandall and Neal Langford; after Flake Music dissolved, the Shins signed to Sub Pop. Their debut, Oh, Inverted World (2001), recorded largely in Mercer's home, earned critical acclaim; Chutes Too Narrow (2003) broadened their audience. The band's breakthrough to mainstream recognition came when the songs "New Slang" and "Caring Is Creepy" were featured in Zach Braff's 2004 film Garden State, whose script famously promised that the Shins "will change your life" — the soundtrack won a Grammy Award and the album went platinum.

Wincing the Night Away (2007), produced with Joe Chiccarelli, debuted at No. 2 on the Billboard 200 and was nominated for the Grammy for Best Alternative Music Album. In 2009 Mercer effectively reconstituted the band, parting with the other original members and recruiting a new line-up; he simultaneously worked with producer Danger Mouse in the duo Broken Bells. Under a deal with Columbia Records and Mercer's own Aural Apothecary label, the Shins released Port of Morrow (2012), which debuted at No. 3, and Heartworms (2017). Drummer Jon Sortland and other touring members have supported Mercer in later line-ups, and the band has continued to tour and release music into the 2020s.

Style and influence

Mercer's songwriting combines 1960s pop craftsmanship with folk and psychedelic influences and dense, oblique lyricism; critics have credited the Shins with helping to define the sound and sensibility of 2000s indie rock and with demonstrating the commercial power of film and television placements for independent bands.
