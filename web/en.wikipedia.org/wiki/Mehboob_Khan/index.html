Mehboob Khan

Mehboob Khan (9 September 1907 – 28 May 1964) was an Indian film director and producer, widely regarded as one of the pioneers of Indian cinema. He is best known for directing Mother India (1957), which was India's first submission for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film and remains one of the most celebrated films in the history of Indian cinema.

Early life and career

Born Mehboob Khan Ramzan Khan in Bilimora, Gujarat, he ran away from home to Bombay to work in films, starting as an extra and labourer before rising to become an actor and then a director. In 1935 he directed his first film, Al Hilal. He founded his own production company, Mehboob Productions, in 1942, and established Mehboob Studios in Bandra, Bombay, in the early 1950s.

Films

Khan's notable films include Aurat (1940), Roti (1942), Andaz (1949), Aan (1952) — India's first film in Technicolor, Amar (1954), and Mother India (1957), a remake of his own Aurat, starring Nargis as a heroic village mother. His last film, Son of India (1962), was a commercial failure. His films often combined social themes — poverty, class conflict, and the dignity of labour — with lavish production values and melodrama.

Legacy

Mehboob Khan died of a heart attack in 1964, aged 56. The Indian government honoured him with the Padma Shri. His studio's famous logo, showing a sickle and hammer motif with the tagline "Mudde Jai Jai" in spirit, reflected his socialist leanings. Mehboob Studios remains an active film facility in Mumbai.
