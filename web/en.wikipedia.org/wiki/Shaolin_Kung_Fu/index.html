Shaolin kung fu (Chinese: 少林功夫; pinyin: Shàolín gōngfu), also called Shaolin wushu or Shaolin quan, is one of the oldest, largest and most famous styles of wushu (Chinese martial arts). It combines Chan (Zen) Buddhism with martial arts and was developed at the Shaolin Monastery in Henan province, China, over roughly 1,500 years. Shaolin kung fu is widely regarded as the first institutionalised form of Chinese martial art, and its name is synonymous with kung fu in popular culture worldwide.

History

The Shaolin Monastery was founded in 496 CE during the Northern Wei dynasty at the foot of Mount Song. According to tradition, the Indian monk Bodhidharma arrived in the 5th or 6th century, founded Chan Buddhism, and taught the monks exercises — later elaborated into the Yijin Jing (Muscle/Tendon Changing Classic) — to strengthen bodies weakened by long meditation. While Bodhidharma's role as founder of the martial art is largely legendary, the monastery's fighting monks are historically documented: in 621 CE, Shaolin monks aided the future Tang emperor Li Shimin in battle, earning the monastery imperial patronage and the right to maintain a monk army.

The monastery was repeatedly destroyed and rebuilt. A famous episode, retold in novels and films, holds that in the 16th century Shaolin monks fought Japanese pirates (wokou) on the coast. The temple suffered a devastating burning in 1928 during warlord fighting. In the later 20th century the 1982 film The Shaolin Temple, starring Jet Li, revived interest, and the surrounding Dengfeng area filled with large martial arts schools teaching Shaolin methods to tens of thousands of students.

Content and styles

Shaolin kung fu is an "external" (waijia) style emphasising speed, power, acrobatic movement and a vast repertoire of empty-hand and weapon forms (taolu). Traditional curricula are said to include 72 secret arts. Its many sub-styles include:

  • Animal forms imitating the tiger, leopard, snake, crane, dragon, mantis, monkey and others.
  • Weapons such as the staff (gun), spear (qiang), broadsword (dao), straight sword (jian), and the distinctive monk's spade.
  • Qigong and conditioning arts, including "iron body" skills demonstrated in popular performances.
  • Xinyiba and other internal-leaning Shaolin methods preserved in nearby villages.

A common saying holds that "all martial arts under heaven originated from Shaolin" (天下武功出少林), reflecting the monastery's outsized influence on northern Chinese styles.

Philosophy

Shaolin training is presented as a path of Chan practice — "Chan and fist as one" — in which physical discipline supports meditation and self-cultivation. Monks historically distinguished between "civil" scholarly monks and "warrior monks" (wuseng), the latter guarding the monastery and its lands. Modern Shaolin has also become a global cultural brand, with affiliated temples and schools abroad, cultural performances, and the monastery precinct inscribed as part of the UNESCO World Heritage "Historic Monuments of Dengfeng" (2010).
