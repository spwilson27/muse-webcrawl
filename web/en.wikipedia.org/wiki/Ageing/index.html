Ageing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ageing (or aging in American English) is the process of becoming older. The term refers mainly to humans, many other animals, fungi and perennial plants. In the broadest sense, ageing can refer to single cells within an organism which have ceased dividing, or to the population of a species.

In humans, ageing is the accumulation of changes over time, encompassing physical, psychological and social changes. Reaction time may slow with age, while memories and general knowledge may increase. Ageing increases the risk of human diseases such as cancer, Alzheimer's disease, diabetes, cardiovascular disease and many more. Of the roughly 150,000 people who die each day across the globe, about two-thirds die from age-related causes.

Biology of ageing

Current ageing theories are assigned to the damage concept, whereby the accumulation of damage (such as DNA oxidation) may cause biological systems to fail, or to the programmed ageing concept, whereby processes (such as DNA methylation) inherently cause ageing. A widely cited framework lists hallmarks of ageing including genomic instability, telomere attrition, epigenetic alterations, loss of proteostasis, disabled macroautophagy, deregulated nutrient-sensing, mitochondrial dysfunction, cellular senescence, stem cell exhaustion, altered intercellular communication, chronic inflammation and dysbiosis.

The evolutionary biology of ageing explains senescence through theories such as mutation accumulation, antagonistic pleiotropy and the disposable soma theory: because the force of natural selection declines with age, traits that harm late life but help early reproduction can persist.

Ageing and society

Population ageing — a rising median age driven by falling fertility and increasing life expectancy — is a defining demographic trend of the 21st century, with implications for pensions, healthcare systems and labour markets. The world's population aged 60 and over is projected by the United Nations to double between 2015 and 2050. Human maximum lifespan is debated; the longest verified lifespan is that of Jeanne Calment of France (1875–1997), who lived to 122 years and 164 days.
