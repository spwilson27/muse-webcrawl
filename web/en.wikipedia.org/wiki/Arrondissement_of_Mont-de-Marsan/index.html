The arrondissement of Mont-de-Marsan is an arrondissement of France in the Landes department in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region. It has 175 communes, making it one of the two arrondissements of Landes. Its prefecture — and the capital of the department — is Mont-de-Marsan. Its population is roughly 180,000.

Composition

The arrondissement covers the northern and eastern parts of the department, dominated by the Landes de Gascogne forest, the largest maritime pine forest in Europe, along with the Armagnac wine region in the east. Its communes are grouped into cantons including Adour Armagnac, Chalosse Tursan, Coteau de Chalosse, Grands Lacs, Haute Lande Armagnac, Mont-de-Marsan-1, Mont-de-Marsan-2 and Pays morcenais tarusate (shared).

History

The arrondissement of Mont-de-Marsan was created in 1800. Between 1926 and 1942 it was enlarged by the temporary suppression of the arrondissement of Saint-Sever, whose territory was merged into it. The 2017 arrondissement reorganization in Nouvelle-Aquitaine adjusted its boundaries slightly with the arrondissement of Dax.
