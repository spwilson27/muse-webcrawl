Griffith Observatory is a public observatory and planetarium in Los Angeles, California, situated on the south-facing slope of Mount Hollywood in Griffith Park. It commands views of the Los Angeles Basin, downtown Los Angeles, and the Hollywood Sign. Since opening in 1935 it has been one of the most-visited public observatories in the world, fulfilling the wish of its benefactor, mining magnate Griffith J. Griffith, that astronomy be made accessible to everyone.

History and exhibits

Griffith donated the land for the park in 1896 and left funds in his will for an observatory, Greek theatre and planetarium, declaring that free access to astronomy would uplift humanity. The building, designed in Art Deco style with Greek and Egyptian Revival motifs, opened on 14 May 1935. Its exhibits include a Zeiss refracting telescope (used by more people than any other telescope in the world), a Foucault pendulum, a Tesla coil, the Leonard Nimoy Event Horizon theater and the Samuel Oschin Planetarium. The observatory was renovated and greatly expanded between 2002 and 2006, with new exhibit space excavated beneath the front lawn.

In popular culture

The observatory is a Los Angeles landmark and a celebrated film location, most famously in Rebel Without a Cause (1955, commemorated by a bust of James Dean on the grounds), and later in The Terminator and La La Land.
