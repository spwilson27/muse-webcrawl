Three Secrets of Fátima

The Three Secrets of Fátima are a series of apocalyptic visions and prophecies said to have been given to three young Portuguese shepherds — Lúcia dos Santos and her cousins Francisco and Jacinta Marto — by the Virgin Mary, beginning on 13 May 1917 at Fátima, Portugal.

The secrets

According to Sister Lúcia's memoirs, the first secret was a vision of Hell. The second concerned the end of the First World War, a prediction of another war if people continued to offend God, and a request for the consecration of Russia to the Immaculate Heart of Mary. The third secret, written down in 1944, was kept sealed by the Vatican until its release in 2000; it described a bishop in white being killed by soldiers, which the Vatican interpreted as foretelling the 1981 assassination attempt on Pope John Paul II.

Reactions and controversy

The secrets have inspired widespread devotion as well as speculation and conspiracy theories, particularly around whether the full text of the third secret was disclosed. The Fátima apparitions were officially recognised as "worthy of belief" by the Catholic Church in 1930, and the Sanctuary of Fátima became a major pilgrimage site.
