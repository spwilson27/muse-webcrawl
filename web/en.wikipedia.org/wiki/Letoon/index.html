The Letoon (Greek: Λητῷον) was the principal sanctuary of the ancient Lycian federation, dedicated to the goddess Leto and her twin children Apollo and Artemis. It lies near the village of Kumluova in present-day Muğla Province, Turkey, a few kilometres south of the ancient city of Xanthos, with which it shares a UNESCO World Heritage listing.

History

The site was sacred from at least the 6th century BC. Three temples stood side by side: the westernmost to Leto, the central one to Artemis, and the easternmost — the largest — to Apollo. The sanctuary served as the religious heart of Lycia, where federal festivals and assemblies were held. In 1973 excavators recovered the famous Xanthos trilingual stele nearby, inscribed in Greek, Lycian, and Aramaic, which proved a key to deciphering the Lycian language.

The site

Besides the three temples, the ruins include a nymphaeum built over a sacred spring — associated with the myth in which Leto, fleeing Hera, turned Lycian peasants who refused her water into frogs — a Hellenistic theatre, and an early Byzantine church incorporating a mosaic floor. Much of the site remains partly waterlogged because of the high local water table, which lends it an evocative atmosphere.
