The Jin dynasty (1115–1234), officially the Great Jin, was an imperial dynasty of China founded by the Jurchen people, ancestors of the later Manchus. Its founder, Wanyan Aguda (Emperor Taizu), rebelled against the Khitan-led Liao dynasty in 1114 and proclaimed the Jin the following year.

History

In alliance with the Song dynasty, the Jin destroyed the Liao by 1125, then turned on the Song in the Jin–Song Wars. In the Jingkang Incident of 1127, Jin forces captured the Northern Song capital Kaifeng and the emperors Huizong and Qinzong, forcing the Song court to retreat south of the Yangtze. The Jin thereafter ruled northern China and, after the Shaoxing Treaty of 1142, settled into an uneasy peace with the Southern Song.

Over time the Jurchen rulers adopted Chinese administrative forms and Confucian court ritual, while preserving elements of Jurchen identity through institutions such as the hereditary meng'an-mouke military units. The dynasty reached its height under emperors Shizong and Zhangzong in the later 12th century.

Fall

Weakened by war with the Western Xia and the Song, the Jin faced the Mongol invasions beginning in 1211. The capital Zhongdu (present-day Beijing) fell in 1215, and the court fled to Kaifeng. Despite desperate resistance, the last Jin emperor, Aizong, died as Caizhou fell to a joint Mongol–Song assault in 1234, ending the dynasty after 119 years.
