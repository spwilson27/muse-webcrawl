Nebhepetre Mentuhotep II (reigned c. 2061 BC – 2010 BC) was a Pharaoh of the 11th Dynasty who reigned for 51 years. Around his 39th year on the throne, he reunited Egypt, thus ending the First Intermediate Period. Consequently, he is considered the first pharaoh of the Middle Kingdom.

Mentuhotep II was the son of Intef III and his mother Iah. His throne name, Nebhepetre, means "The Lord of the rudder is Ra".

Reunification of Egypt

From his capital at Thebes in Upper Egypt, Mentuhotep campaigned against the rival Herakleopolitan kings of Lower Egypt. By the 39th year of his reign he had defeated them and reunified the Two Lands, taking the Horus name Sematawy, "He who unifies the Two Lands". He also campaigned in Nubia and the Levant.

Mortuary complex

His mortuary temple at Deir el-Bahari, built against the cliffs of western Thebes, was an innovative terraced structure combining elements of a pyramid, a mastaba, and a rock tomb. It later served as the inspiration for Hatshepsut's famous temple built beside it some five centuries later. In 1881, a painted sandstone statue of the king wearing the red crown of Lower Egypt was discovered in a shaft beneath the temple.

Legacy

Later king lists and tradition regarded Mentuhotep II as a second founder of the Egyptian state, comparable to Menes, the legendary unifier of the Old Kingdom.
