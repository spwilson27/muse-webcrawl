Nitra Castle

Nitra Castle (Slovak: Nitriansky hrad) is a castle in the old town of Nitra, Slovakia, standing on a limestone hill above a meander of the river Nitra. It is the seat of the Bishop of Nitra and one of Slovakia's most important historical monuments.

History

The hill was a fortified centre of the Principality of Nitra and of Great Moravia in the 9th century. The present complex grew around the Romanesque church of St Emmeram (11th–12th centuries), the Gothic and later rebuilt St Emmeram's Cathedral with its Baroque upper church, and the Bishop's Palace (18th century). The castle withstood Tartar attack in 1241 but was damaged in fires and anti-Habsburg uprisings, and rebuilt in the 17th century with bastioned fortifications.

Today

The castle precinct includes the cathedral treasury and diocesan museum, displaying medieval liturgical treasures; the site overlooks the modern city and hosts festivals and the annual Nitra pilgrimage traditions associated with Saints Cyril and Methodius.
