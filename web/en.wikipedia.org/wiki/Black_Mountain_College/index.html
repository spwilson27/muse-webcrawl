Black Mountain College was a private liberal arts college in Black Mountain, North Carolina. Founded in 1933 by John Andrew Rice, Theodore Dreier, and others, it operated until 1957. Despite its short life and chronic financial difficulties, the college became legendary as a seedbed of American avant-garde art, counting among its faculty and students Josef and Anni Albers, John Cage, Merce Cunningham, Buckminster Fuller, Robert Rauschenberg, and Willem de Kooning.

History

Rice, a classics scholar dismissed from Rollins College, founded the school on progressive principles drawn from John Dewey: the arts at the center of the curriculum, no required courses, no traditional grades or examinations, and democratic self-governance in which students and faculty shared work and decision-making. The college first occupied the Blue Ridge Assembly campus, then moved in 1941 to Lake Eden, where students helped construct the campus buildings.

Faculty and students

Josef Albers, who arrived in 1933 after the Bauhaus closed under Nazi pressure, led the art program; his wife Anni taught weaving and textile design. The summer sessions of the 1940s and 1950s drew an extraordinary roster: John Cage staged his first "happening" (Theatre Piece No. 1) there in 1952 with Merce Cunningham and David Tudor; Buckminster Fuller erected his first successful geodesic dome with students in 1949. Charles Olson, who became rector in the college's final years, made it a center of the Black Mountain poets, including Robert Creeley and Robert Duncan.

Legacy

Never accredited and always near insolvency, the college closed in 1957. Its influence on postwar American art, dance, music, and poetry far exceeded its size — enrollment rarely passed one hundred. The Black Mountain College Museum + Arts Center in Asheville preserves its legacy, and the Lake Eden campus is now home to the Black Mountain College Museum's successor events and a summer camp.
