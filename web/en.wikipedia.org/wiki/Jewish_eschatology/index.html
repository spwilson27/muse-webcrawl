Jewish eschatology

Jewish eschatology is the area of Jewish theology concerned with events that will happen in the end of days and related concepts. This includes the ingathering of the exiled diaspora, the coming of the Jewish Messiah, the afterlife, and the resurrection of the dead. In Judaism, the end times are usually called the "end of days" (aḥarit ha-yamim, אחרית הימים), a phrase that appears several times in the Tanakh.

Messianic age

In classical Jewish belief the Messiah (Mashiach) is a future human leader descended from David who will rebuild the Temple in Jerusalem, gather the exiles to the Land of Israel, and inaugurate an era of universal peace and knowledge of God. Maimonides codified belief in the Messiah's coming as the twelfth of his Thirteen Principles of Faith: "I believe with perfect faith in the coming of the Messiah, and though he tarry, still I await him every day."

The World to Come

Olam Ha-Ba, "the World to Come", refers both to the afterlife and to the messianic era on earth, depending on the source. Rabbinic tradition teaches the resurrection of the dead (teḥiyyat ha-metim) at the time of the Messiah and a final judgement. Sheol, the shadowy realm of the dead in the Hebrew Bible, developed into the concepts of Gan Eden (paradise) and Gehinnom, where souls are purified for at most twelve months.

Development

Apocalyptic eschatology flourished in the Second Temple period in works such as the Book of Daniel and the Dead Sea Scrolls, which describe a final war of the "sons of light" against the "sons of darkness". After the destruction of the Temple in 70 CE, rabbinic Judaism emphasised Torah observance and cautioned against calculating the end, while belief in redemption remained central to liturgy and hope.
