The MLS Cup Playoffs is the annual postseason elimination tournament of Major League Soccer (MLS), the top professional soccer league in the United States and Canada. The playoffs conclude the MLS season and culminate in the MLS Cup final, which determines the league champion; the winner receives the Philip F. Anschutz Trophy. Unlike most soccer leagues worldwide, MLS uses a playoff system similar to other North American sports leagues rather than crowning the team with the best regular-season record (which is instead recognized by the Supporters' Shield).

Format

The format has changed repeatedly since the league's inaugural season in 1996. Early editions included best-of-three series and the short-lived "first to five points" system. From 2012, the format settled into knockout rounds and two-legged conference semifinals and finals, hosted by the higher seed. In 2019 the league moved to an entirely single-elimination bracket with all matches hosted by the higher-seeded team, and in 2023 it introduced a best-of-three series in the first round alongside a wild-card round.

As of the 2023 format, 18 of the league's clubs qualify — nine from each conference — with the top seven in each conference entering the first round directly and the eighth- and ninth-placed teams contesting a single wild-card match. Seeding is based on regular-season points, so the Supporters' Shield winner is guaranteed the top seed and home-field advantage throughout.

History

The first MLS Cup, in 1996, was won by D.C. United, who defeated the LA Galaxy 3–2 in extra time at Foxboro Stadium. The Galaxy and D.C. United became the dominant playoff teams of the league's first two decades. Notable playoff moments include the Galaxy's 2011–2014 run of three titles in four years, Toronto FC's 2017 victory as the first Canadian champion, and Atlanta United's 2018 title in only its second season.
