Lagos State is a state in southwestern Nigeria, bordered by Ogun State to the north and east, the Republic of Benin to the west, and the Atlantic Ocean (Gulf of Guinea) to the south. Though the smallest Nigerian state by area, it is the country's economic centre and its most populous state, containing the metropolitan area of Lagos — the largest city in Nigeria and one of the largest urban agglomerations in Africa.

History

The state was created on 27 May 1967 from the former Lagos Colony and parts of the Western Region. Lagos city served as Nigeria's federal capital until 1991, when the capital moved to Abuja; the state capital is Ikeja. The city's port at Apapa and Tin Can Island handles the bulk of Nigeria's imports, and its districts of Ikeja, Victoria Island, Ikoyi and Lekki concentrate finance, media, technology and entertainment, including the Nollywood film industry.

Geography and governance

The state consists of a mainland section and a chain of islands and peninsulas around Lagos Lagoon, connected by major bridges including the Third Mainland Bridge, one of the longest in Africa. It is divided into 20 Local Government Areas and numerous local council development areas. Rapid, largely unplanned growth has made housing, traffic congestion and flooding the state's defining challenges, addressed in part through the Eko Atlantic reclamation project and the Lagos rail mass transit lines, the first of which opened in 2023.
