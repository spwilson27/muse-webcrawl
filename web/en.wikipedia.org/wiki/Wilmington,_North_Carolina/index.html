Wilmington is a port city in and the county seat of New Hanover County in coastal southeastern North Carolina, United States. With a population of 115,451 at the 2020 census, it is the eighth-most populous city in the state. The city was founded on the Cape Fear River and grew as a major port and naval-stores centre; the Port of Wilmington remains one of North Carolina's two deep-water ports.

History

Settled in the 1730s and incorporated in 1739/40, Wilmington was named after Spencer Compton, Earl of Wilmington. During the American Revolution it was occupied by British forces under Cornwallis in 1781. In the Civil War it became the Confederacy's most important port for blockade-running, protected by Fort Fisher, whose fall in January 1865 sealed the Confederacy's fate.

In 1898 Wilmington was the site of the only successful coup d'état in United States history, when white supremacists overthrew the city's elected biracial Fusionist government, expelling Black leaders and killing dozens of African American residents in the Wilmington insurrection of 1898.

Geography and economy

Wilmington lies between the Cape Fear River and the Atlantic Ocean beaches of Wrightsville, Carolina and Kure beaches. Its economy rests on the port, tourism, healthcare, the University of North Carolina Wilmington, and film and television production — its EUE/Screen Gems studios earned it the nickname "Wilmywood". The city's large historic district, one of the largest in the National Register, features antebellum homes and the riverwalk along the Cape Fear.

Notable sites

Attractions include the battleship USS North Carolina, moored across the river as a museum, the Airlie Gardens, and nearby Fort Fisher State Historic Site.
