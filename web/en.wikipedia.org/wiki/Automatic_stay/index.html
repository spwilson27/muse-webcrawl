Automatic stay

The automatic stay is an automatic injunction that halts actions by creditors, with certain exceptions, to collect debts from a debtor who has declared bankruptcy. Under section 362 of the United States Bankruptcy Code, the stay arises the moment a bankruptcy petition is filed, without any action by the court or the debtor. It is one of the fundamental debtor protections in American bankruptcy law, giving the debtor a "breathing spell" from creditors.

Scope

The stay generally prohibits the commencement or continuation of lawsuits, enforcement of judgments, foreclosure and repossession, acts to create or enforce liens, collection calls and letters, and setoff of debts. It protects the debtor and property of the bankruptcy estate. Knowing violation of the stay can result in sanctions, including actual damages and attorney's fees, and punitive damages in appropriate circumstances.

Exceptions and limitations

Certain proceedings are excepted, including criminal prosecutions, many domestic-support and family-law matters, some tax proceedings, and actions by governmental units enforcing police and regulatory powers. The stay is limited or terminated for repeat filers: under the Bankruptcy Abuse Prevention and Consumer Protection Act of 2005, the stay expires after 30 days if the debtor had one case dismissed in the previous year, and does not arise at all after two prior dismissals unless the court orders otherwise.

Relief from stay

A creditor may ask the court to lift (grant relief from) the stay, for example where the creditor's collateral is not adequately protected, the debtor has no equity in the property and it is not necessary to an effective reorganization, or the petition was filed in bad faith as part of a scheme to hinder or defraud creditors. The stay otherwise ends when the case is closed or dismissed, the property leaves the estate, or the debtor receives a discharge.
