OPUS card

The OPUS card (French: Carte OPUS) is a rechargeable contactless smart card used for public transit fares in the Greater Montreal area of Quebec, Canada, and in Quebec City. Introduced in 2008, it replaced magnetic-stripe tickets and tokens across the region's transit agencies, which are now coordinated by the Autorité régionale de transport métropolitain (ARTM).

Use

Riders load fares — single tickets, monthly passes, or fare products for multiple agencies — onto the card at ticket machines, service points, and authorised retailers, and validate by tapping the card on a reader when boarding buses or passing metro turnstiles. Reduced-fare OPUS cards carry the holder's photograph and are issued to students, children, and seniors; standard cards are anonymous and valid for several years. The system covers the STM (Montreal metro and buses), exo commuter trains and buses, the RTL in Longueuil, the STL in Laval, and the RTC network in Quebec City, whose card is branded in the same system.

Technology

The card uses contactless RFID technology operating at 13.56 MHz. Because fares from several operators can coexist on one card, the system requires interoperability agreements on fare distribution and revenue sharing among agencies.
