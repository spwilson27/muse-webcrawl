Endodontics

Endodontics (from the Greek roots endo- "inside" and odont- "tooth") is the dental specialty concerned with the study and treatment of the dental pulp and the tissues surrounding the roots of a tooth. Endodontists perform root canal therapy, treat dental trauma and cracked teeth, and carry out surgical procedures such as apicoectomy.

Scope of practice

The most common endodontic procedure is root canal treatment, in which diseased or dead pulp is removed, the root canal system is cleaned, shaped, and disinfected, and the space is filled (obturated), most often with gutta-percha, before the tooth is restored. Endodontists also perform pulp capping and pulpotomy (vital pulp therapy), retreatment of failed root canals, repair of perforations, treatment of resorption, and periradicular surgery.

Training and practice

Endodontists complete two or more years of advanced specialty training after dental school. They typically use operating microscopes, digital radiography, and cone-beam computed tomography for diagnosis and treatment, and handle cases referred by general dentists because of complex root anatomy or diagnostic difficulty. Diagnosis centres on distinguishing reversible from irreversible pulpitis and identifying pulpal necrosis and apical periodontitis, often through cold and electric pulp testing and periapical imaging.
