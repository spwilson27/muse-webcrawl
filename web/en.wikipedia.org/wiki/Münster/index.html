Münster is an independent city in North Rhine-Westphalia, Germany, in the northern part of the state and the capital of the Münsterland region. With about 320,000 inhabitants, it is a major university city: the University of Münster is one of Germany's largest universities, and students make up a large share of the population.

History

Münster was founded in 793 when Charlemagne sent the missionary Liudger to build a monastery (monasterium, hence the name) there; Liudger became its first bishop in 805. In the 16th century the city was the centre of the Münster rebellion, in which radical Anabaptists seized power in 1534 and established a theocratic commune before the city was retaken in 1535; the bodies of the leaders were displayed in iron cages hung from the tower of St Lambert's Church, where the cages remain.

In 1648 the Peace of Westphalia was negotiated and signed in Münster and Osnabrück, ending the Thirty Years' War in the Holy Roman Empire and the Eighty Years' War between Spain and the Dutch Republic. The city was heavily bombed in the Second World War, and its old town was rebuilt largely along historic lines, including the arcaded Prinzipalmarkt.

Today

Münster is famous in Germany as a bicycle city, with hundreds of kilometres of cycle paths and more bicycles than inhabitants. Landmarks include St Paulus Cathedral, the Erbdrostenhof, Schloss Münster, the Aasee lake and the Pablo Picasso art museum.
