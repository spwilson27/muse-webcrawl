Eryngium foetidum is a tropical perennial herb in the family Apiaceae. Its many common names include culantro, recao, chadon beni (or shado beni), Mexican coriander, bandhaniya, long coriander, sawtooth coriander and ngò gai. It is native to Mexico, the Caribbean, and Central and South America, but is cultivated and naturalised worldwide in the tropics.

Description and uses

The plant grows in a basal rosette of lanceolate leaves with serrated margins and has a strong flavour similar to, but more intense than, coriander (cilantro). It is widely used as a seasoning in Caribbean, Latin American and Southeast Asian cuisines — in dishes such as Puerto Rican sofrito and recaíto, Panamanian sancocho, Vietnamese phở garnishes, and Thai and Cambodian soups and salads. Unlike coriander, its leaves dry well and retain flavour, and it is hardier in hot climates.

Traditional medicine

In folk medicine, E. foetidum has been used for fevers, chills, vomiting, burns, earache and as an anticonvulsant; in Trinidad it is a traditional remedy associated with its name "fitweed". The plant is rich in calcium, iron, carotene and riboflavin, and its essential oils contain the aldehyde (E)-2-dodecenal.
