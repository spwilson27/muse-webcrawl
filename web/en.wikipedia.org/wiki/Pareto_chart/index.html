Pareto chart

A Pareto chart is a type of chart that combines a bar graph and a line graph. The individual values are represented in descending order by bars, and the cumulative total is represented by the line. It is one of the seven basic tools of quality control.

The chart is named after the Italian economist Vilfredo Pareto and embodies the Pareto principle, often summarised as the "80/20 rule": roughly 80 percent of effects come from 20 percent of causes. In quality management, the chart is used to identify the "vital few" defect categories or problems that account for most of the total, so that improvement efforts can be focused where they will have the greatest effect.

Construction

Categories are counted and sorted from most to least frequent. Bars are plotted in descending order of frequency, with an "other" category, if used, placed last regardless of size. A cumulative percentage line is drawn on a secondary axis, rising to 100 percent at the final category. The quality pioneer Joseph M. Juran popularised the chart and the distinction between the "vital few" and the "useful many" (originally "trivial many").

Uses

Pareto charts are used in manufacturing, software engineering (to prioritise bug types), healthcare, customer service and complaint analysis — anywhere categorical frequency data must be prioritised. Weighted versions, in which bars represent cost or severity rather than simple counts, are common.
