Diverticulitis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


CT scan showing colonic diverticulitis

Diverticulitis, also called colonic diverticulitis, is a gastrointestinal disease characterized by inflammation of abnormal pouches - diverticula - which can develop in the wall of the large intestine. Symptoms typically include lower abdominal pain of sudden onset, but the onset may also occur over a few days. There may also be nausea, diarrhea or constipation, fever, and blood in the stool.

Diverticula develop when naturally weak places in the colon wall give way under pressure, forming marble-sized pouches, most often in the sigmoid colon. The presence of these pouches without inflammation is called diverticulosis. Diverticulitis occurs when one or more pouches become inflamed or infected.

Causes and risk factors

Risk factors include obesity, lack of exercise, smoking, a family history of the disease, and use of nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs). The role of dietary fiber is unclear; although low-fiber diets were long blamed, evidence is mixed. Diverticular disease is common in the Western world and becomes more frequent with age.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is typically made by CT scan, with blood tests, ultrasound, or MRI as alternatives. Treatment may include bowel rest, a liquid diet, and antibiotics; uncomplicated cases are often managed as an outpatient. Surgery is reserved for complications such as abscess, perforation, fistula, or obstruction, or for repeated severe episodes.
