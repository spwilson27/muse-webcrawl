United States Army Infantry School

Infantry School
Location: Fort Benning (Fort Moore), Georgia
Branch: United States Army
Motto: "Follow Me!"

The United States Army Infantry School is a school located at Fort Benning (renamed Fort Moore in 2023) in Georgia that is run by the United States Army. It is the home of the infantry branch and trains soldiers in the skills of the infantry, the largest branch of the Army. The school is part of the Maneuver Center of Excellence.

Mission

The Infantry School trains and educates soldiers, including the Infantry Basic Officer Leader Course, the Infantry Officer Basic Course historically, one-station unit training for enlisted infantrymen, and advanced courses for noncommissioned officers. It also oversees the U.S. Army Ranger School and airborne training conducted nearby.

History

The school traces its origins to the School of Musketry established at the Presidio of Monterey, California, in 1907, and to the Infantry School of Arms organized at Fort Sill, Oklahoma, during the First World War. In 1919 the school moved to Camp Benning, Georgia, which became Fort Benning in 1922.

During the Second World War the school expanded enormously, training officers for the rapidly growing Army. Generals such as George C. Marshall served as assistant commandant, and Marshall's reforms emphasized practical, realistic field training.

Organization

The school is commanded by the Chief of Infantry, a brigadier general who also serves as the commandant. Subordinate units include training brigades responsible for basic and advanced infantry training.
