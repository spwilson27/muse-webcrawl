County

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A county is a geographical region of a country used for administrative or other purposes in some modern nations. Historically the district governed by a count (continental Europe) or an earl (England, from the shires reorganised under Norman rule), the county survives in the United Kingdom as ceremonial and (variably) administrative geography, and in the United States as the principal subdivision of states: there are some 3,000 US counties and county-equivalents (Louisiana parishes, Alaska boroughs), ranging from Loving County, Texas (fewer than 100 residents) to Los Angeles County (about 10 million, more populous than most states).

US county governments typically handle courts, records, sheriffs and jails, elections, roads and public health, governed by elected boards of commissioners or supervisors; their powers vary by state (and Connecticut and Rhode Island have abolished county government functions). Counties remain the building block of American political statistics, party organisation and congressional geography, even where cities and special districts do much of the governing.
