Volga Delta


Satellite view of the Volga Delta

The Volga Delta is the largest river delta in Europe and occurs where Europe's largest river system, the Volga River, enters the Caspian Sea in Russia's Astrakhan Oblast, north-east of the Caspian Depression. The delta covers roughly 27,000 square kilometres and is growing seaward as sediment accumulates in the shallow northern Caspian.

Geography

The river splits into hundreds of channels and distributaries, the largest being the Bakhtemir, producing a fan of wetlands, reed beds and shallow lagoons. Because the level of the Caspian Sea fluctuates, the delta's shoreline has advanced and retreated significantly over the past century.

Ecology

The delta is a vital habitat for migratory birds, spawning sturgeon and other fish, and lotus beds famous in summer. Much of it is protected by the Astrakhan Biosphere Reserve, established in 1919, and the delta is listed as a Ramsar wetland site.

Economy

Fisheries, including historically sturgeon and caviar production, navigation to Astrakhan, and agriculture on reclaimed land are the main human activities in the delta region.
