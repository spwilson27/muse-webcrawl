Ignacy Ewaryst Daszyński (26 October 1866 – 31 October 1936) was a Polish socialist politician, journalist and statesman. A co-founder of the Polish Social Democratic Party of Galicia and later a leading figure of the Polish Socialist Party (PPS), he headed the provisional government formed at Lublin in November 1918 and later served as Marshal (speaker) of the Sejm.

Early career

Born in Zbaraż in Austrian-ruled Galicia, Daszyński studied in Stanisławów and Kraków, where he became involved in socialist movements while still a student. In 1892 he helped found the Polish Social Democratic Party. He represented his party in the Austrian Reichsrat from 1897, becoming known as a gifted parliamentary orator advocating universal suffrage and Polish independence.

Interwar Poland

On 7 November 1918 Daszyński became prime minister of the Provisional People's Government of the Republic of Poland in Lublin, one of the first governments of the reborn Polish state; it soon subordinated itself to Józef Piłsudski's government in Warsaw. Daszyński later served as deputy prime minister (1920–1921) and as Marshal of the Sejm from 1928. After Piłsudski's 1926 coup, he defended parliamentary democracy and clashed with the Sanacja regime, notably refusing to open a Sejm session under military pressure in 1929.

Legacy

Daszyński is remembered as a founding figure of Polish democratic socialism and of the independent Polish state.
