Codorníu

TypePrivate (Grupo Raventós Codorníu)

HeadquartersSant Sadurní d'Anoia, Catalonia, Spain

Codorníu is a Spanish wine company based in Sant Sadurní d'Anoia, Catalonia, and the oldest family business in Spain, with winegrowing origins documented in 1551. It is best known as the creator of cava: in 1872 Josep Raventós Fatjó produced the first Spanish sparkling wine made by the traditional (Champagne) method, and the company became the leading producer of cava.

History

The Codorníu family's winegrowing at the Can Codorníu estate dates to a document of 1551 recording Jaume Codorníu as a vine grower. In 1659 Anna Codorníu married Miquel Raventós, joining the two families whose name the business still bears. Josep Raventós Fatjó, after studying sparkling-wine production in Champagne, made the first bottles of what would become cava in 1872, using the native Macabeo, Parellada and Xarel·lo grapes.

In 1885 Manuel Raventós began marketing the wine commercially and commissioned the architect Josep Puig i Cadafalch to design the great modernista cellars at Sant Sadurní d'Anoia, built between 1895 and 1915 and now a national monument. The cellars extend for some 30 kilometres underground.

Group

The Raventós Codorníu group owns wineries across Spain (including Raimat in Costers del Segre and Bodegas Bilbaínas in Rioja) and abroad, in California (Artesa) and Mendoza, Argentina. In 2018 a majority stake was sold to the investment firm Carlyle Group, while the family retained a minority shareholding.
