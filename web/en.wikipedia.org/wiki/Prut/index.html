Prut

The Prut (Romanian: Prut; Ukrainian: Прут) is a 953-kilometre (592 mi) long river in Eastern Europe and a left tributary of the Danube. It rises in the Carpathian Mountains of western Ukraine, near Hoverla, and flows southeast to join the Danube near Reni, Ukraine, close to Galați, Romania.

Course

From its source in Ivano-Frankivsk Oblast, the Prut passes Chernivtsi and then forms much of the border between Moldova and Romania (and briefly Ukraine) for several hundred kilometres. Its principal tributary is the Jijia. The Stânca–Costești Dam, a joint Romanian–Moldovan project completed in 1979, regulates the river's flow and generates hydroelectric power.

History

Known in antiquity as the Pyretus or Porata, the Prut marked boundaries between Dacia, the Principality of Moldavia, the Russian Empire and Romania. In the Russo-Turkish War of 1710–11, Peter the Great's army was encircled on the Prut and forced to sign the Treaty of the Prut. After 1812 the river became the border between Russian Bessarabia and Moldavia, and after 1940 between the USSR and Romania — a border largely retained today between Moldova and Romania.
