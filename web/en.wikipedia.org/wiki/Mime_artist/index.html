Mime artist

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A mime artist, or simply mime (from Greek mimos, "imitator, actor"), is a person who uses mime — the theatrical technique of suggesting action, character or emotion without words, using only gesture, expression and movement — as a medium for performance. In earlier centuries, mime referred broadly to a performer in a particular genre of Roman theatre; in the twentieth century it came to be associated with the silent, white-faced performer popularised by French artists such as Jean-Gaspard Deburau and Marcel Marceau.

History

Ancient mime

Mime originated in ancient Greece, where performers presented short, often comic or satirical sketches of daily life, sometimes with dialogue but relying heavily on gesture. In Rome, mime (mimus) became a popular, unmasked form of entertainment featuring plots drawn from everyday life and contemporary scandal, performed by both men and women — unusual in ancient theatre.

Pantomime and commedia dell'arte

Elements of mime survived in medieval and Renaissance popular theatre, notably in the Italian commedia dell'arte, whose stock characters and improvised physical comedy influenced the English harlequinade and the French pantomime tradition at the Parisian fair theatres.

Modern mime

In the nineteenth century, Jean-Gaspard Deburau refined the character of Pierrot at the Théâtre des Funambules in Paris, establishing the silent, melancholy white-faced mime of modern imagination. In the twentieth century, Étienne Decroux developed "corporeal mime", a rigorous technique of body articulation that influenced his students Jean-Louis Barrault and Marcel Marceau. Marceau's stage persona "Bip" — modelled partly on Charlie Chaplin's Little Tramp — brought mime to worldwide audiences through touring and television from the 1940s until his death in 2007.

Technique

Mime technique centres on making the invisible visible: the performer uses fixed points, resistance and precise isolation of body parts to suggest objects and forces that are not present. Classic routines include walking against the wind, climbing stairs or a rope, pulling a heavy rope, and being trapped inside an invisible box. Unlike the popular stereotype, modern mime training at schools such as the École de Mime Corporel Dramatique emphasises abstract movement and physical theatre rather than white face and striped shirts.

Notable mime artists

  • Jean-Gaspard Deburau (1796–1846)
  • Étienne Decroux (1898–1991)
  • Jean-Louis Barrault (1910–1994)
  • Marcel Marceau (1923–2007)
  • Jacques Lecoq (1921–1999), teacher of physical theatre
  • Lindsay Kemp (1938–2018)
