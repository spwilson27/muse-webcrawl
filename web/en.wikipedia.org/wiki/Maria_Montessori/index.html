Maria Montessori

DiedMay 6, 1952, Noordwijk, Netherlands
OccupationPhysician, educator

Maria Tecla Artemisia Montessori (August 31, 1870 – May 6, 1952) was an Italian physician and educator best known for developing the Montessori method of education, a philosophy built on scientific observation of children. One of the first women to graduate in medicine in Italy, she worked first with children with intellectual disabilities and in 1907 opened the Casa dei Bambini ("Children's House") in a Rome tenement, where her methods produced striking results with working-class children.

The Montessori method

Montessori education emphasises independence, freedom within limits, and respect for a child's natural psychological, physical, and social development. Its characteristic features include mixed-age classrooms, long uninterrupted work periods, specially designed didactic materials that embody abstract concepts in concrete form, and a teacher who prepares the environment and guides rather than lectures. Montessori identified "sensitive periods" in development and a progression through planes of development from birth to adulthood.

Later life and legacy

Montessori lectured and trained teachers internationally, spending years in Spain, India, and the Netherlands, and was nominated three times for the Nobel Peace Prize. Her books, including The Montessori Method (1912) and The Absorbent Mind (1949), remain in print. By the early 21st century thousands of schools worldwide used her name and approach, and her image appeared on the Italian 1,000 lire banknote.
