Quéribus

Château de Quéribus
Location: Cucugnan, Aude, France
Elevation: 728 m (2,388 ft)
Type: Rock castle
Fall of the castle: 1255

The Château de Quéribus is a ruined castle in the commune of Cucugnan in the Aude department of southern France. Perched on a narrow rocky pinnacle at 728 metres above the Corbières hills, it is one of the so-called Cathar castles and was the last stronghold of the Cathars to fall to the forces of the Albigensian Crusade.

History

The castle is first mentioned in 1020, in the will of Bernard I of Besalú, and passed among the counts of Besalú, Barcelona, and the lords of Cucugnan. During the Albigensian Crusade it became a refuge for Cathar clergy and faithful, under the protection of its lord Chabert de Barbaira, who defied the French crown long after the main crusade had ended.

Quéribus finally surrendered in 1255, eleven years after the fall of Montségur — Chabert de Barbaira was captured by his old comrade Olivier de Termes, who exchanged him for the castle. Thereafter Quéribus became a French royal border fortress facing Aragon, one of the "five sons of Carcassonne", and was garrisoned until the Treaty of the Pyrenees (1659) moved the border south and made it obsolete.

Description

The fortress crowns its peak in three successive enclosures following the rock. Its polygonal keep contains a remarkable Gothic hall whose ribbed vault springs from a single central pillar. From the summit there are panoramic views over the Corbières vineyards to the Mediterranean and the Pyrenees. The castle, classified as a historic monument since 1907, underwent restoration from the 1990s and is open to visitors.
