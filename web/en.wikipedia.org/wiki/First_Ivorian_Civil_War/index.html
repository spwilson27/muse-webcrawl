First Ivorian Civil War

The First Ivorian Civil War was a conflict in Côte d'Ivoire (Ivory Coast) from September 2002 to 2007 between the government of President Laurent Gbagbo in the south and rebel Forces Nouvelles, who controlled the largely Muslim north, amid disputes over citizenship and land embodied in the politics of Ivoirité.

Date19 September 2002 – 4 March 2007
LocationCôte d'Ivoire
ResultOuagadougou Political Agreement; country reunified in principle

Origins

After Houphouët-Boigny's death in 1993, succession struggles, exclusion of northern politician Alassane Ouattara from elections on nationality grounds, land tensions and a 1999 coup eroded stability. Disgruntled soldiers launched attacks on 19 September 2002, failing to seize Abidjan but holding Bouaké and the north.

Course

France deployed Opération Licorne and later the UN sent UNOCI peacekeepers, policing a buffer "zone of confidence". The Linas-Marcoussis Accord (2003) created a unity government; violence flared in 2004 when Ivorian bombing killed French soldiers and France destroyed the Ivorian air force. Disarmament repeatedly stalled.

Resolution

The Ouagadougou Political Agreement of March 2007, brokered by Burkina Faso's Blaise Compaoré, made rebel leader Guillaume Soro prime minister and set elections — eventually held in 2010, whose disputed result triggered the Second Ivorian Civil War.
