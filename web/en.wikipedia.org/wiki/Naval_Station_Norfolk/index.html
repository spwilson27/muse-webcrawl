Naval Station Norfolk

Location: Norfolk, Virginia, United States
Type: Naval station
Controlled by: United States Navy
Claim: World's largest naval station

Naval Station Norfolk is a United States Navy base in Norfolk, Virginia, at the mouth of Chesapeake Bay on Hampton Roads. It is the world's largest naval station, supporting a concentration of U.S. Fleet Forces Command ships, aircraft, and personnel, and serves as the home port for aircraft carrier strike groups of the Atlantic Fleet.

History

The site traces its origins to the Jamestown Exposition of 1907, held on Sewell's Point; the Navy acquired the exposition grounds and additional land during the First World War, establishing a naval operating base in 1917. The base expanded greatly during the Second World War and the Cold War, absorbing neighbouring facilities. In 1999 the shore establishment was reorganised and the installation took its present name, Naval Station Norfolk.

Facilities and role

The station has roughly 14 piers along about 11 miles of waterfront and can berth dozens of major warships, including nuclear-powered aircraft carriers such as those of the Nimitz and Gerald R. Ford classes, cruisers, destroyers, submarines, and amphibious ships. Chambers Field, the station's airfield, supports naval aviation and the movement of personnel and cargo. Tens of thousands of sailors, Marines, and civilian employees work at the installation, making it the largest employer in the Hampton Roads region.

The base hosts headquarters including U.S. Fleet Forces Command and the U.S. Second Fleet. Its location and the surrounding sea-level terrain have made planning for sea-level rise and recurrent flooding a growing concern for the Navy.
