Jardin Majorelle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The cobalt-blue villa in the Majorelle Garden, Marrakech

The Jardin Majorelle (Majorelle Garden) is a botanical garden and artist's landscape garden in Marrakech, Morocco. It was created by the French Orientalist artist Jacques Majorelle over almost forty years, beginning in 1923, around a Cubist villa designed by architect Paul Sinoir.

History

Majorelle planted exotic species from five continents in the two-and-a-half-acre garden and in 1937 painted its structures in a distinctive shade of intense cobalt blue, later known as "Majorelle blue". After the artist's death in 1962 the garden fell into neglect; in 1980 it was bought and restored by fashion designer Yves Saint Laurent and his partner Pierre Bergé. The villa now houses the Berber Museum, and the adjacent Yves Saint Laurent Museum Marrakech opened in 2017.

Today

The garden, with its cacti, palms, bougainvillea, fountains and pools, is one of Marrakech's most visited attractions. Saint Laurent's ashes were scattered there after his death in 2008.
