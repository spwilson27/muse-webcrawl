The Fiat 128 is a small family car produced by the Italian manufacturer Fiat from 1969 to 1985. It was notable for its transverse-engine, front-wheel-drive layout with an end-on gearbox, a configuration that became the template for most modern front-wheel-drive cars.

Development

Designed under Dante Giacosa, the 128 was launched in 1969 as a replacement for the Fiat 1100. Its compact powertrain arrangement — engine mounted transversely with the gearbox in line with the crankshaft and unequal-length driveshafts — allowed an unusually spacious passenger compartment for the car's size. The 128 was named European Car of the Year in 1970.

Models

The 128 was offered as a two- and four-door saloon and a three-door estate (Familiare/Panorama). Sporty versions included the 128 Rally and the 128 3P coupé. Its overhead-camshaft engine, designed by Aurelio Lampredi, was later used in modified form in other Fiat models.

Licensed production

The design was built under licence in many countries, including by Zastava in Yugoslavia (as the Zastava 128 and the related Yugo line), by SEAT in Spain (SEAT 128), in Argentina, and in Egypt, where production continued until 2001.
