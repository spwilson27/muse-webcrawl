Larock indole synthesis

The Larock indole synthesis is a chemical reaction used in organic synthesis to prepare indoles. The reaction is a palladium-catalyzed heteroannulation of an o-iodoaniline with a disubstituted alkyne, typically using palladium acetate as catalyst, a base such as potassium carbonate or sodium acetate, and often a chloride additive, in a polar solvent such as DMF.

Mechanism and regioselectivity

The catalytic cycle involves oxidative addition of the aryl iodide to palladium(0), coordination and migratory insertion of the alkyne, attack by the aniline nitrogen, and reductive elimination to close the five-membered ring. With unsymmetrical alkynes, the more sterically bulky alkyne substituent generally ends up at the less hindered C-2 position of the indole, giving useful regioselectivity.
