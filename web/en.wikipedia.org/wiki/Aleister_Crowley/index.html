Aleister Crowley
Born: Edward Alexander Crowley, 12 October 1875, Leamington Spa, England
Died: 1 December 1947 (aged 72), Hastings
Occupations: Occultist, writer, mountaineer, artist
Known for: Founder of Thelema; author of The Book of the Law

Aleister Crowley (/ˈkroʊli/; born Edward Alexander Crowley; 12 October 1875 – 1 December 1947) was an English occultist, ceremonial magician, poet, novelist, mountaineer and artist. He founded the religion of Thelema, identifying himself as the prophet entrusted with guiding humanity into the Æon of Horus in the early 20th century.

Life

Born to a wealthy Plymouth Brethren family, Crowley rejected Christianity and studied at Trinity College, Cambridge. He joined the Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn in 1898, clashing with W. B. Yeats and S. L. MacGregor Mathers. As a mountaineer he attempted K2 (1902) and Kangchenjunga (1905).

In Cairo in 1904 he claimed his wife Rose channelled messages from a being named Aiwass, which he wrote down as The Book of the Law, the central scripture of Thelema, summarised by "Do what thou wilt shall be the whole of the Law" and "Love is the law, love under will." He founded the order A∴A∴ and later led the Ordo Templi Orientis (O.T.O.). From 1920 to 1923 he ran the Abbey of Thelema in Cefalù, Sicily, until expelled by Mussolini's government.

Reputation and influence

The British press dubbed him "the wickedest man in the world." His works on magick — including Magick in Theory and Practice and The Book of Thoth (with the Thoth tarot painted by Frieda Harris) — influenced modern occultism, Wicca, chaos magic and popular culture, from The Beatles' Sgt. Pepper cover to Led Zeppelin's Jimmy Page, who bought Crowley's former home Boleskine House on Loch Ness.

Death

Crowley died impoverished and addicted to heroin (originally prescribed for asthma) in Hastings in 1947.
