The Ainu language (Ainu: アイヌ・イタㇰ, Aynu itak) is the sole surviving member of the Ainu language family, spoken by the Ainu people, the indigenous inhabitants of northern Japan. It was historically spoken across Hokkaido, northern Honshu, the Kuril Islands, and the southern half of Sakhalin; today it survives mainly in Hokkaido and is classified as critically endangered.

Classification and dialects

Ainu is generally treated as a language isolate, though some scholars regard the historically distinct varieties — Hokkaido Ainu, Sakhalin Ainu, and Kuril Ainu — as separate languages. The Sakhalin and Kuril varieties are extinct, the last native speaker of Sakhalin Ainu having died in 1994. Mainland dialects are usually named for their districts, such as Saru, Ishikari, and Tokachi.

Features

Ainu is a polysynthetic, agglutinative language with subject–object–verb word order. It marks person and number on the verb, uses personal affixes rather than independent pronouns in many contexts, and has a rich system of verbal morphology including applicative and causative formations. Its phonology is relatively simple, with syllables of the form (C)V(C) and a pitch-accent system. Ainu has no traditional writing system; it is written today in a modified katakana syllabary or in the Latin alphabet.

Oral literature

Ainu oral tradition is famous for the yukar, long epic poems chanted by skilled performers, recorded in the early twentieth century by folklorists such as Kyōsuke Kindaichi and by Ainu authors including Yukie Chiri, whose Ainu shin'yōshū (1923) preserved epics of the gods (kamuy yukar).

Status and revitalization

Assimilation policies of the Meiji period and later discouraged Ainu language use, and fluent first-language speakers are now few, though estimates vary. Since the 1980s a revitalization movement has promoted Ainu-language classes, radio broadcasts, and dictionaries; the 1997 Ainu Culture Promotion Act and the 2019 Ainu Policy Promotion Act, which recognized the Ainu as an indigenous people of Japan, gave official support. The national Ainu museum and park Upopoy opened in Shiraoi in 2020.
