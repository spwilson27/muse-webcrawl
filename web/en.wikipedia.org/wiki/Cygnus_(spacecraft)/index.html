Cygnus (spacecraft)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cygnus (spacecraft)
ManufacturerNorthrop Grumman
OperatorNorthrop Grumman / NASA
First flight18 September 2013
Launch vehiclesAntares, Atlas V, Falcon 9
Cygnus approaches the ISS.

Cygnus is an expendable American cargo spacecraft developed by Orbital Sciences (now Northrop Grumman) under NASA's Commercial Orbital Transportation Services programme to resupply the International Space Station. Named for the swan constellation, it consists of a European-built pressurised cargo module and a service module with distinctive circular 'Ultraflex' solar arrays; it cannot return cargo, burning up on reentry like Progress.

Cygnus is grappled by the station's Canadarm2 and berthed — it does not dock itself. It has flown on three different rockets (its own Antares, and as guests on Atlas V and Falcon 9 after Antares groundings and redesigns), a flexibility unique among freighters.

Notable flights

The 2014 Orb-3 mission was destroyed seconds after launch when its Antares exploded — the programme recovered by buying Atlas rides. Cygnus vehicles are named for space pioneers (the 'S.S.' series), from Deke Slayton to Sally Ride and Kalpana Chawla; since 2022 an XL variant carries roughly five tonnes per flight.
