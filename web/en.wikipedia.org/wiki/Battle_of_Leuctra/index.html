Battle of Leuctra

Battle of Leuctra
Date: 371 BC
Location: Leuctra, Boeotia
Combatants: Thebes and Boeotian allies vs Sparta
Commanders: Epaminondas, Pelopidas vs King Cleombrotus I
Result: Decisive Theban victory; end of Spartan hegemony
[Image: The Theban oblique phalanx, massed fifty deep, advancing against the Spartan line]

The Battle of Leuctra (371 BC) was a decisive Theban victory over Sparta fought near the village of Leuctra in Boeotia. Won by the innovative tactics of Epaminondas, it shattered the myth of Spartan invincibility, ended Sparta's hegemony over Greece and raised Thebes to brief supremacy.

Background

After the Peloponnesian War, Sparta dominated Greece, but its occupation of Thebes' citadel (382 BC) and harsh diplomacy alienated the Greek world. Liberated by Pelopidas in 379 BC, Thebes rebuilt the Boeotian League and refused at a peace conference in 371 BC to swear except on behalf of all Boeotia. The Spartan king Cleombrotus was ordered to invade.

The battle

Outnumbered overall, Epaminondas inverted hoplite convention: he massed his best troops on the left wing in a column fifty shields deep — headed by the elite Sacred Band under Pelopidas — while refusing and echeloning back his weaker right, the first clear use of the oblique order. The deep Theban left smashed the Spartan right where the king and the Spartiate peers stood; Cleombrotus and some 400 of the 700 Spartiates present were killed, a demographic catastrophe for a city already short of full citizens. The rest of the Spartan army withdrew.

Aftermath

Epaminondas followed up by invading the Peloponnese and liberating Messenia, whose helots had supplied Sparta's wealth for centuries; the new city of Messene was founded in 369 BC. Sparta never recovered its power. Theban hegemony lasted until Epaminondas's death at Mantinea in 362 BC, and Leuctra's tactics influenced Philip II of Macedon, who had been a hostage in Thebes.
