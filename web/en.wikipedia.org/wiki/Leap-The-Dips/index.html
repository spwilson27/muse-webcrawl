Leap-The-Dips

Leap-The-Dips is a wooden roller coaster located at Lakemont Park in Altoona, Pennsylvania. Built in 1902 by the Federal Construction Company and designed by E. Joy Morris, it is widely regarded as the oldest operating roller coaster in the world and the last surviving example of a figure-eight side-friction coaster.

Design

Leap-The-Dips is a side-friction figure-eight coaster, an early design in which the cars run in a trough-like track and are kept in place by friction wheels running along the sides of the trough, limiting the steepness of the drops the ride can safely make. The coaster stands about 41 feet (12.5 m) tall, has a track roughly 1,452 feet (443 m) long, and reaches modest speeds of around 10 miles per hour (16 km/h). Its name refers to the series of gentle dips along the course.

History

The coaster opened in 1902, when Lakemont Park was a trolley park. It operated for much of the twentieth century but fell into disrepair and closed in 1985. A campaign led by local preservationists and the American Coaster Enthusiasts raised funds for its restoration, and the coaster reopened in 1999. It was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1996 and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places; it is also recognized as an ACE Coaster Landmark.

Significance

As the sole surviving side-friction figure-eight coaster still in operation, Leap-The-Dips is a rare intact example of the earliest generation of American wooden roller coasters, predating the up-stop wheels that made more extreme coaster designs possible.
