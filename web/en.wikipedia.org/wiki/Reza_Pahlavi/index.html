Reza Pahlavi

Reza Pahlavi (born 31 October 1960) is the eldest son of Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, the last Shah of Iran, and Farah Pahlavi. He was crown prince before the Iranian Revolution of 1979 and has since lived in exile, becoming a prominent advocate for a secular, democratic and nonviolent transition in Iran.

Early life

Reza Pahlavi spent his childhood in the Iranian imperial court and was formally proclaimed crown prince after his father’s coronation in 1967. As a teenager he trained as a pilot and went to the United States for flight training shortly before the monarchy collapsed. He later studied political science at the University of Southern California.

Exile

After the revolution, the Pahlavi family lived in several countries before Reza settled principally in the United States. His father died in Egypt in 1980. Supporters of the former monarchy regard Reza as head of the imperial house; he generally presents himself less as a claimant seeking automatic restoration than as a national figure supporting a future referendum on Iran’s system of government.

Political activity

Pahlavi has written and spoken in favor of civil disobedience, human rights, separation of religion and state, and a constituent assembly chosen by Iranians. He has attempted to build cooperation among opposition groups whose goals range from constitutional monarchy to a secular republic. His prominence rises during waves of protest in Iran, but the extent of organized support inside the country is difficult to measure and remains politically contested.

Personal life

He married Yasmine Etemad-Amini in 1986; they have three daughters. His siblings include Farahnaz, Ali Reza, who died in 2011, and Leila, who died in 2001.
