Matchbox (brand)

Matchbox is a British toy brand introduced by Lesney Products in 1953, and is now owned by the American toy company Mattel, Inc. The brand was so named because the original die-cast toy vehicles were sold in boxes similar in style and size to those in which matches were sold. Matchbox became widely popular and the name has been genericised in many languages to mean small toy cars, regardless of brand.

History

Lesney was founded by Leslie Smith and Rodney Smith (unrelated) in 1947. The Matchbox line began with small die-cast models, the first of which included a road roller and a cement mixer; a model of Queen Elizabeth II's coronation coach was an early hit in 1953. The "1-75" series, numbered 1 through 75, became the brand's core range for decades.

In 1968, Mattel introduced its rival Hot Wheels line with low-friction wheels and customised designs, cutting sharply into Matchbox sales. Lesney responded with the faster "Superfast" wheels in 1969 but continued to lose ground, and went into receivership in 1982. The brand passed to Universal Toys, then to Tyco Toys in 1992, and to Mattel in 1997, which has since operated Matchbox alongside Hot Wheels.

Products and collecting

Matchbox vehicles are typically smaller and more realistic than their Hot Wheels counterparts, modelling everyday cars, trucks, and construction vehicles, at scales around 1:64. The brand also produced larger models (such as the "Models of Yesteryear" range), kits, and slot cars. Early Lesney models in good condition with their boxes are highly sought by collectors.
