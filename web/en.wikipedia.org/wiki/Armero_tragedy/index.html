Armero tragedy


Armero after the lahars of 13 November 1985.

The Armero tragedy (Spanish: Tragedia de Armero) was one of the consequences of the eruption of the Nevado del Ruiz stratovolcano in Tolima, Colombia, on 13 November 1985. After 69 years of dormancy, the volcano's pyroclastic flows melted about 10 percent of its glacial cap, sending lahars (volcanic mudflows) down the river valleys at up to 60 kilometres per hour. The lahars destroyed the town of Armero, killing more than 20,000 of its almost 29,000 inhabitants, and killed roughly 23,000 people in total across the region. It remains the deadliest lahar and the fourth-deadliest volcanic eruption in recorded history.

Background

Nevado del Ruiz had erupted in 1595 and 1845, both times producing destructive mudflows down the Lagunilla River valley, where Armero was later built — directly on top of the old lahar deposits. The volcano began showing signs of unrest in late 1984, with increased fumarolic activity and small explosions. Scientists prepared hazard maps identifying Armero as being at risk from mudflows, and these were published in Colombian newspapers in October 1985, but no evacuation was ordered and local officials publicly reassured residents.

The eruption and mudflows

On the evening of 13 November 1985, a moderate explosive eruption began at 9:08 pm. Hot pyroclastic material melted the summit ice cap, and the meltwater mixed with ash and debris to form enormous lahars that swept down the Azufrado, Gualí and Lagunilla valleys. The first mudflow reached Armero, about 48 km from the summit, shortly after 11:30 pm. Successive waves, some several metres deep, buried most of the town. Because the disaster struck at night during a storm, and electricity and communications failed, few residents understood what was happening in time to escape to high ground.

Aftermath

Relief efforts were hampered by the destruction of roads and the choking mud, which made rescue by vehicle nearly impossible. The tragedy became known worldwide through the story of Omayra Sánchez, a 13-year-old girl trapped in the mud and debris of her destroyed home; despite rescue attempts broadcast on television, she died after some 60 hours, and photographs of her became a symbol of the disaster and of official failure.

The Colombian government was widely criticised for failing to act on scientific warnings. The disaster led to reforms in Colombia's disaster-management system and became a landmark case study in volcanology for the importance of hazard maps and evacuation planning. Armero was never rebuilt; the site is preserved as a memorial, and survivors were resettled in nearby towns such as Guayabal (renamed Armero-Guayabal) and Lérida.
