Frobenius theorem (differential topology)

In mathematics, Frobenius' theorem gives necessary and sufficient conditions for finding a maximal set of independent solutions of an overdetermined system of first-order homogeneous linear partial differential equations. In modern geometric terms, given a family of vector fields, the theorem tells whether there exists a foliation of the manifold by integral manifolds — submanifolds whose tangent spaces at every point are spanned by the given vector fields.

Statement

Let D be a smooth distribution of constant rank on a manifold M (an assignment of a subspace of each tangent space). The distribution is called involutive if, whenever two vector fields X and Y take values in D, their Lie bracket [X, Y] also takes values in D. The distribution is called integrable if through every point there passes an integral manifold of maximal dimension.

Frobenius' theorem states that a distribution is integrable if and only if it is involutive. Equivalently, in terms of differential forms, the ideal of forms annihilating the distribution must be closed under exterior differentiation.

History and generalisations

The theorem is named after Ferdinand Georg Frobenius, who proved a key version in 1877, building on work of Clebsch and Deahna; an assertion in one direction goes back to Jacobi. The result is fundamental in differential geometry and its applications: it underlies the theory of foliations, the integrability of almost complex structures' real analogues, and the solution of systems of PDEs by the method of characteristics. Infinite-dimensional and holomorphic versions of the theorem also hold under suitable hypotheses.
