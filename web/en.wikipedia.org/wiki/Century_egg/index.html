Century egg

A century egg (Chinese: 皮蛋, pídàn), also known as a preserved egg, hundred-year egg, or millennium egg, is a Chinese preserved food product and delicacy made by preserving duck, chicken, or quail eggs in a mixture of clay, ash, salt, quicklime, and rice hulls for several weeks to several months.

Characteristics

During curing, the alkaline mixture raises the egg's pH, transforming its appearance and flavour: the white becomes a dark brown or black translucent jelly with a pine-needle pattern (hence the Chinese name "pine-patterned egg"), and the yolk turns deep green or grey with a creamy texture and a strong flavour of ammonia and sulfur.

Use

Century eggs are eaten on their own with pickled ginger, sliced over tofu, or cooked into dishes such as century egg and lean pork congee, a classic Cantonese comfort food. Despite occasional notoriety in Western media, they are prized across China and in Chinese diaspora communities.
