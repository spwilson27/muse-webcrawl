Elizabeth I

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Armada Portrait of Elizabeth I, with the defeated Spanish fleet in the background]

Elizabeth I (7 September 1533 – 24 March 1603) was Queen of England and Ireland from 1558 until her death — the last Tudor monarch. Daughter of Henry VIII and Anne Boleyn, declared illegitimate after her mother's execution and imprisoned under her half-sister Mary I, she came to the throne at twenty-five and reigned for forty-four years. Her reign, the Elizabethan era, is celebrated for the defeat of the Spanish Armada (1588), the religious settlement that shaped the Church of England, the growth of English sea power and colonisation ventures, and the flowering of drama in the age of Shakespeare and Marlowe.

Rule

Elizabeth governed with a small Privy Council dominated by William Cecil, Lord Burghley, balancing factions and — famously — refusing every marriage proposal while using her hand as a diplomatic instrument. The Religious Settlement of 1559 established a moderately Protestant national church. Catholic plots in favour of Mary, Queen of Scots — executed in 1587 after nineteen years of captivity in England — papal excommunication (1570) and war with Spain from 1585 formed the backdrop to the Armada's failure in 1588.

Later years and legacy

The 1590s brought war costs, poor harvests, the Essex rebellion (1601) and a gruelling war in Ireland. Dying childless in 1603, Elizabeth was succeeded peaceably by James VI of Scotland, uniting the crowns. Her image — Gloriana, the Virgin Queen — was carefully manufactured in portraits, progresses and Tilts, and her reign was later romanticised as a golden age; modern historians add the caveats of religious persecution, Irish devastation and fiscal strain.
