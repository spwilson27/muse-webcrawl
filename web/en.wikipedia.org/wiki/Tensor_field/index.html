Tensor field

In mathematics and physics, a tensor field assigns a tensor to each point of a space — typically a Euclidean space or a manifold — in a way that varies smoothly (or piecewise smoothly) from point to point. Tensor fields generalise scalar fields and vector fields, which assign scalars and vectors respectively.

Definition

Formally, a tensor field of type (r, s) on a manifold M is a section of the tensor bundle formed from tangent and cotangent bundles. In coordinates, its components are functions of position that transform tensorially under coordinate changes.

Applications

Tensor fields are fundamental in differential geometry and physics: the metric tensor field defines lengths and angles on a manifold, the stress tensor in continuum mechanics and the electromagnetic tensor vary through space, and in general relativity the curvature of spacetime is described by the Riemann curvature tensor field.
