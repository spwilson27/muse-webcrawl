Richard F. Heck

Richard Fred Heck
Born: 15 August 1931, Springfield, Massachusetts
Died: 9 October 2015, Manila, Philippines
Known for: The Heck reaction
Award: Nobel Prize in Chemistry (2010)

Richard Fred Heck (August 15, 1931 – October 9, 2015) was an American chemist noted for the discovery and development of the Heck reaction, which uses palladium to catalyse organic chemical reactions coupling aryl halides with alkenes. He shared the 2010 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Ei-ichi Negishi and Akira Suzuki "for palladium-catalyzed cross couplings in organic synthesis."

Education and career

Heck studied at the University of California, Los Angeles, earning his PhD in 1954 under Saul Winstein, followed by postdoctoral work at ETH Zurich and UCLA. He worked at the Hercules Powder Company in Delaware from 1957, where his research on palladium chemistry began, and in 1971 joined the University of Delaware, where he remained until retiring in 1989 as Willis F. Harrington Professor Emeritus.

The Heck reaction

In a series of papers in the late 1960s and 1970s, Heck showed that palladium catalysts allow carbon–carbon bonds to be formed between aryl or vinyl halides and alkenes. The reaction became one of the most widely used tools in organic synthesis, applied in the manufacture of pharmaceuticals, agrochemicals and advanced materials, and laid the groundwork for the modern field of cross-coupling chemistry.

Later life

Heck spent his retirement in the Philippines with his wife Socorro, living quietly and largely out of the public eye until the Nobel Prize brought renewed attention in 2010. He died in Manila in 2015, aged 84.
