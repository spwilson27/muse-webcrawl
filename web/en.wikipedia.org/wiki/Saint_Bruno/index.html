Saint Bruno

Bruno of Cologne (c. 1030 – 6 October 1101), known as Saint Bruno, was a German priest and scholar who founded the Carthusian Order, one of the strictest contemplative orders of the Catholic Church. Born in Cologne and educated at Reims, he became chancellor of the Reims diocese and a celebrated teacher — among his pupils was the future Pope Urban II.

Foundation of the Grande Chartreuse

After a dispute with the corrupt Archbishop of Reims, Bruno withdrew from public life. In 1084, with six companions and the support of Bishop Hugh of Grenoble — who is said to have dreamed of seven stars marking the place — he settled in the remote Chartreuse mountains north of Grenoble, building the hermitage that became the Grande Chartreuse, mother house of the Carthusians. The monks lived as hermits in individual cells, meeting for prayer and shared meals only on certain days, in silence and austerity: a pattern codified in the Consuetudines of his successor Guigo I, summed up in the order's motto Stat crux dum volvitur orbis — "the cross stands while the world turns".

Later life

Called to Rome in 1090 as adviser to Urban II, Bruno longed for solitude and obtained leave to found a second hermitage, Santa Maria della Torre, in Calabria, where he died in 1101. He was canonised in 1623; his feast day is 6 October. The order he founded survives famously in the 2005 documentary film Into Great Silence and in the Chartreuse liqueur made by its monks.
