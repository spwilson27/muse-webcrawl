Nemi ships

The Nemi ships were two enormous Roman vessels built on Lake Nemi in Italy during the reign of the emperor Caligula (AD 37–41). Among the largest ships of the ancient world — roughly 70 metres long — they are thought to have served as floating palaces or as temple barges connected with the cult of Diana at Nemi.

Recovery

The wrecks, lying in the volcanic lake, attracted salvage attempts from the Renaissance onward. Between 1928 and 1932, under the Fascist government of Benito Mussolini, the lake was partially drained with pumps, allowing archaeologists to recover both hulls. They were housed in a purpose-built museum on the lakeshore. The ships revealed advanced Roman technology, including lead-sheathed hulls, plumbing, mosaics, and ingenious anchor and bearing designs.

Destruction

On the night of 31 May–1 June 1944, during the Second World War, a fire destroyed the museum and both ships. Responsibility for the fire remains disputed. Only bronzes, timbers removed earlier, and artefacts in other museums survive; a full-scale reconstruction of part of one ship is displayed at the Museo delle Navi Romane in Nemi.
