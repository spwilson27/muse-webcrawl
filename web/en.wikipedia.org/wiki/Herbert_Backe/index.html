Herbert Friedrich Wilhelm Backe (1 May 1896 – 6 April 1947) was a German Nazi politician and SS-Obergruppenführer. As State Secretary in the Reich Ministry of Food and Agriculture and, from 1942, Reich Minister, he devised the Hunger Plan, the programme of deliberately starving tens of millions of people in the German-occupied Soviet Union so that German troops and the German home front could be fed from seized Soviet food supplies.

Early life

Backe was born in Batumi in the Russian Empire to a German merchant family, and attended the German gymnasium in Tiflis. Interned as an enemy alien during the First World War, he reached Germany after the war and studied agronomy in Göttingen, writing a doctorate on the Russian grain economy. He joined the Nazi Party and the SA in the 1920s, later transferring to the SS, and became a close collaborator of Agriculture Minister Richard Walther Darré, the regime's "blood and soil" ideologue.

Hunger Plan

Appointed State Secretary in 1933, Backe effectively ran the ministry as Darré's influence faded. In the spring of 1941, ahead of Operation Barbarossa, Backe drew up the economic guidelines for the invasion: food from the Soviet "surplus" zones (Ukraine and southern Russia) was to be diverted wholesale to Germany, while the urban "deficit" zones of the north and centre were to be cut off entirely. Meeting notes from May 1941 state with bureaucratic calm that "umpteen million people" would thereby starve. In practice the plan contributed to the deaths of millions of Soviet civilians and prisoners of war, including the starvation of Leningrad's population and some 3.3 million Soviet POWs.

Minister and downfall

Backe replaced Darré as Reich Minister of Food and Agriculture in 1942 (formally confirmed 1944) and kept German rations comparatively stable deep into the war — at the direct expense of occupied Europe. Arrested by the Allies in 1945, he was to be tried in the subsequent Nuremberg "Ministries Trial," but hanged himself in his cell at Nuremberg on 6 April 1947.
