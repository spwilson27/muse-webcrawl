PDVSA


Full name: Petróleos de Venezuela, S.A. Founded: 1976. Headquarters: Caracas, Venezuela. Owner: Government of Venezuela.

Petróleos de Venezuela, S.A. (PDVSA) is the Venezuelan state-owned oil and natural gas company. Created on 1 January 1976 following the nationalization of Venezuela's oil industry, it has for most of its history been the economic engine of the country, which sits atop the world's largest proven crude oil reserves, concentrated in the Orinoco Belt.

History

PDVSA absorbed the operations of foreign concessionaires such as Creole (Exxon), Shell, and Gulf under the 1976 nationalization law. Through the 1980s and 1990s it was regarded as one of the most professionally managed national oil companies, expanding internationally — most notably by acquiring the U.S. refiner Citgo. During the 1990s apertura petrolera ("oil opening"), it invited foreign participation in marginal fields and Orinoco heavy-oil projects.

Strike and politicization

After President Hugo Chávez moved to assert political control over the company, a national strike in 2002–2003 led to the dismissal of roughly 18,000–19,000 PDVSA employees — about half the workforce. Thereafter the company took on extensive social-spending roles funding government "missions," while investment and maintenance lagged.

Decline and sanctions

Venezuelan crude production, around 3 million barrels per day in the late 1990s, fell steeply in the 2010s amid mismanagement, corruption scandals, accidents such as the 2012 Amuay refinery explosion, and, from 2017–2019, financial and oil sanctions imposed by the United States. Production dropped below 1 million barrels per day around 2020 before partially recovering.
