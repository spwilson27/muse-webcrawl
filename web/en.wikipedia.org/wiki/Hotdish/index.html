Hotdish

A hotdish is a casserole-style dish of the Upper Midwest United States, typically containing a starch, meat and canned soup.

A hotdish (or hot dish) is a dish that typically combines a starch, a meat and a canned or condensed soup, baked together in a single baking dish and served hot at the table. It is a mainstay of the cuisine of the Upper Midwest of the United States — especially Minnesota, North Dakota, South Dakota and Wisconsin — where it is a fixture of church suppers, potlucks and family gatherings. Elsewhere in the US the same kind of dish is generally called a casserole (the term used for both the food and its pan).

Composition

The classic formula, sometimes summarised as "a can, a can, a can and a pound", pairs ground beef or another meat with canned cream of mushroom (or celery) soup, canned vegetables such as green beans or corn, and a starch. The best-known version, tater tot hotdish, tops the mixture with a layer of frozen potato nuggets that crisp as they bake; others use egg noodles, rice, chow mein noodles or macaroni.

History and culture

Hotdish emerged in the early twentieth century as an economical way to stretch meat for farm families and church congregations; one of the earliest printed recipes appeared in a 1930 Grace Lutheran Ladies Aid cookbook in Mankato, Minnesota. It became an emblem of Minnesotan identity — celebrated at community contests and, since 2011, in an annual hotdish competition among Minnesota's congressional delegation in Washington. The dish is closely associated with Scandinavian-American and German-American foodways of the region.
