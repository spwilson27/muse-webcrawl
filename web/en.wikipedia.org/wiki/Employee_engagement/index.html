Employee engagement

Employee engagement is a fundamental concept in the effort to understand and describe, both qualitatively and quantitatively, the nature of the relationship between an organization and its employees. An "engaged employee" is defined as one who is fully absorbed by and enthusiastic about their work and so takes positive action to further the organization's reputation and interests.

Origins and definitions

The term was popularized by the Gallup Organization, building on the academic work of William Kahn, whose 1990 ethnographic study described personal engagement as the harnessing of employees' selves to their work roles—physically, cognitively, and emotionally. Related academic constructs include job involvement, organizational commitment, and work engagement as measured by the Utrecht Work Engagement Scale (vigour, dedication, and absorption).

Measurement and business claims

Engagement is typically measured through employee surveys, such as Gallup's Q12 questionnaire. Consulting firms report correlations between high engagement scores and outcomes such as productivity, profitability, retention, safety, and customer satisfaction. Organizations attempt to raise engagement through leadership development, recognition, career growth opportunities, communication, and job design.

Criticism

Scholars have criticized the construct for overlapping with established concepts like job satisfaction and commitment, for inconsistent definitions between commercial providers, and for claims of causality resting on correlational survey evidence. Others argue engagement programmes can shift responsibility for systemic workplace problems onto individual employees. Despite criticism, engagement remains a staple of human-resource management practice.
