Pulmonary valve
LatinValva trunci pulmonalis
LocationBetween the right ventricle and the pulmonary trunk
TypeSemilunar valve (three cusps)

The pulmonary valve (or pulmonic valve) is the semilunar valve of the heart that lies between the right ventricle and the pulmonary artery, guarding the entrance to the pulmonary circulation. It has three cusps — the anterior, left, and right semilunar cusps.

Function

During ventricular systole the valve opens as pressure in the right ventricle exceeds that in the pulmonary artery, allowing blood to be ejected toward the lungs. When the ventricle relaxes, pressure in the pulmonary trunk exceeds ventricular pressure, the cusps fill with blood and snap shut, preventing regurgitation into the right ventricle. In the normal heart, the pulmonary valve opens after the tricuspid valve's closure produces the first heart sound, and its closure contributes to the pulmonary component of the second heart sound (P2), which is normally later than the aortic component (A2) during inspiration because of increased venous return.

Clinical significance

Pulmonary valve stenosis — narrowing of the valve, most often congenital — forces the right ventricle to work harder and can produce a characteristic systolic ejection murmur at the upper left sternal border. It is commonly treated by balloon valvuloplasty. Pulmonary regurgitation is usually secondary to pulmonary hypertension or to surgical repair of tetralogy of Fallot, and is often tolerated for years. Infective endocarditis of the pulmonary valve is rare compared with left-sided valves. A bicuspid pulmonary valve is uncommon.
