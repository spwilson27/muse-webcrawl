The Sikorsky CH-148 Cyclone is a twin-engine, multi-role maritime helicopter developed by Sikorsky Aircraft for the Canadian Armed Forces. A militarized derivative of the Sikorsky S-92, it is operated by the Royal Canadian Air Force from Halifax-class frigates and other vessels, replacing the long-serving CH-124 Sea King in anti-submarine warfare, anti-surface warfare, and search-and-rescue-support roles.

Development

Canada's Maritime Helicopter Project, launched to replace the Sea King (in service since 1963), selected the Sikorsky H-92 Superhawk in July 2004, with a contract for 28 aircraft. Development of the extensively redesigned CH-148 — with folding main rotor and tail, fly-by-wire controls, composite blades, and a full mission suite including radar, electro-optical/infrared turret, electronic support measures, sonobuoys, dipping sonar, and torpedoes — proved protracted, with deliveries repeatedly delayed and contractual disputes between Canada and Sikorsky. The first Cyclone was formally delivered in 2015, and the type made its first operational deployment aboard HMCS Calgary in 2018.

Accident

On April 29, 2020, a CH-148 (serial 148822) from HMCS Fredericton crashed into the Ionian Sea during a NATO exercise, killing all six Canadian Armed Forces members aboard. The investigation found that the flight control system's electronic control laws could command an unanticipated maneuver during a turn under certain conditions; software changes and procedural mitigations followed.

Characteristics

The Cyclone is powered by two General Electric CT7-8A engines (a version of the T700 family), carries a crew of four (two pilots, a tactical coordinator, and a sensor operator), and can carry Mk 46 torpedoes or door-mounted machine guns, plus up to 22 passengers in a utility configuration. Its fly-by-wire system and composite airframe distinguish it from the civil S-92.
