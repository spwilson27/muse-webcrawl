Heaven Lake

Heaven Lake (Korean: 천지, Ch'ŏnji; Chinese: 天池, Tiānchí) is a crater lake on the border between China and North Korea. It lies within a caldera atop the volcanic Paektu Mountain, the highest peak of the Changbai Mountains. The lake is the source of the Songhua, Tumen and Yalu rivers.

Heaven Lake occupies the summit caldera of Paektu Mountain.

Geography

The lake sits at an elevation of about 2,189 metres above sea level, making it one of the highest crater lakes in the world. Its surface covers approximately 9.82 square kilometres, and with a maximum depth of around 384 metres it is also among the deepest alpine lakes. The caldera was formed by the colossal "Millennium Eruption" of Paektu Mountain in 946 CE, one of the largest volcanic eruptions of the last 5,000 years, which expelled an enormous volume of ash as far as northern Japan.

Name and significance

The lake is sacred in Korean culture, where Paektu Mountain (Baekdu) is regarded as the ancestral home of the Korean people and, in Korea mythology, the birthplace of Dangun, the legendary founder of the first Korean kingdom. In North Korea the mountain and lake feature prominently in state symbolism, and the lake was officially claimed as the birthplace of Kim Jong Il. In China the mountain is called Changbai and the lake Tianchi ("Heavenly Lake").

Lake Tianchi monster

Like Loch Ness in Scotland, Heaven Lake has a longstanding legend of a lake monster, the "Tianchi monster", with reported sightings and blurry photographs occasionally reported by visitors and Chinese media.

Access

The Chinese side, within the Changbaishan National Nature Reserve in Jilin Province, is developed for tourism, with viewing platforms above the lake. The North Korean side, in Ryanggang Province, is far less accessible to foreign visitors.
