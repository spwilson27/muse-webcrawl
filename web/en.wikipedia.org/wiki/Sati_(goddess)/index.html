Sati (Sanskrit: Satī, "the virtuous one"), also known as Dakshayani, is a Hindu goddess of marital devotion and longevity, and the first consort of the god Shiva. She is regarded as an incarnation of the supreme goddess Adi Shakti and is the daughter of the priest-king Daksha. Her story is told in the Mahabharata, the Puranas and later devotional literature.

Sati, daughter of Daksha and first wife of Shiva, whose story frames the origin of the Shakti Peethas.

Legend

Against her father's wishes, Sati married the ascetic Shiva, whom Daksha despised. When Daksha held a great sacrifice (yajna) and deliberately excluded Shiva, Sati attended uninvited to protest the insult and, in rage and humiliation, immolated herself in the sacrificial fire. Shiva's fury at her death led him to destroy Daksha's sacrifice; carrying Sati's body across the cosmos, he dismembered it (or Vishnu did, in some versions), and the places where her body parts fell became the Shakti Peethas, major shrines of goddess worship. Sati was later reborn as Parvati, daughter of the Himalayas, who again won Shiva as her husband.

Cultural note

The goddess's self-immolation is the mythological origin claimed for the historical practice of sati — the immolation of widows on their husbands' funeral pyres — although the myth predates secure evidence of the practice and the rite was outlawed in India in the 19th century and remains prohibited.
