François Truffaut

Born6 February 1932, Paris, France
Died21 October 1984, Neuilly-sur-Seine, France
OccupationFilm director, critic, actor
MovementFrench New Wave

François Roland Truffaut (6 February 1932 – 21 October 1984) was a French filmmaker, critic and actor who became one of the founders of the French New Wave movement. His career as a director spanned more than a quarter of a century and he remains one of France's most celebrated filmmakers.

Films

Truffaut's debut feature, The 400 Blows (1959), introduced his alter ego Antoine Doinel, played by Jean-Pierre Léaud, a character he revisited in several films. Other notable works include Shoot the Piano Player (1960), Jules and Jim (1962), Day for Night (1973), which won the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film, and The Last Metro (1980).

Criticism

Before directing, Truffaut wrote for Cahiers du Cinéma, where his 1954 essay "A Certain Tendency of the French Cinema" attacked the "Tradition of Quality" and laid the groundwork for the auteur theory.
