Saturn Corporation

The Saturn Corporation was an American automobile manufacturer and brand, a subsidiary of General Motors established on January 7, 1985, as "a different kind of car company." It was created to compete with Japanese imports in the small-car market and operated with unusual autonomy from the rest of GM.

History

Saturn built a dedicated plant in Spring Hill, Tennessee, and launched its first cars, the S-Series, for the 1991 model year. The brand was noted for its no-haggle pricing, friendly dealerships, dent-resistant polymer body panels, and a cooperative labour agreement with the UAW. Later models included the L-Series, the Vue SUV, the Ion, the Sky roadster, the Aura sedan, and the Outlook crossover, increasingly based on shared GM platforms rather than unique Saturn engineering.

Discontinuation

During GM's 2009 bankruptcy restructuring, Saturn was put up for sale; a planned sale to Penske Automotive Group fell through in September 2009, and GM wound the brand down, with the last Saturn vehicles produced in 2009 and the franchise terminated by October 31, 2010. About 3.5 million Saturns were built in total.
