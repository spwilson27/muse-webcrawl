The Guy Medal is awarded by the Royal Statistical Society in three categories: Gold, Silver and Bronze. Silver and Bronze are awarded annually, while Gold is awarded more rarely, roughly every three years. The medals are named after William Guy, a British physician and statistician who bequeathed funds to the society in his will.

The Guy Medal in Gold is the society's highest honour and is awarded to fellows or others judged to have made a signal mark on the theory or practice of statistics through contributions of exceptional merit. The Silver medal is typically awarded to fellows for a paper, or series of papers, of special merit communicated to the society. The Bronze medal, introduced in 1982, recognises work by younger researchers and is generally restricted to fellows under the age of 35.

History

William Augustus Guy (1810–1885) was an English physician, medical statistician and fellow of the Royal Statistical Society. In his will he left a bequest to the society, which used the funds to establish medals in his name. The first Guy Medal in Silver was awarded in 1892 to John Glover, and the first Gold medal was awarded in 1892 to Charles Booth, in recognition of his pioneering survey work on the life and labour of the people of London.

The medals quickly became among the most prestigious awards in statistics. Gold medallists include many of the defining figures of the discipline, including Francis Galton, Karl Pearson, R. A. Fisher, Jerzy Neyman, Maurice Kendall, David Cox and Peter Whittle.

Categories

Gold

The Guy Medal in Gold is awarded for lifetime achievement of the highest distinction. It is not necessarily awarded every year; the society's council confers it only when a candidate of sufficient stature is identified, typically about once every three years.

Silver

The Guy Medal in Silver is awarded annually, usually for a specific paper or body of work presented to the society and published in its journal. Recipients need not be fellows of the society.

Bronze

The Guy Medal in Bronze was instituted to encourage younger statisticians. It is awarded for excellent work presented to the society or published in its journals, and eligibility is generally limited to researchers in the early stages of their careers.
