Kusile Power Station is a coal-fired power plant in Mpumalanga, South Africa, built by the state utility Eskom. With six 800 MW supercritical units giving a total installed capacity of 4,800 MW, it is one of the largest coal-fired power stations in the world. Kusile was the first power station in Africa to install flue-gas desulphurisation (FGD) technology, in line with World Bank lending conditions, substantially reducing sulphur dioxide emissions.

Construction

Construction began in 2008, alongside the sister station Medupi. The project — originally planned for completion around 2014 at a fraction of its final cost — suffered severe delays and cost overruns caused by design changes, labour unrest, contractor disputes, and technical defects, with costs rising to well over R200 billion. The first unit was synchronised to the grid in 2016 and entered commercial operation in 2017; the remaining units were progressively commissioned into the 2020s, with the final unit reaching commercial operation in the mid-2020s.

Incidents

In October 2022 the flue-gas duct of Unit 1 collapsed, and Units 2 and 3, which shared the chimney structure, were also taken offline, removing over 2,000 MW from the national grid during South Africa's load shedding crisis. Temporary bypass stacks were constructed to return the units to service while permanent repairs proceeded.

Environmental concerns

The station sits in the already heavily polluted Mpumalanga Highveld. Environmental groups have criticised its emissions and water use; Kusile uses air-cooled condensers to limit water consumption, and its FGD system is intended to meet stricter air-quality standards than older Eskom stations.
