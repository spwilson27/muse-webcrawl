Robert Borden


Born: June 26, 1854, Grand-Pré, Nova Scotia
Died: June 10, 1937, Ottawa, Ontario
Office: 8th Prime Minister of Canada (1911–1920)

Sir Robert Laird Borden (June 26, 1854 – June 10, 1937) was a Canadian lawyer and politician who served as the eighth prime minister of Canada from 1911 to 1920, leading the country through the First World War.

Political career

A successful Halifax lawyer, Borden entered Parliament in 1896 and became leader of the Conservative Party in 1901. He won the 1911 election — the "reciprocity election" — by opposing a free-trade agreement with the United States. As prime minister he nationalised failing railways, introduced income tax as a "temporary" war measure, and built up Canada's war effort, committing some 620,000 Canadians to service.

First World War and legacy

The conscription crisis of 1917 split the country along linguistic lines; Borden formed a Union Government and won re-election that year. He pressed successfully for Canada's separate signature at the Treaty of Versailles and membership in the League of Nations, milestones in Canadian autonomy. Exhausted and ill, he retired in 1920 and was succeeded by Arthur Meighen. Borden's portrait appears on the Canadian $100 bill.
