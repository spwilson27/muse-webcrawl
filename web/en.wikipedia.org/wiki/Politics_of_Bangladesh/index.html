The politics of Bangladesh take place within a unitary parliamentary republic. The President is head of state, while the Prime Minister, as head of government, exercises executive power. Legislative authority rests with the unicameral Jatiya Sangsad (National Parliament), with 350 seats, 50 of them reserved for women.

The Jatiya Sangsad Bhaban in Dhaka, designed by Louis Kahn.

History

Bangladesh emerged as an independent state in 1971 after the Liberation War against Pakistan. Its early years saw one-party rule under Sheikh Mujibur Rahman, his assassination in 1975, and periods of military government under Ziaur Rahman and Hussain Muhammad Ershad. Parliamentary democracy was restored in 1991, and power alternated between the Awami League and the Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP), led respectively by Sheikh Hasina and Khaleda Zia.

Recent developments

Sheikh Hasina's Awami League won successive elections in 2008, 2014, 2018, and January 2024, amid opposition boycotts and allegations of authoritarian drift. In July–August 2024, a mass student-led uprising against a job-quota system escalated into a nationwide movement; Hasina resigned and fled the country on 5 August 2024. An interim government headed by Nobel laureate Muhammad Yunus as Chief Adviser was sworn in, pledging institutional reforms and fresh elections.

Political parties

Major parties include the Awami League, the Bangladesh Nationalist Party, the Jatiya Party, and Jamaat-e-Islami (whose registration has been contested), alongside a range of smaller leftist and regional parties.
