Isma'il Pasha

Isma'il Pasha (Arabic: إسماعيل باشا; 31 December 1830 – 2 March 1895), also known as Ismail the Magnificent, was the Khedive of Egypt and conqueror of Sudan from 1863 to 1879, when he was removed at the behest of Britain and France. He was the grandson of Muhammad Ali Pasha.

Reign

Educated in Paris, Isma'il pursued an ambitious programme of modernisation: he completed the Suez Canal (opened 1869), expanded railways, telegraphs, schools and cotton production, and extensively rebuilt Cairo, famously aspiring to make it a "Paris on the Nile". He obtained from the Ottoman sultan the title of Khedive in 1867. His expansion into Sudan and a costly war against Ethiopia deepened Egypt's debts.

Deposition

Egypt's mounting foreign debt led to the sale of its Suez Canal shares to Britain in 1875 and to Anglo-French financial control. In 1879 the European powers pressured the sultan to depose Isma'il in favour of his son Tewfik. Isma'il lived in exile in Naples and later Constantinople, where he died in 1895.
