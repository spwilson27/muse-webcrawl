Zion

Zion (Hebrew: צִיּוֹן, Ṣīyyōn) is a placename in the Hebrew Bible, often used as a synonym for Jerusalem and for the Land of Israel as a whole. The word is found in texts dating from the sixth century BCE. Originally the name of a specific hill in Jerusalem — Mount Zion — it came to denote the city, the Temple site, and by extension the Jewish homeland and, in later religious usage, an idealised or heavenly realm.

Etymology and biblical usage

The etymology of "Zion" is uncertain; proposed roots include a Semitic word meaning a dry or parched place, or a fortress. In the Bible, Zion first appears as the stronghold captured by David, which became the "City of David". After Solomon built the Temple on the adjacent hill, the name's referent expanded to include the Temple Mount, then Jerusalem as a whole, and poetically the people of Israel — as in the phrase "Daughter of Zion". In the Psalms and prophets, Zion is portrayed as the dwelling place of God and the focus of eschatological hope.

Later usage

In Jewish tradition Zion symbolises longing for the homeland, a theme central to prayer and to the modern movement of Zionism, which took its name from the term. In Christianity, "Zion" can denote the heavenly Jerusalem or the church. The term also holds significance in the Rastafari movement, where Zion represents a promised land (often associated with Ethiopia) in contrast to Babylon. Numerous churches, settlements, and geographic features around the world — including Zion National Park in Utah — bear the name.
