Vytautas (c. 1350 – 27 October 1430), also known as Vytautas the Great (Lithuanian: Vytautas Didysis), was a ruler of the Grand Duchy of Lithuania. He was also the Prince of Grodno (1370–1382), Prince of Lutsk (1382–1401), and the postulated king of the Hussites. One of the most famous rulers of medieval Lithuania, Vytautas brought the Grand Duchy to the height of its territorial expansion — stretching from the Baltic Sea to the Black Sea — and presided over its Christianisation and cultural flourishing.

Rise to power

Vytautas was the son of Kęstutis, co-ruler of Lithuania. In the civil war of 1381–84 he fought against his cousin Jogaila, was imprisoned in Kreva Castle, escaped in 1382 (legend says disguised in women's clothing), and twice allied with the Teutonic Knights against Jogaila before reconciling. By the Ostrów Agreement (1392) he became Grand Duke under Jogaila's overlordship.

Battle of Grunwald

Together with Jogaila — now King Władysław II Jagiełło of Poland — Vytautas commanded the Lithuanian forces at the Battle of Grunwald (15 July 1410), one of the largest battles of medieval Europe, decisively defeating the Teutonic Order. The victory ended the Knights' expansion into Lithuania; the subsequent Treaty of Thorn restored Samogitia to Lithuania.

Eastern expansion and later years

Vytautas expanded Lithuanian influence east and south, campaigning toward Moscow and the steppe; after an early defeat at the Vorskla River (1399), he secured the Black Sea coast and allied with and against steppe powers. In 1429 the Congress of Lutsk and Emperor Sigismund proposed crowning him King of Lithuania, but he died at Trakai on 27 October 1430 before the crown could arrive.

Legacy

Vytautas is Lithuania's foremost national hero; his name adorns Vytautas Magnus University in Kaunas, and his cult was revived during the interwar independence period. Under his rule, Lithuania became a multi-ethnic, multi-confessional state with a flowering of written culture.
