Mohammad Reza Shajarian

Mohammad-Reza Shajarian (23 September 1940 – 8 October 2020) was an Iranian singer and master (ostad) of Persian traditional music. Universally regarded as the greatest singer in the history of Iranian classical music, he was celebrated for his command of the vocal radif repertoire, his settings of classical Persian poetry, and his influence on generations of Iranian musicians.

Life and career

Born in Mashhad, Shajarian first learned Quranic recitation from his father, a noted reciter. He began singing for Radio Khorasan in 1959 and moved to Tehran in the 1960s, studying with masters including Ahmad Ebadi and Abdollah Davami, from whom he learned the old vocal radif. His breakthrough came with Radio Iran's Golha programmes, and in the 1970s he became the pre-eminent voice of Persian classical song.

Artistry and legacy

Shajarian's singing set the poetry of Hafez, Rumi, Saadi, and Omar Khayyam, as well as modern poets. His albums — including Bidad, Dastan, and collaborations with Hossein Alizadeh, Kayhan Kalhor and his son Homayoun Shajarian — are landmarks of the genre. With the Masters of Persian Music ensemble he toured internationally. He received numerous honours, including UNESCO's Picasso Award and Mozart Medal, and was nominated for two Grammy Awards. After he supported the 2009 Green Movement protests, state media largely boycotted his work, though his popularity never waned. His Ramadan prayer Rabbana remains an iconic part of Iranian culture. He died of kidney cancer in Tehran in 2020 and was buried in Mashhad beside the tomb of the poet Ferdowsi; vast crowds mourned him across Iran.
