Cupellation

Cupellation
ProcessMetallurgical refining / assaying
MaterialsLead, bone-ash cupel, noble metals
Known sinceBronze Age

Cupellation is a refining and assaying process in metallurgy in which ores or alloyed metals are treated under very high temperatures in a shallow porous vessel called a cupel, traditionally made of bone ash. The sample is alloyed with lead and heated in an oxidising blast: the lead oxidises to litharge (lead oxide), carrying base-metal impurities with it, and the molten litharge is absorbed into the porous cupel, leaving behind a bead (the doré) of noble metals — gold and silver — that do not oxidise.

History and use

Cupellation is among the oldest metallurgical techniques, practised since the Bronze Age and widely used from Roman times for extracting silver from lead ores and for testing the purity of precious metals. As fire assay, it remained the standard method of determining gold and silver content for centuries and is still used as a reference method in modern assay laboratories, because of its precision. The process enabled large-scale silver production in the ancient and medieval worlds and, later, in the Spanish Americas.
