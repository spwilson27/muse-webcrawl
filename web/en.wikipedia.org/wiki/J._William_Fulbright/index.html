James William Fulbright (April 9, 1905 – February 9, 1995) was an American politician, academic, and statesman who represented Arkansas in the United States Senate from 1945 until his resignation in 1974. As chairman of the Senate Foreign Relations Committee from 1959 to 1974 — the longest tenure in the committee's history — he became one of the most influential figures in American foreign policy during the Cold War. He is best known as the founder of the Fulbright Program, the international academic exchange scheme established in 1946.

Early life and education

Fulbright was born in Sumner, Missouri, and raised in Fayetteville, Arkansas, where his family owned several businesses, including the local newspaper. He attended the University of Arkansas, where he was a star football player, and in 1925 won a Rhodes Scholarship to Pembroke College, Oxford. After legal studies at George Washington University, he joined the faculty of the University of Arkansas School of Law and in 1939, at age 34, became president of the University of Arkansas — one of the youngest university presidents in the country.

Congressional career

Fulbright was elected to the U.S. House of Representatives in 1942 and to the Senate in 1944. In 1949 he proposed the creation of the Senate Foreign Relations Committee chairmanship he would later hold. He was the only senator to vote against an appropriation for the Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations directed by Joseph McCarthy, and was its prominent critic. In 1961 he famously warned President John F. Kennedy against the Bay of Pigs invasion in a prescient memorandum.

Vietnam War opposition

Although Fulbright helped steer the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution through the Senate in 1964, he became the most prominent congressional critic of the Vietnam War. His 1966 book The Arrogance of Power dissected the assumptions of American interventionism, and the televised Senate hearings he chaired in 1966 and 1971 gave the antiwar movement a national platform. His break with President Lyndon B. Johnson defined the widening domestic debate over the war.

Legacy

The Fulbright Program, funded initially by the sale of surplus war property, has supported hundreds of thousands of students, scholars, and teachers in exchanges between the United States and more than 160 countries, and is widely considered one of the most prestigious academic exchange programs in the world. Fulbright lost his Senate seat in the 1974 Democratic primary to Dale Bumpers and resigned shortly thereafter. He was awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 1993.
