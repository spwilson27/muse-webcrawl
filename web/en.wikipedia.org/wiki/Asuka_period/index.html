The Asuka period (飛鳥時代, Asuka jidai) was a period in the history of Japan lasting from 538 to 710 (or 592 to 645), although its beginning could be said to overlap with the preceding Kofun period. The Yamato polity evolved greatly during the Asuka period, named after the Asuka region, south of modern Nara, where a series of imperial capitals were established.

The period is marked by significant artistic, social, and political transformations, including the arrival of Buddhism from China by way of the Korean kingdom of Baekje, traditionally dated to 538 or 552. Buddhism's introduction — championed by the powerful Soga clan against the anti-Buddhist Mononobe and Nakatomi clans — reshaped Japanese religion and court culture.

Politics and reform

Prince Shōtoku (574–622), regent under Empress Suiko, is credited with the Seventeen-Article Constitution of 604 and with promoting Buddhism and centralised government modelled on China. He sponsored the building of temples such as Shitennō-ji and Hōryū-ji and sent official embassies to Sui China beginning in 607.

In 645, Prince Naka no Ōe (later Emperor Tenji) and Nakatomi no Kamatari destroyed the Soga main branch in a coup and launched the Taika Reforms, which sought to nationalise land, reform taxation, and concentrate power in the emperor. After the Jinshin War of 672, Emperor Tenmu further strengthened the imperial state, and the Taihō Code of 701 gave Japan a comprehensive Chinese-style legal and administrative system. In 710 the capital moved to Heijō-kyō (Nara), ending the period.

Art and culture

Asuka art was strongly influenced by continental styles transmitted through Korea and China. Surviving masterpieces include the bronze Shaka Triad by the sculptor Tori Busshi at Hōryū-ji (623) and the Kudara Kannon statue. Temple architecture adopted Chinese forms with tiled roofs and stone podiums, and Hōryū-ji preserves some of the world's oldest surviving wooden buildings. The period also saw the development of the Japanese writing system through the adoption of Chinese characters.
