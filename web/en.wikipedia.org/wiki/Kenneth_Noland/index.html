Kenneth Noland

A "target" painting of concentric stained rings

Kenneth Noland (April 10, 1924 – January 5, 2010) was an American painter of the Color Field school and one of the best-known American abstract artists of the postwar period. Together with Helen Frankenthaler, Morris Louis and Jules Olitski, he developed a style of painting in which thinned acrylic paint was stained directly into unprimed canvas.

Life and career

Noland was born in Asheville, North Carolina. After serving in the US Army Air Forces in the Second World War, he studied at Black Mountain College under Josef Albers and Ilya Bolotowsky, and later in Paris. A pivotal 1953 visit with critic Clement Greenberg to Helen Frankenthaler's studio introduced him and Morris Louis to stain painting.

Work

Noland's mature work uses simple, symmetrical formats — concentric circles ("targets"), chevrons, stripes and later shaped canvases — to present colour as the subject of painting, free of gesture and representation. His first solo exhibition was in Paris in 1948; American recognition grew through the 1950s, and he represented the United States at the Venice Biennale in 1964. Major retrospectives were held at the Guggenheim Museum in 1977 and elsewhere.

Death and legacy

Noland died of kidney cancer at his home in Port Clyde, Maine, on January 5, 2010, aged 85. His paintings are held by major museums worldwide, including the Museum of Modern Art and the Tate.
