Keizersgracht

Keizersgracht
Location: Amsterdam, Netherlands
Length: 2.8 km
Construction: from 1615

The Keizersgracht ("Emperor's Canal") is one of the main canals of Amsterdam, the second of the three major canals in the city centre's canal belt (Grachtengordel), between the Herengracht and the Prinsengracht.

History

Construction began in 1615 as part of Amsterdam's great expansion during the Dutch Golden Age. Named after Holy Roman Emperor Maximilian I, the canal is the widest in the centre, at about 28 metres. Its grand canal houses were built for wealthy merchants and regents.

Notable buildings

Landmarks include the Felix Meritis building, the Huis Marseille photography museum, and the 'Golden Bend' near Leidsestraat, where the most opulent mansions stand. In winter the frozen canal is a celebrated skating venue and a stage for the annual light festival.
