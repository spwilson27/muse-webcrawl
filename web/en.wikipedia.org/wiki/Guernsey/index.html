Guernsey

Guernsey is an island in the English Channel off the coast of Normandy and the main island of the Bailiwick of Guernsey, a self-governing British Crown Dependency. The bailiwick also includes Alderney, Sark, Herm and several smaller islands. The capital is St Peter Port, and the population is around 64,000.

Guernsey was part of the Duchy of Normandy from the 10th century; when England's King John lost mainland Normandy in 1204, the Channel Islands remained loyal to the English Crown, a relationship preserved to the present day. The island was occupied by German forces from 1940 to 1945 during the Second World War.

Finance is the dominant industry, followed by tourism and horticulture. Guernsey issues its own pound sterling notes and coins, at par with the UK pound. The island is also known for the Guernsey breed of dairy cattle and for Victor Hugo, who wrote Les Misérables while in exile at Hauteville House in St Peter Port.
