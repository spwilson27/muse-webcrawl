Microexpression

A microexpression is a facial expression that only lasts for a short moment. It is the innate result of a voluntary and an involuntary emotional response occurring simultaneously and conflicting with one another, and occurs when a person consciously tries to repress visible signs of emotion.

Research

Microexpressions were first described in the 1960s and were popularised by psychologist Paul Ekman, who linked brief expressions to basic emotions such as anger, disgust, fear, happiness, sadness, surprise and contempt. They typically last between 1/25 and 1/5 of a second.

Applications and criticism

Training programmes claim to help law enforcement and negotiators detect concealed emotions, but the scientific reliability of microexpression-based deception detection is contested, and expressions alone do not prove lying.
