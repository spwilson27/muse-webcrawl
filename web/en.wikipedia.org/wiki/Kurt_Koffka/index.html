Kurt Koffka (17 March 1886 – 22 November 1941) was a German psychologist and professor. He was one of the founders of Gestalt psychology, together with Max Wertheimer and his close associate Wolfgang Köhler.

Life

Koffka was born and educated in Berlin, studying under Carl Stumpf and earning his PhD in 1908 with work on rhythm perception. After work in Frankfurt and Würzberg, he joined the University of Giessen in 1911, where he remained (apart from wartime service as a psychiatrist) until 1924 — the period of his closest collaboration with Köhler and Wertheimer. In 1921 he published Die Grundlagen der psychischen Entwicklung (The Growth of the Mind), applying Gestalt principles to child psychology, which brought him an American audience. He emigrated to the United States in 1924, taught at Cornell and the University of Wisconsin–Madison, and in 1927 became a professor at Smith College in Massachusetts, remaining there until his death in 1941.

Contributions

Koffka's 1922 article "Perception: An Introduction to the Gestalt-Theorie" introduced Gestalt ideas to English-speaking psychologists, though it led to a lasting misunderstanding — that Gestalt theory concerned only perception. His magnum opus, Principles of Gestalt Psychology (1935), attempted a systematic statement of the theory across perception, learning, memory, and social behaviour, introducing concepts such as the behavioural environment and the distinction between the geographical and behavioural worlds. He insisted that the famous dictum be rendered "the whole is other than the sum of the parts", not merely "greater".
