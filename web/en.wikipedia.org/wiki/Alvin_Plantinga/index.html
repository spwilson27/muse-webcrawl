Alvin Carl Plantinga (born 15 November 1932) is an American analytic philosopher who works primarily in the fields of philosophy of religion, epistemology, metaphysics and Christian apologetics. He is widely credited with reviving scholarly interest in the philosophy of religion within analytic philosophy and with making belief in God philosophically respectable in the academy.

Life and career

Plantinga was born in Ann Arbor, Michigan, to Dutch immigrant parents. He studied at Calvin College and the University of Michigan before completing his doctorate at Yale University in 1958. He taught at Wayne State University and Calvin College, and from 1982 at the University of Notre Dame, where he held the John A. O'Brien Chair of Philosophy until his retirement in 2010. He was a founding figure of the Society of Christian Philosophers (1978) and served as president of the American Philosophical Association's Central Division. Time magazine described him in 1980 as "America's leading orthodox Protestant philosopher of God". In 2017 he received the Templeton Prize.

Philosophy

Plantinga's free-will defence, developed in God, Freedom, and Evil (1974), argues that the existence of evil is logically compatible with an omnipotent, wholly good God, because God could not create free creatures who never choose evil. His modal version of the ontological argument in The Nature of Necessity (1974) uses possible-worlds semantics.

In epistemology, Plantinga co-founded Reformed epistemology, holding that belief in God can be "properly basic" — rational without needing evidence from other beliefs. His trilogy Warrant: The Current Debate (1993), Warrant and Proper Function (1993) and Warranted Christian Belief (2000) develops a theory of knowledge as true belief produced by cognitive faculties functioning properly according to a design plan. His evolutionary argument against naturalism contends that if naturalism and evolution are both true, our cognitive faculties are unreliable, giving a defeater for naturalism itself.
