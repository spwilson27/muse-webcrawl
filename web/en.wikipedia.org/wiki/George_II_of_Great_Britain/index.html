George II of Great Britain

George II

Reign: 11 June 1727 – 25 October 1760

Born: 30 October 1683, Hanover

Died: 25 October 1760, Kensington Palace, London

Spouse: Caroline of Ansbach

House: Hanover

George II (George Augustus; 30 October 1683 – 25 October 1760) was King of Great Britain and Ireland, Duke of Brunswick-Lüneburg (Hanover) and Prince-elector of the Holy Roman Empire from 1727 until his death in 1760.

Reign

Born and brought up in northern Germany, George became king on the death of his father, George I. His early reign was dominated by Sir Robert Walpole, Britain's first de facto prime minister, whom George came to rely on despite initial hostility. His influential wife, Queen Caroline, acted as regent during his absences in Hanover and was a key political adviser until her death in 1737.

Wars

George II was the last British monarch to lead troops in battle, commanding at the Battle of Dettingen in 1743 during the War of the Austrian Succession. His reign also saw the last Jacobite rising of 1745, defeated at Culloden, and the opening campaigns of the Seven Years' War, in which Britain under William Pitt the Elder won major victories over France in 1759, the "Year of Victories".

Death and legacy

George died suddenly in 1760 and was succeeded by his grandson, George III. During his reign the powers of Parliament continued to grow and the office of prime minister became established, while Britain expanded its overseas empire.
