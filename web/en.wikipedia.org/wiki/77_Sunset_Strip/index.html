77 Sunset Strip

77 Sunset Strip
American television detective series
ABC, 1958–1964
Created from Roy Huggins’s earlier stories and the film Girl on the Run.

77 Sunset Strip was an American television private-detective series broadcast by ABC from 1958 to 1964. Set in Los Angeles, it followed investigators working from an office at the title address on the Sunset Strip. Its blend of crime plots, glamour, humour and youthful style made it one of Warner Bros. Television’s defining hits of the period.

Premise and characters

The agency was led by Stu Bailey, played by Efrem Zimbalist Jr., and Jeff Spencer, played by Roger Smith; both characters were presented as educated, polished former government agents turned private investigators. Edd Byrnes became a breakout star as Gerald Lloyd “Kookie” Kookson III, initially the office parking attendant whose slang and hair-combing mannerisms became a national fad. Kookie was later promoted to partner. Jacqueline Beer played secretary Suzanne Fabray, and Byron Keith appeared as Lionel “Mac” MacMurray.

Production and popularity

The series grew out of characters created by producer Roy Huggins and a 1958 pilot film. At its peak it helped establish the Warner Bros. formula of stylish detective programmes and shared guest stars and formats with sister series such as Hawaiian Eye and Surfside 6. Byrnes’s popularity produced the novelty hit “Kookie, Kookie (Lend Me Your Comb).”

Later seasons and legacy

Ratings declined as the original creative team departed and tastes changed. A drastic fifth/sixth-season retool under Jack Webb removed most characters and made Bailey a globe-trotting investigator, but the revival did not last. The show remains remembered for Kookie’s cultural moment, its theme music and its role in shaping the television private-eye genre.
