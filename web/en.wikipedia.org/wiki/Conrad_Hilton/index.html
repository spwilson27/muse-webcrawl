Conrad Hilton

Conrad Nicholson Hilton Sr. (December 25, 1887 – January 3, 1979) was an American businessman who founded the Hilton Hotels chain, building one of the world's largest hospitality companies and pioneering the modern hotel industry.

Early life

Hilton was born in New Mexico to a Norwegian immigrant father and German-American mother who ran a general store and small inn. He served in World War I and worked in his family's businesses and banking before entering hotels.

Career

In 1919 Hilton bought the Mobley Hotel in Cisco, Texas, then expanded across Texas during the oil boom. He built the first hotel bearing his name in Dallas (1925) and survived near-bankruptcy in the Great Depression. In 1946 he formed the Hilton Hotels Corporation, in 1949 acquired New York's Waldorf Astoria, and in 1954 bought the Statler Hotels chain — then the largest real-estate transaction in history. Hilton International expanded globally, standardising American-style hotels abroad.

Personal life and legacy

Hilton married three times, including to Zsa Zsa Gabor (1942–1946). He is the great-grandfather of Paris Hilton. A devout Catholic, he established the Conrad N. Hilton Foundation (1944), later a major humanitarian funder, and wrote the autobiography Be My Guest (1957), still placed in Hilton hotel rooms.
