Platform-based design

Platform-based design is a methodology for the design of embedded systems and systems-on-chip (SoC) that emphasises reuse of pre-designed components and architectures. A platform is a library of components and interconnects, usually including processors, memories, and communication structures, characterised by a set of abstraction layers, from which many derivative products can be generated by customisation.

Principles

The approach separates application functionality from implementation architecture: applications are mapped onto platforms through well-defined interfaces, and design proceeds by successive refinement through layers of abstraction. The goal is to meet stringent time-to-market demands by avoiding redesign from scratch for each product.

History

The concept was developed in the 1990s by researchers including Alberto Sangiovanni-Vincentelli and colleagues at the University of California, Berkeley, and has influenced industrial flows for consumer electronics, automotive electronics, and communication systems. Related notions include product platforms in manufacturing and software product lines.
