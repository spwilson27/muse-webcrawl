End time

The Last Judgment, a common image of the end time

The end time (also called the end times, end of days, last days or eschaton) is a future period described in the eschatologies of several world religions as the culminating events of history, in which the present world order comes to an end and is replaced by a final state — often involving divine judgment, the resurrection of the dead, and the renewal or destruction of the world.

In Abrahamic religions

Judaism

Jewish eschatology speaks of the "end of days" (aḥarit ha-yamim), a period associated with the coming of the Messiah, the ingathering of the exiles, the resurrection of the dead and the world to come (Olam Ha-Ba).

Christianity

Christian eschatology centres on the Second Coming of Christ, the resurrection of the dead, the Last Judgment and the establishment of a new heaven and new earth, as described in the Book of Revelation and other New Testament texts. Interpretations vary widely — preterist, historicist, futurist and idealist readings, and millennial schemes such as premillennialism, postmillennialism and amillennialism. Some movements have predicted specific dates for the end, most famously the Millerites, whose expectation of 1844 ended in the "Great Disappointment".

Islam

Islamic eschatology describes the signs of the Hour (Qiyamah), including the appearance of the Mahdi, the return of Isa (Jesus), the emergence of the Dajjal (a false messiah figure), the resurrection and the Day of Judgment (Yawm al-Qiyamah).

In other traditions

Hindu cosmology describes vast cycles of time ending in dissolution (pralaya), with the present age being the degenerate Kali Yuga. Zoroastrianism teaches a final renovation of the world (Frashokereti) after the triumph of good over evil. Norse mythology tells of Ragnarök, the prophesied destruction and rebirth of the world. Buddhist eschatology speaks of the future Buddha Maitreya and the decline of the Dharma.

Secular uses

The phrase is also used loosely for catastrophic scenarios — nuclear war, climate change, pandemics or impact events — that could end civilisation, sometimes discussed through the imagery of the Doomsday Clock maintained by the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists.
