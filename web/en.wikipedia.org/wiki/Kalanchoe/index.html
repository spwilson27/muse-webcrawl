Kalanchoe

FamilyCrassulaceae
SubfamilyKalanchoideae
SpeciesAbout 125
Native rangeMadagascar and tropical Africa, Asia

Kalanchoe is a genus of about 125 species of tropical, succulent flowering plants in the stonecrop family Crassulaceae, mainly native to Madagascar and tropical Africa, with some species in Asia. The genus was described by Michel Adanson in 1763; its name derives from a Chinese name for one of the species.

Description

Most kalanchoes are shrubs or perennial herbs with fleshy, often scalloped leaves adapted for water storage, and they use crassulacean acid metabolism (CAM) photosynthesis. Many species produce plantlets along their leaf margins — notably Kalanchoe daigremontiana, the "mother of thousands" — which drop off and root readily, making some species invasive weeds in dry regions.

Cultivation

The florist's kalanchoe, Kalanchoe blossfeldiana, is one of the most popular flowering houseplants, sold in flower colours from red and orange to pink, yellow and white. It is a short-day plant, and commercial growers manipulate day length to schedule blooming. In 1999 a kalanchoe became one of the first plants sent into space, carried to the Soviet Salyut/Mir station experiments on plant growth.

Medicine and toxicity

Kalanchoe species contain bufadienolide cardiac glycosides and can be toxic to livestock and pets. Extracts of Kalanchoe pinnata (syn. Bryophyllum pinnatum) are used in traditional medicine in many countries.
