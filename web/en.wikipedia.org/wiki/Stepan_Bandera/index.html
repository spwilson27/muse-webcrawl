Stepan Bandera

Stepan Andriyovych Bandera (1 January 1909 – 15 October 1959) was a Ukrainian nationalist politician and theorist who led a radical faction of the Organisation of Ukrainian Nationalists (OUN-B). He remains one of the most controversial figures in Ukrainian history: celebrated by many Ukrainians as a champion of independence, and condemned by others for the OUN's collaboration with Nazi Germany and the involvement of Ukrainian nationalist forces in ethnic violence during the Second World War.

Early life and activism

Bandera was born in the village of Staryi Uhryniv in Austrian Galicia, the son of a Ukrainian Greek Catholic priest. He studied at the Lviv Polytechnic and became active in Ukrainian nationalist scouting and underground organisations. In interwar Poland he rose through the OUN, was arrested for his role in organising the assassination of Polish interior minister Bronisław Pieracki, and was sentenced to death in 1936; the sentence was commuted to life imprisonment, and he was freed when Germany invaded Poland in 1939.

Second World War

After the split of the OUN in 1940, Bandera headed its more radical wing. On 30 June 1941, days after Germany invaded the Soviet Union, his followers proclaimed a Ukrainian state in Lviv. The German authorities, unwilling to accept Ukrainian statehood, arrested Bandera within weeks; he spent most of the war in custody, including in the Sachsenhausen concentration camp's special barracks. Forces aligned with his movement created the Ukrainian Insurgent Army (UPA), which fought the Germans at times while also carrying out massacres of Polish civilians in Volhynia in 1943 and collaborating in anti-Jewish violence — actions that continue to drive historical and political dispute.

Exile and assassination

Released by the Germans in 1944 and living in West Germany after the war, Bandera continued to direct the OUN's anti-Soviet activities from Munich. On 15 October 1959 he was assassinated there by Bohdan Stashynsky, a KGB agent who sprayed cyanide into his face; Stashynsky later defected and confessed, causing an international scandal.

Legacy

Bandera's memory is sharply contested. In 2010 the Ukrainian president Viktor Yushchenko posthumously awarded him the title Hero of Ukraine, a decision annulled by the courts the following year. Streets in many Ukrainian cities bear his name, while critics in Poland, Russia, Israel and elsewhere regard him as a fascist collaborator. His image regained prominence in Ukrainian public life after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.
