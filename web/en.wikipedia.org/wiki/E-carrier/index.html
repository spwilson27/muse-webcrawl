TypeDigital transmission hierarchy

The E-carrier is a member of the series of carrier systems developed for digital transmission of many simultaneous telephone calls using time-division multiplexing. The European Conference of Postal and Telecommunications Administrations (CEPT) originally standardized the E-carrier system, which was later adopted by the ITU-T and used in most countries outside the United States, Canada and Japan, where the analogous T-carrier system is used instead.

Hierarchy

The basic E-carrier signal, E1, carries 30 voice channels plus one signalling channel and one synchronization channel, each of 64 kbit/s, giving an aggregate rate of 2.048 Mbit/s. Higher levels are formed by multiplexing four lower-level streams:

The E1 frame consists of 32 time slots of 8 bits each, transmitted 8,000 times per second. Time slot 0 carries framing and alarm information, while time slot 16 carries channel-associated signalling (or, in ISDN primary rate service, common channel signalling).

Applications

E1 lines were widely used to connect business telephone systems (PBXs) to the public switched telephone network, and to provide ISDN primary rate access. E-carrier links also underpinned leased-line data services and early backbone connections for Internet service providers. Much of this infrastructure has since been superseded by packet-based transport over SDH/SONET and Ethernet, though E1 interfaces remain common in legacy telephony.
