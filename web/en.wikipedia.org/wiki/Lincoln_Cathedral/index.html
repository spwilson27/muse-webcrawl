Lincoln Cathedral, or the Cathedral Church of the Blessed Virgin Mary of Lincoln, is a Church of England cathedral in Lincoln, England. It is the seat of the Bishop of Lincoln and the mother church of the Diocese of Lincoln. Construction began in 1072 by order of William the Conqueror, and the cathedral was rebuilt in the Gothic style after fire and earthquake, principally from 1192 under St Hugh of Lincoln.

For 238 years (1311–1548) the cathedral was the tallest building in the world, the first to surpass the Great Pyramid of Giza, until its central spire collapsed in a storm. It remains one of the finest examples of English Gothic architecture, celebrated for its "crazy vaults", the Dean's Eye and Bishop's Eye rose windows, and the carved Lincoln Imp.

History

Bishop Remigius founded the first cathedral in 1072–1092. Fire in 1141 and an earthquake in 1185 ruined it; Bishop Hugh of Avalon (St Hugh) began rebuilding in 1192 in the new Gothic style, producing the earliest large-scale Gothic church in England. The central tower was raised and crowned with a timber-and-lead spire around 1311, reaching an estimated 160 metres. The spire fell in 1548 and was never rebuilt. One of the four surviving original copies of Magna Carta was kept at Lincoln from 1215.

Architecture

The cathedral displays Early English Gothic at its most inventive: the nave's tierceron vaults ("crazy vaults") use ribs asymmetrically for decorative effect; St Hugh's Choir has the earliest ribbed vault in England; the Angel Choir (1256–1280) is rich in carved foliage and angels. The west front is a vast "screen front" studded with statues. The chapter house, a ten-sided building with a central pillar, is one of England's finest.

The Lincoln Imp

High on a pillar in the Angel Choir sits the Lincoln Imp, a small grotesque stone figure that has become the symbol of the city. Legend tells that the Devil sent imps to make mischief in the cathedral; one was turned to stone by an angel, and sits there still.

Library and Magna Carta

The medieval library, designed by Christopher Wren in 1674, holds early printed books and manuscripts. Lincoln's Magna Carta is now displayed in Lincoln Castle. The cathedral has served as a filming location, standing in for Westminster Abbey in The Da Vinci Code (2006).
