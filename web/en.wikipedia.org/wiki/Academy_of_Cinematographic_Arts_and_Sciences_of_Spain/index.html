Academy of Cinematographic Arts and Sciences of Spain

The Academy of Cinematographic Arts and Sciences of Spain (Spanish: Academia de las Artes y las Ciencias Cinematográficas de España) is a professional organisation founded on 8 January 1986 to promote Spanish cinema. Its best-known activity is the presentation of the annual Goya Awards, Spain's principal national film awards.

History

The Academy was created at the initiative of producer Alfredo Matas and a group of film professionals, following a series of meetings in 1985 among prominent figures of Spanish cinema. Fernando Trueba, Luis García Berlanga and others were instrumental in its founding. The first Goya Awards ceremony was held in March 1987 in Madrid.

Goya Awards

The Goya Awards (Premios Goya) honour the best Spanish films of the year in categories including Best Film, Best Director, Best Actor and Best Actress, as well as an honorary award. The trophy is a bust of the painter Francisco Goya, after whom the awards are named.
