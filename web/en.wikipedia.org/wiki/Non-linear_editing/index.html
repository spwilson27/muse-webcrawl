Non-linear editing

Non-linear editing (NLE) is a form of offline editing for audio, video and image production. In offline editing, original content is not modified in the course of editing. In non-linear editing, edits are specified and modified by specialised software. A pointer-based playlist, effectively an edit decision list (EDL), for video and audio, or a directed acyclic graph for stills, is used to keep track of edits. Each time the edited audio, video or image is rendered, played back or accessed, it is reconstructed from the original source and the specified editing steps.

History

The first non-linear editor was the CMX 600, introduced in 1971, which stored video on modified disk drives. Through the 1980s systems such as the EditDroid and Montage Picture Processor demonstrated tapeless workflows. The launch of the Avid/1 (later Media Composer) in 1989, followed by Adobe Premiere (1991) and Apple's Final Cut Pro (1999), brought non-linear editing to desktop computers, and by the 2000s it had displaced tape-based linear editing in film and television almost entirely. Unlike linear editing, shots can be inserted, removed or rearranged at any point without re-recording everything after them.

Workflow

Media is ingested (digitised or imported), organised into bins, and assembled on a timeline. Editors trim clips, add transitions, titles, effects and mixed audio; because editing is non-destructive, any decision can be revised. Projects can be exchanged between systems using formats such as EDL, XML and AAF.
