Croatian Apoxyomenos

Material: Bronze

Height: 192 cm

Created: 2nd–1st century BC (Hellenistic copy of a 4th-century BC original)

Discovered: 1996, near Vele Orjule, Adriatic Sea

Present location: Apoxyomenos Museum, Mali Lošinj, Croatia

The Croatian Apoxyomenos is an ancient Greek bronze statue of an athlete scraping oil and dust from his body with a strigil (the apoxyomenos type), recovered from the Adriatic Sea near the island of Lošinj, Croatia. It is one of the best-preserved of the small number of surviving large Greek bronzes of this type.

Discovery and recovery

The statue was spotted in 1996 by a Belgian tourist diving near the islet of Vele Orjule, at a depth of about 45 metres, lying on a sandy seabed among rocks. It was raised in 1999 in a complex recovery operation by Croatia's Ministry of Culture and special police divers. The statue had probably been cargo on a ship — possibly jettisoned during a storm in the early Roman period — travelling from a Greek workshop toward the northern Adriatic.

Conservation

Years of desalination and painstaking mechanical cleaning removed thick marine encrustation. Unusually, the statue retained its inlaid eyes, copper nipples and lips, and even traces of ancient repairs. Restoration also revealed organic material inside the hollow casting, including wood, seeds, and the nest of a small rodent — evidence the statue stood outdoors or in storage for some time in antiquity. The conservation project won international recognition, including a Europa Nostra award.

Display

After exhibition in Zagreb and abroad, the statue was given a permanent home in the Apoxyomenos Museum in Mali Lošinj, opened in 2016 in the former Kvarner Palace, where the bronze is displayed alone in a dedicated sequence of rooms culminating in a white chamber.
