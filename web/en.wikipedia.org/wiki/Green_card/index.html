Green card

A green card, known officially as a Permanent Resident Card, is an identity document which shows that a person has permanent residency in the United States. Green card holders are formally known as lawful permanent residents (LPRs). As of 2023, there are an estimated 12.7 million green card holders, of whom 9 million are eligible to apply for U.S. citizenship.

Overview

Lawful permanent residents have the right to live and work permanently in the United States, though they are not U.S. citizens and generally cannot vote in federal elections. The card's nickname comes from its historical greenish coloring; the card itself has been redesigned several times over the decades, and current versions contain various security features.

Eligibility categories

Foreign nationals may obtain permanent residency through family sponsorship, employment sponsorship, the Diversity Visa Lottery, refugee or asylee status, and various humanitarian and special immigrant programs. Most applicants go through consular processing abroad or adjustment of status within the United States.

Rights and responsibilities

Permanent residents may work for nearly any employer in the United States, travel abroad (subject to limits on absence), and sponsor certain family members for immigration. They must obey U.S. laws, file tax returns, and register for selective service if applicable. Permanent residency can be lost through abandonment, extended absence from the country, or certain criminal offenses.

Path to citizenship

Most green card holders become eligible to apply for naturalization after five years of permanent residence (three years if married to a U.S. citizen), provided they meet requirements regarding continuous residence, physical presence, good moral character, English language ability, and knowledge of U.S. civics.

History

The modern system derives from the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1952 and was substantially reshaped by the Immigration and Nationality Act of 1965, which abolished national-origin quotas. The Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986 legalized millions of unauthorized residents.
