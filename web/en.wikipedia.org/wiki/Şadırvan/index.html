Şadırvan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A şadırvan (from Persian shadervan) is a type of fountain found in the courtyards of mosques, caravanserais, and other public buildings in Turkey and the former Ottoman lands, providing water for ritual ablutions (wudu) before prayer, as well as drinking water. Unlike the selsebil, a decorative wall fountain, the şadırvan is typically a free-standing, often polygonal or circular structure with faucets arranged around its sides, sheltered by a wide canopy or roof, frequently topped by a small dome or eaves supported on columns.

Design

Because several worshippers perform ablutions at once, the şadırvan is designed with many taps around its perimeter, sometimes in two tiers. Wooden or marble stools are placed before the faucets. Historic examples, such as the şadırvans of Istanbul's great imperial mosques, are elaborately carved and roofed; many are considered notable works of Ottoman civic architecture in their own right. Modern mosques continue to include şadırvans, often in simplified form.
