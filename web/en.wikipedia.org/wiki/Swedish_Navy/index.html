The Swedish Navy (Swedish: Svenska marinen) is the naval branch of the Swedish Armed Forces. It traditionally celebrates its founding on 7 June 1522, when Gustav Vasa purchased a fleet of warships from Lübeck — making it one of the world's oldest continuously existing navies.

Organisation

The navy is built around a small, technologically advanced force designed for operations in the confined, shallow waters of the Baltic Sea. Its principal units include Visby-class stealth corvettes, Göteborg-class corvettes, Koster-class mine countermeasure vessels, patrol boats, and a submarine force operating the Gotland class and the newer Blekinge-class (A26) boats, designed and built domestically by Saab Kockums. The navy also maintains an amphibious corps equipped with fast Combat Boat 90 assault craft.

History

Sweden was a major Baltic naval power in the 17th century, though its great ship Vasa famously sank on her maiden voyage in 1628. The naval base at Karlskrona, founded in 1680, remains the principal base and a UNESCO World Heritage site. During the Cold War the navy focused on coastal defence against a potential Soviet invasion; after 1990 it downsized considerably, then began rebuilding as regional tensions rose in the 2010s and Sweden moved toward NATO membership.
