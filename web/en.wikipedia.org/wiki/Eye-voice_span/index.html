Eye-voice span

The eye-voice span (EVS) is the distance, measured in words or characters, between the point at which the eyes are fixating during reading aloud and the word currently being spoken. When people read aloud, their eyes run ahead of their voice, taking in upcoming text while the vocal apparatus pronounces earlier words.

For skilled adult readers of English, the eye-voice span is typically about two to three words, or roughly 15 characters, though it varies with text difficulty, word predictability, and the reader's skill. Difficult or unpredictable words shorten the span, as the reader slows the voice to let the eyes catch up; errors and hesitations occur when the span collapses.

Research

The eye-voice span is studied with eye tracking and is closely related to the eye movements in reading and the perceptual span in silent reading. Research by Buswell and later by Levin and others showed the span grows with reading development in children. EVS measures are used in studies of dyslexia, stuttering, and second-language reading, and in training simultaneous interpreters, whose analogous "ear-voice span" (décalage) reflects the lag between listening and speaking.
