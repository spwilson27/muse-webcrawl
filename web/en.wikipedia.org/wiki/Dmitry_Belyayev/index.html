Dmitry Belyayev

Dmitry Konstantinovich Belyayev (Russian: Дми́трий Константи́нович Беля́ев; 17 July 1917 – 14 November 1985) was a Soviet geneticist and director of the Institute of Cytology and Genetics of the Siberian Division of the USSR Academy of Sciences from 1959 to 1985.

Domesticated silver foxes produced by Belyayev's selective breeding experiment.

Fox domestication experiment

Belyayev is best known for the long-running experiment in Novosibirsk, begun in 1959 with his student Lyudmila Trut, in which silver foxes were selectively bred solely for tameness toward humans. Within a few dozen generations the foxes displayed dog-like behaviour and correlated physical changes — floppy ears, piebald coats, curled tails, and altered reproductive cycles — a phenomenon Belyayev explained through his theory of destabilizing selection, linking the changes to developmental and hormonal pathways (the neural crest hypothesis was proposed later by others).

The experiment is frequently cited as evidence for the "domestication syndrome" and for how selection on behaviour alone can produce sweeping phenotypic change. The project continued after Belyayev's death under Trut.
