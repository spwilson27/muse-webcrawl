The American Federation of Teachers (AFT) is the second largest teacher's labor union in the United States, behind the National Education Association. Founded in Chicago in 1916, it represents public and private school teachers, paraprofessionals, higher education faculty, nurses and other health professionals, and public employees. It is affiliated with the AFL–CIO.

History

The AFT was chartered by the American Federation of Labor in 1916, uniting several local teacher unions. It grew substantially in the 1960s under leaders such as Albert Shanker, who organised major strikes in New York City, including the Ocean Hill–Brownsville strike of 1968. The union expanded beyond K-12 teachers to include healthcare workers and public employees in later decades.

Structure and politics

The AFT is composed of state and local affiliates, including the United Federation of Teachers in New York City. It is politically active, generally supporting Democratic Party candidates and advocating for public education funding, smaller class sizes and collective bargaining rights. Its presidents have included Albert Shanker (1974–1997), Sandra Feldman (1997–2004), Edward J. McElroy (2004–2008) and Randi Weingarten (since 2008).

Criticism

The union has been criticised by opponents of teachers' unions over tenure rules, work rules and school closures during the COVID-19 pandemic, while supporters credit it with improving teacher pay and working conditions.
