Clan Ogilvy

The crest badge of Clan Ogilvy, bearing the motto A Fin ("to the end").

Clan Ogilvy is a Scottish clan from Angus. The clan takes its name from the land of Ogilvy in the parish of Glamis, Angus. The Ogilvys were hereditary sheriffs of Angus and rose to become Earls of Airlie, a title created in 1639 that the chiefly line still holds.

History

Origins

The name is derived from the Old Welsh or Pictish words ochel (high) and ma (plain), describing a high plain. The family is said to be descended from Gilchrist, Earl of Angus, whose younger son Gilbert received the lands of Ogilvy in the 12th century. Patrick de Ogilvy appears on the Ragman Rolls of 1296, swearing fealty to Edward I of England.

Wars of Scottish Independence

During the Wars of Scottish Independence the Ogilvys supported Robert the Bruce. Sir Patrick Ogilvy of Auchterhouse was killed at the Battle of Methven in 1306, and later members of the family served as sheriffs of Angus and ambassadors to England.

15th and 16th centuries

Sir Walter Ogilvy was killed at the Battle of Harlaw in 1411. In 1440 the Battle of Arbroath was fought between the Ogilvys and the Lindsays over the office of justiciar of the Abbey of Arbroath, in which Chief Alexander Ogilvy of Inverquharity was wounded and captured, thereafter being murdered. Members of the clan fought at the Battle of Flodden in 1513 and, largely as Royalists, at Pinkie Cleugh in 1547.

Civil war and Jacobite risings

James Ogilvy was created Earl of Airlie by Charles I in 1639. The Ogilvys were staunch Royalists during the Wars of the Three Kingdoms; James Ogilvy, 2nd Earl of Airlie, fought for the Marquess of Montrose and was captured after the Battle of Philiphaugh in 1645 but escaped execution. The clan supported the Jacobite cause in the risings of 1715 and 1745: David Ogilvy, de jure 6th Earl of Airlie, commanded a regiment at the Battle of Culloden in 1746 and afterwards escaped to France, where he served as a general in the French army. The Ogilvy regiment's colours, and its survival as a unit in French service, became a notable episode of the Jacobite exile.

Chief and seat

The chief of Clan Ogilvy is the Earl of Airlie. The clan seat is Airlie Castle in Angus, and Cortachy Castle, also in Angus, is the principal residence of the earls of Airlie.
