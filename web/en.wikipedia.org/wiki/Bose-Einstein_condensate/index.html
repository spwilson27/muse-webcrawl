Bose–Einstein condensate

Bose–Einstein condensate
Predicted: 1924–25, Bose & Einstein
First created: 1995 (rubidium-87)
State: Fifth state of matter

A Bose–Einstein condensate (BEC) is a state of matter formed when a gas of bosons cooled to temperatures very near absolute zero (typically nanokelvins) collapses into the lowest quantum state, so individual atoms behave as a single quantum entity with wave-like properties.

History

Predicted by Satyendra Nath Bose and Albert Einstein in 1924–25 from Bose statistics. First achieved in June 1995 by Eric Cornell and Carl Wieman at JILA using rubidium-87 cooled to 170 nK, and independently by Wolfgang Ketterle with sodium; the three shared the 2001 Nobel Prize in Physics.

Properties

BECs exhibit superfluidity, interference, and slow light, and are used in precision measurement, quantum simulation and atom lasers. See Boson, Absolute zero, Superfluidity.
