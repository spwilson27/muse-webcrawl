108 St Georges Terrace, Perth's second tallest building

108 St Georges Terrace is a 50-storey office skyscraper in Perth, Western Australia. Completed in 1988 on the site of the Palace Hotel, it rises 214 metres to its roof — 247 metres including its spire — making it the second tallest building in Perth after Central Park. Built for businessman Alan Bond, it was originally known as the Bond Tower and later as the BankWest Tower.

Design and construction

The tower is distinguished by its triangular floor plan and inclined northern glass façade, a design intended to maximise views over the Swan River while responding to the heritage-listed Palace Hotel at its base, whose façade and public areas were retained and restored as part of the development. Its distinctive profile made it one of the defining landmarks of the Perth central business district skyline.

Tenancy and names

After Bond Corporation's collapse, the tower passed to the R&I Bank and then Bankwest, whose name it bore for many years. Following Bankwest's relocation, it was renamed the South32 Tower when the mining company South32 took up residence in 2015, before reverting to its street address name, 108 St Georges Terrace.
