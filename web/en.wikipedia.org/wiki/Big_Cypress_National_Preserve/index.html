Big Cypress National Preserve is a United States National Preserve located in South Florida, about 72 km (45 mi) west of Miami on the Atlantic coastal plain. The 720,000-acre (2,900 km²) Big Cypress, along with Big Thicket National Preserve in Texas, became the first national preserves in the United States National Park System when they were authorised on October 11, 1974.

Geography and ecology

The preserve protects a mosaic of cypress swamps, sawgrass prairies, pinelands, hardwood hammocks and mangrove estuaries that form the western watershed of the Everglades. Its freshwater sheet flow is essential to the health of Everglades National Park and the Ten Thousand Islands. Wildlife includes alligators, white-tailed deer, black bears, river otters and the endangered Florida panther, for which the preserve is prime habitat.

History

The Big Cypress was home to the Seminole and Miccosukee peoples, who retain reserved rights and villages within the preserve. In the late 1960s, plans for the world's largest jetport in the swamp — with runways begun near the Tamiami Trail — provoked an environmental campaign led by Marjory Stoneman Douglas, hunter-conservationist Nathaniel Reed and others; the jetport was cancelled in 1970 in a landmark victory of the modern environmental movement, and the preserve was established four years later. As a "preserve" rather than a park, it permits traditional uses including hunting, off-road vehicle use in designated areas, and limited oil exploration that predates its creation.

Recreation

Visitors use the Loop Road scenic drive, boardwalks such as Kirby Storter, canoeing, camping and the Florida National Scenic Trail, which begins at the preserve's Oasis Visitor Center. The annual Swamp Heritage Festival celebrates local culture.
