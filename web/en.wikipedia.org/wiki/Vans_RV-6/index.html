Van's RV-6

The Van's RV-6 and RV-6A are two-seat, single-engine, low-wing homebuilt aircraft sold in kit form by Van's Aircraft of Aurora, Oregon. Designed by Richard VanGrunsven, the RV-6 first flew in 1985 and became the most popular kit aircraft design of its era: more than 2,700 RV-6/6A aircraft have been completed and flown. The RV-6 has a conventional tailwheel landing gear, while the RV-6A has a nosewheel.

Design and development

The RV-6 was developed from the earlier single-seat RV-3 and two-seat RV-4, widening the fuselage for side-by-side seating and cross-country comfort. It is an all-metal (aluminium) semi-monocoque design with a cantilever low wing, marketed for its combination of speed (around 200 mph top speed with 180 hp), gentle handling, short-field performance and mild aerobatic capability. Van's discontinued the RV-6/6A kits in favour of the pre-punched RV-7/7A, but the large existing fleet remains supported; the type helped establish the modern experimental amateur-built market and Van's position as one of the largest kit-aircraft producers in the world.
