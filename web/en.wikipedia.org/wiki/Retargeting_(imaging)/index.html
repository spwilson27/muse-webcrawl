Retargeting (imaging)

Image retargeting, also called content-aware resizing, is the process of changing the size or aspect ratio of an image while preserving its important visual content. Unlike standard scaling, cropping, or image padding, retargeting algorithms attempt to remove or insert pixels in ways that minimize distortion of salient regions such as faces, objects, and structures.

Methods

The best-known retargeting method is seam carving, introduced by Shai Avidan and Ariel Shamir in 2007, which removes paths of low-energy pixels ("seams") from the image. Other approaches include warping methods that continuously deform the image according to a saliency map, multi-operator methods that combine cropping, scaling, and seam carving, and patch-based synthesis methods.

Applications

Retargeting is used to adapt images and video to displays of different sizes, such as mobile devices, and in photo editing software for resizing images while keeping subjects undistorted.
