Denatured alcohol

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Denatured alcohol, also known as methylated spirits, is ethanol that has additives to make it poisonous, bad-tasting, foul-smelling, or nauseating to discourage recreational consumption. It is sometimes dyed so that it can be identified visually. Pyridine and methanol are commonly used denaturants, and denatured alcohol may be dyed purple with methyl violet.

Denatured alcohol is used as a solvent and as fuel for alcohol burners and camping stoves. Because of the diversity of industrial uses for denatured alcohol, hundreds of additives and denaturing methods have been used. The main additive has traditionally been 10% methanol, giving rise to the term "methylated spirits". Other typical additives include isopropyl alcohol, acetone, methyl ethyl ketone, methyl isobutyl ketone, and denatonium.

Purpose

Ethanol suitable for drinking is heavily taxed in many countries. Ethanol for industrial use is denatured so that it can be sold without the beverage tax while being rendered unsuitable for human consumption. The denaturant does not need to impair the alcohol's usefulness as a solvent or fuel; it need only make the product undrinkable and difficult to purify back into potable alcohol.

Formulations

Different countries mandate different formulations. Completely denatured alcohol (CDA) in the United Kingdom historically contained wood naphtha, crude pyridine and methyl violet dye. Specially denatured alcohol (SDA) formulations in the United States are specified for particular industries, such as cosmetics and pharmaceuticals. Denatonium benzoate, one of the bitterest substances known, is now widely used as an aversive agent.

Safety

Despite its poisonous content, denatured alcohol is sometimes consumed as a surrogate alcohol, which can result in blindness or death if the denaturant is methanol. Consumption of methanol-containing products was responsible for many deaths during Prohibition in the United States.

Uses

  • Solvent for shellac, varnishes and inks
  • Fuel for spirit burners, camping stoves and fondue sets
  • Cleaning agent and disinfectant
  • Preservative for biological specimens
  • Feedstock in chemical manufacture
