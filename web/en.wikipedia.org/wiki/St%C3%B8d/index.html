Stød

Stød (Danish for "push" or "thrust") is a suprasegmental phonetic feature of the Danish language, a form of laryngealisation or creaky voice that occurs on certain syllables. It is phonemic: it can distinguish otherwise identical words, as in hun "she" (without stød) versus hund "dog" (with stød).

Description

Phonetically, stød consists of a constriction of the glottis during part of a long vowel or a vowel followed by a sonorant consonant. Its distribution is governed by "stød basis" — syllables with sufficient sonority weight — and by morphological structure. Most Danish dialects have stød, though it is absent in southern varieties such as those of Ærø and southern Funen. A similar feature, the tonal word accents of Swedish and Norwegian, is historically related but phonetically distinct.
