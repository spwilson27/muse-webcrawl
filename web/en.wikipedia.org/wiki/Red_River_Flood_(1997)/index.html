1997 Red River flood

The Red River flood of 1997 was a major flood that occurred in April 1997 along the Red River of the North in Minnesota, North Dakota, and Manitoba, Canada. It was the most severe flood of the river since 1826, and caused extensive damage in the twin cities of Grand Forks, North Dakota, and East Grand Forks, Minnesota.

Causes

A winter of extreme snowfall followed by a rapid spring melt and heavy rains sent the Red River far above flood stage. At Grand Forks the river crested at about 54 feet (16.5 m), well above the 28-foot flood stage, overtopping dikes on 18 April 1997.

Impact

Nearly the entire population of Grand Forks — some 50,000 people — was evacuated, the largest evacuation of an American city since the Civil War at the time. A fire in the flooded downtown destroyed eleven buildings. Total damages across the valley exceeded US$3.5 billion, prompting major new flood-protection works including the Grand Forks greenway and higher dikes.
