LASIK

LASIK or Lasik (laser-assisted in situ keratomileusis), commonly referred to as laser eye surgery or laser vision correction, is a type of refractive surgery for the correction of myopia, hyperopia and astigmatism. During the procedure a thin flap is created in the cornea, folded back, and an excimer laser reshapes the underlying stromal bed before the flap is replaced. LASIK is one of the most commonly performed elective surgical procedures in the world, with tens of millions of operations performed since its introduction in the 1990s.

Procedure

After topical anaesthesia, the surgeon creates a hinged corneal flap using a microkeratome or, in "all-laser" LASIK, a femtosecond laser. The flap is lifted and an excimer laser (typically 193 nm argon-fluoride) ablates a computer-calculated amount of stromal tissue to change the cornea's refractive power: flattening it for myopia, steepening it for hyperopia, and reshaping it asymmetrically for astigmatism. Eye-tracking systems follow the eye during ablation. The flap is then repositioned, adhering without sutures. The operation takes only a few minutes per eye, and most patients notice improved vision within a day.

Outcomes

Large studies report that the great majority of patients achieve 20/20 vision or better and report high satisfaction. However, the surgical flap never heals as strongly as unwounded corneal tissue, and the procedure permanently reduces corneal thickness and alters its biomechanics.

Risks and side effects

Common side effects include dry eye symptoms and night-vision disturbances such as halos, glare and starbursts, which usually improve over months. Less common complications include diffuse lamellar keratitis, epithelial ingrowth, flap dislocation, infection, under- or over-correction, and post-LASIK corneal ectasia (keratectasia), a progressive thinning and bulging of the cornea that can severely degrade vision. Careful preoperative screening for keratoconus and thin corneas is used to reduce risk. LASIK does not prevent presbyopia, so many patients still require reading glasses after about age 45.

History

LASIK grew out of José Barraquer's keratomileusis work in Colombia from the 1950s, the development of the excimer laser for corneal surgery in the 1980s, and the flap technique combined with in situ ablation demonstrated by Ioannis Pallikaris in Greece around 1989–1991. It received regulatory approval in the United States in the late 1990s and rapidly displaced radial keratotomy.
