Moksha (Jainism)

Moksha, also called mukti, is the ultimate goal of Jainism: the liberation of the soul (jiva) from the cycle of birth and death (samsara). In Jain belief, every soul is inherently pure, omniscient, and blissful, but is obscured and bound by karma, which Jains understand as a subtle form of matter that adheres to the soul as a result of its actions.

Path to liberation

The Jain path to moksha is summarised in the formula of the three jewels (ratnatraya): right faith (samyak darshana), right knowledge (samyak jnana), and right conduct (samyak charitra). Through ethical discipline — above all the five vows of non-violence (ahimsa), truthfulness, non-stealing, chastity, and non-possession — the influx of new karma is stopped (samvara) and accumulated karma is shed (nirjara). Ascetic practices and meditation hasten this shedding.

The liberated soul

When all karma is destroyed, the soul attains perfect knowledge (kevala jnana) and, at death, rises to the Siddhashila, the summit of the universe, where it abides forever in its natural state of omniscience and bliss, free from embodiment. Such liberated beings are called siddhas and are venerated by Jains alongside the tirthankaras, who attained liberation after re-establishing the path.
