The Hongwu Emperor (21 October 1328 – 24 June 1398), personal name Zhu Yuanzhang, was the founder and first emperor of the Ming dynasty of China. Rising from extreme poverty — orphaned by famine and plague, he had been a novice Buddhist monk and a beggar — he joined the Red Turban rebels, defeated rival warlords and the Mongol-led Yuan dynasty, and reunified China under his rule in 1368.

Rise to power

Zhu Yuanzhang joined the Red Turban movement in 1352 and rose under the warlord Guo Zixing, whose adopted daughter he married. Building a disciplined army based at Nanjing, he defeated Chen Youliang at the Battle of Lake Poyang (1363), one of the largest naval battles in history, and Zhang Shicheng in 1367. In 1368 he proclaimed the Ming dynasty and sent Xu Da north to expel the Yuan court from Dadu (Beijing) to Mongolia.

Reign

As emperor, Zhu worked to rebuild a war-torn country: he restored irrigation, planted trees, resettled peasants on abandoned land, conducted land surveys and censuses (the Yellow Registers and fish-scale maps), and issued paper currency. He abolished the chancellorship in 1380 after the Hu Weiyong case, concentrating power in the throne, and carried out sweeping purges — including the Lan Yu case — in which tens of thousands of officials, generals and their families were executed. He established the Jinyiwei (Embroidered Uniform Guard) secret police and enfeoffed his many sons as princes.

Death and legacy

The Hongwu Emperor died in 1398 and was buried in the Ming Xiaoling Mausoleum at Nanjing. He was succeeded by his grandson, the Jianwen Emperor, who was soon overthrown by Zhu's son the Yongle Emperor. His reign laid the institutional foundations of the Ming — authoritarian, agrarian and suspicious of merchants — and his rise from peasant to emperor remains one of history's most dramatic.
