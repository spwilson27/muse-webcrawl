Lake Manly

Lake Manly
Location: Death Valley, California, United States
Type: Former pluvial lake (endorheic)
Max. length: approx. 140–145 km (90 mi)
Max. depth: approx. 180 m (600 ft)
Basin: Death Valley watershed

Lake Manly was a large pluvial lake that filled Death Valley in California during the wetter glacial periods of the Pleistocene epoch. Named in honour of William Lewis Manly, one of the 1849 pioneers who crossed and named Death Valley, the lake at its greatest extent was about 145 km (90 miles) long and up to 180 m (600 feet) deep.

History

During glacial maxima, runoff from the Amargosa River, the Mojave River (via overflow from Lake Mojave) and the Owens River system (via Owens Lake, China Lake, Searles Lake and Panamint Lake) fed a chain of lakes ending in Death Valley. Shorelines, tufa deposits and wave-cut terraces on the valley's gravel fans record multiple high stands, the most recent substantial lake persisting into the early Holocene before the basin desiccated into the present salt pan at Badwater Basin, the lowest point in North America.

Temporary modern lake

Exceptional rains occasionally recreate a shallow, short-lived lake on the salt pan, a phenomenon also nicknamed Lake Manly. Notable re-formations occurred in 2005 and in August 2023, when the remnants of Hurricane Hilary dropped about a year's worth of rain on Death Valley in a day, producing a lake several kilometres wide that lingered for months and was deep enough in places for kayaking in early 2024.
