Qorikancha


Inca stonework of Qorikancha beneath the Santo Domingo convent

Qorikancha (Quechua: Quri Kancha, "golden courtyard"; hispanicised spellings Coricancha, Koricancha, Qurikancha) was the most important temple in the Inca Empire, located in Cusco, Peru. It was dedicated primarily to Inti, the sun god, and was said to have walls and floors once covered in sheets of solid gold, with a golden image of the sun and a garden whose plants and animals were modelled in gold and silver.

Layout

The temple complex contained shrines to Inti, the Moon (Mama Killa), Venus (Chaska), the Pleiades, and deities of thunder and rainbows. Its perfectly fitted ashlar masonry — blocks joined without mortar — has survived repeated earthquakes that destroyed later colonial buildings erected on top of it. The grounds were organised around a courtyard, and lines called ceques were said to radiate from Qorikancha to hundreds of shrines across the empire.

Colonial period

After the Spanish conquest, the temple's gold was stripped as part of Atahualpa's ransom and later loot, and the site was granted to the Dominican Order, which built the Convent of Santo Domingo directly on the Inca walls. An earthquake in 1950 exposed the Inca interior temple, which had been hidden by the convent.

Today

The site is now the Museo del Convento de Santo Domingo – Coricancha, one of Cusco's principal visitor attractions, displaying Inca masonry alongside colonial art.
