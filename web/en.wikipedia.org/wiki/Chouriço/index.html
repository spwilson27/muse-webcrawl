Chouriço

Chouriço (pronounced [ʃo(w)ˈɾisu]) is a type of pork sausage originating in Portugal, traditionally made from pork meat and fat seasoned with paprika, garlic, salt and sometimes wine, then stuffed into natural casings and often smoked. It is a close relative of Spanish chorizo, though Portuguese chouriço is typically smoked and has a distinct seasoning profile.

Varieties

Regional varieties include chouriço de carne (meat), chouriço de sangue (blood sausage), and products from regions such as Trás-os-Montes, Alentejo and the Azores; some carry protected geographical status. Goan chouriço, from the former Portuguese colony in India, is a distinctive spiced, vinegar-laden version made with Indian chillies and spices, reflecting the fusion of Portuguese and Goan cuisines.

Culinary use

Chouriço is eaten cooked — grilled, roasted (famously flamed with aguardente in a clay assador), or sliced into stews and soups such as caldo verde and Portuguese bean stews. It is a staple of Portuguese festivals and of the diaspora, notably in New England and Brazil, where linguiça and chouriço feature in local cooking.
