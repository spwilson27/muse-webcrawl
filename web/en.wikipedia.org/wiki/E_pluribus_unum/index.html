E pluribus unum

E pluribus unum (Latin for "Out of many, one") is a traditional motto of the United States, appearing on the Great Seal. Adopted by an Act of Congress in 1782, the motto appears on the obverse of the Great Seal on a scroll clutched in the beak of a bald eagle. It originally referred to the union of the thirteen individual states into one nation.

Origins

The phrase had circulated earlier in British and American print culture, including on the title page of the London magazine The Gentleman's Magazine, and echoes classical Latin poetry — a line in Virgil's poem Moretum describes ingredients forming one dish ("color est e pluribus unus"). The motto was suggested by Pierre Eugene du Simitiere, artistic consultant to the first Great Seal committee, in 1776.

Usage

E pluribus unum appears on most US coinage and has long been treated as a de facto national motto. In 1956, however, Congress officially designated "In God We Trust" as the national motto; the Latin phrase retains its place on the Great Seal and on coins and is widely interpreted today as celebrating both federal union and the nation's pluralistic society.
