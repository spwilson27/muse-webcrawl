The Socialist Republic of Vietnam is the official name of Vietnam, a country in Southeast Asia, since the formal reunification of the communist Democratic Republic of Vietnam (North Vietnam) and the Provisional Revolutionary Government of South Vietnam on 2 July 1976, following the end of the Vietnam War in April 1975. It is a one-party state governed by the Communist Party of Vietnam.

History

After the fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975, the country was administered jointly by the North and the Provisional Revolutionary Government until the National Assembly proclaimed the unified Socialist Republic in 1976, with Hanoi as capital and Saigon renamed Ho Chi Minh City. The new state faced post-war devastation, a US-led trade embargo, border wars with the Khmer Rouge (1977–78) and China (1979), and a decade-long occupation of Cambodia. The planned economy's failures — hyperinflation and food shortages — prompted the Communist Party's Sixth Congress in December 1986 to launch Đổi Mới ("Renovation"), introducing market mechanisms while retaining one-party rule.

Government and politics

Under the 2013 constitution, the Communist Party of Vietnam is the leading force of the state and society. The principal state offices — President, Prime Minister, Chair of the National Assembly — together with the party's General Secretary form the "four pillars" of leadership. The National Assembly is the highest state organ; elections are held every five years, with candidates vetted by the party-led Vietnam Fatherland Front. The People's Army of Vietnam plays an unusually large economic as well as military role.

Economy

Since Đổi Mới, Vietnam has been one of the world's fastest-growing economies, shifting from central planning to a "socialist-oriented market economy". Poverty fell dramatically, and the country became a major exporter of electronics, textiles, footwear, coffee and rice, attracting large foreign investment, notably from South Korean, Japanese and increasingly diversified manufacturers. Vietnam joined ASEAN in 1995, normalised relations with the United States the same year, and joined the World Trade Organization in 2007.
