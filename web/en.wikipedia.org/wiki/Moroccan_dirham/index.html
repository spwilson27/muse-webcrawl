The Moroccan dirham (Arabic: درهم مغربي, romanized: dirham maghribi; sign: د.م.; code: MAD) is the official currency of the Kingdom of Morocco. It is issued by Bank Al-Maghrib, the country's central bank. One dirham is divided into 100 centimes (Arabic: سنتيم, santim). The dirham is a restricted currency: it cannot be exported freely, and it is not traded on international foreign-exchange markets in the ordinary way; its exchange rate is managed against a basket of currencies dominated by the euro and the US dollar.

History

The name dirham derives from the ancient Greek drachma and was borne by silver coins used across the Islamic world for centuries, including in Morocco under dynasties such as the Idrisids and Almoravids. Before the French protectorate, Moroccan currency included the rial. Under the protectorate, the Moroccan franc was introduced in 1921. After independence, Morocco reintroduced the dirham in 1960, replacing the franc at a rate of 100 francs to 1 dirham, although franc-denominated coins and the term continued in colloquial use for many years (Moroccans still commonly quote small prices in francs, rials, or centimes).

Exchange rate regime

Bank Al-Maghrib sets a daily reference rate for the dirham against a basket weighted roughly 60% to the euro and 40% to the US dollar, reflecting Morocco's trade patterns. In January 2018, Morocco widened the dirham's fluctuation band from ±0.3% to ±2.5% around the central rate as a first step toward a more flexible exchange-rate regime, and the band was widened further to ±5% in March 2020.

Coins and banknotes

Coins currently circulate in denominations of 10 and 20 centimes and ½, 1, 2, 5, and 10 dirhams; the bimetallic 5- and 10-dirham coins feature images of Moroccan heritage and industry. Banknotes circulate in denominations of 20, 50, 100, and 200 dirhams, bearing portraits of Moroccan kings — Hassan II on older series and Mohammed VI on the current series — together with Moroccan architecture and cultural motifs. A 25-dirham commemorative banknote, the world's first printed on a paper-polymer composite substrate, was issued in 2012.
