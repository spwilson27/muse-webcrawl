Cryptocrystalline

Cryptocrystalline
FieldGeology, mineralogy
DefinitionRock texture of crystals too small to be recognised under an optical microscope
Crystal sizeSub-microscopic; requires electron microscopy or X-ray diffraction to resolve

Cryptocrystalline is a rock texture describing crystalline material so finely grained that the individual crystals cannot be distinguished even under a conventional optical microscope — the crystallinity is "hidden" (Greek kryptos). Identification of the crystal structure requires techniques such as X-ray diffraction or electron microscopy.

Terminology

Cryptocrystalline is the finest end of a texture scale that runs from macrocrystalline (crystals visible to the naked eye) through microcrystalline (visible only under a microscope) to cryptocrystalline. The term is often used loosely alongside "microcrystalline" for silica rocks; strictly, chalcedony comprises both fibrous microcrystalline quartz and moganite.

Examples and formation

  • Chalcedony and its varieties — agate (banded), onyx, carnelian, chrysoprase, bloodstone.
  • Chert and flint — sedimentary silica forming nodules in chalk and limestone; flint's fine, even texture made it the premier material for knapped stone tools in prehistory.
  • Jasper — an opaque, impure cryptocrystalline silica.

Such materials typically form by precipitation of silica from solution at low temperatures, or by devitrification and alteration of volcanic glass; obsidian, by contrast, is truly amorphous rather than cryptocrystalline.
