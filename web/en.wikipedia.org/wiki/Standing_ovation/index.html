Standing ovation

A standing ovation is a form of applause where members of a seated audience stand up while applauding after a performance or a speech. It is widely regarded as a special honour awarded to a performer or speaker in recognition of an exceptional achievement.

Usage

Standing ovations are most common in the performing arts, including theatre, concerts, opera, and film festivals, but they also occur in politics, sport, and other public events. At film festivals such as Cannes, the length of standing ovations is often reported in the press as an informal measure of a film's reception. In legislative settings, members of an assembly may rise to applaud a distinguished speaker or a significant address.

Criticism

Some commentators have noted an inflation in the frequency of standing ovations, particularly on Broadway, where audiences may feel social pressure to stand even for average performances, diminishing the gesture's significance. Others argue that the standing ovation is a natural, spontaneous expression of enthusiasm that should not be rationed.
