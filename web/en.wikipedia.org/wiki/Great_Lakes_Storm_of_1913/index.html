The Great Lakes Storm of 1913 (historically referred to as the "Big Blow", the "Freshwater Fury" and the "White Hurricane") was a blizzard with hurricane-force winds that devastated the Great Lakes Basin in the Midwestern United States and Southwestern Ontario, Canada, from November 7 to November 10, 1913. The storm was the deadliest and most destructive natural disaster ever to hit the lakes.

Impact

The storm killed more than 250 people, destroyed 19 ships outright and stranded 19 others. Waves exceeding 35 feet (11 m) and whiteout snowsqualls made navigation nearly impossible. Lake Huron was hit hardest, with eight ships lost with all hands, including the Charles S. Price and the Regina. On land, the storm dumped heavy snow on cities including Cleveland, where it remains one of the worst snowstorms in the city's history.

Legacy

The disaster prompted major improvements in weather forecasting, storm warnings and ship construction standards on the Great Lakes. Wrecks from the storm, such as the Regina and the Carruthers, have been located by divers and shipwreck hunters over the following century.
