Comma below

Comma below is a diacritical mark used in the orthographies of several languages, most prominently Romanian, where the letters Ș/ș (S with comma below) and Ț/ț (T with comma below) represent the sounds /ʃ/ and /ts/ respectively. The comma below resembles a small comma placed under the letter and must not be confused with a cedilla, which is a different diacritic historically substituted for it in much typography.

Romanian Ș and Ț with comma below (left) versus the cedilla forms Ş and Ţ (right).

Use in Romanian

Romanian's standard orthography prescribes S and T with comma below. For decades, however, typewriters, printing, and early computer character sets lacked these glyphs, so the similar-looking cedilla letters Ş and Ţ were widely used instead. Unicode encodes them distinctly: Ș is U+0218, ș is U+0219, Ț is U+021A, and ț is U+021B. Support in fonts and operating systems for the correct comma-below forms became widespread in the 2000s, and Romanian official and educational publishing now requires them.

Other uses

A comma below is also used in transliteration systems for some Semitic and other languages (for example in romanising certain consonants), and in Latvian typography, certain letters (such as ņ and ļ) are traditionally drawn with a mark that looks like a comma below or above, although in Unicode these are encoded as cedillas for historical reasons. In phonetic transcription, a subscript comma-like mark can denote syllabicity or other features depending on the system.

Typography and encoding

The comma below should sit centred beneath the base letter and be clearly comma-shaped, distinct from the hook-shaped cedilla. Because of the long history of substitution, Romanian text on the web contains a mixture of both encodings, which can complicate searching and text processing.
