Marinus Jacobus Hendricus "Rinus" Michels (9 February 1928 – 3 March 2005) was a Dutch football player and coach, widely regarded as one of the greatest managers of all time. He is best known as the architect of Total Football (Totaalvoetbal), the fluid tactical system in which any outfield player can take over the role of any other, developed with AFC Ajax and the Netherlands national team in the 1970s.

Playing career

Michels spent his entire playing career as a forward with Ajax, the club of his native Amsterdam, between 1946 and 1958, scoring regularly in the Dutch league. He won the Netherlands Football League Championship with Ajax in 1947 and 1957. He earned five caps for the Netherlands national team between 1950 and 1954. A back injury ended his playing career in 1958.

Coaching career

Ajax

After coaching amateur sides JOS and DWS, Michels returned to Ajax as head coach in 1965. With players such as Johan Cruyff, Johan Neeskens and Ruud Krol, he built a side that dominated Dutch and European football, winning four Eredivisie titles and culminating in the 1971 European Cup, the first of three consecutive European Cups for the club. His system demanded technical skill, relentless pressing and constant positional interchange.

Barcelona

Michels moved to FC Barcelona in 1971, winning the Copa del Rey in 1978 and the Inter-Cities Fairs Cup trophy play-off. He signed Cruyff in 1973, and the pair's philosophy profoundly influenced Barcelona's later identity.

Netherlands

Michels coached the Netherlands at the 1974 FIFA World Cup, where the "Clockwork Orange" side enthralled the world before losing the final 2–1 to hosts West Germany. He returned for a celebrated second spell in the 1980s, guiding the Dutch to victory at UEFA Euro 1988 — the country's first and only major international trophy — with a team built around Ruud Gullit, Marco van Basten and Frank Rijkaard.

Later career

He also managed 1. FC Köln, winning the DFB-Pokal in 1983, the Los Angeles Aztecs in the NASL, and Bayer Leverkusen. In 1999 FIFA named him Coach of the Century.

Legacy

Michels' Total Football reshaped modern football tactics; his emphasis on pressing, spatial awareness and versatility echoes in the work of Cruyff, Pep Guardiola and many others. The Dutch football coach of the year award is named the Rinus Michels Award in his honour. He died in Aalst, Belgium, in 2005 following heart surgery.
