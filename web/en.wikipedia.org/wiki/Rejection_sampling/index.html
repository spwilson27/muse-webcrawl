Rejection sampling

Rejection sampling, also known as the accept–reject method, is a basic technique used to generate observations from a probability distribution. It is a type of Monte Carlo method, attributed to John von Neumann.

Method

To sample from a target density f(x), one chooses an easier proposal density g(x) and a constant c such that f(x) ≤ c·g(x) everywhere. A candidate point is drawn from g and a uniform random point is drawn under the curve c·g(x); the candidate is accepted if it falls under f(x), and rejected otherwise. The accepted points are distributed exactly according to f.

Properties

The method is simple and general but can be inefficient when the acceptance probability is low, as in high dimensions or when the proposal poorly matches the target. Adaptations such as adaptive rejection sampling improve efficiency for log-concave densities.
