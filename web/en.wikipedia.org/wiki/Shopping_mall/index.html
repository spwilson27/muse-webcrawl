Shopping mall

A shopping mall is a large indoor or open-air complex of retail stores, restaurants, and entertainment venues, typically developed and managed as a single property with common walkways and parking. Anchored by department stores or supermarkets, malls became a defining feature of suburban life in the 20th century and spread worldwide.

History

Precursors include ancient markets, 19th-century arcades, and bazaars. The first fully enclosed, climate-controlled mall is generally considered to be Southdale Center in Edina, Minnesota, opened in 1956 and designed by Victor Gruen, who envisioned malls as civic centres for the suburbs. Northland Center near Detroit (1954) pioneered the open-air regional mall format. By the 1980s giant "megamalls" such as the West Edmonton Mall in Canada and the Mall of America in Minnesota (1992) combined shopping with theme parks and aquariums.

Decline and reinvention

From the 1990s, department-store consolidation, e-commerce, and overbuilding led to widespread mall closures in North America — the "dead mall" phenomenon — while malls in Asia and the Middle East, such as the Dubai Mall, grew ever larger and more luxurious. Surviving centres increasingly mix in housing, offices, medical services, and experiential tenants, or are redeveloped as mixed-use town centres.

Types

Industry classifications range from small neighbourhood and community centres to regional and super-regional malls, outlet centres, and vertical malls in dense cities. Design conventions — inward-facing shops, controlled entrances, and deliberate disorientation known as the "Gruen transfer" — aim to maximise browsing time and spending.
