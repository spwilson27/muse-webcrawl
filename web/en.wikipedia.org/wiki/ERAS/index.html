Enhanced recovery after surgery

Enhanced recovery after surgery (ERAS) is a perioperative care pathway designed to achieve early recovery for patients undergoing major surgery. Initially termed "fast-track surgery", ERAS protocols re-examine traditional practices — prolonged fasting, routine bowel preparation, heavy opioid use, bed rest — replacing them with evidence-based practices. It was pioneered by Professor Henrik Kehlet in the 1990s in colorectal surgery in Denmark, and institutionalised by the ERAS Society, founded in 2010.

Elements

ERAS programmes bundle multiple interventions across the patient journey:

  • Preoperative: patient education and counselling, no prolonged fasting (clear fluids up to 2 hours before anaesthesia), carbohydrate loading, no or selective bowel preparation, antibiotic and thrombosis prophylaxis, and optimisation of comorbidities.
  • Intraoperative: short-acting anaesthetic agents, regional/neuraxial blocks where appropriate, minimally invasive surgical technique, avoidance of drains and nasogastric tubes, maintenance of normothermia and euvolemia (goal-directed fluid therapy), and multimodal opioid-sparing analgesia.
  • Postoperative: early oral nutrition, early mobilisation, chewing gum and early feeding to prevent ileus, nausea prophylaxis, and defined discharge criteria.

Evidence and adoption

Meta-analyses in colorectal surgery show ERAS reduces overall complications by roughly 30–50% and shortens hospital stay by about two to three days compared with conventional care, without increasing readmissions. Protocols have since been published by the ERAS Society for many specialties, including gynecology, urology, hepatobiliary, thoracic, cardiac, orthopedic (hip and knee arthroplasty), and emergency surgery. Audit of compliance is a core principle: outcomes improve with higher protocol adherence, and the ERAS Interactive Audit System (EIAS) is used by participating centres for benchmarking.
