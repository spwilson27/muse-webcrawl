Xie He (artist)

Xie He (Chinese: 謝赫; pinyin: Xiè Hè; fl. 5th–6th century CE) was a Chinese art historian, critic, and painter of the Liu Song and Southern Qi dynasties. He is best known as the author of the Guhua Pinlu ("Record of the Classification of Old Painters"), the earliest surviving systematic work of Chinese art criticism.

The Six Principles

In the preface to his treatise, Xie He set out the famous Six Principles (liufa) of painting, which shaped Chinese — and later East Asian — art theory for more than a millennium:

  1. Spirit resonance and life movement (qiyun shengdong)
  2. Bone method in the use of the brush
  3. Correspondence to the object in depicting forms
  4. Suitability to type in applying colors
  5. Division and planning in composition
  6. Transmission by copying (studying the old masters)

The first principle, spirit resonance, was regarded as a gift that could not be taught; the others were progressively more technical. Xie's ranking of earlier painters into graded classes also established a model for connoisseurship.

Legacy

Later theorists, most famously Zhang Yanyuan in the Tang dynasty, quoted and commented on the Six Principles extensively. The principles were eventually transmitted to Japan, where they influenced evaluations of painting in the nihonga tradition.
