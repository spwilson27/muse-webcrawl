Desmond Rebellions

The Desmond Rebellions occurred in 1569–1573 and 1579–1583 in Munster, Ireland. They were led by the FitzGerald dynasty, the Earls of Desmond, and their allies against the Elizabethan Government of Ireland, which sought to extend its control and impose English law and Protestantism on the province.

First rebellion (1569–1573)

The first rebellion was led by James FitzMaurice FitzGerald, cousin of the imprisoned Earl, in opposition to the colonising schemes of Sir Henry Sidney and adventurers such as Sir Peter Carew, who claimed lands in south Munster. FitzMaurice surrendered in 1573 after a brutal campaign by Sir John Perrot.

Second rebellion (1579–1583)

FitzMaurice returned in 1579 with a small papal-sponsored force, landing at Smerwick and proclaiming a holy war. Gerald, the Earl of Desmond, reluctantly joined the revolt after government forces plundered his lands. A Spanish and Italian expeditionary force was besieged and massacred at Smerwick in 1580. English forces under Lord Grey de Wilton and others used scorched-earth tactics, producing a devastating famine; contemporary estimates suggested some 30,000 deaths in Munster. The Earl was captured and killed on 11 November 1583.

Aftermath

The Desmond lands — some 480,000 acres — were confiscated and distributed to English "undertakers" in the Plantation of Munster, a model for later plantations in Ireland. The rebellions tied down large English resources and foreshadowed the Nine Years' War.
