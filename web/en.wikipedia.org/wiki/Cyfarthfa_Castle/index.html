Cyfarthfa Castle

Cyfarthfa Castle (Welsh: Castell Cyfarthfa) is a castellated mansion that was the home of the Crawshay family, ironmasters of Merthyr Tydfil in South Wales. The house and its extensive parkland now house a museum and art gallery, are managed by Merthyr Tydfil Leisure Trust, and are open to the public. It is a Grade I listed building.

History

The castle was built in 1824–1825 for William Crawshay II, owner of the Cyfarthfa Ironworks, to the designs of architect Robert Lugar. Standing on an eminence overlooking the works and the town, it was intended as a grand family residence befitting the wealth generated by the iron industry; the building cost around £30,000. The castellated Gothic style, with its battlements and towers, was symbolic of the Crawshays' industrial power.

The Crawshay family remained at Cyfarthfa until the early 20th century. In 1908 the borough council purchased the castle and its grounds, opening them as a museum and public park in 1910.

Museum and art gallery

The museum houses collections relating to the industrial history of Merthyr Tydfil, including artefacts of the iron industry, the story of the world's first steam locomotive journey (Richard Trevithick's Pen-y-darren locomotive ran nearby in 1804), and social history displays. The art gallery holds works including pieces by Penry Williams and other Welsh artists.

Grounds

The grounds include a lake, woodland and formal gardens and form Cyfarthfa Park, a major recreational space for the town.
