Borzoi

Borzoi
Origin: Russia
Group: Sighthound
Height: 68–85 cm
Coat: Long, silky

The Borzoi (Russian: борзая, "swift") is a Russian breed of sighthound dog, formerly known in English-speaking countries as the Russian wolfhound. Developed by the Russian aristocracy for coursing wolves and hares on the open steppe, it is distinguished by its tall, slender build, long narrow head and silky coat.

History

Borzois descend from hunting dogs of the Russian nobility, with ancestry plausibly including the Saluki-like coursing dogs of Central Asia crossed with a thick-coated Russian breed for endurance in cold weather. The first written standard dates to 1650. Grand Duke Nicholas Nikolaevich maintained the famous Perchino kennel, which produced many of the dogs exported to Europe and America in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. After the Russian Revolution the breed survived mainly through foreign stock.

Characteristics

Males stand about 75–85 cm at the shoulder and weigh 34–47 kg. The coat is long and silky, flat or wavy, in almost any colour, most traditionally white with markings. Like other sighthounds, Borzois hunt by sight and are capable of great speed over short distances. Temperament is typically quiet, gentle and dignified; the breed is aloof with strangers but affectionate with its family, and retains a strong prey drive outdoors.

Health

Borzois are generally healthy with a lifespan of about 9–14 years. Like other deep-chested breeds they are susceptible to gastric dilatation-volvulus (bloat), and like other sighthounds they can be sensitive to certain anaesthetics.
