La Niña

Phenomenon: Ocean–atmosphere climate pattern
Counterpart: El Niño
Region: Equatorial Pacific Ocean

La Niña is a coupled ocean–atmosphere phenomenon that is the colder counterpart of El Niño, as part of the broader El Niño–Southern Oscillation (ENSO) climate pattern. During La Niña, sea surface temperatures across the eastern equatorial Pacific are lower than average, and the easterly trade winds strengthen.

Effects

La Niña typically brings wetter conditions to Australia, Indonesia, and parts of South America such as Colombia, drier conditions to the southwestern United States and Peru's coast, and an increased number of Atlantic hurricanes. Its effects often persist for one to three years, longer than El Niño events.

Monitoring

Agencies such as NOAA track La Niña using sea-surface temperature anomalies in the Niño 3.4 region; an event is declared when anomalies fall at or below −0.5 °C for sustained periods together with matching atmospheric changes.
