Human enhancement

Human enhancement (HE) is the natural, artificial, or technological alteration of the human body in order to enhance physical or mental capabilities. It encompasses interventions that go beyond restoring health or treating disease, aiming instead to augment abilities beyond typical human ranges — from pharmaceuticals and prosthetics to genetic modification and neural interfaces.

Forms of enhancement

  • Physical enhancement — performance-enhancing drugs in sport, advanced prosthetics, exoskeletons, and cosmetic surgery.
  • Cognitive enhancement — nootropics and stimulants such as modafinil and methylphenidate used by healthy individuals, as well as brain stimulation techniques.
  • Genetic enhancement — germline or somatic gene editing (for example with CRISPR) intended to improve traits or prevent disease.
  • Reproductive technologies — preimplantation genetic diagnosis and embryo selection.
  • Life extension — interventions intended to slow or reverse aging.
  • Mood enhancement — antidepressants and related drugs used to alter personality or mood in healthy people.

Ethical debate

Human enhancement is the subject of extensive ethical and political debate. Critics, sometimes called bioconservatives, argue that enhancement threatens human dignity, could widen social inequality between the enhanced and unenhanced, and may distort competition in sport and education. Defenders — including transhumanists and philosophers such as Julian Savulescu and Nick Bostrom — argue that enhancement is continuous with accepted practices like education and vaccination, and that individuals should generally be free to improve their capacities ("procreative beneficence" and related doctrines). The distinction between therapy and enhancement is contested, since many technologies sit on a continuum between treatment and augmentation.

Regulation

Regulatory responses vary: performance-enhancing drugs are banned in competitive sport by bodies such as the World Anti-Doping Agency; germline genome editing is prohibited or heavily restricted in most countries, a restriction thrown into relief by the 2018 He Jiankui affair, in which gene-edited babies were born in China. Scholars and policy bodies continue to debate frameworks for governing emerging enhancement technologies.
