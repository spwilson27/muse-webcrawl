Napier, New Zealand

Napier (Māori: Ahuriri) is a city on the eastern coast of New Zealand's North Island and the seat of the Hawke's Bay region. Together with neighbouring Hastings it forms one of the country's main urban areas. Napier is internationally known for its Art Deco architecture.

History

The area was settled by Māori, and the town was founded in the 1850s, named after Sir Charles Napier. On 3 February 1931 the Hawke's Bay earthquake devastated Napier, killing 256 people in the region, raising land around Ahuriri Lagoon and destroying the city centre.

Art Deco heritage

Rebuilt in the early 1930s in the styles then fashionable — Art Deco, Spanish Mission and Stripped Classical — Napier's city centre is now regarded as one of the finest collections of Art Deco buildings in the world. The annual Art Deco Festival draws visitors each February.

Economy

Napier is a major port (Port of Napier) exporting wine, fruit, wool and timber from the surrounding Hawke's Bay wine region and horticultural plains.
