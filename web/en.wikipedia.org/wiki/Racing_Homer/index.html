Racing Homer

The Racing Homer is a breed of domestic pigeon developed through selective breeding from the rock dove (Columba livia). It is bred and trained for pigeon racing, in which birds are released at a distant point and timed as they return to their home loft.

History

Homing pigeons were used as messengers for millennia, but systematic racing began in Belgium in the early nineteenth century, where breeders crossed several homing varieties to produce faster, hardier birds. The sport spread to Britain, the Netherlands, Germany and North America. Racing Homers were still employed for military communication in both World Wars; famous birds such as Cher Ami were decorated for their service.

Racing

Races range from about 100 km to over 1,000 km. Birds are transported to the release point in baskets, released together, and clocked on arrival using leg bands and traditional clocking devices or modern electronic timing systems. Speed is calculated as distance divided by time, expressed in metres or yards per minute. Elite birds can command very high prices — the Belgian racing pigeon New Kim sold for about €1.6 million in 2020.

Training

Young birds are settled to the loft, taught to enter through a trap, and gradually trained from increasing distances in the direction of expected races. Motivation systems such as widowhood (showing a racing cock his hen before basketting) are widely used.
