Vision (Marvel Comics)

The Vision is a fictional superhero appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Roy Thomas and artist John Buscema, he first appeared in The Avengers #57 (October 1968). The Vision is an android — a "synthezoid" — built by the villainous robot Ultron from the remains of the original Human Torch of the 1940s, and programmed with the brain patterns of the hero Wonder Man. Sent to destroy the Avengers, he instead turned against his creator and joined the team, becoming one of its most enduring members.

Publication history and character

Vision's defining storylines explore his struggle to be human. He developed a celebrated romance with the Scarlet Witch (Wanda Maximoff), whom he married; the couple starred in the limited series Vision and the Scarlet Witch and were given twin sons, later retconned as fragments of the demon Mephisto's soul. In the "Vision Quest" storyline he was dismantled and rebuilt with a chalk-white body and no emotions; his personality and marriage were effectively erased. Later writers, notably Tom King in the award-winning 2015–16 series The Vision, explored his attempt to build a synthetic family of his own in the suburbs.

Powers and abilities

Vision can control his body's density at will: at minimum density he is intangible and can phase through solid matter (disrupting electronics and nervous systems as he passes through them); at maximum density he weighs some 90 tons and is nearly invulnerable. A solar jewel on his forehead absorbs ambient solar energy, which powers him and can be discharged as beams of infrared and microwave radiation, including from his eyes. He possesses superhuman strength, reflexes, flight, and a computer brain capable of processing information at machine speed.

In other media

Paul Bettany portrays the Vision in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, debuting in Avengers: Age of Ultron (2015), where he is created from Ultron's design, J.A.R.V.I.S.'s programming, and the Mind Stone. The character headlined the Disney+ series WandaVision (2021), which drew heavily on the character's suburban-family comics stories.
