House of Habsburg-Lorraine

The House of Habsburg-Lorraine (German: Haus Habsburg-Lothringen) is the ruling house of Austria and later Austria-Hungary from 1780 until 1918. It arose from the marriage of Maria Theresa of Austria, heiress of the House of Habsburg, to Francis Stephen of Lorraine.

Origins

When Emperor Charles VI died in 1740 without a male heir, his daughter Maria Theresa succeeded to the Habsburg hereditary lands under the Pragmatic Sanction. Her husband, Duke Francis Stephen of Lorraine, was elected Holy Roman Emperor in 1745 as Francis I. Their descendants combined the two names into the dynastic name Habsburg-Lorraine.

Rule

The house produced the emperors Joseph II, Leopold II, Francis II (later Francis I of Austria), Ferdinand I, Franz Joseph I, and Charles I. Members of cadet branches ruled as grand dukes of Tuscany and dukes of Modena, and Maximilian of Habsburg-Lorraine briefly reigned as Emperor of Mexico (1864–1867).

After 1918

The monarchy was abolished at the end of the First World War, and the Habsburg Law of 1919 exiled the family from Austria and confiscated dynastic property. The headship later passed to Otto von Habsburg, eldest son of Charles I, who became a member of the European Parliament. The current head of the house is Karl von Habsburg.
