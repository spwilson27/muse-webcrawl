Filter (mathematics)

In mathematics, a filter is a special subset of a partially ordered set (poset). Filters appear in order theory, lattice theory and topology, where they provide a way of formalising notions of "large" sets and convergence. The concept was introduced by Henri Cartan in 1937.

Definition

A subset F of a partially ordered set (P, ≤) is called a filter if it satisfies the following conditions:

  1. F is non-empty.
  2. For every x, y in F, there exists an element z in F such that z ≤ x and z ≤ y (i.e. F is downward directed).
  3. For every x in F and every y in P with x ≤ y, y is also in F (i.e. F is upward closed).

A filter is proper if it is not equal to the whole of P; on a Boolean algebra this is equivalent to the filter not containing the bottom element. In a lattice, the conditions can be restated by requiring that F be closed under finite meets and upward closed.

Filters on a set

The most common case takes P to be the power set of a set X, ordered by inclusion. A filter on X is then a family of subsets of X that does not contain the empty set, is closed under finite intersection, and is closed under taking supersets. Intuitively, the members of a filter are the "large" subsets of X.

Examples include the Fréchet filter of cofinite subsets of an infinite set, and the neighbourhood filter of a point in a topological space.

Ultrafilters

An ultrafilter is a maximal proper filter: one that is not strictly contained in any other proper filter. On a set, an ultrafilter has the additional property that for every subset A of X, exactly one of A and its complement belongs to the filter. Ultrafilters are central to the construction of ultraproducts in model theory and to the Stone–Čech compactification in topology.

Applications

Filters generalise the notion of a sequence: in a topological space, a filter is said to converge to a point if it contains the neighbourhood filter of that point. This allows convergence to be studied in spaces that are not first-countable, where sequences alone do not suffice. Filters are also used in forcing in set theory, in the theory of ideals (the dual notion), and in point-free topology.
