Omar Bongo Ondimba (born Albert-Bernard Bongo; 30 December 1935 – 8 June 2009) was a Gabonese politician who was the second President of Gabon from 1967 until his death in 2009. Serving nearly 42 years, he was Africa's longest-serving ruler at the time of his death and one of the longest-serving non-royal heads of state in the world.

Rise and rule

The youngest of twelve children of a Bateke family, Bongo served in the French Air Force, worked in the postal service, and rose rapidly after independence in the administration of President Léon M'ba, becoming vice-president in 1966. On M'ba's death in November 1967, Bongo succeeded him. In 1968 he founded the Gabonese Democratic Party (PDG) as the country's sole legal party; he converted to Islam in 1973, taking the name Omar, and later added Ondimba. Multiparty politics was restored in 1990 under street pressure, but Bongo won the elections of 1993, 1998, and 2005 — each disputed by the opposition — helped by oil revenues that made Gabon one of Africa's richest states per capita, though wealth remained starkly unequally distributed.

Foreign relations and criticism

Bongo was a cornerstone of Françafrique, France's network of influence in its former African colonies, and a regional mediator in Congolese, Chadian, and Central African conflicts. His rule was marked by corruption allegations, including the Elf affair in France and investigations into his family's extensive foreign property holdings; French magistrates pursued "ill-gotten gains" cases against his assets. At home he was credited with keeping Gabon stable and comparatively prosperous, though critics described an authoritarian patronage state.

Death and succession

Bongo died of a heart attack while being treated for intestinal cancer in Barcelona on 8 June 2009. After a contested succession, his son Ali Bongo Ondimba won the August 2009 presidential election and ruled until he was deposed in a coup in August 2023.
