Shin Kokin Wakashū

The Shin Kokin Wakashū ("New Collection of Poems Ancient and Modern"), often shortened to Shin Kokinshū, is an imperial anthology of Japanese waka poetry compiled in the early 13th century. It is the eighth imperial anthology and is considered one of the finest achievements of classical Japanese poetry.

Compilation

The anthology was ordered by the retired Emperor Go-Toba in 1201 and completed around 1205. Its principal editors included Fujiwara no Teika, Fujiwara no Ietaka, Minamoto no Michitomo, Fujiwara no Ariie, and Jakuren (who died before completion). It contains roughly 2,000 poems in twenty books, arranged by season and theme, with a Japanese preface attributed to Fujiwara no Yoshitsune and a Chinese preface.

Style and influence

The Shin Kokinshū embodies the aesthetic ideals of yūgen (mysterious depth) and ushin (deep feeling), favouring evocative, allusive verse by poets such as Saigyō, Fujiwara no Shunzei, Princess Shikishi, and Teika himself. Techniques like honkadori (allusive variation on earlier poems) pervade the collection. It profoundly influenced later Japanese literature, linked verse (renga), and the tea ceremony aesthetic.
