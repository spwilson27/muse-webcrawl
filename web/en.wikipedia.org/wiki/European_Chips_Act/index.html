European Chips Act

Image: Semiconductor manufacturing (placeholder)

The European Chips Act is a legislative package of the European Union intended to strengthen Europe's semiconductor ecosystem, increase the security of supply, and raise the EU's share of global chip production to 20 percent by 2030. Proposed by the European Commission in February 2022, the regulation entered into force on 21 September 2023.

Content

The act rests on three pillars: the Chips for Europe Initiative, supporting research, pilot lines, and design capacity; a framework to attract large-scale manufacturing investment, including first-of-a-kind facilities eligible for state aid; and a coordination and crisis-monitoring mechanism among member states to anticipate shortages. The package mobilises more than €43 billion in public and private investment.

Context

The act followed the global semiconductor shortage of 2020–2023 and parallels industrial policy measures elsewhere, notably the United States CHIPS and Science Act of 2022. Under it, companies including Intel, TSMC, STMicroelectronics, and GlobalFoundries announced major fabrication plants in Germany, France, and Italy.
