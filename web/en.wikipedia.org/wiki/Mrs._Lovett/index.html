Mrs. Nellie Lovett is a fictional character appearing in the story of Sweeney Todd. She is the barber Todd's accomplice and landlady, a widowed baker whose failing pie shop on Fleet Street in London becomes prosperous when she begins baking Todd's victims into her meat pies. The character first appeared in the penny dreadful The String of Pearls (1846–47) and has been reshaped by every major adaptation, reaching her most famous form in Stephen Sondheim's musical Sweeney Todd: The Demon Barber of Fleet Street (1979).

Character

In Sondheim's version (book by Hugh Wheeler), Mrs. Lovett is pragmatic, cheerful and amorally enterprising: it is she who proposes the pie scheme in the duet "A Little Priest", and her love for Todd — and her concealment that his wife Lucy is alive as a beggar woman — drives the tragedy. When Todd discovers her deception he throws her into her own bakehouse oven, moments before his own death. In earlier versions she is a more straightforward villainess; the 1847 stage adaptation has her strangled by a victim's spirit in some retellings, while the original novel leaves her arrested and dying in prison.

Portrayals

Angela Lansbury originated the musical role on Broadway in 1979, winning a Tony Award; Patti LuPone played her in later productions, and Helena Bonham Carter played the role in Tim Burton's 2007 film. Bryn Terfel and Emma Thompson headlined concert versions. The role requires a mezzo-soprano with formidable verbal dexterity for patter songs such as "The Worst Pies in London".
