Closed timelike curve

In mathematical physics, a closed timelike curve (CTC) is a world line in a Lorentzian manifold, of a material particle in spacetime, that is "closed", returning to its starting point. This possibility was first discovered by Willem Jacob van Stockum in 1937 and later confirmed by Kurt Gödel in 1949, who discovered a solution to the equations of general relativity (the Gödel metric) allowing CTCs known as closed timelike curves.

Examples

CTCs appear in exact solutions of general relativity including the interior of rotating Kerr black holes, the Tipler cylinder, traversable wormholes, and certain cosmological models. Their existence would permit time travel into the past and raises paradoxes such as the grandfather paradox.

Chronology protection

Stephen Hawking proposed the chronology protection conjecture, according to which the laws of physics would prevent the formation of closed timelike curves. The Novikov self-consistency principle instead holds that only self-consistent solutions occur, so paradoxes cannot arise.
