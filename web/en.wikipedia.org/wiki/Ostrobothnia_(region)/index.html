Ostrobothnia (region)

Country: Finland

Capital: Vaasa

Population: ~175,000

Official languages: Swedish (majority), Finnish

Finnish name: Pohjanmaa; Swedish: Österbotten

Ostrobothnia (Finnish: Pohjanmaa; Swedish: Österbotten) is a region of western Finland on the eastern shore of the Gulf of Bothnia, one of the country's 19 regions. Its capital and largest city is Vaasa. The modern region forms the southern, largely Swedish-speaking coastal strip of the historical province of Ostrobothnia, which once extended far to the east and north.

Geography

The region is exceptionally flat: post-glacial land uplift here is among the fastest on Earth, at roughly 8–9 mm per year, continuously pushing the coastline westward and creating new land. The Kvarken archipelago, shared with Sweden, is a UNESCO World Heritage site recognised for this ongoing geological process.

Language and culture

Ostrobothnia is Finland's most Swedish-speaking mainland region: a majority of residents, around 50–60%, speak Swedish as their first language, and municipalities such as Korsholm, Närpes and Larsmo are overwhelmingly Swedish-speaking. The region is known for greenhouse vegetable cultivation around Närpes, shipbuilding, and the energy technology cluster around Vaasa, sometimes called EnergyVaasa.

Municipalities

The region comprises 14 municipalities, including Vaasa, Jakobstad (Pietarsaari), Kristiinankaupunki (Kristinestad), Kaskinen, Korsnäs, Laihia, Malax, Korsholm (Mustasaari), Närpes, Pedersöre, Larsmo, Kronoby, Vörå and New Karelia of the coast — administered in both national languages.
