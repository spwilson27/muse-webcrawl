2017 Las Vegas shooting

The 2017 Las Vegas shooting occurred on October 1, 2017, when Stephen Paddock, a 64-year-old former accountant and high-stakes gambler, opened fire from a suite on the 32nd floor of the Mandalay Bay hotel into the crowd of the Route 91 Harvest country music festival on the Las Vegas Strip below. In about ten minutes he fired more than 1,000 rounds, killing 58 people that night — two further victims died of their injuries in 2019 and 2020 — and wounding hundreds more. It is the deadliest mass shooting committed by an individual in modern United States history. Paddock was found dead of a self-inflicted gunshot wound when police breached the suite.

Attack

Paddock had stockpiled 24 firearms in the suite, most of them rifles, many fitted with bump stocks that allowed them to simulate automatic fire, along with high-capacity magazines. He had also placed surveillance cameras in the hallway. The shooting began at about 10:05 p.m. during the headline performance by Jason Aldean, and initially many concertgoers mistook the gunfire for fireworks. Hotel security guard Jesus Campos, who was shot in the leg while investigating a door alarm before the mass shooting began, helped direct police to the suite. Officers breached the room at about 11:20 p.m.

Investigation and motive

The FBI and Las Vegas Metropolitan Police Department investigations found that Paddock acted alone, left no manifesto, and had no established ideological motive; the FBI's Behavioral Analysis Unit concluded he sought notoriety and had planned the attack meticulously over at least a year, spurred in part by a steep decline in his finances and status as a gambler. No definitive motive has ever been determined.

Aftermath

The shooting revived debate over gun control in the United States. Bump stocks were banned federally by regulation in 2018 (the rule took effect in 2019). MGM Resorts and its insurers agreed to a settlement of up to $800 million with victims and families in 2019. The festival site remained closed, and annual commemorations are held in Las Vegas for the "58" victims originally counted.
