Božić (custom)

Božić
Observed bySouth Slavs (Serbs, Croats, Bosnians, Montenegrins, Macedonians)
Meaning"Little God" — Christmas
Key customsBadnjak (Yule log), česnica bread, polažajnik (first guest)

Božić is the South Slavic word for Christmas, literally a diminutive of Bog, "God"—"little God," referring to the infant Christ (compare Serbian Božić, Croatian Božić). Beyond the word itself, "Božić" denotes a rich complex of folk customs surrounding Christmas among Serbs, Croats, Bosniaks, Montenegrins, and other South Slavs, many of them of great antiquity and blending Christian observance with pre-Christian winter-solstice ritual.

Customs

On Badnji dan (Christmas Eve), the head of the household traditionally cuts a young oak—the badnjak—which is brought into the home with ceremony and burned in the hearth through the night, its sparks greeted with wishes for health and prosperity. Straw is spread on the floor, and a fasting meal is served. At the Christmas breakfast, the family shares the česnica, a round bread baked with a coin inside; whoever finds the coin is expected to have good luck in the coming year. The polažajnik, the first guest to enter the house on Christmas Day, stirs the badnjak embers and receives gifts, and is believed to influence the household's fortune for the year.

Dates

Roman Catholic Croats celebrate on 25 December. Orthodox Serbs, Montenegrins, and Macedonians who follow the Julian calendar celebrate on 7 January. The greeting exchanged is Hristos se rodi! ("Christ is born!"), answered Vaistinu se rodi! ("Truly He is born!") among Orthodox Serbs, and Sretan Božić ("Happy Christmas") in Croatian.
