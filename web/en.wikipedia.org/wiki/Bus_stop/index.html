Bus stop

A bus stop is a designated place where buses stop for passengers to get on and off the bus. The construction of bus stops tends to reflect the level of usage, with simple poles and signs at lightly used stops and shelters with seating and real-time information at busy locations. Bus stops are, in some locations, clustered into transport hubs, stations, or interchanges that allow interchange between routes or other modes of transport.

Types

  • Flag stop — a minimal stop marked only by a sign ("flag"), where riders hail the bus.
  • Shelter stop — includes a weather shelter, bench, timetable, and sometimes lighting and real-time departure displays.
  • Request stop — buses stop only when requested by a passenger wishing to alight or signalled by one waiting.
  • Bus station / terminus — a larger facility where routes begin and end, with multiple bays.

Design and infrastructure

Design elements may include a raised kerb or Kassel kerb for level boarding, tactile paving for visually impaired passengers, lay-bys or bus bulbs that keep the bus in the traffic lane, and clear signage showing route numbers and destinations. Spacing between stops affects journey time and accessibility; transit agencies balance walk distance against operating speed, with typical urban spacings of 200–400 metres.

Fake bus stops

Some nursing homes for people with dementia install fake, or "phantom", bus stops that will never be served. Patients who become restless and wish to "go home" wait at the stop and calm down, having forgotten their intention by the time staff redirect them; the practice originated in Germany.
