1930 FIFA World Cup

The 1930 FIFA World Cup was the inaugural FIFA World Cup, held in Uruguay from 13 to 30 July 1930. Thirteen teams took part — seven from South America, four from Europe and two from North America — all by invitation rather than qualification. Hosts Uruguay defeated Argentina 4–2 in the final at the Estadio Centenario in Montevideo to become the first world champions.

Background

FIFA chose Uruguay because the country had won the 1924 and 1928 Olympic football tournaments and was celebrating its centenary in 1930; Uruguay offered to pay the travelling expenses of visiting teams. Many European nations declined the long sea voyage during the Great Depression, and only France, Belgium, Romania and Yugoslavia made the trip, several after personal appeals by FIFA president Jules Rimet.

Tournament

The teams were divided into four groups. In the semi-finals Uruguay beat Yugoslavia 6–1 and Argentina beat the United States by the same score. The final, refereed by Belgian John Langenus, was preceded by a dispute over which team's ball would be used; a compromise saw each half played with a different ball. Uruguay trailed 2–1 at half-time but scored three times after the break.
