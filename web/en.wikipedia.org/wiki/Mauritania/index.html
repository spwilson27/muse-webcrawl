Mauritania

Islamic Republic of Mauritania
Capital: Nouakchott
Official language: Arabic
Population: about 4.7 million (2023)
Currency: Ouguiya (MRU)

Mauritania, officially the Islamic Republic of Mauritania, is a country in Northwest Africa. It is bordered by the Atlantic Ocean to the west, Western Sahara to the north, Algeria to the northeast, Mali to the east and southeast, and Senegal to the southwest. The country derives its name from the ancient Berber kingdom of Mauretania. Its capital and largest city is Nouakchott.

Geography

Roughly 90 percent of Mauritania's territory lies within the Sahara desert. The population is concentrated in the south along the Senegal River and in the capital. Notable features include the Richat Structure (the "Eye of the Sahara") and the ancient caravan towns of Chinguetti and Ouadane.

History

The region was Islamized by Berber and Arab groups over centuries. France colonized the territory in the early twentieth century; Mauritania gained independence in 1960 under Moktar Ould Daddah. Its politics since have been marked by coups, including those of 2005 and 2008. Slavery, though officially abolished (1981) and later criminalized, remains a subject of international concern regarding its continued practice.

Economy

The economy depends on mining (iron ore and gold), fishing, agriculture and livestock, with offshore natural gas development (the Greater Tortue Ahmeyim field shared with Senegal) beginning in the 2020s.

Demographics

The population comprises Arab-Berber Moors and Black African groups including the Halpulaar, Soninke and Wolof. Arabic is official; French is widely used.
