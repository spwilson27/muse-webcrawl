Creating Helpful Incentives to Produce Semiconductors

The Creating Helpful Incentives to Produce Semiconductors (CHIPS) programme is the set of United States federal incentives established by the CHIPS and Science Act of 2022 to strengthen domestic semiconductor manufacturing, research and workforce development. The programme is administered by the CHIPS Program Office within the Department of Commerce.

Funding

The Act appropriated about $52.7 billion for semiconductors, including roughly $39 billion in direct manufacturing incentives for constructing, expanding or modernising fabrication facilities ("fabs") in the United States, $13.2 billion for research, development and workforce programmes, and additional funds for defence and international supply-chain purposes. It also created a 25% investment tax credit for semiconductor manufacturing equipment and plants.

Awards

Beginning in late 2023, the Commerce Department announced preliminary and then final awards to companies including TSMC, Samsung, Intel, Micron, GlobalFoundries, Microchip Technology and BAE Systems, supporting new fabs in states such as Arizona, Texas, New York, Idaho and Ohio. Awards combine grants with, in some cases, federal loans, and carry conditions on workforce training, childcare for workers, and "guardrails" restricting recipients from expanding advanced manufacturing in China for ten years.

Aims and debate

The programme responds to the decline of the American share of global chip production from about 37% in 1990 to roughly 10–12% by the 2020s, and to supply disruptions and geopolitical concerns about dependence on East Asian fabrication. Supporters describe it as essential industrial policy for economic and national security; critics question its cost, implementation speed and risk of subsidising projects companies might have built anyway.
