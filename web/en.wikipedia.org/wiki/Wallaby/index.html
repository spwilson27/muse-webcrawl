Wallaby

Wallaby
Family: Macropodidae
Range: Australia and New Guinea
Diet: Herbivorous

A wallaby is any of several species of small or medium-sized macropod marsupials native to Australia and New Guinea. Wallabies are not a single taxonomic group but are generally smaller than kangaroos and wallaroos within the family Macropodidae, which contains roughly thirty species commonly called wallabies.

Description

Wallabies have powerful hind legs adapted for hopping, large hind feet, muscular tails used for balance and support, and forelimbs with clawed digits. Females carry their young, called joeys, in a pouch. Species range from the tiny dwarf wallaby to agile wallabies of open grassland, rock wallabies of rugged terrain and swamp wallabies of dense forest.

Ecology

Most wallabies are nocturnal or crepuscular grazers and browsers. Introduced populations exist in New Zealand, the United Kingdom and elsewhere. Some species, such as the bridled nail-tail wallaby, are endangered owing to habitat loss and introduced predators. See also kangaroo.
