Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church

The Malankara Orthodox Syrian Church, also known as the Indian Orthodox Church, is an autocephalous Oriental Orthodox church centred in Kerala, India. It traces its origins to the evangelistic activity of Saint Thomas the Apostle in the first century CE and is headed by the Catholicos of the East and Malankara Metropolitan, seated at Devalokam, Kottayam.

History

The Saint Thomas Christians of the Malabar Coast maintained ties with the Church of the East and later the Syriac Orthodox Church. After the Coonan Cross Oath of 1653, protesting Portuguese Jesuit domination, the community split. In 1912 a Catholicate was established in India, asserting the church's autocephaly. A schism in the twentieth century produced two factions — the Orthodox Church and the Jacobite Syrian Christian Church — whose long-running dispute over church properties has been the subject of major Indian court rulings, including the Supreme Court verdict of 2017 favouring the Orthodox faction.

Faith and structure

The church belongs to the Oriental Orthodox communion, sharing faith with the Coptic, Syriac, Armenian, Ethiopian, and Eritrean churches. It uses the West Syriac Rite in Malayalam and Syriac. It comprises dozens of dioceses in India and abroad, serving a large diaspora in the Middle East, Europe, and North America.
