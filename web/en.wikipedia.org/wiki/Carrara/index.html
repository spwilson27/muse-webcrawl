Carrara is a city in Tuscany, northern Italy, in the province of Massa and Carrara, famous worldwide for the white and blue-grey marble quarried in the nearby Apuan Alps since Roman times.

Carrara marble

The quarries — some 300 are active — have supplied stone for monuments from the Pantheon and Trajan's Column to Michelangelo's David; Michelangelo personally selected blocks at Carrara. Varieties include statuario, calacatta, and bardiglio. The marble is metamorphosed limestone formed from Jurassic carbonate sediments.

History

Quarrying began under the Romans at nearby Luni. The city grew around the trade, passing among the Malaspina and Cybo families; in the 19th century its quarrymen (cavatori) became a stronghold of anarchism — Carrara hosted the founding congress of the Italian anarchist federation in 1945 and remains a symbol of that movement.

Sights

Sights include the Romanesque-Gothic cathedral with its rose window, the Ducal Palace (now the Academy of Fine Arts), and the marble museum. The city is paired administratively with Massa as a provincial capital.
