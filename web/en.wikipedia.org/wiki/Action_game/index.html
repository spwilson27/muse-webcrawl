An action game is a video game genre that emphasises physical challenges, including hand–eye coordination and reaction time. The genre includes a large variety of subgenres, such as fighting games, beat 'em ups, shooter games, and platform games. Combat is typically a primary feature, and the player usually controls a character who must navigate levels, avoid hazards and defeat enemies in real time.

Action games challenge players with real-time combat and movement.

Defining characteristics

Action games test the player's speed, reflexes and timing rather than deliberation. Failure usually means loss of a life, health or progress within a level, after which play restarts from a checkpoint. Many also include resource management and puzzle elements, but the emphasis remains on real-time physical execution.

History and subgenres

The genre grew out of late-1970s arcade titles such as Space Invaders and Asteroids. It diversified into side-scrolling platformers (Super Mario Bros.), run-and-gun and beat 'em up games in the 1980s, first-person shooters in the 1990s (Doom), and character-action, stealth and action-adventure hybrids thereafter. Related hybrid categories include action role-playing games and the action-adventure genre.
