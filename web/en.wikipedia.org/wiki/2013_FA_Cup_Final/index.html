The 2013 FA Cup Final was played at Wembley Stadium on 11 May 2013 between Manchester City and Wigan Athletic. It was the 132nd final of the FA Cup, the world's oldest football cup competition. Against all expectations, Wigan won 1–0 with a stoppage-time header from substitute Ben Watson, producing one of the greatest upsets in the competition's history and winning the first major trophy in the club's 81-year existence.

Route to the final

Manchester City, Premier League champions the previous season, reached the final without conceding a goal, beating Watford, Stoke City, Leeds United, Barnsley and, in the semi-final, holders Chelsea. Wigan, managed by Roberto Martínez, defeated Bournemouth, Macclesfield Town, Huddersfield Town, Everton and Millwall to reach their first FA Cup final, all while fighting relegation from the Premier League.

Match

City started as overwhelming favourites, but Wigan matched them throughout, with goalkeeper Joel Robles saving from Carlos Tevez and Callum McManaman tormenting the City defence. City's Pablo Zabaleta was sent off in the 84th minute for a second bookable offence. In the first minute of added time, Watson — who had only recently returned from a broken leg — rose to head in Shaun Maloney's corner, sparking wild celebrations.

Aftermath

Three days later, Wigan were relegated from the Premier League after defeat at Arsenal, becoming the first club to win the FA Cup and be relegated in the same season. Martínez left that summer for Everton, taking Wigan's captain's armband-wearing squad's cup-winning reputation with him into folklore. Manchester City manager Roberto Mancini was dismissed two days after the final.
