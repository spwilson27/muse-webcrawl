Earl Van Dorn

Earl Van Dorn (September 17, 1820 – May 7, 1863) was a United States Army officer and a major general in the Confederate States Army during the American Civil War. A dashing cavalry commander, he is best remembered for his defeats at Pea Ridge and Corinth and for his successful raid on Holly Springs, and he was murdered in 1863 by a civilian who accused him of an affair with his wife.

Early life and career

Van Dorn was born near Port Gibson, Mississippi, and graduated from the United States Military Academy at West Point in 1842, standing near the bottom of his class. He served in the Mexican–American War, where he was wounded and brevetted for gallantry, and afterwards fought in campaigns against the Seminole and the Comanche on the Texas frontier, being wounded several times.

Civil War service

Resigning his U.S. commission in 1861, Van Dorn rose quickly in Confederate service. Appointed to command the Trans-Mississippi District, he led the Army of the West at the Battle of Pea Ridge (Elkhorn Tavern) in Arkansas in March 1862, where his decision to divide his force and exhaust his men on a forced march contributed to a decisive Confederate defeat. Transferred east, he was beaten again at the Second Battle of Corinth in Mississippi in October 1862 and was court-martialled, though acquitted, over the loss.

Reassigned to cavalry command, Van Dorn redeemed his reputation in December 1862 with a raid on the Union supply depot at Holly Springs, Mississippi, capturing large stores and helping force Ulysses S. Grant to abandon his first overland advance on Vicksburg. In the spring of 1863 he won a victory over Union cavalry at Thompson's Station in Tennessee.

Death

On May 7, 1863, at his headquarters in Spring Hill, Tennessee, Van Dorn was shot and killed by Dr. James Bodie Peters, who claimed the general had carried on an affair with Peters' wife. Peters was never brought to trial.
