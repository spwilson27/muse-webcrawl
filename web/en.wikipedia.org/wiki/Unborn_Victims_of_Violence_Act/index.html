Unborn Victims of Violence Act

The Unborn Victims of Violence Act of 2004 (Public Law 108-212) is a United States law that recognizes an embryo or fetus in utero as a legal victim if injured or killed during the commission of any of over 60 listed federal crimes of violence. The law, codified at 18 U.S.C. § 1841, was also known as Laci and Conner's Law, after Laci Peterson and her unborn son Conner, whose murders in California drew national attention.

Provisions

Under the Act, a person who engages in conduct violating specified federal criminal statutes and thereby causes death or bodily injury to a "child in utero" is guilty of a separate offense. The punishment mirrors that for the same conduct had it injured or killed the pregnant woman. The law defines "child in utero" as a member of the species Homo sapiens at any stage of development carried in the womb.

Exclusions and debate

The Act expressly does not permit prosecution for conduct relating to lawful abortion, for actions of the pregnant woman regarding her own pregnancy, or for medical treatment. It was signed by President George W. Bush on April 1, 2004. Supporters described it as a measure to punish violence against pregnant women and their unborn children, while critics, including abortion-rights organizations, opposed it as a step toward conferring legal personhood on fetuses. Many U.S. states have similar fetal homicide laws.
