George Washington Maher (25 December 1864 – 12 September 1926) was an American architect associated with the Prairie School of design.

Career

Maher began his career in Chicago, working in the office of Joseph Lyman Silsbee alongside Frank Lloyd Wright and George Grant Elmslie. He opened his own practice in 1888 and became known for large residences in Chicago's suburbs, particularly in Kenilworth, Illinois, where he designed many houses and influenced the village's plan.

Motif-rhythm theory

Maher developed a design philosophy he called the "motif-rhythm" theory, in which a single natural or geometric motif — such as the thistle or the lion — was repeated throughout a building's architecture, furnishings, and landscaping to unify the composition. Notable works include the Pleasant Home (John Farson House) in Oak Park and the Swift House in Chicago.
