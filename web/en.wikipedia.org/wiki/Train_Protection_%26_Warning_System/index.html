Train Protection & Warning System

The Train Protection & Warning System (TPWS) is a train protection system used throughout the two main line railway networks in the United Kingdom, and in Victoria, Australia. It automatically applies the brakes if a train approaches a signal at danger too fast or passes a signal at danger without authority (a SPAD). Installation across the British network was largely completed by the end of 2003 under the Railway Safety Regulations 1999.

How it works

TPWS uses pairs of electronic loops placed between the rails. At an overspeed sensor system (OSS) on the approach to a signal or speed restriction, two loops spaced a known distance apart time the passing train; if the train is travelling too fast to stop, the system makes an emergency brake application. At a train stop sensor system (TSS) at the signal itself, the loops are placed together so that any train passing the signal at danger receives an immediate brake application regardless of speed. On the train, a receiver detects the loop frequencies; the brakes are applied and cannot be released by the driver for about a minute.

Background and limitations

TPWS was developed as a cost-effective way to reduce the consequences of signals passed at danger across the whole network, following accidents such as the Ladbroke Grove rail crash of 1999. It mitigates many SPAD outcomes but is not a full automatic train protection system: at high approach speeds it may not stop a train within the overlap, and it does not provide the continuous speed supervision of systems such as the European Train Control System (ETCS).
