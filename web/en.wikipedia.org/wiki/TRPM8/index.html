TRPM8

GeneTRPM8 (human chromosome 2q37)
ProteinTransient receptor potential cation channel, subfamily M, member 8
Also known asCold and menthol receptor 1 (CMR1)
ActivatorsCool temperatures, menthol, icilin

TRPM8 is a protein encoded by the TRPM8 gene in humans. It is an ion channel of the transient receptor potential (TRP) family that functions as the principal molecular sensor of cool temperatures and cooling compounds such as menthol, and is therefore often called the "cold and menthol receptor".

Function

TRPM8 is expressed in a subpopulation of sensory neurons of the trigeminal and dorsal root ganglia. The channel opens when temperature falls below roughly 26–28 °C, allowing an influx of Na⁺ and Ca²⁺ that generates the sensation of cooling. Menthol, eucalyptol, and the synthetic agonist icilin activate the channel at normal temperatures, producing the familiar cool feeling of mint. The channel is also expressed in other tissues, including the prostate, where its role is less fully understood.

Research history and significance

TRPM8 was identified independently in 2002 by the groups of David Julius and Ardem Patapoutian; Patapoutian and Julius shared the 2021 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for the discovery of temperature and touch receptors including TRPM8 and TRPV1. TRPM8 knockout mice show deficits in cold sensation. Because of its roles in cold pain, migraine, and some cancers, the channel is an active target for drug development, with both agonists and antagonists under investigation.
