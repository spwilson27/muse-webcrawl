Titan (supercomputer)

Titan (or OLCF-3) was a supercomputer built by Cray at Oak Ridge National Laboratory for use in a variety of science projects. Announced in October 2011 and deployed in 2012, it was an upgrade of the earlier Jaguar system, replacing its CPUs with AMD Opteron processors and adding NVIDIA Tesla K20X GPUs.

Performance

With 18,688 compute nodes, Titan achieved 17.59 petaFLOPS on the LINPACK benchmark, making it the world's fastest supercomputer on the November 2012 TOP500 list, before being surpassed by China's Tianhe-2 in June 2013. Its theoretical peak was about 27 petaFLOPS, and it drew roughly 8.2 megawatts of power — notably energy-efficient for its class thanks to GPU acceleration.

Use and retirement

Titan served the US Department of Energy's open-science programme, running simulations in materials science, climate, combustion, astrophysics and nuclear energy. It was decommissioned in 2019–2020 and succeeded by Summit (OLCF-4).
