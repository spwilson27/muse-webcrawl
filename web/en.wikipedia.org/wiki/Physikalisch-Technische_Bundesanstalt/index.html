Physikalisch-Technische Bundesanstalt

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


PTB headquarters in Braunschweig.

The Physikalisch-Technische Bundesanstalt (PTB) is the national metrology institute of Germany, providing scientific and technical services for measurement science. Founded in 1887 as the Physikalisch-Technische Reichsanstalt (PTR), it is headquartered in Braunschweig with a second site in Berlin-Charlottenburg, where it originated. It works under the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and employs around 2,000 staff.

History

The PTR was founded through the initiative of Werner von Siemens and Hermann von Helmholtz, who became its first president; it was the world's first national metrology institute and a model for institutions such as Britain's National Physical Laboratory and the U.S. National Bureau of Standards. Research at the institute in the 1890s on black-body radiation, carried out for standardising light measurements, provided the experimental foundation on which Max Planck built quantum theory in 1900.

Work

PTB realises and disseminates the units of the International System of Units (SI) in Germany, maintains national standards for length, mass, time, electricity, temperature, and other quantities, performs calibration and type approval of measuring instruments, and conducts research in fundamental metrology — including atomic clocks that contribute to International Atomic Time, single-electron and quantum electrical standards, and the realisation of the redefined SI. Its clock ensemble in Braunschweig provides the basis for legal time in Germany, disseminated by the DCF77 long-wave transmitter.
