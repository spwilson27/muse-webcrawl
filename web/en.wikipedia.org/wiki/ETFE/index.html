Ethylene tetrafluoroethylene (ETFE) is a fluorine-based plastic. It was designed to have high corrosion resistance and strength over a wide temperature range. ETFE is a polymer and its source-based name is poly(ethene-co-tetrafluoroethene). It is sold under trade names such as Tefzel and Fluon.

Properties

ETFE has a relatively high melting temperature (about 265 °C) for a fluoropolymer, excellent chemical and electrical resistance, and good mechanical toughness. It is resistant to ultraviolet radiation and can be used continuously at temperatures up to about 150 °C. Unlike PTFE, it can be melt-processed by conventional injection moulding and extrusion, and it is more abrasion-resistant, though also somewhat less chemically inert.

Applications

ETFE foil is best known as an architectural material: pneumatic cushions of ETFE foil, inflated with low-pressure air, form lightweight, transparent cladding that transmits more light and weighs a small fraction of glass. Landmark examples include the biomes of the Eden Project in Cornwall, the Beijing National Aquatics Centre ("Water Cube"), the Allianz Arena in Munich and the roof of the U.S. Bank Stadium in Minneapolis. ETFE is also used as insulation for electrical wiring in aerospace and computing applications, as a release film, and in tubing and linings for the chemical and nuclear industries.
