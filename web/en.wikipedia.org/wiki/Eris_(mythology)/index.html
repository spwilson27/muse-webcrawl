Eris (mythology)

Eris
Greek: Ἔρις
Personification of strife and discord
Parents: Nyx (Hesiod) or Zeus and Hera (other traditions)
Symbol: Golden Apple of Discord
Roman equivalent: Discordia

Eris (Greek: Ἔρις, "Strife") is the Greek goddess and personification of strife and discord. Her Roman equivalent is Discordia. In Hesiod's Theogony she is a daughter of Nyx (Night), and the mother of a brood of personified evils including Ponos (Hardship), Lethe (Forgetfulness), Limos (Starvation), the Algea (Pains), Neikea (Quarrels), Androktasiai (Manslaughters), and Dysnomia (Lawlessness).

The Apple of Discord

Eris is best known for the Apple of Discord. Uninvited to the wedding of Peleus and Thetis — the parents of Achilles — she threw into the banquet a golden apple inscribed "for the fairest" (τῇ καλλίστῃ). Hera, Athena and Aphrodite each claimed it, and Zeus appointed the Trojan prince Paris to judge. His award of the apple to Aphrodite, who had promised him the most beautiful woman in the world, Helen, led directly to the abduction of Helen and the outbreak of the Trojan War.

In literature

Hesiod's Works and Days distinguishes two Erises: one who fosters cruel war, and a second, elder daughter of Night whom Zeus set in the roots of the earth, who stirs men to work through healthy competition and emulation — "potter is angry with potter, and craftsman with craftsman". Eris also appears in the Iliad, growing from small beginnings until her head reaches the heavens as she incites the armies to battle. The dwarf planet Eris, whose discovery in 2005 prompted the reclassification of Pluto, is named after her for the discord its discovery caused among astronomers.
