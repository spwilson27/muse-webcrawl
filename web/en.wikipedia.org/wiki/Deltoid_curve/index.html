Deltoid curve

The deltoid curve, a three-cusped hypocycloid

In geometry, a deltoid curve, also known as a tricuspoid curve or Steiner curve, is a hypocycloid of three cusps. In other words, it is the roulette created by a point on the circumference of a circle as it rolls without slipping along the inside of a circle with three times its radius.

Equations

A deltoid can be represented parametrically by x = 2a cos t + a cos 2t and y = 2a sin t − a sin 2t, where a is the radius of the rolling circle. The area enclosed by a deltoid is 2πa², and its arc length is 16a.

History and properties

Deltoids were studied by Leonhard Euler in 1745 in connection with an optical problem, and were investigated by Jakob Steiner in 1856, from whom the alternative name derives. Every triangle has an associated Steiner deltoid: the envelope of its Simson lines is a deltoid. The deltoid is the dual curve of related envelopes and generalizes to hypocycloids with more cusps, such as the four-cusped astroid.
