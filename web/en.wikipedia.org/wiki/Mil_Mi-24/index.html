Mil Mi-24

Mil Mi-24
NATO name: Hind
Role: Attack helicopter / troop transport
First flight: 1969
Introduced: 1972

The Mil Mi-24 (NATO reporting name: Hind) is a large Soviet and Russian helicopter gunship and attack helicopter with transport capacity for eight passengers. It was the first helicopter in the USSR dedicated to both assault transport and close air support — a "flying infantry fighting vehicle".

Design

The Mi-24 combines a heavily armed gunship — chin turret or side-mounted cannon, anti-tank guided missiles such as the Shturm, rockets and bombs on stub wings — with a cabin for a squad of infantry. The stub wings also provide lift at speed. Later variants include the Mi-24V, the Mi-24P with a fixed twin 30 mm cannon, export Mi-25 and Mi-35 versions, and modernised Mi-35M with updated avionics.

Service

More than 2,300 were built. The type saw extensive combat in the Soviet–Afghan War, where mujahideen nicknamed it the "Devil's Chariot" and targeted it with Stinger missiles, as well as in conflicts across Africa, the Middle East and in Ukraine. It remains in service with dozens of air forces.
