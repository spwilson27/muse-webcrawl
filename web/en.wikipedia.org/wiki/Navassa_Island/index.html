Navassa Island (French: Île de la Navasse) is a small uninhabited island in the Caribbean Sea. Located between Haiti and Jamaica, about 55 km west of Haiti's Tiburon Peninsula, it is subject to an ongoing territorial dispute between Haiti and the United States, which administers it as part of the United States Minor Outlying Islands.

Geography

The island is a raised coral and limestone plateau ringed by vertical white cliffs 9 to 15 metres high, with a rugged terrain of exposed rock and caves. It has no natural harbour or fresh water, and supports scrub vegetation, a colony of seabirds, and feral goats descended from animals left by Haitian fishermen.

History

Columbus's expedition sighted the island in 1504. In 1857 the United States claimed Navassa under the Guano Islands Act, and the Navassa Phosphate Company mined guano there from 1865 to 1898 under notoriously harsh labour conditions; a revolt by Black American workers in 1889 led to the U.S. Supreme Court case Jones v. United States (1890), which upheld American jurisdiction. A lighthouse was built in 1917 and automated before being deactivated in 1996. Haiti has claimed the island since its 1801 constitution.

Wildlife refuge

In 1999 the island was transferred to the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service and designated the Navassa Island National Wildlife Refuge, protecting endemic species including the Navassa curly-tailed lizard and colonies of red-footed boobies. Access requires a permit and visits are rare.
