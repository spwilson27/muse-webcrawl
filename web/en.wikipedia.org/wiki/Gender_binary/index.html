Gender binary

Also calledGender binarism
FieldGender studies, sociology
CategoriesMan / woman

The gender binary (also known as gender binarism) is the classification of gender into two distinct forms of masculine and feminine, whether by social system, cultural belief, or both. Most cultures use a gender binary, having two genders (boys/men and girls/women).

Overview

In this model, people are assigned a gender at birth based on perceived sex characteristics, and are expected to conform to the roles, behaviours and attributes associated with that gender. The binary model treats gender as aligned with biological sex and as exhaustive: every person is placed into one of the two categories.

Criticism and alternatives

Scholars and activists note that the binary does not account for intersex people, whose sex characteristics do not fit typical definitions, nor for non-binary and genderqueer people whose identities fall outside the two categories. Many cultures historically recognised additional gender categories, such as hijra in South Asia, Two-Spirit identities among some Indigenous North American peoples, and fa'afafine in Samoa. Contemporary gender theory often treats gender as a spectrum or as distinct from sex altogether.
