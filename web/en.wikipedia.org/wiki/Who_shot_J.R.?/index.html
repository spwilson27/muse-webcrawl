"Who shot J.R.?" was a promotional slogan and cultural phenomenon surrounding a 1980 cliffhanger on the American prime-time soap opera Dallas. In the season finale "A House Divided", the scheming oil tycoon J. R. Ewing (played by Larry Hagman) was shot by an unseen assailant, leaving viewers to speculate over the summer about both the shooter's identity and whether J.R. would survive.

The phenomenon

The mystery became an international media sensation. Bookmakers took bets on suspects — including J.R.'s wife Sue Ellen, his brother Bobby, his enemy Cliff Barnes, and even Hagman himself in jest — and the phrase appeared on T-shirts, magazine covers, and political campaign materials during the 1980 U.S. presidential election. Production delays caused by a writers' and actors' strike stretched the suspense to eight months. To protect the secret, the producers filmed versions in which several different characters were shown as the shooter.

The reveal

In "Who Done It", broadcast on 21 November 1980, the shooter was revealed to be Kristin Shepard (Mary Crosby), J.R.'s sister-in-law and former mistress, who had shot him after he threatened her and broke promises to her. An estimated 83 million American viewers watched — at the time the largest audience for a single television episode, surpassed only by the M*A*S*H finale in 1983. The stunt permanently changed television: the summer cliffhanger became a standard device for serialised drama.

Legacy

"Who shot J.R.?" remains shorthand for a suspenseful cliffhanger and is frequently cited in discussions of television history, marketing, and the power of event television in the pre-internet era.
