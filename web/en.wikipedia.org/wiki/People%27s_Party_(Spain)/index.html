The People's Party (Spanish: Partido Popular, PP) is a conservative and Christian-democratic political party in Spain. It is one of the country's two dominant parties, alongside the Spanish Socialist Workers' Party (PSOE), and has governed Spain under José María Aznar (1996–2004) and Mariano Rajoy (2011–2018).

History

The PP was founded in 1989 as a refoundation of the People's Alliance, itself led since 1976 by Manuel Fraga, a former Franco-era minister. Under Aznar the party moved to the centre, winning power in 1996 and an absolute majority in 2000. Its first government was ended by defeat in 2004, days after the Madrid train bombings. Rajoy returned the party to power in 2011 amid the eurozone crisis, imposing austerity measures, but was removed by a no-confidence vote in 2018 following corruption convictions involving the party (the Gürtel case).

Recent years

After two general elections in 2019 in which it lost ground to new parties including Vox and Ciudadanos, the PP recovered under Alberto Núñez Feijóo, who became leader in 2022. The party won the most seats in the July 2023 general election but could not form a government, remaining the main opposition party.
