DefinitionPopulation per unit land area

Population density is a measurement of population per unit land area. It is mostly applied to humans, but sometimes to other living organisms. It is a key geographical term, expressing how crowded a place is, and is usually given in inhabitants per square kilometre or square mile.

Calculation

Arithmetic population density is calculated by dividing the total population of an area by its total land area. Because political boundaries often include large uninhabitable regions — deserts, ice caps, mountain ranges — alternative measures exist: physiological density divides population by arable land area, and agricultural density relates the rural population to farmland.

Distribution

Global average population density is roughly 60 people per square kilometre of land. The most densely populated sovereign state is the city-state of Monaco, with more than 18,000 inhabitants per km², while Mongolia is among the least dense, at about 2 per km². Much of humanity is concentrated in South and East Asia, Europe and coastal urban regions; vast areas of the Sahara, Siberia, Canada and Australia are nearly empty. Cities such as Manila, Dhaka and Monaco's districts record densities far above national averages.

Effects

High population densities influence housing costs, transport, public health and the environment, but density itself is neither inherently beneficial nor harmful: well-planned dense cities can have low per-capita emissions and rich services, while poorly served dense settlements face sanitation and disease challenges. In ecology, density also affects species through competition, predation and disease transmission, and is central to population dynamics.
