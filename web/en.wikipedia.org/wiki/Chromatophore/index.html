Chromatophore

Chromatophores are pigment-containing, light-reflecting cells found in many animals — notably amphibians, fish, reptiles, crustaceans, and cephalopods — that enable colour change for camouflage, signalling, and thermoregulation.

Types

Classes include melanophores (black/brown melanin), xanthophores (yellow), erythrophores (red), iridophores (reflective/iridescent), leucophores (white), and cyanophores (blue). In cephalopods, chromatophores are muscular organs controlled directly by the brain, permitting rapid pattern change; in vertebrates colour change is slower and often hormonally controlled.

Function

Animals redistribute pigment within chromatophores or change cell shape to alter skin appearance, as in chameleons, flatfish, and octopuses.
