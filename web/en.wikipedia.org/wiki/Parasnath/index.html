Parasnath

Elevation1,365 m (4,478 ft)
LocationGiridih district, Jharkhand, India
Religious significanceHoliest Jain pilgrimage site (Siddhakshetra); also sacred to the Santal people as Marang Buru
Also known asShikharji (peak complex), Sammed Shikhar

Parasnath is a mountain peak in the Parasnath hill range, in the Giridih district of Jharkhand, India. At about 1,365 metres, it is the highest mountain in Jharkhand. The hill is one of the most important pilgrimage sites in Jainism: Jains believe that 20 of the 24 Tirthankaras attained moksha (liberation) here, including Parshvanatha, the 23rd Tirthankara, after whom the hill is named. The summit temple complex is commonly known as Shikharji or Sammed Shikharji.

Parasnath hill, crowned with Jain temples (tonks) marking the sites of liberation.

Jain pilgrimage

Along the ridge of the hill stand small shrines called tonks, each marking the spot where a Tirthankara is believed to have attained nirvana. Pilgrims traditionally ascend from the town of Madhuban at the foot of the hill, beginning before dawn, and complete a circumambulation of about 27 km visiting the tonks. The site is sacred to both major Jain traditions, Digambara and Shvetambara, though sectarian disputes over the hill's management have occurred. Parshvanatha's tonk is the most visited; his footprint shrine commemorates his liberation here traditionally dated to the 8th century BCE in Jain chronology.

Other significance

The hill is also the sacred mountain (Marang Buru, "the great mountain") of the Santal tribal people, and the surrounding area is a wildlife sanctuary (Parasnath Wildlife Sanctuary) with leopards, elephants, and other fauna. The coexistence of Jain religious use, tribal sacred claims, and conservation designations has periodically produced tensions over access and development.

Geography

Parasnath rises from the Chota Nagpur Plateau. The nearest town is Isri (Parasnath station on the Grand Chord railway line is a common access point), and the hill is visible for many kilometres across the surrounding plain.
