Taksim (politics)

Taksim advocated the partition of Cyprus into Greek and Turkish states

Taksim (Turkish for "division" or "partition") was the Turkish Cypriot nationalist goal of partitioning the island of Cyprus into separate Greek Cypriot and Turkish Cypriot states. It emerged in the 1950s as the Turkish and Turkish Cypriot counter-demand to enosis, the Greek Cypriot movement for union of Cyprus with Greece.

Background

As the Greek Cypriot EOKA campaign for enosis began in 1955, Turkish Cypriot leaders — with the support of Turkey — argued that if British rule ended, the island should be divided, with part returning to Turkish sovereignty or forming a separate Turkish Cypriot state. The doctrine was promoted by figures such as Fazıl Küçük and Rauf Denktaş, and backed by the Turkish Resistance Organisation (TMT). Partition was discussed at various points during the intercommunal negotiations of the late 1950s, though the 1960 settlement instead established a bicommunal Republic of Cyprus.

After 1974

Following the 1974 Greek Cypriot coup and the subsequent Turkish military intervention, the island was effectively divided along the Attila Line, with population transfers consolidating two zones. Turkish Cypriots proclaimed a federated state in 1975 and, in 1983, unilaterally declared the "Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus", recognised only by Turkey. While official Turkish Cypriot positions later shifted toward federation and then a two-state solution, taksim remains the historical name for the partitionist doctrine.
