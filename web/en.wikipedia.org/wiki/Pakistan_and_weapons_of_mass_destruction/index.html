Pakistan and weapons of mass destruction

Pakistan is one of nine states known to possess nuclear weapons. Its programme began in earnest in 1972, under Prime Minister Zulfikar Ali Bhutto, in response to India's growing nuclear capability and the loss of East Pakistan in the 1971 war. Pakistan conducted its first public nuclear tests, codenamed Chagai-I, on 28 May 1998, in direct response to India's Pokhran-II tests earlier that month, making it the seventh country to publicly test nuclear weapons.

History

The Pakistan Atomic Energy Commission was established in 1956, but the weapons programme was only formalised after January 1972, when Bhutto convened scientists at Multan and directed them to build a bomb. Physicist Munir Ahmad Khan led much of the early effort; from 1975, metallurgist A. Q. Khan established the uranium enrichment programme at Kahuta using centrifuge designs he had acquired in Europe. In 2004 Khan confessed to running an illicit proliferation network supplying technology to Iran, Libya and North Korea, a major international scandal.

Arsenal and delivery systems

Independent estimates in the 2020s place Pakistan's stockpile at about 170 warheads. Delivery systems include aircraft and a family of ballistic missiles — the Hatf series, Shaheen-I/II/III and Ghauri — as well as the Babur cruise missile, the short-range Nasr tactical system, and the Ababeel missile tested with multiple-warhead capability in view. Pakistan maintains the Strategic Plans Division and a National Command Authority for control of the arsenal.

Doctrine and policy

Pakistan has not adopted a no-first-use policy, reserving the option of first use to deter conventional attack by India; its posture is often described as "full spectrum deterrence". Pakistan is not a signatory to the Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty or the Comprehensive Nuclear-Test-Ban Treaty. Officially, Pakistan states that it does not possess chemical or biological weapons and is a party to the Chemical Weapons Convention and the Biological Weapons Convention.
