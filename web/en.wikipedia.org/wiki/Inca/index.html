The Inca Empire was the largest empire in pre-Columbian America, centred on Cusco in the Andes of modern Peru. From 1438 under Pachacuti it expanded along the mountains from Colombia to Chile, ruling perhaps ten million people without writing (records were kept on knotted cords, quipu), markets or wheeled transport.

Conquest and legacy

Inca engineers built superb stone roads, terraces and cities such as Machu Picchu. Francisco Pizarro's small Spanish force, exploiting civil war and disease, captured the emperor Atahualpa in 1532 and conquered the empire. Quechua language and Andean culture persist: millions speak Quechua today, and crops domesticated in the Andes — potato above all, eaten beside maize — feed the world.
