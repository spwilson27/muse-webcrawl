Rolls-Royce Trent 900

The Rolls-Royce Trent 900 is a high-bypass turbofan jet engine developed to power the Airbus A380, for which it was a launch engine alongside the Engine Alliance GP7000.

A three-shaft design derived from the Trent family, the Trent 900 has a 2.95 m fan with swept blades, a bypass ratio of about 8.7, and is certified at thrust ratings from roughly 71,000 to 84,000 lbf; A380 operators use versions rated around 70,000–76,000 lbf. First run in 2003, it first flew on an A340 testbed in 2004 and entered service with Singapore Airlines in 2007.

On 4 November 2010 a Trent 900 suffered an uncontained intermediate-pressure turbine disc failure on Qantas Flight 32, leading to fleet inspections and engine replacements; Rolls-Royce attributed the failure to a defective oil pipe. Customers included Singapore Airlines, Lufthansa, Qantas, Malaysia Airlines and Asiana.
