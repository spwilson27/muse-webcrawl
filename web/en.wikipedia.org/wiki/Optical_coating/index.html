Optical coating

An optical coating is one or more thin layers of material deposited on an optical component, such as a lens or mirror, to alter the way it reflects and transmits light. Coatings work through interference effects in the thin films and through the refractive indices of the chosen materials.

The most common type is the antireflection coating, which reduces unwanted reflections from lens surfaces, improving transmission and reducing ghost images; camera lenses, eyeglasses and solar cells use them. Other types include high-reflector (mirror) coatings, beam-splitter coatings, and filters that transmit or block selected wavelengths, such as dichroic and neutral-density filters.

Coatings are typically applied by physical vapour deposition techniques such as evaporation or sputtering, with layer thicknesses controlled to a fraction of the wavelength of light.
