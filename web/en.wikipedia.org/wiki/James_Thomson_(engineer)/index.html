James Thomson (engineer)

Born: 16 February 1822, Belfast, Ireland

Died: 8 May 1892 (aged 70), Glasgow, Scotland

Occupation: Engineer, professor

Known for: Inward-flow water turbine; work on thermodynamics and tides

James Thomson (16 February 1822 – 8 May 1892) was a British engineer and physicist, born in Belfast, Ireland. He was a professor of engineering at Queen's College, Belfast, and then at the University of Glasgow, and was the elder brother of the physicist Lord Kelvin (William Thomson).

Career

Thomson studied engineering and mathematics at the University of Glasgow and worked on the design of water turbines, most notably an inward-flowing vortex turbine that greatly influenced the development of modern Francis-type turbines. He also made early contributions to the concepts of stress in materials, tidal analysis, and the flow of rivers.

Contributions

Thomson's work included improvements to the compound steam engine, studies of the plastic flow of glaciers, and the invention of instruments for harmonic analysis of tides. He is remembered for his careful experimental and theoretical work bridging classical mechanics, hydraulics, and engineering practice.
