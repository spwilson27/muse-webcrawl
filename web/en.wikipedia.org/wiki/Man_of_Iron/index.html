Man of Iron (Polish: Człowiek z żelaza) is a 1981 Polish drama film directed by Andrzej Wajda. It continues the story begun in Man of Marble (1977), following activist Maciej Tomczyk, son of the earlier film's bricklayer Mateusz Birkut, during the Gdańsk strikes of August 1980 that gave birth to the Solidarity trade union.

Plot

An alcoholic television journalist, Winkel, is sent to Gdańsk to discredit the striking workers and their activist Tomczyk. As he interviews people who know Tomczyk and pieces together the repression of the workers' protests of 1970 and the events of the strike — incorporating authentic documentary footage and appearances by real figures including Lech Wałęsa — Winkel gradually sympathises with the movement and resigns from his assignment.

Production and reception

The film was made with extraordinary speed and unprecedented official tolerance during the Solidarity period, premiering less than a year after the strikes it depicts. It won the Palme d'Or at the 1981 Cannes Film Festival and was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film. Months after its release, martial law was imposed in Poland and the film was restricted.
