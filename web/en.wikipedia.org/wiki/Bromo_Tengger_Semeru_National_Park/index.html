Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park

LocationEast Java, Indonesia
AreaAbout 5,250 hectares (plus buffer zones)
Established1982 (national park status; protected earlier)
Highest pointMount Semeru, 3,676 m
VisitorsOne of Indonesia's most visited natural attractions

Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park (Indonesian: Taman Nasional Bromo Tengger Semeru) is a national park in East Java, Indonesia, centred on the Tengger massif. It contains two of Java's most famous volcanoes: Mount Bromo (2,329 m), an active cone rising from the vast caldera floor known as the Sea of Sand (Segara Wedi), and Mount Semeru (3,676 m), the highest mountain in Java and one of Indonesia's most active volcanoes, which looms behind Bromo and erupts at frequent intervals.

Geography and nature

The Tengger caldera, about 10 km across, encloses the Sea of Sand and a group of cones including Batok, Kursi, Watangan and Widodaren alongside Bromo. The park spans a range of ecosystems from lowland rainforest to subalpine slopes, with lakes such as Ranu Pani and Ranu Kumbolo on the Semeru climbing route, waterfalls, orchids, and wildlife including Javan leopard, muntjac, marbled cat and many bird species. Elevations range from about 750 m to Semeru's summit.

People and tourism

The park is named in part for the Tenggerese people, a Hindu community descended from the Majapahit era, whose Yadnya Kasada festival involves offerings cast into Bromo's crater. Sunrise viewpoints such as Penanjakan draw large numbers of tourists to watch dawn light over Bromo, Batok and Semeru, and jeep tours cross the Sea of Sand to the crater stairs. Climbing Semeru is a popular but demanding multi-day trek, subject to closure during heightened volcanic activity; Bromo's eruptions periodically close access and spread ash over surrounding villages and farmland.
