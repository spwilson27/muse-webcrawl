The Hours is a 2002 psychological drama film directed by Stephen Daldry and written by David Hare, based on the 1998 Pulitzer Prize-winning novel of the same name by Michael Cunningham. The film stars Meryl Streep, Nicole Kidman, and Julianne Moore as three women of different generations whose lives are interconnected by Virginia Woolf's 1925 novel Mrs Dalloway.

Plot

The film interweaves three storylines, each following one day in the life of a woman in a different era. In 1923, Virginia Woolf (Kidman) struggles with depression in the suburbs of London as she begins writing Mrs Dalloway; the film opens with her suicide by drowning in the River Ouse in 1941. In 1951, Los Angeles housewife Laura Brown (Moore), pregnant and unhappy in her marriage, reads Mrs Dalloway and contemplates suicide while planning a birthday party for her husband. In 2001, New York editor Clarissa Vaughan (Streep)—a modern-day analogue of Woolf's Clarissa Dalloway—prepares a party for her friend Richard, a poet dying of AIDS, who is revealed to be Laura Brown's son.

Reception

The film received critical acclaim, particularly for its performances and Philip Glass's score. At the 75th Academy Awards it was nominated for nine awards, including Best Picture, and Nicole Kidman won the Academy Award for Best Actress for her portrayal of Woolf, for which she wore a prosthetic nose. The film also won the Golden Globe Award for Best Motion Picture – Drama, and Kidman and Streep shared the Silver Bear for Best Actress at the Berlin International Film Festival.
