Masonry heater
FuelWood
EfficiencyOften 75–90%
Also known asKachelofen; Russian stove

A masonry heater (also called a masonry stove, Kachelofen, or Russian stove) is a space-heating appliance made of brick, stone, soapstone, or tile that captures heat from a brief, hot fire and radiates it slowly over many hours through its large thermal mass.

Operation

A full charge of wood is burned quickly at high temperature, giving nearly complete combustion and low emissions. Hot gases wind through internal flue channels, heating tonnes of masonry that then release warmth steadily, often for 12–24 hours from one or two daily firings.

History

Masonry heaters evolved in northern and central Europe and Russia during centuries of fuel scarcity; designs include the Finnish contraflow heater, the Swedish tiled Kakelugn, and the great Russian pech that also served for cooking and sleeping.
