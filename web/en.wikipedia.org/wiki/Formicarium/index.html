Formicarium

A formicarium (plural formicaria) is a vivarium designed for keeping and studying ant colonies. Usually constructed so that the nest's tunnels and chambers are visible — by placing soil, sand, gel, or a carved nesting block between two panes of glass or acrylic — a formicarium lets an observer watch the queen, workers, brood care, foraging, and waste disposal that would otherwise take place hidden underground. The name derives from Formicidae, the ant family.

History

Scientists such as Charles Janet in the late 19th century built plaster observation nests for research, and William Morton Wheeler used similar apparatus in his classic ant studies. The formicarium entered popular culture in 1956, when Milton Levine and E. J. Cossman marketed the "Ant Farm" — a thin soil-filled frame sold with a coupon for live harvester ants — which became a classic educational toy. Later designs include gel-based farms (originally inspired by a NASA experiment on ant behaviour in microgravity), acrylic modular nests, and 3D-printed or ytong (aerated concrete) nests used by hobbyist "ant keepers".

Design

A typical setup pairs a nesting area with an attached foraging arena (or "outworld") where food and water are offered. Requirements include controlled humidity — usually provided by a water reservoir or moistened substrate — an escape barrier such as fluon or talcum paste on open surfaces, ventilation that excludes the ants, and protection from vibration and direct light, since colonies are stressed by disturbance. Red film or covers are often used because ants perceive red light poorly, allowing observation while the nest seems dark to them.

Care

Colonies are founded from a single mated queen, often collected after a nuptial flight, and raised first in a test tube setup before moving to a formicarium. Diets combine carbohydrates (sugar water, honey) with protein (feeder insects) for brood production. Many temperate species require a cool hibernation period. Escape, mould, mite infestations, and desiccation are common problems.
