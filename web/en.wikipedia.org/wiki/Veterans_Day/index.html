Veterans Day

Observed byUnited States
TypeFederal holiday
DateNovember 11
Related toArmistice Day, Remembrance Day

Veterans Day (originally known as Armistice Day) is a federal holiday in the United States observed annually on November 11, for honouring military veterans of the United States Armed Forces. It coincides with other holidays including Armistice Day and Remembrance Day which are celebrated in other countries that mark the anniversary of the end of World War I.

Major hostilities of World War I were formally ended at the 11th hour of the 11th day of the 11th month of 1918 when the Armistice with Germany went into effect. At the urging of major U.S. veteran organisations, Armistice Day was renamed Veterans Day in 1954, broadening the holiday to honour all American veterans rather than only those who served in World War I.

History

On November 11, 1919, President Woodrow Wilson issued the first Armistice Day proclamation. In 1926, Congress passed a concurrent resolution requesting an annual proclamation, and in 1938 an Act of Congress made November 11 a legal holiday dedicated to the cause of world peace. In 1954, after World War II and the Korean War, Congress amended the act, replacing "Armistice" with "Veterans" at the urging of veterans' service organisations. President Dwight D. Eisenhower signed the bill into law on May 26, 1954.

Observance

Veterans Day is distinct from Memorial Day, which honours those who died in military service; Veterans Day celebrates the service of all U.S. military veterans. Federal government offices are closed, many schools and businesses close or hold assemblies, and ceremonies are held at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier at Arlington National Cemetery, where the President traditionally lays a wreath. Parades and church services are held across the country.
