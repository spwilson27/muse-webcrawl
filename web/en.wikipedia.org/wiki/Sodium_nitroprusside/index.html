Sodium nitroprusside


Formula: Na2[Fe(CN)5NO]·2H2O
Molar mass: 297.95 g/mol
Appearance: Deep red crystals
Use: Vasodilator; analytical reagent

Sodium nitroprusside (SNP), sold under the brand name Nipride among others, is a medication used to lower blood pressure. It is a potent vasodilator administered by continuous intravenous infusion, used for hypertensive emergencies and to produce controlled hypotension during surgery. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Medical use

Nitroprusside acts as a prodrug of nitric oxide, relaxing both arterial and venous smooth muscle and thereby reducing preload and afterload. Its onset is within seconds and its effects dissipate within minutes of stopping the infusion, requiring careful monitoring of blood pressure.

Adverse effects

Each nitroprusside molecule contains five cyanide ions; metabolism releases cyanide, which is detoxified to thiocyanate by the liver enzyme rhodanese. Prolonged or high-dose infusion, particularly in patients with liver or kidney dysfunction, can cause cyanide or thiocyanate toxicity. Other side effects include excessive hypotension and headache.

Chemistry

The compound consists of an octahedral iron(II) centre coordinated to five cyanide ligands and one linear nitrosyl ligand. In analytical chemistry, sodium nitroprusside is used in tests for ketones, aldehydes, and sulphur compounds, including the Legal test for ketone bodies in urine.
