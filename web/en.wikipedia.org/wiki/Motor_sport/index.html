Motor sport

Motorsport, motorsports or motor sport is a global term used to encompass the group of competitive sporting events which primarily involve the use of motorised vehicles. The terminology can also be used to describe forms of competition of two-wheeled motorised vehicles under the heading of motorcycle racing, and off-road racing such as motocross.

Organised motorsport began shortly after the invention of the automobile: the 1894 Paris–Rouen trial is often considered the first motoring competition, followed by city-to-city races and, in 1907, the opening of Brooklands in England, the first purpose-built motor racing circuit. Major categories today include single-seater racing (Formula One, IndyCar), sports car and endurance racing (headlined by the 24 Hours of Le Mans), touring cars, stock cars (NASCAR), rallying (World Rally Championship), drag racing, kart racing and motorcycle racing (MotoGP, Superbike).

International motorsport for cars is governed by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA) and for motorcycles by the Fédération Internationale de Motocyclisme (FIM). Safety has been a driving concern throughout the sport's history, with advances in circuit design, crash structures, helmets and the HANS device progressively reducing fatalities. In recent years electric racing, such as Formula E, has grown in prominence.
