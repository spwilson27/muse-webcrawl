The Nanjing Yangtze River Bridge is a double-deck road-rail truss bridge across the Yangtze River in Nanjing, Jiangsu, China. The upper deck carries a four-lane highway and pedestrian walkways, while the lower deck carries a double-track railway on the Beijing–Shanghai line. When it opened in 1968 it was celebrated in China as a landmark of national self-reliance, the first bridge over the lower Yangtze designed and built by Chinese engineers.

Construction

Before the bridge, trains crossed the river at Nanjing by ferry, a serious bottleneck. Construction began in 1960 with Soviet assistance planned, but after the Sino-Soviet split China completed the project alone, casting some foundation caissons in difficult river conditions. The railway deck opened in September 1968 and the road deck in December of the same year. The bridge proper is about 1,576 metres long, with approach viaducts bringing the total length to about 6,772 metres; its nine piers stand in the river, and the bridgehead towers are crowned with red flags and sculptures in the socialist realist style.

Significance and later history

The bridge became a potent national symbol, reproduced on posters, stamps and the earlier renminbi notes, and visited by generations of Chinese tourists. Its deck is also the site of several towers and an exhibition hall. The structure underwent a major renovation in 2016–2018, during which it was closed to road traffic. Because additional Yangtze crossings have since been built in Nanjing, its transport role is now shared, but it remains one of the city's defining landmarks.
