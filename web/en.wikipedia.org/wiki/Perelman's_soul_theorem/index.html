The soul: a compact core capturing a noncompact manifold's topology

In Riemannian geometry, Perelman's soul theorem is a result proved by Grigori Perelman in 1994 that completed the classification programme begun by the soul theorem of Jeff Cheeger and Detlef Gromoll (1972). The soul theorem states that any complete, noncompact Riemannian manifold of nonnegative sectional curvature contains a compact, totally geodesic submanifold — its soul — to which the whole manifold is diffeomorphic to a vector bundle (the normal bundle of the soul).

Statement

Cheeger and Gromoll had conjectured that if the curvature is strictly positive at even one point, then the soul must reduce to a single point, meaning the manifold is diffeomorphic to Euclidean space itself. This "soul conjecture" resisted proof for over two decades. Perelman proved it in 1994 with a short and elegant argument, published as "Proof of the soul conjecture of Cheeger and Gromoll" in the Journal of Differential Geometry.

Significance

Together, the soul theorem and Perelman's result give a nearly complete picture of the topology of open manifolds of nonnegative curvature: such a manifold is the normal bundle of a compact nonnegatively curved soul, and if it is positively curved anywhere it is simply Euclidean space. Perelman's proof was an early demonstration of the power of Alexandrov geometry techniques that he would later use, far more famously, in his proof of the Poincaré conjecture via Ricci flow.
