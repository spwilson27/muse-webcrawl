The 'Obby 'Oss festival is a folk custom that takes place each 1 May (May Day) in Padstow, a harbour town on the north coast of Cornwall, England. Two hobby horses, known locally as 'osses, dance through the town's streets accompanied by drums, accordions and large crowds singing traditional May songs. It is one of Britain's oldest and best-known folk festivals and is often regarded as a survival of ancient spring fertility celebrations, although its documented history is considerably shorter.

The festival

The two 'osses are the Old 'Oss, supported by the "red" team, and the Blue Ribbon (or "Peace") 'Oss, supported by the "blue" team. Each 'oss consists of a dancer wearing a large circular frame covered with black oilskin and a tall conical mask with a fierce horse-like face; a club is carried in one hand. The 'oss dances and swirls through the streets, teasing the crowd and ritually "capturing" young women under its skirts, while a "teaser" with a padded club dances before it. At midday and at other points the 'oss "dies" to a slow, mournful rendition of the song, then springs back to life. The day begins at midnight on 30 April with the singing of the Morning Song ("Unite and unite and let us all unite...") and ends as the 'osses return to their stables in the evening.

History

References to a Padstow hobby horse custom date back to at least the early nineteenth century. The Blue Ribbon 'Oss was introduced in 1919 by temperance campaigners as a "peace" alternative to the alcohol-fuelled Old 'Oss celebrations. The festival draws thousands of visitors, and access for filming and research has made it a fixture of British folk tradition.
