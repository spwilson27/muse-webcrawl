The London Overground is a suburban rail network serving London and its environs. Established in 2007 to take over Silverlink Metro routes, the network is operated by Arriva Rail London under a concession awarded by Transport for London (TfL), which specifies the service levels, fares and branding. Trains carry the orange version of the TfL roundel and are painted in a livery of white, blue and orange.

The network radiates from central London terminals including London Victoria, Liverpool Street, Euston and Clapham Junction, serving many areas without a London Underground connection. It is fully integrated into London's fare system, accepting Oyster cards and contactless payment.

History

The Overground brand was launched on 11 November 2007 when TfL took control of the former Silverlink Metro services: the North London line, the West London line, the Watford DC line and the Gospel Oak to Barking line. The East London line, converted from the former East London line of the Underground, reopened in 2010 and was extended to Clapham Junction in 2012, creating an orbital railway around inner London. In 2015 the network expanded again, taking over Liverpool Street suburban services to Enfield Town, Cheshunt and Chingford, and the Romford to Upminster line. The line to Battersea Park from Clapham Junction, and services to Lioness line (Watford DC), Mildmay line (North London), Windrush line (East London), Liberty line (Romford–Upminster), Weaver line (Liverpool Street) and Suffragette line (Gospel Oak–Barking) received individual names in 2024 to make the network easier to navigate.

Rolling stock

The core fleet consists of Bombardier Class 378 Capitalstar electric multiple units, built with longitudinal seating similar to Underground trains to maximise standing capacity. Class 710 Aventra units entered service from 2019 on the Watford DC, Gospel Oak to Barking and Liverpool Street routes.
