Laser (dinghy)

Laser (ILCA)

Designer: Bruce Kirby
Year: 1970 (launched 1971)
Crew: 1
Hull length: 4.23 m
Rigs: ILCA 7 (Standard), ILCA 6 (Radial), ILCA 4 (4.7)

The Laser is a single-handed, one-design sailing dinghy, one of the most popular boats of its kind in the world, with well over 200,000 built. Since a trademark dispute that began in 2019, boats supplied for class racing are officially branded ILCA dinghies after the International Laser Class Association, with the three rigs renamed ILCA 7, ILCA 6, and ILCA 4. The Laser has been an Olympic class since the 1996 Atlanta Games for men, and the women's radial rig joined the Olympics in 2008.

Design

Designed by Canadian Bruce Kirby in 1970 — famously sketched during a telephone conversation — the Laser is a lightweight car-toppable boat with a simple unstayed mast and a single sail. Strict one-design rules keep boats nearly identical so that racing tests the sailor rather than the equipment. Three interchangeable rigs with different sail areas (about 7.06 m² for the Standard/ILCA 7, 5.76 m² for the Radial/ILCA 6, and 4.70 m² for the 4.7/ILCA 4) use the same hull, letting sailors of different weights and ages compete.

Sailing characteristics

The Laser is physically demanding: with no trapeze and a powerful sail, sailors hike hard to keep the boat flat, and downwind the boat is lively and prone to death rolls. It is raced in huge fleets worldwide, and its masters and youth circuits are among the largest in sailing. Notable champions include Robert Scheidt of Brazil, the most successful Laser sailor in Olympic history, and Ben Ainslie, who won his first Olympic gold in the class in 2000.
