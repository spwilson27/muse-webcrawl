The Los Angeles metropolitan area, officially the Los Angeles–Long Beach–Anaheim metropolitan statistical area, is the second most populous metropolitan area in the United States, after New York, with roughly 12.9 million residents. It comprises Los Angeles County and Orange County in Southern California. The still larger Los Angeles combined statistical area, which adds the Inland Empire, Ventura County and other surrounding areas, is home to more than 18 million people.

Overview

The region is famous for its sprawling, low-density, polycentric form, its dependence on the automobile, and a freeway network that includes some of the most heavily travelled roads in the world. Unlike many older metropolitan areas, Greater Los Angeles has no single dominant centre; employment and population are distributed among numerous cities including Los Angeles, Long Beach, Anaheim, Santa Ana, Irvine, Glendale, Pasadena and Riverside–San Bernardino in the wider combined area.

Economy and culture

The metropolitan economy is one of the largest in the world, anchored by the entertainment industry centred on Hollywood, the twin ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach (together the busiest container port complex in the Americas), aerospace and defence, technology — the so-called "Silicon Beach" — fashion, healthcare and international trade. The region is also noted for its ethnic diversity; hundreds of languages are spoken, and it contains the largest communities of several immigrant groups in the United States.

Geography and climate

The metropolitan area occupies a coastal plain ringed by mountain ranges including the San Gabriels and Santa Monicas, with a Mediterranean climate of mild, wet winters and warm, dry summers. The region is prone to earthquakes along faults such as the San Andreas and Newport–Inglewood, and to wildfires in its wildland–urban interface.
