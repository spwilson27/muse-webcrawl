Golf handicap

PurposeEqualising competition between golfers of differing ability
Current systemWorld Handicap System (from 2020)
Governed byThe R&A and the USGA

A golf handicap is a numerical measure of a golfer's potential playing ability, used to enable players of different standards to compete against one another on equitable terms. Lower handicaps indicate better players; a golfer who plays to "scratch" has a handicap of zero, while elite professionals effectively play to positive handicaps. In match and stroke play competitions, weaker players receive strokes on the hardest holes according to their handicap.

World Handicap System

Historically, different regions used different systems, including the USGA Handicap System in the United States and the CONGU system in Britain and Ireland. From 2020 these were unified into the World Handicap System, administered by The R&A and the USGA. Under the system, a player's Handicap Index is calculated from the best eight of their most recent twenty score differentials, where a differential measures performance relative to a course's difficulty as expressed by its Course Rating and Slope Rating. A Course Handicap then converts the index into the number of strokes the player receives on a specific course and set of tees.

Features

The system includes safeguards such as caps on upward handicap movement, reductions for exceptional scores, and adjustments for abnormal course or weather conditions. Handicaps apply to both competitive and casual rounds submitted for handicap purposes, and maximum handicaps are generally 54.0 for both men and women, allowing beginners to participate in net competitions from the outset.
