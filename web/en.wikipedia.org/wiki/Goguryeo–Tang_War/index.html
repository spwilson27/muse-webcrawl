The Goguryeo–Tang War was a series of conflicts between the Korean kingdom of Goguryeo and China's Tang dynasty between 645 and 668, ending in the destruction of Goguryeo by the allied forces of Tang and the Korean kingdom of Silla. The war followed the earlier, unsuccessful campaigns of the Sui dynasty, which had contributed to Sui's collapse.

Background

Goguryeo controlled much of the northern Korean Peninsula and large parts of Manchuria, including the Liaodong Peninsula claimed by the Chinese dynasties. In 642 the Goguryeo general Yeon Gaesomun seized power in a coup, killing King Yeongnyu. Tang's Emperor Taizong used this as a pretext for invasion.

First campaign (645)

In 645 Taizong personally led a large expedition that captured several fortresses including Gaemo, Liaodong and Baekam, but stalled before the fortress of Ansi, defended by Yang Manchun. Facing winter and supply problems, Taizong withdrew — the campaign's most famous episode, remembered in Korean tradition as a great defensive victory.

Later campaigns and fall of Goguryeo

After Taizong's death, Emperor Gaozong continued the war in alliance with Silla. Tang and Silla destroyed Goguryeo's ally Baekje in 660. Campaigns against Goguryeo resumed in 661 and 666–668; weakened by a succession struggle among Yeon Gaesomun's sons after his death, Goguryeo collapsed, and its capital Pyongyang fell in 668. Tang established the Protectorate General to Pacify the East over the conquered territory, though Silla later expelled Tang forces from the peninsula in the Silla–Tang War.
