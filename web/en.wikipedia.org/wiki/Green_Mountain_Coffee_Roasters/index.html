Green Mountain Coffee Roasters is an American coffee company founded in 1981 by Robert Stiller in Waitsfield, Vermont. The company grew from a small café and roastery into one of the largest specialty coffee roasters in the United States, and became the parent of the Keurig single-cup brewing system before rebranding itself around that business.

History

Robert Stiller, having sold his stake in the E-Z Wider rolling-paper company, bought a small coffee shop in Waitsfield, Vermont, in 1981 and began roasting coffee. Green Mountain Coffee Roasters went public in 1993. The company invested early in fair trade and organic coffees and became one of the largest roasters of Fair Trade certified coffee in North America.

In 2006 Green Mountain acquired Keurig, Inc., maker of the K-Cup single-serving coffee system, in which it had held a stake since 1993. As K-Cup sales surged, the coffee-roasting identity receded: in March 2014 the company renamed itself Keurig Green Mountain. In December 2015, JAB Holding Company led a US$13.9 billion buyout that took the company private. In 2018, Keurig Green Mountain combined with Dr Pepper Snapple Group to form Keurig Dr Pepper.

Products and sourcing

Green Mountain's roasting operations centred on Waterbury, Vermont, with additional facilities elsewhere in North America. Its brands included Green Mountain Coffee, Newman's Own Organics (under licence), and a wide range of licensed K-Cup pod coffees and teas. The company was noted for its sustainability programmes and its early commitment to Fair Trade certification, though it also faced criticism — and legal challenges from competitors — over its K-Cup patents and the environmental impact of single-use plastic pods.
