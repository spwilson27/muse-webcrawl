Holomorphic function

In complex analysis, a function that is complex-differentiable in a neighbourhood of every point of its domain.


Domain colouring of a holomorphic function; such functions are conformal where the derivative is non-zero.

In mathematics, a holomorphic function is a complex-valued function of one or more complex variables that is complex differentiable in a neighbourhood of each point in a domain in complex coordinate space. The existence of a complex derivative in a neighbourhood is a very strong condition: it implies that any holomorphic function is infinitely differentiable and equal, locally, to its own Taylor series (that is, analytic).

Definition

Given a complex-valued function f of a single complex variable, the derivative of f at a point z0 in its domain is the limit, as z approaches z0 along any path, of [f(z) − f(z0)] / (z − z0). If this limit is the same for every path, f is (complex) differentiable at z0. If f is differentiable at every point of an open set, it is holomorphic there.

Cauchy–Riemann equations

Writing f(x + iy) = u(x, y) + iv(x, y), differentiability is equivalent (given continuity of the partial derivatives) to the Cauchy–Riemann equations: ∂u/∂x = ∂v/∂y and ∂u/∂y = −∂v/∂x. The real and imaginary parts of a holomorphic function are harmonic functions, each satisfying Laplace's equation.

Properties

Holomorphic functions enjoy remarkable rigidity compared with real differentiable functions. Cauchy's integral theorem states that the integral of a holomorphic function around a closed curve is zero; the Cauchy integral formula then expresses all derivatives of the function in terms of its values on a surrounding contour. Consequences include Liouville's theorem (a bounded entire function is constant), the maximum modulus principle, Morera's theorem, and the identity theorem: two holomorphic functions agreeing on a set with a limit point agree everywhere on a connected domain.

Examples

All polynomials in z are entire (holomorphic on the whole complex plane), as are the exponential function and the trigonometric functions. Rational functions are holomorphic away from their poles. The complex logarithm can be made holomorphic on a slit plane. By contrast, the conjugation map z ↦ z̄ and the real and imaginary part functions are nowhere holomorphic.

Several variables

In several complex variables, a function is holomorphic if it is holomorphic in each variable separately; by Hartogs's lemma this implies joint analyticity, a phenomenon with no one-variable analogue. Unlike in one variable, domains of holomorphy can be genuinely restricted.
