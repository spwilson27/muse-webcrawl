Supercruise

Supercruise is sustained supersonic flight of a supersonic aircraft with a useful cargo, passenger, or weapons load, performed efficiently without the use of the highly fuel-inefficient afterburner ("reheat"). Many military supersonic aircraft have limited capability — they can briefly exceed Mach 1 in dry (non-afterburning) thrust, but only in a clean configuration and with marginal performance. A dedicated supercruising aircraft can maintain supersonic speed for extended periods with a combat load, giving advantages in range, intercept geometry and survivability.

History

One of the earliest aircraft to exceed Mach 1 in level flight on dry power alone was the English Electric Lightning, and the BAC TSR-2 was designed around sustained supersonic cruise. The Concorde airliner — in a different sense the classic supercruiser — spent most of each transatlantic flight at Mach 2 without afterburner (reheat was used only for take-off and the transonic acceleration phase), as did the Tupolev Tu-144 after its early flights. For combat aircraft, supercruise was elevated to a formal requirement in the US Advanced Tactical Fighter programme of the 1980s, which produced the Lockheed Martin F-22 Raptor.

Aircraft with supercruise capability

  • Lockheed Martin F-22 Raptor — supercruises at about Mach 1.5 at altitude with internal weapons; the defining example of the capability.
  • Eurofighter Typhoon — can supercruise around Mach 1.2–1.5 in a light configuration.
  • Dassault Rafale and Saab Gripen E — limited dry supersonic cruise (about Mach 1.1–1.2) with some stores.
  • Sukhoi Su-57 — designed for supercruise with its final-stage engines.
  • Concorde and Tu-144 — sustained Mach 2 cruise airliners.

Engineering

Supercruise demands high dry thrust relative to drag: powerful engines with high specific thrust and a high thrust-to-weight ratio, an airframe and inlets optimised for supersonic efficiency (wave drag dominates at such speeds), and structures and systems that tolerate sustained kinetic heating. Avoiding the afterburner reduces fuel consumption, extends range, and greatly lowers the infrared signature compared with reheated flight.
