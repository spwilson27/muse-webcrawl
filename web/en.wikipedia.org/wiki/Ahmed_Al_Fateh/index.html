Ahmed bin Muhammad Al Khalifa (Arabic: أحمد بن محمد آل خليفة), remembered as Ahmed Al Fateh — "Ahmed the Conqueror" — was the founder of the Al Khalifa dynasty's rule over Bahrain. In 1783 he led the family's forces, based at Zubarah in Qatar, in the conquest of Bahrain from its Persian garrison, ending direct Persian control of the archipelago and beginning the rule of the House of Khalifa that continues to the present day.

Background

The Al Khalifa family, a branch of the Bani Utbah tribal confederation, had migrated from Kuwait and established themselves at Zubarah on the northwestern coast of Qatar in the 1760s under Ahmed's father, Muhammad bin Khalifa. Zubarah grew into a prosperous pearl-trading and commercial port, but its success drew conflict with the Al Musallam and other rivals, and the family's ambitions extended across the strait to Bahrain, then administered for Persia by the Al Nasr family of Bushire.

Conquest of Bahrain, 1783

In 1782 fighting between Zubarah and Bahrain escalated after a dispute involving a servant of the Al Khalifa and a retaliatory Bahraini attack on Zubarah was repelled. The following year, Ahmed led the Utbah forces across to Bahrain. The Persian garrison at Manama Fort was defeated, and by 1783 Ahmed had taken control of the islands. Bahraini tradition honors him with the title Al Fateh, "the Conqueror," for this achievement. In the settlement that followed, rule was shared within the family, and after Ahmed's death in 1795 his sons Salman and Abdullah governed jointly — a pattern of dual rule that later produced the separate branches of the dynasty.

Legacy

Ahmed Al Fateh is regarded in Bahrain as the founder of the modern Bahraini state. His name is commemorated by the Ahmed Al Fateh Islamic Centre in Manama, which houses the Grand Mosque — among the largest mosques in the world — and by numerous schools, streets, and institutions. His descendants, through the Al Abdullah and Al Salman lines, constitute the ruling family of the Kingdom of Bahrain.
