Große Fuge

ComposerLudwig van Beethoven
OpusOp. 133 (originally finale of Op. 130)
Composed1825
KeyB-flat major
InstrumentationString quartet

The Große Fuge ("Great Fugue"), Op. 133, is a single-movement composition for string quartet by Ludwig van Beethoven, written in 1825 as the final movement of his String Quartet No. 13 in B-flat major, Op. 130. It is one of the most demanding and forward-looking works of the string quartet repertoire.

History

Beethoven's publisher, concerned that the immense fugue made the quartet unmarketable, persuaded the nearly deaf composer to replace it with a lighter finale — the only time Beethoven agreed to such a substitution. The fugue was published separately as Op. 133, and a four-hand piano arrangement appeared as Op. 134. The original finale was restored in modern performances after Beethoven's intentions were reconsidered in the 20th century.

Music

Lasting about 15–20 minutes, the movement opens with an Overtura presenting its main motifs, then unfolds through contrasting fugal sections of extreme rhythmic and harmonic intensity, interspersed with lyrical episodes. Igor Stravinsky called it "an absolutely contemporary piece of music that will be contemporary forever."

Reception

Contemporary audiences found the work baffling, but it is now recognised as a pinnacle of Beethoven's late style and a precursor of 20th-century modernism; it is frequently performed both within Op. 130 and as a standalone piece.
