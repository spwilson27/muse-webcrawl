Richard Donner

Richard Donner (born Richard Donald Schwartzberg; April 24, 1930 – July 5, 2021) was an American film director and producer. He directed the horror film The Omen (1976), the influential superhero film Superman (1978), The Goonies (1985), Scrooged (1988), and the Lethal Weapon film series (1987–1998). With his wife, producer Lauren Shuler Donner, he ran the production company The Donners' Company.

Early career

Donner began as an actor before moving into directing for television, working on series including The Twilight Zone (directing the episode "Nightmare at 20,000 Feet"), The Fugitive, Get Smart and The Wild Wild West. His early feature credits included X-15 (1961) and Salt and Pepper (1968).

Breakthrough

The Omen was a major commercial success and led to Superman, whose reverent, large-scale treatment of a comic-book hero — under the production motto "verisimilitude" — set the template for modern superhero films. Donner was controversially replaced during Superman II (1980) by Richard Lester; his own cut, Superman II: The Richard Donner Cut, was released in 2006. He went on to direct Ladyhawke, The Goonies, the four Lethal Weapon films, Maverick and Conspiracy Theory.

Legacy

Donner's warm, character-driven style influenced generations of filmmakers, and Superman was selected for preservation in the US National Film Registry in 2017.
