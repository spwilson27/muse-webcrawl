Republic P-47 Thunderbolt

The Republic P-47 Thunderbolt was a World War II-era American fighter aircraft produced by Republic Aviation from 1941 through 1945. It was the largest, heaviest, and most expensive fighter aircraft in history to be powered by a single piston engine, and was effective as a short- to medium-range escort fighter, high-altitude air-to-air fighter, and ground-attack aircraft.

Design

Designed under Alexander Kartveli, the P-47 was built around the powerful Pratt & Whitney R-2800 Double Wasp radial engine with a large turbosupercharger housed in the rear fuselage. It was armed with eight .50-calibre M2 Browning machine guns. The cockpit was roomy, earning the aircraft the nickname "Jug" (short for juggernaut). Its rugged construction allowed it to absorb heavy damage and still return home.

Service

The P-47 first flew in May 1941 and entered combat over Europe in 1943. Later variants with "bubble-top" canopies (the P-47D) improved visibility. The long-range P-47N served in the Pacific. More than 15,000 were built, making it one of the most-produced American fighters. Top-scoring American aces in Europe, such as Francis "Gabby" Gabreski, flew the Thunderbolt.
