Sapphire is a precious gemstone, a variety of the mineral corundum, consisting of aluminium oxide (α-Al2O3) with trace amounts of elements such as iron, titanium, copper, magnesium, or chromium. Although blue is the best-known colour, sapphires occur in many hues; corundum of red colour is classified separately as ruby.

Properties

Sapphire has a hardness of 9 on the Mohs scale, making it one of the hardest natural materials after diamond and moissanite. The characteristic blue colour is caused by intervalence charge transfer between iron and titanium impurities. "Fancy sapphires" include pink, yellow, green, and the rare pink-orange padparadscha variety.

Sources

Historically important sources include Sri Lanka (Ceylon), Myanmar, Thailand, and Kashmir, whose intensely coloured "cornflower blue" stones are prized. Significant deposits are also found in Australia, Madagascar, Montana (USA), and Cambodia.

Synthetic sapphire

Synthetic sapphire is produced industrially by methods such as the Verneuil process. Because it is hard and transparent, it is used for watch crystals, optical windows, scientific instruments, and semiconductor substrates, including in blue LED production.

Famous sapphires

Notable stones include the Star of India (563 carats), the Logan Sapphire, and the blue sapphire in the engagement ring famously worn by Catherine, Princess of Wales.
