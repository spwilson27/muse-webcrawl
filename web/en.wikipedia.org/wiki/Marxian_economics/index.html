Marxian economics is a school of heterodox economic thought based on the work of Karl Marx, principally Das Kapital (1867). Its core concepts include the labour theory of value, surplus value, the falling tendency of the rate of profit, and the analysis of capitalist crisis.

Value and exploitation

In Marx's analysis the value of a commodity is determined by the socially necessary labour time required to produce it. Workers sell their labour power for a wage below the value their labour creates; the difference, surplus value, is the source of profit and, in Marx's view, of exploitation.

Criticisms and legacy

Mainstream economists largely abandoned the labour theory of value after the marginal revolution, and the 'transformation problem' remains contested. Marxian economics nonetheless persists in heterodox departments and influenced dependency theory, world-systems analysis, and theories of imperialism.
