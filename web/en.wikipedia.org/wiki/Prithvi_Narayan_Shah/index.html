Prithvi Narayan Shah (1723–1775) was the last ruler of the Gorkha Kingdom and the first monarch of the unified Kingdom of Nepal. He is credited with launching the campaign that united the many small principalities of the Himalayan region into a single state, and he is regarded in Nepal as the founder of the modern nation.

Early life

Prithvi Narayan Shah was born in Gorkha, west of the Kathmandu Valley, the son of King Nara Bhupal Shah. He ascended the throne of Gorkha in 1743 at the age of about twenty, already determined to conquer the wealthy Kathmandu Valley and unite the surrounding petty kingdoms.

Unification campaign

Beginning with the strategic town of Nuwakot in 1744, Prithvi Narayan Shah combined military campaigns with diplomacy, blockades and alliances. After repeated attempts and setbacks — including the battles of Kirtipur, where his forces suffered heavy losses — he captured the valley kingdoms of Kathmandu and Patan in 1768 and Bhaktapur in 1769. He then made Kathmandu his capital and continued expanding the state's territory east and west.

His sayings and instructions to his heirs were later collected as the Divya Upadesh ("Divine Counsel"), in which he described Nepal as "a yam between two boulders" — a small state situated between the great powers of China and British India — and advised caution in dealings with both.

Legacy

Prithvi Narayan Shah died in 1775 at Devghat. The Shah dynasty he founded ruled Nepal until the abolition of the monarchy in 2008. His birthday was long celebrated as National Unity Day in Nepal, and he remains a central figure in Nepali national identity, though modern historians debate aspects of his conquests and their effects on the ethnic groups incorporated into the new state.
