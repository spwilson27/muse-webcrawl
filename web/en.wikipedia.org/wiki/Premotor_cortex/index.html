Premotor cortex

Location of the premotor cortex, anterior to the primary motor cortex

The premotor cortex is an area of the motor cortex in the frontal lobe of the brain, located just anterior to the primary motor cortex. It occupies much of Brodmann area 6 and plays a central role in the planning and sensory guidance of movement, particularly movements of the proximal and trunk musculature and visually guided reaching and grasping.

Divisions

The premotor cortex is usually divided into lateral and medial portions, each of which is further subdivided:

  • Dorsal premotor cortex (PMd) — involved in reaching movements and in associating arbitrary sensory cues with particular movements.
  • Ventral premotor cortex (PMv) — important for grasping and for shaping the hand to objects; in primates it contains mirror neurons and corresponds to part of Broca's area (Brodmann areas 44 and 45) in the left hemisphere.
  • Medial premotor areas — include the supplementary motor area (SMA) and pre-supplementary motor area (pre-SMA) on the medial surface, which are involved in internally generated and sequenced movements.

Function

The premotor cortex contributes to motor control in several ways. It prepares and selects movements in response to external cues, integrates sensory information (especially visual and somatosensory) with motor commands, and controls the axial and proximal muscles that position the body and limbs. Unlike the primary motor cortex, which sends the majority of direct projections to spinal motor neurons for fine distal movements, the premotor cortex acts largely through interconnections with the primary motor cortex and through brainstem pathways such as the reticulospinal tract.

Neurons in the premotor cortex become active during the preparation of movement, before movement onset, and some encode the intended direction or target of a reach. Ventral premotor neurons discharge both when a monkey performs a grasping action and when it observes another individual performing a similar action — these are the celebrated mirror neurons, which have been proposed to underlie action understanding and imitation.

Connections

The premotor cortex receives input from the posterior parietal cortex, the prefrontal cortex, and the cerebellum (via the thalamus), and it projects to the primary motor cortex, the brainstem, and the spinal cord. This position in the network allows it to translate perceptual information and goals into motor plans.
