George Catlett Marshall Jr. (December 31, 1880 – October 16, 1959) was an American statesman and soldier, widely regarded as one of the most accomplished organisers of victory in the Second World War and as the author of the Marshall Plan for European recovery.

Military career

A graduate of the Virginia Military Institute (1901), Marshall served in the Philippines and in France during the First World War, where he distinguished himself as a staff planner. As Chief of Staff of the Army from 1939, he presided over the enormous expansion of the U.S. Army and Army Air Forces and coordinated Allied strategy alongside George Kennan-era policymakers; Winston Churchill described him as the "organiser of victory". Dwight D. Eisenhower and many other senior commanders were officers Marshall had identified and promoted.

Secretary of State and the Marshall Plan

After a special mission to China (1945–1947), Marshall became Secretary of State under President Harry Truman. In a Harvard University commencement address on 5 June 1947 he proposed the European Recovery Program — the Marshall Plan — which channelled some $13 billion of aid to rebuild Western Europe. As Secretary of Defense (1950–1951) during the Korean War he helped restore civilian-military relations after the relief of General MacArthur. In 1953 he received the Nobel Peace Prize, the only career soldier to do so for such statecraft.
