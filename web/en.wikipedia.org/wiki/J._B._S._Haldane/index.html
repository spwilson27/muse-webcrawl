J. B. S. Haldane

John Burdon Sanderson Haldane (5 November 1892 – 1 December 1964), known as J. B. S. Haldane, was a British-born scientist who worked in physiology, genetics, evolutionary biology, and mathematics. With Ronald Fisher and Sewall Wright, he was a founder of population genetics and the modern evolutionary synthesis.

Contributions

Haldane's mathematical series A Mathematical Theory of Natural and Artificial Selection (1924–1934) quantified natural selection. He derived Haldane's rule on hybrid sterility, mapped human genes (including early work on haemophilia and colour blindness linkage), developed (with George Edward Briggs) the Briggs–Haldane treatment of enzyme kinetics, and in his 1929 essay "The Origin of Life" independently proposed, alongside Oparin, the "primordial soup" hypothesis.

Life

Son of physiologist John Scott Haldane, he experimented on himself in diving and gas physiology, served with distinction in the First World War, and became a celebrated popularizer of science (Possible Worlds, 1927, contains his famous remark that the universe is "queerer than we can suppose"). A committed Marxist for much of his life, he emigrated to India in 1957, becoming an Indian citizen and working at the Indian Statistical Institute in Kolkata and later Bhubaneswar, where he died.
