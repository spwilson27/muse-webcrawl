Defence industry of India
Overseen byMinistry of Defence; Department of Defence Production
Key organisationsDRDO, HAL, Bharat Electronics, Mazagon Dock, Ordnance Factory Board (corporatised 2021)
Major productsTejas fighter, Arjun tank, BrahMos missile, INS Vikrant carrier
Policy"Make in India" / Atmanirbhar Bharat self-reliance drive

The defence industry of India is a strategically important sector comprising state-owned enterprises, research organisations led by the Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO), and a growing private sector. India has historically been one of the world's largest arms importers — long relying heavily on Russia — but since the 2010s governments have pursued self-reliance under the "Make in India" and Atmanirbhar Bharat programmes, with import bans on hundreds of items and rising defence exports.

Structure

Public-sector undertakings such as Hindustan Aeronautics Limited (HAL), Bharat Electronics, Bharat Dynamics, and the shipyards Mazagon Dock, Garden Reach, and Goa Shipyard produce aircraft, electronics, missiles, and warships. In 2021 the two-century-old Ordnance Factory Board was dissolved and its factories reorganised into seven new defence public-sector companies. Private firms — including Tata Advanced Systems, Larsen & Toubro, Mahindra Defence, and Adani Defence — have expanded from components into complete platforms.

Flagship programmes

  • HAL Tejas — indigenous light combat aircraft, in service with the Indian Air Force.
  • BrahMos — supersonic cruise missile developed jointly with Russia; exported to the Philippines.
  • INS Vikrant — India's first indigenously designed and built aircraft carrier, commissioned in 2022.
  • Agni and Prithvi missile families, Pinaka rocket artillery, and the Akash air-defence system.

Exports and challenges

Indian defence exports have grown rapidly from a low base, reaching record levels in the mid-2020s, with sales of artillery, rockets, radars, and patrol vessels to partners in Asia and Africa. Persistent challenges include development delays, engine dependence (notably for fighters), and the armed forces' historical preference for proven foreign equipment.
