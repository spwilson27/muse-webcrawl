Capitalization

Script featureBicameral scripts
Related conceptsLetter case, proper nouns

Capitalization (American English) or capitalisation (British English) is writing a word with its first letter as a capital letter (uppercase letter) and the remaining letters in lower case, in writing systems with a case distinction. The term also may refer to the choice of case, that is, writing with capital letters throughout ("all caps") or in some other pattern. Conventional writing systems (orthographies) for different languages have different conventions for capitalization, based on a variety of structural and discourse considerations.

Conventions in English

In English, capitalization is used for: the first word of a sentence; the pronoun "I"; proper nouns (names of specific people, places, and things) and adjectives derived from them; days of the week, months, and holidays (but not seasons); languages and nationalities; and the significant words in titles of works, depending on the style guide. Religious terms, academic degrees, and courtesy titles are also commonly capitalised.

Title case and sentence case

Style guides differ on headline capitalisation. Title case capitalises the first word and all nouns, pronouns, verbs, adjectives, and adverbs, leaving short articles, conjunctions, and prepositions lowercase. Sentence case capitalises only the first word and proper nouns, and is preferred by many newspapers, journals, and websites.

History

Early English printing capitalised erratically, often any noun considered important; by the late 18th century, grammarians had regularised usage close to modern practice. The 1701–1800 fashion for capitalising all nouns, echoed in the United States Constitution, survives today only in German orthography.

Other languages

French capitalises proper adjectives only selectively (un vin français keeps the adjective lowercase, while the noun for a French person, un Français, is capitalised). Dutch, Spanish, and Italian have their own rules for days, months, and titles, which are generally lowercase. In German, all nouns are capitalised.
