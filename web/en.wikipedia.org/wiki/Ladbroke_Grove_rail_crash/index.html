The Ladbroke Grove rail crash (also known as the Paddington rail crash) occurred on 5 October 1999 at Ladbroke Grove in London, England, when two passenger trains collided almost head-on after one of them had passed a signal at danger. Thirty-one people were killed, including both drivers, and 417 were injured. It remains one of the worst rail accidents in British history.

The accident

At 08:06, a Thames Trains three-car Class 165 Turbo diesel multiple unit departed London Paddington station for Bedwyn in Wiltshire. Minutes later it passed signal SN109, which was showing red, on the main line out of Paddington. At 08:11 it collided almost head-on with a First Great Western InterCity 125 high-speed train travelling from Cheltenham Spa to Paddington, which was approaching at about 130 km/h (81 mph). The combined closing speed was approximately 210 km/h (130 mph).

The leading coach of the Thames train was destroyed, and a fire, fuelled by diesel spilled from the trains' tanks, engulfed the first-class saloon coach H of the HST. Both drivers — Brian Cooper of the HST and Michael Hodder of the Thames train — were among the 31 who died.

Causes

The public inquiry, chaired by Lord Cullen, found that signal SN109 was notoriously difficult to see, having been passed at danger eight times in the previous six years. Its sighting was obscured by overhead line equipment and the gantry structure, and the low morning sun made matters worse. Hodder, the Thames driver, had only recently qualified and had not been warned of the signal's poor sighting record. The inquiry also criticised Railtrack, the infrastructure operator, for failing to act on the known risk, and Thames Trains for deficiencies in driver training.

Aftermath and reforms

The crash, coming two years after the Southall rail crash in the same area, led to major changes in British railway safety: accelerated installation of the Train Protection and Warning System (TPWS), improvements in signal sighting standards, the creation of an independent Rail Safety and Standards Board, and ultimately the replacement of Railtrack by the not-for-dividend Network Rail in 2002. Thames Trains was fined £2 million and Railtrack £4 million for health-and-safety offences.

Memorial

A memorial garden was created near the site at Ladbroke Grove, and a memorial service is held each year on the anniversary.
