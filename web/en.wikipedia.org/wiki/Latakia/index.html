Latakia

Country: Syria
Governorate: Latakia
Role: Chief seaport of Syria

Latakia (Arabic: اللاذقية, al-Lādhiqiyya) is the principal port city of Syria and capital of Latakia Governorate on the Mediterranean coast. Its population was roughly 400,000 before the Syrian civil war.

History

The site near ancient Ugarit was refounded by Seleucus I Nicator around 300 BC as Laodicea in Syria, named for his mother Laodice. It flourished under Rome and Byzantium, suffered earthquakes and Arab, Crusader and Ottoman rule. Under the French Mandate it was capital of the Alawite State. Latakia became Syria's main port, handling most of its maritime trade.

Geography and economy

The city lies on a headland with beaches popular as domestic resorts. Its port, tobacco trade (Latakia tobacco is a famed pipe blend, fire-cured and aromatic), agriculture in the surrounding coastal plain, and Tishreen University are central to its economy. The region is home to many Alawites, alongside Sunni and Christian communities.

Civil war

Latakia saw protests in 2011 and remained largely under government control during the war, hosting many displaced people; its hinterland experienced fighting. After the fall of the Assad government in December 2024 the coastal region underwent major political change.
