National Guard Bureau

The National Guard Bureau (NGB) is the federal agency responsible for administering the United States National Guard. Located in Arlington, Virginia, it is a joint activity of the Department of Defense and serves as the channel of communication between the states and the federal government concerning National Guard matters. It administers federal funding and policy for the Army National Guard and the Air National Guard.

History

The bureau traces its origin to the Militia Bureau created after the Militia Act of 1903, which brought greater federal standardization to state militias. It took the name National Guard Bureau in 1933. The National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2008 elevated the Chief of the National Guard Bureau to the rank of four-star general and made the office a statutory member-level adviser; in 2012 the Chief became a formal member of the Joint Chiefs of Staff.

Leadership

The bureau is headed by the Chief of the National Guard Bureau, a four-star general or flag officer appointed by the President with the advice and consent of the Senate. The Chief is the principal adviser to the Secretary of Defense on National Guard matters and ensures that Guard forces are properly organized, trained, and equipped for both their federal missions and state duties under governors.

Functions

The NGB oversees a force of more than 400,000 soldiers and airmen across the 50 states, the District of Columbia, and U.S. territories. It supports domestic operations such as disaster response and border support, as well as overseas deployments in federal status. The bureau also administers the State Partnership Program, which links state Guards with partner nations around the world.
