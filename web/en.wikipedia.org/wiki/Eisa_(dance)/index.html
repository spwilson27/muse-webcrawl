Eisa (dance)

Eisa (Okinawan: エイサー, eisaa) is a form of folk dance originating in the Ryukyu Islands, most closely associated with Okinawa, Japan. It is performed by young people during the Bon festival (Obon) to honour the spirits of their ancestors, with dancers moving to songs accompanied by sanshin (three-stringed lutes), drums, and chanting.

Performance

Eisa troupes, usually organised by neighbourhood youth associations, parade through their districts during the nights of Obon, dancing in formation. Male dancers typically carry large barrel drums (ōdaiko), smaller hand drums (shimedaiko), or hand gongs, while female dancers often perform graceful hand movements; performers wear colourful costumes and chant calls such as "ha iya sasa." The dancing is vigorous and acrobatic, combining drumming with leaps, spins, and martial-arts-like stances said to derive in part from karate.

History and modern development

Eisa's roots are traced to Buddhist songs and dances brought to Okinawa centuries ago, blending with local folk traditions. After World War II, and particularly after the Battle of Okinawa, eisa was revived as an expression of community and recovery; competitions between youth groups formalised the modern style. Today eisa is a symbol of Okinawan identity, performed at festivals throughout Japan and by Okinawan diaspora communities abroad, and features in events such as the Zento Eisa Matsuri (All-Island Eisa Festival) in Okinawa City, founded in 1956. Contemporary styles include "traditional" eisa preserving village forms and "creative" eisa mixing modern music and choreography.
