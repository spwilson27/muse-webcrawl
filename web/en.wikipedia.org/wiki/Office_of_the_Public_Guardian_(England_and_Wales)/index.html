Office of the Public Guardian (England and Wales)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
The OPG is an executive agency of the Ministry of Justice.

The Office of the Public Guardian (OPG) is an executive agency of the Ministry of Justice of the United Kingdom, established under the Mental Capacity Act 2005. It supports the Public Guardian, an official charged with protecting people in England and Wales who lack the mental capacity to make certain decisions for themselves. The OPG took over the functions of the former Public Guardianship Office when the Act came into force on 1 October 2007.

Functions

The OPG's principal responsibilities include:

  • Registering lasting powers of attorney (LPAs) and the older enduring powers of attorney (EPAs), which let a person appoint attorneys to make decisions about property and finance, or health and welfare, should they lose capacity.
  • Supervising deputies appointed by the Court of Protection to manage the affairs of people who lack capacity and have no attorney, including requiring reports, accounts and security bonds.
  • Investigating concerns and allegations of abuse or financial misconduct by attorneys and deputies, and where necessary applying to the Court of Protection for their removal.
  • Maintaining registers of powers of attorney and deputyship orders, and providing information to attorneys, deputies and the public.

Public Guardian

The Public Guardian is appointed by the Lord Chancellor and works closely with the Court of Protection, which makes decisions on financial and welfare matters for people who lack capacity. Applications to register LPAs grew enormously in the 2010s and 2020s as awareness of dementia and advance planning increased, and the Powers of Attorney Act 2023 provided for a modernised, largely digital registration process.
