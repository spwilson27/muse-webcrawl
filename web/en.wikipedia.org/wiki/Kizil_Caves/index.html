Kizil Caves

The Kizil Caves (also Kizil Grottoes) are a set of Buddhist rock-cut caves near Baicheng County, Xinjiang, China, on the northern bank of the Muzat River in the ancient kingdom of Kucha on the Silk Road. Dating mainly from the 3rd to 8th centuries CE, the complex contains over 230 numbered caves with murals in Indo-Iranian and later styles depicting Jataka tales, Buddhas and Tocharian donors.

The caves are among the earliest major Buddhist cave complexes in China, earlier than Mogao. Many murals were removed by early 20th-century German Turfan expeditions to Berlin, some destroyed in World War II. The site is a protected cultural heritage site.
