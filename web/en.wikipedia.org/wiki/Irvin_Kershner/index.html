Irvin Kershner (born Isadore Kershner; April 29, 1923 – November 27, 2010) was an American director of film and television. He is best known for directing Star Wars: Episode V – The Empire Strikes Back (1980), widely regarded as among the finest of the Star Wars films, as well as the James Bond film Never Say Never Again (1983) and RoboCop 2 (1990).

Early life and career

Kershner was born in Philadelphia to a family of Ukrainian-Jewish background and studied music, photography and art before attending the University of Southern California's film school, where one of his instructors was cinematographer Slavko Vorkapić. He also taught at USC, where George Lucas was among his students. After work with the US Information Service and television documentaries, he directed low-budget features including Stakeout on Dope Street (1958), produced by Roger Corman, and went on to dramas such as Hoodlum Priest (1961), The Luck of Ginger Coffey (1964) and A Fine Madness (1966) with Sean Connery.

Later work

His 1970s films included Loving, Up the Sandbox (1972) with Barbra Streisand, The Return of a Man Called Horse (1976) and the thriller Eyes of Laura Mars (1978). Lucas selected Kershner for The Empire Strikes Back because of his emphasis on character; the film became the highest-grossing release of 1980 and is frequently cited among the greatest sequels ever made. Kershner later directed Sean Connery's return as Bond in Never Say Never Again (1983), RoboCop 2 (1990) and episodes of television series including seaQuest DSV. He died in Los Angeles in 2010 following lung cancer.
