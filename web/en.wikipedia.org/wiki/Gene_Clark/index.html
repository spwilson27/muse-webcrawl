Harold Eugene Clark (November 17, 1944 – May 24, 1991) was an American singer-songwriter and founding member of the folk rock band the Byrds. He was the band's principal songwriter during its early years, writing classics such as "I'll Feel a Whole Lot Better", "Here Without You" and "Eight Miles High" (with Jim McGuinn and David Crosby).

The Byrds

Clark formed the Byrds in Los Angeles in 1964 with McGuinn and Crosby. Fronting the band on vocals and tambourine, he wrote much of its early material, but frustration over his share of royalties, a fear of flying that limited touring, and internal tensions led him to leave in 1966, just as "Eight Miles High" was released.

Solo career

His solo debut Gene Clark with the Gosdin Brothers (1967) is now regarded as a country-rock landmark. With banjoist Doug Dillard he formed Dillard & Clark, releasing the acclaimed The Fantastic Expedition of Dillard & Clark (1968). Solo albums White Light (1971) and especially No Other (1974) — an expensive, lavishly produced record that initially sold poorly — built Clark's cult reputation; No Other is now widely considered a masterpiece. Brief Byrds reunions and the McGuinn, Clark & Hillman project (which yielded the hit "Don't You Write Her Off") followed, but ill health and substance problems curtailed his later career.

Legacy

Clark died in 1991 of complications from a bleeding ulcer. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame with the Byrds the same year. His songs have been covered by artists including Tom Petty, and No Other was reissued to acclaim in 2019.
