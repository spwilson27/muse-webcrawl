Túpac Amaru II

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Túpac Amaru II (born José Gabriel Condorcanqui, 1738–1781) was an Indigenous Peruvian leader who led the largest rebellion in Spanish colonial America against the abuses of the colonial administration. He claimed descent from Túpac Amaru, the last Inca ruler of the Neo-Inca State.

The rebellion

Condorcanqui was a kuraka (hereditary chief) and muleteer who had petitioned in vain for relief from the mita labour draft and the repartimiento de mercancías. In November 1780 he arrested and executed the corregidor Antonio de Arriaga at Tungasuca, launching a revolt that swept the southern Peruvian highlands and Upper Peru (modern Bolivia), drawing support from Indigenous people, mestizos and some creoles.

Defeat and execution

After initial victories, including the Battle of Sangarará, the rebels failed to take Cusco. Túpac Amaru was captured in April 1781 and executed in Cusco's main plaza on 18 May 1781, forced to watch the killing of his wife Micaela Bastidas and family before being quartered.

Legacy

His revolt shook the Spanish empire, prompted reforms, and made him a national hero of Peru. His name was later adopted by the Tupamaros of Uruguay and Peru's MRTA guerrilla movement.
