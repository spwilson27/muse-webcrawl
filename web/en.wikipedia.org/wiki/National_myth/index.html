National myth

A national myth is an inspiring narrative or anecdote about a nation's past, often partly legendary, that serves to symbolise and legitimate national identity, values and aspirations. Such myths may be based on real events embellished over time.

Examples and functions

Examples include the founding of Rome by Romulus, the American frontier and "city upon a hill", the French revolutionary heritage, and origin stories of many modern states. Scholars such as Ernest Renan and Benedict Anderson have noted how shared stories — and shared forgetting — help create imagined communities.

National myths can foster cohesion but may also obscure violence, exclusion or historical complexity, and are frequently contested and revised.
