Test (assessment)

A test or examination (exam) is an educational assessment intended to measure a test-taker's knowledge, skill, aptitude, physical fitness, or classification in many other topics (e.g., beliefs). A test may be administered verbally, on paper, on a computer, or in a predetermined area that requires a test taker to demonstrate or perform a set of skills. Tests vary in style, rigor, and requirements; they may be closed-book or open-book, timed or untimed, and individually proctored or unproctored.

Types

Tests may be formative — intended to monitor learning and provide feedback — or summative, evaluating achievement at the end of instruction. Standardized tests are administered and scored under consistent conditions and are widely used for admissions and accountability, such as the SAT and ACT in the United States. Other forms include oral examinations, practical and performance assessments, driving tests, language proficiency tests, and psychological tests.

Scoring and properties

Scores may be reported as raw marks, percentages, grades, or scaled scores. Psychometrics studies the measurement properties of tests, particularly reliability (consistency of scores) and validity (whether the test measures what it claims to measure). Test design must also address fairness, accessibility, and security against cheating.

History

Formal examinations originated in imperial China, where the civil service examination system (keju), established under the Sui and Tang dynasties, selected officials by merit for over a millennium and influenced later examination systems worldwide.
