Kalashnikov Concern (Russian: Концерн «Калашников», Kontsern Kalashnikov) is a Russian defense manufacturing enterprise headquartered in Izhevsk. It is best known as the manufacturer of the AK family of assault rifles designed by Mikhail Kalashnikov, and is one of the largest producers of small arms in the world.

History

The enterprise traces its origins to the Izhevsk weapons factory founded by decree of Tsar Alexander I in 1807. During the Soviet era the plant, known as Izhmash, produced the AK-47 and its many derivatives, as well as other infantry weapons, artillery, and civilian products. In 2013, Izhmash and the Izhevsk Mechanical Plant were merged and rebranded as Kalashnikov Concern under the state corporation Rostec, in a consolidation of Russian small-arms production.

Products

The concern produces the AK-12 and AK-15 rifles adopted by the Russian armed forces, earlier AK-100 series export rifles, the SVD Dragunov and newer SVCh sniper rifles, Saiga sporting and hunting firearms, and the MP line of pistols through its Izhevsk Mechanical Plant division. In the 2010s and 2020s it expanded into unmanned aerial vehicles, including loitering munitions, as well as guided artillery ammunition, military boats, and electric vehicles.

Sanctions

Following Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014, the United States and the European Union imposed sanctions on Kalashnikov Concern, cutting it off from the American civilian market, where its Saiga rifles and shotguns had been popular. Sanctions were expanded after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.
