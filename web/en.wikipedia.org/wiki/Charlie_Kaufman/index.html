Charles Stuart Kaufman (born November 19, 1958) is an American screenwriter, film director, and novelist. Regarded as one of the most celebrated screenwriters of his era, Kaufman has been praised by Roger Ebert and others for exploring universal themes of identity, mortality, and the workings of the mind within highly inventive, surreal narratives. He first gained attention for the film Being John Malkovich (1999), and won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay for Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind (2004).

Early career

Kaufman grew up in Massapequa, Long Island, and West Hartford, Connecticut. After a period writing comedy pieces, he broke into television in the early 1990s, writing for the sketch and sitcom circuit including Get a Life and The Dana Carvey Show. His screenplay for Being John Malkovich, directed by Spike Jonze, became a surprise critical hit.

Film work

He reunited with Jonze for Adaptation (2002), a self-referential film about his own struggle to adapt Susan Orlean's The Orchid Thief, in which he credited a fictional twin brother, Donald Kaufman, as co-writer. Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind (2004), directed by Michel Gondry, concerns a couple who undergo a procedure to erase each other from their memories. Kaufman's directorial debut, Synecdoche, New York (2008), follows a theater director who builds a life-size replica of New York inside a warehouse; Roger Ebert later called it the best film of its decade. He followed it with the stop-motion film Anomalisa (2015, co-directed with Duke Johnson) and the psychological thriller I'm Thinking of Ending Things (2020).

Other work

In 2020 Kaufman published his first novel, Antkind, a sprawling comic work about a film critic who discovers the only copy of a three-month-long stop-motion film. He has also written for the stage, including the play Hope Leaves the Theater.
