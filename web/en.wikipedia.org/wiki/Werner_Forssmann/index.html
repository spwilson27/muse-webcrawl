Werner Forssmann

Werner Theodor Otto Forssmann (29 August 1904 – 1 June 1979) was a German physician who, in 1929, performed the first human cardiac catheterisation — on himself. He shared the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1956 with André Cournand and Dickinson W. Richards for work relating to heart catheterisation and pathological changes in the circulatory system.

The 1929 experiment

While working at Eberswalde, Forssmann reasoned that a catheter could be inserted into the heart through a vein to deliver drugs or measure pressures. His chief forbade the experiment, so Forssmann performed it on himself: he anaesthetised his own elbow, inserted a ureteral catheter into his antecubital vein and advanced it some 65 cm to his right atrium, then walked to the X-ray department where a radiograph confirmed the catheter tip's position. The experiment initially cost him advancement and he left cardiology, but it opened an entirely new field of invasive cardiology.

Later life

Forssmann worked as a urologist and country doctor in Germany, served as a military surgeon during the Second World War, and was a prisoner of war. After the Nobel award recognised his foundational contribution, he received honorary positions and memberships. He died in 1979 in Schopfheim.
