Little Bighorn

Little Bighorn may refer to the Little Bighorn River in Wyoming and Montana, a tributary of the Bighorn River about 222 km (138 mi) long, and to the battle named for it.

The river

The Little Bighorn rises in the Bighorn Mountains of northern Wyoming and flows north into Montana, joining the Bighorn River near Hardin. Its Little Bighorn Valley lies partly within the Crow Indian Reservation.

The battle

The Battle of the Little Bighorn (25–26 June 1876) — often called "Custer's Last Stand" and known to the Lakota as the Battle of the Greasy Grass — was fought along the river during the Great Sioux War of 1876. Lieutenant Colonel George Armstrong Custer's detachment of the U.S. 7th Cavalry was annihilated by a combined Lakota, Northern Cheyenne and Arapaho force led in battle by leaders including Crazy Horse and Chief Gall, in a camp whose spiritual leader was Sitting Bull. All 210 men with Custer died, and total U.S. losses were 268 killed.

The battlefield is preserved as Little Bighorn Battlefield National Monument in Montana, where white marble markers dot Last Stand Hill and a memorial to the Native American warriors was dedicated in 2003.
