Fairmount Bagel is a Montreal-style bagel bakery on Fairmount Avenue in the Mile End neighbourhood of Montreal, Quebec, Canada. Founded in 1919, it is widely credited as the first bagel bakery in Montreal and remains one of the city's most famous food institutions, along with its later rival St-Viateur Bagel.

History

The bakery was founded by Isadore Shlafman, a Jewish immigrant from Kiev, who began selling bagels from a pushcart and then a small storefront before settling on Fairmount Avenue. According to family tradition, the bagel recipe came from Shlafman's grandfather. Fairmount Bagel is still owned and operated by the Shlafman family, now in its fourth generation.

Montreal-style bagels

Montreal-style bagels differ from the better-known New York style: they are smaller, denser and sweeter, with a larger hole. They are made with malt and egg, poached in honey-sweetened water, and baked in a wood-fired oven, giving them a crisp, slightly caramelised exterior. Fairmount's most popular varieties are sesame and poppy seed, along with all-dressed, cinnamon raisin and other flavours. The bakery operates around the clock and supplies restaurants and shops across the city.

Cultural significance

Fairmount Bagel is a landmark of Montreal's Jewish culinary heritage and a stop on many food tours of the city. In 2008, Montreal-born astronaut Greg Chamitoff carried Fairmount sesame bagels aboard the Space Shuttle, the first bagels in space.
