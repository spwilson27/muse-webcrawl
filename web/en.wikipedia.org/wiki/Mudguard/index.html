Mudguard

A mudguard (called a fender in North American English for bicycles and motorcycles) is a part of a bicycle, motorcycle or other vehicle that shields the rider and the machine from mud, water and grit thrown up by the wheels. Mudguards typically curve over the top of the wheel, following its arc, and may extend down behind it; a flexible extension at the bottom of a front mudguard is called a mudflap.

Bicycles

Bicycle mudguards are common on utility, touring and commuting bicycles, especially in wet climates such as northern Europe, where full-length guards with close clearances keep spray off the rider's back and feet and reduce contamination of the drivetrain. Racing bicycles traditionally omitted them for lightness, relying on tight frame clearances, but modern disc-brake frames increasingly provide mounts for full guards. Clip-on and short "ass-saver" style guards offer partial protection for bikes without mounting eyelets. Tandem and cargo cycles use similar arrangements.

Motor vehicles

On cars, the bodywork panels surrounding the wheel arches perform the mudguard function (and are called fenders in American English), while trucks and trailers are often fitted with separate rubber or plastic mudflaps behind the wheels; in many jurisdictions, spray-suppression devices are legally required on heavy vehicles. Early automobiles had separate, cycle-style mudguards before fenders were integrated into the body in the 1930s and 1940s.
