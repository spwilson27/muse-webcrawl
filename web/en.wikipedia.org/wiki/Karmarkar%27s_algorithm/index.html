Karmarkar's algorithm is an algorithm introduced by Narendra Karmarkar in 1984 for solving linear programming problems. It was the first reasonably efficient polynomial-time algorithm for linear programming: the ellipsoid method of Leonid Khachiyan (1979) was polynomial but impractical, while the simplex method, though fast in practice, has exponential worst-case complexity.

Description

Unlike the simplex method, which travels along the boundary of the feasible polytope, Karmarkar's method is an interior-point method: it moves through the interior of the feasible region. The algorithm applies a projective transformation to the problem, repeatedly recentres the current iterate by mapping the feasible region onto a simplex, takes a step in the direction of steepest descent of a potential function, and transforms back. Each iteration reduces the potential function by a constant amount, giving an overall polynomial bound.

Impact and controversy

The announcement generated enormous publicity. Because Karmarkar worked at AT&T Bell Laboratories, AT&T sought patents on the algorithm and marketed a commercial solver (the KORBX system). The patenting of a mathematical algorithm provoked debate about software patents. Karmarkar's work spurred a flood of research into interior-point methods, which — through later primal-dual variants by others — became the standard for large-scale linear and convex optimisation.
