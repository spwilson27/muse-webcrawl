USS Yorktown (CV-5) was an aircraft carrier of the United States Navy, the lead ship of the Yorktown class, which was designed following the experience gained with earlier converted carriers. She was named after the Battle of Yorktown in 1781. In the early phase of the Pacific War she participated in the Marshalls–Gilberts raids, the Lae–Salamaua raid, and the Battle of the Coral Sea, before being sunk at the Battle of Midway in June 1942.

Coral Sea

In May 1942, Yorktown and Lexington intercepted the Japanese invasion force bound for Port Moresby. Their aircraft sank the light carrier Shōhō and damaged Shōkaku. Yorktown was hit by a single bomb that killed or wounded 66 men, but she was repaired with remarkable speed at Pearl Harbor — in about 48 to 72 hours — in time to take part in the decisive battle at Midway barely a month later.

Midway

At the Battle of Midway (4 June 1942), dive bombers from Yorktown fatally damaged the Japanese carrier Sōryū. Japanese aircraft from Hiryū twice crippled Yorktown with bombs and aerial torpedoes, and she was abandoned, though salvage parties later re-boarded her and had nearly saved the ship when the Japanese submarine I-168 torpedoed both Yorktown and the destroyer Hammann alongside her on 6 June. Yorktown capsized and sank the following morning.

Her wreck was discovered in May 1998 by Dr. Robert Ballard, sitting upright in about 16,650 feet (5,070 m) of water near Midway Atoll, remarkably well preserved.
