USS Nautilus (SSN-571) was the world's first operational nuclear-powered submarine and the first submarine to complete a submerged transit of the North Pole, on 3 August 1958. She was named after the fictional submarine in Jules Verne's Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Seas and earlier U.S. Navy ships of the same name. The project was championed by Captain (later Admiral) Hyman G. Rickover, head of the Navy's nuclear propulsion programme.

Career

On 17 January 1955, Nautilus put to sea and transmitted the historic message "Underway on nuclear power." Freed from the need to surface or snorkel for air, she shattered submerged speed and endurance records, travelling over 1,300 nautical miles submerged on her shakedown cruise. In 1958, under Commander William R. Anderson, she crossed beneath the Arctic ice cap in Operation Sunshine, passing the geographic North Pole — a powerful demonstration for the United States after the Soviet Sputnik launch.

Legacy

Nautilus served until 1980, by then having travelled more than 490,000 nautical miles. She demonstrated the practicality of the nuclear submarine and set the pattern for the nuclear navies that followed. Designated a National Historic Landmark, she is preserved as a museum ship at the Submarine Force Library and Museum in Groton, Connecticut.
