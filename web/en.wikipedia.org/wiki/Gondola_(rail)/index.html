Gondola (rail)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In American railroad terminology, a gondola is an open-topped railroad car with low side walls used for transporting loose bulk materials such as coal, ore, scrap metal, steel products, and timber. Because the car is open at the top, loads are added by crane, conveyor, or front loader, and the car generally provides no protection from the weather.

Design and types

Standard gondolas have fixed sides and ends, with loads often removed by crane with a magnet or clam-shell bucket. Rotary gondolas, widely used in unit coal trains, have a rotating coupler at one end so that the entire car can be inverted in a rotary dumper and emptied without uncoupling the train. Other variants include coil cars for steel coils, covered gondolas with removable tops for weather-sensitive loads, and high-sided "bathtub" gondolas built for coal service.

Use

Gondolas were among the earliest freight car types and remain one of the most common. In the Association of American Railroads reporting system, gondolas are classed as type G. Drop-bottom designs of the 19th and early 20th centuries were largely displaced by the hopper car for free-flowing bulk commodities, but the gondola remains preferred for dense, irregular, or crane-loaded freight.
