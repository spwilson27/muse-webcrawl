"All Day and All of the Night" is a song by the English rock band the Kinks, written by frontman Ray Davies. Released as a single in October 1964, it reached No. 2 on the UK Singles Chart and No. 7 on the US Billboard Hot 100. Like the band's earlier hit "You Really Got Me", it is built on a driving power-chord riff played by guitarist Dave Davies.

Composition and recording

The song was written quickly while the band toured, consciously following the formula of "You Really Got Me": a two-chord riff, shouted backing phrases and a raw, distorted guitar sound produced by Dave Davies's slashed amplifier speaker cone. Recorded at Pye Studios in London with producer Shel Talmy, the track also features session drummer Bobby Graham. Its lyric is a straightforward declaration of romantic obsession — the narrator wants to be with his girl "all day and all of the night".

Reception and legacy

Critics regard the song as a foundational text of garage rock and hard rock, demonstrating the commercial power of the distorted riff. In 1999 it was inducted into the Grammy Hall of Fame along with the Kinks' other early singles. The song has been covered by the Stranglers (whose 1988 version reached the UK top ten), Van Halen, Metallica and many others.

Similarities controversy

The Doors' 1968 single "Hello, I Love You" bore a noted resemblance to "All Day and All of the Night"; Ray Davies later said the matter was settled out of court with the Doors' publisher, and Jim Morrison acknowledged the debt. The Kinks themselves were sometimes accused of imitating their own earlier hit — an assessment Davies embraced as consistency of style.
