Rivastigmine

Rivastigmine, sold under the brand name Exelon among others, is a cholinesterase inhibitor used for the treatment of mild to moderate Alzheimer's disease and for dementia associated with Parkinson's disease. It is taken by mouth or applied to the skin as a transdermal patch.

Medical uses

Rivastigmine increases concentrations of the neurotransmitter acetylcholine in the brain by reversibly inhibiting both acetylcholinesterase and butyrylcholinesterase, the enzymes that break acetylcholine down. This cholinergic enhancement is believed to modestly improve cognition and daily functioning in dementia, though it does not alter the underlying course of the disease. It was approved for medical use in the late 1990s and is available as a generic medication.

Side effects

Common side effects include nausea, vomiting, loss of appetite, diarrhoea and weight loss. These gastrointestinal effects are dose-related and tend to be less frequent with the transdermal patch, which delivers the drug steadily through the skin. Rivastigmine was developed in Israel by Marta Weinstock and colleagues at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, and was brought to market by Novartis.
