Bézout domain

Bézout domain
FieldCommutative algebra
DefinitionIntegral domain in which every finitely generated ideal is principal
Named afterÉtienne Bézout
ExamplesPrincipal ideal domains, valuation domains, the ring of entire functions

In mathematics, a Bézout domain is a form of a Prüfer domain, namely an integral domain in which every finitely generated ideal is principal. Equivalently, a domain is Bézout if any two elements have a greatest common divisor that is a linear combination of them — the ring-theoretic form of Bézout's identity.

Properties

Every principal ideal domain is a Bézout domain, but not conversely: the ring of all algebraic integers and the ring of entire functions are Bézout domains that are not PIDs. A Bézout domain is always a GCD domain, and its finitely generated ideals are invertible. A local Bézout domain is a valuation domain.

Examples and counterexamples

Discrete valuation rings, valuation domains generally, and rings of holomorphic functions on open subsets of the complex plane are standard examples. By contrast, a polynomial ring in two variables over a field, such as k[x, y], is a unique factorization domain but not a Bézout domain, since the ideal (x, y) is not principal.
