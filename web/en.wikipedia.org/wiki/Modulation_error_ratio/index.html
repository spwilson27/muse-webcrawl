Modulation error ratio

The modulation error ratio (MER) is a measure used to quantify the performance of a digital radio (or digital television) transmitter or receiver in a communications system using digital modulation, such as quadrature amplitude modulation (QAM). A signal sent by an ideal transmitter or received by a perfect receiver would have all constellation points exactly at the ideal locations; however, various imperfections in the implementation (such as carrier leakage, low image rejection ratio, phase noise, and nonlinear distortion) cause the actual constellation points to deviate from the ideal locations.

Definition

MER is defined as the ratio of the average power of the signal constellation to the average power of the error vector, usually expressed in decibels (dB):

MER(dB) = 10 log10 ( Psignal / Perror )

where Psignal is the RMS power of the ideal transmitted signal and Perror is the RMS power of the error vector. MER is closely related to the error vector magnitude (EVM), but is defined on a power rather than an amplitude basis; it is also related to, but easier to measure than, the signal-to-noise ratio of the modulated signal.

Use in digital television

Transmitter MER is a key figure of merit for digital television standards such as DVB-T, DVB-C, and ATSC, since it gives a single comprehensive measure of transmitter quality that combines the effects of many individual impairments. Typical requirements range from roughly 30 dB to over 40 dB depending on the modulation order.
