Association of Summer Olympic International Federations

The Association of Summer Olympic International Federations (ASOIF) is a non-profit association of international sports federations that compete in the Summer Olympic Games. Founded in 1983 and headquartered in Lausanne, Switzerland, ASOIF brings together the international federations (IFs) governing the sports on the summer Olympic programme to coordinate matters of common interest and to represent them in dealings with the International Olympic Committee (IOC) and other bodies.

History

ASOIF was established in 1983, at a time when the international federations sought a stronger collective voice within the Olympic movement, particularly over the distribution of Olympic television revenues among the sports. It was modelled in part on the older Association of International Olympic Winter Sports Federations (AIOWF), which represents the winter sports federations.

Role and activities

ASOIF works to promote and protect the common interests of its member federations, to coordinate their activities in relation to the Olympic Games, and to ensure the representation of the summer sports in the wider governance of sport. It consults with the IOC on issues such as the Olympic programme, revenue distribution, the Olympic qualification systems and the calendar of events. ASOIF also organises workshops and produces studies on governance, and since the mid-2010s it has published periodic governance assessments of its member federations.

Membership

Membership is open to the international federations recognised by the IOC whose sports feature on the programme of the Summer Olympic Games. The membership numbers around thirty federations, including athletics (World Athletics), swimming (World Aquatics), gymnastics (FIG), cycling (UCI) and football (FIFA). Federations of sports newly added to the programme, such as surfing, skateboarding and sport climbing, have also joined.

Leadership

ASOIF is governed by a council elected by its general assembly. Its long-serving president from 2013 is the Italian sports administrator Francesco Ricci Bitti, a former president of the International Tennis Federation. Ricci Bitti succeeded Denis Oswald, the Swiss rowing official who led the organisation for many years.
