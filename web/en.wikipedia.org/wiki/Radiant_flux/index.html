In radiometry, radiant flux or radiant power is the radiant energy emitted, reflected, transmitted or received per unit time, and spectral flux or spectral power is the radiant flux per unit frequency or wavelength, depending on whether the spectrum is taken as a function of frequency or of wavelength. The SI unit of radiant flux is the watt (W), one joule per second. Radiant flux is one of the fundamental quantities of radiometry, from which all other radiometric quantities are derived.

Mathematical definitions

Radiant flux, denoted Φe ("e" for energetic, to avoid confusion with photometric quantities), is defined as the time rate of flow of radiant energy:

Φe = dQe / dt

where Qe is the radiant energy. Spectral flux in frequency, Φe,ν, is the radiant flux per unit frequency; integrating it over all frequencies gives the total radiant flux.

Photometry

The corresponding photometric quantity is luminous flux, measured in lumens, in which radiant flux at each wavelength is weighted by the luminosity function modelling the sensitivity of the human eye. A source's luminous efficacy is the ratio of luminous flux to radiant flux, with a theoretical maximum of 683 lm/W for monochromatic green light at 555 nm.
