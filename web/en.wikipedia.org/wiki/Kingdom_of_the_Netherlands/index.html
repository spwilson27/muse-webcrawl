The Kingdom of the Netherlands (Dutch: Koninkrijk der Nederlanden) is a sovereign state and constitutional monarchy composed of four constituent countries: the Netherlands in Europe, and Aruba, Curaçao, and Sint Maarten in the Caribbean. The kingdom as a whole is the internationally recognized sovereign entity, while each constituent country has its own government and constitution for internal affairs.

Structure

Relations within the kingdom are governed by the Charter for the Kingdom of the Netherlands, adopted in 1954 and amended several times since. The Charter reserves kingdom-level affairs — including defense, foreign relations, and Dutch nationality — to the kingdom's institutions, while the constituent countries manage their own internal matters. The monarch is head of state of the kingdom and of each country; the European Netherlands, through its population and representation, dominates kingdom decision-making in practice.

History

The kingdom was proclaimed in 1815 after the Napoleonic Wars. The 1954 Charter originally also included Suriname and the Netherlands Antilles as equal partners in a decolonizing framework. Suriname became independent in 1975. Aruba obtained separate status within the kingdom in 1986, and on 10 October 2010 the Netherlands Antilles was dissolved: Curaçao and Sint Maarten became constituent countries, while Bonaire, Sint Eustatius, and Saba became special municipalities of the Netherlands proper.

Geography and population

The kingdom spans European territory bordering Germany and Belgium and several Caribbean islands in the Leeward Antilles and Leeward Islands. The overwhelming majority of its roughly 17.8 million citizens live in the European Netherlands; the Caribbean countries together have a few hundred thousand inhabitants. Dutch is an official language throughout, with Papiamento, English, and Frisian also holding official status in parts of the kingdom.
