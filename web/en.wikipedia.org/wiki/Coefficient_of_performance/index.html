Coefficient of performance

The coefficient of performance or COP (sometimes CP or CoP) of a heat pump, refrigerator or air conditioner is the ratio of useful heating or cooling provided to the work (energy) required. Higher COPs mean higher efficiency and lower operating costs; unlike thermal efficiency, a COP can exceed 1 because the device moves heat rather than converting work into heat.

A heat pump moves more heat than the work it consumes

SymbolCOP
Definition (heating)Qhot / W
Typical heat pump COP3–5

Equations

For heating, COPheating = Qhot/W; for cooling, COPcooling = Qcold/W, and the two differ by exactly 1 for the same machine. The theoretical maximum is set by the Carnot limit using absolute temperatures: COPheating,max = Thot/(Thot − Tcold), so efficiency falls as the temperature lift grows — why air-source heat pumps perform worse on very cold days.

Applications

COP underlies ratings such as SEER and HSPF for air conditioners and heat pumps. Ground-source heat pumps commonly achieve COPs of 3–5, making them several times more efficient than resistance heating (COP = 1).
