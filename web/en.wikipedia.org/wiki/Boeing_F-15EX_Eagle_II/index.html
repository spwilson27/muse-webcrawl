The Boeing F-15EX Eagle II is a single-seat, all-weather multirole strike fighter developed by Boeing for the United States Air Force, derived from the F-15QA built for Qatar. It modernizes the venerable F-15 airframe with fly-by-wire controls, an advanced electronic-warfare suite, modern cockpit displays, and greatly expanded weapons carriage, intended to replace aging F-15C/D Eagles and complement fifth-generation fighters.

Development

The Air Force, facing declining fighter capacity and high operating costs for its 1980s-era F-15C fleet, selected the EX in the fiscal 2020 budget — the first new F-15 purchase by the service since 2001. The first aircraft flew on February 2, 2021, and was delivered to Eglin Air Force Base the following month for testing. In April 2021 the type was officially named Eagle II. Initial operational units include the Oregon Air National Guard's 142nd Wing, which began receiving aircraft in 2024.

Design

The F-15EX features the EPAWSS electronic-warfare/self-protection system, the APG-82 AESA radar family heritage from the F-15E fleet upgrades, a large-area cockpit display, and AMBER racks allowing carriage of up to 12 air-to-air missiles — earning it descriptions as a "missile truck" to supplement stealth fighters. Its airframe is rated for up to 20,000 flight hours, and Boeing cites substantially lower operating cost per hour than re-engining the legacy fleet.

Operators and orders

The Air Force program of record has varied between roughly 80 and 144 aircraft across budget years. In 2023 Indonesia signed a memorandum of understanding to purchase up to 24 F-15EX (as the F-15IDN configuration), and other F-15 operators have studied the type.
