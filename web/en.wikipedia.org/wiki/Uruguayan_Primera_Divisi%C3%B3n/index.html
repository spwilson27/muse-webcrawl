Uruguayan Primera División

Founded: 1900

Country: Uruguay

Organiser: Asociación Uruguaya de Fútbol (AUF)

Most championships: Peñarol

The Uruguayan Primera División, officially the Liga AUF Uruguaya, is the highest professional football division in Uruguay. Founded in 1900, it is one of the oldest football leagues in the world and has been dominated throughout its history by Nacional and Peñarol.

Format

The season is structured around the Apertura and Clausura tournaments, with an annual championship decided by playoffs between the tournament winners and the best team in the aggregate table. Teams qualify for the Copa Libertadores and Copa Sudamericana through the annual standings, and the lowest-ranked clubs are relegated.

History

The league began as an amateur competition in 1900, organised by the Uruguay Association Football League. Uruguay's amateur-era success, including Olympic gold medals in 1924 and 1928 and the first World Cup in 1930, led to professionalism in 1932. Despite Uruguay's small population, its clubs have remained competitive in South American competition.
