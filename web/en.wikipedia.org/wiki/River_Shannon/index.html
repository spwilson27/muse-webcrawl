The River Shannon (Irish: Abha na Sionainne) is the major river on the island of Ireland, and at 360.5 km (224 mi) in length, is the longest river in the British Isles. It drains the Shannon River Basin, which has an area of 16,865 km² — about one fifth of the area of Ireland.

Course

The Shannon rises at the Shannon Pot, a pool in the karst limestone landscape of the Cuilcagh Mountains in County Cavan, close to the Fermanagh border. It flows generally south through a series of great lakes — Lough Allen, Lough Ree, and Lough Derg — passing Carrick-on-Shannon, Athlone, and Shannonbridge. Below Lough Derg it passes Killaloe and the city of Limerick, then widens into the long Shannon Estuary before entering the Atlantic Ocean between County Clare and County Kerry.

By tradition the river is named after Sionainn, a figure of Irish mythology said to have drowned at the Shannon Pot seeking the wisdom of the Salmon of Knowledge.

Navigation and power

The Shannon is navigable for most of its length and forms the backbone of Ireland's inland waterways, connected to the Grand Canal, the Royal Canal, and the Shannon–Erne Waterway. At Ardnacrusha, the Shannon hydroelectric scheme (1929) — once supplying most of the young Irish Free State's electricity — draws water from the river below Lough Derg. The river is famous for coarse fishing, cruising, and the callows — flood meadows rich in birds such as the corncrake — along its middle course.
