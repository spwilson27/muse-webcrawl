Golden Ring of Russia

The Golden Ring of Russia (Russian: Золото́е кольцо́ Росси́и) unites a group of ancient towns north-east of Moscow, in the region formerly known as Zalesye. These towns played a central role in the early history of Russia and in the formation of the Russian Orthodox Church, and they preserve outstanding monuments of Russian architecture from the 12th to the 18th centuries — white-stone churches, kremlins, monasteries and museums of wooden architecture.

The route

The term "Golden Ring" was coined in 1967 by the Soviet writer and conservationist Yuri Bychkov, who published a series of essays under that title in the newspaper Sovetskaya Kultura. There is no single official list of towns, but the classic route is usually taken to include eight principal cities:

  • Sergiyev Posad — home of the Trinity Lavra of St. Sergius, one of the most important Russian monasteries.
  • Pereslavl-Zalessky — founded in 1152 by Prince Yuri Dolgoruky on Lake Pleshcheyevo.
  • Rostov Veliky — one of the oldest Russian towns, with a celebrated kremlin on Lake Nero.
  • Yaroslavl — a UNESCO-listed historic centre at the confluence of the Volga and Kotorosl rivers.
  • Kostroma — associated with the Ipatiev Monastery and the accession of the Romanov dynasty.
  • Ivanovo — the youngest city of the ring, a former textile centre known as the "city of brides".
  • Suzdal — a small town with an exceptionally preserved ensemble of churches and the Museum of Wooden Architecture.
  • Vladimir — former capital of Vladimir-Suzdal Rus, with the UNESCO-listed Assumption and St. Demetrius cathedrals.

Other towns frequently included are Alexandrov, Bogolyubovo, Gorokhovets, Gus-Khrustalny, Kalyazin, Murom, Palekh, Plyos, Rybinsk, Tutaev, Uglich and Yuryev-Polsky.

Tourism

The Golden Ring is one of the most popular tourist routes in Russia, traditionally travelled by road from Moscow in a loop of roughly 700 km. The towns are known for their onion-domed churches, frescoes, icon painting traditions (notably Palekh lacquer miniatures) and folk crafts.
