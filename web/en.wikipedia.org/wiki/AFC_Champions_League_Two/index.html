The AFC Champions League Two (abbreviated ACL2) is an annual continental club football competition organised by the Asian Football Confederation. It is the second tier of Asian club football, ranking below the AFC Champions League Elite and above the AFC Challenge League. The tournament began in 2004 as the AFC Cup, a competition for clubs from "developing" football nations in Asia, and was rebranded and restructured for the 2024–25 season as part of a three-tier overhaul of AFC club competitions.

History

The AFC Cup was launched in 2004 for clubs from member associations ranked below the top tier of the old AFC Champions League. Its finals were dominated by West Asian clubs, including Kuwait SC, Al-Kuwait's rivals Al-Qadsia, Al-Faisaly of Jordan and Al-Quwa Al-Jawiya of Iraq, who won three consecutive titles from 2016 to 2018; Lebanon's Al-Ahed and Oman's Al-Seeb also won titles. In December 2022 the AFC announced a revamp: from 2024 the top competition became the 24-team Champions League Elite, the AFC Cup was renamed the AFC Champions League Two with a 32-team field drawn from a wider range of associations, and a new third tier, the AFC Challenge League, was created for the lowest-ranked leagues.

Format

The tournament begins with a group stage of 32 teams drawn into eight groups of four, split between West and East regions. Group winners and runners-up advance to a knockout phase of two-legged ties, culminating in a single-match final. Entrants include cup winners and high league finishers from mid-ranked associations, plus clubs eliminated from Champions League Elite qualifying play-offs. The champion does not automatically qualify for the Elite tournament but receives a play-off place in some seasons' allocation frameworks.

First edition under the new name

The inaugural AFC Champions League Two final was played in May 2025, when Sharjah of the United Arab Emirates defeated Singapore's Lion City Sailors 2–1 in Singapore to become the first champions under the new brand. The result was a notable success for Singaporean club football, whose representatives had rarely reached continental finals.
