Operation Cobra

Operation Cobra
Part ofOperation Overlord, Battle of Normandy, World War II
Date25–31 July 1944
LocationSaint-Lô area, Normandy, France
CommandersOmar Bradley; J. Lawton Collins; (U.S. First Army)
ResultAllied victory; breakout from Normandy achieved

Operation Cobra was the codename for an offensive launched by the United States First Army under General Omar Bradley at the end of July 1944, during the Battle of Normandy in the Second World War. Its purpose was to break through the German defences south of Saint-Lô, which had held the Americans in the bocage country since the D-Day landings, and to exploit into Brittany and the open French countryside. Cobra achieved the long-sought breakout and transformed the campaign in north-west Europe.

Background

Seven weeks after Operation Neptune landed the Allied armies, the American sector was confined to a shallow lodgement hemmed in by hedgerow terrain. The capture of Saint-Lô on 18 July provided a start line. Bradley's plan opened with a massive aerial bombardment along a narrow front on the Périers–Saint-Lô road, after which infantry divisions of VII Corps under J. Lawton Collins would rupture the line and armoured divisions would pour through.

The offensive

Bad weather delayed the attack, and a preliminary bombing on 24 July was partially called off; some bombs fell short, killing American soldiers. The main attack on 25 July used over 1,500 heavy bombers, but short bombing again caused heavy friendly casualties, including Lieutenant General Lesley J. McNair, the highest-ranking American officer killed in Normandy. Nevertheless the bombardment shattered the Panzer Lehr Division and the front. By 27–28 July resistance collapsed; American columns liberated Coutances and then Avranches by 31 July.

Aftermath

The breakout at Avranches opened the way for George S. Patton's newly activated Third Army to sweep into Brittany and then east across France. The German counterattack at Mortain (Operation Lüttich) failed, and German forces in Normandy were largely destroyed in the Falaise pocket in August 1944.
