Conspiracy (criminal)

Conspiracy is an inchoate offence in most common law jurisdictions.

In criminal law, a conspiracy is an agreement between two or more persons to commit a crime at some time in the future. Criminal law in some countries or for some conspiracies may require that at least one overt act be committed in furtherance of the agreement in order to constitute the offence. Conspiracy is classified as an inchoate offence, like attempt and solicitation, because it does not require the target crime to be completed.

Common law

In the United States, conspiracy law allows prosecutors to charge defendants who planned or assisted a crime even if they did not carry it out. Under the Pinkerton doctrine, a conspirator may be liable for substantive offences committed by co-conspirators in furtherance of the conspiracy. In England and Wales, statutory conspiracy is governed by the Criminal Law Act 1977, which requires an agreement that a course of conduct be pursued which, if carried out, will necessarily amount to or involve the commission of an offence.

Defences and doctrines associated with conspiracy include withdrawal, Wharton's rule (which limits conspiracy charges where the target offence necessarily requires two participants), and rules on the admissibility of co-conspirators' statements as an exception to hearsay.
