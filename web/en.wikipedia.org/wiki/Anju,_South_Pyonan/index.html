Anju, South Pyongan

Anju (Korean: 안주; Hanja: 安州) is a city in South Pyongan Province, North Korea. It lies on the Chongchon River near its mouth on Korea Bay, northwest of the provincial capital Pyongsong. The population was estimated at about 240,000 in the 2008 census, making it one of the larger cities of the province.

Geography and economy

Anju sits on a fertile plain at the crossing of major roads and railways—the Pyongui and Kaechon lines—linking Pyongyang with the northwest. The surrounding Anju plain is an important rice-growing area. Nearby, the Namhung Youth Chemical Complex, built with Soviet assistance, produces fertiliser and chemical goods, and coal is mined in the vicinity, the Anju coalfield being one of North Korea's principal anthracite sources.

History

Anju was a significant garrison town in the Joseon period, guarding the route north toward the Yalu River frontier. During the Korean War, the area saw heavy fighting in late 1950, as United Nations forces advanced toward and then retreated from the Chongchon River line in the face of Chinese intervention.

Transport

The city is served by rail on the Pyongui Line between Pyongyang and Sinuiju, with a branch to Kaechon, and is connected by road to the capital and the west coast.
