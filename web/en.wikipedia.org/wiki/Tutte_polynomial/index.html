Tutte polynomial

The Tutte polynomial, also called the dichromate or the Tutte–Whitney polynomial, is a graph polynomial defined for an undirected graph. It encodes a great deal of information about the graph's structure, and specialisations of the polynomial recover many classical graph invariants, including the chromatic polynomial, the flow polynomial, and the reliability polynomial.

Definition

The Tutte polynomial TG(x, y) of a graph G can be defined by a deletion–contraction recurrence: if e is an edge that is neither a bridge nor a loop, then TG = TG∖e + TG/e, where the two graphs on the right are obtained by deleting and by contracting e, respectively. Bridges contribute a factor of x and loops a factor of y; the empty graph (no edges) has polynomial 1.

An equivalent definition sums over all spanning subgraphs: TG(x, y) = Σ (x − 1)r (y − 1)n, where the sum runs over spanning subgraphs and r and n are the rank and nullity defects of each subgraph.

Specialisations

Evaluating the polynomial at particular points yields famous quantities: T(2,1) counts spanning forests; T(1,1) counts spanning trees; T(2,2) counts spanning subgraphs; and T(1,2) counts connected spanning subgraphs. Along the hyperbola xy = 2 it relates to the Jones polynomial of an associated alternating knot. The chromatic polynomial PG(λ) equals (−1)r λk TG(1 − λ, 0), tying the Tutte polynomial to graph colouring.

History and complexity

W. T. Tutte introduced the polynomial in work beginning with his 1947 PhD thesis and his 1954 paper "A contribution to the theory of chromatic polynomials" (where he called it the dichromate). Computing the Tutte polynomial is #P-hard in general, even for planar graphs at most evaluation points, which places it at the centre of research in algebraic graph theory and computational complexity. The polynomial generalises to matroids, where much of its theory is most naturally developed.
