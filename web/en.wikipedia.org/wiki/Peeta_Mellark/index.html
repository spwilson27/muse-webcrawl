Peeta Mellark is a fictional character from The Hunger Games trilogy by Suzanne Collins. He is the male tribute from District 12 selected to compete in the 74th Hunger Games alongside Katniss Everdeen, and serves as one of the series' central characters and Katniss's primary love interest.

Background

Peeta is the youngest son of District 12's baker. As a child, he famously burned a loaf of bread on purpose and threw it to a starving Katniss in the rain, an act of kindness she never forgot. He is known for his strength (developed from hauling flour sacks), his skill at decorating cakes, his talent for camouflage and painting, and above all his charisma and way with words.

Role in the series

During the 74th Hunger Games, Peeta confesses his love for Katniss in his pre-Games interview, creating the "star-crossed lovers" angle that wins them sponsors. The two become the first co-victors in Games history after threatening to eat poisonous nightlock berries rather than kill each other.

In Catching Fire, Peeta returns to the arena for the Third Quarter Quell and is captured by the Capitol. In Mockingjay, he is rescued but has been "hijacked" — tortured with tracker jacker venom to turn his memories of Katniss into a weapon against her. He gradually recovers, and after the war he returns to District 12, where he and Katniss eventually marry and have two children.

Characterisation

Peeta is characterised by his moral steadfastness and his insistence on remaining himself despite the Capitol's manipulation — captured in his wish to die "as myself" rather than become a piece in the Capitol's games. Critics have noted his subversion of traditional masculine hero roles, with Katniss in the protector role and Peeta as the emotionally expressive partner.
