AKB48 is a Japanese idol girl group formed in 2005 by producer Yasushi Akimoto. Named after Tokyo's Akihabara district (shortened to "Akiba", hence AKB) and the concept of an original roster of 48 members, the group is built around the idea of "idols you can meet": members perform almost daily in the group's own small theatre in Akihabara and interact with fans at handshake events. The model proved immensely successful, making AKB48 one of the best-selling musical acts in Japan and spawning franchised sister groups across Japan and Asia.

History

Akimoto auditioned the first generation in 2005; the AKB48 Theater opened in December that year to tiny audiences. Word of mouth and internet buzz grew attendance, and the group's major-label singles from 2009 onward — including "River" and the million-selling "Sakura no Shiori" — began topping the Oricon charts. Singles such as "Heavy Rotation" (2010), "Flying Get" (2011) and "Koisuru Fortune Cookie" (2013) became national phenomena. AKB48-related acts repeatedly set Japanese sales records, driven in part by fan voting for single line-ups.

System and elections

Members are organised into teams that rotate through theatre stages. Line-ups for major singles (senbatsu) are partly chosen by annual general elections in which fans vote using ballots packaged with CDs — an innovation that drew both commercial success and criticism for encouraging bulk buying. Members "graduate" from the group to pursue solo or acting careers; prominent graduates include Atsuko Maeda, Yuko Oshima and Rino Sashihara (who went on to lead HKT48). The franchise expanded with sister groups SKE48 (Nagoya), NMB48 (Osaka), HKT48 (Hakata), NGT48, STU48, and international groups including JKT48 (Jakarta), BNK48 (Bangkok), MNL48 (Manila), AKB48 Team SH (Shanghai) and AKB48 Team TP (Taipei).

Impact

AKB48 reshaped the Japanese idol industry, codified the theatre-based, fan-participation model, and remains a major cultural export and an economic force in Akihabara. The group has held concerts at the Tokyo Dome and national stadiums, and its total Japanese single sales number in the tens of millions.
