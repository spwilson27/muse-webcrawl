Canadian National Vimy Memorial

The Canadian National Vimy Memorial is a war memorial site in France dedicated to the memory of Canadian Expeditionary Force members killed during the First World War. It also serves as the place of commemoration for Canadian soldiers of the First World War killed or presumed dead in France who have no known grave. The monument is the centrepiece of a 100-hectare (250-acre) preserved battlefield park that encompasses a portion of the ground over which the Canadian Corps made their assault during the initial Battle of Vimy Ridge offensive of the Battle of Arras.

The battle

The Battle of Vimy Ridge was fought from 9 to 12 April 1917 as part of the Battle of Arras. All four divisions of the Canadian Corps attacked together for the first time and succeeded in capturing the ridge where French and British forces had previously failed, at a cost of 3,598 Canadian dead and over 7,000 wounded.

The monument

The memorial stands on Hill 145, the highest point of the ridge, on land granted to Canada by France in 1922 in perpetuity. Designed by Toronto sculptor and architect Walter Seymour Allward, the monument took eleven years to build and was unveiled on 26 July 1936 by King Edward VIII in the presence of over 50,000 Canadian and French veterans and their families. Built of Seget limestone, it features two pylons rising 27 metres, representing Canada and France, and twenty symbolic stone figures, including Canada Bereft, a cloaked figure mourning her fallen. Inscribed on the monument's walls are the names of 11,285 Canadian soldiers killed in France whose final resting place is unknown.

Restoration and centenary

The memorial underwent a major restoration and was rededicated by Queen Elizabeth II on 9 April 2007, the 90th anniversary of the battle. A new visitor education centre opened for the centenary in 2017. The monument is maintained by Veterans Affairs Canada and is widely regarded as Canada's most significant overseas war memorial.
