Friedrich Mohs

Carl Friedrich Christian Mohs (29 January 1773 – 29 September 1839) was a German geologist and mineralogist, best known for creating the Mohs scale of mineral hardness in 1812.

Life and work

Mohs was born in Gernrode, in present-day Saxony-Anhalt, and studied mathematics, physics and chemistry at Halle, then mining at the Freiberg Mining Academy under Abraham Gottlob Werner. He worked in Austria, cataloguing the mineral collection of the banker J. F. van der Nüll, and in 1812 became professor at the Joanneum in Graz, where he devised his hardness scale, ranking minerals from talc (1) to diamond (10) by which could scratch which. He later held chairs at Freiberg and Vienna. He died in Agordo, Italy, in 1839.
