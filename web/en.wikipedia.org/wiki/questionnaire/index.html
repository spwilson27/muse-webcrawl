Questionnaire

An example survey questionnaire.

A questionnaire is a research instrument that consists of a set of questions (or other types of prompts) for the purpose of gathering information from respondents through survey or statistical study. A research questionnaire is typically a mix of close-ended questions and open-ended questions. Open-ended, long-term questions offer the respondent the ability to elaborate on their thoughts, while closed questions provide predefined response options such as yes/no, multiple choice, or rating scales (for example, Likert scales).

The questionnaire was invented by the Statistical Society of London in 1838. Questionnaires are used in fields including psychology, marketing, public health, sociology, and government statistics, and may be administered on paper, by telephone, in person, or online.

Design considerations

Good questionnaire design requires clear, unambiguous wording, logical ordering, and avoidance of leading, double-barreled, or negatively phrased questions. Researchers pilot-test instruments to check reliability and validity, and must consider response biases such as acquiescence, social desirability, and nonresponse. Ethical practice involves informed consent, anonymity or confidentiality guarantees, and care with sensitive questions.
