Enucleation of the eye

SpecialtyOphthalmology
PurposeRemoval of the entire eyeball

Enucleation is the removal of the eye that leaves the eye muscles and remaining orbital contents intact. This type of ocular surgery is indicated for a number of ocular tumours, in eyes that have suffered severe trauma, and in eyes that are otherwise blind and painful.

Indications

Enucleation is performed for intraocular malignancies such as retinoblastoma and uveal melanoma, for a blind painful eye that does not respond to other treatment, for severe ocular injury with no prospect of vision, and to prevent sympathetic ophthalmia in the fellow eye.

Procedure and prosthesis

The surgeon detaches the extraocular muscles and optic nerve and removes the globe, usually placing an orbital implant in the socket. After healing, an ocular prosthesis (artificial eye) is fitted over the implant by an ocularist.
