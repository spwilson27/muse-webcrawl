Pythagorean comma

Interval531441:524288
Size≈ 23.46 cents
Also calledDitonic comma

The Pythagorean comma, also known as the ditonic comma, is the small interval that separates twelve justly tuned perfect fifths from seven octaves. In Pythagorean tuning, a perfect fifth has the ratio 3:2 and an octave the ratio 2:1, but stacking twelve fifths gives (3/2)12 = 531441/4096, while seven octaves give 27 = 128 = 524288/4096. The two paths to "the same" note therefore differ by the ratio 531441:524288, about 23.46 cents — roughly a quarter of a semitone or an eighth of a whole tone.

Significance

The Pythagorean comma demonstrates that a tuning system based purely on octaves and perfect fifths cannot close: the circle of fifths does not return exactly to its starting pitch. In practice, one fifth in the circle (the "wolf fifth") must absorb the comma and sounds badly out of tune, limiting usable keys. Historical responses to the problem include meantone temperament, which instead spreads error over the thirds, well temperaments, and eventually twelve-tone equal temperament, which divides the Pythagorean comma equally among all twelve fifths, narrowing each by about 1.96 cents.
