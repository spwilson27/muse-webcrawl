Raised bog

Raised bogs, also called ombrotrophic bogs, are acidic, wet habitats that are poor in mineral nutrients, being fed only by precipitation. The accumulated peat grows above the level of surrounding land, forming a gently domed surface. They are a characteristic wetland type of cool temperate regions of the Northern Hemisphere.

Raised bogs are dominated by Sphagnum mosses and support specialised plants such as bog cotton, cross-leaved heath and sundews. Many have been damaged by peat extraction, drainage and afforestation, and are now the subject of restoration projects because of their importance for biodiversity and carbon storage.
