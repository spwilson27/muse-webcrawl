The gwyniad (Coregonus pennantii) is a freshwater whitefish in the salmon family, endemic to Bala Lake (Llyn Tegid) in Gwynedd, north Wales. Its name derives from the Welsh gwyn, "white", referring to its silvery colouring. Like other British and Irish coregonids — the vendace, powan and schelly — it is a glacial relict: a cold-water species left behind in deep lakes after the retreat of the ice sheets at the end of the last Ice Age.

Biology

The gwyniad is a slender, silvery fish growing to about 30 cm. It feeds mainly on planktonic crustaceans and insect larvae, rising in the water column at night. Spawning takes place in winter, over gravel shallows around the lake shore. The species is distinguished from other coregonids by details of its gill rakers and fin-ray counts, and is treated either as a full species or as a distinctive population of the widespread Coregonus lavaretus complex.

Conservation

The gwyniad is listed as Critically Endangered. Its threats are specific to its single home: eutrophication of Bala Lake from agricultural run-off, and the introduction of the ruffe, a small percid fish alien to the lake, which eats gwyniad eggs. As an insurance policy, conservationists from Natural Resources Wales and partners have translocated eggs and fry to establish a refuge population in another Welsh lake, Llyn Arenig Fawr. The gwyniad is protected under UK wildlife legislation and is a symbol of the lake's unique ecology, central to local legend as the home of the monster "Teggie".
