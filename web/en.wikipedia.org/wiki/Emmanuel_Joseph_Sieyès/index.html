Emmanuel Joseph Sieyès

Emmanuel Joseph Sieyès (3 May 1748 – 20 June 1836), usually known as the Abbé Sieyès, was a French Catholic clergyman, political writer, and one of the chief political theorists of the French Revolution. His pamphlet What Is the Third Estate? (January 1789) became a manifesto of the Revolution.

What Is the Third Estate?

In the pamphlet, Sieyès argued that the Third Estate — the common people — constituted a complete nation in itself and required no privileged orders. His famous formulation asked: "What is the Third Estate? Everything. What has it been until now in the political order? Nothing. What does it want to be? Something." His ideas on national sovereignty and constituent power strongly influenced the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen.

Revolutionary career

Elected to the Estates-General as a deputy of the Third Estate of Paris, Sieyès proposed that the body declare itself the National Assembly. He survived the Terror, served in the Council of Five Hundred, and as a member of the Directory he sought a strong executive — helping to engineer the Coup of 18 Brumaire (1799) that brought Napoleon Bonaparte to power, though Napoleon soon outmanoeuvred him. Sieyès became a senator and count of the Empire, was exiled as a regicide during the Bourbon Restoration, and returned to France after the July Revolution of 1830.
