Holland–Dozier–Holland

Holland–Dozier–Holland was a songwriting and production team of Lamont Dozier and brothers Brian and Eddie Holland. They created the Motown sound for The Supremes, The Four Tops, Martha and the Vandellas and others, writing many of the defining hits of 1960s soul and pop.

Holland–Dozier–Holland
Members: Brian Holland, Lamont Dozier, Eddie Holland
Years active at Motown: 1962–1967
Labels: Motown, Invictus, Hot Wax
Honours: Rock and Roll Hall of Fame (1990), Songwriters Hall of Fame

Motown years

Brian Holland and Dozier wrote/produced while Eddie Holland wrote lyrics and coached vocals. Their hits include "Where Did Our Love Go", "Baby Love", "Stop! In the Name of Love", "You Can't Hurry Love" and "You Keep Me Hangin' On" for the Supremes (10 of their 12 No. 1s), "Reach Out I'll Be There" and "I Can't Help Myself" for the Four Tops, "Heat Wave" for Martha and the Vandellas, and songs for Marvin Gaye, The Isley Brothers and The Miracles.

Departure and later work

A royalties dispute led them to leave Motown in 1968 amid litigation (Berry Gordy countersued for breach). They founded Invictus and Hot Wax Records, scoring hits with Freda Payne ("Band of Gold"), Chairmen of the Board ("Give Me Just a Little More Time") and Honey Cone. Dozier left in 1973 for a solo career ("Why Can't We Be Lovers"); the brothers continued as Holland Brothers.

Legacy

Their blend of gospel call-and-response, pop structure, tambourine-driven rhythm and orchestration made Motown a crossover phenomenon. Lamont Dozier died in 2022, aged 81.
