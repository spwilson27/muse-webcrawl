Drag king

Drag kings are mostly female performance artists who dress in masculine drag and personify male gender stereotypes as part of an act. Drag king shows can include singing, lip-syncing, dancing, comedy and theatrical sketches, often satirising or celebrating masculinity. The counterpart performers who perform femininity are known as drag queens.

History

Women performing in male costume have a long history on stage, from breeches roles in opera to music-hall male impersonators such as Vesta Tilley in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries. The modern drag king scene emerged from lesbian and queer communities in the 1980s and 1990s, with notable scenes in New York, San Francisco and London; the term "drag king" itself gained currency in that period.

Performance

A drag king persona is built with costume, facial hair made from crepe wool or makeup, binding or padding, and mannerisms drawn from male archetypes — rock stars, rappers, cowboys, businessmen or romantic leads. Performances range from affectionate homage to pointed gender commentary.
