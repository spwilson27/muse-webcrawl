Sancerre (wine)

Sancerre is a French wine Appellation d'origine contrôlée (AOC) in the eastern Loire Valley, centred on the town of Sancerre. It is most famous for crisp white wines made from Sauvignon blanc, though rosé and light red wines are made from Pinot noir.

Sancerre AOC
Region: Loire Valley, France
Grapes: Sauvignon blanc, Pinot noir
Soils: Kimmeridgian limestone, flint (silex), limestone-clay

Geography and terroir

Vineyards surround Sancerre and villages including Bué, Chavignol and Ménétréol-sous-Sancerre. Three soil types recur: terres blanches (Kimmeridgian marl), caillottes (stony limestone) and silex (flint), the last giving wines a distinctive smoky, gunflint note.

Wines

White Sancerre is dry, aromatic and high in acidity, with citrus, gooseberry, cut grass and mineral flavours, typically drunk young. Red and rosé Sancerre from Pinot noir are light and fruity. The wines pair famously with the local goat cheese, Crottin de Chavignol.

History

Viticulture dates to Roman times; the AOC was established in 1936 for white wines and extended to reds and rosés in 1959.
