Freedom of navigation

Freedom of navigation (FON) is a principle of customary international law that ships flying the flag of any sovereign state shall not suffer interference from other states, apart from the exceptions provided for in international law. In the realm of international law, it has been codified in the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS), which recognises rights including innocent passage through territorial seas, transit passage through international straits, and freedom of navigation on the high seas and in exclusive economic zones.

United States Freedom of Navigation Program

The United States Freedom of Navigation Program, established in 1979 under President Jimmy Carter, combines diplomatic protests and operational assertions by US military forces to challenge maritime claims considered excessive under international law. Its stated purpose is to prevent such claims from hardening into customary law through acquiescence. The programme is applied worldwide, including against the claims of US allies as well as adversaries.

Freedom of navigation operations

Freedom of navigation operations (FONOPs) are transits by naval or air units designed to demonstrate a right of navigation. US FONOPs in the South China Sea, where several states assert expansive territorial claims, have drawn particular attention and protests from China. Other states, including the United Kingdom, France, Australia and Japan, have also conducted naval transits asserting navigational rights.

Legal basis

UNCLOS grants all ships, including warships, the right of innocent passage through territorial waters, provided passage is continuous, expeditious and not prejudicial to the peace, good order or security of the coastal state. In straits used for international navigation, the stronger right of transit passage applies and may not be suspended. Disputes arise where coastal states require prior notification or authorisation for warships, draw straight baselines enclosing large sea areas, or claim historic rights inconsistent with the Convention.
