V formation

A V formation is a symmetric V-shaped flight formation. It is used by many species of migratory birds, particularly geese and ducks, and also appears in military aviation as a formation flying pattern.

Birds

In birds, the V formation is thought to serve two main purposes. First, it improves energy efficiency: each bird flies slightly above the bird in front, taking advantage of the upwash from the wingtip vortices of the bird ahead, which reduces induced drag. Studies of northern bald ibises fitted with data loggers showed that the birds precisely position themselves and time their wingbeats to exploit this effect. Second, the formation facilitates communication and coordination within the flock, as each bird can keep the others in view.

The lead position is the most demanding, and birds rotate it among flock members during long flights. Formation flying can increase range substantially; estimates suggest a flock can fly much farther than a solitary bird on the same energy.

Military use

Vic formations were standard for military aircraft in the early twentieth century, including bombers and fighters of the Second World War. The formation allowed mutual defensive support and visual signalling. Modern military formations have largely moved to more dispersed patterns, but the V or "vic" remains in ceremonial flypasts.
