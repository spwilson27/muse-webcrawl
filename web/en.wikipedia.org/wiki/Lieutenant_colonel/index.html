Lieutenant colonel is a rank of commissioned officer in the armies, most marine forces and some air forces of the world, ranking above a major and below a colonel. The rank is the most senior of the field grade officers and corresponds to NATO code OF-4.

Role

Lieutenant colonels typically command battalions or regiments of 300 to 1,000 soldiers, or serve as senior staff officers at brigade and division level. In the United States armed forces the pay grade is O-5; the insignia is a silver oak leaf. In the British Army, a lieutenant colonel usually commands a battalion or serves as a senior staff officer.

History

The title originated as the deputy (lieutenant) of a colonel, who in the 16th and 17th centuries was the proprietor-commander of a regiment. The lieutenant colonel exercised day-to-day command while the colonel was a senior or often absent figure. By the 19th century the rank was a standard substantive grade in most European and American armies.

Pronunciation

In British and Commonwealth usage "colonel" is pronounced "kernel", so the rank is spoken as "leftenant kernel"; in American usage it is "loo-tenant kernel".
