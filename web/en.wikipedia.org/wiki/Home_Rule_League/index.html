Home Rule League

The Home Rule Leagues campaigned for Indian self-government

The Home Rule League was an organisation founded in British India in 1916 that campaigned for self-government ("home rule") for India within the British Empire, modelled on Irish home rule movements. Two separate leagues were founded: one by Bal Gangadhar Tilak in April 1916, based at Poona and active mainly in western and central India, and another by Annie Besant in September 1916, based at Adyar (Madras) and covering the rest of India, including Bombay city.

Background and aims

The movement arose during the First World War, when Indian nationalists argued that India's contribution to the war effort should be rewarded with self-government. The leagues aimed to build a mass movement for home rule through public meetings, pamphlets, and newspapers — Tilak's Kesari and Mahratta, and Besant's New India and Commonweal. Besant's league alone established hundreds of branches, and the movement attracted many younger nationalists, including Jawaharlal Nehru.

Impact

The agitation, and Besant's internment by the Madras government in 1917, provoked widespread protest and pushed the British government to respond: in August 1917 Secretary of State Edwin Montagu announced the "responsible government" of India as the goal of British policy, a step that led to the Montagu–Chelmsford Reforms and the Government of India Act 1919. The leagues also helped unite the moderate and extremist wings of the Indian National Congress, which had reunited at the Lucknow session of 1916, where Congress and the Muslim League concluded the Lucknow Pact. After 1918 the movement declined as Mahatma Gandhi's mass campaigns took centre stage; Tilak's league merged into Congress, and Besant opposed Gandhi's non-cooperation movement.
