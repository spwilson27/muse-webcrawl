Agenda (meeting)

An agenda is a list of meeting activities in the order in which they are to be taken up, beginning with the call to order and ending with adjournment. It usually includes one or more specific items of business to be acted upon. The word comes from Latin agenda, "things to be done," the gerundive of agere, "to do." In parliamentary procedure and business practice, a well-prepared agenda helps a meeting run efficiently, ensures that important topics are covered, and allows participants to prepare in advance.

Typical contents

A standard meeting agenda may include:

  • Call to order
  • Approval of the agenda itself
  • Approval of the minutes of the previous meeting
  • Reports of officers and committees
  • Unfinished (old) business carried over from earlier meetings
  • New business
  • Announcements
  • Adjournment

Agendas often note the time allocated to each item and the person responsible for leading it. For formal bodies—corporate boards, legislatures, and courts—agenda rules may be set by statute or bylaws, and items sometimes must be published in advance under open-meeting ("sunshine") laws.

In parliamentary procedure

Under Robert's Rules of Order, an agenda is a form of special order of business adopted by the assembly; once adopted, items are taken up in the listed sequence unless suspended by a two-thirds vote. An organisation may adopt a standing "order of business" in its rules instead of preparing an agenda for each session. In legislative bodies the equivalent document is often called an order paper or calendar.

Best practice

Meeting-management guidance commonly recommends distributing the agenda in advance with supporting documents, stating the purpose and desired outcome of each item, distinguishing items for information, discussion, and decision, and assigning an owner and time limit to each item. A "consent agenda" groups routine, non-controversial items into a single item approved in one vote, saving time for substantive discussion.
