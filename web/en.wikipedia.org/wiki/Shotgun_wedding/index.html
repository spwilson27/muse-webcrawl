Shotgun wedding

A shotgun wedding is a wedding arranged hastily, typically after an unplanned pregnancy. The phrase is an American colloquialism derived from the supposed practice of a pregnant woman's father forcing the man who impregnated her to marry her at gunpoint — with a shotgun. More broadly, the term describes any marriage entered into under pressure rather than by free choice.

History and social context

In societies where premarital sex and out-of-wedlock births carried heavy stigma, a pregnancy before marriage often triggered a rapid wedding so the child would be born "legitimate" and the family's honour preserved. Such marriages were a recognised social institution in rural North America and parts of Europe in the 18th and 19th centuries, and studies of marriage and first-birth dates in parish and census records show they were far from rare. The "shotgun" element is likely largely folklore, though the social compulsion — from family, church, and community — was real enough.

Modern usage

As stigma around cohabitation and non-marital births declined in the late 20th century, genuinely forced hasty weddings became rarer, and the phrase is now used mostly jokingly for any wedding organised quickly after a pregnancy announcement. The term has also been extended metaphorically to business and politics, describing mergers or alliances formed under duress — a "shotgun marriage".

In popular culture

The shotgun wedding is a long-running comic trope in cartoons, country music, and film, typically featuring a stern father with a shotgun ushering a nervous groom to the altar. The comic strip Li'l Abner popularised related hillbilly wedding imagery, and the trope remains instantly recognisable in American humour.
