One-design

One-design is a racing method in sailboat racing which may be defined by a class association whereby all boats within a class are identical or near-identical in design. The theory is that the identical nature of the boats makes competition a test of the skill of the sailor rather than of technology, equipment funds, or design.

Concept

In one-design racing there is no handicapping or time allowance: the first boat across the finish line wins. Class rules specify dimensions, weight, sail area, materials, and permitted equipment, sometimes extremely tightly. One-design classes range from small dinghies such as the Optimist, Laser (ILCA), and Finn to keelboats such as the J/70, Melges 24, and the Star. Most Olympic sailing classes are one-designs.

Related formats

One-design is contrasted with development classes, in which boats may differ within a measurement rule (such as the former 12 Metre class), and with handicap systems such as IRC and ORC, which rate dissimilar boats against each other. Strict one-designs are sometimes called "strict one-designs" to distinguish them from looser classes; "box rule" classes are an intermediate form.
