Parable of the talents

The Parable of the Talents (also the Parable of the Minas in its Lucan version) is one of the parables of Jesus, appearing in the Gospel of Matthew (25:14–30) and in a related form in the Gospel of Luke (19:11–27). It tells of a master who, before going on a journey, entrusts his property to three servants, and on his return judges them according to how they used what they were given.

The story

In Matthew's version, the master gives five talents to one servant, two to another, and one to a third, "each according to his ability." A talent was a large unit of money — roughly 6,000 denarii, or many years of a labourer's wages. The first two servants trade with the money and double it; the third, fearing his master, buries his single talent in the ground. On the master's return, the productive servants are praised and rewarded — "Well done, good and faithful servant" — while the third is rebuked as "wicked and slothful," loses his talent to the first servant, and is cast "into the outer darkness."

Interpretation

The parable is traditionally read as a teaching about stewardship and the faithful use of God-given gifts in expectation of judgment; in the context of Matthew 25, it stands between the Parable of the Ten Virgins and the description of the Last Judgment. Remarkably, the English word "talent" in the sense of a natural ability derives from this parable, via the figurative interpretation of the entrusted money as gifts to be developed.

In Luke's version, the sum is ten minas (much smaller amounts), and the story is combined with a narrative about a nobleman who goes to receive a kingdom and punishes subjects who rejected his rule — a framing often connected with Archelaus's journey to Rome to claim the kingship of Judea.
