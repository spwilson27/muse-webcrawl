Sendai Tanabata Festival

The Sendai Tanabata Festival (仙台七夕まつり, Sendai Tanabata Matsuri) is a Japanese Tanabata festival held every year from 6 to 8 August in Sendai, Miyagi Prefecture. It is one of the three great festivals of the Tōhoku region, alongside Aomori's Nebuta Matsuri and Akita's Kantō Matsuri, and attracts around two million visitors.

Decorations

The festival is famous for its spectacular sasakazari: huge bamboo poles hung through the city's shopping arcades and streets, adorned with colourful streamers (fukinagashi) of washi paper, some reaching ten metres. Shops, schools and community groups traditionally create seven kinds of paper ornaments—the paper kimono, paper cranes, purses, nets, rubbish bags, paper strips for calligraphy, and streamers—symbolising wishes for health, longevity, prosperity, good fishing, cleanliness, learning and skill in weaving, in honour of the star-crossed lovers Orihime and Hikoboshi of the Tanabata legend.

History

The festival dates to the era of Date Masamune, founder of Sendai in the early 17th century, and was revived in its modern form in 1928 to lift the city during economic depression. Fireworks on the Hirose River precede the festival on 5 August.
