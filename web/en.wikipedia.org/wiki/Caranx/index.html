Caranx is a genus of tropical and subtropical marine fishes in the jack family, Carangidae, commonly known as jacks, trevallies, and kingfishes. Its species are fast-swimming predators found in the Atlantic, Pacific, and Indian Oceans, often around reefs, in estuaries, and in open coastal waters.

Species and characteristics

The genus includes about 18 recognised species, among them the giant trevally (C. ignobilis), crevalle jack (C. hippos), blacktip trevally (C. sexfasciatus), bluefin trevally (C. melampygus), and horse-eye jack (C. latus). Members share deep, compressed silvery bodies, a lateral line bearing scutes toward the tail, and forked caudal fins. Many attain large sizes — the giant trevally can exceed 1.5 m and 80 kg.

Fisheries

Caranx species support commercial and subsistence fisheries and are prized by sport anglers, particularly on light tackle and fly gear. Large individuals of some species may carry ciguatera toxin. Most are schooling predators feeding on smaller fishes, crustaceans, and cephalopods.
