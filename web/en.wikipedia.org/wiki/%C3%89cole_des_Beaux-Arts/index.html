École des Beaux-Arts

École des Beaux-Arts refers to a number of influential art schools in France, of which the most famous is the École Nationale Supérieure des Beaux-Arts in Paris, descending from the Académie Royale de Peinture et de Sculpture founded in 1648. For over two centuries it set the standard for academic art and architecture training in Europe and the Americas, giving its name to the Beaux-Arts style.

TypeArt school (grande école)
Founded1648 (as the Académie Royale); school reorganised 1816
LocationParis, France
Known forAcademic art; Beaux-Arts architecture; Prix de Rome

History and teaching

Students were trained through a rigorous system of drawing from life and from casts, ateliers led by master patrons, and competitions culminating in the Prix de Rome, which sent winners to study at the French Academy in Rome. Architecture students learned composition through the esquisse and the grand projet; the resulting classical, symmetrical, richly ornamented manner was exported worldwide, notably to the United States, where many architects trained in Paris.

Reform and legacy

The academic monopoly was challenged in the 19th century by independent exhibitions such as the Salon des Refusés (1863). After the student revolts of May 1968, architecture teaching was removed to new units (later the écoles nationales supérieures d'architecture), and the Paris school reoriented toward contemporary fine art. Its buildings on the rue Bonaparte, including the glass-roofed Palais des Études, remain landmarks of art education history.
