Thomas P. Stafford

Thomas Patten Stafford
Born: 17 September 1930, Weatherford, Oklahoma, US
Died: 18 March 2024, Satellite Beach, Florida, US
Occupation: Astronaut, test pilot, Air Force officer
Missions: Gemini 6A, Gemini 9A, Apollo 10, Apollo–Soyuz

Thomas Patten Stafford (September 17, 1930 – March 18, 2024) was an American Air Force officer, test pilot, and NASA astronaut, and one of only 24 people to have flown to the Moon. He flew four space missions: Gemini 6A and Gemini 9A in 1965–66, Apollo 10 in 1969 — the "dress rehearsal" for the first Moon landing — and commanded the American crew of the Apollo–Soyuz Test Project in 1975, the first joint US–Soviet spaceflight.

Early life and career

Stafford was born in Weatherford, Oklahoma, and graduated from the United States Naval Academy in 1952, taking his commission in the US Air Force. He became a test pilot at Edwards Air Force Base and was selected in NASA's second astronaut group in 1962.

Spaceflights

On Gemini 6A (December 1965), Stafford and Wally Schirra achieved the first rendezvous in space with Gemini 7. On Gemini 9A (1966), Stafford and Eugene Cernan performed the second American spacewalk mission and three rendezvous manoeuvres after their target vehicle failed. As commander of Apollo 10 (May 1969), Stafford flew the Lunar Module Snoopy with Cernan to within about 15 kilometres of the lunar surface, scouting the Apollo 11 landing site. In July 1975 he commanded the Apollo spacecraft that docked with the Soviet Soyuz 19, shaking hands in orbit with cosmonaut Alexei Leonov — a symbolic end to the Space Race.

Later career

Stafford rose to the rank of lieutenant general in the Air Force, commanding the Air Force Flight Test Center. After retiring in 1979 he worked in the aerospace industry and advised NASA, including chairing the committee that planned the servicing mission for the Hubble Space Telescope. The Stafford Air & Space Museum in Weatherford, Oklahoma, is named after him. He died in Florida in March 2024, aged 93.
