Rhine–Meuse–Scheldt delta

The Rhine–Meuse–Scheldt delta is a river delta in the Netherlands and Belgium formed by the confluence of the Rhine, the Meuse and the Scheldt rivers. It results in a network of river branches, distributaries and estuaries discharging into the North Sea, and contains some of the most densely populated and economically important land in Europe, including the port of Rotterdam, the largest seaport in Europe.

Geography

The delta begins where the Rhine enters the Netherlands near Millingen aan de Rijn and Spijk, dividing into the Waal, the Nederrijn and the IJssel. The Meuse joins this system from the south, and the Scheldt estuary (the Westerschelde) forms the south-western part in Zeeland and Flanders. Major distributaries include the Nieuwe Waterweg, the Hollands Diep, the Haringvliet and the Oosterschelde.

Flood protection

Much of the delta lies below sea level and is protected by dikes, dunes and storm surge barriers. After the catastrophic North Sea flood of 1953, the Netherlands built the Delta Works, a series of dams and barriers including the Oosterscheldekering and the Maeslantkering, widely regarded as one of the greatest hydraulic engineering projects in the world.

Economy

The delta hosts the ports of Rotterdam, Antwerp (reached via the Scheldt) and Vlissingen, extensive petrochemical industry, and intensive agriculture on fertile marine clay soils.
