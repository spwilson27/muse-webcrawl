HMAS Perth (FFH 186) is an Anzac-class frigate of the Royal Australian Navy (RAN). Named for the city of Perth, Western Australia, she is the third ship of the RAN to bear the name, after the Second World War cruiser HMAS Perth (D29), sunk at the Battle of Sunda Strait in 1942, and the Perth-class destroyer HMAS Perth (D 38). Built by Tenix Defence at Williamstown, Victoria, she was commissioned in 2006 as the last of the ten Anzac-class frigates built for Australia and New Zealand.

Design

The Anzac class is based on Blohm + Voss's MEKO 200 design. Perth displaces about 3,600 tonnes at full load and is armed with a 5-inch gun, Evolved Sea Sparrow surface-to-air missiles, Harpoon anti-ship missiles, torpedo tubes, and carries a single MH-60R Seahawk helicopter. Between 2014 and 2016, Perth was the test ship for the Anti-Ship Missile Defence upgrade, receiving the CEAFAR phased-array radar with its distinctive mast structure, and she later received the full ASMD capability roll-out.

Service

Perth has deployed repeatedly to the Middle East on Operation Manitou, Australia's maritime security contribution in the region, and has participated in multinational exercises such as RIMPAC. She is homeported at Fleet Base West (HMAS Stirling) on Garden Island, Western Australia.
