John II of France

John II (French: Jean II; 26 April 1319 – 8 April 1364), called John the Good (Jean le Bon), was King of France from 1350 until his death in 1364. His reign was marked by the Hundred Years' War, the Black Death's aftermath, and his capture by the English at the Battle of Poitiers (1356), after which he was held in England and ransomed under the Treaty of Brétigny.

Reign

Son of Philip VI, John succeeded amid war with Edward III of England and conflict with Charles of Navarre. He created the Order of the Star, debased coinage, and faced fiscal crises requiring taxes from the Estates-General. At Poitiers on 19 September 1356, fighting bravely on foot, he was captured by the Black Prince.

Captivity and death

While John was a prisoner in London, his son Charles as regent faced the Jacquerie revolt and Étienne Marcel's uprising in Paris. The Treaty of Brétigny (1360) set a ransom of three million gold crowns and ceded territory; John was released leaving hostages, including his son Louis of Anjou. When Louis escaped in 1363, John famously returned voluntarily to captivity in England — earning his epithet "the Good" — and died in London in 1364.
