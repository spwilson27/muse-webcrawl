National Highway System (Canada)

Established: 1988
Length: About 38,000 km
Managed by: Provincial and territorial governments
Includes: Trans-Canada Highway

The National Highway System (NHS) of Canada is a network of key interprovincial and international highways designated jointly by the federal, provincial and territorial governments, first established in 1988.

Network

The system comprises roughly 38,000 kilometres of core, feeder and northern/remote routes, including the Trans-Canada Highway, Highway 401 in Ontario, the Yellowhead Highway, and routes connecting provincial capitals, major cities, border crossings with the United States and ports. Highways in Canada are owned and operated by the provinces and territories, so the NHS is a designation and funding framework rather than a federally operated network.

Funding

The federal government contributes to NHS projects through infrastructure programmes negotiated with the provinces, prioritising safety, trade corridors and completion of core routes such as twinning of the Trans-Canada Highway.
