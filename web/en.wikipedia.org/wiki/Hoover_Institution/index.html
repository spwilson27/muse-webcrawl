The Hoover Institution, officially the Hoover Institution on War, Revolution and Peace, is an American public policy think tank that conducts research in economics, history, and politics, and maintains one of the world's largest archival collections on 20th-century political history. Founded in 1919 by Herbert Hoover, a Stanford alumnus who later became the 31st president of the United States, it is part of Stanford University and is housed around the landmark Hoover Tower on the Stanford campus.

History

Hoover, who had directed famine relief in Europe during and after the First World War, donated funds and documents in 1919 to found the Hoover War Collection at Stanford, aiming to preserve records of war, revolution, and peace that might otherwise be destroyed or seized. The collection grew through agents gathering material across Europe and Russia — including records of the Russian Revolution — and in 1946 the organisation took its present name as a research institution. The iconic 285-foot Hoover Tower was completed in 1941 for Stanford's 50th anniversary.

Library and archives

The Hoover Institution Library & Archives holds millions of documents, including the papers of figures across the political spectrum, extensive Chinese, Russian, and Middle Eastern collections, and materials such as the secret speech archive of the Soviet era and the Chiang Kai-shek diaries. It is a major resource for historians of the Cold War and of revolutionary movements.

Research and fellows

The Institution hosts fellows and research programmes in areas including economics, education (the Koret Task Force), national security, and American politics. Prominent fellows have included Milton Friedman, George Shultz, Thomas Sowell, Niall Ferguson, and Condoleezza Rice, who became director in 2020. The Institution is generally described as conservative or centre-right, while describing its mission as advancing ideas of individual, economic, and political freedom, private enterprise, and limited government. Its scholars have frequently moved into US government service.
