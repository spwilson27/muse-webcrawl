John Henry "Jack" Yates (July 11, 1828 – December 22, 1897) was an African American Baptist minister, educator, and community leader in Houston, Texas. Born into slavery in Virginia, he became one of the most influential Black religious leaders in post-Civil War Texas.

Life

Yates was born enslaved in Gloucester County, Virginia. Though teaching enslaved people to read was against the law, he learned literacy, and after the Civil War he moved with his family to Houston in 1865. There he became the first pastor of the Antioch Missionary Baptist Church, organized in 1866 by formerly enslaved people and widely regarded as Houston's first African American Baptist congregation; the church's early services were held under a brush arbor before its first buildings rose in what became Freedmen's Town.

Yates championed education for emancipated people, helping organize schools and encouraging property ownership; he purchased land in the Fourth Ward and built a home on Andrews Street in Freedmen's Town, where he supported institutions that anchored Houston's early Black community.

Legacy

Yates died in Houston in 1897 and was buried in Olivewood Cemetery. Jack Yates High School in Houston's Third Ward, opened in 1926 as the city's second high school for African American students, was named in his honor. His former home in Sam Houston Park is preserved by the Heritage Society, and Yates is remembered as a founding figure of Houston's Freedmen's Town.
