Michel Gondry

Born: 8 May 1963, Versailles, France

Occupations: Film director, screenwriter, producer

Years active: 1989–present

Notable work: Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind; The Science of Sleep

Michel Gondry (born 8 May 1963) is a French filmmaker noted for his inventive visual style, distinctive manipulation of mise en scène, and pioneering work in music videos. His films include Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind (2004), which won the Academy Award for Best Original Screenplay, and The Science of Sleep (2006).

Music videos and commercials

Gondry began his career as a drummer in the French band Oui Oui, for which he directed videos. His breakthrough came with Björk's "Human Behaviour" (1993), the first of seven collaborations with the singer. He went on to direct era-defining videos for Daft Punk ("Around the World"), the White Stripes ("Fell in Love with a Girl", built from Lego animation), the Chemical Brothers ("Star Guitar", "Let Forever Be"), Beck, Radiohead ("Knives Out"), and Kylie Minogue ("Come into My World"), in which the singer walks loops around a Paris street corner as the scene multiplies. His Levi's "Drugstore" commercial was listed in the Guinness World Records as the most awarded commercial of all time.

Feature films

Gondry's feature debut was Human Nature (2001), written by Charlie Kaufman. Their second collaboration, Eternal Sunshine of the Spotless Mind (2004), starring Jim Carrey and Kate Winslet, was a critical triumph celebrated for its handmade dream imagery and nonlinear love story. Gondry followed with The Science of Sleep (2006), the documentary Dave Chappelle's Block Party (2005), Be Kind Rewind (2008) — whose homemade "sweded" remakes of famous films became an internet phenomenon — and the superhero film The Green Hornet (2011). His later features include Mood Indigo (2013), adapted from Boris Vian, Microbe & Gasoline (2015), and The Book of Solutions (2023). He also co-created the television series Kidding (2018–2020) with Jim Carrey.

Style

Gondry favors practical, in-camera effects — forced perspective, stop-motion, cardboard models, and elaborate single-take choreography — over digital effects, drawing on childhood memories, dreams, and the aesthetics of handmade craft.
