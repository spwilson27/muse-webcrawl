Steelyard

The Steelyard, from the German Stalhof, was the main trading base (Kontor) of the Hanseatic League in London during the Middle Ages and early modern period, located on the north bank of the River Thames near present-day Cannon Street station.

Steelyard
German: Stalhof
Location: City of London
Active: 13th century – 1853
Affiliation: Hanseatic League

History

German merchants from Cologne and the Baltic towns received privileges from the 12th–13th centuries, and the walled Steelyard compound grew to include warehouses, a weigh house (from which the English name derives, via the steelyard balance), residences, a chapel and a guildhall. Its merchants enjoyed tax and customs privileges resented by London traders.

Decline

Elizabeth I revoked the League's privileges in 1598 and expelled its merchants, though they later returned in reduced form. The Great Fire of 1666 destroyed the complex, which was rebuilt. The property was sold in 1853 and the site redeveloped for Cannon Street station.

Legacy

A plaque commemorates the site. The Steelyard was one of the League's four main Kontore, alongside Novgorod, Bergen and Bruges.
