VESA (the Video Electronics Standards Association) is an international non-profit corporation that develops and supports standards for the display and video electronics industry. Its best-known standards include the VESA Local Bus, the Flat Display Mounting Interface, DisplayPort, and the DisplayHDR specifications.

History

VESA was founded in 1989 by NEC Home Electronics and a group of graphics-card manufacturers to promote uniform standards for PC displays. One of its early achievements was standardising Super VGA modes and BIOS extensions (VBE), and in 1992 it introduced the VESA Local Bus (VLB), briefly a popular expansion slot for graphics cards.

Major standards

  • VESA Mounting Interface Standard (MIS) — the "VESA mount" hole patterns used to attach monitors and TVs to stands and wall brackets.
  • DisplayPort (2006) and its Mini DisplayPort and embedded (eDP) variants — a royalty-free digital display interface.
  • EDID and DisplayID — data formats by which displays communicate their capabilities to a source device.
  • DisplayHDR — a certification programme for high-dynamic-range displays.
  • Display Stream Compression (DSC) — a visually lossless compression standard used in DisplayPort and HDMI 2.1.

Membership

VESA counts hundreds of member companies across the electronics industry, including major display, semiconductor, and computer manufacturers.
