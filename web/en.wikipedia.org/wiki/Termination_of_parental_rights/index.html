Termination of parental rights

The termination of parental rights (TPR) is a legal process in which a court permanently ends the legal relationship between a parent and a child. After termination, the parent loses all rights, duties, and obligations toward the child, who becomes legally free for adoption or other permanent placement.

Grounds

Termination may be voluntary, when a parent consents to relinquish rights (often in connection with adoption), or involuntary, when a court finds statutory grounds such as abuse, neglect, abandonment, prolonged failure to support or maintain contact, parental incapacity, or certain criminal convictions. In the United States, the Adoption and Safe Families Act of 1997 generally requires states to petition for termination when a child has been in foster care for 15 of the most recent 22 months, subject to exceptions.

Procedure and standards

Because parental rights are a fundamental liberty interest under the U.S. Constitution, involuntary termination requires clear and convincing evidence, the standard established by the Supreme Court in Santosky v. Kramer (1982). Courts generally conduct a two-step inquiry: whether statutory grounds exist, and whether termination is in the best interests of the child.

Consequences

Termination is considered the most severe outcome in child welfare law, sometimes called the "civil death penalty" of family law. It severs inheritance rights and legal ties in both directions unless a statute provides otherwise, though some jurisdictions allow post-termination contact agreements in adoption cases.
