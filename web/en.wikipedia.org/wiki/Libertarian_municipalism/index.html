Libertarian municipalism

Libertarian municipalism is a political theory developed by the American social theorist Murray Bookchin, set out most fully in works such as The Rise of Urbanization and the Decline of Citizenship (1987) and From Urbanization to Cities. It proposes reorganising society around directly democratic, face-to-face citizens' assemblies in municipalities — villages, towns and city neighbourhoods — confederated into networks that gradually displace the nation-state.

Principles

Under libertarian municipalism, policy is made by popular assemblies of all residents, while administration is carried out by mandated, recallable delegates to confederal councils. Land and major enterprises are municipalised — placed under community stewardship rather than private or state ownership. The programme is a central component of Bookchin's broader philosophy of social ecology and, in his later work, "communalism".

Libertarian municipalists do run in local elections, but as a strategy to gain a legal platform for creating assemblies that ultimately transfer power away from municipal councils — a point distinguishing the approach from both conventional local politics and insurrectionary anarchism.

Influence

Bookchin's ideas influenced the Kurdish leader Abdullah Öcalan, whose adaptation as "democratic confederalism" was implemented in the autonomous administration of North and East Syria (Rojava). Municipalist movements have also appeared in Spain and elsewhere under banners of "fearless cities" and new municipalism, though not all share Bookchin's full programme.
