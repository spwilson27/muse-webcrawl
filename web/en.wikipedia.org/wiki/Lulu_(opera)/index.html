Lulu is an opera in three acts by Alban Berg, composed between 1929 and 1935 to a libretto the composer adapted from Frank Wedekind's plays Earth Spirit (Erdgeist, 1895) and Pandora's Box (Die Büchse der Pandora, 1904) — the so-called "Lulu plays". The opera traces the rise and fall of Lulu, a seductive young woman whose relationships destroy the men (and women) drawn to her, until she is herself murdered by Jack the Ripper. It is written in Berg's characteristic style combining twelve-tone technique with passages of tonal, expressive lyricism.

History

Berg saw Wedekind's play performed in Vienna in 1905, in a production organised by Karl Kraus, and began the opera in 1929 after the success of Wozzeck. He completed the short score and orchestrated most of it, but died in December 1935 leaving the third act partly unorchestrated. His widow, Helene Berg, forbade completion of the score, so the opera was premiered at the Zurich Opera on 2 June 1937 in a two-act version with a concluding selection from Berg's Lulu Suite. After Helene's death, the composer Friedrich Cerha's completion of Act 3 was revealed, and the full three-act opera was premiered on 24 February 1979 at the Paris Opéra under Pierre Boulez, in a production by Patrice Chéreau.

Synopsis

A prologue presents Lulu as a serpent in a menagerie of human "animals". In Act 1 she is the wife of Dr Goll, who dies of a stroke on discovering her with the painter Schwarz; she marries Schwarz, who kills himself when he learns of her past; finally she shoots her lover and protector Dr Schön backstage at a theatre. In Act 2 her admirer Countess Geschwitz helps her escape prison; Schön's son Alwa, a composer, takes her to Paris, where she is blackmailed and flees to London. In Act 3, destitute, Lulu works as a prostitute in a London garret and is murdered by one of her clients, Jack the Ripper, who also kills the Countess.

Music

The opera is constructed with large-scale palindromic symmetry around a central film interlude, and uses serial rows associated with individual characters. The roles of Lulu's husbands and clients are designed to be doubled by the same singers as her later clients, underlining the work's mirror structure.
