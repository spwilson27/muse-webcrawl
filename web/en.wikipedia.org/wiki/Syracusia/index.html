Syracusia

Syracusia (Greek: Συρακουσία) was an ancient Greek ship sometimes claimed to be the largest transport ship of antiquity. It was designed by Archimedes and built around 240 BC by Archias of Corinth on the orders of Hiero II of Syracuse. Historians put its length at roughly 55 metres (180 ft) and its carrying capacity at 1,600 to 1,800 tons, comparable to some of the largest wooden sailing ships of later centuries.

Description

The ship is known from a description by the historian Athenaeus, drawing on an account by Moschion. According to that description it had multiple decks, a gymnasium, shrines, gardens, stables, a library and even a small temple of Aphrodite, as well as a bath and accommodations for hundreds of passengers. Its hull was fastened with the shell-first mortise-and-tenon technique and sheathed in lead over tarred cloth. Archimedes is said to have launched it single-handedly using a compound pulley or screw device, and to have designed a screw pump to clear its bilge water.

Fate

The ship was reportedly too large for any port in Sicily except Syracuse. After a single voyage carrying grain and gifts, Hiero presented it to Ptolemy III Euergetes of Egypt, where it was renamed Alexandris (or Alexandria) and laid up at Alexandria.
