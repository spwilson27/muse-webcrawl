Selma, Alabama

Selma is a city in and the county seat of Dallas County, Alabama, United States, located on the banks of the Alabama River. Its population was about 17,971 at the 2020 census. Selma is internationally known as the site of key events in the American civil rights movement.

History

The area was inhabited for centuries by Native American peoples before Hernando de Soto's expedition passed through in 1540. Selma was incorporated in 1820 and named, at the suggestion of future U.S. Vice President William Rufus King, after the legendary Scottish bard Ossian's "Songs of Selma." During the American Civil War, Selma was a major Confederate industrial and manufacturing centre; it fell to Union forces at the Battle of Selma in April 1865.

Civil rights movement

In 1965 Selma became the focal point of the campaign for African American voting rights. On 7 March 1965—"Bloody Sunday"—state troopers and a sheriff's posse violently attacked peaceful marchers crossing the Edmund Pettus Bridge. The nationally televised assault galvanised support for the Voting Rights Act of 1965. Later that month, Martin Luther King Jr. led the completed Selma to Montgomery marches. The route is now the Selma to Montgomery National Historic Trail.

Geography and economy

Selma lies in Alabama's Black Belt region, named for its fertile dark soil. Historic downtown Selma contains one of the largest intact historic districts in the state, with antebellum and Victorian architecture.
