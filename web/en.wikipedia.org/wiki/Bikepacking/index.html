Bikepacking

Bikepacking is a style of self-supported, multi-day bicycle travel, usually over mixed terrain including trails, gravel roads and backcountry routes. It combines elements of bicycle touring and backpacking: gear is carried in soft bags attached directly to the bicycle frame rather than in panniers on racks, keeping the bike light and manoeuvrable off-road.

Soft bags on the frame replace racks and panniers.

Equipment

A typical setup combines a frame bag in the main triangle, a large seat pack, a handlebar roll, and accessory pouches, carrying ultralight camping gear, clothing, food and tools. Bikes are usually rigid or hardtail mountain bikes, gravel bikes, or fat bikes for snow and sand. Minimalist packing is essential because capacity is far smaller than in traditional touring.

Routes and events

Long-distance dirt routes such as the Great Divide Mountain Bike Route (from Banff, Canada, to the Mexican border) and the Colorado Trail are classic bikepacking journeys. Self-supported ultra-endurance races, notably the Tour Divide, popularised the discipline from the late 2000s, and a growing route-publishing community now documents routes worldwide.
