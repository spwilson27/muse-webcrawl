Basic metabolic panel

The basic metabolic panel (BMP) is a group of eight chemical blood tests frequently ordered by health care providers in the United States. It provides information about fluid balance, kidney function, blood sugar and electrolyte levels.

Components

The standard panel measures four electrolytes — sodium, potassium, chloride and bicarbonate (reported from measured total CO2) — together with blood urea nitrogen (BUN), creatinine, glucose and calcium. From sodium, chloride and bicarbonate the anion gap can be calculated, helping classify metabolic acidosis. BUN and creatinine together are the principal markers used to assess renal function and to estimate the glomerular filtration rate (eGFR).

Use

The BMP is among the most common laboratory tests performed, used in routine screening, emergency assessment, monitoring of chronic kidney disease and diabetes, and checking for side effects of medicines such as diuretics and ACE inhibitors. The expanded comprehensive metabolic panel (CMP) adds albumin, total protein, bilirubin and liver enzymes.
