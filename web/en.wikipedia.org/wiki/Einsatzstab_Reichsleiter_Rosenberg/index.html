The Einsatzstab Reichsleiter Rosenberg (ERR, "Special Task Force of Reich Leader Rosenberg") was a Nazi Party organization directed by Alfred Rosenberg that engaged in the systematic looting of cultural property from museums, libraries, archives, and private — especially Jewish — collections across German-occupied Europe during World War II.

History

Established in July 1940 after the fall of France, the ERR initially targeted Jewish and Masonic libraries and archives ostensibly for Rosenberg's planned Institute for the Study of the Jewish Question in Frankfurt. Its mandate expanded to fine art, furnishings, and musical instruments.

Operations

In Paris, the Jeu de Paume museum served as a central depot where art historian Rose Valland secretly recorded shipments. In Eastern Europe, ERR units seized millions of books and artworks from Poland and the Soviet Union. Confiscated items were shipped to depots in Germany, including Neuschwanstein Castle and mines at Altaussee in coordination with other looting bodies.

Aftermath

Allied Monuments, Fine Arts, and Archives ("Monuments Men") officers used ERR records to trace and restitute works. Rosenberg was convicted at Nuremberg and executed in 1946. Restitution research continues using the ERR's meticulous inventories.
