Straw purchase

TypeFraud / evasion technique

A straw purchase or nominee purchase is any purchase wherein an agent agrees to acquire goods or services for someone who is often unable or unwilling to purchase the goods or services themselves, and the agent transfers the goods or services to that person. The "straw buyer" acts as a front, concealing the identity of the true buyer, and the practice is illegal when used to evade legal requirements or restrictions.

Firearms

In the United States, a straw purchase of a firearm occurs when the actual buyer of a firearm is unable to pass required federal background checks or is otherwise prohibited from purchasing, and a proxy buyer who can pass the checks purchases the firearm on the prohibited person's behalf. Under the Gun Control Act of 1968, lying on ATF Form 4473 about being the actual buyer is a federal felony. In Abramski v. United States (2014), the Supreme Court upheld the prohibition even where both the straw buyer and the true buyer were legally eligible to own firearms.

Real estate and lending

In mortgage fraud, a straw buyer lends their name and credit to obtain a loan for a property actually controlled by someone else, often as part of flipping or foreclosure-rescue schemes. Such arrangements typically violate loan covenants requiring the borrower to be the true purchaser and occupant.

Other contexts

The term also applies to adults purchasing alcohol or tobacco for minors, political contributions routed through "straw donors" to evade campaign-finance limits, and vehicle or insurance purchases made to conceal the real party in interest.
