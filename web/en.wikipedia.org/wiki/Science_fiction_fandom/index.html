Science fiction fandom

Science fiction fandom is a worldwide community of fans of the science fiction genre, and one of the earliest organised fandoms of any medium. It emerged in the United States in the late 1920s and 1930s around the letter columns of pulp magazines such as Amazing Stories, whose editor Hugo Gernsback published readers' addresses, enabling fans to correspond, form clubs, and publish their own magazines.

Fanzines and conventions

Fans created fanzines — amateur publications exchanged among clubs and individuals — developing a rich culture of criticism, fiction, and insider slang ("fanspeak"). In 1939 fans organised the first World Science Fiction Convention (Worldcon) in New York, which continues annually and administers the Hugo Awards (established 1953), voted by convention members. Regional conventions and specialised awards followed around the world.

Influence

Many fans became professional writers, editors, and publishers — figures such as Isaac Asimov, Ray Bradbury, and Frederik Pohl came up through fandom — and fandom's practices (conventions, awards, fan fiction, and participatory culture) spread to media fandoms in general. From the 1970s onward, science fiction fandom increasingly overlapped with Star Trek and Star Wars fandoms, anime fandom, gaming, and costuming (cosplay), while traditional literary fandom continued at Worldcon and regional conventions. In the internet era, fanzine culture partly migrated online to mailing lists, forums, and archives such as the Organization for Transformative Works' Archive of Our Own.
