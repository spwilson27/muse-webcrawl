The Dassault MD.454 Mystère IV is a French fighter-bomber aircraft developed by Dassault Aviation in the 1950s. An evolution of the Mystère II with a thinner, more swept wing and a more powerful engine, the prototype first flew on 28 September 1952, and the type became the first transonic aircraft to enter service with the French Air Force.

Operational history

Much of the production run was financed by the United States under the Mutual Defense Assistance Program. Mystère IVAs saw extensive combat with the Israeli Air Force in the Suez Crisis of 1956 and the Six-Day War of 1967, and with the Indian Air Force in the Indo-Pakistani War of 1965. France retired its last Mystère IVs in the early 1980s, some having served as trainers until 1982.

Design

The Mystère IVA was armed with two 30 mm DEFA cannon and could carry bombs, rockets and napalm under the wings. It was powered by a Hispano-Suiza Verdon turbojet, a licence-built Rolls-Royce Tay, giving a maximum speed around Mach 0.92 in level flight and supersonic speed in a dive.
