Land and water hemispheres

The land hemisphere and water hemisphere are the hemispheres of Earth containing the largest possible total areas of land and ocean, respectively. The determinations of the hemispheres vary slightly: one determination places the centre of the land hemisphere near Nantes in France, at about 47°13′N 1°32′W, while the centre of the water hemisphere lies in the Pacific Ocean east of New Zealand, at about 47°13′S 178°28′E.

Characteristics

The land hemisphere contains about seven-eighths of Earth's land, including Europe, Africa, North America, most of Asia and most of South America. Even so, the land hemisphere still contains more ocean than land. The water hemisphere contains only about one-eighth of Earth's land, principally Australasia, a few Pacific islands and the southern tip of South America; the rest is ocean, mostly the Pacific.

History

The concept was popularised in the nineteenth century; Alphonse Berget's 1912 determination put the land hemisphere pole near Nantes. Modern calculations using digital elevation and land-cover data shift the centres slightly depending on how coasts and islands are counted.
