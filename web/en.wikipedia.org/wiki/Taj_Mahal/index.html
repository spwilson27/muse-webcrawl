Taj Mahal

Taj Mahal (ताज महल, "Crown of the Palace")
Location: Agra, Uttar Pradesh, India (south bank of the Yamuna)
Commissioned: 1632 by Mughal emperor Shah Jahan
Architect: attributed to Ustad Ahmad Lahauri
UNESCO World Heritage Site (1983); among the New7Wonders of the World
[Image: The Taj Mahal at dawn, reflected in the charbagh pool]

The Taj Mahal is an ivory-white marble mausoleum in Agra, India, commissioned in 1632 by the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan to house the tomb of his favourite wife, Mumtaz Mahal, who had died in childbirth in 1631. Universally admired as the jewel of Mughal architecture and one of the most beautiful buildings in the world, it symbolises India's rich history and draws millions of visitors each year.

Architecture

The complex combines Persian, Islamic and Indian styles. Its centrepiece, raised on a marble plinth at the end of a formal charbagh garden divided by raised waterways, is the tomb chamber crowned by a great onion dome about 35 metres high, flanked by four slender minarets — built tilted slightly outward so that in an earthquake they would fall away from the tomb. The white marble is inlaid with pietra dura: semi-precious stones set in floral designs, along with calligraphy of Quranic verses by Amanat Khan. Inside, an octagonal chamber holds the cenotaphs of Mumtaz Mahal and, beside her, Shah Jahan himself — added after his deposition and death in 1666; the actual graves lie in a crypt below.

Construction and history

Some 20,000 artisans worked under a board of architects; the main mausoleum was essentially complete by 1643, with outworks finished about a decade later. Materials came from across Asia — marble from Makrana, jade and crystal from China, turquoise from Tibet, lapis from Afghanistan. In later centuries the Taj suffered plunder (notably in 1857), British-era alterations, and environmental threats from air pollution, which has yellowed the marble and led to strict emission controls around Agra.

Legacy

Rabindranath Tagore called it "a teardrop on the cheek of time." The Taj Mahal was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1983 and was named among the New7Wonders of the World in 2007.
