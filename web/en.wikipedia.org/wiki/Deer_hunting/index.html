Deer hunting

Deer hunting is the practice of hunting deer for food, sport, or population management. Deer have been hunted by humans since prehistoric times and remain among the most widely pursued game animals in the world. In North America the principal quarry species are the white-tailed deer and mule deer; in Europe red deer, roe deer, and fallow deer are commonly hunted.

Methods

Common methods include still hunting (moving slowly through habitat), stalking, hunting from a stand or raised platform overlooking feeding areas or trails, and, where legal, drives with dogs or groups of hunters. Weapons include rifles, shotguns, muzzleloaders, bows, and crossbows, each often given its own season. Hunters use calls, scent control, camouflage, and decoys; baiting and hunting over food plots are regulated differently by jurisdiction.

Regulation and management

Most jurisdictions require licences and tags, restrict seasons and bag limits, and set rules on antlered versus antlerless deer to manage herd structure. Because natural predators have been reduced in many regions, regulated hunting is used as a primary tool to limit overpopulation, crop damage, vehicle collisions, and the spread of diseases such as chronic wasting disease.

Venison

Meat from deer is called venison. Hunters field-dress carcasses promptly and many donate surplus meat to food banks through organised donation programmes.
