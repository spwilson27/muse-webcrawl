Protostome

In protostomes the blastopore becomes the mouth.

Protostomia (from Greek prōto- "first" and stoma "mouth") is the clade of animals once thought to be characterised by the formation of the organism's mouth before its anus during embryonic development. Protostomes form one of the two great divisions of bilaterian animals, the other being the deuterostomes (including chordates and echinoderms).

Development

In the classical account, protostome embryos undergo spiral cleavage, the fate of cells is determinate (mosaic development), the coelom forms by splitting of the mesoderm (schizocoely), and the blastopore — the first opening of the embryo — becomes the mouth, with the anus forming secondarily. Not all members show every trait, and modern classification rests on molecular phylogenetics rather than embryology alone.

Classification

The protostomes comprise two major superphyla. The Ecdysozoa are animals that moult a cuticle, including arthropods, nematodes, velvet worms, tardigrades and priapulids. The Spiralia (or Lophotrochozoa) include molluscs, annelids, brachiopods, flatworms and rotifers. Together they account for the overwhelming majority of animal species, above all the insects.
