Aal-E-Imran (Arabic: آل عمران, Āl ʿImrān, "The Family of Imran") is the third chapter (surah) of the Quran, containing 200 verses (ayat). It is a Medinan surah, believed to have been revealed largely in the years after the Battle of Badr and around the Battle of Uhud (3–4 AH / c. 625 CE).

Content

The surah takes its name from the family of Imran, the father of Mary in Islamic tradition. Its opening verses include the famous distinction between clear (muhkam) and ambiguous (mutashabih) verses. Major themes include the absolute oneness of God, the stories of the births of Mary and Jesus (Isa), who is affirmed as a prophet born of a virgin but not divine, dialogues with Jews and Christians (the "People of the Book"), the story of Zechariah and John the Baptist, and extensive passages on the Battle of Uhud, consoling and admonishing the Muslim community after its setback there. The verse of the Throne's surah-neighbour, Ayat al-Kursi, is in Al-Baqarah, but Aal-E-Imran contains the well-known verse "Allah — there is no god but He, the Living, the Self-Subsisting" (3:2) and the declaration that the only religion with God is Islam (3:19).

Context

Together with Al-Baqarah, Aal-E-Imran is one of the two longest Medinan surahs and is sometimes paired with it as al-Zahrawan, "the two bright ones", in hadith literature, which recommends their recitation.
