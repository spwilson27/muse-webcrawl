German federal highway system

A Bundesstraße sign

The German federal highway system (German: Bundesstraßen, singular Bundesstraße) is the network of federal trunk roads in Germany, forming the second tier of the country's public road hierarchy below the Autobahn motorway network. Federal highways are numbered with the prefix "B" (for example B 1, B 96) and are marked by yellow rectangular signs with black numerals.

Structure

Federal highways are owned by the federal government but administered on its behalf by the individual states. The network covers roughly 38,000 kilometres, linking major cities and towns and providing connections between Autobahns. Below the federal level are state roads (Landesstraßen), district roads (Kreisstraßen) and municipal roads.

History

The numbering scheme dates from the Weimar era, when long-distance roads were designated Fernverkehrsstraßen in 1932; under the Nazi regime they became Reichsstraßen, and after 1949 the Federal Republic renamed them Bundesstraßen. After German reunification in 1990 the system was extended to the new eastern states.

Characteristics

Unlike Autobahns, Bundesstraßen generally have a single carriageway, at-grade intersections and pass through towns and villages, though many sections have been upgraded to dual carriageway expressway-like standards ("gelbe Autobahn", yellow motorways). The general speed limit outside built-up areas is 100 km/h.
