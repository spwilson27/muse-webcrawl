Safiye Sultan, one of the most powerful women of the Ottoman "Sultanate of Women".

Safiye Sultan (c. 1550 – after 1619) was the Haseki Sultan of the Ottoman sultan Murad III and the Valide Sultan to her son Mehmed III. One of the most prominent figures of the period known as the Sultanate of Women, she wielded great political influence for nearly three decades, conducting diplomacy in her own name and amassing a vast fortune.

Early life

Safiye was of Albanian origin, born in the Dukagjin highlands, and was captured and sold into the imperial harem as a girl. In about 1563 she was presented to Şehzade Murad, then governor of Manisa, and became his favourite concubine. In 1566 she bore his first son, the future Mehmed III, and upon Murad's accession in 1574 she became Haseki Sultan.

Valide Sultan

When Mehmed III came to the throne in 1595, Safiye became Valide Sultan — the most powerful position a woman could hold in the empire — with an enormous daily stipend. She intervened in appointments, received foreign envoys, and corresponded directly with Queen Elizabeth I of England, exchanging letters and gifts; Elizabeth sent her a jewelled portrait and a carriage, and Safiye's agent (kira) Esperanza Malchi handled much of her diplomacy and finances until Malchi's murder by mutinous cavalrymen in 1600.

Later life

After Mehmed III's death in 1603, his son Ahmed I retired Safiye to the Old Palace in January 1604, ending her political dominance, though she retained enormous wealth. She was a great patron of architecture: in 1598 she commissioned the Yeni Valide (New Mosque) complex at Eminönü in Istanbul, though construction stalled after her fall and was completed only in the 1660s by Turhan Sultan.
