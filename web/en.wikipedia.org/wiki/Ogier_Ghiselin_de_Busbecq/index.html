Ogier Ghiselin de Busbecq (1522–1592), also known by his Latinised name Augerius Gislenius Busbequius, was a Flemish writer, herbalist and diplomat in the service of the Austrian Habsburgs. He served as ambassador of Ferdinand I to the Ottoman Empire under Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent from 1554 to 1562, and is famous for his Turkish Letters, one of the most vivid European accounts of Ottoman life.

Life and embassy

Busbecq was educated at the universities of Louvain, Paris, Venice, Bologna and Padua. Entering Habsburg service, he was sent to Constantinople in 1554, succeeding to the embassy charged with negotiating a durable peace along the Hungarian frontier. His mission culminated in an eight-year truce agreed in 1562. During his years in the Ottoman capital he was frequently held in a state little better than house arrest, yet he observed Ottoman society with unusual intelligence and sympathy.

The Turkish Letters and other work

His four Turkish Letters (Legationis Turcicae epistolae quatuor), written in elegant Latin and published from 1581 onwards, describe Ottoman politics, the court, the military system, baths, gardens and daily life, and remain a classic of travel literature. Busbecq was also a passionate naturalist: he sent home specimens that introduced the tulip, lilac and horse chestnut to northern European gardens, earning him a place in the prehistory of "tulip mania". His botanical collections fed into the work of his friend Carolus Clusius. He also copied the inscription of the Monumentum Ancyranum in Ankara, preserving the text of the Res Gestae Divi Augusti, and assembled a notable collection of Greek manuscripts, including the famous Vienna Dioscorides tradition of herbals.

Later years and death

After returning from Turkey, Busbecq served as tutor and librarian at the imperial court in Vienna and later guarded the French queen Elisabeth of Austria. Travelling in France in 1592, he was detained by Leaguers near Rouen and died shortly after his release, on 28 October 1592.
