Logopenic progressive aphasia (LPA) is a form of primary progressive aphasia (PPA), a neurodegenerative syndrome characterised by slowly progressive language impairment. It was delineated as a distinct PPA variant by Marsel Mesulam and colleagues, with diagnostic criteria formalised in 2011.

Clinical features

The core features are impaired single-word retrieval in spontaneous speech and naming, and impaired repetition of sentences and phrases. Speech is slow and hesitant, with frequent pauses for word-finding, and phonological errors (sound substitutions) in speech and naming. In contrast to the semantic variant, single-word comprehension and object knowledge are largely preserved, and in contrast to the nonfluent/agrammatic variant, grammar and motor speech are spared.

Neuroanatomy and pathology

LPA is associated with atrophy and hypometabolism in the left posterior perisylvian region, including the posterior superior temporal gyrus and inferior parietal lobule. Unlike the other PPA variants, which are usually caused by frontotemporal lobar degeneration, LPA is most commonly underpinned by Alzheimer disease pathology, and patients often show biomarkers positive for amyloid.

Diagnosis and management

Diagnosis is clinical, supported by neuropsychological testing of language, neuroimaging, and increasingly by cerebrospinal fluid or PET biomarkers. There is no disease-modifying treatment; management centres on speech and language therapy and communication strategies.
