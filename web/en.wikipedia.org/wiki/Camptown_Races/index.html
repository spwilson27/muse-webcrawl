Camptown Races

"Camptown Races" is a minstrel song by American composer Stephen Foster, published in 1850 as "Gwine to Run All Night, or De Camptown Races." The song, written in a dialect associated with African American Vernacular English of the period, depicts a day at the horse races in a camp town. Its refrain, with the famous "doo-dah! doo-dah!" lines, has become one of the most recognizable melodies in American popular music.

Lyrics and structure

The verses describe the race track as five miles long ("Camptown race-track five miles long / Oh! doo-dah-day!") and recount betting on the bay and the bobtail nag. The singer resolves to go home with his "hat caved in" but hopes to win money on the race. Like many of Foster's songs, it consists of verses for a solo voice with a chorus sung by a group after each verse.

History

Foster composed the song in 1849 and it was published in Baltimore in February 1850 by F. D. Benteen. It was an immediate success and became a staple of minstrel troupes. The song's popularity has endured: it has been referenced or parodied countless times, notably in the 1974 film Blazing Saddles, and the Bugs Bunny cartoons of the mid-20th century. The melody is commonly played by ice cream trucks in some countries and is widely used to accompany clown routines and comic horse-race scenes.

Cultural legacy

Although written for the minstrel stage, a form of entertainment now regarded as rooted in racist caricature, "Camptown Races" has transcended its origins and is now often heard without lyrics as an emblem of old-time Americana. It is Foster's second most famous song after "Oh! Susanna."
