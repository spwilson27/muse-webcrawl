Wharfinger

A wharfinger is the owner or keeper of a wharf — the person or business responsible for a waterfront landing place where ships load and unload cargo. The wharfinger typically charges fees, known as wharfage, for the use of the wharf and any associated storage.

Duties and law

Historically the wharfinger supervised the berthing of vessels, the handling and warehousing of goods, and the day-to-day operation of the quay. In common law, wharfingers occupy a legal position similar to that of other bailees and carriers: they may have a lien on goods in their custody for unpaid wharfage, and they owe a duty of reasonable care for goods stored with them.

Usage

The word dates from the 16th century and derives from "wharf" plus the suffix -inger. Though less common in everyday language today, the term survives in shipping law, port administration and the names of some harbour offices.
