Agordat (also spelled Akordat or Ak'ordat) is a town in western Eritrea, in the Gash-Barka Region, situated on the banks of the Barka River roughly west of Keren. It is an important market and transport town for the surrounding lowlands, known for its large livestock market, dates, fruit and vegetables grown in local plantations, and its role as a trading point between the Eritrean highlands and Sudan.

History

Agordat entered colonial history in December 1893, when Italian colonial troops under Colonel Giuseppe Arimondi defeated Mahdist forces from Sudan at the Battle of Agordat — one of the first victories of Italian arms in the Horn of Africa. Under Italian rule the town acquired a railway station on the line from Massawa via Asmara (the extension towards Agordat was completed in the 1920s–30s), a mosque, churches and administrative buildings. During the East African campaign of the Second World War it again saw fighting in early 1941 as British and Commonwealth forces advanced from Sudan into Eritrea.

Geography and economy

The town lies at the edge of the western escarpment at an elevation of roughly 600 metres, in a hot, semi-arid climate tempered by the Barka's seasonal flow. Its economy rests on agriculture, trade with Sudan, and its position on the road from Keren to Tesseney and the Sudanese border.
