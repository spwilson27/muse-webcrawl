Almack's

Founded1765
LocationKing Street, St James's, London
TypeAssembly rooms / social club
Closed1871 (demolished 1905)

Almack's was the name of a number of social clubs and assembly rooms in London that ranked among the most exclusive meeting places of the British upper classes, particularly during the Regency era. Its Wednesday-night subscription balls were governed by a committee of lady patronesses whose approval was essential for social success.

The patronesses

Admission was by voucher, controlled by patronesses including Lady Castlereagh, Lady Jersey and the Princess Lieven. Rules were strict: doors closed at 11 p.m., dress codes were enforced, and even the Duke of Wellington was famously turned away for arriving late. The refreshments were proverbially plain — bread, cake and lemonade or orgeat.

Later history

The rooms were renamed Willis's Rooms in 1871 after their proprietor, and the building was demolished in 1905. Almack's survives in historical fiction, notably the Regency novels of Georgette Heyer, as the emblem of fashionable exclusivity.
