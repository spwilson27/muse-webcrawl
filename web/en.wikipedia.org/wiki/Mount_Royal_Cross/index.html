The Mount Royal Cross (French: Croix du Mont-Royal) is a monumental illuminated cross standing on the north-east peak of Mount Royal in Montreal, Quebec, Canada. At 31.4 metres (103 feet) tall, it is one of the city's most recognisable symbols and can be seen from much of the eastern part of Montreal and from the St. Lawrence River.

History

The first cross on the mountain was erected in 1643 by Paul de Chomedey, Sieur de Maisonneuve, founder of Montreal, in fulfilment of a vow he made to the Virgin Mary after the young colony was spared from a flood. The present steel cross was erected in 1924 by the Société Saint-Jean-Baptiste, funded by public subscription. It was illuminated for the first time on Christmas Eve 1924.

In 1992, timed lighting controls were installed so that the cross could signal the election of a pope — burning white during a vacancy of the Holy See and changing on the announcement of a new pope, a tradition demonstrated in 2005 and 2013. In 2009 the lighting system was converted to LED, allowing the cross to be lit in different colours for special occasions; it is normally illuminated white, and purple during Lent and following the death of a pope or sovereign.
