Malvern is a spa town and civil parish in Worcestershire, England, lying at the foot of the Malvern Hills. It comprises several settlements, chiefly Great Malvern, Malvern Link, Malvern Wells, West Malvern and Little Malvern. The town grew to prominence in the Victorian era as a spa resort famed for the purity of its spring water.

History

Great Malvern developed around an 11th-century Benedictine priory, whose church survives. The town's fame rests on its water: springs in the hills, such as St Ann's Well and the Holy Well, were promoted from the 18th century, and in the 1840s doctors James Wilson and James Manby Gully established hydropathic clinics that drew patients including Charles Darwin, Florence Nightingale and Alfred Tennyson. The arrival of the railway in 1859 cemented Malvern's resort status.

Culture and science

Composer Edward Elgar lived much of his life in the area and is buried at Little Malvern. In 1942 the Telecommunications Research Establishment moved to Malvern, where crucial radar development work took place; its successor organisations made the town a centre of defence research. The Morgan Motor Company, maker of traditional sports cars since 1909, is based in Malvern Link. The Malvern Hills, an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty, rise to 425 metres at the Worcestershire Beacon and dominate the town's setting.
