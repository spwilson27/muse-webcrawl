The Batavian Republic (Dutch: Bataafse Republiek) was the successor state to the Dutch Republic, proclaimed on 19 January 1795 after French revolutionary armies, aided by Dutch Patriot exiles, overran the country and Stadtholder William V fled to England. It was a client state of the French Republic, modelled on it as a "sister republic".

Reforms

The Republic introduced major reforms: the first Dutch constitution (1798), a unitary state replacing the old confederation of provinces, legal equality for Jews and Catholics, abolition of guilds and seigneurial rights, standardised measures, and a national education law in 1806. The name "Batavian" linked the new state to the ancient Batavi, symbols of liberty in Dutch tradition.

Politics and economy

Political life oscillated between unitary democrats and federalist moderates, punctuated by coups in 1798 and 1801 encouraged by France. Under the Treaty of The Hague (1795) the Republic paid a heavy indemnity and maintained a French occupation army; war with Britain cost it colonies including the Cape Colony and Ceylon.

End

In 1806 Napoleon converted the Republic into the Kingdom of Holland under his brother Louis Bonaparte, seeking tighter control.
