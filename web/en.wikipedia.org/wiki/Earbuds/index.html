Earbuds

Earbuds are small earphones that fit directly in the outer ear, facing the ear canal but without fully sealing it, as distinct from in-ear monitors which insert into the canal with ear tips. They are typically sold with portable media players, smartphones, and other mobile devices for listening to audio.

History

Lightweight earbuds became ubiquitous with the Sony Walkman era and were later popularized globally as the bundled earphones of the Apple iPod (2001) and iPhone. In 2016 Apple introduced the wireless AirPods, removing the headphone jack from the iPhone 7, which accelerated the market for "truly wireless" earbuds that pair over Bluetooth and charge in a carrying case. Competing products include Samsung's Galaxy Buds, Google's Pixel Buds, and Sony's WF series, many offering active noise cancellation, transparency modes, and spatial audio.

Health and safety

Health authorities caution that listening at high volumes through earbuds placed close to the eardrum can contribute to noise-induced hearing loss, and recommend volume-limiting practices such as the 60/60 rule. Use while driving or cycling is regulated in some jurisdictions because earbuds can mask ambient sounds.
