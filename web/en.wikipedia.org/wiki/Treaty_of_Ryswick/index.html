Treaty of Ryswick

Signed20 September 1697
LocationRijswijk, Dutch Republic
PartiesFrance; England, Spain, the Dutch Republic, the Holy Roman Empire

The Treaty of Ryswick, or Peace of Ryswick, was a series of treaties signed in the Dutch city of Rijswijk between 20 September and 30 October 1697. They ended the Nine Years' War (1688–1697) between France and the Grand Alliance.

Terms

  • France recognized William III as King of England, Scotland and Ireland, and withdrew support from the Jacobite claimants;
  • France returned most of the territories taken since the Treaty of Nijmegen (1678), including Freiburg, Breisach and the Duchy of Lorraine, but retained Strasbourg and Alsace;
  • France gave up its claims in Catalonia and the Spanish Netherlands frontier fortresses it had occupied were restored to Spain;
  • The Dutch were permitted to garrison a line of "Barrier" fortresses in the Spanish Netherlands against future French attack;
  • In the Americas, France and England restored wartime conquests; the western part of Hispaniola (Saint-Domingue, modern Haiti) was formally ceded by Spain to France.

Significance

Ryswick marked the first check on the expansion of Louis XIV's France and established a precedent for negotiated settlements based on restored possessions. It proved only a truce: the unresolved question of the Spanish succession led within four years to the War of the Spanish Succession (1701–1714).
