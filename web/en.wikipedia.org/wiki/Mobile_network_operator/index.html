Mobile network operator

A mobile network operator (MNO), also known as a wireless service provider or cellular carrier, is a provider of wireless communication services that owns or controls all the elements necessary to sell and deliver services to an end user, including radio spectrum allocation, wireless network infrastructure (base stations and core network), backhaul, billing and customer care systems. Examples of large MNOs include Verizon, AT&T and T-Mobile US in the United States; Vodafone, Deutsche Telekom and Orange in Europe; and China Mobile, the world's largest operator by subscribers.

MNOs and MVNOs

An MNO is distinguished from a mobile virtual network operator (MVNO), which buys wholesale access to an MNO's network and resells service under its own brand without owning spectrum or radio infrastructure. Regulators license spectrum to MNOs, usually by auction, and impose coverage and competition conditions. Major MNOs are typically members of the GSMA industry association and interconnect through roaming agreements that let their subscribers use foreign networks.

Services and technology

MNOs provide voice, SMS and mobile data services over successive generations of technology — GSM (2G), UMTS (3G), LTE (4G) and NR (5G) — to consumers, businesses and increasingly to connected devices (IoT). They issue SIM cards (and now eSIM profiles) that authenticate subscribers to the network. Competition among the usually three to five MNOs in each national market is a central focus of telecommunications regulation, particularly at merger review and spectrum auctions.
