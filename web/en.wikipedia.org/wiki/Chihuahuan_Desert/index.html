The Chihuahuan Desert is a desert ecoregion in northern Mexico and the southwestern United States. It is the largest desert in North America, covering roughly 360,000 square kilometres (about 140,000 square miles), primarily in the Mexican states of Chihuahua, Coahuila, Durango, and Nuevo León, and extending into West Texas, southern New Mexico, and southeastern Arizona. It is the most biologically diverse desert in the Western Hemisphere and one of the most diverse arid regions in the world.

Geography

The desert occupies the Mexican Plateau between the Sierra Madre Occidental to the west and the Sierra Madre Oriental to the east. Higher and cooler than the neighbouring Sonoran Desert, most of the Chihuahuan Desert lies between 600 and 1,675 metres in elevation. The Rio Grande (Río Bravo del Norte) flows through its northern portion, and numerous endorheic basins (bolsones) with playas characterize the interior. "Sky island" mountain ranges scattered across the desert support pine-oak woodland at higher elevations and harbour many endemic species.

Climate

The climate is arid, with most of the roughly 235 mm of annual precipitation falling during summer monsoon thunderstorms from July to September. Winters can be cold, with hard freezes, owing to the altitude and continental location.

Flora and fauna

Characteristic plants include creosote bush (Larrea tridentata), lechuguilla (Agave lechuguilla) — a near-endemic indicator species — tarbush, ocotillo, sotol, yuccas, and numerous cacti. The region is a global centre of cactus diversity, with hundreds of species. Fauna includes pronghorn, mule deer, coyotes, javelina, kangaroo rats, and a rich herpetofauna. Major protected areas include Big Bend National Park and Guadalupe Mountains National Park in Texas, White Sands National Park in New Mexico, and the Cuatro Ciénegas basin in Coahuila, famous for its endemic aquatic species.
