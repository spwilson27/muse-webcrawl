Adolph Hermann Josef Coors (born Kuhrs; February 4, 1847 – May 5, 1929) was a German-American brewer who founded the Coors Brewing Company in Golden, Colorado, in 1873. Starting as an orphaned apprentice brewer in Prussia, he stowed away to the United States in 1868 and built one of America's major brewing enterprises, famous for its lager marketed as "brewed with pure Rocky Mountain spring water."

Early life

Coors was born in Barmen in the Rhine Province of Prussia (now part of Wuppertal, Germany), the son of Joseph Kuhrs and Helena Hein. He was apprenticed at the Henry Wenker brewery in Dortmund at age thirteen and later worked at breweries in Kassel and Berlin. After both parents died, leaving him responsible for younger siblings, he emigrated to the United States in 1868 — by his own later account as a stowaway — and worked in breweries in Naperville, Illinois, and for the Stenger brewery, where he became foreman.

Founding the brewery

Coors moved west to Denver, Colorado, in 1872, bought into a bottling business, and in 1873 partnered with Jacob Schueler to purchase and convert an abandoned tannery in Golden into the Golden Brewery. Coors supplied the brewing expertise while Schueler provided most of the capital; Coors bought out his partner in 1880 and renamed the firm Adolph Coors Golden Brewery. The brewery's location gave access to Clear Creek water and, later, to spring water Coors promoted as the secret of his beer's quality. The business survived Colorado's early adoption of Prohibition (1916 in Colorado, before national prohibition) by producing malted milk, near beer, and porcelain and ceramic products through the Coors Porcelain Company — ventures that later grew into the technical-ceramics firm CoorsTek.

Later life and death

Coors married Louisa Weber in 1879, and the couple had six children, including Adolph Coors Jr., who succeeded him in running the company. On May 5, 1929, while staying at a hotel in Virginia Beach, Virginia, Coors fell (or, as some accounts say, jumped) from a sixth-floor window and died. He was buried in Crown Hill Cemetery in Wheat Ridge, Colorado. His company remained family-controlled for generations, merging with Miller in the 2000s to form MillerCoors and later becoming part of Molson Coors.
