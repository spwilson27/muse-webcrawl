British home front during World War II

British home front, WWII
Period: 1939–1945
Key features: the Blitz, rationing, evacuation

The British home front during World War II covers the experience of civilians in the United Kingdom between 1939 and 1945: bombing during the Blitz, the evacuation of children from cities, rationing of food, clothing and petrol, blackout restrictions and mass mobilisation of labour, including women's war work.

The Blitz

From September 1940 to May 1941 the Luftwaffe bombed London and other cities, killing more than 40,000 civilians. Air-raid shelters, barrage balloons and civil defence wardens became part of daily life. Bombing returned in 1944 with the V-1 and V-2 weapons.

Rationing and daily life

Food rationing began in January 1940 and some items remained rationed into the 1950s. The Ministry of Food promoted the "Kitchen Front", Dig for Victory gardens and communal British Restaurants. Conscription extended to women from 1941, and by 1943 some 90 per cent of single women were in war work or the services.
