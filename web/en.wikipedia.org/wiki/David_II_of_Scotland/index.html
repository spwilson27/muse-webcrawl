David II (5 March 1324 – 22 February 1371) was King of Scots from 1329 until his death, the only surviving son of Robert the Bruce. He succeeded to the throne aged five, and his reign was dominated by the Second War of Scottish Independence against England.

Reign

After the Scottish defeat at Halidon Hill in 1333, the young king and his wife Joan of the Tower were sent for safety to France, where they were hosted by Philip VI at Château Gaillard. David returned to Scotland in 1341 and began ruling in his own right. In 1346, invading England in support of France, he was defeated and captured at the Battle of Neville's Cross, and spent eleven years as Edward III's prisoner. He was released in 1357 under the Treaty of Berwick for a ransom of 100,000 marks, payable in instalments that burdened Scotland for years.

Later reign and death

David quarrelled with his heir presumptive, his nephew Robert Stewart, and controversially proposed that a son of Edward III succeed him in return for cancelling the ransom, a plan the Scottish parliament rejected in 1364. He died unexpectedly at Edinburgh Castle in 1371 and was succeeded by Robert II, founder of the Stewart dynasty.
