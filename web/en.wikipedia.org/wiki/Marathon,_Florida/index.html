Marathon, Florida

Marathon is a city in Monroe County, Florida, United States. It is located in the middle Florida Keys, largely on Key Vaca, with portions extending onto several neighboring islands. The population was 9,689 at the 2020 census. The city stretches along the Overseas Highway and is a popular destination for boating, fishing, and diving.

History

The name Marathon dates back to the Florida East Coast Railway, when the Knights Key station and the marathon-like effort of workers building the Key West Extension of Henry Flagler's railroad inspired the name. The railroad workers' strenuous labor in constructing the line through the Keys in the early twentieth century was compared to running a marathon. Marathon was incorporated as a city in 1999.

Key Vaca, the principal island, was once home to a settlement of railroad workers. The area grew as a fishing community and later as a tourist destination following the completion of the Overseas Highway in 1938.

In September 2017, Marathon was severely affected by Hurricane Irma, which made landfall in the Lower Keys. The city sustained significant damage but has since largely recovered.

Geography

Marathon is located along U.S. Route 1, the Overseas Highway, approximately 50 miles (80 km) northeast of Key West and 50 miles southwest of Key Largo. The city encompasses parts of Boot Key, Crawl Key, Deer Key, Fat Deer Key, Grassy Key, Key Vaca, Knight's Key, Little Crawl Key, Long Point Key, and Pigeon Key.

The Seven Mile Bridge, one of the longest bridges in the world when completed, connects Knight's Key in Marathon to Little Duck Key. The older parallel bridge is now used as a fishing pier and provides access to historic Pigeon Key.

Attractions

Marathon is home to Sombrero Beach, a popular public beach, and Crane Point Museum and Nature Center, a 63-acre tropical hardwood hammock. The Dolphin Research Center on Grassy Key and the Turtle Hospital, a veterinary hospital dedicated to the rehabilitation of injured sea turtles, are well-known attractions. The city also hosts the annual Marathon Seafood Festival.

Demographics

As of the census of 2020, there were 9,689 people residing in the city. The population includes a significant number of seasonal residents and retirees, and tourism and commercial fishing are major components of the local economy.
