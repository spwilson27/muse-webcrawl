Parinirvana

In Buddhism, parinirvana (Sanskrit: parinirvāṇa; Pali: parinibbāna) is the final nirvana attained at death by an arhat or a Buddha who has achieved enlightenment during life. It marks the end of the cycle of death and rebirth (samsara) and the dissolution of the five aggregates (skandhas).

The Buddha's parinirvana

According to tradition, Siddhartha Gautama attained parinirvana at Kushinagar at the age of 80, lying on his right side between two sal trees. His final days, last meal, final instructions and the distribution of his relics are described in the Mahāparinibbāna Sutta of the Pali Canon. Reclining Buddha images throughout Asia commemorate the event.

Doctrine

Nirvana attained during life is sometimes called nirvana with remainder, because the aggregates persist until death. Parinirvana is nirvana without remainder: after death the enlightened person is not reborn, and questions about their state are among the unanswered questions in early Buddhist texts.
