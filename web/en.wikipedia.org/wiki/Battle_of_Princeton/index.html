Battle of Princeton

The Battle of Princeton was fought on 3 January 1777 near Princeton, New Jersey, in the American Revolutionary War. George Washington's Continental Army defeated a British force under Lt. Col. Charles Mawhood, capping the brilliant "Ten Crucial Days" that began with the crossing of the Delaware and the victory at Trenton.

Background

After his second success at Trenton (Assunpink Creek, 2 January), Washington slipped away at night from Cornwallis's front — leaving campfires burning — and marched around the British to strike the garrison at Princeton.

Battle

At dawn Brig. Gen. Hugh Mercer's brigade collided with Mawhood's regiments marching to join Cornwallis. Mercer was mortally wounded and his men gave way, but Washington personally rallied them, riding into the fire. Reinforced Americans outflanked the British, who retreated; some briefly held Nassau Hall before surrendering. Washington then withdrew to winter quarters at Morristown as Cornwallis hurried back.

Aftermath

Princeton completed the reversal of the disastrous 1776 campaign, revived enlistments and morale, and forced the British to abandon most of New Jersey. Mercer County and the battle monument commemorate the fight.
