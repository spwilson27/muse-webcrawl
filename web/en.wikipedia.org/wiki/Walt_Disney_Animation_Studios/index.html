Walt Disney Animation Studios

Walt Disney Animation Studios (WDAS), sometimes shortened to Disney Animation, is an American animation studio that creates animated features and short films for the Walt Disney Company. Founded on October 16, 1923, by brothers Walt and Roy O. Disney, it is the longest continually operating animation studio in the world and the flagship label of Walt Disney Studios. The studio has produced 63 feature films, from Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937) to Moana 2 (2024).

History

The studio began as the Disney Brothers Cartoon Studio in Los Angeles, producing the Alice Comedies and Oswald the Lucky Rabbit, then created Mickey Mouse in 1928's Steamboat Willie, a landmark in synchronised sound. Through the 1930s it pioneered the multiplane camera, Technicolor cartoons and the animated feature film. Snow White (1937) was a colossal success, inaugurating the "Golden Age" that included Pinocchio, Fantasia, Dumbo and Bambi.

Renaissance and modern era

After Walt Disney's death in 1966 the studio's output declined, then revived spectacularly in the "Disney Renaissance" (1989–1999) with The Little Mermaid, Beauty and the Beast, Aladdin and The Lion King. From the mid-2000s, under Pixar leaders John Lasseter and Ed Catmull, the studio shifted fully to computer animation, producing Tangled, Wreck-It Ralph, Frozen (the highest-grossing animated film of its time), Zootopia, Moana and Encanto. Its headquarters is the Roy E. Disney Animation Building in Burbank, recognisable by its giant Sorcerer's Hat.
