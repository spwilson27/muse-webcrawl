Queenstown, New Zealand

Queenstown (Māori: Tāhuna) is a resort town in Otago in the south-west of New Zealand's South Island. It is built around an inlet on Lake Wakatipu, a long, thin Z-shaped lake formed by glacial processes, and has views of nearby mountains including the Remarkables, Cecil Peak, and Walter Peak. The town is the seat of the Queenstown-Lakes District and had a resident population of around 16,000 in the district area, with many more visitors year-round.

History

The area was visited by Māori travelling to gather pounamu (greenstone). European settlement began in the 1860s when gold was discovered in the Arrow River, drawing prospectors including Jack Tewa, and the town grew rapidly during the Otago gold rush. It is said to have been named because it was "fit for Queen Victoria".

Tourism

Queenstown is a major centre for adventure tourism: skiing and snowboarding at nearby fields (Coronet Peak, the Remarkables, Cardrona), jet boating, white-water rafting, and commercial bungy jumping, which was popularized at the Kawarau Gorge Suspension Bridge by A. J. Hackett from 1988. The town also serves as a gateway to Fiordland and was a filming location for The Lord of the Rings films.
