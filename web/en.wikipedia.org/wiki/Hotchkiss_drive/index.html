Hotchkiss drive

The Hotchkiss drive is a shaft drive form of power transmission used in automobiles, named after the French manufacturer Hotchkiss. It was the most popular rear-wheel drive layout for cars from the 1920s to the 1960s, and remains common on trucks and SUVs.

Description

In a Hotchkiss layout the propeller shaft is open (exposed) and connects the gearbox to a live rear axle through universal joints. Crucially, the driving and braking torque reactions are taken by the leaf springs that locate the axle, which also handle side thrust. This contrasts with the earlier torque tube arrangement, in which a rigid tube enclosing the shaft transmitted the torque reaction to the chassis.

The system is simple and robust, but the use of the springs for both suspension and axle location can produce axle "wind-up" and tramp under hard acceleration or braking. Later designs often added separate torque arms or links while retaining the open shaft.
