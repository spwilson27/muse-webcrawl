Reading Festival

The Reading Festival is an annual rock music festival held at Richfield Avenue in Reading, England, over the August bank holiday weekend. Descended from the National Jazz and Blues Festival launched in 1961, it is the world's oldest popular music festival still in existence, and since 1999 has been twinned with the Leeds Festival, with line-ups rotating between the two sites.

History

The festival began in 1961 at Richmond as the National Jazz Festival, evolving through jazz, blues, and progressive rock incarnations in the 1960s and settling permanently at Reading in 1971. Through the 1970s and 1980s it became a bastion of hard rock and heavy metal. Its most famous moment came in 1992, when Nirvana's headline set — with Kurt Cobain arriving on stage in a wheelchair — became one of the most celebrated live performances in rock history, released as Live at Reading.

The modern festival

Today the paired Reading and Leeds festivals span rock, indie, metal, hip-hop, dance, and pop across multiple stages, drawing a notably young audience and serving as a traditional rite of passage for British teenagers after exam results. Headliners in recent decades have included Metallica, Radiohead, Eminem, Arctic Monkeys, The Cure, Billie Eilish, and Oasis-era stadium acts.
