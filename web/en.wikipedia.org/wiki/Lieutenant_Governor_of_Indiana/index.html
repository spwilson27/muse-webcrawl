The Lieutenant Governor of Indiana is a constitutional office in the US state of Indiana. The lieutenant governor is elected jointly with the governor on a single ticket and serves as president of the Indiana Senate, casting tie-breaking votes. The office holder is first in the line of succession to the governor.

Duties

Under the Indiana Constitution the lieutenant governor presides over the state senate and succeeds the governor in case of death, resignation or removal. By statute the office also oversees several agencies, including the Indiana State Department of Agriculture, the Office of Community and Rural Affairs, and tourism and housing bodies, and serves as Secretary of Agriculture and Rural Development.

Election and term

The lieutenant governor is nominated at a state party convention rather than in a primary election, then elected on a joint ticket with the governor in the general election. The term is four years with no term limit.

History

The office was created with Indiana's statehood in 1816. Several lieutenant governors have ascended to the governorship, and the office has occasionally been a significant power base in its own right because of its senate role — the Indiana lieutenant governor is often described as one of the most powerful in the United States.
