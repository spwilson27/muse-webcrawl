Retinoic acid

Retinoic acid is a metabolite of vitamin A (retinol) that mediates the functions of vitamin A required for growth and development. Retinoic acid is required in chordate animals, which include all higher animals from fishes to humans. During early embryonic development, retinoic acid generated in a specific region of the embryo appears to act as an intercellular signaling molecule that guides development of the posterior portion of the embryo.

Biological role

Retinoic acid acts through the retinoic acid receptors (RAR) and retinoid X receptors (RXR), which are nuclear receptors that regulate gene transcription. It plays a key role in cell differentiation, embryonic patterning (including development of the hindbrain and limbs), and the maintenance of epithelial tissues. Both deficiency and excess of retinoic acid during pregnancy are teratogenic: excess can cause malformations of the craniofacial structures, central nervous system, and heart.

Medical uses

All-trans retinoic acid (tretinoin) is used topically for acne and photoaging, and orally as a treatment for acute promyelocytic leukemia, where it induces differentiation of leukemic promyelocytes. Related synthetic retinoids include isotretinoin (13-cis-retinoic acid), used for severe acne, and adapalene and tazarotene.
