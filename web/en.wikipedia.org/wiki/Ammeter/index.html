An ammeter (abbreviation of ampere meter) is an instrument used to measure the electric current in a circuit. Electric currents are measured in amperes, hence the name. An ammeter is connected in series with the circuit whose current is to be measured, and it is designed to have very low internal resistance so that it does not significantly change the current it is measuring.

Types

The classic analogue ammeter is based on the D'Arsonval (moving-coil) galvanometer: current through a coil suspended in a magnetic field produces a torque that deflects a needle against a spring. Because the movement responds to direct current and only small currents, shunts are used to extend its range, and rectifiers to measure alternating current. Other historical designs include moving-iron, electrodynamic and hot-wire instruments. Modern digital ammeters convert current to a voltage across a precision shunt and digitise it, while clamp meters measure current non-invasively using a current transformer or Hall-effect sensor around a single conductor.

Use

Ammeters range from picoammeters for laboratory leakage measurements to switchboard instruments monitoring thousands of amperes in power systems. Connecting an ammeter directly across a voltage source (in parallel) can blow its fuse or destroy the instrument, because of its near-zero resistance.
