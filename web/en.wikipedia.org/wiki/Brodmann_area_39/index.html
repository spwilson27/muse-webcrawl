Brodmann area 39

Part ofParietal lobe (inferior parietal lobule)
LocationAngular gyrus, surrounding the caudal tip of the superior temporal sulcus
SystemLanguage and association cortex

Brodmann area 39, or BA39, is part of the parietal cortex of the human brain corresponding to the angular gyrus, which surrounds the caudal tip of the superior temporal sulcus near the junction of the temporal, parietal and occipital lobes. It is one of the cytoarchitectonic regions defined by Korbinian Brodmann in 1909, and together with Brodmann area 40 (the supramarginal gyrus) it forms the inferior parietal lobule.

Functions

The angular gyrus is a cross-modal association area involved in aspects of language (including reading and writing), number processing and arithmetic, spatial cognition, attention, memory retrieval and theory of mind. In the dominant (usually left) hemisphere, damage to this region contributes to Gerstmann syndrome — a tetrad of agraphia, acalculia, finger agnosia and left–right disorientation — and to alexia with agraphia. BA39 is also frequently reported as part of the default mode network active during rest and internally directed thought, and neuroimaging studies implicate it in semantic processing and episodic memory.

Clinical significance

Lesions, stimulation and functional imaging of area 39 have been studied in relation to dyslexia, semantic dementia, out-of-body experiences (following disruption near the temporoparietal junction) and mathematical cognition. Its role as a hub integrating visual, auditory and somatosensory information makes it central to accounts of how the brain constructs concepts and symbols.
