Howth Castle

Howth Castle, County Dublin.

Howth Castle is a historic residence on the Howth Peninsula near Dublin, Ireland. A castle has stood on the site since the 13th century, and the lands were held for some eight centuries by the St Lawrence family, Lords and later Earls of Howth, making it one of the longest continuously inhabited family homes in Ireland. The present building is a layered structure: a medieval tower house core with substantial additions of the 18th and 19th centuries, including work of the 1730s and a major Victorian remodelling with castellated features.

History and legends

Local legend associates the castle with the 16th-century pirate queen Grace O'Malley (Gráinne Mhaol), who, refused hospitality on a visit, is said to have abducted the Earl's heir until the family promised that the castle gates would always remain open at dinner time — a tradition long honoured at Howth. The castle's grounds contain a Neolithic portal tomb known as Aideen's Grave, said to mark the burial of Aideen, wife of the hero Oscar in Irish mythology. The estate is also noted for its gardens and its famous rhododendron gardens, planted in the 19th century.

Recent history

The St Lawrence family sold the castle and most of the estate in 2019, ending more than 800 years of family ownership; the house had latterly operated in part as a cookery school and event venue. The demesne includes a deer park, a golf course and the transport museum site, and much of the peninsula is protected as a Special Area of Conservation.
