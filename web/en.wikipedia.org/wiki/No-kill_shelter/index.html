No-kill shelter

A no-kill shelter is an animal shelter that does not kill healthy or treatable animals, reserving euthanasia only for animals that are terminally ill or considered dangerous. The "no-kill" movement emerged in the United States in the 1980s and 1990s as an alternative to high euthanasia rates in municipal shelters.

Definition

The most commonly used benchmark is a save rate of 90 percent or above, allowing roughly 10 percent for animals with irremediable suffering or dangerous behaviour. No-kill shelters may be limited-admission (accepting animals selectively) or open-admission.

Strategies

No-kill programmes typically combine high-volume adoption, foster networks, trap–neuter–return for feral cats, low-cost spay/neuter, transfer partnerships with rescue groups, and pet-retention support to keep animals out of shelters.

History and debate

San Francisco became the first major US city to guarantee placement for healthy adoptable animals in 1994. Austin, Texas adopted a no-kill plan in 2010 and became the largest no-kill city in the US. Critics note that limited-admission no-kill shelters can shift euthanasia to other facilities, while supporters point to large national declines in shelter killing since the 1970s.
