Instantaneous frequency

In signal processing, the instantaneous frequency of a signal is the time rate of change of the phase of its analytic signal. For a real-valued signal, the analytic signal is formed using the Hilbert transform, giving a complex signal whose argument is the instantaneous phase; differentiating that phase with respect to time (and dividing by 2π) yields the instantaneous frequency.

Interpretation

The concept is most meaningful for monocomponent signals — those with a single, slowly varying frequency component, such as a chirp or a frequency-modulated carrier. For multicomponent signals the instantaneous frequency represents a weighted average of the components and can behave counterintuitively.

Applications

Instantaneous frequency is used in FM demodulation, radar and sonar, seismic analysis, speech processing, and biomedical signal analysis. Together with instantaneous amplitude (the magnitude of the analytic signal), it underlies time–frequency methods such as the Hilbert spectral analysis used with empirical mode decomposition.
