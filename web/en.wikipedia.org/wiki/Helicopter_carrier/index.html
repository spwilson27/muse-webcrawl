Helicopter carrier

A helicopter carrier is a type of aircraft carrier whose primary purpose is to operate helicopters. A ship classed as a helicopter carrier has a full-length flight deck and hangar facilities for a substantial rotary-wing air group but lacks facilities—catapults, arresting gear, or (in most cases) a ski-jump—for operating conventional fixed-wing aircraft, though many also embark STOVL jets where fitted with a suitable deck.

Roles

Helicopter carriers are used principally for:

  • Anti-submarine warfare (ASW): embarking helicopters with dipping sonar and torpedoes to screen a fleet—the original Cold War role of types such as the Japanese Hyūga class and the former Soviet Moskva class.
  • Amphibious assault: landing ships (designated LPH, LHA or LHD in US service) that put troops ashore by helicopter and landing craft; many have well decks and are more precisely called amphibious assault ships.
  • Commando/assault and humanitarian roles: e.g., the Royal Navy's HMS Ocean.

Terminology

The label is also applied diplomatically: Japan's Izumo-class vessels are officially "helicopter destroyers" for domestic political reasons despite their size and full flight decks, and have since been modified to operate F-35B fighters. Helicopter carriers are distinguished from escort carriers (a Second World War type) and from through-deck cruisers; hull classification symbols include CVH, LPH and DDH.

Operators

Navies operating helicopter carriers have included those of the United States, France (the Mistral class), Spain, Italy, South Korea (ROKS Dokdo), Japan, Egypt, Australia (Canberra class, an LHD type), China (Type 075), Russia and the United Kingdom.
