Shallow foundation

A shallow foundation, also called a spread footing or open foundation, is a type of foundation that transfers building loads to the earth very near the surface, rather than to a subsurface layer or a range of depths as does a deep foundation. Shallow foundations are generally used where the soil close to the surface has sufficient bearing capacity and where the structure's loads are relatively modest; by convention their depth is less than about 3 metres, or less than the width of the footing.

Types

Individual (pad) footings support a single column. Combined footings support two or more columns where space is restricted, for example at a property line. Strip footings run continuously under a load-bearing wall. Raft or mat foundations are large slabs supporting the whole structure, used on weak or expansive soils to spread the load and reduce differential settlement. Strap footings connect an eccentrically loaded footing to an interior footing with a rigid beam.

Design considerations

Design must check bearing capacity (the maximum pressure the soil can sustain without shear failure, classically estimated with Terzaghi's bearing-capacity equation), total and differential settlement, sliding, overturning, frost depth, and the water table. Footings are typically cast in reinforced concrete and must extend below the frost line in cold climates to prevent frost heave.
