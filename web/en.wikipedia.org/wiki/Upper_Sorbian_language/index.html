Upper Sorbian (Upper Sorbian: hornjoserbšćina) is a West Slavic minority language spoken by the Sorbs in the historical region of Upper Lusatia, today part of the German state of Saxony around the towns of Bautzen (Budyšin), Kamenz (Kamjenc), and Hoyerswerda (Wojerecy). Together with Lower Sorbian, spoken in Brandenburg, it forms the Sorbian branch of the West Slavic languages, most closely related to Polish, Kashubian, and Czech.

History

Sorbian has been spoken in Lusatia since Slavic tribes settled the region in the early Middle Ages. Written Upper Sorbian developed in the 16th century with the Reformation; Catholic and Protestant literary standards initially diverged in spelling before being unified in the 19th century during a Sorbian national revival led by figures such as Jan Arnošt Smoler and the cultural society Maćica Serbska (founded 1847). The language survived suppression during the Nazi era and institutional support under the GDR, but the number of speakers has declined since reunification, and UNESCO classifies Upper Sorbian as definitely endangered.

Linguistic features

Upper Sorbian retains the grammatical dual number alongside singular and plural — a rarity among living Slavic languages. It has seven cases and a distinctive letter ř (as in Czech) and the digraph dź. Its vocabulary shows a heavy historical layer of German loanwords reflecting centuries of bilingualism.

Status and revitalization

Upper Sorbian enjoys legal protection in Saxony, where bilingual signs are common in the Sorbian settlement area. The Witaj ("welcome") programme provides Sorbian-medium kindergarten and school education. Media include the newspaper Serbske Nowiny and broadcasts by MDR. The Catholic parishes north-west of Bautzen form the demographic heartland where the language is still transmitted to children.
