Francesco Bartolomeo Rastrelli (1700 – 29 April 1771) was an Italian architect who worked in the Russian Empire. He developed an easily recognizable style of Late Baroque, both sumptuous and majestic. His major works, including the Winter Palace in Saint Petersburg and the Catherine Palace in Tsarskoye Selo, are famous for extravagant luxury and opulence of decoration.

Biography

Francesco Rastrelli was born in Paris in 1700, the son of Carlo Bartolomeo Rastrelli, a Florentine sculptor and architect who had been invited to France by Louis XIV. In 1716, when the younger Rastrelli was sixteen, the family moved to Saint Petersburg, where his father entered the service of Peter the Great.

The younger Rastrelli travelled in Europe in the 1720s to study architecture. On his return he entered Russian service and in 1730 was appointed court architect to Empress Anna. Under Empress Elizabeth, who reigned from 1741, his career reached its height: Elizabeth favoured elaborate Baroque design, and Rastrelli produced a stream of palaces, churches and pavilions for the court.

Major works

Winter Palace

Rastrelli's most famous building is the Winter Palace in Saint Petersburg (1754–1762), the fourth palace of that name on the site. With its green-and-white facade stretching some 215 metres along the Neva, more than 1,000 rooms and a richly modelled exterior, it became the principal residence of the Russian emperors and is now the core of the State Hermitage Museum.

Catherine Palace

At Tsarskoye Selo, Rastrelli rebuilt the Catherine Palace (from 1749, consecrated 1756) into a vast summer residence for Elizabeth. Its azure facade, white columns and gilded ornament, and the famous Amber Room within, made it one of the most celebrated palaces in Europe.

Other works

His other projects in and around Saint Petersburg include the Smolny Convent with its sky-blue cathedral, the Stroganov Palace and Vorontsov Palace on Nevsky Prospekt and Sadovaya Street, the Peterhof Grand Palace enlargements, and the Rundāle and Jelgava palaces built for Ernst Johann von Biron in Courland (present-day Latvia).

Later life

When Catherine the Great came to the throne in 1762, court taste shifted decisively toward Neoclassicism, and Rastrelli's Baroque style fell from favour. He retired and died in Saint Petersburg in 1771. A square in front of the Smolny Convent now bears his name.
