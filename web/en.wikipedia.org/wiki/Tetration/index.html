Tetration

In mathematics, tetration (or hyper-4) is an operation based on iterated, or repeated, exponentiation. There is no standard notation for tetration, though Knuth's up-arrow notation a ↑↑ b and the left-exponent ba are common. Tetration is the next hyperoperation after exponentiation, just as exponentiation is iterated multiplication and multiplication is iterated addition: a tetration na is a power tower of n copies of a.

Definition

For positive integers, 1a = a, and n+1a = a(na). The values grow extremely rapidly: 32 = 222 = 16, 42 = 65,536, and 52 already has nearly 20,000 digits; 43 = 37,625,597,484,987 is astronomically large. Because evaluation proceeds from the top of the tower down, tetration is not associative in the way exponent towers with different bases are not.

Extensions

Extending tetration to real or complex heights — defining, for example, π2 in a smooth, natural way — is an active research problem. Approaches use functional equations, asymptotic expansions, and analogies with the super-logarithm, the inverse of tetration. Tetration also appears in the study of the Ackermann function and in Knuth's up-arrow notation for very large numbers.
