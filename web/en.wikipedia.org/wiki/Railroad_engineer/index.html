Railroad engineer

A railroad engineer (in North America), also called a locomotive engineer, train operator or train driver, is a person who drives a train or locomotive. The engineer is responsible for operating the train safely, controlling speed, and observing signals and rules, usually working with a conductor who manages the train and its schedule.

Duties

Before departure, the engineer inspects the locomotive and reviews orders and restrictions. En route they regulate the throttle and brakes, manage slack in freight couplings, sound the horn at crossings, and respond to signals and dispatchers. Engineers must know the physical characteristics of their territory — grades, curves, sidings and speed limits.

Qualification

In the United States, engineers are certified under federal regulations (49 CFR Part 240) after training and examinations; in Britain, train drivers train under licensed operators and assessors. The role historically developed from steam locomotive drivers, whose skills with fire, boiler and throttle were highly prized.

Terminology

"Engineer" in this sense is distinct from professional engineering; in most of the world the equivalent term is train driver, while "engine driver" survives in British usage informally.
