Titanichthys

Titanichthys
PeriodLate Devonian, ~375 million years ago
ClassPlacodermi
OrderArthrodira
LengthEstimated up to 4–5 metres

Titanichthys is an extinct genus of giant arthrodire placoderm fish from the Devonian of Morocco, Europe, and North America. It was among the largest marine animals of its time.

Description

Unlike its fearsome relative Dunkleosteus, which had sharp bony shearing plates, Titanichthys had narrow, relatively weak jaw plates unsuited to cutting prey. Its anatomy suggests it was a suspension feeder, swimming with open mouth to strain plankton and small krill-like animals from the water — convergent with modern baleen whales and whale sharks.

Discovery

Fossils were described by John Strong Newberry in the 19th century from the Cleveland Shale of Ohio, and further species were later found in the Devonian deposits of the Anti-Atlas in Morocco. Several species have been proposed, including T. agassizii and T. clarkii.

Paleobiology

Its great size and filter-feeding lifestyle make Titanichthys one of the earliest known examples of gigantism linked to plankton feeding in vertebrates, foreshadowing strategies that re-evolved many times in later seas.
