Bedřich Smetana (2 March 1824 – 12 May 1884) was a Czech composer who pioneered the development of a musical style that became closely identified with his people's aspirations to a cultural and political "revival". He has been regarded in his homeland as the father of Czech music. Internationally he is best known for his 1866 opera The Bartered Bride and for the symphonic cycle Má vlast ("My Fatherland"), which portrays the history, legends and landscape of the composer's native Bohemia.

Life

Smetana was born in Litomyšl, in eastern Bohemia, the son of a brewer. He showed early musical talent, studying piano and violin, and performed in public as a child. After studies in Prague he became a piano teacher and, inspired by Franz Liszt, opened his own piano school in 1848. From 1856 to 1861 he worked in Gothenburg, Sweden, as a teacher, conductor and composer.

Returning to Prague, Smetana became a central figure in the city's musical life: he conducted at the Provisional Theatre, founded a music school, and championed Czech-language opera. In 1874 he lost his hearing, probably as a result of syphilis, but continued composing, including the cycle Má vlast (1874–1879). His health, mental and physical, deteriorated in his final years, and he died in an asylum in Prague in 1884.

Music

Smetana's output includes eight completed operas, of which The Bartered Bride (1866) remains the most popular, alongside Dalibor and Libuše. His orchestral masterpiece, the six symphonic poems of Má vlast, includes the celebrated Vltava ("The Moldau"), depicting the great Bohemian river. His chamber music includes the autobiographical String Quartet No. 1 in E minor, "From My Life" (1876), whose finale famously portrays the onset of his deafness.

Legacy

Smetana is regarded as the founder of the Czech national school of composition, an influence on Antonín Dvořák and the generations that followed. The Prague Spring International Music Festival opens each year on the anniversary of his death with a performance of Má vlast. The Smetana Museum in Prague stands near the Charles Bridge.
