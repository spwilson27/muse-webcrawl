Tate's thesis

Tate's thesis is the 1950 Princeton doctoral dissertation of the American mathematician John Tate, titled Fourier Analysis in Number Fields and Hecke's Zeta Functions. It reformulated Erich Hecke's theory of L-functions using harmonic analysis on the adele ring and idele class group of a number field.

Content

Tate applied Fourier analysis and Pontryagin duality on the locally compact abelian group of adeles to prove the analytic continuation and functional equation of Hecke L-functions in a unified way, avoiding the classical case-by-case arguments. The local factors at each place are treated through zeta integrals, and the global result follows from the Poisson summation formula.

Influence

Although not published until years later, the thesis circulated widely and transformed algebraic number theory, laying foundations for the Langlands program and the modern theory of automorphic forms.
