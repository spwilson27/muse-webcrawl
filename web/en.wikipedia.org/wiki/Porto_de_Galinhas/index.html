Porto de Galinhas

Porto de Galinhas is a beach and village in the municipality of Ipojuca, on the southern coast of the Brazilian state of Pernambuco, about 60 kilometres south of Recife. It is one of Brazil's most popular beach resorts, famous for its natural pools (piscinas naturais) — warm, crystal-clear tidal pools formed among coral reefs just offshore, where visitors raft out at low tide to swim among tropical fish. The beach has repeatedly been voted among the best in Brazil by travel magazines.

Name and history

The name means "Port of the Chickens." According to local tradition, after the abolition of the slave trade to Brazil in the 19th century, clandestine slave ships still landed enslaved Africans at the port concealed beneath crates of guinea fowl; the code phrase "there are new chickens at the port" (tem galinha nova no porto) announced their arrival — and the place became Porto de Galinhas. Chicken motifs, including colourful carved and painted hens, remain the village's mascot and fill its craft shops and street art.

Beaches and activities

The main beach stretches for kilometres, protected by a line of reefs. Traditional flat sailing rafts called jangadas ferry visitors to the reef pools. Neighbouring beaches include Maracaípe, a surfing beach that hosts championship events and the Pontal de Maracaípe, where seahorses are observed in the mangrove estuary; Cupe; and Muro Alto, where a reef wall forms a giant calm lagoon fronting many resorts. Buggy tours famously run "from point to point" (ponta a ponta) along the whole beach strip. Environmental rules now limit visitor numbers in the most sensitive pools to protect the reef ecosystem.
