Pope Honorius III (c. 1150 – 18 March 1227), born Cencio Savelli, was head of the Catholic Church from 18 July 1216 until his death in 1227. He inherited from Innocent III the project of the Fifth Crusade and is remembered for confirming the Dominican and Franciscan orders and for compiling the Liber Censuum earlier in his career.

Early career

Cencio belonged to the Roman Savelli family. As chamberlain (camerarius) of the Roman Church from 1188, he compiled the Liber Censuum Romanæ Ecclesiæ (1192), a valuable register of the revenues and rights of the Holy See, which also preserved important historical documents. He was made cardinal by Celestine III and served Innocent III in financial administration.

Pontificate

Elected two days after Innocent's death, Honorius pursued the Fifth Crusade (1217–1221), which conquered Damietta in Egypt but ultimately failed when the crusaders refused Sultan al-Kamil's peace offers and were forced to surrender the city. He crowned Frederick II as Holy Roman Emperor in 1220 in exchange for Frederick's renewed promise to go on crusade — a promise repeatedly postponed during Honorius' lifetime.

Honorius gave formal papal confirmation to the Order of Preachers (Dominicans) in 1216 and to the Franciscan Rule in 1223 (Solet annuere), and he encouraged the preaching campaigns against heresy in Languedoc. He also authorised missions to the Baltic and supported the young mendicant orders and several universities, granting privileges to Paris and Bologna. He compiled the decretal collection known as the Compilatio Quinta.
