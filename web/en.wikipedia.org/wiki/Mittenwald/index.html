Mittenwald is a German municipality in the district of Garmisch-Partenkirchen, in Bavaria, situated in the upper Isar valley near the Austrian border, beneath the Karwendel mountains. Goethe described it as a "living picture book", and it is renowned for its painted house façades (Lüftlmalerei) and its centuries-old tradition of violin making.

Violin making

Violin making was established in Mittenwald by Matthias Klotz, who learned the craft in Italy and returned to his home town in the late 17th century. The Klotz family and other workshops made Mittenwald one of Germany's principal centres of stringed-instrument making alongside Markneukirchen. A violin-making school and a violin-making museum (Geigenbaumuseum) continue the tradition.

Geography and tourism

The town lies on the Mittenwald Railway between Garmisch-Partenkirchen and Innsbruck. Tourism centres on hiking, skiing on the Karwendel, and the nearby gorges and lakes such as the Lautersee and Ferchensee. The Bundeswehr's mountain infantry (Gebirgsjäger) have a presence in the area.
