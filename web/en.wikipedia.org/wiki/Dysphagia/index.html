Dysphagia

Dysphagia is difficulty in swallowing. Although classified under "symptoms and signs" in ICD-10, in some contexts it is classified as a condition in its own right. People with dysphagia may be unaware of having it, and in some cases untreated dysphagia leads to aspiration, dehydration, malnutrition and aspiration pneumonia.

Classification

Oropharyngeal dysphagia

Difficulty arises in moving food from the mouth into the oesophagus, often due to neurological conditions such as stroke, Parkinson's disease, multiple sclerosis or motor neurone disease. Symptoms include coughing or choking when eating, a wet or gurgly voice after swallowing, and nasal regurgitation.

Oesophageal dysphagia

Food feels as if it sticks in the chest. Causes include mechanical obstruction (strictures, rings such as Schatzki ring, tumours) and motility disorders such as achalasia and diffuse oesophageal spasm. Dysphagia for solids only suggests a structural narrowing, while difficulty with both solids and liquids suggests a motility problem.

Diagnosis and management

Assessment may involve a clinical swallow examination, videofluoroscopy (modified barium swallow), fibreoptic endoscopic evaluation of swallowing, oesophageal endoscopy and manometry. Management treats the underlying cause where possible and commonly includes speech and language therapy, dietary modification (altered food textures and thickened fluids), and postural techniques; severe cases may require tube feeding.
