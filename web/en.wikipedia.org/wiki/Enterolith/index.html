Enterolith

SpecialtyGastroenterology
SymptomsAbdominal pain, obstruction
CausesMineral concretion in the intestinal tract

An enterolith is a mineral concretion or calculus formed within the intestinal tract. Enteroliths occur in humans and in animals, most notably in horses, where they can grow large enough to cause colic and intestinal obstruction.

Formation

Enteroliths form around a nidus, often a small foreign object such as a piece of wire, a nail, or a stone swallowed by the animal. Layers of mineral salts, commonly magnesium ammonium phosphate (struvite), deposit around the nidus in concentric rings over months or years.

Clinical significance

In horses, enteroliths are particularly common in regions where animals consume alfalfa hay and water rich in magnesium. Small stones may pass without incident, but large ones can lodge in the colon and require surgical removal. In humans, enteroliths are rare and usually associated with intestinal stasis, diverticula, or strictures.
