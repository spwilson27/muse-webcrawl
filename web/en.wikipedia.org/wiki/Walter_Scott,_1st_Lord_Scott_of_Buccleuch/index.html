Walter Scott, 1st Lord Scott of Buccleuch

Born: 1565
Died: 15 December 1611 (aged about 46)
Known for: Border reiver; rescue of Kinmont Willie (1596)

Walter Scott, 1st Lord Scott of Buccleuch (1565 – 15 December 1611) was a Scottish nobleman and head of the Scott clan in the Scottish Borders, remembered as one of the most famous border reivers and for the daring rescue of the Armstrong laird Kinmont Willie from Carlisle Castle in 1596.

Life

Scott succeeded his father, Sir Walter Scott of Branxholme and Buccleuch, in 1574 while still a child. As chief of the Scotts — among the most powerful families of the Middle March — he was drawn into the clan feuds and cross-border raiding that characterised the Anglo-Scottish frontier, alternating between service to the Scottish crown as a keeper of Liddesdale and imprisonment and exile for his own raiding.

Rescue of Kinmont Willie

In March 1596, William Armstrong of Kinmont ("Kinmont Willie") was seized by English officers on a truce day and imprisoned in Carlisle Castle, contrary to border law. On the night of 13 April 1596, Buccleuch led a small party through a storm, scaled the castle walls, freed Armstrong, and carried him back across the flooded Eden. The exploit, condemned by Elizabeth I but admired even in England, was celebrated in the ballad "Kinmont Willie", later collected in Sir Walter Scott's Minstrelsy of the Scottish Border. Questioned by Elizabeth about his audacity, Buccleuch is said to have replied that there was nothing a brave man might not dare.

Later life

Scott served in the Netherlands under Maurice of Nassau, learning the military methods he used to police the Borders after the Union of the Crowns in 1603. He was raised to the peerage as Lord Scott of Buccleuch in 1606 and died in 1611. His son became the first Earl of Buccleuch; the line later produced the Dukes of Buccleuch, among Scotland's greatest landowners.
