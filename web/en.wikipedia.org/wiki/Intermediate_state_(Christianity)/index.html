Intermediate state (Christianity)

The intermediate state is the condition of the dead between death and the general resurrection.

In Christian theology, the intermediate state or interim state is a person's "intermediate" existence between one's death and the universal resurrection. It stands between the earthly life that has ended and the final state after the Last Judgment, when the dead are raised and the new creation is established.

Views by tradition

In Roman Catholic theology, the souls of the departed undergo a particular judgment at death. Those who die in God's grace but still imperfectly purified undergo purification in purgatory; the blessed enjoy the beatific vision, while the damned are in hell. Eastern Orthodox theology teaches that the soul experiences a foretaste of its final condition after death, often described through the imagery of Hades, while reserving final judgment for the Second Coming.

Most Protestant traditions teach that at death the soul goes immediately to be with Christ or to a state of separation from God, consciously awaiting the resurrection of the body. Some traditions, including Seventh-day Adventists and Christadelphians, teach "soul sleep" (psychopannychy) or mortalism — that the dead are unconscious until the resurrection.

Biblical basis and debate

Texts cited in discussion include the parable of the rich man and Lazarus (Luke 16), Christ's words to the penitent thief ("today you will be with me in Paradise", Luke 23:43), Paul's desire "to depart and be with Christ" (Philippians 1:23), and passages describing the dead as "asleep" (1 Thessalonians 4). Theologians debate how these texts fit together and whether the intermediate state is experienced temporally.
