History of Azerbaijan

The history of Azerbaijan covers the territory of the modern Republic of Azerbaijan, at the crossroads of Eastern Europe and Western Asia between the Caucasus and the Caspian Sea, a region successively shaped by Caucasian Albania, Persian and Arab rule, Turkic migration, and the Safavid, Russian, and Soviet empires.

Antiquity and middle ages

The region was home to the ancient kingdom of Caucasian Albania, Christianized in the 4th century, and to the fire temples of Zoroastrianism — the name Azerbaijan derives (by common etymology) from Atropates, a Persian satrap, or from words for "land of fire". Arab conquest in the 7th century brought Islam; from the 11th century Seljuk and later Turkic settlement gradually Turkified the population linguistically. The Shirvanshahs ruled the northeast for nearly a millennium, while the Safavid order arose at Ardabil and in the 16th century made Twelver Shi'a Islam the state religion of Persia.

Russian and Soviet periods

The khanates of the region were ceded by Persia to the Russian Empire under the treaties of Gulistan (1813) and Turkmenchay (1828). Baku's oil boom made it one of the world's first petroleum centers. A short-lived Azerbaijan Democratic Republic (1918–1920) — the first secular democratic republic in the Muslim-majority world — was absorbed into the Soviet Union, within which Azerbaijan became a union republic in 1936.

Independence

Azerbaijan restored independence in 1991 amid war with Armenia over Nagorno-Karabakh, which left large territories under Armenian control after the 1994 ceasefire. Heydar Aliyev (president 1993–2003) stabilized the state and signed major Caspian oil contracts; his son Ilham Aliyev succeeded him. In the 2020 Second Nagorno-Karabakh War Azerbaijan recovered much of the disputed territory, and in 2023 regained full control of Nagorno-Karabakh, prompting the exodus of its ethnic Armenian population.
