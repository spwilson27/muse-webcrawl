Disorientation

Disorientation is an altered mental state in which a person's sense of direction, time, place, or identity is impaired. It is a common feature of delirium and various neurological and psychiatric conditions, and may also occur transiently in healthy individuals, for example during spatial disorientation in aviation.

Types

Disorientation is commonly assessed along several axes: orientation to time (date, day, season), place (where one is), person (one's own identity and recognition of others), and situation. Clinicians often record whether a patient is "alert and oriented × 3" or × 4.

Causes

Causes include head injury, stroke, dementia, infections, metabolic disturbances, intoxication or withdrawal, seizures, and severe stress or sleep deprivation. Sudden disorientation is a medical warning sign warranting evaluation. In aviation, spatial disorientation — a pilot's inability to correctly perceive the aircraft's attitude — is a significant cause of accidents and is trained against using instrument flying techniques.
