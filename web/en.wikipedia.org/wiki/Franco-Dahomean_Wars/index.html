Franco-Dahomean Wars

The Franco-Dahomean Wars were two conflicts, in 1890 and in 1892–1894, between France and the Kingdom of Dahomey in West Africa (modern Benin). They ended with the French conquest of Dahomey, the exile of King Béhanzin, and the incorporation of the kingdom into French West Africa.

First war (1890)

Fighting broke out over French claims to a protectorate over Porto-Novo and the coastal port of Cotonou, which Dahomey also claimed. Dahomean forces under King Béhanzin attacked French positions but were repelled at the Battle of Atchoukpa. A treaty in October 1890 recognised the French protectorate over Porto-Novo and ceded Cotonou in return for an annual payment.

Second war (1892–1894)

After Dahomean raids into the Ouémé valley, France sent a larger expedition under Colonel Alfred-Amédée Dodds, including Senegalese and Algerian troops. The French fought their way up the Ouémé in a series of hard battles — at Dogba, Poguessa and Adegon — against a Dahomean army famous for its Agojie, the all-female regiments Europeans called "Amazons". The capital, Abomey, was occupied in November 1892 after Béhanzin burned and abandoned it. Béhanzin surrendered in January 1894 and was exiled first to Martinique and later to Algeria, where he died in 1906.

Aftermath

Dahomey became a French colony, initially ruled through the puppet king Agoli-agbo, Béhanzin's brother, and was later folded into the federation of French West Africa. The wars and the Agojie have become central to Beninese national memory.
