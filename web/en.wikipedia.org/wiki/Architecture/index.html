Architecture

[Image: a modern museum building of sweeping titanium curves — the Guggenheim Museum Bilbao, described from memory]

Architecture is the art and technique of designing and building buildings and other structures. It combines function, technology and aesthetics: a work of architecture must be useful, durable and pleasing — the three qualities the Roman writer Vitruvius called firmitas, utilitas and venustas. The word also describes the style of buildings of a period or place, as in 'Gothic architecture'.

Architects plan everything from houses and schools to skyscrapers, museums, airports and whole districts, working with engineers, builders and clients, and their work is regulated for safety, planning and heritage reasons almost everywhere.

History

Architecture begins with prehistoric shelters and the first monumental buildings of the ancient world: the pyramids of Egypt, the temples and cities of Mesopotamia, Greece and Rome, whose orders, arches and concrete transformed construction. Medieval builders raised Romanesque and then Gothic cathedrals of extraordinary height and light; Islamic architecture developed distinctive domes, minarets and geometric ornament.

The Renaissance revived classical principles and perspective; the Baroque added drama and movement. Industrialisation brought iron, steel and glass — railway stations, exhibition halls such as the Crystal Palace (1851) and the first skyscrapers — and reinforced concrete enabled the modern movement of the twentieth century (Bauhaus, Le Corbusier, Frank Lloyd Wright), with its emphasis on function, open plans and new materials.

Contemporary practice

Recent architecture encompasses postmodernism's playful historicism, high-tech and deconstructivist designs, and a growing emphasis on sustainability: energy efficiency, low-carbon materials and adaptation to climate change. Parametric, computer-aided design has enabled complex curved forms impractical to draw by hand.

Major international recognition includes the Pritzker Architecture Prize, often described as architecture's Nobel Prize, first awarded in 1979.
