Bachelor's degree

A bachelor's degree (from Medieval Latin baccalaureus) is an undergraduate academic degree awarded by colleges and universities upon completion of a course of study lasting typically three to six years, depending on the institution and discipline. The most common types are the Bachelor of Arts (BA) and Bachelor of Science (BS or BSc).

History

The bachelor's degree originated in medieval European universities, where the baccalaureate was the first degree below the master's and doctorate. The bachelor was originally a junior member of a guild of teachers; the modern undergraduate degree developed as universities organized liberal arts instruction into a preliminary course of study.

Variations by country

In the United States and Canada a bachelor's degree usually requires four years of full-time study, including general education and a major. In England, Wales, and much of the Commonwealth, honours degrees take three years (four in Scotland, where the ordinary degree structure differs). Under the Bologna Process, European bachelor's degrees typically represent 180 ECTS credits over three years. Professional degrees such as medicine (MBBS) and law vary in level by country.

Honours and classification

Many systems award classified honours — e.g., first-class, upper second-class (2:1), lower second-class (2:2), and third-class in the United Kingdom — while others use grade point averages or Latin honours such as cum laude.
