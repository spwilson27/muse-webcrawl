Newton's law of universal gravitation

FieldClassical mechanics, gravitation
Proposed byIsaac Newton
Published1687, Principia Mathematica
StatementEvery mass attracts every other mass with a force proportional to the product of the masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance

Newton's law of universal gravitation states that every particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a force that is proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres. Isaac Newton published the law in 1687 in his Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica, unifying the motion of falling bodies on Earth with the motion of the Moon and planets under a single principle.

Formulation

In modern notation the magnitude of the attractive force between two masses m₁ and m₂ separated by distance r is F = G m₁m₂ / r², where G is the gravitational constant, first measured by Henry Cavendish in 1797–1798. The force acts along the line joining the two bodies and is mutual: each body attracts the other equally.

From the law Newton derived Kepler's laws of planetary motion and explained the tides, the precession of the equinoxes and the trajectories of comets.

Domain of validity

Newtonian gravity dominated physics until superseded in precision regimes by Albert Einstein's general theory of relativity, published in 1915, which explained anomalies such as the perihelion precession of Mercury. Newton's law remains an excellent approximation for most everyday and engineering purposes, including the calculation of satellite orbits and interplanetary trajectories.

In vector form the law generalises to systems of many bodies by superposition, although the resulting gravitational three-body problem has no general closed-form solution.
