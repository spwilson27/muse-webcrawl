Yoga Sutras of Patanjali

The Yoga Sūtras of Patañjali are a collection of 196 Sanskrit sutras (aphorisms) on the theory and practice of yoga, attributed to the sage Patañjali and compiled between the 2nd century BCE and the 4th century CE. With the commentary (Bhāṣya) attributed to Vyāsa, the text became the foundational scripture of Rāja yoga and one of the most translated Indian spiritual classics.

Content

The work is divided into four chapters (pāda): Samādhi (contemplation), Sādhana (practice), Vibhūti (powers/accomplishments) and Kaivalya (liberation). Its famous definition opens the text: "Yoga is the stilling of the fluctuations of the mind" (yogaś citta-vṛtti-nirodhaḥ).

The Sādhana chapter sets out the eight limbs (aṣṭāṅga) of yoga: yama (ethical restraints), niyama (observances), āsana (posture), prāṇāyāma (breath control), pratyāhāra (sense withdrawal), dhāraṇā (concentration), dhyāna (meditation) and samādhi (absorption).

Influence

After centuries of relative obscurity, the text was revived in the 19th and 20th centuries and now underpins much modern yoga philosophy; Swami Vivekananda's Raja Yoga (1896) helped popularise it in the West.
