Newfoundland (island)

Newfoundland is a large island in the North Atlantic Ocean, off the east coast of Canada. With an area of about 108,860 square kilometres, it is the world's 16th-largest island and the most populous part of the Canadian province of Newfoundland and Labrador. The capital, St. John's, is on the Avalon Peninsula in the southeast.

Geography

The island has a rugged, deeply indented coastline, a cool maritime climate, and terrain ranging from coastal barrens to the Long Range Mountains in the west. Gros Morne National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, exposes ancient oceanic crust and mantle rock.

History

Indigenous peoples, including ancestors of the Beothuk, lived on the island for millennia. Norse settlers built a short-lived settlement at L'Anse aux Meadows around 1000 CE — the only confirmed Norse site in North America. John Cabot reached the island in 1497, and its rich cod fisheries drew European migratory fishermen, then English and French settlers. Newfoundland was a British colony and later a self-governing dominion before joining Canada in 1949.

Economy and culture

Fishing, especially cod, shaped settlement in hundreds of outports; the 1992 cod moratorium devastated the fishery. Offshore oil, tourism, and aquaculture are now important. The island has a distinctive dialect, folk music tradition, and strong regional identity.
