Fish therapy

Species usedGarra rufa ("doctor fish")
OriginKangal, Turkey

Fish therapy, also called ichthyotherapy, is the use of live fish — most commonly the cyprinid Garra rufa, known as "doctor fish" — to nibble away dead or diseased skin. It is practised both as a spa pedicure treatment and as an alternative therapy for skin conditions such as psoriasis.

Practice

The best-known source is the hot springs at Kangal in Turkey, where patients with psoriasis bathe in pools populated by the fish. From the mid-2000s, "fish spas" offering cosmetic pedicures spread across Asia, Europe and North America: customers immerse their feet in tanks while the fish remove dead skin.

Health concerns

Health authorities have raised concerns that water and fish shared between customers can transmit bacterial and viral infections, and that the treatment poses risks to people with weakened immune systems or broken skin. Several jurisdictions have banned or restricted fish pedicures on hygiene or animal-welfare grounds. Claims of lasting therapeutic benefit for psoriasis remain supported mainly by small studies and anecdotal reports.
