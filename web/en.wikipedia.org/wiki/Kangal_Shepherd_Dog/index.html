Kangal Shepherd Dog

The Kangal Shepherd Dog (Turkish: Kangal Çoban Köpeği) is a large livestock guardian dog breed from the Sivas Province of Turkey, named after the town and district of Kangal. Bred for centuries to protect sheep flocks from wolves, bears, and jackals on the Anatolian plateau, it is noted for its size, speed, courage, and measured temperament. It is recognised internationally by the Fédération Cynologique Internationale and is regarded as a national treasure of Turkey.

Characteristics

Males typically stand 77–86 cm at the withers and weigh 50–66 kg; females are somewhat smaller. The coat is short and dense, usually pale fawn to grey, always with a black mask and black ears. The Kangal is reputed to have one of the most powerful bites of any domestic dog and can reach speeds around 50 km/h over rough ground when confronting predators. Unlike herding breeds, the Kangal works by patrolling and confronting threats rather than gathering livestock, and typically works in pairs or trios with the flock.

History and use abroad

The breed developed in relative isolation around Kangal, where shepherds historically fitted dogs with spiked iron collars (tokalı) to protect their necks in fights with wolves. Exported dogs have become popular livestock guardians internationally: in Namibia, Kangals supplied by the Cheetah Conservation Fund guard herds and reduce cheetah predation without killing the cats. In their homeland, government breeding centres in Sivas maintain the breed, and export of pure Kangals from Turkey is restricted.

Temperament

Kangals are calm, independent, and deeply attached to their flock or family. They are wary of strangers and other dogs but should be steady and non-aggressive when not provoked; their size and guarding instinct demand experienced handling and secure fencing.
