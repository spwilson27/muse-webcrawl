Somali diaspora


The Somali diaspora comprises Somali communities outside the Horn of Africa, numbering in the millions.

The Somali diaspora refers to communities of ethnic Somalis living outside their traditional homeland in the Horn of Africa. Migration accelerated dramatically with the Somali Civil War beginning in 1991, which displaced hundreds of thousands of people.

Communities

Large communities exist in Kenya, Yemen, and Ethiopia regionally, and further afield in the United States (notably Minnesota), Canada, the United Kingdom (London), the Netherlands, Scandinavia, and the Gulf states. Somali seamen had formed smaller communities in British port cities such as Cardiff and Liverpool from the late 19th century.

Remittances and culture

Remittances from the diaspora, sent largely through hawala networks, are a lifeline for Somalia's economy, estimated in billions of dollars annually. Diaspora Somalis have also been prominent in politics, business, sport, and literature in their host countries, and many maintain close ties with a view to return or investment.
