Limaçon

In geometry, a limaçon or limacon (from the Latin limax, "snail") is a plane curve defined as a roulette formed by the path of a point fixed to a circle when that circle rolls around the outside of a circle of equal radius. In polar coordinates it has the equation r = b + a cos θ.

Forms

Depending on the ratio of a to b, the limaçon takes different forms. When b ≥ 2a the curve is convex; when b = a it is a cardioid; when a > b it develops an inner loop; and in an intermediate range it is dimpled. The curve can also be defined as a conchoid of a circle, or as a pedal curve.

History

Albrecht Dürer described a limaçon-like construction in his Underweysung der Messung (1525), and the curve was investigated by Étienne Pascal, father of Blaise Pascal, after whom the curve is sometimes called the limaçon of Pascal.
