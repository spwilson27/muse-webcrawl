Glossary of vexillology


A selection of flag designs illustrating vexillological terminology.

Vexillology is the study of flags, and the discipline has developed a specialised vocabulary for describing the parts of a flag, its patterns, shapes and the techniques used in its construction. This glossary lists the principal terms used by vexillologists.

Parts of a flag

The hoist is the edge of a flag that is attached to the flagpole, while the opposite edge is the fly. The field (or ground) is the background colour or pattern of the flag. The upper hoist-side corner is the canton; on the flag of the United States, for example, the blue rectangle bearing the stars is the canton. The length is the horizontal measurement from hoist to fly and the width (or breadth) the vertical measurement; their relationship is expressed as a ratio or proportion, such as 3:5 or 2:3.

Common charges and patterns

A charge is any emblem or device borne on the field. A horizontal band across the middle of a flag is a fess; a vertical band is a pale. A diagonal band is a bend, running from the upper hoist to the lower fly, or a bend sinister running in the opposite direction. A cross whose arms reach the edges is a Greek cross when its arms are equal and its centre is at the flag's centre, while an upright cross offset toward the hoist is a Nordic cross, characteristic of Scandinavian flags. A saltire is a diagonal cross. A pile is a triangular charge issuing from the hoist. Flags divided into three vertical bands are tribands; those with three horizontal bands are horizontal tribands, and a flag with three bands of two colours is a triband in either orientation. A flag divided quarterly is quartered; a diamond-shaped division is a lozenge.

Construction terms

The header is the strip of heavy cloth along the hoist through which the halyard runs, and grommets are the metal eyelets used to attach the flag. A flag that is a mirror image on both sides is said to be the same on the obverse and reverse; where the design shows through from the other side in reverse, it is described as "show-through". A swallowtail is a V-shaped cut at the fly end, and a flag ending in two points is a burgee when triangular. The small flag flown at the bow of a vessel is a jack; a national flag flown by ships is an ensign, and a flag flown by a merchant vessel a civil ensign, contrasted with a state or war ensign flown by government and naval vessels.

Design assessment

Vexillologists sometimes classify designs by heraldic principles adapted from heraldry. Whitney Smith, who coined the term vexillology in 1957, founded the International Federation of Vexillological Associations (FIAV), whose identification symbols indicate a flag's function — civil, state, war, on land or at sea — and its status. The North American Vexillological Association's 2001 guidelines recommend simplicity, meaningful symbolism, a limited palette of two or three basic colours, no lettering or seals, and distinctiveness.
