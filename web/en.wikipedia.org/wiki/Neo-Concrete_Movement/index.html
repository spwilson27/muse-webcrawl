Neo-Concrete Movement

The Neo-Concrete Movement (Movimento Neoconcreto) was a Brazilian art movement founded in Rio de Janeiro in 1959 by artists and poets including Lygia Clark, Hélio Oiticica, Lygia Pape, Ferreira Gullar, Amilcar de Castro, Franz Weissmann and Reynaldo Jardim. It emerged from the Grupo Frente and positioned itself against the rigid rationalism of the São Paulo-based Concrete art (Grupo Ruptura).

Manifesto

The Neo-Concrete Manifesto, written principally by Ferreira Gullar and published in the Jornal do Brasil in March 1959, argued that the artwork was not a mere object or machine but a living "quasi-body" that demanded spectator participation and embodied expression.

Legacy

Neo-Concretism emphasised intuition, phenomenology and the viewer's bodily engagement, laying foundations for participatory and installation art in Brazil and influencing movements such as Tropicália.
