Supporters' trust

A supporters' trust is a formal, democratic, non-profit organisation of sports fans — most commonly in British football — established to promote supporter influence in the running of their club and to safeguard the club's long-term future. Trusts are usually constituted as community benefit societies (a form of co-operative) under the principle of one member, one vote, and more than 100 have been formed in the United Kingdom.

Origins and development

The movement grew from supporter campaigns of the 1990s, such as the trust formed at Northampton Town in 1992 — generally regarded as the first — when fans organised to rescue and gain representation at clubs in financial crisis. In 2000 the UK government backed the creation of Supporters Direct to help fans establish trusts and acquire shares and board representation. Trusts proliferated following high-profile club collapses and controversial takeovers, and Supporters Direct later merged into the Football Supporters' Association.

Objectives and activities

Trusts typically seek to:

  • buy shares in their club, sometimes becoming majority or outright owners;
  • secure elected supporter representation on the club board;
  • raise funds to rescue clubs in administration or to found "phoenix" clubs;
  • campaign on ticket prices, stadium issues, and the preservation of club heritage.

Fan-owned clubs

Several trusts have taken full ownership of their clubs, with notable examples including Exeter City (owned by its supporters' trust since 2003), Newport County, and fan-founded phoenix clubs such as AFC Wimbledon, established by supporters after Wimbledon's relocation to Milton Keynes, and FC United of Manchester. Supporter ownership is far more extensive in Germany under the 50+1 rule, though usually through member associations rather than trusts.
