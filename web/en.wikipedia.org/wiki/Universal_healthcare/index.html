Universal healthcare

Universal healthcare (also called universal health coverage) is a healthcare system in which all residents of a particular country or region are assured access to healthcare. It is generally organized around providing either all residents or only those unable to afford healthcare on their own, with health services funded largely out of general government revenue or through compulsory health insurance contributions.

Models

Universal systems take several forms. In the Beveridge model, exemplified by the United Kingdom's National Health Service, healthcare is provided and financed by the government through taxes. In the Bismarck model, used in Germany and elsewhere, care is delivered by private providers but paid for by non-profit insurance funds financed by employer and employee contributions. Other systems use a single-payer insurance model, as in Canada, where private providers bill a public insurer. The World Health Organization treats universal health coverage as a key goal, and it is part of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals.

History

Germany under Otto von Bismarck established the first national compulsory health insurance system in 1883. After the Second World War, universal systems spread widely in Europe and beyond; by the early twenty-first century nearly all high-income countries except the United States had some form of universal coverage.
