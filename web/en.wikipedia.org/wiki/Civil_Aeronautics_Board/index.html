Civil Aeronautics Board

Civil Aeronautics Board (CAB)
Agency: United States federal government
Established: 1938 (Civil Aeronautics Act; operating as CAB from 1940)
Abolished: 1985 (sunset under deregulation)

The Civil Aeronautics Board (CAB) was an agency of the United States federal government that regulated commercial aviation: it awarded routes, controlled fares, and approved mergers for US airlines from 1938 until deregulation.

Regulation era

Created by the Civil Aeronautics Act of 1938, the CAB tightly controlled entry — no new trunk carriers were certificated for decades — and set prices, on the theory that aviation was a public utility requiring stability. Safety regulation passed to the Federal Aviation Agency (later FAA) in 1958, and accident investigation to the NTSB in 1967.

Deregulation

Under chairman Alfred Kahn, the CAB itself promoted liberalisation, and the Airline Deregulation Act of 1978 phased out its economic controls. The CAB was abolished on 1 January 1985, residual functions passing to the Department of Transportation.
