Gorkha Janmukti Morcha

Abbreviation: GJM
Founded: 7 October 2007
Founder: Bimal Gurung
Headquarters: Darjeeling, West Bengal
Ideology: Gorkhaland statehood
Colours: Green, yellow and white

The Gorkha Janmukti Morcha (GJM; "Gorkha People's Liberation Front") is a political party in the Darjeeling hills of West Bengal, India. Founded on 7 October 2007 by Bimal Gurung, a former leader of the Gorkha National Liberation Front (GNLF), the party campaigns for the creation of a separate state of Gorkhaland for the Nepali-speaking Gorkha people of the Darjeeling, Kalimpong, Kurseong and Siliguri region, to be carved out of West Bengal.

History

The Gorkhaland movement had previously been led by Subhash Ghisingh's GNLF, whose violent agitation in the 1980s led to the creation of the Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council (DGHC) in 1988. Gurung split from the GNLF after a falling-out with Ghisingh, and the GJM rapidly became the dominant force in the hills, demanding a separate state and opposing a proposed Sixth Schedule tribal status for the DGHC.

GJM-backed agitations, strikes and blockades roiled the hills between 2008 and 2011. Tripartite talks between the party, the West Bengal government and the Government of India produced the Gorkhaland Territorial Administration (GTA) agreement of 18 July 2011, and the GJM swept the GTA elections in 2012, with Gurung becoming its chief executive.

Later developments

The demand for full statehood resurfaced repeatedly, most dramatically in 2017, when the West Bengal government's decision to make Bengali language teaching compulsory in schools triggered a 104-day general strike and violent unrest in the hills. Gurung went into hiding facing police cases, and the party split into factions, one led by Binay Tamang, which cooperated with the state government, and one led by Gurung. The factions have since contended for leadership of the Gorkhaland movement.
