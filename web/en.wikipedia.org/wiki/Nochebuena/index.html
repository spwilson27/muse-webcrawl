Nochebuena (Spanish for "the Good Night") is an annual festival observed on the night of 24 December, Christmas Eve. The Spanish Spanish word "Nochebuena" refers to the night of Christmas Eve, which is celebrated in Spain, Latin America, and the Philippines. It is traditionally a time for families to gather for a festive meal and, in many countries, to attend Midnight Mass (Misa de Gallo, literally "Rooster's Mass").

Observance by region

Spain

In Spain, Nochebuena is centred on a large family dinner, typically featuring seafood, roast lamb or suckling pig, and sweets such as turrón (nougat) and marzipan. Although Santa Claus (Papá Noel) has become popular, gifts are traditionally exchanged on Epiphany (6 January), brought by the Three Wise Men.

Latin America

In Mexico, families gather for posadas and a late-night meal that may include tamales, bacalao (salt cod), romeritos and ponche. In Cuba and among Cuban Americans, the centrepiece is lechón asado (roast pork), served with black beans, rice, yuca and mojo sauce. In Colombia, natilla and buñuelos are traditional, while in Venezuela hallacas and pan de jamón are prepared. In Peru and other Andean countries, panettone and hot chocolate accompany the meal.

Philippines

In the Philippines, Noche Buena is the midnight feast following the Misa de Gallo. Typical dishes include lechon, queso de bola (Edam cheese), hamon, pancit, bibingka and tsokolate. The feast is a major family reunion occasion, with many Filipinos travelling home for the holiday.
