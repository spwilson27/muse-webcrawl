Sermon

A minister preaching from a raised pulpit.

A sermon is a religious discourse or oration by a preacher, usually a member of clergy. Sermons are often delivered during worship services and address a scriptural, theological, or moral topic, usually expounding a type of belief, law, or behaviour within both past and present contexts. In Christian practice the sermon, or homily, typically follows a reading of scripture and is regarded as a central act of worship in many Protestant traditions.

History

The sermon has antecedents in the synagogue exposition of the Torah and in the preaching of Jesus, whose most famous discourse is the Sermon on the Mount. The early Church Fathers, such as John Chrysostom (whose epithet means "golden-mouthed"), made preaching a major literary genre. In the medieval church, mendicant friars carried sermons to lay audiences, while the Protestant Reformation elevated preaching to the centre of worship; Martin Luther and John Calvin both emphasised expository preaching of the Bible.

Types and structure

Sermons may be expository (working through a text), topical (addressing a subject), or textual (built on a single verse). A traditional structure comprises an introduction, an exegesis of the text, application to the hearers, and a conclusion or exhortation. In Islam, the Friday sermon is called a khutbah; in Judaism, a derasha.
