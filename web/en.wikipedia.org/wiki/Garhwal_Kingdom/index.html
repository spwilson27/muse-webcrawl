Garhwal Kingdom

The Garhwal Kingdom was a Himalayan kingdom in the Garhwal region of present-day Uttarakhand, India. Ruled by the Panwar (Parmar) dynasty from its capital at Srinagar, it unified dozens of small hill principalities (garhs) from the 14th century onward and endured, through a devastating Gorkha occupation and a period as the British-protected princely state of Tehri Garhwal, until its merger into India in 1949.

History

Tradition credits Kanak Pal with founding the dynasty around the 9th century, but it was Ajay Pal (c. 1358–1370) who consolidated the numerous garhs into a single kingdom and moved the capital to Srinagar on the Alaknanda River. The kingdom reached its height under rulers such as Man Shah and the regent Karnavati, who repelled a Mughal invasion in 1635. In 1803–1804 the Gorkhas of Nepal conquered Garhwal after an earthquake weakened the state, and King Pradyumna Shah died fighting at Dehradun. Following the Anglo-Nepalese War, the Treaty of Sugauli (1815) split Garhwal: the eastern part became British Garhwal, while Sudarshan Shah was restored in the west as Raja of Tehri.

Legacy

The Tehri state acceded to India in 1949. Today the old kingdom's territory forms the Garhwal division of Uttarakhand, a region of major Hindu pilgrimage sites including Badrinath and Kedarnath.
