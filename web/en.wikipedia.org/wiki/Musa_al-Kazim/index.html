Musa al-Kazim

Musa ibn Ja'far al-Kazim (Arabic: موسى بن جعفر الكاظم; c. 745 – 799 CE), also spelled Musa al-Kadhim, was a descendant of the Islamic prophet Muhammad and the seventh Imam in Twelver Shia Islam. His epithet al-Kazim means "the forbearing" or "the one who restrains his anger".

Born in Abwa near Medina, he succeeded his father Ja'far al-Sadiq as imam in 765. His imamate coincided with the rule of the Abbasid caliphs al-Mansur, al-Mahdi, al-Hadi and Harun al-Rashid, under whom he was repeatedly imprisoned; he died in prison in Baghdad in 799, and Twelver tradition holds he was poisoned on the caliph's orders. He is buried in the al-Kazimayn shrine in the Kadhimiya district of Baghdad alongside his grandson, the ninth Imam Muhammad al-Jawad. He is also recognised as a transmitter of hadith in Sunni sources and as a link in the golden chain of many Sufi orders.
