The Egg is named for its distinctive sculptural shape.

The Egg is a performing arts venue located in Albany, New York, at the Empire State Plaza, the seat of New York state government. Named for its shape, the building was designed as part of the plaza complex conceived under Governor Nelson A. Rockefeller and constructed between 1966 and 1978. It was formally dedicated in 1978.

Architecture

The Egg appears as a giant egg-shaped sculpture resting on a stem; the visible form is a tilted, truncated cone sheathed in steel and concrete, supported by a central stem that descends six stories into the plaza. Inside are two theatres: the Lewis A. Swyer Theatre, seating about 450, and the Kitty Carlisle Hart Theatre, seating about 982, named for the actress who championed arts funding in New York. Despite its rounded exterior, the interior spaces are conventional theatre halls.

Programming

The Egg presents dance, theatre, music, comedy, and family programming, and hosts events such as the New York State Summer School of the Arts. It is operated under the auspices of the state Office of General Services and a presenting organization. Its silhouette has become an icon of Albany.
