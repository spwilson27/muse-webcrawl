Crowthorne is a town and civil parish in the Bracknell Forest district of Berkshire, England. It lies in the south-east of the county, near the borders with Surrey and Hampshire, and had a population of around 14,000 at the 2021 census.

History

Crowthorne was a small hamlet within the Royal Forest of Windsor until the nineteenth century. Its growth followed two institutions founded in the area: Wellington College, opened in 1859 as a memorial to the Duke of Wellington, and Broadmoor Hospital, the high-security psychiatric hospital, which opened in 1863 on the outskirts of the village. The arrival of the railway in 1860, with a station serving Wellington College, spurred further residential development, and Crowthorne expanded substantially during the twentieth century as a commuter settlement.

Geography and amenities

The town is surrounded by woodland, including parts of Swinley Forest, and has a high street with shops, churches, and a library. Crowthorne railway station provides services on the North Downs Line between Reading and Redhill/Gatwick Airport. Local state schools include Edgbarrow School, a secondary academy.

Notable institutions

  • Wellington College — independent boarding and day school, founded 1859.
  • Broadmoor Hospital — high-security psychiatric hospital located in the parish, opened 1863.
