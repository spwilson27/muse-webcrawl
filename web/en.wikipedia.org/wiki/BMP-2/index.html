The BMP-2 has been one of the most widely used infantry fighting vehicles in the world since 1980.

The BMP-2 (Boyevaya Mashina Pekhoty, "infantry combat vehicle") is a second-generation, amphibious infantry fighting vehicle introduced by the Soviet Union in 1980, following the BMP-1 of the 1960s. Tens of thousands were built, and the type remains in service with Russia, Ukraine, India (as the licence-built Sarath), and dozens of other armies, making it one of the most widely used IFVs ever produced.

Development and design

Lessons from the BMP-1's 73 mm gun — inaccurate beyond short range and ineffective against helicopters and troops in elevated positions — led to a redesigned two-man turret armed with the high-velocity 30 mm 2A42 autocannon, capable of high elevation for engaging aircraft and targets on high ground, plus a pintle/launcher for 9M111 Fagot or 9M113 Konkurs anti-tank missiles. The troop compartment was rearranged to seat seven infantry, who can fire their weapons through firing ports; the commander moved into the turret. Armour remains light — steel up to about 33 mm — proof against heavy machine guns over the frontal arc, and the vehicle is fully amphibious, swimming at about 7 km/h using its tracks. The UTD-20 diesel of 300 hp gives a road speed of about 65 km/h.

Service

The BMP-2 first saw combat in Afghanistan, where its cannon's elevation and rate of fire suited mountain warfare. It has since fought in the Iran–Iraq War, the Gulf War, Chechnya, the Yugoslav wars, the Russo-Georgian War, the Syrian Civil War, and on both sides in the Russo-Ukrainian War, where it has been one of the most numerous armoured vehicles employed and lost. Numerous upgrades have been offered, with improved sights, add-on armour and new turrets; Czechoslovakia and India produced licensed versions (BVP-2 and Sarath respectively).
