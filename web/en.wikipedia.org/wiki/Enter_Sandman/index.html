Enter Sandman

"Enter Sandman"
Artist: Metallica
Album: Metallica (The Black Album)
Released: 29 July 1991 (single)
Genre: Heavy metal
Length: 5:31
Writers: James Hetfield, Lars Ulrich, Kirk Hammett

"Enter Sandman" is a song by American heavy metal band Metallica. It was released as the first single from their self-titled fifth album (commonly called The Black Album) in 1991, and is the band's best-known and most commercially successful song, helping the album sell tens of millions of copies worldwide.

Composition

The song grew from a riff by guitarist Kirk Hammett, developed by James Hetfield and Lars Ulrich into a mid-tempo, groove-oriented track notably simpler and more radio-friendly than the band's earlier thrash metal. Its lyrics, sung from the perspective of the Sandman of folklore, address a child's bedtime fears and nightmares; Hetfield's original draft, touching on crib death, was softened at producer Bob Rock's suggestion. A spoken prayer section ("Now I lay me down to sleep") features a child's voice answered by Hetfield's menacing rejoinder.

Reception and legacy

The single reached the top 20 in the United States and several other countries and was certified platinum. It has appeared on lists of the greatest metal songs and guitar riffs, is a staple of sports arenas — long used as entrance music by baseball closer Mariano Rivera and by the Virginia Tech Hokies football team — and was among the songs reportedly used by US interrogators at Guantanamo Bay, to the band's public discomfort. Metallica has played it at nearly every concert since its release.

Music video

The Wayne Isham-directed video, depicting a child's nightmares intercut with the band performing, won Best Hard Rock Video at the 1992 MTV Video Music Awards.
