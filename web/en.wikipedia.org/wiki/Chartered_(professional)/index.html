Chartered (professional)

Chartered status
OriginUnited Kingdom and Commonwealth countries
Granted byProfessional bodies incorporated by royal charter, under Privy Council oversight
Typical fieldsEngineering, accountancy, surveying, chemistry, physics, architecture, management
ExamplesChartered Engineer (CEng), Chartered Accountant (ACA/FCA), Chartered Surveyor (MRICS/FRICS)

Chartered is a term used to describe a professional designation or status awarded to individuals — such as Chartered Engineer, Chartered Accountant, or Chartered Surveyor — by a professional body that has itself been incorporated by royal charter. The system originated in the United Kingdom and is found throughout the Commonwealth. Chartered status generally denotes that the holder has satisfied a chartered body as to academic qualifications, professional competence, experience, and adherence to a code of conduct, and it is widely regarded as a gold standard of professional standing.

How chartered status works

In the United Kingdom, professional institutions petition the Privy Council for a royal charter; the charter empowers the institution to award chartered designations to its members. Because the title "chartered" in combination with a profession is protected, non-members may not lawfully use it. Applicants typically need an accredited degree, a period of supervised professional development, a competence review or interview, and a commitment to continuing professional development and ethical standards. Members use post-nominal letters such as CEng, CPhys, or MRICS.

Examples by profession

Chartered Accountants are members of bodies such as ICAEW, ICAS, and Chartered Accountants Ireland (and their international equivalents). Chartered Engineers (CEng) are registered through the Engineering Council by licensed institutions such as the IET, IMechE, and ICE. Chartered Surveyors belong to the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors. Other designations include Chartered Chemist (Royal Society of Chemistry), Chartered Physicist (Institute of Physics), Chartered Psychologist (British Psychological Society), Chartered Banker, and Chartered Manager.

International parallels

Other countries use different titles for comparable standing — "Professional Engineer" (PE) in the United States, "Chartered Professional Engineer" (CPEng) in Australia, or the European Engineer (EUR ING) designation — but the chartered model remains distinctively tied to royal charters and the Commonwealth tradition.
