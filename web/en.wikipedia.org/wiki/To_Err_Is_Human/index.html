To Err Is Human


The landmark 1999 patient safety report

To Err Is Human: Building a Safer Health System is a landmark report issued in November 1999 by the U.S. Institute of Medicine (now the National Academy of Medicine). Drawing on studies of hospital care in Colorado and Utah and in New York, the report estimated that between 44,000 and 98,000 people die in U.S. hospitals each year as a result of preventable medical errors — more than die from motor vehicle accidents or breast cancer.

Findings and recommendations

The report argued that most errors arise not from reckless individuals but from faulty systems, processes, and conditions that lead people to make mistakes or fail to prevent them. It called for a nationwide, mandatory reporting system for adverse events, voluntary reporting for near misses, safety standards set by regulators and accreditors, and the creation of a culture of safety within healthcare organizations, with the goal of reducing deaths from medical errors by half within five years.

Impact

To Err Is Human focused public and professional attention on patient safety and is credited with launching the modern patient safety movement. It led to new research funding, safety reporting systems, and initiatives such as the follow-up report Crossing the Quality Chasm (2001). The phrase itself — from the saying "to err is human, to forgive divine", popularized by Alexander Pope — has become shorthand for the report and its message.
