Therblig

A therblig is one of a set of fundamental motions used in motion study to analyse the movements of a worker performing a manual task. The system was developed by Frank Bunker Gilbreth and Lillian Moller Gilbreth in the early twentieth century; the name is "Gilbreth" spelled nearly backwards. Therbligs provided the vocabulary of the Gilbreths' motion-study method, complementing Frederick Winslow Taylor's time study.

The therbligs

The Gilbreths ultimately defined 18 elementary motions, each with a name, symbol and standard colour, used to chart a job in simo (simultaneous motion) charts. They include:

Purpose

By breaking a job into therbligs, analysts could identify and eliminate ineffective motions — searches, avoidable delays, awkward transports — and redesign the workplace around the productive ones. The system influenced later predetermined motion-time systems such as MTM and remains a foundation of industrial engineering and ergonomics.
