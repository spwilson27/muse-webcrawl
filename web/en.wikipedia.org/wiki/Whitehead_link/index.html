Whitehead link

In knot theory, the Whitehead link is a two-component link with five crossings, named after the British mathematician J. H. C. Whitehead. It consists of two unknotted circles intertwined in a twisted, figure-eight-like pattern, and it is one of the most studied elementary links in low-dimensional topology.

Properties

Although the two components of the Whitehead link cannot be separated, their linking number is zero: the twists in the diagram cancel, so the link is not detected by the simplest invariant — the two loops are linked in a way that is "algebraically" invisible. This made the Whitehead link historically important: it shows that linking number zero does not imply two components can be pulled apart. The link is alternating, achiral in some descriptions, and has hyperbolic volume of about 3.664, since the complement of the Whitehead link admits a complete hyperbolic structure; indeed its complement can be built from a single regular ideal octahedron.

Constructions and uses

The Whitehead link arises as the closure of the 3-braid (σ₁σ₂−1)², and one component wound around the other twice gives the Whitehead double construction, central to the study of satellite knots: Whitehead doubles of knots have Alexander polynomial 1, and Whitehead's name also attaches to the Whitehead manifold, built by iterating the link pattern infinitely — a contractible open 3-manifold that is not homeomorphic to Euclidean space.

Related links

The Whitehead link is the second in the twist-link family after the Hopf link, and is closely related in spirit to the Borromean rings, another link whose components are collectively but not pairwise linked.
