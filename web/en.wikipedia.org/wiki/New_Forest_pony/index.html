The New Forest pony is one of the recognised mountain and moorland pony breeds native to the British Isles. It originates from the New Forest in Hampshire, southern England, where ponies have roamed semi-feral over the forest's heaths and woodlands for centuries. It is the largest of the British native pony breeds and is prized as a versatile, good-tempered riding and driving pony for children and adults.

History

Ponies have existed in the New Forest since at least the 11th century; local commoners held rights of pasture there recorded around the time of William the Conqueror, who made the area a royal hunting forest. Over the centuries outside stallions — including Welsh, Thoroughbred, Arab, and other native breeds — were turned out to improve the stock. In the 19th and 20th centuries the breed was standardised, and the New Forest Pony Breeding and Cattle Society, founded in 1906, manages the stud book and the traditional system of "drifts" (round-ups) at which the semi-feral ponies are gathered, health-checked, and marked.

Characteristics

New Forest ponies stand up to 14.2 hands high with no lower height limit, though ponies in the forest are often smaller. They have a dished or straight head, sloping shoulders, powerful quarters, and hard, well-shaped feet. All colours are accepted except piebald, skewbald, and blue-eyed cream. They are noted for hardiness, agility over rough ground, and a calm, willing temperament.

Uses

The breed makes an all-round family pony, suited to riding, gymkhana, show jumping, dressage, and driving. The grazing of the semi-feral herds also plays an important conservation role in maintaining the New Forest's distinctive landscapes.
