The names of God in Islam (Arabic: أسماء الله الحسنى‎, Asmāʾ Allāh al-Ḥusnā, "Allah's Most Beautiful Names") are names by which Muslims refer to and describe God (Allah). According to a well-known hadith, there are 99 names; a traditional list was compiled from names and attributes mentioned in the Quran and the Sunnah. Each name expresses an attribute of God — such as mercy, knowledge, power or justice — and reciting and reflecting on them is a long-established devotional practice.

Sources

The Quran exhorts believers to call upon God by His beautiful names (Q 7:180; see also Q 17:110, 20:8 and 59:24). A hadith reported in the collections of al-Bukhari and Muslim states that God has ninety-nine names and that whoever learns them by heart (aḥṣāhā) will enter Paradise. A specific enumeration appears in a narration in al-Tirmidhi's collection, and although scholars have debated whether the list itself is part of the Prophet's words or a narrator's compilation from the Quran, the al-Tirmidhi list became the basis of the popular 99-names tradition reproduced in calligraphy, posters and devotional manuals.

The Greatest Name

Islamic tradition also speaks of God's "Greatest Name" (al-ism al-aʿẓam), by which supplications are answered. Various reports associate it with verses such as the Throne Verse (Q 2:255, containing Al-Ḥayy and Al-Qayyūm) or the opening of Surah Āl ʿImrān. The personal name Allah itself is regarded by many scholars as the supreme name, comprehending all the attributes.

Devotional use

The names are recited in dhikr (remembrance), often counted on prayer beads (misbaha), and feature prominently in Sufi practice, poetry and Islamic calligraphy. Many Arabic and Muslim personal names are formed from them with the prefix ʿAbd ("servant of"), as in Abdullah ("servant of God"), Abd al-Rahman ("servant of the Most Merciful") and Abd al-Aziz. Theological schools have differed over the relationship between God's names, His attributes and His essence — a central topic in classical kalām — but the devotional list itself is shared across the Muslim world.
