Sulpicius Severus (c. 363 – c. 425) was a Christian writer from Aquitaine in Roman Gaul, best known as the biographer of Saint Martin of Tours. Born into a wealthy senatorial family and trained in law at Bordeaux, he abandoned a promising legal career after the death of his wife and, influenced by Martin and by his mother-in-law Bassula, withdrew to an estate at Primuliacum to live an ascetic life with a small community.

Works

His Vita Martini (Life of Saint Martin), written while Martin was still alive, became one of the most widely read works of the Latin Middle Ages and a model for medieval hagiography, depicting Martin's miracles, his confrontations with demons, and his famous charity in dividing his cloak for a beggar. His Chronicle (Chronicorum Libri Duo, also called the Sacred History) narrates world history from the Creation to about AD 400, valuable especially for its account of the Priscillianist controversy in Spain and Gaul. Three letters and the Dialogues, defending Martin's reputation and comparing him favourably with the monks of Egypt, complete his surviving corpus. His elegant Latin, modelled on Sallust and Tacitus, earned him the nickname "the Christian Sallust."
