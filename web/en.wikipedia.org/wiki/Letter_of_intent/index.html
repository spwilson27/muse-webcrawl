Letter of intent (LOI)
Also called: Memorandum of understanding, term sheet, heads of terms

A letter of intent (LOI) is a document outlining the preliminary understanding between two or more parties before a formal agreement is concluded. In mergers and acquisitions, an LOI records the main terms — price, structure, exclusivity, due diligence period and closing conditions — before definitive documents are drafted.

Legal effect

Most LOIs are largely non-binding as to the main transaction but often contain binding clauses on confidentiality, exclusivity (no-shop), governing law and expenses. Courts may enforce an LOI or a duty to negotiate in good faith if language and conduct suggest intent to be bound, as in Texaco v. Pennzoil.

Uses

Beyond M&A, LOIs are used in real estate, employment, university admissions and athletics (National Letter of Intent for U.S. college sports recruiting), grant applications, and construction, where they may authorise early work before a full contract.
