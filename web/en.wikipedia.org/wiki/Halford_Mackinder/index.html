Halford Mackinder

Sir Halford John Mackinder (15 February 1861 – 6 March 1947) was an English geographer, academic and politician, regarded as one of the founding fathers of geopolitics and geostrategy. He was the first Principal of University College, Reading (later the University of Reading), a founder of the London School of Economics, and a Member of Parliament.

The Heartland theory

Mackinder is best known for the Geographical Pivot of History paper of 1904 and his Heartland theory, developed further in Democratic Ideals and Reality (1919). He argued that control of the vast interior "Heartland" of Eurasia — inaccessible to sea power but traversable by land — conferred decisive strategic advantage, summarised in his famous dictum: "Who rules East Europe commands the Heartland; who rules the Heartland commands the World-Island; who rules the World-Island commands the world." The theory influenced German Geopolitik and, later, Cold War thinking about containment.

Life and career

Educated at Christ Church, Oxford, Mackinder played a central role in establishing geography as an academic discipline in Britain, helping found the Oxford School of Geography in 1899. He was present at the first ascent of Mount Kenya in 1899. As a Unionist MP for Glasgow Camlachie (1910–1922) he served as British High Commissioner to South Russia in 1919–20, reporting on the Bolshevik threat.
