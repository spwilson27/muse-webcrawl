Deep learning super sampling

Deep learning super sampling (DLSS) is a family of real-time image upscaling and anti-aliasing technologies developed by Nvidia for use in video games. DLSS renders a game at a lower resolution and then uses a neural network, running on the tensor cores of GeForce RTX graphics cards, to reconstruct an image at a higher target resolution.

Technology

The technique uses temporal information — motion vectors and data from previous frames — together with a trained neural network to produce output intended to match or exceed the quality of native-resolution rendering while reducing the rendering cost. DLSS 2.0 (2020) moved to a generalised network that no longer required per-game training. Later versions added:

  • DLSS 3 — Frame Generation, which inserts AI-generated intermediate frames between rendered frames.
  • DLSS 3.5 — Ray Reconstruction, replacing hand-tuned denoisers in ray-traced rendering.
  • DLSS 4 — Multi Frame Generation and a transformer-based model for super resolution and ray reconstruction.

Quality modes

Games typically offer Quality, Balanced, Performance, and Ultra Performance presets corresponding to increasing internal upscaling factors. DLSS is often compared with AMD's FidelityFX Super Resolution (FSR) and Intel's XeSS, which pursue similar goals through different methods.

Reception

Analysts have generally praised DLSS for its performance gains and image quality, while noting occasional visual artefacts and its initial limitation to Nvidia hardware.
