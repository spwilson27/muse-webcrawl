The Precambrian (or Pre-Cambrian, sometimes abbreviated pC) is the earliest part of Earth's history, set before the current Phanerozoic Eon. It spans from the formation of Earth about 4.6 billion years ago to the beginning of the Cambrian Period, about 538.8 million years ago — roughly seven-eighths of the planet's history. It is subdivided into the Hadean, Archean, and Proterozoic eons.

Stromatolites, layered structures built by microbial communities, among the oldest traces of life on Earth

Overview

Relatively little is known about the Precambrian compared with later eras, because its rocks have often been heavily metamorphosed, eroded, or buried. Life appears to have originated early in the Precambrian; photosynthesis by cyanobacteria gradually oxygenated the atmosphere in the Great Oxidation Event about 2.4 billion years ago, and banded iron formations record this transformation. The first multicellular organisms appeared in the later Proterozoic, and the soft-bodied Ediacaran biota of the final Precambrian period preceded the rapid diversification of animal life in the Cambrian explosion.

Subdivisions

Hadean Eon (4,567–4,031 Ma)

Earth accreted from the solar nebula, a giant impact is hypothesised to have formed the Moon, and a magma ocean gradually cooled to form the first crust and oceans.

Archean Eon (4,031–2,500 Ma)

The first stable continental cratons formed, and the earliest evidence of life — microbial mats and stromatolites, and possible chemical signatures in rocks more than 3.7 billion years old — dates to this eon.

Proterozoic Eon (2,500–538.8 Ma)

Oxygen accumulated in the atmosphere, eukaryotic cells evolved, supercontinents such as Columbia and Rodinia assembled and broke apart, global "Snowball Earth" glaciations occurred, and the first animals appeared near its end.
