Inn

An inn is an establishment similar to a hotel, providing lodging, food and drink for travellers, and in some cases stabling and fodder for their horses. Inns were found all over the ancient world and provided a vital service on trade routes; many survive today as pubs, guest houses and hotels.

History

Inns in Europe were possibly first established when the Romans built their system of roads two millennia ago. Some inns in Europe are several centuries old. In addition to providing for the needs of travellers, inns traditionally acted as meeting places for the local community. In the Roman world, mansiones and cauponae served officials and merchants along the cursus publicus; in the Islamic world, caravanserais provided similar lodging on caravan routes, and in Japan, honjin and hatago served travellers on the great highways of the Edo period.

Coaching inns

In Britain and its colonies, the coaching inn became central to travel from the seventeenth to the nineteenth century. Built along turnpike roads at intervals of roughly a day's coach journey, they offered fresh horses for stagecoaches, meals, and beds for passengers. Large coaching inns had archways leading to cobbled yards surrounded by stables and guest rooms. The arrival of the railways in the nineteenth century ended the coaching trade, and many inns declined or converted into ordinary pubs, though some famous examples — such as The George Inn in Southwark, London — survive.

Inns of Court

In London, the term "inn" survives in the Inns of Court — the four professional associations for barristers (Inner Temple, Middle Temple, Lincoln's Inn and Gray's Inn) — which originated as hostels where lawyers lodged, studied and practised near the royal courts at Westminster.

Modern usage

Today the word is used both for traditional establishments, often with historic character, and as part of brand names for modern lodging chains. In North America "inn" is often applied to smaller hotels and motels; in Japan, the ryokan is the traditional inn, offering tatami rooms, communal baths and kaiseki meals.
