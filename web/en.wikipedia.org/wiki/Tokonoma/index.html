Tokonoma

A tokonoma (床の間) is a recessed alcove in a traditional Japanese reception room (zashiki), in which items for artistic appreciation are displayed — typically a hanging scroll (kakemono), a flower arrangement (ikebana), and sometimes an incense burner or ceramic piece.

Design

The tokonoma developed during the Muromachi period from the altars and study alcoves of Zen temples and the shoin style of residential architecture. Its floor is raised slightly above the tatami, and a natural-finish wooden post, the tokowaki, marks one side. The display is changed with the seasons and the status of the guest.

Etiquette

The seat of honour in a tatami room is the one in front of the tokonoma (kamiza); guests are seated there with their backs to the display. One must never step into the alcove or place luggage in it. In the tea ceremony, the scroll in the tokonoma — often a Zen calligraphy — sets the theme of the gathering.
