Jean-Étienne-Marie Portalis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Jean-Étienne-Marie Portalis (1 April 1746 – 25 August 1807) was a French jurist and politician, one of the principal drafters of the French Civil Code of 1804. A Provençal advocate of the old regime, he defended the Protestants' civil status and wrote on philosophy and religion; imprisoned during the Terror, he survived to become a councillor of state under Napoleon and Minister of Religious Affairs (1804), in which capacity he implemented the Concordat of 1801.

Portalis is remembered for the preliminary discourse (Discours préliminaire) he wrote for the draft Code, a classic statement of codification philosophy: laws should be few, general and clear; the legislator should fix “general maxims” and leave detail to judges and doctrine; codification should preserve what experience has validated rather than remake society from theory — a deliberate middle way between revolutionary rationalism and blind traditionalism that shaped the Code's conservative-liberal character.
