Bézout domain

In mathematics, a Bézout domain is a form of Prüfer domain in which every finitely generated ideal is principal: for any elements a and b of the ring there exists an element d (a greatest common divisor of a and b) and elements x and y with ax + by = d, generalising Bézout's identity from the integers. The concept is named after Étienne Bézout.

Properties

Every principal ideal domain is a Bézout domain, as is every valuation domain and every discrete valuation ring. Unlike principal ideal domains, Bézout domains need not be Noetherian — the ring of all algebraic integers and the ring of entire functions are standard examples of non-Noetherian Bézout domains.

Bézout domains are exactly the Prüfer domains in which every finitely generated ideal is principal; they are GCD domains, meaning any two elements possess a greatest common divisor. A Noetherian Bézout domain is the same thing as a principal ideal domain. Over a Bézout domain, finitely generated torsion-free modules have structure theorems analogous to modules over a PID, and matrices admit a Smith normal form.

Examples

Examples include the integers, any field, polynomial rings in one variable over a field, valuation rings, the ring of entire functions on the complex plane, and the ring of algebraic integers.
