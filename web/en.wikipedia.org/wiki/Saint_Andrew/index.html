Saint Andrew

Andrew the Apostle (Greek: Ἀνδρέας, Andreas; died 30 November 60 AD), also called Saint Andrew, was an apostle of Jesus. According to the New Testament, he was a fisherman and one of the Twelve Apostles chosen by Jesus. The brother of Saint Peter, he is referred to in the Orthodox tradition as the First-Called (Prōtoklētos).

Life

Andrew was born in Bethsaida on the Sea of Galilee. He and his brother Simon Peter were fishermen. In the Gospel of John, Andrew was first a disciple of John the Baptist, who pointed him to Jesus; Andrew then brought his brother Simon to Jesus, leading John to call Andrew the "First-Called". In the Synoptic Gospels, Andrew is among the first called at the Sea of Galilee and is consistently listed among the twelve.

Mission and martyrdom

Early tradition, recorded by Eusebius, held that Andrew preached in Scythia and around the Black Sea, and he is honoured as the founder of the see of Byzantium (Constantinople). According to tradition he was martyred at Patras in Achaea, bound to an X-shaped cross — later called the Saint Andrew's cross or saltire — which became his iconographic emblem.

Veneration

Andrew is the patron saint of Scotland, where his saltire forms the national flag, as well as of Russia, Greece, Romania, and the Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople. His feast day, 30 November, is a national day in Scotland. Relics attributed to him are kept at Patras, at the Duomo of Amalfi in Italy, and elsewhere; some relics were returned to Patras by Pope Paul VI in 1964 as a gesture of goodwill toward the Orthodox Church.
