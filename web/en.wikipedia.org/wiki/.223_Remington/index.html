.223 Remington

The .223 Remington (designated as the 223 Remington by SAAMI and as 5.56×45mm by the CIP) is a rimless, bottlenecked, centerfire intermediate rifle cartridge. It was developed in the late 1950s and early 1960s by Remington Arms in cooperation with Fairchild Industries for the ArmaLite AR-15 rifle, and was introduced commercially in 1964.

History

The cartridge grew out of U.S. Army small-calibre high-velocity research of the 1950s, which sought a lighter round that would remain supersonic at 500 yards while allowing soldiers to carry more ammunition. Derived from the .222 Remington, the .223 Remington was lengthened and given greater powder capacity. In 1964 it was adopted by the U.S. military as the cartridge, 5.56mm ball M193, chambered in the M16 rifle.

Relationship to 5.56×45mm NATO

The military 5.56×45mm NATO cartridge was developed from the .223 Remington and the two are externally almost identical, but they are not completely interchangeable: 5.56mm NATO ammunition is loaded to higher pressures and military chambers have a longer leade (freebore). Firing 5.56mm NATO ammunition in a barrel chambered strictly for .223 Remington can produce excessive pressures, whereas .223 Remington ammunition can be fired safely in a 5.56mm chamber. Hybrid chamberings such as .223 Wylde are designed to handle both.

Use

The .223 Remington is one of the most popular rifle cartridges in the United States. It is widely used for varmint and small-game hunting, target shooting and competition, and in semi-automatic sporting rifles of the AR-15 pattern, as well as bolt-action rifles. Typical factory loads drive a 55-grain (3.6 g) bullet at about 3,200 ft/s (980 m/s).
