Uusimaa

Uusimaa (Finnish: [ˈuːsimɑː]; Swedish: Nyland) is a region of Finland on the southern coast of the Gulf of Finland. It is Finland's most populous region, containing the capital, Helsinki, along with the cities of Espoo and Vantaa, which together form the Helsinki metropolitan area. Its name means "new land" in both Finnish and Swedish.

Geography

Uusimaa covers roughly 9,500 square kilometres and has a population of about 1.7 million, more than a quarter of Finland's total. The coast is fringed by a large archipelago. In 2011 the neighbouring region of Eastern Uusimaa was merged into Uusimaa.

History

The coast of Uusimaa was settled by Swedes from the 12th and 13th centuries, and Swedish remains widely spoken in coastal communities. Helsinki was founded in 1550 by Gustav I of Sweden. The region has historically been part of the province of Southern Finland and earlier of the historical province of Uusimaa within the Swedish realm.

Economy

Uusimaa dominates Finland's economy, hosting the headquarters of most major Finnish companies, the Port of Helsinki (the country's busiest passenger port), and Helsinki Airport in Vantaa. It also contains major universities, including the University of Helsinki and Aalto University.
