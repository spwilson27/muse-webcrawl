The Paracel Islands, also known by other names, are a group of more than 130 small coral islands, reefs, and banks in the South China Sea, roughly equidistant from the coasts of China and Vietnam. Known in Chinese as the Xisha Islands (西沙群岛) and in Vietnamese as Quần đảo Hoàng Sa, the archipelago is controlled by the People's Republic of China but is also claimed by Vietnam and by the Republic of China (Taiwan). It is one of the principal disputed territories in the South China Sea.

Geography

The islands lie southeast of Hainan and east of central Vietnam. They form two main groups: the Amphitrite Group in the northeast and the Crescent Group in the southwest. Woody Island (Yongxing Island) is the largest, with about 2.6 km² of land area; it hosts an airstrip, harbor, and the administrative seat of Sansha, the prefecture-level city established by China in 2012 to administer its South China Sea claims. Most of the features are only a few metres above sea level.

History

Chinese and Vietnamese records both cite centuries of activity in the area. French Indochina administered the islands in the colonial era, and France transferred its involvement to the State of Vietnam in the 1950s. In January 1974, Chinese forces fought the Battle of the Paracel Islands against the South Vietnamese navy, seizing the Crescent Group; China has controlled the entire archipelago since. After the Vietnam War, reunified Vietnam reasserted its claim, and the dispute remains unresolved.

Development and militarization

Since the 2010s China has expanded infrastructure on the islands — harbours, runways on Woody Island, lighthouses, and a small civilian population — and has deployed military equipment there, drawing protests from Vietnam and criticism from other claimants and the United States. The surrounding waters are believed to hold oil and gas resources and are important fishing grounds and shipping routes.
