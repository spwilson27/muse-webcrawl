Overview

Coronary artery disease (CAD), also called coronary heart disease or ischaemic heart disease, is the narrowing of the coronary arteries by atherosclerotic plaques, reducing blood flow to the heart muscle. It is the most common type of heart disease and a leading cause of death worldwide.

Presentation and treatment

Reduced flow may cause no symptoms, exertional chest pain (stable angina), or — when a plaque ruptures and a clot forms — a heart attack (myocardial infarction). Management combines lifestyle measures, risk-factor control, and drugs (antiplatelets, statins, beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors), with revascularisation by percutaneous coronary intervention (stenting) or coronary artery bypass surgery for suitable disease patterns. Sudden cardiac death may be the first manifestation.
