Thermal imaging camera

Thermal imaging camera
Type: Infrared imaging device
Measures: Infrared radiation (heat)

A thermal imaging camera (also known as a thermographic camera or infrared camera) is a device that creates an image using infrared radiation, similar to an ordinary camera forming an image using visible light. Instead of the 400–700 nanometre range of the visible light camera, infrared cameras operate in wavelengths as long as 14,000 nm (14 µm).

Working principle

All objects with a temperature above absolute zero emit thermal infrared energy. A thermal camera detects this radiation with an array of infrared detectors, typically a microbolometer focal plane array, and converts the measurements into a visible image called a thermogram, in which hotter areas appear brighter or in different colors.

Applications

Thermal imagers are used by firefighters to see through smoke and locate people, by electricians and maintenance technicians to detect overheating components, in building inspections to find heat loss and moisture, in medicine and veterinary practice, and by military and law-enforcement personnel for surveillance and night vision.

History

Infrared-sensitive detectors grew out of military research in the mid-20th century. Early systems required cryogenic cooling; the development of uncooled microbolometer arrays in the 1990s made compact, affordable cameras widely available.
