The Mixtecs (Mixtec: Ñuù savi, "People of the Rain") are an Indigenous people of Mexico inhabiting the region known as La Mixteca, which covers parts of the modern states of Oaxaca, Guerrero, and Puebla. They are renowned in the pre-Columbian history of Mesoamerica for their codices, goldwork, and the powerful city-state kingdoms of the Postclassic period.

History

Mixtec civilisation developed over many centuries in the Mixteca Alta highlands, with important centres such as Monte Negro and Yucuita. In the Postclassic period (c. 900–1521) competing Mixtec kingdoms (ñuu) — including Tilantongo, Achiutla, and Tututepec — expanded across Oaxaca, intermarrying with Zapotec royalty at sites such as Zaachila and Mitla. The culture hero Lord 8 Deer "Jaguar Claw" of Tilantongo (1063–1115) unified many kingdoms and features prominently in the screenfold codices. The Mixtecs resisted both Aztec expansion and, after 1521, Spanish conquest.

Culture and language

The Mixtec languages form a branch of the Oto-Manguean family and remain widely spoken, with several hundred thousand speakers, though the group comprises many mutually unintelligible varieties. Pre-conquest Mixtec artisans produced some of Mesoamerica's finest treasures, including the gold objects from Tomb 7 at Monte Albán, turquoise mosaics, and polychrome ceramics. Today many Mixtec people migrate within Mexico and to the United States for agricultural work while maintaining strong community ties.
