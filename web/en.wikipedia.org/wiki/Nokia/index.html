Nokia Corporation is a Finnish multinational telecommunications and information technology company founded in 1865. It began as a pulp mill and passed through rubber, cable, electronics and mobile phone businesses before becoming a network infrastructure company.

History

Founded by mining engineer Fredrik Idestam on the Nokianvirta river, Nokia made rubber boots, tyres and cables during the 20th century. From the 1980s it pioneered mobile telephony; by 1998 it was the world's largest mobile phone maker, a position it held until 2011–2012, selling iconic handsets such as the Nokia 3310.

Smartphone decline and transformation

Nokia's Symbian platform lost ground to Apple's iOS and Google's Android. In 2011 it adopted Microsoft's Windows Phone; in 2014 it sold its devices business to Microsoft. The brand later returned on phones made under licence by HMD Global.

Modern business

Today's Nokia focuses on network infrastructure (fixed, mobile and optical networks, including Nokia Bell Labs) and technology licensing. Its 2016 acquisition of Alcatel-Lucent made it one of the top global telecom equipment vendors alongside Ericsson and Huawei.
