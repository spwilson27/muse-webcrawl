The Moses Mabhida Stadium is a multi-purpose stadium in Durban, KwaZulu-Natal, South Africa. It is named after Moses Mabhida, a former General Secretary of the South African Communist Party. The stadium was built for the 2010 FIFA World Cup and has since become one of Durban's most recognisable landmarks.

Design

The stadium's most distinctive feature is its 106-metre (348 ft) high arch, inspired by the Y-shape of the South African flag and by the arch of Wembley Stadium in London. The arch spans 350 metres and is the largest of its kind in the world. A cable car, the SkyCar, carries visitors up one leg of the arch to a viewing platform at its apex, which offers panoramic views over Durban and the Indian Ocean. The stadium also hosts the world's tallest swing, the Big Rush Big Swing, from which visitors can swing out from the arch.

Construction and opening

The stadium was built on the site of the demolished Kings Park Soccer Stadium and was completed ahead of schedule, opening officially on 28 November 2009. During the 2010 World Cup it hosted seven matches, including a semi-final between Spain and Germany.

Uses

The stadium is the home ground of the Premier Soccer League club AmaZulu and has hosted concerts, athletics events and other major occasions, including matches of the South Africa national football team.
