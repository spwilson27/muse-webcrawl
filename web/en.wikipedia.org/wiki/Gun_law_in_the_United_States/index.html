Gun law in the United States

Gun law in the United States regulates the sale, possession, and use of firearms and ammunition. State laws vary considerably and supplement federal law. Access to firearms is constitutionally protected by the Second Amendment, which the Supreme Court has interpreted as guaranteeing an individual right to keep and bear arms for self-defence in the home (District of Columbia v. Heller, 2008) and, in New York State Rifle & Pistol Association v. Bruen (2022), a right to carry handguns in public subject to a text, history, and tradition standard of review.

Federal law

Key federal statutes include:

  • The National Firearms Act of 1934, which taxes and registers machine guns, short-barrelled rifles and shotguns, and suppressors.
  • The Gun Control Act of 1968, which regulates interstate commerce in firearms, prohibits sales to felons and other specified persons, and requires dealers to be federally licensed.
  • The Brady Handgun Violence Prevention Act of 1993, which established the National Instant Criminal Background Check System (NICS).
  • The Federal Assault Weapons Ban of 1994, which expired in 2004.
  • The Bipartisan Safer Communities Act of 2022, which expanded background checks for buyers under 21 and funded state red-flag laws.

Federal law is administered principally by the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF).

State law

States regulate matters such as permits to purchase, concealed and open carry, assault-weapon and magazine-capacity restrictions, and safe storage. States such as California, New York, New Jersey, and Illinois impose comparatively extensive licensing and feature-based restrictions, while a majority of states now allow permitless ("constitutional") carry of handguns. Some states preempt local firearm ordinances.

Statistics and debate

The United States has the highest rate of civilian firearm ownership in the world — an estimated 120 firearms per 100 residents — and firearm deaths (including suicides, which form the majority) number in the tens of thousands annually. Gun law is among the most contested topics in American politics, dividing largely along partisan lines.
