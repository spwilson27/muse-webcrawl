Wig

A wig is a head covering made of human hair, animal hair or synthetic fibre, worn for fashion, disguise, religious observance, medical hair loss, or professional dress. Related partial pieces include toupees and hair extensions.

History

Wigs were worn in ancient Egypt and the classical world. They became emblems of status in 17th–18th-century Europe: the peruke or periwig, popularised at the court of Louis XIV, was powdered and worn by gentlemen and by British judges and barristers — a tradition surviving in UK court dress. The macaroni and queue styles marked later fashions before men's wigs declined after the 1790s hair powder tax.

Uses

Wigs serve chemotherapy and alopecia patients, Orthodox Jewish married women's hair covering (sheitel), theatre and film, drag performance, and everyday fashion. Lace-front and hand-tied constructions aim for a natural hairline.

Manufacture

A global trade collects and processes human hair — much of it from Asia — alongside synthetic fibres such as modacrylic, which permit heat styling in advanced forms.
