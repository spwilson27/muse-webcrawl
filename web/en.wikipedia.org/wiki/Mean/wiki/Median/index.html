Median

The median of a set of numbers is the value separating the higher half from the lower half of a data sample, a population, or a probability distribution. For a data set, it may be thought of as the "middle" value. The basic advantage of the median in describing data compared to the mean (often simply described as the "average") is that it is not skewed so much by a small proportion of extremely large or small values, and therefore it may give a better idea of a "typical" value.

Definition

For a finite list of numbers, the median is found by arranging all the observations from lowest to highest value and picking the middle one. If there is an even number of observations, the median is usually defined as the mean of the two middle values.

Properties

  • The median is a robust statistic: up to half the data may be arbitrarily extreme without moving the median much (a breakdown point of 50%).
  • The median minimises the average absolute deviation from the values in the data set.
  • For a continuous probability distribution, the median m satisfies P(X ≤ m) = P(X ≥ m) = 1/2.
  • Medians can be defined for ordered (ordinal) data where the mean cannot.

Uses

The median is widely used in economics and household statistics: median household income and median house prices are preferred to means because distributions of income and property values are heavily skewed. It is also used in survival analysis (median survival time) and in image processing (median filters for noise reduction).
