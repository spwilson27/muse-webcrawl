Harmonic mean

In mathematics, the harmonic mean is one of several kinds of average, and in particular, one of the Pythagorean means. It is sometimes appropriate for situations when the average rate is desired. The harmonic mean can be expressed as the reciprocal of the arithmetic mean of the reciprocals of the given set of observations.

Definition

For positive real numbers x1, ..., xn, the harmonic mean is defined as

H = n / (1/x1 + 1/x2 + ... + 1/xn)

For two numbers a and b, this reduces to H = 2ab / (a + b).

Relationship to other means

The harmonic mean is one of the three Pythagorean means, together with the arithmetic mean and the geometric mean. For any set of positive numbers that are not all equal,

harmonic mean < geometric mean < arithmetic mean

The harmonic mean is the reciprocal of the arithmetic mean of the reciprocals, and it is a Schur-concave, homogeneous function. It is strongly influenced by small values in the data set.

Applications

  • Averaging rates: the average speed of a journey made over equal distances at different speeds is the harmonic mean of the speeds.
  • Finance: averaging multiples such as price/earnings ratios.
  • Machine learning: the F1 score is the harmonic mean of precision and recall.
  • Physics and engineering: parallel resistors and capacitors in series combine according to the harmonic-sum rule; the harmonic mean appears in averaging densities and flow rates.
