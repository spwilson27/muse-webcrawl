Average

An average is a single value intended to represent or summarise a set of data — a measure of central tendency. In everyday language, "average" most often refers to the arithmetic mean, the sum of the values divided by their number, but in statistics the term encompasses several distinct measures, principally the mean, median, and mode, each appropriate in different situations.

Types of average

The median is the middle value when data are ordered, and is preferred for skewed distributions such as household income, where a few very large values distort the mean. The mode is the most frequent value, useful for categorical data. Other averages include the geometric mean, used for growth rates and ratios; the harmonic mean, used for rates such as average speed over equal distances; and weighted averages, in which some values contribute more than others. Trimmed means, which discard extreme values before averaging, compromise between mean and median.

History and usage

The word "average" derives from maritime law: from Arabic ʿawār (damage) via Italian avaria, it originally denoted the shared apportionment of losses from cargo jettisoned at sea, and by extension any proportional distribution, then a mean value. Averages are ubiquitous in science, finance, sport (such as batting averages), and public policy, but can mislead when distributions are skewed or multimodal; statisticians therefore pair averages with measures of spread such as the standard deviation and range.
