Geometric mean

In mathematics, the geometric mean is a mean or average which indicates a central tendency of a finite set of positive real numbers by using the product of their values (as opposed to the arithmetic mean which uses their sum). The geometric mean is defined as the nth root of the product of n numbers.

Definition

For a set of numbers x1, ..., xn, the geometric mean is defined as

(Π xi)1/n = (x1 x2 ... xn)1/n

It is equal to the arithmetic mean of the logarithms of the numbers, converted back by exponentiation:

exp( (1/n) Σ ln xi )

Properties

  • The geometric mean is less than or equal to the arithmetic mean, with equality only when all numbers are equal (the AM–GM inequality).
  • It is greater than or equal to the harmonic mean.
  • The geometric mean preserves ratios: it is the appropriate average for values that are multiplied together or that grow exponentially.

Applications

The geometric mean is used in finance to compute average growth rates and compound annual growth rates (CAGR), in geometry (for example in finding the side of a square with the same area as a given rectangle), in signal-processing filter design, and in benchmark normalization. In the social sciences it is used in the Human Development Index.
