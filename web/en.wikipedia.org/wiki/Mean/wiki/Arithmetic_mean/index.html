Arithmetic mean

In mathematics and statistics, the arithmetic mean (pronounced /ˌærɪθˈmɛtɪk ˈmiːn/) is a measure of central tendency defined as the sum of a collection of numbers divided by the count of numbers in the collection. It is what is ordinarily meant by the word "average". For numbers x1, …, xn, the arithmetic mean is:

x̄ = (1/n) · Σ xi

Properties

The arithmetic mean is the unique value for which the sum of the deviations of the data from it is zero, and it is the point that minimises the sum of squared deviations, which underlies least-squares methods and the definition of variance. It is sensitive to outliers: a single extremely large or small value can shift it substantially, unlike the median. The mean of a linear transformation of data equals the corresponding transformation of the mean.

Uses and related means

The arithmetic mean is used throughout statistics, economics, and the sciences as a summary of a data set, and the sample mean is the standard estimator of a population's expected value; by the law of large numbers it converges to that expectation as sample size grows. It is one of the Pythagorean means, alongside the geometric mean and the harmonic mean, and for any non-constant data set the ordering harmonic ≤ geometric ≤ arithmetic holds (the AM–GM–HM inequality).
