Tribe of Manasseh

According to the Hebrew Bible, the Tribe of Manasseh was one of the twelve tribes of Israel, descended from Manasseh, the elder son of Joseph and grandson of Jacob. When Jacob blessed Joseph's sons, he crossed his hands to give the greater blessing to the younger brother, Ephraim, foretelling that Ephraim's descendants would be greater, though Manasseh would also become a great people.

Territory

Uniquely among the tribes, Manasseh's inheritance was divided in two by the Jordan River. The "half-tribe of Manasseh" east of the Jordan, together with Reuben and Gad, received pasture land in Gilead and Bashan, a grant associated with Machir, Manasseh's son. The western half-tribe received territory in the central hill country north of Ephraim, including the region around Shechem and extending to the coastal plain near Dor. Cities of refuge and Levitical cities were assigned within both portions.

History

Figures from Manasseh include the judge Gideon, from the clan of Abiezer, and Jephthah came from Gilead in the Transjordanian portion. After Solomon's death the tribe formed part of the northern Kingdom of Israel, and its population was deported following the Assyrian conquest of 722 BCE, becoming part of the "Ten Lost Tribes". Some groups, such as the Bnei Menashe of north-eastern India, claim descent from Manasseh in modern times.
