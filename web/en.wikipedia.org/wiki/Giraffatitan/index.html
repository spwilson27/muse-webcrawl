Giraffatitan

Giraffatitan is a genus of brachiosaurid sauropod dinosaur that lived during the Late Jurassic, about 150 million years ago, in what is now Tanzania. The species Giraffatitan brancai was originally described as an African species of Brachiosaurus before being given its own genus in 1988.

Discovery

Fossils were recovered by German expeditions to the Tendaguru Formation between 1909 and 1912. A mounted skeleton at the Museum für Naturkunde in Berlin — for decades the tallest dinosaur mount in the world at over 13 metres — is composed largely of Giraffatitan material.

Description

Like other brachiosaurids, Giraffatitan had forelimbs longer than its hindlimbs, giving it a giraffe-like sloping posture, and a long neck held steeply upward. Estimates of its mass commonly fall between about 23 and 50 tonnes, making it one of the largest well-known dinosaurs.
