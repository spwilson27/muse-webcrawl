Pomoan languages

The Pomoan languages are a small family of seven indigenous languages of Northern California spoken by the Pomo people. They were once spoken across a compact territory in present-day Sonoma, Mendocino, Lake and Glenn counties. All Pomoan languages are severely endangered, with fluent first-language speakers numbering only in the dozens or fewer.

Classification

The family is generally divided into a Western branch — Kashaya (Southwestern Pomo), Southern Pomo, Central Pomo and Northern Pomo — and three more divergent languages: Northeastern Pomo, Eastern Pomo and Southeastern Pomo. Pomoan has often been included in the proposed Hokan stock, though the affiliation remains unproven.

History and revitalisation

European colonisation and the mission and rancheria systems caused rapid language shift. In recent decades, tribal communities and linguists have run revitalisation programmes, including master-apprentice teaching, immersion preschools and the development of dictionaries and teaching grammars, notably for Kashaya and Central Pomo.

Characteristics

Pomoan languages are known for complex consonant inventories with ejective and aspirated stops, instrumental prefixes on verbs (classifying the means or instrument of an action) and elaborate systems of evidential suffixes in Eastern Pomo, which famously distinguishes multiple types of reported and inferred knowledge.
