Windsor is a historic town in the Hawkesbury region of New South Wales, Australia, situated on the Hawkesbury River about 46 kilometres north-west of Sydney. It is one of the oldest European settlements in Australia and one of the five "Macquarie Towns" established by Governor Lachlan Macquarie in 1810.

History

Settled in 1794 as Green Hills, the district was farmed to supply the infant colony at Sydney. Governor Macquarie renamed it Windsor in 1810 and laid out the town plan, including Thompson Square, which survives as one of Australia's oldest public squares. St Matthew's Anglican Church (1817–1820), designed by Francis Greenway, is among the nation's oldest churches. The town is prone to major Hawkesbury River floods, most recently severe events in 2021 and 2022.

Transport and features

Windsor is served by Windsor railway station on the Richmond line and by the Windsor Bridge over the Hawkesbury River, whose replacement opened in 2020. Heritage buildings include the Macquarie Arms Hotel (1815), often cited as Australia's oldest hotel in continuous operation.
