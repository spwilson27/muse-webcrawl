Subsoil

Subsoil
Soil horizonMainly the B horizon
LocationBeneath the topsoil (A horizon)
CharacteristicsLow organic matter; accumulation of leached minerals

Subsoil is the layer of soil beneath the topsoil at the surface of the ground. It consists largely of weathered mineral material — sand, silt, and clay — with little organic matter, and corresponds mainly to the B horizon of a soil profile. Materials leached from the topsoil, such as iron and aluminium oxides and clay particles, accumulate in the subsoil, a process called illuviation, often giving it yellowish, reddish, or mottled colours.

Properties

Because it contains few nutrients and little humus, subsoil is generally less fertile than topsoil, though deep-rooted plants draw water and some minerals from it. Its structure and clay content strongly affect drainage: dense clay subsoils can impede water movement and root penetration, while compacted subsoil layers (hardpans) may require deep ripping in agriculture to restore productivity.

Uses and significance

Subsoil is important in construction as the bearing layer for foundations, and in environmental science it acts as a filter for water percolating toward groundwater. Exposure of subsoil by erosion or excavation is usually detrimental to plant growth, and restoration projects typically conserve and replace topsoil separately from subsoil.
