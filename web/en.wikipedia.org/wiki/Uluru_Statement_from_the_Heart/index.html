Uluru Statement from the Heart

The Uluru Statement from the Heart is a document issued on 26 May 2017 by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander delegates at the First Nations National Constitutional Convention held at Uluru in the Northern Territory. A one-page statement of about 440 words, it invites the Australian people to support constitutional reforms giving Indigenous Australians a greater voice in the nation's affairs: a constitutionally enshrined First Nations Voice to Parliament, and a Makarrata Commission to supervise agreement-making (treaty) and truth-telling about Australian history. A referendum to enshrine the Voice, held on 14 October 2023, was defeated nationally and in every state.

Background

The statement emerged from the Referendum Council appointed in 2015 by Prime Minister Malcolm Turnbull and Opposition Leader Bill Shorten, which conducted regional dialogues with First Nations communities across Australia in 2016–17. The dialogues rejected merely symbolic constitutional mention and prioritised substantive reform: a Voice to Parliament, treaty-making and truth-telling. The date of issue, 26 May 2017, was the fiftieth anniversary of the successful 1967 referendum that allowed Aboriginal people to be counted in the census and the Commonwealth to legislate for them.

Content

The statement asserts that Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples are "the first sovereign Nations of the Australian continent", that their sovereignty "has never been ceded or extinguished, and co-exists with the sovereignty of the Crown". It describes the "structural problem" of Indigenous powerlessness — "the torment of our powerlessness" — and proposes: "We seek constitutional reforms to empower our people and take a rightful place in our own country." Its most quoted passage invites all Australians: "We invite you to walk with us in a movement of the Australian people for a better future."

Response and the 2023 referendum

The Turnbull government rejected the Voice proposal in October 2017 as neither desirable nor capable of winning a referendum. After the election of the Albanese government in 2022, a referendum was held on 14 October 2023 asking whether to alter the Constitution to recognise Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples by establishing an Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Voice. The proposal was defeated with about 60 per cent voting No nationally and majorities against in all six states. Work on treaty and truth-telling continued at state and territory level, notably in Victoria.
