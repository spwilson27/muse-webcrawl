Bob Graham Round

The Bob Graham Round is a fell running challenge in the English Lake District. It is named after Bob Graham (1889–1966), a Keswick guest-house owner who in June 1932 broke the Lakeland 24-hour record by traversing 42 fells within 24 hours. Contenders must summit the same 42 fells, starting and finishing at the Moot Hall in Keswick, within 24 hours.

The round

The circuit covers about 66 miles (106 km) with roughly 26,900 feet (8,200 m) of ascent. The peaks include Skiddaw, Helvellyn, Scafell Pike (the highest ground in England) and Great Gable. Rounds are traditionally attempted clockwise or anticlockwise from Keswick and must be witnessed by pacers on each summit.

History

Graham's 1932 round stood as a target for decades; Alan Heaton first repeated it in 1960. The Bob Graham Club, founded in 1971, ratifies successful rounds; membership numbers well over two thousand. The fastest known times have been steadily lowered, with both men's and women's records now around 12–14 hours.
