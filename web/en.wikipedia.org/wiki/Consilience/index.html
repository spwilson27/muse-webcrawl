Consilience

Consilience is the principle that evidence from independent, unrelated sources can converge toward strong conclusions. In science, it describes the unity of knowledge: explanations of phenomena spanning different fields should agree — literally, a "jumping together" of knowledge.

History of the concept

The term was coined by William Whewell in 1840, who called it the "consilience of inductions": when a theory explains cases of different kinds from those it was designed for, confidence grows. Charles Darwin's argument in On the Origin of Species is a classic consilient case, uniting biogeography, anatomy, embryology and the fossil record.

Wilson's Consilience

E. O. Wilson popularised the term in his 1998 book Consilience: The Unity of Knowledge, arguing for the unification of the natural sciences, social sciences and humanities on a foundation of evolutionary biology and reductionism — a thesis that drew both praise and criticism from humanists and philosophers.
