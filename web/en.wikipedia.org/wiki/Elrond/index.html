Elrond is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium. He is a Half-elven Lord of Rivendell, one of the mighty Elf-rulers who remained in Middle-earth during the Third Age. He was the father of Arwen Undómiel and the father-in-law of Aragorn.

Biography

Elrond was born in the First Age in the Havens of Sirion, son of Eärendil and Elwing. After the War of Wrath he and his brother Elros chose their fates: Elros chose mortality and founded Númenor, while Elrond chose to be counted among the Elves. In the Second Age Elrond served as herald of the High King Gil-galad and founded Rivendell (Imladris) in T.A. 1697 during the war with Sauron. He bore Vilya, the Ring of Air, mightiest of the Three Rings of the Elves.

In the Third Age, Elrond's house provided refuge to the heirs of Isildur, including the young Aragorn. In The Hobbit, Elrond reads the moon-letters on Thrór's map for Thorin's company. In The Lord of the Rings he convenes the Council of Elrond, at which the Fellowship of the Ring is formed. After the War of the Ring he sails into the West with the other Ring-bearers.

Adaptations

Elrond was played by Hugo Weaving in Peter Jackson's The Lord of the Rings and The Hobbit film trilogies, and by Robert Aramayo in the television series The Rings of Power.
