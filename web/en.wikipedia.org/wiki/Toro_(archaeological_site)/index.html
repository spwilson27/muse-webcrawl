Toro (archaeological site)

LocationShizuoka City, Shizuoka Prefecture, Japan
PeriodYayoi period (1st century CE)
Discovered1943
DesignationSpecial Historic Site of Japan

The Toro archaeological site (登呂遺跡, Toro iseki) is an archaeological site in Suruga Ward of Shizuoka City, Japan, containing the ruins of a Yayoi-period wet-rice farming settlement dating to the 1st century CE. Discovered in 1943 during wartime construction of a munitions factory, it was the first Yayoi settlement to be excavated in Japan in a complete state, and it transformed scholarly understanding of the period. The site was designated a Special Historic Site of Japan in 1952.

Description

Excavations revealed twelve pit dwellings, eight raised-floor storehouses, and some 25,000 square metres of paddy fields divided by wooden bunds, together with wells, irrigation channels and large quantities of wooden farming tools — hoes, spades, mortars and rice-harvesting knives — preserved by the waterlogged soil. Carbonised rice and the layout of fields showed a fully developed irrigated-rice agricultural community, evidence in the long-running debate over the nature of Yayoi society.

Museum and reconstructions

The site is now a public park with reconstructed dwellings and storehouses, and the Shizuoka City Toro Museum (opened 2010) displays the artefacts and explains the excavation history. Toro stands alongside the Yoshinogari site in Kyushu as one of the emblematic Yayoi settlements presented to the public in Japan.
