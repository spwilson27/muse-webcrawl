The Atlantic bluefin tuna (Thunnus thynnus) is a species of tuna in the family Scombridae. It is variously known as the northern bluefin tuna, giant bluefin tuna, and historically as the tunny. It is one of the largest bony fish in the world and among the fastest swimmers in the ocean.

Description

The Atlantic bluefin has a robust, torpedo-shaped body, metallic dark blue above and silvery below. Adults commonly reach 2–2.5 m and several hundred kilograms; the largest recorded specimens exceeded 3 m and 680 kg. Like other bluefin species it can maintain a body temperature above that of the surrounding water, an adaptation that allows it to hunt in cold northern waters.

Distribution and life cycle

The species ranges across the North Atlantic and the Mediterranean Sea. Two spawning populations are recognised: a western stock spawning in the Gulf of Mexico and an eastern stock spawning in the Mediterranean. Bluefin are highly migratory, crossing the Atlantic in seasonal feeding migrations, and long-lived, with lifespans potentially exceeding 30 years.

Fisheries

Atlantic bluefin is highly prized for sushi and sashimi, particularly in Japan, where single fish have sold for record prices at Tokyo's New Year auctions. Intensive fishing drove severe declines in the late 20th century, and the stock is managed internationally by ICCAT (the International Commission for the Conservation of Atlantic Tunas). Quota tightening in the 2010s allowed substantial recovery, and the IUCN moved the species from Endangered to Least Concern in 2021.
