Niger–Congo languages

RegionSub-Saharan Africa
SpeakersAbout 700 million (2024)

Major branchesAtlantic-Congo (incl. Bantu), Mande, Dogon, Kordofanian

Niger–Congo is a language family hypothesised to unite most of the languages of sub-Saharan Africa. With roughly 1,540 languages, it is the world's largest language family by number of languages, and by speakers (about 700 million) the third largest. Its best-known members include Swahili, Yoruba, Igbo, Zulu, Shona and the widespread Bantu languages.

Classification

The phylum was consolidated by Joseph Greenberg's classifications of African languages (1949–1963). Almost all Niger–Congo languages are tonal and many use noun-class systems, in which nouns are grouped into classes marked by prefixes — most elaborately in Bantu. The internal unity of the family, especially the status of groups such as Mande and Kordofanian, remains debated among linguists.

Distribution

The family spans West, Central, East and Southern Africa. The Bantu expansion carried its languages from the Nigeria–Cameroon borderland across most of Africa south of the equator over the past several thousand years.
