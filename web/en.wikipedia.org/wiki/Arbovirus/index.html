Arbovirus

Arbovirus is an informal name for any virus that is transmitted by arthropod vectors — a portmanteau of "arthropod-borne virus". The term is ecological rather than taxonomic: arboviruses belong to several different virus families but share a life cycle in which they replicate in a blood-feeding arthropod (such as a mosquito, tick, or sandfly) and are transmitted when the arthropod bites a vertebrate host.

Diseases

Important arboviral diseases of humans include dengue fever, yellow fever, Zika fever, chikungunya, West Nile fever, Japanese encephalitis, and tick-borne encephalitis. Many are maintained in sylvatic cycles between wild animals and vectors, spilling over into humans when habitats overlap. Symptoms range from mild fever and rash to haemorrhagic fever and encephalitis. Arboviruses of animals, such as bluetongue virus and African horse sickness virus, cause major veterinary losses.

Control and emergence

Because transmission depends on the vector, control emphasises mosquito and tick abatement, insecticides, repellents, bed nets, and vaccines where available (yellow fever and Japanese encephalitis vaccines are long established; dengue vaccines have been licensed with restrictions). Climate change, urbanisation, and global travel are expanding the ranges of vectors such as Aedes aegypti and Aedes albopictus, pushing arboviral diseases into new regions.
