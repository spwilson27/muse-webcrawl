Christmas in Mexico


Placeholder image: a Christmas posada in Mexico

Christmas in Mexico is a season of festivities lasting roughly from 12 December, the feast of Our Lady of Guadalupe, to 6 January, Epiphany (Día de los Reyes), and in some traditions to 2 February (Candlemas, Día de la Candelaria). The celebrations blend Spanish colonial Catholic traditions with indigenous customs.

Las Posadas

From 16 to 24 December, communities hold Las Posadas ("the inns"), nightly processions re-enacting Mary and Joseph's search for lodging in Bethlehem. Participants carry candles and statues of the Holy Family, sing the traditional litany asking for shelter, and are finally admitted to a house for prayer, food and the breaking of a star-shaped piñata — seven-pointed for the seven deadly sins — filled with fruit and sweets.

Nochebuena and Christmas Day

The main celebration is on Christmas Eve (Nochebuena), with a late family dinner and midnight Mass (Misa de Gallo, "Rooster's Mass"). Typical dishes include roast turkey or pork leg, bacalao (salt cod), romeritos in mole, tamales, pozole and the hot fruit punch ponche navideño; a salad of fruits and beets, ensalada de Nochebuena, is traditional. Christmas Day itself is usually a quiet day of rest. The poinsettia (flor de Nochebuena), native to Mexico, is the season's characteristic flower, and elaborate nativity scenes (nacimientos) are set up in homes, churches and public squares.

Epiphany and Candelaria

Gifts are traditionally brought to children not at Christmas but on 6 January by the Three Kings (Los Reyes Magos). Families share a ring-shaped Rosca de Reyes cake containing a small figurine of the Christ child; whoever finds it hosts the Candlemas gathering on 2 February, serving tamales and atole — a custom concluding the season, with the nativity scene's figurines formally "lifted" that day.
