Heidelberg is a city in the south-west of Germany, in the state of Baden-Württemberg, situated on the river Neckar where it emerges from the Odenwald hills into the Rhine plain. It is famous for the romantic ruins of Heidelberg Castle, its picturesque Old Town (Altstadt), and Heidelberg University, founded in 1386 — the oldest university in modern Germany.

History

The area has been settled since prehistory; a 600,000-year-old jawbone of Homo heidelbergensis was found nearby at Mauer in 1907. The town took shape in the 12th–13th centuries as residence of the Counts Palatine of the Rhine. It was repeatedly devastated in the Nine Years' War (1688–1697), when French troops destroyed the castle. In 1803 Heidelberg passed to Baden. Largely spared bombing in the Second World War, it became a major US Army headquarters in post-war Germany.

Culture and science

Heidelberg was a centre of German Romanticism — poets such as Brentano and Eichendorff gathered there — and its castle-and-river scenery inspired Goethe, Turner, and Mark Twain, who described the city in A Tramp Abroad. Today the university, the European Molecular Biology Laboratory, and several Max Planck Institutes make it a leading research centre; students form roughly a quarter of the population, and tourism draws millions of visitors a year.
