Lucy Ella Gonzales Parsons (1853 – March 7, 1942) was an American labor organizer, radical journalist, and anarchist. She was married to Albert Parsons, one of the men executed after the Haymarket affair of 1886, and she spent more than five decades agitating for workers' rights afterward.

Life and activism

Born in Texas, possibly into slavery, Lucy Parsons moved to Chicago in the 1870s. With Albert she founded the newspaper The Alarm in 1884 and played a central role in the movement for the eight-hour day. After Albert's execution in 1887, she raised their children while continuing to write, lecture, and organize across the country, defying police bans on her speeches in Chicago.

In 1905 she helped found the Industrial Workers of the World (IWW). In later years she worked with the International Labor Defense, supported the Scottsboro defendants and other causes, and joined campaigns of the Communist Party USA while retaining her anarchist commitments. She died in a house fire in Chicago in 1942; police seized her library and papers after her death. Chicago named a park in her honor in 2004.
