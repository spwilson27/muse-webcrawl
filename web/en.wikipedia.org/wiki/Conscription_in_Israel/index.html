Conscription in Israel refers to the mandatory military service required of most Jewish, Druze, and Circassian citizens of Israel in the Israel Defense Forces (IDF). Israel is unusual in drafting women as well as men. Service is followed by periodic reserve duty for many veterans, and the system is a central institution of Israeli society, though exemptions — above all for ultra-Orthodox (Haredi) Jews and Arab citizens — are a recurring source of political controversy.

Who is drafted

Under the Defense Service Law, Jewish men and women are drafted at 18; men serve 32 months and women 24 months, with longer terms in combat and officer roles. Druze men have been conscripted since 1956, at the request of community leaders; Circassian men are also drafted. Most Arab citizens are exempt from conscription, though some volunteer; Bedouin citizens have a tradition of voluntary service.

Exemptions are granted for medical or psychological reasons, for married women and mothers, and on religious grounds for women who declare a religious lifestyle. Historically, Haredi men studying full-time in yeshivas received deferments that became effectively permanent — an arrangement originating in Ben-Gurion's 1948 "Torato Omanuto" agreement for a few hundred students that grew to cover tens of thousands. In June 2024 the Israeli Supreme Court ruled there was no legal basis for the blanket exemption, and drafting of Haredi men became a major political dispute.

Alternative service

Those exempt from military service may volunteer for Sherut Leumi (national service) in hospitals, schools, and welfare organisations. Religious Zionist women commonly choose this route.

Reserve duty

After regular service, most soldiers are assigned to reserve units and may be called up for training and operational duty, traditionally up to around age 40 (45 for some roles and officers). Reservists were mobilised on a massive scale following the 7 October 2023 attacks and during the ensuing Gaza war, prompting debates about the economic and social burden and about the fairness of its distribution.

Societal role and debate

The IDF describes itself as a "people's army", and military service has functioned as a melting pot and a pathway into Israeli civic and professional life. Critics of the exemption system argue that the burden of service is unequally shared; defenders of the Haredi arrangement argue that Torah study is itself a national contribution. The conscription ("recruitment") law is among the most contentious recurring issues in Israeli politics and has repeatedly destabilised governing coalitions.
