Gerry Conway

Born: Gerard Francis Conway, 10 September 1952, Brooklyn, New York, U.S.
Occupation: Comic book writer, television writer-producer
Known for: The Punisher, Firestorm, "The Night Gwen Stacy Died"

Gerard Francis "Gerry" Conway (born September 10, 1952) is an American comic book writer, comic book editor, screenwriter, and television producer. He is best known for co-creating the Marvel Comics antihero the Punisher (with artists Ross Andru and John Romita Sr.) and for writing the landmark Spider-Man story "The Night Gwen Stacy Died", in which Spider-Man's girlfriend Gwen Stacy was killed — a turning point frequently cited as the end of the Silver Age of Comic Books.

Comics career

Conway sold his first professional script at age 16 and began writing regularly for Marvel Comics while still a teenager. At 19 he succeeded Stan Lee as writer of The Amazing Spider-Man, producing the Gwen Stacy death storyline (issues #121–122, 1973) and, with Andru, introducing the Punisher in issue #129 (1974). He also wrote for DC Comics, where with artist Al Milgrom he co-created Firestorm, the Nuclear Man, and wrote a long run on Justice League of America. He served briefly as editor-in-chief of Marvel Comics in 1976.

Television and film

From the late 1980s onward Conway worked extensively in television, writing and producing for series including Law & Order and its spin-offs, Diagnosis: Murder, and animated series. He co-wrote the screenplay for the animated film Fire and Ice (1983) with Roy Thomas and worked on the script of Conan the Destroyer (1984).

Creators' rights advocacy

In later years Conway became a prominent advocate for comic book creators' rights and royalty equity, lobbying publicly for fairer treatment and credit for the writers and artists whose characters underpin major film and television franchises.
