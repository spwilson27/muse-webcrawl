Malacca

Malacca (Melaka)
Country: Malaysia
Founded: c. 1400 by Parameswara
UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2008

Malacca, officially Melaka, is a state in Malaysia and its historic capital city, located on the Strait of Malacca. Founded around 1400 by Parameswara, a prince from Palembang, the Malacca Sultanate became the dominant entrepôt of Southeast Asian trade, controlling the strait between the Indian Ocean and South China Sea.

History

The sultanate adopted Islam in the 15th century and hosted traders from China, India, Arabia and Europe. Afonso de Albuquerque conquered it for Portugal in 1511; the Dutch, allied with Johor, took it in 1641; Britain acquired it under the Anglo-Dutch Treaty of 1824 and it became part of the Straits Settlements.

Heritage

Malacca City's core preserves Portuguese, Dutch, British, Malay, Chinese and Indian layers: A Famosa fortress, St Paul's Church, Stadthuys, Christ Church and Jonker Street. With George Town, Penang, it was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2008. The city is also home to Peranakan (Baba-Nyonya) culture.
