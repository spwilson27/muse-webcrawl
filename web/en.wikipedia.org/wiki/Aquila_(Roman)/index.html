Aquila (Roman)

An aquila (Latin for "eagle") was a prominent symbol used in ancient Rome, especially as the standard of a Roman legion. It consisted of a silver or golden eagle with raised wings mounted on a pole, and losing it in battle was considered a grave dishonour.

Type: Military standard
Used by: Roman legions
Bearer: Aquilifer
Symbol: Eagle (aquila)

History

According to Pliny the Elder, the eagle became the sole legionary standard under Gaius Marius, replacing earlier standards including the wolf, minotaur, horse, and boar. The aquila was kept in a shrine in the legion's camp and carried into battle by the aquilifer, a senior soldier. Great efforts were made to recover lost eagles, as after the Battle of the Teutoburg Forest (9 AD), where three legions lost theirs; some were later recovered through diplomacy and military campaigns.

Significance

The aquila embodied the honour and spirit of the legion; soldiers held it in near-religious reverence, and it served as a rallying point in combat.
