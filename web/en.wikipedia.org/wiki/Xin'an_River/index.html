Xin'an River

Length: approx. 373 km
Provinces: Anhui, Zhejiang
Notable feature: Qiandao Lake reservoir

The Xin'an River (Chinese: 新安江; pinyin: Xīn'ānjiāng) is a river in eastern China flowing through Anhui and Zhejiang provinces. It is the upper course of the Qiantang River system and is celebrated in Chinese travel literature for its exceptionally clear water and misty, mountainous scenery.

Course

The river rises near Xiuning in southern Anhui, in the Huangshan region, and flows southeast through She County and the old Huizhou prefecture, historically a prosperous merchant region. At Jiande in Zhejiang it passes the Xin'anjiang Dam, completed in 1960, whose reservoir formed Qiandao Lake ("Thousand Island Lake"), one of China's best-known scenic areas. Below the dam the river continues as the Fuchun River and then the Qiantang River to Hangzhou Bay.

Water quality and culture

The Xin'an is famed for cool, clean water — the dam's deep-water discharge keeps downstream reaches unusually cold in summer. In 2011–2012 the river became the site of China's first interprovincial ecological compensation scheme, in which Zhejiang pays Anhui based on measured water quality at the provincial border, a model later extended nationwide. The landscape along the river, together with Huangshan and Huizhou villages, is a major cultural tourism destination.
