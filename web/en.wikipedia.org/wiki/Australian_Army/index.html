The Australian Army is the principal land warfare force of Australia, one of three armed services of the Australian Defence Force (ADF), alongside the Royal Australian Navy and the Royal Australian Air Force. It is commanded by the Chief of Army and is formally organised under the command of the Chief of the Defence Force for operations.

History

The Army was formed on 1 March 1901, two months after Federation, by amalgamating the military forces of the six colonies. Australian soldiers had already served in the Sudan, the Boxer Rebellion, and the Second Boer War. In the First World War the all-volunteer First Australian Imperial Force fought at Gallipoli, on the Western Front, and in Palestine, establishing a formidable reputation and the Anzac legend. During the Second World War, Australians fought in North Africa, Greece, Syria, Malaya, and the Pacific, and a militia and second AIF together numbered nearly a million personnel across the war. Since 1945 the Army has served in Korea, Malaya, Borneo, South Vietnam, Somalia, East Timor, the Solomon Islands, Iraq, and Afghanistan, as well as in numerous peacekeeping missions.

Structure

The modern Army combines a regular force of professional soldiers with the part-time Army Reserve. Its principal combat formations have traditionally been organised around three regular multi-role combat brigades (the 1st, 3rd, and 7th Brigades), supported by the 6th (combat support and intelligence) and 17th Sustainment Brigades and the 16th Aviation Brigade, with special forces under Special Operations Command. A major restructure announced in 2023 concentrated the combat brigades by role and location—light, littoral, and motorised/armoured formations centred on Townsville, Darwin, Adelaide, and Brisbane.

Equipment and ethos

Principal equipment includes the M1A1 (being replaced by the M1A2) Abrams tank, ASLAV and Boxer armoured vehicles, Bushmaster protected mobility vehicles, and Tiger and MRH-90 (being replaced by UH-60M Black Hawk and AH-64E Apache) helicopters. The Army is known for the slouch hat, the Rising Sun badge, and a culture emphasising initiative, mateship, and the legacy of Anzac Day, observed on 25 April.
