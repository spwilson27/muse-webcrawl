Polarized 3D system

A polarized 3D system uses polarization glasses to create the illusion of three-dimensional images by restricting the light that reaches each eye. Separate images for the left and right eyes are projected superimposed onto the same screen through polarizing filters of orthogonal orientation; glasses with matching filters ensure each eye sees only its intended image, allowing the brain to fuse them into a single stereoscopic picture.

Types

Early systems used linearly polarized light, which requires the viewer to keep the head level, since tilting changes the filter alignment and causes ghosting. Most modern cinema systems, such as RealD 3D, use circular polarization, generated with a rapidly alternating polarizer in front of a single digital projector, which tolerates head movement and allows inexpensive passive glasses. Dual-projector installations and special silver screens that preserve polarization are also used.

Applications

Polarized 3D is the dominant technology for stereoscopic cinema exhibition, and has been used in passive 3D televisions and monitors. Compared with active shutter systems, it offers cheaper, lighter glasses without batteries, at the cost of halving the light or resolution delivered to each eye in some implementations.
