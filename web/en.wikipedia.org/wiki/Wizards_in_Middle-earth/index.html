Wizards in Middle-earth

The Wizards of J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium, collectively known as the Istari (Quenya for "the Wise"), are five Maiar — angelic spirits of the same order as Sauron and the Balrogs — sent by the Valar to Middle-earth around the year 1000 of the Third Age to aid the Free Peoples against Sauron. They were embodied in the forms of old Men, subject to the pains and limits of flesh, and were forbidden to match Sauron's power with power or to dominate Elves and Men by force. Their mission was to counsel, encourage and unite resistance.

The five Wizards

Saruman the White

Curumo, chief of the order and head of the White Council. A Maia of Aulë, learned in the lore of the Rings of Power. His study of Sauron's arts corrupted him: he desired the One Ring for himself, fortified Isengard, bred an army of Uruk-hai, and imprisoned Gandalf atop Orthanc. After his defeat he was expelled from the order, his staff broken; he ended his life as the petty tyrant "Sharkey" of the Shire, slain by his servant Gríma Wormtongue.

Gandalf the Grey (later the White)

Olórin, a Maia of Manwë and Varda, accounted the wisest of the Maiar. Reluctant to go, fearing Sauron, he was nevertheless the only Wizard to remain faithful to the mission. He discovered the identity of Bilbo's ring, orchestrated the Quest of Erebor and the Fellowship of the Ring, died fighting the Balrog of Moria, and was sent back as Gandalf the White to complete his task. After Sauron's fall he departed over the Sea with the Ring-bearers.

Radagast the Brown

Aiwendil, a Maia of Yavanna, lover of birds and beasts. He settled at Rhosgobel on the edge of Mirkwood and largely neglected the affairs of Men and Elves; Tolkien suggested his failure was a lesser one, since he became absorbed in nature rather than seeking power.

The Blue Wizards

Alatar and Pallando, Maiar of Oromë, who travelled into the far East of Middle-earth and never returned to the West. Tolkien wrote conflicting accounts: in one they failed or founded secret cults of magic; in a later note he suggested they may have succeeded in weakening Sauron's support in the East. Their colour, blue, is known from Tolkien's letters.

Role and limits

The Wizards' power was deliberately veiled: they could not reveal their full might or use it for domination, and their chief weapon was wisdom. The White Council, formed in T.A. 2463, drove Sauron (as the Necromancer) from Dol Guldur in T.A. 2941. Only Gandalf completed the mission, and commentators have often read him, in his death and return, as the legendarium's closest approach to a saintly or angelic emissary figure.
