Atomic Energy Research Establishment

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Atomic Energy Research Establishment (AERE) was the main centre for atomic energy research and development in the United Kingdom from the 1940s to the 1990s. It was established in 1946 at the former RAF Harwell airfield in Oxfordshire (then Berkshire), with John Cockcroft as its first director.

History

Harwell's first reactor, GLEEP (Graphite Low Energy Experimental Pile), went critical in August 1947 — the first nuclear reactor in Western Europe. Its successor BEPO (1948) served as a workhorse for isotope production and materials research. The establishment developed expertise across reactor physics, chemistry, metallurgy and isotope production, supporting both the British nuclear weapons programme and civil nuclear power.

In 1954 AERE became part of the newly created United Kingdom Atomic Energy Authority (UKAEA). The ZETA fusion experiment, announced in 1958, briefly and mistakenly appeared to have achieved thermonuclear fusion. From the 1970s, as the nuclear programme matured, Harwell diversified into non-nuclear contract research; in the 1990s its commercial arm was privatised as AEA Technology, while the site evolved into the Harwell Science and Innovation Campus.

Legacy

The site hosted pioneering work including the first European reactor, early transistor and computer research, and neutron diffraction science. Decommissioning of the early reactors continues under the Nuclear Decommissioning Authority.
