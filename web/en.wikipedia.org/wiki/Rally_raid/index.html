Rally raid

Rally raid, also known as cross-country rallying, is a form of long-distance off-road racing that takes place over several days. The length of the event can be as short as 2–3 days for cross-country "bajas" or as long as 15 days for marathon rallies such as the Dakar Rally. Navigation is a key skill, as competitors follow a roadbook through deserts, mountains and other remote terrain, with long special stages linked by road sections (liaisons).

A competitor crosses dunes during a marathon rally raid.

Format

Unlike conventional rallying, which uses short stages on closed roads, rally raids cover thousands of kilometres of mostly unmade tracks. Daily distances can exceed 800 km. Competitors race in categories for motorcycles, quads, cars (buggies and 4x4s), SSVs (side-by-sides) and trucks. Navigation is by roadbook and, in modern events, limited GPS waypoints that must be validated in sequence. Missing a waypoint incurs time penalties. Riders and drivers must also manage fuel range, mechanical repairs and physical endurance, with marathon stages sometimes forbidding outside assistance.

Major events

The best-known rally raid is the Dakar Rally, founded in 1978 as the Paris–Dakar Rally across Africa, later moved to South America (2009–2019) and then to Saudi Arabia (from 2020). Other major events include the Abu Dhabi Desert Challenge, the Rallye du Maroc, the Silk Way Rally, the Sonora Rally and the Baja events of North America. Since 2022 the leading events have been united under the FIA and FIM World Rally-Raid Championship (W2RC).

Vehicles

Raid vehicles are heavily modified for endurance: long-travel suspension, reinforced chassis, large fuel tanks (over 400 litres for trucks), multiple spare wheels and onboard tyre-inflation systems. Purpose-built two-wheel-drive buggies compete against four-wheel-drive prototypes in the top car class (T1/Ultimate), while motorcycles are limited to 450 cc engines.
