Võ Nguyên Giáp (25 August 1911 – 4 October 2013) was a Vietnamese general, politician, and journalist who served as the commander-in-chief of the People's Army of Vietnam (PAVN) and as Minister of Defence of North Vietnam. He is best known for leading Vietnamese forces to victory over the French at the Battle of Điện Biên Phủ in 1954 and over South Vietnam and the United States during the Vietnam War.

Early life

Giáp was born in Quảng Bình Province into a family of scholar-landowners. He studied law at the University of Indochina in Hanoi and worked as a history teacher and journalist. Drawn to nationalist and communist ideas as a young man, he joined the Indochinese Communist Party and became a close associate of Hồ Chí Minh.

First Indochina War

During the First Indochina War (1946–1954), Giáp built the Việt Minh's regular forces from guerrilla bands into a conventional army. His victory at Điện Biên Phủ, achieved after a 56-day siege in which Vietnamese artillery was hauled over mountain trails, forced France to negotiate at Geneva and ended French colonial rule in Indochina.

Vietnam War

As commander of the PAVN during the Vietnam War, Giáp oversaw the strategy that combined guerrilla warfare in the south with large conventional operations. He is associated with the planning of the Tết Offensive of 1968 and the final campaigns that led to the fall of Saigon in 1975, although his direct influence declined after 1968.

Later life and legacy

Giáp served as Minister of Defence until 1980 and remained a deputy prime minister until 1991. He died in Hanoi in 2013 at the age of 102 and received a state funeral. He is widely regarded as one of the leading military figures of the twentieth century and a master of revolutionary warfare and logistics.
