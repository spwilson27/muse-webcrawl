Hawk-Eye

Hawk-Eye is a computer vision system used in numerous sports to visually track the trajectory of a ball and display a profile of its statistically most likely path as a moving image. Invented in 1999 by Paul Hawkins, it was first used in cricket broadcasting and later as an officiating aid at major tennis tournaments for line-call challenges.

How it works

Multiple high-speed cameras positioned around the playing area triangulate the ball's position in each frame, and software combines the images to reconstruct its three-dimensional path. In tennis the system determines whether a ball landed in or out with a reported margin of error of a few millimetres.

Use in sport

Hawk-Eye is used in tennis, cricket (as part of the Decision Review System), football (goal-line technology), badminton, volleyball, and snooker, among others. It is also widely used for broadcast enhancement. The company Hawk-Eye Innovations is owned by Sony.
