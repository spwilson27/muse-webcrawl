Professor

A professor is an academic rank at universities and other post-secondary and research institutions in most countries. Literally, the word derives from Latin professor, "person who professes", one who affirms expertise in an art or science and teaches others. Professors typically conduct original research and teach undergraduate and graduate courses in their field.

Usage by country

In the United States and Canada, professor is the highest academic rank, reached after a probationary period as assistant professor and promotion to associate professor; holders may receive tenure. In the United Kingdom and many Commonwealth countries, professor is a senior rank above reader or senior lecturer, often attached to a named chair. In much of continental Europe, such as Germany (Professor) and France (professeur des universités), the title is similarly the top academic grade, while in some countries it is applied more broadly to school teachers.

Duties

Professors design curricula, supervise doctoral students, publish scholarly work, sit on academic committees, and provide service to their institutions and professions. Many hold endowed chairs, lead research groups or laboratories, and advise governments and industry.

Etymology and history

The rank emerged with the medieval universities, where a professor was a licensed master (magister) admitted to the guild of teachers. The modern research professoriate developed in nineteenth-century Germany and spread worldwide.
