Comparison of note-taking software

Note-taking software comprises applications for creating, organising, searching and synchronising personal notes, ranging from simple text editors with folders to networked "second brain" tools with bidirectional links, databases and handwriting recognition. This article compares notable products by platform support, storage model, licence and features such as offline access, end-to-end encryption, handwriting input and web clipping.

Trends

Early digital notebooks imitated paper hierarchies of notebooks, sections and pages. More recent tools emphasise networked thought: block-based editors, backlinks and graph views (as in Roam Research and Obsidian), local-first storage in plain text formats for longevity, and AI-assisted search and summarisation. Handwriting and stylus support are central to tablet-focused apps such as GoodNotes and Notability, which are popular with students. Privacy-focused products differentiate on end-to-end encryption and self-hosting options.
