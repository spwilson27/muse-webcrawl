Hunsrückisch is a German dialect spoken in the Hunsrück region of Rhineland-Palatinate, Germany. It belongs to the Moselle Franconian and Rhine Franconian branches of Central German. Emigrants from the Hunsrück carried the dialect to southern Brazil in the 19th century, where its descendant, Riograndenser Hunsrückisch, developed and is still spoken by large numbers of German Brazilians, principally in Rio Grande do Sul, Santa Catarina, and Paraná.

The Hunsrück uplands, the dialect's homeland.

In Germany

In its homeland the dialect varies considerably from village to village, differing in whether local speech follows the Moselle Franconian pattern (with dat/wat) or the Rhine Franconian pattern (with das/was), split by the dat–das line running through the region. Like other regional dialects, Hunsrückisch has receded under the influence of standard German, schooling, and media, and is now strongest among older speakers in rural areas.

In Brazil

German immigration to Rio Grande do Sul began in 1824, and many settlers came from the Hunsrück. Over nearly two centuries their speech developed independently into Riograndenser Hunsrückisch, absorbing vocabulary from Portuguese and other contact languages while preserving archaic features lost in Germany. It is estimated to have been spoken by hundreds of thousands of people, and in some municipalities it holds co-official status alongside Portuguese. Linguists, notably Cléo Vilson Altenhofen, have documented the dialect extensively.
