The Claremont Colleges (known colloquially as the 7Cs) are a consortium of seven private institutions of higher education located in Claremont, California, United States. They were founded in 1925 on the model of the collegiate universities of Oxford and Cambridge, in which autonomous colleges share central resources while retaining distinct identities and missions.

Member institutions

The consortium comprises five undergraduate colleges and two graduate institutions:

  • Pomona College (founded 1887) — the founding member, a coeducational liberal arts college.
  • Claremont Graduate University (1925) — a graduate-only research university.
  • Scripps College (1926) — a women's liberal arts college.
  • Claremont McKenna College (1946) — known for economics, government, and public affairs.
  • Harvey Mudd College (1955) — focused on science, engineering, and mathematics.
  • Pitzer College (1963) — emphasizing the social sciences and social responsibility.
  • Keck Graduate Institute (1997) — graduate institute for applied life sciences and pharmacy.

Structure and shared resources

The colleges occupy roughly 546 acres of contiguous campus and share facilities including the Claremont Colleges Library, dining halls, health services, and intercollegiate athletic programs (Pomona–Pitzer Sagehens and Claremont–Mudd–Scripps Stags and Athenas). Students may cross-register for courses at any of the member institutions and typically graduate with degrees from their home college. Claremont Colleges Services (TCCS) provides central administrative support. The consortium is frequently cited as one of the leading clusters of liberal arts colleges in the United States.

History

The plan for a cluster of colleges was developed in the 1920s by Pomona College president James A. Blaisdell, who envisioned small residential colleges federated around shared resources, modeled on Oxford and Cambridge. Claremont Graduate University was the first new institution founded under the plan in 1925, followed by Scripps College in 1926. The model expanded over the following decades as new colleges were added, most recently Keck Graduate Institute in 1997.
