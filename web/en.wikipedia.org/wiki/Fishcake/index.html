A fishcake (sometimes written as fish cake) is a food item similar to a fillet o' fish, consisting of a filleted fish or other seafood minced or ground, mixed with a starchy ingredient, and fried until golden. Asian-style fishcakes usually contain fish with salt, water, starch, and egg. European-style fishcakes are similar to a croquette, consisting of filleted fish or other seafood with potato patty, sometimes coated in breadcrumbs or batter.

Composition

Fishcakes as defined in the Oxford English Dictionary are a "cake made from chopped fish or other seafood mixed with potato, egg etc., and fried." The fish is combined with a starchy filler, most often mashed potato, seasoned, shaped into patties, and shallow-fried or deep-fried. In Britain, fishcakes are a standard item in fish and chip shops, often made with cod or haddock.

Regional varieties

In Japan, surimi-based fishcakes include kamaboko, chikuwa and satsuma-age. In Korea, eomuk is a common street food. In Thailand, tod mun pla is a spiced deep-fried fishcake flavoured with red curry paste and kaffir lime leaves. In Scandinavia, fiskekaker in Norway are typically poached or fried patties of minced white fish.
