Eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE) is a chronic, immune- and allergen-mediated disease of the esophagus in which eosinophils, a type of white blood cell, accumulate in the esophageal lining, causing inflammation and, over time, fibrosis and narrowing.

Signs and symptoms

Adults typically present with dysphagia (difficulty swallowing solids) and episodes of food bolus impaction; children may have feeding difficulty, vomiting, abdominal pain and poor growth. EoE is strongly associated with atopic conditions such as asthma, eczema and food allergy, and is more common in males.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis requires endoscopy—often showing rings ("trachealization"), linear furrows, white exudates and strictures—plus biopsies showing at least 15 eosinophils per high-power field, after excluding other causes.

Treatment

Management includes dietary elimination of trigger foods (commonly milk and wheat), swallowed topical corticosteroids such as fluticasone or budesonide, proton-pump inhibitors, esophageal dilation for strictures, and the biologic dupilumab, approved for EoE in 2022.

Epidemiology

Prevalence has risen markedly since the disease was characterized in the 1990s, now estimated at roughly 1 in 2,000 people in Western countries.
