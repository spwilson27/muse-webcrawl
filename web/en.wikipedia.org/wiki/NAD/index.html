NAD

NAD most commonly refers to nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, a central coenzyme in cellular metabolism. This page lists other uses of the abbreviation.

Structure of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+), the most common referent of the abbreviation NAD.

Science and technology

  • Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD) — a coenzyme involved in redox reactions, carrying electrons from one reaction to another; found in all living cells. Its phosphorylated relative is NADP.
  • National Academy of Design — an honorary association of American artists, founded in New York in 1825.
  • NAD Electronics — originally "New Acoustic Dimension", a manufacturer of hi-fi audio equipment founded in 1972.
  • North American Datum — geodetic datums (NAD27, NAD83) used for mapping and surveying in North America.

Other uses

  • Namibian dollar (ISO currency code NAD) — the currency of Namibia, pegged to the South African rand.
  • National Advertising Division of the BBB National Programs in the United States.
  • Nothing abnormal detected — a shorthand annotation in medical notes.
  • Nad — a place in County Cork, Ireland, and several villages elsewhere.

NAD+ and its reduced form NADH play key roles in energy production: they shuttle electrons in glycolysis, the citric acid cycle, and oxidative phosphorylation. NAD is synthesised from dietary precursors including niacin (vitamin B3) and the amino acid tryptophan; its deficiency causes the disease pellagra. Beyond redox chemistry, NAD+ is consumed by enzymes such as sirtuins, PARPs, and CD38 in signalling and DNA-repair processes, which has made NAD metabolism a major focus of ageing research.
