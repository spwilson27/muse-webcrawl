Kate Raworth (born 1970) is an English economist known for developing "doughnut economics," a framework that seeks to meet the needs of all people within the ecological means of the planet. She is a senior associate at the Environmental Change Institute at the University of Oxford and a co-founder of the Doughnut Economics Action Lab (DEAL).

Early life and career

Raworth studied Philosophy, Politics and Economics at the University of Oxford, followed by a master's degree in economics for development. She spent four years working with micro-entrepreneurs in Zanzibar, then co-authored the United Nations Development Programme's Human Development Report from 1997 to 2001. From 2001 to 2013 she was a senior researcher at Oxfam, and during this period developed the doughnut framework.

Doughnut economics

In 2012 Raworth published an Oxfam discussion paper, A Safe and Just Space for Humanity, which combined the concept of planetary boundaries—developed by Johan Rockström and colleagues at the Stockholm Resilience Centre—with a social foundation of twelve dimensions of human well-being drawn from international human rights declarations. The resulting diagram resembles a ring doughnut: the hole represents deprivation, in which people lack life's essentials; the space beyond the outer ring represents ecological overshoot; and the doughnut itself is the "safe and just space" for humanity. Her 2017 book Doughnut Economics expanded the model into a broader critique of mainstream economic thinking and a call for economies that are regenerative and distributive by design.

Influence

The model attracted wide attention in policy circles. In April 2020 the city of Amsterdam adopted the doughnut as a framework for its post-COVID-19 recovery strategy, working with Raworth's team to produce a "City Doughnut" portrait. Variants have since been adopted by other cities and regions, and Raworth has addressed bodies including the United Nations General Assembly.
