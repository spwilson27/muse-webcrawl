White Hart Lane was a football stadium in Tottenham, North London, and the home of Tottenham Hotspur Football Club from 1899 until its closure in 2017. It stood on the High Road, and its name derived from the nearby White Hart public house and the lane beside it. The stadium was demolished in 2017 and replaced on an overlapping site by the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, which opened in 2019.

History

Tottenham Hotspur moved to the site in 1899, converting a former nursery ground. The first match at White Hart Lane was a friendly against Notts County on 4 September 1899, which Spurs won 4–1. The ground was developed extensively under architect Archibald Leitch in the early 20th century, and its capacity at its peak exceeded 70,000. The record attendance was 75,038, set for an FA Cup tie against Sunderland in 1938.

The stadium's four stands were the West Stand, the North Stand (Paxton Road), the East Stand, and the South Stand (Park Lane) — the latter two lending their names to the famous ends where the club's most vocal supporters gathered. White Hart Lane hosted many of the club's great European nights, including the 1963 European Cup Winners' Cup run and the 1984 UEFA Cup final victory.

Closure and demolition

By the 2010s Tottenham sought a larger stadium to compete financially with rivals. Under the Northumberland Development Project, a new stadium was built partly on the White Hart Lane site. The final match at the old ground was played on 14 May 2017, a 2–1 victory over Manchester United, followed by a farewell ceremony. Demolition was completed later in 2017, and the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium opened in April 2019.

Other uses

The ground occasionally hosted other events, including boxing, baseball during the Second World War era, and American football exhibitions. It also gave its name to the nearby White Hart Lane railway station.
