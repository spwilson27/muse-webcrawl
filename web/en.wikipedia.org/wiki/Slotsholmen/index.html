Slotsholmen

Slotsholmen (English: The Castle Islet) is an island in the harbour of Copenhagen, Denmark, and part of Copenhagen's Inner City. The name is derived from the several castles and palaces that have been located on the island since Bishop Absalon constructed the first castle on the island in 1167, at the site where Christiansborg Palace, the seat of the Danish Parliament, lies today.

Recognised as the centre of power in Denmark, many governmental institutions are also located on the island. In addition to the palace, these include the Danish Parliament building, the Supreme Court, the Prime Minister's Office, and the historic buildings of the Old Stock Exchange, the Church of Holmen nearby, the Black Diamond extension of the Royal Library, the Thorvaldsens Museum, and the Jewish Museum.

History

The island was originally one of several islets that formed between the mainland of Zealand and the island of Amager. From the founding of Copenhagen, it grew in importance as successive castles were built there: Absalon's Castle, Copenhagen Castle, and three successive Christiansborg palaces, two of which burned down.

Christiansborg

The present Christiansborg Palace, completed in the early 20th century, is unique in housing all three branches of government under one roof: the executive branch (the Prime Minister's Office), the legislative branch (the Folketing), and the judicial branch (the Supreme Court). The palace also contains the Royal Reception Rooms used by the Danish monarch.
