Lost Battalion (World War I)

Lost Battalion

Dates: 2–7 October 1918

Location: Argonne Forest, France

Unit: 308th Infantry Regiment, 77th Division, U.S. Army

Commander: Major Charles W. Whittlesey

The Lost Battalion is the name given to the nine companies of the United States 77th Division, roughly 554 men, isolated by German forces during World War I after an American attack in the Argonne Forest in October 1918. Roughly 197 were killed in action and approximately 150 missing or taken prisoner before the relief, 194 remaining were able to walk out of their pocket.

The entrapment

As part of the Meuse-Argonne Offensive, Major Charles W. Whittlesey's battalion advanced on 2 October 1918 beyond the units on its flanks, which had been held up, and occupied a position along the Charlevaux ravine. Unaware that the French and American units beside them had not kept pace, the men were soon surrounded and cut off, enduring days of German attacks, friendly artillery fire, hunger and thirst.

Cher Ami

The battalion's plight became famous through its carrier pigeons. The last pigeon, Cher Ami, was released carrying a message that stopped the friendly barrage falling on the Americans — "Our own artillery is dropping a barrage directly on us. For heaven's sake stop it." — despite being shot through the breast and losing a leg, the bird delivered the message and became a decorated hero.

Relief and legacy

After five days, relief forces reached the survivors on the evening of 7 October. Whittlesey was awarded the Medal of Honor. The episode became one of the most celebrated small-unit actions of the American war and has been dramatised in film and literature.
