Vercors Massif

Highest pointGrand Veymont
Elevation2,341 m (7,680 ft)
LocationDrôme and Isère, France
RangeFrench Prealps

The Vercors Massif is a mountain range in eastern France, in the departments of Drôme and Isère, forming part of the French Prealps. It is a great limestone plateau bounded by steep cliffs, separated from the rest of the Alps by the valleys of the Isère, Drôme, and Drac rivers. Its highest summit is the Grand Veymont, at 2,341 metres.

The cliffs of the Vercors plateau near the Col de Rousset.

Geography

The massif is roughly 30 km by 40 km and is characterised by karst landscapes: caves, sinkholes, underground rivers, and narrow gorges such as the Gorges de la Bourne. Much of the area is protected as the Vercors Regional Natural Park, established in 1970, one of the largest regional parks in France. The plateau includes extensive forests and high pastures, and its cliffs make it popular for climbing, caving, cross-country skiing, and hiking. The Mont Aiguille, a striking isolated mesa at the eastern edge, is famous as the site of one of the first ascents in mountaineering history, in 1492.

History

During the Second World War the Vercors became a stronghold of the French Resistance, the Maquis du Vercors. In the summer of 1944, as part of the plan to support the Allied landings, the maquisards proclaimed the Free Republic of the Vercors. German forces, including glider-borne troops, attacked in July 1944 and crushed the resistance with great brutality; hundreds of fighters and civilians were killed, and villages such as Vassieux-en-Vercors were destroyed. The events are commemorated by memorials throughout the massif.
