Immunosuppressive drug

Immunosuppressive drugs
UsesOrgan transplantation; autoimmune diseases
Major classesCalcineurin inhibitors, antimetabolites, corticosteroids, mTOR inhibitors, biologics
Key risksInfection, malignancy, organ toxicity

Immunosuppressive drugs (also called immunosuppressants or antirejection medicines) are drugs that inhibit or prevent activity of the immune system. They are used in immunosuppressive therapy to prevent the rejection of transplanted organs and tissues, and to treat autoimmune diseases such as rheumatoid arthritis, multiple sclerosis, Crohn's disease and lupus, in which the immune system attacks the body's own tissues.

Classes

  • Corticosteroids (e.g. prednisone, methylprednisolone): broad anti-inflammatory and immunosuppressive agents used since the 1950s.
  • Calcineurin inhibitors (ciclosporin, tacrolimus): revolutionised transplantation from the 1980s by blocking T-cell activation via interleukin-2.
  • Antimetabolites (azathioprine, mycophenolate): inhibit lymphocyte proliferation.
  • mTOR inhibitors (sirolimus, everolimus): block a later stage of T-cell proliferation.
  • Biologics: monoclonal antibodies and fusion proteins such as basiliximab, rituximab and TNF inhibitors (e.g. infliximab), targeting specific immune components.

Use in transplantation

Recipients of kidney, liver, heart and other transplants typically receive combination therapy: an induction agent around the time of surgery, followed by lifelong maintenance therapy, usually a calcineurin inhibitor with an antimetabolite, with or without steroids. Doses are balanced between preventing rejection and limiting toxicity.

Adverse effects

Suppression of the immune system increases susceptibility to bacterial, viral and fungal infections and to certain cancers, especially skin cancer and post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder. Individual drugs carry specific risks: ciclosporin and tacrolimus can damage the kidneys and cause tremor and diabetes; corticosteroids cause weight gain, osteoporosis and metabolic disturbance. Patients require monitoring of drug levels, vaccination review and cancer screening.
