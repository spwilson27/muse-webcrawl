Niche (architecture)

In architecture, a niche (from Italian nicchia, "shell") is a recess or cavity in a wall, typically arched or semicircular in plan, designed to hold a statue, urn, or other decorative object. Niches have been used since antiquity to enliven wall surfaces and to display sculpture.

History

Niches appear in Roman architecture, lining tombs, temples and baths, and in the exedrae of public buildings. In Renaissance and Baroque churches and palaces they were framed with classical orders and used for statues of saints and heroes. In Islamic architecture the related mihrab is a niche in a mosque wall indicating the direction of Mecca.

Types

Niches may be simple rectangular recesses or elaborate aedicules with columns, entablature and pediment. In burial contexts, a niche in a columbarium holds funerary urns. Garden walls and facades also use blind niches purely as ornament.
