Phenylpropene

Phenylpropene
Chemical formulaC9H10 (parent hydrocarbon)
ClassAromatic hydrocarbons / phenylpropanoid derivatives
Representative membersAllylbenzene, cis/trans-β-methylstyrene, propenylbenzene

Phenylpropenes are a class of organic compounds comprising a benzene ring attached to a three-carbon propene chain (C6H5–C3H5 skeleton). They form the unsaturated hydrocarbon parents of the much larger family of phenylpropanoids found in plants.

Isomers

The position and geometry of the double bond give several isomers. Allylbenzene (3-phenylpropene) has a terminal double bond; propenylbenzene (1-phenyl-1-propene, β-methylstyrene) has an internal double bond and exists as cis and trans isomers, the trans form predominating in nature and commerce.

Occurrence and related compounds

Substituted phenylpropenes are widespread components of essential oils and spices. Important examples include eugenol (from clove oil), isoeugenol, estragole (methyl chavicol, from tarragon and basil), anethole (from anise and fennel), safrole, and elemicin. These oxygenated derivatives are biosynthesised from phenylalanine via the shikimate pathway and underpin the flavours and fragrances of many culinary plants, as well as serving as precursors to lignin and other phenylpropanoid natural products.
