Ayushman Bharat

Ayushman Bharat is a national health protection programme of the Government of India, launched in 2018 by Prime Minister Narendra Modi, aimed at moving towards universal health coverage. It has two main components: the Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana (PM-JAY), a health insurance scheme, and a network of Health and Wellness Centres (now Ayushman Arogya Mandirs) delivering primary care.

PM-JAY

PM-JAY, launched on 23 September 2018, provides cashless cover of ₹5 lakh (500,000 rupees) per family per year for secondary and tertiary hospitalisation to India's poorest households — originally about 100 million families, or 500 million people, identified from the Socio-Economic Caste Census. Beneficiaries receive an Ayushman card and can use empanelled public and private hospitals across India. In 2024 the scheme was extended to all citizens aged 70 and above regardless of income.

The programme aimed to upgrade 150,000 sub-centres and primary health centres into Health and Wellness Centres offering expanded primary care, including screening for non-communicable diseases. A third strand, the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission, creates digital health IDs (ABHA) and registries to link health records across providers.
