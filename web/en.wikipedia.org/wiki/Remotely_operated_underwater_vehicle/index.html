Remotely operated underwater vehicle

A remotely operated underwater vehicle (ROV) is a free-swimming submersible craft used to perform tasks such as valve operations, hydraulic functions and other general tasks within the subsea oil and gas industry. ROVs can also carry tooling packages for undertaking specific tasks such as pull-in of flexible flowlines and umbilicals, and component replacement.

An ROV is an unoccupied underwater robot that is connected to a ship by a series of cables (an umbilical or tether) that transmit power and data signals between the operator aboard the ship and the vehicle, allowing remote navigation and control of the vehicle. Cameras and lights allow the operator to view the underwater environment in real time.

Classes

ROVs range from small observation-class vehicles, little more than self-propelled cameras used for inspection, to large work-class vehicles equipped with manipulator arms, tooling skids, and thrusters capable of heavy construction and maintenance work at depths of several thousand metres. Micro and mini ROVs are widely used for hull inspection, aquaculture, search and recovery, and scientific sampling.

Applications

Beyond the offshore energy industry, ROVs are used in oceanographic research, military mine countermeasures, wreck exploration, and infrastructure inspection. Famous scientific ROVs include the Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution's Jason and Nereus (the latter lost in 2014 during a dive in the Kermadec Trench), and NOAA's exploration vehicles used to document deep-sea ecosystems and shipwrecks.
