Düsseldorf school of painting

Düsseldorf school of painting
Years active: c. 1819–1918
Location: Düsseldorf, Germany
Centre: Kunstakademie Düsseldorf
Major figures: Wilhelm von Schadow, Johann Wilhelm Schirmer, Andreas Achenbach, Emanuel Leutze

The Düsseldorf school of painting is a term referring to a group of painters who taught or studied at the Düsseldorf Academy of Art (Kunstakademie Düsseldorf) in the 19th and early 20th centuries, when the academy was directed by the painter Wilhelm von Schadow. The school became internationally renowned, attracting artists from Germany, Scandinavia, Russia and the United States.

Characteristics

The school was initially associated with landscape painting in the Romantic tradition, characterised by meticulous detail, dramatic lighting and carefully observed nature. Johann Wilhelm Schirmer, the first professor of landscape painting at the academy, was central to its early development. Works often featured the scenery of the Rhine valley and the Lower Rhine region.

International influence

American artists associated with the school, including Emanuel Leutze, Worthington Whittredge and Albert Bierstadt, helped carry its influence to the Hudson River School in the United States. Leutze's Washington Crossing the Delaware (1851) is among its most famous products. Scandinavian painters such as Hans Gude also studied in Düsseldorf and reshaped landscape painting in Norway.

Notable artists

Other prominent members included Andreas and Oswald Achenbach, Carl Friedrich Lessing, Adolph Tidemand and August Leu. In the later 19th century the school declined as new movements such as Impressionism arose elsewhere in Europe.
