The League of Legends World Championship (commonly abbreviated as Worlds) is the annual world championship tournament for the video game League of Legends, organised by Riot Games. Held at the end of each competitive season, it brings together the top professional teams from regional leagues around the world to compete for the Summoner's Cup and the title of world champion. It is one of the most watched events in esports, with finals drawing audiences of tens of millions of concurrent and total viewers.

History

The first championship was held in 2011 at DreamHack in Jönköping, Sweden, with a modest prize pool of $100,000 and victory for the European team Fnatic. As League of Legends grew into the world's most-played PC game, Worlds expanded rapidly: the 2012 final in Los Angeles was won by Taiwan's Taipei Assassins, and South Korean teams then began a long period of dominance. The 2017 final filled Beijing's National ("Bird's Nest") Stadium, and opening ceremonies featuring elaborate augmented-reality performances — such as the virtual K-pop group K/DA (2018) and a holographic performance in 2019 — became signature spectacles.

Format

The tournament format has evolved over time, typically comprising a play-in stage for emerging-region teams, a group or Swiss stage, and a single-elimination knockout bracket of best-of-five series. In 2023 the main stage adopted a Swiss format before the quarter-finals. The winning team traditionally receives in-game championship skins commemorating its roster.

South Korea's T1 (formerly SK Telecom T1), led by mid-laner Lee Sang-hyeok ("Faker"), is the most successful organisation in the tournament's history, winning five titles (2013, 2015, 2016, 2023 and 2024); Faker is widely regarded as the greatest player in the game's history.
