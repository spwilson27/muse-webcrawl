Beatrix of the Netherlands

Reign: 30 April 1980 – 30 April 2013
Born: 31 January 1938, Soestdijk Palace, Baarn
Predecessor: Juliana
Successor: Willem-Alexander

Beatrix (Beatrix Wilhelmina Armgard; born 31 January 1938) is a member of the Dutch royal house who reigned as Queen of the Netherlands from 1980 until her abdication in 2013. She is the eldest daughter of Queen Juliana and Prince Bernhard, and since her abdication she has been styled Princess Beatrix.

Early life and education

Beatrix was born at Soestdijk Palace and spent much of the Second World War in exile in Canada. She studied law at Leiden University, graduating in 1961, and received honorary doctorates. In 1966 she married the German diplomat Claus von Amsberg, a match that provoked protests at the time but who became a popular prince consort. The couple had three sons: Willem-Alexander, Friso, and Constantijn.

Reign

Beatrix succeeded her mother on 30 April 1980. Her inauguration in Amsterdam was marred by serious riots under the slogan "no housing, no coronation". She was noted for a more formal and businesslike style than her mother, took an active interest in government formation and state visits, and worked closely with successive prime ministers. She endured personal losses during her reign, including the death of Prince Claus in 2002. On 28 January 2013 she announced her intention to abdicate, and on 30 April 2013 — exactly 33 years after her accession — her son Willem-Alexander succeeded her.

Later life

As Princess Beatrix she continues to perform some public duties and lives at Drakensteyn Castle. Her younger son, Prince Friso, died in 2013 after a skiing accident. She remains a widely respected figure in Dutch public life.
