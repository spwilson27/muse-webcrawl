Faras Cathedral

LocationFaras, Nubia (present-day Sudan)
Period7th–14th centuries
ReligionChristianity (Coptic / Byzantine rite)
Famous forMedieval Nubian wall paintings

Faras Cathedral was a cathedral church in the ancient Nubian city of Faras, in what is now northern Sudan near the border with Egypt. It served as the seat of the bishops of Faras in the Christian kingdom of Makuria and is renowned for the extraordinary collection of medieval wall paintings preserved inside it.

History

A first church on the site was built in the seventh century, shortly after the conversion of Nubia to Christianity. The cathedral was repeatedly rebuilt and enlarged, most notably under Bishop Paulos in the early eighth century and again in the tenth and eleventh centuries. As the city declined in the late medieval period, the cathedral was gradually abandoned and filled with sand, which protected its paintings.

Excavation and the paintings

The cathedral was excavated in the 1960s by a Polish archaeological mission led by Kazimierz Michałowski, working as part of the UNESCO campaign to save Nubian monuments threatened by the construction of the Aswan High Dam and the flooding of Lake Nasser. Archaeologists uncovered more than 120 wall paintings dating from the eighth to the fourteenth centuries, depicting Christ, the Virgin Mary, saints, angels, and Nubian kings and bishops under divine protection. The paintings were detached from the walls and divided between the National Museum in Khartoum and the Faras Gallery of the National Museum in Warsaw, where they form one of the largest collections of medieval Nubian art in the world.

Significance

Faras Cathedral is considered a key monument of Nubian Christianity, and its paintings — including the famous image of Saint Anne and the depiction of the Three Youths in the Fiery Furnace — are masterpieces of medieval African Christian art.
