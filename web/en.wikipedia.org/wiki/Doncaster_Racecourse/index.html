Doncaster Racecourse

Doncaster Racecourse (also known as the Town Moor course) is a horse racing venue in Doncaster, South Yorkshire, England. It is one of the oldest established centres for horse racing in Britain, with records of regular race meetings going back to the 16th century. Doncaster is home to the St Leger Stakes, run since 1776 and the world's oldest classic horse race still regularly contested.

History

Racing at Doncaster can be traced to the 1500s; the corporation attempted to ban racing in 1603 because of the crowds of ruffians it attracted, but by 1614 the course had its own Gold Cup. The St Leger, founded by Anthony St Leger and first run in 1776, became the final leg of the English Triple Crown. The Lincoln Handicap, traditionally the first major race of the flat season, has been held at Doncaster since 1965, having moved from Lincoln.

The course

The track is a left-handed, pear-shaped, galloping course of almost two miles, generally flat apart from a slight rise in the home straight. It stages both flat racing in the summer and National Hunt (jump) racing in the winter. Following a major redevelopment completed in 2007, the course gained a new five-storey grandstand and modern facilities, and is operated by Arena Racing Company.

Major races

  • St Leger Stakes (September)
  • Racing Post Trophy (October), a leading Group 1 race for two-year-olds
  • Doncaster Cup, one of Britain's oldest races, established in 1766
  • Lincoln Handicap (March/April)
  • November Handicap
