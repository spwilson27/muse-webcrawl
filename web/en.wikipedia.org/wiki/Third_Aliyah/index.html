Third Aliyah


Pioneers paving a road during the Third Aliyah

The Third Aliyah (Hebrew: העלייה השלישית) refers to the third wave of Jewish immigration to Palestine from Europe, covering the period from 1919 to 1923. Approximately 40,000 Jewish immigrants arrived during these years, mostly young pioneers (halutzim) from Eastern Europe.

Background

The Third Aliyah was a direct continuation of the Second Aliyah, interrupted by the First World War. Its main catalysts were the Balfour Declaration of 1917, the British conquest of Palestine from the Ottoman Empire and the establishment of the British Mandate, and the upheavals in Eastern Europe following the Russian Revolution and civil war, including pogroms in Ukraine.

Character

The immigrants were strongly influenced by socialist and pioneering ideals. The Third Aliyah established many of the institutions that shaped Jewish society in the Mandate period:

  • HeHalutz — the pioneering movement that trained young Jews for agricultural labour before emigration.
  • Histadrut — the General Federation of Labour, founded in 1920.
  • Gdud HaAvoda — the Joseph Trumpeldor Labour Battalion, which undertook large public works such as road and railway construction.
  • Kibbutzim and moshavim — new collective and cooperative settlements were founded, including the first large kibbutz, Ein Harod, in 1921.

The Haganah, the main Jewish paramilitary organization, was also founded in 1920 in response to Arab riots. The wave subsided around 1923 owing to an economic crisis in Palestine, and was followed by the Fourth Aliyah beginning in 1924, which brought a more middle-class immigration from Poland.
