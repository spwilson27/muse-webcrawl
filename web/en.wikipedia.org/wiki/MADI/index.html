MADI

MADI connections on professional audio equipment

Multichannel Audio Digital Interface (MADI) or AES10 is a standard published by the Audio Engineering Society (AES) for the transmission of many channels of digital audio over a single cable. It is widely used in recording studios, broadcast facilities and live sound for connecting mixing consoles, stage boxes, multitrack recorders and audio interfaces.

Overview

Originally standardized in 1991, MADI carries up to 64 channels of 24-bit audio at a 48 kHz sample rate (fewer channels at higher sample rates, such as 32 channels at 96 kHz) over coaxial cable with BNC connectors or over optical fiber. The electrical version supports cable runs of up to about 50 metres, while optical links can extend much further, making MADI popular for connecting stages to front-of-house positions.

Technical details

MADI transmits audio serially at 125 Mbit/s, using a data format derived from FDDI networking technology. Channels are time-multiplexed, and the link includes synchronization information. Later revisions of AES10 increased channel counts and sample-rate flexibility. MADI is commonly compared with other multichannel audio transports such as ADAT Lightpipe, AES3, Dante and AES67; it remains valued for its simplicity, low latency and point-to-point reliability.
