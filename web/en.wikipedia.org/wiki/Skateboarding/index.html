Skateboarding

Skateboarding is an action sport and recreational activity that involves riding and performing tricks using a skateboard. It originated in the United States in the late 1940s and early 1950s, when surfers in California sought a way to surf when the waves were flat — an activity originally dubbed "sidewalk surfing". Skateboarding has since developed a distinctive global subculture, its own equipment industry and media, and a wide variety of styles including street, park, vert and freestyle. It became an Olympic sport at the Tokyo 2020 Games.

History

The first commercially produced skateboards appeared in 1959, and the pastime boomed in the early 1960s. After a crash in popularity, the invention of urethane wheels in the early 1970s (notably by Frank Nasworthy and Cadillac Wheels) revolutionised the sport, giving riders far better grip and speed. In drought-stricken California, the Z-Boys of Venice and Santa Monica pioneered aggressive vertical skating in empty swimming pools, laying the foundations of modern "vert" and street styles. Alan "Ollie" Gelfand's invention of the ollie in 1978 enabled an entire vocabulary of aerial street tricks.

Skateboarding declined again in the late 1970s, revived through the 1980s with the growth of skate parks, videos and companies run by skaters themselves, and became firmly mainstream in the 1990s alongside events such as the X Games. Street skating, centred on tricks performed on urban obstacles such as stairs, handrails and ledges, became the dominant cultural style.

Equipment

A skateboard consists of a deck — typically seven or nine plies of maple wood — with grip tape on top, two metal trucks, four urethane wheels and bearings. Riders ("goofy" if the right foot leads, "regular" if the left) propel themselves by pushing, and perform tricks by manipulating the board with their feet, often using the kicktail.

Culture

Skateboarding is closely associated with a DIY ethic, distinctive fashion, punk and hip-hop music, photography and videography. Municipal hostility — anti-skateboarding laws and defensive architecture — has been a recurring theme, countered by the growth of public skateparks. Notable figures include Tony Hawk, Rodney Mullen, Nyjah Huston and, in the Olympic era, Sky Brown and Rayssa Leal.

Olympic inclusion

Skateboarding debuted at the Olympic Games in Tokyo in 2021 (held as Tokyo 2020) with street and park events for men and women, and returned at Paris 2024.
