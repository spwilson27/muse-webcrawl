Thomas Hardy (2 June 1840 – 11 January 1928) was an English novelist and poet. A Victorian realist influenced by George Eliot and Charles Dickens, and by Romantic poetry, he was also highly critical of Victorian society.

Life

Born in Higher Bockhampton, Dorset, Hardy trained as an architect in Dorchester and London before turning to writing. He lived much of his life at Max Gate, Dorchester, in the fictionalised 'Wessex' of his novels.

Novels

His major novels—Under the Greenwood Tree, Far from the Madding Crowd (1874), The Return of the Native, The Mayor of Casterbridge (1886), The Woodlanders, Tess of the d'Urbervilles (1891) and Jude the Obscure (1895)—depict characters struggling against fate, chance and social convention. Scandalised reception of the last two drove him to abandon fiction.

Poetry

Hardy always considered himself a poet first; his verse—including poems for his late wife Emma, the epic-drama The Dynasts, and some 900 poems—deeply influenced 20th-century poets from Robert Frost to Philip Larkin.
