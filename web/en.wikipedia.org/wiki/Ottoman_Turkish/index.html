Ottoman Turkish

Ottoman Turkish (لسان عثمانی, Lisân-ı Osmânî) was the variety of the Turkish language used as the administrative and literary language of the Ottoman Empire. It was written in a modified form of the Arabic script and contained a very large proportion of loanwords from Arabic and Persian, in some formal registers making it largely incomprehensible to ordinary Turkish speakers.

The language developed through several phases, from Old Anatolian Turkish to the highly Persianised classical court language of the 16th–19th centuries. In the 19th century, reformers of the Tanzimat era sought to simplify the written language, and journals and novels helped narrow the gap between written and spoken Turkish.

After the founding of the Republic of Turkey, the 1928 alphabet reform replaced the Arabic script with a Latin-based alphabet, and the subsequent language reform led by the Turkish Language Association replaced many Arabic and Persian borrowings with Turkish-derived words, producing modern standard Turkish. Ottoman Turkish continues to be studied by historians and is taught in Turkish universities for reading archival documents.
