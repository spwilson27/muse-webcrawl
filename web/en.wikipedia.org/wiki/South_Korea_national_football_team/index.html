South Korea national football team

The South Korea national football team (Korean: 대한민국 축구 국가대표팀) represents South Korea in men's international football and is governed by the Korea Football Association. Nicknamed the Taegeuk Warriors and supported by the Red Devils supporters' group, South Korea is widely regarded as one of the most successful national teams in Asia.

History

South Korea made its World Cup debut in 1954 in Switzerland. The team's golden moment came as co-hosts of the 2002 FIFA World Cup, when under Dutch coach Guus Hiddink it defeated Poland, Portugal, Italy, and Spain to reach the semi-finals — the best result ever achieved by an Asian nation at the tournament — before losing to Germany and finishing fourth. South Korea has qualified for every World Cup since 1986, a streak of consecutive appearances matched by few nations, and reached the round of 16 in 2010 and 2022.

In continental competition, South Korea won the first two AFC Asian Cups in 1956 and 1960, but has not recaptured the title since, finishing runners-up several times including in 2015. The team has also won gold medals at the Asian Games, which in recent editions has been contested by under-23 teams with limited senior involvement.

Notable players

Cha Bum-kun was a pioneering star in the German Bundesliga in the 1980s. Park Ji-sung, a Champions League winner with Manchester United, captained the side in the 2000s. Son Heung-min, of Tottenham Hotspur, is the team's most prominent modern player and captain, and among the most decorated Asian footballers in European club football. Other notable names include Hong Myung-bo, the most-capped outfield player of the 2002 side, and goalkeeper Kim Seung-gyu.

Rivalries

South Korea's fiercest rivalry is with Japan, matches between the two carrying historical and political overtones. Matches against North Korea, Iran, and Australia are also significant fixtures.
