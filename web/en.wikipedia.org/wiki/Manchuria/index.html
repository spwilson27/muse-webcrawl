Manchuria is a historical and geographical region in Northeast Asia. Depending on the definition, it encompasses the modern Chinese provinces of Heilongjiang, Jilin and Liaoning (the "Three Northeastern Provinces"), parts of Inner Mongolia, and, in the broader "Outer Manchuria", territory ceded by Qing China to the Russian Empire in 1858–1860, including Primorsky Krai and the area around Vladivostok. The region takes its name from the Manchu people, who founded the Qing dynasty that ruled China from 1644 to 1912.

Geography

Manchuria is bounded by the Greater Khingan mountains to the west, the Changbai mountains and the Yalu and Tumen rivers on the Korean frontier, the Amur (Heilongjiang) river to the north, and the Ussuri river to the east. Its heartland is the Manchurian Plain, drained by the Liao, Songhua and Nen rivers — one of China's great agricultural regions, famous for its fertile black soil (chernozem) and production of soybeans, maize, sorghum and wheat. Winters are long and extremely cold under the Siberian High, while summers are warm and monsoon-fed. Major cities include Shenyang (formerly Mukden), Harbin, Changchun, Dalian and Jilin City.

History

The region was historically home to Tungusic, Mongolic and Korean peoples and to kingdoms such as Goguryeo, Balhae, the Khitan Liao dynasty and the Jurchen Jin dynasty. In the early seventeenth century Nurhaci united the Jurchen tribes, and his descendants — renamed Manchus — conquered Ming China and founded the Qing dynasty. The Qing restricted Han settlement beyond the Willow Palisade, but from the late nineteenth century mass Han migration ("Chuang Guandong") transformed the region's demography.

Manchuria became a flashpoint of imperial rivalry between Russia and Japan, leading to the Russo-Japanese War (1904–1905). In 1931 the Japanese Kwantung Army staged the Mukden Incident and occupied the region, installing the puppet state of Manchukuo (1932–1945) under the former Qing emperor Puyi. After Japan's defeat in 1945, the region was a key arena of the resumed Chinese Civil War, and the Communists' victory in the Liaoshen Campaign of 1948 paved the way for the founding of the People's Republic of China in 1949.

Economy

Under the People's Republic, Manchuria became China's industrial heartland — the "rust belt" of heavy industry, steel (Anshan), oil (Daqing), coal, machinery and automobiles (Changchun). From the 1990s state-owned enterprise reform caused severe dislocation, and in 2003 the government launched the "Revitalize the Northeast" strategy to modernise the regional economy.
