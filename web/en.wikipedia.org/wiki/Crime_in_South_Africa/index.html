Crime in South Africa

Crime in South Africa
Governing bodySouth African Police Service (SAPS)
Notable issuesViolent crime, robbery, gender-based violence, farm attacks, gang violence

Crime in South Africa is a major social and political concern. South Africa has one of the highest reported rates of violent crime in the world, including murder, robbery, carjacking, and sexual offences. In recent years the murder rate has been roughly 45 per 100,000 people, with more than 27,000 murders recorded in a single year.

Causes and context

Analysts attribute high crime levels to a combination of factors: extreme inequality, high unemployment, the legacy of apartheid spatial planning, widespread firearm availability, organised crime and drug markets, and weaknesses in policing and the criminal justice system. A large private security industry, employing more guards than the police and army combined, has grown in response.

Categories

Particular concerns include gender-based violence and femicide, which prompted national protests and government action plans; gang violence on the Cape Flats; cash-in-transit heists; attacks on farms; and kidnapping for ransom, which increased sharply in the 2020s. Crime statistics are published annually by the South African Police Service, though under-reporting, especially of sexual offences, is widely acknowledged.

Responses

Government responses have included community policing forums, specialised units, and periodic military deployments in high-crime areas. Crime remains one of the most salient issues in South African elections.
