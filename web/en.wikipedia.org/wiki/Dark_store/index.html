Dark store

A dark store is a retail facility — typically a converted shop or purpose-built warehouse — that is closed to walk-in customers and used exclusively to fulfil online orders for pickup or delivery. The concept emerged with online grocery shopping in the United Kingdom in the early 2000s and expanded rapidly worldwide, especially during the COVID-19 pandemic and the rise of rapid "q-commerce" delivery services.

Operation

Dark stores are laid out like supermarkets but optimised for order pickers rather than shoppers: aisles, shelving and cold storage are arranged to maximise picking speed, and staff or gig-economy couriers assemble orders for home delivery, often within an hour or less. Because no browsing customers are present, stock control is tighter and operations can run longer hours. Locations are typically chosen near dense urban demand rather than for retail visibility.

Criticism and regulation

Critics argue that dark stores can hollow out high streets, generate courier traffic and noise, and operate in a regulatory grey area between retail and warehousing. Several cities, including Amsterdam and Paris, have moved to restrict new dark stores in residential and central districts. Some rapid-delivery operators have also integrated customer-facing storefronts by adding small lobbies, blurring the definition.
