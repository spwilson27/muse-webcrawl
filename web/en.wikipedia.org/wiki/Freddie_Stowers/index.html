Freddie Stowers

Freddie Stowers (January 12, 1896 – September 28, 1918) was an African American corporal in the United States Army who was killed in action during World War I while serving in an American unit attached to the French Army. He was posthumously awarded the Medal of Honor in 1991 — the only African American to receive the medal for service in World War I.

Born: January 12, 1896, Sandy Springs, South Carolina, U.S.
Died: September 28, 1918, Ardeuil-et-Montfauxelles, France
Unit: 371st Infantry Regiment, 93rd Division
Award: Medal of Honor (1991), Purple Heart, Croix de Guerre

Early life

Stowers was born in Sandy Springs, South Carolina, the grandson of an enslaved man, and worked as a farm labourer before being drafted in 1917. He was assigned to Company C of the 371st Infantry Regiment, an African American regiment of the segregated 93rd Division that the U.S. Army placed under French command, fighting in French helmets and with French weapons.

The action at Côte 188

On September 28, 1918, during the Meuse-Argonne Offensive, Stowers' squad attacked a German machine-gun position near Ardeuil-et-Montfauxelles. When the Germans feigned surrender and then opened fire, Stowers was mortally wounded, but he continued to direct his men, ordering them forward and destroying the nest, and urged them on against a second line of trenches before dying of his wounds. His squad took the position. He was 22 years old.

The long-delayed medal

Stowers was recommended for the Medal of Honor immediately after the action, but the paperwork was misplaced amid the Army's institutional racism, and he received only the Purple Heart and the French Croix de Guerre. In the late 1980s a congressional inquiry prompted an Army review, which recovered the recommendation. On April 24, 1991, President George H. W. Bush presented the Medal of Honor to Stowers' surviving sisters at the White House. Stowers is buried at the Meuse-Argonne American Cemetery in France, and barracks and schools in South Carolina bear his name.
