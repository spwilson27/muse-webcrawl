Book

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Photograph of a stack of hardcover books with cloth bindings]

A book is a medium for recording information in the form of writing or images, typically composed of many pages bound together and protected by a cover. Books are one of the oldest formats for storing and transmitting knowledge, and they may be produced in printed or electronic (e-book) and audio form.

History

Early books took forms including scrolls, clay tablets and codices; the codex of bound pages became standard in the first centuries CE. The invention of the printing press by Johannes Gutenberg in the 15th century — and the Gutenberg Bible of the 1450s — transformed book production in Europe, making copies far cheaper and more plentiful.

Parts and production

A modern printed book typically includes a cover, title page, front matter, the main text divided into chapters, and back matter such as an index or bibliography. Books are commonly identified by an ISBN (International Standard Book Number).
