Spinon

A spinon is a quasiparticle that can arise in condensed matter systems through the phenomenon of spin–charge separation. In certain strongly correlated materials—most clearly in one-dimensional systems such as quantum wires and antiferromagnetic spin chains—the electron can effectively split into separate quasiparticles carrying its individual quantum numbers: the spinon carries the electron's spin (spin-1/2) but no charge, while its partner, the holon (or chargon), carries the charge but no spin. A third quasiparticle, the orbiton, can carry the orbital quantum number.

Spin chains

The canonical setting for spinons is the one-dimensional Heisenberg antiferromagnetic chain. Flipping a single spin in such a chain creates two domain walls, each carrying spin-1/2, which can separate and propagate independently through the chain; these propagating domain walls are spinons. Excitations of the interacting chain thus form a continuum of spinon pairs rather than the sharp magnon modes of higher-dimensional magnets, a prediction confirmed by neutron scattering experiments on materials such as KCuF3 and copper pyrazine dinitrate.

Quantum spin liquids

Spinons are also central excitations of quantum spin liquids, two- and three-dimensional states of matter in which spins remain disordered and highly entangled down to zero temperature. In the resonating valence bond picture, breaking a singlet bond produces two free spinons. Experimental signatures of fractionalized spinon excitations have been reported in candidate spin-liquid materials including herbertsmithite and certain organic salts, typically as broad continua in inelastic neutron scattering rather than sharp spin-wave peaks.
