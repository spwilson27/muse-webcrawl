Glavlit, the Main Administration for Literary and Publishing Affairs (Russian: Главное управление по делам литературы и издательств, Glavnoe upravlenie po delam literatury i izdatel'stv, abbreviated Главлит), was the official censorship and state-secrets protection agency of the Soviet Union. It was established by decree of the Council of People's Commissars on 6 June 1922 and operated continuously until 1991.

Functions

Glavlit exercised preventive censorship over all printed matter in the USSR — books, newspapers, journals, plays, radio and television scripts, and even labels and posters. Publishers were required to submit materials for approval before printing, and Glavlit censors, known as "political editors", could require deletions, rewrites or outright bans. The agency maintained a secret list of information prohibited from publication, the famous Perechen' ("List of information constituting state secrets"), covering topics from military and economic data to disasters, crime statistics and anything deemed harmful to the image of the state.

Beyond pre-publication censorship, Glavlit controlled the import of foreign printed matter, the purging of libraries of works by banned or repressed authors, and the work of the spetskhran (special storage) collections where restricted publications were kept.

History

Created under the People's Commissariat for Education (Narkompros), Glavlit expanded greatly in the 1930s under the leadership of figures such as Boris Volin, and its powers grew during the Great Purge and again in the post-war Zhdanovshchina. After Stalin's death its role gradually shifted from ideological policing toward protection of state secrets, although ideological censorship continued. Under glasnost in the late 1980s its powers were curtailed, and the 1990 USSR Law on the Press formally prohibited censorship. Glavlit was abolished in 1991 as the Soviet Union dissolved.
