Hawaiʻi Ponoʻī

"Hawaiʻi Ponoʻī" ("Hawaii's Own") is the anthem of the State of Hawaii in the United States. It was composed by King Kalākaua, the last king of the Hawaiian Kingdom, with music arranged by Captain Henri Berger, leader of the Royal Hawaiian Band. Written in 1874, it served as the national anthem of the Kingdom of Hawaii and was later adopted as the state anthem when Hawaii became a U.S. state.

History

Kalākaua wrote the lyrics in 1874 for a hymn sung at Kawaiahaʻo Church in Honolulu, to music adapted by Berger from the Prussian hymn "Heil dir im Siegerkranz." It replaced Liliʻuokalani's earlier anthem "He Mele Lāhui Hawaiʻi" and remained the kingdom's anthem through the overthrow of the monarchy in 1893, the Republic of Hawaii, and annexation by the United States in 1898. In 1967 the Hawaii State Legislature officially designated it the state anthem.

Lyrics

The anthem consists of three verses in the Hawaiian language and a repeated chorus praising the king (Ka moʻī) and the chiefs of the land. The first verse begins "Hawaiʻi ponoʻī / Nānā i kou mōʻī" ("Hawaii's own, look to your king").

Usage

"Hawaiʻi Ponoʻī" is sung at official state functions and at sporting events in Hawaii, often alongside "The Star-Spangled Banner" and "Hawaiʻi Aloha." Its performance remains a symbol of Native Hawaiian identity and of the former monarchy.
