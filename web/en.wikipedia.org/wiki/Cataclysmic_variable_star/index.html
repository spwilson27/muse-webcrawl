Cataclysmic variable star

Cataclysmic variable stars (CVs) are binary star systems consisting of a white dwarf primary accreting matter from a closely orbiting secondary star, usually a low-mass main-sequence star that fills its Roche lobe. The transferred gas forms an accretion disk (except in strongly magnetic "polars", where the white dwarf's field channels the flow) and produces dramatic, irregular changes in brightness.

Types

Novae undergo rare, enormous outbursts ( brightening by 7–16 magnitudes) caused by a thermonuclear runaway in hydrogen accumulated on the white dwarf's surface. Dwarf novae such as SS Cygni and U Geminorum show smaller, frequent outbursts of 2–6 magnitudes from instabilities in the accretion disk. Nova-like variables remain in a high-luminosity state; magnetic systems include polars (AM Herculis stars), whose white dwarfs rotate synchronously with the orbit, and intermediate polars, which spin faster.

Evolution and significance

Angular momentum loss through magnetic braking and gravitational radiation drives mass transfer and shrinks the orbit toward a "period minimum" near 80 minutes. CVs are important laboratories for accretion physics and for the study of binary evolution; the accumulation of mass also raises the question of whether some systems can approach the Chandrasekhar limit and explode as Type Ia supernovae.
