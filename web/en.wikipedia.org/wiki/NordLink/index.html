NordLink is a subsea high-voltage direct current (HVDC) power cable between Norway and Germany, opened for trial operation in December 2020 and officially inaugurated on 27 May 2021. The 623-kilometre interconnector runs from Tonstad in Norway, across the North Sea, to Wilster in Schleswig-Holstein, Germany. With a capacity of 1,400 MW, it was designed to exchange Norwegian hydropower with German wind and solar generation.

Description

The link consists of about 516 km of submarine cable through Norwegian and Danish waters, a section of overhead line on the Norwegian side, and underground cable in Germany. It operates as a bipole at ±525 kV using voltage-source converters. The project was developed by the Norwegian transmission system operator Statnett together with TenneT and the German state-owned bank KfW, at a cost of roughly €1.5–2 billion.

Purpose

The interconnector allows Norway's flexible hydropower reservoirs to act as storage for variable German wind and solar output: Germany can export surplus renewable electricity to Norway, which can then reduce its own hydro generation, and import hydroelectric power when German renewable output is low. NordLink was the first direct power link between the two countries and a flagship project of European grid integration.
