Licht ("Light"), subtitled "The Seven Days of the Week", is a cycle of seven operas by the German composer Karlheinz Stockhausen, composed between 1977 and 2003. With roughly 29 hours of music, it is one of the largest works in the operatic repertoire. Each opera is named for a day of the week and is associated with a planet, colours and scents.

Dramatis personae and structure

The cycle revolves around three central figures: Michael (associated with Thursday and Saturday), Eve (Monday and Friday) and Lucifer (Tuesday and Saturday), with Wednesday as a day of their cooperation. The operas — Donnerstag (Thursday), Samstag (Saturday), Montag (Monday), Dienstag (Tuesday), Freitag (Friday), Mittwoch (Wednesday) and Sonntag (Sunday) — combine singing, instrumental performance, dance, mime and electronics, and are built from a single musical "super-formula". Individual scenes, such as the Helicopter String Quartet from Mittwoch, are often performed separately.

Performances

Several of the operas were premiered at La Scala, Milan, and by other major houses, but complete performances of individual days — let alone the whole cycle — remain rare because of their scale and technical demands. A landmark production of the complete Sonntag took place in Cologne in 2011, and a staged Donnerstag was given in Basel in 2016.
