A modern replica of the Pinta, one of Columbus's three ships.

The Pinta ("the Painted One," possibly a nickname from a previous owner) was one of the three ships of Christopher Columbus's first voyage of 1492, alongside the Niña and the flagship Santa María. A square-rigged caravel of roughly 60 tons, she was the fastest of the three vessels.

The voyage

The Pinta was captained by Martín Alonso Pinzón, an experienced mariner from Palos whose support had been essential in recruiting crews. On 6 August 1492 her rudder broke and was repaired at the Canary Islands. In the early hours of 12 October 1492, her lookout, Rodrigo de Triana (Juan Rodríguez Bermejo), made the fleet's first sighting of land in the Americas — the island Columbus named San Salvador — though Columbus later claimed the promised reward for himself, saying he had seen a light hours earlier.

Separation and return

On 21 November 1492 Pinzón separated from Columbus off Cuba and sailed east on his own, apparently in search of gold and an island called Babeque, rejoining the expedition in January 1493 after the wreck of the Santa María. The Pinta and Niña were separated again by a storm on the return voyage; the Pinta reached Bayona in Galicia — the first European port to receive news of the voyage — before arriving at Palos on 15 March 1493. Pinzón died within days of his return.
