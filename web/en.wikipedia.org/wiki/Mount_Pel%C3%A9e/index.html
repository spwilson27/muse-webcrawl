Mount Pelée

Mount Pelée (French: Montagne Pelée, "bald mountain") is an active volcano at the northern end of Martinique, an island and French overseas department in the Lesser Antilles Volcanic Arc of the Caribbean. Its elevation is 1,397 m (4,583 ft). It is a stratovolcano composed of layers of hardened lava and solidified volcanic ash.

Pelée is infamous for its eruption on 8 May 1902, which destroyed the city of Saint-Pierre, then the largest city and commercial capital of Martinique, killing some 29,000–30,000 people within minutes through a pyroclastic surge — making it the deadliest volcanic event of the 20th century. Of the city's population, only a handful survived, most famously Ludger Sylbaris, a prisoner held in a thick-walled underground cell, and the shoemaker Léon Compère-Léandre.

The eruption gave its name to a class of eruptions — Pelean eruptions — characterised by glowing avalanches (nuées ardentes) and the growth of lava domes and spines; a further eruptive phase from 1929 to 1932 formed the present dome. The volcano is monitored by the Observatoire Volcanologique et Sismologique de Martinique, and Saint-Pierre has been rebuilt, though it remains far smaller than the "Paris of the Caribbean" destroyed in 1902.
