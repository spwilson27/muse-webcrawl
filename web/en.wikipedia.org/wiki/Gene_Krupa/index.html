Eugene Bertram Krupa (January 15, 1909 – October 16, 1973) was an American jazz drummer, bandleader and composer. One of the most influential drummers in jazz history, Krupa is widely regarded as the first drummer to be a superstar soloist, and he largely defined the modern drum kit.

Career

Born in Chicago to Polish immigrant parents, Krupa began playing professionally in the mid-1920s with the "Austin High Gang" circle of musicians. He made his first recordings with Eddie Condon in 1927, including some of the earliest records to feature a full drum kit with bass drum. In 1934 he joined Benny Goodman's orchestra; their 1937 recording of "Sing, Sing, Sing", with Krupa's extended tom-tom solos, became a swing anthem. His "drum battles" with Buddy Rich later became legendary.

After leaving Goodman in 1938, Krupa formed his own orchestra, which had hits including "Drum Boogie". A 1943 drug arrest (he was eventually cleared of the main charge) briefly broke up the band, but he re-formed it with stars such as Anita O'Day and Roy Eldridge. He continued performing into the 1960s and co-founded a drum school with Cozy Cole.

Legacy

Krupa worked with the Slingerland company to develop tunable tom-toms and helped standardise the modern drum kit and cymbal setup. He was inducted into the Big Band and Jazz Hall of Fame and Modern Drummer Hall of Fame.
