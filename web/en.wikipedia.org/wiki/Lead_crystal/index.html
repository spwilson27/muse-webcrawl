Lead crystal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lead crystal (commonly called crystal, or lead glass) is a variety of glass in which lead oxide replaces the calcium content of typical potash glass. It typically contains 18–40% lead(II) oxide (PbO); European Union regulations require glass to contain at least 24% PbO to be sold as "lead crystal". Despite the name, lead crystal is amorphous glass, not a crystalline material.

History

The technique was developed in England by George Ravenscroft, who patented his formulation in 1674. Lead oxide improved the glass's working properties — lengthening the time during which it remained workable — and gave it a high refractive index and dispersion, making cut pieces sparkle brilliantly. Lead crystal became the material of fine stemware, decanters, and chandeliers; famous makers include Waterford, Baccarat, Lalique, and Swarovski (whose products use a proprietary composition).

Properties and safety

Lead glass is softer and easier to cut and engrave than soda-lime glass, rings when struck, and feels heavier. It is also used outside tableware: high-lead glasses serve in radiation shielding and in optical and electronic applications. Because lead can leach into acidic liquids stored for long periods, health authorities advise against storing beverages in lead-crystal decanters for extended periods, and manufacturers have increasingly developed lead-free "crystalline" alternatives using barium or zinc oxide.
