Brigade of Gurkhas

Active: 1947–present

Country: United Kingdom

Branch: British Army

Size: approx. 4,000 personnel

Recruiting: Nepal

The Brigade of Gurkhas is the collective name for the units of the British Army composed of Gurkha soldiers from Nepal. The brigade draws on a tradition of Gurkha service under the British Crown that began after the Anglo-Nepalese War of 1814–1816, and it was formally established when four Gurkha regiments of the British Indian Army transferred to the British Army in 1948 following the independence of India.

History

Gurkhas first served the East India Company from 1815, and Gurkha regiments fought with distinction in numerous campaigns of the British Empire, including both world wars; Gurkha soldiers have won 13 Victoria Crosses in British service. Under the Tripartite Agreement of 1947 between Britain, India, and Nepal, four regiments joined the British Army while six remained with the new Indian Army.

The brigade was heavily engaged during the Malayan Emergency, the Indonesia–Malaysia confrontation in Borneo, and the Falklands War of 1982. A major reorganisation in 1994 amalgamated the four infantry regiments into the Royal Gurkha Rifles. Gurkha units have since served in the Balkans, Sierra Leone, Iraq, and Afghanistan.

Structure

The brigade includes:

  • The Royal Gurkha Rifles (two battalions)
  • The Queen's Gurkha Engineers
  • The Queen's Gurkha Signals
  • The Queen's Own Gurkha Logistic Regiment
  • Gurkha staff and personnel support companies, the Band of the Brigade of Gurkhas, and a Gurkha company at the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst

Recruitment and welfare

Recruitment takes place annually in Nepal and is intensely competitive, with tens of thousands of applicants for a few hundred places; selection includes the famous doko race, carrying a heavy basket uphill. For many years Gurkha pay, pensions, and settlement rights were less favourable than those of British soldiers, a disparity addressed by reforms in the 2000s, including the 2009 decision granting veteran Gurkhas the right to settle in the United Kingdom following a campaign supported by actress Joanna Lumley.
