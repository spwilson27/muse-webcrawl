Bob Fosse

Robert Louis Fosse (June 23, 1927 – September 23, 1987) was an American dancer, choreographer, and director whose distinctive, jazz-inflected style reshaped Broadway and film musicals. His choreography — turned-in knees, rolled shoulders, slouched hats, gloves, and isolated, angular movements — is instantly recognisable. Fosse is the only person to win an Academy Award, a Tony Award, and an Emmy Award in the same year (1973).

Career

Fosse danced in vaudeville as a child, served in the Navy, and appeared in MGM films before choreographing The Pajama Game (1954) and Damn Yankees (1955) on Broadway, winning Tony Awards. He directed and choreographed Sweet Charity (1966), Pippin (1972), and Chicago (1975), the latter created with his wife and frequent star Gwen Verdon.

Film work

His film of Cabaret (1972) won eight Academy Awards including Best Director. The semi-autobiographical All That Jazz (1979), about a driven, self-destructive choreographer, won the Palme d'Or at Cannes and is widely considered his masterpiece. He also directed the biopic Lenny (1974) and Star 80 (1983).

Life and legacy

Fosse's perfectionism, chain-smoking, and hard living mirrored his art; he died of a heart attack in Washington, D.C., on the opening night of a Sweet Charity revival. His style remains foundational to musical theatre; the 2002 film Chicago and the 2019 miniseries Fosse/Verdon testify to his continuing influence.
