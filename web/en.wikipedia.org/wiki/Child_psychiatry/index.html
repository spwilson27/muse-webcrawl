Child psychiatry, formally child and adolescent psychiatry, is a subspecialty of psychiatry concerned with the diagnosis, treatment, and prevention of mental, emotional, and behavioural disorders in children, adolescents, and their families. It integrates developmental psychology, neurology, education, and family systems into clinical practice.

History

The field grew out of the child guidance movement of the early 20th century; the first child guidance clinic was founded in Chicago in 1909 under William Healy. Leo Kanner established the first academic child psychiatry clinic at Johns Hopkins in 1930 and described early infantile autism in 1943. The specialty gained formal recognition in the United States in 1959 with board certification.

Assessment and treatment

Assessment combines interviews with the child and caregivers, developmental history, school reports, and standardized rating scales. Treatment typically centres on psychotherapies — including cognitive behavioural therapy, parent management training, and family therapy — with medication used for conditions such as ADHD, depression, and psychosis, usually alongside psychological and school-based interventions.

Scope

Child psychiatrists treat neurodevelopmental disorders (ADHD, autism spectrum disorder, learning disorders), anxiety and mood disorders, eating disorders, trauma-related disorders, and early-onset psychosis. There is a recognized global shortage of practitioners relative to the prevalence of youth mental disorders.
