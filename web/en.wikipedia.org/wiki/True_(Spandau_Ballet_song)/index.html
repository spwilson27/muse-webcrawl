"True" is a song by the English new wave band Spandau Ballet, released in 1983 as the title track and third single from their third studio album, True. Written by guitarist Gary Kemp, the song became the band's only number-one single on the UK Singles Chart and their biggest international hit, reaching the top ten in numerous countries, including number four on the US Billboard Hot 100.

Background and writing

Kemp wrote "True" at his parents' house in Islington as a tribute to Motown and to soul singer Marvin Gaye, whose name is referenced in the lyrics ("Listening to Marvin all night long"). Kemp had met Gaye shortly before writing the song and later described it as a love song about the difficulty of writing a love song — the line "This is the sound of my soul" captures the song's self-conscious sincerity.

The song marked a stylistic shift for the band from the stark electronic sound of their earlier records toward a smoother, soul-influenced style. The recording features a prominent saxophone part by Steve Norman and a memorable keyboard arrangement.

Reception

"True" spent four weeks at number one in the United Kingdom in the spring of 1983 and went on to become one of the defining songs of the decade. It was named the UK's best-selling single of 1983 by some measures and has been certified multi-platinum in the United States. Critics have frequently listed it among the greatest love songs of the 1980s.

Legacy and samples

The song's chorus was famously sampled by P.M. Dawn on their 1991 hit "Set Adrift on Memory Bliss", which itself reached number one in the United States. "True" has appeared in numerous films, advertisements and television programmes, including Sixteen Candles and The Simpsons, and remains a staple of soft-rock and adult contemporary radio.
