Recreational mathematics

Recreational mathematics is mathematics carried out for recreation (entertainment) rather than as a strictly research and application-based professional activity or as part of a student's formal education. It often involves mathematical puzzles and games.

Topics

Recreational mathematics covers a wide range of topics, including magic squares, fractals, logic puzzles such as Sudoku, tangrams, tiling puzzles, and paradoxes. Many classical problems, such as the Königsberg bridges problem that led to graph theory, began as recreations.

People

Martin Gardner popularized the field through his "Mathematical Games" column in Scientific American from 1956 to 1981. Other notable figures include Sam Loyd, Henry Dudeney, Raymond Smullyan, and John Horton Conway, whose Game of Life became a famous recreational creation.

Publications

The field is served by journals and puzzle columns, and recreational problems have often inspired serious research in areas such as combinatorics, number theory, and geometry.
