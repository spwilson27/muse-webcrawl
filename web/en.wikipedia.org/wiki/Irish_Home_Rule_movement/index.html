Irish Home Rule movement

Period1870–1921
LeadersIsaac Butt, Charles Stewart Parnell, John Redmond
GoalIrish parliament within the United Kingdom

The Irish Home Rule movement was a political movement, dominant from about 1870 until the end of the First World War, that campaigned for self-government for Ireland within the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Ireland, through an Irish parliament subordinate to Westminster.

Origins

Isaac Butt founded the Home Government Association in 1870 and the Home Rule League in 1873. Under Charles Stewart Parnell in the 1880s the movement became a disciplined parliamentary party — the Irish Parliamentary Party — that held the balance of power at Westminster and allied with William Gladstone's Liberals.

The Home Rule bills

Gladstone's first bill (1886) split the Liberal Party and was defeated in the Commons; the second (1893) passed the Commons but was rejected by the Lords. The Parnell split of 1890–91, after Parnell's divorce scandal, weakened the party for a decade until reunification under John Redmond in 1900.

Third bill and Ulster crisis

The Parliament Act 1911 removed the Lords' veto, and a third bill passed in 1914. Ulster unionists, led by Edward Carson, fiercely opposed it, signing the Ulster Covenant (1912), forming the Ulster Volunteers and arming at Larne. Implementation was suspended for the First World War.

Supersession

The Easter Rising (1916) and the conscription crisis (1918) shifted nationalist opinion from Home Rule to independence. Sinn Féin swept the 1918 election, proclaimed an Irish parliament, and the Anglo-Irish War led instead to partition under the Government of Ireland Act 1920 and the Irish Free State in 1922.
