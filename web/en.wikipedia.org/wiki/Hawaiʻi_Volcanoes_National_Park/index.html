Hawaii Volcanoes National Park

LocationIsland of Hawaiʻi, USA
VolcanoesKīlauea, Mauna Loa
StatusUNESCO World Heritage
[Image: Lava glow in Halemaʻumaʻu crater]
Lava glow in Halemaʻumaʻu crater

Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park protects the summit and rift zones of two of the world's most active volcanoes, Kīlauea and Mauna Loa, on the island of Hawaiʻi. It is a UNESCO World Heritage site.

Features

Kīlauea's summit caldera and Halemaʻumaʻu crater — home, in Hawaiian tradition, of the goddess Pele — changed dramatically in 2018 when summit collapse accompanied a major lower East Rift eruption that destroyed hundreds of homes outside the park. Mauna Loa, the largest shield volcano on Earth, erupted in 2022 for the first time in 38 years. Chain of Craters Road descends past pit craters and lava flows to the sea cliff, where the park's landscape is still being written.
