Dublin Port

Dublin Port (Irish: Calafort Átha Cliath) is the largest seaport in Ireland, located at the mouth of the River Liffey where it enters Dublin Bay. It handles roughly two-thirds of the Republic of Ireland's port traffic and a large share of its unitised (roll-on/roll-off and lift-on/lift-off) trade, as well as ferry passengers travelling to Britain and France. The port is operated by the state-owned Dublin Port Company.

History

Dublin's port grew from the Viking and medieval quays upriver, but silting in the Liffey estuary long hampered shipping. Engineering works transformed it: the Great South Wall, built in the 18th century, and the North Bull Wall of the 1820s (built to a design associated with Captain William Bligh, of Bounty fame) channelised the river's flow so that the ebb tide scoured a deep channel, while the accreted sand formed Bull Island. The Ballast Board, established in 1707, and its successor bodies managed the port through the era of the Custom House and the city quays; the modern port concentrated downstream on reclaimed land on both banks through the 20th century.

Operations

The port estate covers about 260 hectares (with further lands at the Dublin Inland Port near the airport). It operates ferry terminals serving routes to Holyhead, Liverpool, and Cherbourg (operators include Irish Ferries and Stena Line), container terminals, bulk and break-bulk facilities, a cruise berth, and oil and energy terminals. Annual throughput has been on the order of 35–38 million tonnes of cargo and well over a million ferry passengers in peak years. A masterplan (Masterplan 2040) provides for major redevelopment of berths and terminals to expand capacity, parts of which have been delivered since 2016.

Heritage

Port heritage features include the Great South Wall and its Poolbeg Lighthouse, the Diving Bell on Sir John Rogerson's Quay, and the former Pigeon House generating station with its twin chimneys, long a Dublin landmark.
