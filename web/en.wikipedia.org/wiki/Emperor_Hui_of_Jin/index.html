Emperor Hui of Jin (259 – 8 January 307), personal name Sima Zhong, courtesy name Zhengdu, was the second emperor of the Western Jin dynasty of China. He was developmentally disabled and for most of his reign was a figurehead controlled by a succession of regents and princes, whose struggle for power at his court erupted into the devastating civil war known as the War of the Eight Princes.

Early life and accession

Sima Zhong was the second son of Emperor Wu, the dynasty's founder, and his eldest surviving son after the early death of his elder brother. Despite concerns about his intellectual disability — officials reported that when told the people were starving for lack of rice, he asked "Why don't they eat meat porridge?" (何不食肉糜), an anecdote that became a byword for rulers out of touch with their subjects — Emperor Wu kept him as crown prince, partly through the influence of his mother Empress Yang Yan and the hope that Zhong's son Sima Yu would prove capable. He succeeded to the throne in 290.

Reign

Real power was exercised successively by the regent Yang Jun, then by Empress Dowager Yang's faction, and from 291 by Emperor Hui's wife, the ruthless Empress Jia Nanfeng, who dominated the court for a decade, deposing and murdering Crown Prince Sima Yu in 300. Her coup triggered the War of the Eight Princes (291–306), in which imperial princes fought over control of the emperor, repeatedly abducting and deposing him. Emperor Hui was dethroned briefly in 301 by Sima Lun, restored, and thereafter shuffled between rival princes' camps; he was even carried off to Chang'an in 304.

Death

Emperor Hui died in January 307, reportedly poisoned, and was succeeded by his brother Sima Chi (Emperor Huai). The civil war exhausted the dynasty, and within a decade Luoyang and Chang'an fell to Xiongnu-led forces, ending the Western Jin.
