Odrysian kingdom

The Odrysian kingdom (Ancient Greek: Βασίλειον Ὀδρυσῶν) was an ancient Thracian state that existed from the early 5th century BC until its conquest by the Roman Empire in AD 46. The first unified state of the Thracians, it was founded by King Teres I of the Odrysae tribe and at its height stretched from the Black Sea to the Danube and toward the Aegean, covering much of modern Bulgaria and parts of Greece, Turkey, and Romania.

Rise and expansion

Teres (reigned c. 480–445 BC) united the Odrysians in the wake of the Persian withdrawal from Thrace after the Greco-Persian Wars. His son Sitalces (c. 431–424 BC) brought the kingdom to its greatest power: Thucydides describes its revenue and military strength — it could field some 150,000 men — as second in Europe only to Scythia. Sitalces allied with Athens during the Peloponnesian War and campaigned into Macedon and against the Triballi, in whose territory he was killed. Seuthes I and later kings maintained a loose hegemony over the Thracian tribes, administering the realm through subordinate dynasts and collecting tribute.

Decline and Roman conquest

After the death of Cotys I in 360 BC the kingdom split among rival kings, and Philip II of Macedon conquered much of Thrace in the 340s BC. An Odrysian state revived under Seuthes III (c. 331–300 BC), who founded the city of Seuthopolis near modern Kazanlak — now submerged but extensively excavated, like the rich Odrysian tombs of the Valley of the Thracian Rulers. Thereafter the kingdom shrank under Celtic, Macedonian, and finally Roman pressure; its last kings ruled as Roman clients until the emperor Claudius annexed Thrace as a Roman province in AD 46.

Culture

The Odrysian kings minted coins, intermarried with Greek and Macedonian dynasties, and patronised workshops that produced celebrated gold and silver work, such as the Panagyurishte Treasure. Thracian religion, including the worship of the Thracian Horseman and the Orphic mysteries associated with Zalmoxis and Orpheus, flourished under the kingdom.
