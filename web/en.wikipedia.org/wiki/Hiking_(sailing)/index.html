Hiking (sailing)

In sailing, hiking (also called sitting out) is the technique by which the crew of a small boat — typically a dinghy or small keelboat without enough ballast to counteract the wind — move their body weight as far to windward as possible in order to reduce heeling and keep the boat upright and sailing efficiently.

Technique and equipment

Sailors hike by sitting on the windward side deck and leaning their bodies out over the water, supporting themselves with their feet hooked under hiking straps (toe straps) fitted across the cockpit. In stronger winds the whole torso may extend horizontally beyond the gunwale, with only the legs inside the boat. Hiking benches, padded hiking shorts and harnesses assist comfort and leverage. On some boats, such as trapeze-equipped skiffs, crew members go further and stand outside the hull on a trapeze wire, a related but distinct technique.

Effect and rules

Hiking increases the righting moment, allowing the boat to carry more sail, sail closer to the wind and plane more readily. Because it is physically demanding, it rewards athletic crews, and racing rules regulate how far sailors may extend their bodies and forbid artificial aids beyond permitted equipment; some one-design classes restrict hiking posture to protect sailors' backs and knees.
