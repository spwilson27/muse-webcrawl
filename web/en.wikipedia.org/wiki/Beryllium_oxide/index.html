Beryllium oxide (BeO), also known as beryllia, is the inorganic compound of beryllium and oxygen. It is a white, refractory ceramic notable for an unusual combination of properties: it is an excellent electrical insulator yet conducts heat better than most metals and nearly as well as diamond among insulators, with thermal conductivity rivalling aluminium nitride. It crystallises in the hexagonal wurtzite structure.

Production and properties

Beryllium oxide is produced industrially by calcining beryllium hydroxide or beryllium carbonate at high temperature. The sintered ceramic has a very high melting point (2,507 °C), high strength, low dielectric loss at microwave frequencies, and good resistance to thermal shock, though it is attacked by molten metals only slightly and is chemically stable. Unlike most metal oxides, it is amphoteric, dissolving in both acids and alkalis.

Applications

Because it insulates electrically while removing heat efficiently, BeO ceramic is used as a heat-sink substrate in high-power semiconductor devices, microwave and radio-frequency electronics, laser components, and formerly in vacuum-tube and gas-laser construction. Its neutron-moderating and reflecting properties led to use in nuclear reactors, and beryllia crucibles serve in high-temperature metallurgy.

Safety

Beryllium oxide dust is highly toxic. Inhalation can cause chronic beryllium disease (berylliosis), a potentially fatal granulomatous lung disease, and beryllium compounds are classified as Group 1 human carcinogens. Handling is therefore tightly controlled: the sintered ceramic is safe as a solid article, but machining or grinding it requires stringent dust controls.
