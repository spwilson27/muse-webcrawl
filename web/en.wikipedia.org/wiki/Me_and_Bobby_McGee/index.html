"Me and Bobby McGee" is a song written by American singer-songwriter Kris Kristofferson and originally performed by Roger Miller. Kristofferson's former roommate and fellow songwriter, Fred Foster, is credited as co-writer. Numerous artists have recorded the song, but the best-known version is the posthumous recording by Janis Joplin, which topped the Billboard Hot 100 in 1971, making the song Joplin's second posthumous number-one single (after "(Sittin' On) The Dock of the Bay" by Otis Redding).

Background and composition

Fred Foster, owner of Monument Records and Combine Music, suggested the title "Me and Bobby McKee" to Kristofferson, naming the character after Barbara "Bobbi" McKee, a secretary in Foster's building. Kristofferson misheard the name as "McGee". The song tells of two drifters, the narrator and Bobby, who travel through the American South together before breaking up; Bobby's gender is unspecified in the lyrics. Kristofferson has said the final verse was influenced by the film La Strada by Federico Fellini.

Janis Joplin version

Joplin recorded the song for her album Pearl only days before her death in October 1970. Her version, with an acoustic arrangement and altered lyrics ("Busted flat in Baton Rouge..."), reached number one on the Billboard Hot 100 in March 1971. In 2004, Joplin's rendition was ranked number 148 on Rolling Stone's list of the 500 Greatest Songs of All Time. The Grateful Dead also performed the song frequently in concert.
