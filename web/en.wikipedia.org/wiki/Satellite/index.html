A satellite is an object in orbit around another body. Natural satellites are moons — Earth's Moon in the wider crawl — while artificial satellites, beginning with Sputnik 1 in the Space Race, now number many thousands and underpin modern life.

Orbits and uses

Communications satellites relay signals; navigation constellations (GPS, Galileo) position everything from phones to ships; Earth-observation satellites track weather, crops and climate for the conservation pages; science satellites include the great observatories Hubble and Webb (in their L2 halo 'orbit'). Debris and mega-constellations raise congestion concerns.
