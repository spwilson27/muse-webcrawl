Francisco Vázquez de Coronado

Francisco Vázquez de Coronado (1510 – 22 September 1554) was a Spanish conquistador and explorer who led a large expedition from what is now Mexico to present-day Kansas through parts of the southwestern United States between 1540 and 1542. The expedition was the first European sighting of the Grand Canyon and the Colorado River, among other places. His name is often anglicized as "Vasquez de Coronado" or "Coronado".

The expedition

Coronado was commissioned by Viceroy Antonio de Mendoza to investigate reports by Álvar Núñez Cabeza de Vaca and Fray Marcos de Niza of the wealthy Seven Cities of Gold (Cíbola). In 1540 he set out with some 300 Spaniards, over a thousand Indigenous allies, and herds of livestock. He conquered the Zuni pueblos (the supposed Cíbola) but found no gold, then wintered at Tiguex on the Rio Grande, where fighting broke out with the Pueblo people. Detachments explored widely: García López de Cárdenas reached the Grand Canyon, and Hernando de Alarcón sailed up the Gulf of California. In 1541 Coronado marched onto the Great Plains as far as Quivira in present-day Kansas in search of another rumored golden kingdom, again finding none.

Later life

The expedition returned to Mexico in 1542 without the hoped-for riches. Coronado was charged with abuses during the campaign but was cleared of the most serious charges; he remained a member of the Mexico City council until his death in 1554.
