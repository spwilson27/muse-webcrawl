Up from Slavery

Up from Slavery is the 1901 autobiography of Booker T. Washington detailing his personal history from enslavement in Virginia through his struggles for education to his establishment of the Tuskegee Institute in Alabama. It is Washington's best-known book and a classic of African-American literature.

Up from Slavery
Author: Booker T. Washington
Published: 1901 (Doubleday, Page)
Genre: Autobiography

Contents

Washington recounts birth into slavery in 1856, emancipation, work in salt furnaces and coal mines, learning to read, walking to Hampton Institute, and later founding Tuskegee in 1881. He expounds his philosophy of industrial education, self-help, and gradual racial uplift, including his 1895 Atlanta Exposition Address, the so-called Atlanta Compromise.

Reception

The book was a bestseller translated into many languages. W. E. B. Du Bois criticized Washington's accommodationism in The Souls of Black Folk (1903), sparking a famous debate over strategy for Black advancement.
