Neuropsychological test

A neuropsychological test is a standardised task or procedure designed to measure a psychological function known to be linked to a particular brain structure or pathway. Such tests are the main tools of neuropsychological assessment, used to evaluate cognition in the presence of brain injury, disease or developmental conditions.

The clock-drawing test screens visuospatial and executive function.

Domains assessed

Tests sample domains including intelligence, memory, language, executive function, attention, processing speed, visuospatial ability and motor skills. Well-known instruments include the Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale, the Wisconsin Card Sorting Test, the Rey–Osterrieth complex figure, the Trail Making Test, and screening measures such as the Mini–Mental State Examination (MMSE) and the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA).

Use

Results are compared against normative data matched for age and education, and interpreted alongside history and imaging. Assessments help diagnose conditions such as dementia, localise the effects of stroke or tumour, evaluate traumatic brain injury, guide rehabilitation, and track change over time. Because performance is influenced by effort, mood, culture and language, neuropsychologists interpret scores cautiously and in context.
