Almanac

An almanac is a regularly published listing of a set of current information about one or multiple subjects. It usually includes information on subjects such as weather forecasts, farmers' planting dates, sea tides, and other tabular data arranged according to the calendar. The word derives from the medieval Latin almanach, possibly from an Arabic term for the climate.

History

The earliest almanacs grew out of astronomical tables used to predict celestial events. Roger Bacon produced one in the 13th century, and printed almanacs became common after the invention of the printing press. In colonial America, Poor Richard's Almanack (1733–1758) by Benjamin Franklin became famous for its aphorisms and practical information.

Types

Farmers' almanacs such as The Old Farmer's Almanac (founded 1792) emphasize weather lore and planting advice. Modern almanacs like The World Almanac and Book of Facts compile statistics, election results, sports records, and general reference data, updated annually.

Contents

Traditional almanacs contain calendars, sunrise and sunset times, tide tables, astronomical data, and lists of holidays; contemporary reference almanacs add demographic, economic, and political summaries.
