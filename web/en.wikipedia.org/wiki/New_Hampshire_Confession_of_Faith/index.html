New Hampshire Confession of Faith

The New Hampshire Confession of Faith is a Baptist confession of faith adopted in 1833 by the New Hampshire Baptist Convention. Drafted principally by the Reverend John Newton Brown, it was intended as a moderate, concise statement of Baptist doctrine that could unite churches divided by the era's theological controversies.

Content

The confession contains eighteen articles covering the scriptures, the true God, the fall of man, the way of salvation, justification, repentance and faith, God's purpose of grace, baptism and the Lord's Supper, the resurrection and the world to come. In tone it is broadly Calvinistic but deliberately softer than earlier confessions such as the Philadelphia Confession, avoiding detailed treatment of contested points.

Influence

Through Brown's widely circulated church manual, the New Hampshire Confession became one of the most influential Baptist statements of faith in the United States. It formed the basis of the doctrinal statement of the Southern Baptist Convention's Baptist Faith and Message, first adopted in 1925, and of the statements of many individual Baptist churches and associations.
