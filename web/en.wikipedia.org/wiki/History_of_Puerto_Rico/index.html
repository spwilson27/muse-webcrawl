History of Puerto Rico

The history of Puerto Rico began with the settlement of the archipelago by Ortoiroid and later Taíno peoples, continued through more than four centuries of Spanish colonial rule after Columbus's arrival in 1493, and took its modern form after the United States acquired the island in 1898. Puerto Rico remains an unincorporated US territory whose political status is still debated.

Indigenous era and Spanish conquest

The Taíno called the island Borikén. Columbus landed in 1493, and Juan Ponce de León founded the first Spanish settlement, Caparra, in 1508. Taíno resistance, including the 1511 rebellion led by Agüeybaná II, was crushed; disease, forced labour in the encomienda system and emigration devastated the indigenous population, and enslaved Africans were imported from the 16th century to work sugar and coffee estates.

Spanish colony

San Juan, founded in 1521, became a fortified gateway to Spain's American empire, guarded by the castles of El Morro and San Cristóbal and repelling Dutch and British attacks. For centuries Puerto Rico was subsidised as a military outpost. In the 19th century, reformers pressed Madrid for change: slavery was abolished in 1873, and in 1897 Spain granted an Autonomic Charter providing an elected insular government.

American rule and the Commonwealth

During the Spanish–American War, US forces invaded at Guánica on 25 July 1898; the Treaty of Paris ceded Puerto Rico to the United States. The Foraker Act (1900) created a civilian government, and the Jones Act (1917) granted US citizenship to Puerto Ricans. Under Governor Luis Muñoz Marín and the Popular Democratic Party, Operation Bootstrap industrialised the island, and in 1952 Puerto Rico adopted its own constitution as a Commonwealth (Estado Libre Asociado) in free association with the United States.

Contemporary era

Plebiscites on status — statehood, independence or the current arrangement — have been held repeatedly since 1967 without a binding resolution, though statehood won the most recent votes in 2020 and 2024. The island suffered a prolonged debt crisis, leading Congress to impose a fiscal control board under PROMESA in 2016, and catastrophic damage from Hurricane Maria in 2017. Mass protests in 2019 forced Governor Ricardo Rosselló to resign.
