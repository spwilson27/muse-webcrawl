Celebrity

Celebrity is a condition of fame and broad public recognition of a person or group, or an individual who has attained such status, usually as a result of a successful career or conspicuous achievement, or — increasingly — through prominence in media and social networks. A person whom the public follows closely is said to have "celebrity status".

History

Although fame is ancient — Olympic victors, Roman generals and saints were widely celebrated — modern celebrity is generally traced to the mass media of the 18th and 19th centuries: cheap newspapers, theatre and popular song created stars such as the opera singer Jenny Lind. The film industry of the early 20th century industrialised fame, with studio publicity machines building figures like Charlie Chaplin and Marilyn Monroe into global icons. Television, and later the internet and social media, progressively lowered the threshold for visibility, producing reality-television stars and "influencers" and the phenomenon of being "famous for being famous".

Nature and study

Scholars distinguish celebrity from mere renown: in Daniel Boorstin's phrase, the celebrity is "a person who is known for his well-knownness". Celebrity culture is studied in sociology and media studies for its effects on identity, consumption and politics, including parasocial relationships — the one-sided bonds audiences form with media figures. Andy Warhol's remark that in the future "everyone will be world-famous for 15 minutes" has become a byword for the ephemerality of fame.

By field

Major sources of celebrity include film and television actors, musicians, athletes, politicians, businesspeople, chefs, and internet personalities. Celebrity carries economic value — endorsements, appearances and brand partnerships — and social capital, but also costs: loss of privacy, tabloid scrutiny and the attention of stalkers.
