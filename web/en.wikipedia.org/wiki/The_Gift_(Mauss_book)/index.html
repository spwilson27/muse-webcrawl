Essai sur le don (1925)

The Gift: Forms and Functions of Exchange in Archaic Societies (French: Essai sur le don. Forme et raison de l'échange dans les sociétés archaïques) is a 1925 essay by French sociologist and anthropologist Marcel Mauss. It is one of the foundational texts of economic anthropology.

Argument

Drawing on ethnographic material from Polynesia, Melanesia, and the Indigenous peoples of the Pacific Northwest Coast (notably the potlatch), Mauss argued that gift exchange in such societies is governed by three linked obligations: to give, to receive, and to reciprocate. Gifts, he contended, are never truly free or disinterested; they are "total social facts" that bind persons and groups together, because something of the giver — the hau, or spirit of the gift — travels with the thing given and demands return.

Influence

The essay profoundly shaped later anthropology and social theory, influencing Claude Lévi-Strauss, Marshall Sahlins, and Pierre Bourdieu, and remains a standard reference in debates over reciprocity, exchange, and the gift economy.
