Mandu, Madhya Pradesh

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Mandu or Mandavgad is an ancient fort city in the present-day Mandav area of Dhar district in the Madhya Pradesh state of Western India. It is located on a rocky plateau of the Vindhya Range some 35 km from Dhar, at an elevation of about 555 metres.

History

Mandu rose to prominence as the fort capital of the Paramara kings of Malwa and flourished as capital of the independent Malwa Sultanate in the 15th century, under rulers such as Hoshang Shah and Mahmud Khalji. It was later conquered by the Mughal emperor Humayun, and by Akbar in 1562, after which it declined.

Monuments

Its celebrated Afghan architecture includes the Jami Masjid, Hoshang Shah's tomb (said to have inspired the Taj Mahal's marble tomb design), the Jahaz Mahal ("ship palace") between two lakes, the Hindola Mahal, and the pavilion of Rupmati overlooking the Narmada plain. Mandu is also remembered for the romance of Sultan Baz Bahadur and Rani Rupmati.
