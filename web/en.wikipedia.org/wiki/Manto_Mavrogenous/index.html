Manto Mavrogenous (Greek: Μαντώ Μαυρογένους; 1796 – 1848) was a heroine of the Greek War of Independence who spent her entire fortune funding the revolutionary cause, outfitting ships and troops and taking part in person in campaigns.

Life and war service

Born in Trieste to a wealthy Phanariot merchant family, Mavrogenous was educated in Greek, Italian, French and Turkish. On the outbreak of the revolution in 1821 she went to Mykonos, her family's island, and urged its people to revolt against Ottoman rule. With her own money she equipped and manned two ships to hunt pirates and fight the Ottomans, and in October 1822 helped repel an attack on Mykonos by Algerine corsairs. She later outfitted a fleet of six ships and an infantry force of some 800 men which she sent to the Peloponnese, and she funded troops at the sieges of Tripolitsa and Karystos. Her famous appeal "to the ladies of Paris" sought European support for the Greek cause in the salons of France.

Later years and legacy

After independence, Governor Ioannis Kapodistrias awarded her the rank of lieutenant general, but feuds and the loss of her fortune left her in poverty; she died on Paros in 1848. Greece has honoured her on coins — her portrait appeared on the 2-drachma coin — on stamps and in street names, and her house on Mykonos is preserved as a landmark of the revolution.
