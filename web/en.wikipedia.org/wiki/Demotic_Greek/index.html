Demotic Greek
Native to: Greece
Era: 19th–20th centuries, basis of Standard Modern Greek
Family: Indo-European, Hellenic
Related: Katharevousa, Modern Greek

Demotic Greek (Dimotiki) is the vernacular form of the Greek language, based on the spoken language of the Greek people, as opposed to the archaizing Katharevousa. It became the basis of Standard Modern Greek.

History

For centuries Greece experienced diglossia: official and literary writing used Katharevousa, while everyday speech was Demotic. Writers and educators advocating Demotic sparked the Greek language question, at times provoking violent controversy in the early 20th century.

Official status

In 1976 Demotic was made the official language of education and government, and in 1982 the monotonic accent system replaced polytonic orthography in official use. Modern standard Greek blends Demotic grammar with many learned words.

Features

Demotic simplified ancient inflections, developed new verb forms, and absorbed loanwords from Italian, Turkish and other languages through everyday contact.
