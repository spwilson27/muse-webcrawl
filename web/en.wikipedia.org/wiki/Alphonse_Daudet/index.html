Alphonse Daudet (13 May 1840 – 16 December 1897) was a French novelist and playwright, celebrated for his vivid depictions of his native Provence and for works of gentle humour and pathos. Born in Nîmes, he moved to Paris in 1857 and worked as secretary to the duc de Morny.

Works

His best-known books include Lettres de mon moulin (Letters from My Windmill, 1869), a collection of Provençal tales; Tartarin de Tarascon (1872), a comic novel about a boastful small-town hero; and the sentimental novel Le Petit Chose (1868). His play L'Arlésienne (1872), with incidental music by Georges Bizet, became especially famous. Daudet was associated with the naturalist circle of Émile Zola, though his style remained more lyrical and humorous.

Later life

He suffered for years from syphilis and its neurological effects, which he chronicled in the posthumously published La Doulou (In the Land of Pain). His son Léon Daudet became a prominent writer and political figure.
