Doc Savage is a fictional character of the competent man hero type, who first appeared in American pulp magazines during the 1930s and 1940s. Real name Clark Savage Jr., he is a physician, scientist, inventor, explorer and adventurer of extraordinary physical and mental abilities, nicknamed the "Man of Bronze." The character was created by publisher Henry W. Ralston and editor John L. Nanovic at Street & Smith Publications, with the bulk of the stories written by Lester Dent under the house name Kenneth Robeson.

The character

Raised from birth by a team of scientists selected by his father, Savage was trained to the peak of human capability in strength, endurance and intellect. From his headquarters on the 86th floor of a New York skyscraper (implicitly the Empire State Building) and his Arctic retreat, the Fortress of Solitude, he travels the world righting wrongs with the aid of his five companions, the "Fabulous Five." Savage follows a strict moral code, famously including an oath sworn by his associates.

Publications and legacy

The original magazine Doc Savage Magazine ran from 1933 to 1949, publishing 181 novels. Bantam Books reprinted the series in paperback from 1964, bringing the character to a new generation. Doc Savage is widely regarded as a precursor of the modern superhero — the Fortress of Solitude, superhuman training, and even aspects of Superman and Batman are traced to him. A film adaptation, Doc Savage: The Man of Bronze, appeared in 1975.
