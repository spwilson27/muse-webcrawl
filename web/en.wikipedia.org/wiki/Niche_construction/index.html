Niche construction

Niche construction
FieldEvolutionary biology, ecology
Key proponentsJohn Odling-Smee, Kevin Laland, Marcus Feldman
Classic examplesBeaver dams, earthworm soil engineering, human agriculture
Related ideasEcosystem engineering, extended phenotype, extended evolutionary synthesis

Niche construction is the process by which organisms, through their metabolism, activities, and choices, modify their own and each other's niches — the components of the environment relevant to their survival and reproduction. Unlike the standard view in which environments pose selection pressures to which organisms adapt, niche construction theory emphasises that organisms also systematically alter those selection pressures, creating a reciprocal causation between organism and environment. The modified conditions can be inherited by descendants as ecological inheritance, alongside genetic inheritance.

A beaver dam: beavers reshape their environment, altering selection pressures on themselves and other species.

Examples

  • Beavers build dams that flood areas into ponds, changing hydrology, nutrient cycles, and the community of other species.
  • Earthworms aerate and enrich soil through burrowing and digestion; Darwin devoted his last book to their effects on soil formation.
  • Humans engage in niche construction on an unmatched scale through agriculture, animal domestication, urbanisation, and medicine — with evolutionary consequences such as adult lactase persistence in dairying populations, a classic case of gene–culture coevolution.
  • Microbes constructing biofilms, ants and termites building climate-controlled mounds, and birds building nests are further examples.

Theory and debate

The framework was developed by F. John Odling-Smee, Kevin N. Laland, and Marcus W. Feldman, most fully in their 2003 monograph Niche Construction: The Neglected Process in Evolution. Proponents argue niche construction is a distinct evolutionary process that should be incorporated into an extended evolutionary synthesis. Critics respond that niche construction is fully compatible with the modern synthesis and requires no theoretical revision — a debate about how fundamental the process is, rather than whether it occurs. The concept overlaps with ecosystem engineering in ecology and with Richard Dawkins's notion of the extended phenotype, though niche construction theory stresses the feedback of environmental modification on selection itself.
