Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture

The Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture is a theorem of number theory concerning the Fourier coefficients of modular forms. It generalises a conjecture made by Srinivasa Ramanujan in 1916 about the tau function, and a refinement by Hans Petersson for modular forms of general weight.

Statement

Ramanujan conjectured that the tau function τ(n) satisfies |τ(p)| ≤ 2p11/2 for primes p, equivalently that the associated local factors have eigenvalues of absolute value p11/2. Petersson's version asserts that for a cusp form of weight k that is an eigenform for the Hecke operators, the p-th Fourier coefficient ap satisfies |ap| ≤ 2p(k−1)/2.

Proof

The conjecture was proved by Pierre Deligne in 1974 as a consequence of his proof of the Weil conjectures on zeta functions over finite fields, work for which he received the Fields Medal in 1978. The reduction of the Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture to the Weil conjectures had been established through the work of Eichler, Shimura, Kuga, Ihara and Deligne on the cohomology of modular varieties. The analogous statement for Maass forms (the Ramanujan conjecture for GL(2) in full generality) remains an active area connected with the Langlands program.
