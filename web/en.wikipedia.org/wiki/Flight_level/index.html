Flight level

A flight level (FL) is an aircraft's altitude at standard air pressure, expressed in hundreds of feet. The air pressure is computed assuming an International Standard Atmosphere pressure of 1013.25 hPa (29.92 inHg) at sea level, and therefore is not necessarily the same as the aircraft's actual geometric altitude.

Definition

Flight levels are designated by the letters FL followed by the pressure altitude in hundreds of feet: FL310, for example, corresponds to an indicated altitude of 31,000 feet with the altimeter set to the standard pressure setting. Flight levels are used above a transition altitude, which varies by country — 18,000 feet in the United States and Canada, but much lower in much of Europe.

Assignment

Under the semicircular (hemispheric) rule, instrument flight rules (IFR) flights on magnetic courses of 000–179 degrees are assigned odd flight levels (FL310, FL330...), while flights on courses of 180–359 degrees use even flight levels. Reduced vertical separation minima (RVSM) allow 1,000-foot spacing between FL290 and FL410.
