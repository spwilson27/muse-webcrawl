The Temple of Athena Nike is a small temple on the Acropolis of Athens, dedicated to the goddesses Athena and Nike. Built around 427–424 BC during the Peloponnesian War, it is the earliest fully Ionic temple on the Acropolis and stands on the steep bastion at the southwest corner of the rock, beside the Propylaea, the monumental entrance to the sanctuary.

History

The cult of Athena Nike, Athena as bringer of victory, was established on the bastion in the early fifth century BC. The classical marble temple replaced an earlier shrine destroyed during the Persian occupation of 480–479 BC. Construction is generally dated to the 420s BC, in the years following the Peace of Nicias (421 BC). The temple was designed as a small tetrastyle amphiprostyle building — four Ionic columns at the front and four at the back — measuring roughly 8.3 by 5.6 metres. It is most often attributed to the architect Callicrates, who also worked on the Parthenon.

During the Ottoman period the temple was dismantled around 1686 and its blocks were built into a defensive bastion protecting the Propylaea. After Greek independence, the temple was one of the first monuments of the Acropolis to be excavated and reconstructed, in 1834–1838. It has been dismantled and rebuilt again in modern conservation programmes, most recently in a restoration completed in the early twenty-first century.

Architecture and sculpture

The temple stands on a bastion revetted with a high parapet. The parapet was decorated with a famous sculpted frieze showing figures of Nike in various activities, including the celebrated Sandalbinder Nike, depicted adjusting her sandal. The temple's own frieze depicted scenes from mythology and from historical battles between Greeks and Persians. Inside the cella stood a cult image of Athena Nike, described by the travel writer Pausanias as a wooden statue holding a pomegranate in one hand and a helmet in the other; unusually, the Nike of this cult was wingless, so that — according to legend — she could never fly away from Athens.
