Bangladesh Bank (Bengali: বাংলাদেশ ব্যাংক) is the central bank of Bangladesh. It was established by the Bangladesh Bank Order, 1972, effective from 16 December 1971, taking over from the Dhaka branch of the State Bank of Pakistan after the independence of Bangladesh. It is the country's monetary authority, issuer of the taka, and regulator of banks and financial institutions.

Functions

Bangladesh Bank formulates and implements monetary policy, manages foreign exchange reserves, issues currency notes, supervises scheduled banks and non-bank financial institutions, and operates the payment system. The governor is the chief executive, appointed by the government. The bank has promoted financial-inclusion policies, including agent banking, mobile financial services and refinancing schemes for agriculture and small enterprises, and is known for its emphasis on "green" and inclusive central banking.

2016 cyber heist

In February 2016, hackers used fraudulent SWIFT messages from Bangladesh Bank's systems at the Federal Reserve Bank of New York to steal US$81 million, which was laundered through Philippine casinos; a further attempted transfer was stopped partly because of a spelling error in the beneficiary name ("fandation"). It remains one of the largest bank cyber-thefts in history. Governor Atiur Rahman resigned in the aftermath.
