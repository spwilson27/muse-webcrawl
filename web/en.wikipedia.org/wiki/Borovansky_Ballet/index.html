Borovansky Ballet

The Borovansky Ballet was an Australian ballet company founded by Czech-born dancer Edouard Borovansky (1902–1959). Based in Melbourne, it was the leading Australian ballet company of the 1940s and 1950s and the direct forerunner of The Australian Ballet.

History

Borovansky arrived in Australia in 1929 as a member of Anna Pavlova's touring company and settled in Melbourne, where he opened a ballet school in 1939. By 1940 his student company was performing full-length classical ballets such as Giselle and Swan Lake, an ambitious undertaking for wartime Australia. After years of touring with thin resources, the company achieved professional status in 1952 with commercial backing.

Legacy

Following Borovansky's death in 1959, his widow Xenia continued the school until the company was absorbed in 1962 into the newly formed Australian Ballet, whose founding artistic director, Peggy van Praagh, had worked with Borovansky. The Borovansky repertoire and training style shaped the first generation of Australian classical dancers.
