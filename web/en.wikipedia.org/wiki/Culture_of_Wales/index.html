Culture of Wales

The culture of Wales is the Welsh customs, traditions, and practices, centred on the Welsh language, music, literature, and festivals such as the eisteddfod. Although Wales forms part of Great Britain and the United Kingdom, it retains a distinct cultural identity that has been actively revived since the nineteenth century.

Language

Welsh (Cymraeg), a Celtic language, is spoken by roughly a fifth of the population, with strongholds in the north and west. After centuries of decline, the language has been supported by Welsh-medium education, the Welsh Language Act 1993, and the Welsh-language television channel S4C. Bards and poets still compose in strict traditional metres such as the cynghanedd.

Music

Wales is often called the "land of song". Male voice choirs, which grew out of the chapel and industrial communities of the south Wales valleys, remain emblematic, as does congregational hymn singing. The harp is the national instrument. The National Eisteddfod, Europe's largest festival of music and poetry, awards chairs and crowns to winning poets, and local eisteddfodau are held across the country.

Symbols and festivals

National symbols include the red dragon (Y Ddraig Goch) of the Welsh flag, the daffodil and the leek, and St David, whose feast day, 1 March, is celebrated with traditional dress and school eisteddfodau. The Welsh lovespoon, carved as a token of affection, is a distinctive folk craft.

Sport

Rugby union is the national sport, and match days at the Principality Stadium in Cardiff are major cultural events. Football also commands a large following, and Welsh clubs Cardiff City and Swansea City play in the English league system.
