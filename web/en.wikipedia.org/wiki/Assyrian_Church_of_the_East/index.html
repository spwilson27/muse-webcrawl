The Assyrian Church of the East, officially the Holy Apostolic Catholic Assyrian Church of the East, is an Eastern Christian church that traces its origins to the ancient Church of the East, which evangelised Mesopotamia and Persia and, by the medieval period, maintained dioceses as far east as China and India. Its adherents are predominantly ethnic Assyrians.

History

The Church of the East organised itself at Seleucia-Ctesiphon and declared its independence from the western patriarchates at the Synod of Dadisho in 424. After the Council of Ephesus (431) condemned Nestorius, the Persian church — largely for political and theological reasons — remained outside the imperial church's settlement, and was long labelled "Nestorian," a term the church itself rejects as inaccurate. Under the Catholicos-Patriarchs at Baghdad it flourished under the Abbasid Caliphate, then suffered catastrophic losses under Timur in the 14th century. A schism in 1552 produced a rival line that entered communion with Rome, becoming the Chaldean Catholic Church. The Assyrian genocide (Sayfo) of 1915 and the Simele massacre of 1933 devastated the church in its homeland; a further schism in 1968 over calendar reform produced the Ancient Church of the East, partially healed in recent decades.

Liturgy and language

The church celebrates the East Syriac Rite, centred on the ancient Liturgy of Addai and Mari, notable for lacking an Institution narrative in its oldest form. Worship is traditionally in Syriac, a dialect of Aramaic. In 1994 the church and the Roman Catholic Church signed a Common Christological Declaration resolving the central doctrinal dispute dating to 431.
