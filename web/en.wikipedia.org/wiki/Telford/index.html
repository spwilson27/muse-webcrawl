Telford

Telford is a town in Shropshire, England, about 21 km east of Shrewsbury and 48 km northwest of Birmingham. Designated a new town in 1963 (as Dawley New Town, renamed Telford in 1968), it grew around older settlements including Wellington, Oakengates, Madeley and Dawley. It is named after the civil engineer Thomas Telford.

History

The area includes Ironbridge Gorge, a cradle of the Industrial Revolution where Abraham Darby first smelted iron with coke in 1709. The new town was planned to relieve congestion in the West Midlands conurbation and to provide housing and industry; Telford Development Corporation oversaw rapid growth around a new town centre and shopping mall.

Economy and transport

Telford hosts manufacturing and service employers on estates such as Hortonwood and Stafford Park. The M54 motorway and Telford Central railway station on the Wolverhampton–Shrewsbury line link it to the national networks. Nearby attractions include the Ironbridge Gorge Museums and The Wrekin hill.
