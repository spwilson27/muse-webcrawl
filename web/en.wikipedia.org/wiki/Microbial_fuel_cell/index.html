Microbial fuel cell

A microbial fuel cell (MFC) is a bio-electrochemical system that drives an electric current by using bacteria and a high-energy oxidant such as oxygen. It consists of an anode and a cathode separated by a cation-specific membrane.

Operation

Bacteria in the anode compartment oxidise organic substrates, releasing electrons and protons. Electrons travel through an external circuit to the cathode, where they combine with protons and an electron acceptor, typically oxygen, to form water. Natural wastewater and sediments can serve as fuel sources.

Applications and limits

MFCs are studied for wastewater treatment combined with electricity generation, remote power sources and biosensors. Power densities remain low compared with conventional fuel cells, and scaling up while maintaining efficiency is a major research challenge.
