Volkswagen emissions scandal

On-road testing exposed emissions far above laboratory results

The Volkswagen emissions scandal, also known as Dieselgate, began on 18 September 2015, when the United States Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) issued a notice of violation accusing Volkswagen Group of intentionally programming turbocharged direct injection (TDI) diesel engines to activate their emissions controls only during laboratory testing. In normal driving the vehicles emitted nitrogen oxides (NOx) up to 40 times the legal standard.

Background and discovery

From 2009 to 2015 Volkswagen marketed "clean diesel" cars in the United States — including the Jetta, Golf, Passat, Beetle and Audi A3 — fitted with a "defeat device" that detected test conditions from steering position, speed, engine duration and barometric pressure. The discrepancy was uncovered by researchers at West Virginia University, working with the International Council on Clean Transportation, whose on-road measurements in 2013–2014 showed real-world NOx emissions far above certification values. Confronted with the data, Volkswagen admitted that about 11 million cars worldwide, including 8.5 million in Europe, carried the software.

Consequences

CEO Martin Winterkorn resigned within days, and Volkswagen's share price lost roughly a third of its value in a week. The scandal has cost the group more than €35 billion in fines, settlements and buybacks, including a US$14.7 billion settlement in the United States, a criminal guilty plea with a $2.8 billion fine, and prison sentences for executives including Oliver Schmidt; former Audi CEO Rupert Stadler was convicted in Germany in 2023. In the United Kingdom, a group claim settled for £193 million in 2022. Dieselgate badly damaged the reputation of diesel passenger cars, accelerated manufacturers' shift to electric vehicles, prompted real-driving emissions (RDE) testing in the EU, and drew scrutiny of emissions practices across the industry, with investigations and fines also affecting other manufacturers.
