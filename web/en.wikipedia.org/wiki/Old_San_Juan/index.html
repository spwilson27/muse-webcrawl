Old San Juan

Old San Juan (Spanish: Viejo San Juan) is the oldest settlement within San Juan, Puerto Rico, and the historic colonial section of the city. It occupies the western end of the Isleta de San Juan, connected to the mainland by bridges, and is characterized by blue cobblestone streets and colourful Spanish colonial buildings dating to the 16th and 17th centuries.

History

San Juan was founded by Spanish colonists under Juan Ponce de León in 1521. To defend the entrance to its rich harbour, Spain built massive fortifications, including Castillo San Felipe del Morro and Castillo San Cristóbal, among the largest Spanish forts in the Americas. In 1983, La Fortaleza and the San Juan National Historic Site were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Landmarks

Notable sites include San Juan Cathedral, La Fortaleza (the governor's residence and the oldest executive mansion in continuous use in the Americas), the San Juan Gate, and the Paseo del Morro. The district is now a major centre for tourism, dining, and festivals such as the San Sebastián Street Festival.
