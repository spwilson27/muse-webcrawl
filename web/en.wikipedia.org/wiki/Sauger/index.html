Sauger

The sauger (Sander canadensis) is a freshwater perciform fish of the family Percidae, native to North America. It is closely related to the larger walleye, with which it shares much of its range in the Mississippi, Missouri, and Ohio river systems and the Great Lakes watershed.

Description

Saugers are slimmer than walleyes, typically 12–18 inches (30–46 cm) long, with a mottled brown or olive coloration and distinct dark saddle-like blotches. They can be distinguished from walleyes by the rows of spots on the spiny dorsal fin and by the absence of the white tip on the lower lobe of the tail fin that walleyes possess. Saugers are better adapted to turbid water, having large, light-sensitive eyes.

Life history and fishing

Saugers spawn in spring over gravel and rock in flowing water. They feed on smaller fish and invertebrates and are popular sport fish, valued as food. Where saugers and walleyes overlap, they sometimes produce a hybrid known as the saugeye, which is also stocked in some reservoirs.
