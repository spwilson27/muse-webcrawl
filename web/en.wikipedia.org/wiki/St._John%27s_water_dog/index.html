St. John's water dog

The St. John's water dog, also called the St. John's dog or lesser Newfoundland, was a landrace dog breed from Newfoundland, Canada. It is now extinct, but it is the ancestor of modern retrievers, including the Labrador Retriever, Golden Retriever, and Flat-coated Retriever.

Description and history

These medium-sized, strongly built dogs had short, water-repellent coats, usually black with white markings on the chest, chin, and feet. Fishermen on the Grand Banks used them to retrieve nets, ropes, and fish from the cold Atlantic and to haul lines between boats. Dogs exported to England in the nineteenth century were crossed into British gundog stock and founded the retriever breeds. Back home, a heavy dog tax and rabies quarantine restrictions in the late nineteenth and twentieth centuries led to the breed's decline; the last known specimens died in the early 1980s.
