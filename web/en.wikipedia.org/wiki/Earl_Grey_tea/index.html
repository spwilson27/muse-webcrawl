Earl Grey tea

Earl Grey tea is a tea blend flavoured with oil of bergamot, a citrus fruit grown mainly in Calabria, Italy. Traditionally made with black tea — often Chinese or blended Chinese and Indian leaves — Earl Grey is one of the most popular flavoured teas in the world. Variations include Lady Grey (with lemon and orange peel and cornflowers), green Earl Grey, and rooibos versions.

Origin legend

The blend is named after Charles Grey, 2nd Earl Grey, British Prime Minister from 1830 to 1834. According to the best-known legend, a grateful Chinese mandarin — whose son was saved from drowning by one of the Earl's servants — presented the tea to Grey, whose household at Howick Hall found the bergamot masked the lime taste of the local water. Another version holds that the tea was a diplomatic gift. The Jacksons of Piccadilly tea merchants claimed to have created the blend from a recipe given to them by the Earl in the 1830s.

Production

Bergamot flavour is applied by spraying black tea with bergamot essential oil pressed from the rind of Citrus bergamia, or with natural or artificial flavouring. Quality grades range from whole-leaf orthodox teas to finely broken fannings used in tea bags.

In culture

Earl Grey is famously the favourite tea of Captain Jean-Luc Picard in Star Trek: The Next Generation, ordered as "Tea. Earl Grey. Hot." It is also associated with British afternoon tea culture generally.
