Nordic Museum

[Image: Nordiska museet, Stockholm]

The Nordic Museum (Swedish: Nordiska museet) is a museum located on Djurgården, an island in central Stockholm, Sweden, dedicated to the cultural history and ethnography of Sweden from the early modern period to the contemporary era. Founded in 1873 by Artur Hazelius, who also founded the open-air museum Skansen, it is Sweden's largest museum of cultural history.

The building

The present museum building, designed by architect Isak Gustaf Clason in a Renaissance Revival style inspired by Danish and Swedish castles, was constructed between 1888 and 1907. Its vast main hall, overlooked by a large statue of Gustav Vasa by Carl Milles, is among the largest non-church interior spaces in Sweden.

Collections

The museum holds over 1.5 million objects, including folk costumes, textiles, furniture, table settings, jewellery, and an extensive photographic and archival collection. Permanent exhibitions address Swedish traditions, fashion, folk art, the Sámi people, and homes and interiors across five centuries. The museum also manages several historic properties, including Julita Manor and Tyresö Palace.

History

Hazelius first exhibited his Scandinavian ethnographic collection in 1873; the foundation governing the museum was established in 1880. The museum's collections helped define the study of Swedish folk culture, and its approach influenced ethnographic museums across Europe.
