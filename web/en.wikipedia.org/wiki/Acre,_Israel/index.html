Acre, Israel

Acre (Hebrew: עַכּוֹ, ʿAkko; Arabic: عكّا, ʿAkkā), known in the Hellenistic and Crusader periods as Ptolemais and Saint Jean d'Acre, is a port city in the Northern District of Israel, on a promontory at the northern end of Haifa Bay on the Mediterranean coast. Continuously inhabited since the Bronze Age, it is one of the oldest continuously settled sites in the world.

History

Acre was a major Phoenician and later Hellenistic port. During the Crusades it became the principal port of the Kingdom of Jerusalem; its fall to the Mamluks in 1291 marked the end of Crusader rule in the Levant. In 1799 the Ottoman governor Ahmad al-Jazzar, aided by the British navy, repelled Napoleon Bonaparte's siege of the city. The Old City, with its Crusader and Ottoman remains — citadel, mosques, khans and the underground Crusader city — was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2001.

Today

Modern Acre is a mixed city of Jews and Arabs, known for its historic old town, fishing port, markets and the Baháʼí holy places nearby, including the Shrine of Baháʼu'lláh at Bahjí.
