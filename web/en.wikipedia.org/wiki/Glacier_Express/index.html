Glacier Express

The Glacier Express is a direct train connecting the railway stations of the two major mountain resorts of Zermatt and St. Moritz via Andermatt in the central Swiss Alps. Operated jointly by the Matterhorn Gotthard Bahn and the Rhaetian Railway, it is marketed as the "slowest express train in the world," taking about eight hours to traverse its scenic route.

Route

The journey covers 291 kilometres (181 miles), crossing 291 bridges and passing through 91 tunnels, including the 15.4-kilometre Furka Base Tunnel. From Zermatt, beneath the Matterhorn, the train descends the Matter valley to Brig, passes the Rhone Glacier region, climbs to the Oberalp Pass — at 2,033 metres the highest point of the journey — and descends to Disentis/Mustér. There the operating railway changes, and the train continues through the dramatic Rhine Gorge, over the Landwasser Viaduct near Filisur, and through Chur's rail network to St. Moritz via the Albula line, a UNESCO World Heritage site.

History and service

The first Glacier Express ran in 1930, made possible by the completion of the line between Brig and Disentis. Year-round operation began in 1982 with the opening of the Furka Base Tunnel, which bypassed the old summit line over the Furka Pass (now preserved by a heritage steam railway). Modern trainsets feature panoramic coaches with large windows and at-seat dining; a premium "Excellence Class" was introduced in 2019. A seat reservation is compulsory in addition to the ticket, and the service is popular with international tourists touring Switzerland by rail.
