Hornbostel–Sachs

Type: Musical instrument classification
Authors: Erich Moritz von Hornbostel, Curt Sachs
Published: 1914

Hornbostel–Sachs is a system of musical instrument classification devised by Erich Moritz von Hornbostel and Curt Sachs, first published in 1914. Modeled on the Dewey Decimal system, it classifies instruments by how they produce sound and remains the most widely used scheme in organology and ethnomusicology.

Categories

The top level divides instruments into idiophones (sound from the vibrating body itself), membranophones (vibrating membrane), chordophones (vibrating strings), aerophones (vibrating air), and later electrophones (electric sound production). Each category is subdivided with decimal suffixes describing playing method and construction.

Usage

Museums, instrument collections, and the MIMO (Musical Instrument Museums Online) consortium use Hornbostel–Sachs numbers as standard catalogue identifiers.
