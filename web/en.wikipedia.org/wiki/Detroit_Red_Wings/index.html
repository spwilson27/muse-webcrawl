Detroit Red Wings

The Detroit Red Wings are a professional ice hockey team based in Detroit, Michigan. They compete in the National Hockey League (NHL) as a member of the Atlantic Division of the Eastern Conference. One of the "Original Six" teams, the Red Wings are one of the most successful franchises in NHL history, having won the Stanley Cup eleven times — the most of any American-based NHL team.

History

The franchise began in 1926 as the Detroit Cougars, became the Falcons in 1930 and the Red Wings in 1932 under owner James Norris, who adopted the winged-wheel logo inspired by the Montreal Winged Wheelers. Under coach Jack Adams and stars such as Gordie Howe, Ted Lindsay, Sid Abel (the "Production Line") and Terry Sawchuk, Detroit dominated the early 1950s.

After a long decline, the team revived under owner Mike Ilitch and coach Scotty Bowman, with captain Steve Yzerman, Nicklas Lidström and the "Russian Five". Detroit won Cups in 1997, 1998, 2002 and 2008 and reached the playoffs in 25 consecutive seasons (1990–91 to 2015–16), the longest active streak in North American major league sports at the time.

Traditions

Fans throw octopuses onto the ice during the playoffs, a tradition begun in 1952 symbolising the eight wins then needed to claim the Cup. The team played at Joe Louis Arena from 1979 to 2017 before moving to Little Caesars Arena.
