Andrey Andreyevich Markov Jr. (Russian: Андре́й Андре́евич Ма́рков; 9 September 1903 – 11 October 1979) was a Soviet mathematician and logician, the son of the famous probabilist Andrey Markov Sr. He is regarded as the founder of the Soviet school of constructive mathematics and logic.

Work

Markov's most celebrated contribution is Markov's principle, a statement in constructive mathematics asserting that if it is impossible for a decidable predicate over the natural numbers to fail for every value, then there is some value for which it holds. He also developed the theory of Markov algorithms (also called normal algorithms), a string-rewriting model of computation equivalent in power to Turing machines.

In 1947, independently of Emil Post, Markov proved the undecidability of the word problem for semigroups, one of the first undecidability results in algebra. He directed the Laboratory of Mathematical Logic at the Steklov Institute and corresponded member of the USSR Academy of Sciences from 1953.
