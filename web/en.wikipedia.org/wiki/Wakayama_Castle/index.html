Wakayama Castle

Wakayama Castle (和歌山城, Wakayama-jō) is a Japanese castle located in the city of Wakayama, Wakayama Prefecture, Japan. Built in 1585 on the orders of Toyotomi Hideyoshi, it later became the seat of the Kishū branch of the Tokugawa clan, one of the gosanke — the three collateral houses of the shogunate — and the birthplace of the eighth shōgun, Tokugawa Yoshimune.

History

The castle was constructed on Mount Torafusu in 1585 by Toyotomi Hidenaga, half-brother of Hideyoshi. It passed through several lords before Tokugawa Yorinobu, tenth son of Tokugawa Ieyasu, took possession in 1619 and greatly expanded it as the seat of Kishū Domain. The Kishū Tokugawa produced two shōguns, Yoshimune and Iemochi.

The original keep survived until 1945, when it was destroyed in an American air raid on Wakayama. The current main keep was rebuilt in ferroconcrete in 1958 following the original design, and now houses a museum. The reconstructed Nishinomaru garden (with the Ohashi Rōka bridge) and surviving gates are designated cultural properties; the grounds, planted with cherry trees, are a popular site for hanami.
