Multi-function display

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Multi-function displays in a modern glass cockpit.

A multi-function display (MFD) is a small screen surrounded by configurable buttons (often called bezel keys or line-select keys) that can display information in a variety of user-selectable formats. MFDs are a core component of the glass cockpit: instead of dedicating a separate instrument to each function, a single MFD can present flight plans, moving maps, weather radar, traffic, engine and systems synoptics, checklists, and sensor video, with the crew switching "pages" as needed.

In aviation

MFDs were introduced in military fighters in the 1970s and 1980s (for example in the F/A-18 Hornet) and spread to airliners and general aviation with electronic flight instrument systems (EFIS) and integrated avionics suites such as the Garmin G1000. In a typical airliner layout, each pilot has a primary flight display (PFD) and one or more MFDs; by convention the display that presents navigation information is also called a navigation display (ND), and the central display presenting engine indications is the EICAS or ECAM display. Reversionary modes let a surviving display substitute for a failed one, so flight-critical information is never lost with a single screen failure.

Other applications

The term is also used for the central information screen in automobiles (navigation, media, vehicle settings), for marine chartplotters combining radar, sonar, and GPS charts, and for military vehicle and mission-system displays. In all cases the design principle is the same: a reconfigurable display unit whose function is defined by software and by the bezel keys or touchscreen selections around it, rather than by fixed hardware.
