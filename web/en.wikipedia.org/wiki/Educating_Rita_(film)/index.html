Educating Rita is a 1983 British comedy-drama film directed by Lewis Gilbert and written by Willy Russell, adapted from Russell's 1980 stage play of the same name. It stars Michael Caine as Frank Bryant, a disillusioned, alcoholic university lecturer, and Julie Walters — reprising her stage role — as Rita, a working-class Liverpool hairdresser who enrols with the Open University determined to get an education and change her life.

Plot

Susan "Rita" White, a 26-year-old hairdresser frustrated by the narrow horizons of her life and her husband Denny's expectation that she settle down and have a baby, signs up for an Open University literature course. Her assigned tutor is Frank Bryant, a once-promising poet now cynically teaching to fund his drinking, whose study hides bottles behind the bookshelves. At first bewildered by academic conventions — her first essay compares Howard's End to a Mills & Boon romance — Rita's freshness and determination revive Frank's interest in teaching, while her growing confidence transforms her dialect, dress and ambitions, straining her marriage and, eventually, her relationship with Frank, who fears he has created a monster of middle-class pretension. After passing her examinations, Rita gains the freedom to choose her own future, while Frank, dismissed for drunkenness, departs for a new start in Australia.

Reception and awards

The film was a critical and commercial success. Caine and Walters both won BAFTA Awards (Best Actor and Best Actress) and Golden Globe Awards for their performances, and both were nominated for Academy Awards; Russell's screenplay also received an Oscar nomination. Walters's performance launched her film career. Filmed in Dublin, with Trinity College standing in for the unnamed northern English university, Educating Rita is frequently compared to Pygmalion and remains a staple of British cinema and school syllabuses.
