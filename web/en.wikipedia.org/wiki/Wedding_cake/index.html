Wedding cake

A wedding cake is the traditional cake served at wedding receptions following dinner, a centrepiece of the celebration in many cultures. In modern Western weddings it is often a large, elaborately decorated multi-tiered cake that the couple cut together in a ceremonial first slice.

History

Wedding cake traditions trace partly to ancient Rome, where a wheat or barley cake was broken over the bride's head for good fortune. In Britain the traditional form became a rich fruitcake covered with marzipan and royal icing, whose white colour signified purity and the expense of refined sugar. The stacked, tiered design is said to have been inspired by the spire of St Bride's Church in London, and was popularised in the nineteenth century, notably at royal weddings such as Queen Victoria's.

Customs

Customs surrounding the cake include the couple cutting the first slice together, feeding each other a bite, saving the top tier for a christening (in Britain) or first anniversary (in the United States), and the later, more boisterous practice of smashing cake into a spouse's face. Guest portions derive from the older custom of giving cake to guests for luck.

Modern styles

Contemporary cakes range from fondant-covered showpieces with sugar flowers to "naked" cakes, cupcakes and dessert tables. Sponge and flavour varieties have largely displaced fruitcake in many countries, though fruitcake remains traditional in Britain and parts of the Commonwealth.
