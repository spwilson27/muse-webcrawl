Queer Nation is an LGBTQ activist organisation founded in New York City in March 1990 by members of ACT UP. Frustrated by escalating anti-gay violence and by what they saw as cautious, respectability-oriented gay politics, its founders sought to assert queer visibility through confrontational direct action. Within months, chapters formed in dozens of cities across the United States and abroad.

Tactics and campaigns

Queer Nation popularised the chant "We're here! We're queer! Get used to it!" and the defiant reclamation of the slur "queer" as a self-description — a usage that spread through activism into academia as "queer theory" and ultimately into mainstream language. Its actions included "Queer Nights Out" (visible incursions into straight bars and suburbs), kiss-ins, and protests against homophobic media coverage and public figures. In 1990 its members disrupted events and distributed manifestos such as "Queers Read This" at New York's Pride march. A famous 1991 campaign targeted the film Basic Instinct for its depiction of lesbian and bisexual characters.

Decline and influence

The New York chapter, organised without formal leadership, fragmented by the mid-1990s amid internal disputes over race, gender, and strategy. Though short-lived, Queer Nation permanently changed LGBT politics: it accelerated the adoption of "queer" as an umbrella identity, modelled a more confrontational style of activism, and influenced later movements and organisations.
