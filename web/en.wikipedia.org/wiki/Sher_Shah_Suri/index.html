Sher Shah Suri
Born: 1486 (or 1472), Sasaram, India
Died: 22 May 1545, Kalinjar
Reign: 1540–1545, founder of the Suri Empire

Sher Shah Suri (born Farid Khan) was an Afghan ruler who founded the Suri Empire in northern India after defeating the Mughal emperor Humayun at Chausa (1539) and Kannauj (1540). Though he reigned only five years, he is remembered as a brilliant administrator.

Reforms

He reorganised revenue assessment, issued the standardised silver rupiya (ancestor of the rupee), built and policed the Grand Trunk Road from Bengal to Kabul with sarais and dak (postal) stations, and established a centralised administration later adopted by Akbar. He died in a gunpowder explosion during the siege of Kalinjar; his island mausoleum stands at Sasaram, Bihar.
