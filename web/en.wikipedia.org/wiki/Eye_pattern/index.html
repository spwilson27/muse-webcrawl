Eye pattern

In telecommunication, an eye pattern, also known as an eye diagram, is an oscilloscope display in which a digital data signal from a receiver is repetitively sampled and applied to the vertical input, while the data rate is used as the horizontal sweep trigger. It is so called because, for several types of coding, the pattern looks like a series of eyes between two rails.

Interpretation

By overlaying many symbol periods, the eye diagram reveals the quality of a digital link at a glance. The vertical "eye opening" measures noise margin, the horizontal opening measures timing margin and jitter, and the thickness of the traces shows distortion and intersymbol interference. A widely open eye indicates a signal that a receiver can easily decode; a closed eye indicates severe degradation. Engineers quantify eye diagrams with mask tests, in which the trace must not intrude into a specified polygon region.

Applications

Eye diagrams are standard diagnostic tools in the design and testing of high-speed serial links, fibre-optic communications, USB, HDMI, Ethernet, and memory interfaces. Modern oscilloscopes construct them from captured waveforms in software and can decompose jitter into random and deterministic components.
