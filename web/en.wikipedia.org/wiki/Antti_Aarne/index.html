Antti Amatus Aarne (5 December 1867 – 2 February 1925) was a Finnish folklorist best known for developing the tale-type classification system that bears his name. Working within the Finnish historic-geographic ("Finnish method") school of folklore studies associated with Kaarle Krohn, Aarne sought to catalogue the world's folktales by their underlying narrative types.

The tale-type index

In 1910 Aarne published his Verzeichnis der Märchentypen ("Index of Tale Types"), classifying folktales — chiefly European and West Asian animal tales, fairy tales, religious tales, realistic tales, tales of the stupid ogre, jokes and formula tales — into numbered types. The American folklorist Stith Thompson translated and expanded the index (1928, 1961), producing the Aarne–Thompson index, later revised again by Hans-Jörg Uther as the ATU index (2004).

Career and legacy

Aarne taught at the University of Helsinki, where he became professor of Finnish and comparative folklore. His classification remains a standard reference for folklorists, even as scholars debate the limits of type-based approaches to oral narrative.
