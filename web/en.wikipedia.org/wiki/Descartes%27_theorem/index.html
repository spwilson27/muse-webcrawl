Descartes' theorem

In geometry, Descartes' theorem states that for every four kissing (mutually tangent) circles, the radii of the circles satisfy a certain quadratic equation. By solving this equation, one can construct a fourth circle tangent to three given, mutually tangent circles.

Statement

If the curvatures (signed bends) of the four circles are k1, k2, k3, k4, then (k1+k2+k3+k4)² = 2(k1²+k2²+k3²+k4²). The theorem is named for René Descartes, who discussed it in a 1643 letter to Princess Elisabeth of the Palatinate. It was later rediscovered and popularised in the poem "The Kiss Precise" by Frederick Soddy.
