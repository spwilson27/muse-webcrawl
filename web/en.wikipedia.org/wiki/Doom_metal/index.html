Doom metal

Doom metal
Stylistic originsHeavy metal, blues rock, psychedelic rock
Cultural originsEarly to mid-1970s, United Kingdom and United States
Fusion genresStoner metal, sludge metal, drone metal
SubgenresEpic doom, traditional doom, death-doom, funeral doom, gothic doom

Doom metal is an extreme subgenre of heavy metal music that typically employs slower tempos, low-tuned guitars, and a much "thicker" or "heavier" sound than other heavy metal genres. Both the music and the lyrics intend to evoke a sense of despair, dread, and impending doom. The genre is strongly influenced by the early work of Black Sabbath, whose songs such as "Black Sabbath," "Electric Funeral," and "Hand of Doom" set the template.

History

Proto-doom sounds emerged in the late 1960s and early 1970s with bands such as Black Sabbath, Pentagram, Blue Cheer, and Sir Lord Baltimore. In the late 1970s and early 1980s, acts including Witchfinder General, Trouble, Saint Vitus, Candlemass, and Pentagram shaped traditional doom metal during the New Wave of British Heavy Metal era and its aftermath. Swedish band Candlemass, with Epicus Doomicus Metallicus (1986), defined "epic doom" with operatic vocals.

Subgenres

Death-doom and funeral doom

In the early 1990s, British bands Paradise Lost, My Dying Bride, and Anathema fused doom with death metal growls and gothic atmospheres ("the Peaceville Three"), while acts such as Thergothon and Skepticism slowed the music further into funeral doom.

Stoner, sludge, and drone

Stoner metal (Sleep, Kyuss) emphasized groove and psychedelia; sludge metal (Eyehategod, Crowbar) added hardcore punk aggression; and drone metal (Earth, Sunn O)))) pushed minimalism and volume to extremes.

Characteristics

Guitars and bass are typically down-tuned, heavily distorted, and played at slow tempos. Lyrics focus on depression, death, religion, and the occult. Vocals range from mournful clean singing (Candlemass) to harsh growls (death-doom).
