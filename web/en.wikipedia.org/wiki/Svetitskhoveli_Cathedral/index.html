Svetitskhoveli Cathedral

Svetitskhoveli Cathedral (Georgian: სვეტიცხოველი, "the Living Pillar Cathedral") is an Eastern Orthodox cathedral in the historic town of Mtskheta, Georgia, about 20 km northwest of Tbilisi. A masterpiece of medieval Georgian architecture, it is one of the most sacred places in Georgia and, together with the other historical monuments of Mtskheta, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History and legend

The present church was built in 1010–1029 by the architect Arsukisdze on the site of earlier churches dating to the 4th century, when Georgia adopted Christianity. According to tradition, the cathedral enshrines the robe of Christ, buried beneath the spot where a great cedar grew; when the cedar was cut to build the church, one pillar — the "living pillar" — is said to have miraculously risen, giving the cathedral its name.

Significance

For centuries Georgian kings were crowned and buried here, and it remains the seat of major ceremonies of the Georgian Orthodox Church. The building is a cross-in-square church with a tall drum and cone, decorated with stone carving and partly surviving frescoes.
