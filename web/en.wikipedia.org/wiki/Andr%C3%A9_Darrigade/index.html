André Darrigade (born 24 April 1929) is a French former professional road racing cyclist, regarded as one of the greatest sprinters in cycling history. Nicknamed "le Lévrier des Landes" (the Greyhound of the Landes), he won 22 stages of the Tour de France between 1953 and 1964 and wore the yellow jersey on numerous occasions.

Career

Darrigade won the points classification (green jersey) at the Tour de France in 1959 and 1961 and was famously a regular winner of the opening stages. His finest achievement came at the 1959 World Championships in Zandvoort, where he won the road race title. He also won the Giro di Lombardia in 1956 and stages of the Giro d'Italia and numerous criteriums.

Later life

After retiring, Darrigade settled in the Landes region of southwestern France, where a stadium and streets bear his name. In 2016 he was seriously injured when struck by a car while attending the Tour de France, but recovered. He remains the oldest surviving wearer of the yellow jersey from his era of the sport.
