Coal mining in the United States

Coal mining in the United States has been a major industry since the late 19th century, fuelling industrialisation, rail transport and electricity generation. Production peaked in 2008 and has since declined sharply as natural gas, wind and solar power displaced coal in electricity markets.

Methods and regions

Coal is extracted by surface mining, including mountaintop removal in Appalachia, and by underground longwall and room-and-pillar mining. Wyoming's Powder River Basin accounts for roughly 40 percent of US output, followed by West Virginia, Pennsylvania, Illinois and Kentucky.

Decline

Employment, once numbering in the hundreds of thousands, has fallen to tens of thousands through mechanisation and declining demand, with significant economic effects on coal communities.
