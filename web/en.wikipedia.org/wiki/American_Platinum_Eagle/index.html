The American Platinum Eagle is the official platinum bullion coin of the United States. First issued by the United States Mint in 1997, it was authorized by Congress in the Platinum Coin Bullion program (Public Law 104-208). The coins are struck in 1, 1/2, 1/4, and 1/10 troy ounce sizes with face values of $100, $50, $25, and $10 respectively, though fractional sizes were discontinued after 2008.

Design

The obverse, designed by John Mercanti, features a close-up portrait of the face of the Statue of Liberty. The reverse of bullion issues depicts a bald eagle soaring over the American landscape, designed by Thomas D. Rogers. From 1998 to 2017, proof versions carried annually changing reverse designs in themed series, including "Vistas of Liberty", patriotic scenes, and tributes to the foundations of American democracy such as the Preamble to the Constitution and the Bill of Rights.

Production and sales

Platinum Eagles are legal tender and are eligible for inclusion in individual retirement accounts (IRAs). Mintages have generally been far lower than those of the American Gold Eagle and American Silver Eagle, reflecting the smaller market for platinum bullion, and the Mint suspended sales at times during periods of price volatility, including in 2008.
