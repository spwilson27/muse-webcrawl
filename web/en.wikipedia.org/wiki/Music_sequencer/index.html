Music sequencer

A music sequencer (or simply sequencer) is a device or application software that can record, edit or play back music in the form of note and performance information — typically MIDI data controlling pitch, timing, velocity and other parameters — rather than recorded audio. Sequencers separate the instructions for making music from the sounds themselves, so a performance can be edited note by note and replayed through different instruments.

History

Precursors include player pianos and the electromechanical drum machines and step sequencers of the 1960s–70s, in which a pattern of switches or pins triggered sounds in a loop; analogue sequencers such as those built into the Moog modular systems controlled early synthesizers via control voltages. The introduction of MIDI in 1983 standardised digital control, and software sequencers on home computers — notably Steinberg's Cubase and C-Lab's Notator on the Atari ST — became the centre of music production. Hardware workstation sequencers and MPC-style sampling sequencers (Akai MPC60, 1988) defined hip-hop and electronic dance production.

Modern sequencers

Today sequencing is a core function of every digital audio workstation (DAW) such as Ableton Live, FL Studio, Logic Pro and Cubase, offering piano-roll editors, step sequencers, drum grids and automation of any parameter. Dedicated hardware sequencers remain popular in modular synthesis and live electronic performance, with devices from Elektron, Arturia and others controlling instruments via MIDI and CV/gate.
