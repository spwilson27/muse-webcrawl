Thor Odinson is a superhero appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by editor Stan Lee, scripter Larry Lieber and penciller Jack Kirby, the character first appeared in Journey into Mystery #83 (August 1962) in the Silver Age of Comic Books. Thor is based on the Norse mythological god of the same name, the Asgardian God of Thunder who wields the enchanted hammer Mjolnir, which grants him the ability to fly and manipulate weather, among other superhuman attributes. A founding member of the superhero team the Avengers, Thor has a host of supporting characters and enemies, including his father Odin, his younger brother and archenemy Loki, and the Warriors Three.

Publication history

Thor debuted as the feature of Journey into Mystery, in a story in which lame physician Donald Blake found a magical cane in a Norwegian cave that transformed him into the thunder god and became Mjolnir. The series was retitled Thor with issue #126 (1966). Jack Kirby's cosmic, mythic art defined the character through the 1960s, and Walt Simonson's acclaimed run (1983–1987) introduced Beta Ray Bill and the Surtur saga. Decades of storylines followed, including Thor's death and the destruction of Asgard in the Ragnarök arc, his return in 2007, his loss of worthiness in Original Sin (2014) — after which Jane Foster wielded Mjolnir as Thor — and his eventual ascension as All-Father, King of Asgard.

Fictional character biography

Thor is the crown prince of Asgard, son of Odin, the All-Father, and the elder goddess Gaea. Arrogant in his youth, he was humbled by Odin, who banished him to Earth in mortal form to learn humility. As Thor, he defends both Asgard and Earth, standing alongside the Avengers against threats such as Loki, the Enchantress, the Executioner, Hela, Malekith, Gorr the God Butcher and the fire demon Surtur. Mjolnir, forged from the mystical metal uru, can be lifted only by one who is "worthy".

In other media

Chris Hemsworth portrays Thor in the Marvel Cinematic Universe, appearing in Thor (2011), The Avengers (2012), Thor: The Dark World (2013), Avengers: Age of Ultron (2015), Thor: Ragnarok (2017), Avengers: Infinity War (2018), Avengers: Endgame (2019) and Thor: Love and Thunder (2022). The character also appears in numerous animated series, video games and other adaptations, and is consistently ranked among the greatest comic book characters of all time.
