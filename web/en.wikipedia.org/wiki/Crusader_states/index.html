Crusader states

The Crusader states were a number of feudal states established by Western European Christians in the Levant during the Crusades in the 12th and 13th centuries. The four original states founded after the First Crusade were the County of Edessa, the Principality of Antioch, the Kingdom of Jerusalem, and the County of Tripoli. Collectively they were known to contemporaries as Outremer ("overseas").

History

The First Crusade captured Jerusalem in 1099, and the new states survived through a combination of fortifications, military orders such as the Knights Templar and Knights Hospitaller, Italian maritime trade, and divisions among their Muslim neighbours. Edessa fell to Zengi in 1144, prompting the Second Crusade. Saladin's victory at Hattin in 1187 led to the fall of Jerusalem, though a reduced kingdom centred on Acre persisted through the Third Crusade until Acre itself fell to the Mamluks in 1291, ending the Crusader presence on the mainland.

Government and society

The states were ruled by a Latin Christian aristocracy presiding over a mostly indigenous population of Muslims, Jews, and Eastern Christians. The Kingdom of Jerusalem developed a distinctive legal system recorded in the Assizes of Jerusalem. Later Crusader states were founded in Greece after the Fourth Crusade, including the Latin Empire of Constantinople and the Principality of Achaea. See also Crusades and Kingdom of Jerusalem.
