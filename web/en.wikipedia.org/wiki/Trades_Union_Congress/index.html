Trades Union Congress

The Trades Union Congress (TUC) is a national trade union centre, a federation of trade unions that collectively represent most unionised workers in England and Wales. There are currently 48 affiliated unions, with a total of about 5.7 million members. The current general secretary is Paul Nowak, who succeeded Frances O'Grady in January 2023.

History

The TUC was founded in 1868 by the United Kingdom Alliance of Organized Trades, which sponsored the meeting of 34 delegates at the Mechanics' Institute, Manchester on 2 June 1868. The founding meeting became something of a pretext to hold the recently banned trade unions' congress as a platform for both national and international union opinion. The TUC grew rapidly, later setting up a Parliamentary Committee to lobby government.

A major difficulty that the Congress encountered was the growth of New Unionism in the late 1880s, which drew unskilled and poorly paid workers into unions for the first time. The TUC supported the eight-hour day, and was closely involved in the founding of the Labour Party in 1900, though it deliberately kept out of electoral politics for its first decades.

General Strike of 1926

The TUC coordinated the General Strike of 1926 in support of coal miners whose wages were threatened by mine owners. The strike, involving some 1.7 million workers, lasted nine days before the TUC called it off without concessions, an episode that shaped British industrial relations for a generation.

Governance

The TUC's decision-making body is the annual Congress, attended by delegates from each affiliated union. Between congresses, decisions are taken by the General Council, which includes 56 members elected by affiliated unions as well as ex-officio members. The General Council elects a president each year. Since 2019, the TUC has also held a Youth Conference for members under 30.

Relations with government

During the 1970s the TUC was a central actor in the "social contract" with the Labour government of Harold Wilson and James Callaghan. Its influence declined sharply under the Conservative governments of Margaret Thatcher, whose industrial relations legislation restricted union activity. The TUC continues to campaign on pay, employment rights, health and safety, and public services.
