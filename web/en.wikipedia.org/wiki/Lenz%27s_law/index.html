Lenz's law

Lenz's law states that the direction of the electric current induced in a conductor by a changing magnetic field is such that the magnetic field created by the induced current opposes the change in the initial magnetic field. It is named after physicist Emil Lenz, who formulated it in 1834.

Statement

Lenz's law is expressed by the negative sign in Faraday's law of induction: the induced electromotive force around a closed loop equals the negative of the rate of change of magnetic flux through the loop. The law is a consequence of the conservation of energy: if the induced current aided the change that produced it, a runaway process would create energy from nothing.

Applications

The law explains eddy-current braking used in trains and roller coasters, the behaviour of inductors in circuits, and the opposition encountered when a magnet is pushed toward a coil or a conducting ring — the induced current generates a field that repels the approaching pole.
