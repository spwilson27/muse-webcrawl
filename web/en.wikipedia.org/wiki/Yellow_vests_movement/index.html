The yellow vests movement (French: mouvement des gilets jaunes) was a populist, grassroots protest movement that began in France in November 2018 and continued, in recurring waves of Saturday demonstrations, for more than a year. Named for the fluorescent yellow high-visibility vests that French motorists are required to carry in their vehicles, the movement grew out of anger over a planned rise in fuel taxes and broadened into a wider protest against the cost of living, economic inequality, and the presidency of Emmanuel Macron.

Origins

The movement emerged in autumn 2018 through online petitions and social media — notably a petition by Priscillia Ludosky against fuel prices — rather than through unions or political parties. Its first nationwide day of action, "Act I" on 17 November 2018, drew hundreds of thousands of protesters who blocked roads and occupied roundabouts across France, especially in rural and peri-urban areas whose residents depended on cars.

Demands and development

Beyond cancellation of the fuel tax — which the government first suspended and then abandoned — protesters demanded higher minimum wages, restoration of the wealth tax abolished by Macron, and institutional reforms such as citizens' initiative referendums (RIC). Some demonstrations in Paris, including those of 1 December 2018 around the Arc de Triomphe, turned violent, and the policing of the protests caused numerous serious injuries, drawing criticism from human rights bodies.

Government response and decline

In December 2018 Macron announced a package of concessions worth roughly €10 billion, including a rise in the minimum wage supplement and tax-free overtime, and launched a "Great National Debate" in early 2019. Turnout gradually declined through 2019, though protesters continued to gather, and the movement's remnants later overlapped with demonstrations against pension reform and, later still, pandemic health measures.
