Siege of Veii

Date: c. 405–396 BC
Location: Veii, Etruria (Italy)
Result: Roman victory; annexation of Veii

The siege of Veii was a legendary ten-year siege of the wealthy Etruscan city of Veii by the Roman Republic, traditionally dated to about 405–396 BC and concluding under the dictator Marcus Furius Camillus. The fall of Veii was the first great Roman conquest, roughly doubling the territory of the Roman state.

Account in the sources

Livy and Plutarch describe a long blockade modelled on the siege of Troy. Rome introduced pay for soldiers, allowing campaigns to continue through the winter. An oracle reportedly declared that Veii would fall when the waters of the Alban Lake were drawn off; the Romans built a drainage tunnel (emissarium) and also drove a tunnel (cuniculus) under Veii's walls into the citadel. Camillus vowed a temple to Juno Regina and invited the goddess to transfer her seat to Rome (evocatio).

Aftermath

Veii was captured, plundered, and its territory annexed; its surviving population was enslaved or dispersed, and the site was largely abandoned. The conquest was a turning point in Rome's expansion in Italy, though the triumph was soon followed by the Gallic sack of Rome in 390 BC.
