Joséphine de Beauharnais

Joséphine de Beauharnais (born Marie Josèphe Rose Tascher de La Pagerie; 23 June 1763 – 29 May 1814) was the first wife of Napoleon and the first Empress of the French, crowned beside him at Notre-Dame in 1804. A Creole from Martinique who had survived imprisonment during the French Revolution and the execution of her first husband, she became the centre of the Consulate's and Empire's social world at Malmaison. Napoleon divorced her in 1810 because she had borne him no heir; she kept the title of Empress and Malmaison, where she died in 1814.

Life

Rose Tascher came to France in 1779 to marry Alexandre de Beauharnais; the marriage was unhappy but produced Eugène and Hortense. During the Terror Alexandre was guillotined and Joséphine imprisoned at Les Carmes, escaping the same fate by weeks after Robespierre's fall. In the Directory's salons she met the rising General Bonaparte, who adored her as "Joséphine" (he disliked "Rose") and married her in March 1796, days before leaving for Italy — the campaign from which his passionate letters to her survive.

Empress

As consort she softened the regime's image, dispensed patronage, interceded for émigrés, and created at Malmaison one of Europe's famous gardens — roses, black swans and kangaroos among its wonders. The marriage strained under mutual infidelities and, decisively, her failure to produce an heir after years of trying. In December 1809 Napoleon announced the divorce in a formal ceremony; she retained her rank, income and Malmaison, and the two remained on affectionate terms. She died there in May 1814, weeks after receiving the victorious Alexander I of Russia.

Descendants

Through Hortense, Joséphine was grandmother of Napoleon III; through Eugène's children she is an ancestor of the present royal houses of Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Belgium and Luxembourg — an irony, given that she was divorced for dynastic infertility.
