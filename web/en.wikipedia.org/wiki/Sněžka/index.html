Sněžka (Czech: [ˈsɲɛʃka]; Polish: Śnieżka; German: Schneekoppe) is a mountain on the border between the Czech Republic and Poland, the most prominent point of the Silesian Ridge in the Giant Mountains. At 1,603 metres (5,259 ft), its summit is the highest point in the Czech Republic and in the entire Sudetes mountain system.

Summit structures

The summit, a broad truncated pyramid of metamorphic rock, is known for severe weather and is often in cloud. Structures on top include the Polish meteorological observatory (built 1974, shaped like three stacked discs), the 17th-century Chapel of St Lawrence, and the Czech post office — the highest point in the Czech Republic where one can obtain a postal stamp — rebuilt in 2007. A chairlift from the Czech town of Pec pod Sněžkou has carried visitors near the summit since 1949, replaced by a modern gondola lift in 2014.

History and tourism

The mountain has been a tourist destination since the 19th century and is climbed by large numbers of visitors each year; the first recorded ascent dates to 1456. The summit lies within Krkonoše National Park on the Czech side and Karkonosze National Park on the Polish side.
