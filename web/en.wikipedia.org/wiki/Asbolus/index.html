8405 Asbolus is a centaur orbiting in the outer Solar System between the orbits of Jupiter and Neptune. It was discovered on 5 April 1995 by James Scotti and Robert Jedicke of Spacewatch at Kitt Peak Observatory in Arizona, United States. It is named after Asbolus, a centaur in Greek mythology.

Orbit and classification

Asbolus orbits the Sun at a distance of roughly 5.3 to 29 astronomical units once every 76 years. Its orbit is highly eccentric and inclined, typical of the dynamically unstable centaur population, whose members are thought to originate in the Kuiper belt.

Physical characteristics

Observations with the Hubble Space Telescope revealed an exceptionally fresh impact crater on Asbolus, less than 10 million years old, exposing bright water ice beneath the body's dark, reddened surface. Its rotation period is about 8.9 hours.

Naming

In Greek mythology Asbolus was a centaur seer who read omens in the flight of birds and foresaw the centaurs' fatal battle with the Lapiths.
