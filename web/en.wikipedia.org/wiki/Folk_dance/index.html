Folk dance

A folk dance is a dance that reflects the life of the people of a certain country or region. Not all ethnic dances are folk dances. For example, ritual dances or dances of ritual origin are not considered to be folk dances. Ritual dances are usually called "religious dances" because of their purpose. The terms "ethnic" and "traditional" are used when it is required to emphasize the cultural roots of the dance. In this sense, nearly all folk dances are ethnic ones.

Description

Folk dances share some or all of the following attributes: they are performed at social gatherings by people with little or no professional training, often to traditional music; they are generally designed for participation rather than audience viewing; new dancers often learn informally by observing others or receiving help from more experienced dancers; and they change over time through improvisation and community practice rather than being fixed by a choreographer. Many folk dances are performed in circles, lines, or sets of couples, and dancers frequently wear distinctive regional costumes, especially at festivals and staged performances.

Examples by region

Europe has a rich folk dance heritage: the polka and waltz of Central Europe, the Morris dance and ceilidh dances of Britain and Ireland, the Greek syrtos and kalamatianos, the Serbian kolo, the Bulgarian horo, and the flamenco traditions of Andalusia (often classed separately as a professional art). In Asia, examples include the bhangra of Punjab, the Japanese bon odori, and Chinese yangge. In the Americas, folk traditions include square dance and contra dance in the United States, the Mexican jarabe, and numerous Indigenous dances; African and Afro-diasporic traditions underpin forms such as capoeira in Brazil.

Revival and performance

From the late 19th century, collectors and revivalists such as Cecil Sharp in England documented folk dances that were disappearing with rural social change, sparking folk revivals. Today, folk dance is practised both socially and by performance ensembles — including state folk dance companies such as the Moiseyev Dance Company of Russia — and international folk dance groups teach recreational repertoires drawn from many countries.
