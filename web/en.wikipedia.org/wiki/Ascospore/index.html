Ascospore

An ascospore is a spore contained in an ascus, the sac-like cell in which sexual reproduction occurs in fungi of the phylum Ascomycota (the "sac fungi"). Ascospores are the defining reproductive structure of the group, which includes yeasts, morels, truffles and many moulds.

Formation

Each ascus typically produces eight ascospores: following fusion of two haploid nuclei (karyogamy), the diploid nucleus undergoes meiosis to yield four haploid nuclei, and in most species a further mitotic division produces eight. The spores are then delimited by membranes within the ascus and often released forcibly when the ascus tip ruptures — in some fungi over considerable distances.

Characteristics

Ascospores vary greatly in size, shape, colour and wall ornamentation, features widely used in fungal taxonomy. Many are resistant resting spores able to survive heat and desiccation; the heat-resistant ascospores of genera such as Neurospora and Talaromyces are significant in food spoilage and genetics research.
