Allan Joseph Champneys Cunningham (1842–1928) was a British mathematician and army officer whose lifelong study of the factorisation of numbers of the forms an ± 1 made him a leading figure in the number theory of his era. The Cunningham numbers and the Cunningham project are named in his honour.

Life

Cunningham was born in Delhi, the son of Sir Alexander Cunningham, the archaeologist who founded the Archaeological Survey of India. He followed a military career, joining the Royal Engineers and serving in India, where he rose to the rank of lieutenant-colonel. Alongside his duties he taught mathematics at the Thomason College at Roorkee and began the numerical researches he pursued for more than fifty years.

Mathematical work

Cunningham devoted himself to finding factors of numbers of the form 2n ± 1 and 10n ± 1, and to primality testing, publishing extensive tables in his Binomial Factorizations (1923) and many papers in the Messenger of Mathematics and elsewhere. He corresponded with other table-makers and computers, and his results were extended by later workers. Numbers of the form bn ± 1 are called Cunningham numbers, and chains of primes related to them are Cunningham chains. The modern Cunningham Project, started in 1925, continues the task of factoring such numbers with computers.
