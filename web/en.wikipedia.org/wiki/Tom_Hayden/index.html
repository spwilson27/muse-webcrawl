Tom Hayden

Tom Hayden
Born: 11 December 1939, Royal Oak, Michigan
Died: 23 October 2016, Santa Monica, California
Offices: California State Senator, Assemblyman

Thomas Emmet Hayden (December 11, 1939 – October 23, 2016) was an American social and political activist, author, and politician. A founding member of Students for a Democratic Society (SDS), he was the principal author of its Port Huron Statement (1962) and a leading figure in the 1960s movements for civil rights and against the Vietnam War.

Activism

Hayden was a Freedom Rider in the South, helped organize community projects in Newark, and travelled to North Vietnam, bringing home prisoners of war and controversy. He was one of the "Chicago Seven" tried for conspiracy after protests at the 1968 Democratic National Convention; his conviction was overturned on appeal. He was married for a time to actress and activist Jane Fonda, with whom he campaigned against the war.

Political career

After an unsuccessful Senate primary bid in 1976, Hayden was elected to the California State Assembly in 1982 and the State Senate in 1992, serving 18 years in Sacramento, where he championed environmental protection, campaign finance reform, and education. He wrote or edited some nineteen books, including Rebellion in Newark and The Long Sixties.
