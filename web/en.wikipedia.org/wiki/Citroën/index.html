Citroën

Citroën (French pronunciation: [sitʁɔɛn]) is a French automobile brand and manufacturer founded in 1919 by industrialist André Citroën. It is part of Stellantis since 2021. In 1934, the firm established its reputation for innovative technology with the Traction Avant, the world's first unitized-body, front-wheel-drive car. In 1954 the company introduced the first hydropneumatic self-levelling suspension, and in 1955 launched the revolutionary DS, the first production car with disc brakes.

History

André Citroën converted his munitions factory to car production after the First World War, applying mass-production methods he had observed in the United States. The firm grew rapidly in the 1920s and pioneered modern advertising, including displaying its name in lights on the Eiffel Tower. Overstretched by development costs, it was taken over by Michelin in 1934.

Iconic models include the 2CV (1948), the DS (1955), the SM, and the CX. Citroën won numerous World Rally Championships, particularly with Sébastien Loeb in the 2000s. In 1976 the company became part of the PSA Group alongside Peugeot; PSA merged with Fiat Chrysler Automobiles in 2021 to form Stellantis, within which Citroën remains a core volume brand, with the DS marque spun off as a premium line.
