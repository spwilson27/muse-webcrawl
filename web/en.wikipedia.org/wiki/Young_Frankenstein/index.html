Young Frankenstein

Directed by: Mel Brooks
Written by: Gene Wilder, Mel Brooks
Released: 1974

Young Frankenstein is a 1974 American comedy horror film directed by Mel Brooks and written by Brooks and Gene Wilder, who also stars. It is a parody of the classic horror films of the 1930s, particularly Universal Pictures' adaptations of Mary Shelley's Frankenstein, and was filmed in black and white using period-style techniques and original laboratory equipment props from the 1931 film.

Plot

Dr. Frederick Frankenstein (pronounced "Fronkensteen"), a respected American neurosurgeon embarrassed by his grandfather Victor's reputation, inherits the family castle in Transylvania. There, assisted by the hunchbacked Igor ("Eye-gore"), the lab assistant Inga and the forbidding housekeeper Frau Blücher, he discovers his grandfather's private library and repeats the reanimation experiment, creating a creature played by Peter Boyle. Comic complications follow, including the creature's encounter with a blind hermit and a musical performance of "Puttin' on the Ritz".

Cast and reception

The cast includes Gene Wilder, Peter Boyle, Marty Feldman, Cloris Leachman, Teri Garr, Kenneth Mars and Madeline Kahn. The film was a major commercial and critical success and is regularly ranked among the greatest comedy films ever made. Brooks later adapted it into a Broadway musical, which premiered in 2007. It is listed in the American Film Institute's 100 Years... 100 Laughs and was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry.
