Douglas Richard Flutie (born October 23, 1962) is an American former football quarterback who played for 21 seasons in the NFL, CFL, and USFL. Undersized for an NFL quarterback at 5 ft 10 in, Flutie became famous at Boston College, where he won the Heisman Trophy in 1984 and threw the legendary "Hail Flutie" touchdown pass to beat Miami. He became a superstar in the Canadian Football League — three Grey Cup championships and six Most Outstanding Player awards, and a vote as the greatest CFL player of all time — before a late-career NFL resurgence with the Buffalo Bills and San Diego Chargers.

Flutie's scrambling style and late-game heroics made him a fan favourite in both the CFL and NFL.

College career and the Hail Mary

On November 23, 1984, in a nationally televised game against the defending champion Miami Hurricanes, Flutie threw a 48-yard Hail Mary pass to Gerard Phelan on the final play to win 47–45 — one of the most famous plays in college football history. Weeks later he became the first quarterback to win the Heisman Trophy since 1971.

Professional career

Drafted by the Los Angeles Rams but signing with the USFL's New Jersey Generals, Flutie had limited early NFL chances with Chicago and New England, and a players' strike marred his 1987 season. Moving to Canada in 1990, he dominated the CFL with the BC Lions, Calgary Stampeders, and Toronto Argonauts, winning Grey Cups in 1992, 1996, and 1997. Returning to the NFL in 1998, he sparked the Buffalo Bills to the playoffs — "Flutie Flakes" cereal became a phenomenon — and made the Pro Bowl. He later played for San Diego and finished his career back in New England in 2005, where in his final game he converted the NFL's first successful drop kick since 1941. He was inducted into the College Football Hall of Fame in 2007.
