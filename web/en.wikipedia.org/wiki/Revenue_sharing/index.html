Revenue sharing

Revenue sharing is the distribution of revenue, the total amount of income generated by the sale of goods and services among the stakeholders or contributors. It should not be confused with profit sharing, in which only the profit is distributed, or with shared earning methods such as commission systems.

Applications

Revenue sharing is used in a variety of contexts. In professional sports leagues, gate receipts, broadcast rights, and merchandising income may be pooled and redistributed among teams to promote competitive balance. In online advertising and content platforms, creators and publishers receive a share of advertising revenue generated by their content — for example, video platforms share advertising income with uploaders. In business partnerships and franchising, revenue shares are a common form of compensation.

Government revenue sharing

In federal systems, revenue sharing refers to transfers of tax revenue from a central government to state or local governments. The United States operated a general revenue sharing program from 1972 to 1986 under the State and Local Fiscal Assistance Act. Similar mechanisms exist in other countries, where centrally collected taxes such as income tax or VAT are shared with subnational jurisdictions by formula.
