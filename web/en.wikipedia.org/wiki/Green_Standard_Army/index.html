The Green Standard Army (Chinese: 綠營兵; pinyin: Lǜyíngbīng) was the main military and police force of Han Chinese troops under China's Qing dynasty (1644–1912). Named after the green banners it carried, it was recruited from the Han population, organised in small garrisons scattered across the empire, and commanded largely by Han officers under Manchu supervision. Together with the hereditary Eight Banners of the Manchus, it formed the standing military establishment of the Qing state.

Organisation

The Green Standard was built from Ming troops who had surrendered to the Qing during the conquest of China. Its units were deliberately dispersed in small detachments across provinces, prefectures and counties, performing garrison duty, guarding granaries and courier routes, patrolling against banditry and smuggling, and providing the local muscle of Qing administration. Strength peaked at roughly 600,000 men in the 18th century, far outnumbering the Banner forces, but the fragmentation that made it politically safe also made it militarily weak when concentrated operations were needed.

Decline

By the 19th century the force was notoriously corrupt, underpaid, undertrained and addicted to opium; posts were often held nominally or sold. During the Taiping Rebellion (1850–1864) Green Standard troops proved largely ineffective, and the Qing came to depend on regional Han armies such as Zeng Guofan's Xiang Army and Li Hongzhang's Huai Army. Green Standard units lingered as little more than police until the dynasty fell in 1912.
