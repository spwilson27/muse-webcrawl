Stereolithography

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stereolithography (SLA or SL; also known as vat photopolymerization) is a form of 3D printing technology used for creating models, prototypes, patterns, and production parts layer by layer using photochemical processes. A focused beam of ultraviolet light — traditionally a laser, in newer machines a projector (DLP) or masked LCD — selectively cures a liquid photopolymer resin, solidifying each cross-section of the object on a build platform immersed in a vat of resin.

History

The technology was developed in the early 1980s. Japanese researcher Hideo Kodama published an early account of a functional rapid-prototyping system using photopolymers in 1981. The term "stereolithography" was coined by Charles W. "Chuck" Hull, who patented the method in 1986 and co-founded 3D Systems, which released the SLA-1, the first commercial rapid-prototyping machine, in 1987–88. Hull also developed the STL file format, still a standard for 3D printing. Stereolithography was the first commercially successful additive manufacturing technology and is regarded as the origin of the modern 3D printing industry.

Process

A 3D model is sliced into thin layers. The build platform sits just below the resin surface (or, in bottom-up machines, just above a transparent vat floor); the light source traces or projects each layer, curing the resin. The platform then moves by one layer thickness and the process repeats. Supports are typically required for overhangs. After printing, parts are washed in a solvent such as isopropyl alcohol to remove uncured resin and are often post-cured under UV light to complete polymerization and achieve full mechanical properties.

Properties and applications

SLA produces parts with very smooth surfaces and fine detail — layer thicknesses of 25–100 micrometres are common — making it suited to prototypes, dental and medical models, hearing-aid shells, jewelry casting patterns, and detailed miniatures. Limitations include the relative brittleness and UV sensitivity of standard resins, though engineering, tough, flexible, castable, and biocompatible formulations are available. Related vat-polymerization variants include DLP (digital light processing) and LCD/mSLA printing, which expose whole layers at once.
