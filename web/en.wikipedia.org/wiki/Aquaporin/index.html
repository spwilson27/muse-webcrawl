Aquaporin

An aquaporin channel lets water molecules pass through the membrane in single file while excluding ions.

Aquaporins, also called water channels, are channel proteins from a larger family of major intrinsic proteins that form pores in the membrane of biological cells, mainly facilitating transport of water between cells. They are found in bacteria, plants and animals; thirteen types (AQP0–AQP12) are known in mammals.

Discovery

The first aquaporin was discovered by Peter Agre and colleagues at Johns Hopkins University. While studying red blood cell membrane proteins, they identified a 28 kDa protein (initially called CHIP28) and showed in 1992 that expressing it in frog oocytes made the cells swell rapidly in hypotonic solution — proof that it was the long-sought water channel. It was renamed aquaporin-1 (AQP1). Agre shared the 2003 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the discovery.

Structure and mechanism

Aquaporins assemble as tetramers in the membrane, each monomer forming its own pore. Each monomer has six transmembrane alpha-helices and two short helices bearing the conserved NPA (asparagine-proline-alanine) motifs, which meet at the centre of the channel. The pore narrows to about 2.8 ångströms at the aromatic/arginine selectivity filter, forcing water molecules to pass in single file; electrostatic repulsion at the NPA region prevents the passage of protons, so the channel conducts water without leaking the proton gradient. A single channel can conduct on the order of a billion water molecules per second.

Function and disease

AQP1 is abundant in red blood cells, kidney tubules and capillaries. In the kidney, AQP2 in collecting-duct cells is regulated by the hormone vasopressin: when water must be conserved, AQP2 channels are inserted into the membrane and urine is concentrated; mutations in AQP2 or its receptor cause nephrogenic diabetes insipidus, in which patients excrete large volumes of dilute urine. AQP0 is a lens protein whose mutation causes cataracts; AQP4 in the brain is the target of autoantibodies in neuromyelitis optica. Plant aquaporins (TIPs and PIPs) govern water uptake by roots and the opening of stomata.
