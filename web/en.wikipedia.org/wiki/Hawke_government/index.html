Hawke government

The Hawke government refers to the federal executive government of Australia led by Prime Minister Bob Hawke of the Australian Labor Party, from 11 March 1983 to 20 December 1991. Hawke won four consecutive federal elections (1983, 1984, 1987, and 1990), becoming Labor's longest-serving prime minister before being succeeded by his treasurer, Paul Keating, after a leadership challenge.

Bob Hawke, prime minister of Australia 1983–1991.

Reforms

The government pursued sweeping economic modernisation. Within its first year it floated the Australian dollar (December 1983) and began deregulating the financial system. Working through the Prices and Incomes Accord with the trade union movement, it traded wage restraint for social-wage improvements, including the reintroduction of universal health insurance as Medicare in 1984. Other major measures included tariff reductions, partial deregulation of industries, corporatisation and privatisation of public assets such as Qantas and the Commonwealth Bank, tax reform, and the establishment of the superannuation guarantee system that Keating completed.

Other achievements and end

The government also enacted the Sex Discrimination Act 1984, floated the idea of a national bill of rights debate, advanced reconciliation discussions with Aboriginal Australians, and stopped the Franklin Dam in Tasmania via the World Heritage Properties Conservation Act, upheld by the High Court. Facing recession and caucus discontent, Hawke lost the Labor leadership to Keating in December 1991.
