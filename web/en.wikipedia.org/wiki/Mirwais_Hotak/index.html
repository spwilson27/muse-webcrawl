Mirwais Khan Hotak (Pashto: ميرويس خان هوتک; 1673 – November 1715) was an ethnic Pashtun and leader of the Ghilji tribe from Kandahar who founded the Hotak dynasty. In 1709 he led a successful rebellion against Safavid Persian rule, killing the Georgian governor George XI (Gurgin Khan) and declaring independence, making him a national hero in Afghanistan, where he is remembered as Mirwais Neeka, "Mirwais the Grandfather".

Revolt of 1709

Mirwais, a wealthy and influential chief of the Ghilji Pashtuns and mayor of Kandahar, had been deported to the Safavid court at Isfahan, where he studied Persian weakness and won permission to perform the Hajj. In Mecca he obtained a fatwa sanctioning revolt against the Safavid effort to convert the Sunni Pashtuns to Shia Islam by force. Returning to Kandahar in 1709, he ambushed and killed Gurgin Khan and his escort at a banquet, then defeated successive Persian and Georgian armies sent against him.

Rule and legacy

Mirwais ruled an independent Kandahar until his natural death in 1715, refusing a crown and styling himself leader by the will of the tribal assembly. He was succeeded by his brother Abdul Aziz, who was soon overthrown by Mirwais's son Mahmud Hotak, who went on to conquer Persia itself in 1722. Mirwais's mausoleum stands in the Kokaran district of Kandahar.
