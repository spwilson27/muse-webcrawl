Leo Jogiches

Leo Jogiches (Lithuanian: Leonas Jogichas; June 17, 1867 – March 10, 1919) was a Marxist revolutionary of Lithuanian-Polish-Jewish origin, a co-founder of the Social Democracy of the Kingdom of Poland and Lithuania (SDKPiL), and a leading figure of the German Spartacus League. He was the long-time political collaborator and intimate partner of Rosa Luxemburg.

Life and activity

Born into a wealthy family in Vilnius, Jogiches became a socialist organiser while still a young man, funding and directing clandestine publishing operations under the codename "Tyszka," which he used for most of his political life. In Zurich and then Berlin he worked closely with Rosa Luxemburg, with whom he founded the SDKPiL in 1893 and edited its newspapers. A formidable organiser and theorist of the party apparatus, Jogiches often worked behind the scenes, shaping strategy for both the Polish and German revolutionary movements.

German Revolution and death

After opposing the First World War, Jogiches helped build the Spartacus League alongside Luxemburg and Karl Liebknecht and was imprisoned for anti-war activity in 1918. Following the murders of Luxemburg and Liebknecht in January 1919, he took over leadership of the newly founded Communist Party of Germany (KPD) and edited its paper Die Rote Fahne, investigating his comrades' killings. He was arrested in March 1919 and murdered in Moabit prison in Berlin, shot by a police officer during an alleged escape attempt.
