YMCA

The YMCA (Young Men's Christian Association), often called simply the Y, is a worldwide youth organization founded in London in 1844. Headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland, it is one of the oldest and largest movements for youth in the world, operating in about 120 countries and reaching tens of millions of people.

History

George Williams, a draper's assistant, founded the first YMCA in London to provide young men drawn to the city by the Industrial Revolution with a wholesome alternative to street life, centred on Bible study and prayer. The movement spread rapidly; the first North American YMCA opened in Montreal in 1851, and the World Alliance of YMCAs was formed in Paris in 1855, adopting the red triangle symbol representing body, mind, and spirit.

Activities

Local YMCAs run fitness centres, swimming pools, youth sports, summer camps, childcare, and community services. Basketball and volleyball were both invented by YMCA physical education instructors — James Naismith in Springfield, Massachusetts in 1891, and William G. Morgan in 1895 respectively. The YMCA also played a major role in relief work during both world wars and in the development of camping and adult education.

Today

Although founded as a Christian organization, the modern YMCA is generally non-denominational and open to all. In the United States it rebranded as "the Y" in 2010 while retaining YMCA as its legal name.
