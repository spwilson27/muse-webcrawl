Middle East Command was a British Army command established shortly before the Second World War to coordinate military forces in Egypt, Sudan, Palestine, Transjordan, and Cyprus, and, as the war developed, across North and East Africa, Greece, and the Levant. Its headquarters were in Cairo.

Second World War

Formed in June 1939 under General Archibald Wavell, the command grew from about 40,000 men into a vast organization responsible for some of the war's most important campaigns: Operation Compass against the Italians in Libya (1940–41), the East African campaign, the defence of Egypt against Rommel's Afrika Korps at the First Battle of El Alamein under Claude Auchinleck, and the victory at the Second Battle of El Alamein under Bernard Montgomery's Eighth Army (October–November 1942). It also directed operations in Iraq, Syria–Lebanon, Persia, Greece, Crete, and Malta's support, and later coordinated with Allied forces through the North African and Italian campaigns.

Post-war

After 1945 the command (from 1946, Middle East Land Forces) managed Britain's military presence east of Suez amid decolonization: the Palestine emergency, the Canal Zone dispute with Egypt, the Suez Crisis of 1956, and operations in Cyprus, Jordan, and Aden. Headquarters moved from Egypt to Cyprus in the 1950s, and the command was reorganized in the 1960s as British forces withdrew from the region, ending with the Aden evacuation of 1967.
