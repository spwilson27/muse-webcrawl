Edward Carson

Edward Henry Carson, Baron Carson (9 February 1854 – 22 October 1935), sometimes known as Sir Edward Carson, was an Irish unionist politician, barrister and judge. Born in Dublin, he became the leader of the Irish Unionist Alliance and Ulster Unionist Party between 1910 and 1921, and the central figure in the resistance to Irish Home Rule in Ulster.

Career

Carson was called to the Irish bar in 1877 and became a formidable advocate in London; his cross-examination of Oscar Wilde in the playwright's disastrous libel action against the Marquess of Queensberry in 1895 ruined Wilde. In politics, Carson organised Ulster's opposition to the Third Home Rule Bill: he was the first to sign the Ulster Covenant in 1912, raised the Ulster Volunteer Force, and was implicated in the Larne gun-running of 1914. During the First World War he served in Asquith's coalition as Attorney General in 1915 and later in Lloyd George's war cabinet as First Lord of the Admiralty and minister without portfolio. He later sat as a Lord of Appeal in Ordinary.

Legacy

Carson is commemorated by a statue outside the Parliament Buildings at Stormont in Belfast. Although the partition of Ireland was not his original aim — he sought to defeat Home Rule for Ireland as a whole — he is remembered as a founding figure of Northern Ireland.
