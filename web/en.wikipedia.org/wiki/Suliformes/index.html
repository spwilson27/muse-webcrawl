Suliformes

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Aves
Order: Suliformes
Families: Fregatidae, Sulidae, Phalacrocoracidae, Anhingidae

The Suliformes are an order of birds recognised by the International Ornithologist's Union. Formerly included in the Pelecaniformes, the group was separated on the basis of DNA evidence showing that the traditional pelican order was not a natural (monophyletic) group. The Suliformes comprise the frigatebirds, gannets and boobies, cormorants and shags, and darters (anhingas) — all predominantly fish-eating, aquatic birds found worldwide.

Characteristics

Members of the order share a number of traits: all four toes are joined by webbing (totipalmate feet); the nostrils are typically closed or reduced to slits, an adaptation to diving; and a gular pouch is present in some form. Most pursue fish underwater or by plunge-diving. The extinct flightless "mancalline auks" are unrelated; however, extinct plotopterids of the North Pacific are considered close relatives of the group and filled a penguin-like niche in the Oligocene and Miocene.

Families

  • Fregatidae — frigatebirds (5 species): aerial pirates with enormous wingspans and, in males, inflatable red throat pouches.
  • Sulidae — gannets and boobies (10 species): plunge-divers with torpedo-shaped bodies.
  • Phalacrocoracidae — cormorants and shags (about 40 species): foot-propelled pursuit divers that often perch with wings spread to dry.
  • Anhingidae — darters or anhingas (4 species): spear-billed birds that swim with only the snake-like neck above water.

Evolution

Fossils attributed to early suliforms date to the Eocene. The group's relationships with the pelicans (now placed in a narrower Pelecaniformes with herons and ibises) reflect a broader reclassification of waterbird phylogeny driven by molecular studies in the 2000s.
