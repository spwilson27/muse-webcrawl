Michael Douglas

Born: Michael Kirk Douglas, 25 September 1944, New Brunswick, New Jersey, U.S.

Occupations: Actor, film producer

Years active: 1966–present

Spouses: Diandra Luker (m. 1977; div. 2000); Catherine Zeta-Jones (m. 2000)

Michael Kirk Douglas (born September 25, 1944) is an American actor and film producer. The eldest son of actor Kirk Douglas, he has received numerous accolades, including two Academy Awards, a Primetime Emmy Award, and five Golden Globe Awards.

Douglas first rose to prominence for his role in the television series The Streets of San Francisco (1972–1976). As a producer, he won the Academy Award for Best Picture for One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest (1975), having acquired the rights from his father. His film stardom grew with The China Syndrome (1979), Romancing the Stone (1984), and Jewel of the Nile (1985).

Career highlights

Douglas won the Academy Award for Best Actor for playing Gordon Gekko in Oliver Stone's Wall Street (1987), whose line "Greed is good" became iconic; he reprised the role in Wall Street: Money Never Sleeps (2010). His other notable films include Fatal Attraction (1987), Basic Instinct (1992), Falling Down (1993), The American President (1995), The Game (1997), Traffic (2000), and Wonder Boys (2000).

He won a Primetime Emmy Award for portraying Liberace in the HBO film Behind the Candelabra (2013) and starred in the Netflix series The Kominsky Method (2018–2021). To a new generation he is known as Dr. Hank Pym in Marvel's Ant-Man films (2015–2023) and as Benjamin Franklin in the Apple TV+ miniseries Franklin (2024).

Personal life and advocacy

Douglas married Catherine Zeta-Jones in 2000; they share the same birthday, 25 years apart. He survived stage IV tongue cancer diagnosed in 2010. Long active in public affairs, he was named a United Nations Messenger of Peace in 1998 and has advocated for nuclear disarmament and gun control. His son Cameron Douglas's struggles with addiction were documented in the family's public advocacy for drug-policy reform.
