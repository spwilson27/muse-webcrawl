Rudra veena

The rudra veena (also spelled Rudraveena or Rudra vina) is a large plucked string instrument used in Hindustani classical music, particularly in the dhrupad style. It is one of the oldest instruments of the veena family.

Description

The instrument consists of a long tubular body of wood or bamboo with two large gourd resonators (tumba), one at each end. It has four main playing strings, three drone and rhythm (chikari) strings, and frets fixed in wax on the neck. The player holds the instrument over the left shoulder, with one gourd resting on the shoulder and the other on the thigh.

History

The rudra veena is associated in Hindu tradition with the god Shiva in his aspect as Rudra. It was the principal instrument of dhrupad court musicians for centuries; notable exponents include the Dagar family of musicians. By the twentieth century the instrument had become rare, and revival efforts have focused on a small number of makers and performers.
