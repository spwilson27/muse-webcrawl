Luckenwalde

Luckenwalde (German: [ˈlʊkn̩ˌvaldə]; Lower Sorbian: Łukowc) is the capital of the Teltow-Fläming district in the German state of Brandenburg. It is situated on the Nuthe river, about 50 km (31 mi) south of Berlin. The town has a population of roughly 20,000.

History

A Slavic settlement and castle at the site preceded the German town, first mentioned in records in 1216; town rights followed in 1430. Luckenwalde lay in the Margraviate of Brandenburg and later Prussia. Water power from the Nuthe drove textile and cloth-making from the 17th century, and in the 19th century the town industrialised with factories producing hats, metal goods, and later felt and building materials.

An outstanding monument of industrial modernism is the former Friedrich Steinberg, Herrmann & Co. hat factory (1921–1923), designed by Erich Mendelsohn, whose striking dye-works hall, shaped like a hat crown, is a landmark of expressionist/functionalist architecture. During the Second World War the Stalag III-A prisoner-of-war camp was located near the town. After 1945 Luckenwalde became part of East Germany; following reunification many factories closed, and the town has since focused on biotechnology, logistics, and services.

Landmarks

The Market Tower (Marktturm), the churches of St. Johannis and St. Petri, and the Mendelsohn hat factory are the principal historic sights. The nearby Flaeming-Skate inline-skating network is a regional tourist attraction.
