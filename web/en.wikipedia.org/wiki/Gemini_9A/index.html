Gemini 9A (officially Gemini IX-A) was a 1966 crewed spaceflight in NASA's Gemini program. It was the seventh crewed Gemini flight, the 13th crewed American flight, and the 25th spaceflight of all time (including X-15 flights over 100 kilometers). The crew was Thomas Stafford, who flew in space for the third time, and Eugene Cernan, on his first flight.

Background

The mission was originally designated Gemini 9, with Stafford and Cernan as the backup crew. When the prime crew, Elliot See and Charles Bassett, were killed in a jet crash on February 28, 1966, the backups became the prime crew and the flight was redesignated Gemini 9A. The mission's goals were to rendezvous and dock with an Agena Target Vehicle and for Cernan to perform an extravehicular activity (EVA) including testing the Air Force's Astronaut Maneuvering Unit (AMU).

Flight

After the Agena target failed to reach orbit on May 17, 1966, a substitute Augmented Target Docking Adapter (ATDA) was launched on June 1. Its launch shroud failed to separate fully, opening only partially — Stafford described it as resembling an "angry alligator" — so docking was impossible, though the crew practiced three rendezvous maneuvers and flew formation with the ATDA. Gemini 9A launched on June 3, 1966, after delays.

Cernan's spacewalk on June 5 lasted 2 hours and 7 minutes. Moving to the AMU at the rear of the spacecraft proved far more difficult than expected: without adequate handholds, Cernan's exertion overwhelmed his suit's cooling system, his visor fogged, and his heart rate soared; Stafford ordered him back inside before he could don or test the AMU. The difficult EVA taught NASA crucial lessons about restraints, workload, and cooling that shaped later Gemini and Apollo spacewalks. The spacecraft splashed down on June 6, 1966, remarkably close to the recovery ship USS Wasp.
