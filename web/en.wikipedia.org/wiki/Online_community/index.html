An online community, also called an internet community or virtual community, is a community whose members interact with each other primarily via the Internet. Members generally pursue shared interests, and the sense of community can develop around discussion forums, social networking services, chat rooms, multiplayer games, and collaborative projects such as wikis.

History

Early online communities formed on bulletin board systems, Usenet newsgroups, and mailing lists in the 1970s and 1980s. The WELL, founded in 1985, became one of the most influential early communities and the subject of Howard Rheingold's 1993 book The Virtual Community, which popularised the term. The spread of the Web in the 1990s brought communities to a mass audience through services such as GeoCities, phpBB forums, and LiveJournal; the 2000s added social networking platforms and community sites such as Reddit, founded in 2005, which hosts thousands of topic-based sub-communities called subreddits.

Structure and participation

Participation in online communities is highly unequal: a widely cited pattern, sometimes called the 1% rule, holds that only a small minority of members create content, a larger group comments and modifies, and the majority observe without contributing ("lurking"). Communities are typically governed by a mix of written rules, human moderators, and automated tools, and many develop strong local norms, jargon, and in-jokes.

Effects and research

Research examines online communities' roles in social support, political mobilisation, fandom, education, and professional collaboration, as well as risks including harassment, misinformation, and echo-chamber effects. Open-source software projects and Wikipedia itself are frequently studied as large-scale examples of self-organising online collaboration.
