Governor-General of India

The Governor-General of India (1833–1950, and as Viceroy of India 1858–1947) was the representative of the monarch of the United Kingdom and, after Indian independence in 1947, the head of state of the Dominion of India. The office was created in 1773 as Governor-General of Bengal, with Warren Hastings as the first holder, to oversee the East India Company's territories.

The Charter Act of 1833 made William Bentinck the first Governor-General of all India. After the Indian Rebellion of 1857, the Crown assumed direct rule (the British Raj), and the Governor-General — beginning with Lord Canning — also bore the title Viceroy. Governors-General such as Dalhousie, Curzon, and Mountbatten shaped policy on annexation, administration, and ultimately partition.

The last Viceroy, Lord Mountbatten, oversaw the transfer of power in August 1947 and became the first Governor-General of independent India. He was succeeded by C. Rajagopalachari, the only Indian to hold the office, which was abolished when India became a republic on 26 January 1950 and replaced by the President of India.
