Renaixença

The Renaixença (Catalan pronunciation roughly "renaissance") was a cultural and literary movement in the Catalan-speaking territories of Spain in the 19th century, aiming to revive Catalan as a language of literature and high culture after centuries of decline in public and official use. It is generally dated from the 1830s to the 1880s and is seen as the starting point of modern Catalan nationalism.

Origins

The movement is often said to begin with Bonaventura Carles Aribau's ode La Pàtria (1833). Romantic interest in medieval Catalan literature and history, combined with the industrial prosperity of Catalonia, encouraged writers and scholars to recover the language's past. Joaquim Rubió i Ors, known as "Lo Gaiter del Llobregat", published Catalan poetry and campaigned for the revival of the medieval Jocs Florals (Floral Games), a poetry contest restored in Barcelona in 1859.

Development

The Renaixença produced poets such as Jacint Verdaguer — whose epic poems L'Atlàntida (1877) and Canigó (1886) became landmarks — and playwrights and novelists such as Àngel Guimerà and Narcís Oller, who established the modern Catalan novel. Newspapers and journals in Catalan, grammars and dictionaries, and regionalist political thought all grew out of the movement, which extended to Valencia and the Balearic Islands as well as Catalonia.

Legacy

The Renaixença laid the cultural foundations for Catalanism and for the later literary flowering of Modernisme and Noucentisme, and it remains central to Catalan cultural identity.
