The royal arms of Scotland: the red lion rampant within a double tressure. [Image placeholder]

The coat of arms of Scotland, historically the royal arms of the Kingdom of Scotland, consists of a gold (Or) shield bearing a red lion rampant within a red double tressure decorated with fleurs-de-lis. The lion rampant was adopted in the 12th century, traditionally by William the Lion, and the design also forms the Royal Banner of Scotland, popularly called the "Lion Rampant".

Blazon and features

Above the shield sits the Crown of Scotland and the crest of a lion sejant affronté wearing the crown and holding a sword and sceptre. The supporters are two crowned unicorns — the unicorn being Scotland's national animal — one holding the banner of St Andrew and the other the royal banner. Below are the mottoes of the Order of the Thistle, Nemo me impune lacessit ("No one provokes me with impunity"), and the royal motto In My Defens God Me Defend.

Use

Since the Union of the Crowns in 1603 and the Acts of Union 1707, the Scottish arms have been combined with those of England (and later Ireland) in the royal coat of arms of the United Kingdom; in Scotland, a distinct version is used in which the Scottish quarter takes precedence. The arms appear on Scottish government buildings, court documents and one-pound coins.
