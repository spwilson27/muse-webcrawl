The National Resident Matching Program (NRMP), also called The Match, is a United States–based non-profit organization that places applicants into residency and fellowship training programs at teaching hospitals. Each year it matches tens of thousands of applicants — principally final-year medical students and international medical graduates — with graduate medical education positions.

How the Match works

Applicants apply to programs through the Electronic Residency Application Service (ERAS), interview during the autumn and winter, and then submit a rank order list of preferred programs. Programs likewise rank the applicants they interviewed. The NRMP runs a variant of the deferred-acceptance algorithm (related to the Gale–Shapley algorithm, in the applicant-proposing form) that produces a stable matching: it is in an applicant's best interest to rank programs in true order of preference. Results are released during Match Week in mid-March; applicants learn whether they matched on Monday and where on "Match Day" Friday. Applicants who do not match may enter the Supplemental Offer and Acceptance Program (SOAP) to fill unfilled positions.

History

The Match was created in 1952 in response to a chaotic market in which hospitals competed to sign students earlier and earlier, sometimes two years before graduation. The algorithm has been refined over the decades, notably a 1990s redesign — involving economist Alvin Roth, whose work on matching markets later contributed to a Nobel Prize — that made the process applicant-proposing and added provisions for couples matching together.

Scale

Recent Matches have been the largest in history, with more than 40,000 applicants competing for roughly 40,000 categorical and preliminary positions across all specialties, making the NRMP one of the largest centralized matching markets in the world.
