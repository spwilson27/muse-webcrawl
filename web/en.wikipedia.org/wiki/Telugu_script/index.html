Telugu script

The Telugu script (Telugu: తెలుగు లిపి, Telugu lipi) is an abugida used to write the Telugu language of the Indian states of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, as well as several minority languages. It is a member of the Brahmic family of scripts and is closely related to the Kannada script, from which it diverged in the medieval period.

History

Telugu writing descends from the Bhattiprolu script, a variant of Brahmi used in the region by the 3rd century BCE. The Telugu and Kannada scripts remained nearly identical for centuries as the Telugu-Kannada script before standardising separately by about 1500 CE. Its rounded letterforms developed from writing on palm leaves, where straight lines would split the leaf.

Structure

The script has independent letters for vowels, and consonant letters carry an inherent vowel /a/ that is modified by vowel signs (matras) or suppressed by a virama. Consonant clusters form ligatures, and the script includes letters for the full Sanskrit sound inventory, reflecting Telugu's heavy Sanskritisation. The traditional inventory is sometimes counted as 56 letters (aksharamulu).

Numerals and computing

Telugu has its own set of digits, though Arabic numerals are common in modern use. The script is encoded in the Unicode block Telugu (U+0C00–U+0C7F).
