The Tiger is the third of the twelve-year cycle of animals which appear in the Chinese zodiac related to the Chinese calendar. The Year of the Tiger is associated with the Earthly Branch symbol 寅.

Years and elements

People born within these date ranges can be said to have been born in the "Year of the Tiger", bearing the following elemental sign: for example, 1950–51 (Metal), 1962–63 (Water), 1974–75 (Wood), 1986–87 (Fire), 1998–99 (Earth), 2010–11 (Metal), and 2022–23 (Water). The exact boundaries follow the Lunar New Year (or Lichun, depending on tradition).

Personality traits

In Chinese astrology, Tigers are said to be brave, competitive, unpredictable and confident, natural leaders who are also prone to impulsiveness and rebellion. The Tiger is traditionally considered compatible with the Horse and Dog, and in conflict with the Monkey.

Cultural notes

Because tigers were historically feared, in some regions parents were once reluctant to have children — particularly daughters — in Tiger years; conversely the sign is widely regarded as auspicious and powerful. The Tiger ranks behind the Rat and Ox in the zodiac race legend.
