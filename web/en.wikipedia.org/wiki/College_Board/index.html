College Board

The College Board is an American not-for-profit organization that was formed in December 1899 as the College Entrance Examination Board (CEEB) to expand access to higher education. It develops and administers standardized tests and curricula used by K–12 and post-secondary institutions, most notably the SAT and the Advanced Placement (AP) program.

History

The Board administered its first college entrance examinations in 1901. The SAT was first administered in 1926, developed under psychologist Carl Brigham. The Advanced Placement program began in the 1950s, allowing high-school students to earn college credit.

Programs

Beyond the SAT and AP, the College Board runs the PSAT/NMSQT, the CSS Profile financial-aid application, and BigFuture, a college-planning platform. Its tests and fees have drawn criticism over cost and equity, prompting many colleges to adopt test-optional admissions.
