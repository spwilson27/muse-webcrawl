The forty-seven rōnin, also known as the Akō incident or Akō vendetta, were a group of masterless samurai (rōnin) who in 1702 avenged the death of their lord, Asano Naganori of Akō. Their story, retold in countless plays, prints, and films as Chūshingura, is Japan's most famous tale of samurai loyalty and revenge.

Background

In 1701, Asano was provoked by the shogunal official Kira Yoshinaka inside Edo Castle and wounded him with a dagger. Drawing a sword within the castle was a capital offence: Asano was ordered to commit seppuku the same day, his domain was confiscated, and his retainers became rōnin. Kira went unpunished.

The vendetta

Forty-seven of the retainers, led by Ōishi Yoshio, spent nearly two years dissembling — Ōishi famously feigning drunken dissipation — before attacking Kira's Edo mansion on a snowy night in December 1702. They killed Kira, carried his head to Asano's grave at Sengaku-ji temple, and surrendered. The shogunate, torn between law and admiration, ordered them to commit seppuku, which 46 of them did in February 1703 (one messenger was later pardoned). They were buried beside their lord at Sengaku-ji, where their graves remain a place of pilgrimage.
