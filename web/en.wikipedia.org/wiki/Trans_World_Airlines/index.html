Trans World Airlines

Trans World Airlines (TWA) was a major American airline that existed from 1930 until 2001. It was formed as Transcontinental & Western Air to operate a route from New York City to Los Angeles via St. Louis, Kansas City, and other stops. Under Howard Hughes's leadership from 1939 to 1961, the airline expanded to become a major international carrier, competing with Pan American World Airways. TWA was acquired by American Airlines in 2001 and its last flights operated on December 1, 2001.

History

The airline began in 1930 through the merger of Transcontinental Air Transport and Western Air Express, encouraged by the Post Office to create a transcontinental airmail route. Jack Frye and his team developed the Douglas DC-1 and DC-2 for the airline after the Ford Trimotor proved inadequate. In 1939 Howard Hughes acquired control and drove TWA's postwar international expansion; the name Trans World Airlines was adopted in 1950. The airline's New York terminal, the TWA Flight Center designed by Eero Saarinen, opened in 1962 and became an icon of jet-age architecture.

Decline and final years

Deregulation, costly leveraged deals, and the 1985 hijacking of Flight 847 and the 1996 crash of Flight 800 weakened the carrier. After a third bankruptcy filing in 2001, TWA was purchased by American Airlines; operations were folded into American by the end of that year. St. Louis, once TWA's largest hub, was progressively downsized. See also Howard Hughes and TWA Flight Center.
