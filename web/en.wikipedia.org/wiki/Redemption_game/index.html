Redemption game

Redemption games are arcade games of skill that reward the player proportionally to their score with tickets or tokens, which can be redeemed ("cashed in") at a central counter for prizes such as toys, novelty items, and increasingly, electronics. They are a staple of family entertainment centres and arcades such as Chuck E. Cheese and Dave & Buster's.

A redemption game dispensing tickets based on the player's score. [Image placeholder]

Gameplay

Typical redemption games include skee-ball, whac-a-mole-style games, coin pushers, basketball shooting games, wheel-spinning games such as Cyclone, and timed reaction games. Prizes are tiered so that low scores yield small items such as candy, while high-value prizes require thousands of tickets, encouraging repeat play. In many modern venues paper tickets have been replaced by points stored on a swipe card.

Regulation

Because redemption games reward prizes based on skill-based play with fixed prize values, they are generally distinguished in law from gambling devices; however, machines in which chance dominates, or which pay out cash-equivalent prizes, have attracted regulation in various jurisdictions. Some regions have restricted ticket values or banned certain mechanics resembling slot machines.
