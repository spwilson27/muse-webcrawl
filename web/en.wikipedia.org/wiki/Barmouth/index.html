Barmouth

Barmouth (Abermaw)
Location: Gwynedd, Wales, at the mouth of the River Mawddach
Population: about 2,500
Known for: seaside resort; Barmouth Bridge

Barmouth (Welsh: Abermaw) is a seaside town and community in Gwynedd, north-west Wales. It lies on the estuary of the River Mawddach and Cardigan Bay, within Snowdonia (Eryri) National Park, and developed from a medieval shipbuilding and fishing port into a popular Victorian resort.

History

Barmouth grew in the eighteenth century as a port for the Welsh wool and slate trades, and shipbuilding flourished on the estuary. The arrival of the railway in the 1860s transformed it into a resort: the Barmouth Bridge, a long wooden viaduct of 1867 carrying the Cambrian Coast line across the Mawddach, remains one of Wales's most distinctive structures and includes a swing section that once opened for shipping. Henry Tudor (later Henry VII) is said to have been sheltered at a house in the town before Bosworth, and the Tudor merchant house Tŷ Gwyn, on the quay, is traditionally linked to the plot to place him on the throne. The town was also associated with the last invasion of Britain in 1797, when a French flag captured at Fishguard was presented to a Barmouth woman whose husband had witnessed the landing.

Geography and tourism

The town climbs steeply from the harbour up "The Rock," with old cottages on terraced lanes, and faces a long sandy beach popular for swimming and sailing. The Panorama Walk above the town, gifted in 1895, offers celebrated views across the estuary to Cadair Idris. Barmouth is a base for walking the Mawddach Trail to Dolgellau along the former railway line, and for climbing in the Rhinog mountains.
