Elaine May

Elaine May (born Elaine Iva Berlin, 21 April 1932) is an American comedian, filmmaker, playwright and actress. She first rose to fame in the late 1950s as one half of the improvisational comedy duo Nichols and May with Mike Nichols, whose sharp, satirical sketches made them stars of stage, radio, television and bestselling records. She went on to become a pioneering film director and a sought-after screenwriter, receiving an Academy Honorary Award in 2022 for her lifetime of work.

Nichols and May

May met Mike Nichols at the University of Chicago, where both performed with the Compass Players, a forerunner of The Second City. Their act — wry, literate improvisations on modern manners, psychiatry, dating and mothers — took them to Broadway in An Evening with Mike Nichols and Elaine May (1960) and the Grammy-winning album Nichols and May Examine Doctors. The duo split in 1961 at the height of their success.

Film career

May wrote, directed and starred in A New Leaf (1971) opposite Walter Matthau, directed the acclaimed The Heartbreak Kid (1972) and Mikey and Nicky (1976), and directed the notorious box-office failure Ishtar (1987), later reclaimed by many critics as a cult comedy. As a screenwriter she earned Academy Award nominations for Heaven Can Wait (1978) and Primary Colors (1998), and she gave a memorable performance in Woody Allen's Small Time Crooks (2000). In 2019, at the age of 87, she won the Tony Award for Best Actress for her performance in Kenneth Lonergan's play The Waverly Gallery on Broadway.
