Royal Society of Literature

Somerset House, London

The Royal Society of Literature (RSL) is a learned society and charity founded in 1820 to "reward literary merit and excite literary talent". It is the United Kingdom's senior organisation representing writers, based at Somerset House in London.

History

The society was founded under the patronage of George IV, with the bishop and writer Thomas Burgess as its first president. Among its early fellows were Samuel Taylor Coleridge and Walter Scott. The society received its royal charter in 1825 and has since elected fellows distinguished in literature; fellows sign the society's roll using a famous pen — at various times Byron's, Dickens's or George Eliot's.

Activities

The RSL runs a programme of lectures, debates and masterclasses, and administers literary prizes and awards including the Ondaatje Prize, the Encore Award for second novels, the Benson Medal and the Christopher Bland Prize. It also undertakes campaigns for literacy and for the value of literature in public life.

Fellowship

Fellowship (FRSL) is awarded to writers of distinction across all forms; roughly 600 fellows are living at any time. In recent years the society broadened election routes, including public nomination, and launched its Open Boundaries and "40 under 40" initiatives to diversify the fellowship.
