Small nucleolar RNA

Small nucleolar RNAs (snoRNAs) are a class of small RNA molecules that primarily guide chemical modifications of other RNAs, mainly ribosomal RNAs, transfer RNAs and small nuclear RNAs. They are a component of the small nucleolar ribonucleoproteins (snoRNPs) located in the nucleolus of the eukaryotic cell.

Classes

There are two principal classes defined by conserved sequence motifs. Box C/D snoRNAs contain C (UGAUGA) and D (CUGA) motifs and guide 2′-O-methylation of target RNAs, associating with the methyltransferase fibrillarin. Box H/ACA snoRNAs have a hairpin-hinge-hairpin-tail structure with H (ANANNA) and ACA motifs and guide pseudouridylation, associating with the enzyme dyskerin. A third group, the scaRNAs, are found in Cajal bodies and guide modification of small nuclear RNAs.

Function

Each snoRNA contains an antisense element that base-pairs with its target RNA, positioning the modifying enzyme at exactly the right nucleotide. Most snoRNA genes are encoded in the introns of protein-coding genes in vertebrates. Loss of particular snoRNAs can be consequential: the SNORD116 cluster deleted in Prader–Willi syndrome accounts for much of that disorder's phenotype, and some snoRNAs are implicated in cancer. A number of "orphan" snoRNAs have no known RNA target and may have regulatory roles.
