U.S. National Parks

The United States has 63 national parks operated by the National Park Service (NPS), an agency of the Department of the Interior. National parks must be established by an act of Congress; they are chosen for natural beauty, unique geological features, unusual ecosystems, and recreational opportunities. The first, Yellowstone, was signed into law by President Ulysses S. Grant in 1872. Together the parks cover about 52.4 million acres (212,000 km²).

Facts

Alaska is home to the largest parks; Wrangell–St. Elias alone exceeds 8 million acres. Great Smoky Mountains National Park regularly leads in visitation, drawing over 12 million visitors a year. The National Park Service, created in 1916, administers the parks together with monuments, battlefields, recreation areas, and other units—more than 430 in all. Fauna across the system includes species such as the jaguar, which occasionally ranges into border parks of the Southwest.
