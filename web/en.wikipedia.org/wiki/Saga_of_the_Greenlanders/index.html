Saga of the Greenlanders

The Saga of the Greenlanders (Grœnlendinga saga) is an Icelandic saga, one of the Vinland sagas, describing the Norse exploration of North America around the year 1000. Together with the Saga of Erik the Red, it is the principal written source for the Norse voyages to Vinland, though the two sagas differ in details and credit different leaders for the discoveries.

Content

The saga recounts how Bjarni Herjólfsson, blown off course on a voyage to Greenland, sighted unknown lands to the west but did not land. Leif Erikson later retraced the route, naming Helluland ("stone land", probably Baffin Island), Markland ("forest land", probably Labrador) and Vinland ("wine land"), where he built houses at a place identified with L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland — excavated in the 1960s and the only confirmed Norse site in North America. The saga goes on to describe the voyages of Leif's brothers Thorvald and Thorstein, his sister Freydís, and the merchant Thorfinn Karlsefni, whose expedition attempted settlement and encountered the indigenous peoples the Norse called Skrælingjar.

Manuscript and historicity

The saga survives in the late 14th-century manuscript Flateyjarbók. Composed probably in the 13th century, centuries after the events, it blends history with storytelling, but archaeology has confirmed its core claim: the Norse reached America nearly 500 years before Columbus.
