Kwan Tak-hing

Kwan Tak-hing (27 June 1905 – 28 June 1996), known as "Master Kwan", was a Hong Kong martial arts actor best remembered as the definitive screen incarnation of the folk hero Wong Fei-hung, whom he portrayed in some 77 films — a world record for an actor playing the same character — in a series running from 1949 to 1981.

Kwan Tak-hing Born: 27 June 1905, Canton (Guangzhou)
Died: 28 June 1996, Hong Kong
Occupation: Actor, martial artist
Known for: Wong Fei-hung film series

Early life and career

Born in Canton, Kwan trained in Cantonese opera and in hung ga kung fu under the lineage of Wong Fei-hung's own disciples. He began acting in films in the 1930s in San Francisco and Singapore before returning to Hong Kong, debuting as Wong Fei-hung in The Story of Wong Fei-hung, Part One (1949).

The Wong Fei-hung series

The black-and-white series defined the image of Wong Fei-hung as a dignified Confucian master and established conventions of Hong Kong martial arts cinema, including Kwan's famous "shadowless kick". Later actors such as Jackie Chan, Jet Li and Sammo Hung reinterpreted the role; Kwan himself reprised it in later productions and television well into old age.

Later life

Kwan ran a herbal medicine shop in Hong Kong and practised martial arts into his nineties. He died in 1996, the day after his 91st birthday, and was posthumously honoured with a star on Hong Kong's Avenue of Stars.
