Primrose Hill

The view across central London from the summit of Primrose Hill.

Primrose Hill is a Grade II listed public park and the 65-metre (213 ft) grassy hill that gives it its name, located on the northern side of Regent's Park in the London Borough of Camden. It is one of six protected viewpoints in London, and its summit offers a celebrated panorama of the city skyline. The name also denotes the surrounding affluent residential district.

History

The land was once part of a great forest and was enclosed by Henry VIII as a royal hunting ground. It took its name from the primroses that once flowered there. In the 17th century the hill was a notorious duelling spot and the site of the mysterious 1678 murder of Sir Edmund Berry Godfrey, a magistrate involved in the Popish Plot. The Crown sold the land in 1841 and it was landscaped as a public park under an 1842 Act of Parliament; nearby Regent's Park terraces and villas made the neighbourhood fashionable.

The district

Primrose Hill village, centred on Regent's Park Road and Chalcot Square with its pastel-painted Victorian terraces, is known for independent shops, cafés, and celebrity residents. In the 1990s the media dubbed a circle of actors and models living locally the "Primrose Hill set".

In culture

The hill appears in H. G. Wells's The War of the Worlds and in the opening of Blur's-era Britpop iconography; an engraved William Blake quotation ("I have conversed with the Spiritual Sun...") set into the summit path commemorates the poet's association with the spot. On Shakespeare's Tree, an oak planted in 1864 to mark the tercentenary of Shakespeare's birth, a replacement was planted in 1964.
