Ulna

The ulna is one of the two long bones of the forearm, lying on the medial (little-finger) side opposite the radius. It is the principal bone of the elbow joint, where its proximal end forms the hinge articulation with the humerus.

Structure

At its upper end the ulna bears the olecranon — the bony point of the elbow — and the coronoid process, which together grip the trochlea of the humerus in the trochlear notch. Down the shaft runs the interosseous border, attached to the radius by the interosseous membrane. At the wrist the ulna ends in a small rounded head that does not directly contact the carpal bones, being separated from them by a fibrocartilage disc of the triangular fibrocartilage complex.

Clinical significance

Fractures of the olecranon and of the ulnar shaft are common injuries; a fracture of the proximal ulna with dislocation of the radial head is known as a Monteggia fracture. The ulnar nerve passes behind the medial epicondyle near the elbow, where a knock produces the "funny bone" sensation.
