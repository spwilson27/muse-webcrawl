Cospas-Sarsat

Cospas-Sarsat Programme
Founded1979 (memorandum of understanding); operational 1982
Founding membersCanada, France, the Soviet Union, the United States
HeadquartersMontreal, Canada
PurposeSatellite-based search and rescue distress alerting

Cospas-Sarsat is a satellite-based search and rescue (SAR) distress alert detection and information distribution system, established by Canada, France, the former USSR and the United States in 1979. Its name combines the Russian acronym COSPAS (Cosmicheskaya Sistema Poiska Avariynyh Sudov, "Space System for the Search of Vessels in Distress") with SARSAT ("Search And Rescue Satellite-Aided Tracking").

Operation

The system detects emergency beacons operating at 406 MHz — emergency position-indicating radio beacons (EPIRBs) on ships, emergency locator transmitters (ELTs) on aircraft, and personal locator beacons (PLBs) carried by individuals. Instruments aboard satellites in low Earth orbit (LEOSAR), geostationary orbit (GEOSAR) and, increasingly, medium Earth orbit navigation satellites (MEOSAR, using Galileo, GPS and GLONASS payloads) relay the alerts to ground stations called local user terminals, which compute the beacon's position (historically by Doppler shift) and pass the alert to mission control centres and rescue coordination centres.

History and impact

The first satellite was launched in 1982, and the system made its first rescue that September, locating a crashed light aircraft in British Columbia. The older 121.5 MHz analogue beacon processing was terminated in 2009 in favour of digital 406 MHz beacons, which transmit a unique identifier and can encode a GNSS position. Cospas-Sarsat states that the system has contributed to the rescue of more than 57,000 people in over 17,000 SAR events since 1982.
