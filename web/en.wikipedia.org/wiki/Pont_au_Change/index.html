Pont au Change

The Pont au Change is a bridge over the River Seine in Paris, France. It connects the Île de la Cité, at the level of the Palais de Justice and the Conciergerie, to the Right Bank at the place du Châtelet. Its name, "Exchange Bridge", recalls the money changers and goldsmiths whose shops lined its medieval predecessors.

History

Bridges have stood on this site since late antiquity; a succession of wooden bridges were swept away by floods. In 1647 a stone bridge designed by Androuet du Cerceau was completed for Louis XIV, lined with the shops of jewellers and changers. A decree of Napoleon I in 1806 cleared the houses from Parisian bridges, and the present structure was built under Napoleon III to designs of engineers Vaudrey and Lagalissery, opening in 1860 as part of Haussmann's renovation of Paris.

Design

The bridge is a three-arched masonry structure about 103 metres long and 30 metres wide, decorated with imperial "N" insignia and wreaths of the Second Empire.

In culture

The Pont au Change is a setting in Victor Hugo's novel Les Misérables, and appears in Patrick Süskind's Perfume, as the site of the perfume shop of Giuseppe Baldini, which collapses into the Seine in the story.
