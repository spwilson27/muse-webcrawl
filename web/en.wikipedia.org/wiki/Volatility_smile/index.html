Volatility smile

The volatility smile is a pattern in options markets in which the implied volatility of options on the same underlying asset, plotted against strike price for a fixed expiry, forms a curve resembling a smile: implied volatility is lowest for at-the-money options and rises for both in-the-money and out-of-the-money options.

Explanation

The Black–Scholes model assumes constant volatility and log-normal returns, which would produce a flat line. The smile reflects the market's pricing of fatter tails and, in equity markets since the 1987 crash, a pronounced skew: deep out-of-the-money puts carry much higher implied volatility than equivalent calls, reflecting demand for crash protection and the leverage effect. Foreign-exchange options show a more symmetric smile, while commodity markets may exhibit an inverse pattern.

Use

Traders use the volatility surface — smile across strikes and maturities — to price exotic options, calibrate local and stochastic volatility models such as SABR, and measure market sentiment and event risk.
