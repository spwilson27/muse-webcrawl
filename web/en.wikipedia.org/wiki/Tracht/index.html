Tracht

Tracht (German: [tʁaxt]) refers to traditional garments in German-speaking countries and regions, including Austria, Bavaria, South Tyrol and parts of Switzerland. Although the word is often used to describe any folk costume of the region, Tracht in the narrower sense denotes the distinctive dress that developed among the rural population and was later codified in the 19th century.

Garments

For men, the best-known items are lederhosen — leather breeches, often embroidered and worn with braces, a checked or plain shirt, a loden jacket (Janker), woollen knee socks and sturdy Haferl shoes, sometimes topped with an Alpine hat adorned with a tuft of chamois hair (Gamsbart). For women, the dirndl consists of a fitted bodice, blouse, full skirt and apron; details of cut, colour and the position of the apron bow traditionally signalled the wearer's village and marital status.

History and modern use

The conservation of Tracht was promoted in the 19th century by folk-costume associations (Trachtenvereine) and by the Bavarian and Austrian courts, which adopted rural dress at court to encourage national sentiment. Today Tracht is worn at festivals such as Oktoberfest, church feasts, weddings and Corpus Christi processions, and a fashion industry has grown around both authentic hand-made garments and inexpensive festival imitations. Regional styles differ considerably, and museum collections preserve district patterns that vary from valley to valley.
