Tefnakht I (Greek: Tnephachthos) was a prince of Sais and founder of the short-lived 24th Dynasty of ancient Egypt, ruling in the western Nile Delta during the fragmented Third Intermediate Period, conventionally c. 732–725 BC.

Rise

As Great Chief of the West and chief of the Libyan Ma (Meshwesh), Tefnakht expanded from Sais over Memphis and much of Lower and Middle Egypt, leading a coalition against the Kushite king Piye of the 25th Dynasty, who had moved north from Napata. His advance, joined by rulers such as Osorkon IV of Bubastis and Nimlot of Hermopolis, provoked Piye's campaign recorded on the Victory Stela from Gebel Barkal.

Defeat and kingship

Piye defeated the coalition, captured Memphis and Hermopolis, and received the submission of the Delta rulers — Tefnakht alone held out on an island in the marshes before submitting by envoy and oath. After Piye withdrew south, Tefnakht assumed full pharaonic titles as Shepsesre Tefnakht. He was succeeded by his son Bakenranef, whom the later Kushite king Shabaka defeated and, according to Manetho, burned alive, ending the dynasty. Sais later produced the 26th (Saite) Dynasty.
