The Morrígan

Other namesMórrígan, Morrígu
AbodeOweynagat, Ireland
TextsTáin Bó Cúailnge, Cath Maige Tuired
GenderFemale

The Morrígan ("phantom queen") is a figure from Irish mythology, a goddess of war and fate who is often shown hovering over the battlefield in the form of a crow or raven. She is associated with prophecy, sovereignty, and the land, and is sometimes described as a triad of goddesses together with Badb and Macha.

Mythology

In the Ulster Cycle, the Morrígan appears in the Táin Bó Cúailnge (Cattle Raid of Cooley), where she offers love and assistance to the hero Cú Chulainn; when he rejects her, she attacks him in various forms and later heals him. She also prophesies the doom of the world at the end of the Cath Maige Tuired. She is said to be able to predict — and influence — the outcome of battles.

Interpretation

Scholars have linked the Morrígan with the sovereignty goddesses of early Irish literature, figures who confer kingship, and with a broader Celtic complex of battle goddesses such as Cathubodua. Her name is commonly interpreted as "great queen" (from mór, "great") or "phantom queen" (from mor, "terror").
