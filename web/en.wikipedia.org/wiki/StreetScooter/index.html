StreetScooter GmbH is a German manufacturer of electric vehicles based in Aachen. It became known for developing and producing battery-electric delivery vans, most prominently for Deutsche Post DHL, which acquired the company in 2014 and deployed tens of thousands of its vehicles in its delivery fleet — for a time making StreetScooter one of the largest electric commercial vehicle fleets in Europe.

History

StreetScooter originated in 2010 as a research project at RWTH Aachen University, led by professors Günther Schuh and Achim Kampker. The goal was to develop an affordable electric vehicle designed from the outset for short-range commercial and delivery use, using a modular design and low-cost components. A first prototype was shown at the Frankfurt Motor Show (IAA) in 2011.

Deutsche Post DHL, seeking an electric van suited to stop-and-go postal delivery routes, cooperated with the project and in December 2014 acquired StreetScooter GmbH outright. Production of the WORK model began in Aachen, and a second plant opened in Düren in 2018. Deutsche Post built its own fleet of StreetScooter vans — over 10,000 were in service by 2018, alongside electric bicycles and trikes from the brand.

Despite the fleet's visibility, the business struggled to become profitable and to win large third-party orders. Deutsche Post explored a sale or stock listing, and in 2020 announced it would wind down production, later reversing course under new arrangements. In 2022 the rights and production assets were sold to an international consortium (Odin Automotive, later B-On), which continued the vehicle line under the B-On brand.

Vehicles

  • WORK — a compact electric panel van developed for postal delivery, with a box body, sliding doors, and a range of roughly 80–200 km depending on battery version.
  • WORK L — a larger version with greater cargo volume.
  • WORK XL — based on the Ford Transit, built jointly with Ford in Cologne from 2018, with about 20 m³ of cargo space.
  • e-Bike and e-Trike — electric cargo cycles for inner-city delivery.

Significance

StreetScooter is frequently cited as an example of a logistics company vertically integrating into vehicle manufacturing when established automakers did not offer a suitable electric van. Its low-cost, purpose-built design approach influenced later electric commercial-vehicle startups.
