Arthedain

Arthedain, the northernmost successor kingdom of Arnor

Arthedain is a fictional kingdom in J. R. R. Tolkien's Middle-earth legendarium. It was the northernmost and longest-lived of the three successor kingdoms into which Arnor divided in T.A. 861, the others being Cardolan and Rhudaur. Its history is told in Appendix A of The Lord of the Rings.

History

When Arnor was partitioned among the three sons of King Eärendur, the eldest, Amlaith, took Arthedain, the north-western portion, which included the original capital Annúminas, the Tower Hills, Lake Evendim and the ancient royal lands. In time the king moved the seat of government from decaying Annúminas to Fornost Erain on the North Downs. Arthedain retained the sceptre of Annúminas and two of Arnor's palantíri, at Amon Sûl (Weathertop) and Annúminas.

The royal line of Isildur survived unbroken in Arthedain, and its kings maintained the claim to the whole of Arnor. In T.A. 1940 Arvedui of Arthedain married Fíriel, daughter of Ondoher of Gondor, and on the death of Gondor's king later put forward a claim to the southern crown, which the Council of Gondor rejected.

Fall

Arthedain endured a centuries-long struggle against the Witch-king's realm of Angmar. Despite help from Gondor — including the great expedition of Eärnil's son Eärnur in T.A. 1975, which destroyed Angmar at the Battle of Fornost — Arthedain itself had already fallen: Fornost was captured in T.A. 1974, and the last king, Arvedui, drowned in the Ice Bay of Forochel in T.A. 1975 when the Elven ship sent to rescue him was crushed by ice. His son Aranarth became the first Chieftain of the Dúnedain, and the people of Arthedain became the Rangers of the North.
