Monotone polygon

In geometry, a polygon is called monotone with respect to a straight line L if every line perpendicular to L intersects the polygon in at most one connected segment. Equivalently, a polygon is monotone in a given direction if its boundary can be split into two chains that are each monotone along that direction.

Properties

A polygon is monotone with respect to the y-axis if, when traversed from the topmost to the bottommost vertex along either side, the y-coordinates never increase. Such a polygon has a single "start" vertex and a single "end" vertex, and its interior angles along each chain behave regularly, which makes monotone polygons especially tractable in algorithms.

Algorithms

Monotone polygons can be triangulated in linear time by a simple stack-based sweep. More generally, any simple polygon can be partitioned into monotone pieces in O(n log n) time, and this decomposition is the standard first step in classic polygon triangulation algorithms. Monotone polygons are also used in motion planning and in testing whether a polygon is star-shaped.
