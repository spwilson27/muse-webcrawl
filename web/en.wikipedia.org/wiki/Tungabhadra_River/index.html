The Tungabhadra River is a major river in southern India, a principal tributary of the Krishna River. It is formed by the confluence of two rivers, the Tunga and the Bhadra, which rise in the Western Ghats in the state of Karnataka and join at Kudli, near Shimoga. From there the Tungabhadra flows eastward for about 531 kilometres across the Deccan Plateau through Karnataka, and along the borders of Andhra Pradesh and Telangana, before joining the Krishna near Alampur.

Course and importance

The river is the lifeline of a largely semi-arid region. The Tungabhadra Dam, completed in 1953 near Hospet, impounds a large reservoir used for irrigation, hydroelectric power and drinking water in Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh. The river's basin supports extensive cultivation of paddy, sugarcane and other crops, and disputes over the sharing of its waters among Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh and Telangana have been the subject of inter-state tribunals.

History and culture

The Tungabhadra's banks are steeped in history. Near its northern bank stands Hampi, capital of the Vijayanagara Empire (14th–16th centuries), whose spectacular ruined temples and palaces among granite boulders form a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The river is regarded as sacred — identified in tradition with the goddess Pampa, consort of Shiva as Virupaksha — and pilgrims bathe at shrines along its course. In ancient texts the Tungabhadra marked, in places, the boundary between rival kingdoms of the Deccan.
