Academic ranks

Academic ranks are the titles, relative importance, and power of professors, researchers, and administrative personnel held in academia. Systems of ranks vary widely between countries, but most share a progression from junior teaching and research posts to senior professorial chairs.

United States and Canada

RankNotes
Assistant professorEntry-level tenure-track rank
Associate professorMid-level, usually tenured
ProfessorSenior rank, often called "full professor"
Distinguished / endowed professorNamed chairs and honours
Professor emeritusRetired professors retaining the title

Non-tenure-track positions include lecturers, adjunct professors, research professors, and visiting professors. Tenure is typically granted after a probationary period of about seven years, following review of research, teaching, and service.

United Kingdom and Commonwealth

The traditional ladder runs lecturer, senior lecturer, reader (or associate professor), and professor. Since the 2010s many UK universities have adopted North American titles, renaming lecturers as assistant professors and senior lecturers as associate professors.

Continental Europe

In Germany the traditional ranks include Juniorprofessor, Privatdozent, and the graded professor ranks W1–W3, with the Habilitation historically required for a full chair. France distinguishes maître de conférences from professeur des universités.
