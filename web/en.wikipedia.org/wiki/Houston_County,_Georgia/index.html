Houston County (pronounced "HOW-stən") is a county in the central portion of the U.S. state of Georgia. Its county seat is Perry and its largest city is Warner Robins. The population was about 164,000 at the 2020 census.

History

The county was created in 1821 from land ceded by the Creek Nation and named for John Houstoun, a governor of Georgia during the Revolutionary era. It is one of the state's original large counties, later reduced as new counties were carved from it.

Economy

Robins Air Force Base, one of the largest employers in Georgia, lies partly within the county and anchors the Warner Robins area's economy. Agriculture, including peaches and pecans, remains important in the rural districts around Perry.
