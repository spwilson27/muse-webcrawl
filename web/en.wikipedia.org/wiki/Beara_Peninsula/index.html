Beara Peninsula

Location: Counties Cork and Kerry, Ireland
Bounded by: Kenmare River (north) and Bantry Bay (south)
Highest point: Hungry Hill, 685 m

The Beara Peninsula (Irish: Béarra) is a peninsula on the south-west coast of Ireland, bounded between the Kenmare River to the north and Bantry Bay to the south. It is shared between County Cork and County Kerry and is the most rugged and least populated of the great peninsulas of Ireland's south-west, with the Caha and Slieve Miskish mountain ranges forming its spine.

Geography and settlements

The main towns and villages include Castletownbere — one of Ireland's principal fishing ports — Glengarriff, Kenmare, Ardgroom, Allihies, Eyeries, and Lauragh. Bere Island and Dursey Island lie off its coast; Dursey is linked to the mainland by Ireland's only cable car. The Healy Pass, a winding mountain road completed during the Famine era, crosses the Caha Mountains.

History and heritage

Beara has a dense concentration of Bronze Age monuments — stone circles, standing stones, wedge tombs, and ring forts — including the Uragh Stone Circle near Lough Inchiquin. Allihies was the centre of copper mining in the 19th century, and many miners emigrated to Butte, Montana. The peninsula is the ancestral home of the O'Sullivan Beare clan; Donal Cam O'Sullivan Beare's famous winter march of 1603, after the defeat at Kinsale, ended with barely 35 of his thousand followers reaching safety.

Tourism

The Ring of Beara driving route and the Beara Way long-distance walking trail circuit the peninsula. The area is known for seafood, the gardens of Glengarriff and Ilnacullin (Garinish Island), and a mild Gulf Stream climate.
