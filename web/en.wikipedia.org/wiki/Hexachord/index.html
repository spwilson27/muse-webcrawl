Hexachord

In music theory, a hexachord is a series of six pitches. The term is most associated with the Guidonian hexachord of medieval theory — ut, re, mi, fa, sol, la — spanning a major sixth with a single semitone between mi and fa, used to teach sight-singing through solmisation.

Guidonian system

Guido d'Arezzo's system interlocked three kinds of hexachord: the natural (C–A), hard (G–E, with B natural) and soft (F–D, with B flat). Singers mutated between hexachords to cover the full gamut. The syllables derive from the hymn Ut queant laxis.

Twentieth-century usage

In twelve-tone theory a hexachord is a six-note pitch-class set; two complementary hexachords can form an aggregate, a property exploited in combinatoriality by Babbitt and others.
