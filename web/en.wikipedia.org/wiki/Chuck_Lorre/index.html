Chuck Lorre

Chuck Lorre (born Charles Michael Levine, 18 October 1952)
Occupation: Television producer, writer, director, composer
Years active: 1984–present
Known for: The Big Bang Theory, Two and a Half Men, Mom

Chuck Lorre (born Charles Michael Levine; October 18, 1952) is an American television producer, writer, and director. Nicknamed the "King of Sitcoms", he is the creator or co-creator of many successful multi-camera sitcoms, including Grace Under Fire, Dharma & Greg, Two and a Half Men, The Big Bang Theory, Mike & Molly, Mom, Young Sheldon, and Bob Hearts Abishola.

Career

Born in Plainview, New York, Lorre began in music and animation, writing for shows such as Heathcliff and composing the theme song for Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles (1987). He moved into live-action comedy as a writer on Roseanne, before creating Grace Under Fire (1993) and Cybill.

His biggest successes came with Two and a Half Men (2003–2015) and The Big Bang Theory (2007–2019), the latter becoming one of the most-watched comedies in the world. He also received critical acclaim for the Netflix single-camera series The Kominsky Method (2018–2021), which won the Golden Globe for Best Television Series – Musical or Comedy.

Vanity cards

Lorre is famous for the vanity cards shown for a few seconds after the closing credits of his shows, in which he writes short essays, rants, and jokes on topics ranging from politics to censorship by network standards departments. The cards have been collected in book form.

Recognition

Lorre was inducted into the Television Hall of Fame in 2012 and received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame in 2009.
