Taurus (Latin for "the Bull") is one of the constellations of the zodiac and is located in the Northern celestial hemisphere. One of the oldest recognised constellations, known since at least the Early Bronze Age, it is prominent in winter skies. Its brightest star is the orange giant Aldebaran, which marks the bull's eye, and it contains two of the nearest open star clusters: the Pleiades and the Hyades.

Features

The V-shaped Hyades cluster forms the bull's face, with Aldebaran (apparently, though it is a foreground star) at one end. Behind the bull's shoulder lies the Pleiades (Messier 45), perhaps the most famous star cluster in the sky, long used in cultures worldwide to mark the seasons. Taurus also contains the Crab Nebula (M1), the remnant of the supernova of 1054, at the tip of the southern horn, and the variable red giant Betelgeuse lies just across the border in Orion. In 2012 the exoplanet host star systems of Taurus were joined by discoveries around Aldebaran itself.

Mythology

In Greek myth the bull was identified with Zeus, who took the form of a gentle white bull to carry the Phoenician princess Europa across the sea to Crete. The constellation's antiquity in Mesopotamian astronomy — as GU4.AN.NA, the "Bull of Heaven" — and in Egyptian and other traditions reflects the importance of cattle to early agrarian cultures.
