Alexandra Feodorovna (Alix of Hesse)

Alexandra Feodorovna (6 June 1872 – 17 July 1918), born Princess Alix of Hesse and by Rhine, was the last Empress of Russia as wife of Emperor Nicholas II from 1894 until his abdication in 1917.

Early life

A granddaughter of Queen Victoria, Alix was nicknamed "Sunny" as a child. Her mother died of diphtheria when Alix was six. She was raised partly in Darmstadt and at the British court, and was a carrier of haemophilia, a gene she passed to her son.

Marriage and reign

She married Nicholas shortly after his accession in 1894 and converted to Russian Orthodoxy, taking the name Alexandra Feodorovna. Shy and increasingly unpopular, she was criticised for her German birth during the First World War and for her reliance on Grigori Rasputin, whom she believed could heal her haemophiliac son Alexei.

Revolution and death

After Nicholas abdicated in March 1917, the family was held under house arrest at Tsarskoye Selo, then Tobolsk and Yekaterinburg. On the night of 16–17 July 1918 Alexandra, Nicholas, their five children and retainers were murdered by Bolsheviks in the Ipatiev House.

Canonisation

The family was canonised by the Russian Orthodox Church Outside Russia in 1981 and as passion-bearers by the Russian Orthodox Church in 2000. Their remains, recovered from near Yekaterinburg, were reburied in St Petersburg in 1998.
