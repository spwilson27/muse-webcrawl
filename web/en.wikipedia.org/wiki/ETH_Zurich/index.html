ETH Zurich (German: Eidgenössische Technische Hochschule Zürich; English: Swiss Federal Institute of Technology in Zurich) is a public research university in Zurich, Switzerland. Founded by the Swiss federal government in 1855 as the Eidgenössische Polytechnische Schule, it is one of the two Swiss federal institutes of technology (with EPFL in Lausanne) and is consistently ranked among the world's leading universities in science, engineering and mathematics, and usually as the top university in continental Europe.

History

ETH opened in 1855 with departments in architecture, civil, mechanical and chemical engineering, forestry and a general section. Its main building, completed in 1864, was designed by the architect Gottfried Semper, who was also one of its first professors. The institution adopted the name Eidgenössische Technische Hochschule in 1911 and gained the right to award doctorates in 1909. Albert Einstein studied and obtained his teaching diploma there in 1900 and later held a professorship (1912–1914). More than twenty Nobel laureates have been students or professors at ETH, including Wilhelm Röntgen, Wolfgang Pauli, Richard Ernst and Kurt Wüthrich.

Organisation and study

ETH has 16 departments covering engineering, natural sciences, architecture, mathematics, systems-oriented sciences and social sciences, including D-BIOL, D-CHAB, D-MATH, D-PHYS and D-INFK (computer science). It has roughly 24,500 students, of whom about 40% come from outside Switzerland. Bachelor's programmes are taught mainly in German; most master's programmes are in English. Admission to undergraduate study is unrestricted for holders of the Swiss Matura. ETH is a member of the IDEA League and is internationally known for research in robotics, computer science, materials, climate science and architecture.

Campuses

The historic Zentrum campus sits above Zurich's old town, around the main building and the Semperaula; since the 1960s a second campus has been developed at Hönggerberg, now called "Science City".
