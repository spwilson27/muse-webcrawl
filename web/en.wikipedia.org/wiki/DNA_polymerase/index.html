DNA polymerase

DNA polymerase
Function: DNA synthesis 5'→3'
Discovery: Arthur Kornberg (1956, Pol I)
Uses: PCR (Taq polymerase)

A DNA polymerase is an enzyme that synthesises DNA molecules from nucleotide building blocks, using a template strand. DNA polymerases are essential for DNA replication and repair in all living organisms.

Function

Polymerases add deoxyribonucleotides to the 3' end of a primer, reading the template 3'→5'. Many possess proofreading 3'→5' exonuclease activity, keeping error rates near one in a hundred million bases. They cannot start from scratch, requiring an RNA or DNA primer laid by primase.

Families

In E. coli, DNA polymerase III is the main replicative enzyme and polymerase I (Kornberg's enzyme) removes primers and fills gaps. Eukaryotes use polymerases α, ε and δ for replication and many specialised enzymes (such as translesion polymerases) for repair. Viral enzymes include reverse transcriptases, which copy RNA into DNA.

Applications

Thermostable Taq polymerase from Thermus aquaticus made the polymerase chain reaction (PCR) practical; engineered polymerases power DNA sequencing and diagnostics.
