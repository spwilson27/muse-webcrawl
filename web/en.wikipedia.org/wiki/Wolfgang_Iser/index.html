Wolfgang Iser

Wolfgang Iser (22 July 1926 – 24 January 2007) was a German literary scholar associated with the Constance School of reception aesthetics. Along with Hans Robert Jauss, he reshaped literary studies by focusing on the reader's role in producing meaning.

Work

His major books include The Implied Reader (1972) and The Act of Reading: A Theory of Aesthetic Response (1976). Iser argued that literary texts contain gaps or blanks (Leerstellen) that readers fill through an active, temporal process of anticipation and retrospection, guided by the text's repertoire and strategies.

Later career

Iser taught at the University of Constance and later at the University of California, Irvine. His later work turned to literary anthropology, notably The Fictive and the Imaginary (1991).
