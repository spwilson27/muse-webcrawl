The Bertelsmann Foundation (German: Bertelsmann Stiftung) is a German private operating foundation and think tank based in Gütersloh. It was founded in 1977 by Reinhard Mohn, the post-war head of the Bertelsmann media group, and is the majority shareholder of Bertelsmann SE & Co. KGaA. The foundation describes itself as non-partisan and is among the largest and most influential think tanks in Germany.

Work

Unlike a grant-making foundation, the Bertelsmann Stiftung primarily designs and runs its own projects, studies, and pilot programmes. Its fields of activity include education policy, democratic governance, social cohesion, health care, European integration, and sustainable economic policy. Well-known outputs include the Transformation Index (BTI), which assesses political and economic transformation in developing and transition countries, and the Social Justice Index for EU member states.

History and structure

Mohn transferred the majority of Bertelsmann's capital shares to the foundation in 1993, linking the company's ownership to the foundation's charitable purposes and ensuring the continuity of the family enterprise. The foundation is governed by an executive board and a board of trustees; Liz Mohn, Reinhard Mohn's widow, has been a prominent figure in its leadership. An affiliated think tank, the Bertelsmann Foundation North America, is based in Washington, D.C.

Criticism

Critics have questioned the foundation's influence on German public policy — particularly in education and health reform — arguing that its combination of think-tank activity and corporate ownership blurs the line between private interest and public advice, and have scrutinised its tax-privileged status. The foundation responds that its work is transparent, scientific, and non-partisan.
