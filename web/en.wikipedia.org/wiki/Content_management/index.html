Content management

Content management (CM) is a set of processes and technologies that supports the collection, managing, and publishing of information in any form or medium. When stored and accessed via computers, this information may be more specifically referred to as digital content, or simply as content.

Features

A content management system (CMS) typically provides capabilities for:

  • Creating and editing content, often through a web-based interface
  • Storing content in a repository with metadata
  • Workflow management, including drafting, review and approval
  • Version control and audit trails
  • Access control and user roles
  • Publishing to one or more channels, such as websites or mobile applications
  • Indexing, search and retrieval

Types

Major categories include web content management (WCM), focused on websites; enterprise content management (ECM), covering documents and records across an organisation; component content management, which manages content at a granular, reusable-component level, often using XML standards such as DITA; and digital asset management (DAM), focused on rich media files. Headless CMS platforms separate the content repository from presentation, delivering content through APIs.

Examples

Widely used systems include WordPress, Drupal and Joomla for web publishing, and enterprise platforms such as SharePoint and Documentum. Content management practices overlap with records management, knowledge management and information governance.
