Cape gannet

The Cape gannet (Morus capensis) is a large seabird of the gannet family, Sulidae. It breeds only on a handful of islands off the coasts of Namibia and South Africa, and is readily identified by its white body, black flight feathers, golden-yellow crown and nape, and a distinctive black line running down the centre of the throat. The species is classified as Endangered by the IUCN because of rapid population declines driven by collapsing stocks of its sardine and anchovy prey.

Description

Adults measure about 84–94 cm (33–37 in) in length with a wingspan of roughly 171–185 cm, and weigh around 2.6 kg. The plumage is white with black primaries and secondaries and a black tail; the head is washed golden yellow. The long, pointed bill is pale blue-grey with a black line extending from the gape down the throat — a feature that distinguishes it from the similar Australasian gannet, which it was once considered conspecific with.

Distribution and breeding

Cape gannets breed at only six island colonies: Lambert's Bay, Malgas Island, and Bird Island (Algoa Bay) in South Africa, and Mercury Island, Ichaboe Island, and Possession Island in Namibia. Breeding runs from about August to April. Pairs are monogamous and return to the same nest site; a single bluish egg is laid in a mound nest of guano and debris, and both parents incubate and feed the chick by regurgitation. The guano produced by the colonies was historically harvested commercially as fertiliser.

Feeding

Like other gannets, Cape gannets are spectacular plunge-divers, folding their wings and striking the water at high speed to seize schooling fish such as sardines (Sardinops sagax) and anchovies. They also scavenge discards behind fishing vessels.

Conservation

The world population fell from roughly a quarter of a million breeding pairs in the mid-twentieth century to well under half that by the 2010s. The main cause is the eastward shift and decline of sardine stocks off South Africa's west coast, linked to overfishing and ocean warming, which left the western colonies short of food. The species was uplisted from Vulnerable to Endangered in 2017.
