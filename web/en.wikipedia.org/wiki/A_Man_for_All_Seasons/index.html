A Man for All Seasons

A Man for All Seasons is a play by Robert Bolt, first staged in London in 1960 and on Broadway in 1961, about Sir Thomas More, the 16th-century Lord Chancellor of England who refused to endorse King Henry VIII's wish to divorce Catherine of Aragon so that he could marry Anne Boleyn. The title reflects More as a man of integrity in all circumstances — "a man for all seasons" being the description of More by his contemporary Robert Whittington.

Plot

The play follows More as Henry VIII, Cardinal Wolsey and Thomas Cromwell pressure him to approve the King's divorce and break with Rome. More resigns the chancellorship and withdraws to private life, using silence and legal subtlety to avoid condemning or endorsing the King. When an Act of Parliament requires an oath recognising the King's new marriage and supremacy over the Church, More refuses to swear, is imprisoned in the Tower of London, tried on perjured testimony by Richard Rich, and executed in 1535. A narrator figure, the Common Man, plays multiple small roles and comments on the action.

Film adaptation

Bolt adapted the play into the 1966 film directed by Fred Zinnemann and starring Paul Scofield as More, with Robert Shaw as Henry VIII, Orson Welles as Wolsey, Leo McKern as Cromwell and Susannah York as Margaret More. The film won six Academy Awards, including Best Picture, Best Director, Best Actor (Scofield) and Best Adapted Screenplay (Bolt). A television film version starring Charlton Heston appeared in 1988.

Themes

The play is a study of conscience, integrity and the rule of law versus expediency. More's defence of his silence — that silence in law signifies consent to nothing and is itself protected — is among its most quoted passages, along with his rebuke to Rich: "Why Richard, it profits a man nothing to give his soul for the whole world... but for Wales?"
