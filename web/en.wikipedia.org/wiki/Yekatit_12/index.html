Yekatit 12

Yekatit 12 (Amharic: የካቲት ፲፪, "12th of Yekatit", the month corresponding to February; 19 February 1937 in the Gregorian calendar) is the date of the attempted assassination of Marshal Rodolfo Graziani, the Italian viceroy of Italian East Africa, and of the massacre that followed. During a ceremony at the viceroy's palace in Addis Ababa distributing alms to the poor, two Eritrean nationalists — Abraha Deboch and Moges Asgedom — threw hand grenades at Graziani and other Italian officials, seriously wounding the viceroy. In reprisal, Italian colonial authorities ordered a systematic massacre of Ethiopian civilians over the following days.

The massacre

Over three days, Italian carabinieri, Blackshirt squadre, and fascist militias carried out house-to-house searches and summary executions in Addis Ababa; Ethiopian accounts report the burning of homes and the murder of some 1,400–1,500 prisoners held at Alem Bekagn ("End of the World") prison in the capital. The Italian authorities also used the occasion to target the Ethiopian intelligentsia — students, priests, and deacons — and to execute much of the remaining leadership of the young Ethiopian elite, including victims taken from the Debre Libanos monastery, where hundreds of monks were killed in May 1937.

Remembrance

After the war, Ethiopia commemorated the victims: Emperor Haile Selassie dedicated the Yekatit 12 Monument in Addis Ababa, and February 19 remains a national day of remembrance. The two assassins, Abraha Deboch and Moges Asgedom, fled but were later captured and executed by the Italians. In 2012 Italy returned the obelisk of Axum, looted during the occupation, a further gesture addressing the fascist colonial past.
