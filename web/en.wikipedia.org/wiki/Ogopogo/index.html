The Ogopogo is a lake monster reported to live in Okanagan Lake in British Columbia, Canada. It is one of Canada's best-known cryptids, often compared to Scotland's Loch Ness Monster. Descriptions typically speak of a serpentine creature 12 to 15 metres long, with a dark body, several humps, and a head said to resemble that of a horse or goat.

Indigenous tradition

Long before European settlement, the Syilx (Okanagan) people told of N'ha-a-itk, a powerful water spirit inhabiting the lake, associated in particular with the area near Rattlesnake Island. Offerings were traditionally made before crossing the lake, and the being figured in Syilx spirituality as a guardian and a force to be respected.

Sightings

Settler reports began in the 19th century. The name "Ogopogo" — a palindrome — was popularised in the 1920s, borrowed from a comic music-hall song performed at a Vernon Board of Trade luncheon in 1924. A frequently cited sighting occurred in 1926, when occupants of some thirty cars at Okanagan Mission beach reportedly saw the creature simultaneously. In 1968 Arthur Folden filmed an object moving across the lake near Peachland, and in 1989 a car salesman, Ken Chaplin, shot videotape of an animal-like figure, later argued to be a beaver. A life-sized statue of Ogopogo stands on the waterfront in Kelowna, and the creature is a beloved regional mascot.

Explanations

Sceptics attribute sightings to misidentified wildlife such as otters or sturgeon, to boat wakes, floating logs, and to wave phenomena on the long, narrow lake. No conclusive physical evidence has been produced, and no scientific consensus accepts the creature's existence; it is classed as a cryptid.
