Cai Lun

Bornc. 50–62 CE, Guiyang (present-day Leiyang, Hunan)
Died121 CE
OccupationEunuch court official, inventor
Known forImprovements to papermaking

Cai Lun (蔡倫; courtesy name Jingzhong) was a Chinese eunuch court official of the Eastern Han dynasty who is traditionally credited with the invention of paper and the papermaking process. In 105 CE he presented his improved paper to Emperor He of Han, an event recorded in the official dynastic history, the Book of the Later Han.

Life

Cai Lun entered the palace as a eunuch attendant in 75 CE and rose under Emperor He and the Empress Dowager Deng Sui, becoming a senior official in charge of the imperial workshops (Shangfang Ling), responsible for manufacturing instruments and weapons for the court. He was enfeoffed as a marquis with a small fief. After the death of the Empress Dowager Deng in 121 CE, Cai Lun was implicated in an earlier court intrigue — he had been involved in the death of Emperor He's grandmother, Consort Song — and, ordered to face interrogation, he took his own life by poison.

Papermaking

Before Cai Lun, Chinese writing materials included bamboo slips, wooden tablets, and silk — heavy or expensive. Cai Lun's innovation, building on earlier experiments, was to make sheets from a pulp of tree bark, hemp waste, old rags, and fishing nets: the fibres were beaten in water, suspended, caught on a porous mould, and dried into a light, cheap, uniform writing surface. Archaeological finds show that cruder fibre sheets existed in China before 105 CE, so Cai Lun is now generally regarded as having refined and standardised papermaking and promoted its adoption, rather than creating it from nothing.

Legacy

Papermaking spread from China along the Silk Road — to Korea, Vietnam, and Japan, then westward to the Islamic world (after the 8th century) and eventually to medieval Europe — transforming record-keeping, literature, and administration everywhere it arrived. Cai Lun was later venerated as the patron deity of papermakers in China, and he is frequently ranked among the most influential inventors in history.
