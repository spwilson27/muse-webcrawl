Corepressor

A corepressor is a molecule that represses the expression of genes. In prokaryotes, corepressors are small molecules. In eukaryotes, corepressors are proteins. A corepressor does not bind DNA directly, but instead indirectly regulates gene expression by binding to a repressor.

A corepressor downregulates (or represses) the expression of genes by binding to and activating a repressor transcription factor. The repressor in turn binds to a gene's operator sequence (a segment of DNA adjacent to the genes regulated by the repressor), thereby blocking transcription of that gene.

In prokaryotes

In prokaryotes, the term corepressor is used to denote the activating ligand of a repressor protein. For example, the Escherichia coli tryptophan repressor (TrpR) is only able to bind to DNA and repress transcription of the trp operon when its corepressor, tryptophan, is bound to it. TrpR in the absence of tryptophan is known as an aporepressor and is inactive in repressing gene transcription. The trp operon encodes enzymes responsible for the synthesis of tryptophan. Hence TrpR provides a negative feedback mechanism that regulates the biosynthesis of tryptophan.

In short tryptophan functions as a feedback inhibitor: the more tryptophan is available, the more it binds to TrpR, which then represses production of the enzymes that make more tryptophan.

In eukaryotes

In eukaryotes, a corepressor is a protein that binds to transcription factors known as repressors. Eukaryotic corepressors include the nuclear receptor corepressors NCoR1 and NCoR2 (SMRT), which are recruited by unliganded nuclear receptors and other transcription factors and in turn recruit histone deacetylases (HDACs), producing a chromatin environment that represses transcription. Other well-known corepressors include Groucho/TLE proteins in animals, Tup1 in yeast, and the plant protein TOPLESS.

Corepressors also play roles in epigenetic silencing: for example, the corepressor CoREST forms a complex with the lysine-specific demethylase LSD1 and HDACs to repress neuronal genes in non-neuronal tissues.
