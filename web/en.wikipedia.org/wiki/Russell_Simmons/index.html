Russell Simmons

Russell Wendell Simmons (born October 4, 1957) is an American entrepreneur, writer, and record executive. He co-founded the hip-hop label Def Jam Recordings with Rick Rubin in 1984 and is often described as one of the architects of hip-hop's commercial breakthrough.

Career

Born in Queens, New York, Simmons began promoting concerts and managing artists in the late 1970s, including Kurtis Blow and his younger brother Joseph Simmons's group Run-DMC. Def Jam went on to sign acts such as LL Cool J, the Beastie Boys, Public Enemy, and Kanye West's early roster, becoming one of the most influential labels in popular music. Simmons later founded the clothing line Phat Farm, the Rush Communications media company, and television's Def Comedy Jam.

Philanthropy and controversies

Simmons has been involved in charitable work through the Rush Philanthropic Arts Foundation and has promoted yoga and veganism. In 2017, multiple women publicly accused him of sexual misconduct and assault, allegations he has denied; he subsequently stepped down from his companies.
