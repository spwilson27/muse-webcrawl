Upsampling

In digital signal processing, upsampling, expansion, and interpolation are terms associated with the process of resampling in a multi-rate digital signal processing system. Upsampling can be synonymous with expansion, or it can describe an entire process of expansion and filtering (interpolation). When upsampling is performed on a sequence of samples of a signal, it produces an approximation of the sequence that would have been obtained by sampling the signal at a higher rate (or density).

Process

Upsampling by an integer factor L expands the signal by inserting L−1 zero-valued samples between each pair of original samples. The resulting sequence runs at L times the original sampling rate but has a spectrum containing unwanted spectral images of the original signal. An interpolation (low-pass) filter then removes these images, effectively "filling in" the zero samples with smooth interpolated values derived from the original samples.

Applications

Upsampling is used in sample-rate conversion between systems operating at different rates, in digital-to-analog converters (where oversampling eases the requirements on the analog reconstruction filter), in image scaling, and in modern audio processing. It is the conceptual inverse of downsampling (decimation), which reduces the sampling rate.
