Communications of the ACM is the monthly journal of the Association for Computing Machinery (ACM). First published in 1958, it is sent to all ACM members and is one of the best-known publications in computer science, bridging research and practice for a broad computing audience.

The magazine publishes peer-reviewed research survey articles, contributed articles, editorials, technical opinion pieces and news of the computing field. Many landmark papers in computing first appeared in its pages, including Edsger W. Dijkstra's 1968 letter "Go To Statement Considered Harmful", C. A. R. Hoare's Turing Award lecture, and Ken Thompson's 1984 lecture "Reflections on Trusting Trust". It also publishes the well-known "CACM" regional editions and practice-oriented content associated with ACM Queue.

History

The journal began in 1958 as the printed communications outlet of the ACM, succeeding earlier bulletins. Over the decades it evolved from a research journal into a magazine format covering the whole profession, while the ACM's research literature migrated to specialised journals and conference proceedings. In 2023 the ACM announced that Communications would move to an online-only, fully open-access model beginning in 2024.
