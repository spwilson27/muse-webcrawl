Cream were a British rock supergroup formed in London in 1966, consisting of bassist and lead vocalist Jack Bruce, guitarist Eric Clapton, and drummer Ginger Baker. Regarded as the first successful supergroup, Cream combined blues, psychedelia, and hard rock in a power-trio format built on extended improvisation, and sold more than 35 million records worldwide during a career of barely two years.

[Image: Cream on stage, 1967]

Career

Clapton, Bruce, and Baker were already leading figures of the British blues scene — Clapton from the Yardbirds and John Mayall's Bluesbreakers, Bruce and Baker from the Graham Bond Organisation — and the band's name reflected their status as the "cream" of the crop. Their debut album Fresh Cream (1966) was followed by Disraeli Gears (1967), featuring "Sunshine of Your Love" and "Strange Brew," and the double album Wheels of Fire (1968), which topped the American charts and included "White Room" and a celebrated live version of Robert Johnson's "Crossroads."

Breakup and legacy

Clashing personalities — especially between Bruce and Baker — relentless touring, and the deafening volume of their live shows led the band to split in late 1968, after the farewell album Goodbye (1969) and a final concert at the Royal Albert Hall. Cream were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1993 and reunited for concerts at the Royal Albert Hall and Madison Square Garden in 2005. Their improvisational power-trio model and fusion of blues with heavy, amplified rock were foundational for hard rock and heavy metal.
