Mobile River

The Mobile River is a river in the U.S. state of Alabama. It is formed by the confluence of the Alabama River and the Tombigbee River at the southern end of the Mobile–Tensaw River Delta, and flows generally southward for about 45 miles (72 km) before emptying into Mobile Bay on the Gulf of Mexico.

Course

The confluence of the Alabama and Tombigbee rivers lies north of the city of Mobile. From there the Mobile River flows south through the delta's maze of channels, bayous and wetlands. Near its mouth the river passes the Port of Mobile, one of the busiest ports in the United States, and the city of Mobile on its western bank.

Delta

The Mobile–Tensaw River Delta, through which the lower Mobile River flows, is one of the largest intact wetland ecosystems in the United States, covering roughly 260,000 acres. It supports an exceptional diversity of fish, birds and other wildlife, and is sometimes called "America's Amazon" for its biological richness.

History

The river takes its name from the Maubila (Mobile) Native American people encountered by early European explorers. The French founded the settlement of Mobile near the river's mouth in the early 18th century, and the river became an important artery for cotton and other trade from the interior of Alabama and Mississippi.

During the American Civil War, the river and bay were strategically important; the Battle of Mobile Bay was fought at the bay's entrance in 1864.

Crossings

Major crossings near the mouth include the Cochrane–Africatown USA Bridge, carrying U.S. Route 90 and U.S. Route 98, and the tunnels and bridges serving Interstate 10 and Interstate 165 in the Mobile area.
