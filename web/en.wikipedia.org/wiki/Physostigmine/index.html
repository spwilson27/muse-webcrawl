Physostigmine

Physostigmine
ClassReversible acetylcholinesterase inhibitor
SourceCalabar bean (Physostigma venenosum)
UsesAnticholinergic toxicity, glaucoma

Physostigmine (also known as eserine, from éséré, the West African name for the Calabar bean) is a highly toxic parasympathomimetic alkaloid, specifically a reversible cholinesterase inhibitor. It occurs naturally in the Calabar bean and the fruit of the manchineel tree.

Mechanism

Physostigmine inhibits acetylcholinesterase, the enzyme responsible for breaking down acetylcholine, thereby increasing acetylcholine concentrations at cholinergic synapses. Unlike neostigmine and pyridostigmine, it crosses the blood–brain barrier and thus acts on the central nervous system as well.

Medical uses

Physostigmine is used to treat the central and peripheral effects of anticholinergic poisoning, including delirium caused by agents such as atropine and scopolamine, and historically was applied in eye drops for glaucoma. It was first isolated in 1864 by Jobst and Hesse, and its study helped establish the concept of neurotransmission.
