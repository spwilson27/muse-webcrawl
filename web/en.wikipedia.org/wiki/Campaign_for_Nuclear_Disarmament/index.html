Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament

The Campaign for Nuclear Disarmament (CND) is a British organisation advocating unilateral nuclear disarmament by the United Kingdom, international nuclear disarmament, and tighter regulation of nuclear weapons worldwide. Founded in 1958, it became one of the largest peace movements in British history.

History

CND was launched in February 1958 with figures such as Bertrand Russell, Canon John Collins, and J. B. Priestley among its founders. Its first Aldermaston March at Easter 1958 drew thousands. The CND symbol — designed by Gerald Holtom from the semaphore letters N and D within a circle — became a universal peace sign.

Later campaigns

Membership surged again in the early 1980s over cruise missiles at Greenham Common and the proposed Trident system. CND continues to campaign against the replacement of Trident and for the cancellation of Britain's nuclear weapons programme.
