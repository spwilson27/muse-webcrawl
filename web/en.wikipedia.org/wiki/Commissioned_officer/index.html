Commissioned officer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A commissioned officer is a member of an armed force who holds a position of authority granted by a formal document called a commission, issued by the head of state or government. Commissioned officers are distinguished from non-commissioned officers (NCOs) and enlisted personnel by the legal authority they exercise, including command responsibility, and by their training in leadership, management, and the specialist functions of their service branch.

The commission

The commission is a written instrument, traditionally signed by the sovereign or president, that appoints the holder to an office in the armed forces and charges subordinates with obeying the officer's lawful commands. In the United Kingdom officers hold the King's Commission; in the United States, officers of the federal armed forces are appointed by the President with the advice and consent of the Senate. The commission carries with it accountability under military law, and in many countries officers swear an oath of allegiance on appointment.

Entry and training

Candidates for a commission typically complete a course at a military academy — such as Sandhurst, West Point, or Saint-Cyr — or a university-based programme such as the Reserve Officers' Training Corps (ROTC) or an officer training school. Specialist entries include doctors, lawyers, chaplains, and engineers who receive commissions in recognition of professional qualifications. Promotion thereafter depends on seniority, performance reports, and selection boards.

Ranks

Commissioned ranks in land forces commonly run from second lieutenant through the field-grade ranks (major, lieutenant colonel, colonel) to the general officer grades (brigadier, major general, lieutenant general, general, and field marshal in some armies). Naval equivalents run from ensign or sub-lieutenant to admiral. Within NATO, commissioned ranks are standardised in the OF-1 to OF-10 scale for comparison between nations.

Roles

Commissioned officers exercise command at every level, from a platoon of some thirty soldiers to entire formations. They are responsible for planning operations, administering discipline, and the welfare of those under their command. Staff officers support commanders in headquarters, while specialist officers serve in logistics, intelligence, signals, and medical branches.
