Inter caetera ("Among other [works]") was a papal bull issued by Pope Alexander VI on 4 May 1493 that granted to the Catholic Monarchs Ferdinand II of Aragon and Isabella I of Castile all lands to the "west and south" of a pole-to-pole line 100 leagues west of the Azores and Cape Verde islands, provided those lands were not already in the possession of another Christian monarch.

Background

The bull followed the return of Christopher Columbus from his first voyage in March 1493 and formed part of the "Bulls of Donation" by which the Spanish-born pope supported Spain's claims to the newly encountered lands. It superseded and refined the earlier bull Inter caetera of 3 May 1493 and the bull Eximiae devotionis.

Terms

The bull assigned to Spain all discovered and to-be-discovered lands west of the demarcation line, together with the duty of evangelising their inhabitants, while reserving lands east of the line — in practice, Africa and India — to Portugal. Any lands found by Spain east of the line in the southern ocean, the bull stated, were also to remain Spanish, a clause whose imprecision fuelled later disputes over the Moluccas.

Aftermath

Portugal, dissatisfied with the line, negotiated directly with Spain, and the two kingdoms concluded the Treaty of Tordesillas in 1494, moving the line to 370 leagues west of the Cape Verde islands. The bull and treaty became foundational — and contested — documents in the European legal justification of colonisation of the Americas, and their legacy is still invoked in debates over the "Doctrine of Discovery".
