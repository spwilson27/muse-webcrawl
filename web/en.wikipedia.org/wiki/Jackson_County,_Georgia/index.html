Jackson County is a county located in the northeastern part of the U.S. state of Georgia. As of the 2020 census, the population was 75,907. The county seat is Jefferson. Jackson County comprises the Jefferson, GA Micropolitan Statistical Area, which is also included in the Atlanta–Athens–Clarke County–Sandy Springs combined statistical area.

History

The county was created on February 11, 1796, from part of Franklin County, and named for James Jackson, a Revolutionary War officer who served as governor of Georgia and United States senator. The first county seat was at Clarksboro; it later moved to Jefferson, named for Thomas Jefferson. In 1911 the western part of the county was combined with parts of Gwinnett and Walton counties to form Barrow County.

Geography

According to the U.S. Census Bureau, the county has a total area of 343 square miles (890 km²), of which 340 square miles (880 km²) is land and 3.4 square miles (8.8 km²) (1.0%) is water. The county lies in the Piedmont region, drained by branches of the Oconee River. Interstate 85 and U.S. Route 129 cross the county, supporting rapid suburban and industrial growth west of Athens and northeast of metropolitan Atlanta.

Communities

  • Cities: Arcade, Braselton (part), Commerce, Hoschton, Jefferson, Nicholson, Pendergrass, Talmo
  • Town: Maysville (part)

Economy and demographics

Historically agricultural — the area around Commerce was a centre of cotton trade and later of discount retailing — the county has become a distribution and manufacturing hub along the I-85 corridor. Its population roughly tripled between 1990 and 2020 as it became one of Georgia's faster-growing counties, with a median household income above the state average.
