Libyan civil war (2011)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Rebel fighters on a technical (pickup truck with mounted gun) near the front line in 2011]
Rebel fighters on a technical (pickup truck with mounted gun) near the front line in 2011

The Libyan civil war of 2011 was an armed conflict in Libya between forces loyal to Muammar Gaddafi, who had ruled since 1969, and rebel groups seeking to oust him, as part of the Arab Spring. Following a United Nations Security Council resolution, an international coalition led by NATO intervened militarily on the rebels' side. The war ended with the fall of Tripoli in August and Gaddafi's capture and death in Sirte on 20 October 2011.

Outbreak

Protests erupted in Benghazi in mid-February 2011 after the arrest of a human-rights lawyer; security forces fired on demonstrators, and the east rapidly fell to the rebels' National Transitional Council. Gaddafi's counter-offensive in March retook ground and threatened Benghazi with a massacre, prompting Resolution 1973 (17 March), which authorised a no-fly zone and 'all necessary measures' to protect civilians.

Intervention and fall

French, British and American forces began strikes on 19 March; NATO assumed command of Operation Unified Protector at the end of March. A stalemated summer gave way in August when rebels, with NATO air support, took Tripoli in Operation Mermaid Dawn. Gaddafi fled to Sirte, where he was captured and killed by fighters on 20 October — filmed on mobile phones — and the NTC declared liberation on 23 October.

Aftermath

Estimates of the dead range from several thousand to around 25,000. Libya held elections in 2012 but slid into fragmentation and a second civil war from 2014, militia rule, migrant trafficking and an arms outflow that destabilised Mali and the Sahel. The intervention remains debated: defended as preventing a massacre, criticised as exceeding its mandate into regime change and leaving state collapse behind.
