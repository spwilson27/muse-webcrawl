Secunda is a town in Mpumalanga province, South Africa, built around the operations of the petrochemical company Sasol. It is situated on the Highveld coalfields, roughly 130 kilometres east of Johannesburg, and owes its existence to Sasol's decision in the 1970s to build a second coal-to-liquids plant after the original facility at Sasolburg.

History

Following the 1973 oil crisis, the South African government sought greater energy self-sufficiency, and Sasol Two — later joined by Sasol Three — was constructed in the late 1970s and early 1980s. The town of Secunda (from the Latin secunda, "second," referring to the second Sasol refinery) was laid out to house the plant's workforce. The Secunda complex became the world's largest single-site coal-to-liquids facility and one of the largest single point sources of greenhouse gas emissions on Earth.

Economy and infrastructure

The Sasol complex dominates the local economy, producing synthetic fuels, chemicals, and feedstocks from coal gasification. The town is served by the R546 and R580 roads and has expanded with suburbs such as Trichardt and Evander nearby. A notable landmark is the Secunda skyline of tall cooling towers and distillation columns, visible for many kilometres across the flat Highveld.
