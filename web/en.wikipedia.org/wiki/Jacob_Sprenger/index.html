Jacob Sprenger

Jacob Sprenger (c. 1436/1438 – December 6, 1495) was a Dominican inquisitor and theologian. He is most widely known as the named co-author of the Malleus Maleficarum ("Hammer of Witches"), the infamous treatise on the prosecution of witches, although modern scholars debate how much of the work he actually wrote, as opposed to its principal author, Heinrich Kramer.

Life

Sprenger was born in Rheinfelden, in present-day Switzerland, around 1436. He joined the Dominican Order in 1452 and studied at the University of Cologne, where he later became a professor of theology and dean. In 1480 he was appointed Dean of the Faculty of Theology at Cologne, one of the most prestigious academic positions in the Empire.

In 1484, Pope Innocent VIII issued the bull Summis desiderantes affectibus, which named Sprenger and Kramer as inquisitors empowered to prosecute witchcraft in Germany. The Malleus Maleficarum, published in 1487, was largely Kramer's work; Sprenger's name may have been added to lend it authority. Some scholars believe Sprenger, who was skeptical of aspects of witch persecution and later clashed with Kramer, contributed little beyond the introduction or nothing at all.

Death

Sprenger died in Strasbourg in 1495. Despite the uncertain extent of his authorship, his name has remained inseparably associated with the witch-hunt manual that bore it for centuries.
