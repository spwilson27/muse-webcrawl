Hypotonia

Specialty: Neurology, pediatrics
Definition: Decreased muscle tone

Hypotonia, commonly known as floppy baby syndrome when it presents in infants, is a state of low muscle tone (the amount of tension or resistance to stretch in a muscle), often involving reduced muscle strength. Hypotonia is not a specific medical disorder but a potential manifestation of many different diseases and disorders that affect motor nerve control by the brain or muscle strength.

Causes

Recognising hypotonia at birth prompts a search for central causes (the majority) such as Down syndrome, Prader–Willi syndrome, hypoxic–ischemic encephalopathy, and metabolic or genetic disorders; or peripheral causes including spinal muscular atrophy, congenital myopathies and muscular dystrophies, myasthenia, and peripheral neuropathies. Clues include reflexes (often preserved or brisk in central hypotonia, reduced in peripheral), associated dysmorphic features, and developmental history. Benign congenital hypotonia is a diagnosis of exclusion where tone improves with age without a specific cause found.

Signs and assessment

Hypotonic infants may slip through the hands when held under the arms, show a "rag doll" posture with poor head control, and fall behind motor milestones; older children may show joint hyperextensibility and poor endurance. Evaluation combines history, examination, and investigations such as genetic testing, creatine kinase, MRI, and sometimes muscle or nerve studies.

Management

Treatment targets the underlying condition where one is identified, and otherwise centres on physical, occupational, and speech therapy to support motor development, feeding, and posture.
