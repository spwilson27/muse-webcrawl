Marion Mahony Griffin (née Marion Lucy Mahony; 1871–1961) was an American architect and artist, one of the first licensed female architects in the world and an original member of the Prairie School. Her celebrated watercolour renderings defined the visual presentation of early modern architecture.

Career

Mahony graduated from MIT in 1894 and worked in Frank Lloyd Wright's Oak Park studio for fourteen years, producing the exquisite Japanese-influenced drawings that made Wright's Wasmuth Portfolio so influential, and designing furniture, mosaics and the Unity Temple murals.

Australia and Canberra

In 1911 she married Walter Burley Griffin; her presentation drawings were central to his winning entry in the competition to design Canberra, Australia's new capital. The Griffins spent over twenty years in Australia, developing Castlecrag in Sydney, before moving to India in 1935.

Legacy

Her memoir, "The Magic of America", and her renderings are held by the Art Institute of Chicago. She is now recognised as a major figure in her own right in the history of modern architecture and landscape design.
