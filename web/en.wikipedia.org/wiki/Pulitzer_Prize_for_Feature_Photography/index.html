Pulitzer Prize for Feature Photography

The Pulitzer Prize for Feature Photography is one of the fourteen Pulitzer Prizes awarded annually for American journalism. It honours a distinguished example of feature photography in black and white or colour, which may consist of a single photograph or a portfolio. The category was created in 1968 when the single photography prize was split into Spot News Photography (now Breaking News Photography) and Feature Photography.

Selected winners

Early winners included Toshio Sakai (1968) for combat photography in Vietnam and William C. Beall successors in the feature tradition. Notable later portfolios include Anthony Suau's 1984 photographs of famine in Ethiopia; Stephanie Welsh's 1996 images of a Kenyan circumcision rite; Damon Winter's 2009 coverage of Barack Obama's campaign; Craig F. Walker, who won twice (2010 and 2012) for stories of an Iraq veteran with PTSD and a returning soldier; Deanne Fitzmaurice (2005) for an Iraqi boy's treatment in an Oakland hospital; and the Associated Press team honoured in 2021 for elderly Spaniards during COVID-19, and in 2024 for migrants' journeys to the US border.

Criteria

Entries, submitted by US news organisations, may contain up to fifteen photographs. Juries look for humanistic storytelling, composition and sustained narrative rather than the single dramatic moment prized by the Breaking News category. Winners receive $15,000 and a certificate; the award is administered by Columbia University and announced each spring (since 2017, in May).
