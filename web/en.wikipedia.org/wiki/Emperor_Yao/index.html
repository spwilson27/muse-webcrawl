Emperor Yao

Emperor Yao (simplified Chinese: 尧; traditional Chinese: 堯; pinyin: Yáo; traditionally c. 2356 – 2255 BC) was a legendary Chinese ruler, one of the Three Sovereigns and Five Emperors. He is held up in the Confucian tradition as a model sage-king of moral perfection and intelligence, whose benevolence and diligence served as an example to later emperors.

Legend

According to tradition, Yao became ruler at twenty years of age and reigned for nearly a century. His achievements are said to include ordering court astronomers to observe the heavens and fix the calendar and the seasons for farmers, establishing rituals, and suppressing the great flood by appointing Gun, and later Gun's son Yu the Great, to control the waters. His capital is traditionally placed at Pingyang, near modern Linfen in Shanxi.

Abdication and succession

Yao is most famous for passing over his own son Danzhu, whom he judged unworthy, and abdicating in favour of Shun, a man of humble origin whose filial piety Yao had tested — first by sending officials to observe him, and then by marrying his two daughters, Ehuang and Nüying, to him. This abdication to a worthy successor (shanrang) became one of the most celebrated acts in Chinese political legend and a standing ideal cited in later debates about succession. Yao was succeeded by Shun, who in turn yielded to Yu the Great, founder of the Xia dynasty.

Legacy

Yao, together with Shun and Yu, forms the trio of sage rulers of China's golden age in Confucian historiography. The opening chapter of the Book of Documents, the "Canon of Yao", is devoted to his reign. The game of Go (weiqi) is traditionally said to have been invented by Yao to teach discipline to his son Danzhu.
