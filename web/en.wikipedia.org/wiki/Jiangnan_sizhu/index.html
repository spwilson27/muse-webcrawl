Jiangnan sizhu (Chinese: 江南絲竹; pinyin: Jiāngnán sīzhú) is a style of traditional Chinese instrumental music, originating in the Jiangnan region of southeastern China—Shanghai, southern Jiangsu and northern Zhejiang. The name literally means "silk and bamboo of Jiangnan", referring to the silk-stringed instruments (erhu, pipa, yangqin, sanxian) and bamboo wind instruments (dizi, xiao, sheng) that make up the ensemble.

Characteristics

The music is played mostly by amateur music clubs and at weddings, festivals and teahouse gatherings. Its texture is heterophonic: all instruments play variants of the same melody, with the dizi and erhu prominent, ornamenting freely within a shared framework. Pieces are often in a slow, lyrical style described as refined and elegant, in contrast to the louder "gongs and drums" genres.

Repertoire

The core repertoire includes the "Eight Great Pieces" (Ba Da Qu), among them Zhonghua Liuban (from which the famous Lao Liuban skeleton melody derives), Huan Le Ge ("Song of Joy"), San Liu ("Three Sixes"), Xing Jie ("Walking the Street") and Si He Ru Yi. Many pieces are elaborations of a small stock of mother tunes through the variation technique known as fangman jiahua ("slowing down and adding flowers").

History

Jiangnan sizhu crystallised in the late Qing dynasty and flourished in the music societies of Shanghai and Suzhou in the early 20th century. It strongly influenced the development of modern Chinese orchestral music, and many of its tunes were adapted for the conservatory repertoire.
