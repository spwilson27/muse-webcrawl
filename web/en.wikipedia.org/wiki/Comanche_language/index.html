Comanche language

Native to: United States (Oklahoma)
Ethnicity: Comanche
Family: Uto-Aztecan → Numic → Central Numic
Endangered: Severely endangered

Comanche (Nʉmʉ Tekwapʉ, "the People's language") is a Uto-Aztecan language of the Numic branch, spoken by the Comanche people, who split from the Shoshone in the early 18th century as they moved south onto the Great Plains. It is very closely related to Shoshoni, from which it diverged only a few centuries ago.

Status

Comanche is severely endangered. During the 20th century, children in boarding schools were discouraged from speaking it, and transmission to younger generations largely ceased. By the 21st century, the number of fully fluent first-language speakers had dwindled to a small number of elders, though the Comanche Nation in Oklahoma supports language preservation and teaching programs, classes, and documentation efforts.

Features

Comanche is a polysynthetic, agglutinative language with extensive use of instrumental prefixes and a system of vowel devoicing. It has a relatively small consonant inventory and a vowel system with length and voiceless contrasts. Word order is generally subject–object–verb.

In popular culture

Comanche code talkers served with the United States Army in both World Wars; a group of Comanche soldiers transmitted coded messages in the European theater of World War II. The 2022 film Prey was released with a full Comanche-language dub, a first for a major feature film.
