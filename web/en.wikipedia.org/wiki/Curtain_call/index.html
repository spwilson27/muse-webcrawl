Curtain call

Placeholder image: Curtain call

A curtain call (often known as a walkdown or a final bow) occurs at the end of a performance when one or more performers return to the stage to be recognized by the audience for the performance. In musical theatre, the performers typically recognize the orchestra and its conductor at the end of the curtain call. Luciano Pavarotti holds the record for receiving 165 curtain calls, more than any other artist, for his February 24, 1988 performance of Nembo in Berlin.

Usage

Curtain calls may continue for many minutes if the audience shows exceptional enthusiasm, with performers returning repeatedly to bow or curtsy. Orchestras and conductors may also take bows; in opera, principal singers, the conductor, and sometimes the director and designers are acknowledged in a customary order from smaller roles to principals. Audience members may throw flowers onto the stage as a tribute.

In film

In film, a curtain call is a scene at the end of a movie where characters appear in a tableau or are reintroduced with credits. Examples include the closing sequence of many classic musicals and ensemble films, where cast members bow or appear in character vignettes as credits roll.

In sports

In sports, a curtain call is the act of a player coming out of the dugout or bench, in response to a request from a cheering audience, to acknowledge applause after an exceptional performance — for example, after a baseball player hits a milestone home run or pitches a notable game. Curtain calls are customary at some venues and considered exceptional at others.
