Shibuya Station (渋谷駅, Shibuya-eki) is a major railway station in Shibuya, Tokyo, Japan. Operated jointly by JR East, Keio Corporation, Tokyu Corporation and Tokyo Metro, it is one of the busiest stations in the world and the gateway to the Shibuya shopping and entertainment district, famous for the Shibuya Crossing scramble intersection just outside the Hachikō exit.

Lines

JR East serves the station with the Yamanote, Saikyō and Shōnan-Shinjuku lines; Tokyu operates the Tōyoko and Den-en-toshi lines; Keio runs the Inokashira Line; and Tokyo Metro provides the Ginza, Hanzōmon and Fukutoshin subway lines. Through services link the station directly with Yokohama, Saitama and beyond.

History and redevelopment

The station opened in 1885 on what became the Yamanote Line. The faithful dog Hachikō waited at the station for his deceased master through the 1920s and 1930s; his statue outside the station is Tokyo's most famous meeting point. Since the 2000s the station area has undergone continuous large-scale redevelopment, including the Shibuya Hikarie tower (2012), the relocation of the Tōyoko Line platforms underground (2013), Shibuya Stream (2018) and the 230-metre Shibuya Scramble Square (2019), with further works on the JR station continuing into the 2020s.

Surroundings

Exits lead to the scramble crossing, Center Gai shopping street, the department stores of the Tokyu and Seibu groups, and the statue of Hachikō. A preserved Moai-style statue (Moyai) stands at the west side. The station's labyrinthine layout across four operators is frequently noted by first-time visitors.
