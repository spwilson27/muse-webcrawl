Energy Star

Energy Star (stylised as ENERGY STAR) is a programme run by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) and the U.S. Department of Energy to promote energy efficiency. Launched in 1992, the programme provides information on the energy consumption of products and devices using standardised methods; products meeting specified efficiency criteria may display the Energy Star logo.

The Energy Star label identifies energy-efficient products and buildings.

Scope

The label applies to appliances, electronics, lighting, heating and cooling equipment, and also to buildings: homes, commercial buildings and industrial plants can earn Energy Star certification for superior energy performance. Certified products typically use 10–50% less energy than standard models.

Impact

The EPA estimates that the programme has saved American families and businesses billions of dollars in energy costs and prevented billions of metric tons of greenhouse gas emissions since its inception. Similar labelling schemes exist in other countries, and Energy Star specifications are adopted by several partner countries.
