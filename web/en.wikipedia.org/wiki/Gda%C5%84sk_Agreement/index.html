Gdańsk Agreement

The Gdańsk Agreement was an accord signed on 31 August 1980 between the Polish communist government and striking workers of the Lenin Shipyard in Gdańsk, led by Lech Wałęsa. It ended the August 1980 strikes and led directly to the creation of Solidarity (Solidarność), the first independent, self-governing trade union in the Soviet bloc.

Background

Strikes erupted in July and August 1980 after increases in food prices, spreading along the Baltic coast. At the Gdańsk shipyard the dismissal of crane operator Anna Walentynowicz triggered a strike that broadened into an interfactory movement with 21 demands, including free trade unions, the right to strike, freedom of speech and the release of political prisoners.

Terms

The agreement accepted the workers' key demands: independent unions and the right to strike were legalised, censorship was to be limited, and political prisoners released. In return the strikers recognised the leading role of the Polish United Workers' Party and Poland's existing alliances.

Significance

Similar agreements were signed at Szczecin and Jastrzębie. Solidarity was registered in November 1980 and grew to some ten million members. Though martial law suppressed the union in December 1981, the Gdańsk Agreement is regarded as a turning point that opened the way to the negotiated end of communist rule in Poland in 1989.
