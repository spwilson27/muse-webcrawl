French literature

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

French literature comprises the body of written works in the French language, produced in France and in francophone countries from the Middle Ages to the present. For centuries it stood at the centre of European letters, and French authors have won more Nobel Prizes in Literature than those of any other country.

The bouquinistes of Paris, emblematic of the city's literary culture.

Middle Ages and Renaissance

The earliest French texts include the Strasbourg Oaths (842) and the epic chansons de geste such as The Song of Roland. Medieval literature flourished in courtly romance (Chrétien de Troyes), lyric poetry, the fabliaux, and the theatre of mystery plays, while François Villon gave voice to the fifteenth-century city. The Renaissance brought Rabelais's comic epics, the Pléiade poets under Ronsard, and Montaigne, who invented the essay.

The classical age and Enlightenment

Seventeenth-century classicism produced the tragedies of Corneille and Racine, the comedies of Molière, and the maxims of La Rochefoucauld, under the standardising eye of the Académie française. The eighteenth century was dominated by the philosophes — Voltaire, Diderot and his Encyclopédie, Rousseau and Montesquieu — whose works shaped the Enlightenment across Europe.

The nineteenth century

Romanticism opened with Chateaubriand and Madame de Staël and crested in the poetry of Lamartine and Victor Hugo, whose novels Les Misérables and Notre-Dame de Paris became world classics. Realism followed in Balzac's Comédie humaine, Stendhal and Flaubert; naturalism in Zola; and symbolism in Baudelaire, Verlaine and Rimbaud.

Twentieth century and after

The modern era spans Proust's In Search of Lost Time, the existentialism of Sartre and Camus, the theatre of the absurd, and the experimental nouveau roman. Francophone writing from Africa, the Caribbean, Quebec and the Maghreb has greatly widened the tradition, and contemporary French authors — among them Nobel laureates Annie Ernaux and Patrick Modiano — continue to reach a global audience.
