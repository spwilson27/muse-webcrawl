Nicole Kidman

Born Nicole Mary Kidman, June 20, 1967, Honolulu, Hawaii, U.S.

Citizenship Australia and the United States

Occupation Actress, producer

Years active 1983–present

Nicole Mary Kidman (born June 20, 1967) is an Australian and American actress and producer. Known for her work in film and television productions across many genres, she has consistently ranked among the world's highest-paid actresses since the late 1990s. Her accolades include an Academy Award, a BAFTA Award, two Primetime Emmy Awards, and six Golden Globe Awards. She was named by Time as one of the 100 most influential people in the world in 2004 and 2018.

Career

Born in Honolulu to Australian parents and raised in Sydney, Kidman began her career in Australian film and television, breaking through with the thriller Dead Calm (1989). She gained international recognition with Days of Thunder (1990), opposite her future husband Tom Cruise, and went on to star in Far and Away (1992), Batman Forever (1995), and the dark comedy To Die For (1995), which won her a Golden Globe.

Kidman starred in Stanley Kubrick's Eyes Wide Shut (1999) and achieved a celebrated run of roles in the early 2000s: the musical Moulin Rouge! (2001), the horror film The Others (2001), and The Hours (2002), in which her portrayal of Virginia Woolf won her the Academy Award for Best Actress. Later films include Cold Mountain (2003), Birth (2004), Rabbit Hole (2010), Lion (2016), and Being the Ricardos (2021), in which she played Lucille Ball. On television, she won Emmy Awards for the HBO series Big Little Lies (2017–2019) and starred in The Undoing (2020) and Nine Perfect Strangers (2021). Through her company Blossom Films she has produced many of her projects. In 2024 she received the AFI Life Achievement Award.

Personal life

Kidman was married to Tom Cruise from 1990 to 2001, with whom she adopted two children. She married country singer Keith Urban in 2006; they have two daughters. She has served as a Goodwill Ambassador for UNICEF and UN Women.
