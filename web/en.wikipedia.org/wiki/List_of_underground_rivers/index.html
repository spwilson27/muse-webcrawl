List of underground rivers

An underground river navigable by boat inside a limestone cave

An underground river is a river that runs wholly or partly beneath the surface of the ground. Many flow through cave systems in limestone (karst) regions, where surface streams sink into sinkholes and re-emerge at springs. Others are surface rivers that have been culverted or buried by urban development. This list includes notable natural underground rivers and significant buried urban watercourses.

Natural underground rivers

  • Puerto Princesa Subterranean River (Philippines) — an 8.2 km navigable underground river in Palawan, flowing through a cave before emptying into the sea; a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the New7Wonders of Nature.
  • Rio Secreto (Mexico) — a semi-flooded limestone cave system in the Yucatán Peninsula near Playa del Carmen.
  • Lost River (Indiana, United States) — flows underground for about 13 km through karst terrain in southern Indiana.
  • Saint-Léonard underground river (Montreal, Canada) — a cave river beneath the Saint-Léonard borough.
  • Xiaozhai Tiankeng drainage (China) — underground streams beneath the world's deepest sinkhole, in Chongqing.
  • Cruzob cave rivers (Yucatán) — watercourses within cenote systems sacred to the Maya.
  • Mammoth Cave system streams (Kentucky, United States) — including the Echo River and River Styx within the world's longest cave system.
  • Sistema Sac Actun (Mexico) — flooded cave passages carrying groundwater in Quintana Roo, part of the longest underwater cave system in the world.
  • Labouiche (France) — a navigable underground river in Ariège, visited by boat tours.
  • Hölloch cave streams (Switzerland) — underground waterways in one of Europe's longest cave systems.

Buried urban rivers

  • River Fleet (London) — the largest of London's subterranean rivers, now a sewer flowing beneath Farringdon Road into the Thames.
  • River Westbourne (London) — passes over Sloane Square station in an iron pipe.
  • Bièvre (Paris) — a tributary of the Seine largely covered in the early twentieth century.
  • Saw Mill River (Yonkers, New York) — long buried beneath the city, partly daylighted in 2012.
  • Sunswick Creek and other culverted streams in many industrial cities.

In mythology

Underground rivers feature in many mythologies, most famously the River Styx of Greek mythology, which separated the world of the living from Hades; other underworld rivers include the Acheron, Lethe, Phlegethon, and Cocytus.
