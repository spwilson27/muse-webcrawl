Cities of refuge

The Cities of Refuge (Hebrew: arei miklat) were six Levitical towns in the Kingdom of Israel and the Kingdom of Judah in which those who had accidentally committed manslaughter could claim asylum, as described in the Hebrew Bible (Numbers 35, Deuteronomy 19 and Joshua 20).

The six cities

Three cities were designated west of the Jordan River — Kedesh in Galilee, Shechem in Mount Ephraim, and Hebron in Judah — and three east of the Jordan — Bezer in the territory of Reuben, Ramoth-Gilead in Gad, and Golan in Bashan (Manasseh). Roads to them were to be kept in good repair so that a fugitive could reach safety quickly.

Legal function

Under the biblical law, a person who had killed another unintentionally could flee to a city of refuge and be protected from the "avenger of blood" (go'el ha-dam), the victim's kinsman charged with blood vengeance. The refugee stood trial before the congregation; if judged innocent of intent, he remained in the city until the death of the high priest, after which he could return home. Intentional murderers, by contrast, were handed over for execution even if they fled to a city of refuge.
