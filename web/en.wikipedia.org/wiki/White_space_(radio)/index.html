White space (radio)

In telecommunications, white spaces are frequencies allocated to a broadcasting service but not used locally. National and international bodies assign frequencies for specific uses and, to prevent interference, leave gaps — "guard bands" — between channels, and in many places entire television channels lie fallow because using them would interfere with distant stations.

Unused gaps in the television spectrum constitute white spaces

White space devices

Advances in cognitive radio made it possible for unlicensed devices to detect and use white spaces without interfering with incumbents such as television broadcasts and wireless microphones. In the United States the Federal Communications Commission authorised unlicensed use of TV white spaces in 2008–2010, subject to checking a geolocation database of protected users. The resulting technology, sometimes called Super Wi-Fi, propagates farther and penetrates obstacles better than conventional Wi-Fi because of its lower frequencies, making it attractive for rural broadband. Standards include IEEE 802.11af. The UK regulator Ofcom and other national regulators have adopted similar frameworks.
