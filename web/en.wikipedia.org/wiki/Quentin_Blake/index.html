Sir Quentin Saxby Blake (born 16 December 1932) is an English cartoonist, caricaturist, illustrator and children's writer. He has illustrated over 300 books, including works by Roald Dahl, with whom he is most closely associated, as well as books by Russell Hoban, Joan Aiken, Michael Rosen, and David Walliams, and many of his own picture books.

Blake's distinctive style combines lively ink line with watercolour washes.

Career

Blake published his first drawing in the magazine Punch at the age of 16 and contributed cartoons to Punch and The Spectator while studying English at Downing College, Cambridge, and later at Chelsea School of Art. He taught illustration at the Royal College of Art for over twenty years, heading its illustration department from 1978 to 1986. His collaboration with Roald Dahl began in 1978 with The Enormous Crocodile and continued through most of Dahl's later books, including The BFG, Matilda, and The Witches.

Recognition

Blake served as the first British Children's Laureate from 1999 to 2001. He received the Hans Christian Andersen Award in 2002, the highest international recognition for creators of children's books, and was knighted in 2013 for services to illustration. The Quentin Blake Centre for Illustration, developed from the House of Illustration which he founded, opened in Clerkenwell, London.
