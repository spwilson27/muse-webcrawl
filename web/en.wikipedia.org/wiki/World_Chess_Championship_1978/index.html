The World Chess Championship 1978 was played between champion Anatoly Karpov of the Soviet Union and challenger Viktor Korchnoi, a Soviet defector, in Baguio City, Philippines. Karpov won the first-to-six-wins match 6–5 to retain his title, in one of the most politically charged and bizarre championship matches in chess history.

Background

Karpov had become champion by default in 1975 when Bobby Fischer refused to defend. Korchnoi, who had defected from the USSR in 1976, defeated Tigran Petrosian, Lev Polugaevsky and Boris Spassky in the Candidates matches to earn his challenge. The pairing was framed in Cold War terms: the loyal Soviet champion against the defector, whose wife and son remained in the USSR.

The match

Karpov raced to a 4–1 lead in wins, but Korchnoi fought back to 5–5 after 31 games, including wins in games 29 and 31. Karpov won the 32nd game to close the match. The event was marked by disputes over a Soviet psychologist (Vladimir Zukhar) placed in the front rows, Korchnoi's hiring of parapsychologists and Ananda Marga members, protests about yoghurt delivered to Karpov during play, and arguments over flags and seating.
