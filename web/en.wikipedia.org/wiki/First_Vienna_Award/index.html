First Vienna Award

The First Vienna Award was a treaty signed on 2 November 1938 by which Czechoslovakia was forced to cede southern Slovakia and southern Subcarpathian Ruthenia to Hungary. The award was the result of arbitration by Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy at the Belvedere Palace in Vienna, following the Munich Agreement of September 1938, and it transferred to Hungary a territory of some 11,900 square kilometres with over a million inhabitants, many of them ethnic Hungarians.

Background

Hungary had lost two-thirds of its territory under the Treaty of Trianon (1920), including southern Slovakia, which had a large Hungarian-speaking population. After the Munich Agreement dismembered Czechoslovakia's defences and ceded the Sudetenland to Germany, Hungary pressed its own territorial claims. Direct Czechoslovak–Hungarian negotiations at Komárno in October 1938 broke down, and both sides accepted arbitration by Germany and Italy — Britain and France, the other Munich signatories, declined to participate.

Terms

The award, announced on 2 November 1938 by German foreign minister Joachim von Ribbentrop and Italian foreign minister Galeazzo Ciano, assigned to Hungary the southern strip of Slovakia and Ruthenia, including the cities of Košice (Kassa), Uzhhorod (Ungvár) and Mukachevo, as well as the town of Komárno. According to the 1930 Czechoslovak census the ceded area's population was majority Hungarian in most districts, though it included substantial Slovak and Ruthenian minorities, and the ethnic border drawn was disputed. Hungarian troops occupied the territory between 5 and 10 November 1938, and Regent Miklós Horthy rode into Košice on a white horse.

Consequences

The award deepened the disintegration of Czechoslovakia, which was occupied by Germany in March 1939; Hungary then seized the remainder of Carpathian Ruthenia. A brief Slovak–Hungarian war followed in March 1939. The Second Vienna Award of 1940 applied the same mechanism to Romania, awarding Northern Transylvania to Hungary. After the Second World War the Paris Peace Treaties of 1947 declared both Vienna Awards null and restored Hungary's Trianon frontiers, apart from a small additional cession to Czechoslovakia near Bratislava.
