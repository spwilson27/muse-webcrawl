Fulgence Bienvenüe

Fulgence Marie Auguste Bienvenüe
Born: 27 January 1852, Uzel, Brittany, France
Died: 3 August 1936, Paris, France
Occupation: Civil engineer
Known for: Chief engineer of the Paris Métro

Fulgence Marie Auguste Bienvenüe (27 January 1852 – 3 August 1936) was a French civil engineer best known as the chief engineer of the Paris Métro, a project that made him known as "Le Père du Métro" ("Father of the Métro").

Career

Bienvenüe graduated from the École Polytechnique in 1870 and the École des Ponts et Chaussées in 1872. He lost his left arm in a railway accident in 1881 but continued a distinguished career in the Paris municipal engineering service, designing parks, roads, and water systems. In 1896 he became chief engineer of the planned metropolitan railway, and he oversaw the design and construction of the first lines, beginning with Line 1, which opened on 19 July 1900 in time for the Exposition Universelle.

Legacy

Bienvenüe retired in 1932 at the age of 80. The Paris Métro station Montparnasse–Bienvenüe bears his name (the station sign fittingly uses his name without its diaeresis). One of the 72 names inscribed on the Eiffel Tower, that of his colleague and contemporary engineers, commemorates the scientific generation to which he belonged.
