The Firth of Clyde is the estuary of the River Clyde on the west coast of Scotland, opening into the North Channel and the Irish Sea. It is among the largest and deepest coastal waters in the British Isles, sheltered from the Atlantic by the Kintyre peninsula.

Geography

The firth contains numerous islands, including the Isle of Arran, the Isle of Bute, Great Cumbrae, and Holy Isle. It is bounded by the council areas of Argyll and Bute, Inverclyde, North Ayrshire and South Ayrshire. Upper reaches of the firth extend past Greenock and Dumbarton toward Glasgow.

History and use

The Clyde was one of the world's great shipbuilding centres, and the firth served naval purposes through both world wars; the naval base at Faslane on the Gare Loch remains a major installation. In the Victorian era, resorts along the firth — Rothesay, Dunoon, Largs — were popular holiday destinations reached by Clyde steamers. The firth supports fishing, ferry routes operated by Caledonian MacBrayne, and significant marine wildlife, including basking sharks and porpoises.
