Cleaner wrasse

The cleaner wrasses are fishes of the genus Labroides, found on coral reefs in the tropical Pacific and Indian Oceans. They are famous for their cleaning behaviour: they establish "cleaning stations" where larger fish — including predators — queue to have ectoparasites, dead skin, and mucus removed, a classic example of mutualism.

Behaviour

The best-known species is the bluestreak cleaner wrasse (Labroides dimidiatus). Cleaner wrasses advertise their services with a distinctive "dance" and bright coloration. Client fish pose in characteristic postures, allowing cleaners to enter their mouths and gill chambers unharmed. Cleaners occasionally cheat by eating the client's nutritious mucus instead of parasites, an interaction studied extensively in the evolution of cooperation.

Cognition research

Bluestreak cleaner wrasses have attracted attention in animal cognition research, including experiments suggesting they can pass a version of the mirror test, though interpretation of these results remains debated.
