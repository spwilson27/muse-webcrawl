Search and rescue dog

A disaster search dog searching collapsed-structure rubble

A search and rescue (SAR) dog is a dog trained to locate missing people, whether live victims trapped under the rubble of collapsed buildings, lost walkers in wilderness, or human remains. Exploiting the dog's extraordinary sense of smell — a dog's nose has up to 300 million scent receptors, compared with about 6 million in humans — trained dogs can cover large areas far faster than human searchers and detect scent through water, snow, and debris.

Types

Air-scent dogs work off-lead, following airborne scent carried on the wind to any person in an area. Trailing and tracking dogs follow the ground scent of a specific individual, usually starting from a scent article. Disaster dogs are trained for collapsed structures and must be agile, steady on unstable rubble, and able to distinguish live victims from remains and from rescuers. Specialisations include water search (locating drowning victims from boats), avalanche dogs, and cadaver dogs (human remains detection dogs), which are also used in forensic work. When a dog finds its target it gives a trained alert, such as barking at the spot or returning to the handler and leading them in.

Training and deployment

Training typically begins in puppyhood and takes 18 months to two years; common breeds include Border Collies, German Shepherds, Labrador Retrievers, and spaniels, chosen for drive, agility, and temperament. Handler–dog teams certify through national bodies and deploy with fire-service urban search and rescue task forces, mountain rescue teams, and volunteer organisations such as those under the International Search and Rescue Dog Organisation (IRO), founded in 1993. Famous deployments include the Swiss disaster dogs of Barry fame's tradition at the Great St Bernard Hospice (avalanche rescue), the hundreds of dog teams that worked at the World Trade Center after September 11, 2001, and international responses to earthquakes in Haiti (2010), Nepal (2015), and Türkiye–Syria (2023).
