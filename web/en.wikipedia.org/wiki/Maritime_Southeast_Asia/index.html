Maritime Southeast Asia

Maritime Southeast Asia comprises the Southeast Asian countries and regions that are predominantly archipelagic or partly archipelagic, as opposed to the mainland states of Indochina. It corresponds broadly to the historical maritime world of the Malay Archipelago and the Austronesian peoples, and includes modern-day Indonesia, the Philippines, Malaysia (including East Malaysia on Borneo), Singapore, Brunei, and Timor-Leste.

Geography and history

The region contains more than 25,000 islands straddling the equator between the Indian and Pacific Oceans, crossed by vital sea lanes such as the Strait of Malacca. Its peoples share Austronesian languages and a seafaring heritage; historically it was tied together by thalassocratic empires such as Srivijaya and Majapahit, by the spice trade, and by the spread of Islam through merchant networks before European colonisation divided it among the Dutch, Spanish, British, Portuguese and American empires.

The Malay world

Maritime Southeast Asia is sometimes equated with the “Malay world” (Alam Melayu) on account of the historical influence of Malay language and culture across the archipelago, and with the older European term East Indies.
