Neutral theory of molecular evolution

The neutral theory of molecular evolution, proposed by Motoo Kimura in 1968 and independently by King and Jukes, holds that most evolutionary changes at the molecular level are caused not by natural selection but by genetic drift of mutant alleles that are selectively neutral (or nearly so).

Content of the theory

The theory does not claim genes are functionless or that morphological evolution is random; it concerns the great majority of DNA substitutions, many of which do not change amino acids. It predicts a roughly constant molecular clock — a prediction broadly borne out within lineages.

Tomoko Ohta's nearly neutral theory extended it to slightly deleterious mutations, whose fate depends on population size.

Impact

Neutral theory supplies the null model for tests of selection (e.g. dN/dS ratios) throughout Phylogenetics and Population genetics, and remains one of the most debated ideas in evolutionary biology.
