Hitachi A-train

The A-train is a family of railway rolling stock built by Hitachi Rail using a modular design concept based on double-skin aluminium extruded body shells joined by friction stir welding. The design concept — marketed as "A-train" for its use of aluminium, advanced design and articulated concepts — reduces weight, improves crashworthiness and recyclability, and allows a common platform for commuter, express, Shinkansen and monorail vehicles. A-train products operate extensively in Japan and have been exported, notably to the United Kingdom, where Hitachi built the Class 395 Javelin and the Intercity Express Programme trains (Classes 800, 801, 802, 803, 805, 807 and 810).

A Class 800 Intercity Express Train, built by Hitachi at Newton Aycliffe, England.

Design

The A-train bodyshell uses large double-skin aluminium extrusions welded by friction stir welding, producing a smooth, strong and light structure with fewer parts than conventional steel or single-skin designs. The modular concept standardises components — bogies, traction equipment, cab modules — across vehicle types, shortening development and production times.

Japanese applications

The concept debuted with trains such as the Seibu 20000 series (2000) and was adopted widely: JR Kyushu's 885 series tilting trains, the Tokyo Metro and Toei fleets, JR East's E257 series, and Hitachi-built monorails (including systems in Chongqing and Kuala Lumpur). Hitachi's Kasado Works in Yamaguchi Prefecture is the principal Japanese plant.

Exports

In Britain, the Class 395 (2009) for Southeastern high-speed services on High Speed 1 was the first Japanese-built train fleet for a European railway. The government's Intercity Express Programme ordered hundreds of A-train vehicles assembled at Hitachi's Newton Aycliffe factory (opened 2015), replacing InterCity 125 and 225 fleets. A-train-derived trains have also been supplied to Taiwan (EMU3000 series) and for other Asian and Middle Eastern projects.
