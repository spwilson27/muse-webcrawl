Louis Vernet (born Luis Vernet; 6 August 1791 – 29 January 1871) was a Hamburg-born merchant of Huguenot descent who established a settlement at Port Louis on East Falkland Island, and in 1829 was appointed by the United Provinces of the Río de la Plata as the first commandant (governor) of the Falkland Islands (Spanish: Comandante Político y Militar de las Islas Malvinas).

Settlement in the Falklands

Vernet had first come to Buenos Aires in 1819 and became involved in the wild-cattle business of the Falklands in the 1820s, in partnership with Jorge Pacheco. After obtaining grants from the Buenos Aires government, he founded a settlement at Port Louis (the former French Port Louis and Spanish Puerto Soledad) in 1826, bringing settlers, gauchos and livestock. In June 1829 the government of Buenos Aires formally created the Political and Military Command of the Malvinas Islands and appointed Vernet its governor, a move protested by the British consul.

Conflict and the Lexington raid

Vernet's efforts to regulate the unlicensed sealing activities of American vessels led him in 1831 to seize three United States schooners, including the Harriet. In response, the USS Lexington, under Captain Silas Duncan, raided Puerto Soledad in December 1831, spiked the guns, destroyed the settlement's powder store and declared the islands free of government. Vernet never returned to live in the islands. Britain reasserted its claim and took possession in January 1833.

Later life

Vernet spent years seeking compensation from Britain and the United States, presenting his claims in London and publishing accounts of his governorship. He returned to Argentina and died at San Isidro, Buenos Aires, in 1871. His daughter Matilde ("Malvina") Vernet y Sáez is often said to be the origin of the Argentine use of the name "Malvinas" for the islands.
