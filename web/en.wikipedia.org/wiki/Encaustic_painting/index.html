Encaustic painting

Encaustic painting

Medium: Pigments mixed with hot beeswax

Origin: Ancient Greece and Egypt (by the 1st century BC)

Famous examples: Fayum mummy portraits

Modern practitioners: Jasper Johns and others

Encaustic painting, also known as hot wax painting, is a painting technique that involves using heated beeswax to which coloured pigments are added. The liquid or paste is then applied to a surface — usually prepared wood, though canvas and other materials are often used. The word encaustic comes from the Greek enkaustikos, "to burn in", because heat is used throughout the process, from melting the beeswax to fusing the layers of paint.

History

The technique was used in ancient Greece — Pliny the Elder describes it in the 1st century AD, including its use on ships and for portrait painting — and reached its most famous expression in the Fayum mummy portraits of Roman Egypt (roughly 1st century BC to 3rd century AD). Because wax is exceptionally durable and resistant to moisture and mould, these portraits survive with their colours remarkably fresh. Encaustic was also used for early Byzantine icons, including the Christ Pantocrator icon at Saint Catherine's Monastery, Sinai. The technique fell out of general use in the Middle Ages, supplanted by tempera and then oil paint.

Technique

The simplest encaustic mixture can be made from pigment and melted beeswax; dammar resin is often added to raise the melting point and harden the surface. Wax is kept liquid on a heated palette, applied with brushes or metal spatulas, and each layer is re-fused to the one beneath with a heat gun, torch, or heated tool. The surface can be polished to a sheen, built up in relief, incised, or combined with collage, photography, and oil paint.

Modern revival

Interest revived in the 20th century, notably in the work of Jasper Johns, whose encaustic flags and targets of the 1950s exploited the medium's fast-setting, sculptural quality. Electric heating tools have made the technique more accessible, and it remains a niche but active contemporary medium.
