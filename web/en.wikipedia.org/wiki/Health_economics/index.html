Health economics

Health economics is the branch of economics concerned with the efficient allocation of healthcare resources, the behaviour of consumers, providers and insurers, and the value of health outcomes.

Health economics is a branch of economics concerned with issues related to efficiency, effectiveness, value and behaviour in the production and consumption of health and healthcare. It studies how scarce resources are allocated among alternative uses for the care of sickness and the promotion, maintenance and improvement of health.

Foundations

The field is conventionally dated to Kenneth Arrow's 1963 article "Uncertainty and the Welfare Economics of Medical Care", which analysed how risk, uncertainty, and asymmetric information between doctor and patient cause healthcare markets to depart from the competitive model. Michael Grossman's 1972 human-capital model treated health as a durable stock that individuals invest in through medical care, diet and exercise.

Topics

Major topics include health insurance and the problems of moral hazard and adverse selection; the valuation of health outcomes using quality-adjusted life years (QALYs) and cost-effectiveness analysis, applied by bodies such as the UK's NICE; supplier-induced demand; the economics of pharmaceuticals and innovation; and the determinants of health beyond medical care, including income, education and behaviour. Healthcare spending rises with national income and reaches around 17–18% of GDP in the United States, far above other developed countries — a central puzzle of the discipline.
