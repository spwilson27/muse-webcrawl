Marcel Marceau (born Marcel Mangel; 22 March 1923 – 22 September 2007) was a French mime artist and actor, widely considered the master of modern mime. He was internationally famous for his silent persona, "Bip the Clown", with his striped pullover, battered silk opera hat adorned with a flower, and whiteface make-up.

Early life and war

Born in Strasbourg to a Polish-Jewish immigrant family, Mangel changed his surname to Marceau when he joined the French Resistance as a teenager, helping to smuggle Jewish children to safety across the Swiss border; his father was deported and murdered at Auschwitz. After the liberation of Paris, he enlisted in the French army and served as a liaison officer with Patton's Third Army. His first public performance was for American troops after the Battle of the Bulge.

Career

Marceau studied under Charles Dullin and Étienne Decroux, and in 1947 created Bip, inspired by Charlie Chaplin's Little Tramp and the classic Pierrot. His Compagnie Marcel Marceau, founded the same year, was for many years the world's only permanent mime troupe. Signature routines such as "Walking Against the Wind" — later echoed in Michael Jackson's moonwalk — "The Cage", and "Youth, Maturity, Old Age and Death" played to audiences on every continent for six decades. His one spoken film line, famously, was "Non!" in Mel Brooks's comedy Silent Movie (1976), in which he delivered the film's only word of dialogue.

Marceau founded a school of mime in Paris, was made an officer of the Légion d'honneur, and is credited with reviving the art of pantomime in the post-war world. He died in Cahors in 2007 and is buried in Père Lachaise Cemetery.
