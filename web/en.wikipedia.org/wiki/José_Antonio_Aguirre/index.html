José Antonio Aguirre y Lecube (6 March 1904 – 22 March 1960) was a Basque politician of the Basque Nationalist Party (PNV) and the first Lehendakari (president) of the Basque Government, an office he held from 1936 until his death in exile in 1960.

Early life

Aguirre was born in Bilbao into a family of chocolate manufacturers and studied law at the University of Deusto. As a young man he was also a footballer, playing for Athletic Bilbao. He entered politics with the PNV, was elected mayor of Getxo at the age of 27, and championed the cause of Basque autonomy within the Spanish Republic.

The Basque Government and war

When the Cortes granted the Basque Country its Statute of Autonomy in October 1936, in the midst of the Spanish Civil War, Aguirre was sworn in as Lehendakari beneath the Tree of Gernika. His government organised an army, police and administration loyal to the Republic. After the bombing of Gernika and the fall of Bilbao in 1937 he continued the struggle from Catalonia until 1939.

Exile

Aguirre escaped Franco's Spain through occupied Europe, travelling under a false identity via Berlin to the Americas. From 1941 he re-established the Basque Government in exile in New York and later Paris, lobbying for Basque and democratic Spanish interests and for the restoration of self-government. He never returned to office in the Basque Country, dying of a heart attack in Paris in 1960; his remains were later repatriated. He is remembered as the founding figure of modern Basque self-government.
