Oral candidiasis

Oral candidiasis (oral thrush)
Cause: Overgrowth of Candida yeast, usually Candida albicans
Symptoms: White patches in the mouth, soreness, altered taste

Oral candidiasis, commonly called oral thrush, is a fungal infection of the mouth caused by overgrowth of Candida species, most often Candida albicans. It typically appears as creamy white, slightly raised patches on the tongue and inner cheeks that can be wiped away, sometimes leaving red, sore tissue beneath.

Risk factors

Candida normally lives harmlessly in the mouth, but overgrowth is favoured by a weakened immune system, infancy or old age, diabetes, dentures, inhaled corticosteroids, dry mouth, and antibiotics that disturb the normal oral flora.

Treatment

Mild cases are treated with topical antifungal medicines such as nystatin suspension or miconazole gel; more severe or recurrent infections may require oral antifungal tablets such as fluconazole, together with attention to the underlying cause.
