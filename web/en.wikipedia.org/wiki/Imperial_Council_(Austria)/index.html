The Imperial Council (German: Reichsrat, pronounced [ˈʁaɪçsˌʁaːt]) was the bicameral legislature of the Austrian Empire from 1861, and after the Compromise of 1867 the parliament of Cisleithania — the Austrian half of Austria-Hungary — until the monarchy's collapse in 1918. It should not be confused with the Hungarian Diet at Budapest or with the later German Reichsrat of the Weimar Republic.

The Parliament Building on Vienna's Ringstrasse housed the Reichsrat from 1883.

Composition

The upper house, the House of Lords (Herrenhaus), consisted of princes of the imperial house, hereditary nobles, senior clergy, and life members appointed by the emperor. The lower house, the House of Deputies (Abgeordnetenhaus), was elected — initially by provincial diets and then directly — under a curial franchise weighted by wealth. Successive reforms widened the vote; the electoral reform of 1907 introduced universal, equal male suffrage for the House of Deputies, which thereafter had 516 members representing the empire's many nationalities.

Politics and nationalities

The Reichsrat became famous (and frequently paralyzed) as the arena of the Habsburg monarchy's nationality conflicts. Czech, Polish, German, Italian, Slovene, Croatian, Ukrainian, and other deputies formed shifting national blocs; obstruction, filibusters, and uproar — notoriously during the Badeni crisis of 1897 over language ordinances in Bohemia — repeatedly forced emperors to govern by emergency decree under Article 14 of the constitution. Minister-presidents of Cisleithania were responsible to the emperor rather than to parliament, limiting the council's control over governments even as its budgetary and legislative powers grew.

Final years

Suspended at the outbreak of the First World War, the Imperial Council was reconvened by Emperor Karl I in May 1917. In October–November 1918, as the empire dissolved, the German-speaking deputies constituted themselves as the Provisional National Assembly of German-Austria, and the Reichsrat's history ended with the proclamation of the Republic.
