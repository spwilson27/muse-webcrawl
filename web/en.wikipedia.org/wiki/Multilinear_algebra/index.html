Multilinear algebra

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Multilinear algebra is the branch of mathematics that extends the methods of linear algebra from single vectors to functions of several vector arguments that are linear in each argument separately. Its central objects are multilinear maps and the algebraic structures built from them: tensor products, tensor algebras, exterior algebras and symmetric algebras.

Key concepts

A bilinear map, such as an inner product or the multiplication in an algebra, is the simplest case of a multilinear map: a function of two arguments that is linear in each when the other is held fixed. The tensor product of vector spaces provides a universal construction that turns multilinear maps out of a product of spaces into ordinary linear maps out of a single new space. Building on it, the exterior algebra — whose multiplication, the wedge product, is alternating — underlies the theory of determinants and of differential forms, while the symmetric algebra governs polynomial functions on vector spaces.

Applications

Multilinear algebra supplies the language of tensors used throughout physics and engineering: the stress and strain tensors of continuum mechanics, the electromagnetic field tensor and metric tensor of relativity, and moments of inertia all depend on its formalism. In differential geometry, differential forms and their exterior calculus are direct applications of the exterior algebra. More recently, tensor decompositions have become tools in data analysis, signal processing and machine learning.

History

The subject grew out of 19th-century work on determinants, invariants and Grassmann's exterior algebra (1844), was developed in the absolute differential calculus of Ricci and Levi-Civita around 1900 for use in general relativity, and received its modern abstract formulation through the universal-property definition of the tensor product in the mid-20th century.
