Proxemics

Proxemics is the study of how human beings use space in communication — the distances people maintain between one another, territorial behaviour, and the organization of personal and public space. The term was coined by the anthropologist Edward T. Hall, who set out the framework in his 1966 book The Hidden Dimension.

Hall's distance zones

ZoneDistanceTypical use
Intimate0–45 cmEmbracing, whispering, close relationships
Personal45 cm–1.2 mConversations with friends and family
Social1.2–3.6 mImpersonal and business interaction
Publicover 3.6 mPublic speaking

Hall stressed that these distances are culturally variable: people from many Latin American, Middle Eastern and southern European cultures routinely interact at closer distances than northern Europeans or East Asians, and violations of expected distance can cause discomfort or offence.

Territoriality

Proxemics also covers territoriality — claims to space ranging from a desk or seat to national borders — and the distinction between fixed-feature space (rooms, buildings), semi-fixed-feature space (furniture arrangement) and informal space (the distance carried around the body).

Applications

Proxemic research informs architecture and office design, cross-cultural training, human–robot interaction and virtual-reality design, where avatar spacing reproduces real-world norms.
