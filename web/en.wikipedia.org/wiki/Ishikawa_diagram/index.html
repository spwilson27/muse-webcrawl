Ishikawa diagram

An Ishikawa diagram, also called a fishbone diagram or cause-and-effect diagram, is a visualisation tool for categorising the potential causes of a problem or effect in order to identify its root causes. It was developed by Kaoru Ishikawa, a Japanese quality control expert, who pioneered its use at Kawasaki shipyards in the 1960s. It is considered one of the basic tools of quality control.

Structure

The effect or problem is written at the "head" of the diagram on the right. A horizontal spine leads to it, from which major categories of causes branch off diagonally like the bones of a fish. In manufacturing the typical categories are the "6 Ms": Machine, Method, Material, Man (people), Measurement and Milieu (environment). Services often use the "4 Ss" (Suppliers, Systems, Surroundings, Skills) or "8 Ps". Each category is broken into sub-causes, which are generated by brainstorming and repeatedly asking "why?" — related to the five whys technique.

Uses

Ishikawa diagrams are used in quality management, product design and defect prevention to structure group discussion of the many factors that can contribute to a problem, moving analysis beyond symptoms to underlying causes. They are frequently combined with Pareto analysis and the five whys.
