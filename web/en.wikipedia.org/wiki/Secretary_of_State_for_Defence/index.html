The secretary of state for defence, also referred to as the defence secretary, is a secretary of state in the Government of the United Kingdom, with overall responsibility for the business of the Ministry of Defence (MoD). The post is a member of the Cabinet of the United Kingdom, sixth in the ministerial ranking.

The incumbent is a member of the Council of Defence and chairs the Defence Board. The role works closely with the Chief of the Defence Staff, the professional head of the British Armed Forces, and with the Permanent Under Secretary of the ministry.

History

The modern post was created on 1 April 1964, when the Admiralty, War Office and Air Ministry were merged into a single Ministry of Defence and the separate service ministries — First Lord of the Admiralty, Secretary of State for War and Secretary of State for Air — were abolished. Peter Thorneycroft was the first holder. The office inherited responsibilities for all three armed services: the Royal Navy, the British Army and the Royal Air Force.

Responsibilities

The defence secretary is accountable to Parliament for defence policy, the deployment and conduct of the armed forces, defence procurement and the defence budget. The postholder represents the United Kingdom at NATO defence ministers' meetings and supervises the United Kingdom's nuclear deterrent at the policy level.
