Rosalyn Sussman Yalow (July 19, 1921 – May 30, 2011) was an American medical physicist who co-developed the radioimmunoassay (RIA) technique, which made it possible to measure minute concentrations of hormones and other substances in the blood. She shared the 1977 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for the development of radioimmunoassays of peptide hormones — the second woman ever to win the Nobel Prize in that category.

Career

Yalow earned her PhD in nuclear physics from the University of Illinois in 1945, one of very few women in the field. At the Bronx Veterans Administration hospital she built a radioisotope laboratory with physician Solomon Berson; together they used radioiodine to study thyroid function and then insulin metabolism. Their finding that diabetics treated with animal insulin developed antibodies led them, by the late 1950s, to invert the observation into the RIA method: using antibody binding and radioactively labelled antigen to measure unlabelled substances at picogram levels.

Legacy

RIA revolutionized endocrinology and clinical diagnostics, enabling blood screening, fertility and drug testing. Yalow and Berson refused to patent the technique. After Berson's early death in 1972, the laboratory was renamed for him at her insistence. Yalow received the Albert Lasker Award (1976) and the National Medal of Science (1988), and was a forthright advocate for women in science.
