The beta-2 adrenergic receptor (β2 adrenoceptor), also known as ADRB2, is a cell membrane-spanning beta-adrenergic receptor that binds epinephrine (adrenaline), a hormone and neurotransmitter. It is a G protein-coupled receptor coupled primarily to Gs, though it can also signal through Gi and β-arrestin pathways.

Function

Beta-2 receptors are widely distributed, notably in bronchial and gastrointestinal smooth muscle, blood vessels supplying skeletal muscle, the uterus, and the liver. Their activation relaxes smooth muscle, producing bronchodilation and vasodilation, and stimulates glycogenolysis and potassium uptake into cells. In the lung they are the dominant adrenergic receptors of airway smooth muscle.

Clinical significance

Selective beta-2 agonists are mainstays of respiratory medicine: short-acting agents such as salbutamol (albuterol) relieve acute asthma symptoms, while long-acting agents such as salmeterol and formoterol are used in maintenance therapy for asthma and COPD. Beta-2 agonists are also used as tocolytics to delay preterm labour. The receptor's structure, determined by X-ray crystallography in 2007, was a landmark in GPCR structural biology and contributed to the 2012 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.
