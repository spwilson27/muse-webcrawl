Kisho Kurokawa (1934–2007) was a Japanese architect and one of the founders of the Metabolism movement in 1960, which proposed cities and buildings as evolving, biological systems whose parts could be replaced over time. He studied under Kenzo Tange at the University of Tokyo.

Work

His best-known building, the Nakagin Capsule Tower in Tokyo (1972), stacked 140 prefabricated capsules onto two concrete cores as replaceable living units; it was demolished in 2022 after a long preservation campaign, with some capsules saved for museums. Later works embraced his philosophy of "symbiosis", blending Japanese tradition with modern technology: the National Art Center in Tokyo (2007), the Van Gogh Museum wing in Amsterdam, Kuala Lumpur International Airport in Malaysia, and the master plan of Astana, Kazakhstan. Kurokawa also ran for Governor of Tokyo in 2007, months before his death from heart failure.
