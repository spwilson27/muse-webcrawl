East Coast Main Line

East Coast Main Line
Termini: London King's Cross – Edinburgh Waverley
Length: 393 miles (632 km)
Electrified: 1980s–1991

The East Coast Main Line (ECML) is a major railway link between London and Edinburgh via Peterborough, Doncaster, York, Darlington, Durham and Newcastle, with branches to Leeds, Hull and Glasgow. It is one of Britain's most important rail routes.

History

Formed from lines of the North Eastern, Great Northern and North British railways, the route was famed for expresses hauled by locomotives such as Flying Scotsman and the A4 Pacifics, including record-holder Mallard. Electrification at 25 kV AC was completed in 1991, enabling InterCity 225 services; Azuma trains now operate principal services.

Operations

Long-distance services are mainly operated by London North Eastern Railway (LNER), with open-access operators and freight also using the line. Capacity constraints, notably the two-track Welwyn viaduct section, are long-standing issues. See also West Coast Main Line.
