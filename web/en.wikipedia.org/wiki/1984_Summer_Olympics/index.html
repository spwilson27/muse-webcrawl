The 1984 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the XXIII Olympiad and commonly known as Los Angeles 1984, were an international multi-sport event held from July 28 to August 12, 1984, in Los Angeles, California, United States. There were 140 nations represented by 6,797 athletes, making them the largest Games up to that point in terms of participating teams.

Los Angeles was the only city to bid for the Games. The Games were boycotted by the Soviet Union and most of its allies in retaliation for the United States-led boycott of the 1980 Summer Olympics in Moscow. Despite the boycott, the Los Angeles Games were a financial success, becoming the first Olympics to turn a profit since 1932, largely because existing venues were used and the organizing committee, led by Peter Ueberroth, raised funds through corporate sponsorship and television rights.

Highlights

Carl Lewis of the United States won four gold medals in track and field, matching Jesse Owens's feat at the 1936 Berlin Games. Mary Lou Retton became the first American woman to win the Olympic all-around title in gymnastics. China participated in the Summer Olympics for the first time since 1952 and won 15 gold medals. Other notable performers included British middle-distance runners Sebastian Coe and Steve Ovett, American swimmer Michael Gross of West Germany won four medals, and Joan Benoit won the first women's Olympic marathon.

Medal table

The United States topped the medal table with 83 gold medals and 174 medals overall, followed by Romania and West Germany. The Games are often credited with reviving international interest in the Olympic movement and demonstrating that the event could be staged profitably.
