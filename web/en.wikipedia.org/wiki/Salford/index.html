Salford is a city in Greater Manchester, England, on the western bank of the River Irwell, directly across from Manchester, with which it forms the core of a single urban area. The name derives from the Old English sealhford, "ford by the willows". Historically part of Lancashire, Salford grew from a market town into one of the world's first great industrial cities during the Industrial Revolution, and is now also the administrative centre of the wider City of Salford metropolitan borough.

History

A royal manor under Edward the Confessor, Salford became a chartered market town in 1230. Its fortune rose with the textile industry: the opening of the Bridgewater Canal in 1761 and the Manchester Ship Canal in 1894 — whose terminal docks at Salford Quays made the city an inland port — turned it into a centre of cotton spinning, engineering, and shipping. Dense working-class districts around the docks and mills were studied by Friedrich Engels, who lived and worked in Manchester and described the "Little Ireland" slums of the Irwell in The Condition of the Working Class in England. The painter L. S. Lowry, born in nearby Stretford and a Salford rent collector for decades, made its streets and mill scenes world-famous. Industrial decline after the 1960s and the closure of the docks in 1982 devastated the local economy.

Regeneration

From the 1980s, Salford Quays was redeveloped from derelict dockland into a major commercial and cultural quarter, home to The Lowry arts centre, the Imperial War Museum North (across the canal in Trafford), and, from 2011, MediaCityUK, which hosts BBC North and ITV studios and anchors a large digital and creative industries cluster. The University of Salford, with roots in the Royal Technical Institute of 1896, has some 20,000 students including a campus presence at MediaCity. Long-running television soap Coronation Street was formerly filmed in Manchester and is now produced at MediaCityUK; its fictional setting, Weatherfield, was modelled partly on Salford.

Sport and culture

Salford is home to Salford Red Devils rugby league club, founded in 1873, and to Salford City F.C., the football club whose rise through the divisions under the ownership of former Manchester United players drew national attention. The Salford Museum and Art Gallery, opened in 1850, was among Britain's first free public libraries and museums. Ordsall Hall, a Tudor manor house, survives as a museum amid the city's later development.
