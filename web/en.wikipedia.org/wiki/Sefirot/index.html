The sefirot (Hebrew: סְפִירוֹת, singular sefirah) are the ten emanations or attributes in Kabbalah through which Ein Sof ("the Infinite") reveals itself and continuously creates both the physical realm and the chain of higher metaphysical realms (Seder hishtalshelut). They are usually depicted as the "Tree of Life," arranged in three columns and understood both as divine attributes and as channels of divine light or energy.

The ten sefirot

  • Keter — "Crown": divine will, the highest and most hidden sefirah.
  • Chokmah — "Wisdom": the initial flash of insight, masculine principle.
  • Binah — "Understanding": elaboration and development of the idea, feminine principle.
  • Chesed — "Kindness/Lovingkindness": unbounded giving and expansion.
  • Gevurah — "Strength/Severity": judgment, restraint and discipline.
  • Tiferet — "Beauty": harmony and balance between Chesed and Gevurah, compassion.
  • Netzach — "Victory/Eternity": endurance and active propagation.
  • Hod — "Splendour/Glory": humility, submission and receptivity.
  • Yesod — "Foundation": the channel gathering the higher forces.
  • Malkuth — "Kingship": the receiving vessel, identified with the Shekhinah and the material world.

A sometimes-counted intermediate sefirah, Da'at ("Knowledge"), occupies the place between Keter and the lower attributes in some schemes, so that the total remains ten.

Development

The term appears in the early mystical text Sefer Yetzirah ("Book of Creation"), where the ten sefirot are "ten and not nine, ten and not eleven" but are not yet the named attributes. The mature system appears in the Zohar (13th-century Spain) and was elaborated by Moses Cordovero and, most influentially, by Isaac Luria in the 16th century, whose doctrines of tzimtzum (divine contraction), the breaking of the vessels (shevirat ha-kelim), and cosmic repair (tikkun) made the sefirot the centre of a dramatic account of creation. Through Kabbalah's influence, the Tree of Life was also adopted by Western esoteric and Hermetic traditions.
