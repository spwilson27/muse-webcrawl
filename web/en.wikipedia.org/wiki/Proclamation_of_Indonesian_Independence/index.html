The Proclamation of Indonesian Independence (Indonesian: Proklamasi Kemerdekaan Indonesia, or simply Proklamasi) was read at 10:00 a.m. on Friday, 17 August 1945 in Jakarta, two days after the surrender of Japan in the Pacific, ending the Second World War and the Japanese occupation of the Dutch East Indies. The short declaration, signed by Sukarno and Mohammad Hatta on behalf of the Indonesian people, asserted the independence of what became the Republic of Indonesia.

Background

Japan had occupied the Dutch East Indies since 1942. As the war turned against Japan, its authorities promised independence to the Indonesians, and in March 1945 the Investigating Committee for Preparatory Work for Independence (BPUPK) was formed. Following the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and Japan's announcement of surrender on 15 August 1945, nationalist leaders moved quickly to declare independence before the Dutch could return.

The Rengasdengklok incident

On the morning of 16 August 1945, radical youth activists (pemuda) took Sukarno and Hatta to the town of Rengasdengklok, east of Jakarta, in an attempt to pressure them into proclaiming independence immediately and away from Japanese influence. The two leaders initially hesitated, wanting assurance of Japan's surrender, but eventually agreed. That night they returned to Jakarta and drafted the proclamation text at the home of Rear Admiral Tadashi Maeda.

The declaration

The final text was composed by Sukarno, Hatta and Achmad Soebardjo in the early hours of 17 August. Sukarno read it aloud from the porch of his house at Jalan Pegangsaan Timur No. 56 in central Jakarta, before a small gathering. The Indonesian flag, sewn by Sukarno's wife Fatmawati, was raised, and the day became Indonesia's Independence Day, celebrated annually on 17 August.

Text

The proclamation, in the original Indonesian, reads (in translation):

PROCLAMATION — We, the people of Indonesia, hereby declare the independence of Indonesia. Matters concerning the transfer of power and other matters will be executed in an orderly manner and in the shortest possible time. Jakarta, 17 August 1945. In the name of the people of Indonesia, Sukarno — Hatta.

Aftermath

The proclamation began the Indonesian National Revolution, a four-year diplomatic and armed struggle against the Netherlands, which sought to re-establish colonial rule. The Dutch finally transferred sovereignty to the Republic of the United States of Indonesia on 27 December 1949. The original proclamation manuscript is preserved in the National Archives of Indonesia.
