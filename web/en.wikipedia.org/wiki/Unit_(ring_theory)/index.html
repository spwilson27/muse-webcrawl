Unit (ring theory)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In algebra, a unit of a ring or algebra is any element that has a multiplicative inverse: an element u of a ring R is a unit if there exists v in R such that uv = vu = 1, where 1 is the multiplicative identity. The set of units of R forms a group under ring multiplication, called the group of units, the unit group, or the group of invertible elements of R, often denoted R× or R*.

Less commonly, the term unit is used for the multiplicative identity element itself, so that a "ring with unit" simply means a ring with a multiplicative identity (see also "unital"). Because of this ambiguity, some authors avoid the term and use "invertible element" instead.

Examples

  • In the ring of integers Z, the only units are 1 and −1.
  • In the ring Z/nZ of integers modulo n, the units are the residue classes coprime to n; they form the multiplicative group of integers modulo n.
  • In a field, every nonzero element is a unit, so the group of units is the multiplicative group of the field.
  • In the ring of Gaussian integers, the units are 1, −1, i, and −i.
  • A polynomial ring element is a unit only if it is a nonzero constant that is a unit of the coefficient ring.
  • In the ring of n × n matrices over a field, the units are the invertible matrices, forming the general linear group GL(n).

Properties

If u and v are units, so are uv and u−1; hence the units form a group. The identity element 1 is always a unit, and 0 is a unit only in the zero ring. In a commutative ring, an element that is not a unit and not zero is called a non-unit; the set of non-units forms an ideal exactly when the ring is a local ring, in which case that ideal is the unique maximal ideal.

Dirichlet's unit theorem describes the structure of the unit group of the ring of integers of a number field: it is finitely generated, with rank determined by the numbers of real and complex embeddings of the field. Units play a central role in algebraic number theory, K-theory, and the study of factorization, since two elements differing by a unit factor are called associates.
