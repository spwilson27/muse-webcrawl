Padauk

Padauk is the name given to the timber of several species of the genus Pterocarpus, leguminous trees native to tropical Africa and Asia. The best-known kinds are Burmese padauk (Pterocarpus macrocarpus), Andaman padauk (P. dalbergioides) and African padauk (P. soyauxii).

Properties

Freshly cut padauk heartwood is a vivid orange-red to crimson that darkens with exposure to light and air to a rich purplish-brown. The wood is hard, heavy, durable and resistant to termites, with a generally straight to interlocked grain that finishes well.

Uses

Padauk is valued for fine furniture, cabinetry, flooring, boatbuilding, turnery and musical instruments, and in Asia for carved work and construction. In Myanmar the padauk flower (Pterocarpus indicus in local usage) is a national symbol associated with the Thingyan New Year water festival.

Conservation

Heavy demand, particularly for rosewood-like luxury furniture in China, has driven over-exploitation of several Pterocarpus species, and the genus has been brought under CITES controls to regulate the international trade.
