Dual space

A vector and a linear functional (covector) acting on it

In mathematics, any vector space V has a corresponding dual vector space (or simply dual space) consisting of all linear forms on V, together with the vector space structure of pointwise addition and scalar multiplication. A linear form is a linear map from V to its field of scalars. The dual space is denoted V* or V′.

Algebraic dual space

If V is finite-dimensional, then V* has the same dimension as V. Given a basis of V, one can construct a specific corresponding dual basis of V* defined by the Kronecker delta relations. There is a canonical linear map from V into its double dual V** (evaluation at a point), which is an isomorphism in finite dimensions; hence finite-dimensional spaces are often identified with their double duals. In infinite dimensions, the algebraic dual is strictly larger than the original space, and the evaluation map is injective but not surjective.

Continuous dual space

For topological vector spaces, one usually restricts attention to the continuous dual space, consisting only of continuous linear functionals. This is the setting of functional analysis: the dual of a Banach space is again a Banach space under the operator norm. Important examples include the identification of the dual of the sequence space ℓp with ℓq (for conjugate exponents p and q), and the Riesz representation theorem, which identifies the dual of a Hilbert space with the space itself.

Applications

Dual spaces are fundamental in differential geometry, where the dual of the tangent space at a point of a manifold is the cotangent space, whose elements are covectors and differential 1-forms; in physics, where bras in Dirac's bra–ket notation are elements of a dual space; and in optimisation and economics, where dual variables encode shadow prices in linear programming duality.
