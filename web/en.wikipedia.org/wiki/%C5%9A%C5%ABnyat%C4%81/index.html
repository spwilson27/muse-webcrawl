Śūnyatā

Śūnyatā (Sanskrit: शून्यता; often translated "emptiness") is a key concept in Buddhism, especially in the Mahāyāna tradition, holding that all phenomena are empty of inherent, independent existence (svabhāva), because everything arises dependently on causes and conditions.

Development

The idea grows out of the early Buddhist doctrines of impermanence, no-self (anattā) and dependent origination (pratītyasamutpāda). Around the 2nd century CE, the philosopher Nāgārjuna systematised emptiness in his Mūlamadhyamakakārikā, equating emptiness with dependent origination itself and warning that emptiness, too, is empty — it is not a void or a thing.

Interpretation

Śūnyatā does not mean that things do not exist at all, but that they exist only relationally and conventionally. Realising emptiness is held to dissolve attachment and conceptual fixation, and is paired in Mahāyāna practice with compassion. The concept also appears in Hindu philosophy and in Jain thought in differing senses.
