The 31st G8 summit was held at the Gleneagles Hotel in Perthshire, Scotland, from 6 to 8 July 2005, chaired by British prime minister Tony Blair. The summit focused on aid and debt relief for Africa and on climate change, in line with the aims of the Make Poverty History campaign and the Commission for Africa.

Events

The summit coincided with the Live 8 concerts, including a finale in Edinburgh, which pressed leaders for action on poverty. On 7 July, the second day of the meeting, a series of coordinated bomb attacks struck London's transport network, killing 52 people; Blair briefly left the summit for London before returning, and the leaders issued a joint statement condemning the attacks.

Outcomes

Leaders committed to doubling aid to Africa by 2010 (an increase of about $25 billion per year), cancelling debt for the most indebted poor countries, and providing universal access to HIV/AIDS treatment by 2010. The climate communiqué acknowledged the need for action but produced no binding emissions targets, reflecting US opposition. Large protests, coordinated under the "Dissent!" network and a Make Poverty History march in Edinburgh attended by around 225,000 people, surrounded the event.
