Formative assessment

Formative assessment, or formative evaluation, is a range of formal and informal assessment procedures used by teachers during the learning process to monitor student progress and modify teaching activities accordingly. Unlike summative assessment, which evaluates learning at the end of a unit, formative assessment is assessment for learning rather than of learning.

Practices

Examples include questioning and classroom discussion, quizzes with immediate feedback, exit tickets, peer and self-assessment, and comments on drafts without grades. The approach was influentially reviewed by Paul Black and Dylan Wiliam in their 1998 paper "Inside the Black Box", which found substantial evidence that formative practices raise standards of attainment.

Feedback

Effective formative assessment depends on feedback that tells learners what they have achieved, what the goal is, and what to do next to close the gap. It also informs teachers about misconceptions so instruction can be adjusted in real time.
