A barangay (pronounced [baɾaŋˈɡaj]), historically known as a barrio, is the smallest administrative division in the Philippines and is the native Filipino term for a village or district. Cities and municipalities are subdivided into barangays, each an independent local government unit with elected officials, its own budget share and a community dispute-resolution system. As of 2024 there are 41,933 barangays in the country.

History

The word derives from balangay, the plank boat used by Austronesian peoples of the archipelago; before Spanish colonisation, barangays were kinship-based settlements of 30 to 100 households led by a datu. Under Spanish rule they were reorganised as barrios under a cabeza de barangay. The modern barangay was revived under President Ferdinand Marcos in the 1970s and entrenched by the Local Government Code of 1991 (Republic Act No. 7160), which defines its powers and institutions.

Government

Each barangay is governed by a Punong Barangay (barangay captain) and seven councilors (kagawad) forming the Sangguniang Barangay, together with a youth council (Sangguniang Kabataan) and appointed secretary and treasurer. Barangays deliver basic services, maintain order through tanod watchmen, and administer the Katarungang Pambarangay system of mediation for neighbourhood disputes. They are funded by a share of national tax revenues (the National Tax Allotment) and local fees.
