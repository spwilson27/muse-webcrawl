Diocese of St Davids

The Diocese of St Davids is a diocese of the Church in Wales, a province of the Anglican Communion, covering the historic counties of Carmarthenshire, Cardiganshire and Pembrokeshire in south-west Wales, together with parts of neighbouring counties. Its cathedral is St Davids Cathedral in the city of St Davids, Pembrokeshire, traditionally founded by Saint David, the patron saint of Wales, in the 6th century.

History

The see traces its origins to the monastery founded by Saint David (Dewi) at Menevia, later St Davids. In the medieval period the bishops of St Davids at times claimed metropolitan status over Wales, in rivalry with Canterbury. The diocese survived the Reformation as part of the Church of England, becoming part of the disestablished Church in Wales in 1920. In 1923 the eastern part of the diocese was divided to form the new Diocese of Swansea and Brecon.

Organisation

The diocese is led by the Bishop of St Davids and is divided into archdeaconries, traditionally St Davids, Carmarthen and Cardigan, and in more recent structures also Meidrim. It contains several hundred parishes and church buildings, many of them of great antiquity along the pilgrimage routes to St Davids, which in the Middle Ages was said to be worth two pilgrimages to Rome.
