Aragorn II, son of Arathorn II, is a fictional character in J. R. R. Tolkien's legendarium. He appears in The Lord of the Rings as one of the nine members of the Fellowship of the Ring. A Ranger of the North, he is the last Chieftain of the Dúnedain and the heir of Isildur to the thrones of Gondor and Arnor. After the War of the Ring he is crowned King Elessar of the Reunited Kingdom.

Conception and creation

Aragorn evolved considerably during Tolkien's writing. In early drafts the character who met the hobbits at Bree was a hobbit named Trotter; the figure became a Man only in later revisions, and his identity as Isildur's heir and his romance with Arwen (told in the Appendix "The Tale of Aragorn and Arwen") were developed late in the composition of the novel.

Fictional biography

Aragorn was born in T.A. 2931. After his father was killed by orcs when Aragorn was two, he was fostered in Rivendell by Elrond, who concealed his identity and gave him the name Estel ("hope"). At twenty he learned his true lineage and received the shards of Narsil and the Ring of Barahir. He fell in love with Elrond's daughter Arwen, whom he later met again in Lothlórien, where they plighted their troth.

For decades Aragorn travelled widely: he served Thengel of Rohan and Ecthelion II of Gondor under the name Thorongil, led a raid that burned the Corsair fleet at Umbar, journeyed in Rhûn and Harad, and guarded the Shire's borders as a Ranger. Known in Bree as Strider, he guided Frodo Baggins and his companions from Bree to Rivendell, then led the Fellowship after Gandalf's fall in Moria. After the breaking of the Fellowship he pursued the Orcs who had captured Merry and Pippin, fought at Helm's Deep, took the Paths of the Dead, and led the Army of the Dead to Pelargir. At the Pelennor Fields he arrived with reinforcements by ship, and before the Black Gate he led the Army of the West in a diversion to draw Sauron's attention from Frodo.

Crowned King of Gondor and Arnor as Elessar Telcontar, he married Arwen and reigned 120 years, dying in F.A. 120 at the age of 210, one of the longest-lived Men of his line since the early Kings.

Analysis

Scholars have noted Aragorn's combination of the hidden king of folklore, the healing king ("the hands of the king are the hands of a healer"), and the Beowulf-like hero; his long exile and late coronation have also been read against Tolkien's Catholic themes of providence and humility.

Portrayals

Aragorn was voiced by John Hurt in Ralph Bakshi's 1978 animated film and played by Viggo Mortensen in Peter Jackson's film trilogy (2001–2003), a performance widely praised by critics and audiences.
