Next Generation Air Transportation System

The Next Generation Air Transportation System (NextGen) is an ongoing modernization programme of the United States National Airspace System, led by the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA). Initiated in the early 2000s, it shifts air traffic management from ground-based radar and voice communications toward satellite-based navigation, surveillance and digital data communications.

Key technologies

Core components include Automatic Dependent Surveillance–Broadcast (ADS-B), which became mandatory for most U.S. airspace in 2020 and gives controllers and pilots precise GPS-based position information; Performance Based Navigation (PBN) procedures that let aircraft fly more direct, fuel-efficient routes; Data Communications (Data Comm) to supplement voice instructions; and System Wide Information Management (SWIM) for sharing operational data. Terminal automation programmes such as TBFM and TFDM aim to manage traffic flows and airport surface movements more efficiently.

Goals and status

NextGen is intended to increase capacity, safety and predictability while reducing fuel burn, emissions and delays as traffic grows. Implementation proceeds in stages through the 2020s and beyond, coordinated in part with Europe's parallel SESAR programme. Oversight reports have periodically criticised cost growth and shifting timelines, while the FAA reports measurable benefits in equipped airspace.
