White rice

White rice is milled rice that has had its husk, bran and germ removed. This polishing alters the flavour and texture of the grain and greatly extends its storage life, which is why white rice became the dominant form of rice consumed worldwide.

Processing and nutrition

After harvest, rice grains (paddy) are husked to give brown rice. Milling and polishing then strip away the bran and germ, leaving the white starchy endosperm. The removed layers contain most of the grain's fibre, B vitamins and oils, so white rice is nutritionally poorer than brown rice. Historic dependence on polished rice caused epidemics of beriberi, a thiamine-deficiency disease, in 19th- and early-20th-century Asia. Many countries now require white rice to be enriched with iron and B vitamins, and parboiling — soaking and steaming paddy before milling — drives some nutrients into the grain before the bran is removed.

Varieties and cooking

White rice is sold in long-, medium- and short-grain forms, including aromatic varieties such as jasmine and basmati, and glutinous (sticky) rice whose polished grains are nearly pure amylopectin. It is typically cooked by absorption in about one and a half parts water, or in electric rice cookers, and forms the staple base of countless dishes, from pilafs and risottos to sushi and rice puddings.
