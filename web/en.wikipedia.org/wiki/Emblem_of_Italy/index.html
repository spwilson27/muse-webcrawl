Emblem of Italy

Emblem of the Italian Republic
Adopted: 5 May 1948
Designer: Paolo Paschetto
Elements: Stella d'Italia, cogwheel, oak and olive branches

The emblem of the Italian Republic is the national symbol of Italy, adopted on 5 May 1948. Although it is often loosely called a coat of arms, it was deliberately designed as an emblem rather than a heraldic shield, reflecting the republic founded after the 1946 referendum that abolished the monarchy.

Design

At its centre, a white five-pointed star — the Stella d'Italia, an ancient symbol of the country — is superimposed on a steel cogwheel, representing labour and the constitutional statement that Italy is a democratic republic founded on work. The composition is framed by an oak branch, symbolising strength and dignity, and an olive branch, symbolising peace. A red ribbon below bears the inscription "REPVBBLICA ITALIANA".

Creation

The design by artist Paolo Paschetto won a public competition held in two rounds after an initial contest in 1946 failed to produce an accepted entry. The emblem appears on official documents, public buildings, and the standard of the President of Italy alongside the tricolour.
