Kerma (physics)

Kerma, an acronym for "kinetic energy released per unit mass", is a quantity in radiation physics defined as the sum of the initial kinetic energies of all charged particles liberated by uncharged ionizing radiation (such as photons and neutrons) in a sample of matter, divided by the mass of the sample. It is measured in grays (joules per kilogram).

Definition

Kerma K is defined as K = dEtr/dm, where dEtr is the sum of the initial kinetic energies of all charged particles liberated by indirectly ionizing particles in a mass dm of material. It includes energy later radiated away as bremsstrahlung, which distinguishes it from absorbed dose.

Relation to absorbed dose

Under conditions of charged particle equilibrium, absorbed dose is approximately equal to the collision component of kerma. Air kerma is the standard quantity used to calibrate radiation instruments in photon fields, having replaced exposure in the SI system.
