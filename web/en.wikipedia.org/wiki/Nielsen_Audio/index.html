Nielsen Audio, formerly known as Arbitron, is the audience measurement division of Nielsen that measures radio listening in the United States. Its ratings are the standard currency used by broadcasters, advertisers and agencies to buy and sell radio advertising.

Methodology

In the largest markets Nielsen Audio uses the Portable People Meter (PPM), a pager-like device worn by panellists that detects inaudible codes embedded in audio broadcasts. In smaller markets it relies on diary surveys in which respondents record their listening. Results are published as "The Ratings" for hundreds of local markets several times a year.

History

Founded in 1949 as the American Research Bureau, the company became Arbitron and focused on radio after divesting its television business. Nielsen acquired Arbitron in 2013 for about .26 billion and renamed it Nielsen Audio.
