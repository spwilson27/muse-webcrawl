Leto II Atreides is a fictional character in Frank Herbert's Dune universe. The son of Paul Atreides (Muad'Dib) and the Fremen concubine Chani, and twin brother of Ghanima, Leto is the central figure of Children of Dune (1976) and God Emperor of Dune (1981). He rules the known universe as a human–sandworm hybrid for three and a half thousand years.

Fictional biography

Born "pre-born" — awakened to consciousness in the womb and possessed of the memories of all his ancestors — Leto escapes the fate of his aunt Alia by embracing a transformation his father refused. In Children of Dune he allows sandtrout to encase his body, beginning his metamorphosis into a giant sandworm with a human face and arms. The fusion grants him near-immortality, superhuman strength, and prescient vision.

As God Emperor, Leto enforces his "Golden Path," a millennia-long programme of enforced peace and stagnation designed to teach humanity a lesson it will never forget and to ensure that mankind, once scattered beyond any single ruler's reach, can never again be threatened with extinction by a single power or prescient tyrant. He breeds generations of loyal Duncan Idaho gholas as his companions and commanders, and maintains an army of female Fish Speaker warriors.

After 3,500 years, Leto permits his assassination by Siona Atreides and the ghola Duncan Idaho, plunging from a bridge into a river — water being lethal to his worm form. His body breaks apart into sandtrout, beginning a new cycle of spice production on Arrakis (by then called Rakis), and his "pearl of awareness" persists within every future sandworm.

Portrayals

Leto was played by young actor Alec Newman as an adult in the 2003 miniseries Frank Herbert's Children of Dune (with James McAvoy playing the older Leto).
