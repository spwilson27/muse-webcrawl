Louis Napoléon Bonaparte (born Luigi Buonaparte; 2 September 1778 – 25 July 1846) was a brother of Napoleon I of France and, as Louis I, King of Holland from 1806 to 1810. He was the father of Napoleon III, the last monarch of France, and is remembered in the Netherlands as "Louis the Good" for his attempts to govern in the interest of his Dutch subjects rather than as his brother's prefect.

Early life and marriage

Born in Ajaccio, Corsica, Louis followed Napoleon to France and served as his aide-de-camp in Italy and Egypt, rising to general. In 1802 Napoleon arranged his marriage to Hortense de Beauharnais, daughter of the Empress Joséphine; the marriage was unhappy, though it produced three sons, the youngest of whom, Charles-Louis Napoléon, became President of the French Second Republic and then Emperor Napoleon III.

King of Holland

In 1806 Napoleon converted the Batavian Republic into the Kingdom of Holland and placed Louis on its throne. To Napoleon's frustration, Louis took his role seriously: he learned Dutch, adopted the Dutch form of his name (Lodewijk), worked to relieve the victims of the Leiden gunpowder disaster of 1807 and the floods of 1809 — earning his enduring Dutch nickname "Lodewijk de Goede" — founded institutions that became the Royal Netherlands Academy and the Rijksmuseum, and resisted enforcing the Continental System and conscription as strictly as Paris demanded.

The conflict with his brother came to a head after British forces landed at Walcheren in 1809 and Napoleon accused Louis of failing to defend the kingdom. French troops occupied Amsterdam in 1810; Louis abdicated on 1 July 1810 in favour of his young son (who reigned briefly as Louis II) and fled to Austria, whereupon Napoleon annexed the Netherlands outright to France.

Later life

Louis lived in exile, chiefly in Graz, Rome and Florence, under the title Count of Saint-Leu, writing novels, poetry and an account of his Dutch reign (Documents historiques et réflexions sur le gouvernement de la Hollande, 1820). After the death of his elder brother Joseph in 1844 he became, in Bonapartist eyes, the senior member of the family, though he refused political ambitions for himself. He died at Livorno in 1846 and was buried at Saint-Leu-la-Forêt near Paris.
