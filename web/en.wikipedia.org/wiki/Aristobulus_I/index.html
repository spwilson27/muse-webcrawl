Aristobulus I (Greek: Ἀριστόβουλος) was the first Hasmonean ruler to take the title of king, reigning over Judea in 104–103 BC. The eldest son of John Hyrcanus, he succeeded his father as high priest and ethnarch and, according to Josephus and Strabo, was the first Jewish ruler since the Babylonian exile to crown himself king.

Reign

On coming to power, Aristobulus imprisoned his mother — whom Hyrcanus had designated as his successor in government — and three of his brothers, keeping only Antigonus, his favorite, at liberty. His mother died of starvation in prison, and Antigonus was later murdered by the royal guard after courtiers aroused the king's suspicion against him; Josephus tells the story as a tragedy of intrigue that filled the remorseful king with grief.

Campaigns

Aristobulus campaigned in the north, conquering part of the Ituraean territory in the Galilee and Lebanon region and compelling its inhabitants, according to Josephus, to accept circumcision and Jewish law. He was also credited with further securing Jewish control of Galilee, paving the way for its Judaization.

Death and succession

Aristobulus died after a painful illness after a reign of only one year. His widow, Salome Alexandra, released his imprisoned brothers and married Alexander Jannaeus, who succeeded to the throne. Aristobulus was remembered as a lover of Greek culture, earning the epithet Philhellene ("friend of the Greeks").
