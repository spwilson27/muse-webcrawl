TypePublic inquiry
Set up byGovernment or head of state

A commission of inquiry is a body appointed by a government or other authority to investigate a matter of public concern and to report findings and recommendations. Such commissions are common in Commonwealth countries, where they may be styled royal commissions, and in several other jurisdictions.

Powers and procedure

Commissions are typically chaired by a judge or senior lawyer and are given defined terms of reference. Statute usually empowers them to compel witnesses, obtain documents, and hold public hearings, though their findings are generally recommendations rather than binding judgments.

Examples

Well-known inquiries include the Warren Commission into the assassination of President John F. Kennedy in the United States, the 9/11 Commission, the United Kingdom's Chilcot Inquiry into the Iraq War, and numerous royal commissions in Australia and Canada on matters from child abuse to banking misconduct.
