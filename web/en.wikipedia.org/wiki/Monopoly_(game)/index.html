Monopoly (game)

DesignerLizzie Magie (The Landlord's Game); Charles Darrow
PublisherParker Brothers; Hasbro
Publication1935 (mass-market edition)
Players2–8
GenreBoard game; economic strategy

Monopoly is a multiplayer economics-themed board game. In the game, players roll two dice to move around the game board, buying and trading properties and developing them with houses and hotels. Players collect rent from their opponents and aim to drive them into bankruptcy. Money can also be gained or lost through Chance and Community Chest cards and tax squares. Players receive a salary every time they pass "Go" and can end up in jail, from which they cannot move until they have met one of three conditions. The game has numerous house rules and hundreds of editions exist as well as many spin-offs and related media. Monopoly has become a part of international popular culture, having been licensed in more than 103 countries and printed in more than 47 languages.

History

The game's origins lie in The Landlord's Game, created by Lizzie Magie and patented in 1904, which was designed to demonstrate the economic ill effects of land monopolism and the Georgist remedy of a single tax on land. Folk variants circulated in the United States for decades, using Atlantic City street names in the version developed around 1930. Charles Darrow sold the game to Parker Brothers in 1935; Parker Brothers bought Magie's patent for $500 and marketed Darrow as the game's sole inventor, a version of events only corrected publicly decades later. Waddingtons produced the London edition in Britain, replacing Atlantic City locations with London streets.

Gameplay

Players move tokens around a square board of 40 spaces: 28 properties (22 streets in colour groups, 4 railroads/railways, and 2 utilities), 3 Chance spaces, 3 Community Chest spaces, 2 tax spaces, and the corners GO, Jail, Free Parking, and Go to Jail. Owning all properties of a colour group — a "monopoly" — allows a player to build houses and hotels that sharply increase rents. Negotiation and trading between players are central to the game, which ends when all but one player are bankrupt.

Legacy

Monopoly is one of the best-selling board games of all time, with an estimated 275 million sets sold. World championships have been held since 1973. The game has been adapted into video games, a live game show, a film in development, and countless localised and themed editions, and its mascot, Rich Uncle Pennybags ("Mr. Monopoly"), is a widely recognised figure.
