2017 Turkish constitutional referendum

A constitutional referendum was held in Turkey on 16 April 2017 on a set of 18 proposed amendments to the Constitution. Voters narrowly approved the package, with 51.4 percent voting "Yes".

The amendments

The changes replaced Turkey's parliamentary system with an executive presidency: the office of prime minister was abolished, the president became both head of state and head of government with power to appoint ministers and issue decrees, the number of MPs rose from 550 to 600, and the voting age for candidacy fell to 18. The amendments largely took effect following the June 2018 general elections, after which Recep Tayyip Erdoğan began a term under the new system.

Campaign and controversy

The referendum was held under the state of emergency declared after the failed 2016 coup attempt. The campaign was criticised by the OSCE and the Council of Europe for an uneven playing field, and controversy erupted over the electoral board's decision, on voting day, to accept ballots lacking official stamps. The opposition parties contested the result, but appeals were rejected.
