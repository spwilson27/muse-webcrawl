Sunan Ampel (1401–1481), born Raden Ahmad Rahmatullah, was one of the Wali Sanga, the nine revered saints credited with spreading Islam in Java, Indonesia. He is regarded as the senior figure among the Wali Sanga and as a founding figure of Islamic education and society on the north coast of East Java.

Life

According to Javanese tradition (babad chronicles), Raden Rahmat was the son of a Champa princess and a descendant of missionaries from Central Asia or Arabia. He came to Java in the 15th century and was granted land at Ampel Denta, near present-day Surabaya, by the Majapahit court. There he established a pesantren (Islamic boarding school) — among the first in Java — which became a major centre of Islamic learning. His students and kin included several other Wali Sanga; his sons Sunan Bonang and Sunan Drajat became saints in their own right, and his nephew Sunan Giri was another prominent missionary. Tradition credits him with coining the Javanese Muslim teaching of Moh Limo ("the five refusals"): refusing gambling, intoxicants, theft, illicit sex, and opium.

Legacy

Sunan Ampel is buried beside the Ampel Mosque (Masjid Ampel) in Surabaya, which he is said to have founded in 1421; the mosque and tomb complex remain a major pilgrimage (ziarah) destination. The State Islamic University of Sunan Ampel (UIN Sunan Ampel Surabaya) is named after him.
