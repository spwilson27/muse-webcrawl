International federation

An international federation (IF) is an international non-governmental organization that administers one or more sports at the world level. International federations typically consist of national federations, which administer the sport in their respective countries, and they establish the rules of competition, oversee international events and promote the sport globally.

Role

International federations organize or sanction world championships, maintain world rankings and records, train and appoint international officials, and represent their sport within the Olympic movement. For Olympic sports, the federation works with the International Olympic Committee (IOC), which recognizes one federation per sport.

Recognition

Many federations are members of umbrella bodies such as the Global Association of International Sports Federations (GAISF, formerly SportAccord) and the Association of IOC Recognised International Sports Federations (ARISF) or the Association of Summer Olympic International Federations (ASOIF) and its winter counterpart. Prominent examples include FIFA for association football, World Athletics, and the Fédération Internationale de Natation (World Aquatics).

Governance

Federations are generally governed by a congress of member associations, an executive board or council, and a president, and are usually headquartered in Switzerland, benefiting from its favourable legal framework for international associations.
