Lorenzo Valla (c. 1407 – 1 August 1457) was an Italian humanist scholar, philologist, and critic. A master of Latin eloquence, he is best remembered for demonstrating in 1440 that the Donation of Constantine — the document by which the Emperor Constantine supposedly granted the Papacy temporal rule over the West — was a medieval forgery, a founding achievement of modern textual and historical criticism.

Life

Valla was born in Rome and educated there and in Piacenza. Denied a university post at Rome, he taught rhetoric at Pavia, where his attacks on the jurist Bartolus and scholastic Latin made him enemies. He travelled between Italian courts before entering the service of Alfonso V of Aragon, king of Naples, whose rivalry with Pope Eugenius IV formed the political background to the Donation treatise. After the reconciliation of Alfonso and the Papacy, Valla paradoxically obtained a post as apostolic secretary in Rome under Nicholas V, where he translated Greek historians and worked until his death in 1457.

Works

On the Donation of Constantine

In De falso credita et ementita Constantini Donatione (1440), Valla showed from the document's anachronistic Latin — words such as "satrap", references to Constantinople before its founding, and feudal vocabulary impossible in the 4th century — that the Donation could not be genuine. The treatise, later printed by Ulrich von Hutten in 1517, became a weapon of Reformation polemic.

Elegantiae and philology

His Elegantiae linguae Latinae (printed 1471) was a hugely influential manual of classical Latin usage, designed to purge medieval corruptions from the language; it went through scores of editions and shaped Renaissance Latinity. His Annotations on the New Testament compared Jerome's Vulgate with the Greek text, anticipating Erasmus's biblical scholarship.

Philosophy

In De voluptate (On Pleasure) Valla staged a dialogue defending a Christianised Epicurean view of pleasure against Stoic and scholastic positions, and in his Dialectical Disputations he attacked Aristotelian logic and metaphysics in favour of a rhetoric- and language-based philosophy.

Legacy

Valla's philological method — judging documents by the historical development of language — made him a forerunner of modern historical criticism, and his combative humanism set a pattern for Renaissance scholarship.
