Atchafalaya River

The Atchafalaya River is a 137-mile-long (220 km) distributary of the Mississippi River and Red River in south central Louisiana in the United States. It flows southward through the Atchafalaya Basin — the largest wetland and river swamp in the United States — to the Gulf of Mexico at Atchafalaya Bay near Morgan City. The name derives from the Choctaw hacha falaia, "long river".

Course and the Old River Control Structure

Beginning in the 15th century, the Mississippi progressively began shifting its course into the shorter, steeper Atchafalaya channel — a process that would eventually have captured the river entirely, leaving Baton Rouge and New Orleans on a side channel. To prevent this, the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers completed the Old River Control Structure in 1963, regulating the flow so that about 30% of the combined Mississippi–Red flow passes into the Atchafalaya. The structure was nearly undermined in the flood of 1973, prompting the addition of an auxiliary structure in 1990. Part of the flow is also diverted for hydroelectric power at the Sidney A. Murray Jr. Hydroelectric Station.

The basin

The Atchafalaya Basin Floodway serves as a major flood-relief outlet for the lower Mississippi, including the Morganza Spillway, opened during the floods of 1973 and 2011. The basin's swamps support rich wildlife and a major crawfish industry, and — unusually for a delta — land in the Atchafalaya delta is building, as river sediment accumulates in the bay.
