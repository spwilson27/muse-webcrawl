Early Cretaceous

Chronology145 to 100.5 Ma
Preceded byLate Jurassic
Followed byLate Cretaceous
SubdivisionsBerriasian, Valanginian, Hauterivian, Barremian, Aptian, Albian

The Early Cretaceous (geochronological name) or the Lower Cretaceous (chronostratigraphic name) is the earlier or lower of the two major divisions of the Cretaceous. It is usually considered to stretch from 145 Ma to 100.5 Ma.

Geology

During the Early Cretaceous, the supercontinent Pangaea continued to break apart. The South Atlantic Ocean began to open as South America separated from Africa, and India detached from Antarctica and Australia and began moving northward. Sea levels were generally lower than in the Late Cretaceous but rose through the epoch, flooding continental interiors.

Proposals for the exact boundary between the Jurassic and Cretaceous have proven difficult, as the transition is marked by relatively little faunal change in many sections; the base of the Berriasian Stage, at about 145 million years ago, conventionally opens the Cretaceous.

Climate

The Early Cretaceous was a predominantly warm greenhouse interval with no permanent polar ice caps, although a possible brief glacial episode has been proposed for the Valanginian. Cooling episodes punctuated an otherwise warm climate, and oceanic anoxic events — notably the Aptian OAE 1a (the Selli Event) — record major perturbations of the global carbon cycle.

Life

Flowering plants (angiosperms) first appeared and began their rapid diversification during the Early Cretaceous, transforming terrestrial ecosystems. Dinosaurs dominated large terrestrial herbivore and carnivore niches: sauropods remained abundant in many regions, while iguanodontian ornithopods diversified widely. Feathered dinosaurs and early birds are richly documented in the Jehol Biota of northeastern China. In the seas, ichthyosaurs declined toward their extinction near the Early to Late Cretaceous boundary, while ammonites and rudist bivalves flourished.
