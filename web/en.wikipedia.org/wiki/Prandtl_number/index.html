Prandtl number

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Prandtl number (Pr) or Prandtl group is a dimensionless number, named after the German physicist Ludwig Prandtl, defined as the ratio of momentum diffusivity to thermal diffusivity. The Prandtl number is given as:

Pr = ν / α = (μ / ρ) / (k / (ρ cp)) = cp μ / k

where ν is the momentum diffusivity (kinematic viscosity), α is the thermal diffusivity, μ is the dynamic viscosity, k is the thermal conductivity, cp is the specific heat at constant pressure, and ρ is the density. Note that whereas the Reynolds number and Grashof number are subscripted with a scale variable, the Prandtl number contains no such length scale and depends only on the fluid and the fluid state.

Interpretation

The Prandtl number expresses the relative effectiveness of momentum and energy transport by diffusion in the velocity and thermal boundary layers. For small Pr, thermal diffusion outpaces momentum diffusion, so the thermal boundary layer is much thicker than the velocity boundary layer; the opposite is true for large Pr. Heat conduction is more efficient for liquid metals (Pr ≪ 1) whereas for oils (Pr ≫ 1) convection is more efficient for momentum transfer relative to heat conduction.

Applications

The Prandtl number appears in the analysis of forced and natural convection, in correlations for the Nusselt number, and in the analogy between heat and momentum transfer (the Reynolds–Colburn analogy). In turbulent flow, the turbulent Prandtl number is used with eddy diffusivities.
