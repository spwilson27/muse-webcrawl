The Joint Security Area (JSA), also known as the Truce Village or Panmunjom, is the only portion of the Korean Demilitarized Zone (DMZ) where North and South Korean forces stand face-to-face, and the site used by the two Koreas for diplomatic engagements. It lies about 800 metres south of the original village of Panmunjom and roughly 50 kilometres northwest of Seoul. Its row of blue negotiation huts, straddling the Military Demarcation Line, is one of the most recognisable symbols of the divided Korean Peninsula.

History

The JSA originated in the armistice negotiations of 1951–53 and was formalised by the Korean Armistice Agreement of 27 July 1953. For its first decades the area was a neutral zone in which both sides moved freely; this ended after the 1976 "axe murder incident", in which North Korean soldiers killed two United States officers leading a tree-trimming party. The JSA was then divided along the demarcation line, with separate security arrangements on each side.

The compound includes the blue Military Armistice Commission conference buildings (T1–T3) through which the demarcation line runs — visitors inside can step across the line into the other Korea — the North's Panmungak pavilion and the South's Freedom House and House of Peace. The nearby "Bridge of No Return" was used for prisoner exchanges at the end of the Korean War.

Recent history

The JSA hosted the historic meetings of 2018: on 27 April 2018 South Korean president Moon Jae-in and North Korean leader Kim Jong-un met at the line, Kim stepping briefly into the South and Moon into the North, before their summit at the House of Peace. Donald Trump met Kim there again on 30 June 2019, becoming the first sitting US president to set foot in North Korea. Under the 2018 inter-Korean military agreement, the JSA was to be demilitarised — guards disarmed and the area opened to tourists — though implementation stalled as relations deteriorated. In November 2017 the area was also the site of a North Korean soldier's dramatic defection under fire.

Tourism

Before their suspension, JSA tours from Seoul were among the most popular DMZ excursions, subject to strict dress codes and security briefings by the United Nations Command.
