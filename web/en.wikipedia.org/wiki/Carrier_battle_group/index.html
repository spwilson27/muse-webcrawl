A carrier battle group (CVBG) is a naval fleet consisting of an aircraft carrier and its supporting warships. In the United States Navy the formation is now formally called a carrier strike group (CSG). It is one of the most powerful instruments of power projection available to a modern navy.

Composition

A typical US group centres on a nuclear-powered supercarrier with an embarked air wing of roughly 70 aircraft. Escorts usually include a guided-missile cruiser, two or three guided-missile destroyers providing air defence, anti-submarine and strike capability, one or two nuclear attack submarines, and a fast combat support ship for fuel, ammunition and stores. The group defends itself in depth: aircraft patrol at long range, escorts cover the middle distance, and point-defence systems guard the inner zone.

History

The concept emerged in the Second World War, when fast carrier task forces of the US Pacific Fleet grouped carriers with battleships, cruisers and destroyers for mutual protection. Carrier battle groups were central to Cold War maritime strategy and to post-Cold-War operations, from the Gulf War to strikes launched from the Arabian Sea. Other navies operating carriers — including the United Kingdom, France, India and China — deploy comparable groups.
