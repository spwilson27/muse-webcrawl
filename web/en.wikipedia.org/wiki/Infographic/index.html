Infographic

Infographics (a clipped compound of "information" and "graphics") are graphic visual representations of information, data, or knowledge intended to present information quickly and clearly. They can improve cognition by utilising graphics to enhance the human visual system's ability to see patterns and trends. The process of creating infographics is sometimes called data visualisation, information design, or information architecture.

History

Early examples include William Playfair's statistical charts of the 1780s, Florence Nightingale's polar area diagrams of mortality in the Crimean War (1858), and Charles Joseph Minard's celebrated 1869 map of Napoleon's Russian campaign, which combined geography, troop numbers, temperature and time in a single figure. In the twentieth century, Otto Neurath's Isotype picture language and the diagrammatic work of Edward Tufte further developed the field.

Modern use

Infographics spread widely with the growth of the web and social media in the 2000s and 2010s, where long vertical graphics summarise a topic for quick sharing. Newspapers, magazines, museums, transit systems and businesses use them for wayfinding, reporting and marketing. Critics caution that simplified graphics can mislead when scales are distorted or context omitted, and stress accuracy, clear labelling and honest proportions as essentials of good practice.
