Grandes écoles

The grandes écoles form a parallel, elite track within French higher education.

The grandes écoles (French: [ɡʁɑ̃dz ekɔl], literally "great schools") are a group of French higher-education institutions outside the main framework of the French public university system. They are highly selective, admit small cohorts, and traditionally train France's senior civil servants, military officers, engineers, scientists and business leaders.

Admission

Most students enter through fiercely competitive national examinations (concours) taken after two years of preparatory classes (classes préparatoires aux grandes écoles, or prépas), which follow the baccalauréat. Others are admitted on file or from universities in smaller numbers.

Principal schools

Notable grandes écoles include the École Normale Supérieure (ENS) and École Polytechnique (founded 1794) in science and scholarship; HEC Paris in business; Sciences Po in political science and public affairs; the École Nationale d'Administration (ENA, replaced in 2022 by the Institut National du Service Public, INSP) for senior civil servants; and engineering schools such as CentraleSupélec, Mines Paris and Télécom Paris. Graduates of certain schools feed into prestigious state corps such as the Corps des Mines and the Inspection des Finances.

Criticism

The system, whose roots go back to the Revolutionary and Napoleonic era, is criticised for reproducing social elites: its students are drawn disproportionately from affluent, Parisian, highly educated families. Reforms have sought to widen access and diversify admissions, and the place of the grandes écoles alongside research universities remains a recurring topic of French educational debate.
