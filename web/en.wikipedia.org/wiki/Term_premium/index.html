Term premium

The term premium is the compensation that investors require for bearing the risk of holding a long-term bond rather than rolling over a series of short-term securities. In the decomposition of a bond yield, the yield equals the average expected short-term interest rate over the bond's life plus the term premium.

Behaviour and measurement

The term premium is generally positive but is not directly observable; it is estimated from term-structure models (for example the Adrian–Crump–Moench and Kim–Wright models published by U.S. Federal Reserve researchers) or from surveys. It fluctuates with uncertainty about inflation and interest rates, the demand for safe long-term assets, and monetary policy — during large-scale asset purchase programs, estimated U.S. term premiums fell to near zero or negative levels.

Significance

Distinguishing the term premium from expected rates matters for interpreting the yield curve: a flattening curve may reflect a falling term premium rather than expectations of lower future policy rates.
