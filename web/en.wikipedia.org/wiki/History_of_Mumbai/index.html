History of Mumbai

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The Gateway of India (1924) faces Mumbai's natural harbour.

The history of Mumbai traces the transformation of an archipelago of seven islands inhabited by Koli fishing communities into India's commercial and financial capital. Ruled successively by Hindu dynasties, the Gujarat Sultanate, Portugal (from 1534) and the English East India Company (from 1661), Bombay grew on land reclamation, cotton and opium trade, and textiles. Renamed Mumbai in 1995, the city is the centre of the Hindi film industry (Bollywood) and home to India's major stock exchanges.

Early history and Portuguese rule

The islands were part of Ashoka's Mauryan Empire and later of the Silhara dynasty; the Kanheri and Elephanta caves preserve Buddhist and Hindu rock-cut art. In 1534 Bahadur Shah of Gujarat ceded the islands to the Portuguese, who named the harbour Bom Bahia and built churches and fortifications, while Jesuit and Franciscan missions converted many inhabitants.

British Bombay

In 1661 the islands passed to Charles II of England as part of Catherine of Braganza's dowry and were leased to the East India Company in 1668 for £10 a year. Under governors such as Gerald Aungier the port attracted merchants from across western India. Reclamation joined the seven islands into one landmass by the mid-19th century. The American Civil War cotton boom of the 1860s enriched the city; the first Indian railway ran from Bombay to Thane in 1853, and the first cotton mill opened in 1854. Gothic Revival landmarks — the university, Victoria Terminus (now Chhatrapati Shivaji Terminus) — rose in the following decades.

Independence and modern Mumbai

Bombay was a centre of the Indian independence movement, where Gandhi launched the Quit India movement in 1942. After 1947 the city became capital of Bombay State and, in 1960, of Maharashtra after the Samyukta Maharashtra movement, which also saw the rise of Bal Thackeray's Shiv Sena. The 1992–93 communal riots and the 1993 bombings, the 2008 terrorist attacks, and chronic monsoon flooding mark its recent history, alongside growth as a hub of finance, technology and film.
