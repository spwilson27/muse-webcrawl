Overview

Albany is the capital of New York state, on the west bank of the Hudson River about 150 miles north of New York City. It is one of the oldest continuously chartered cities in the United States: the Dutch built Fort Orange here in 1624, the settlement became Beverwijck, and the English renamed it Albany in 1664 for the Duke of York and Albany. Its city charter of 1686 is among the oldest still in effect in the US.

Albany became the state capital in 1797. The Erie Canal (1825) and early railroads made it a transport hub, and in the 1960s–70s Governor Nelson Rockefeller's Empire State Plaza project rebuilt downtown with marble government towers around the ornate 19th-century State Capitol.

Modern city

Government dominates Albany's economy, alongside universities and a growing semiconductor and nanotechnology sector centred on the University at Albany's College of Nanotechnology. The Egg, a distinctive performing-arts building on the Plaza, is a local landmark.
