Moshing

Moshing (also known as slam dancing) is an extreme style of dancing at live music concerts in which participants push or slam into each other. It typically takes place in a "pit", usually near the stage, and is most associated with hardcore punk, heavy metal and related genres. Though energetic and often appearing violent, moshing is generally understood by participants to follow informal rules of mutual care, such as helping fallen dancers back to their feet.

History

The practice evolved from the pogo and slam dancing of the late 1970s and early 1980s punk scenes in California and Washington, D.C., where hardcore bands such as Black Flag and Bad Brains inspired increasingly physical audience participation. By the late 1980s the style had spread across thrash metal, crossover and later metalcore, pop-punk and electronic music scenes.

Variations

Common variations include the circle pit, in which participants run in a ring; the "wall of death", in which two crowds charge at each other when the music resumes; and hardcore dancing, which incorporates swinging arms, kicks and spin moves. Crowd surfing and stage diving are closely related practices.

Safety and criticism

Moshing can cause injuries and has occasionally led to deaths, prompting some venues and performers to ban or discourage it. Supporters argue that injuries are comparatively rare and that mosh pits embody a consensual, cathartic release governed by community etiquette.
