Victoria (plant)

KingdomPlantae
OrderNymphaeales
FamilyNymphaeaceae
GenusVictoria

Victoria is a genus of water lilies in the plant family Nymphaeaceae, with very large green leaves that lie flat on the water's surface. Victoria amazonica has a leaf up to 3 metres (9.8 ft) in diameter, on a stalk up to 8 metres (26 ft) in length. The genus name was given in honour of Queen Victoria of the United Kingdom.

The enormous floating leaves of Victoria

Description

The leaves of Victoria are remarkable for their size and strength: a mature leaf can support the weight of a small child. The underside of the leaf bears a network of ribs and hollow chambers that provide buoyancy and rigidity. The rim of the leaf turns up, forming a shallow basin. The flowers are large, fragrant, and open at night; they are pollinated by beetles. The plant is native to shallow waters of the Amazon River basin and other South American river systems.

Species

The genus includes Victoria amazonica, Victoria cruziana, and Victoria boliviana, the latter described in 2022 and considered the largest species of water lily in the world, with leaves over 3 metres wide.

Cultivation

Victoria species are popular exhibits in botanical gardens, where they are grown in heated pools. Seedlings were first successfully raised in England in 1849 at Kew Gardens and Chatsworth House, where gardener Joseph Paxton designed a glasshouse inspired by the structure of the leaf itself.
