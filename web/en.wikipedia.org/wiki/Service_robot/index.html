Service robot

A service robot is a robot that performs useful tasks for humans or equipment, excluding industrial automation applications. The International Organization for Standardization defines a service robot as a robot "in personal use or professional use that performs useful tasks for humans or equipment". Service robots are commonly categorised as personal (for home or everyday use by non-experts) or professional (used in commercial and institutional settings by trained operators).

Types

Personal service robots

The most widespread personal service robots are domestic robots: robotic vacuum cleaners such as iRobot's Roomba, robotic lawn mowers, and pool cleaners. Other examples include entertainment and companion robots, telepresence robots, educational robots, and assistive robots that help elderly or disabled people with mobility and daily tasks.

Professional service robots

Professional service robots operate in fields such as:

  • Medicine — surgical robots (such as the da Vinci system), rehabilitation robots, hospital logistics and disinfection robots.
  • Agriculture — milking robots, harvesting robots, drones and autonomous tractors.
  • Logistics — automated guided vehicles and warehouse robots that move goods in distribution centres.
  • Field robotics — inspection and maintenance robots for pipelines, power lines, and underwater structures; firefighting and bomb-disposal robots; cleaning robots for public spaces.
  • Hospitality and retail — delivery robots, reception and guide robots in hotels, airports, and shopping centres.

Market and adoption

The International Federation of Robotics (IFR) publishes annual statistics on service robot sales. Sales of professional service robots have grown steadily, driven by labour shortages, ageing populations, and advances in navigation, computer vision, and artificial intelligence that allow robots to operate safely alongside people outside controlled factory environments.

Challenges

Service robots must cope with unstructured, human-populated environments, which raises challenges in perception, manipulation, human–robot interaction, safety certification, privacy, and cost. Unlike industrial robots isolated behind safety fences, service robots share space with untrained users, so standards such as ISO 13482 (personal care robots) address their safe design.
