Strategic Petroleum Reserve

The Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) is an emergency stockpile of petroleum maintained by the United States Department of Energy. It is the largest publicly known emergency supply in the world; its underground salt caverns in Louisiana and Texas have a capacity of 714 million barrels. The United States started the reserve in 1975 after oil supplies were interrupted during the 1973–1974 embargo by the Organization of Arab Petroleum Exporting Countries.

Storage and operation

Crude oil is stored in solution-mined salt caverns at four Gulf Coast sites: Bryan Mound and Big Hill in Texas, and West Hackberry and Bayou Choctaw in Louisiana. The caverns, thousands of feet below the surface, are secure, relatively inexpensive to maintain, and allow oil to be pumped to refineries and terminals by pipeline or marine loading. The reserve can draw down at a maximum rate of about 4.4 million barrels per day.

Releases

Emergency drawdowns have been ordered by presidents in response to supply disruptions, including during Operation Desert Storm (1991), after Hurricane Katrina (2005), during the Libyan civil war coordinated IEA release (2011), and in 2022 following Russia's invasion of Ukraine, when a record 180 million barrels were released over six months. Congress has also mandated periodic sales to fund federal programmes, and the reserve has exchanged oil with companies after hurricanes and other local disruptions.
