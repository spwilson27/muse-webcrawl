Pshavi (Georgian: ფშავი) is a small historic region in the highlands of north-eastern Georgia, in the valley of the Pshavi Aragvi river and its tributaries, on the southern slopes of the Greater Caucasus. It is today part of the Dusheti Municipality of the Mtskheta-Mtianeti region, bordering Khevsureti to the north and Tusheti further east.

People and culture

The inhabitants, the Pshavs, are ethnic Georgians who speak a distinctive highland dialect of Georgian. Like the neighbouring Khevsurs and Tush, the Pshavs historically lived in fortified mountain villages, practised transhumant sheep-herding, and maintained a blend of Georgian Orthodox Christianity with older folk beliefs centred on shrines (jvari) tended by priests and oracle-shrines. In the late nineteenth century the region had some forty villages; many have since been emptied by migration to the lowlands, and only a handful remain permanently inhabited.

History

Together with Khevsureti, Pshavi formed the medieval district of Pkhovi, whose inhabitants resisted feudal subjugation and were known for the cult of the icon of the Archangel of Gudani and other shrine-communities organised under elected elders. The region is celebrated in Georgian literature, notably in the poetry of Vazha-Pshavela ("the one from Pshavi"), the pen name of the writer Luka Razikashvili, born in the Pshavi village of Chargali.
