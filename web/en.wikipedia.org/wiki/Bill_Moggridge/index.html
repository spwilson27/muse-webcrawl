William Grant "Bill" Moggridge (25 June 1943 – 8 September 2012) was a British designer, author and educator, regarded as a pioneer of interaction design and a co-founder of the design consultancy IDEO. He designed the GRiD Compass (1979–82), widely considered the first laptop computer in the modern clamshell form.

Career

Moggridge studied industrial design at the Central School of Art and Design in London and founded his first studio, Moggridge Associates, in London in 1969, moving to California in 1979. His London firm merged with David Kelley Design and Mike Nuttall's Matrix Product Design in 1991 to form IDEO.

While designing the GRiD Compass — used by NASA on Space Shuttle missions — Moggridge became interested not only in the physical form of computing but in how people interacted with software, and he is credited with applying the term "interaction design" to that discipline. He taught at Stanford University and the Royal College of Art, and wrote the books Designing Interactions (2006) and Designing Media (2010). In 2010 he became director of the Cooper-Hewitt, National Design Museum in New York, a post he held until his death from cancer in 2012.

Honours

Moggridge received the Prince Philip Designers Prize in 2010 and was made a Royal Designer for Industry. His work is held in the permanent collection of the Museum of Modern Art in New York.
