Statistical graphics

Statistical graphics, also known as statistical graphical techniques, are graphics used in the field of statistics for data visualization. They allow results to be displayed graphically and help in exploring data, checking assumptions, detecting outliers and relationships, and communicating findings.

History

Modern statistical graphics began with William Playfair, who in his Commercial and Political Atlas (1786) introduced the line graph and bar chart, and later the pie chart. Nineteenth-century pioneers included Florence Nightingale, whose polar area diagrams dramatised mortality in the Crimean War, and Charles Joseph Minard, whose famous flow map of Napoleon's Russian campaign of 1812 combined geography, troop numbers, and temperature in a single figure. In the twentieth century, John Tukey championed graphics for exploratory data analysis, including the box plot and stem-and-leaf display, while Edward Tufte codified principles of graphical excellence and integrity.

Common types

  • Histogram and bar chart
  • Box plot
  • Scatter plot
  • Line chart
  • Pie chart
  • Heat map
