Vasubandhu (Sanskrit: वसुबन्धु; Tibetan: དབྱིག་གཉེན་, Dbyig gnyen) was an influential Indian Buddhist monk and scholar from Gandhara. He was one of the founders of the Yogācāra school of Mahāyāna Buddhism and is regarded, together with his half-brother Asaṅga, as one of the most important figures in the history of Buddhist philosophy. The traditional accounts of his life are preserved in works by Paramārtha and Xuanzang.

Life

According to tradition, Vasubandhu was born in Puruṣapura (modern Peshawar) and ordained in the Sarvāstivāda school. He studied Abhidharma in Kashmir and composed the Abhidharmakośa, a systematic verse summary of Sarvāstivāda doctrine together with an auto-commentary that frequently criticises Sarvāstivāda positions from a Sautrāntika standpoint. Later, influenced by his half-brother Asaṅga, he converted to Mahāyāna and wrote a series of works expounding the Yogācāra doctrine of "consciousness-only" or "representation-only".

Works

  • Abhidharmakośa ("Treasury of Higher Dharma") — a foundational treatise on Abhidharma, still studied across Buddhist traditions.
  • Vimśatikā ("Twenty Verses") — argues that experience can be explained without external objects, as in a dream.
  • Trimśikā ("Thirty Verses") — a concise statement of Yogācāra doctrine, including the storehouse consciousness and the three natures (trisvabhāva).
  • Madhyānta-vibhāga-bhāṣya and commentaries on Mahāyāna sutras and treatises.

Influence

Vasubandhu's works were translated into Chinese and Tibetan and shaped the development of Buddhism in East and Central Asia. The Abhidharmakośa became the standard Abhidharma textbook in many monastic curricula, while his Yogācāra writings profoundly influenced the Chinese Faxiang school and later Tibetan scholasticism. Modern scholars continue to debate the chronology of his life and whether the Sarvāstivādin and Yogācāra authors of that name were one person or two.
