Hofmeister series

The Hofmeister series is a classification of ions in order of their ability to salt out or salt in proteins — that is, to decrease or increase protein solubility in water. The series was first described by the German chemist Franz Hofmeister in 1888 from studies of egg-white proteins.

The series

For anions, the typical ordering from strongly salting-out (kosmotropic) to strongly salting-in (chaotropic) is: sulfate > hydrogen phosphate > hydroxide > fluoride > chloride > bromide > nitrate > iodide > perchlorate > thiocyanate. Cations show a weaker but analogous ordering. Ions at the salting-out end tend to stabilise protein structure and precipitate proteins, while those at the salting-in end tend to denature and solubilise them.

Explanations

Hofmeister effects arise from the interplay of ion hydration, interactions with the protein surface and the peptide backbone, and changes in water structure near interfaces. They remain important in biochemistry, colloid science, chromatography (for example in hydrophobic interaction chromatography) and the formulation of protein drugs, even though a single unified theory is still debated.
