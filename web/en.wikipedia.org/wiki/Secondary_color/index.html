Secondary color

A secondary color is a color made by mixing two primary colors of a given color model in equal proportions. Secondary colors sit between the primaries on the color wheel and are contrasted with primary colors and tertiary colors.

In traditional painting (RYB)

In the traditional red–yellow–blue model used in art education, the primary colors are red, yellow, and blue. Mixing them in pairs gives the three secondary colors:

MixtureSecondary color
Red + YellowOrange
Yellow + BlueGreen
Blue + RedViolet (purple)

In additive mixing (RGB)

In the additive RGB color model used for light and displays, the primaries are red, green, and blue. Their pairwise mixtures are cyan, magenta, and yellow — which are exactly the primaries of the subtractive CMY model:

MixtureSecondary color
Red + GreenYellow
Green + BlueCyan
Blue + RedMagenta

In subtractive mixing (CMY/CMYK)

In the subtractive model used in printing, the primaries are cyan, magenta, and yellow. Mixing pairs of them produces red, green, and blue — the primaries of the additive system. This symmetry between the two models is a consequence of how each primary pigment absorbs one component of white light.
