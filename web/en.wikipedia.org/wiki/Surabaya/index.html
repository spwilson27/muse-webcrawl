Surabaya is the capital of the Indonesian province of East Java and the second-largest city in Indonesia, after Jakarta. Located on the northeastern coast of Java on the Madura Strait, it is a major port and commercial centre; its metropolitan area, Gerbangkertosusila, is among the largest in Southeast Asia.

Surabaya, connected to Madura island by the Suramadu Bridge

History

Surabaya was a trading port of the Majapahit era and later a stronghold contested by the Demak and Mataram sultanates. Under Dutch colonial rule it grew into the largest city of the Dutch East Indies and a hub of the sugar trade. In November 1945, during the Indonesian National Revolution, the city was the scene of the Battle of Surabaya, in which Indonesian fighters resisted British and Dutch forces; the fighting is commemorated annually on 10 November as Heroes' Day, and Surabaya is known as the "City of Heroes".

Geography and economy

The city occupies a low-lying delta at the mouth of the Mas River. Tanjung Perak is one of Indonesia's busiest ports, and the Suramadu Bridge — Indonesia's longest — links the city to Madura island. Surabaya's economy rests on trade, manufacturing, shipbuilding and services, and it hosts Juanda International Airport and major universities including Airlangga University and the Sepuluh Nopember Institute of Technology (ITS).

Culture

Residents, known as Suroboyoan, speak a distinctive dialect of Javanese. Local specialities include rawon (black beef soup) and rujak cingur. The Suroboyo Carnival and the Tunjungan area are popular landmarks.
