Yoshino (吉野町, Yoshino-chō) is a town located in Yoshino District, Nara Prefecture, Japan. It is best known for Mount Yoshino (Yoshinoyama), celebrated for more than a thousand years as Japan's most famous site for viewing cherry blossoms, with some 30,000 cherry trees planted across the mountain's ridges.

Mount Yoshino

The cherry trees of Mount Yoshino are grouped into four areas — Shimo, Naka, Kami, and Oku Senbon ("lower, middle, upper, and inner thousands") — which bloom in succession from late March to mid-April as the flowering front climbs the mountain. The mountain is also a sacred centre of Shugendō mountain asceticism, associated with the legendary founder En no Gyōja, and its main hall, Zaōdō of Kinpusen-ji, is among the largest wooden buildings in Japan.

History

Yoshino has deep imperial associations: Emperor Tenmu's bid for the throne began here in the Jinshin War of 672, and in the 14th century Emperor Go-Daigo established his Southern Court at Yoshino during the Nanboku-chō period. The area forms part of the UNESCO World Heritage listing "Sacred Sites and Pilgrimage Routes in the Kii Mountain Range" (2004).
