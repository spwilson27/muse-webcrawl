Biopiracy

Biopiracy (also known as scientific colonialism) is the unauthorised appropriation of knowledge and genetic resources of farming and indigenous communities by individuals or institutions seeking exclusive monopoly control through patents or intellectual property.

Concept

The term, popularised in the 1990s by activist Pat Mooney and organisations such as ETC Group, criticises bioprospecting and patenting that extract value from biodiversity and traditional knowledge without consent or benefit-sharing.

Famous cases

Disputed patents and claims have involved neem (revoked at the European Patent Office), turmeric wound-healing (US patent revoked after Indian challenge), basmati rice (RiceTec), the rosy periwinkle drugs vincristine/vinblastine from Madagascar, ayahuasca, and hoodia from southern Africa, where a benefit-sharing agreement with the San followed controversy.

Legal responses

The Convention on Biological Diversity (1992) and its Nagoya Protocol (2010) establish access and benefit-sharing; India's Traditional Knowledge Digital Library documents prior art to defeat wrongful patents.
