Withnail and I

Withnail and I is a 1987 British black comedy film written and directed by Bruce Robinson, and starring Richard E. Grant as Withnail, Paul McGann as "I" (Marwood), and Richard Griffiths as Uncle Monty. Produced by HandMade Films, it is loosely based on Robinson's own experiences as a struggling actor in London in the late 1960s.

Plot

In 1969, two unemployed actors — the flamboyant, embittered Withnail and the anxious Marwood — live in squalor in Camden Town. Desperate for a break, they persuade Withnail's eccentric Uncle Monty to lend them his cottage in the Lake District. The holiday proves miserable: the cottage is freezing, the locals hostile, and Monty arrives with designs on Marwood. The film ends with Marwood landing a leading role and leaving, while Withnail recites Hamlet's soliloquy to wolves in Regent's Park zoo.

Reception and legacy

The film was a modest commercial success on release but has since become a major cult film, celebrated for its quotable dialogue ("We want the finest wines available to humanity!"), and is regularly ranked among the greatest British films and greatest comedies of all time.
