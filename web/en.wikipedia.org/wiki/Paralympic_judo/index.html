Paralympic judo

Paralympic judo is an adaptation of the Japanese martial art of judo for athletes with visual impairments, and has been a Paralympic sport since the 1988 Games in Seoul for men and since Athens 2004 for women. It is one of only two Paralympic sports open exclusively to visually impaired athletes (the other being goalball) and is governed internationally in association with the International Blind Sports Federation (IBSA) and the International Judo Federation.

Rules and adaptations

The rules differ only slightly from Olympic judo. The principal adaptation is that competitors begin each bout already gripping their opponent's judogi (kumi-kata established by the referee) rather than fighting for a grip at a distance, so that they start in contact. Referees use audible signals, and the mat area is textured so athletes can feel its boundaries. Bouts last four minutes; a throw scoring ippon wins immediately, as in able-bodied judo.

Classification

Judoka were historically classified B1 (totally blind), B2 and B3, but all competed together in weight categories. For the Paris 2024 cycle the sport moved to two sport classes, J1 (blind) and J2 (visually impaired), competing separately across weight classes, increasing the number of medal events.
