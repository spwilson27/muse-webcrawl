Francis Vere

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sir Francis Vere (1560/61 – 28 August 1609) was an English soldier, the outstanding English commander of the Eighty Years' War in the Low Countries. A grandson of John de Vere, 15th Earl of Oxford, he volunteered for service in the Netherlands in 1585 with the Earl of Leicester's expedition and rose, after Leicester's failure, to command the English forces fighting alongside the Dutch Republic against Spain.

Career

Vere distinguished himself at the sieges and battles of the 1590s, including the capture of Cádiz expedition (1596), where he was knighted by the Earl of Essex. His greatest fame came at the Battle of Nieuwpoort (2 July 1600), where the English contingent under his command bore the hardest fighting in a rare pitched-battle victory over the Spanish Army of Flanders. He then organised the celebrated defence of Ostend during the opening phase of its long siege (1601–1604), holding the ruined town against overwhelming Spanish forces for months before handing over command. In 1604, with peace negotiations under way, he retired to England.

Character and legacy

Vere was a strict disciplinarian and an early exponent of combined-arms tactics, careful reconnaissance, and the rotation of units in the line — his regiments formed a celebrated "fighting school" in which many younger English officers (including his brother Horace Vere, later Lord Vere of Tilbury) learned their trade. He died in 1609 and was buried in Westminster Abbey near the tomb of John Norris, his predecessor in the Netherlands command.
