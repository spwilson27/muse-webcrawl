Coursera

Founded 2012 by Andrew Ng and Daphne Koller; Mountain View, California.

Coursera Inc. is an American for-profit online learning platform founded in 2012 by Stanford University computer science professors Andrew Ng and Daphne Koller. It works with universities and other organizations to offer online courses, certifications, and degrees in a variety of subjects, and is one of the best-known providers of massive open online courses (MOOCs).

History

Coursera grew out of Stanford's experiments with free online courses in 2011, when Ng's machine learning class and two other courses each attracted over 100,000 students. The company launched in April 2012 with courses from Stanford, Princeton, the University of Michigan, and the University of Pennsylvania, and raised venture capital to expand rapidly. Koller served as co-CEO and then president; Jeff Maggioncalda became CEO in 2017. Coursera went public on the New York Stock Exchange in March 2021.

Offerings and business model

The catalogue includes individual courses, multi-course "Specializations", professional certificates (including programs developed with companies such as Google and IBM), and full online bachelor's and master's degrees from partner universities. While many courses can be audited for free, graded assignments and certificates require payment, and the company sells subscriptions (Coursera Plus) and enterprise and government training services. Courses typically combine short video lectures, readings, auto-graded quizzes, and peer-reviewed assignments.

Reach and criticism

By the early 2020s Coursera reported well over 100 million registered learners worldwide, with demand surging during the COVID-19 pandemic. Critics note low completion rates typical of MOOCs, questions about the rigour of peer grading, and the commercialisation of university teaching; supporters credit the platform with broadening access to education from elite institutions and workforce-relevant skills.
