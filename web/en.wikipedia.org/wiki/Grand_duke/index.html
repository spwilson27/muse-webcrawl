Grand duke

[Image placeholder: Heraldic crown of a grand duke]

Grand duke (feminine: grand duchess) is a European hereditary title, used either by certain monarchs or by members of certain monarchs' families. In past decades, the territory ruled by a grand duke was called a grand duchy. The rank is generally placed below emperor and king and above prince and duke. In English, the title is used particularly for the rulers of the historical grand duchies of Germany and Italy, for the sovereign of Luxembourg, and as the usual translation of the Russian title veliky knyaz borne by children and grandchildren of emperors.

History

The title emerged in late medieval Europe. The rulers of Tuscany received the style of Grand Duke from Pope Pius V in 1569, when Cosimo I de' Medici became the first Grand Duke of Tuscany. In the German lands, the title was adopted by rulers such as those of Baden, Hesse, Mecklenburg and Oldenburg, several of whom were raised to grand-ducal rank at the Congress of Vienna in 1815.

Present day

The only reigning grand duke today is the Grand Duke of Luxembourg, Henri, whose country is the world's sole surviving grand duchy. Grand Duke Henri acceded in 2000; in October 2025 he abdicated in favour of his son Guillaume, who became Grand Duke of Luxembourg.

Russian usage

In the Russian Empire, "Grand Duke" (Великий князь) was the title of sons and grandsons of the emperor, with the style of Imperial Highness; it was not a sovereign title but a dynastic one. Translators also use "grand prince" for the medieval rulers of Kievan Rus' and Moscow.
