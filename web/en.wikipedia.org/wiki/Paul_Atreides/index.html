Paul Atreides is a fictional character in the Dune universe created by Frank Herbert. He is the main protagonist of the 1965 novel Dune and Dune Messiah (1969), and appears in Children of Dune (1976). The son of Duke Leto Atreides and the Bene Gesserit Lady Jessica, Paul is the product of generations of selective breeding and becomes the Fremen messiah Muad'Dib and, ultimately, Padishah Emperor.

Character arc

Raised on the water-rich world of Caladan, Paul is trained in combat by Gurney Halleck and Duncan Idaho and in Mentat reasoning by Thufir Hawat, while his mother teaches him Bene Gesserit disciplines. When House Atreides takes control of the desert planet Arrakis — sole source of the spice melange — a Harkonnen and Imperial attack destroys his family. Paul flees into the desert with Jessica and is taken in by the Fremen.

Living among the Fremen and drinking the Water of Life, Paul gains prescient visions and becomes their leader under the name Muad'Dib. He rides sandworms, defeats the Emperor's Sardaukar, and in single combat kills Feyd-Rautha Harkonnen. By threatening to destroy spice production he forces the Emperor Shaddam IV to abdicate and takes the throne, unleashing a jihad across the universe in his name — a fate he foresaw but could not escape. In Dune Messiah he walks blind into the desert, returning years later as the Preacher in Children of Dune.

Interpretation

Herbert wrote Paul as a cautionary figure: a charismatic superhuman hero whose worship leads to catastrophic violence — billions of deaths in the jihad — warning readers against trusting heroic leaders. The character has been portrayed by Kyle MacLachlan in David Lynch's 1984 film, Alec Newman in the 2000 miniseries, and Timothée Chalamet in Denis Villeneuve's Dune (2021) and Dune: Part Two (2024).
