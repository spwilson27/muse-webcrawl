Maresha

Maresha was an Iron Age city of ancient Judah, mentioned in the Hebrew Bible, and later an Idumean city of the Hellenistic period, known in Greek as Marisa. Its ruins, including an extensive network of underground caves, are preserved in Beit Guvrin-Maresha National Park in the Judean lowlands of Israel, and the site was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2014.

History

Maresha is listed among the cities of Judah in the Book of Joshua and was fortified by Rehoboam. It was the home town of the prophet Eliezer and the setting of the battle in which Asa defeated Zerah the Ethiopian, as related in 2 Chronicles. After the Babylonian exile the town became Idumean, and under Hellenistic rule it flourished as a cosmopolitan trading city inhabited by Idumeans, Sidonians, Greeks and Jews.

Archaeology

The site is famous for its hundreds of man-made bell-shaped caves cut into the soft chalk beneath the city, used as quarries, cisterns, olive presses, dovecotes and burial chambers. The painted Sidonian burial caves, decorated with animals and mythological scenes, are among the finest examples of Hellenistic funerary art in the Levant. The city was destroyed by the Parthians in 40 BC and never rebuilt, its population moving to nearby Beth Gabra (Beit Guvrin).
