Hippodrome

A hippodrome was an ancient Greek stadium for horse racing and chariot racing. The name derives from the Greek hippos (horse) and dromos (course).

Greek hippodromes

The most famous was the hippodrome at Olympia, site of chariot races at the Olympic Games. Unlike the Roman circus, the Greek hippodrome typically had one rounded and one squared end, with an embanked seating area.

Constantinople

The Hippodrome of Constantinople, begun by Septimius Severus and enlarged by Constantine the Great, was the social and sporting centre of the Byzantine capital, famous for chariot races and the factions of the Blues and Greens.
