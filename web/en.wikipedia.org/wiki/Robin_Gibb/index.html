Robin Hugh Gibb CBE (22 December 1949 – 20 May 2012) was a British singer and songwriter best known as a member of the Bee Gees, which he formed with his brothers Barry and Maurice. Born in the Isle of Man and raised in Manchester and Brisbane, Australia, the fraternal trio became one of the best-selling music groups of all time.

Career

Robin sang lead on many early Bee Gees hits, including "Massachusetts", "I Started a Joke" and "I've Gotta Get a Message to You", his quivering vibrato a signature of the group's 1960s sound. A dispute over lead vocals on the 1969 album Odessa prompted a brief solo career ("Saved by the Bell") before the group reunited. In the 1970s the Bee Gees defined the disco era with Saturday Night Fever; Robin also reached the charts solo with "Juliet" (1983) and composed with his brothers for many other artists. The Bee Gees were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997.

Later life and death

Gibb was active in charitable and political causes, including campaigns to build a permanent memorial to RAF Bomber Command. He died in London in May 2012 of liver and kidney failure following colorectal cancer. His final work, the classical Titanic Requiem, co-composed with his son Robin-John, premiered in April 2012.
