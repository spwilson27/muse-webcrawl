Double star

In observational astronomy, a double star or visual double is a pair of stars that appear close to each other as viewed from Earth, especially with the aid of optical telescopes. This occurs because the pair either forms a binary star (a physically bound system in mutual orbit) or is an optical double, a chance alignment of two unrelated stars at different distances.


The double star Albireo, famous for its gold and blue contrasting components.

Distinction from binaries

Long-term observation can reveal whether two stars share a common proper motion or trace a curved orbital path around each other, indicating a true binary. If they drift apart linearly, the pair is merely optical. Famous doubles include Albireo in Cygnus, noted for its colour contrast, and Epsilon Lyrae, the "Double Double", each component of which is itself double.

Observation

Double stars are popular targets for amateur astronomers because splitting close pairs tests telescope resolution and atmospheric seeing. Measurements of separation and position angle are catalogued in resources such as the Washington Double Star Catalog.
