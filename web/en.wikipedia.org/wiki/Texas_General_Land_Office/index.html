The Texas General Land Office (GLO) is a state agency of Texas, the oldest existing state agency in Texas, established by the Congress of the Republic of Texas on 22 December 1836. It is headed by the Commissioner of the General Land Office, an elected executive official chosen statewide to a four-year term.

History

The office was created to manage the public domain of the republic: surveying lands, issuing titles and recording the land grants made under Spanish, Mexican and republican authority. Uniquely among US states formed from public-land territories, Texas retained ownership of its public lands upon annexation in 1845, so the GLO — rather than the federal government — continued to administer them. The agency's archives preserve millions of documents, including Spanish and Mexican land grants and the records of the Republic.

Functions

The GLO manages approximately 13 million acres of state lands and mineral rights, including the submerged lands of the Gulf Coast (Texas claims a three-league — about 10.35 statute mile — boundary). Oil and gas leasing revenues from state lands flow principally to the Permanent School Fund, which supports public education. The agency also oversees coastal management and oil-spill response, the Texas Veterans Land Board's land and housing loan programmes, and, since 2011, custody of the Alamo. Following Hurricane Harvey in 2017, the GLO took a lead role in administering federal disaster-recovery housing funds.

Commissioners

The first commissioner was John P. Borden (1837–1838). Long-serving commissioners include Jacob de Cordova and Bob Armstrong (1971–1985). Later commissioners have included Garry Mauro, Jerry Patterson, George P. Bush (2015–2023) and Dawn Buckingham (from 2023), the first woman to hold the office.
