Pokémon Go is a 2016 augmented reality (AR) mobile game, part of the Pokémon franchise, developed and published by Niantic in collaboration with Nintendo and The Pokémon Company for iOS and Android devices. It uses mobile devices with GPS to locate, capture, train, and battle virtual Pokémon, which appear as if they are in the player's real-world location. The game is free-to-play; it uses a freemium business model combined with local advertising, and supports in-app purchases for additional in-game items.

Gameplay

After creating an avatar, players navigate a stylized map based on the real world. Wild Pokémon spawn at real locations; players tap them to enter a catching encounter, optionally using the AR mode to view the creature through the phone's camera. PokéStops — tied to landmarks and points of interest — dispense items, while Gyms allow players, aligned with one of three teams (Valor, Mystic, or Instinct), to battle and defend locations. Later updates added raids against powerful boss Pokémon, player-versus-player Trainer Battles, trading, field research, Community Days, and Pokémon from successive generations of the main-series games.

Release and reception

Released in most regions in July 2016, Pokémon Go was downloaded more than 500 million times in its first year and became a global cultural phenomenon, credited with popularizing location-based and AR gaming and getting players outdoors and exploring their surroundings. It passed $1 billion in revenue faster than any mobile game before it and by 2020 had grossed over $6 billion. The game won numerous awards, including five Guinness World Records.

Criticism and incidents

The launch was plagued by server failures, bugs, and the controversial removal of the tracking feature. The game's real-world play caused widely reported incidents of trespassing, distracted driving, and players congregating in inappropriate places, prompting warnings from authorities; some locations, including the United States Holocaust Memorial Museum and Arlington National Cemetery, asked to be removed from the game. Scholars have debated its effects on physical activity, local businesses, and data privacy.

Legacy

Pokémon Go remained one of the world's most profitable mobile games years after release and established Niantic's real-world AR platform, later used for titles such as Harry Potter: Wizards Unite and Pikmin Bloom. In 2025, Niantic's games business, including Pokémon Go, was acquired by Scopely.
