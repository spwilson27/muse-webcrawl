Black Seminoles

A Black Seminole settlement in 19th-century Florida

The Black Seminoles, also called Seminole Maroons or Seminole Freedmen, are a group of African Americans and people of mixed African and Native American ancestry associated with the Seminole people of Florida and Oklahoma. Their history begins in the 18th century, when enslaved Africans escaping from plantations in South Carolina and Georgia fled south into Spanish Florida, where they found refuge and formed communities allied with, but distinct from, the Seminole villages.

Origins in Spanish Florida

Under Spanish rule, fugitive slaves who converted to Catholicism and swore loyalty to the Spanish crown could win their freedom. In 1693 King Charles II of Spain issued a royal edict granting freedom to fugitives reaching Florida, and in 1738 the Spanish governor established Fort Mose (Gracia Real de Santa Teresa de Mose) near St. Augustine, the first legally sanctioned free Black settlement in what is now the United States. Others joined Seminole communities in the Florida interior, where they lived in their own villages, paid a modest annual tribute of crops to Seminole chiefs, and served as interpreters, advisers, and warriors.

Seminole Wars

The presence of free Black communities so near the slaveholding United States was a major cause of the Seminole Wars. During the First Seminole War (1817–1818), Andrew Jackson invaded Florida and destroyed the "Negro Fort" on the Apalachicola River. In the Second Seminole War (1835–1842), Black Seminoles such as Abraham and John Horse (Juan Caballo) were key leaders and negotiators. After the war, many were removed with the Seminole to Indian Territory (present-day Oklahoma); facing threats of re-enslavement there, John Horse led hundreds of Black Seminoles and Seminoles to Mexico in 1849–1850, where the Mexican government granted them land in Coahuila.

Descendants

Descendant communities survive in several places: the Seminole Freedmen bands of Oklahoma (the Dosar Barkus and Caesar Bruner bands of the Seminole Nation), the Mascogos of Coahuila, Mexico, the Black Seminole community of Brackettville, Texas (descendants of the Seminole Negro Indian Scouts of the U.S. Army), and Red Bays on Andros Island in the Bahamas, settled by Black Seminoles who fled Florida by boat in the 1820s. The legal status of the Seminole Freedmen within the Seminole Nation of Oklahoma has been contested since the late 20th century.
