Poincaré conjecture

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Poincaré conjecture was a famous problem in topology, stated by Henri Poincaré in 1904, asserting that every simply connected, closed 3-manifold is homeomorphic to the 3-sphere. Intuitively, it says that a closed three-dimensional space in which every loop can be shrunk to a point must be a three-dimensional sphere.

History

The conjecture resisted proof for nearly a century and became one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems announced by the Clay Mathematics Institute in 2000, each carrying a prize of one million dollars. Analogues in higher dimensions were settled earlier: Stephen Smale proved the generalised conjecture for dimensions five and above in 1961, and Michael Freedman settled dimension four in 1982.

Solution

In 2002 and 2003 the Russian mathematician Grigori Perelman posted three papers outlining a proof using Richard Hamilton's Ricci flow programme, completing Thurston's geometrisation conjecture, of which the Poincaré conjecture is a special case. The proof was verified by several teams by 2006. Perelman declined both the Fields Medal in 2006 and the Millennium Prize in 2010.
