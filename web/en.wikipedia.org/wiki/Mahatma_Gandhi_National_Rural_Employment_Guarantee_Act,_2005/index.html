Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005

The Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act 2005 (MGNREGA), earlier known as the National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (NREGA), is an Indian labour law and social security measure that aims to guarantee the "right to work". Enacted in August 2005 and coming into force on 2 February 2006, it provides for the enhancement of livelihood security of households in rural areas of the country by providing at least one hundred days of guaranteed wage employment in every financial year to every household whose adult members volunteer to do unskilled manual work.

Provisions

Under the Act, rural households can apply for work through their Gram Panchayat; employment must be provided within 15 days of application, failing which an unemployment allowance is payable. Wages are paid at rates notified for each state, and work is generally provided within 5 kilometres of the applicant's residence. At least one-third of beneficiaries are required to be women. Permissible works focus on water conservation, drought proofing, irrigation, land development, rural connectivity and other durable rural assets.

Administration and funding

The programme is funded jointly by the central and state governments, with the centre bearing the full cost of unskilled wages and most of the material cost. Implementation runs through Panchayati Raj institutions, with social audits by Gram Sabhas mandated as a transparency measure. The scheme was renamed after Mahatma Gandhi on 2 October 2009.

Scale and assessment

MGNREGA is among the largest public employment programmes in the world, covering hundreds of millions of rural workers. Studies have credited it with raising rural wages, supporting incomes in lean seasons and increasing women's participation in paid work, while critics point to delays in wage payments, uneven asset quality and leakage in implementation.
