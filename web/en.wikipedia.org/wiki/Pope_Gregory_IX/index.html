Pope Gregory IX (Latin: Gregorius IX; born Ugolino di Conti, c. 1145 or before 1170 – 22 August 1241) was head of the Catholic Church from 19 March 1227 until his death in 1241. A nephew of Innocent III, he continued his uncle's work of strengthening papal authority, and is known for his Decretales, for founding the Papal Inquisition, and for his bitter struggles with Emperor Frederick II.

Early career

Ugolino studied law at Bologna and became chancellor of the Roman Church under Innocent III, who made him cardinal. As papal legate he worked to reconcile Pisa and Genoa and supported the preaching of the crusades. He was a friend and protector of Francis of Assisi and of Dominic, and a member of the family of the Counts of Segni.

Pontificate

Gregory excommunicated Frederick II in 1227 for failing to fulfil his crusade vow, and again after Frederick's crusade and recovery from illness placed him at odds with papal interests in Italy. Their conflict escalated into open war in the Italian peninsula, and Gregory convened a council against Frederick in 1241; the emperor's forces intercepted prelates travelling to it at the naval Battle of Giglio.

In canon law, Gregory commissioned Raymond of Penyafort to compile the Decretales Gregorii IX (1234), a systematic collection that remained fundamental for centuries. In 1231 his bull Parens scientiarum granted privileges to the University of Paris. To combat heresy, especially in southern France and Germany, he established permanent papal inquisitors — the foundation of the medieval Inquisition — and approved the rules of new mendicant orders. He canonised Francis of Assisi (1228), Dominic (1234), Anthony of Padua and Elizabeth of Hungary.
