Irish Football Association

Founded: 1880
Headquarters: Belfast
Affiliation: FIFA (1907), UEFA
Teams: Northern Ireland national teams

The Irish Football Association (IFA) is the governing body for association football in Northern Ireland. Founded in Belfast on 18 November 1880, it is the fourth-oldest national football association in the world, after those of England, Scotland, and Wales. The IFA is one of the four associations represented on the International Football Association Board, which determines the Laws of the Game.

History

Before the partition of Ireland, the IFA governed football across the whole island and selected an all-Ireland national team. Following the creation of the Irish Free State, a rival body — the Football Association of Ireland (FAI) — was founded in Dublin in 1921. After decades of dispute over jurisdiction and player selection, the IFA's remit settled on Northern Ireland, whose national team it continues to run.

Competitions and role

The IFA organises the Irish Cup, one of the oldest national cup competitions, and oversees the Northern Ireland national teams. Its home stadium is the National Football Stadium at Windsor Park in Belfast. League football in Northern Ireland is administered by the Northern Ireland Football League in association with the IFA.
