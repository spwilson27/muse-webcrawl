Cray Inc., founded as Cray Research, is an American manufacturer of supercomputers. The company was founded in 1972 by Seymour Cray, the designer widely regarded as the father of supercomputing, and for decades its name was nearly synonymous with the fastest computers in the world. Cray was acquired by Hewlett Packard Enterprise in 2019 for approximately $1.3 billion, and the Cray brand continues within HPE's high-performance computing business.

History

Seymour Cray left Control Data Corporation to found Cray Research in Chippewa Falls, Wisconsin, in 1972. The company's first product, the Cray-1 (1976), with its distinctive C-shaped cabinet and vector-processing architecture, became an icon of supercomputing and was installed at laboratories such as Los Alamos. It was followed by the Cray-2 (1985) and numerous later systems. After financial difficulties in the 1990s, Cray Research was acquired by Silicon Graphics in 1996; the Cray name was revived when Tera Computer Company purchased the business in 2000 and renamed itself Cray Inc.

Later systems

In the twenty-first century Cray built many of the world's leading machines, including the Cray XT and XE series, the XK7 system "Titan" at Oak Ridge National Laboratory, the XC series system "Piz Daint" in Switzerland, and the Cray Shasta architecture, which underlies the exascale systems Frontier, Aurora, and El Capitan deployed for the United States Department of Energy. The company also developed the Chapel parallel programming language.
