Operation Cobra

Operation Cobra was the codename for an offensive launched by the United States First Army under Lieutenant General Omar Bradley during the Normandy campaign of World War II. Beginning on 25 July 1944, seven weeks after the D-Day landings, the operation was designed to break through the German defences south of the Cotentin Peninsula and end the stalemate of the bocage country.

The breakout

The attack opened with a massive carpet-bombing by Allied heavy bombers — which also caused significant American casualties from short bombing, including Lieutenant General Lesley J. McNair, the highest-ranking American officer killed in Normandy. Nevertheless, the bombardment shattered the Panzer Lehr Division holding the sector, and VII Corps under Major General J. Lawton Collins broke through. American armoured divisions then fanned out, taking Coutances and Avranches within days.

Consequences

The success of Cobra opened the way for Lieutenant General George S. Patton's Third Army to pour into Brittany and then wheel eastward, transforming the campaign from a grinding attritional battle into a war of movement that culminated in the encirclement of German forces in the Falaise pocket and the liberation of Paris.
