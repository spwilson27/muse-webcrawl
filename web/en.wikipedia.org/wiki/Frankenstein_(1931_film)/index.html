Frankenstein is a 1931 American pre-Code science fiction horror film directed by James Whale and produced by Universal Pictures. Adapted from Mary Shelley's 1818 novel, it stars Colin Clive as Henry Frankenstein, a young scientist who assembles a creature from exhumed body parts and brings it to life, and Boris Karloff — credited only as "?" in the opening cast — as the Monster. The film was a major commercial success and is regarded as one of the greatest horror films ever made.

Plot

Henry Frankenstein, obsessed with creating life, robs graves with his hunchbacked assistant Fritz and constructs a body from corpses. Using electrical apparatus in a watchtower laboratory during a storm, he animates the creature, exclaiming "It's alive!" The Monster, given an abnormal criminal brain accidentally supplied by Fritz, proves violent and escapes after killing Fritz and Frankenstein's mentor, Dr. Waldman. Later the Monster, seeking kindness, drowns a little girl, Maria, in a flower game gone wrong. An enraged mob hunts him to an old windmill, which they set ablaze; Frankenstein survives the fall from the mill and eventually recovers at home as his father toasts the family future.

Production

Following the success of Dracula (1931), Universal assigned Whale the property; he chose it over other offered projects. Makeup artist Jack Pierce designed the Monster's iconic look — the flat head, heavy eyelids, and neck bolts (electrodes) — which remains the definitive popular image of Frankenstein's monster. The film's electrical laboratory equipment was created by Kenneth Strickfaden.

Legacy

The film launched the Universal Monsters cycle and was followed by the acclaimed sequel Bride of Frankenstein (1935). Lines such as "It's alive!" and scenes including the Monster's creation and the windmill climax have become staples of popular culture. In 1998 the film was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry as culturally, historically, and aesthetically significant.
