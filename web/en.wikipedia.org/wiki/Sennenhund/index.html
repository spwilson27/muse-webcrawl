Sennenhund


A Greater Swiss Mountain Dog, the largest of the Sennenhunde (placeholder image)

The Sennenhund, plural Sennenhunde, are a group of four Swiss dog breeds: large, sturdy farm dogs of the Swiss Alps. The name derives from the Senn or Senner, the Alpine herdsmen and dairymen who kept them. The breeds share a distinctive tricolour coat — black, white and rust — and were traditionally used for herding cattle, guarding farms and pulling carts.

The four breeds

From largest to smallest, the four recognised Sennenhund breeds are:

  • Greater Swiss Mountain Dog (Grosser Schweizer Sennenhund) — the largest and oldest, a draught and cattle dog.
  • Bernese Mountain Dog (Berner Sennenhund) — long-coated, from the canton of Bern; popular worldwide as a companion.
  • Appenzeller Sennenhund — spirited, curly-tailed herding dog from the Appenzell region.
  • Entlebucher Mountain Dog (Entlebucher Sennenhund) — the smallest, an agile cattle-herding dog from the Entlebuch valley.

History and characteristics

The breeds descend from mastiff-type dogs brought to the Alps in Roman times and crossed with local farm dogs. By the late nineteenth century several of the types were close to extinction; the Swiss cynologist Franz Schertenleib and the geologist Albert Heim were instrumental in their preservation and recognition in the early twentieth century. Sennenhunde are known for their strength, intelligence, calm temperament and loyalty, though some of the breeds, notably the Bernese, are prone to a comparatively short lifespan and hereditary cancers.
