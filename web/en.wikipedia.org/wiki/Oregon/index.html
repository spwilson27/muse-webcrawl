Overview

Oregon is a state in the Pacific Northwest of the United States, bordered by the Pacific Ocean to the west, California and Nevada to the south, Idaho to the east and Washington across the Columbia River to the north. Its capital is Salem and its largest city Portland.

The region was inhabited by many Native American peoples and was claimed by Spain, Britain, Russia and the United States before the Oregon Treaty of 1846 settled on the 49th parallel. Settlers arrived in large numbers via the Oregon Trail in the 1840s–50s, and Oregon became a state in 1859.

Geography and economy

Oregon's landscape divides between the wet western valleys — the Willamette Valley holds most of the population — and the dry high desert east of the Cascade Range, whose volcanoes include Mount Hood, the state's highest point. The Columbia River Gorge, Crater Lake (the deepest lake in the United States) and a rugged public coastline are signature landscapes. Timber, agriculture (including wine in the Willamette Valley), outdoor recreation and a technology cluster nicknamed the Silicon Forest anchor the economy.
