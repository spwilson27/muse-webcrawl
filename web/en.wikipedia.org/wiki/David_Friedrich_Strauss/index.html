David Friedrich Strauss (German: [ˈdaːvɪt ˈfʁiːdʁɪç ʃtʁaʊs]; 27 January 1808 – 8 February 1874) was a German liberal Protestant theologian and writer, whose scandalous book The Life of Jesus, Critically Examined (1835–36) pioneered the historical investigation of Jesus and applied the category of myth to the Gospel narratives.

Early life

Strauss was born at Ludwigsburg and educated at the seminary at Blaubeuren and at the University of Tübingen, where he studied under Ferdinand Christian Baur and absorbed the philosophy of Hegel and Schleiermacher. He briefly taught at the seminary at Maulbronn and spent time in Berlin, where he heard Hegel lecture shortly before the philosopher's death.

The Life of Jesus

Published when Strauss was only 27, Das Leben Jesu applied Hegelian dialectic and a rigorous source criticism to the Gospels. Neither accepting the supernaturalist reading (the miracles happened as described) nor the rationalist one (the miracles were misunderstood natural events), Strauss argued that the Gospel stories are myths — the unconscious poetic expression of the early Christian community's messianic ideas, clothing the historical Jesus in the imagery of the Old Testament. The historical residue, he held, was small: Jesus was a preacher executed under Pontius Pilate, around whom legend rapidly accreted.

The book caused a sensation and effectively ended Strauss's academic career: his appointment to a chair at Zurich in 1839 provoked riots that forced the cantonal government to pension him before he could take up the post. The controversy made him a hero to the Young Hegelians and a symbol of the clash between critical scholarship and ecclesiastical authority.

Later works

Strauss wrote biographies of the reformer Ulrich von Hutten and the poet-scholar Hermann Samuel Reimarus, a life of Voltaire, and a popular biography of Jesus for the German people (1864). His late work The Old Faith and the New (1872) renounced Christianity altogether in favour of a Darwinian, monistic worldview, asking pointedly "Are we still Christians?" and answering in the negative. He died at Ludwigsburg in 1874.

Influence

Strauss's mythical method launched the first "quest for the historical Jesus", influencing Ernest Renan, Albert Schweitzer's survey The Quest of the Historical Jesus (1906), and generations of New Testament scholarship. George Eliot's English translation of The Life of Jesus (1846) was her first published work.
