Gemini is a family of multimodal large language models (LLMs) developed by Google DeepMind, and also the name of the generative artificial intelligence chatbot powered by those models, formerly known as Bard. Gemini serves as the successor to LaMDA and PaLM 2 and was positioned as a competitor to OpenAI's GPT-4.

History

Following the launch of ChatGPT in late 2022, Google declared a "code red" and accelerated its AI programme, first releasing the Bard chatbot in March 2023, powered initially by LaMDA and later PaLM 2. In April 2023 Google merged its Google Brain and DeepMind divisions into Google DeepMind. Gemini was announced on 6 December 2023 in three sizes: Ultra, Pro and Nano, and was described as natively multimodal, able to process text, images, audio, video and code.

In February 2024, Bard and Duet AI were unified under the Gemini brand, and an Android app and integration into the Google app on iOS were launched. Subsequent releases included Gemini 1.5, with a context window of up to one million (later two million) tokens, and Gemini 2.0 in December 2024, emphasising agentic capabilities and "thinking" (reasoning) models such as Gemini 2.0 Flash Thinking and later Gemini 2.5 models with built-in reasoning.

Models

The Gemini family has been released in tiers such as Nano (for on-device tasks), Flash (optimised for speed and cost) and Pro (for advanced reasoning and coding). Models are integrated across Google products including Search (AI Overviews), Workspace, Android (as an assistant replacing Google Assistant) and the Gemini mobile apps.

Reception

Gemini's launch drew scrutiny after a promotional demonstration video was criticised for being staged, and in February 2024 Google paused Gemini's ability to generate images of people after historically inaccurate depictions generated controversy; the feature was later restored with safeguards.
