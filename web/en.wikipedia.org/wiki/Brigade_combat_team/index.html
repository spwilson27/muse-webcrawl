Brigade combat team

The brigade combat team (BCT) is the basic deployable unit of manoeuvre in the United States Army. A BCT carries out offensive, defensive and stability operations and combines combat battalions with supporting artillery, reconnaissance, engineer, signal and logistics units so that it can fight as a self-contained combined-arms formation, normally under the command of a colonel or brigadier general and comprising roughly 4,400 soldiers.

Types

The Army fields three types of BCT:

  • Infantry brigade combat team (IBCT) — light infantry, including airborne and air assault variants, optimised for rapid deployment.
  • Stryker brigade combat team (SBCT) — built around the wheeled Stryker armoured vehicle family.
  • Armored brigade combat team (ABCT) — the Army's heaviest formation, built around Abrams tanks and Bradley fighting vehicles.

Organisation

A typical BCT contains three combined-arms or infantry battalions, an armed reconnaissance (cavalry) squadron, a field artillery battalion, a brigade engineer battalion and a brigade support battalion. The structure was created during the Army's transformation to a modular force in the 2000s, which reorganised the service around standardised brigades rather than divisions, so that BCTs could be rotated through deployments like interchangeable building blocks.
