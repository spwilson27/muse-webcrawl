Ben-Hadad III

Ben-Hadad III was a king of Aram-Damascus in the late 9th and early 8th centuries BC, the son and successor of Hazael. His name, meaning "son of Hadad", invokes the storm god Hadad. In the Hebrew Bible he is called simply Ben-Hadad, and some scholars identify him with the Bir-Hadad of the Zakkur stele.

Reign

Ben-Hadad III inherited a kingdom weakened by Assyrian pressure. According to the Books of Kings, Jehoash of Israel defeated him three times and recovered Israelite cities that Hazael had captured, fulfilling a prophecy attributed to Elisha. Assyrian records of Adad-nirari III describe tribute received from Damascus in this period, indicating the kingdom's declining independence.

Identification

The numbering "III" distinguishes him from Ben-Hadad I and Ben-Hadad II, earlier Aramean rulers of the same dynastic name. His reign marks the continuing decline of Damascus as the dominant power in the southern Levant before its eventual fall to Assyria in 732 BC.
