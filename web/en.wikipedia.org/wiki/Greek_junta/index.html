The Greek junta, also known as the Regime of the Colonels, was a military dictatorship that ruled Greece from 1967 to 1974. On 21 April 1967, a group of army officers led by Colonel Georgios Papadopoulos seized power in a coup d'état, citing the danger of a communist takeover ahead of scheduled elections.

Rule

The junta suspended civil liberties, banned political parties, censored the press and imprisoned, exiled or tortured thousands of suspected opponents. It promoted Greece as a bastion of "Helleno-Christian civilisation" and enjoyed initial tolerance from the United States during the Cold War. In 1973 Papadopoulos attempted a controlled transition, abolishing the monarchy and declaring a republic with himself as president; after the Athens Polytechnic uprising of November 1973, hardliner Dimitrios Ioannidis overthrew him.

Fall

The regime collapsed in July 1974 after it sponsored a coup in Cyprus that provoked a Turkish invasion of the island. Unable to manage the crisis, the military handed power to a civilian government under Konstantinos Karamanlis, beginning the Metapolitefsi — Greece's transition to democracy. The junta's leaders were tried and convicted in 1975.
