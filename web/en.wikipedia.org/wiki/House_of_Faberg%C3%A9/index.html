House of Fabergé

IndustryJewellery
Founded1842, Saint Petersburg
FounderGustav Fabergé

The House of Fabergé was a Russian jewellery firm founded in 1842 in Saint Petersburg by Gustav Fabergé. Under his son Peter Carl Fabergé it became famous worldwide, above all for the jewelled Fabergé eggs created for the Russian imperial family between 1885 and 1916.

Imperial Easter eggs

Beginning with the Hen Egg commissioned by Alexander III in 1885, the firm produced about 50 Imperial eggs, each containing an elaborate surprise. The eggs are among the most celebrated works of the jeweller's art and are now held in museums and private collections.

Other work

Fabergé's workshops employed hundreds of craftsmen and produced hardstone figurines, enamelled objects, tableware and jewellery in a style combining Russian tradition with contemporary European design.

Closure and brand

The firm was nationalised by the Bolsheviks in 1918; Peter Carl Fabergé died in exile in Switzerland in 1920. The Fabergé name was later revived by unrelated companies for jewellery and luxury goods.
