Woody plant

A woody plant is a plant that produces wood as its structural tissue and thus has a hard stem. In cold climates, woody plants further survive winter or dry season above ground, as opposed to herbaceous plants that die back to the ground until spring.

Characteristics

Woody plants are usually either trees, shrubs, or lianas. Their stems live for a number of years and grow in diameter each year through secondary growth from the vascular cambium, which lays down new xylem (wood) inward and phloem outward beneath a protective bark.

Growth and development

In spring, woody plants begin renewed growth from buds. Some lose their leaves in autumn (deciduous), while others retain foliage year-round (evergreen). Growth rings in the wood record seasonal cambial activity and are used in dendrochronology to date past climates and events.

Symbolic use

Woody plants hold symbolic, religious and economic importance worldwide — as sources of timber, fruit, resin, cork and medicine, and as long-lived landmarks. Familiar examples include oak, pine, grapevine and rose.
