Worcester, Massachusetts

Worcester (/ˈwʊstər/ WUUS-tər) is a city in and the county seat of Worcester County, Massachusetts, United States. With a population of 206,518 at the 2020 census, it is the second-most populous city in Massachusetts and in New England after Boston. Located about 40 miles (64 km) west of Boston in Central Massachusetts, it is nicknamed the "Heart of the Commonwealth" — a heart is also its official symbol — because of its position near the geographical centre of the state.

History

The area was inhabited by the Nipmuc people, who called it Quinsigamond. English settlement began in 1673 but was twice destroyed during King Philip's War and Queen Anne's War; permanent settlement dates from 1713, and the town was incorporated in 1722 and named for Worcester, England. Worcester became a centre of revolutionary activity — the newspaper Massachusetts Spy of Isaiah Thomas was published here — and grew rapidly in the 19th century with the Blackstone Canal (1828) and railroads, becoming a manufacturing hub for textiles, wire (Washburn and Moen), machinery and abrasives (Norton Company). Large Irish, French-Canadian, Swedish, Italian, Polish, Armenian and later Vietnamese and Latino communities shaped the city. Worcester hosted the first national Women's Rights Convention in 1850.

The city was devastated by an F4 tornado in June 1953 that killed 94 people. Like other New England mill cities it suffered deindustrialisation after mid-century; its economy has since re-centred on higher education and medicine — it is home to the College of the Holy Cross, Worcester Polytechnic Institute (WPI), Clark University, and UMass Memorial Health / UMass Chan Medical School — and on biotechnology.

Claims to fame

Worcester is associated with several inventions and cultural artefacts: the modern valentine card industry (Esther Howland), the smiley-face design (Harvey Ball, 1963, created for a local insurer), barbed wire and the monkey wrench are linked to its industrial firms, and Robert Goddard, Clark professor and rocket pioneer, grew up here. The Worcester Sharks and later the Worcester Railers (hockey) and the Worcester Red Sox ("WooSox", Triple-A baseball, playing at Polar Park from 2021) represent the city in sport.

Geography and climate

The city covers about 38.6 square miles of hilly terrain at the headwaters of the Blackstone River, organised traditionally around "seven hills". It has a humid continental climate with cold, snowy winters — Worcester is among the snowiest large cities in the United States — and warm summers.
