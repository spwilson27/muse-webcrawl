Dilatant

A mixture of corn starch and water ("oobleck") is a classic dilatant fluid.

A dilatant (also called a shear thickening fluid) is a non-Newtonian fluid whose viscosity increases with the rate of shear strain. Such behaviour is one type of response seen among non-Newtonian fluids; the opposite, shear thinning, is far more common in everyday materials.

Behaviour

In a dilatant fluid, gentle stirring meets little resistance, but a sudden impact or rapid shearing causes the fluid to stiffen dramatically, sometimes appearing almost solid. The effect is fully reversible: once the stress is removed, the material returns to its fluid state.

The familiar demonstration is a concentrated suspension of corn starch in water, often called "oobleck" after the Dr. Seuss story. A person can run across a pool of it because each footfall applies a large, rapid stress, while standing still causes them to sink.

Mechanism

The thickening arises because the suspended particles are forced into close contact under shear. Hydrodynamic lubrication between particles breaks down, frictional contacts form between them, and the particles jam into transient networks — a state sometimes described through dilatancy, the tendency of a densely packed granular material to expand (dilate) when sheared, first characterised by Osborne Reynolds.

Applications

Shear-thickening fluids have been investigated for flexible body armour: fabric impregnated with a dilatant suspension remains pliable in normal use but stiffens on impact. Related "active" vehicle suspension and traction-control applications use the same principle.
