Cysteine (symbol Cys or C) is a semi-essential proteinogenic amino acid with the formula HOOC–CH(NH2)–CH2–SH. It is unique among the standard amino acids for its reactive thiol (sulfhydryl) side chain, which readily oxidizes to form the disulfide-linked dimer cystine.

Role in proteins

Disulfide bonds between cysteine residues stabilize the folded structures of many extracellular and secreted proteins, including insulin and keratin — cysteine-rich keratin accounts for the strength of hair, nails, and feathers, and the chemistry of permanent-wave hair treatments works by breaking and reforming these bonds. Cysteine thiols also act as catalytic nucleophiles in cysteine proteases and as ligands for iron and zinc in metalloproteins.

Biosynthesis and metabolism

In humans, cysteine can be synthesized from methionine via the transsulfuration pathway (through homocysteine and cystathionine), making it conditionally essential when methionine is scarce. It is a precursor of the antioxidant glutathione, of taurine, and of coenzyme A.

Sources and uses

Dietary cysteine occurs in meat, eggs, dairy, and legumes. Industrially, L-cysteine is used as a food additive (E920, a dough conditioner) and supplement; its derivative N-acetylcysteine (NAC) is a medicine for paracetamol (acetaminophen) overdose and a mucolytic.
