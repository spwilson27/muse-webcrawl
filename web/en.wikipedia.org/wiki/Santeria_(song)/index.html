Santeria (song)

Artist: Sublime
Album: Sublime
Released: January 7, 1997 (single)
Genre: Reggae rock, ska punk
Length: 3:03
Writers: Bradley Nowell, Floyd Gaugh, Eric Wilson

"Santeria" is a song by American ska punk band Sublime from their third, self-titled album (1996). It was released as a single on January 7, 1997. Although the single did not reach the Billboard Hot 100 at the time, it became one of the band's signature songs and a staple of alternative rock radio.

Background

The song blends reggae, ska, and punk influences characteristic of the Long Beach band's sound. Its lyrics, written by lead singer Bradley Nowell, describe a man's regret and jealous rage after his girlfriend leaves him for another man; the title references Santería, the Afro-Cuban religion, obliquely through the narrator's talk of "voodoo" and what he "really wanna know". The guitar solo is widely noted, and the bass line is a reworking of one from the band's earlier song "Lincoln Highway Dub".

Music video and reception

The music video, released after Nowell's death from a heroin overdose in May 1996, features Lou Dog — Nowell's Dalmatian — and was shot in San Pedro, California; it received heavy rotation on MTV. "Santeria" has been certified platinum in the United States, covered by numerous artists, and appeared in video games such as Guitar Hero World Tour. It reached No. 3 on the Billboard Modern Rock Tracks chart.
