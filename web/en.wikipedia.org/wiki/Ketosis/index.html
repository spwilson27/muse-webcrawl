Ketosis

Ketosis is a metabolic state characterized by elevated levels of ketone bodies in the blood or urine. Ketones are produced naturally by the liver when glucose is in short supply, such as during fasting, prolonged exercise, or a very low-carbohydrate diet. The three ketone bodies are acetoacetate, beta-hydroxybutyrate, and acetone.

Physiology

When blood glucose and insulin levels fall, the body breaks down stored fat into fatty acids, which the liver converts into ketone bodies through ketogenesis. These ketones serve as an alternative fuel for the brain, heart, and muscles. Mild ketosis is a normal physiological response and is distinct from ketoacidosis, a dangerous condition seen mainly in uncontrolled type 1 diabetes in which ketone levels rise far higher and blood pH falls.

Ketogenic diet

A ketogenic diet — high in fat, moderate in protein, and very low in carbohydrate — is used to induce nutritional ketosis. Such diets were developed in the 1920s to treat epilepsy in children and remain an option for drug-resistant epilepsy. They have also been studied for weight loss, type 2 diabetes, and various neurological conditions, though long-term evidence is mixed.

Measurement

Ketosis can be assessed by urine strips (detecting acetoacetate), breath analyzers (detecting acetone), or blood meters measuring beta-hydroxybutyrate, the most accurate method. Nutritional ketosis is often defined as blood ketone concentrations of roughly 0.5–3.0 mmol/L.
