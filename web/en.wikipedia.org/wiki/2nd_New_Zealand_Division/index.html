The 2nd New Zealand Division was an infantry division of the New Zealand Military Forces raised for service in the Second World War. Commanded for most of its existence by Major-General Bernard Freyberg, it was the principal fighting formation of the 2nd New Zealand Expeditionary Force and developed a formidable reputation fighting in Greece, Crete, North Africa, and Italy.

Formation and early campaigns

The division was assembled in Egypt in early 1941 from the first echelons of the 2NZEF. Its first campaigns were disasters imposed by wider Allied defeat: the division was evacuated from Greece in April 1941 and then fought in the defence of Crete, where it could not prevent the German airborne invasion despite inflicting heavy losses at Maleme airfield.

North Africa

Rebuilt, the division fought through the North African campaign as a key infantry component of the Eighth Army, taking part in Operation Crusader, the fighting at Minqar Qaim — where it broke out of encirclement at bayonet point — both battles of El Alamein, the pursuit across Libya, and the battles of Medenine, Tebaga Gap and Enfidaville in Tunisia. At Takrouna in April 1943, Lance Sergeant Haane Manahi's actions became one of the division's most celebrated feats. The famous Māori Battalion served as one of its infantry battalions throughout.

Italy

Converted to a mixed division with an armoured brigade, the New Zealanders fought in Italy from late 1943, enduring the costly battles of the Sangro, the Winter Line, and Cassino, where repeated assaults in February and March 1944 failed to take the monastery position. The division advanced up the Adriatic coast and across the Po Valley in 1945, liberating Trieste at the war's end. Some 11,625 New Zealanders of the 2NZEF died during the war, the majority from the division.
