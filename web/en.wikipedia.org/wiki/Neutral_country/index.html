Neutral country

A neutral country is a state that does not take sides in a war or conflict and does not join military alliances. Neutrality may be declared for a specific conflict or adopted as a permanent policy — permanent neutrality — recognised by other states or guaranteed by treaty. The rights and duties of neutral states in wartime are codified in the Hague Conventions of 1907, which require belligerents to respect neutral territory and require the neutral state to intern belligerent troops entering it and to prevent its territory being used for military operations.

Forms of neutrality

Armed neutrality is neutrality backed by military strength sufficient to deter attack, historically practised by Switzerland and Sweden. A neutralised state has its neutrality imposed or guaranteed by a treaty among the great powers, as with Belgium (1839) and Luxembourg (1867). Non-alignment, as practised by members of the Cold War Non-Aligned Movement such as India and Yugoslavia, avoided formal alliances with either superpower bloc without adopting legal neutrality. Ireland describes its policy as "military neutrality" while participating in EU political structures.

During the Second World War, neutral states including Switzerland, Sweden, Spain, Portugal, Ireland and Turkey navigated intense pressure from both sides, trading with belligerents while trying to avoid invasion. Several neutrals were nevertheless occupied, including Denmark, Norway, the Netherlands, Belgium and Luxembourg. Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Finland joined NATO in 2023 and Sweden in 2024, substantially shrinking the ranks of European neutrals and prompting debate in remaining neutral states about the meaning of their status.
