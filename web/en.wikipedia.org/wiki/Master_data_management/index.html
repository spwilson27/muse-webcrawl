Master data management

Master data management (MDM) is a set of tools and processes used by organisations to maintain a single, accurate view of data about their core business entities — such as customers, products, suppliers, employees, and locations — across all their applications and systems. Master data is distinguished from transactional data (orders, sales, events) and from unstructured data; it refers to the persistent, non-transactional identifiers and attributes of the business.

Purpose

Large organisations often store information about the same entity in multiple systems, leading to duplicates, inconsistencies, and errors. MDM aims to produce a single "golden record" for each entity by matching records from different systems, merging duplicates, resolving conflicts, and applying data-quality rules. The resulting master data is shared across the organisation so that the same customer or product is treated consistently by finance, sales, support, and analytics systems.

Implementation models

Implementations of MDM include registry models (which simply link records), consolidation hubs (which aggregate master data for reporting), coexistence models, and transaction hubs (in which the central system is the authoritative source for creating and maintaining master data). MDM and related approaches are used in data governance, customer relationship management, and enterprise resource planning, and are closely connected to data quality management and data architecture.
