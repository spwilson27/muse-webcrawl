Leon Botstein (born December 14, 1946) is a Swiss-American educator, historian, and conductor, best known as the long-serving president of Bard College in Annandale-on-Hudson, New York, a post he has held since 1975, when he became, at 29, one of the youngest college presidents in American history. He is also the music director of the American Symphony Orchestra and founder of the Bard Music Festival.

Early life and education

Botstein was born in Zurich, Switzerland, to Polish-Jewish parents and immigrated to the United States as a young child, growing up in the Bronx, New York. A violin prodigy, he studied at the High School of Music & Art and later at the University of Chicago, where he earned his bachelor's degree at 19. He received his PhD in history from Harvard University in 1985. Before Bard, he served from 1970 to 1975 as president of Franconia College in New Hampshire.

Presidency of Bard College

When Botstein arrived at Bard, the small liberal arts college was near bankruptcy with a diminished enrollment. Over five decades he transformed it into a wealthy, internationally connected institution distinguished by innovative programs: the Bard Prison Initiative, which provides college education to incarcerated people; early-college high schools in several American cities; international partnerships in Russia, Central Asia, and Palestine; and the addition of graduate programs including the Levy Economics Institute. He is known for reinstating intellectual rigor in admissions, including the Bard Entrance Examination, an essay-based alternative to standardized tests.

Musical career

As a conductor, Botstein has led the American Symphony Orchestra since 1992 and is noted for thematic programming and revivals of neglected works. In 1990 he founded the Bard Music Festival, held each summer at the Richard B. Fisher Center for the Performing Arts, designed by Frank Gehry. He was also artistic co-director of the Summerscape festival and has edited The Musical Quarterly.
