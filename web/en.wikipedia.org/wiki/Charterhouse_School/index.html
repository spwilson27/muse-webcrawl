Charterhouse is a public school (English independent boarding school for pupils aged 13–18) in Godalming, Surrey, England. Founded in 1611 by Thomas Sutton at the London Charterhouse, it moved to its present site in 1872. It is one of the original seven public schools named in the Public Schools Act 1868 and a member of the Rugby Group.

Charterhouse's main buildings at Godalming

History

Sutton's foundation of 1611 established a school for poor scholars alongside the almshouse at the former Carthusian monastery in Smithfield, London. Over the 18th and early 19th centuries Charterhouse grew into one of England's leading schools, though constrained by its urban site. Under headmaster William Haig Brown, the school purchased land on a hill above Godalming and moved there in 1872, building the chapel, halls, and boarding houses of the present campus, designed in the Gothic Revival style.

School life

Charterhouse educates around 800 pupils in a dozen boarding houses. Girls were first admitted to the sixth form in the 1970s, and the school has moved to full co-education across all year groups in the 2020s. It is noted for music, drama, and sport; its footballers were influential in the early history of association football — Old Carthusians won the FA Cup in 1881, and the school played under both association and its own handling codes in the game's formative years.

Notable alumni

Old Carthusians include John Wesley (founder of Methodism), William Makepeace Thackeray, Robert Baden-Powell (founder of Scouting), Ralph Vaughan Williams, Max Beerbohm, and members of the band Genesis, who formed at the school in the 1960s.
