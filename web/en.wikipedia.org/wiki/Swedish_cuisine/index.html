Swedish cuisine

Swedish cuisine is the traditional food of Sweden, shaped by the country's northern climate, long winters and reliance on preserved foods. It centres on dairy products, crisp and soft breads, berries, stone fruits, beef, chicken, lamb, pork, eggs and seafood. Potatoes, especially new potatoes served with dill, are a staple, and simple, hearty flavours predominate.

Traditional dishes

Internationally the best-known Swedish dish is köttbullar — Swedish meatballs, typically served with cream sauce (gräddsås), mashed or boiled potatoes, lingonberry jam and pickled cucumber. Other traditional dishes include:

  • Pytt i panna — a hash of fried diced meat, onions and potatoes, served with fried egg and pickled beetroot
  • Raggmunk — potato pancakes served with fried pork and lingonberries
  • Ärtsoppa — yellow pea soup, traditionally eaten on Thursdays with pancakes for dessert
  • Gravlax — salmon cured in salt, sugar and dill
  • Surströmming — fermented Baltic herring, famed for its powerful odour
  • Smörgåsbord — a buffet of hot and cold dishes served at celebrations, especially Christmas (julbord)

Breads and pastries

Crispbread (knäckebröd) has been baked in Sweden for over a thousand years. Sweetened breads and buns are common, notably the cinnamon bun (kanelbulle), cardamom buns, and the saffron bun (lussekatt) served on Saint Lucia's Day. The semla, a cardamom bun filled with almond paste and whipped cream, is eaten around Shrove Tuesday. The coffee break known as fika, accompanied by pastries, is a central social institution.

Beverages

Coffee consumption in Sweden is among the highest in the world. Traditional alcoholic drinks include snaps (aquavit) drunk at festive meals, punsch, and glögg (mulled wine) at Christmas. Milk is a common mealtime drink, as is filmjölk, a fermented milk product eaten at breakfast.

New Nordic cuisine

Since the early 2000s, chefs such as those behind Stockholm's high-end restaurants have reinterpreted traditional ingredients under the banner of New Nordic cuisine, emphasising seasonal, local and foraged produce.
