Yuan Shikai (16 September 1859 – 6 June 1916) was a Chinese general and statesman who played a decisive role in the end of the Qing dynasty and the early Republic of China. As commander of the modernised Beiyang Army — the strongest military force in China — he held the balance of power during the 1911 Revolution and negotiated the abdication of the last emperor, Puyi, in exchange for the presidency of the new republic.

Rise to power

Yuan made his reputation as imperial resident and military trainer in Korea, then as a reforming commander in Zhili, where he built the Beiyang Army into a professional force loyal to himself. He was sidelined after the Empress Dowager Cixi's death but recalled in 1911 when revolution broke out, becoming premier and commander-in-chief of imperial forces.

Presidency and the Hongxian monarchy

As president from 1912, Yuan consolidated power, suppressed the "Second Revolution" of Sun Yat-sen's supporters, dissolved parliament and ruled increasingly as a dictator. In late 1915 he accepted a manufactured offer of the throne, proclaiming himself the Hongxian Emperor and founding a new dynasty. The move provoked the National Protection War, with provinces declaring independence in revolt. Yuan abandoned the monarchy after 83 days and died soon afterwards, in June 1916, leaving China to fragment into the Warlord Era.
