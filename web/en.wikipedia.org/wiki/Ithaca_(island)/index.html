Ithaca (Greek: Ιθάκη, Ithaki), also spelled Ithaka, is a Greek island in the Ionian Sea, off the northeast coast of Cephalonia, from which it is separated by the Strait of Ithaca. It is administered as a municipality within the regional unit of Ithaca, part of the Ionian Islands region. The island is world-famous as the legendary home of Odysseus, hero of Homer's Odyssey, whose ten-year journey home from Troy structures the poem.

Geography

Ithaca has an area of 117.8 square kilometres and consists essentially of two mountainous lobes joined by a narrow isthmus; its long, indented coastline features deep bays, of which the Gulf of Molos is the largest. The highest peak is Mount Neriton (806 m), named after the mountain mentioned by Homer. The capital and largest settlement is Vathy (Vathi), on a sheltered bay in the south-east. Other villages include Stavros, Frikes and Kioni in the north. The island is green and relatively fertile by Aegean standards, with olive groves, vineyards and cypresses.

History

Ithaca has been inhabited since the Neolithic period; Mycenaean remains, including finds at the Cave of the Nymphs near Vathy and at Agios Athanasios in the north, attest to occupation in the Late Bronze Age, though whether modern Ithaca is identical with Homeric Ithaca has been debated since antiquity — some scholars have proposed neighbouring islands or the Paliki peninsula of Cephalonia instead. In classical times the island formed part of the Cephalonian sphere. It later passed through Roman, Byzantine, Norman and Venetian hands; the Venetians ruled from 1504 until 1797. After a brief French interlude and a period in the Septinsular Republic, Ithaca came under the British-dominated United States of the Ionian Islands until union with Greece in 1864. The catastrophic 1953 Ionian earthquake destroyed much of Vathy, which was rebuilt.

Economy and culture

Today the island's economy rests on tourism, small-scale agriculture (olives, grapes, currants) and fishing; it is reached by ferry from Patras, Cephalonia and Lefkada. The association with Odysseus — celebrated in Cavafy's poem Ithaka (1911), which made the island a byword for the meaning found in a journey — draws literary pilgrims, and sites such as the School of Homer at Agios Athanasios are shown to visitors.
