Brevity code

Brevity codes compress common tactical messages into short code words.

Brevity codes are used by police, firefighters, medical personnel and the military to convey information quickly and accurately over voice radio channels. A brevity code is a word or phrase, often defined in an official publication, intended to correspond to a complete sentence or a complex concept — for example the NATO air brevity word "Fox Three", meaning that an active radar-guided missile has been launched, or "Bingo", meaning an aircraft has reached the minimum fuel state needed to return to base.

Types

Well-known systems include police ten-codes in North America, the NATO phonetic alphabet (strictly a spelling alphabet rather than a brevity code), Q codes from telegraphy, and the multiservice tactical brevity codes standardised among the United States and allied armed forces in publications such as APP-7 / MTTP.

Use

Brevity codes reduce transmission time and misunderstanding in noisy channels. They are not intended as secrecy devices, since most are published or widely known. In recent years some emergency services have moved toward plain language to improve interoperability between agencies.
