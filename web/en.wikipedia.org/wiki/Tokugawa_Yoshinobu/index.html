Prince Tokugawa Yoshinobu (徳川 慶喜; 28 October 1837 – 22 November 1913), also known as Keiki, was the 15th and last shōgun of the Tokugawa shogunate of Japan. His resignation in 1867 ended over 260 years of Tokugawa rule and paved the way for the Meiji Restoration.

Early life and rise

Yoshinobu was the seventh son of Tokugawa Nariaki, the reform-minded daimyō of Mito, and was adopted into the Hitotsubashi branch of the Tokugawa family, making him eligible for the shogunate. A leading candidate in the succession dispute of 1858, he lost to Tokugawa Iemochi and was placed under house arrest after the Ansei Purge. He returned to prominence as guardian to the young shōgun Iemochi and as shogunal inspector-general of military affairs, pushing for reform of the shogunate along French lines. On Iemochi's death in 1866, Yoshinobu reluctantly accepted appointment as the 15th shōgun.

End of the shogunate

Faced with mounting pressure from the Satsuma and Chōshū domains and the imperial court, Yoshinobu in November 1867 returned political authority to Emperor Meiji (the Taisei Hōkan), hoping to retain a leading role in a new council of daimyō. When imperial forces instead declared the restoration of direct imperial rule and demanded his resignation and lands, fighting broke out in the Boshin War (1868–1869). After the defeat at Toba–Fushimi, Yoshinobu withdrew to Edo, surrendered Edo Castle without a siege, and retired into seclusion.

Later life

Yoshinobu lived quietly in Shizuoka, pursuing photography, cycling, hunting, and oil painting, largely avoiding politics. In 1902 the Meiji government granted him the title of prince (kōshaku) and permitted him to establish his own Tokugawa Yoshinobu house in the peerage, partly in recognition of his role in Japan's peaceful transition. He died in Tokyo in 1913 and is buried at Yanaka Cemetery. Historians generally regard him as an able, reform-minded leader whose decision to relinquish power helped spare Japan a prolonged civil war.
