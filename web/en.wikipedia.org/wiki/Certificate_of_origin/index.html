Certificate of origin

A certificate of origin (often abbreviated CO or CoO) is a document used in international trade that certifies that goods in a particular export shipment are wholly obtained, produced, manufactured or processed in a particular country. It declares the "nationality" of the product for customs purposes.

Types

Non-preferential certificates state origin without claiming any tariff benefit and are issued or certified by chambers of commerce in many countries. Preferential certificates support claims for reduced or zero duty under a free trade agreement or a preference scheme such as the Generalized System of Preferences; examples include the EUR.1 movement certificate in Europe and certificates issued under agreements such as USMCA.

Rules of origin

Whether goods qualify as originating is determined by rules of origin: typically wholly obtained status, a change of tariff heading, or a minimum percentage of value added in the country claimed. Incorrect certificates can lead to duty demands, penalties and loss of trading privileges.

Issuance

Exporters usually prepare the certificate, which may be stamped by a chamber of commerce or customs authority; increasingly, electronic certificates of origin (eCO) are used to reduce fraud and processing time.
