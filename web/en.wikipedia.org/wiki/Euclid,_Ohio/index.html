Euclid is a city in Cuyahoga County, Ohio, United States, an inner-ring suburb of Cleveland located on the southern shore of Lake Erie. Its population was 49,692 at the 2020 census. The city is named after Euclid, the Greek mathematician, reportedly because the surveyors who laid out the township admired his work.

History

Euclid Township was surveyed in 1796 as part of the Connecticut Western Reserve and settled from 1798; the township's early economy was agricultural, later notable for grape growing and wineries along the lake. The village incorporated in 1903 and became a city in 1930. In the 20th century Euclid developed as an industrial and residential suburb, hosting plants of companies such as Lincoln Electric nearby and, in 1949, the Euclid Homes housing project became the subject of a landmark federal court ruling striking down a residents-only referendum requirement for public housing. Euclid Creek, which flows through the city to the lake, was the focus of early conservation work by naturalist and city namesake of a local park system.

Geography and demographics

The city covers about 11.5 square miles, including a long Lake Erie shoreline with parks such as Sims Park. Major routes include Interstate 90 and Lakeland Boulevard. Historically home to large Slovenian and other European immigrant communities — Euclid's Slovenian Home is a noted cultural institution — the city has become increasingly diverse, with an African American majority or plurality in recent censuses. It is served by the Euclid City School District and by Lakeland Community College nearby, and its city hall complex hosts the Euclid Historical Society and Museum.
