Swiss International Air Lines AG, stylised as SWISS, is the flag carrier of Switzerland, based at Zurich Airport. It operates scheduled services in Europe and to North America, South America, Africa, and Asia. The airline was formed in 2002 following the bankruptcy of Swissair, Switzerland's previous flag carrier, building on the regional airline Crossair. It has been a wholly owned subsidiary of the Lufthansa Group since 2007 and a member of Star Alliance since 2006.

History

After Swissair was grounded in October 2001 and entered liquidation, the Swiss government and private investors funded a new airline based on Crossair, which took over much of Swissair's fleet, staff, and route network. Swiss International Air Lines began operations on 31 March 2002. After several years of losses, the airline was gradually acquired by Lufthansa between 2005 and 2007, while retaining its brand, management, and Swiss identity.

Fleet

Swiss operates a fleet of Airbus aircraft. Its long-haul fleet centres on the Airbus A330-300 and the Boeing 777-300ER, which serves as its flagship, while short- and medium-haul services are flown by the Airbus A220 (originally the Bombardier CSeries, for which Swiss was the launch operator in 2016) and the A320 family, including the A320neo and A321neo. The airline has also ordered Airbus A350-900s to modernise its long-haul fleet.

Destinations and services

Swiss serves more than 100 destinations in over 40 countries. The airline markets a premium image emphasising Swiss hospitality, with regional Swiss cuisine in its premium cabins and Swiss chocolate offered to passengers. Its business model combines its own operations with those of its subsidiaries, including leisure carrier Edelweiss Air.
