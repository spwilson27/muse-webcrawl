Shortcrust pastry

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Shortcrust pastry is a type of pastry often used for the base of a tart, quiche, meat pie or (in British English) flan. Shortcrust pastry can be used to make both sweet and savory pies such as apple pie, quiche, lemon meringue or chicken pie. It is the simplest and most common pastry: a crumbly, tender dough made by rubbing fat into flour before binding with a small amount of water. The term "short" refers to the texture produced when fat shortens the gluten strands in the flour, preventing them from forming a tough, elastic network.

Ingredients and proportions

The classic ratio is half fat to flour by weight, with just enough cold water to bind — for example 200 g plain flour, 100 g fat (butter, lard, or a mixture; lard gives extra shortness, butter better flavour), a pinch of salt, and 2–3 tablespoons of cold water. Sweet shortcrust (pâte sucrée in French) adds sugar and often egg yolk; rich shortcrust uses egg yolk in place of some water. French pâte brisée is a close relative, and pâte sablée a richer, more biscuit-like sweet version.

Method

Fat is cut or rubbed into the flour until the mixture resembles breadcrumbs, keeping everything as cold as possible to avoid melting the fat. Water is added sparingly and the dough brought together with minimal handling; overworking develops gluten and toughens the result, while too much water makes the pastry shrink and harden. After chilling, the dough is rolled out and used to line a tin, and is often baked "blind" — weighted with baking beans or dried pulses over parchment — to keep the base crisp before adding wet fillings. Baked shortcrust should be pale gold, crisp and crumbly, with no raw or soggy base.

Chemistry

The tenderness of shortcrust results from fat coating flour particles and inhibiting gluten formation, so the baked dough fractures into short, crumbly layers rather than a chewy matrix. Chilling rests the gluten that does form and re-firms the fat, improving both handling and texture.

Uses

  • Savoury: quiche Lorraine, meat pies, Cornish pasties, sausage rolls.
  • Sweet: fruit tarts and pies, lemon tart, mince pies, Bakewell tart, jam tarts.
