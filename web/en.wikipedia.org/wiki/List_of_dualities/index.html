List of dualities

In mathematics, a duality is a correspondence between two structures, concepts or theories in which statements about one can be translated into statements about the other, often by an order-reversing or involutive transformation. Dualities also appear in physics, philosophy and other fields. This list collects notable examples.

Mathematics

  • Projective duality — points and lines (or hyperplanes) interchange in projective geometry; a theorem's dual swaps "point" and "line".
  • Dual polyhedron — faces and vertices interchange (cube ↔ octahedron, dodecahedron ↔ icosahedron).
  • Dual vector space — the space of linear functionals on a vector space; double dual is canonically isomorphic in finite dimensions.
  • Dual graph — faces and vertices of a planar embedding interchange.
  • Poincaré duality — homology and cohomology groups of a closed orientable manifold are isomorphic with a degree shift.
  • Alexander duality — relates homology of a subset of a sphere to cohomology of its complement.
  • Serre duality — duality for coherent sheaf cohomology on algebraic varieties.
  • Pontryagin duality — locally compact abelian groups correspond to their character groups; the Fourier transform is its analytic expression.
  • Stone duality — Boolean algebras correspond contravariantly to Stone spaces.
  • Gelfand duality — commutative C*-algebras correspond to compact Hausdorff spaces.
  • Isbell duality — spectrum and cospectrum between geometry and algebra.
  • Duality of linear programming — every linear program has a dual whose optimum equals the primal optimum (strong duality).
  • Legendre transformation — convex conjugacy relating functions; underlies Lagrangian/Hamiltonian mechanics duality.
  • Wave–particle and Fourier duality — position and momentum representations.
  • Duality in category theory — reversing all arrows gives the opposite category and dual statements (limits ↔ colimits, products ↔ coproducts).
  • Duality (order theory) — meet and join, top and bottom interchange in lattices.
  • Duality in logic — De Morgan duals swap ∧/∨ and ∀/∃; classical negation is involutive.
  • Koszul duality, Verdier duality, Matlis duality, Tate duality, Spanier–Whitehead duality, Grothendieck duality.

Physics

  • Electric–magnetic duality — symmetry of Maxwell's equations under exchanging electric and magnetic fields.
  • Wave–particle duality — quantum objects exhibit both wave and particle behaviour.
  • S-duality — strong/weak coupling duality in quantum field theory and string theory.
  • T-duality — equivalence of string theories on circles of reciprocal radius.
  • Mirror symmetry — equivalence of string theories on pairs of Calabi–Yau manifolds.
  • AdS/CFT correspondence — holographic duality between gravity in anti-de Sitter space and a conformal field theory on its boundary.
  • Kramers–Wannier duality — high/low temperature duality of the Ising model.

Other fields

  • Dualism (philosophy of mind) — mind and body as distinct substances or properties.
  • Cosmic dualities such as yin and yang.
  • Duality of structure and agency in social theory.
