Munich air disaster
Date: 6 February 1958
Site: Munich-Riem Airport, West Germany
Aircraft: Airspeed Ambassador, British European Airways Flight 609
Fatalities: 23 of 44 on board

The Munich air disaster occurred on 6 February 1958, when British European Airways Flight 609, carrying the Manchester United football team — the "Busby Babes" — crashed on its third attempt to take off from a slush-covered runway at Munich-Riem Airport, West Germany.

The crash

The team was returning from a European Cup match against Red Star Belgrade in Yugoslavia, with a refuelling stop in Munich. Of the 44 people on board, 23 died, including eight players: Geoff Bent, Roger Byrne, Eddie Colman, Duncan Edwards (who died in hospital 15 days later), Mark Jones, David Pegg, Tommy Taylor, and Liam Whelan. Manager Matt Busby was gravely injured; Bobby Charlton and goalkeeper Harry Gregg survived, Gregg famously rescuing passengers from the wreckage.

Cause and aftermath

German investigators initially blamed the captain, James Thain, for failing to de-ice the wings; he was cleared a decade later when slush on the runway was shown to have prevented the aircraft reaching takeoff speed. United, rebuilt by Busby, won the European Cup ten years later in 1968. The disaster is commemorated each year at Old Trafford, where the Munich clock stands stopped at the time of the crash.
