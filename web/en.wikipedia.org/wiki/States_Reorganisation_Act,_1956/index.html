States Reorganisation Act, 1956

India's states and union territories after the 1956 reorganisation.

The States Reorganisation Act, 1956 was a major reform of the boundaries of India's states and territories, implementing the recommendations of the States Reorganisation Commission. It reorganised the states principally along linguistic lines and came into force on 1 November 1956, together with the Seventh Amendment to the Constitution of India, which abolished the earlier classification of states into Part A, Part B, and Part C categories.

Background

At independence in 1947, India consisted of British-administered provinces and more than 500 princely states, which were integrated into the union in patterns that owed more to history than to language or administration. Demands for states organized by language grew steadily. In December 1952, Potti Sreeramulu died after a fast in support of a separate Telugu-speaking state, and the resulting unrest led the government to create Andhra State in 1953. Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru then appointed the States Reorganisation Commission in December 1953, chaired by Fazal Ali, with H. N. Kunzru and K. M. Panikkar as members. The commission reported in 1955.

Changes

The Act redrew the map of India into 14 states and 6 union territories. Among its principal changes:

  • Kerala was created by merging Travancore-Cochin with the Malabar district of Madras State.
  • Karnataka (as enlarged Mysore State) united the Kannada-speaking districts of Bombay, Hyderabad, and Coorg.
  • Andhra Pradesh combined Andhra State with the Telugu-speaking Telangana region of Hyderabad State.
  • Madras State was reduced, transferring its southern districts to Kerala and receiving Kanyakumari in return.
  • Bombay State remained bilingual (Marathi and Gujarati) until it was divided in 1960 into Maharashtra and Gujarat.
  • New union territories included Himachal Pradesh and a merged Punjab hill region, while Vidarbha was merged into Bombay rather than becoming a separate state as the commission had proposed.

Legacy

The Act settled most linguistic boundaries in southern and western India, but further reorganisations followed: Punjab was divided in 1966, creating Haryana and transferring hill areas to Himachal Pradesh, and new states continue to be formed, most recently Telangana in 2014. The 1956 settlement remains the foundational template of the Indian federation.
