Ifá

Ifá is a faith and divination system originating among the Yoruba people of West Africa. It is a corpus of oral literature — the odu — consulted by a priest-diviner called a babalawo ("father of secrets") to learn the will of the orisha Orunmila, the deity of wisdom and divination.

Divination

In Ifá divination, the babalawo casts sacred palm nuts (ikin) or a divining chain (opele) to generate one of 256 binary signs known as odu. Each sign is associated with a body of verses (ese Ifá) containing myths, proverbs, and prescriptions, which the diviner recites and interprets for the client, often prescribing a sacrifice (ebo) to secure a favourable outcome.

Spread

Carried by enslaved Yoruba people to the Americas, Ifá gave rise to related traditions such as Cuban Santería (Regla de Ocha) and Ifá practice in Brazil and the Caribbean. In 2005 UNESCO proclaimed the Ifa Divination System a Masterpiece of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity, and inscribed it on the Representative List in 2008.
