HMG-CoA reductase

HMG-CoA reductase (3-hydroxy-3-methyl-glutaryl-coenzyme A reductase, officially HMGCR) is the rate-controlling enzyme of the mevalonate pathway, which produces cholesterol and other isoprenoids. It catalyses the conversion of HMG-CoA to mevalonate, a four-electron oxidoreduction.

Function and regulation

The enzyme is a target of feedback inhibition by cholesterol: when sterol levels rise, transcription of HMGCR is suppressed via SREBP signalling and the enzyme is degraded. It is anchored in the membrane of the endoplasmic reticulum and has eight transmembrane domains.

Clinical significance

HMG-CoA reductase is the target of statins, a class of cholesterol-lowering drugs that competitively inhibit the enzyme, reducing hepatic cholesterol synthesis and upregulating LDL receptors.
