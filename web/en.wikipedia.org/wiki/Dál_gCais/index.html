Dál gCais

The Dál gCais (older spelling Dál Cais) were an Irish clan and dynasty of early medieval Ireland, centred in Thomond (modern County Clare) in northern Munster. Traditionally said to descend from Cormac Cas, a son of Ailill Aulom of the Deirgtine, they rose from relative obscurity in the 10th century to dominate Munster and, briefly, all of Ireland.

Rise to power

The Dál gCais first rose to prominence under Cennétig mac Lorcáin (died 951), king of Thomond. His sons Mathgamain and Brian Boru (Brian Bóruma) transformed their fortunes: Mathgamain seized the kingship of Munster in 970, and after his murder in 976, Brian succeeded him, defeated the Norse of Limerick at the Battle of Sulcoit (967, under Mathgamain) and went on to become King of Munster (978) and eventually High King of Ireland (1002).

Brian Boru and Clontarf

Brian Boru's reign culminated in the Battle of Clontarf on Good Friday, 23 April 1014, where his forces defeated the Norse-Irish alliance of Dublin and Leinster, though Brian himself was killed. His descendants, the Ua Briain (O'Brien) dynasty, remained Kings of Thomond and supplied several High Kings, including Toirdelbach Ua Briain and Muirchertach Ua Briain.

Later history

The Dál gCais retained the lordship of Thomond for centuries and resisted Norman encroachment longer than most Irish kingdoms. Their deeds were celebrated in the 12th-century propaganda text Cogadh Gáedhel re Gallaibh ("The War of the Irish with the Foreigners"). Many prominent Irish families — O'Brien, MacMahon, Kennedy (of Ormond), and others — claim Dál gCais descent.
