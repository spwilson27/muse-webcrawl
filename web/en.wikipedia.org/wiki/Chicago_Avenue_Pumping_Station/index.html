The Chicago Avenue Pumping Station is a historic municipal water pumping station located at 811 North Michigan Avenue in the Near North Side community area of Chicago, Illinois. Designed by architect William W. Boyington and completed in 1869, the limestone building is one of the oldest structures in the downtown area and is famous for having survived the Great Chicago Fire of 1871. Together with the Chicago Water Tower, which stands directly across Michigan Avenue, it forms one of the most recognizable landmarks of the city and a symbol of Chicago's rebirth after the fire.

History

The pumping station was built in 1869 as part of Chicago's municipal water system. Water was drawn from Lake Michigan through an intake crib and a tunnel extending two miles out under the lakebed, then pumped by massive steam engines housed in the station to supply the growing city. The building's interior originally contained large Cornish beam engines capable of pumping millions of gallons of water per day.

When the Great Chicago Fire swept through the city on October 8–10, 1871, the pumping station's roof and interior machinery were destroyed, but its thick limestone walls survived. The neighboring Chicago Water Tower, also designed by Boyington, likewise survived, and the two structures became enduring symbols of the city's resilience. Water service was restored within weeks, aiding the rebuilding effort.

Architecture

Boyington designed the station in the Gothic Revival style, using yellowish Joliet limestone. The building features castellated towers, pointed-arch windows, and decorative stonework that echoes the design of the water tower across the street. Although the interior has been altered over the years for modern pumping equipment, the exterior retains its 19th-century appearance.

Later use

The station remains an active facility of the Chicago Department of Water Management. In 2003–2004, part of the building was renovated to house a visitor information center, and the Lookingglass Theatre Company established a performance space within the station's former water basin area. The building was designated a Chicago Landmark and was listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1975.
