Enoch (Hebrew: חֲנוֹךְ, Ḥănōḵ) is a biblical figure and patriarch prior to Noah's flood, the son of Jared and father of Methuselah. He was of the Antediluvian period in the Hebrew Bible. The text of the Book of Genesis says Enoch lived 365 years before he was taken by God. The passage reads: "Enoch walked with God, and he was not, for God took him" (Genesis 5:24).

In scripture and tradition

Enoch is mentioned briefly in the New Testament — in the genealogy of Jesus in Luke, in the Epistle to the Hebrews, which praises his faith, and in the Epistle of Jude, which quotes a prophecy attributed to him. His short biblical biography generated a vast body of later legend: in Jewish and early Christian tradition he became a visionary scribe and heavenly traveller, and in some traditions was identified with the archangel Metatron. In Islam he is identified with the prophet Idris.

Books of Enoch

Several works are attributed to Enoch. The Book of Enoch (1 Enoch), an apocalyptic work describing the fall of the Watchers and Enoch's journeys through heaven, is canonical only in the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church but was widely read in antiquity, surviving complete in Ge'ez translation. 2 Enoch (Slavonic Enoch) and 3 Enoch (Hebrew Enoch) are later compositions. Fragments of 1 Enoch in Aramaic were found among the Dead Sea Scrolls.
