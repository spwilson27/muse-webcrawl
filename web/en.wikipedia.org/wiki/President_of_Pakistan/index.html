President of Pakistan

The President of Pakistan (Urdu: صدرِ پاکستان) is the head of state of the Islamic Republic of Pakistan. The president is the nominal head of the executive and the supreme commander of the Pakistan Armed Forces. The office was established on 23 March 1956, when Pakistan became the first Islamic republic in the world.

Powers and role

Pakistan is a parliamentary republic, and real executive authority is exercised by the Prime Minister. The president acts on the advice of the prime minister in most matters, but retains discretionary and reserve powers, including appointing provincial governors, granting pardons and, formally, appointing the chiefs of the armed services and the chief election commissioner. The president summons and prorogues sessions of Parliament and gives assent to legislation.

Election

The president is elected indirectly by an electoral college comprising the members of the Senate, the National Assembly and the four provincial assemblies, for a term of five years, and may serve a maximum of two consecutive terms. A candidate must be a Muslim citizen of Pakistan of at least 45 years of age qualified to be elected to the National Assembly.

History

From 1947 to 1956 Pakistan was a dominion with a governor-general representing the British monarch. Iskander Mirza, the last governor-general, became the first president under the 1956 constitution. The power balance between president and prime minister has shifted repeatedly: military rulers such as Ayub Khan, Zia-ul-Haq and Pervez Musharraf concentrated authority in the presidency, while the Eighteenth Amendment (2010) restored a more parliamentary distribution of power, removing the president's power to dissolve the National Assembly unilaterally.
