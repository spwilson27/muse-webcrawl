Physical Review is a peer-reviewed scientific journal established in 1893 that publishes original research in all areas of physics. It is published by the American Physical Society (APS). Over time the single journal grew into a family of journals, including the flagship rapid-communication journal Physical Review Letters and the review journal Reviews of Modern Physics.

History

The journal was founded at Cornell University in 1893 by Edward Nichols and Ernest Merritt. The American Physical Society took over its publication in 1913. Landmark papers published in the journal include Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen's 1935 paper on quantum entanglement (the EPR paradox) and John Bardeen, Leon Cooper and John Robert Schrieffer's 1957 theory of superconductivity (BCS theory).

Journal family

In 1970 the main journal was split by subject into Physical Review A (atomic, molecular and optical physics), B (condensed matter and materials), C (nuclear physics), D (particles, fields, gravitation and cosmology) and later E (statistical, nonlinear and soft matter physics). Specialised siblings include Physical Review X (open access), Physical Review Applied, Physical Review Fluids, Physical Review Materials and Physical Review Research.

Notable papers

Among the most cited works in the Physical Review family are the EPR paper, the BCS theory paper, Bell's theorem paper (published in the short-lived journal Physics but central to the field), and many foundational papers of quantum mechanics, particle physics and condensed matter physics.
