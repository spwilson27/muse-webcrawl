Lord President of the Court of Session

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Lord President of the Court of Session is the head of the judiciary in Scotland and the presiding judge of the College of Justice, Scotland's supreme civil court. The office dates from the foundation of the College of Justice in 1532, and its holder is also, by virtue of that office, the Lord Justice General, head of the High Court of Justiciary, Scotland's supreme criminal court.

Functions

The Lord President presides over the First Division of the Inner House of the Court of Session and sits as one of the three judges of that division. As Lord Justice General, he or she is the titular head of the High Court of Justiciary, although much of the day-to-day administration of the criminal courts is delegated to the Lord Justice Clerk. The Lord President also chairs the Scottish Civil Justice Council and has statutory responsibility for the efficient disposal of business in the Scottish courts.

Appointment and rank

Lord Presidents are appointed by the monarch on the recommendation of the First Minister of Scotland, who must consult the Lord President and, if the office is vacant, the Lord Justice Clerk. The office holder ranks second in the Scottish order of precedence, after the Lord High Commissioner to the General Assembly of the Church of Scotland when it is sitting. Under the Scotland Act 1998 the Lord President is a member of the Judicial Committee procedures for removal only through an address procedure involving the Scottish Parliament.

History

Notable holders of the office include Lord Stair, author of the influential Institutions of the Law of Scotland (1681), and Lord Clyde in the twentieth century. In 2015 Lord Carloway became Lord President; he was succeeded in 2025 by Lord Pentland, the first holder in modern times to have served as a solicitor advocate before appointment to the bench.
