Robert Jay Lifton


Born: May 16, 1926, Brooklyn, New York, U.S.
Occupation: Psychiatrist, author
Known for: Studies of thought reform and war survivors

Robert Jay Lifton (born May 16, 1926) is an American psychiatrist and author, known for his studies of the psychological causes and effects of war and political violence, and for his theory of thought reform ("brainwashing").

Work

Serving as an Air Force psychiatrist in Japan and Korea in the 1950s, Lifton interviewed former prisoners of war and refugees from China; the research became Thought Reform and the Psychology of Totalism (1961), a classic study of coercive persuasion that identified eight criteria of ideological totalism. In Death in Life: Survivors of Hiroshima (1968), which won the National Book Award, he examined the psychological world of atomic-bomb survivors (hibakusha).

The Nazi Doctors (1986) — based on interviews with physicians who participated in the Holocaust — introduced his concept of "doubling", the splitting of the self that allowed ordinary professionals to take part in atrocity. Lifton later wrote on Vietnam veterans, apocalyptic movements such as Aum Shinrikyo, and the psychology of climate change awareness in The Climate Swerve (2017).
