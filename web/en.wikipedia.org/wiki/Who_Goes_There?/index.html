Who Goes There? is a science fiction horror novella by American author John W. Campbell, published under his pen name Don A. Stuart in the August 1938 issue of Astounding Science Fiction. The story follows the crew of an Antarctic research station who discover an alien spacecraft buried in the ice, and the shape-shifting creature that emerges from it, capable of perfectly imitating any person it kills — leading to mounting paranoia as the men struggle to determine who among them is still human.

Plot

A secondary polar expedition uncovers a magnetic anomaly and crashes investigating it, revealing an alien ship frozen in the ice for millions of years. The scientists recover the frozen pilot, which thaws and revives. The creature can assume the form and memories of any organism it absorbs. Led by the biologist Blair and the resourceful second-in-command McReady, the men develop a blood test to distinguish humans from imitations — since imitation blood will react defensively when heated — and ultimately destroy the creature before it can build a craft to escape to civilisation.

Influence and adaptations

The novella is among the most influential works of science fiction horror. It was adapted, loosely, as the 1951 film The Thing from Another World, and more faithfully as John Carpenter's 1982 film The Thing, whose blood-test scene closely follows Campbell's. A prequel film of the same name appeared in 2011. In 2018 a longer manuscript version of the novella, Frozen Hell, was published after being rediscovered among Campbell's papers.
