Street fashion is fashion that is considered to have emerged not from studios, but from grassroots streetwear. It is generally linked to youth culture and subcultures, and is most often seen in major urban centres. Unlike the "trickle-down" model, in which styles descend from haute couture to the mass market, street fashion follows a "bubble-up" pattern: innovations begin with ordinary young people and are later adopted and commercialised by designers.

History

Distinctive street styles have been documented since the mid-20th century — British Teddy Boys, mods and rockers, punks in the 1970s, and hip-hop fashion in the 1980s. The photographer Bill Cunningham chronicled New York street style for The New York Times for decades, and the Japanese magazine FRUiTS (founded 1997) documented the flamboyant styles of Tokyo's Harajuku district, including Lolita, decora, and visual kei fashions.

Streetwear and the mainstream

From the 1990s, streetwear brands such as Supreme grew from skate and hip-hop culture into global luxury phenomena, collaborating with houses like Louis Vuitton. Fashion weeks now feature street-style photography of attendees as an influential genre in its own right, amplified by social media.
