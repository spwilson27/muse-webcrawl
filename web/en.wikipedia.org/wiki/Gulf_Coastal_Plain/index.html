Gulf Coastal Plain

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Gulf Coastal Plain is a physiographic province of low, flat to gently rolling land extending along the northern shore of the Gulf of Mexico from Florida in the east through Alabama, Mississippi, Louisiana and Texas, and southward into northeastern Mexico. It forms the southern part of the Atlantic–Gulf Coastal Plain and reaches inland as far as the Fall Line and the Mississippi embayment up to southern Illinois.

Geology

The plain is built of Cretaceous and Cenozoic marine and river sediments — sands, clays, marls and limestones — deposited as the Gulf shoreline advanced and retreated, and tilted gently seaward. Near the coast lie marshes, barrier islands and river deltas; inland bands of slightly older strata form low cuestas and belts such as Alabama's Black Belt prairies. Salt domes rising through the sediments trap much of the region's oil and natural gas.

Ecology and land use

Longleaf and loblolly pine flatwoods, bottomland hardwood swamps and cypress bayous originally covered most of the province; much has been cleared for agriculture (cotton, soybeans, rice, sugar cane), pasture, pine plantations and cities such as Houston, New Orleans and Mobile. The western portion grades into the Texas coastal prairies and, in Mexico, the Tamaulipan lowlands.

Climate

The plain has a humid subtropical climate with hot summers, mild winters, abundant rainfall and exposure to Gulf hurricanes, which periodically devastate its low-lying coastal zones.
