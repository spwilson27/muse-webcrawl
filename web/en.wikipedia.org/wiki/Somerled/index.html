Somerled

Somerled (Old Norse: Sumarliði; Scottish Gaelic: Somhairle; died 1164) was a mid-12th-century Norse-Gaelic lord who, through marital alliance and military conquest, rose in prominence to create a sea kingdom on the western seaboard of Scotland and the Isle of Man. He is regarded as the founder of the Lordship of the Isles and the progenitor of several Scottish clans, including Clan Donald, Clan MacDougall and Clan MacRory.

Origins

Somerled was the son of GilleBride, a lord of Argyll of mixed Norse and Gaelic descent. Later medieval genealogies traced his line back to the legendary Colla Uais, though modern historians treat his ancestry as a blend of Scandinavian and native Gaelic elements typical of the Norse-Gaelic world of the Hebrides.

Rise to power

By the 1130s Somerled had established himself as thane of Argyll. His marriage to Ragnhild, daughter of Olaf Godredsson, King of Man and the Isles, gave him a claim to the island kingdom. In 1156, Somerled defeated his brother-in-law Godred Olafsson in the naval Battle of Epiphany, and the Kingdom of the Isles was divided between them: Godred retained Man and the northern Hebrides, while Somerled took the southern Hebrides. Two years later Somerled invaded Man and took the whole kingdom.

Death

In 1164 Somerled led a great fleet — tradition says 160 ships — up the River Clyde to attack the Scottish mainland, possibly in alliance with disaffected lords or in response to the expansion of royal authority under Malcolm IV. He was killed at Renfrew, in circumstances that remain obscure; later accounts attribute his death to an assassin, a spear in battle, or sickness.

Legacy

Somerled's descendants divided his lands among his sons, founding dynasties that dominated the western seaboard for three centuries. Genetic studies of modern clan members suggest that Somerled himself was of Norse paternal ancestry. He remains a celebrated figure in Gaelic tradition as a champion who recovered the Isles from foreign rule.
