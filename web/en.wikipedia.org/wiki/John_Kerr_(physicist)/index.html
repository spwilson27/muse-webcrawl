John Kerr FRS (December 17, 1824 – August 15, 1907) was a Scottish physicist and a pioneer in the field of electro-optics. He is best known for discovering the Kerr effect, the change in the refractive index of a material in response to an applied electric field.

Life and career

Kerr was born in Ardrossan and educated at the University of Glasgow, where he studied under William Thomson, later Lord Kelvin. Ordained as a minister of the Free Church of Scotland, he instead devoted himself to science teaching, lecturing in natural philosophy at the Free Church Training College in Glasgow and later at the University of Glasgow.

Discoveries

In 1875 Kerr demonstrated that glass and other dielectric materials become doubly refracting when subjected to a strong electric field — the (electro-optic) Kerr effect, the basis of the Kerr cell used as a fast light shutter. Two years later he discovered the magneto-optic Kerr effect, in which polarised light reflected from a magnetised surface has its plane of polarisation rotated; the phenomenon remains important in studying magnetism and magnetic data storage. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1890.
