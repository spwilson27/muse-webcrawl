Treaty of Waitangi claims and settlements

Treaty of Waitangi claims and settlements are the processes by which Māori grievances arising from breaches of the Treaty of Waitangi (1840) by the New Zealand Crown have been investigated and redressed since the late twentieth century.

The Waitangi Tribunal

The Treaty of Waitangi Act 1975 established the Waitangi Tribunal to inquire into claims that Crown acts or omissions breached the principles of the Treaty; a 1985 amendment allowed investigation of historical claims back to 1840. The Tribunal issues findings and recommendations (binding only in limited categories), whose reports—such as those on the Orakei, Muriwhenua, and Ngāi Tahu claims—reshaped public understanding of the confiscations (raupatu) and land sales of the colonial era.

Settlements

Beginning in the 1990s, the Crown negotiated "full and final" settlements combining a formal apology, cash and land redress, and cultural redress such as co-governance arrangements and return of significant sites. Major settlements include Waikato-Tainui (1995, NZ$170 million, the first), Ngāi Tahu (1997, NZ$170 million), the Sealord fisheries settlement (1992), the Waikato River settlement, Tūhoe's Te Urewera settlement (2014), and the Whanganui River settlement (2017), which granted the river legal personhood. By the 2020s, settlements with most major iwi had been completed, with total redress exceeding NZ$2 billion, though debate continues over whether "full and final" clauses foreclose future claims and over remaining unsettled claims.
