H.R. 40

H.R. 40
Full titleCommission to Study and Develop Reparation Proposals for African Americans Act
IntroducedFirst in 1989 by Rep. John Conyers
Sponsor (recent)Rep. Sheila Jackson Lee (2017–2024)
SubjectStudy of slavery reparations in the United States

H.R. 40 is the popular designation of a bill introduced in the United States House of Representatives that would establish a commission to study the legacy of slavery in the United States and develop proposals for reparations for African Americans. Formally titled the Commission to Study and Develop Reparation Proposals for African Americans Act, the bill has been introduced in every Congress since 1989, originally by Representative John Conyers of Michigan, who chose the number 40 to evoke the Civil War-era promise of "forty acres and a mule".

Provisions

The bill would create a 13-member commission to examine the institution of slavery in the United States from 1619 to 1865, the subsequent de jure and de facto discrimination against freed slaves and their descendants, and the lingering effects of that discrimination on African Americans today. The commission would be empowered to hold hearings, subpoena witnesses and documents, and would report to Congress with findings and recommendations, including proposals for remedies and a formal national apology. The bill authorises an appropriation of about $12 million for the study. It does not itself authorise any payment of reparations.

Legislative history

Conyers first introduced the measure in 1989 and reintroduced it in each Congress until his resignation in 2017, without it receiving a committee vote. Representative Sheila Jackson Lee of Texas then took over as lead sponsor. The bill received its first-ever congressional hearing in the House Judiciary Subcommittee on the Constitution, Civil Rights and Civil Liberties on 19 June 2019 — Juneteenth — in an event widely covered in the national media. On 14 April 2021 the House Judiciary Committee voted to report the bill favourably, the first time in its three-decade history that it advanced out of committee; it was not brought to a vote of the full House.

Support for H.R. 40 became a notable topic in the 2020 Democratic presidential primary, and public discussion was further stimulated by Ta-Nehisi Coates's 2014 essay "The Case for Reparations", whose author testified in favour of the bill at the 2019 hearing. After Jackson Lee's death in July 2024, the bill's sponsorship in the House passed to other members, and companion measures have been introduced in the Senate.

Arguments

Supporters argue that a commission is a modest first step toward a national reckoning with slavery and its aftermath, comparable to the commission whose work led to reparations for Japanese Americans interned during the Second World War under the Civil Liberties Act of 1988. Opponents argue that present-day Americans should not be made to pay for wrongs committed generations before they were born, that identifying eligible recipients would be impractical, and that existing civil-rights and social programmes are the proper response. Polling has consistently shown reparations to be unpopular with the general public while attracting majority support among African Americans.

Related efforts

Parallel study commissions have been created at state and local level, most prominently the California Reparations Task Force (established 2020, final report 2023) and the municipal reparations programme in Evanston, Illinois, the first in the nation to disburse funds, directed at housing assistance for Black residents harmed by discriminatory housing policies.
