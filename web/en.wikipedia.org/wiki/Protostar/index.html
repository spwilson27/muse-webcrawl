Protostar

Protostar
StageEarly phase of star formation
Powered byGravitational contraction (Kelvin–Helmholtz), accretion
Observational classesClass 0, I (protostellar); II, III (pre-main-sequence)
DurationRoughly 100,000–500,000 years (Class 0/I)

A protostar is a very young star that is still gathering mass from its parent molecular cloud. It is the earliest phase in the process of stellar evolution, beginning when a dense core of a molecular cloud first collapses under its own gravity and ending when the star becomes visible and begins its contraction toward the main sequence.

Formation

Star formation starts in cold, dense cores of giant molecular clouds. When a core becomes gravitationally unstable — often described by the Jeans criterion — it collapses, at first isothermally, then heating as it becomes opaque to its own radiation. The central hydrostatic object that forms is the protostar, surrounded by a rotating infalling envelope and, as collapse proceeds, a circumstellar accretion disk formed because the cloud's angular momentum prevents direct infall onto the star.

Evolution and outflows

The protostar grows by accretion through the disk while radiating away gravitational energy; its luminosity is dominated by accretion and contraction rather than nuclear fusion, although deuterium burning may begin in the core. Magnetic fields channel spectacular bipolar jets and outflows (seen as Herbig–Haro objects) that clear the surrounding envelope. Observationally, deeply embedded Class 0 sources are visible mainly in the far-infrared and submillimetre; Class I sources show infrared excess from envelope plus disk; Class II and III objects are pre-main-sequence stars (T Tauri stars) with disks only.

End of the protostellar phase

Once the envelope is dispersed and the star becomes optically visible, it appears on the "birthline" of the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram and contracts along Hayashi tracks until core temperatures ignite sustained hydrogen fusion, marking arrival on the zero-age main sequence. The remaining disk may form a planetary system.
