Edward Elgar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Edward Elgar with his bicycle in the Malvern Hills: walrus moustache, tweeds, the Severn valley spread below]

Sir Edward William Elgar (2 June 1857 – 23 February 1934) was an English composer, many of whose works have entered the British and international classical concert repertoire. Among his best-known compositions are orchestral works including the Enigma Variations, the Pomp and Circumstance Marches, concertos for violin and cello, and two symphonies. He also composed choral works, including The Dream of Gerontius, chamber music and songs.

A self-taught Catholic shopkeeper's son from Worcestershire, Elgar broke through at 42 with the Enigma Variations (1899), each movement a portrait of a friend, its 'Nimrod' movement becoming Britain's music of remembrance. 'Land of Hope and Glory', set to the first Pomp and Circumstance march, is a second national anthem. His Cello Concerto (1919), written in the shadow of the First World War, carries an elegiac valediction.
