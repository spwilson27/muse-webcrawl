Bi Sheng

Bi Sheng (Chinese: 畢昇; 972–1051) was a Chinese artisan and engineer who invented the world's first known movable type printing technology during the Song dynasty. His system used individual characters made of baked clay, a method described by the polymath Shen Kuo in his Dream Pool Essays.

Born972, Yingshan, Hubei (Song dynasty)
Died1051
OccupationArtisan, engineer

Invention of movable type

According to Shen Kuo, during the Qingli era (1041–1048) Bi Sheng, a commoner, created movable type by cutting Chinese characters into small pieces of sticky clay, one character per type piece, and firing them until hard. The types were set in an iron frame on an iron plate coated with a mixture of resin and wax; the plate was warmed so the types could be fixed, then loosened for reuse.

Common characters had multiple type pieces, and rare characters could be made on demand. Bi Sheng also prepared a second frame so that one could be printed while the other was being typeset, greatly speeding production. His ceramic type was later developed further by successors using wooden and metal type.

Legacy

Although the complexity of Chinese characters limited the spread of movable type in China relative to woodblock printing, Bi Sheng's invention preceded Johannes Gutenberg's metal movable type press in Europe by about four centuries. A lunar crater is named Bi Sheng in his honour.
