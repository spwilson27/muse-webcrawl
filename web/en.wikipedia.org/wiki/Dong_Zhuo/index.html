Dong Zhuo

Dong Zhuo (died 22 May 192), courtesy name Zhongying, was a Chinese military general, politician and warlord who lived in the late Eastern Han dynasty. In 189 he seized control of the capital, Luoyang, deposed the young Emperor Shao and installed Emperor Xian in his place, ruling the imperial court as a tyrant. His seizure of power is generally taken as marking the effective end of the Han dynasty and the beginning of the warlord era that led to the Three Kingdoms period.

Early career

Dong Zhuo came from Lintao in Longxi Commandery (modern Min County, Gansu), on the empire's northwestern frontier, and built his reputation fighting the Qiang peoples and in campaigns against the Yellow Turban rebels from 184. He commanded hardened frontier troops in Liang Province and rose to become General of the Vanguard.

Seizure of power

Summoned toward the capital during the political crisis of 189 — when the eunuchs of the Ten Attendants murdered the regent He Jin — Dong Zhuo marched into Luoyang, intimidated the court, and deposed Emperor Shao in favour of his younger half-brother Liu Xie (Emperor Xian), whom he believed he could control. He appointed himself Chancellor of State and ruled through terror, executing opponents and plundering the capital region.

Coalition and downfall

In 190 a coalition of regional governors and warlords led by Yuan Shao formed against him. Dong Zhuo burned Luoyang, forcibly moved the court and population west to Chang'an, and looted the imperial tombs. At Chang'an his cruelty continued until he was assassinated in 192 by his own adopted son and bodyguard Lü Bu, acting in concert with the minister Wang Yun — in legend, a plot involving the beauty Diaochan, one of the Four Beauties of ancient China.

After his death his followers Li Jue and Guo Si stormed Chang'an and kept the emperor captive, and central authority collapsed entirely into competing warlord regimes.
