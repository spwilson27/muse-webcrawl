Merthyr Tydfil (Welsh: Merthyr Tudful) is the main town in Merthyr Tydfil County Borough, Wales, administered by Merthyr Tydfil County Borough Council. It is located about 23 miles (37 km) north of Cardiff, at the confluence of the Taff and Taf Fechan rivers, on the edge of the Brecon Beacons National Park.

History

The town's name derives from the Welsh for "martyr", referring to Saint Tydfil, a Welsh princess said to have been murdered by pagans in the 5th century. During the Industrial Revolution, Merthyr Tydfil became the largest iron-producing town in the world, with great ironworks at Dowlais, Cyfarthfa, Penydarren and Plymouth. In 1804 the world's first steam-hauled railway journey took place on the Penydarren tramroad, when Richard Trevithick's locomotive hauled iron to Abercynon.

The Merthyr Rising of 1831, sparked by falling wages and rising prices, saw workers raise the red flag of revolt, an early use of the symbol. The town declined with the iron and coal industries in the 20th century.

Economy and culture

Today the town is a centre for light industry, services and tourism, with Cyfarthfa Castle and the Brecon Mountain Railway among local attractions. Merthyr Town F.C. plays at Penydarren Park.
