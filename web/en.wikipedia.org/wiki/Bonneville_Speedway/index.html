Bonneville Speedway

Bonneville Speedway (also called the Bonneville Salt Flats Race Track) is an area of the Bonneville Salt Flats in northwestern Utah designated for motor sports. It is particularly noted as the venue of numerous land speed records, including famous runs by Malcolm Campbell in the 1930s. The site is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

Tracks and events

The speedway comprises several tracks laid out on the salt each season: a long course for high-speed record attempts (historically up to about 10 miles including run-out), a short course, and special tracks. Major events include Speed Week in August, organised by the Southern California Timing Association (SCTA) since 1949, the World of Speed, and the World Finals in autumn. Thin or flooded salt has repeatedly forced cancellations, and the racing surface has thinned over recent decades, prompting restoration efforts by racers and the state of Utah.

Land speed records

The flats were first used for racing in 1912 and for record attempts from 1914 (Teddy Tetzlaff). They rose to international prominence in the 1930s as British drivers, above all Malcolm Campbell — who in 1935 became the first to exceed 300 mph there, in Blue Bird — and later George Eyston and John Cobb, exploited the long, flat salt. After the Second World War, record attempts largely moved between Bonneville and Black Rock Desert, where Thrust SSC set the absolute record in 1997; Gary Gabelich's rocket car Blue Flame set a 630 mph record at Bonneville in 1970. The flats are also a popular filming location, and events such as the film The World's Fastest Indian dramatise the Speed Week tradition.
