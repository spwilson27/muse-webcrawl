Suppression of the Society of Jesus

Suppression of the Jesuits
Date1759 – 1773 (expulsions); 21 July 1773 (papal suppression)
InstrumentBrief Dominus ac Redemptor of Pope Clement XIV
Restoration7 August 1814, by Sollicitudo omnium ecclesiarum

The suppression of the Society of Jesus was a series of expulsions of the Jesuits from Catholic countries in the mid-18th century, culminating in the formal abolition of the order by Pope Clement XIV in 1773. The Jesuits were accused by enlightened and regalist governments of political meddling, excessive wealth, and loyalty to the papacy over national monarchies.

Jesuits being expelled and deported, 1767

Background

The order's missions in Paraguay (the reductions), its commercial ventures, and episodes such as the Lavallette bankruptcy in France made it a target of suspicion. The Marquis of Pombal expelled the Jesuits from Portugal and its empire in 1759 after the Távora affair; France followed in 1764, and Spain under Charles III expelled them from all Spanish territories in 1767 in a secret, overnight operation.

The brief of 1773

Under sustained diplomatic pressure from the Bourbon courts, Clement XIV issued the brief Dominus ac Redemptor on 21 July 1773, dissolving the Society. Jesuit property was confiscated and their famous colleges closed or handed to other orders. The order survived informally in Prussia and Russia, where Frederick the Great and Catherine the Great refused to promulgate the brief, valuing Jesuit schools. Pope Pius VII restored the Society universally on 7 August 1814 with the bull Sollicitudo omnium ecclesiarum.
