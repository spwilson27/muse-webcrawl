The California Department of Corrections and Rehabilitation (CDCR) is the penal law enforcement agency of the government of California, responsible for the state prison and parole systems. It is one of the largest correctional systems in the United States, employing tens of thousands of staff and supervising an incarcerated population that, even after major reductions, numbers around one hundred thousand people.

History

California's prison system began in 1851 with a prison ship, the Waban, and the opening of San Quentin State Prison in 1852. The Department of Corrections was created in 1944. In 2005, under Governor Arnold Schwarzenegger, the state's correctional agencies were reorganised and renamed the Department of Corrections and Rehabilitation, adding an explicit rehabilitative mission; the Division of Juvenile Justice was later moved toward closure and transfer to counties, with the last state youth facilities closing in 2023.

Overcrowding and reform

By the mid-2000s California's prisons held nearly twice their designed capacity. In Brown v. Plata (2011), the US Supreme Court upheld an order requiring the state to reduce its prison population to 137.5% of design capacity, finding that overcrowding had made medical and mental-health care unconstitutionally deficient. The 2011 "realignment" law (AB 109) shifted responsibility for many lower-level offenders to county jails and probation, and later measures such as Proposition 47 (2014) and Proposition 57 (2016) further reduced the population and expanded credit-earning and parole consideration.

Organisation

CDCR operates adult institutions including San Quentin, Folsom State Prison, Pelican Bay, and the California Institution for Men, as well as fire camps run jointly with CAL FIRE in which incarcerated people serve as wildland firefighters. Its Division of Adult Parole Operations supervises people released to the community, and the department provides education, vocational, and rehabilitative programming through its Division of Rehabilitative Programs.
