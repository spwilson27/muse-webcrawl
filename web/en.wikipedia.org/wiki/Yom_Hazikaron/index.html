Yom Hazikaron (Hebrew: יום הזיכרון, lit. "Day of Remembrance"), in full Yom HaZikaron LeHalelei Ma'arkhot Yisrael ul'Nifge'ei Pe'ulot HaEivah ("Memorial Day for the Fallen Soldiers of Israel and Victims of Terrorism"), is Israel's official day of remembrance for soldiers killed in the line of duty and for civilians killed in acts of terrorism. It is observed on the 4th of Iyar, immediately preceding Independence Day (Yom Ha'atzmaut), which begins at sundown as the memorial day ends.

History

Commemoration of the war dead was attached to Independence Day in 1949; in 1951 the memorial was separated and given its own day on David Ben-Gurion's initiative, following recommendations by a public committee. The day was enacted in law in 1963. In 1998, after years of debate, victims of terrorism were formally added to the commemoration, and a central ceremony for them is held at Mount Herzl.

Observances

The day begins at sundown with a one-minute memorial siren, followed by the state ceremony at the Western Wall plaza attended by the President and the IDF Chief of Staff. Places of entertainment close for the evening. The following morning a two-minute siren at 11:00 brings the country to a standstill, after which official ceremonies are held at military cemeteries across Israel, at which government ministers, Knesset members and senior officers represent the state. School ceremonies, media broadcasts of the names of the fallen, and documentaries mark the day. At sundown, the mood shifts abruptly to Independence Day celebrations, beginning with the torch-lighting ceremony on Mount Herzl — a juxtaposition intended to link the cost of statehood with its achievement.
