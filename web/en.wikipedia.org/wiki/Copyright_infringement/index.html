Copyright infringement

Copyright infringement (colloquially, piracy) is the use of works protected by copyright without permission where such permission is required, thereby infringing the exclusive rights granted to the copyright holder — such as the rights to reproduce, distribute, display, perform, or create derivative works. Most jurisdictions treat infringement as a civil wrong, and many also criminalise wilful infringement on a commercial scale.

Unauthorised copying of protected works constitutes infringement in most jurisdictions.

Elements and defences

A claimant generally must prove ownership of a valid copyright and copying of original, protectable expression. Copying may be shown by access plus substantial similarity. Defences and limitations vary: the United States recognises fair use; Commonwealth jurisdictions recognise fair dealing; many systems exempt quotation, news reporting, parody, and certain educational or archival uses. Liability may be direct, contributory, or vicarious, and online intermediaries often rely on safe-harbour regimes such as the US Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA) notice-and-takedown system.

Remedies

Courts may grant injunctions, actual damages and profits, or (in the US) statutory damages per work infringed, which can be substantial for wilful infringement. Criminal penalties apply especially to commercial-scale operations, under instruments such as the TRIPS Agreement, which obliges members to criminalise wilful copyright piracy on a commercial scale.

Online piracy

The internet transformed infringement's scale: Napster (shut down in 2001), peer-to-peer networks such as BitTorrent, file-hosting "cyberlockers", stream-ripping, and unauthorised streaming services have successively been targets of litigation and site-blocking orders in many countries. Industry and critics dispute the net economic harm of unauthorised downloading, with studies reaching differing conclusions on displacement of sales versus promotional effects.

History

The term "piracy" for unauthorised publication predates modern copyright; unauthorised reprinting was rampant before and after the Statute of Anne (1710). International protection was progressively harmonised by the Berne Convention (1886) and the TRIPS Agreement (1994).
