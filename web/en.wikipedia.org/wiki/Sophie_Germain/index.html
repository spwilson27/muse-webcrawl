Sophie Germain

Marie-Sophie Germain
Born: 1 April 1776, Paris, France
Died: 27 June 1831, Paris
Fields: Number theory, elasticity, philosophy
Known for: Sophie Germain primes; work on Fermat's Last Theorem; theory of vibrating surfaces

Marie-Sophie Germain (1 April 1776 – 27 June 1831) was a French mathematician, physicist and philosopher. Largely self-taught because women were barred from formal scientific education, she made foundational contributions to number theory and to the mathematical theory of elasticity.

Number theory

Corresponding with Carl Friedrich Gauss under the male pseudonym "M. LeBlanc", Germain developed a strategy for proving Fermat's Last Theorem for a class of prime exponents. A prime p for which 2p + 1 is also prime is now called a Sophie Germain prime. Her work was the most substantial advance on the theorem of its era, and her grand plan for the general case anticipated later methods.

Elasticity

Motivated by Ernst Chladni's vibrating-plate figures, Germain entered the Paris Academy's prize competition three times, finally winning in 1816 with a theory of elastic surfaces — the first woman to win such a prize. Her equation for vibrating plates underpinned the later development of elasticity theory.

Legacy

Gauss arranged an honorary doctorate from Göttingen, but Germain died of breast cancer before receiving it. Primes, a theorem, prizes and schools in France bear her name.
