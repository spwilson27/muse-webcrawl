The Adventures of Tom Sawyer is an 1876 novel by Mark Twain about Tom Sawyer, a boy growing up along the Mississippi River in the fictional town of St. Petersburg, Missouri, modelled on Hannibal, Missouri, where Twain lived as a boy. It is considered a classic of American literature and a foundational work of the coming-of-age genre.

Plot

Tom lives with his Aunt Polly and half-brother Sid. Famous episodes include Tom tricking his friends into whitewashing a fence for him and falling for Becky Thatcher. With his friend Huckleberry Finn, Tom witnesses a murder in a graveyard committed by Injun Joe, who frames the innocent Muff Potter. Later, Tom and Becky become lost in a cave where Injun Joe is hiding; after their rescue, Injun Joe is found dead, and Tom and Huck recover his buried treasure.

Legacy

The novel introduced characters who would return in Adventures of Huckleberry Finn (1884), often called the great American novel. Despite its popularity, the book has periodically been challenged in schools over its language and racial content. It has been adapted many times for film, theatre, and television.
