25143 Itokawa (provisional designation 1998 SF36) is a sub-kilometre near-Earth asteroid of the Apollo group and a potentially hazardous object. It was discovered on 26 September 1998 by astronomers of the Lincoln Near-Earth Asteroid Research (LINEAR) programme at the Lincoln Laboratory Experimental Test Site in Socorro, New Mexico. The asteroid was named after Japanese rocket scientist Hideo Itokawa.

Description

Itokawa measures roughly 535 by 294 by 209 metres and has an elongated, "peanut" or "sea otter" shape. Its surface is covered with boulders and smooth regolith-covered regions, and its very low density indicates that it is a rubble pile — a loose aggregation of fragments held together by weak gravity rather than a single solid rock. It is an S-type asteroid composed largely of silicate minerals similar to those of LL chondrite meteorites.

Hayabusa mission

Itokawa was the target of the Japanese Hayabusa spacecraft, which rendezvoused with the asteroid in 2005, mapped its surface, and collected samples of surface material. Despite numerous technical failures, Hayabusa returned its sample capsule to Earth in June 2010, making Itokawa the first asteroid from which samples were returned. Analysis of the returned grains confirmed the link between S-type asteroids and ordinary chondrite meteorites.

Orbit

Itokawa orbits the Sun at a distance of 0.95–1.70 AU once every 1.52 years (556 days). Its orbit crosses that of Earth, and it approaches within about 0.013 AU of Earth at minimum.
