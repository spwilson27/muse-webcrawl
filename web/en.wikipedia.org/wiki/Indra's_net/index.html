Indra's net

Indra's net (also called Indra's jewels) is a metaphor used to illustrate the concepts of interpenetration and interdependence in Mahayana Buddhism, particularly in the Huayan school. The metaphor comes from the image of the net of the Vedic god Indra: a vast net spread over his palace, at each vertex of which hangs a brilliant jewel, and each jewel reflects all the other jewels in the net, in an infinite process of reflection.

Meaning

In the Avatamsaka Sutra the image expresses the idea that all phenomena arise together in a mutually interdependent web, that each thing contains and is contained by everything else, and that no phenomenon exists independently. Huayan thinkers such as Fazang developed the metaphor into a systematic doctrine of the mutual interpenetration of all dharmas.

Influence

The metaphor has been widely borrowed in modern writing on ecology, systems thinking and philosophy as an image of radical interconnectedness.
