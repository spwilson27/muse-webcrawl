Non-official cover

Non-official cover (NOC) is a term used in espionage for an operative who assumes a covert role without any official connection to the government they serve — for example posing as a businessperson, journalist, charity worker or student rather than as an embassy official.

Comparison with official cover

Officers under official cover work in embassies or consulates and hold diplomatic immunity: if caught spying they are usually declared persona non grata and expelled. A NOC officer has no such protection and, if exposed, can face imprisonment or execution, and their government may deny any connection to them.

Requirements and risks

NOC work demands elaborate backstopped legends — verifiable employment, finances and biography — and often years of preparation. In the CIA, NOC officers are considered among the most demanding and exposed roles in clandestine service.

Exposure cases

The 2010 exposure of the Russian "Illegals" network in the United States illustrated long-term non-official cover in practice; the agents lived under assumed identities for years before arrest and a prisoner exchange.
