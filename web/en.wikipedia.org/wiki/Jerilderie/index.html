Jerilderie is a small town in the southern Riverina region of New South Wales, Australia. Situated on the Newell Highway along Billabong Creek, it is best known as the site of bushranger Ned Kelly's famous "Jerilderie Letter" episode of 1879.

History

The Jerilderie area was originally inhabited by the Jeithi Aboriginal people. European squatters established pastoral runs in the 1840s and 1850s, and the township was surveyed in the following decades, taking its name from a local pastoral property. The town developed as a service center for surrounding sheep and wheat farms in the Murrumbidgee Irrigation Area's fringe.

The Jerilderie Letter

In February 1879, Ned Kelly and his gang rode into Jerilderie from Victoria, locked the town's policemen in their own cells, and held the town for two days. Kelly attempted to have his 8,000-word manifesto — now known as the Jerilderie Letter — printed by the local newspaper editor; failing that, he entrusted the manuscript to a bank accountant, and it survived to become one of the most important documents of Australian history, a passionate denunciation of police persecution and Irish-Australian grievance. The gang robbed the Bank of New South Wales before escaping. The letter is now held by the State Library Victoria.

Today

Jerilderie serves a mixed farming district producing wool, grains, and irrigated crops. The town commemorates the Kelly visit with heritage displays along "Ned Kelly Way," and Lake Jerilderie provides recreation on Billabong Creek. It is administered by the Murrumbidgee Council.
