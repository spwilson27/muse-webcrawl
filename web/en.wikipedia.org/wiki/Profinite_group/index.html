In mathematics, a profinite group is a topological group that is in a certain sense assembled from finite groups. Equivalently, a profinite group is a compact, totally disconnected topological group: that is, a topological group that is also a Stone space. Profinite groups are important in number theory and algebraic geometry, most famously because the absolute Galois group of a field is profinite.

Definition

A profinite group can be defined as an inverse limit of an inverse system of finite groups, each given the discrete topology:

G = lim← Gᵢ

Equivalently, G is a compact Hausdorff totally disconnected topological group, or a compact group in which the open normal subgroups form a neighbourhood base of the identity.

Examples

The ring of profinite integers, the profinite completion of the integers Z, is the inverse limit of the rings Z/nZ. The additive group of p-adic integers Z_p is profinite (it is the inverse limit of Z/pⁿZ). Every finite group is profinite in its discrete topology, and every Galois group of an infinite algebraic extension, with the Krull topology, is profinite.

Properties

Profinite groups are compact, hence possess a normalised Haar measure; closed subgroups of profinite groups are profinite; and quotients by closed normal subgroups are profinite. The theory of profinite groups generalises many results about finite groups via approximation, and continuous cohomology of profinite groups (Galois cohomology) is a central tool in modern number theory.
