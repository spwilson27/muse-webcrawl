Reginald Marsh

Reginald Marsh (March 14, 1898 – July 3, 1954) was an American painter, printmaker and illustrator, best known for his lively depictions of New York City life in the 1920s and 1930s. Born in Paris to American artist parents, he studied at Yale University and the Art Students League of New York.

Career

Marsh worked as an illustrator for the New York Daily News and The New Yorker before turning to painting. His crowded scenes of Coney Island beaches, burlesque theatres, subways, dance halls and Bowery streets captured the energy and social mix of Depression-era New York. He painted in egg tempera and watercolour and produced many etchings and lithographs. During the Second World War he worked as a war artist-correspondent. Marsh taught at the Art Students League for many years, and his anatomy teaching influenced a generation of American artists.
