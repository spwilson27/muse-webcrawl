Fossil Butte National Monument is a United States national monument in south-western Wyoming, preserving a portion of the Green River Formation — the deposits of Eocene Fossil Lake, about 52 million years old. The monument protects some of the world's best-preserved fossil fish, along with fossil plants, insects, birds, bats, reptiles and mammals, and has been called an "aquarium in stone".

Palaeontology

Fossil Lake was the smallest and shortest-lived of three great lakes that filled basins in the region during the Eocene epoch. Fine limestone sediments settled in its quiet, sometimes anoxic bottom waters, entombing organisms with extraordinary completeness: articulated fish such as Knightia and the herring-like Diplomystus, stingrays, turtles, palm fronds and even complete early bats and the tiny horse Protorohippus have been recovered from the formation. Commercial and private quarries outside the monument continue to produce fossils in large numbers.

The monument

Authorised in 1972, the monument covers about 8,200 acres of sagebrush steppe and aspen groves. Its visitor centre displays more than 300 fossils and a film about the ancient lake, and rangers lead walks to a historic quarry site. No collecting is permitted within the monument itself.
