Sinjar (Arabic: سنجار, Sinjār; Kurdish: شنگال, Şingal) is a town in the Sinjar District of Nineveh Governorate in northwestern Iraq, near the Syrian border and south of the Sinjar Mountains. Before 2014 it was home to a population of roughly 80,000–90,000, composed mainly of Yazidis, along with Sunni Arab and Kurdish residents. The town is the principal population centre of the Yazidi community in Iraq.

History

The Sinjar area has been inhabited since antiquity and lay within the Roman and later Islamic worlds; the town's citadel dates to the medieval period. Under the Ottoman Empire and the modern Iraqi state, the Yazidi population of Sinjar experienced repeated campaigns of persecution. In the twentieth century the Ba'athist government subjected Yazidis to forced Arabisation and collective village resettlement.

2014 massacre and its aftermath

On 3 August 2014, fighters of the Islamic State (IS) attacked Sinjar after Kurdish Peshmerga forces withdrew. Thousands of Yazidi civilians were killed, thousands of women and girls were abducted into sexual slavery, and tens of thousands fled onto Mount Sinjar, where they were besieged without food or water until a corridor was opened by Kurdish forces, aided by US airstrikes. The United Nations and other bodies have characterised the campaign as a genocide against the Yazidis. Kurdish and Iraqi forces retook the town in November 2015 with coalition air support, but much of Sinjar lay in ruins, and reconstruction and the return of displaced residents have been hindered by disputes between Baghdad, Erbil, and local armed groups. In 2021 Iraq enacted the Yazidi Survivors Law to provide reparations to survivors.
