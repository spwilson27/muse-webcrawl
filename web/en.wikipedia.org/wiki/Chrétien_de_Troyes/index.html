Chrétien de Troyes

Chrétien de Troyes
Flourished: c. 1160–1191, France
Language: Old French
Genre: Arthurian romance
Patrons: Marie de Champagne, Philip of Flanders

Chrétien de Troyes was a French poet of the late 12th century, regarded as the founder of Arthurian romance and one of the greatest medieval authors. Little is known of his life beyond his association with the court of Marie de Champagne at Troyes.

Works

His five surviving romances in octosyllabic verse are Erec and Enide, Cligès, Yvain, the Knight of the Lion, Lancelot, the Knight of the Cart — which introduced Lancelot's love for Guinevere — and the unfinished Perceval, the Story of the Grail, which introduced the Holy Grail into literature.

Influence

Chrétien shaped the Arthurian tradition across Europe, influencing German poets such as Wolfram von Eschenbach and Hartmann von Aue, and establishing conventions of courtly love, knightly quests and psychological characterisation.
