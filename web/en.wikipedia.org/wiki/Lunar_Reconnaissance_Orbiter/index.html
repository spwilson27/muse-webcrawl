Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter
OperatorNASA / Goddard
Launch18 June 2009
RoleLunar mapping; Apollo site imaging
StatusOperating (extended mission)
LRO over the lunar pole (artist's concept).

The Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) is a NASA robotic spacecraft that has orbited the Moon since June 2009, mapping its surface in unprecedented detail to scout landing sites and study its geology, radiation environment and polar ice. Its camera (LROC) resolves objects under a metre across from its low polar orbit — sharp enough to photograph every Apollo landing site, showing descent stages, rover tracks and astronaut footpaths still visible decades later.

LRO's instruments confirmed water ice in shadowed polar craters (with its companion LCROSS impactor mission), measured the coldest temperatures recorded in the solar system in those shadows, and built the topographic maps on which Artemis south-pole planning depends. It also re-imaged the Earthrise geometry from Apollo 8 and returned modern Earthrise portraits of its own.

Longevity

Conceived for a one-year prime mission, LRO has operated for over fifteen years, its orbit gradually evolving; it remains the Moon's principal mapping asset alongside India's Chandrayaan-2 orbiter and Korea's Danuri.
