High-rise building

A high-rise building is a tall building whose height requires mechanical vertical transportation and special structural and fire-safety systems; definitions vary by jurisdiction, commonly starting around 23–35 metres or roughly 7–12 stories. Buildings far taller are usually classed as skyscrapers, and the tallest category is sometimes termed supertall or megatall.

History

Multi-story insulae existed in ancient Rome, but the modern high-rise followed the invention of the safe passenger elevator (Elisha Otis, 1850s) and steel-frame construction in Chicago and New York in the 1880s. Zoning laws, curtain-wall construction, and post-war housing programs spread residential towers worldwide during the 20th century.

Design considerations

High-rise design must address lateral wind and seismic loads (met by cores, outriggers, and tube structures), vertical circulation, stack effect, water pressure zoning, and fire compartmentation with protected escape stairs and sprinklers. Mixed-use towers combine offices, hotels, and apartments with different floor plates and service needs.

Criticism

Residential towers have been criticized for social isolation, wind effects at street level, and energy demands, and after fires such as Grenfell Tower (2017), regulation of cladding and evacuation in many countries was tightened. Nevertheless, high-rises remain central to dense urban land use.
