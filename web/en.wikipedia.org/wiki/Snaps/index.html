Snaps

Snaps (pronounced [ˈsnaps] in Danish and Swedish) is a Danish and Swedish word for a small shot of a strong alcoholic beverage taken during the course of a meal. A ritual that is associated with drinking snaps is to toast with a particular song (a snapsvisa) before drinking.

In Denmark and Sweden, snaps is a catch-all term for aquavit and other flavoured and unflavoured spirits distilled from grain or potato. In Sweden, snaps can also be bitters, especially the traditional bitter spiced varieties. The beverage is typically served chilled in small, stemmed, tulip-shaped glasses.

Traditions

Snaps is closely associated with festive meals such as the Swedish midsummer celebration, Christmas (the julbord), crayfish parties, and Danish smørrebrød lunches. Before each shot, diners raise their glasses, make eye contact, sing a short drinking song, drink, and make eye contact again as they set the glass down. The classic Swedish snaps songs include "Helan går" ("The whole goes down"), referring to the first glass of the series; subsequent shots are named in sequence (Halvan, "the half"; Tersan, and so on).

Production

Traditional snaps is flavoured with herbs and spices such as caraway, dill, anise, fennel, wormwood, or St. John's wort. Danish snaps is often based on grain and tends to be neutral or caraway/dill flavoured, while Swedish snaps commonly uses a wider range of botanicals. Many households also prepare homemade flavoured snaps by steeping herbs or berries in plain spirit.
