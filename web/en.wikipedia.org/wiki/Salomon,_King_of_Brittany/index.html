Salomon, King of Brittany

Salomon (Breton: Salaün; died 874) was King of Brittany from 857 to 874. A cousin of his predecessor Erispoe, whom he murdered, Salomon became the most powerful Breton ruler of the Carolingian era, expanding Breton territory and governing as an effectively independent king while maintaining formal relations with the West Frankish kingdom of Charles the Bald.

Salomon, King of Brittany (r. 857–874).

Reign

In 857 Salomon conspired with the Breton Almarchus to murder Erispoe at the altar of a church and seized power. He initially rebelled against Charles the Bald but came to terms, receiving additional lands; by 867, after the Treaty of Compiègne, he held the Cotentin and Avranchin and other territories, and Breton control reached its greatest extent. Salomon styled himself king, minted coins in his own name, and dealt with the Vikings — sometimes fighting them, sometimes allying with them against the Franks, notably joining the Viking leader Hastein in the campaign that captured Angers in 873.

Death and legacy

In 874 Salomon was overthrown and murdered by a conspiracy of Breton nobles, including his son-in-law Pasquitan and Wrhwant. His death plunged Brittany into civil war. In later Breton tradition he was venerated as a saint and martyr (Saint Salomon), with a feast day on 25 June, though he was never formally canonised.
