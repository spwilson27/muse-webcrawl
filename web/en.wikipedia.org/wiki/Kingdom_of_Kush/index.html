Kingdom of Kush

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The steep Nubian pyramids of Meroë rising from the Sudanese desert]

The Kingdom of Kush was an ancient African kingdom in Nubia, centred along the Nile valley in what is now northern Sudan and southern Egypt. Emerging after the collapse of Egypt's New Kingdom, Kushite kings conquered Egypt itself in the 8th century BC and ruled it as the Twenty-fifth Dynasty — the 'Black Pharaohs' — until Assyrian invasions drove them south. From capitals at Napata and then Meroë, the kingdom endured for over a thousand years as a centre of ironworking and trade between Africa and the Mediterranean, building more pyramids than Egypt itself, before falling to Aksum in the 4th century AD.

Napata and the Twenty-fifth Dynasty

Kushite kings such as Kashta and Piye expanded northward from Napata; Piye's victory stele records his conquest of Egypt (c. 744 BC), and his successors — Shabaka, Taharqa — ruled a unified Nile empire, reviving Egyptian art, building at Karnak and fighting the Assyrians for the Levant. Taharqa's wars with Esarhaddon and Ashurbanipal ended in the Assyrian sack of Thebes (663 BC) and the loss of Egypt; the Kushites withdrew to Nubia.

Meroë and decline

From about 590 BC the capital moved south to Meroë, where abundant timber and iron ore made a major iron-smelting centre and trade reached deep into Africa. Meroitic rulers — including ruling queens, the kandakes (Candaces) — developed their own script (still not fully deciphered), built hundreds of steep-sided pyramids, and traded with Rome, Arabia and India. The kingdom faded in the 4th century AD under pressure from desert peoples and was finally overrun by Ezana of Aksum (c. 350 AD).
