Hermann Föttinger

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Hermann Föttinger (9 February 1877 in Nuremberg – 28 April 1945 in Berlin) was a German engineer and inventor. In 1902, while working at the AG Vulcan shipyard in Stettin, he patented the hydrodynamic transmission known as the Föttinger transformator — a fluid coupling combined with a torque converter that transmitted power from ship steam turbines to propeller shafts.

Work

Föttinger's device used circulating fluid in a casing to transfer torque between an impeller and a turbine wheel, allowing high-speed turbines to drive slow propellers efficiently. His hydrodynamic principle became foundational for fluid couplings and torque converters later adopted in railway locomotives and automatic transmissions for vehicles.

Academic career

From 1924 he held the chair of applied fluid mechanics at the Technical University of Berlin, where he also contributed to problems in hydrodynamics and ship propulsion. He died in Berlin in the final days of the Second World War.
