Wellesley is a town in Norfolk County, Massachusetts, United States. It is part of Greater Boston and is known for its college, Wellesley College, and for Babson College, as well as for its high household incomes and highly ranked public schools. The town is located about 15 miles (24 km) west of downtown Boston.

History

Wellesley was settled in the 1660s as part of Dedham and later Needham. Its western district separated in 1881 and was named Wellesley in honour of Horatio Hollis Hunnewell's estate, "Wellesley", itself named after his wife's family. Wellesley College, a women's liberal arts college, was founded in 1870 by Henry and Pauline Durant and opened in 1875 on the shore of Lake Waban.

Geography

The town covers about 10.5 square miles and borders Natick, Needham, Newton, Weston and Dover. The Charles River forms part of its southern boundary, and features include Lake Waban, Morses Pond and the town's extensive trail system. Wellesley is crossed by Routes 9, 16 and 135 and has three stops on the MBTA Framingham/Worcester commuter rail line.

Education

Besides Wellesley College and Babson College (known for entrepreneurship), the town hosts a campus of Massachusetts Bay Community College. Wellesley High School is consistently ranked among the best public high schools in Massachusetts. The town is also home to Dana Hall School, an independent girls' school, and Tenacre Country Day School.
