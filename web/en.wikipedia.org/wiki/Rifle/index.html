A rifle is a long-barreled firearm designed for accurate shooting, distinguished by a set of helical grooves ("rifling") cut into the bore of its barrel so that the projectile spins as it travels, gyroscopically stabilising its flight. In common usage, "rifle" also denotes a shoulder-fired, two-handed weapon firing a single projectile per barrel pull, in contrast to a shotgun. Rifles are used for warfare, law enforcement, hunting, target shooting, and other shooting sports.

Terminology and mechanism

The word "rifle" derives from the rifling process (from Old French rifler, to scrape or scratch). The rifling consists of lands (the raised portions) and grooves; the calibre of a rifle is commonly specified by groove or land diameter, or by the cartridge it chambers. Rifles are classified by their action — muzzleloading, break-action, bolt-action, lever-action, pump-action, semi-automatic, and fully automatic — and by their role, such as battle rifles, assault rifles, sniper rifles, and sporting or hunting rifles. Modern examples include the AR-15 pattern, the AK-pattern series, and precision bolt-action rifles such as the Remington 700.

History

Rifled barrels appeared in German-speaking Europe in the late 15th and early 16th centuries, but slow loading (a tight-fitting ball had to be forced down the grooves) kept rifles as specialist weapons for hunters and sharpshooters while smoothbore muskets armed line infantry. Rifled long arms saw notable use by American frontiersmen (the "long rifle") and by British rifle regiments such as the 95th Rifles during the Napoleonic Wars. The advent of the Minié ball in the 1840s–50s, which expanded to grip the rifling while loading easily, made rifled muskets general-issue, and breechloading designs (such as the Dreyse needle gun and later bolt-action Mausers) completed the transition. In the 20th century, semi-automatic and select-fire rifles firing intermediate cartridges — culminating in the assault rifle — became the standard infantry arm.

Ballistics and accuracy

Spin stabilisation dramatically improves accuracy and effective range over smoothbore projectiles. Accuracy depends on barrel quality and harmonics, ammunition consistency, sighting system (iron sights or telescopic sights), and shooter technique. Effective ranges range from under 100 metres for light carbines in some roles to well over 1,500 metres for dedicated long-range precision rifles.
