The Battle of Cuito Cuanavale was a series of engagements fought between August 1987 and March 1988 around the town of Cuito Cuanavale in southeastern Angola, during the Angolan Civil War and the South African Border War. It pitted the Angolan armed forces (FAPLA), supported by Cuban troops, against UNITA rebels backed by the South African Defence Force (SADF).

Background

In mid-1987 FAPLA launched an offensive toward the UNITA strongholds of Mavinga and Jamba. South Africa intervened with artillery, armour and air support, mauling the advancing FAPLA brigades at the Lomba River in September–October 1987. The survivors fell back on Cuito Cuanavale, where, reinforced by Cuban units, they dug in on the Tumpo triangle east of the town.

Battle

Between January and March 1988 the SADF and UNITA launched repeated assaults on the Tumpo positions, all of which were repulsed with heavy fighting around minefields and river crossings. Meanwhile Cuba reinforced its contingent in Angola to some 50,000 men and advanced toward the Namibian border in the southwest, while Cuban MiG-23s contested South African air superiority and struck the Calueque dam in June 1988.

Aftermath

Both sides claimed victory: South Africa had destroyed much of FAPLA's offensive capability but failed to take Cuito Cuanavale; Angola and Cuba had held the town and shifted the strategic balance. The campaign led directly to the Tripartite (New York) Accords of December 1988, Cuban withdrawal from Angola, and the independence of Namibia in 1990.
