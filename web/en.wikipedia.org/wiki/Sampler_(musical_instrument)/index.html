Sampler (musical instrument)

Sampler
TypeElectronic musical instrument
Early examplesMellotron (tape-based); Fairlight CMI (1979)
Key modelsE-mu Emulator, Akai S900, Akai MPC

A sampler is an electronic musical instrument that records and plays back samples — snippets of audio — which can be pitched, looped, sliced and otherwise manipulated, typically from a keyboard or pad controller. Unlike a synthesizer, which generates sound from oscillators, a sampler begins with recorded audio.

History

The concept has roots in the tape-based Mellotron of the 1960s and in musique concrète. The Australian Fairlight CMI (1979) was the first commercially successful digital sampler, albeit extremely expensive. The E-mu Emulator (1981) and especially the affordable Akai S900 (1986) and the MPC series designed with Roger Linn brought sampling into mainstream production, where it became a foundation of hip hop and electronic dance music.

Technique

Samples can be "chopped" into segments, time-stretched, reversed, filtered and mapped across a keyboard so a single note recording becomes a full instrument. Sampling of existing recordings raised significant copyright questions, addressed in cases such as Grand Upright Music v. Warner Bros. (1991), after which clearance of samples became standard practice in commercial releases.

Software samplers

Since the 2000s, software samplers inside digital audio workstations — such as Kontakt, EXS24 and Ableton's Sampler — have largely replaced hardware units, offering vast multi-sampled instrument libraries, while pad-based hardware samplers remain popular for live performance and beat-making.
