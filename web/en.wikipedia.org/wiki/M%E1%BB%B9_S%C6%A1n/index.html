Mỹ Sơn

Mỹ Sơn
Location: Quảng Nam Province, Vietnam
Built: 4th–14th centuries
Culture: Champa kingdom
Religion: Hinduism (Shaivism)
Designation: UNESCO World Heritage Site (1999)

Mỹ Sơn is a cluster of abandoned and partly ruined Hindu temples in central Vietnam, constructed between the 4th and 14th centuries by the kings of Champa. The sanctuary, dedicated chiefly to Shiva under the name Bhadreshvara, lies in a jungle valley in Quảng Nam Province, near the former Cham capital of Trà Kiệu.

History

Founded under King Bhadravarman I in the fourth century, Mỹ Sơn served as the religious and political capital of the Champa kingdom for most of its history; kings were buried there and worshipped as gods after death. More than seventy structures once stood in the valley, built of fired red brick joined by a still-debated technique, with sandstone carvings showing strong Indian influence.

Destruction and preservation

French scholars of the École française d'Extrême-Orient documented and partly restored the site in the early twentieth century. During the Vietnam War the valley was a Viet Cong base area and was heavily bombed in 1969, destroying many towers, including the great temple known as A1. UNESCO inscribed Mỹ Sơn as a World Heritage Site in 1999, and international teams have since worked on conservation.

Layout

The temple groups are arranged with a main sanctuary (kalan), a gate tower, a meditation hall, and a library or fire house, aligned with the sacred mountain Mahaparvata (Cat's Tooth Mountain) across the valley.
