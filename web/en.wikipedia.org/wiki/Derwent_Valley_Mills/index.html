Derwent Valley Mills

The Derwent Valley Mills are a series of 18th- and 19th-century cotton mills, including some of the world's first modern factories, stretched along the River Derwent in Derbyshire, England. The site was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in December 2001.

The valley saw the pioneering work of Richard Arkwright, who built his first water-powered cotton spinning mill at Cromford in 1771, and of the Strutt family, whose mills at Belper and Milford applied fireproof construction and developed the factory system. The Derwent Valley's mills and workers' housing influenced industrial development in Europe and North America; the factory system developed there was later exported to New England, where it helped shape the Lowell mill towns.

Key sites include Cromford Mill, Masson Mills, the mills at Belper and Darley Abbey, and Derby's Silk Mill, now the Museum of Making.
