Disney Animation: The Illusion of Life is a 1981 book by Frank Thomas and Ollie Johnston, two of Walt Disney's "Nine Old Men", documenting the techniques of hand-drawn character animation developed at the Walt Disney Studios from the 1930s onwards. It is regarded as the definitive reference on classical Disney animation, and its title is often used for the book as a whole, which is sometimes informally called "the Animator's Bible".

Contents

The book traces the history of the Disney studio's approach to animation, from the early shorts through the golden age of feature films such as Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs and Pinocchio. Richly illustrated with production drawings, storyboards, and background paintings, it explains how animators gave drawings the appearance of living, thinking characters rather than merely moving figures.

The twelve principles

The book's most influential contribution is its codification of the twelve basic principles of animation, distilled from the working methods of Disney animators since the 1930s:

  1. Squash and stretch
  2. Anticipation
  3. Staging
  4. Straight ahead action and pose to pose
  5. Follow through and overlapping action
  6. Slow in and slow out
  7. Arcs
  8. Secondary action
  9. Timing
  10. Exaggeration
  11. Solid drawing
  12. Appeal

These principles remain foundational in traditional, computer, and stop-motion animation, and are taught in animation schools worldwide.

Legacy

The book has remained continuously in print in various editions and is consistently cited by animators as an essential text. In 1995, an expanded companion work by the same authors, The Illusion of Life content related to villains and other topics appeared in additional volumes. The principles articulated in the book shaped the practice of Pixar and other studios founded by animators trained in the Disney tradition.
