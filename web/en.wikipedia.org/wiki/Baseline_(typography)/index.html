Baseline (typography)

In European and West Asian typography and penmanship, the baseline is the line upon which most letters "sit" and below which descenders extend. It is the most important of the horizontal guide lines used to organise type, and the reference line from which other measurements — such as x-height, cap height and line height — are taken.

Description

Most letters of the Latin, Greek and Cyrillic alphabets rest directly on the baseline. Letters with rounded forms, such as o and c, slightly overshoot the baseline (and the x-height and cap-height lines) so that they appear the same size as flat-topped letters — a compensation known as overshoot. Descenders, as in g, p and y, extend below the baseline to the descender line.

Related lines

The cap height is the height of capital letters above the baseline; the x-height is the height of lowercase letters such as x; the ascender line marks the top of letters like b and h; and the descender line marks the lowest extent of letters like g and p. In digital typography and CSS, text is aligned to the baseline by default, and the vertical-align: baseline property keeps elements on a shared baseline.
