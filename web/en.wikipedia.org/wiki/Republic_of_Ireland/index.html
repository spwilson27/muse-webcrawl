Republic of Ireland is the official description of the country commonly known as Ireland (Irish: Éire). It is a sovereign state in north-western Europe occupying 26 of the island of Ireland's 32 counties; the remaining six counties form Northern Ireland, part of the United Kingdom. The capital and largest city is Dublin.

The Irish Free State was founded in 1922 under the Anglo-Irish Treaty, following the Irish War of Independence. A new constitution in 1937 named the state "Ireland", and the Republic of Ireland Act 1948, which came into force on 18 April 1949, declared Ireland a republic and removed the British monarch's remaining functions.

History

Prehistoric settlement of the island dates back millennia, followed by Celtic cultures, early Christian monasticism, Viking towns, and Norman invasion from 1169. English and later British rule deepened after the Tudor conquest and the Plantations. The Act of Union (1801) joined Ireland to Great Britain. The Great Famine (1845–1852) killed about a million people and drove mass emigration. The Easter Rising of 1916 and the War of Independence (1919–1921) led to partition and the Free State, followed by the Irish Civil War (1922–1923).

Politics

Ireland is a parliamentary democracy. The President is head of state, elected for a seven-year term. Legislative power rests with the Oireachtas, comprising the Dáil Éireann (lower house) and Seanad Éireann (upper house). The Taoiseach is head of government. Ireland joined the European Economic Community in 1973 and adopted the euro in 1999 (coins and notes from 2002).

Economy

Ireland has a highly globalised economy concentrated in pharmaceuticals, medical devices, chemicals, and information and communications technology services, with many multinational corporations domiciled there. The rapid growth of the 1990s earned the nickname "Celtic Tiger"; the economy contracted sharply after the 2008 financial crisis and an EU–IMF programme (2010–2013), then recovered strongly.
