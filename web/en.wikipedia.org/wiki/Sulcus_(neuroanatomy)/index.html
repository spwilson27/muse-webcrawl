Sulcus (neuroanatomy)

In neuroanatomy, a sulcus (Latin for "furrow"; plural sulci) is a depression or groove in the surface of the cerebral cortex. Sulci surround the raised ridges of cortex called gyri (singular gyrus), producing the characteristic folded appearance of the brain in humans and other mammals. This folding — gyrification — packs a much larger cortical surface area into the skull than a smooth brain of the same volume would allow; roughly two-thirds of the human cortex is hidden within sulci.

Major sulci

Several sulci are constant landmarks used to divide the cortex into lobes and functional areas. The central sulcus (of Rolando) separates the frontal lobe from the parietal lobe and divides the primary motor cortex (precentral gyrus) from the primary somatosensory cortex (postcentral gyrus). The lateral sulcus (Sylvian fissure) separates the temporal lobe from the frontal and parietal lobes above it. On the medial surface, the cingulate sulcus arches above the cingulate cortex, and the calcarine sulcus in the occipital lobe contains much of the primary visual cortex along its banks. Other prominent examples include the superior temporal, intraparietal, collateral, and parieto-occipital sulci.

Development and variation

Sulci appear in a broadly predictable sequence during fetal development as the cortex expands and buckles, with the primary sulci forming first and the finer secondary and tertiary folds later. The deepest and most consistent sulci are used as anatomical landmarks in brain atlases and neurosurgery, although the exact pattern of smaller sulci varies considerably between individuals and even between the two hemispheres of one brain. Some authors reserve the term fissure for the deepest sulci, such as the longitudinal fissure separating the two cerebral hemispheres.

Clinical significance

Abnormal sulcal patterns are associated with developmental disorders of cortical formation, such as lissencephaly (an abnormally smooth brain). Widening of sulci is a visible sign of cortical atrophy on imaging in aging and dementia. Sulcal anatomy is also mapped before and during neurosurgery to plan safe approaches to underlying structures.
