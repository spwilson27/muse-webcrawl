Lana Wachowski (born June 21, 1965) and Lilly Wachowski (born December 29, 1967), collectively known as the Wachowskis, are American film and television directors, writers, and producers. The sisters are best known as the creators of The Matrix film franchise.

Early life

The Wachowskis were born in Chicago, Illinois, to Lynne, a nurse and painter, and Ron Wachowski, a businessman of Polish descent. They attended Whitney Young High School and were avid fans of comic books, role-playing games, and cinema. Before filmmaking, they worked in carpentry and wrote for Marvel Comics' Ectokid series under editor and author Clive Barker's Razorline imprint.

Career

Their screenplay for Assassins (1995) was heavily rewritten, prompting them to direct their own work. Their directorial debut was the neo-noir thriller Bound (1996). They achieved worldwide fame with The Matrix (1999), a landmark science fiction action film that won four Academy Awards and pioneered the "bullet time" visual effect. Its sequels, The Matrix Reloaded and The Matrix Revolutions (both 2003), followed. Subsequent work includes writing and producing V for Vendetta (2005), directing Speed Racer (2008), co-directing Cloud Atlas (2012) with Tom Tykwer, Jupiter Ascending (2015), and co-creating the Netflix series Sense8 (2015–2018). Lana directed The Matrix Resurrections (2021) solo.

Personal lives

Both sisters are transgender women. Lana publicly came out during the press for Cloud Atlas in 2012 and received the Human Rights Campaign's Visibility Award; Lilly came out in 2016. The transgender themes and allegorical readings of The Matrix have been widely discussed in relation to their experiences.

Style and influence

The Wachowskis' work blends anime, cyberpunk, Hong Kong action cinema, philosophy, and comic-book aesthetics. The Matrix is considered one of the most influential films of its era, reshaping action filmmaking and popularizing virtual-reality narratives.
