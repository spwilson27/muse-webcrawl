Big Four accounting firms

The Big Four are the four largest professional services networks in the world by revenue and headcount: Deloitte, PwC (PricewaterhouseCoopers), EY (Ernst & Young), and KPMG. Together they audit the vast majority of publicly traded companies and provide tax, consulting, and advisory services globally.

Structure

Each "firm" is in fact a global network of legally independent member firms coordinated by a central entity. Collectively the Big Four employ well over a million people and earn combined revenues in the hundreds of billions of US dollars, with Deloitte generally the largest by revenue.

History

The group was the "Big Eight" until mergers in 1989 reduced it to the Big Six, then the Big Five; the collapse of Arthur Andersen in 2002 following the Enron scandal left the Big Four. Their dominance of the audit market has prompted recurring regulatory debate about competition, audit quality, and conflicts between audit and consulting work.

Services

Beyond auditing, the firms offer tax advice, management and technology consulting, risk advisory, and deals services, and are major employers of accounting and business graduates worldwide.
