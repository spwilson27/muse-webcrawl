2015 Greek bailout referendum

The 2015 Greek bailout referendum was a referendum held in Greece on 5 July 2015, announced by Prime Minister Alexis Tsipras in the early hours of 27 June. Voters were asked whether Greece should accept the bailout conditions proposed jointly by the European Commission, the International Monetary Fund and the European Central Bank. The proposal was rejected by a margin of 61.31% to 38.69%.

Referendum details

Date: 5 July 2015
Location: Greece
Question: Acceptance of creditors' draft agreement
Result: No — 61.31%; Yes — 38.69%
Turnout: 62.5%

Background

The referendum came amid the Greek government-debt crisis. After months of negotiations between the Syriza-led government and Greece's creditors over the terms of an extended bailout, talks broke down in late June 2015. Greece's existing programme expired on 30 June, the day it also missed a payment to the IMF, and Greek banks were closed and capital controls imposed in the week before the vote.

Campaign and criticism

Tsipras and his finance minister Yanis Varoufakis campaigned for a "No" vote, arguing it would strengthen Greece's negotiating position. Opposition parties and many European officials campaigned for "Yes", framing the vote as a choice on Greece's membership of the eurozone. The question itself was criticised for referring to lengthy technical documents and for asking voters to decide on proposals that had already lapsed.

Aftermath

Despite the "No" victory, within a week Tsipras accepted a third bailout programme with conditions broadly similar to those rejected, after the European Central Bank maintained pressure on Greek banks. Varoufakis resigned the day after the referendum, and Greece remained in the euro.
