Udzungwa Mountains

LocationSouth-central Tanzania
RangeEastern Arc Mountains
Highest peakLuhombero, 2,579 m
Protected areaUdzungwa Mountains National Park

The Udzungwa Mountains are a mountain range in south-central Tanzania, forming part of the ancient Eastern Arc Mountains. They are covered in extensive tropical moist forest and are recognized as one of the world's outstanding centers of biodiversity and endemism.

Biodiversity

The Udzungwas host exceptional levels of endemic species, including the Udzungwa red colobus and Sanje crested mangabey monkeys, the Udzungwa forest partridge (discovered in 1991), and the Kihansi spray toad, which lived only in the mist zone of the Kihansi Falls. New species of elephant shrew (the grey-faced sengi, described in 2008) have also been found here. The range is part of the Eastern Afromontane biodiversity hotspot.

Conservation

Udzungwa Mountains National Park, established in 1992 and covering about 1,990 square kilometres, protects a large portion of the range. It is unusual among Tanzanian national parks in being primarily a hiking destination without roads for game driving, known for the Sanje Waterfalls trail.
