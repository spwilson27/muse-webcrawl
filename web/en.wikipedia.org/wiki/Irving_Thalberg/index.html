Irving Thalberg

Irving Grant Thalberg
Born: May 30, 1899, Brooklyn, New York, U.S.
Died: September 14, 1936 (aged 37), Santa Monica, California, U.S.
Occupation: Film producer
Spouse: Norma Shearer (m. 1927)

Irving Grant Thalberg (May 30, 1899 – September 14, 1936) was an American film producer during the early years of motion pictures. He was called "The Boy Wonder" for his youth and ability to make profitable films with quality productions. He was the head of production at Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer (MGM) and was instrumental in defining the studio's prestige image during the Golden Age of Hollywood.

Early life

Thalberg was born in Brooklyn, New York, to German Jewish immigrant parents. He suffered from a congenital heart condition ("blue baby" syndrome) and was told he would not live past thirty. Frail in health, he was largely home-schooled. He began working as a secretary and stenographer, and at Universal Pictures he became Carl Laemmle's executive secretary at age 20.

Career

At Universal, Thalberg became known for his clashes with director Erich von Stroheim over excessive budgets and lengths. In 1924 he moved to Louis B. Mayer Productions, which merged into the newly formed Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer, where he became vice-president in charge of production.

At MGM, Thalberg supervised production of hundreds of films, insisting on careful preparation, script rewrites, retakes, and preview screenings. His productions included Ben-Hur (1925), The Big Parade (1925), Grand Hotel (1932), Mutiny on the Bounty (1935), A Night at the Opera (1935), and The Good Earth (1937, released posthumously).

Personal life and death

Thalberg married actress Norma Shearer in 1927; they had two children. After suffering a heart attack in 1932, his power at MGM was diminished by Louis B. Mayer and he formed his own production unit within the studio. He died of pneumonia in 1936, aged 37.

Legacy

The Irving G. Thalberg Memorial Award, presented by the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences to producers for consistently high-quality production, is named in his honor. F. Scott Fitzgerald modeled the protagonist of his unfinished novel The Last Tycoon on Thalberg.
