The Orthodox Union (OU), formally the Union of Orthodox Jewish Congregations of America, is one of the oldest and largest umbrella organizations of Orthodox Jewish synagogues in the United States, founded in 1898. It supports a network of synagogues, youth programs, and communal services, and is best known publicly for its kosher food certification.

Kosher certification

OU Kosher, established in 1923, is the world's largest kosher certification agency. Its symbol — a U inside a circle, Ⓤ — appears on hundreds of thousands of products in dozens of countries, certifying that they comply with Jewish dietary law (kashrut). The agency employs many hundreds of rabbinic field representatives (mashgichim) and coordinators, and its certifications include OU, OU-D (dairy), OU-P (Passover), OU-F (fish), and OU-Glatt designations.

Programs

The OU operates NCSY, a major Orthodox youth movement; Yachad, serving people with disabilities; the OU Advocacy Center, which represents Orthodox interests to government; and publishing and educational ventures including OU Torah and the Jewish Learning Initiative on Campus. It is a founding partner of synagogue and community services across North America and Israel through OU Israel.

History

Founded as the Union of Orthodox Jewish Congregations of America in 1898, in part as a traditionalist counterpart to Reform and Conservative institutions, the OU helped standardize kosher supervision in America in the 1920s — its early certification of Heinz products in 1923 being an early landmark. It remains closely associated with Modern Orthodox Judaism and with the Rabbinical Council of America.
