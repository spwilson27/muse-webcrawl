Avvakum

Avvakum Petrov (Russian: Аввакýм Петро́в; 20 November 1620/21 – 14 April 1682) was a Russian archpriest who led the opposition to Patriarch Nikon's reforms of the Russian Orthodox Church. His uncompromising defence of the "old rites" made him the foundational figure of the Old Believers (raskolniki), and his autobiography is a landmark of early Russian literature.

Life

Ordained young, Avvakum gained a reputation as a zealous parish priest and joined the circle of reforming clergy known as the Zealots of Piety. He initially supported Nikon but broke with him over the reforms of 1653, which revised liturgical books and practices to conform to Greek usage—including making the sign of the cross with three fingers rather than two. Avvakum regarded these changes as apostasy.

For his resistance he was repeatedly exiled, spending years in Siberia (1653–1664) under harsh conditions, then defrocked and anathematised at the council of 1666–67. Confined in an earthen pit at Pustozersk in the far north, he wrote his Life (c. 1672–1675), the first autobiography in Russian literature, written in a vivid mixture of Church Slavonic and colloquial Russian. In 1682 he was burned at the stake in a log hut.

Legacy

The schism (Raskol) Avvakum inspired split Russian Orthodoxy permanently; Old Believer communities preserve the pre-Nikonian rites to this day. He is venerated as a saint by Old Believers, and his Life is regarded as a masterpiece of 17th-century Russian prose.
