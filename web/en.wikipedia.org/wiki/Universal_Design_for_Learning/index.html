Universal Design for Learning

Universal Design for Learning (UDL) is an educational framework developed by the Center for Applied Special Technology (CAST) that aims to improve and optimize teaching and learning for all people based on scientific insights into how humans learn. It adapts the concept of universal design from architecture to curriculum design.

Principles

The UDL Guidelines are organised around three principles, each corresponding to a network of the learning brain:

  • Engagement (the "why" of learning) — provide multiple means of recruiting interest, sustaining effort, and self-regulation.
  • Representation (the "what" of learning) — present information in multiple formats: perception, language and symbols, and comprehension.
  • Action and expression (the "how" of learning) — allow learners to demonstrate knowledge through varied physical actions, expression and communication, and executive functions.

Recognition

UDL is referenced in United States federal education law, including the Higher Education Opportunity Act of 2008 and the Every Student Succeeds Act of 2015, and CAST publishes periodically updated versions of the Guidelines, most recently version 3.0 in 2024.
