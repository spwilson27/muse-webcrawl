Rose Revolution

The Rose Revolution (Georgian: ვარდების რევოლუცია) was a nonviolent protest movement and change of power in Georgia in November 2003 that forced President Eduard Shevardnadze to resign. It takes its name from the red roses carried by opposition demonstrators who stormed the parliament session, and it became the first of the "colour revolutions" in the post-Soviet space.

Course of events

Parliamentary elections on 2 November 2003 were widely condemned by observers as fraudulent. The opposition, led by Mikheil Saakashvili, Zurab Zhvania and Nino Burjanadze, organized mass protests in Tbilisi supported by the youth movement Kmara and independent media. On 22 November, as Shevardnadze opened the disputed new parliament, Saakashvili led protesters carrying roses into the chamber; Shevardnadze was escorted out by his bodyguards and resigned the next day. Burjanadze became acting president, and fresh elections in January 2004 brought Saakashvili to the presidency with over 96% of the vote.

Aftermath

The new government pursued rapid anti-corruption and market reforms, dismissed the entire traffic police, and sought NATO and EU integration, straining relations with Russia. The revolution inspired Ukraine's Orange Revolution (2004) and Kyrgyzstan's Tulip Revolution (2005), though Saakashvili's later rule drew criticism for concentrating power, and his party lost power peacefully in 2012.
