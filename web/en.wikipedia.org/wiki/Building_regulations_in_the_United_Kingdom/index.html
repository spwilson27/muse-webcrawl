Building regulations in the United Kingdom

Building control inspection of construction work

RegulationsBuilding Regulations 2010

Building regulations in the United Kingdom are statutory instruments that seek to ensure that the policies set out in the Building Act 1984, as applicable, are carried out. Building regulations approval is required for most building work in the UK. They are distinct from planning permission: building regulations concern how a building is constructed, while planning concerns whether development may take place at all and its appearance.

Structure

The functional requirements are set out in Schedule 1 of the Building Regulations 2010, divided into parts A to R: structure (A), fire safety (B), site preparation and resistance to contaminants and moisture (C), toxic substances (D), resistance to the passage of sound (E), ventilation (F), sanitation and water efficiency (G), drainage (H), combustion appliances (J), protection from falling and collision (K), conservation of fuel and power (L), access (M), glazing safety (N), electrical safety (P), security (Q), infrastructure for high-speed electronic communications (R), and materials and workmanship (Regulation 7). Practical guidance on meeting the requirements is given in the Approved Documents.

Enforcement

Compliance is overseen by building control bodies — local authority building control departments or private approved inspectors. Work must be notified in advance, is inspected at key stages, and receives a completion certificate. Local authorities may prosecute or serve enforcement notices for breaches.

Reform after Grenfell

Following the Grenfell Tower fire of 2017, the Building Safety Act 2022 introduced a new regime for higher-risk buildings, including a Building Safety Regulator, gateway approvals during design and construction, and a "golden thread" of building information.
