Raja Ramanna

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Raja Ramanna (28 January 1925 – 24 September 2004) was an Indian physicist who is best known for his role in India's nuclear program during its early stages. He directed the team that carried out India's first nuclear test, codenamed Smiling Buddha, in 1974, and later served as chairman of the Atomic Energy Commission of India and as Minister of State for Defence.

Early life and education

Ramanna was born in Tiptur, in the princely state of Mysore (now Karnataka). He studied at Bishop Cotton Boys' School in Bangalore and Madras Christian College, and earned his doctorate in nuclear physics from King's College London in 1954. A talented pianist, he also held a licentiate diploma in piano performance.

Career

After returning to India, Ramanna joined the Tata Institute of Fundamental Research and worked closely with Homi Bhabha. He became director of the Bhabha Atomic Research Centre (BARC) in 1972. Under his leadership, India tested its first nuclear device at Pokhran on 18 May 1974.

Later roles

Ramanna served two terms as chairman of the Atomic Energy Commission, was scientific adviser to the Minister of Defence, and served as Minister of State for Defence in 1990. He was later a member of the Rajya Sabha. He received the Padma Shri, Padma Bhushan and Padma Vibhushan, India's high civilian honours. He died in Mumbai in 2004.
