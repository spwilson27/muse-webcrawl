Spiro Mounds

Spiro Mounds is an archaeological site located in present-day Le Flore County, Oklahoma, near the town of Spiro. It was a major ceremonial center of the Caddoan Mississippian culture, occupied from about 850 to 1450 CE, and is considered one of the most important pre-Columbian sites in the United States, sometimes called the "King Tut of the West" for the richness of its burials.

The site

The site contains twelve earthen mounds arranged around plazas on a terrace above the Arkansas River. The largest, Brown Mound, was a platform mound supporting elite structures. Spiro's inhabitants farmed the river bottomlands and traded across a vast network reaching the Great Lakes, Gulf Coast, and the Southwest, acquiring copper, conch shells, obsidian, and mica.

Craig Mound and the Great Mortuary

Craig Mound, the second largest, contained the "Great Mortuary," a hollow chamber within the mound in which elite burials were interred with extraordinary grave goods: engraved shell gorgets and cups, copper plates and masks (including the famous long-nosed god maskettes), stone effigy pipes, and thousands of pearl beads. These objects are key sources for the Southeastern Ceremonial Complex, a shared body of religious iconography across the Mississippian world.

Looting and preservation

In the 1930s, during the Great Depression, commercial looters leased Craig Mound and tunneled into it, selling artifacts widely; public outrage over the destruction helped prompt Oklahoma antiquities legislation in 1935, after which University of Oklahoma archaeologists excavated under Works Progress Administration funding. The site is now the Spiro Mounds Archaeological Center, operated by the Oklahoma Historical Society, and is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.
