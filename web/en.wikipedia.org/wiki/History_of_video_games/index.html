History of video games

The history of video games began in the 1950s with experiments on early computers, became a mass industry with 1970s arcade games and home consoles, survived the 1983 crash, and grew into one of the largest entertainment sectors, spanning consoles, PC, mobile and cloud gaming.

Video game history
Early games: OXO (1952), Tennis for Two (1958), Spacewar! (1962)
First arcade hit: Pong (1972)
First console: Magnavox Odyssey (1972)

Early history (1950s–1970s)

Academic experiments like Bertie the Brain, OXO, Tennis for Two and MIT's Spacewar! preceded commerce. Ralph Baer's "Brown Box" became the Odyssey; Atari's Pong and Taito's Space Invaders (1978) ignited the golden age of arcades, followed by Pac-Man (1980) and the Atari 2600 home boom.

Crash, Nintendo and 16-bit era

Market saturation and poor titles (notably E.T.) caused the North American crash of 1983. Nintendo's NES (1985) revived the industry with quality control and Super Mario Bros. The Sega–Nintendo console war, Sonic, and PC hits like Doom (1993) defined the early 1990s.

3D, online and mobile

Sony PlayStation (1994), Nintendo 64, and 3D graphics shifted design; online multiplayer (World of Warcraft, 2004), digital distribution (Steam), motion controls (Wii, 2006), smartphones and free-to-play models (post-2007 iPhone) massively expanded audiences. Esports, streaming, indie games and VR grew in the 2010s–2020s, and industry consolidation (e.g. Microsoft–Activision Blizzard, 2023) reshaped the market.

Cultural impact

Games are studied as art, preserved by museums, and debated for effects on behaviour — with research finding little evidence linking games to violent crime, while recognising issues around addiction and monetisation.
