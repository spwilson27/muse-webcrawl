Wax

CompositionLong-chain esters, hydrocarbons, fatty acids
PropertiesPlastic at ambient temperature, low melting point
SourcesBeeswax, paraffin, carnauba, candelilla

Waxes are a diverse class of organic compounds that are lipophilic, malleable solids near ambient temperatures. They include higher alkanes and lipids, typically with melting points above about 40 °C (104 °F), that melt to give low-viscosity liquids. Waxes are insoluble in water but soluble in nonpolar organic solvents.

Types

Natural waxes include beeswax, secreted by honey bees to build honeycomb; carnauba wax from the leaves of a Brazilian palm; candelilla wax; and lanolin from wool. Petroleum-derived paraffin wax and microcrystalline wax are produced from crude oil refining. Synthetic waxes, such as polyethylene waxes, are manufactured for industrial use.

Uses

Waxes are used in candles, polishes, cosmetics, food coatings, crayons, adhesives, chewing gum, and as mould-release and waterproofing agents. Historically, wax was used for writing tablets, seals, encaustic painting and lost-wax casting.
