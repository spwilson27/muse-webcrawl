Fuzuli (poet)

Muḥammad bin Sulaymān (c. 1483 – 1556), better known by his pen name Fuzuli (also spelled Fuzūlī or Fizuli), was a 16th-century poet, prose author and thinker who wrote in Azerbaijani, Persian and Arabic. He is regarded as one of the greatest contributors to the divan tradition of Azerbaijani literature.

Life

Fuzuli was born in what is now Iraq, probably in Karbala or Baghdad, and lived most of his life in Ottoman Iraq. He received a broad education in theology, medicine, astronomy and languages, and served for a time at the shrine of Imam Husayn in Karbala.

Works

His most famous work is the epic poem Leyli and Majnun (1535–36), a retelling of the classic tale of star-crossed lovers, considered the pinnacle of his art. His collected divans in three languages, the allegorical prose work Hadikat as-Suada, and the satirical Şikâyetnâme ("Book of Complaints") are also celebrated.

Legacy

Fuzuli's lyric poetry, marked by themes of love, longing and suffering rendered in rich imagery, profoundly influenced later Azerbaijani, Turkish and Persian literature. A district and town in Azerbaijan bear his name.
