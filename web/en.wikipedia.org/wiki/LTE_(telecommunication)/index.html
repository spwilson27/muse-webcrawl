LTE (telecommunication)

Family3GPP
IntroducedStandard frozen 2008; first commercial service 2009 (Stockholm and Oslo)
GenerationMarketed as 4G; technically pre-4G (3.95G) until LTE Advanced
Access schemeOFDMA (downlink), SC-FDMA (uplink)
Peak rates (Release 8)300 Mbit/s downlink, 75 Mbit/s uplink

LTE, short for Long-Term Evolution, is a standard for wireless broadband communication for mobile devices and data terminals, developed by the 3GPP (3rd Generation Partnership Project) and first specified in Release 8 in 2008. It increased the capacity and speed of networks using a redesigned, all-IP flat architecture and new radio techniques, and became the dominant global mobile broadband technology of the 2010s.

Technical features

LTE uses orthogonal frequency-division multiple access (OFDMA) for the downlink and single-carrier FDMA for the uplink, with scalable channel bandwidths from 1.4 to 20 MHz, multiple-antenna (MIMO) support, and low latency of around 10 ms in the radio access network. The core network, the Evolved Packet Core (EPC), carries all traffic — including voice, via Voice over LTE (VoLTE) — as IP packets, abandoning circuit switching.

Deployment

The first publicly available LTE services were launched by TeliaSonera in Stockholm and Oslo in December 2009. LTE spread rapidly worldwide; later enhancements grouped as LTE Advanced (Release 10) and LTE Advanced Pro added carrier aggregation, higher-order MIMO, and peak rates in the gigabit range, meeting the IMT-Advanced requirements for true 4G.

Successor

LTE networks coexist with and are being succeeded by 5G NR networks; many early 5G deployments operate in non-standalone mode anchored to an LTE core.
