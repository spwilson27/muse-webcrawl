Poisson manifold

In differential geometry, a Poisson manifold is a smooth manifold equipped with a Poisson bracket on its algebra of smooth functions, making that algebra into a Poisson algebra. Equivalently, a Poisson manifold carries a bivector field (the Poisson tensor) whose Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket with itself vanishes.

Definition

A Poisson bracket is a bilinear operation {·,·} on smooth functions that is skew-symmetric, satisfies the Leibniz rule in each argument (so that {f, ·} is a derivation, defining the Hamiltonian vector field of f), and satisfies the Jacobi identity. Every symplectic manifold is a Poisson manifold, with the bracket induced by the symplectic form; more generally the dual of a Lie algebra carries a natural Poisson structure, and Poisson manifolds decompose into symplectic leaves.

Significance

Poisson geometry generalises Hamiltonian mechanics: the phase space of a mechanical system need not be symplectic, and Poisson structures model systems with symmetries and constraints, as well as appearing in deformation quantisation, integrable systems, and noncommutative geometry. The field traces its modern form to work by Lichnerowicz, Weinstein, and others in the 1970s.
