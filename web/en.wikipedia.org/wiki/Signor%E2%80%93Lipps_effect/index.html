Signor–Lipps effect

The Signor–Lipps effect is a paleontological principle proposed by Philip Signor and Jere Lipps stating that, since the fossil record is inherently incomplete, the first and last recorded occurrences of a taxon will rarely coincide with its actual origination or extinction.

Implications

Because of sampling gaps, a mass extinction that was in reality abrupt will appear smeared out gradually in the fossil record, as species seem to disappear one by one before the boundary. Conversely, taxa may appear to survive a boundary when their last fossils are reworked into younger sediments.

Related concepts

The effect complicates the study of events such as the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction. Related biases include the "Lazarus taxon" phenomenon, where a group vanishes from the record and later reappears, and range-through assumptions in diversity studies.
