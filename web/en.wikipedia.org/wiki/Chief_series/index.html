Chief series

In abstract algebra, a chief series is a maximal normal series for a group: a chain of subgroups, each normal in the whole group, that cannot be refined further. Chief series are the group-theoretic analogue of composition series for modules, and they are an important tool in the structure theory of finite groups.

A chain of normal subgroups with chief factors between consecutive terms

Definition

A chief series for a group G is a series of subgroups

1 = N0 ⊲ N1 ⊲ ... ⊲ Nn = G

such that each Ni is normal in G (not merely in Ni+1), and there is no normal subgroup of G strictly between Ni and Ni+1. Equivalently, each quotient Ni+1/Ni — called a chief factor of G — is a minimal normal subgroup of G/Ni.

Properties

  • A group has a chief series if and only if it satisfies both the maximal and minimal conditions on normal subgroups; in particular, every finite group has a chief series.
  • Jordan–Hölder theorem for chief series: any two chief series of a group have the same length, and their multisets of chief factors are isomorphic up to permutation. Thus the chief factors are invariants of the group.
  • Chief factors of a finite group are characteristically simple: each is a direct product of isomorphic simple groups.
  • For a finite solvable group, every chief factor is an elementary abelian p-group for some prime p, so a chief series refines naturally into p-series used in the theory of formations and system normalizers.

Examples

For a cyclic group of prime order, the trivial series of length one is a chief series. For the symmetric group S4, the chain 1 ⊲ V4 ⊲ A4 ⊲ S4, where V4 is the Klein four-group, is a chief series. A simple group has the trivial chief series 1 ⊲ G, its only chief factor being itself.
