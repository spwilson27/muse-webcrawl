Bison antiquus, the antique bison or ancient bison, is an extinct species of bison that lived in Late Pleistocene North America until around 10,000 years ago. It was one of the most common large herbivores on the continent and is a direct ancestor of the living American bison.

Description

Bison antiquus was larger and heavier than the modern American bison, standing about 2.27 m (7.5 ft) tall at the shoulder, reaching 4.6 m (15 ft) in length and weighing roughly 1,590 kg (3,500 lb). Its horns were also larger, spanning up to about 1 m from tip to tip. Despite its size, it closely resembled its modern descendant in general build.

Evolution and extinction

The species is thought to have evolved from the still larger steppe bison, Bison priscus, which crossed the Bering land bridge into North America. Over time B. antiquus decreased in size, giving rise through Bison occidentalis to the smaller modern Bison bison. The species disappeared during the Quaternary extinction event at the end of the last ice age, alongside much of the North American megafauna.

Fossil sites and human interaction

More than 500 specimens were recovered from the La Brea Tar Pits in Los Angeles, making it one of the best-represented large mammals there. Bison antiquus was hunted by Paleo-Indians: at the Folsom site in New Mexico and the Olsen-Chubbuck site in Colorado, mass kills of the related B. occidentalis form document communal bison hunting by early peoples of the Great Plains.
