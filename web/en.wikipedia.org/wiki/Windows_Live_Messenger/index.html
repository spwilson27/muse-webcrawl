Windows Live Messenger (formerly MSN Messenger) was an instant messaging client developed by Microsoft. Launched on 22 July 1999 as MSN Messenger Service, it became one of the world's most popular instant messaging services in the 2000s, with hundreds of millions of users exchanging messages, emoticons, winks, nudges and files, and later making voice and video calls. The service was discontinued in favour of Skype in 2013 (and in China at the end of October 2014).

History

MSN Messenger grew rapidly in the early 2000s, competing with AOL Instant Messenger, ICQ and Yahoo! Messenger. Rebranded as Windows Live Messenger with the Windows Live wave in 2006, the client gained features such as offline messaging, personalised status messages, folder sharing, integration with Xbox Live and the social network Windows Live Spaces, and support for interoperability with Yahoo! Messenger contacts.

At its peak around 2009 the service reported more than 330 million active users per month. Subsequent versions integrated with Facebook Chat and the Windows Live Essentials suite.

Discontinuation

After Microsoft acquired Skype in 2011, it announced in November 2012 that Messenger would be retired and its users migrated to Skype, which adopted the underlying accounts. The Messenger service shut down for most of the world on 8 April 2013, ending a fourteen-year run; the protocol lived on briefly in third-party clients through the MSNP reverse-engineering community, and fan "revival" servers have since recreated aspects of the classic client.

Features and culture

Messenger is remembered for its distinctive culture: custom emoticons, display pictures, "what I'm listening to" status lines from Windows Media Player, the attention-seeking "nudge" that shook a contact's window, and animated "winks". These features made it a defining communication tool for a generation of internet users, particularly in Europe, Latin America and Canada.
