Jan Zajíc

Jan Zajíc (3 July 1950 – 25 February 1969) was a Czech student who committed suicide by self-immolation in Wenceslas Square in Prague in protest against the Soviet-led occupation of Czechoslovakia after the Prague Spring and the passivity of the public. He was the second Czech to do so, after Jan Palach.

Jan Zajíc
Born: 3 July 1950, Vítkov, Czechoslovakia
Died: 25 February 1969, Prague
Known for: Self-immolation protest against the Warsaw Pact invasion

Life

Zajíc was born in Vítkov in Moravian Silesia, son of a teacher and a shop assistant. He studied at an industrial school for railway transport in Šumperk. Deeply affected by Palach's death in January 1969 and by the funeral demonstrations, he resolved to act as "Torch No. 2."

Self-immolation

On 25 February 1969 — the 21st anniversary of the Communist coup of 1948 — Zajíc travelled to Prague with farewell letters calling on Czechs and Slovaks not to resign themselves to occupation. In a passage at No. 39 Wenceslas Square he drank acid to prevent himself crying out, doused himself in petrol and set himself alight, but collapsed before reaching the square itself. He died on the spot.

Aftermath and memory

The secret police pressured his family and forced his burial in Vítkov rather than Prague, fearing a pilgrimage site; even so thousands attended. After the Velvet Revolution of 1989, a memorial to Palach and Zajíc was placed in Wenceslas Square before the National Museum, and Zajíc received posthumous honours including the Order of Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk.
