Just So Stories for Little Children is a 1902 collection of origin stories by the English author Rudyard Kipling. Each story purports to explain how an animal acquired its distinctive features, and is addressed to the narrator's "Best Beloved". The tales are accompanied by poems and by Kipling's own illustrations.

Background

The stories began as bedtime tales told to Kipling's daughter Josephine, who demanded that they be told "just so" — word for word the same each night — which gives the collection its name. Josephine died in 1899, and the published book is a memorial to her. The fables are playful examples of the "pourquoi story" or origin myth, written in a distinctive rhythmic prose.

Legacy

The collection remains one of Kipling's best-loved works, and "The Elephant's Child", with its "satiable curiosity" and the Limpopo River, is among the most famous of children's stories in English.
