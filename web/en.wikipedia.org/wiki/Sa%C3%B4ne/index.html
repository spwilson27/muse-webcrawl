Saône

Length: 473 km (294 mi)
Source: Vioménil, Vosges
Mouth: Rhône at Lyon
Main tributary: Doubs

The Saône (French: Saône, pronounced [soːn]) is a river in eastern France. It is a right tributary of the Rhône, rising at Vioménil in the Vosges mountains and joining the Rhône at Lyon, 473 kilometres (294 mi) downstream.

Course

The Saône flows gently south through the departments of Vosges, Haute-Saône, Côte-d'Or, Saône-et-Loire and Ain, passing Gray, Chalon-sur-Saône and Mâcon before reaching Lyon, where it meets the Rhône at the southern tip of the Presqu'île. Its largest tributary is the Doubs. The river's very low gradient led the Romans to call it Arar; its modern name derives from the Celtic river goddess Souconna.

Navigation

The Saône is navigable for most of its lower course and forms part of the waterway linking the Rhône (and hence the Mediterranean) with the Rhine and the Moselle via canals such as the Canal entre Champagne et Bourgogne.
