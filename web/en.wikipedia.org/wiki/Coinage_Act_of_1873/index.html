Coinage Act of 1873

The Coinage Act of 1873 was a general revision of the laws relating to the Mint of the United States. It ended the right of holders of silver bullion to have it struck into standard silver dollars, effectively placing the United States on a gold standard; critics later dubbed it the "Crime of 1873".

Provisions

The Act moved control of coinage law from Congress to the Treasury, dropped the standard silver dollar from the list of authorised coins, and substituted a trade dollar intended for the China trade. It also raised the Mint Director's salary and codified mint procedures.

The "Crime of 1873"

When silver prices fell in the mid-1870s, farmers and silver miners blamed the Act for deflation and demanded restoration of free silver coinage. The epithet "Crime of 1873" became a rallying cry of the Free Silver movement, culminating in William Jennings Bryan's "Cross of Gold" speech of 1896.
