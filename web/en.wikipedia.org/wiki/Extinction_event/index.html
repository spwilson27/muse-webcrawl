Extinction event

An extinction event, also called a mass extinction or biotic crisis, is a widespread and rapid decrease in the biodiversity on Earth. Such an event is identified by a sharp fall in the diversity and abundance of multicellular organisms, occurring over a geologically short period.

The Big Five

  • End Ordovician (~444 Ma) — glaciation and sea-level fall
  • Late Devonian (~372 Ma) — prolonged crisis
  • End Permian (~252 Ma) — the Great Dying, the largest, linked to Siberian Traps volcanism
  • End Triassic (~201 Ma) — volcanism, opened the way for dinosaurs
  • End Cretaceous (~66 Ma) — Chicxulub asteroid impact, ended non-avian dinosaurs

Causes

Proposed causes include flood basalt eruptions, asteroid impacts, sea-level changes, ocean anoxia, and climate change.

Current crisis

Many scientists argue human activity is driving a sixth mass extinction, sometimes termed the Holocene extinction. See Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction.
