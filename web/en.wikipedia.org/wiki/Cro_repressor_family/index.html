Cro repressor family

Symbol: Cro

Pfam: PF09048

InterPro: IPR015213

SCOP: 2cro

In molecular biology, the Cro repressor family is a family of repressor proteins found in bacteriophages and bacteria. The archetypal member is the Cro protein of bacteriophage lambda, a small DNA-binding protein that plays a central role in the phage's decision between lysogeny and lytic growth.

Function

During infection of Escherichia coli by bacteriophage lambda, two regulatory proteins compete for control of the phage's genetic switch: the cI repressor, which maintains the dormant lysogenic state, and Cro, which promotes the lytic pathway. Cro binds to operator sites in the rightward operator region (OR) and represses transcription of the cI gene, thereby preventing the establishment or maintenance of lysogeny and committing the phage to lytic development.

Cro binds most tightly to the OR3 site, blocking the promoter for cI synthesis, and at higher concentrations also occupies OR1 and OR2, down-regulating its own synthesis. This ordering of binding affinities is the reverse of that of the cI repressor and underlies the bistable character of the lambda switch, one of the best-studied gene regulatory circuits in biology.

Structure

Lambda Cro is a small protein of 66 amino acid residues that forms a homodimer. Each monomer contains a helix-turn-helix DNA-binding motif; the structure, determined by X-ray crystallography, was among the first helix-turn-helix structures known and became a model for protein–DNA recognition. Members of the wider Cro family share this fold and are distributed among temperate phages and prophage elements of many bacterial species.
