Absorbed dose

SI unitgray (Gy) = J/kg
Other unitrad (1 Gy = 100 rad)
SymbolD

Absorbed dose is the amount of energy deposited by ionizing radiation per unit mass of matter. It is a fundamental quantity in radiation dosimetry, radiotherapy and radiation protection, defined as the mean energy imparted divided by the mass of the absorbing volume.

Units

The SI unit of absorbed dose is the gray (Gy), equal to one joule per kilogram. The older unit, the rad, equals 0.01 Gy and is still used in some countries. Absorbed dose alone does not describe biological effect, which depends on radiation type and tissue sensitivity; these are accounted for by the equivalent dose and effective dose, measured in sieverts.

Measurement and use

Absorbed dose is measured with calorimeters, ionisation chambers and chemical dosimeters, and calculated in treatment planning for radiation therapy, where tumour doses of tens of grays are delivered in fractions. Diagnostic imaging doses are typically in the milligray range.
