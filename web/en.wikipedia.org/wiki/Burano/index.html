Burano

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Burano's brightly coloured fishermen's houses line its canals (image placeholder)

Burano is an island in the Venetian Lagoon, northern Italy, about 7 km (4.3 mi) northeast of Venice, with a population of roughly 2,500–2,800. It is famous for its lace-making and for its small, brightly painted houses, and is connected by a bridge to the neighbouring island of Mazzorbo.

Lace

Burano lace (merletto) became renowned across Europe from the 16th century, worked in the air with needle and thread in the punto in aria technique; Leonardo da Vinci is said to have purchased Burano lace for the altar of Milan Cathedral. The craft declined in the 18th century and was revived with the opening of a lace school in 1872; genuine handmade Burano lace remains rare and expensive. The island's lace museum, the Museo del Merletto, occupies the old school.

The coloured houses

By local tradition the houses were painted in vivid colours so that fishermen returning in fog could identify their homes; today the colour scheme is regulated, and owners must seek municipal approval — and choose from a permitted palette — to repaint. The leaning campanile of the church of San Martino, topped by an angel weathervane, is a landmark visible across the lagoon.

Notable people and culture

Burano was the birthplace of the composer Baldassarre Galuppi (1706–1785), commemorated by a statue in the island's single square. The island is also known for its butter cookies, bussolai buranei.
