Ghazanchetsots Cathedral (Armenian: Ղազանչեցոց Սուրբ Ամենափրկիչ Մայր Տաճար, Ghazanchetsots Surb Amenaprkitch Mayr Tacharr), also known as the Holy Saviour Cathedral and the Cathedral of Christ the Saviour, is an Armenian Apostolic cathedral in Shusha (Shushi), in the disputed region of Nagorno-Karabakh. Built between 1868 and 1887, it is one of the largest Armenian churches in the world and a landmark of the city.

History

The cathedral was constructed on the site of an earlier, smaller church. Its name derives from the Ghazanchi village in Nakhchivan, whose inhabitants are said to have funded or been associated with its construction. The church was consecrated in 1888. Its architect is traditionally identified as Simon Ter-Hakobyan.

During the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict the cathedral was damaged in the 1992 capture of Shusha and again during the 2020 war, when shelling in October 2020 struck the building twice in one day, wounding journalists and causing significant damage to the roof and interior. After Azerbaijan regained control of Shusha, restoration work was carried out, which Armenian sources criticised as altering the church's appearance, including the removal of its conical dome.

Architecture

The cathedral is built of white limestone in a cruciform plan with a large conical dome rising over the crossing, a hallmark of Armenian church architecture. The bell tower, a three-storey structure, was famous for its four trumpeting angel statues at the corners of the second floor; most were destroyed during the conflicts. The interior was decorated with frescoes and carved stone ornamentation.
