Edmonton Oilers

The Edmonton Oilers are a professional ice hockey team based in Edmonton, Alberta, Canada. They compete in the National Hockey League (NHL) as a member of the Pacific Division of the Western Conference. The team was founded in 1972 as the Alberta Oilers of the World Hockey Association (WHA) and joined the NHL in 1979 as part of the NHL–WHA merger.

History

The Oilers became one of the great dynasties in hockey during the 1980s, led by Wayne Gretzky, Mark Messier, Paul Coffey, Jari Kurri and Grant Fuhr under coach Glen Sather. Edmonton won the Stanley Cup five times, in 1984, 1985, 1987, 1988 and 1990. Gretzky was traded to the Los Angeles Kings in 1988 in one of the most famous transactions in sports history. The Oilers reached the Stanley Cup Final again in 2006, losing to the Carolina Hurricanes, and in 2024, losing in seven games to the Florida Panthers. In the modern era the team has been built around captain Connor McDavid and Leon Draisaitl.

Arena

The Oilers played at Northlands Coliseum from 1974 until 2016, when they moved to Rogers Place in downtown Edmonton's Ice District.
