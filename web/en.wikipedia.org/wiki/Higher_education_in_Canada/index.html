Higher education in Canada

Higher education in Canada describes post-secondary education provided by universities, colleges, and institutes across Canada's ten provinces and three territories. Education is a provincial responsibility under the Canadian constitution, so there is no national ministry of education; quality assurance is handled by provincial bodies and by Universities Canada, the association of member institutions.

Universities

Canada has more than 100 public universities granting bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees. The largest include the University of Toronto, the University of British Columbia, McGill University, the University of Alberta, and the Université de Montréal. Several Canadian universities rank among the world's top institutions, notably in research output. Instruction is offered in English and French, with bilingual institutions such as the University of Ottawa.

Colleges

Public colleges of applied arts and technology (and CEGEPs in Quebec) offer diplomas, certificates, and increasingly applied bachelor's degrees oriented to the labour market. Quebec's system requires students to pass through a CEGEP between secondary school and university.

Funding and tuition

Higher education is publicly subsidised, though the provincial share of university revenue has declined as tuition fees have risen. Average undergraduate tuition for Canadian citizens is roughly CAD 7,000–8,000 per year, with international students paying several times more. Federal support comes through research granting councils (CIHR, NSERC, and SSHRC) and student aid programmes.
