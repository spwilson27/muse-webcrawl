Diwali

Diwali (also called Deepavali or Dipavali) is the Hindu festival of lights, with variations celebrated in other Indian religions such as Jainism and Sikhism. It symbolises the spiritual "victory of light over darkness, good over evil, and knowledge over ignorance".

Observed byHindus, Jains, Sikhs, some Buddhists
TypeReligious, cultural
CelebrationsDiya and lighting, fireworks, gifts, feasting
Duration5 days

Observance

Diwali is celebrated during the Hindu lunisolar months of Ashvin and Kartika, usually between mid-September and mid-November. Celebrations include lighting oil lamps (diyas) and candles, bursting fireworks, exchanging gifts and sweets, and prayers to Lakshmi, the goddess of prosperity.

Significance

The festival is associated with several legends, most notably the return of Prince Rama to Ayodhya after defeating the demon king Ravana, as told in the Ramayana. Jains commemorate the nirvana of Mahavira, and Sikhs celebrate Bandi Chhor Divas, marking Guru Hargobind's release from prison.
