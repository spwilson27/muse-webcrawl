King cobra

King cobra
Scientific nameOphiophagus hannah
FamilyElapidae
Length3–4 m (up to 5.85 m)

The king cobra (Ophiophagus hannah) is a venomous snake endemic to Asia. It is the world's longest venomous snake, with an average length of 3 to 4 metres and a record of 5.85 metres. With an average lifespan of around 20 years, it is also among the longest-lived venomous snakes. Despite its common name, the king cobra is not a member of the genus Naja, the "true cobras", but is the sole member of its own genus, Ophiophagus, whose name means "snake-eater".

Distribution and habitat

King cobras range across forests from India through southern China to the Malay Peninsula and the Philippines. They inhabit dense highland forests, bamboo thickets, mangrove swamps and areas near streams, and are capable swimmers and climbers.

Behaviour and diet

The king cobra feeds predominantly on other snakes, including venomous species such as kraits and true cobras, and occasionally on lizards and rodents. When threatened it raises the front third of its body, flattens its neck into a hood, hisses and may emit a low growling sound. It is generally shy and avoids humans, but a female guarding her nest — the king cobra is the only snake known to build a nest for its eggs — will defend it vigorously.

Venom

King cobra venom consists primarily of postsynaptic neurotoxins. Although not the most potent venom among elapids, the large quantity delivered in a single bite can kill an adult human, and even an Asian elephant, within hours if untreated. Antivenom is produced in India and Thailand.

Conservation and culture

The species is listed as Vulnerable by the IUCN owing to habitat destruction and hunting. It is venerated in the folklore and mythology of India and Southeast Asia, and is the national reptile of India.
