Elastomer

An elastomer is a polymer with viscoelasticity (having both viscosity and elasticity) and relatively weak intermolecular forces, generally having a low Young's modulus and high failure strain compared with other materials. The term, a portmanteau of "elastic polymer", is often used interchangeably with "rubber", although the latter is preferred when referring to vulcanised material.

Structure and properties

Elastomers are amorphous polymers maintained above their glass transition temperature so that considerable molecular reconformation is possible. Their long polymer chains are lightly cross-linked — by vulcanisation with sulfur in rubbers, or by physical or chemical junctions in thermoplastic elastomers — forming a network that lets the material be stretched to several times its original length and spring back when the stress is released. This elastic behaviour is largely entropic: stretching uncoils the chains into lower-entropy configurations.

Types and uses

Common elastomers include natural rubber (polyisoprene), styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR), nitrile rubber, neoprene (polychloroprene), EPDM, butyl rubber, and silicone rubbers. Thermoplastic elastomers such as SBS block copolymers can be melt-processed like plastics while behaving like rubber in service. Elastomers are used in tyres, seals and gaskets, hoses, belts, adhesives, footwear, medical devices, and vibration damping.
