Victoria Cross

The Victoria Cross (VC) is the highest and most prestigious decoration of the British decorations system. It is awarded for valour "in the presence of the enemy" to members of the British Armed Forces and may be awarded posthumously. It was introduced on 29 January 1856 by Queen Victoria to honour acts of valour during the Crimean War.

Description

The medal is a bronze cross pattée bearing the Royal crest and the scroll "For Valour", suspended by a crimson ribbon. Tradition holds the bronze came from Russian cannon captured at Sevastopol, though research questions the exact provenance. The recipient's name, rank, unit and action date are engraved on the reverse.

History and recipients

Since 1856, 1,358 crosses have been awarded to 1,355 individuals, including three Bars for a second award. Recipients include members of Commonwealth forces; separate versions — the Victoria Cross for Australia, for Canada and for New Zealand — were later created. The VC takes precedence over all other orders and medals, and recipients receive an annuity under certain schemes.
