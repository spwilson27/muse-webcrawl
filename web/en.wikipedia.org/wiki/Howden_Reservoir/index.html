Howden Reservoir is a Y-shaped reservoir in the Upper Derwent Valley in Derbyshire, England. It is the middle of three reservoirs in the valley, lying between Ladybower Reservoir to the south and Derwent Reservoir to the north. The reservoir was built to supply water to the cities of Sheffield, Nottingham, Derby and Leicester.

History

Construction of the Howden Dam began in 1901 under the Derwent Valley Water Board, and the reservoir was formally opened in 1912. The masonry dam, some 36 metres high, was built from gritstone quarried locally. During the Second World War, the valley reservoirs were used by No. 617 Squadron RAF to practise low-level flying for the famous Dambusters raids, owing to their resemblance to the German dams targeted in Operation Chastise.

Geography

The reservoir is fed by the River Derwent and the River Westend. Its two arms extend into the valleys of the Derwent and the Westend, surrounded by the moorlands of the Peak District. The area is popular with walkers and cyclists, and a road runs along the western shore.
