Tank Man

Event1989 Tiananmen Square protests and massacre
DateJune 5, 1989
LocationChang'an Avenue, Beijing, China
IdentityUnknown

The "Tank Man" (also known as the "Unknown Protester" or "Unknown Rebel") is the nickname given to an unidentified Chinese man who stood in front of a column of People's Liberation Army Type 59 tanks on Chang'an Avenue in Beijing on June 5, 1989, the day after the Chinese military forcibly cleared Tiananmen Square of pro-democracy demonstrators. As the lead tank attempted to manoeuvre around him, the man repeatedly shifted position to block its path. The confrontation was photographed and filmed by foreign journalists from the balconies of the Beijing Hotel and broadcast worldwide, producing one of the most iconic images of the 20th century.

The confrontation

The man, carrying a shopping bag in each hand and wearing a white shirt and black trousers, walked into the avenue as the tank column advanced eastward and positioned himself in front of the lead vehicle. After halting the column for several minutes, he climbed onto the lead tank and appeared to speak with its crew before climbing down and resuming his stand. Bystanders then pulled him away into the crowd. His identity and fate remain unknown; the Chinese government has never publicly accounted for him.

Photographs and significance

At least five photographers captured the scene; the most widely reproduced versions are those by Jeff Widener of the Associated Press and Charlie Cole of Newsweek, whose film was smuggled out of China. Time magazine named the Unknown Rebel one of the 100 most influential people of the 20th century. Inside China, the image is heavily censored and largely unrecognised by younger generations, while abroad it remains a universal symbol of individual defiance against state power.
