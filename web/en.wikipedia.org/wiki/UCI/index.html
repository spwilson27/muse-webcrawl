UCI

The Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI) is the world governing body for the sport of cycling. Founded on 14 April 1900 in Paris by the national cycling federations of Belgium, France, Italy, Switzerland, and the United States, it is headquartered in Aigle, Switzerland, at the UCI World Cycling Centre. The UCI is recognized by the International Olympic Committee and organizes the world championships for cycling disciplines.

Responsibilities

The UCI issues licences to riders, sanctions races, enforces disciplinary rules including anti-doping regulations, maintains points rankings, and classifies races from local events up to the UCI WorldTour for men's and women's professional road racing. It oversees eight disciplines: road, track, mountain bike, BMX racing, BMX freestyle, cyclo-cross, trials, and indoor cycling, as well as para-cycling.

World championships

The UCI awards the distinctive rainbow jersey to the winner of each world championship event; champions wear the jersey in competition for the following year. Its flagship events include the UCI Road World Championships, Track World Championships, and Mountain Bike World Championships, and since 2023 a combined multi-discipline "mega" World Championships held every four years, first staged in Glasgow.

History and controversies

For much of the 20th century the UCI shared authority with separate bodies for professionals and amateurs before reunifying in the 1990s. The organization has faced persistent criticism over its handling of doping, particularly during the era of Lance Armstrong; reforms followed the Cycling Independent Reform Commission report of 2015. David Lappartient has served as UCI president since 2017. The UCI also maintains the UCI World Cycling Centre, a training facility in Aigle that supports riders from developing nations.
