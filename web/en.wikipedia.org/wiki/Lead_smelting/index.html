Lead smelting

Lead smelting is the industrial process by which metallic lead is extracted from its ores, principally galena (lead sulfide). Lead has been smelted since antiquity — it is one of the first metals worked by humans — and the modern industry produces both primary lead from ore and secondary lead recycled from scrap, especially used lead-acid batteries.

Process

Traditional primary smelting involves roasting the sulfide ore to convert it to lead oxide (sintering), followed by reduction with coke in a blast furnace to produce lead bullion, which is then refined to remove impurities such as silver, copper, and antimony. Modern direct-smelting technologies reduce emissions and combine roasting and reduction in a single vessel.

Environmental and health issues

Lead smelting is a significant source of toxic pollution. Lead dust and fumes can contaminate soil and water around smelters, and occupational and community exposure causes serious health effects, making smelter sites among the most heavily regulated and remediated in the metals industry.
