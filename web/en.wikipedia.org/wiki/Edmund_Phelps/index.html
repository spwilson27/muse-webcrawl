Edmund Phelps

Edmund Strother Phelps (born July 26, 1933) is an American economist and the recipient of the 2006 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences. He is the McVickar Professor Emeritus of Political Economy at Columbia University and the founder of the Center on Capitalism and Society.

Work

Phelps is best known for introducing expectations into macroeconomics. In the late 1960s he developed, independently of Milton Friedman, the expectations-augmented Phillips curve, implying that there is no permanent trade-off between inflation and unemployment and that unemployment tends toward a "natural rate". He also formulated the "golden rule" of capital accumulation, identifying the savings rate that maximizes consumption per head in steady state. His later work, including the book Mass Flourishing (2013), emphasizes innovation, dynamism and the cultural values that support grassroots creativity in modern economies.

Life

Born in Evanston, Illinois, Phelps studied at Amherst College and earned his PhD from Yale University in 1959. He taught at Yale and the University of Pennsylvania before joining Columbia in 1971.
