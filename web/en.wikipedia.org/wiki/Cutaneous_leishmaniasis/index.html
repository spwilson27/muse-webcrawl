Cutaneous leishmaniasis

Cutaneous leishmaniasis is the most common form of leishmaniasis, a parasitic disease caused by protozoa of the genus Leishmania and transmitted by the bite of infected female phlebotomine sandflies. It causes skin sores and ulcers on exposed parts of the body.

Signs and symptoms

The disease usually begins as small papules at the bite site that enlarge into nodules and may ulcerate, often healing slowly with scarring. Old World forms are caused mainly by L. major and L. tropica; New World forms by species such as L. braziliensis and L. mexicana.

Treatment and distribution

Many lesions heal spontaneously, but treatment with pentavalent antimonials, miltefosine, or local therapies may be used depending on species and risk of mucosal involvement. The disease is endemic in parts of the Middle East, North Africa, Central Asia, and Latin America, affecting hundreds of thousands of people annually.
