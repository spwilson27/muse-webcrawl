Cholula

Cholula (Spanish: [tʃoˈlula]; Nahuatl: Cholōllān) is a city and district located in the metropolitan area of Puebla, Mexico. Cholula is best known for its Great Pyramid, the largest pyramid by volume known to exist in the world today, with a church, Iglesia de Nuestra Señora de los Remedios, built on top of it. The city is divided into two municipalities, San Pedro Cholula and San Andrés Cholula, which together with the city of Puebla form the Puebla-Tlaxcala metropolitan area.

History

Cholula is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the Americas, settled by the pre-classic period. In the pre-Hispanic era it was a major religious and commercial centre, sacred to the god Quetzalcoatl, and one of the largest cities of Mesoamerica. In 1519, during the Spanish conquest, Hernán Cortés's forces, with their Tlaxcalan allies, carried out the Cholula massacre, killing thousands of inhabitants after suspecting a plot against them.

The Great Pyramid

The Great Pyramid of Cholula, dedicated to Quetzalcoatl, was built in stages over more than a thousand years, beginning around the 3rd century BCE. It measures about 450 metres on each side and 66 metres high, making it the largest pyramid and the largest monument ever constructed anywhere in the world by volume. Today it resembles a natural hill, and an extensive network of tunnels, about 8 km in total, has been excavated through it by archaeologists.

Modern city

Cholula is home to the Universidad de las Américas Puebla, one of Mexico's leading universities. The city is noted for its many churches—local legend speaks of 365, one for each day of the year—and for its lively student population and nightlife. It has been designated a Pueblo Mágico by the Mexican government.
