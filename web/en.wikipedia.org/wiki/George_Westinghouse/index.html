George Westinghouse

George Westinghouse Jr. (October 6, 1846 – March 12, 1914) was an American entrepreneur and engineer.

Born: Central Bridge, New York
Died: New York City
Known for: Railway air brake; AC power systems; Westinghouse Electric

George Westinghouse was an American inventor and industrialist who founded the Westinghouse Air Brake Company and the Westinghouse Electric Company. His promotion of alternating current (AC) electrical distribution — against Thomas Edison's direct current — decided the "war of the currents" in favour of AC.

Air brake

Westinghouse grew up around his father's machine shop in Schenectady, New York, and served in the Union Army and Navy during the Civil War. Witnessing a train wreck caused by slow hand-braking, he patented the railway air brake in 1869, using compressed air to apply brakes simultaneously on all cars; the automatic version, which applied brakes if the train parted, made braking fail-safe and transformed rail safety worldwide. He followed it with improved railway signalling and switching systems.

Electric power

In the 1880s Westinghouse moved into electricity, first natural-gas distribution (he drilled a gas well in his own Pittsburgh backyard) and then AC power. He bought the patents of Gaulard and Gibbs, hired William Stanley and later Nikola Tesla, whose polyphase AC motor patents he acquired in 1888. Westinghouse lit the World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago in 1893 and built the first great hydroelectric plant at Niagara Falls (1895), transmitting power to Buffalo — triumphs that made AC the standard for power distribution.

Later life

Westinghouse built a vast industrial empire in Pittsburgh's East Pittsburgh works, pioneering (for his era) relatively decent working conditions, including the Saturday half-holiday. He lost control of his companies in the Panic of 1907 but remained an honoured figure; he also developed the steam turbine under licence from Parsons. He died in New York City in 1914, holding some 360 patents.
