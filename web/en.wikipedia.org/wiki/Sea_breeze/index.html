Sea breeze

A sea breeze (or onshore breeze) is a wind that blows from a large body of water toward land during the day. It develops because land heats faster than water under the sun: warm air rises over the land, lowering surface pressure, and cooler marine air flows inland to replace it, often with a visible sea-breeze front that can trigger cumulus clouds and thunderstorms.

Characteristics

Sea breezes typically begin late in the morning, peak in mid-afternoon and die away in the evening, reaching speeds of 10–20 knots and penetrating from a few kilometres to over 100 km inland in the tropics. They moderate coastal temperatures substantially — Florida, the Persian Gulf and northern Australia experience strong, climatically important sea breezes.

Land breeze

At night the process reverses: the land cools faster than the sea, and a weaker offshore land breeze blows from land to water. Together the two form a daily local circulation exploited since antiquity by coastal sailors and today relevant to aviation, air-pollution dispersion and wind-energy forecasting.
