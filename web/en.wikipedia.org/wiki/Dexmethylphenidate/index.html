Dexmethylphenidate

Dexmethylphenidate, sold under the brand name Focalin among others, is a central nervous system stimulant used to treat attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD). It is the pharmacologically more active d-threo enantiomer of methylphenidate (Ritalin), and acts as a norepinephrine–dopamine reuptake inhibitor.

Medical use

Dexmethylphenidate is approved for ADHD in children aged six and older, adolescents and adults. It is available as immediate-release tablets and as an extended-release capsule (Focalin XR) using a bimodal bead system. Serdexmethylphenidate, a prodrug, is combined with dexmethylphenidate in the product Azstarys. Because it contains only the active enantiomer, doses are roughly half those of racemic methylphenidate.

Pharmacology

The drug blocks the dopamine and norepinephrine transporters, increasing extracellular levels of these neurotransmitters in the prefrontal cortex and striatum, improving attention and impulse control. It is metabolised mainly by carboxylesterase 1A1 to inactive ritalinic acid derivatives.

Side effects

Common adverse effects include decreased appetite, insomnia, headache, abdominal pain, dry mouth and irritability. Like other stimulants it carries warnings about cardiovascular events, growth suppression in children, and abuse potential, and is classified as a Schedule II controlled substance in the United States.
