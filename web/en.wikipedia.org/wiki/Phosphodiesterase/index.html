Phosphodiesterase

Phosphodiesterase
EC number3.1.4
SubstratesCyclic nucleotides (cAMP, cGMP)
Products5′-AMP, 5′-GMP

A phosphodiesterase (PDE) is an enzyme that breaks a phosphodiester bond. Usually, phosphodiesterase refers to cyclic nucleotide phosphodiesterases, which have great clinical significance and are described below. These enzymes hydrolyze the second messengers cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) and cyclic guanosine monophosphate (cGMP) to their inactive 5′-monophosphate forms (5′-AMP and 5′-GMP), thereby terminating the signalling cascades those molecules mediate.

Phosphodiesterases are classified into 11 families (PDE1–PDE11) in mammals, encoded by 21 genes, with multiple splice variants producing over 100 distinct protein isoforms. The families differ in their substrate specificity (some hydrolyze cAMP only, some cGMP only, and some both), their regulatory properties, and their tissue distribution.

Classification

The PDE superfamily includes PDE1 (calcium/calmodulin-stimulated), PDE2 (cGMP-stimulated), PDE3 (cGMP-inhibited), PDE4 (cAMP-specific, the largest family), PDE5 (cGMP-specific, of the corpus cavernosum and vasculature), PDE6 (the photoreceptor enzyme of rod and cone cells), PDE7 and PDE8 (cAMP-specific), PDE9 (cGMP-specific, high affinity), and PDE10 and PDE11 (dual-substrate enzymes).

Clinical significance

PDE inhibitors are important drugs. PDE5 inhibitors such as sildenafil (Viagra), tadalafil, and vardenafil are used to treat erectile dysfunction and pulmonary arterial hypertension, by preventing the degradation of cGMP in vascular smooth muscle. PDE4 inhibitors such as roflumilast are used in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and apremilast in psoriasis and psoriatic arthritis. The PDE3 inhibitor cilostazol is used in intermittent claudication, while nonselective inhibitors include caffeine and theophylline, which also act as adenosine receptor antagonists.

Other phosphodiesterases

The name also covers other enzymes that cleave phosphodiester bonds, including phospholipase C and D (technically phosphodiesterases), DNases and RNases that degrade nucleic acids, and the bacterial enzyme cyclic-di-GMP phosphodiesterases involved in biofilm regulation.
