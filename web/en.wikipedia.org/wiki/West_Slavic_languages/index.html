West Slavic languages

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Geographic distributionCentral Europe: Poland, Czechia, Slovakia, Lusatia
Linguistic classificationIndo-European > Balto-Slavic > Slavic
Major languagesPolish, Czech, Slovak, Kashubian, Sorbian

The West Slavic languages are a subgroup of the Slavic languages spoken in Central Europe. They are traditionally divided into three groups: the Lechitic group (Polish, Kashubian and extinct languages such as Polabian), the Czech–Slovak group (Czech and Slovak), and the Sorbian group (Upper and Lower Sorbian), spoken in Lusatia in eastern Germany.

History

The West Slavs settled in Central Europe in the early medieval period following the Slavic migrations. The separation of West Slavic from Common Slavic is usually dated to the 9th or 10th centuries, as the region fell under the influence of the Roman Catholic Church and the Latin alphabet, in contrast to the Orthodox and Cyrillic traditions of the East and South Slavs.

Polish, the most widely spoken West Slavic language with about 40 million native speakers, became the language of the Kingdom of Poland; Czech developed as the language of Bohemia, with the Czech literary tradition associated with the medieval reformer Jan Hus; Slovak diverged from Czech over the centuries and became the state language of Slovakia after the dissolution of Czechoslovakia in 1993.
