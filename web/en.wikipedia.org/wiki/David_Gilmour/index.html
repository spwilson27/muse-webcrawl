David Jon Gilmour (born 6 March 1946) is an English musician, singer and songwriter who achieved international fame as the guitarist, co-lead vocalist and later leader of the rock band Pink Floyd. He joined the band in 1967, shortly before the departure of founding member Syd Barrett, and became its driving force after Roger Waters left in 1985.

Career

With Pink Floyd, Gilmour played on landmark albums including The Dark Side of the Moon (1973), Wish You Were Here (1975), Animals (1977) and The Wall (1979). His expressive, melodic guitar playing — especially his solos on tracks such as "Comfortably Numb" and "Time" — is widely regarded as among the finest in rock music.

Solo work

Gilmour has released solo albums including David Gilmour (1978), About Face (1984), On an Island (2006), Rattle That Lock (2015) and Luck and Strange (2024). He has also produced and played for other artists, and is noted for his charity work and for selling his famed guitar collection at auction in 2019 for charity, raising over $21 million.

Style and equipment

Gilmour is known for his lyrical phrasing, string bending and sustain, strongly associated with his black Fender Stratocaster ("The Black Strat"). Rolling Stone ranked him among the greatest guitarists of all time. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as a member of Pink Floyd in 1996.
