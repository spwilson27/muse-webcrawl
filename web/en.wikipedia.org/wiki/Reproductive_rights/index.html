Reproductive rights

Reproductive rights are legal rights and freedoms relating to reproduction and reproductive health that vary among countries. They rest on the recognition of the right of couples and individuals to decide freely and responsibly the number, spacing and timing of their children, to have the information and means to do so, and to attain the highest standard of sexual and reproductive health.

Issues

Issues commonly grouped under reproductive rights include access to contraception, sex education, maternal health care, and sterilisation free of coercion; the legality of abortion; and freedom from practices such as female genital mutilation and forced pregnancy. The World Health Organization and United Nations agencies frame reproductive health as a component of the broader right to health.

International law

The 1994 International Conference on Population and Development in Cairo and the 1995 Beijing World Conference on Women affirmed reproductive rights as human rights. The Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Against Women (CEDAW) obliges states to ensure equal access to family-planning information and services. Laws and policies on these questions remain highly contested and differ widely between jurisdictions.
