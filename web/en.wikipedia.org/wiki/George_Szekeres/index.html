George Szekeres AM FAA (29 May 1911 – 28 August 2005) was a Hungarian–Australian mathematician. He is remembered for the Erdős–Szekeres theorem in combinatorial geometry, for naming (with Paul Erdős) the "happy ending problem" — so called because it led to his marriage to Esther Klein — and for contributions ranging from the Szekeres snark in graph theory to the Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates of general relativity.

Life

Szekeres was born in Budapest and trained first as a chemical engineer, working in a leather factory, before mathematics claimed him. In Budapest he belonged to the legendary circle of young Jewish mathematicians around Paul Erdős, which included Esther Klein and Pál Turán. Fleeing Nazism, he and Klein — by then his wife — spent the war years in Shanghai as refugees, where their son Peter was born, before emigrating to Australia in 1948. He taught at the University of Adelaide, then in 1964 became the foundation professor of pure mathematics at the University of New South Wales in Sydney, where he built a distinguished school and founded the journal and competition culture of Australian mathematics, including the magazine Parabola.

Work

  • Erdős–Szekeres theorem (1935): any sequence of (r−1)(s−1)+1 distinct real numbers contains a monotone increasing subsequence of length r or a monotone decreasing subsequence of length s — a classic application of the pigeonhole principle and a seed of Ramsey theory.
  • Happy ending problem: Erdős and Szekeres proved that any set of sufficiently many points in general position in the plane contains a large subset forming a convex polygon; the exact bound remains an open conjecture.
  • Szekeres snark (1973): a 50-vertex snark, for decades among the smallest known non-trivial snarks.
  • Kruskal–Szekeres coordinates: independently of Martin Kruskal, Szekeres found coordinates that remove the coordinate singularity of the Schwarzschild solution at the event horizon.

Later life and honours

Szekeres was elected a Fellow of the Australian Academy of Science and appointed a Member of the Order of Australia in 2002. He and Esther died in Adelaide on the same day, 28 August 2005, within an hour of each other, after 68 years of marriage — a real-life ending to the problem that gave them the "happy ending" name.
