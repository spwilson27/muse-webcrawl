Maasai language

Maasai (ɔl Maa) is an Eastern Nilotic language spoken by the Maasai people of southern Kenya and northern Tanzania. It belongs to the Maa group of languages, closely related to Samburu and Camus, and has roughly 1.5 million speakers. Maasai is a tonal language with vowel harmony based on the feature advanced tongue root, and it distinguishes nine vowel qualities.

Features

Maasai word order is typically verb–subject–object. The language has a rich system of nominal case marking (nominative and accusative distinguished largely by tone), two grammatical genders (masculine and feminine), and singular/plural marking through a variety of suffixes. Its verb morphology encodes direction and applicative meanings through derivational extensions.

Status

Although Maasai remains vigorously spoken in rural communities, it faces pressure from Swahili and English, the languages of schooling and administration in Kenya and Tanzania. It is written in the Latin alphabet, and there are Bible translations, dictionaries, and radio broadcasts in the language.
