Lotteries in the United States are run by 48 jurisdictions: 45 states plus the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico and the United States Virgin Islands. There is no national lottery in the United States, although two multi-state games, Powerball and Mega Millions, are offered in nearly all jurisdictions that operate lotteries. Proceeds from state lotteries are typically earmarked for public purposes, most commonly education, and state lotteries collectively transfer tens of billions of dollars annually to those causes.

History

Lotteries were common in colonial America, financing roads, bridges, churches and colleges including Harvard and Yale. The Continental Congress authorised a lottery in 1776 to fund the Revolutionary War effort. Scandals and corruption — culminating in the Louisiana Lottery affair — led to a nationwide prohibition in the nineteenth century; by 1895 federal law effectively shut down all U.S. lotteries.

The modern era began in 1964, when New Hampshire introduced a sweepstakes tied to horse racing. New York followed in 1967 and New Jersey in 1970; the New Jersey lottery's success triggered rapid adoption elsewhere during the 1970s and 1980s. Multi-state associations formed in the late 1980s, enabling the huge pooled jackpots that now dominate the market. In January 2016 a Powerball jackpot of approximately $1.586 billion — then the largest lottery prize in world history — was split among three tickets.

Games and revenue allocation

State lotteries sell draw games (numbers games such as Pick 3 and Pick 4, and in-state jackpot games), instant scratch-off tickets, keno and, increasingly, online subscriptions. Typically only about 50–70% of sales are returned as prizes; the remainder funds the designated public programmes, retailer commissions and administration.Critics argue that lotteries function as a regressive tax and that earmarked funds often substitute for, rather than supplement, general education appropriations; supporters point to the voluntary nature of play and the popularity of the games.

States without lotteries

As of the mid-2020s, Alabama, Alaska, Hawaii, Nevada and Utah have no state lottery, owing variously to constitutional bans, religious opposition, or — in Nevada's case — the influence of the casino industry.
