The Newlyn School was an art colony of artists based in the fishing village of Newlyn in Cornwall, England, from the 1880s into the early twentieth century. Attracted by cheap living, reliable models among the fishing community, and the clear Cornish light, the painters worked outdoors (en plein air) in a naturalistic style influenced by the French Barbizon school and the Hague School.

Artists

Walter Langley is generally credited as the first to settle, in 1882; Stanhope Forbes arrived in 1884 and became the colony's leader, later founding the Forbes School of Painting with his wife Elizabeth. Frank Bramley's A Hopeless Dawn (1888) was bought for the nation, a landmark success for the school.

Legacy

The Newlyn artists helped shift British painting toward social realism and observed rural life. Their work is well represented at Penlee House Gallery in Penzance.
