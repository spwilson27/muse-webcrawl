Bony fish

Scientific classification
Domain: Eukaryota
Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Chordata
Class: Osteichthyes

Bony fishes, formally the class Osteichthyes, are a diverse superclass of vertebrates that have skeletons primarily composed of bone tissue. They can be contrasted with the Chondrichthyes (cartilaginous fishes), which have skeletons primarily composed of cartilage. The vast majority of extant fish are members of Osteichthyes, which is an extremely diverse and abundant group consisting of 45 orders, over 435 families and 28,000 described species. It is the largest class of vertebrates in existence today.

Bony fish are divided into two subclasses: the ray-finned fishes (Actinopterygii), which include nearly all living species, and the lobe-finned fishes (Sarcopterygii), which include the coelacanths and lungfishes as well as the ancestors of tetrapods.

Characteristics

Bony fishes are characterized by a bony endoskeleton, a swim bladder (in most species), and, in the ray-finned fishes, fins supported by thin bony rays called lepidotrichia. Most species breathe through gills covered by a bony operculum. Many are ectothermic, though some, such as tunas and opah, have evolved forms of endothermy.
