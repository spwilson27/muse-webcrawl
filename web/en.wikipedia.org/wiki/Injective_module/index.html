Injective module

In mathematics, especially in homological algebra, an injective module is a module that is, roughly speaking, characterised by the extendability of homomorphisms into it. A module Q over a ring R is injective if, for every injective homomorphism f : A → B of R-modules and every homomorphism g : A → Q, there exists a homomorphism h : B → Q extending g — that is, with h ∘ f = g.

Properties and examples

By Baer's criterion, it suffices to verify the extension property for inclusions of ideals of R into R itself. Over the ring of integers, the injective modules are exactly the divisible abelian groups, such as the rationals and the Prüfer groups. Every module can be embedded in an injective module, and every module has an injective resolution — a fact that makes injective modules fundamental to the construction of right derived functors such as Ext. A ring is called Noetherian if and only if direct sums of injective modules are injective; a semisimple ring is one over which every module is injective. Injective modules are the dual notion to projective modules, in which homomorphisms out of the module lift instead of extend.

Generalisation

The concept extends to injective objects in general categories: an object I is injective with respect to a class of morphisms if maps into I extend along those morphisms. Injective cogenerators and injective envelopes are related constructions used throughout module theory and representation theory.
