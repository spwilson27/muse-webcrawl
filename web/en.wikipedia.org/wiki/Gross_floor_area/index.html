Gross floor area

Gross floor area (GFA), also called gross internal area in some jurisdictions, is a measure of the total floor area inside a building's envelope. Depending on the applicable standard, it is typically calculated as the sum of the areas of all floors, measured to the external face of the external walls (or the centreline of party walls), and may include or exclude spaces such as basements, parking areas, plant rooms, and voids, according to local definitions.

Definitions and standards

There is no single worldwide definition. The International Property Measurement Standards (IPMS) and national standards such as those of the Royal Institution of Chartered Surveyors (RICS) in the United Kingdom provide rules for consistent measurement. Planning systems commonly define GFA for their own purposes: notably, Hong Kong and Singapore use detailed statutory definitions because plot ratio (floor area ratio) controls development density, while in the United States definitions vary by municipality.

Uses

Gross floor area is used in zoning and planning to compute floor area ratio, in real estate valuation and leasing (cost per square metre of GFA), in construction cost planning, and in building regulations and energy performance calculations. It contrasts with net floor area or usable floor area, which exclude circulation, structure, and service spaces and thus measure the area actually available to occupants.
