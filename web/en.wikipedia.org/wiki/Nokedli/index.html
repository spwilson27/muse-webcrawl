Nokedli

Nokedli (Hungarian: nokedli, also called galuska) are small Hungarian egg noodle dumplings, closely related to German Spätzle and Slovak halušky. They are a staple side dish in Hungarian cuisine, most famously served with stews such as chicken paprikash (paprikás csirke) and goulash (pörkölt).

Preparation

The dough is a simple, soft batter of flour, eggs, salt and water or milk, mixed only briefly so it stays tender. It is formed by pressing the dough through a large-holed grater or dumpling maker (nokedliszaggató) directly into boiling salted water, or in the traditional method by cutting small pieces from a wet board with a knife or spoon. The dumplings are ready when they float to the surface, after which they are drained and often tossed in butter or drippings from the stew.

Variations

Galuska is the traditional accompaniment to paprikash dishes, the dumplings absorbing the sour-cream paprika sauce. A richer variant is made with curd cheese (túrós galuska relatives appear in dishes like sztrapacska, made with potato dough and sheep's cheese). The same family of Central European dumplings includes Austrian Nockerl — the sweet Salzburger Nockerl being a distant baked relative.
