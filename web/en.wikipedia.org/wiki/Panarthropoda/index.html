Panarthropoda is a proposed animal clade within the Ecdysozoa uniting the arthropods (insects, crustaceans, chelicerates, and myriapods) with the two "legged worm" phyla: the velvet worms (Onychophora) and the water bears (Tardigrada). The grouping is supported by shared features including a segmented body plan, paired segmental appendages, a ventral nerve cord, and a chitinous cuticle that is periodically moulted.

Characteristics

Members possess lobopodous or jointed limbs with claws, an open circulatory system with a dorsal heart, and a terminal mouth. The fossil record of the group reaches back to Cambrian lobopodians such as Aysheaia and Hallucigenia, which combine worm-like bodies with stubby clawed legs and illuminate the origin of the arthropod body plan.

Phylogeny

Molecular phylogenetics generally confirms Panarthropoda, though the internal relationships remain debated: most analyses place Tardigrada as sister to a clade of Arthropoda + Onychophora (a grouping sometimes called Tactopoda for arthropods plus tardigrades, with onychophorans as the earliest branch, in alternative schemes). Panarthropoda together with the cycloneuralians (nematodes, priapulids and allies) makes up Ecdysozoa, the moulting animals that dominate modern terrestrial and marine fauna.
