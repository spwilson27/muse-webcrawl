Adar (Hebrew: אֲדָר‎) is the sixth month of the civil year and the twelfth month of the religious year in the Hebrew calendar. It falls in late winter, roughly corresponding to February–March in the Gregorian calendar, and normally has 29 days.

[Image: Purim celebration in Adar]

Leap years

Because the Hebrew calendar is lunisolar, an extra month is intercalated seven times in a 19-year cycle. In such leap years, an additional 30-day month, Adar I, is inserted before the regular Adar, which is renamed Adar II (or Adar Bet). Festivals that fall in Adar — above all Purim — are celebrated in Adar II, while some observances, such as certain yahrzeits, may be marked in Adar I by some traditions.

Holidays and observances

The festival of Purim is celebrated on the 14th of Adar (15th in walled cities such as Jerusalem, as Shushan Purim), commemorating the deliverance of the Jews in the Book of Esther. The 7th of Adar is traditionally held to be the birth and death date of Moses, and the Fast of Esther is observed on the 13th of Adar. A traditional saying holds, "When Adar enters, joy increases" (Mishnah Taanit).
