The Gulf Country is the region of woodland and savanna grassland surrounding the Gulf of Carpentaria in north-western Queensland and eastern Northern Territory, on the northern coast of Australia. The region, also called the Gulf Savannah, covers roughly 186,000 square kilometres of flat plains drained by numerous rivers that flow into the gulf, including the Leichhardt, Nicholson, Gregory, and Flinders rivers.

Geography and climate

The Gulf Country is a low-lying plain, much of it less than 50 metres above sea level. The climate is tropical savanna, with a distinct wet season from December to March, when monsoonal rains flood much of the plain, and a long dry season for the rest of the year. The coastline features extensive tidal flats and some of the largest salt pans in Australia.

History and economy

The region is the traditional home of many Aboriginal peoples, including the Ganggalida, Garawa, Waanyi, and Yanyuwa peoples. European exploration began with Dutch navigators in the 17th century and continued with Matthew Flinders in 1802. The town of Burketown, founded in 1865, became the main port. Today the economy is dominated by cattle grazing on large pastoral stations, mining (notably zinc and lead at Mount Isa and the Century Mine), commercial fishing (especially barramundi and prawns), and tourism.

The Burke and Wills expedition passed through the region in 1861, reaching the Gulf near present-day Normanton. The town of Karumba is the centre of the Gulf's prawning industry.
