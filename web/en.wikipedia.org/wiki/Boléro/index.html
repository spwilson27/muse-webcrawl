Boléro

Boléro is a one-movement orchestral piece by the French composer Maurice Ravel (1875–1937), originally composed as a ballet commissioned by the Russian dancer Ida Rubinstein. Premiered at the Paris Opéra on 22 November 1928, it is Ravel's most famous musical composition.

The work is built over an unchanging ostinato rhythm played by the snare drum for its entire duration, in 3/4 time, while two contrasting melodies are repeated in alternation by different solo instruments and instrumental groups. The piece unfolds as a single enormous crescendo lasting roughly fifteen minutes, ending with a brief modulation and a crashing conclusion.

Composition and premiere

Ravel intended the work as an experiment in orchestral colour: the harmony and rhythm remain essentially static while the orchestration alone sustains interest. The conductor Arturo Toscanini led a celebrated performance in 1930, and the work quickly entered the standard repertory. Ravel himself remarked, with characteristic dryness, that Boléro was his one masterpiece — but that it contained "no music".

Legacy

Boléro has been used in many films and popular contexts, most famously in the 1979 film 10, and was skated to by Torvill and Dean in their gold-medal ice dance at the 1984 Winter Olympics. Because Ravel died in 1937, the piece entered the public domain in most countries in the 2000s.
