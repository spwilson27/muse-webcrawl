Catch points

Catch points and trap points are railway safety devices: short, spring-loaded or controlled sets of points installed on a running line to derail or divert unauthorised or runaway vehicles before they can foul a main line or come into conflict with other traffic.

Function

Catch points are placed where a siding, loop, or goods line joins a running line, or at the exit of a refuge, so that a vehicle moving without authority is derailed at low speed in a safe place rather than entering the main line. Trap points perform the same role at the exits of sidings and yards, typically diverting a runaway into a short headshunt or derailing it. On steep gradients, catch points were historically installed on running lines themselves to stop vehicles that had broken away from a descending train, and were normally held open against the direction of travel except when a train was passing.

Related devices

A derail (or derailer) is a simpler device that lifts a wheel flange over the rail. The use of catch points, together with interlocking and track circuits, was a major factor in preventing collisions caused by runaway wagons in the era of loose-coupled freight trains.
