Broadcom Inc. is an American multinational company that designs, develops and supplies semiconductor and infrastructure software products. Its chips are used in networking equipment, broadband access, set-top boxes, servers and smartphones, while its software division sells mainframe, cybersecurity and virtualisation products. The present company is the product of the 2016 acquisition of the original Broadcom Corporation by Avago Technologies, which adopted the Broadcom name, followed by a series of large software acquisitions.

History

Broadcom Corporation was founded in 1991 by Henry Samueli, a UCLA engineering professor, and his doctoral student Henry Nicholas. Based in Irvine, California, it grew with the market for broadband communications chips — cable modems, Ethernet, Wi-Fi and Bluetooth — going public in 1998 during the dot-com boom and acquiring dozens of smaller firms. Avago Technologies, itself the former semiconductor division of Hewlett-Packard (via Agilent), acquired Broadcom in 2016 for approximately $37 billion, then one of the largest technology deals ever, and took the Broadcom name and ticker. The combined company is led by chief executive Hock Tan, known for disciplined, acquisition-led growth.

Blocked Qualcomm bid and software pivot

In 2017–18 Broadcom pursued a hostile takeover of Qualcomm that, at a proposed value above $100 billion, would have been the largest technology acquisition in history; the bid was blocked in March 2018 by an order of President Donald Trump, following a CFIUS review citing national security concerns, before Qualcomm's shareholders could vote. Broadcom then redomiciled from Singapore to the United States and pivoted to infrastructure software, acquiring CA Technologies in 2018 for about $18.9 billion and Symantec's enterprise security business in 2019 for about $10.7 billion. In November 2023 it completed its acquisition of the cloud-computing company VMware for about $61 billion (about $69 billion including assumed debt), one of the largest technology deals ever closed; subsequent changes to VMware's licensing and partner programmes drew regulatory scrutiny and criticism from customers.

Business

Broadcom's semiconductor solutions segment (roughly three-quarters of revenue) supplies Ethernet switching and routing silicon for data centres — including the Tomahawk and Jericho families — custom AI accelerators designed with hyperscale customers, broadband and wireless chips, and storage controllers. The infrastructure software segment operates the former CA mainframe and enterprise software lines, Symantec security products and VMware's virtualisation platform. Since the mid-2020s Broadcom has been among the world's most valuable technology companies, its market capitalisation passing $1 trillion in December 2024 on demand for AI networking and custom accelerator silicon.
