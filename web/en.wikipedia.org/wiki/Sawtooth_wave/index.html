Sawtooth wave

A bandlimited sawtooth wave pictured in the time domain (top) and frequency domain (bottom)

The sawtooth wave (or saw wave) is a kind of non-sinusoidal waveform. It is so named based on its resemblance to the teeth of a plain-toothed saw with a zero rake angle. A single sawtooth, or an intermittently triggered sawtooth, is known as a ramp waveform.

The convention is that a sawtooth wave ramps upward and then sharply drops. In a reverse (or inverse) sawtooth wave, the wave ramps downward and then sharply rises. It can also be considered the extreme case of an asymmetric triangle wave or the periodic form of a linear chirp.

Definition

A piecewise linear function based on the floor function of time t is an example of a sawtooth wave with period 1:

s(t) = t − floor(t)

A more general form, in the range −1 to 1 and with period p, is:

s(t) = 2 ( t/p − floor( 1/2 + t/p ) )

This sawtooth function has the same phase as the sine function. While a square wave is constructed from only odd harmonics, a sawtooth wave's sound is harsh and clear and its spectrum contains both even and odd harmonics of the fundamental frequency.

Fourier series

The sawtooth wave can be constructed using Fourier series as the sum of harmonically related sine waves with amplitudes decreasing in inverse proportion to the harmonic number:

s(t) = (2A/π) Σk=1..∞ (−1)k+1 sin(2πkft) / k

Because it contains all the integer harmonics, the sawtooth is one of the richest basic waveforms and is widely used as the starting point of subtractive synthesis.

Applications

  • Music synthesis — the sawtooth is a staple oscillator waveform in analogue and virtual-analogue synthesizers, resembling the sound of bowed strings and brass when filtered.
  • Timebase generators — sawtooth waveforms sweep the electron beam horizontally in cathode-ray tube displays and form the basis of early television raster scanning.
  • Modulation — sawtooth waves are used in phase modulation and in some forms of pulse-width modulation for motor control and power conversion.
  • Testing — the linear ramp is used to characterise the linearity of amplifiers and data converters.
