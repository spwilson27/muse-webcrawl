Chipping Campden

Chipping Campden is a market town and civil parish in the Cotswold district of Gloucestershire, England. Renowned for its honey-coloured limestone High Street, it was an important wool-trading centre in the Middle Ages. The name "Chipping" derives from Old English cēping, meaning a market or market-place.

History

The town grew rich on the wool trade; its Market Hall was built in 1627 by Sir Baptist Hicks, whose (now ruined) Campden House stood nearby. St James' Church, one of the great Cotswold "wool churches", contains notable monuments to the Hicks family. In 1612 Robert Dover founded the Cotswold Olimpick Games on Dover's Hill above the town, a rural sporting festival still revived today.

In 1902 C. R. Ashbee moved his Guild of Handicraft to Chipping Campden, making the town a centre of the Arts and Crafts movement; silversmithing and craft workshops continue there today. The northern end of the Cotswold Way long-distance footpath is in the town (its other end being Bath).
