Len "Boogsie" Sharpe (born 1953) is a Trinidadian composer, arranger, and musician widely regarded as one of the most accomplished figures in steelpan music. He is the long-time arranger for the Phase II Pan Groove steel orchestra, based in Woodbrook, Port of Spain, and has won the Panorama competition—the premier steelband contest of the Trinidad and Tobago Carnival—more times than any other arranger.

Early life

Sharpe was born and raised in Woodbrook, a district of Port of Spain long associated with steelband culture. He took up the pan as a child and earned the nickname "Boogsie" from his flamboyant playing style. Largely self-taught in music, Sharpe developed an intuitive ear for harmony and arrangement that allowed him to work with ensembles of more than a hundred players despite having no formal training in notation.

Career

Sharpe rose to prominence arranging for the Starlift Steel Orchestra before co-founding Phase II Pan Groove in the early 1970s. With Phase II he pioneered a more harmonically adventurous style, emphasizing original compositions rather than arrangements of calypso hits. His Panorama-winning compositions include "This Feeling Nice" (1987), "Woman Is Boss" (1988), and multiple later victories, among them a string of wins in the 2000s that cemented his reputation.

Sharpe's style is noted for dense chromatic harmony, intricate counter-melodies, and a jazz-influenced approach that sometimes divided traditionalists but broadened the expressive range of the steelband. He has also mentored generations of younger arrangers.

Recognition

Sharpe has received Trinidad and Tobago's national honours, including the Hummingbird Medal, and in 2009 he was awarded an honorary doctorate by the University of the West Indies in recognition of his contribution to Caribbean music. He is frequently described as the most successful Panorama arranger in history.
