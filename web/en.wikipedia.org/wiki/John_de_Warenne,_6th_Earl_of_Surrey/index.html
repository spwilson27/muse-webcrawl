John de Warenne, 6th Earl of Surrey (1231 – 27 September 1304) was a prominent English nobleman and military commander during the reigns of Henry III and Edward I, and a leading figure in the wars in Wales and Scotland.

Early life

Warenne succeeded his father William de Warenne as Earl of Surrey while still a minor and was raised at court. In 1247 he married Alice de Lusignan, a half-sister of Henry III, tying the Warenne family closely to the king's Lusignan favourites — an unpopular match among the English baronage.

Barons' Wars and Edward I's wars

During the Second Barons' War Warenne at first sided with the reformers but changed allegiance to the king, fought at the Battle of Lewes (1264), where he fled abroad after the royalist defeat, and returned to support the royal cause at Evesham. Under Edward I he became one of the king's most trusted commanders: he campaigned in the conquest of Wales in 1282–83, receiving lands and building castles at Holt.

Scotland

Edward appointed Warenne guardian of Scotland after the Battle of Dunbar (1296), which he won decisively. In 1297, however, his army was routed by William Wallace and Andrew Moray at the Battle of Stirling Bridge, where Warenne's subordinate Hugh de Cressingham was killed; Warenne escaped to Berwick. He returned to win the Battle of Falkirk in 1298 as one of Edward's senior commanders. He is also remembered for the (possibly apocryphal) Quo warranto anecdote: asked by what warrant he held his lands, Warenne allegedly drew his rusty sword and declared his ancestors had won them by it.
