Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne

The Union Internationale de Pentathlon Moderne (UIPM) is the international governing body for the sport of modern pentathlon. Founded in 1948 in Sandhurst, England, and headquartered in Monte Carlo, Monaco, the UIPM oversees the sport's rules, world championships, World Cup circuit, and its place in the Olympic Games, where modern pentathlon has featured continuously since 1912.

History and role

Modern pentathlon was devised by Pierre de Coubertin to simulate the skills of a cavalry soldier: fencing, swimming, equestrian show jumping, and a combined running and shooting event (the laser run). Before 1948 the sport was administered directly by the International Olympic Committee. The UIPM later absorbed biathlon for a period (as UIPMB) before biathlon gained its own federation. The organisation sanctions World Championships annually and promotes development formats such as tetrathlon for youth athletes.

Recent developments

Following controversy over the equestrian discipline at the Tokyo 2020 Olympics, the UIPM voted to replace riding with obstacle course racing after the Paris 2024 Games, a change ratified by the IOC for the Los Angeles 2028 programme. The decision prompted the formation of a rival body by some national federations, while the UIPM retained IOC recognition.
