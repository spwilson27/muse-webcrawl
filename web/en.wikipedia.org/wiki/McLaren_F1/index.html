The McLaren F1 is a sports car designed and manufactured by McLaren Cars, a subsidiary of the McLaren Technology Group. Designed by Gordon Murray with exterior styling by Peter Stevens, it features a naturally aspirated BMW V12 engine, a central driving position, and a carbon-fibre monocoque. On 31 March 1998, the F1 set the record for the world's fastest production car, reaching 240.1 mph (386.4 km/h) with the rev limiter raised—a record for naturally aspirated cars that still stands.

Design

Murray conceived the F1 as the ultimate road car, unconstrained by cost or racing regulations. It was the first production car with a full carbon-fibre monocoque chassis. The engine bay was lined with gold foil as a heat reflector. The driver sits centrally, flanked by two passenger seats set slightly behind—an arrangement Murray had sketched as a student. Aerodynamics relied on ground-effect underbody tunnels and a rear diffuser rather than a large spoiler, and the car famously carried a lightweight Kenwood audio system and custom luggage to keep weight near 1,138 kg (2,509 lb).

Production and variants

From 1992 to 1998, McLaren built 106 cars: 64 standard F1 road cars, 28 GTR racing versions, five LM versions commemorating the car's 1995 Le Mans victory, two long-tail GT prototypes/road cars, and prototypes. The F1 GTR won the 24 Hours of Le Mans outright in 1995, finishing 1st, 3rd, 4th, 5th, and 13th—an extraordinary result for a car derived from a road vehicle.

Legacy and value

The F1 is widely regarded as one of the greatest sports cars ever built. Examples rarely come to market; in August 2021 an F1 sold at auction for $20.5 million, among the highest prices ever paid for a car. Its spiritual successor is the Gordon Murray Automotive T.50.
