Nottoway people

The Nottoway (also called Cheroenhaka in their own Iroquoian language) are a Native American people historically based in southeastern Virginia, around the Nottoway River in present-day Southampton and Sussex counties. They are one of the few Iroquoian-speaking peoples of the Virginia coastal plain, surrounded largely by Algonquian-speaking groups.

History

The Nottoway entered treaty relations with the Virginia colony in the seventeenth century and were assigned reservation lands along the Nottoway River. Through the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries their land base shrank through sales and encroachment. Many community members migrated, while a core community remained in Southampton County. In 2010 the Commonwealth of Virginia granted state recognition to the Cheroenhaka (Nottoway) Indian Tribe and the Nottoway Indian Tribe of Virginia.

Language and culture

The Nottoway language, a member of the Iroquoian family closely related to Tuscarora, ceased to be spoken fluently in the nineteenth century but is documented in word lists recorded by Thomas Jefferson and others, and is a focus of modern revitalisation efforts.
