The Académie des Beaux-Arts (French for "Academy of Fine Arts") is a French learned society based in Paris. It is one of the five academies of the Institut de France. The academy was created in 1816 in Paris as a merger of earlier royal academies of painting and sculpture, music, and architecture.

History

Its roots lie in the Académie royale de peinture et de sculpture founded in 1648 and the Académie royale d'architecture founded in 1671. Suppressed during the Revolution, the academies were reorganised under the Institut de France, and in 1816 the fine arts were consolidated into a single Académie des Beaux-Arts.

Role

The academy advises the French state on artistic matters, administers prizes and foundations, and manages museums and properties including the Villa Medici in Rome and the Musée Marmottan Monet in Paris. Its members — architects, painters, sculptors, engravers, composers, filmmakers, and photographers — are elected for life to a fixed number of seats, divided into sections.
