Montreal Cognitive Assessment

TypeCognitive screening test
DeveloperZiad Nasreddine (1996; published 2005)

The Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA) is a widely used screening assessment for detecting cognitive impairment. It was created in 1996 by Ziad Nasreddine in Montreal, Quebec, and validated in 2005. The test is a one-page, 30-point assessment administered in approximately 10 minutes, designed to detect mild cognitive impairment (MCI) in patients who may score normally on older screens such as the Mini-Mental State Examination.

Contents

The MoCA assesses several cognitive domains: short-term memory recall (5 points), visuospatial abilities (clock-drawing and copying a cube, 4 points), executive function (an adapted trail-making task, letter tapping, and verbal abstraction, 4 points), attention, concentration and working memory (digit span and serial subtraction, 6 points), language (sentence repetition and animal naming fluency, 5 points), and orientation to time and place (6 points).

Scoring and use

Scores range from 0 to 30; a score of 26 or above is generally considered normal, with one point added for people with 12 or fewer years of education. The original validation study found high sensitivity (around 90%) for detecting MCI, substantially better than the MMSE in that role. The test has been translated into dozens of languages, and variants exist for people with visual impairment (MoCA-Blind) and for telephone administration. Since 2019–2020, certification and training requirements have applied to clinical users.
