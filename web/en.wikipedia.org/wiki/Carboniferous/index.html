Carboniferous

The Carboniferous is a geological period of the Paleozoic Era lasting from about 358.9 to 298.9 million years ago, between the Devonian and Permian. Its name means 'coal-bearing', for the extensive coal seams formed from its tropical forests.

Chronology358.9 – 298.9 Ma
SubperiodsMississippian, Pennsylvanian

Life

Vast swamp forests of lycopods (Lepidodendron), horsetails (Calamites) and seed ferns dominated lowlands. High atmospheric oxygen — up to about 35% — supported giant arthropods such as Meganeura dragonfly-like insects and Arthropleura. Amphibians diversified, and the first amniotes (early reptiles and synapsids) appeared, freeing vertebrates from water for reproduction.

Climate and geography

The continents assembled into Pangaea; Gondwana glaciated in the south while tropics stayed wet. Burial of forest carbon helped draw down CO₂ and form coal deposits mined ever since.

End

The Carboniferous rainforest collapse (c. 305 Ma) fragmented forests, favouring amniotes. The period ended with the transition to the drier Permian.
