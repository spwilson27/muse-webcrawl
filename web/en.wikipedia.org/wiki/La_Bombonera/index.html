La Bombonera

Estadio Alberto J. Armando, popularly known as La Bombonera ("the chocolate box"), is an association football stadium in the La Boca neighbourhood of Buenos Aires, Argentina. It is the home ground of Boca Juniors, one of South America's most successful clubs, and is renowned worldwide for its steep stands and intense atmosphere.

History

The stadium was designed by the Slovenian architect Viktor Sulčič and inaugurated on 25 May 1940 with a friendly match against San Lorenzo, which Boca won 2–0. Its distinctive shape — three steep tiered stands and one side composed largely of seating and VIP boxes — arose from the restricted size of the plot; the nickname derives from its resemblance to a box of chocolates. A third tier was completed in 1953, and a large electronic scoreboard was installed in 1996, the year the stadium was renamed for former club president Alberto J. Armando.

Atmosphere

La Bombonera's capacity is around 54,000. Because the stands rise almost vertically close to the pitch, the stadium is famous for seeming to vibrate or "breathe" (La Bombonera no tiembla, late — "La Bombonera doesn't tremble, it beats") when supporters jump in unison, particularly the barra brava group La 12 behind one goal. The stadium hosts the Superclásico derby against River Plate, regularly ranked among world football's fiercest rivalries.

Other uses

Beyond football, the stadium houses the club museum, Museo de la Pasión Boquense, and has hosted concerts by Argentine and international artists.
