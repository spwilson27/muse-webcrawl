Padé approximant

In mathematics, a Padé approximant is an approximation of a function by a rational function — a ratio of two polynomials — chosen so that its Taylor series matches the function's series as closely as possible. The technique is named after the French mathematician Henri Padé, who developed it in his 1892 doctoral thesis.

Definition

The Padé approximant of order [m/n] is the rational function whose numerator is a polynomial of degree at most m and whose denominator is a polynomial of degree at most n, normalised so the denominator equals 1 at zero. Its Taylor expansion agrees with that of the target function through as many terms as the available coefficients allow.

Applications

Padé approximants often converge where Taylor series diverge, and can capture poles and other singular behaviour that polynomials cannot. They are used in numerical analysis, quantum mechanics, fluid dynamics, and the summation of divergent perturbation series, including the epsilon algorithm for sequence acceleration.
