Mount Wutai (Chinese: 五台山; pinyin: Wǔtái shān, "Five Terrace Mountain") is a mountain in Shanxi Province, China, named for its five flat-topped peaks (north, south, east, west and central). Its northern peak, at 3,061 metres, is the highest point in northern China and is called the "Roof of North China". Mount Wutai is one of the Four Sacred Buddhist Mountains of China, sacred to Manjushri (Wenshu), the bodhisattva of wisdom, and has been a pilgrimage destination for over a thousand years.

History

Buddhist temples were established at Wutai from the Northern Wei dynasty onward, spurred by the identification of the mountain with the abode of Manjushri described in the Avatamsaka Sutra. Emperors of the Sui, Tang, Song, Yuan, Ming and Qing dynasties patronized its monasteries, and pilgrims came from across East and Inner Asia, including Japan, Korea, Mongolia and Tibet. The temple town of Taihuai, in the valley between the peaks, holds dozens of monasteries.

Principal temples

Among the most important are Xiantong Temple, one of the largest on the mountain; Tayuan Temple, with its Great White Pagoda; Nanchan Temple, whose main hall of AD 782 is the oldest surviving wooden building in China; and Foguang Temple, whose East Hall (857) is another landmark of Tang-dynasty timber architecture, both rediscovered by Liang Sicheng and Lin Huiyin in 1937.

Heritage status

Mount Wutai was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2009 as a cultural landscape combining temples, architecture and sacred geography.
