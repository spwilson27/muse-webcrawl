Loupe

A loupe (/luːp/) is a simple, small magnification device used to see small details more closely. Unlike a magnifying glass, a loupe does not have an attached handle, and its focusing lens is contained in an opaque cylinder or cone, or in a foldable housing. Loupes may be a single lens or several lenses combined, and are designed to be held close to the eye, giving a wider field of view and less distortion than an ordinary magnifier.

Uses

Loupes are used in many professions where fine detail matters. Jewellers and watchmakers traditionally use a monocular loupe held in the eye socket, commonly with 10× magnification for inspecting gemstones. Geologists use a hand lens, usually 10×, as a basic field tool for identifying rocks and minerals. Dentists, surgeons and ophthalmologists use binocular loupes mounted on glasses, often with an attached light, to magnify the operating field; photographers and printers use loupes to examine negatives, slides and print registration.

Magnification and symbols

Loupe magnification is conventionally given as the ratio of the size of the retinal image with the loupe to that without it at the near point of the eye. Powers are often marked with the "×" symbol; the standard for diamond grading, for example, is the 10× triplet loupe, corrected for spherical and chromatic aberration.
