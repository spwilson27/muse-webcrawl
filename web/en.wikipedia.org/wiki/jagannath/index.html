Jagannath

Jagannath (Sanskrit: जगन्नाथ, romanized: Jagannātha, literally "Lord of the Universe") is a deity worshipped in regional Hindu traditions in India, primarily in the state of Odisha, as a form of Vishnu or Krishna. Together with his siblings Balabhadra and Subhadra, he forms a triad of wooden deities worshipped at the Jagannath Temple in Puri.

Iconography

Jagannath's idol is distinctively abstract: a large painted wooden figure with a round black face, large circular eyes, and minimal limbs. The idols are made of neem wood and are ritually replaced in the Nabakalebara ceremony, traditionally held every 12 or 19 years.

Ratha Yatra

The annual Ratha Yatra (chariot festival) at Puri draws the deities through the streets on enormous wooden chariots, attracting over a million pilgrims. The English word "juggernaut" derives from Jagannath and this festival. Similar festivals are held by devotees worldwide, including through ISKCON.

Significance

Jagannath worship blends Vaishnavism with tribal and other regional traditions; the deity is also revered in some Jain and Buddhist contexts. The Puri temple is one of the four Char Dham pilgrimage sites.
