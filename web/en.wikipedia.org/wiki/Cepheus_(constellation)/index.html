Cepheus (constellation)

Cepheus is a constellation in the northern sky, named after Cepheus, a king of Aethiopia in Greek mythology. It is one of the 48 constellations listed by the 2nd-century astronomer Ptolemy, and remains one of the 88 modern constellations. Its distinctive house-like shape of five bright stars lies between Cassiopeia and Polaris, and it is circumpolar for most northern observers.

Notable stars and objects

Delta Cephei is the prototype of the Cepheid variables, whose period–luminosity relation makes them standard candles for measuring cosmic distances. Mu Cephei, Herschel's "Garnet Star", is one of the largest and reddest supergiant stars known. Because of precession, Alpha Cephei (Alderamin) will become the northern pole star in about 7,500 years, a role Gamma Cephei will also hold. Deep-sky objects include the Iris Nebula (NGC 7023) and the Fireworks Galaxy (NGC 6946).
