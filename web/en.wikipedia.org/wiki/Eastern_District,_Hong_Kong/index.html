The Eastern District (Chinese: 東區) is one of the 18 districts of Hong Kong, located on the northeastern coast of Hong Kong Island. It stretches from Causeway Bay and North Point in the west, through Quarry Bay, Shau Kei Wan and Heng Fa Chuen, to Chai Wan and Siu Sai Wan in the east. It is a largely residential district with a population of over half a million, making it one of the most populous districts in the territory.

Neighbourhoods

The district includes Fortress Hill, North Point, Quarry Bay (including Taikoo Shing, one of Hong Kong's largest private housing estates), Sai Wan Ho, Shau Kei Wan, Aldrich Bay, Heng Fa Chuen, Chai Wan and Siu Sai Wan. The western end around Tin Hau and Causeway Bay borders the Wan Chai District and shares in the island's main commercial strip. Quarry Bay has developed into a secondary office hub around Taikoo Place.

History and landmarks

The area was historically a centre of quarrying (hence Quarry Bay), fishing — Shau Kei Wan was a sheltered fishing harbour — and industry along the harbourfront. The former North Point power station and the docks of Taikoo (Swire) shaped its growth. Landmarks include the Lei Yue Mun Fort, which now houses the Hong Kong Museum of Coastal Defence overlooking the Lei Yue Mun channel; the Fireboat Alexander Grantham exhibition gallery in Quarry Bay Park; and the Tam Kung Temple and Shing Wong Temple in Shau Kei Wan. The Chai Wan end of the district is home to cemeteries on the slopes of Cape Collinson and to the terminus of the MTR Island line.

Transport

The district is served by the MTR Island line (from Tin Hau to Chai Wan), the Tseung Kwan O line branch to North Point and Quarry Bay, the historic Hong Kong Tramways along King's Road, and the Island Eastern Corridor expressway. The Eastern Harbour Crossing connects the district to Kowloon at Kwun Tong.
