Nord Stream (German and Russian names for "North Stream") is a pair of offshore natural gas pipelines in Europe that run under the Baltic Sea from Vyborg, Russia, to Greifswald, Germany. Together with the parallel Nord Stream 2 pipeline, it forms part of the Nord Stream system built to transport Russian natural gas directly to Germany, bypassing transit countries such as Ukraine and Poland. The first line was inaugurated in November 2011 and the second in October 2012, giving a combined annual capacity of 55 billion cubic metres.

Ownership and construction

The pipeline was built and is owned by Nord Stream AG, a consortium originally led by Russia's Gazprom (51%), with German firms Wintershall and E.ON, Dutch Gasunie and French Engie holding the remainder. The twin pipelines are each 1,222 km long, laid on the seabed through the territorial waters and exclusive economic zones of Russia, Finland, Sweden, Denmark and Germany.

2022 sabotage

On 26 September 2022, a series of underwater explosions ruptured both Nord Stream 1 pipelines and one of the two Nord Stream 2 pipelines near the Danish island of Bornholm, releasing large quantities of methane. Gas flows through Nord Stream 1 had already been halted by Russia weeks earlier amid the confrontation over the Russian invasion of Ukraine, and Nord Stream 2 had never entered service after Germany suspended its certification in February 2022. Investigations by Denmark, Sweden and Germany followed; the perpetrators remained a subject of international dispute. The incident became one of the most prominent acts of infrastructure sabotage in modern European history.

Controversy

From its inception the project was criticised by the United States, Ukraine and several Eastern European states, which argued it increased European dependence on Russian gas and undermined Ukraine's transit role, while Germany and the operating companies defended it as a commercial project improving supply security.
