Kola Superdeep Borehole

Kola Superdeep Borehole
LocationKola Peninsula, Russia
Depth12,262 m (40,230 ft)
Drilled1970–1992

The Kola Superdeep Borehole is the result of a Soviet scientific drilling project on the Kola Peninsula, near the Norwegian border, which attempted to drill as deep as possible into the Earth's crust. Its deepest hole, SG-3, reached 12,262 metres in 1989 and remains the deepest artificial point on Earth in true vertical depth.

Drilling and findings

Drilling began in 1970 and proceeded intermittently until funding ended after the dissolution of the USSR. Surprising findings included abundant water deep in the crust, microscopic fossils in rocks at depths of six kilometres, and temperatures of about 180 °C — far higher than predicted — which turned drilling mud to a boil and ultimately limited progress. Contrary to expectations, no transition from granite to basalt was found.

Legacy

The borehole site was abandoned and the hole capped with a welded metal disc; it remains a benchmark of scientific drilling that later projects, including oceanic ones, have not surpassed in vertical depth.
