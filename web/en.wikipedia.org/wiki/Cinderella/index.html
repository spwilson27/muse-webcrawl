Cinderella

"Cinderella", or "The Little Glass Slipper", is a folk tale with thousands of variants throughout the world. The protagonist is a young girl living in forsaken circumstances who is suddenly blessed with remarkable fortune, ultimately marrying royalty. The story of Rhodopis, recounted by the Greek geographer Strabo around 7 BC, about a Greek slave girl who marries the king of Egypt, is usually considered the earliest known variant.

Plot

A wealthy widower marries a proud, haughty woman as his second wife. She has two daughters, who are equally vain and selfish. The man's own daughter, by contrast, is gentle and kind. After his death, the stepmother turns the girl into a servant in her own house, forcing her to do all the chores; because she often sits among the cinders by the fireplace, she is nicknamed "Cinderella". When the prince of the kingdom holds a ball, Cinderella's fairy godmother transforms her rags into a splendid gown and a pumpkin into a coach, warning her to return before midnight. At the ball she enchants the prince but flees at midnight, losing a glass slipper. The prince searches the kingdom for the woman whose foot fits the slipper; when it fits Cinderella, they marry.

Variants

The best-known European versions are by Charles Perrault (1697), which introduced the fairy godmother, the pumpkin carriage, and the glass slipper, and by the Brothers Grimm (1812), in which a hazel tree on her mother's grave provides Cinderella's dresses and the stepsisters mutilate their feet to fit the slipper. The Chinese tale of Ye Xian, recorded around 860 AD, is among the oldest written versions. Opera adaptations include Rossini's La Cenerentola (1817) and Massenet's Cendrillon (1899); Prokofiev composed a ballet (1945). Disney's animated film Cinderella (1950) is the most influential modern retelling.
