Church of Scotland

Church of Scotland
Scots: The Kirk
Founded: 1560 (Scottish Reformation)
Polity: Presbyterian
Head: Moderator of the General Assembly (annual); the monarch is a member, not head

The Church of Scotland (Scots: The Kirk) is the national church in Scotland, founded in the Scottish Reformation of 1560 under figures such as John Knox. It is Presbyterian in government and Reformed (Calvinist) in theology.

History

The Reformation Parliament adopted the Scots Confession in 1560. The church endured conflicts over episcopacy in the 17th century, the Covenanters, and the Disruption of 1843, when a third of ministers left to form the Free Church; most strands reunited in 1929. The Church of Scotland Act 1921 recognised its spiritual independence from the state.

Government

Local congregations are governed by kirk sessions of elders; above them sit presbyteries and the annual General Assembly in Edinburgh, presided over by a Moderator serving a one-year term. The British monarch is represented by a Lord High Commissioner.
