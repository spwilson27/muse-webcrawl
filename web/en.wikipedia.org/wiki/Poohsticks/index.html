Poohsticks

Type: Game

Origin: The House at Pooh Corner (1928) by A. A. Milne

Poohsticks is a simple game invented by the fictional bear Winnie-the-Pooh in A. A. Milne's The House at Pooh Corner (1928). Players drop sticks into a flowing stream from the upstream side of a bridge and rush to the other side; the winner is the player whose stick first appears from beneath the bridge, carried by the current.

Origin

In Milne's story, Pooh invents the game accidentally after dropping a fir cone into the river from a bridge in the Ashdown Forest and noticing which side it came out. The game is then played with sticks (easier to mark than cones) by Pooh, Christopher Robin, Piglet, Rabbit and Eeyore — who, in the course of the story, is found floating in the river himself and is rescued using a stick.

The real bridge

The bridge in the stories corresponds to a real wooden bridge in Ashdown Forest, East Sussex (built 1907 and later known as Poohsticks Bridge), which Christopher Robin Milne played on as a boy. It was restored and reopened in 1979 and remains a place of pilgrimage for Pooh fans.

Championships

The World Poohsticks Championships have been held in Oxfordshire since 1984, first at Little Wittenham and later at Witney on the River Windrush, attracting hundreds of competitors from around the world and raising money for charity. Individual and team events are contested under official rules governing stick size and the drop.
