Outdoor recreation

Hiking is among the most widely practised forms of outdoor recreation.

Outdoor recreation or outdoor activity refers to recreation done in nature and outdoors, most commonly in natural or semi-natural settings out of town. Examples include adventure racing, backpacking, cycling, camping, canoeing, canyoning, caving, disc golf, fishing, hiking, horseback riding, hunting, kayaking, mountaineering, photography, adventure park, rock climbing, running, sailing, skiing, surfing, and sports.

Outdoor recreation may also refer to a team sport game or practice held in an outdoor setting, such as an under-12 flag football league, often organized by a municipality or camp facility. People engage in physical activity outdoors as a form of recreation. Various physical activities can be completed individually or communally. Sports done outdoors are a great way to connect with nature, stay active, and reduce stress.

Purpose

Outdoor recreation involves any kind of activity within an outdoor environment. Individuals participate for many reasons: physical exercise, personal challenge, enjoyment of nature, social interaction, education, and stress relief. Research associates time spent in natural settings with improved mood, attention, and health outcomes, while outdoor pursuits also underpin a large equipment, tourism, and guiding industry.

Types of activity

Activities are commonly grouped by environment: land-based pursuits such as hiking, camping, backpacking, rock climbing, mountain biking, and trail running; water-based pursuits such as canoeing, kayaking, rafting, sailing, surfing, fishing, and swimming; snow and ice activities such as skiing, snowboarding, snowshoeing, and ice climbing; and aerial activities such as paragliding and BASE jumping. Many activities span categories and range from casual day trips to expedition-style undertakings requiring advanced skills and equipment.

Environmental considerations

As participation grows, the environmental impact of outdoor recreation has become a significant management concern. Erosion of trails, disturbance of wildlife, litter, and crowding in popular natural areas have prompted conservation ethics such as Leave No Trace, which encourages visitors to minimise their impact so that natural places remain available and healthy for future users.
