Lars von Trier

Lars von Trier
Born Lars Trier, April 30, 1956, Kongens Lyngby, Denmark
Occupation: film director, screenwriter
Known for: Dogme 95; Breaking the Waves; Dancer in the Dark
Production company: Zentropa (co-founder)

Lars von Trier (born Lars Trier, 30 April 1956) is a Danish film director and screenwriter, widely regarded as one of the most influential—and provocative—European filmmakers of his generation. He co-founded the Dogme 95 movement and the production company Zentropa, and his films, frequently organized in trilogies, are noted for their emotional extremity, formal experimentation and themes of suffering, sacrifice and evil.

Career

Von Trier graduated from the National Film School of Denmark in 1983. His early features—the "Europe trilogy" of The Element of Crime (1984), Epidemic (1987) and Europa (1991)—were visually stylized studies of a continent in decay. The television series The Kingdom (1994) brought him wide recognition in Denmark.

In 1995, with Thomas Vinterberg, he launched Dogme 95, whose "Vow of Chastity" prescribed location shooting, handheld cameras, natural light and no artificial genre effects. His own greatest successes came in the "Golden Heart" trilogy: Breaking the Waves (1996), The Idiots (1998) and Dancer in the Dark (2000), which won the Palme d'Or at Cannes. The "USA: Land of Opportunities" films Dogville (2003) and Manderlay (2005), shot on bare sound stages, divided critics. Later works include the "Depression trilogy"—Antichrist (2009), Melancholia (2011) and Nymphomaniac (2013)—and The House That Jack Built (2018).

Controversies and later life

Von Trier's career has been accompanied by controversy: at a 2011 Cannes press conference he made remarks joking that he sympathized with Hitler, leading the festival to declare him persona non grata for a time, and several actresses have described difficult experiences working with him. He has spoken openly about depression, anxiety and phobias, including a fear of flying that confines his work to Europe. In 2022 it was announced that he had been diagnosed with Parkinson's disease; a third series of The Kingdom (The Kingdom Exodus) premiered that year.
