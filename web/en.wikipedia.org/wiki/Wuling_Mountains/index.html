The Wuling Mountains are a mountain range in southwest China, extending across eastern Guizhou, Hunan and Chongqing. They form part of the watershed between the Yuan River and Wu River systems and are characterised by dramatic karst landforms, deep gorges and dense subtropical forest. The highest peak is Mount Fanjing (2,570 metres), a sacred Buddhist site and UNESCO World Heritage Site noted for its unusual mushroom-shaped summit formations.

Geography and ecology

The range includes the Wulingyuan Scenic Area near Zhangjiajie, famous for its thousands of quartzite sandstone pillars and part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The region is rich in biodiversity and is home to ethnic minority communities including the Tujia and Miao peoples.

Culture

The area gives its name to the Wuling region in historical geography. Terraced agriculture, stilted riverside towns such as Fenghuang, and distinctive minority festivals are features of the cultural landscape.
