Galveston Bay is a large estuary located along the Texas Gulf Coast of the United States, southeast of Houston. It is connected to the Gulf of Mexico and is surrounded by sub-tropical marshes and prairies on the mainland. The bay has played a prominent role in the history and economy of Texas, particularly through the Port of Houston and the Houston Ship Channel, one of the busiest waterways in the world.

Geography

The bay complex consists of several sub-bays, including Galveston Bay proper, Trinity Bay, East Bay, West Bay and Clear Lake. It is roughly 30 miles (48 km) long and 17 miles (27 km) wide, with an average depth of about 6 feet (2 m). Fresh water flows in mainly from the Trinity and San Jacinto rivers, mixing with salt water from the Gulf at Bolivar Roads.

Ecology

Galveston Bay is one of the most productive estuaries in the United States, supporting major commercial and recreational fisheries for shrimp, oysters, blue crab and finfish. Its wetlands provide habitat for migratory waterfowl along the Central Flyway.

History

The area was inhabited by Karankawa and Akokisa peoples before European exploration. The city of Galveston, founded on Galveston Island in 1839, became a major port; the catastrophic Galveston hurricane of 1900 reshaped the region and spurred the development of Houston as the dominant port.
