Terrain awareness and warning system

A terrain awareness and warning system (TAWS) is an aircraft safety system designed to prevent controlled flight into terrain (CFIT) accidents by warning flight crews when the aircraft is in potentially hazardous proximity to terrain or obstacles. The best-known implementations are the ground proximity warning system (GPWS) and its successor, the enhanced GPWS (EGPWS).

Operation

Original GPWS used a radio altimeter and flight parameters to detect excessive descent rate, terrain closure, and altitude loss after takeoff. EGPWS adds a worldwide terrain and obstacle database with GPS position, providing look-ahead alerts ("Terrain ahead, pull up") and colour-coded cockpit terrain displays. TAWS classes (A, B, and C, per TSO-C151) define required functions for different aircraft categories, and the systems are mandated for most turbine-powered passenger aircraft.

Impact

Since mandatory GPWS fitment in the 1970s, CFIT — once a leading cause of airliner fatalities — has declined dramatically.
