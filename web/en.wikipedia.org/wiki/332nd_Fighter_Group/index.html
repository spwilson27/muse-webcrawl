The 332nd Fighter Group was a United States Army Air Forces unit of the Second World War, famous as the fighter unit of the Tuskegee Airmen — the first African-American military aviators in the United States armed forces. Its pilots, who trained at Tuskegee Army Air Field in Alabama and were later popularly known as the "Red Tails" for the crimson paint on their aircraft tails, flew bomber-escort and ground-attack missions over North Africa and Europe.

History

The group was activated in October 1942 at Tuskegee, Alabama, and deployed to the Mediterranean in early 1944. Initially equipped with P-39 Airacobras and P-47 Thunderbolts in coastal patrol and ground-attack roles, the group joined the Fifteenth Air Force in Italy. Under Colonel Benjamin O. Davis Jr. it converted to the P-51 Mustang and was assigned primarily to escorting heavy bombers on long-range missions over Germany, Austria, Romania and other targets.

Record

Flying more than 1,500 missions, the 332nd's pilots were credited with destroying over 100 enemy aircraft in the air and many more on the ground, along with locomotives, rolling stock and a German destroyer damaged by strafing. Sixty-six of its pilots were killed in combat. Its bomber-escort record was among the best in the Fifteenth Air Force; the long-repeated claim that it "never lost a bomber" is an exaggeration, but its losses were notably low. In April 2007 the Tuskegee Airmen collectively received the Congressional Gold Medal.

Legacy

The unit's success helped pave the way for President Harry S. Truman's 1948 executive order desegregating the United States military. Its story has been told in the films The Tuskegee Airmen (1995) and Red Tails (2012).
