Aristagoras

Aristagoras of Miletus (Ancient Greek: Ἀρισταγόρας ὁ Μιλήσιος; fl. late 6th and early 5th centuries BC) was the tyrant of the Ionian city of Miletus under the Persian Empire. He is best known as the instigator of the Ionian Revolt (499–493 BC), the first major conflict between Greece and Persia and a prelude to the Greco-Persian Wars. Our main source for his career is Herodotus.

The Naxos expedition

Aristagoras governed Miletus as deputy for his father-in-law and cousin Histiaeus, who had been detained at the Persian court in Susa. Around 500 BC Aristagoras agreed to help exiled oligarchs recover the island of Naxos, enlisting the support of Artaphernes, the Persian satrap at Sardis, and a Persian fleet under Megabates. The expedition failed after a quarrel between Aristagoras and Megabates, who allegedly warned the Naxians; the siege dragged on four months and exhausted the funds.

The Ionian Revolt

Fearing punishment for the failure and the money he had spent, Aristagoras resolved to revolt. According to Herodotus, he was also encouraged by a message tattooed on a slave's head from Histiaeus. Aristagoras shaved his head, renounced his tyranny, and proclaimed equality (isonomia) for Miletus to win support, then deposed or expelled tyrants in other Ionian cities. He seized the Persian ships returning from Naxos and travelled to mainland Greece to seek allies. At Sparta, King Cleomenes I refused, but Athens sent twenty ships and Eretria five. With this help the rebels marched on Sardis and burned it (498 BC), but were defeated on the way back at Ephesus, and Aristagoras's mainland allies withdrew.

Death

As the revolt faltered, Aristagoras sailed to Myrcinus in Thrace, which Histiaeus had been granted by Darius, seeking a refuge. There, around 497 BC, he was killed with his followers while besieging a Thracian town. The revolt he had started continued without him until the Persian victory at the Battle of Lade and the destruction of Miletus in 494 BC.
