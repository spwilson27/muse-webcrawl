Hairshirt

A hairshirt, also called a cilice or sackcloth, is a garment made of coarse cloth or animal hair worn next to the skin as a form of self-imposed discomfort, traditionally as an act of religious penance and mortification of the flesh.

History

Wearing sackcloth is mentioned in the Hebrew Bible as a sign of mourning and repentance. In Christian tradition, hairshirts were worn by ascetics, monks and saints including St Francis of Assisi and St Thomas More, whose hairshirt is preserved as a relic. The practice was intended to cause constant irritation as a reminder of penitence and to subdue bodily desires.

Modern use

Some religious orders and lay movements continue to use cilices, including modern metal forms worn on the thigh. In secular usage, "hairshirt" is used metaphorically for unnecessary self-denial or austerity, as in "hairshirt economics".
