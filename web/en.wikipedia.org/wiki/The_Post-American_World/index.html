The Post-American World

AuthorFareed Zakaria
SubjectInternational relations
PublisherW. W. Norton & Company
Published2008; updated "Release 2.0" 2011

The Post-American World is a non-fiction book by American journalist Fareed Zakaria, published in 2008. It explores the rise of emerging powers — especially China and India — and argues that the world is moving from unipolar American dominance toward a multipolar order, not through America's decline but through "the rise of the rest."

Argument

Zakaria draws on economic data to show that for two decades growth had been strong across Asia, Latin America, and Africa, producing hundreds of new global firms and tallest buildings, largest malls, and richest individuals increasingly found outside the United States. He argues American political dysfunction, debt, and education weaknesses pose greater risks than foreign rivals, and he advises Washington to engage and legitimate new powers within international institutions rather than resisting them — a strategy he calls shaping a "post-American" order in which the United States remains the pivotal balancer. An updated edition, The Post-American World: Release 2.0 (2011), revised the analysis after the Great Recession.

Reception

The book was a New York Times bestseller and was widely discussed in policy circles; then-Senator Barack Obama was photographed carrying it during the 2008 presidential campaign, boosting its profile. Reviewers praised its accessible synthesis of trends, though some argued Zakaria understated American resilience or the vulnerabilities of China and India — themes Zakaria addressed in the revised edition.
