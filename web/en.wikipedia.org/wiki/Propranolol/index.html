Propranolol

Clinical data
Trade names: Inderal, others
Drug class: Beta blocker (non-selective)
Routes: Oral, intravenous
Legal status: Prescription only
Identifiers
CAS: 525-66-6
Formula: C16H21NO2

Propranolol, sold under the brand name Inderal among others, is a medication of the beta blocker class. It is used to treat high blood pressure, a number of types of irregular heart rate, thyrotoxicosis, capillary hemangiomas, performance anxiety, and essential tremors, and to prevent migraine headaches and further heart problems in those with angina or previous heart attacks.

History

Propranolol was developed by Scottish scientist Sir James Black at Imperial Chemical Industries in the late 1950s and patented in 1962; it entered medical use in 1964. It was the first successful beta blocker and replaced the earlier, toxic pronethalol. Black received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1988 in part for this work. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines and is available as a generic medication.

Mechanism

Propranolol is a non-selective beta blocker, antagonizing both β1 and β2 adrenergic receptors. It thereby reduces heart rate, cardiac contractility, and renin release, and blunts the effects of catecholamines. It is highly lipophilic and crosses the blood–brain barrier, which accounts for some central effects, including its use for anxiety symptoms and its side-effect profile.

Side effects

Common side effects include nausea, abdominal pain, and constipation. It should not be used in those with asthma or certain heart conditions, as blockade of β2 receptors can worsen bronchospasm. It may worsen the symptoms of hypoglycemia in people with diabetes by masking warning signs. Dizziness, fatigue, and cold extremities are also reported.
