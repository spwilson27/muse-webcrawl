The True Whig Party (TWP), also known as the Liberian Whig Party, is a political party in Liberia. Founded in 1869 by Americo-Liberian settlers, it dominated Liberian politics for more than a century, ruling the country continuously from 1878 until the military coup of 1980 — one of the longest periods of single-party dominance in modern history.

History

The party emerged from the political organisation of the Americo-Liberian settler elite, the descendants of freed American slaves who founded Liberia. After the victory of Anthony W. Gardiner in 1878, the True Whigs held the presidency without interruption. Under their rule, Liberia functioned in practice as a one-party state: elections were held regularly, but opposition was minimal and the franchise and political power were concentrated among the small settler population, while the indigenous majority was largely excluded from political life.

Notable True Whig presidents included Arthur Barclay, Edwin Barclay, William Tubman — who served from 1944 until his death in 1971 and pursued an "open door" policy toward foreign investment — and his successor William Tolbert. Tolbert's overthrow and murder in the April 1980 coup led by Master Sergeant Samuel Doe ended the party's rule, and it was banned under Doe's regime.

Later activity

Following Liberia's civil wars and the restoration of multi-party politics, the True Whig Party was re-established and has contested elections, though with limited success, in coalition arrangements with other parties. Despite its diminished role, it remains the oldest political party in Liberia and one of the oldest in Africa.
