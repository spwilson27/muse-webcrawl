Inferno (operating system)

DeveloperBell Labs; later Vita Nuova Holdings
Initial release1996
Written inC, Limbo

Inferno is a distributed operating system started at Bell Labs in 1995 by members of the team that created Plan 9 from Bell Labs and Unix, including Rob Pike, Phil Winterbottom, and Sean Dorward. It was designed for building distributed and networked systems that could run on a wide range of hardware, from large servers down to small embedded devices.

Design

Inferno extends the Plan 9 philosophy that all resources should be accessible as files in a hierarchical namespace. Three principles characterise it: resources are represented as files in namespaces; a single standard protocol, called Styx, is used to access both local and remote resources; and the system runs either natively on hardware or as a virtualised environment on top of a host operating system.

Limbo and Dis

Applications for Inferno are written in Limbo, a concurrent programming language with C-like syntax, built-in support for communicating sequential processes (channels), and automatic garbage collection. Limbo programs compile to bytecode for the Dis virtual machine, making them portable across architectures. The combination anticipated features of later managed platforms.

History

Inferno was commercialised by Lucent and later transferred to the British company Vita Nuova Holdings, which released the fourth edition under free software licences in the 2000s. Its ideas — file-based resource access, the Styx/9P protocol, and lightweight virtual machines — influenced subsequent distributed systems research.
