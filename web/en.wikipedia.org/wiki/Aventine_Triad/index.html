Aventine Triad

Deities: Ceres, Liber, Libera
Temple: Aventine Hill, Rome
Dedicated: 493 BC (traditional)

The Aventine Triad is a group of three deities — Ceres, Liber and Libera — worshipped together on the Aventine Hill in ancient Rome. Their temple, traditionally dedicated in 493 BC, stood near the Circus Maximus and was closely associated with the plebeians, in contrast to the patrician Capitoline Triad of Jupiter, Juno and Minerva.

Origins

The triad reflects Greek influence through the colonies of Magna Graecia: Ceres corresponds to Demeter, Liber to Dionysus and Libera to Persephone or Ariadne. The temple housed the archives of the plebeian aediles and the records of plebeian resolutions.

Significance

The cult was central to plebeian identity during the Conflict of the Orders; the secessions of the plebs were directed to the Aventine, and the temple served as a plebeian sanctuary and treasury.
