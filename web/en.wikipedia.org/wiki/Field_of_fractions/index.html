Field of fractions

In abstract algebra, the field of fractions of an integral domain is the smallest field in which it can be embedded. The construction of the field of fractions is modeled on the relationship between the integral domain of integers and the field of rational numbers. It is also called the quotient field or field of quotients.

Construction

Given an integral domain R, consider pairs (a, b) with a, b ∈ R and b ≠ 0, modulo the equivalence relation (a, b) ~ (c, d) if and only if ad = bc. Addition and multiplication are defined as for fractions: (a/b) + (c/d) = (ad + bc)/bd and (a/b)(c/d) = ac/bd. That these operations are well defined uses the fact that R has no zero divisors. The resulting field Frac(R) contains an isomorphic copy of R via a ↦ a/1, and it is universal: any injective ring homomorphism from R into a field factors uniquely through Frac(R).

Examples

The field of fractions of the integers is the rational numbers. For a polynomial ring k[x] over a field k, the field of fractions is the field of rational functions k(x). The field of fractions of the ring of holomorphic functions on a connected open set is the field of meromorphic functions. If the ring is already a field, its field of fractions is itself.

Generalizations

The construction is the special case of localization in which the multiplicative set inverted is the set of all nonzero elements; for commutative rings that are not domains the analogous concept is the total quotient ring.
