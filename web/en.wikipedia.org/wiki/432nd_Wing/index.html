The 432nd Wing is a United States Air Force unit assigned to Air Combat Command, stationed at Creech Air Force Base, Nevada. It is the USAF's principal wing for remotely piloted aircraft (RPA) operations, flying the MQ-9 Reaper in intelligence, surveillance, reconnaissance and strike missions worldwide.

History

The unit's lineage traces to the 432nd Tactical Reconnaissance Group of the Second World War. It served in the Vietnam War as the 432nd Tactical Reconnaissance Wing, flying RF-4C Phantom II and RF-101 reconnaissance aircraft from Udorn Royal Thai Air Force Base and earning fame for its reconnaissance and later drone operations. After inactivation and a period as a fighter wing flying F-16s in Japan and Nevada, the wing was reactivated at Creech on 1 May 2007 as the first dedicated unmanned aircraft systems wing.

Operations

Aircrews at Creech (and at associated units such as the 432nd's operations at Holloman AFB) fly combat air patrols around the globe by satellite link, supporting operations in the Middle East, Africa and elsewhere. The wing includes the 432nd Operations Group and the 432nd Maintenance Group, and also supports the RQ-170 Sentinel stealth reconnaissance aircraft. The associated 432nd Air Expeditionary Wing oversees deployed RPA operations.
