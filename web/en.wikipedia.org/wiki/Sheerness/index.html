Sheerness

Sheerness is a port town and civil parish in the Borough of Swale in Kent, England, situated at the mouth of the River Medway on the south-east corner of the Isle of Sheppey. Developed around a Royal Navy dockyard, it remains one of the United Kingdom's important car-importing ports.

History

Sheerness began as a fort built under Henry VIII to defend the entrance to the Medway. After the Dutch Raid on the Medway in 1667 humiliated the English fleet at anchor there, Samuel Pepys directed the strengthening of the defences, and a naval dockyard grew into the town's dominant employer for three centuries. Unusually, the dockyard town was laid out in an orderly grid of streets, with workers' houses and officers' residences such as those of Naval Terrace surviving from the Georgian period.

During the Napoleonic Wars the anchorage at the Nore was the scene of a major naval mutiny in 1797. The Royal Navy dockyard closed in 1960, but the site continues as a commercial port, handling vehicles, steel, fruit and bulk cargoes. The town also served as a seaside resort, reached by a branch railway from Sittingbourne.

The SS Richard Montgomery

In the Thames Estuary off Sheerness lies the wreck of the American Liberty ship SS Richard Montgomery, which ran aground in 1944 with a cargo of munitions; about 1,400 tonnes of explosives remain aboard, and an exclusion zone is maintained around the visible masts.
