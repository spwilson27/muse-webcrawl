The Volga Tatars or simply Tatars (Tatar: Идел буе татарлары, İdel buye tatarları) are a Turkic ethnic group native to the Volga-Ural region of western Russia. They are the second-largest ethnic group in the Russian Federation and the titular nation of the Republic of Tatarstan, whose capital is Kazan.

History

The Volga Tatars descend from the population of Volga Bulgaria, the Golden Horde, and the Khanate of Kazan, with admixture from Kipchak Turks and local Finno-Ugric peoples. After Ivan the Terrible conquered Kazan in 1552, the Tatars came under Russian rule, enduring periods of forced Christianization alongside episodes of flourishing trade and scholarship. In the 19th century, Tatar merchants and Jadid reformers made Kazan a center of Muslim education and publishing in the Russian Empire.

After the Russian Revolution, the Tatar Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic was established in 1920. Tatars suffered heavily from collectivization and famine. In 1990 Tatarstan declared sovereignty within Russia, and Kazan's millennium was celebrated in 2005.

Subgroups and language

Major subgroups include the Kazan Tatars, the Mishar Tatars of the western Volga, the Qasim Tatars, and the Orthodox Kryashens, some of whom assert a distinct identity. The Tatar language belongs to the Kipchak branch of Turkic and is written in Cyrillic in Russia; it is co-official with Russian in Tatarstan, though its use in education has been a subject of political debate since the 2010s.

Culture

Volga Tatar culture blends Turkic, Islamic, and Russian influences. The harvest festival Sabantuy, with wrestling (köräş), races, and games, is the best-known Tatar celebration and is held across Russia and the diaspora. Tatar cuisine is noted for pastries such as öçpoçmaq (echpochmak) and çäkçäk, a honey-soaked dessert. Famous Volga Tatars include the poet Gabdulla Tukay, ballet dancer Rudolf Nureyev (of Tatar descent), and theologian and reformer Shihabuddin Marjani.
