Dropping out


Leaving school before graduation is the most studied form of dropping out.

Dropping out refers to leaving an institution or course of study before completing it. The term is most commonly used of students who leave school before graduating, but it also describes withdrawal from universities, training programmes, sports competitions and, in a countercultural sense, voluntarily leaving mainstream society.

School dropouts

A school dropout is a student who leaves secondary (or primary) education without a diploma or equivalent credential. Rates and legal leaving ages vary widely between countries. Research associates dropping out with poverty, low academic achievement and grade retention, disengagement, family instability, and the pull of employment or caregiving duties. Dropouts on average experience lower earnings, higher unemployment and poorer health, which is why governments treat the dropout rate as a key education indicator and fund prevention and re-engagement programmes, alternative schools and equivalency qualifications such as the GED in the United States.

University dropouts

Attrition from higher education attracts attention both as a policy concern and, in notable individual cases, as a prelude to success in business or the arts, where founders who left university early have become a celebrated archetype. Completion statistics are a standard measure of institutional performance.

Countercultural usage

In the 1960s, "dropping out" acquired a positive meaning within the counterculture: Timothy Leary's slogan "Turn on, tune in, drop out" urged withdrawal from conventional careers and institutions in favour of communal and experimental living. In contemporary China, the related ideas of "lying flat" and Japan's freeter and hikikomori phenomena reflect analogous disengagement.
