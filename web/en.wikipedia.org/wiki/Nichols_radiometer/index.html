Nichols radiometer
Inventors: Ernest Fox Nichols, Gordon Ferrie Hull
Date: 1901–1903
Purpose: Measuring radiation pressure of light

The Nichols radiometer was a sensitive torsion-balance apparatus developed by Ernest Fox Nichols and Gordon Ferrie Hull at Dartmouth College in 1901–1903 to measure the pressure exerted by light.

Design

Unlike the Crookes radiometer, whose vanes turn largely because of gas effects, the Nichols instrument worked in a partial vacuum with small mirrored vanes suspended on a fine quartz fibre in a glass vessel. Light reflected from the mirrors exerted a tiny torque, measured by the deflection of a light beam, allowing gas pressure effects to be separated and minimised.

Results

Nichols and Hull measured radiation pressure in agreement, within about one percent, with Maxwell's theoretical prediction that electromagnetic radiation carries momentum. Their work, with Pyotr Lebedev's independent experiments, provided key confirmation of electromagnetic theory and underlies later concepts such as solar sails.
