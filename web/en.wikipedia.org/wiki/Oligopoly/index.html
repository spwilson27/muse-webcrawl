Oligopoly

[Image: two rival airliners of competing manufacturers parked side by side at an airport]

An oligopoly is a Market structure dominated by a few large firms, each aware that its decisions on Price, output and investment affect and are affected by its rivals. It sits between Monopoly and monopolistic Competition, and covers many real industries: commercial aircraft (Boeing–Airbus), soft drinks, mobile networks, supermarkets and oil majors.

Strategic behaviour

Because firms are interdependent, oligopoly is modelled with game theory: Cournot (1838) has firms compete in quantities; Bertrand (1883) in prices, driving price to cost with just two firms; and collusive outcomes — cartels such as OPEC in oil — tempt members to cheat, the prisoner's-dilemma logic formalised by Nash. Firms may instead compete through advertising, product differentiation and loyalty schemes, or tacitly coordinate via price leadership. Competition authorities watch oligopolies closely for collusion and coordinated effects in mergers, since outcomes range from near-competitive to near-monopoly depending on conduct.
