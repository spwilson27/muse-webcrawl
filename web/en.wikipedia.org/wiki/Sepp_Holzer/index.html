Sepp Holzer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Josef "Sepp" Holzer (born 24 July 1942) is an Austrian farmer, author, and international consultant for natural agriculture. He is known for creating the Krameterhof, a permaculture farm in the Lungau region of Austria at elevations between 1,100 and 1,500 metres, where he grows an unusual diversity of crops — including citrus and other frost-sensitive fruit — in an alpine climate, earning him the nickname "the rebel farmer".

The Krameterhof

Holzer took over his parents' mountain farm in 1962. Rejecting the monoculture forestry and intensive methods then being promoted, he developed his own system — guided by observation of nature rather than textbooks — using terraces, raised beds (Hügelkultur), stone heat traps, and dozens of ponds to create favourable microclimates. The farm supports fish, pigs, rare livestock breeds, fruit trees, herbs, and vegetables with little external input.

Work and influence

Holzer's methods, sometimes called "Holzer permaculture", were documented in his books, including The Rebel Farmer and Sepp Holzer's Permaculture (2011), and in films such as The Agricultural Rebel. He has consulted on land-restoration projects in many countries, particularly on water retention landscapes. His earthworks repeatedly brought him into conflict with Austrian agricultural authorities, and he was fined on several occasions for unpermitted ponds and terraces, which he publicised as evidence of bureaucratic hostility to ecological farming.
