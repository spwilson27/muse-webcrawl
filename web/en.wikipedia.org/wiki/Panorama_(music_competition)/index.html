Panorama is an annual music competition for steelbands held in Trinidad and Tobago during the Carnival season. It is regarded as the premier steelpan competition in the world and the centrepiece of the "World Steelband Festival" atmosphere surrounding Carnival. Bands compete in categories by size, culminating in the Large Band finals at the Queen's Park Savannah in Port of Spain.

History

The first Panorama competition was held in 1963, organised as steelbands grew from their roots in the tamboo bamboo and dustbin bands of early 20th-century Port of Spain. The competition helped transform the steelband from a street procession ensemble into a full orchestra capable of performing complex arranged calypso and even classical repertoire. Over the decades Panorama became the main stage on which arrangers — the creative leaders of the bands — built their reputations.

Format

Steel orchestras, some with more than one hundred players for large-band competition, perform an arrangement of a calypso or soca tune of the season. Preliminary rounds are held in panyards and regional venues, followed by semifinals and the finals, traditionally staged on the Saturday night before Carnival Monday at the Savannah. Performances are judged on arrangement, general performance, tone and rhythm, with a maximum of 500 points available. Each band's music is shaped by an arranger, celebrated figures including Ray Holman, Len "Boogsie" Sharpe, and Clive Bradley.

Successful bands

The most successful large bands in Panorama history include Renegades, Desperadoes, Exodus, Trinidad All Stars and Phase II Pan Groove. Renegades and Desperadoes have each won the title numerous times, and their rivalry is one of the great storylines of Carnival. Winning Panorama is the most prestigious achievement in the steelband movement, bringing prize money, sponsorship and lasting fame to the band and its arranger.
