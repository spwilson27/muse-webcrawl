Widgery Tribunal

The Widgery Tribunal was a public inquiry held in 1972 into the events of Bloody Sunday in Derry, Northern Ireland, when British soldiers shot 26 unarmed civilians during a civil rights march on 30 January 1972, killing 14. The tribunal was chaired by Lord Widgery, the Lord Chief Justice of England.

Findings

Widgery's report, published in April 1972 after an inquiry lasting only a few weeks, largely exonerated the soldiers, accepting that some had fired in response to perceived threats while describing some of the firing as "bordering on the reckless." It cast doubt on the innocence of the victims, suggesting some had handled weapons or nail bombs — claims widely rejected by the victims' families and the nationalist community.

Legacy

The report was long regarded in Ireland as a whitewash. A second, far more thorough inquiry — the Saville Inquiry, reporting in 2010 — concluded that the killings were "unjustified and unjustifiable," leading Prime Minister David Cameron to issue a formal apology.
