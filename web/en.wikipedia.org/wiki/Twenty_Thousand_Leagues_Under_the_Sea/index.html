Twenty Thousand Leagues Under the Sea (French: Vingt mille lieues sous les mers) is a classic science fiction adventure novel by French writer Jules Verne. Serialised in 1869–1870, it recounts the voyage of the submarine Nautilus, commanded by the enigmatic Captain Nemo, as experienced by the French marine biologist Professor Pierre Aronnax, who joins the vessel after being taken aboard with his servant Conseil and the harpooner Ned Land.

Plot

In 1866, reports of a gigantic sea creature damaging ships prompt an American expedition aboard the frigate Abraham Lincoln to hunt it. The "creature" proves to be a submarine; Aronnax, Conseil, and Ned Land are swept aboard and meet Captain Nemo, who has renounced life on land. Over a journey of some 20,000 leagues — a distance, not a depth — the Nautilus visits the pearl fisheries of Ceylon, the lost city of Atlantis, the South Pole, and the wrecks of Vigo Bay; the travellers fight a giant squid and witness Nemo's vengeful sinking of a warship. The three prisoners eventually escape in a whirlpool off Norway, leaving Nemo's fate unknown.

Themes and influence

The novel combines meticulous marine science with speculation on electric power, deep-sea exploration, and political rebellion; Nemo's backstory as an Indian prince fighting British colonialism was softened at the publisher's insistence. The work has never been out of print, inspired Disney's 1954 film, and helped define the popular image of the submarine decades before such vessels became practicable. A league is about three miles, so the title's distance equals roughly twice the Earth's circumference.
