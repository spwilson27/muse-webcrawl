AcronymAWB
TypeContract of carriage / receipt for goods
Issued byAirline or freight forwarder
Format11-digit number (3-digit airline prefix + 8-digit serial)
Legal basisWarsaw Convention / Montreal Convention

An air waybill (AWB) is a receipt issued by an international airline for goods carried by air, and evidence of the contract of carriage between the shipper and the carrier. It is the principal document of international air freight, serving as a contract, a receipt, handling instructions and a customs document — though, unlike a bill of lading in sea transport, it is not a document of title and is non-negotiable.

Contents

The AWB records the shipper and consignee, the airports of departure and destination, the number of pieces, weight and dimensions, a description of the goods, freight charges and any declared value for carriage or customs. It carries an 11-digit number: a three-digit airline prefix followed by an eight-digit serial number, used to track the shipment.

Master and house waybills

When a freight forwarder consolidates several shipments, the airline issues a master air waybill (MAWB) to the forwarder, while the forwarder issues house air waybills (HAWB) to each individual shipper. International carriage under an AWB is governed by the Warsaw and Montreal Conventions, which set carrier liability limits.

Electronic air waybill

The industry association IATA has promoted the e-AWB, an electronic contract of carriage replacing the paper document, which became the default contract for enabled trade lanes from 2019, reducing processing time and errors.
