Bolsa Família

Bolsa Família (Portuguese for "Family Allowance") is a Brazilian social welfare programme of conditional cash transfers. Created in 2003 under President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva by merging several earlier transfer programmes, it pays monthly cash benefits to poor families provided they meet conditions such as keeping children in school and following vaccination and health-check schedules. It became one of the largest conditional cash transfer programmes in the world and a model widely studied by development economists.

Design

Eligibility is determined by per-capita household income registered in the Cadastro Único, the federal registry of low-income families. Payments are made principally to the woman of the household through the Caixa Econômica Federal bank, on the reasoning that money controlled by mothers is more likely to be spent on children. Conditionalities include school attendance of at least 85% for children, up-to-date vaccinations, and prenatal care for pregnant women; non-compliance leads first to warnings and eventually to suspension of the benefit.

History

The programme unified Bolsa Escola, Bolsa Alimentação, Cartão Alimentação and Auxílio Gás, programmes launched in the 1990s and under President Fernando Henrique Cardoso. It expanded rapidly to reach more than 13 million families — roughly a quarter of Brazil's population. In 2021 the Bolsonaro government replaced it with Auxílio Brasil; in 2023 the returning Lula government restored the Bolsa Família name and set a minimum benefit of R$600 per family plus supplements per child.

Impact

Studies credit Bolsa Família with a significant share of Brazil's reduction in extreme poverty and inequality during the 2000s, improved school enrolment and child health, and a sizeable economic multiplier in poor municipalities, all at a cost of roughly 0.5% of GDP. Critics have debated its effects on labour supply and its exposure to political manipulation, though research generally finds small disincentive effects.
