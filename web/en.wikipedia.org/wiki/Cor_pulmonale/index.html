Cor pulmonale

DefinitionEnlargement/dysfunction of the right ventricle due to lung disease

Cor pulmonale is the enlargement and eventual failure of the right ventricle of the heart as a response to increased vascular resistance or high blood pressure in the lungs (pulmonary hypertension). The term combines the Latin cor (heart) with pulmonale (of the lungs).

Causes

Chronic cor pulmonale most commonly results from chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), in which chronic hypoxia causes pulmonary vasoconstriction and remodelling of the pulmonary arteries. Other causes include interstitial lung disease, obstructive sleep apnoea, recurrent pulmonary embolism, and living at high altitude; acute cor pulmonale is usually caused by a massive pulmonary embolism that suddenly obstructs pulmonary blood flow.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms include progressive breathlessness, fatigue, exertional syncope, chest pain and swelling of the ankles (peripheral oedema). Signs include a raised jugular venous pressure, a right ventricular heave, a loud pulmonary component of the second heart sound, hepatomegaly and ascites in advanced disease. Blood tests may show polycythaemia from chronic hypoxaemia.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on the clinical picture, electrocardiography (right axis deviation, P pulmonale), chest imaging and echocardiography, which can demonstrate right ventricular dilation and estimate pulmonary artery pressures; right heart catheterisation is the definitive test for pulmonary hypertension. Treatment is directed at the underlying lung disease: long-term oxygen therapy is the intervention shown to improve survival in hypoxaemic COPD, alongside smoking cessation, diuretics for fluid overload, and anticoagulation or thrombolysis for acute embolic causes.
