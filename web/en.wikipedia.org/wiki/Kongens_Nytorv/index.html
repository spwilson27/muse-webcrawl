Kongens Nytorv (literally "The King's New Square") is a public square in central Copenhagen, Denmark, the largest square in the city. Laid out by Christian V in about 1670 as the centrepiece of the extension of the fortified city, it was modelled on the royal squares of Paris, with an equestrian statue of the king at its centre and streets radiating from it.

History

The site had previously been a rough open space, the Hallandsås, just inside the city's eastern rampart. After the ramparts were moved, Christian V had the square laid out as a place royale; in 1688 a gilded lead equestrian statue of Christian V by Abraham-César Lamoureux was erected at its centre (the present bronze copy dates from 1946). The square's Krinsen — an ellipse of trees in the French baroque style — surrounds the statue. The area around the square long marked the boundary between Copenhagen's fashionable district and its rougher quarters.

Buildings

Landmark buildings around the square include Charlottenborg Palace (1672–1683), now home to the Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts; the Royal Danish Theatre (1874); the Hotel d'Angleterre; the Magasin du Nord department store; and the Thott Mansion, now the French Embassy. The Nyhavn canal extends from the eastern side of the square.

Use

Kongens Nytorv is a major transport hub, served by a station on the Copenhagen Metro's City Circle Line (opened 2019). Traditions attached to the square include newly graduated students dancing around the equestrian statue each summer, and it is the city's traditional site for celebrations and demonstrations.
