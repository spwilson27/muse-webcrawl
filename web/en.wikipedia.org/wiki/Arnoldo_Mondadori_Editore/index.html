Arnoldo Mondadori Editore S.p.A., known as Mondadori Group, is an Italian publishing company founded in 1907 in Ostiglia by Arnoldo Mondadori. It is the largest publishing house in Italy, active in books (through imprints including Mondadori, Einaudi, Piemme, Sperling & Kupfer and Rizzoli), magazines, and book retailing through the Mondadori Store network.

History

Arnoldo Mondadori began as a printer in Ostiglia and moved into publishing school texts and children's books before relocating to Milan. In the 1920s and 1930s Mondadori launched popular magazine and book series, published the Italian editions of Walt Disney comics (including Topolino, the Italian Mickey Mouse magazine, from 1935), and founded the Oscar Mondadori paperback series in 1965, which revolutionized the Italian book market with cheap, high-quality paperbacks sold at newsstands.

After Arnoldo's death in 1971, the company passed through family disputes and corporate battles, including the "Segrate war" (guerra di Segrate) of 1989–1991 between Silvio Berlusconi's Fininvest and Carlo De Benedetti's CIR over control of the publisher, resolved when Fininvest retained Mondadori. The group later acquired Rizzoli Libri (2016), consolidating its position as Italy's largest trade publisher.

Imprints and activities

Book imprints include Mondadori, Giulio Einaudi Editore, Piemme, Sperling & Kupfer, Rizzoli, Fabbri, BUR and Mondadori Electa. Magazine titles historically included Panorama, Epoca, Donna Moderna, Grazia, Chi and TV Sorrisi e Canzoni. The group's striking Segrate headquarters was designed by Brazilian architect Oscar Niemeyer and completed in the 1970s.
