Adolph Strecker

Adolph Strecker
Born: 21 October 1822, Darmstadt, Germany
Died: 7 November 1871 (aged 49), Würzburg, Germany
Known for: Strecker synthesis, Strecker degradation
Fields: Organic chemistry

Adolph Strecker (21 October 1822 – 7 November 1871) was a German chemist who is chiefly remembered for his work on amino acids, including the Strecker synthesis, the first laboratory synthesis of an amino acid.

Life

Strecker was born in Darmstadt and studied at the University of Giessen, where he worked under Justus von Liebig and received his doctorate in 1842. After teaching at the gymnasium in Darmstadt, he became a professor at the University of Christiania (now Oslo) in Norway in 1851, before returning to Germany as professor at Tübingen in 1860 and then Würzburg in 1870. He died in Würzburg in 1871.

Work

In 1850 Strecker showed that treating acetaldehyde with ammonia and hydrocyanic acid, followed by hydrolysis, yields alanine — the reaction now bearing his name and the first rational synthesis of an α-amino acid. He later worked out the relationship between amino acids and other natural products, and in a transformation now called the Strecker degradation he showed how amino acids react with dicarbonyl compounds to give aldehydes shortened by one carbon, a reaction important in food chemistry and the Maillard reaction. Strecker also investigated organometallic compounds, the chemistry of bile acids, and the constitution of numerous natural substances, and he estimated atomic weights and analysed minerals.

Legacy

The Strecker synthesis remains a standard textbook method for preparing α-amino acids and is still used industrially and in prebiotic chemistry studies, where it figures in discussions of how amino acids might have formed on the early Earth.
