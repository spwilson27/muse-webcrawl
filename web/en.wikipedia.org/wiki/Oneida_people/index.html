The Oneida people (Oneida: Onʌyoteˀa·ká·, "People of the Upright Stone", or "standing rock") are a Native American people and one of the five founding nations of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) Confederacy in the area of present-day New York State. Their historic territory centred on Oneida Lake and Oneida Creek.

History

The Oneida joined the Iroquois League as one of its original five nations, positioned as "younger brothers" in the confederacy's kinship structure. During the American Revolutionary War, the Oneida (with the Tuscarora) largely sided with the American colonists — providing warriors, scouts and food — in contrast to most other Haudenosaunee nations, a service recognised in the 1794 Treaty of Canandaigua, which guaranteed their lands.

Removal and present communities

Despite treaty guarantees, state land deals shrank the New York homeland drastically in the early 19th century, and many Oneida removed to Wisconsin and to Ontario. Today the principal Oneida governments are the Oneida Indian Nation of New York, the Oneida Nation of Wisconsin, and the Oneida Nation of the Thames in Ontario. In the 21st century the New York nation's land-into-trust claims were substantially resolved, and its enterprises (including the Turning Stone Resort Casino) fund government programmes.

Language and culture

The Oneida language, a Northern Iroquoian language, is critically endangered but is the subject of active revitalisation programmes in all three communities. Traditional social organisation is matrilineal, with Wolf, Turtle and Bear clans.
