Basque pelota

Basque pelota (Basque: pilota; Spanish: pelota vasca) is the name for a variety of court sports played with a ball using one's hand, a racket, a wooden bat or a basket, against a wall (a frontón) or, in some versions, with two teams facing each other separated by a line on the ground or a net. It is native to the Basque Country of northern Spain and southwestern France.

Variants

The principal variants include hand-pelota (bare hand), pala (wooden bat), remonte and cesta punta (jai alai), in which the ball is caught and hurled with a curved wicker basket called a xistera. Cesta punta is among the fastest sports in the world, with the hard rubber ball reaching speeds above 300 km/h. Games are played on courts of several forms: the one-walled fronton, the enclosed trinquet, and the open plaza.

History and spread

Pelota descends from older European handball games and took its modern form in the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries. Basque emigrants carried it to Latin America and the United States, where jai alai became a major betting sport in Florida. Pelota was an official event at the 1900 Paris Olympics and has appeared as a demonstration sport several times since; the International Federation of Basque Pelota governs world championships.
