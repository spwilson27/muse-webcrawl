Construction of the real numbers

In mathematics, there are several standard ways to define the real numbers rigorously starting from the rational numbers. The two classical constructions are Dedekind cuts and Cauchy sequences, both published in 1872, during the arithmetization of analysis.

Dedekind cuts

A Dedekind cut (Richard Dedekind, 1872) is a partition of the rationals into two non-empty sets A and B such that every element of A is less than every element of B, with A having no greatest element. Each real number is identified with such a cut; √2, for example, is the cut {q ∈ ℚ : q < 0 or q² < 2} versus its complement.

Cauchy sequences

A Cauchy sequence (Georg Cantor, 1872) construction considers sequences of rationals whose terms eventually become arbitrarily close together. Two sequences are equivalent when their difference tends to zero; the real numbers are the equivalence classes. The metric space of rationals is thus completed.

Axiomatic approach

Alternatively, the reals can be characterized axiomatically as the unique (up to isomorphism) complete ordered field. All constructions satisfy these axioms and are mutually isomorphic.
