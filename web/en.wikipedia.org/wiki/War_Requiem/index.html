War Requiem

ComposerBenjamin Britten
OpusOp. 66
TextLatin Mass for the Dead; poems by Wilfred Owen
Composed1961–62
Premiere30 May 1962, Coventry Cathedral

The War Requiem, Op. 66, is a choral work with soloists and orchestra by the British composer Benjamin Britten, composed in 1961–62 for the consecration of the new Coventry Cathedral, which had been built to replace the medieval cathedral destroyed by German bombing in November 1940. It is a setting of the Latin Mass for the Dead (Requiem) interspersed with nine poems by the English war poet Wilfred Owen, who was killed in action a week before the Armistice of 1918.

Structure

The work is scored for soprano, tenor and baritone soloists, chorus, boys' choir, organ, and two orchestras: a full orchestra and a chamber orchestra. The tenor and baritone represent two soldiers; the soprano and chorus sing the liturgical Latin text, while the boys' choir, placed at a distance, sings further liturgical passages. The six movements are Requiem aeternam, Dies irae, Offertorium, Sanctus, Agnus Dei and Libera me.

Premiere and reception

The premiere took place on 30 May 1962 in Coventry Cathedral, conducted by Meredith Davies and the composer. The soloists were Heather Harper, Peter Pears and Dietrich Fischer-Dieskau, representing respectively the British, and, in Fischer-Dieskau's case, German traditions, in a deliberate gesture of post-war reconciliation. The work was an immediate critical and popular success; its 1963 recording sold exceptionally well for a classical work.

Significance

The War Requiem is widely regarded as one of the masterpieces of twentieth-century choral music and as a powerful anti-war statement. Britten, a lifelong pacifist and conscientious objector, placed Owen's ironic, bitter poems against the traditional liturgy so that, in Owen's phrase, "the poetry is in the pity."
