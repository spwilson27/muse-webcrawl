Levi Eshkol (Hebrew: לֵוִי אֶשְׁכּוֹל; born Levi Shkolnik, 25 October 1895 – 26 February 1969) was an Israeli statesman who served as the third Prime Minister of Israel from 1963 until his death in 1969. He was the first Israeli prime minister to die in office.

Early life

Eshkol was born in the shtetl of Oratov near Kyiv in the Russian Empire. He immigrated to Ottoman Palestine in 1914 as part of the Second Aliyah, worked in agriculture, and was among the founders of kibbutz Degania Bet. He rose through the Histadrut labour federation and the Haganah, and directed the Mekorot water company, overseeing early national water planning.

Ministerial career

After independence, Eshkol served as Minister of Agriculture and then, from 1952 to 1963, as Minister of Finance, presiding over rapid economic growth, the reparations agreement with West Germany, and the construction of the National Water Carrier, completed in 1964.

Prime Minister

Succeeding David Ben-Gurion in 1963, Eshkol improved relations with the United States, establishing the strategic partnership that supplied Israel with American weapons, and allowed the remains of Ze'ev Jabotinsky to be reburied in Israel. Under public pressure during the crisis preceding the Six-Day War in 1967, he formed a national unity government and ceded the defence portfolio to Moshe Dayan. Israel's victory in the war was followed by the challenges of holding the captured territories. Eshkol died of a heart attack in February 1969 and was succeeded by Golda Meir.
