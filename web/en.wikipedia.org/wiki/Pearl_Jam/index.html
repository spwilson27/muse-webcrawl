Pearl Jam is an American rock band formed in Seattle, Washington, in 1990. The band's debut album, Ten (1991), made Pearl Jam a central act of the grunge movement alongside Nirvana, Soundgarden and Alice in Chains. Pearl Jam is the only one of the four to have remained continuously active, and is considered one of the most influential bands of the 1990s, with more than 85 million albums sold worldwide.

History

Guitarist Stone Gossard and bassist Jeff Ament, veterans of Green River and Mother Love Bone, recruited lead guitarist Mike McCready and San Diego singer Eddie Vedder, who added vocals to the band's demo (the "Mamasan" tape). Drummer Dave Krusen completed the line-up; the drum chair later passed through Matt Chamberlain, Dave Abbruzzese and Jack Irons to Matt Cameron, who joined in 1998 and also played in Soundgarden.

Ten, named after basketball player Mookie Blaylock's jersey number, yielded the singles "Alive", "Even Flow" and "Jeremy" and eventually outsold Nevermind in the US. Vs. (1993) set a record for first-week sales. In 1994 the band famously sued Ticketmaster over service fees and refused to make music videos for several years. Later work includes Vitalogy (1994), Yield (1998), Backspacer (2009), Gigaton (2020) and Dark Matter (2024). Pearl Jam was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2017.
