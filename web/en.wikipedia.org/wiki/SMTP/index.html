SMTP

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
SMTP
Full nameSimple Mail Transfer Protocol
Port25 (relay); 587 (submission)
StandardRFC 5321 (2008); originally RFC 821 (1982)
[Image: Diagram of an email's journey: sender's client to SMTP server, across the Internet to the recipient's mail server]

The Simple Mail Transfer Protocol (SMTP) is an Internet standard communication protocol for electronic mail transmission. Mail servers and other message transfer agents use SMTP to send and receive mail messages; user-level clients typically use it only for sending, retrieving mail with IMAP or POP3 instead.

SMTP was defined by Jon Postel in 1982 (RFC 821), growing out of ARPANET mail protocols of the 1970s. It is a text-based, conversational protocol: the sending server greets with HELO (or EHLO in the extended ESMTP), names the sender and recipients, transmits the message, and the receiving server accepts it for relay or delivery. Messages then hop between servers, each adding a 'Received:' header, until they reach the destination domain found through DNS MX records.

Because SMTP was designed when the network's users were trusted researchers, it originally had no authentication and no encryption — gaps that made spam and spoofing endemic and were later patched by additions such as SMTP AUTH, STARTTLS encryption, and the SPF, DKIM and DMARC anti-forgery frameworks. Despite countless predictions of email's death, SMTP remains the universal, federated backbone of electronic mail, carrying billions of messages a day between independently operated systems.
