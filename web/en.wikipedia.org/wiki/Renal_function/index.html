Renal function

Renal function, in nephrology, is an indication of the kidney's condition and its role in renal physiology. The kidneys filter blood, excrete waste products such as urea and creatinine, regulate fluid and electrolyte balance, control blood pressure, and produce hormones including erythropoietin.

Glomerular filtration rate

The glomerular filtration rate (GFR) describes the flow rate of filtered fluid through the kidney and is the most commonly used index of renal function. A normal GFR in young adults is approximately 90–120 mL/min/1.73 m². GFR can be measured directly using exogenous markers such as inulin or iohexol, or estimated (eGFR) from serum creatinine using equations such as the CKD-EPI formula, which account for age, sex, and sometimes race.

Assessment

Clinical assessment of renal function includes measurement of blood urea nitrogen (BUN), serum creatinine, cystatin C, electrolytes, and urinalysis. Reduced renal function is classified as acute kidney injury (AKI) when it develops rapidly, or chronic kidney disease (CKD) when it persists for more than three months. CKD is staged G1–G5 according to GFR, with stage G5 (GFR below 15) representing kidney failure, which may require dialysis or transplantation.

Other tests

Creatinine clearance, measured from timed urine collections, approximates GFR but overestimates it because of tubular secretion of creatinine. Renal plasma flow can be estimated from para-aminohippurate (PAH) clearance.
