The lux (symbol: lx) is the SI unit of illuminance — the amount of luminous flux incident on a surface per unit area. One lux equals one lumen per square metre: 1 lx = 1 lm/m². It measures how brightly a surface is lit, weighted by the sensitivity of the human eye.

Definition and relation to the lumen

Illuminance (in lux) equals luminous flux (in lumens, the unit of total light output of a source) divided by the area over which that flux is spread. Thus the same lamp gives a high lux reading close to it and a low one far away: for a point source, illuminance follows an inverse-square law, E = I/d², where I is the luminous intensity in candelas.

Typical values

  • Full daylight (not direct sun): 10,000–25,000 lx
  • Direct sunlight: 32,000–100,000 lx
  • Overcast day: ~1,000 lx
  • Office lighting: 320–500 lx
  • Full moon on a clear night: 0.05–0.3 lx
  • Starlight: ~0.0001 lx

Measurement and use

Illuminance is measured with a lux meter (a light meter with a photopically corrected sensor). Building codes and occupational standards specify required lux levels for workplaces, streets, sports venues, and emergency lighting. In photography and cinematography, exposure decisions are tied to incident illuminance, and camera exposure values can be converted to lux.
