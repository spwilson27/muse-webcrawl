Guy Laliberté (born 2 September 1959) is a Canadian billionaire businessman and poker player. He is the founder and former chief executive of Cirque du Soleil, the Montreal-based entertainment company that grew from a troupe of street performers into the world's largest contemporary circus producer.

Laliberté, founder of Cirque du Soleil

Career

Laliberté began as a street performer — accordionist, stilt-walker and fire-breather — travelling in Europe. In 1984, with support from the Quebec government for the 450th anniversary of Jacques Cartier's arrival, he co-founded Cirque du Soleil in Baie-Saint-Paul. The company reinvented circus arts by combining acrobatics, theatre, dance and original music without animal acts, and expanded to resident shows in Las Vegas and touring productions worldwide. Laliberté sold most of his stake in a 2015 transaction valuing the company at about US$1.5 billion and retained a minority interest.

In September–October 2009 Laliberté flew to the International Space Station aboard Soyuz TMA-16 as a spaceflight participant, describing his mission as a "poetic social mission" to raise awareness of water issues through his One Drop Foundation, which works on access to safe water. He is also a high-stakes poker player who appeared on televised cash games and organised the Big One for One Drop charity tournament.
