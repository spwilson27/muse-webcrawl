Theodor Detmers

Theodor Detmers (22 August 1902 – 4 November 1976) was a German naval officer who commanded the auxiliary cruiser (merchant raider) Kormoran during the Second World War. He is best known for the battle of 19 November 1941 in which Kormoran sank the Australian light cruiser HMAS Sydney off Western Australia — and was itself sunk in return.

The Sydney action

Disguised as the Dutch merchantman Straat Malakka, Kormoran was challenged by Sydney, which closed to close range before demanding the raider's secret call sign. Detmers ordered his ship's camouflage dropped and opened fire, fatally damaging the cruiser, which sank with all 645 hands — Australia's greatest naval disaster. Kormoran, itself ablaze, was scuttled; Detmers and most of his crew survived and were taken to Australia as prisoners of war.

Later life

Held at Dhurringile and other camps, Detmers took part in escape planning, including the celebrated plan involving forged documents and a proposed escape attempt in 1945. Repatriated to Germany in 1947, he published a memoir of the Kormoran cruise and lived in Hamburg until his death in 1976.
