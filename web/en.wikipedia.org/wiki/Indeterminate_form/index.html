Indeterminate form

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In calculus and mathematical analysis, an indeterminate form is an algebraic expression obtained when evaluating a limit by substituting the limiting values of its components, where the resulting expression does not by itself determine the limit's value. The classic example is 0/0: if f(x) and g(x) both approach 0, the ratio f(x)/g(x) may approach any value, a finite constant, +∞ or −∞, or fail to converge at all.

The seven forms

0/0    ∞/∞    0⋅∞    ∞−∞    00    1∞    ∞0

These seven expressions are called indeterminate. By contrast, forms such as 1/0 or 0∞ are determinate in the sense that their limiting behaviour is fixed. Indeterminacy is a property of the limiting process, not of arithmetic: the symbol 0/0 is meaningless as a number, and the issue is what the underlying functions do near the limit point.

Evaluation

The principal tool for resolving indeterminate forms of type 0/0 or ∞/∞ is L'Hôpital's rule, which replaces the ratio of the functions with the ratio of their derivatives. Other forms are usually converted — by taking logarithms, rewriting products as quotients, or combining fractions — into the basic quotient forms. Algebraic simplification and series expansion are also standard techniques.
