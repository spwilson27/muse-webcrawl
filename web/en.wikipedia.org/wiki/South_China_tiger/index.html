South China tiger

South China tiger
Scientific name: Panthera tigris amoyensis
Status: Critically endangered; possibly extinct in the wild
Native range: Central and southern China

The South China tiger (Panthera tigris amoyensis) is a tiger subspecies native to central and southern China. It is considered one of the most endangered tiger populations: it has not been confirmed in the wild for decades and is regarded by many experts as functionally extinct in the wild.

Characteristics

It is one of the smaller tiger subspecies, with males weighing roughly 130–175 kg. Its coat is a rich orange with comparatively wide-spaced, often diamond-shaped stripes. The name amoyensis refers to Amoy (Xiamen), near where the type specimen originated.

Decline and conservation

In the 1950s the population was estimated at about 4,000, but tigers were declared pests and hunted on a large scale during government eradication campaigns; habitat loss and poaching compounded the decline. The captive population, descended from only a handful of founders, numbers around a hundred animals in Chinese zoos and breeding centres. A rewilding project in South Africa (the "Save China's Tigers" programme at Laohu Valley Reserve) trained captive-born cubs for eventual release in China, though no release has taken place.
