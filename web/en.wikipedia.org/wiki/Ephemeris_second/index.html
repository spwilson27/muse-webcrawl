Ephemeris second

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The ephemeris second was the unit of time under the standard of ephemeris time (ET). It was defined by the International Astronomical Union in 1952 and adopted as the SI second in 1960: the fraction 1/31,556,925.9747 of the tropical year for 1900 January 0 at 12 hours ephemeris time — that is, a fraction of the time it took the Earth to orbit the Sun as observed at the beginning of the year 1900.

Realization

Because the definition referred to the Earth's orbital motion, the ephemeris second was in practice realized by observations of the Moon's position against the stars, using tables by Simon Newcomb and later corrections. Measurements led by the U.S. Naval Observatory and the National Physical Laboratory determined the frequency of the caesium standard in terms of the ephemeris second.

Replacement

In 1967 the ephemeris second was replaced as the SI definition of the second by the atomic second, defined as 9,192,631,770 periods of the radiation corresponding to the transition between two hyperfine levels of caesium-133. The atomic value was chosen to match the ephemeris second as closely as it could be measured.
