Formula

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A formula is a concise way of expressing information symbolically, as in a mathematical formula or a chemical formula. In mathematics, a formula is an entity constructed using symbols and formation rules of a given logical language; in science, a formula is a mathematical relationship or rule expressed in symbols, such as E = mc2 or the quadratic formula.

Mathematical formulas

Mathematical formulas express relationships between quantities. Some, such as the Pythagorean theorem a2 + b2 = c2, are theorems; others are definitions or empirical relationships. Formulas may be manipulated algebraically to solve for unknown variables.

Chemical formulas

In chemistry, a formula expresses the proportions of atoms in a compound: an empirical formula gives the simplest whole-number ratio, while a molecular formula gives the exact numbers of each atom, e.g. H2O for water and C6H12O6 for glucose.

Other uses

The word also denotes a fixed method or recipe, a set of regulations (as in Formula One motor racing), and infant formula in nutrition.
