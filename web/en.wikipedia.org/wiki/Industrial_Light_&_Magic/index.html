Industrial Light & Magic (ILM)
Founded: 1975
Founder: George Lucas
Headquarters: San Francisco, California
Parent: Lucasfilm (Disney)

Industrial Light & Magic (ILM) is an American motion picture visual effects company founded by George Lucas in 1975. Since its earliest work on the original Star Wars (1977), ILM has been one of the most influential effects houses in cinema history, pioneering techniques from motion-control photography to fully computer-generated characters.

ILM has contributed effects to hundreds of feature films.

History

When Lucas began production of Star Wars, he found that the in-house effects unit at 20th Century Fox had been dismantled, so he assembled a new team in a Van Nuys, California warehouse under supervisor John Dykstra. Among their innovations was the Dykstraflex motion-control camera system, which allowed repeatable, computer-controlled camera moves around miniatures. ILM later relocated to Marin County and then to the Presidio of San Francisco.

In the digital era ILM created landmarks such as the water pseudopod in The Abyss (1989), the liquid-metal T-1000 in Terminator 2: Judgment Day (1991), the dinosaurs of Jurassic Park (1993), and performance-captured characters in the Pirates of the Caribbean and Avengers films. More recently it developed StageCraft, the LED-volume virtual production system used on The Mandalorian. Following Disney's acquisition of Lucasfilm in 2012, ILM became part of the Walt Disney Studios, with studios in San Francisco, Vancouver, London, Singapore, Sydney and Mumbai.

Accolades

ILM artists have won numerous Academy Awards for Best Visual Effects, and the company is regularly cited as the most decorated effects house in the industry.
