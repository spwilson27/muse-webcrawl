The Kyiv Pechersk Lavra (Ukrainian: Києво-Печерська лавра), also known as the Kyiv Monastery of the Caves, is a historic Eastern Orthodox monastery complex in Kyiv, Ukraine, founded in 1051. Spread over the hills above the Dnipro River, it is one of the most important centres of Orthodox Christianity in Eastern Europe and, together with Saint Sophia Cathedral, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The monastery began when the monk Anthony settled in a cave on Berestov Mount, joined by Theodosius, who organised the growing community. Its name derives from pechera, "cave": the Near and Far Caves contain the relics of numerous saints and served as burial places and hermitages. The main church, the Dormition Cathedral, was built in 1073–1089, blown up in 1941 and rebuilt in 2000.

Complex

The Lavra is divided into the Upper Lavra, now largely a state museum reserve, and the Lower Lavra, which houses an active monastery, theological academy and the cave labyrinths. Landmarks include the Great Lavra Bell Tower (1745), the Trinity Gate Church and the Church of the Saviour at Berestove.
