Relativism

Relativism
FieldPhilosophy, anthropology
Key figuresProtagoras, Franz Boas, Thomas Kuhn

Relativism is a family of philosophical views which deny claims to objectivity within a particular domain and assert that facts in that domain are relative to the perspective of an observer or the context in which they are assessed. There are many different forms of relativism, with a great deal of variation in scope and differing degrees of controversy among them.

Forms

Moral relativism holds that moral judgements are true or false only relative to a particular standpoint (for example, a culture or historical period). Cultural relativism, influential in anthropology through Franz Boas and his students, is the methodological principle that a culture's beliefs and practices should be understood in their own terms. Epistemic relativism concerns truth and justification, and is associated with debates raised by Thomas Kuhn's account of scientific paradigms. Linguistic relativity (the Sapir–Whorf hypothesis) proposes that language shapes thought.

Criticism

Critics charge that strong relativism is self-refuting — the claim "all truth is relative" would itself be only relatively true — and that moral relativism undermines criticism of harmful practices. Defenders reply that relativism is often confused with mere subjectivism, and that weaker, domain-specific forms avoid these problems. The ancient sophist Protagoras's dictum "man is the measure of all things" is often cited as an early statement, criticised by Plato in the Theaetetus.
