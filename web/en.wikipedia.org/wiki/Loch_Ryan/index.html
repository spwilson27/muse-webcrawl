Loch Ryan

Loch Ryan (Scottish Gaelic: Loch Rìoghain) is a sea loch in Dumfries and Galloway, south-west Scotland. It opens from the North Channel of the Irish Sea and runs about 13 kilometres (8 miles) inland, forming a sheltered natural harbour with the town of Stranraer at its head.

Ferries and ports

Because it provides the shortest sea crossing from Scotland to Northern Ireland, Loch Ryan has long been a ferry route. For much of the modern era ferries sailed from Stranraer to Belfast and Larne; in 2011 the Stranraer services were relocated to a new port at Old House Point near Cairnryan, on the loch's eastern shore, from where ferries to Belfast continue to operate, alongside the long-established Cairnryan–Larne route. The villages of Cairnryan and Kirkcolm lie on the loch's shores.

History

During the Second World War the loch was an important military anchorage: flying boats operated from it, surrendered German U-boats were assembled at the end of the war before being towed out and scuttled in Operation Deadlight, and the shoreline retains wartime structures. The loch also supports oyster and shellfish beds, and its shores include the Wig Bay nature reserve, noted for wintering wildfowl.
