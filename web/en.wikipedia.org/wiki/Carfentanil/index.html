Carfentanil or carfentanyl (R-33799) is a synthetic opioid analgesic that is an analogue of fentanyl. It is approximately 10,000 times more potent than morphine and 100 times more potent than fentanyl, making it one of the most potent opioids known. It was first synthesised in 1974 by a team of chemists at Janssen Pharmaceutica led by Paul Janssen.

Uses

Carfentanil is marketed under the trade name Wildnil as a general anaesthetic and tranquilliser for large animals such as elephants and rhinoceroses; a dose of a few milligrams can immobilise an elephant. It is not approved for human use. Its antagonist for veterinary reversal is naltrexone or diprenorphine.

Illicit use and hazards

Since around 2016 carfentanil has appeared in the illicit drug supply, mixed into or sold as heroin, contributing to clusters of fatal overdoses in North America and Europe; quantities as small as a few milligrams can be lethal, and standard naloxone doses may be insufficient, requiring repeated administration. It has also been investigated as a chemical incapacitant; an aerosolised fentanyl analogue is widely believed to have been used by Russian security forces during the 2002 Moscow theatre siege.
