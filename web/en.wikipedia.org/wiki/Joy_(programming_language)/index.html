Joy (programming language)

Joy is a functional programming language created by Manfred von Thun of La Trobe University in Melbourne, Australia. It is a concatenative language: programs are built by composing functions that operate on a stack, and — unusually — it dispenses with the lambda calculus and variable binding altogether, defining quoted programs as first-class values.

Overview

In Joy, a program denotes a function from stacks to stacks. Values are pushed onto the stack, and operators transform it. Lists written in square brackets, such as [dup *], are quoted programs that can themselves be manipulated and executed by combinators such as map, fold and i (which executes the program on top of the stack). Because composition is simply juxtaposition, Joy belongs to the family of concatenative languages alongside Forth, though it is purely functional and has no explicit stack-shuffling words in its theoretical core.

Influence

Joy's mathematical treatment of combinators influenced later concatenative and functional languages, including Factor and Cat, and is frequently cited in discussions of point-free (tacit) programming.
