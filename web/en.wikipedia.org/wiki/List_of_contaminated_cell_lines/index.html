List of contaminated cell lines

Many cell lines that are widely used for biomedical research have been found to be contaminated and replaced by other, more aggressive cells. This has been documented for many lines, including initial isolations thought to come from specific tissues but which were actually cross-contaminated by a small number of aggressive, fast-growing lines — most famously HeLa cells, which invaded cultures in laboratories worldwide in the 1950s–1970s. The International Cell Line Authentication Committee (ICLAC) maintains a register of misidentified cell lines, which contains hundreds of entries. The following are among the best documented.

Background

Systematic testing began with the work of Stanley Gartler in the 1960s, who showed that many supposedly independent human lines shared HeLa's distinctive marker chromosomes and glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase type. Walter Nelson-Rees extended this work in the 1970s, provoking controversy but ultimately establishing cell-line authentication by DNA profiling (STR profiling) as standard practice. Misidentified lines have invalidated conclusions in thousands of published studies, and journals and funders now commonly require authentication of cell lines.
