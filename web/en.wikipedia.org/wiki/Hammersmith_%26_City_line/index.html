The Hammersmith & City line is a London Underground line that runs between Hammersmith in west London and Barking in east London. It is shown in pink on the Tube map and serves 29 stations over 15.8 miles (25.5 km). Between Farringdon and Aldgate Junction it skirts the City of London, the capital's financial heart.

The line shares much of its route with the Circle and District lines, and runs in shallow cut-and-cover tunnels just below the surface. It is operated with S7 Stock trains.

History

Much of the route was opened by the Metropolitan Railway in the 1860s, including the world's first underground railway between Paddington and Farringdon in 1863. The service was operated as part of the Metropolitan line until 1990, when it was renamed the Hammersmith & City line to reflect its separate identity.

Route

Trains run from Hammersmith via Shepherd's Bush Market, Wood Lane, Latimer Road and the stations of the former Hammersmith branch to Paddington, then share tracks through King's Cross St Pancras, Liverpool Street and Aldgate, continuing east through Whitechapel, Stepney Green, Mile End, Bow Road, Bromley-by-Bow, Plaistow and East Ham to Barking.
