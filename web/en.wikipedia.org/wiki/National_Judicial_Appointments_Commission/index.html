CountryIndia
Established byConstitution (99th Amendment) Act, 2014
StatusStruck down as unconstitutional, 16 October 2015
PurposeAppointment and transfer of judges to the higher judiciary

The National Judicial Appointments Commission (NJAC) was a proposed body to replace the collegium system for the appointment and transfer of judges to India's higher judiciary — the Supreme Court and the High Courts. It was established by the Constitution (Ninety-ninth Amendment) Act, 2014 and the accompanying National Judicial Appointments Commission Act, 2014.

Composition

The Commission was to consist of six members: the Chief Justice of India (chairperson), the two senior-most judges of the Supreme Court, the Union Minister for Law and Justice, and two "eminent persons" nominated by a committee of the Prime Minister, the Chief Justice and the Leader of the Opposition. Recommendations could be blocked if any two members disagreed.

Supreme Court verdict

On 16 October 2015, a five-judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court, in Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record Association v. Union of India, struck down the 99th Amendment and the NJAC Act by a 4–1 majority, holding that they violated the basic structure of the Constitution by impairing the independence of the judiciary and the primacy of the judiciary in appointments. Justice J. S. Khehar wrote the leading majority opinion; Justice J. Chelameswar dissented. The collegium system was revived as a result.

Debate

Supporters of the NJAC argued that the collegium was opaque and unaccountable, and that executive participation in appointments is common in other democracies. Critics argued that giving the executive and non-judicial members an effective veto threatened judicial independence, a concern the majority of the bench accepted.
