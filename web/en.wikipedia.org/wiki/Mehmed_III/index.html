Mehmed III (Ottoman Turkish: محمد ثالث, Meḥmed-i sālis; 26 May 1566 – 22 December 1603) was Sultan of the Ottoman Empire from 1595 until his death in 1603. He is remembered in Ottoman tradition for leading the army in person on the Eger campaign of 1596 — the last time an Ottoman sultan personally commanded a major campaign for more than half a century — and notoriously for the execution of his nineteen brothers upon his accession.

Early life

Mehmed was born in Manisa, where his father Murad served as provincial governor. His mother was Safiye Sultan, of Albanian origin, who remained a powerful figure at court throughout his reign. He was circumcised in 1582 in a lavish ceremony in Istanbul, and later governed Manisa himself, as was customary for Ottoman princes.

Reign

Mehmed succeeded his father on 27 January 1595. Following the precedent set by Mehmed II's law of fratricide, he had his nineteen younger brothers strangled on the night of his accession — the largest royal fratricide in Ottoman history, which caused public disquiet in the capital.

His reign coincided with the Long Turkish War (1593–1606) against the Habsburg Monarchy in Hungary. In 1596 Mehmed took the field in person; the Ottomans captured the fortress of Eger and then defeated a Habsburg–Transylvanian army at the Battle of Keresztes (Mezőkeresztes) in October 1596, a victory that secured his reputation as a warrior sultan even though the army came close to disaster during the battle. In 1600 the Ottomans took Kanizsa, whose defence by Tiryaki Hasan Pasha the following year became famous in Ottoman historiography.

The reign was troubled by the Celali rebellions in Anatolia, by military revolts in the capital (notably the sipahi uprising of 1600 and the janissary revolt of 1603), and by a costly war with Safavid Persia that began in 1603 with Shah Abbas's reconquest of Tabriz. Mehmed was deeply influenced by his mother Safiye, whose dominance of court patronage attracted criticism.

Death

Mehmed III died at Topkapı Palace on 22 December 1603, aged 37. Contemporary rumours attributed his death to grief or anxiety following the execution of his eldest son Şehzade Mahmud, whom he had ordered killed in June 1603 after suspecting him of plotting. He was succeeded by his thirteen-year-old son Ahmed I, whose accession marked the effective end of the practice of royal fratricide. Mehmed is buried in a mausoleum beside Hagia Sophia in Istanbul.
