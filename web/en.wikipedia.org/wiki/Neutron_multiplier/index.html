Neutron multiplier

A neutron multiplier is a material that increases the number of free neutrons through (n,2n) or (n,3n) reactions, in which one incoming neutron produces two or three outgoing neutrons. Neutron multiplication is used to improve the neutron economy of fission and fusion systems.

Materials

The most common multiplier materials are beryllium and lead. In beryllium-9, an (n,2n) reaction with a threshold of about 1.9 MeV splits the nucleus into two alpha particles and two neutrons. Lead isotopes have higher thresholds but are attractive in fusion blanket designs because lead is also useful as a coolant constituent in lead–lithium eutectic. Uranium-238 also multiplies fast neutrons, both by (n,2n) reactions and by fast fission.

Applications

In fusion reactors, the blanket must breed tritium from lithium, and neutron multipliers such as beryllium or lead compensate for neutrons lost to absorption and leakage, ensuring a tritium breeding ratio above one. Multipliers are also used in some fission designs and in spallation and subcritical systems to make better use of scarce neutrons.
