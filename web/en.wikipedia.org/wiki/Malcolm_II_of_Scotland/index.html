Malcolm II of Scotland

Reign: 1005 – 25 November 1034

Predecessor: Kenneth III

Successor: Duncan I

Died: 25 November 1034, Glamis

House: Alpin

Malcolm II (Scottish Gaelic: Máel Coluim mac Cináeda; c. 954 – 25 November 1034) was King of Scots from 1005 until his death. The son of Kenneth II, he succeeded after defeating and killing Kenneth III at the battle of Monzievaird. Later chroniclers gave him the epithet "the Destroyer" (Forranach).

Reign

Malcolm's reign was marked by campaigns to extend Scottish power southward. An early invasion of Northumbria ended in defeat at Durham in 1006, but after a second major campaign he won the battle of Carham in 1018, securing Lothian — the lands between the Forth and the Tweed — for Scotland. He also exerted influence over Strathclyde, where his grandson Duncan appears to have been installed as king.

His relations with England's rulers shifted with circumstance: he is reported to have submitted to Cnut the Great, who campaigned into Scotland in 1031, while also sheltering Norman and English exiles at his court in some accounts.

Succession

Having no surviving sons, Malcolm arranged the succession through his daughters: Bethóc married Crínán, abbot of Dunkeld, and their son became Duncan I; another daughter married Sigurd Hlodvirsson, Earl of Orkney, making Malcolm grandfather of Thorfinn the Mighty. By breaking the old system of alternating royal succession between lines of the royal house, Malcolm laid groundwork for the dynastic struggles — including Macbeth's reign — that followed Duncan's accession.

Malcolm died at Glamis in 1034, reportedly of wounds or violence in some later traditions, and was buried on Iona. He is generally regarded as the last king of the House of Alpin.
