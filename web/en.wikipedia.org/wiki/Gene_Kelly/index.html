Eugene Curran Kelly (August 23, 1912 – February 2, 1996) was an American dancer, actor, singer, director and choreographer who transformed the Hollywood musical. He is best remembered for An American in Paris (1951), which won the Academy Award for Best Picture, and for Singin' in the Rain (1952), whose title number — Kelly dancing joyfully through puddles — is one of cinema's most famous scenes.

Career

Kelly grew up in Pittsburgh, taught dance at his family's studios, and reached Broadway in Pal Joey (1940). Signed by Hollywood, he debuted opposite Judy Garland in For Me and My Gal (1942), then starred in Cover Girl (1944) — where he danced with his own reflection — and Anchors Aweigh (1945), dancing with the animated Jerry Mouse. With director Stanley Donen he made On the Town (1949), shot partly on location in New York, and co-directed Singin' in the Rain.

Kelly's athletic, working-man style — dancing in loafers and casual clothes, using the full frame and camera movement — contrasted with Fred Astaire's elegance and expanded what screen dance could do. He choreographed the 17-minute closing ballet of An American in Paris and directed later films including Hello, Dolly! (1969).

Legacy

Kelly received an honorary Oscar in 1952 for his versatility and contributions to film choreography, a Kennedy Center Honor and the AFI Life Achievement Award. Singin' in the Rain is routinely ranked among the greatest films ever made, and his influence runs through generations of screen dancers and music-video choreographers.
