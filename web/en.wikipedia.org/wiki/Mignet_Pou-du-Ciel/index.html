Mignet Pou-du-Ciel

The Mignet Pou-du-Ciel ("Louse of the Sky"), also known in English as the Flying Flea, is a family of tandem-wing light aircraft designed by Frenchman Henri Mignet. The original design, the HM.14 of 1933, was intended to be built and flown by amateurs with minimal experience: Mignet promoted it as an aircraft anyone could construct from his book Le Sport de l'Air.

Design

The Pou-du-Ciel uses two wings in tandem rather than a conventional wing-and-tailplane layout. The pilot controls the aircraft in pitch by tilting the entire forward wing; there is no elevator. Directional control uses a large rudder, and roll is produced by the rudder's side force acting through the design's dihedral arrangement, so the control stick works in only two axes.

History and the diving problem

The design sparked a homebuilding craze in France and Britain in 1934–1935. However, a series of fatal crashes revealed a dangerous flaw: in a shallow dive, airflow changes could make the pivoting front wing's incidence shift so that the aircraft entered an unrecoverable dive — the so-called "Pou swoop". Investigations in Britain (including wind-tunnel work at the Royal Aircraft Establishment) led to wing-section and pivot modifications, and Mignet produced revised designs, but the type's reputation never fully recovered. Tandem-wing Mignet-formula aircraft nevertheless continue to be built and flown today in improved forms.
