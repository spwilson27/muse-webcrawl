Electric sail

An electric sail (or E-sail, also called an electric solar wind sail) is a proposed form of spacecraft propulsion using the dynamic pressure of the solar wind as a source of thrust. It creates a "virtual" sail by forming an electric field around a spacecraft from a set of long, thin, electrically charged tethers, rather than using a physical sail as in a solar sail, which uses radiation pressure of sunlight. The concept was invented in 2006 by Pekka Janhunen of the Finnish Meteorological Institute.

Principles of operation

The sail consists of 50–100 thin wires (tethers), each tens of kilometres long (a full-scale design envisages total tether length of hundreds of kilometres), deployed radially from the spacecraft and kept taut by centrifugal force as the craft rotates slowly. An onboard electron gun ejects electrons into space, maintaining the tethers at a high positive potential (tens of kilovolts). The resulting electric field extends tens to hundreds of metres around each wire, forming an effective sail area far larger than the physical structure. Solar wind protons are deflected by this field, and their exchange of momentum pushes the spacecraft. The effective area of a full-scale E-sail could exceed 100 km² while the system mass remains modest, because the tethers are extremely light.

Comparison with other sails

Unlike a solar sail, the E-sail's thrust derives from the solar wind plasma, whose dynamic pressure varies with heliocentric distance roughly as 1/r² (similar to photon pressure), but the sail can be "reefed" electrically and steered by modulating the voltage of individual tethers. Unlike a magnetic sail, it needs no superconducting coils. The concept also has a reverse application: a plasma brake mode uses a negatively charged tether to create drag in low Earth orbit for satellite deorbiting.

Development and tests

The Estonian student satellite ESTCube-1 (launched 2013) carried a 10-metre tether experiment, but the tether failed to deploy. Finland's Aalto-1 (2017) carried a plasma brake tether experiment. Larger demonstrators have been studied by ESA and Finnish groups, with proposed missions including fast trips to the outer Solar System and asteroid resource surveys using multiple small E-sail craft; a full-scale device has not yet flown.
