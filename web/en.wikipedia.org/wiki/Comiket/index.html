Comiket (コミケット, Komiketto), otherwise known as the Comic Market (コミックマーケット, Komikku Māketto), is a biannual dōjinshi fair in Tokyo, Japan. It is a grassroots marketplace where amateur and professional creators sell self-published works, principally fan-made manga, novels, games, music and merchandise based on existing anime, manga, games and other media, though original works are also sold.

History

Comiket was founded in 1975 by manga critic Yoshihiro Yonezawa and friends, attracting some 600 attendees and about 32 circles (creator groups). It grew steadily, moving through several venues before settling at Tokyo Big Sight in 1996. Summer and Winter Comikets now draw on the order of 35,000 circles and hundreds of thousands of visitors, making it the largest fan convention in the world.

Operation

Circles apply for booth space by genre category; popular categories rotate days. Attendees — a substantial share of them women — queue from before dawn, and the event is famous for its disciplined crowds, volunteer staff and elaborate cosplay areas. Because dōjinshi are technically derivative works, Comiket operates within an informal tolerance by Japanese rights holders, widely credited with sustaining Japan's fan culture and feeding talent into the professional manga industry.
