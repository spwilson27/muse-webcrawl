U.S. Route 16 (US 16) is an east–west United States Numbered Highway between the eastern entrance of Yellowstone National Park in Wyoming and Rapid City, South Dakota. It was one of the original highways of 1926, though its western section across Wyoming and Montana was largely replaced by Interstate 90.

Route description

The highway leaves the east gate of Yellowstone National Park and travels through Cody, Wyoming, before crossing the Bighorn Mountains via the scenic Bighorn Scenic Byway and passing through Ten Sleep and Buffalo. In South Dakota it serves the Black Hills, passing Custer and the Mount Rushmore area before ending in Rapid City.

A well-known branch, U.S. Route 16A, is a scenic loop road in the Black Hills featuring single-lane tunnels, pigtail bridges, and views of Mount Rushmore framed by the tunnels.

History

When established in 1926, US 16 ran from Detroit, Michigan, west to Yellowstone, including a ferry connection across Lake Michigan between Ludington, Michigan, and Manitowoc, Wisconsin. Segments east of South Dakota were gradually decommissioned as Interstate highways replaced them; the eastern terminus was truncated to Rapid City in the 1980s.

Major intersections

  • Yellowstone National Park east entrance (west end)
  • U.S. Route 14 in Cody, Wyoming
  • Interstate 90 at Buffalo, Wyoming, and Moorcroft, Wyoming
  • Interstate 190 / U.S. Route 385 area in Rapid City, South Dakota (east end)
