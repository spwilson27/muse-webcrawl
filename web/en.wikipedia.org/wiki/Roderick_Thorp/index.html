Roderick Mayne Thorp Jr. (September 1, 1936 – April 28, 1999) was an American novelist best known for his crime and thriller fiction. His 1966 novel The Detective was adapted into a 1968 film starring Frank Sinatra, and its 1979 sequel, Nothing Lasts Forever, became the basis for the blockbuster action film Die Hard (1988).

Career

Thorp worked as a journalist and taught literature and creative writing before turning to fiction. His other novels include Rainbow Drive, Devlin, Into the Forest, and Westfield. He also wrote true-crime works. Because Nothing Lasts Forever was a sequel to The Detective, the film rights to Die Hard were contractually offered first to Sinatra, then nearly 70, who declined the role of retired detective Joe Leland, which was renamed John McClane and played by Bruce Willis.

Death

Thorp died of a heart attack in Oxnard, California, in 1999, aged 62.
