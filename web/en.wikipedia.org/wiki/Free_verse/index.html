Free verse

Free verse is an open form of poetry that does not use consistent metre patterns, rhyme, or any musical pattern — though it may still use rhythm, line breaks, imagery and cadence shaped by the poet. In French it is known as vers libre.


Related forms: Prose poetry, blank verse

History

Antecedents include the cadenced prose of the King James Bible and Walt Whitman's Leaves of Grass (1855). French symbolist poets in the 1880s formalised vers libre. In English, modernists such as T. S. Eliot, Ezra Pound, William Carlos Williams and H.D. made free verse dominant in the 20th century.

Characteristics

Free verse relies on natural speech rhythms, enjambment, repetition and the visual shape of lines on the page rather than a fixed stanza form. Critics have debated whether it is truly "free" or governed by subtler constraints.

Influence

By the later 20th century free verse became the predominant form in English-language poetry, used by poets from Allen Ginsberg to contemporary spoken-word artists.
