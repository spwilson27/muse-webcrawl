Irrawaddy Delta

The Irrawaddy Delta (also Ayeyarwady Delta) is the low-lying alluvial plain where the Irrawaddy River divides into a network of distributaries before emptying into the Andaman Sea in Myanmar. Covering roughly 35,000 square kilometres, it is one of Asia's great rice-growing regions and among the most densely populated parts of the country.

Geography

The delta begins near Myan Aung, about 220 km from the sea, where the river splits into channels including the Pathein, Pyapon, Bogale and Toe rivers. Mangrove forests line the coast, though large areas were cleared for rice paddies during British colonial rule, when the delta became a major rice exporter.

Cyclone Nargis

In May 2008 the delta was devastated by Cyclone Nargis, whose storm surge flooded the low-lying region and killed an estimated 138,000 people, one of the deadliest natural disasters in recorded history. Recovery was hampered by the ruling junta's restrictions on international aid.

Economy

Rice cultivation dominates, supplemented by fishing, aquaculture and salt production. The delta's waterways remain its principal transport network, and its ecosystems support fisheries and migratory birds but face pressure from erosion, salinity intrusion and climate change.
