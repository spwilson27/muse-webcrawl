The National Pan-Hellenic Council (NPHC) is the umbrella organisation for the nine historically African American fraternities and sororities known collectively as the Divine Nine. Founded on 10 May 1930 at Howard University in Washington, D.C., it coordinates the activities of its member organisations on college campuses and through alumni chapters across the United States and abroad.

Member organisations

The Divine Nine are Alpha Phi Alpha (1906), Alpha Kappa Alpha (1908), Kappa Alpha Psi (1911), Omega Psi Phi (1911), Delta Sigma Theta (1913), Phi Beta Sigma (1914), Zeta Phi Beta (1920), Sigma Gamma Rho (1922) and Iota Phi Theta (1963, which joined the council in 1997). Unlike most Greek-letter societies, NPHC membership is a lifetime commitment: intake continues through graduate and alumni chapters long after college, and members traditionally do not join more than one organisation.

Culture and purpose

Founded in an era when Black students were excluded from white fraternities, the NPHC organisations combine fellowship with community service, scholarship, voter registration and civil rights advocacy; their members included Martin Luther King Jr. (Alpha Phi Alpha) and Kamala Harris (Alpha Kappa Alpha). Their public culture is distinctive: coordinated step shows, probate shows for new members, hand signs, calls and chants, and the wearing of organisation colours and letters. The council is distinct from the (predominantly white) National Panhellenic Conference for sororities and the North American Interfraternity Conference — note the differing placement of the hyphen in "Pan-Hellenic".
