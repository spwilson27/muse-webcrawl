School bullying

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

School bullying, like bullying generally, is defined as the activity of repeated, aggressive behaviour intended to hurt another individual, physically, verbally, or psychologically, occurring in an educational setting. It is characterized by an imbalance of power between the aggressor and the victim. The Norwegian researcher Dan Olweus, a pioneer in bullying research, defined it as a student being exposed repeatedly and over time to negative actions on the part of one or more other students.

Types

School bullying takes several forms:

  • Physical — hitting, kicking, tripping, pinching, and damaging or stealing property.
  • Verbal — name-calling, insults, teasing, threats, and intimidation.
  • Relational (social) — spreading rumours, excluding a student from a group, and embarrassing someone in public.
  • Cyberbullying — bullying through digital devices and platforms, including mean messages, impersonation, and sharing humiliating images; unlike traditional bullying it can occur at any hour and reach a wide audience.

Participants

Researchers describe several roles beyond bully and victim: assistants who join in, reinforcers who encourage by laughing or watching, outsiders who stay away, and defenders who comfort or support the victim. "Bully-victims" both bully others and are bullied themselves and tend to have the poorest outcomes.

Effects

Victims of school bullying are at increased risk of depression, anxiety, low self-esteem, psychosomatic complaints such as headaches and sleep problems, poor school performance, and school avoidance; in severe cases bullying is associated with suicidal ideation. Effects can persist into adulthood. Perpetrators also face elevated risks of later antisocial behaviour and substance abuse.

Prevalence

Rates vary by country, age, and methodology, but international surveys such as UNESCO and the Health Behaviour in School-aged Children (HBSC) study find that roughly one in three students worldwide reports being bullied in the past month. Bullying tends to peak in early adolescence and decline in later school years.

Prevention and intervention

Whole-school programmes — such as the Olweus Bullying Prevention Program developed in Norway and Finland's KiVa programme — combine school-level rules, classroom discussion, teacher training, and work with individual students and parents, and have shown measurable reductions in bullying in evaluations. Many jurisdictions have enacted anti-bullying legislation requiring schools to adopt policies and reporting procedures.
