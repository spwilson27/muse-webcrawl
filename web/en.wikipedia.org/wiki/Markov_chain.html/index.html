Markov chain

A Markov chain, or Markov process, is a stochastic process describing a sequence of possible events in which the probability of each event depends only on the state attained in the previous event. This memoryless property is known as the Markov property.

Introduction

Informally, a process satisfies the Markov property if one can make predictions about future outcomes based solely on the present state, without knowledge of the full history. Markov chains are named after the Russian mathematician Andrey Markov.

Types

Markov chains may have discrete or continuous state spaces and discrete or continuous time parameters, giving rise to discrete-time Markov chains (DTMCs) and continuous-time Markov chains (CTMCs).

Applications

Markov chains are used in physics, chemistry, economics, statistics, information theory, and computer science — for example in Markov chain Monte Carlo methods, queueing theory, and Google's PageRank algorithm.
