Malvern water is spring water from the Malvern Hills on the border of Worcestershire and Herefordshire in England. The hills are formed of ancient Precambrian igneous and metamorphic rocks, among the oldest in England; rainwater filtering slowly through their fractures emerges at springs with an exceptionally low dissolved mineral content — historically celebrated on the grounds that the water "contains nothing at all".

History

The Holy Well at Malvern Wells was visited for its supposed curative powers from at least the 17th century, and bottling of Malvern water began there as early as 1622, among the earliest commercial bottling of spring water in Britain. In the 19th century the water cure (hydropathy) clinics of Great Malvern made the town a fashionable spa, and Queen Victoria's fondness for the water spread its reputation. Schweppes began bottling at the Colwall (Primeswell) spring in 1850 under royal warrant, and Malvern water was exhibited at the Great Exhibition of 1851.

Modern bottling

Coca-Cola, which had acquired the Schweppes brand interests, closed the Colwall bottling plant in 2010, ending large-scale commercial bottling. Smaller-scale bottling has since continued from other local sources, and the public spouts and wells around the hills — maintained under a Malvern Spa Association scheme — still provide free water to anyone bringing a container.
