The Grand Mosque of Bursa (Turkish: Bursa Ulu Camii) is a major mosque in Bursa, Turkey, built in the Seljuk style and commissioned by the Ottoman Sultan Bayezid I. It was constructed between 1396 and 1399 to commemorate Bayezid's victory at the Battle of Nicopolis in 1396; according to tradition, the sultan had vowed to build twenty mosques and instead built one mosque with twenty domes.

Architecture

The mosque is a large rectangular structure supported by twelve massive pillars, with twenty domes arranged in four rows of five. One dome is open to the sky above an ablution fountain (şadırvan) inside the prayer hall—a distinctive feature allowing light and rain into the interior. The mosque is renowned for its monumental calligraphy: some 192 panels and inscriptions by famous Ottoman calligraphers adorn its walls and pillars. Its carved walnut minbar, made without nails, is regarded as a masterpiece of Seljuk-Ottoman woodwork, its geometric carving said to encode a model of the solar system.

Significance

The Grand Mosque is the most important historic mosque in Bursa, the first Ottoman capital, and is considered one of the five most important mosques in Turkish Islam. It suffered damage in the 1855 earthquake and was extensively restored thereafter.
