Canaanite religion refers to the family of ancient Semitic religions practised by the Canaanites of the Levant during the Bronze and Iron Ages. It was a polytheistic, agricultural religion centred on a pantheon headed by the creator god El and featuring the storm and fertility god Baal, whose struggles with the sea god Yam and the death god Mot are narrated in the Ugaritic mythological texts. Canaanite religion deeply influenced — and was partly absorbed into — early Israelite religion, and its Phoenician continuation spread across the Mediterranean.

Sources

The fullest evidence comes from the clay tablets excavated since 1929 at Ugarit (Ras Shamra) in Syria, written in alphabetic cuneiform and including the Baal Cycle, the Legend of Keret, and the Tale of Aqhat, alongside ritual texts and offering lists. Additional evidence comes from temples, figurines, and inscriptions across the Levant (such as those from Hazor, Megiddo, and Byblos), from the later Phoenician and Punic tradition, and from polemical references in the Hebrew Bible, whose writers condemned Canaanite cult practices.

Pantheon and mythology

El, "the Bull", presided over the divine assembly with his consort Asherah, mother of the gods. Baal (Hadad), the storm god who brings rain and fertility, became the dominant active deity; his cycle tells of his victory over Yam (Sea), the building of his palace on Mount Zaphon, and his death and return in conflict with Mot (Death) — a myth often read as reflecting the seasonal cycle of the Levantine climate. Other major figures include the warrior goddess Anat, the goddess Astarte, the sun goddess Shapash, the moon god Yarikh, and Dagon, god of grain.

Practice

Worship centred on temples and open-air "high places" (bamot) with standing stones (massebot), altars, and sacred poles or trees associated with Asherah. Offerings included animals, grain, oil, and wine; festivals followed the agricultural year. Priests and temple personnel conducted rituals, and divination, vows, and funerary cults of ancestors all formed part of religious life. Claims of ritual child sacrifice in the Canaanite world (associated with the later Punic "tophet") remain debated among scholars.

Legacy

Early Israelite religion emerged within the Canaanite milieu: the God of Israel bears the name El and many of his epithets, and biblical poetry preserves imagery of Yahweh as storm god and rider on the clouds shared with Baal traditions, even as biblical law and prophecy repudiated Baal worship. Through Phoenician colonisation, Canaanite deities — Baal, Melqart, Astarte, Tanit — were carried to Cyprus, North Africa, and Spain.
