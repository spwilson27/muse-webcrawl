Multiverse (DC Comics)

The Multiverse, within DC Comics publications, is a fictional structure consisting of many parallel universes in which variant versions of DC's characters and histories exist. It allows the company to maintain multiple continuities simultaneously — so that, for example, the Golden Age heroes of the 1940s and their Silver Age successors can both be canonical, living on different Earths.

History

The concept began with "Flash of Two Worlds!" (The Flash #123, 1961), in which Barry Allen met Jay Garrick, the Flash of the 1940s, explained as residing on a parallel world designated Earth-Two, while the contemporary DC heroes lived on Earth-One. Annual Justice League/Justice Society crossovers followed, and more Earths accumulated (Earth-Three of the Crime Syndicate, Earth-X, Earth-S, and others).

In 1985–86, the limited series Crisis on Infinite Earths collapsed the Multiverse into a single Earth to simplify continuity. Alternate realities later returned through concepts such as Hypertime and, in 2005–06's Infinite Crisis and the weekly series 52, a new Multiverse of 52 parallel Earths. Subsequent events — Flashpoint and The New 52 reboot (2011), Convergence (2015), DC Rebirth (2016), and Dark Nights: Death Metal and Flashpoint Beyond — expanded the structure into an "Omniverse" of infinite Earths once more.

Structure

In the map devised for The Multiversity (2014–15) by Grant Morrison, the local Multiverse's 52 worlds sit within nested structures including the Speed Force Wall, the Sphere of the Gods (containing realms such as New Genesis, Apokolips, Heaven, and the Dreaming), and surrounding monitor realms such as Nil, all watched over by the Monitors. The Antimatter Universe and its world Qward are counterparts of the positive-matter universes.

In other media

The DC Multiverse underlies crossover storylines in television (the Arrowverse's "Crisis on Infinite Earths" event), animation, and film, most prominently The Flash (2023).
