Narrowboat

A narrowboat is a long, narrow-hulled boat designed to fit the narrow canals of England and Wales, whose locks are generally 7 ft (2.13 m) wide. Traditional working narrowboats are up to 72 ft (21.9 m) long and 6 ft 10 in (2.08 m) in the beam. Today most narrowboats are leisure craft or liveaboards.

History

Narrow canals proliferated during Britain's Industrial Revolution; narrowboats carried coal, grain, pottery and general cargo, often pulled by horses. The working boatmen developed a distinctive culture: painted 'roses and castles' decoration, and whole families living in tiny boatman's cabins. Railways and then roads eroded commercial traffic; by the 1960s commercial carrying had virtually ended on narrow canals.

Modern use

An estimated tens of thousands of narrowboats are now used for leisure cruising, continuous cruising, holidays and residential living ('liveaboards'). They are fitted with diesel or electric engines, stoves, solar panels and modern amenities, and moor at marinas, on cruising rings or as 'continuous cruisers'. Boaters without a home mooring must move every 14 days under British Waterways (Canal & River Trust) rules. The canals host festivals such as canal cavalcades preserving traditional decoration and costume.
