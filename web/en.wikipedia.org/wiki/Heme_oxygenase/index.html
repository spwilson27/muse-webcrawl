Heme oxygenase

IdentifiersEC 1.14.99.3
ReactionHeme → biliverdin + CO + Fe2+
IsoformsHO-1 (inducible), HO-2 (constitutive), HO-3
GenesHMOX1, HMOX2

Heme oxygenase, or haem oxygenase (HO), is an enzyme that catalyzes the degradation of heme to produce biliverdin, ferrous iron (Fe2+), and carbon monoxide (CO). It is the rate-limiting step in heme catabolism and the first step in the pathway by which aged red blood cells are recycled.

Reaction

Heme oxygenase cleaves the alpha-methene bridge of the heme porphyrin ring in an oxygen- and NADPH-dependent reaction, opening the ring to form biliverdin. Biliverdin is subsequently reduced to bilirubin by the cytosolic enzyme biliverdin reductase. The carbon monoxide released is now recognised as a gaseous signalling molecule with vasodilatory and anti-inflammatory effects, and the iron is recycled for new heme synthesis.

Isoforms

Two main isoforms are found in mammals. HO-1 (encoded by the gene HMOX1) is inducible and is upregulated by oxidative stress, heavy metals, hypoxia, and its substrate heme itself; it functions as a heat shock protein (Hsp32) and has cytoprotective, antioxidant, and anti-inflammatory roles. HO-2 (encoded by HMOX2) is constitutively expressed, particularly in the brain and testes, and is involved in oxygen sensing and neurotransmission. A third form, HO-3, is a poor heme catalyst and may function in heme sensing.

Clinical significance

Deficiency of HO-1 in humans is rare and associated with haemolysis, endothelial damage, and abnormal iron deposition. Induction of HO-1 is an area of therapeutic interest in conditions including transplant rejection, vascular disease, and sepsis. Excessive heme degradation in newborns produces the bilirubin responsible for neonatal jaundice.
