Airbrush
A small, air-operated tool that sprays paint, ink or dye by atomisation.

An airbrush is a small, air-operated tool that sprays various media, most often paint, but also ink and dye, and sometimes cosmetics or other liquids, by the process of atomisation. Spray painting developed from the airbrush and is considered to employ a type of airbrush.

History

The first airbrush, depending on definition, was patented in 1876 by Francis Edgar Stanley of Newton, Massachusetts. A later, more practical design was patented by Charles Burdick in 1893 and marketed by the Thayer and Chandler art materials company in the United States, and by Aerograph in England. Early airbrushes were used by photo retouchers and illustrators.

Liberty Walkup and Abner Peeler developed related spraying devices in the late 19th century. In the 20th century the airbrush became standard for commercial illustration, model painting, automotive custom painting and, later, makeup and nail art.

Mechanism

An airbrush works by passing a stream of fast-moving compressed air through a venturi, which creates a local reduction in air pressure that allows paint to be pulled from an interconnected reservoir at normal atmospheric pressure. The high velocity of the air atomises the paint into very fine droplets. Paint can be gravity-fed from a cup above the body, siphon-fed from below, or side-fed.

Types

Single and dual action

In a single-action airbrush, pressing the trigger releases air and paint at a fixed ratio. In a dual-action (double-action) airbrush, the user presses down for air and pulls back to increase paint flow, giving finer control.

Internal and external mix

Internal-mix airbrushes mix air and paint inside the tip for finer atomisation; external-mix models mix outside the nozzle and are simpler and cheaper.

Uses

Applications include fine art and illustration, photo retouching (historically), scale modelling, custom automotive and motorcycle painting, T-shirt and textile art, cake decoration, cosmetics, temporary tattoos and fingernail art.
