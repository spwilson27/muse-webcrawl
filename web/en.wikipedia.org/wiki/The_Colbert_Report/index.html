The Colbert Report (/koʊlˈbɛər rəˈpɔːr/; the final t in both words is silent) is an American late-night talk and news satire television program hosted by Stephen Colbert that aired on Comedy Central from October 17, 2005, to December 18, 2014, for 1,447 episodes. A spin-off of The Daily Show, it parodied personality-driven political pundit shows of the era, particularly The O'Reilly Factor.

The character and format

Colbert performed in character as a bombastic, self-absorbed right-wing pundit — a "well-intentioned, poorly informed high-status idiot" — who embodied the logic of cable news commentary. The show introduced the word truthiness in its first episode, naming a stance in which gut feeling outranks fact; the term was selected as Merriam-Webster's Word of the Year for 2006. Recurring segments included "The Wørd," in which on-screen bullet points ironically undercut Colbert's monologue, and "Better Know a District," a series of interviews with members of Congress.

Impact

The program's satire frequently crossed into the real political arena: Colbert's in-character testimony before a House subcommittee on immigration (2010), his satirical Super PAC "Americans for a Better Tomorrow, Tomorrow," and his 2006 White House Correspondents' Association Dinner address are frequently cited in scholarship on political comedy. Colbert won multiple Emmy and Peabody Awards. The show ended when Colbert left Comedy Central to succeed David Letterman as host of CBS's The Late Show, leaving the character behind — a persona-based format as distinctive, in its domain, as the improvised theatre games of The Comedy Store Players.
