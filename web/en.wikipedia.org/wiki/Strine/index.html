Strine

Strine is a term coined in the mid-1960s for a heavy form of the Australian accent, and the deliberate attempt to render its phonetics in writing. The word is a syncope of "Australian" as pronounced in a broad Australian accent.

Origin

The term was invented by Alastair Ardoch Morrison under the pen name Afferbeck Lauder, in the best-selling humorous book Let Stalk Strine (1965), which phonetically transcribed expressions of Australian speech as heard by an outsider — for example, "Emma chisit" for "How much is it?", "egg nishner" for "air conditioner", and "Strine" itself for "Australian". A sequel, Nose Tone Unturned, followed. The books affectionately satirized the broad Australian accent's elisions, diphthongs, and rhyming slang.
