Leventina

The Leventina (Italian: Valle Leventina) is a valley in the canton of Ticino in southern Switzerland, running from the Gotthard Pass area southward along the upper Ticino River to Biasca, where it meets the Riviera valley. It forms the Leventina District, one of the eight districts of Ticino, and its principal settlements include Airolo, Faido, and Giornico.

Geography

The valley descends through a series of steps and gorges — including the Piottino and Biaschina gorges — from the high Alps near the Gotthard and Nufenen passes down to about 300 metres above sea level at Biasca. Side valleys include the Val Bedretto, the Val Piora (known for its alpine lakes), and the Val Blenio junction area. The upper valley receives heavy snowfall and is known for avalanche risk.

History and transport

The Leventina lies on the historic Gotthard route between northern Europe and Italy, one of the great Alpine transit corridors. It belonged for centuries to the Duchy of Milan before passing to the Swiss cantons as a subject territory of Uri, and it joined the canton of Ticino in 1803. The Gotthard railway (1882), the A2 motorway, and since 2016 the Gotthard Base Tunnel — the world's longest railway tunnel, whose southern portal near Bodio lies in the lower valley — all pass through the Leventina. Airolo, at the valley's head, is a longstanding transport and military centre, and the upper valley is known for winter sports and for Ambri-Piotta, the ice hockey club based at the valley village of Ambrì.
