Military Sealift Command

Military Sealift Command
Founded: 9 July 1949 (as Military Sea Transportation Service)
Country: United States
Branch: United States Navy
Headquarters: Naval Station Norfolk, Virginia

The Military Sealift Command (MSC) is an organisation of the United States Navy that controls the replenishment and military transport ships of the Navy. It is responsible for providing sealift — the movement of military cargo, fuel and supplies by sea — and operates the Navy's combat logistics force, special mission ships, prepositioning ships and hospital ships. MSC is one of three component commands of the United States Transportation Command.

Organisation

MSC operates around 130 ships manned largely by civilian merchant mariners (civil service mariners, or CIVMARs), organised into programme offices including Naval Fleet Auxiliary Force (underway replenishment), Special Mission, Prepositioning, Sealift, and Service and Command Support. Its fleet includes fast combat support ships, dry cargo/ammunition ships of the Lewis and Clark class, fleet oilers, the hospital ships USNS Mercy and USNS Comfort, submarine tenders, cable repair ships and ocean surveillance vessels.

History

The command began in 1949 as the Military Sea Transportation Service, consolidating the shipping arms of the services. It was renamed Military Sealift Command in 1970. MSC ships supplied operations in Korea, Vietnam, the Persian Gulf War — when sealift delivered the great majority of military cargo to the theatre — and the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan, as well as humanitarian missions such as disaster relief after the 2010 Haiti earthquake.
