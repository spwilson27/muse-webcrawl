Stradivarius

A Stradivarius is one of the string instruments, chiefly violins, built by the luthier Antonio Stradivari and his family in Cremona, Italy, during the late 17th and early 18th centuries. Stradivari made roughly 1,100 instruments, of which about 650 survive, including some 450–512 violins, as well as violas, cellos, guitars and harps. They are among the most coveted and expensive musical instruments in the world.

Sound and the "secret"

The instruments' reputation rests on the power and quality of their sound, especially those of Stradivari's "golden period" (about 1700–1725). Generations of research have sought the "secret" of their tone in varnish chemistry, wood treatment and geometry; modern blind tests have repeatedly found that elite players cannot reliably distinguish Stradivari violins from the best modern instruments, and sometimes prefer the latter. Dendrochronology has confirmed the age and provenance of many instruments.

Famous instruments and prices

Named instruments include the Messiah (1716, Ashmolean Museum), the Lady Blunt (1721), sold in 2011 for £9.8 million to benefit Japanese earthquake relief, and the da Vinci, ex-Seidel (1714), sold in 2022 for US$15.3 million. Many surviving Stradivari are owned by foundations and loaned to leading soloists. Their history also includes celebrated thefts, notably of the Davidoff-Morini (1995, never recovered) and the Ames (recovered after 35 years).
