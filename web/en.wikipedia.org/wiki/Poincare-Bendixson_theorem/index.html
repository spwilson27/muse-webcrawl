Poincare-Bendixson theorem

Phase portrait showing a trajectory spiralling toward a limit cycle

The Poincare-Bendixson theorem is a result in the theory of dynamical systems, named after Henri Poincaré and Ivar Bendixson, concerning the long-term behaviour of orbits of continuous dynamical systems in the plane.

Statement

The theorem states that if a trajectory of a two-dimensional autonomous system of ordinary differential equations remains forever in a closed, bounded region of the plane that contains no fixed points, then the trajectory must approach a closed orbit — a limit cycle — or itself be (part of) a closed orbit. More generally, the omega-limit set of a bounded trajectory in the plane, if it contains no equilibria, is a periodic orbit.

Significance

A key consequence is that continuous planar systems cannot exhibit chaos: their bounded long-term behaviour is limited to fixed points and limit cycles. Chaotic behaviour requires at least three dimensions for continuous autonomous systems, or discrete-time dynamics such as iterated maps. The theorem is a fundamental tool for proving the existence of periodic solutions in models of oscillating circuits, chemical reactions, and biological populations.
