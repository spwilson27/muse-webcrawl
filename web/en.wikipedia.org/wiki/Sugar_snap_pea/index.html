Sugar snap pea

The sugar snap pea is an edible-pod pea cultivar group (Pisum sativum var. macrocarpon) bred for its thick-walled, sweet pods that are eaten whole, peas and pod together. Unlike snow peas, whose pods are flat, sugar snap peas develop plump, rounded pods; unlike garden peas, the pod is tender and edible rather than fibrous. They are eaten raw as a snack or lightly cooked in stir-fries and salads.

Species: Pisum sativum
Cultivar group: Macrocarpon Group
Origin: Idaho, United States (1979)

History

The modern sugar snap pea was developed by plant breeder Calvin Lamborn of the Gallatin Valley Seed Company in Twin Falls, Idaho. Working from a rogue pea plant with unusually thick pod walls discovered in 1952, Lamborn crossed it with snow pea varieties over many years; the resulting cultivar 'Sugar Snap' was released in 1979 and won the All-America Selections gold medal that year. It is considered one of the most influential vegetable introductions of the late 20th century.

Cultivation

Sugar snap peas are cool-season climbing legumes, typically grown on trellises or netting, with vines reaching 1–2 metres. Pods are harvested while still plump and crisp, before the seeds fully mature. Popular cultivars include 'Sugar Snap', 'Sugar Ann' (a dwarf type) and 'Super Sugar Snap'. Like other peas, they fix nitrogen in the soil through symbiosis with rhizobium bacteria.
