Julius Plücker

Born16 June 1801, Elberfeld, Duchy of Berg
Died22 May 1868, Bonn, Prussia
FieldsMathematics, physics
Known forAnalytic geometry; cathode ray research; Plücker coordinates

Julius Plücker (1801–1868) was a German mathematician and physicist who made foundational contributions to analytic geometry and was a pioneer in the study of cathode rays.

Mathematics

After studies at Bonn, Heidelberg, Berlin and Paris, Plücker became a professor at the University of Bonn. His work revolutionized analytic geometry: he introduced homogeneous coordinates, the concept of line coordinates (now called Plücker coordinates), and treated points and lines dually, laying groundwork for projective geometry. His formulas relating the singularities of algebraic curves are known as the Plücker formulas.

Physics

In later life Plücker turned to experimental physics. Working with the glassblower Heinrich Geissler, he studied electrical discharges in rarefied gases and in 1858 observed the fluorescence produced by what came to be called cathode rays, noting their deflection by magnets. His student Johann Hittorf continued this line of research.

Honors

Plücker received the Copley Medal of the Royal Society in 1866. He built the mathematical and physical institutes at Bonn into leading centers of research.
