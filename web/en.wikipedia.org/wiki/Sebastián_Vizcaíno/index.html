Sebastián Vizcaíno

The California coast as charted by Vizcaíno's expedition.

Sebastián Vizcaíno (c. 1548 – 1624) was a Spanish soldier, explorer, cartographer and diplomat. He is best known for his 1602–1603 voyage along the coast of Alta California, during which he charted and named many features that survive on maps today, and for leading an embassy to Japan in 1611.

Born: c. 1548, Spain

Died: 1624, Mexico City, New Spain

Occupation: Explorer, cartographer, diplomat

Known for: Exploration of the California coast (1602–03); embassy to Japan (1611–1614)

California expedition

Commissioned by the viceroy of New Spain, the Count of Monterrey, Vizcaíno sailed from Acapulco in May 1602 with three vessels to find a safe harbour for returning Manila galleons. He re-charted the coast previously visited by Cabrillo, naming San Diego Bay, Santa Catalina Island, Monterey Bay (after the viceroy), Point Conception and other landmarks. His glowing report of Monterey as an ideal port — exaggerated in its praise — was not acted on for more than 160 years, until Alta California was finally settled in 1769.

Embassy to Japan

In 1611 Vizcaíno was sent to Japan to thank the Tokugawa authorities for their treatment of shipwrecked Spaniards, to seek the legendary islands of gold and silver east of Japan, and to survey the Japanese coast. He met the retired shōgun Tokugawa Ieyasu and returned to New Spain in 1613–14 aboard the Japanese-built galleon San Juan Bautista, accompanied by the embassy of Hasekura Tsunenaga.

Legacy

Vizcaíno Bay in Baja California and numerous California place names derive from his voyage, whose charts — drawn by the cosmographer Gerónimo Martínez Palacios — remained the standard for the coast for over a century.
