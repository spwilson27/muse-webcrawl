Tagalog language

Tagalog (təˈɡɑːlɒɡ; Tagalog [tɐˈɡaːloɡ]) is an Austronesian language spoken as a first language by the ethnic Tagalog people, who make up a quarter of the population of the Philippines, and as a second language by the majority of the country. Its standardized form, officially named Filipino, is the national language of the Philippines, and is one of two official languages, alongside English.

History

Tagalog is a Central Philippine language within the Austronesian family. The word Tagalog is thought to derive from tagá- ("native of") and ilog ("river"), i.e. "river dweller", or possibly from tagá-alog, "native of the ford". Before Spanish colonization, Tagalog was written in the Baybayin script, a Brahmic abugida. The first known book printed in the Philippines, the Doctrina Christiana (1593), was printed in Spanish and two versions of Tagalog — one in Latin letters, one in Baybayin.

In 1937, the Institute of National Language chose Tagalog as the basis for the national language; in 1959 it was renamed Pilipino, and in the 1987 Constitution it was designated Filipino.

Grammar

Tagalog has a verb–subject–object or verb–object–subject word order and a complex system of verbal affixes encoding voice/focus: actor, patient, locative, benefactive, and instrumental. Enclitic pronouns and particles such as na, pa, po (a marker of politeness), and ba (question marker) are prominent features.

Vocabulary

About a third of Tagalog vocabulary derives from Spanish, alongside borrowings from Malay, Sanskrit, Arabic, Nahuatl (via the Manila galleon trade), Hokkien Chinese, Tamil, Persian, and increasingly English. Modern colloquial speech, often called "Taglish", mixes Tagalog and English freely.
