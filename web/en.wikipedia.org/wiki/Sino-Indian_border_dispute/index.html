Sino-Indian border dispute

The Sino-Indian border dispute is an ongoing territorial dispute over the sovereignty of several largely uninhabited sections of land between China and India. The two countries claim a border of roughly 3,500 kilometres (the length is itself disputed), but have never agreed a mutually recognised boundary. The disputed areas are usually grouped into a western sector (Aksai Chin), a middle sector along the Himalayan watershed, and an eastern sector (the Indian state of Arunachal Pradesh, claimed by China as "South Tibet"). The unresolved boundary led to the Sino-Indian War of 1962 and to periodic later confrontations, including deadly clashes in 2020.

Background

The dispute originates in the differing boundaries drawn by British India in the 19th and early 20th centuries. In the east, the McMahon Line, agreed between Britain and Tibet at the Simla Convention of 1914, placed the boundary along the Himalayan crest; China never accepted Tibetan competence to conclude the agreement. In the west, competing British proposals (the Johnson Line and the Macartney–MacDonald Line) left the status of the Aksai Chin plateau unclear. India, after independence in 1947, adopted the most expansive claims; China, after 1949, rejected the colonial-era lines as products of imperialism.

The 1962 war

Tensions rose after China built a road across Aksai Chin in the 1950s, India's grant of asylum to the Dalai Lama in 1959, and the failure of official-level talks. In October–November 1962 China launched offensives in both sectors in the Sino-Indian War (also called the Himalayan War), decisively defeating Indian forces before declaring a unilateral ceasefire and withdrawing in the east while retaining Aksai Chin, which it administers as part of Xinjiang and Tibet. China also controls the Trans-Karakoram Tract, ceded by Pakistan in 1963 and claimed by India.

The Line of Actual Control

Since the 1962 war, the two sides have been separated by the poorly defined Line of Actual Control (LAC), which each side perceives differently in many places. India and China signed agreements in 1993 and 1996 to maintain peace along the LAC and to refrain from using firearms, and established mechanisms for consultation, but patrols continued to confront one another in disputed pockets.

Later confrontations

In 2017 Indian and Chinese troops faced off for 73 days at Doklam, near the trijunction with Bhutan. In May–June 2020, clashes along the LAC in Ladakh culminated in a violent hand-to-hand fight in the Galwan Valley on 15 June 2020 in which 20 Indian soldiers and at least four Chinese soldiers were killed — the first combat deaths on the border since 1975. Both sides then built up large forces in the region; a series of disengagement agreements followed, and in October 2024 the two countries announced an agreement on patrolling arrangements, followed by a meeting between their leaders, though the underlying territorial dispute remains unresolved.
