Romeo and Juliet (Prokofiev)

Romeo and Juliet (Russian: Ромео и Джульетта), Op. 64, is a ballet by Sergei Prokofiev based on William Shakespeare's play of the same name. Composed in 1935–36, it is one of Prokofiev's best-known works and one of the most performed ballets of the 20th century.

Composition and premiere

Prokofiev originally gave the ballet a happy ending, in which Romeo arrives in time to save Juliet; this provoked controversy, and he revised it to restore Shakespeare's tragic conclusion. The premiere of the full ballet took place in Brno, Czechoslovakia, in 1938, and the landmark Soviet production at the Kirov Theatre in Leningrad followed in 1940 with Galina Ulanova as Juliet.

Music

Prokofiev extracted three orchestral suites from the score, which are frequently performed in concert. Famous numbers include "Montagues and Capulets" (the Dance of the Knights), the Balcony Scene, and the music for Tybalt's death and Juliet's funeral. The score is noted for its lyrical themes, rhythmic bite, and expressive depiction of both youthful love and violent feuding.
