Intelligence assessment

Intelligence assessment is the evaluation and analysis of collected intelligence to produce judgments that inform policy and military decisions. It transforms raw reporting from many sources into finished products, such as daily briefs and national intelligence estimates, that state what is known, what is uncertain, and what is likely to happen.

Process

Assessment involves grading the reliability of sources and the credibility of information, collating reports across collection disciplines, and applying structured analytic techniques to counter cognitive biases. Well-known techniques include analysis of competing hypotheses, devil's advocacy, and red teaming.

Language of probability

Assessments use standardized estimative language — words such as "likely" and "almost certainly" tied to defined probability ranges — and state levels of confidence based on the quality and quantity of sourcing.

Failures and reform

Famous assessment failures, including those preceding surprise attacks and erroneous judgments about weapons programs, have driven reforms in tradecraft, including greater transparency about uncertainty and alternative analysis.
