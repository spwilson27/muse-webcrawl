Suzanne Valadon

Suzanne Valadon (born Marie-Clémentine Valadon; 23 September 1865 – 7 April 1938) was a French painter who was born in Bessines-sur-Gartempe, Haute-Vienne, France. In 1894, Valadon became the first woman painter admitted to the Société Nationale des Beaux-Arts. She was the mother of painter Maurice Utrillo.

Early life and modelling

Raised in poverty in Montmartre by her mother, a seamstress, Valadon worked many jobs before becoming an artists' model. She posed for Pierre-Auguste Renoir, Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec, Edgar Degas and Puvis de Chavannes, observing their techniques as she worked.

Career

Encouraged by Degas, who admired her drawings, Valadon developed a bold style of painting characterized by strong outlines and vivid colour. Her subjects included portraits, landscapes, still lifes, and frank female nudes painted from a woman's perspective, unusual for the time.

Legacy

Valadon's work is held in major collections including the Musée d'Orsay and the Metropolitan Museum of Art. She is recognized as a pioneering figure for women in modern art.
