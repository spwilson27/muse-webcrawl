List of tourist attractions in Budapest

The Hungarian Parliament Building seen from across the Danube

Budapest, the capital of Hungary, is one of Europe's most visited cities, known for its thermal baths, grand nineteenth-century architecture, and setting on the Danube. The Banks of the Danube, the Buda Castle Quarter, and Andrássy Avenue are inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Landmarks

  • Hungarian Parliament Building — the seat of the National Assembly, completed in 1904, one of the largest parliament buildings in the world.
  • Buda Castle — the historic castle and palace complex of the Hungarian kings, housing the Hungarian National Gallery and the Budapest History Museum.
  • Fisherman's Bastion — a neo-Romanesque terrace on Castle Hill, built between 1895 and 1902, offering panoramic views of Pest.
  • St. Stephen's Basilica — the largest church in Budapest, named for Hungary's first king, Stephen I; it houses his mummified right hand, the Holy Right.
  • Chain Bridge (Széchenyi Lánchíd) — the first permanent bridge across the Danube in Hungary, opened in 1849.
  • Heroes' Square — a major square at the end of Andrássy Avenue with the Millennium Monument, flanked by the Museum of Fine Arts and the Palace of Art.
  • Gellért Hill and the Citadella — a fortress built by the Habsburgs after 1848–49, with the Liberty Statue at its summit.
  • Matthias Church — a Roman Catholic church in the Castle District, site of several coronations, with a distinctive tiled roof.
  • Shoes on the Danube Bank — a memorial to Jews shot into the Danube by Arrow Cross militiamen in 1944–45.

Thermal baths

  • Széchenyi Thermal Bath — one of the largest bath complexes in Europe, opened in 1913 in City Park, famous for its outdoor pools.
  • Gellért Baths — Art Nouveau baths at the foot of Gellért Hill, opened in 1918.
  • Rudas Baths — an Ottoman-era bath with a sixteenth-century octagonal pool.
  • Lukács Baths — popular with artists and writers, fed by hot springs used since Roman times.

Museums

  • Hungarian National Museum — founded in 1802, chronicling Hungary's history from its foundation to 1990.
  • Museum of Fine Arts — holding works by Raphael, El Greco, and an important Egyptian collection.
  • House of Terror Museum — at Andrássy Avenue 60, the former headquarters of the fascist and communist secret police.
  • Ludwig Museum — Hungary's museum of contemporary art, located in the Müpa Budapest arts centre.

Other attractions

  • Margaret Island — a 2.5 km island in the Danube with parks, a musical fountain, and medieval ruins.
  • Great Market Hall — the largest indoor market in Budapest, opened in 1897.
  • Dohány Street Synagogue — the largest synagogue in Europe, built in 1859 in the Moorish Revival style.
  • Budapest Zoo and Botanical Garden — one of the oldest zoos in the world, opened in 1866.
  • Ruin bars — bars established in derelict buildings in the Jewish Quarter, beginning with Szimpla Kert in the early 2000s.
