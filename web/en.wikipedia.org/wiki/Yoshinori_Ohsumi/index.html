Yoshinori Ohsumi (大隅 良典, Ōsumi Yoshinori, born 9 February 1945) is a Japanese cell biologist specialising in autophagy, the process by which cells degrade and recycle their own components. In 2016 he was awarded the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine "for his discoveries of mechanisms for autophagy," becoming the 25th Japanese Nobel laureate.

Life and career

Ohsumi was born in Fukuoka and studied at the University of Tokyo, receiving his doctorate in 1974. After postdoctoral work at Rockefeller University in New York in the laboratory of Gerald Edelman, he returned to Japan, working at the University of Tokyo and later at the National Institute for Basic Biology in Okazaki. In 2009 he moved to the Tokyo Institute of Technology, where he became honorary professor.

Research

Working with baker's yeast in the late 1980s and early 1990s, Ohsumi devised experiments that revealed autophagy in action: by using yeast mutants lacking vacuolar proteases and starving the cells, he caused autophagosomes to accumulate visibly under the microscope. He went on to identify the first autophagy-related genes (the ATG genes), a set of discoveries that transformed a little-studied phenomenon into a major field of cell biology, with implications for diseases including cancer, Parkinson's disease, and type 2 diabetes.

Honours

Among many honours, Ohsumi received the Kyoto Prize in Basic Sciences (2012), the Canada Gairdner International Award (2015), the Wiley Prize (2016), and the Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences (2017).
