New Revised Standard Version

The New Revised Standard Version (NRSV) is an English translation of the Bible published in 1989 by the National Council of Churches. It is a revision of the Revised Standard Version (RSV), itself an update of the American Standard Version. The translation committee, chaired by Bruce Metzger, included Protestant, Catholic, Orthodox, and Jewish scholars.

Characteristics

The NRSV aimed at accuracy, clarity, and contemporary English while following a moderately literal ("formal equivalence") approach. It drew on newly available ancient manuscripts, including the Dead Sea Scrolls. It is notable for its use of gender-inclusive language where the source text was judged to refer to people generally—for example, rendering "brothers" as "brothers and sisters"—while retaining masculine language for God. Editions are published with the Protestant canon, with the Apocrypha, and in Catholic editions.

Reception and update

The NRSV became one of the most widely used translations in academic study and in mainline churches. In 2021 an updated edition, the New Revised Standard Version Updated Edition (NRSVue), was published by the Society of Biblical Literature, incorporating further manuscript evidence and changes in English usage.
