Spin (aerodynamics)

In aviation, a spin is an aggravated stall resulting in autorotation about the aircraft's longitudinal axis and a steep, helical descent. In a spin, one wing is stalled more deeply than the other; the resulting imbalance of lift and drag sustains the rotation. Spins can be entered intentionally for training or aerobatics, or accidentally, typically from a stall during a skidding turn at low altitude, where they have historically been a major cause of fatal accidents.

Phases

A spin develops through an entry phase (stall plus yaw), an incipient phase as rotation begins, a developed phase with stabilised rotation and descent, and a recovery phase after the pilot applies corrective controls. In a fully developed spin the aircraft may lose several hundred feet per turn.

Recovery

The standard recovery technique, often remembered by the acronym PARE, is: Power to idle, Ailerons neutral, Rudder fully opposite to the rotation, and Elevator forward to break the stall — then neutralise the rudder and ease out of the resulting dive. Modern certification standards (such as those descended from NASA spin research in the 1970s and 1980s) require most light aircraft to recover from a one-turn spin within an additional turn.
