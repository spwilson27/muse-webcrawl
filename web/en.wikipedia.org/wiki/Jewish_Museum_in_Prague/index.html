The Jewish Museum in Prague (Czech: Židovské muzeum v Praze) is a museum of Jewish heritage located in Prague, Czech Republic. Founded in 1906, it is one of the oldest Jewish museums in Europe and among the most visited museums in the Czech Republic. Its collections, drawn largely from synagogues across Bohemia and Moravia, are among the largest collections of Judaica in the world.

History

The museum was founded in 1906 by the historian Salomon Hugo Lieben and August Stein, a representative of the Czech Jewish movement, with the aim of preserving valuable artifacts from Prague synagogues that had been demolished during the clearance of the Jewish ghetto at the end of the 19th century. During the Nazi occupation of Czechoslovakia, the museum was taken over by the occupation authorities; in 1942 the Central Jewish Museum was established under Nazi administration, and Jewish property from dissolved communities throughout the Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia was gathered in Prague — a collection effort in which museum staff worked to preserve the heritage of the destroyed communities. After the Second World War the museum passed to the Czechoslovak state, and in 1994 it was returned to the Jewish community and re-established as an independent institution.

Sites and collections

The museum administers several historic sites in the former Jewish Quarter (Josefov), including the Maisel Synagogue, the Pinkas Synagogue — whose walls bear the names of nearly 78,000 Bohemian and Moravian victims of the Shoah — the Spanish Synagogue, the Klausen Synagogue, the Ceremonial Hall, and the Old Jewish Cemetery. Its holdings include some 40,000 objects and around 100,000 books, among them a renowned collection of synagogue textiles, silver, and printed books and manuscripts.
