A knarr (Old Norse: knǫrr, plural knerrir) is a type of Norse merchant ship used by the Vikings for long ocean voyages and bulk cargo transport. Compared with the sleek longship, the knarr was broader, deeper and slower, depending almost entirely on its single large square sail, with oars used only for manoeuvring in harbour.

Characteristics

Knarrs were clinker-built of oak, typically around 16 metres long with a beam of about 5 metres, a deep hold amidships, and small raised decks fore and aft. They carried cargoes of trade goods, livestock, foodstuffs and settlers, and needed only a small crew. Their sturdy, seaworthy hulls made them the workhorses of Norse expansion across the North Atlantic: it was in knarrs that settlers, with their animals and household goods, reached Iceland, Greenland and ultimately Vinland around the year 1000.

Archaeology

The best-known surviving example is Skuldelev 1, one of the five Skuldelev ships recovered from Roskilde Fjord in Denmark in 1962. Built in western Norway around 1030, the vessel was about 16 metres long and capable of carrying roughly 40 tonnes. Sailing replicas, such as the Icelandic Íslendingur, have retraced Viking routes across the Atlantic, demonstrating the type's remarkable ocean-going capability.
