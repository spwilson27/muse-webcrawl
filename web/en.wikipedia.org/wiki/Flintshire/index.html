Flintshire (Welsh: Sir y Fflint) is a county in north-east Wales, bordering the Dee estuary and the English county of Cheshire. It is named after the historic town of Flint and is one of the most industrialised counties in Wales, home to the Deeside manufacturing zone.

History

The historic county of Flintshire was one of the smallest in Wales. It was merged into Clwyd in 1974 and re-established in 1996 as a principal area, though with different boundaries — the Maelor district passing to Wrexham. Flint Castle, begun in 1277, was the first castle built by Edward I during his conquest of Wales.

Economy

Deeside hosts major employers including the Airbus wing factory at Broughton, steelmaking at Shotton, and Toyota's engine plant. The county's north coast follows the Dee estuary toward the Point of Ayr.
