Fernando de la Rúa (September 15, 1937 – July 9, 2019) was an Argentine statesman and academic who served as President of Argentina from 1999 to 2001. A leading figure of the Radical Civic Union (UCR), his presidency ended prematurely amid the Argentine great depression and the December 2001 riots, when he resigned and famously left the Casa Rosada by helicopter.

Early life and education

De la Rúa was born in Córdoba into a politically prominent family; his father Antonio de la Rúa was a deputy and minister. He studied law at the National University of Córdoba, graduating with honours at 21, and became a professor of procedural law. He earned a reputation as a serious, legalistic politician, nicknamed "Chupete" in his youth.

Political career

De la Rúa was elected national deputy in 1973 and senator for the City of Buenos Aires the same year, becoming one of the youngest senators in Argentine history. After the 1976 coup he returned to academic life. With the restoration of democracy he was elected senator again in 1983 and 1992, and deputy in 1991. In 1996 he became the first elected Chief of Government (mayor) of the Autonomous City of Buenos Aires.

Presidency (1999–2001)

Heading the Alliance for Work, Justice and Education (a coalition of the UCR and Frepaso), de la Rúa defeated Peronist candidate Eduardo Duhalde in the 1999 presidential election, promising to preserve the peso–dollar convertibility regime while ending corruption. His government struggled with recession, rising unemployment, a fiscal deficit and the constraints of the fixed exchange rate. His vice president, Carlos "Chacho" Álvarez, resigned in October 2000 over a Senate bribery scandal.

In December 2001, after the government froze bank deposits (the corralito), mass protests and looting erupted. De la Rúa declared a state of siege, which provoked further unrest; after repression left dozens dead, he resigned on December 21, 2001, departing the presidential palace by helicopter—an enduring image of the crisis.

Later life and death

De la Rúa faced several judicial proceedings after leaving office, including charges related to the 2001 repression and the Senate bribery affair; he was acquitted in the latter trial in 2013. He died on July 9, 2019, in Loma Hermosa, Buenos Aires Province, at the age of 81.
