Neptune (mythology)

Neptune (Latin: Neptūnus) is the Roman god of the sea, fresh water, and horses, the brother of Jupiter and Pluto and the counterpart of the Greek god Poseidon. He is conventionally depicted as a mature, bearded figure wielding a trident, riding a chariot drawn by hippocamps or horses across the waves. The planet Neptune is named after him.

Origins and worship

Neptune's name likely derives from an Indo-European root connected with moisture or water, and in early Roman religion he may have been a god of fresh water, springs, and irrigation rather than the sea. His festival, the Neptunalia, was celebrated on July 23, at the height of summer heat and drought, with the building of leafy shelters. His temple in Rome stood near the Circus Flaminius. After Rome's identification of its gods with Greek ones, Neptune absorbed the attributes and mythology of Poseidon, including dominion over the sea and earthquakes.

Mythology

Like Poseidon, Neptune contested with Minerva for the patronage of Athens (in the Greek version), fathered monsters and heroes, and was famed for his changeable temper, stirring storms and earthquakes when angered. His wife was Salacia, goddess of salt water, corresponding to Amphitrite; among his offspring was the messenger Triton, who blew a conch-shell trumpet. As Neptune Equester he was also a patron of horse racing and horses.

Depictions

Neptune is a frequent subject in European art and civic fountains, including the Fountain of Neptune in Bologna, the Trevi Fountain in Rome, and the Neptune Fountain in Berlin, where the god personifies command over water and, by extension, maritime power.
