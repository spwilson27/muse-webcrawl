Banská Štiavnica

Banská Štiavnica (German: Schemnitz; Hungarian: Selmecbánya) is a town in central Slovakia, in the Banská Bystrica Region, with a population of around 10,000. Set in the caldera of an ancient volcano, it was for centuries one of the most important mining towns in Europe, famous for its gold and silver. Its historic centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Mining around Štiavnica dates to the Bronze Age, and by the Middle Ages the town was a leading producer of silver for the Kingdom of Hungary. In 1762 Empress Maria Theresa founded a mining academy there, among the first technical universities in the world. Gunpowder was first used for blasting in mining at Štiavnica in 1627.

Water management

To power and drain the mines, engineers built an elaborate system of reservoirs called tajchy in the surrounding hills, connected by channels and adits — a pioneering work of hydraulic engineering.

Sights

Landmarks include the Old Castle, the New Castle, Trinity Square with its plague column, and the open-air mining museum. UNESCO inscribed the town and its technical monuments in 1993.
