Getúlio Dornelles Vargas (19 April 1882 – 24 August 1954) was a Brazilian lawyer and politician who served as president of Brazil for the longest combined period of any Brazilian leader: first from 1930 to 1945, as provisional president, constitutional president and then dictator of the Estado Novo regime, and again from 1951 until his suicide in 1954. Often called "the father of the poor" (pai dos pobres), Vargas reshaped Brazilian politics through centralisation, industrialisation, labour legislation and populist appeals to the working class.

Rise to power

Born in São Borja in Rio Grande do Sul, Vargas became a lawyer and rose through his state's politics, serving as finance minister under President Washington Luís. Defeated in the 1930 presidential election, he led the armed Revolution of 1930 that ended the Old Republic of the coffee oligarchies. As head of the provisional government he dissolved Congress, replaced state governors with federal interventors, and in 1934 promulgated a new constitution and had himself elected president by the constituent assembly.

Estado Novo

In November 1937, using the pretext of a fabricated communist plot (the Cohen Plan), Vargas carried out a self-coup (autogolpe), dissolved Congress and proclaimed the authoritarian Estado Novo ("New State"), modelled partly on European corporatist regimes. The regime censored the press, banned parties and imprisoned opponents, but also created state companies such as the National Steel Company (CSN) and Vale do Rio Doce, introduced labour courts and the minimum wage, and drew Brazil into the Second World War on the Allied side in 1942.

Return and suicide

Deposed by the military in 1945, Vargas returned triumphantly by election in 1950, campaigning as a nationalist and friend of labour. His second presidency created the state oil company Petrobras (1953) but was beset by inflation, corruption scandals and opposition from military and conservative circles. After an assassination attempt on his critic Carlos Lacerda killed an air-force officer, pressure for his resignation mounted. On 24 August 1954 Vargas shot himself in the Catete Palace, leaving a famous farewell letter (Carta Testamento) denouncing "subterranean forces" — an act that delayed Brazil's political reckoning and cemented his legend.
