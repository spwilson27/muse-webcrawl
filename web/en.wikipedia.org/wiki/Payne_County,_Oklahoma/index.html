Payne County is a county in the U.S. state of Oklahoma. As of the 2020 census, the population was 81,646. Its county seat is Stillwater. The county was formed in 1890 as "County Six" in Oklahoma Territory and was renamed at statehood in 1907 for David L. Payne, a leader of the "Boomers" who campaigned to open the Unassigned Lands to settlement.

History

The area was opened to non-Native settlement in the Land Run of 1889. Stillwater grew up on the site of a trading post on Stillwater Creek. In 1891 the territorial legislature located the Oklahoma Agricultural and Mechanical College — now Oklahoma State University — in Stillwater, which remains the county's principal economic and cultural centre.

Geography

Payne County covers about 697 square miles in north-central Oklahoma, drained by the Cimarron River along its northern edge. Lake Carl Blackwell and Lake McMurtry provide recreation and water storage. Neighbouring counties include Noble, Pawnee, Creek, Lincoln, Logan and Garfield.

Communities

Cities and towns in the county include Stillwater, Cushing, Perkins, Yale, Glencoe, Ripley, Mulhall (partly) and Quay (partly). Cushing became a major crude-oil storage and pipeline hub, often called the "Pipeline Crossroads of the World", and is a key pricing point for West Texas Intermediate crude oil.

Transportation

The county is served by Interstate 35 along its western edge, U.S. Route 177, and State Highways 33, 51 and 108. Stillwater Regional Airport provides commercial air service.
