Foot Guards

Foot Guards
The five regiments of Foot Guards of the British Army's Household Division.

Foot guards are senior infantry regiments forming part of a sovereign's household troops. The term is most closely associated with the Foot Guards of the British Army: five regiments — Grenadier, Coldstream, Scots, Irish, and Welsh Guards — that form, with the two Household Cavalry regiments, the Household Division. Historically, many European monarchies maintained foot guards regiments (such as the French Gardes françaises, the Prussian Potsdam Giants, and Spain's Guardia Real), and the institution survives in several countries.

The British Foot Guards

The five regiments, in order of precedence, are:

  • Grenadier Guards — formed 1656; white plume (left); buttons evenly spaced
  • Coldstream Guards — formed 1650 (raised for the Commonwealth, retained at the Restoration); red plume (right); buttons in pairs
  • Scots Guards — origins in 1642; no plume; buttons in threes
  • Irish Guards — formed 1900; blue plume (right); buttons in fours
  • Welsh Guards — formed 1915; white-and-green plume (left); buttons in fives

These distinctions of plume and button spacing allow the regiments to be identified in their otherwise similar ceremonial dress: scarlet tunic, tall bearskin cap, and white belt. In their public duties role, Foot Guards battalions mount guard at Buckingham Palace, St James's Palace, the Tower of London, and Windsor Castle (the King's Guard, famous for the Changing of the Guard), and provide the troops for Trooping the Colour.

Operational role

Guardsmen are first and foremost front-line infantry soldiers. Guards battalions served with distinction in both World Wars, the Falklands War, Iraq, and Afghanistan, working in the modern era as mechanised and light-role infantry, and as armoured infantry in the past. Each regiment maintains one battalion (the Irish and Welsh Guards one each; the Grenadiers, Coldstream, and Scots have also had companies held as independent public-duties increments). Guards training at the Infantry Training Centre, Catterick, is traditionally two weeks longer than for line infantry, reflecting the additional drill required.

Other countries

Canada maintains two foot guards regiments in its primary reserve — the Governor General's Foot Guards and the Canadian Grenadier Guards — modelled on the British Guards and performing ceremonial duties in Ottawa. Historical foot guards included the French Royal Guard regiments, the Russian Imperial Guard (Preobrazhensky and Semyonovsky regiments), and the Prussian/German Garde du Corps and 1st Foot Guards. Several modern republics retain guard units with a foot guards character for state ceremony.
