Empirical Bayes method

Empirical Bayes methods are procedures for statistical inference in which the prior distribution is estimated from the data. This approach stands in contrast to standard Bayesian methods, for which the prior distribution is fixed before any data are observed. Empirical Bayes, also called maximum marginal likelihood, was introduced by Herbert Robbins in the 1950s and developed extensively by Bradley Efron and Carl Morris in the 1970s.

Introduction

Suppose many related parameters θ1, …, θN are each estimated from separate observations. In an empirical Bayes analysis, the observations are pooled to estimate a common prior distribution for the parameters, and each individual estimate is then shrunk toward the overall mean by an amount that depends on the relative sizes of the sampling noise and the spread of the true parameters. The classic demonstration is the James–Stein estimator, which dominates the usual sample means under squared-error loss when three or more quantities are estimated simultaneously.

Methods of estimating the prior

Two broad strategies are used. The parametric empirical Bayes approach assumes the prior belongs to a family with a few hyperparameters, which are estimated by maximising the marginal likelihood of all the data; Efron and Morris's work on baseball batting averages is a famous application. The nonparametric empirical Bayes approach, originating with Robbins, avoids specifying the prior family and instead estimates the required posterior quantities directly from the observed distribution of the data.

Applications

Empirical Bayes methods are widely used in genomics, where thousands of genes are tested simultaneously; in small-area estimation in official statistics; in actuarial credibility theory; and in the moderation of variance estimates in high-throughput experiments. They are closely related to hierarchical Bayes models, from which they differ mainly in treating the hyperparameters as estimated constants rather than random quantities with their own priors.
