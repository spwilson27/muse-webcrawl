Trident (UK nuclear programme)

Trident, also known as the Trident nuclear deterrent, is the United Kingdom's nuclear weapons programme based on submarine-launched ballistic missiles. It consists of four Vanguard-class ballistic missile submarines based at HMNB Clyde (Faslane) in Scotland, armed with American-supplied Trident II D5 missiles carrying British-built nuclear warheads. Since April 1969, under its predecessor Polaris and then Trident, the Royal Navy has maintained continuous at-sea deterrence (CASD): at least one submarine is always on patrol.

History

The government of Margaret Thatcher announced in 1980 that Britain would buy Trident to replace Polaris. The first Vanguard submarine entered service in 1994, and by 1998 Trident had become Britain's sole nuclear weapons system, with free-fall bombs retired. The stockpile was reduced after the Cold War to operationally available warheads numbering in the low hundreds, though the 2021 Integrated Review raised the stockpile cap.

Replacement

Parliament voted in 2016 to renew the deterrent. The Vanguard boats will be replaced from the early 2030s by four Dreadnought-class submarines, which will carry the Trident D5 missile into the 2060s. Renewal has been contested by disarmament campaigners and by the Scottish National Party, which opposes basing the system in Scotland.

Operation

Missiles are drawn from a shared US/UK pool maintained at Kings Bay, Georgia, while warheads are designed and made at the Atomic Weapons Establishment, Aldermaston. Release authority rests with the prime minister; each new prime minister writes sealed "letters of last resort" instructing submarine commanders what to do if the UK government is destroyed.
