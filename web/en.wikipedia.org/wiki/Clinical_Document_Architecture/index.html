Clinical Document Architecture

Clinical Document Architecture (CDA)
Developer: Health Level Seven (HL7)
Format: XML-based document markup standard
Based on: HL7 Reference Information Model (RIM)
First release: 2000 (Release 1)

The Clinical Document Architecture (CDA) is an XML-based markup standard developed by Health Level Seven (HL7) that specifies the structure and semantics of clinical documents for the purpose of exchange between healthcare providers and patients. It is one of the most widely implemented standards for sharing clinical notes and summaries.

Structure

A CDA document consists of a header and a body. The header identifies the patient, author, custodian organisation, encounter and document type, enabling the document to be filed and retrieved in an electronic health record. The body contains the clinical content, which may be unstructured text or "structured entries" coded with standard vocabularies such as SNOMED CT and LOINC, so that key facts — diagnoses, medications, allergies, results — can be processed by computer.

Implementation guides

Because CDA is highly generic, its practical use depends on templates and implementation guides that constrain it for specific document types. In the United States, the Consolidated CDA (C-CDA) defines templates for documents such as the Continuity of Care Document, discharge summaries and progress notes, and was required by the Meaningful Use programme for certified health IT. The older Continuity of Care Document (CCD) harmonised CDA with the ASTM Continuity of Care Record.

Relationship to FHIR

CDA documents are persistent, attested clinical records, in contrast to the granular, API-oriented resources of HL7's newer FHIR standard. The two coexist: many organisations generate CDA documents from FHIR data, and document-sharing networks based on IHE XDS remain largely CDA-based.
