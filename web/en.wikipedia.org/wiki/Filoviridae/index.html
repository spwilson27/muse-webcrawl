Filoviridae

Filoviridae is a family of single-stranded, negative-sense RNA viruses in the order Mononegavirales. The family name is derived from the Latin filum, meaning "thread-like", referring to the filamentous morphology of the virions.

Taxonomy

The family includes the genera Ebolavirus, Marburgvirus, Cuevavirus, Dianlovirus, Striavirus, and Thamnovirus. Members include Ebola virus and Marburg virus, which cause severe viral hemorrhagic fever in humans and other primates.

Biology

Filovirus virions are enveloped, pleomorphic filaments about 80 nm in diameter and up to 14,000 nm long. Their genomes encode seven proteins, including the nucleoprotein, polymerase cofactor VP35, matrix protein VP40, and surface glycoprotein GP. Natural reservoirs are thought to include fruit bats.
