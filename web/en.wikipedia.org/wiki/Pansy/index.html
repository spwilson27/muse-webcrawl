The garden pansy (Viola × wittrockiana) is a type of large-flowered hybrid plant cultivated as a garden flower. It is derived by hybridisation from several species in the section Melanium of the genus Viola, particularly the wild pansy or heartsease, Viola tricolor. The name "pansy" comes from the French pensée, "thought", because the flower was regarded as a symbol of remembrance and of thoughtful reflection.

Cultivation

Pansies are hardy but short-lived perennials grown as annuals or biennials in temperate gardens. They flower in cool weather — in spring and autumn in mild climates and through winter in warmer zones — in a wide range of colours, often with dark central blotches ("faces"). Nineteenth-century breeders in Britain, notably at the nurseries of James Lee and others, transformed the small wild heartsease into the large show pansies displayed at flower shows.

In culture

The pansy appears in Shakespeare: Ophelia distributes "pansies, that's for thoughts", and in A Midsummer Night's Dream the juice of "love-in-idleness" (a pansy) is the love potion. The flower is also connected with the language of flowers as a token of remembrance.
