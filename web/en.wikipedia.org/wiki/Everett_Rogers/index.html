Everett M. Rogers (6 March 1931 – 21 October 2004) was an American communication theorist and sociologist, best known for originating the diffusion of innovations theory and for introducing the term "early adopter". His 1962 book Diffusion of Innovations became one of the most cited works in the social sciences.

Work

Rogers studied agricultural extension at Iowa State University, where field studies of how farmers adopted hybrid seed corn shaped his theory. He proposed that adopters of an innovation fall into five categories — innovators, early adopters, early majority, late majority, and laggards — distributed roughly in a bell curve, and that cumulative adoption follows an S-shaped curve. He also described the innovation-decision process and the roles of opinion leaders and communication channels.

Rogers taught at Ohio State University, Michigan State University, Stanford University, and the University of New Mexico, and applied diffusion research to family planning, public health, and development communication in Asia, Africa, and Latin America. Five editions of Diffusion of Innovations appeared between 1962 and 2003.
