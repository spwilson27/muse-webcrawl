Carol is a 2015 romantic drama film directed by Todd Haynes, with a screenplay by Phyllis Nagy based on Patricia Highsmith's 1952 novel The Price of Salt (later republished as Carol). Set in New York City in the early 1950s, it tells the story of a forbidden love affair between Therese Belivet, a young department-store clerk and aspiring photographer, and Carol Aird, an older woman trapped in a failing marriage and a custody battle.

Plot

Therese (Rooney Mara) meets Carol (Cate Blanchett) at the Manhattan department store where she works during the Christmas season. As their friendship deepens into love, Carol's estranged husband uses her relationship with Therese as evidence in their divorce proceedings, threatening Carol's custody of her daughter. The two women take a road trip westward, pursued by a private investigator, before Carol must choose between her daughter and living truthfully.

Reception

Carol premiered in competition at the 2015 Cannes Film Festival, where Mara won the Best Actress award (shared with Emmanuelle Bercot) and the film received the Queer Palm. It was widely hailed by critics for Haynes's direction, Edward Lachman's grainy Super 16mm cinematography, Carter Burwell's score, and Sandy Powell's costumes. The film received six Academy Award nominations, including Best Actress for Blanchett and Best Supporting Actress for Mara. In 2016 the BBC's poll of critics named it among the greatest films of the 21st century, and it regularly tops lists of the finest LGBTQ films ever made.
