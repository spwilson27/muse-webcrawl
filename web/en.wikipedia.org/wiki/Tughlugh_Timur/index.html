Tughlugh Timur (also transliterated Tughluq Temür; died 1363) was the first khan of Moghulistan, the eastern part of the divided Chagatai Khanate, reigning from about 1347 until his death. He is remembered for reunifying much of the Chagatai realm and for embracing Islam, which he promoted among the Mongol tribes of the east.

Reign

When the Chagatai Khanate split in the 1340s into the western Transoxiana and the eastern Moghulistan, Tughlugh Timur was raised to the throne in the east, reportedly at Aksu, at about eighteen years of age. Around 1354 he converted to Islam under the influence of Muslim clerics — a landmark in the Islamisation of the eastern Mongols. In 1360 and again in 1361 he invaded Transoxiana, temporarily bringing both halves of the khanate under his rule and appointing his son Ilyas Khoja as governor; resistance from local emirs, including the rising Amir Husayn and the young Timur (Tamerlane), eventually forced a Moghul withdrawal.

Death and succession

Tughlugh Timur died in 1363. His succession by Ilyas Khoja was contested, and the Dughlat emir Qamar-ud-Din soon seized power in Moghulistan, driving out the khan's family. His mausoleum stands near Almaliq in present-day Xinjiang.
