Masoud Barzani (Kurdish: مەسعوود بارزانی, Mesûd Barzanî; born 16 August 1946) is a Kurdish politician who has been the leader of the Kurdistan Democratic Party (KDP) since 1979, and was President of the Kurdistan Region of Iraq from 2005 to 2017. He is the son of Mustafa Barzani, the founder of the KDP and leader of the Kurdish nationalist movement in Iraq.

Early life

Barzani was born in Mahabad, Iran, where his father was in exile during the short-lived Republic of Mahabad. After its collapse the family returned to Iraq and was later exiled to the Soviet Union following the suppression of the Kurdish movement; they returned after the 1958 Iraqi revolution. Masoud joined the Peshmerga, the KDP's armed wing, as a young man and rose through its ranks.

Leadership of the KDP

After Mustafa Barzani's death in 1979, Masoud was elected leader of the KDP. During the Iran–Iraq War and the Anfal campaign the family suffered heavy losses, including the disappearance of thousands of Barzani men in 1983. In 1991, after the Gulf War and the Kurdish uprising, he became one of the principal leaders of the autonomous Kurdistan Region, though the 1990s saw a civil war between the KDP and the rival Patriotic Union of Kurdistan, ended by the US-brokered Washington Agreement of 1998.

Presidency

Following the 2003 invasion of Iraq, Barzani served on the Iraqi Governing Council. In 2005 he became the first President of the Kurdistan Region, and was re-elected in 2009. His term was controversially extended beyond 2013 amid disputes with opposition parties. During the war against the Islamic State from 2014, the Peshmerga under his presidency held a long front line and retook territory including areas around Mosul. In September 2017 he championed the Kurdistan independence referendum, which passed overwhelmingly but triggered a backlash from Baghdad, including the retaking of Kirkuk; Barzani stepped down as president on 1 November 2017, remaining head of the KDP.
