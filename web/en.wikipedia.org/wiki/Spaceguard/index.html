Spaceguard

The term Spaceguard loosely refers to a number of efforts to discover and study near-Earth objects (NEOs), especially those that may pose an impact hazard to Earth. It takes its name from Arthur C. Clarke's 1973 novel Rendezvous with Rama, in which a fictional Project Spaceguard is established after an asteroid impact.

History

In 1992, a report to the United States Congress led to a mandate directing NASA to catalogue 90 percent of near-Earth asteroids larger than one kilometer within ten years — an effort informally known as the Spaceguard Survey. The goal was substantially achieved in the following decades.

Organizations and surveys

Related efforts include the Spaceguard Foundation, a private organization founded in Rome in 1996 to promote the study and coordination of NEO observations, and national bodies such as the UK's Spaceguard Centre in Wales. Observational programs associated with the broader Spaceguard effort include LINEAR, the Catalina Sky Survey, Pan-STARRS, and Spacewatch. In 2005 the U.S. Congressional goal was extended by the George E. Brown, Jr. Near-Earth Object Survey Act to include objects as small as 140 meters.
