Pepper LaBeija (born William Jackson; November 5, 1948 – May 14, 2003) was an American drag queen and fashion designer, best known as the mother of the House of LaBeija and for her appearance in the 1990 documentary film Paris Is Burning. She was a central figure in New York City's ball culture and one of the last of the queens who founded the modern ball "house" system.

Life and career

LaBeija was born in the Bronx, New York City. She took over leadership of the House of LaBeija — the first ball house, founded by Crystal LaBeija — in the 1970s, becoming its second "mother" and presiding over it for roughly three decades. Under her leadership the house became renowned for its ballroom walks and for the elaborate productions with which she herself competed, arriving at balls in theatrical spectacles that became legend within the culture.

She gained wider public recognition through Jennie Livingston's documentary Paris Is Burning (1990), which chronicled the ball culture of 1980s New York. In the film LaBeija explains the house system, in which houses function as surrogate families for queer youth, many of them Black and Latino, who had been rejected by their biological families. She also appears in the 2006 follow-up documentary How Do I Look.

Later life and death

In her later years LaBeija lived in the Bronx and suffered from diabetes, which led to the amputation of both of her feet; she was largely bedridden for the last decade of her life. She died of a heart attack on May 14, 2003, at the age of 54. Following her death, leadership of the House of LaBeija passed to others, and the house — the oldest continuously active ball house — continues in her tradition.

Legacy

LaBeija is remembered as one of the foundational personalities of ball culture, and Paris Is Burning preserved her persona for a worldwide audience. She has been referenced and portrayed in subsequent works about the scene, including the television series Pose, in which the house system and its mothers figure centrally.
