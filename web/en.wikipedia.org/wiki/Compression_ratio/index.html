Compression ratio

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The compression ratio is the ratio between the volume of the cylinder and combustion chamber of an internal combustion engine when the piston is at the bottom of its stroke (maximum volume) and when it is at the top of its stroke (minimum, or clearance, volume). It is a fundamental specification for engines and a major determinant of thermal efficiency and of an engine's susceptibility to knocking.

Definition

The static compression ratio is (Vd + Vc) / Vc, where Vd is the displacement (swept) volume of one cylinder and Vc is the clearance volume remaining at top dead centre, including the combustion chamber and head gasket volume.

Typical values

Petrol (gasoline) engines in modern cars typically run compression ratios between about 9:1 and 12:1, with some direct-injection and Atkinson-cycle designs reaching 13:1 or 14:1. Diesel engines, which rely on compression ignition, use much higher ratios, commonly 14:1 to 22:1. Racing engines and engines designed for high-octane or alcohol fuels can exceed automotive norms, while forced-induction (turbocharged or supercharged) engines usually use lower static ratios to limit peak cylinder pressure.

Effects

A higher compression ratio raises the thermal efficiency of the Otto and Diesel cycles and increases torque and power for a given displacement. The limit in spark-ignition engines is knock (detonation): compressing the fuel–air mixture heats it, and beyond a point the end-gas auto-ignites. Fuel octane rating, direct injection, charge cooling, and combustion-chamber design all push the knock limit higher.

Variable compression ratio

Some production engines (notably Nissan's VC-Turbo) vary the compression ratio mechanically — roughly between 8:1 for power and 14:1 for efficiency — by changing the effective piston stroke with a multi-link crank mechanism. Variable valve timing that closes the intake valve late (Atkinson/Miller cycle) reduces the effective compression ratio without changing the geometric ratio.
