Esther

Esther (Hebrew: אֶסְתֵּר, ʾEstēr), born Hadassah, is the heroine of the Book of Esther in the Hebrew Bible. According to the narrative, she was a Jewish woman living in the Persian Empire who became queen of Persia and thwarted a planned genocide of her people.

The narrative

Esther was orphaned and raised by her cousin Mordecai in Susa, the Persian capital. When King Ahasuerus (generally identified with Xerxes I) deposed his queen Vashti, Esther was chosen to succeed her, concealing her Jewish identity. Mordecai uncovered a plot against the king and was recorded in the royal chronicles.

The king's chief minister, Haman, offended by Mordecai's refusal to bow to him, obtained a decree authorising the annihilation of all Jews in the empire on a day chosen by lot (pur). At Mordecai's urging—"who knows whether you have come to the kingdom for such a time as this?"—Esther risked her life by approaching the king unsummoned, revealed her identity, and exposed Haman's plot. Haman was hanged on the gallows he had built for Mordecai, and a counter-decree allowed the Jews to defend themselves.

Purim

The deliverance is commemorated by the festival of Purim, whose name derives from the lots Haman cast. Purim is celebrated with the public reading of the Book of Esther (the Megillah), gifts of food, charity to the poor, and a festive meal.

Historicity and interpretation

Most scholars regard the Book of Esther as a work of historical fiction or novella; it is notable among biblical books for never naming God. The book exists in a Hebrew version and in a longer Greek version containing additions read as canonical in Roman Catholic and Orthodox traditions.
