"Bela Lugosi's Dead" is a song by the English post-punk band Bauhaus. Released in August 1979 on the independent label Small Wonder Records, it was the band's debut single and is widely regarded as the first gothic rock record and one of the foundational releases of the genre. Running to over nine minutes, the song is a tribute to the Hungarian horror actor Bela Lugosi, famous for playing Count Dracula.

Recording and style

The song was written within weeks of the band's formation in Northampton in 1978, after guitarist Daniel Ash remarked on a television screening of a Lugosi film. It was recorded live in the studio in a single take at Beck Studios in Wellingborough. Musically it is built on a descending, heavily echoed guitar figure, a dub-influenced bass line by David J, sparse drums and Peter Murphy's deep, theatrical vocal, intoning lines such as "Undead, undead, undead". The arrangement's space, echo and funereal pace owed as much to dub reggae as to rock, distinguishing it from contemporary punk.

Release and influence

The 12-inch single, pressed on white vinyl with a sleeve image from the film The Sorrows of Satan, initially received little press attention but became a fixture of alternative club nights. It remained on the UK independent charts for years and was reissued by the band's later label Beggars Banquet. Because of the song, Bauhaus were dubbed the founders of goth — a label the band themselves resisted — but its influence on gothic rock, darkwave and later industrial and alternative music is widely acknowledged. The track became famous to a wider audience through its use in the opening sequence of Tony Scott's 1983 vampire film The Hunger, in which Bauhaus perform it in a nightclub.

Legacy

"Bela Lugosi's Dead" has been covered and sampled by numerous artists, performed by Bauhaus at virtually every concert since 1979, and frequently cited in lists of the greatest singles of the post-punk era. A live version recorded at Coachella in 2005 was released as a single. The song's title later became a byword for goth culture itself.
