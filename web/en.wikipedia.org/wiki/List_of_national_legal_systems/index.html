List of national legal systems

The legal systems of the world generally fall into a handful of traditions: civil law, common law, customary law, religious law (especially Islamic law) and mixed systems combining elements of several traditions. This list summarises which systems predominate in each country.

Legal traditions

Civil law

The most widespread family, derived from Roman law and codified in comprehensive codes. It predominates in most of continental Europe (France, Germany, Italy, Spain), Latin America, much of Asia (Japan, South Korea, China largely), and francophone Africa. Sub-traditions include the Napoleonic, Germanic, Nordic and socialist-influenced families.

Common law

Developed in England and spread through the former British Empire; law is built on judicial precedent. It predominates in England and Wales, the United States (except Louisiana), Canada (except Quebec), Australia, India, Pakistan, Nigeria, Bangladesh, Malaysia and Ireland.

Mixed systems

Many jurisdictions blend traditions: Scotland (civil/common), South Africa (Roman-Dutch and English), Quebec, Louisiana, Sri Lanka, the Philippines (Spanish civil and American common law), and Israel (Ottoman, British and religious elements).

Religious and customary law

Islamic law (Sharia) is the principal or a major source of law in Saudi Arabia, Iran and parts of other states, and applies to personal-status matters in many Muslim-majority countries. Customary law remains important in much of sub-Saharan Africa and the Pacific. Jewish law (Halakha) and canon law operate mainly within religious communities and specific personal-status domains.
