Scripps Institution of Oceanography

FocusEarth and ocean sciences

The Scripps Institution of Oceanography (SIO) is a centre for ocean and Earth science research and graduate education in La Jolla, California. Founded in 1903, it is one of the oldest and largest centres for marine research in the world and is part of the University of California, San Diego (UC San Diego), which was established around it in 1960.

History

Scripps began as a marine biological station supported by newspaper magnate E. W. Scripps and his half-sister Ellen Browning Scripps, with zoologist William Emerson Ritter as its first director. It moved to its present La Jolla site in 1905 and became part of the University of California in 1912. Under directors including Harald Sverdrup and Roger Revelle, it expanded into physical oceanography, geophysics and climate science, and played a major role in ocean research during and after the Second World War.

Research

Scripps scientists are known for the Keeling Curve — the continuous measurement of atmospheric carbon dioxide begun by Charles David Keeling at Mauna Loa in 1958 — as well as work on El Niño, plate tectonics, ocean circulation, marine biology and seismology. The institution operates research vessels, including R/V Roger Revelle and R/V Sally Ride, the FLIP research platform, and the Birch Aquarium at Scripps, its public aquarium-museum.
