Church of the Holy Cross, Aghtamar

The Church of the Holy Cross (Armenian: Ախթամարի Սուրբ Խաչ եկեղեցի) on Akdamar Island in Lake Van, Turkey, is a medieval Armenian Apostolic cathedral built between 915 and 921. It was commissioned by King Gagik I Artsruni of Vaspurakan and designed by the architect-monk Manuel. It is renowned for the exceptionally rich bas-relief carvings covering its exterior walls.

Architecture and sculpture

Built of red tuff stone, the church follows a domed, tetraconch-influenced cruciform plan. Its fame rests on the extensive carved friezes around the exterior, depicting biblical scenes — Adam and Eve, Jonah and the whale, David and Goliath, Daniel in the lions' den — together with saints, animals, vines, and courtly figures, including a celebrated image of King Gagik presenting a model of the church to Christ.

History

The church served as the seat of the Catholicosate of Aghtamar from 1113 until the late 19th century. The monastery was abandoned in 1915 during the Armenian genocide. In 2005–2007 the Turkish government restored the building, reopening it as a museum in March 2007; an annual liturgy has been permitted since 2010.
