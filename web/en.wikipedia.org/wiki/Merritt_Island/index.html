Merritt Island is a peninsula, commonly referred to as an island, in Brevard County, Florida, United States, located on the Atlantic coast of eastern Central Florida. It is also the name of a census-designated place in the central, southern part of the island, spanning 34,518 residents at the 2020 census.

The island is home to NASA's Kennedy Space Center and the Cape Canaveral Space Force Station lies just to the east across the Banana River. The southern and central parts contain residential communities, while the northern part is largely the Merritt Island National Wildlife Refuge, established in 1963 on land surplus to the space programme.

Geography

Merritt Island extends some 74 km (46 mi) from north to south and averages about 10 km in width, bordered by the Indian River to the west, the Banana River to the east, and the Banana Creek and Newfound Harbour. Despite the name, it is connected to the mainland at its northern end.

Wildlife refuge

The Merritt Island National Wildlife Refuge covers about 140,000 acres and provides habitat for more than 1,500 species of plants and animals, including threatened species such as the Florida scrub jay, sea turtles, and manatees. It overlaps with Canaveral National Seashore.

History

The island was settled by citrus growers and fishermen in the 19th century and is named for the Merritt family. Its transformation began in the early 1960s when NASA acquired land for the Apollo programme's launch facilities.
