Cole Albert Porter (1891–1964) was an American composer and songwriter. Born to a wealthy family in Indiana, he defied his grandfather's wish that he become a lawyer and took up music as his profession. Unusual among the major songwriters of his era, Porter wrote both the music and the lyrics of his songs, which are noted for their witty, urbane wordplay—including famous catalogues of rhyming lists—and sophisticated harmonies.

Career

After early years in Paris, Porter found Broadway success in the late 1920s and 1930s with shows such as Paris, Fifty Million Frenchmen, Gay Divorce, and Anything Goes (1934), whose score includes "I Get a Kick Out of You" and "You're the Top." His songs—among them "Night and Day," "Begin the Beguine," "I've Got You Under My Skin," "My Heart Belongs to Daddy," and "Love for Sale"—became pillars of the Great American Songbook, recorded by generations of jazz and popular singers.

Later life

A 1937 horseback-riding accident crushed both of Porter's legs, leaving him in chronic pain; after dozens of operations, his right leg was amputated in 1958. He nevertheless produced his greatest stage success, Kiss Me, Kate (1948), which won the first Tony Award for Best Musical, and film scores including High Society (1956). His life was portrayed, partly fictionalized, in the films Night and Day (1946) and De-Lovely (2004).
