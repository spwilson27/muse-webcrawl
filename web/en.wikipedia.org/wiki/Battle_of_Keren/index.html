Battle of Keren

The Battle of Keren was fought from 3 February to 27 March 1941 between Italian colonial forces and British Commonwealth forces during the East African campaign of the Second World War. Keren, a mountain town in Italian Eritrea, guarded the road and railway from the Red Sea port of Massawa to Asmara and the Eritrean interior.

Background

Advancing from Sudan, Lieutenant-General William Platt's Northern Force invaded Eritrea in January 1941. Italian commander Carnimeo chose Keren, ringed by steep granite peaks, as the position on which to make a stand, blocking the Dongolaas Gorge through which the road and railway passed.

The battle

For nearly eight weeks, the 4th and 5th Indian Divisions fought for the commanding heights — Mount Ziban, Fort Dologorodoc, Mount Sanchil and the feature known as Cameron Ridge — against determined Italian and Eritrean defenders. After heavy artillery preparation, a final assault in late March, supported by engineers clearing the gorge obstruction, broke the position; the Italians withdrew on the night of 26–27 March.

Aftermath

Keren was the hardest-fought battle of the East African campaign and is often compared to Monte Cassino for its mountain warfare character. Its fall opened the way to Asmara (1 April) and Massawa (8 April), effectively ending Italian resistance in Eritrea.
