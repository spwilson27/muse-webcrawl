Abbas Kiarostami

Abbas Kiarostami (Persian: عباس کیارستمی; 22 June 1940 – 4 July 2016) was an Iranian film director, screenwriter, poet and photographer, widely regarded as one of the most influential filmmakers of world cinema. A leading figure of the Iranian New Wave, he directed more than forty films over a career of five decades, noted for their use of non-professional actors, rural settings, long takes, and blending of documentary and fiction.

Career

Kiarostami began making films for Kanun, the Institute for the Intellectual Development of Children and Young Adults, in Tehran; his first feature was The Traveller (1974). International recognition came with the Koker trilogy — Where Is the Friend's Home? (1987), And Life Goes On (1992) and Through the Olive Trees (1994) — set in and around the village of Koker in northern Iran, the later films reflecting on the 1990 Manjil–Rudbar earthquake.

Close-Up (1990), a docufiction about a man who impersonated the filmmaker Mohsen Makhmalbaf, is frequently listed among the greatest films ever made. Taste of Cherry (1997), about a man seeking someone to bury him after his planned suicide, won the Palme d'Or at Cannes. Later works included The Wind Will Carry Us (1999), the experimental documentary Ten (2002), filmed entirely on dashboard cameras, and two films made abroad: Certified Copy (2010), shot in Tuscany with Juliette Binoche, and Like Someone in Love (2012), shot in Japan.

Death and legacy

Kiarostami died in Paris in July 2016, aged 76, following gastrointestinal surgery and complications that prompted controversy in Iran over his medical treatment. Directors including Akira Kurosawa, Jean-Luc Godard and Martin Scorsese praised his work; Kurosawa remarked that when Satyajit Ray died he became depressed, "but when I saw Kiarostami's films, I thanked God for giving us a man to take his place."
