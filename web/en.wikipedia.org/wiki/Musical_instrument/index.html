Musical instrument

A musical instrument is a device created or adapted to make musical sounds. In principle, any object that produces sound intentionally for musical purposes can be considered a musical instrument, though the term generally denotes artefacts purpose-built or conventionally used for music-making, from a simple folk flute to a concert grand piano or a computer running synthesis software.

History

Instruments are among the oldest human artefacts: bone and ivory flutes from the Swabian Jura caves (Geissenklösterle, Hohle Fels) date to roughly 40,000 years ago. Ancient civilisations developed lyres, harps, trumpets, drums and double-reed pipes; the medieval and Renaissance periods added bowed strings, keyboards and early brass. The modern orchestral families crystallised in the 17th–19th centuries, alongside industrial manufacturing, valves for brass instruments and Boehm-system woodwinds. The 20th century added electronic instruments — the theremin, Ondes Martenot, electric guitar, synthesizer and drum machine — and digital instruments controlled by computer.

Classification

The most widely used scholarly classification is the Hornbostel–Sachs system (1914), which groups instruments by how sound is produced: idiophones (the body of the instrument vibrates), membranophones (a stretched membrane), chordophones (strings), aerophones (a column of air) and electrophones (electric or electronic sound generation). In orchestral practice, instruments are more loosely grouped as strings, woodwind, brass and percussion, with keyboard instruments treated separately.
