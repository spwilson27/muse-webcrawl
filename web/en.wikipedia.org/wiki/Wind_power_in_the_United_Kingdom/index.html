Wind power in the United Kingdom


Installed capacity Over 30 GW (mid-2020s)
Share of electricity Roughly a quarter to a third of generation in recent years

Wind power in the United Kingdom is the country's largest source of renewable electricity and a major contributor to the national grid. The UK's long coastline and windy climate, especially in Scotland and the surrounding shallow seas, make it one of the world's best locations for both onshore and offshore wind energy.

History

Modern development began in the 1990s with small onshore farms supported by the Non-Fossil Fuel Obligation and later the Renewables Obligation and Contracts for Difference. Growth accelerated dramatically offshore from the 2010s, with the UK becoming the world leader in installed offshore capacity. Major projects include Hornsea, Dogger Bank — among the largest offshore wind farms in the world — and large Scottish onshore clusters.

Capacity and output

Total installed wind capacity passed 30 gigawatts in the mid-2020s, split between onshore and an expanding offshore fleet of ever-larger turbines. Wind regularly supplies more than a quarter of UK electricity annually and has on occasion supplied over half of demand at a given moment. In 2020, wind output first exceeded that of fossil gas generation for extended periods, and coal has been phased out of the grid entirely.

Policy and future

Government targets call for a substantially larger offshore fleet by 2030 as part of legally binding net-zero commitments. Challenges include grid connection queues, planning consent for onshore wind in England, supply-chain capacity, and the need for storage and interconnection to manage variability.
