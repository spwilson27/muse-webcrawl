The American Legion Riders (ALR) is a motorcycle riding association composed of members of The American Legion, the American Legion Auxiliary, and the Sons of The American Legion who share an interest in motorcycling. The program was founded in 1993 at Garden City, Michigan, Post 396 by Chuck "Tramp" Dare and Bill "Polka" Kaledas, and has grown to more than a thousand chapters and over 100,000 members across the United States and several other countries.

Activities

Legion Riders chapters organize charity rides, participate in memorial ceremonies and military funerals, escort military units returning from deployment, and raise funds for veterans' causes. Their best-known event is the annual American Legion Legacy Run, a multi-day cross-country motorcycle ride that raises money for the American Legion Legacy Scholarship Fund, which provides college scholarships to children of U.S. service members killed on active duty since September 11, 2001, and to children of post-9/11 veterans with significant VA disability ratings. The Legacy Run has raised millions of dollars since its inception in 2006.

Members also support the Patriot Guard Riders and take part in local community service and youth programs consistent with the aims of The American Legion.

Membership

Membership is open to current members of The American Legion family organizations who legally own and operate a motorcycle of at least 350cc. Riders are not a motorcycle club and do not wear three-piece patches; they wear a distinctive back patch identifying them as Legion Riders.
