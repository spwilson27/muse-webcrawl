1998 Russian financial crisis

The Russian financial crisis of 1998, also called the ruble crisis, began on 17 August 1998 when the Russian government devalued the ruble, defaulted on domestic debt (GKOs), and declared a moratorium on payments to foreign creditors.


Date: 17 August 1998
Country: Russia
Causes: fiscal deficits, Asian financial crisis, falling oil prices, short-term debt

Causes

Chronic fiscal deficits after the Soviet collapse, reliance on short-term GKO treasury bills at high interest rates, the 1997 Asian financial crisis, declining oil and metals prices, and political instability under President Boris Yeltsin undermined confidence.

Crisis and effects

The ruble fell from about 6 to 21 per US dollar within months; inflation reached 84% in 1998 and GDP contracted. Banks failed and savings were wiped out. The default contributed to the collapse of US hedge fund Long-Term Capital Management.

Recovery

Under Prime Minister Yevgeny Primakov and with rising oil prices, devaluation boosting domestic industry, Russia recovered surprisingly quickly, growing in 1999.
