Shaftesbury Memorial Fountain

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Shaftesbury Memorial Fountain, popularly known as Eros, is a fountain surmounted by a winged statue located at the south-eastern side of Piccadilly Circus in London. It was erected in 1892–93 to commemorate the philanthropic work of the 7th Earl of Shaftesbury, the Victorian politician and social reformer. The fountain was sculpted by Alfred Gilbert and was unveiled in 1893.

The statue

The fountain is topped by a nude winged archer cast in aluminium — one of the first statues in Britain to be made of that metal — depicting Anteros, the Greek god of selfless, reflective love, rather than his brother Eros. Gilbert chose Anteros deliberately, to reflect the Earl's character as a champion of the poor and the oppressed. The figure drew controversy on its unveiling because of its nudity, and it was popularly misidentified as Eros, the name by which it remains universally known. The archer aims his bow toward Shaftesbury Avenue, a punning gesture that was probably intentional.

History

The memorial stands at the centre of Piccadilly Circus, which had been laid out in the 1880s as Shaftesbury Avenue was cut through the surrounding slums. The statue was removed for safekeeping during both world wars and temporarily relocated during post-war reconstruction works. It has become one of London's best-known landmarks, frequently used as a meeting place and as a backdrop for public celebrations.
