North Korea and weapons of mass destruction

[Image: Missile display in Pyongyang]

North Korea (the Democratic People's Republic of Korea) has active programmes developing weapons of mass destruction, most prominently nuclear weapons and ballistic missiles, as well as assessed chemical and possible biological weapons capabilities. North Korea withdrew from the Treaty on the Non-Proliferation of Nuclear Weapons in 2003 and has conducted six nuclear tests, in 2006, 2009, 2013, twice in 2016, and 2017, the last claimed to be a thermonuclear device.

Nuclear programme

The programme is centred on the Yongbyon Nuclear Scientific Research Center, with reactors, reprocessing, and uranium enrichment facilities. External estimates of the arsenal have generally ranged in the dozens of warheads, with fissile material production continuing. In 2022 North Korea adopted a nuclear forces law declaring its nuclear status irreversible and authorising pre-emptive use in defined circumstances.

Missiles

The Korean People's Army fields short-, medium-, intermediate-, and intercontinental-range ballistic missiles, including the Hwasong-14, Hwasong-15, and solid-fuel Hwasong-18 ICBMs, submarine-launched missiles, and hypersonic glide vehicle claims. Tests resumed at record pace in 2022–2023 after a self-imposed moratorium.

International response

The United Nations Security Council has imposed successive rounds of sanctions since 2006. Diplomatic efforts have included the Six-Party Talks (2003–2009) and the 2018–2019 summits between Kim Jong Un and U.S. President Donald Trump, which produced statements on denuclearisation but no agreement. North Korea is also widely assessed to hold a large chemical weapons stockpile, and its agents were blamed for the 2017 assassination of Kim Jong-nam with VX nerve agent.
