Guard (grappling)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


The closed guard in Brazilian jiu-jitsu

In grappling, the guard is a ground position where one combatant has their back to the ground while the other combatant is within, on top of, or in control of the first combatant's legs. In the guard position, the combatant on the bottom can control the distance and position of the top combatant using the legs, and can apply joint locks, chokes, and sweeps. The guard is a defining position of Brazilian jiu-jitsu (BJJ) and is also used in judo (where it is known as dō-osae), mixed martial arts, and other submission grappling styles.

Types of guard

  • Closed guard (full guard): the bottom player's legs are wrapped around the opponent's waist with ankles crossed behind the back.
  • Open guard: the legs are not closed; variants include the butterfly guard (feet inside the opponent's thighs), spider guard (gripping sleeves with feet on the arms), De La Riva guard (a hook behind the opponent's leg), and the lasso guard.
  • Half guard: the bottom player traps one of the top player's legs between their own.
  • Rubber guard, X-guard, and 50/50 guard: more specialised systems developed for sport BJJ and MMA.

Strategy

From the guard, the bottom player seeks to submit the opponent (for example with a triangle choke, armbar, or kimura), to sweep them to reverse positions, or to return to the feet. The top player's objectives are to pass the guard — advancing to side control or mount — or, in MMA, to strike. Guard play is generally scored favourably in BJJ competition for the player who can attack from the bottom, while MMA judging has historically rewarded top control and ground-and-pound.
