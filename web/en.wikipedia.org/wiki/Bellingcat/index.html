Bellingcat

Bellingcat is a Netherlands-based investigative journalism organisation specialising in fact-checking and open-source intelligence (OSINT). Founded by British citizen journalist Eliot Higgins in 2014, it grew out of his blog analysing weapons in the Syrian civil war and took its name from the fable of "belling the cat." Bellingcat pioneered the public use of satellite imagery, geolocation and social-media forensics to investigate conflicts, assassinations and corruption, and trains journalists and researchers in the same techniques.

Notable investigations

Bellingcat's best-known work includes its investigation of the 2014 downing of Malaysia Airlines Flight MH17 over Ukraine, which traced the Buk missile launcher to Russia's 53rd Anti-Aircraft Missile Brigade — findings consistent with the official Dutch-led investigation. It also identified the Russian GRU officers suspected in the 2018 poisoning of Sergei and Yulia Skripal in Salisbury, investigated the poisoning of Alexei Navalny, geolocated atrocities in the Russo-Ukrainian war since 2022, and documented events in Yemen, Syria and elsewhere. Its methods guides, freely published, have been widely adopted by newsrooms.

Organisation and reception

The group is funded by grants, donations, training workshops and book sales; Higgins published We Are Bellingcat in 2021. It has received journalism awards including the Hanns Joachim Friedrichs Award, but has also been criticised — particularly by the Russian government, which designated it a "foreign agent" and an "undesirable organisation" — over its funding sources and perceived alignment with Western governments, criticisms Bellingcat rejects.
