51 Eridani is a young F-type main-sequence star in the constellation Eridanus, about 96 light-years from the Sun. It is a member of the Beta Pictoris moving group and is estimated to be only about 20 million years old. Although of the fifth magnitude, it is just barely visible to the naked eye under dark skies.

Planetary system

In 2015, astronomers using the Gemini Planet Imager announced the direct imaging of an exoplanet orbiting the star, designated 51 Eridani b. The planet is a young gas giant of roughly twice the mass of Jupiter, orbiting at about 13 astronomical units — comparable to Saturn's distance from the Sun — and is the faintest, coolest exoplanet yet directly imaged at the time of discovery. Its spectrum shows strong methane absorption, resembling the giant planets of the Solar System, and its youth means it still glows from the heat of its formation.

Debris disk

The star also shows an infrared excess indicating a circumstellar debris disk, consistent with its youth and with ongoing planet formation processes in the system.
