Le Canarien

Le Canarien is the name given to the chronicle of the conquest of the Canary Islands by the Norman knight Jean de Béthencourt and Gadifer de la Salle in the early 15th century. Composed by the expedition's chaplains Pierre Bontier and Jean Le Verrier, it is the principal narrative source for the Castilian conquest of the islands.

Content

The chronicle recounts the expedition that sailed from La Rochelle in 1402, the landings on Lanzarote, Fuerteventura, El Hierro, and La Gomera, the founding of settlements, conflicts and conversions among the indigenous Guanche population, and the growing rivalry between Béthencourt and Gadifer. It exists in two main versions: one more favorable to Gadifer, and a later redaction favoring Béthencourt, with which the work is generally associated.

Historical importance

Le Canarien is one of the earliest European chronicles of Atlantic expansion and a key source for the customs and languages of the Canary Islanders before colonization. It also documents how Béthencourt did homage to Henry III of Castile, bringing the islands into the Castilian sphere.
