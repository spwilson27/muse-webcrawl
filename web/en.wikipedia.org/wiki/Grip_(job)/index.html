Grip (job)

Occupation typeSkilled trade / film crew
IndustryFilm, television, theatre, live events
Key skillsRigging, carpentry, mechanics, teamwork, safety
UnionIATSE (in North America)

A grip is a member of a film, television, theatre, or live-event crew responsible for building, rigging, and operating the physical equipment that supports cameras and shapes lighting. On film sets the job sits within the grip department, headed by the key grip.

Duties

Depending on the production, a grip may lay dolly track, assemble cranes, rig lights and overhead frames, hang backdrops, move scenery and set pieces, and maintain safe working loads on all rigging. In theatre and touring, grips also handle stage machinery and flying scenery.

Training and career path

Grips typically learn on the job, progressing from trainee or "third grip" through best boy grip to key grip. The work demands mechanical aptitude, knowledge of knots and rigging hardware, physical stamina, and strict attention to safety. In North America most professional grips are members of IATSE locals.

Related roles

Related jobs include the gaffer (head electrician), rigging grip, dolly grip, stagehand, and rigger. In some production traditions, such as in much of Europe, comparable duties fall to the machinist (machiniste) crew.
