Morgan Library & Museum

The Morgan Library & Museum (formerly the Pierpont Morgan Library) is a museum and research library in the Murray Hill neighbourhood of Manhattan, New York City, at 225 Madison Avenue at East 36th Street. It was founded to house the private library of the financier J. P. Morgan and opened to the public in 1924.

Collections

The Morgan holds one of the world's great collections of illuminated manuscripts, rare books, old master drawings, prints, and literary and historical manuscripts. Treasures include three Gutenberg Bibles, the Lindau Gospels, the sole surviving manuscript of Milton's Paradise Lost, Dickens's manuscript of A Christmas Carol, original scores by Mozart, Beethoven, and Chopin, and a vast collection of incunabula and bindings. Its drawings collection spans Michelangelo, Leonardo, Rembrandt, and Rubens.

Buildings

The core is Morgan's palatial 1906 library, designed by Charles Follen McKim of McKim, Mead & White in an Italian Renaissance style, with the financier's richly decorated study and library rooms. His son J. P. Morgan Jr. made the collection public in 1924. A major expansion and renovation by Renzo Piano, completed in 2006, unified the campus with a light-filled central court.
