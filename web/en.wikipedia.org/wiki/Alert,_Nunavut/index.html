Alert, in the Qikiqtani Region of Nunavut, Canada, is the northernmost permanently inhabited place in the world. It lies on the northeastern tip of Ellesmere Island, only 817 kilometres (508 mi) from the geographic North Pole, and is operated as Canadian Forces Station Alert.

History

The site is named after HMS Alert, a British ship that wintered nearby in 1875–76. A weather station was established in 1950 as part of the Canada–US Joint Arctic Weather Station system, and in 1958 the Royal Canadian Corps of Signals set up a signals intelligence station, which became CFS Alert. The station also hosts an Environment Canada weather office and research facilities.

Geography and climate

Alert has a polar climate with long, dark winters — the sun does not rise for months around midwinter — and continuous daylight in summer. Temperatures average around −33 °C in February and only a few degrees above freezing in July. The settlement includes an airfield (Alert Airport), among the northernmost in the world.

Operations

Personnel rotate through on tours of duty; there is no permanent civilian population. The station supports signals intelligence, Arctic research, search and rescue, and Canadian sovereignty in the High Arctic. In 1991 a Royal Canadian Air Force CC-130 Hercules crashed on approach to Alert, killing five of the 18 people aboard.
