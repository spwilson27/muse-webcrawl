Capitalism and Slavery

Capitalism and Slavery is a 1944 book by Eric Williams, a historian and later prime minister of Trinidad and Tobago. Developed from his Oxford doctoral thesis, it argues that slavery and the slave trade provided much of the capital that financed Britain's Industrial Revolution, and that abolition followed slavery's economic decline.

Capitalism and Slavery
Author: Eric Williams
Published: 1944
Subject: Economic history of slavery

Thesis

Williams advanced two linked claims: first, that profits from slavery, sugar plantations, and the slave trade funded British industrialisation; second, that as British capitalism matured, the West Indian slave system became less profitable and abolition in 1807 and 1833 reflected economic interest rather than humanitarianism alone — the so-called "decline thesis" or Williams thesis.

Reception

The book was neglected in Britain for decades but became foundational to Caribbean historiography and later debates about slavery's role in modern economic development. Its decline thesis has been challenged by historians such as Seymour Drescher, while its account of slavery's profits has influenced recent scholarship and public debate.
