Sierpinski number

In number theory, a Sierpinski number (also spelled Sierpiński number) is an odd natural number k such that every number of the form k·2n + 1 is composite for all natural numbers n. In other words, when k is a Sierpinski number, all members of the set { k·2n + 1 : n ∈ ℕ } are composite.

The concept is named after the Polish mathematician Wacław Sierpiński, who in 1960 proved that there are infinitely many odd integers k with this property. Numbers of the form k·2n + 1 are called Proth numbers, after François Proth; a prime among them is a Proth prime.

Known Sierpinski numbers

The sequence of known Sierpinski numbers begins 78557, 271129, 271577, 322523, 327739, 482719, 575041, 603713, 903983, 934909, ... The number 78557 was shown to be a Sierpinski number by John Selfridge, using the covering set {3, 5, 7, 13, 19, 37, 73}: for every n, one of these primes divides 78557·2n + 1, because the residues of 2n modulo each prime cycle in a pattern that "covers" all values of n.

The Sierpinski problem

The Sierpinski problem asks: what is the smallest Sierpinski number? Selfridge conjectured that 78557 is the smallest. To prove this, it suffices to show that for every odd k < 78557 there is some n with k·2n + 1 prime. The distributed computing project Seventeen or Bust (2002–2016) eliminated all but the last few candidates below 78557 by finding enormous Proth primes; its surviving searches were continued by PrimeGrid's Seventeen or Bust subproject. As computations proceed, only a handful of candidate values of k remain uneliminated, so the conjecture, while not fully proved, is supported by the elimination of the great majority of cases.

Related concepts

The dual notion is the Riesel number: an odd k such that every number k·2n − 1 is composite. The smallest candidate is 509203, and its status is likewise pursued by distributed search (the Riesel Sieve and PrimeGrid). Further generalisations consider k·bn ± 1 for other bases b.
