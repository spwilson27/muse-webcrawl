Amotz Zahavi, Israeli evolutionary biologist

Amotz Zahavi (1 January 1928 – 12 May 2017) was an Israeli evolutionary biologist and ornithologist, best known for proposing the handicap principle in 1975. Born in Petah Tikva, he studied at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem and spent long periods studying the social behaviour of Arabian babblers in the Negev desert.

Handicap principle

Zahavi argued that costly signals — such as the peacock's extravagant tail — are reliable precisely because they are handicaps: only genuinely fit individuals can afford them, so the signal honestly advertises quality and cannot be profitably faked. Initially controversial, the idea was supported by Alan Grafen's game-theoretic models in 1990 and is now a cornerstone of signalling theory. With his wife Avishag Zahavi he wrote The Handicap Principle (1997).

Other work

Zahavi co-founded the Society for the Protection of Nature in Israel (1953) and won the Israel Prize in 1980 for his contribution to nature conservation. He was a professor at Tel Aviv University.
