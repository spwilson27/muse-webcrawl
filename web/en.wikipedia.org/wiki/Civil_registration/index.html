Civil registration

Civil registration is the system by which a government records the vital events (births, marriages, and deaths) of its citizens and residents into an official register, creating legal documents such as birth certificates, marriage certificates and death certificates.

Purpose

Civil registration provides individuals with legal identity and evidence of status and family relationships, and provides governments with vital statistics for planning health, education and other services. The United Nations promotes complete civil registration and vital statistics (CRVS) systems worldwide.

History

Modern state systems generally replaced or supplemented earlier church parish registers. England and Wales established compulsory civil registration in 1837, while France introduced civil registers during the Revolution, in 1792.
