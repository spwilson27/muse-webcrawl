Provisional State Council

The Provisional State Council (Hebrew: מועצת המדינה הזמנית, Moetzet HaMedina HaZmanit) was the temporary legislature of the State of Israel from its declaration of independence on 14 May 1948 until the first Knesset was elected in February 1949. Together with the Provisional Government, it formed the interim governing apparatus of the new state during the 1948 Arab–Israeli War.

Formation

The Council grew out of Moetzet HaAm (the People's Council), a 37-member body formed in April 1948 by the Jewish Agency and the Vaad Leumi to prepare for statehood, representing the spectrum of Zionist political parties. On 14 May 1948, members of the People's Council signed the Israeli Declaration of Independence; thirteen of them formed the Provisional Government, with David Ben-Gurion as prime minister.

Work

The Council enacted the Law and Administration Ordinance of 1948, which provided legal continuity by retaining Mandatory law except where superseded, and passed emergency regulations governing the war effort, food rationing and the establishment of the Israel Defense Forces. It elected Chaim Weizmann as President of the Provisional State Council, in effect the head of state, before he became Israel's first President.

Transition

Elections for a Constituent Assembly were held on 25 January 1949. The Assembly convened on 14 February 1949 and, under the Transition Law, became the First Knesset, ending the Council's existence.
