Syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion

The syndrome of inappropriate antidiuretic hormone secretion (SIADH), also known as the syndrome of inappropriate antidiuresis, is characterised by excessive release of antidiuretic hormone (vasopressin) from the posterior pituitary gland or another source. The resulting water retention dilutes the blood, producing hyponatraemia despite a normal or slightly increased total body water volume.

Causes

Causes include malignancies (notably small-cell lung cancer), central nervous system disorders, pulmonary disease, and many drugs, including certain antidepressants, anticonvulsants, and cytotoxic agents. In SIADH the urine remains inappropriately concentrated relative to plasma osmolality.

Signs and treatment

Symptoms are mainly those of hyponatraemia: nausea, headache, confusion, and in severe cases seizures. Management centres on treating the underlying cause and restricting free water intake; severe or symptomatic cases may require hypertonic saline, and vasopressin receptor antagonists ("vaptans") are used in selected patients. Chronic hyponatraemia must be corrected slowly to avoid osmotic demyelination syndrome.
