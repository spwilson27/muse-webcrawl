Canned laughter

Canned laughter is pre-recorded laughter added to the soundtrack of television and radio comedy to simulate an audience response. It is closely associated with the laugh track developed in the 1950s.

Medium: Television, radio
Pioneer: Charles Douglass

History

Sound engineer Charles Douglass developed the "Laff Box" in the 1950s to sweeten audience reactions on filmed sitcoms such as I Love Lucy-era productions and later series.

Criticism

Critics have called canned laughter artificial, while producers argue it signals jokes and creates a communal viewing feeling. Many modern single-camera comedies dispense with it entirely.
