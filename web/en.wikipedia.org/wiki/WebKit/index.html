WebKit

WebKit is a browser engine developed by Apple and used primarily in the Safari web browser, as well as all web browsers on iOS and iPadOS. It is open-source software, with components released under the GNU LGPL and BSD licences.

History

WebKit originated in 2001 as a fork of the KHTML and KJS engines from the KDE project, developed by Apple for its Safari browser, released in 2003. Apple opened the source in 2005. The engine's two main components became WebCore (HTML/CSS rendering, from KHTML) and JavaScriptCore (from KJS, later including the SquirrelFish and Nitro JIT engines). A major rework, WebKit2, introduced a multiprocess architecture in 2010.

Forks and users

Google forked WebCore to create the Blink engine in 2013, which now powers Chrome and Chromium-based browsers. WebKit remains the engine for Safari on macOS and, by platform policy, for all browsers on iOS. It is also used by various embedded platforms and formerly powered browsers on BlackBerry, Symbian, and the PlayStation systems.
