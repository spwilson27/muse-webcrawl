Transcarpathia

Transcarpathia (Ukrainian: Закарпаття, Zakarpattia) is a historical region in western Ukraine largely coterminous with the modern Zakarpattia Oblast, on the inner (south-western) slopes of the Carpathian Mountains. It borders Poland, Slovakia, Hungary and Romania, and its administrative centre is Uzhhorod. The region covers about 12,800 km² and has roughly 1.25 million inhabitants.

History

For a thousand years the region belonged to the Kingdom of Hungary, later forming part of Austria-Hungary. After the First World War it was assigned to Czechoslovakia as Subcarpathian Ruthenia; Hungary annexed it in 1938–39; and in 1945 it was ceded to the Ukrainian SSR of the Soviet Union — unusually late, meaning its population had never been part of the Russian Empire. It became part of independent Ukraine in 1991.

Population

Transcarpathia is ethnically diverse: Ukrainians (including the Rusyn highlander groups Boykos, Hutsuls and Lemkos) form the majority, with significant Hungarian, Romanian, Roma, Slovak and Russian minorities concentrated in border districts. Several wooden churches of the region are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

Geography

The landscape is dominated by forested Carpathian ridges, including Hoverla, Ukraine's highest peak (2,061 m), and by the Tisza river lowland in the south-west. The region is known for winegrowing around Berehove and for mineral springs.
