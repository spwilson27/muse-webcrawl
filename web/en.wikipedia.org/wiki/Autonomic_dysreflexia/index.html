Autonomic dysreflexia

Autonomic dysreflexia (AD), also known as autonomic hyperreflexia, is a potentially life-threatening medical emergency occurring in people with spinal cord injury at or above the T6 level, characterised by a sudden, uncontrolled rise in blood pressure triggered by stimuli below the level of injury.

Mechanism

A noxious stimulus below the lesion — most commonly bladder distension, but also bowel impaction, pressure sores or tight clothing — triggers a spinal reflex causing widespread sympathetic vasoconstriction. Because descending inhibitory signals cannot pass the injury, blood pressure rises unchecked; baroreceptor responses cause compensatory bradycardia and vasodilation above the lesion.

Symptoms

Signs include pounding headache, flushing and sweating above the lesion, nasal congestion, anxiety and severe hypertension, which may cause intracranial haemorrhage, seizures or death if untreated.

Management

Immediate steps are to sit the patient upright, loosen clothing and remove the triggering stimulus (e.g. catheterise the bladder). Short-acting antihypertensives such as nifedipine or nitrates may be used. Prevention relies on regular bladder and bowel care.
