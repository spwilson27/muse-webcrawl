John Russell, 1st Earl Russell, KG, GCMG, PC, FRS (18 August 1792 – 28 May 1878), known as Lord John Russell before 1861, was a British Whig and Liberal statesman who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 1846 to 1852 and again from 1865 to 1866. A younger son of the Duke of Bedford, he was a leading architect of nineteenth-century parliamentary reform, instrumental in the passage of the Reform Act 1832 and a lifelong advocate of civil and religious liberty.

Early life and the Reform Act

Russell was born into one of the great Whig families and entered Parliament in 1813. He became the Commons' leading spokesman for parliamentary reform, introducing reform bills through the 1820s, repealing the Test and Corporation Acts (1828) and supporting Catholic emancipation. As Paymaster General in Earl Grey's government he was one of the four-man committee that drafted the Reform Bill, and he piloted it through the Commons in 1831–32 — earning the nickname "Finality Jack" after declaring the settlement "final", a position he later abandoned.

First premiership (1846–1852)

Russell became prime minister after the fall of Sir Robert Peel in 1846, governing with a minority amid the catastrophe of the Great Famine in Ireland, where his doctrinaire free-market relief policies have been heavily criticised. His government nevertheless passed the Public Health Act 1848, promoted state-aided education, repealed the Navigation Acts, and faced down the last Chartist demonstrations and the revolutions of 1848 abroad. The ministry fell in 1852 after a parliamentary defeat.

Foreign secretary and second premiership

Russell served as Foreign Secretary under Lord Aberdeen and then under Lord Palmerston, with whom he frequently quarrelled; he supported Italian unification and sought to keep Britain neutral during the American Civil War. Raised to the peerage as Earl Russell in 1861, he succeeded Palmerston as prime minister in 1865 and, with William Ewart Gladstone, introduced a new reform bill in 1866. Its defeat split the Liberals and ended his career in office; the Conservatives under Derby and Disraeli then passed the Reform Act 1867.

Later life and legacy

Russell remained a revered elder statesman of Liberalism until his death in 1878. His grandson was the philosopher Bertrand Russell, who was raised in the earl's household at Pembroke Lodge, Richmond Park.
