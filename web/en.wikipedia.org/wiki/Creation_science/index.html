Creation science

Image: The Creation Museum in Petersburg, Kentucky (placeholder)

Creation science or scientific creationism is a pseudoscientific form of Young Earth creationism which claims to offer scientific support for the Genesis creation narrative and for a worldwide flood, and to refute or reinterpret the scientific evidence for evolution, geology and cosmology. It began in the 1960s as a Christian fundamentalist effort to present biblical creation in the language and authority of science, notably with John C. Whitcomb and Henry M. Morris's The Genesis Flood (1961) and the founding of the Institute for Creation Research (1970).

Claims

Creation science typically holds that the universe and Earth were created by God a few thousand years ago, that all living kinds were separately created in their present forms ("created kinds" or baramins), that a global flood explains most geological strata and fossils, and that radiometric dating and other evidence for an ancient Earth and common descent are mistaken. Concepts advanced include flood geology, catastrophic plate tectonics, vapor canopy theory and accelerated nuclear decay.

Scientific and legal status

The overwhelming consensus of the scientific community is that creation science is not science: it begins from religious conclusions, is not testable or falsifiable, and misrepresents the evidence. In McLean v. Arkansas (1982) a US federal court ruled creation science fails the criteria of science and is religious doctrine; in Edwards v. Aguillard (1987) the Supreme Court held that teaching creation science in public schools advances religion and violates the Establishment Clause. Creation science continues to be promoted by organisations such as Answers in Genesis, which operates the Creation Museum and Ark Encounter in Kentucky.
