Third Culture

Labelled placeholder: imagery representing the "third culture" of scientist-writers.

The Third Culture is a term popularised by the literary agent and author John Brockman in his 1995 book The Third Culture: Beyond the Scientific Revolution. It refers to scientists and other thinkers who, through their work and expository writing, are taking the place of the traditional intellectual in rendering visible the deeper meanings of our lives — communicating directly with the public rather than through literary intermediaries.

Background

The phrase builds on C. P. Snow's 1959 Rede Lecture, The Two Cultures, which lamented the gulf between the sciences and the humanities. In his 1991 essay "The Third Culture", Brockman argued that a new culture was emerging in which scientists such as Richard Dawkins, Stephen Jay Gould, Daniel Dennett and Steven Pinker wrote for a general audience about fundamental questions — the origin of the universe, life and mind — once monopolised by literary intellectuals.

Edge Foundation

Brockman's ideas are closely associated with the Edge Foundation and its online salon Edge.org, which publishes conversations with scientists and an annual "Question" posed to thinkers across disciplines. Responses to the annual questions have been collected in a series of edited volumes.

Criticism

Critics have argued that the "third culture" framing is largely a marketing device, that it privileges a narrow set of mostly Anglo-American popularisers, and that literary and humanistic scholarship continues to address the same public questions through other channels.
