TICCIH

The International Committee for the Conservation of the Industrial Heritage, usually known by its acronym TICCIH, is the world organisation for industrial heritage. It promotes international cooperation in the conservation, investigation, documentation and interpretation of industrial heritage, and acts as the specialist adviser to ICOMOS on industrial heritage for the UNESCO World Heritage List.

History

TICCIH grew out of the first international conferences on industrial archaeology held in the 1970s, beginning with the First International Congress on the Conservation of Industrial Monuments at Ironbridge, England, in 1973. The committee was formally established in 1978 at the Third International Congress in Stockholm.

Work

In 2003 TICCIH adopted the Nizhny Tagil Charter for the Industrial Heritage, the foundational international statement defining industrial heritage and the principles for its conservation. The organisation works through national committees and specialist sections covering sectors such as railways, textiles and mining, and its assessments have supported the inscription of industrial sites — including mines, mills, canals and factories — on the World Heritage List.
