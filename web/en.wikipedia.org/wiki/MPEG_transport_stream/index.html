The MPEG transport stream (MPEG-TS, MTS, or simply TS) is a standard digital container format for transmission and storage of audio, video, and programme and system information protocol (PSIP) data. It is specified in MPEG-2 Part 1, Systems (ISO/IEC 13818-1).

Structure

A transport stream multiplexes packetised elementary streams into a continuous sequence of fixed 188-byte packets. Each packet begins with a sync byte (0x47) and a header carrying a 13-bit packet identifier (PID), which identifies the elementary stream or table to which the packet belongs. Periodically inserted tables — the Program Association Table and Program Map Tables — describe the programmes and streams contained in the multiplex.

The small packet size and built-in error-correction tolerance make the format well suited to transmission over error-prone media, which is why it is used by digital television systems including DVB, ATSC, and IPTV, as well as Blu-ray discs (in the .m2ts variant with an extra 4-byte header).

Uses

Beyond broadcast television, transport streams are used in HTTP Live Streaming (HLS), where media is delivered as short .ts segments, and in many camcorders and personal video recorders.
