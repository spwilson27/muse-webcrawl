Round Table Talks

Date6 February – 5 April 1989
LocationWarsaw, Poland (Council of Ministers Palace)
PartiesPolish government / PZPR and the Solidarity-led opposition
OutcomeSemi-free elections of 4 June 1989; end of one-party rule in Poland

The Polish Round Table Talks (Rozmowy Okrągłego Stołu) were negotiations held in Warsaw from 6 February to 5 April 1989 between representatives of the communist government of the Polish People's Republic and the opposition centred on the Solidarity trade union. They produced the Round Table Agreement, which legalised Solidarity, introduced a partly free election, and set Poland on the path to the first non-communist government in the Eastern Bloc — a breakthrough that helped trigger the revolutions of 1989 across Central Europe.

Background

Poland had been in recurring crisis since Solidarity's suppression under martial law in 1981. Waves of strikes in 1988, a deteriorating economy, and the reform climate created by Mikhail Gorbachev's glasnost in the Soviet Union persuaded General Wojciech Jaruzelski's government to seek a negotiated "anti-crisis pact" with the opposition, despite the misgivings of hardliners. Preliminary private talks at Magdalenka, brokered with the participation of the Catholic Church, prepared the ground.

The talks

The plenary sessions took place around a specially built round table at the Council of Ministers Palace, while the detailed bargaining was done in three main working groups — on political reform, on trade-union pluralism, and on economic and social policy — and numerous sub-tables. The government side included Interior Minister Czesław Kiszczak; the opposition was led by Lech Wałęsa, with figures such as Bronisław Geremek, Jacek Kuroń, Adam Michnik, and Tadeusz Mazowiecki prominent among the negotiators.

Agreement and consequences

The agreement of 5 April 1989 relegalised Solidarity, created a new Senate elected in fully free voting, reserved 65% of Sejm seats for the ruling coalition while opening 35% to free contest, established a strong presidency, and promised further democratisation. In the elections of 4 and 18 June 1989, Solidarity won every freely contested Sejm seat and 99 of 100 Senate seats. After the communists' satellite parties defected, Mazowiecki became Prime Minister in August 1989 — the first non-communist head of government in the region — and Poland embarked on the transition to democracy and a market economy.
