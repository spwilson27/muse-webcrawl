NBA Jam


NBA Jam
Developer: Midway
Publisher: Midway, Acclaim
Release: 1993 (arcade)
Genre: Sports (basketball)
Modes: Single player, multiplayer

NBA Jam is a basketball arcade game published by Midway in 1993. It is the first entry in the NBA Jam series. The arcade game was a major commercial success and became one of the most popular sports video games of its era.

Gameplay

NBA Jam features two-on-two basketball, a significant departure from the five-on-five format of real basketball. Players choose from real NBA teams and licensed players of the 1992–93 season. The game dispenses with most rules: there are no fouls, free throws, or violations except goaltending and the 24-second shot clock, encouraging fast, physical play.

Players can perform exaggerated, gravity-defying slam dunks, shove opponents to the floor, and shoot from almost anywhere. A player who scores three consecutive baskets becomes "on fire", gaining unlimited turbo, increased accuracy, and the ability to make even more spectacular dunks until the opposing team scores. The catchphrase "Boomshakalaka!" and commentary by Tim Kitzrow, modelled on Marv Albert, became iconic.

Development and release

The game was developed by Midway, the studio behind Mortal Kombat, and used digitised graphics of real players. The NBA initially was reluctant to license the game, fearing association with arcade gambling, but Midway persisted. The arcade cabinet earned extraordinary revenue, reportedly over $1 billion in quarters in its first year. Home versions were published by Acclaim for the Super Nintendo, Sega Genesis, and other platforms.

Legacy

NBA Jam spawned sequels including NBA Jam Tournament Edition (1994), NBA Hangtime, and later revivals by Electronic Arts, including NBA Jam (2010) and NBA Jam: On Fire Edition (2011). The phrase "He's heating up" and "He's on fire" entered sports gaming culture. In 2019, a documentary and retrospective coverage marked the game as one of the defining arcade sports titles.
