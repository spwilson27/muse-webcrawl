The Book-of-the-Month Club (BOMC) is a United States subscription-based service that offers a selection of books monthly to its members, and a marketing and merchandising organization. It was founded in 1926 by Harry Scherman, Robert Haas, and Maxwell Sackheim, and it grew into one of the most influential forces in twentieth-century American publishing and reading, helping to create a national middlebrow reading culture.

History

Scherman had earlier pioneered mail-order book selling with the Little Leather Library. The Club's model: a board of judges — over the years including Clifton Fadiman, Christopher Morley, and other prominent critics — selected a featured book each month, which was mailed automatically to members unless they declined it, pioneering the "negative option" billing scheme. Its first selection was Sylvia Townsend Warner's Lolly Willowes. Membership climbed into the hundreds of thousands, and a BOMC selection could guarantee a bestseller. In 1977 the Club was acquired by Time Inc.; it later passed through Bertelsmann's Bookspan and DirectGroup. The original service wound down in 2014, and in 2015 the brand was relaunched by Pride Tree Holdings as a millennial-oriented subscription box, especially popular with women's fiction readers.

Model and influence

Members committed to buying a minimum number of books and received bonus books and discounts. The Club spawned imitators (the Literary Guild, founded 1927) and parallel operations in Britain and elsewhere. Critics debated whether its judges' selections uplifted or diluted American taste, but its role in distributing serious books to readers far from bookstores is widely acknowledged.
