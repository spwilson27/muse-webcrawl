Cybercrime

Cybercrime encompasses offences committed using computers and networks.

Cybercrime is criminal activity that either targets or uses a computer, a computer network, or a networked device. Most cybercrime is committed by cybercriminals or hackers motivated by profit, though it can also be carried out by individuals or groups seeking to damage systems, disrupt services, spread malware, steal data, or advance political aims.

Classification

Cybercrime is commonly grouped into crimes that target computer systems directly — such as hacking, malware distribution, ransomware, and denial-of-service attacks — and crimes in which computers are the instrument of a more traditional offence, including online fraud, identity theft, phishing, cyberstalking, and the distribution of illegal content. Financially motivated operations range from individual scams to organised ransomware-as-a-service enterprises.

Scale and response

Estimates of the global cost of cybercrime run into the trillions of dollars annually when direct losses, recovery costs, and reputational damage are counted. Responses include national legislation such as computer misuse and computer fraud statutes, international cooperation through instruments like the Budapest Convention on Cybercrime, specialised law-enforcement units, and defensive measures by organisations — patching, multi-factor authentication, backups, and incident-response planning.
