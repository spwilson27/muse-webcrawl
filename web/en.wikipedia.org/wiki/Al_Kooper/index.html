Al Kooper

Al Kooper (born Alan Peter Kuperschmidt)
Born: 5 February 1944, Brooklyn, New York, US
Genres: Rock, blues, R&B
Instruments: Keyboards, guitar, vocals
Years active: 1958–present

Al Kooper (born February 5, 1944) is an American songwriter, record producer and musician, known for organizing the group Blood, Sweat & Tears, providing studio support for Bob Dylan when he played the organ riffs on "Like a Rolling Stone", and bringing together guitarists Mike Bloomfield and Stephen Stills for the Super Session album.

Career

Kooper began as a teenage guitarist in the Royal Teens ("Short Shorts", 1958) and became a successful Brill Building songwriter, co-writing "This Diamond Ring". In 1965 he improvised the famous Hammond organ part on Dylan's "Like a Rolling Stone" despite being primarily a guitarist, then joined the Blues Project. He founded Blood, Sweat & Tears in 1967 but left after its debut album, Child Is Father to the Man (1968).

As a producer and A&R man, Kooper discovered and produced Lynyrd Skynyrd's first three albums, and he played on recordings by the Rolling Stones, B.B. King, Jimi Hendrix and many others. His memoir Backstage Passes and Backstabbing Bastards (1977) is a celebrated account of the era. He was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2023.
