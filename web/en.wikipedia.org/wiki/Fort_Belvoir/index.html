Fort Belvoir

Fort Belvoir is a United States Army installation in Fairfax County, Virginia, along the Potomac River south of Alexandria. One of the largest Army posts by workforce in the United States, it hosts numerous Department of Defense agencies and more than 200 tenant organizations.

History

The land traces to the 18th-century Belvoir plantation of William Fairfax, a cousin-adjacent landowner for whom Fairfax County is named; George Washington, whose Mount Vernon estate lies nearby, was a frequent visitor. The Army acquired the property in 1912 and established Camp A. A. Humphreys in 1918 as an engineer training camp, named for Civil War general and Chief of Engineers Andrew A. Humphreys. It was renamed Fort Belvoir in 1935 and long served as the home of the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers school before that mission moved to Fort Leonard Wood in 1988.

Modern role

Following the 2005 Base Realignment and Closure (BRAC) round, Fort Belvoir grew enormously, receiving the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency's headquarters campus (NGA East), the Fort Belvoir Community Hospital, and thousands of defense workers. The post is also home to the Defense Logistics Agency headquarters and the National Museum of the United States Army, which opened in 2020.

Geography

The installation covers about 8,600 acres in several parcels, including the main post, the Davison Army Airfield, and areas along the Potomac hosting extensive wildlife refuge and shoreline.
