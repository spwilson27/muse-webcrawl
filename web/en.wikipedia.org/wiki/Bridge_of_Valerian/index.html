Bridge of Valerian

Crosses: Karun River

Locale: Shushtar, Khuzestan, Iran

Built: c. 260–270 AD

Part of: Shushtar Historical Hydraulic System

The Bridge of Valerian (Persian: پل والرین) or Band-e Kaisar ("Caesar's Dam") is an ancient arched bridge and dam at Shushtar in southwestern Iran, traditionally said to have been built with Roman labour after the capture of the Roman emperor Valerian by the Sasanian king Shapur I in 260 AD. It is regarded as the easternmost example of Roman bridge and dam engineering, and forms part of the Shushtar Historical Hydraulic System, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

According to later Persian and Arab traditions, Shapur I employed Roman prisoners of war — including, legend holds, the captured emperor Valerian himself — to construct the great hydraulic works at Shushtar around 260–270 AD. The combined weir and bridge diverted water from the Karun River into irrigation channels, raising the water level to supply the city and surrounding farmland. Although the story of Valerian's personal involvement is likely embellished, Roman engineering influence on the structure is considered plausible.

Description

The structure stretches roughly 500 metres across the river on arched masonry piers, combining the functions of bridge and diversion dam. It has been repaired repeatedly over the centuries, including extensive rebuilding in the Islamic era, and parts of the ancient fabric survive. Together with canals, tunnels, and watermills, it anchored an irrigation system that supported Shushtar's agriculture for over a millennium.

Legacy

The Band-e Kaisar remained in use into the modern era. In 2009 UNESCO inscribed the Shushtar Historical Hydraulic System on the World Heritage List, describing it as a masterpiece of creative genius drawing on Elamite, Mesopotamian, and Nabataean knowledge as well as Roman construction techniques.
