Black start

FieldPower systems engineering
PurposeRestarting a power grid after a total or partial blackout

A black start is the process of restoring an electric power grid to operation after a complete or partial shutdown, without relying on an external power supply. It is a core part of grid recovery planning after major blackouts.

Process

Most power stations need electricity from the grid to start their own generators — for fuel pumps, controls, and excitation. Black start resources are units that can start unaided: typically hydroelectric stations, gas turbines with on-site diesel generators or batteries, and specially equipped plants. A black start unit energizes a small "island" of the network, which is then used to start larger power stations in sequence; the islands are gradually synchronized and reconnected until the full grid is restored.

Planning

Grid operators contract black start services in advance and test them regularly, since restoration speed determines how long a blackout lasts. Historically, full restorations have taken from several hours to several days depending on the extent of the failure.
