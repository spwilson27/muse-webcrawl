Fetal rights

Fetal rights are the moral rights or legal rights of the human fetus under natural and civil law. The term is most often used in the context of debates over abortion, where it refers to claims that a fetus has a right to life or other protections before birth. Legal treatment of the fetus varies widely by jurisdiction: in many legal systems a fetus has no independent legal personhood until birth, while others grant varying degrees of protection at stages of gestation, such as viability.

History

Historically, many legal traditions applied the "born alive rule", under which a child had to be born alive to be considered a legal person for purposes such as homicide or inheritance law. Roman law recognized the concept of nasciturus, treating an unborn child as if already born for certain inheritance purposes, provided it was subsequently born alive. Advances in medicine, including ultrasound imaging and neonatal care, shifted public and legal discussion in the twentieth century.

Legal status by country

Countries differ considerably. Some constitutions expressly protect unborn life; others protect fetal interests only through statute. Many jurisdictions have fetal homicide laws that treat the killing of a fetus as a crime when committed against a pregnant woman, often excluding lawful abortion. In the United States, the Unborn Victims of Violence Act of 2004 recognized an embryo or fetus as a legal victim in certain federal crimes. In international law, instruments such as the American Convention on Human Rights state that the right to life shall be protected "in general, from the moment of conception", while other treaties are silent on the question.

Related debates

Fetal rights discourse overlaps with debates about maternal autonomy, medical treatment during pregnancy, substance use in pregnancy, and the legal status of embryos in assisted reproduction and stem cell research.
