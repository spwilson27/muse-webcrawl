The 1904 Summer Olympics, officially the Games of the III Olympiad, were held in St. Louis, Missouri, United States, from 1 July to 23 November 1904, as part of the Louisiana Purchase Exposition (the St. Louis World's Fair). They were the first Olympic Games held outside Europe and the first held in North America.

Background

The Games had originally been awarded to Chicago, but were moved to St. Louis so that they would not compete with the World's Fair. Owing to the cost and difficulty of transatlantic travel — and the ongoing Russo-Japanese War — only 651 athletes from 12 nations took part, and about four-fifths of them were from the United States. Many events were effectively US national championships. The host nation won 76 gold medals and 234 medals in total, by far the largest haul of the Games.

Highlights

These were the first Games at which gold, silver and bronze medals were awarded to the first three finishers. Boxing, freestyle wrestling, weightlifting and the decathlon made their Olympic debuts, while sports such as lacrosse, roque and tug of war appeared for the last or only times. The gymnast George Eyser won six medals despite having a wooden prosthetic leg. Archie Hahn won the 60 m, 100 m and 200 m sprints, and Ray Ewry again swept the standing jumps.

The marathon became notorious: the race was run in extreme heat over dusty unpaved roads, and winner Thomas Hicks was carried over the finish line by his handlers after being given strychnine and brandy. Fred Lorz, who crossed the line first, admitted he had ridden in a car for much of the course and was disqualified. The Games were also marred by the "Anthropology Days" side events, in which indigenous people brought to the fair were made to compete in pseudo-scientific contests — a episode now widely condemned as racist.

Legacy

Because the sporting programme was stretched thinly across nearly five months and subordinated to the fair, the 1904 Games are often considered the low point of the early Olympic movement. Nonetheless, they established the medal system and several sports that remain Olympic staples. Francis Field, built for the Games at Washington University, is still in use.
