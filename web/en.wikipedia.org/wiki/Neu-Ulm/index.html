Neu-Ulm

Neu-Ulm is a town in Bavaria, Germany, on the right (eastern) bank of the Danube, opposite the city of Ulm in Baden-Württemberg. It is the capital of the Neu-Ulm district and has a population of roughly 60,000.

History

Neu-Ulm's history is closely tied to that of Ulm. When Ulm became part of the Kingdom of Württemberg in 1810, the settlements on the Bavarian bank of the Danube were separated from the city and in 1814 formed the town of Neu-Ulm in the Kingdom of Bavaria. In the 19th century it developed as a fortress and garrison town of the German Confederation's fortress of Ulm. After the Second World War it hosted a large United States Army garrison, whose departure in 1991 reshaped the town's development.

Geography

The town lies where the Iller flows into the Danube. Together Neu-Ulm and Ulm form a cross-border urban centre of about 180,000 inhabitants. Landmarks include the water tower, a local symbol, and views across the river to Ulm Minster, which has the tallest church spire in the world.

Economy and transport

Neu-Ulm is a centre for logistics, vehicle manufacturing suppliers and services. It is connected by the A7 and A8 motorways and by rail on the line between Stuttgart and Munich.
