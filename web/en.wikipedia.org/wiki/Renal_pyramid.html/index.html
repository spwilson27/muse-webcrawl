Renal pyramid

Renal pyramids (or malpighian pyramids) are cone-shaped tissues of the kidney. In humans there are usually seven to eighteen pyramids in the renal medulla. The base of each pyramid faces the renal cortex, and its apex, the renal papilla, points toward the renal pelvis, where collecting ducts (papillary ducts of Bellini) open into a minor calyx.

The pyramids consist largely of tubules that transport urine from the cortical part of the kidney, where urine is produced, to the calyces. Their striated appearance comes from straight segments of nephrons and collecting ducts. The columns of Bertin separate adjacent pyramids.
