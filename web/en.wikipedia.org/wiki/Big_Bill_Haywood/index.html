Big Bill Haywood

William Dudley Haywood (February 4, 1869 – May 18, 1928), known as "Big Bill" Haywood, was an American labor organizer and a founding leader of the Industrial Workers of the World (IWW). A former miner, he became one of the most prominent and controversial figures in the American labor movement of the early 20th century.

Early life and the Western Federation of Miners

Haywood began working in mines at age nine and lost the sight in one eye in a childhood accident. He rose through the Western Federation of Miners (WFM), becoming its secretary-treasurer in 1901 and a leader in the violent Colorado Labor Wars.

Murder trial and the IWW

In 1906 Haywood was tried for the murder of former Idaho governor Frank Steunenberg, defended by Clarence Darrow, and acquitted. In 1905 he had chaired the founding convention of the IWW, declaring it would unite all workers into "One Big Union". He led major strikes, including the 1912 Lawrence textile strike and the 1913 Paterson silk strike.

Exile and death

Convicted under the Espionage Act in 1918 for opposing World War I conscription, Haywood jumped bail in 1921 while awaiting appeal and fled to the Soviet Union, where he lived until his death in Moscow in 1928. Half his ashes were buried in the Kremlin Wall Necropolis.
