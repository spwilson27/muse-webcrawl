Axle counter

An axle counter is a railway signalling device that detects the presence of a train on a section of track by counting the axles entering and leaving the section. If the number of axles that have left equals the number that entered, the section is deemed clear. Axle counters are widely used as an alternative to track circuits for train detection.

Operation

Sensors are mounted on the rail at each end of a detection section. As each wheel flange passes, the sensor registers a count; electronic evaluator equipment compares "in" and "out" counts and also determines direction of travel. Modern sensors use magnetic fields: the passage of a steel wheel perturbs the field between a transmitter and receiver, producing a count pulse.

Advantages and limitations

Unlike track circuits, axle counters do not require insulated rail joints or traction-return compatibility, can cover very long sections, work when rails are wet or contaminated by leaves or rust, and are unaffected by flooding. Their principal limitation is that after a power failure an axle counter section is treated as occupied until the section can be proven clear (by a sweep or preparatory reset procedure), since the count is lost; newer systems retain counts through power interruptions. Broken rails, which track circuits may detect, are not detected by axle counters.
