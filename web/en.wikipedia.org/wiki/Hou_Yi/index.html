Hou Yi

Hou Yi (Chinese: 后羿), also simply called Yi, is a mythological Chinese archer. He was also known as Shen Yi and given the title "Lord Archer". He is sometimes portrayed as a god of archery descended from heaven to aid mankind; in other sources, he is a mortal hero who becomes a tyrannical ruler. His wife, Chang'e, was a lunar deity who flew to the Moon after consuming the elixir of immortality.

Shooting down the suns

In the most famous myth, there were originally ten suns, children of the god Di Jun, who took turns rising into the sky. One day all ten rose together, scorching the earth, drying up the seas and threatening all life. The Emperor Yao commanded Hou Yi, who had been given a red bow and ten vermilion arrows, to restore order. Hou Yi shot down nine of the suns, leaving one to provide light and warmth. As a reward he was given the elixir of immortality.

Chang'e and the elixir

In most versions, Hou Yi's wife Chang'e drank the elixir — in some tales to keep it from a would-be thief, the apprentice Feng Meng — and floated up to the Moon, where she remains with the Jade Rabbit. Hou Yi, who had become a harsh and tyrannical king after receiving a second elixir was withheld from him, was eventually killed by his own men, in some versions led by Feng Meng. The Mid-Autumn Festival is associated with the worship of Chang'e and her separation from Hou Yi.

Legacy

Hou Yi appears in classical texts such as the Huainanzi and the Classic of Mountains and Seas. He remains a popular figure in Chinese culture, art and modern media, and the Chinese lunar exploration program's rovers and landers are named in reference to the mythology surrounding his wife.
