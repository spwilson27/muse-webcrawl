The Temptations are an American vocal group formed in Detroit, Michigan, in 1960, who achieved fame as one of the most successful acts on the Motown label. Known for their choreography, matching suits, and smooth harmonies, the group released a string of hits during the 1960s and 1970s, including "My Girl", "Ain't Too Proud to Beg", "Papa Was a Rollin' Stone", and "Just My Imagination (Running Away with Me)".

History

The group formed from the merger of two Detroit vocal groups, the Primes and the Distants, and signed with Berry Gordy's Motown in 1961. Their "classic five" lineup — Otis Williams, Melvin Franklin, Paul Williams, Eddie Kendricks, and David Ruffin — broke through with "The Way You Do the Things You Do" (1964) and the Smokey Robinson-written "My Girl" (1964), their first number-one hit. Working with producer Norman Whitfield from 1966, the group moved toward a harder, psychedelic soul sound on songs like "Cloud Nine" (1968), "I Can't Get Next to You" (1969), and "Papa Was a Rollin' Stone" (1972), which won three Grammy Awards.

Legacy

The Temptations were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1989. Otis Williams, the last surviving original member, continued to lead a version of the group into the 21st century. Their story was dramatized in the 1998 television miniseries The Temptations and the Broadway musical Ain't Too Proud (2019).
