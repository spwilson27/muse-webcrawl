Levi-Civita symbol

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Levi-Civita symbol, also called the permutation symbol or antisymmetric symbol, is a mathematical object used in tensor calculus, named after the Italian mathematician Tullio Levi-Civita. In three dimensions it is written εijk and takes the value +1 if (i, j, k) is an even permutation of (1, 2, 3), −1 if it is an odd permutation, and 0 if any index is repeated.

Definition

εijk = +1 if (i, j, k) is (1,2,3), (2,3,1), or (3,1,2);
εijk = −1 if (i, j, k) is (3,2,1), (1,3,2), or (2,1,3);
εijk = 0 if any two indices are equal.

The symbol generalises to any number of dimensions, where it has one index per dimension and equals the sign of the permutation of the indices, or zero if the indices are not a permutation.

Applications

Using the Einstein summation convention, the cross product of two vectors can be written compactly as (a × b)i = εijkajbk, and the scalar triple product is a · (b × c) = εijkaibjck. The determinant of a 3×3 matrix can likewise be expressed as a sum over products of three Levi-Civita symbols and matrix entries.

Identities

A standard identity contracts two symbols over one index: εijkεimn = δjmδkn − δjnδkm, where δ is the Kronecker delta. Strictly speaking, the Levi-Civita symbol is not a tensor but a tensor density (a pseudotensor), because its sign behaviour under transformations also involves the determinant of the transformation.
