Medium format

Medium format has traditionally referred to a film format in photography and the related cameras and equipment that use it. Generally, the term applies to film and digital sensors larger than 35 mm "full frame" (24×36 mm) but smaller than large format (4×5 inch).

Film formats

Medium format film is usually 120 or 220 roll film, 6 cm wide, exposed in frames such as 6×4.5, 6×6 and 6×7 cm. Classic systems include the Hasselblad V-system, Mamiya, Pentax 67 and twin-lens Rolleiflex cameras, favoured by professional portrait, fashion and landscape photographers for their image quality and modular design.

Digital era

Digital medium format systems from Hasselblad, Fujifilm (GFX) and Phase One use sensors around 44×33 mm and resolutions exceeding 100 megapixels, at correspondingly high prices. The larger negative or sensor yields shallower depth of field, finer tonal gradation and greater detail than smaller formats.
