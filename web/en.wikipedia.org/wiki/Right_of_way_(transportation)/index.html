Right of way (transportation)

FieldTransport engineering; land-use law
UsesHighways, railways, transit lines, trails, utility corridors
Categories (transit)Class A: exclusive; Class B: partially exclusive; Class C: shared (per Vuchic)

In transportation, a right of way (abbreviated ROW or R/W) is a legal right, established by grant, usage, or reservation, to pass along a specific route through property belonging to another party. The term also denotes the physical strip of land itself — the corridor occupied by a road, railway, canal, trail, or public utility line. A right of way may be publicly owned in fee simple, or it may exist as an easement over private land, and the land within it is generally reserved from other development.

Usage

For highways, the right of way encompasses the carriageway, shoulders, medians, drainage, signage, and room for future widening; widths vary from a few metres for local streets to more than 90 metres for major freeways. Railway rights of way were historically obtained through land grants and eminent domain, and many abandoned rail corridors have been converted into rail trails ("rails-to-trails") or preserved for future transit use through railbanking. Utilities routinely share transportation rights of way under encroachment permits or franchise agreements.

Transit classification

Transport planner Vukan Vuchic classifies transit rights of way into three categories by degree of separation from other traffic:

  • Category A — fully controlled, exclusive right of way with no grade crossings (e.g., metro tunnels and elevated lines);
  • Category B — longitudinally separated but with grade crossings (e.g., light rail in a reserved median, busways);
  • Category C — street running in mixed traffic.

The degree of exclusivity largely determines a transit line's speed, capacity, and reliability.

Related meanings

Separately, in traffic law "right of way" also refers to the legal priority of one road user over another at intersections, crosswalks, and merges (see Priority (right of way)). In British land law, a public right of way is a path over private land on which the public has a legally protected right to pass, including footpaths, bridleways, and byways.
