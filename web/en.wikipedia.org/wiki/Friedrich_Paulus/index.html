Friedrich Paulus

Friedrich Wilhelm Ernst Paulus (23 September 1890 – 1 February 1957) was a German field marshal during World War II who is best known for commanding the 6th Army during the Battle of Stalingrad (1942–43). The battle ended in disaster for the Wehrmacht when Soviet forces encircled the army in Operation Uranus; Paulus surrendered on 31 January 1943, the day after Hitler promoted him to field marshal — a pointed hint that no German field marshal had ever surrendered and that he was expected to take his own life instead.

Later life

In Soviet captivity Paulus joined the National Committee for a Free Germany and broadcast appeals to German troops. Released in 1953, he settled in Dresden in East Germany, where he worked as a research institute chief and died in 1957.
