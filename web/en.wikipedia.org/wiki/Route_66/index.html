U.S. Route 66

U.S. Route 66 (US 66), also known as the Will Rogers Highway, the Main Street of America, and the Mother Road, was one of the original highways in the United States Numbered Highway System, established on 11 November 1926. It ran 2,448 miles (3,940 km) from Chicago, Illinois, through Missouri, Kansas, Oklahoma, Texas, New Mexico and Arizona, to Santa Monica, California.

History

Route 66 became a major path for migrants west during the Dust Bowl of the 1930s, immortalised in John Steinbeck's The Grapes of Wrath. It supported businesses — motels, diners, gas stations and tourist attractions — along its entire length and was celebrated in Bobby Troup's song "(Get Your Kicks on) Route 66" (1946) and the 1960s television series Route 66.

Decommissioning and preservation

The highway was gradually bypassed by the Interstate Highway System and officially removed from the numbered system on 27 June 1985. Much of the road survives as "Historic Route 66", a scenic byway and heritage corridor, with preservation associations in all eight states and a corridor management programme run by the National Park Service.
