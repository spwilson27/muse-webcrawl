My Old Kentucky Home

Federal Hill in Bardstown, Kentucky — the song's namesake home.

"My Old Kentucky Home, Good-Night!" is a song written by Stephen Foster, published in 1853. It is the state song of Kentucky, adopted in 1928. Inspired by Harriet Beecher Stowe's anti-slavery novel Uncle Tom's Cabin (1852), the song describes the sorrow of enslaved people being sold away from a Kentucky plantation to the Deep South — although in later performance its meaning was often softened into a nostalgic picture of plantation life.

History

Foster's sketchbook shows an early draft titled "Poor Uncle Tom, Good-Night!" The lyrics tell of summer days, corn tops ripening and children rolling on the cabin floor, contrasted with the hardship awaiting the enslaved singer who must be sold south to Georgia's sugarcane and cotton fields. The song became hugely popular with minstrel troupes and in parlour performance. Federal Hill, the Bardstown home of Foster's relatives the Rowan family, is traditionally cited as the inspiration and is preserved as My Old Kentucky Home State Park.

In 1986 the Kentucky General Assembly revised the official lyrics, replacing the word "darkies" with "people". The song is sung annually at the Kentucky Derby as the horses enter the parade to the post, performed by the University of Louisville marching band while the crowd sings along.
