Accessory nerve

InnervatesSternocleidomastoid, trapezius
FromCranial nucleus (medulla); spinal nucleus (C1–C5)
IdentifiersCranial nerve XI

The accessory nerve, also known as the spinal accessory nerve, is the eleventh cranial nerve (CN XI). It supplies the sternocleidomastoid and trapezius muscles.

Structure

The nerve has traditionally been described as having a cranial root from the nucleus ambiguus in the medulla and a spinal root arising from motor neurons in the upper five or six cervical segments of the spinal cord. The spinal rootlets ascend through the foramen magnum, join briefly with the cranial root, and exit the skull through the jugular foramen. The nerve then crosses the posterior triangle of the neck to reach the trapezius.

Function

The sternocleidomastoid turns the head to the opposite side and flexes the neck, while the trapezius elevates, retracts, and rotates the scapula, enabling shoulder shrugging and arm elevation above the horizontal.

Clinical significance

The nerve is vulnerable during surgery in the posterior triangle of the neck, such as lymph node biopsy or neck dissection. Injury causes weakness of shoulder shrugging, difficulty abducting the arm, and a drooping shoulder with chronic pain. Function is tested by asking the patient to shrug the shoulders and turn the head against resistance.
