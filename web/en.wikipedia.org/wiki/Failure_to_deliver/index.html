Failure to deliver

In finance, a failure to deliver (FTD) is the inability of a party to deliver a tradable asset, or meet a contractual obligation, on the settlement date. A failure to deliver can occur in both long and short sales. For example, in a short sale, the seller may have sold shares without borrowing them (a "naked" short), and therefore cannot deliver them to the buyer at settlement.

Causes and regulation

Failures can arise from operational errors, delays in securities lending, or deliberate naked short selling. Under the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission's Regulation SHO, broker-dealers must close out persistent fails in threshold securities within prescribed periods, and pre-borrow requirements apply to securities with extended failures. Clearing agencies such as the National Securities Clearing Corporation publish aggregate fails data twice a month.

Effects

Economists debate the impact of fails to deliver. Critics argue that large, persistent fails can depress a security's price and distort supply, effectively creating "phantom shares"; defenders counter that fails are usually small relative to trading volume, are often caused by market-making and operational frictions, and provide liquidity. Failures to deliver became a prominent public issue during the 2021 GameStop short squeeze.
