Nobel Prize in Chemistry
Type: Wikipedia article (recreated from memory)

The Nobel Prize in Chemistry is awarded annually by the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences to those who have conferred the greatest benefit to humankind in chemistry, per Alfred Nobel's will (prizes first awarded in 1901). It is one of the five original Nobel Prizes.

Laureates include Marie Curie (1911, her second Nobel), Linus Pauling, Frederick Sanger (twice), and the developers of lithium-ion batteries, CRISPR gene editing and click chemistry. The prize amount is 11 million Swedish kronor in recent years (figure may vary). Chemistry is jokingly called 'the central science'; its Nobel sometimes rewards work also claimed by physics or medicine (radioactivity, molecular biology).
