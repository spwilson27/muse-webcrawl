Singer-songwriter

Singer-songwriters are musicians who write, compose, and perform their own musical material, including lyrics and melodies. In the United States, the category is built on the folk-acoustic tradition, although the term distinguishes itself from the idea of a "singer-songwriter" as a self-contained artist performing original songs — as opposed to the earlier Tin Pan Alley model, in which professional songwriters wrote material for separate performers. A singer-songwriter typically accompanies themselves on guitar or piano, though many front bands, and the songs are often personal, literary, or socially conscious in character.

History

The modern singer-songwriter emerged from the folk revival of the 1950s and 1960s, drawing on troubadour traditions and on figures such as Woody Guthrie, Lead Belly, Hank Williams, and Pete Seeger. Bob Dylan's success from 1962 onward transformed the role of the songwriter-performer in popular music; contemporaries and successors included Joni Mitchell, Leonard Cohen, Paul Simon, Carole King, James Taylor, Jackson Browne, Cat Stevens, Neil Young, and Van Morrison. The early 1970s saw the commercial peak of the "confessional" singer-songwriter genre, centered on Los Angeles venues such as the Troubadour and labels like Asylum Records.

Traditions worldwide

Comparable traditions exist internationally: the French auteur-compositeur-interprète (Georges Brassens, Jacques Brel — the latter Belgian — Barbara, Charles Aznavour), the Italian cantautori (Fabrizio De André, Lucio Dalla, Francesco Guccini), the German Liedermacher, the Spanish and Latin American cantautor and nueva canción movements (Violeta Parra, Víctor Jara, Silvio Rodríguez), Soviet and Russian bard song (Vladimir Vysotsky, Bulat Okudzhava), and Brazil's MPB songwriters such as Chico Buarque and Caetano Veloso.

Contemporary practice

The singer-songwriter model remains a dominant template in folk, Americana, indie, country, and much pop music; artists such as Taylor Swift, Ed Sheeran, and Billie Eilish market themselves substantially as self-writing performers even when collaborating, and co-writing with professional songwriters and producers is now routine — blurring the boundary the term originally marked.
