Breast implant

Breast implant
Uses: Augmentation, reconstruction
Fill types: Silicone gel, saline
Shell: Silicone elastomer
Silicone (left) and saline breast implants

A breast implant is a prosthesis used to change the size, shape and contour of a person's breast. Implants are used in reconstructive surgery after mastectomy, to correct congenital deformities, and in cosmetic breast augmentation. Modern implants consist of a silicone elastomer shell filled either with silicone gel or with sterile saline solution; they may be round or teardrop-shaped and have smooth or textured surfaces.

History

Attempts at augmentation date to the late nineteenth century, using materials from paraffin to glass balls. The first modern silicone gel implant was developed in 1961 by American plastic surgeons Thomas Cronin and Frank Gerow with the Dow Corning company, and first implanted in 1962. Safety controversies in the early 1990s led the US Food and Drug Administration to restrict silicone gel implants in 1992; after extensive studies found no clear link to systemic disease, they were re-approved in 2006 with conditions for monitoring.

Complications

Recognised risks include capsular contracture (tightening of the scar capsule around the implant), rupture, infection and changes in sensation. A rare cancer of the immune system, breast implant-associated anaplastic large cell lymphoma (BIA-ALCL), has been linked mainly to textured implants and prompted recalls, including Allergan's 2019 worldwide withdrawal of its Biocell textured products.
