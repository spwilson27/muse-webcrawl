Atari 2600

Atari 2600
Manufacturer: Atari, Inc.
Released: September 1977 (North America)
CPU: MOS Technology 6507 @ 1.19 MHz
Media: ROM cartridge

The Atari 2600 is a home video game console developed and produced by Atari, Inc., released in September 1977. Originally branded the Atari Video Computer System (VCS), it popularised the use of interchangeable ROM cartridges and microprocessor-based hardware, and is widely credited with making home video gaming a mass-market industry.

History

The console launched with nine games and sold modestly until the 1980 home conversion of the arcade hit Space Invaders quadrupled sales, making the system a phenomenon. Bundled with Combat and later with Pac-Man, the 2600 dominated the early-1980s home market. Its enormous success also seeded the market glut contributing to the video game crash of 1983, symbolised by the failed E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial cartridge.

Hardware and games

Built around the MOS 6507 processor with just 128 bytes of RAM and the TIA graphics chip, the 2600 demanded extreme programming ingenuity. Landmark titles include Adventure (which contained one of the first famous Easter eggs), Pitfall! by Activision — the first major third-party developer — and conversions of Missile Command and Asteroids.

Legacy

Production continued until 1992; total sales exceeded 30 million units. The 2600 established the template of the home console business — hardware, controllers, and a library of cartridge software — and remains an icon of early gaming, supported by an active homebrew scene.
