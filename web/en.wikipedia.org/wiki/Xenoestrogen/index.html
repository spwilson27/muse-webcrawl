Xenoestrogen

Xenoestrogens are a type of xenohormone — chemicals foreign to the body — that imitate the effects of the hormone estrogen. They are a class of endocrine disruptors: substances that can bind to estrogen receptors and interfere with the normal functioning of the endocrine system in humans and wildlife.

Sources

Synthetic xenoestrogens include:

  • Bisphenol A (BPA) – used in polycarbonate plastics and epoxy resins.
  • Phthalates – plasticisers found in many consumer products.
  • PCBs and dioxins – persistent industrial pollutants.
  • DDT and other organochlorine pesticides – now restricted in many countries but persistent in the environment.
  • Parabens – preservatives used in cosmetics and pharmaceuticals.
  • Alkylphenols – breakdown products of industrial detergents.

Naturally occurring estrogenic compounds in plants, such as the isoflavones in soy, are usually distinguished as phytoestrogens, while those produced by fungi are called mycoestrogens.

Effects

Because estrogen receptors are widely distributed in the body, xenoestrogens have been investigated for possible roles in reproductive abnormalities, developmental effects, altered fertility in wildlife — such as the feminisation of fish downstream of sewage outflows — and hormone-sensitive cancers. The potency of most synthetic xenoestrogens is far weaker than that of natural estradiol, and the extent of risk at typical environmental exposure levels remains a subject of scientific and regulatory debate. Several jurisdictions have restricted BPA in baby bottles and food-contact materials as a precaution.
