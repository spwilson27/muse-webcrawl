Alpine Convention

Signed7 November 1991, Salzburg
PartiesAustria, France, Germany, Italy, Liechtenstein, Monaco, Slovenia, Switzerland, and the European Union
SecretariatInnsbruck, Austria

The Alpine Convention (German: Alpenkonvention) is an international treaty between the Alpine countries and the European Union for the sustainable development and protection of the Alps. It was signed in Salzburg in 1991 and entered into force in 1995.

Framework and protocols

The framework convention commits the parties to pursue sustainable development across the Alpine arc, elaborated through protocols on nature protection and landscape conservation, mountain farming, tourism, transport, energy, soil protection, spatial planning, and mountain forests.

Geography

The convention area covers about 190,000 km² and some 14 million inhabitants across the Alpine regions of eight states, from France in the west to Slovenia in the east.

Institutions

The Alpine Conference of ministers meets regularly, supported by a Permanent Secretariat based in Innsbruck with an office in Bolzano/Bozen.
