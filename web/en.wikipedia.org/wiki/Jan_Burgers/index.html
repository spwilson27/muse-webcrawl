Johannes Martinus Burgers (13 January 1895 – 7 June 1981) was a Dutch physicist and professor of applied mathematics and aerodynamics at Delft University of Technology and later at the University of Maryland. He is remembered for the Burgers equation in fluid dynamics, the Burgers vector in dislocation theory, and the Burgers model in viscoelasticity.

Career in the Netherlands

Burgers studied physics at Leiden under Paul Ehrenfest, receiving his doctorate in 1918. At 23 he was appointed professor at the newly founded aerodynamics and applied mathematics chair in Delft, where he built an influential school of fluid mechanics. His 1929 report and later papers popularised the model equation that now bears his name — a nonlinear partial differential equation combining convection and diffusion, widely used as a simplified model for turbulence and shock formation.

In the 1930s Burgers turned also to solid mechanics, introducing the vector that quantifies a crystal dislocation, and the four-parameter model of a viscoelastic material. He helped found the International Union of Theoretical and Applied Mechanics (IUTAM) and served as its secretary.

Later years

In 1955 Burgers emigrated to the United States, joining the Institute for Fluid Dynamics and Applied Mathematics at the University of Maryland, where he remained active in research into statistical mechanics and plasma physics. He died in Washington, D.C. in 1981.
