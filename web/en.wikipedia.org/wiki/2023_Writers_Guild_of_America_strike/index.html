The 2023 Writers Guild of America strike was a labour dispute between the Writers Guild of America (WGA) and the Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers (AMPTP). Beginning on 2 May 2023, more than 11,500 screenwriters went on strike over compensation in the streaming era, staffing levels in writers' rooms, and protections against the use of artificial intelligence. It lasted 148 days, making it one of the longest strikes in the guild's history.

Issues

Writers argued that streaming had eroded residuals and shortened employment, with "mini-rooms" hiring fewer writers for limited periods. The guild sought minimum staffing requirements, viewership-based residuals, and rules preventing AI from writing or rewriting material or being used to reduce writers' pay.

Course of the strike

The strike shut down late-night talk shows immediately and halted production on most scripted television and many films. From July it overlapped with the SAG-AFTRA actors' strike, effectively closing down much of Hollywood. Negotiations resumed in September and produced a tentative agreement on 24 September; the strike ended on 27 September and members ratified the contract on 9 October.

Outcome

The new Minimum Basic Agreement included pay increases, minimum writers' room staffing tied to a show's episode count, streaming residuals based partly on viewership, and guardrails on AI: AI-generated material is not considered literary material, and writers cannot be required to use AI tools.
