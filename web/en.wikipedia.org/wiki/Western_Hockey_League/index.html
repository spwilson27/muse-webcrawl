The Western Hockey League (WHL) is a major junior ice hockey league based in Western Canada and the Northwestern United States. It is one of three leagues that constitute the Canadian Hockey League (CHL), the highest level of junior hockey in Canada, and its players are aged 16 to 20. The league currently has 22 teams, divided between the Eastern and Western Conferences, with most teams in British Columbia, Alberta, Saskatchewan and Manitoba, and several in Washington and Oregon.

History

The league was founded in 1966, originally as the Canadian Major Junior Hockey League, breaking away from the provincial associations of Western Canada in a dispute over the structure of junior hockey and the Memorial Cup. It became the Western Canada Hockey League and, in 1978, after admitting American teams from the Pacific Northwest, adopted its present name, the Western Hockey League. (An earlier, unrelated professional Western Hockey League operated from 1952 to 1974.)

Championship and Memorial Cup

The WHL champion is awarded the Ed Chynoweth Cup, named for the league's long-time president and a founder of the CHL. The champion advances to the Memorial Cup tournament against the champions of the Ontario Hockey League and the Quebec Major Junior Hockey League and a host team. WHL clubs have won the Memorial Cup many times, with dynasties such as the Kamloops Blazers of the early 1990s among the most successful.

Player development

The WHL is a major supplier of talent to the National Hockey League through the NHL Entry Draft, and operates a scholarship programme under which players earn a year of post-secondary education funding for each season played. Notable alumni include numerous Hall of Famers and NHL stars. The league has also faced continuing debate about player welfare, education and the employment status of junior players.
