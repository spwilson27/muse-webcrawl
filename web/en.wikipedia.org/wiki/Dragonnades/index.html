Dragonnades

The Dragonnades were a policy of persecution instituted by King Louis XIV of France in 1681 to pressure Huguenots (French Protestants) into converting to Catholicism. Dragoons — mounted soldiers notorious for harsh discipline — were billeted in Protestant households, where they were effectively licensed to abuse and plunder their hosts until the family converted.

Background

Since the Edict of Nantes (1598), French Protestants had enjoyed limited rights of worship. Louis XIV progressively eroded those rights, and in 1681 the intendant René de Marillac began quartering dragoons in Huguenot homes in Poitou. The policy spread across France; the misery inflicted produced mass nominal conversions, which the crown cited as proof the policy worked.

Consequences

In October 1685 Louis issued the Edict of Fontainebleau, revoking the Edict of Nantes entirely. Despite emigration being forbidden, roughly 200,000 Huguenots fled France for England, the Dutch Republic, Brandenburg-Prussia, Switzerland and beyond — a diaspora that deprived France of skilled merchants and craftsmen and enriched its rivals.
