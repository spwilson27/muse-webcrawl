Whernside is a mountain in the Yorkshire Dales in northern England. At 736 metres (2,415 ft), it is the highest of the Yorkshire Three Peaks — the others being Ingleborough and Pen-y-ghent — and the highest point in North Yorkshire. The summit straddles the boundary between Cumbria and North Yorkshire, near the town of Ingleton.

Description

Unlike the craggy profiles of its neighbours, Whernside is a long, smooth-shouldered ridge of Millstone Grit over limestone, running roughly north–south above the valley of the River Doe to the west and Ribblehead to the east. Its slopes are comparatively gentle and rounded, and the summit carries a trig point and a wind shelter behind a wall that follows the county boundary. On clear days the views extend to Morecambe Bay, the Lake District and across the Dales.

Walking

Whernside is climbed as part of the Yorkshire Three Peaks Challenge, a 24-mile route over all three summits usually attempted within 12 hours, and also on its own from Ribblehead — past the famous Ribblehead Viaduct on the Settle–Carlisle railway — or from Dent and the hamlet of Whernside on its western flanks. The Dales High Way and other long-distance paths also cross the area. The paths on the mountain have been heavily engineered with stone pitching to combat erosion caused by the popularity of the Three Peaks route.
