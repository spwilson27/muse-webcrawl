Bureau of Industry and Security

Bureau of Industry and Security
Abbreviation: BIS
Parent agency: U.S. Department of Commerce
Function: Export controls and trade security
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.

The Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS) is an agency of the United States Department of Commerce that deals with issues involving national security and high technology. Its mission is to advance U.S. national security, foreign policy, and economic objectives by ensuring an effective export control and treaty compliance system and by promoting continued U.S. strategic technology leadership.

Responsibilities

BIS administers the Export Administration Regulations (EAR), issues export licences for dual-use goods and technologies, enforces export controls through its Office of Export Enforcement, and maintains the Entity List of foreign parties subject to licence requirements. It also conducts industrial base surveys and administers the U.S. side of certain chemical weapons convention obligations.

Role in technology controls

The bureau gained prominence in the U.S.–China technology competition, imposing sweeping controls from October 2022 on advanced computing chips and semiconductor manufacturing equipment destined for China, and adding numerous Chinese firms, including Huawei (listed in 2019), to the Entity List. Its rules, including the foreign direct product rule, extend U.S. controls to certain foreign-made items produced with U.S. technology.
