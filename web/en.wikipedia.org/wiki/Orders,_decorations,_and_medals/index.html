Orders, decorations, and medals

Orders, decorations, and medals are the physical insignia of state and dynastic honours systems. An order is a society — usually of merit or chivalry — into which a person is admitted; a decoration is an award for a specific act of bravery or distinguished service; and a medal is a more widely distributed award, often commemorating service in a campaign or an event.

History

The earliest modern orders were the military-religious orders of knighthood founded during the Crusades, such as the Knights Hospitaller and the Knights Templar. Secular orders of chivalry — the Order of the Garter (1348), the Order of the Golden Fleece (1430) — bound leading nobles to a sovereign. Napoleon's Légion d'honneur (1802) established the model of a national order of merit open to citizens of all ranks, which most modern honours systems follow.

Structure

Orders of merit commonly have several grades — grand cross, grand officer, commander, officer, knight or member — with insignia of increasing richness. Recipients' awards are worn according to a regulated order of wear (order of precedence), and in many countries may be recognised by post-nominal letters after the recipient's name, such as KBE or OM.

Decorations and medals

Decorations reward a particular deed: the Victoria Cross and the Medal of Honor are the supreme gallantry decorations of the United Kingdom and the United States respectively. Medals include campaign and service medals issued to all qualifying participants, jubilee and coronation medals, and long-service awards. The academic study and collecting of such items is called phaleristics.
