François Villon

François Villon (pronounced [fʁɑ̃swa vijɔ̃]; born François de Montcorbier or François des Loges, c. 1431 – after 1463) was a French poet of the late Middle Ages. A student, thief and vagabond repeatedly in trouble with the law, he is now considered the greatest French lyric poet of his period, chiefly for his Testament and the "Ballade des pendus" ("Ballad of the Hanged").

Born: c. 1431, Paris, France
Disappeared: 1463 (banished from Paris)
Occupation: Poet
Notable works: Le Testament; Ballade des pendus

Life

Villon was born in poverty in Paris during the Hundred Years' War and raised by Guillaume de Villon, a chaplain of Saint-Benoît-le-Bétourné, whose name he took. He studied at the University of Paris, taking his master's degree in 1452. In 1455 he killed a priest in a brawl and fled Paris; pardoned, he returned, but was implicated in the burglary of the Collège de Navarre in 1456 and fled again, wandering among princely courts including that of Charles d'Orléans at Blois. Imprisoned at Meung-sur-Loire in 1461 and freed by Louis XI, he composed his masterwork Le Testament. In 1462 he was arrested in another affray and condemned to be hanged; the sentence was commuted on appeal in January 1463 to ten years' banishment from Paris. Nothing certain is known of him thereafter.

Works

Villon's poetry combines strict ballade and rondeau forms with thieves' slang (the "jargon" ballads), parody, and an intensely personal voice. Le Lais ("The Legacy", 1456) is a mock will; Le Testament (1461) interweaves further mock bequests with some of the most famous lyrics in French: the "Ballade des dames du temps jadis" with its refrain "Mais où sont les neiges d'antan?" ("Where are the snows of yesteryear?") and the "Ballade des pendus", a plea spoken by corpses on the gallows. His themes — death, decay, lost youth, the fleetingness of fortune — gave him a reputation as the first "poète maudit".

Influence

Clément Marot published an edition of Villon in 1533; Romantic writers and later poets from Baudelaire to the modernists claimed him as a precursor, and his life and works have inspired plays, novels, and Ezra Pound's opera Le Testament de Villon.
