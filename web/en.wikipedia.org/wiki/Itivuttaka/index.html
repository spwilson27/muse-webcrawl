Itivuttaka

The Itivuttaka (Pāli: "as it was said") is a Buddhist scripture, part of the Khuddaka Nikāya in the Sutta Piṭaka of the Theravāda Pāli Canon. It consists of 112 short discourses attributed to the Buddha, each opening with the formula "This was said by the Blessed One" (iti vutta, hence the title).

Contents and form

The text is organised into four sections (nipātas) according to the number of topics treated — ones, twos, threes, and fours — covering themes such as greed, hatred, and delusion, the benefits of merit and loving-kindness, and the path to awakening. Unusually for the Sutta Piṭaka, each discourse combines a prose passage with a verse summary of the same meaning. According to tradition, the sayings were memorised and transmitted by the laywoman Khujjuttarā, a servant of Queen Sāmāvatī, who heard them from the Buddha and repeated them to others.

Translations

The Itivuttaka was translated into English by F. L. Woodward in Minor Anthologies of the Pali Canon (1935) and later by John D. Ireland and by Bhikkhu Ṭhānissaro, among others.
