Marian apparition

A Marian apparition is a reported supernatural appearance by Mary, the mother of Jesus, to one or more persons. Such apparitions form an important element of Catholic devotion, and many have given rise to major pilgrimage shrines. The Catholic Church investigates reported apparitions through the local bishop and, in significant cases, the Vatican's Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith, and may judge them "worthy of belief", while emphasising that no Catholic is obliged to believe in any particular private revelation.

Evaluation by the Church

Investigations consider the mental and moral character of the visionaries, conformity of any messages with Catholic doctrine, and spiritual fruits such as conversions and devotion. Norms for judging apparitions were published by the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith in 1978 (released publicly in 2012) and revised in 2024, when the Church moved toward issuing prudential judgements rather than definitive declarations of supernatural origin.

Devotion and pilgrimage

Approved apparitions typically become centres of pilgrimage, with associated devotions such as the rosary, processions, and feast days. The apparitions at Lourdes and Fátima draw millions of pilgrims annually. Reported but unapproved apparitions, such as those at Medjugorje in Bosnia and Herzegovina (from 1981), have also attracted large followings; in 2024 the Vatican authorised devotion there without declaring the events supernatural.
