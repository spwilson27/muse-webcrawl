Smart beta

Smart beta is an investment strategy that combines the benefits of passive investing with elements of active investing. It seeks to obtain alpha, lower risk, or reduce costs by departing from traditional market capitalisation-weighted indices and instead weighting securities according to alternative schemes based on factors such as volatility, liquidity, value, size, momentum or dividends.

Origins

The approach grew out of academic research on the capital asset pricing model and the Fama–French three-factor model, which showed that portfolios tilted toward small-cap and value stocks historically outperformed the market index. Firms such as Research Affiliates (with its Fundamental Index methodology, introduced in 2005) and index providers including MSCI, S&P Dow Jones Indices and FTSE commercialised rules-based alternative weighting in indices and exchange-traded funds (ETFs). The term "smart beta" itself became widespread in the 2010s as assets in such strategies grew into the hundreds of billions of dollars.

Common strategies

  • Equal weighting — every constituent receives the same weight, introducing a small-cap tilt.
  • Fundamental weighting — weighting by sales, cash flow, dividends or book value.
  • Factor tilts — tilting toward value, quality, momentum, low volatility or size factors.
  • Risk-based weighting — minimum-variance and risk-parity portfolios.
  • Dividend weighting — weighting by dividend yield or total dividends paid.

Criticism

Critics argue that "smart beta" is largely a marketing label: the strategies are frequently repackaged factor exposures known to academics for decades, and fees are higher than for ordinary index funds. Fama and others have cautioned that apparent factor premia may shrink after publication, involve higher turnover and trading costs, or simply compensate investors for bearing additional risk rather than offering a free lunch. Backtested index performance, critics add, is prone to data mining.
