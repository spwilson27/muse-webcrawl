Holden Caulfield

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Holden Caulfield is the fictional protagonist and unreliable narrator of J. D. Salinger's 1951 novel The Catcher in the Rye. A sixteen-year-old who has been expelled from his Pennsylvania prep school, Holden recounts — apparently from a rest home in California — several days spent wandering New York City before Christmas, during which he spirals toward a nervous breakdown.

Character

Holden is alienated, judgmental, and obsessed with the "phoniness" of the adult world, yet he is also sensitive, protective of innocence, and deeply traumatized by the death of his younger brother Allie from leukemia. His fantasy of becoming "the catcher in the rye" — saving children from falling off a cliff as they play in a field of rye — gives the novel its title and symbolizes his wish to preserve childhood innocence. His distinctive red hunting hat, his little sister Phoebe, and his vernacular voice ("and all", "that killed me") are among the novel's most famous elements.

Origins and legacy

Salinger developed the character in short stories published in the 1940s ("Slight Rebellion off Madison", "I'm Crazy"). Holden became an emblem of teenage rebellion and angst for generations of readers; critics have variously read him as a sympathetic truth-teller, a self-pitying depressive, or a study of adolescent grief. The character also attracted notoriety through the novel's association with several violent offenders, including Mark David Chapman, who cited it after murdering John Lennon. Salinger never permitted a film adaptation, and Holden has never officially been portrayed on screen.
