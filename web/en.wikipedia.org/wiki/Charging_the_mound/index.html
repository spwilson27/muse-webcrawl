Charging the mound

Charging the mound is an act in baseball in which a batter, usually after being hit by a pitch or thrown at, runs toward the pitcher's mound to confront the pitcher. The act almost always triggers a bench-clearing brawl or scrum, as players from both teams rush onto the field, and it results in ejections and suspensions for the principal participants. Charging the mound is one of the best-known flashpoints in the sport's culture of retaliation and "unwritten rules."

Causes and conduct

A charge is most often provoked by a pitch that hits the batter or passes close to his head, particularly when the batter believes it was intentional — for example, in retaliation for a home run, a teammate being hit earlier, or a perceived slight. The batter typically drops or flings his bat and helmet and sprints the roughly 60 feet to the mound, while the pitcher prepares to defend himself. Catchers and infielders often try to intercept the batter; within seconds both dugouts and bullpens empty, producing a milling crowd in which serious fighting is comparatively rare but sometimes occurs.

Notable incidents

Famous charges include Nolan Ryan's 1993 encounter with Robin Ventura, in which the 46-year-old Ryan put the charging Ventura in a headlock and landed several punches; Pedro Martínez's 2003 ALCS altercation with coach Don Zimmer; the 2016 fight between Rougned Odor and José Bautista following a hard slide and confrontation; and Bryce Harper's 2017 charge of Hunter Strickland, notable for Harper's errant helmet throw. Batters usually come off worse in such confrontations, and pitchers are seldom seriously injured, but incidents have produced broken hands and season-affecting injuries.

Penalties

Under Major League Baseball rules, charging the mound leads to automatic ejection, and the league routinely suspends the batter (and often the pitcher, if the pitch is judged intentional) for several games, with fines. Umpires may warn both benches after a suspected intentional pitch to deter retaliation and charges.
