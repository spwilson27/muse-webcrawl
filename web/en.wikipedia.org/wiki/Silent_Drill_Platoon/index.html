The United States Marine Corps Silent Drill Platoon is a 24-man rifle platoon of the United States Marine Corps. The unit performs a unique precision drill exhibition, executing a series of calculated drill movements with the standard-issue M1 Garand rifle fitted with bayonets, entirely without verbal commands. The platoon is based at Marine Barracks Washington, D.C. ("8th and I"), the oldest active post in the Marine Corps.

The Silent Drill Platoon performing during the Evening Parade at Marine Barracks Washington.

History

The drill movement of the platoon traces its origins to 1948, when the unit first performed as part of the Marine Corps' sunset parades. Over the years the exhibition drill was refined into its modern form, and the platoon became a fixture of the Friday Evening Parades and Tuesday Sunset Parades held at Marine Barracks Washington during the summer months.

Members of the platoon are selected from the Schools of Infantry located on the east and west coasts of the United States. Marines serve a two-year ceremonial tour with the unit; selection considers bearing, military appearance, and the ability to master the demanding drill routine.

The exhibition drill

The highlight of the performance is the inspection sequence, in which the platoon commander and the two rifle inspectors perform an elaborate inspection of the rifles of a front-rank Marine, including spinning and tossing the weapon between inspectors. The routine also features the famous "rifle toss," in which Marines exchange rifles across the formation in perfect unison. All of this is accomplished without a single spoken command — hence the unit's name.

The Silent Drill Platoon performs before hundreds of thousands of spectators each year at ceremonies, sporting events, and public appearances across the United States and abroad, serving as one of the most visible symbols of Marine Corps discipline and professionalism.
