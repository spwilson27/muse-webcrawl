Weight loss

Weight loss, in the context of medicine, health or physical fitness, refers to a reduction of the total body mass, due to a mean loss of fluid, body fat (adipose tissue), or lean mass (namely bone mineral deposits, muscle, tendon and other connective tissue). Weight loss can occur unintentionally because of malnourishment or an underlying disease, or from a conscious effort to improve an actual or perceived overweight or obese state.

Intentional weight loss

Intentional weight loss is commonly pursued by people with overweight or obesity to reduce the risk of conditions such as type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease and some cancers. The principal methods are dietary changes that reduce energy intake, increased physical activity, and behaviour change programmes. Some individuals use approved weight-loss medications, such as GLP-1 receptor agonists, and metabolic surgery (for example gastric bypass or sleeve gastrectomy) is an option for severe obesity when other measures fail.

Sustained weight loss is difficult for many people because of metabolic adaptation and environmental influences; long-term success rates of diets alone are modest, which is why guidelines emphasise combined lifestyle approaches and ongoing support.

Unintentional weight loss

Unexplained weight loss that is not from reduction of caloric intake or exercise is called cachexia and may be a symptom of a serious medical condition. Causes include cancer, hyperthyroidism, diabetes, gastrointestinal disorders, chronic infections and depression. Clinically significant unintentional loss — often defined as more than 5% of body weight over six to twelve months — warrants medical evaluation.
