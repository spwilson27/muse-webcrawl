Hamilton, Ontario

Hamilton
City in Ontario, Canada
Population (2021): 569,353
Nickname: "The Hammer", "Steeltown"

Hamilton is a port city in the Canadian province of Ontario. Hamilton has a population of 569,353, and its census metropolitan area, which encompasses Burlington and Grimsby, has a population of 785,184. The city is situated approximately 45 kilometres (28 mi) southwest of Toronto in the Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area (GTHA).

Conceived by George Hamilton when he purchased the Durand farm shortly after the War of 1812, the town of Hamilton became the centre of a densely populated and industrialized region at the west end of Lake Ontario known as the Golden Horseshoe. On January 1, 2001, the current boundaries of Hamilton were created through the amalgamation of the original city with other municipalities of the Regional Municipality of Hamilton–Wentworth.

History

In pre-colonial times, the Neutral First Nation used much of the land. Hamilton was initially not covered by the turning purchase made by the Mississaugas from the British, in the land between lakes. The site was purchased in 1784 and settled by United Empire Loyalists. The town was named for George Hamilton, a settler and local politician.

Economy

Hamilton has traditionally been a centre of steel and heavy manufacturing, earning it the nickname "Steeltown" and "the Hammer". Companies such as Stelco and Dofasco have long dominated its industrial landscape. In recent decades the economy has diversified into health care, education (anchored by McMaster University), and services.

Geography

Hamilton lies at the western end of Lake Ontario, with much of the city, including its downtown section, on the south shore. The Niagara Escarpment runs through the middle of the city, dividing it into "upper" and "lower" sections. Hamilton Harbour (formerly Burlington Bay) separates the city from Burlington to the north.
