Power management

Field: Electrical engineering, computer engineering

Related standards: ACPI, Energy Star

Power management is a feature of some electrical appliances, especially copiers, computers, computer CPUs, computer GPUs and computer peripherals such as monitors and printers, that turns off the power or switches the system to a low-power state when inactive. In computing, this is known as PC power management and is built around the Advanced Configuration and Power Interface (ACPI) standard.

Motivations

PC power management reduces energy consumption and cooling requirements, extends the portable and battery-operated system's run time, reduces noise from cooling fans, and lowers operating costs. Reducing energy use also cuts greenhouse gas emissions associated with electricity generation.

Processor power management

Modern processors support dynamic voltage and frequency scaling, adjusting voltage and clock speed to match workload. ACPI defines processor performance states (P-states) and idle processor power states (C-states), from fully active C0 through progressively deeper sleep states in which parts of the processor are powered down. Techniques such as clock gating and power gating disable unused logic at a fine granularity.

Device power management

Peripheral devices implement their own power states, defined by ACPI as D0 (fully on) through D3 (off). Operating systems coordinate system sleep states (S1 through S5), including suspend-to-RAM and hibernation, in which the system state is saved to disk. Features such as Wake-on-LAN allow devices to resume in response to external events.
