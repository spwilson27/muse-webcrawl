Plan Dalet

Plan Dalet (Hebrew: תוכנית ד׳, Tokhnit Dalet, "Plan D") was a Zionist military plan drawn up by the Haganah in Mandatory Palestine in March 1948, during the 1947–1949 Palestine war. Prepared under the direction of Yigael Yadin, head of the Haganah's operations branch, it set out the Haganah's strategy for the final weeks of the British Mandate and the anticipated invasion by Arab states: securing the territory allotted to the Jewish state under the UN Partition Plan, together with Jewish settlements outside those boundaries and the corridors connecting them. The plan was implemented from April 1948 through a series of Haganah (and later IDF) offensives.

Background

Plan Dalet was the fourth in a series of Haganah contingency plans (Plans A–C dated from 1945 onward). Plan A (1945) addressed renewed conflict with the British; Plan B (1947) concerned defence against Palestinian Arab forces; Plan C (November 1947) dealt with reprisals and deterrence. By early 1948, in the civil-war phase that followed the UN partition resolution of 29 November 1947, Jewish forces faced attacks on transport and settlements, while the Arab League states threatened invasion once the British withdrew on 15 May 1948. Plan Dalet marked the Haganah's shift from a defensive to an offensive posture.

Contents

The plan instructed Haganah brigades to secure the areas of the prospective Jewish state, Jewish population centres outside it, and access routes such as the Tel Aviv–Jerusalem road. It authorised operations against Arab villages and urban neighbourhoods situated within or adjacent to the areas to be held, including their capture and, where resistance was encountered, the expulsion of inhabitants and destruction of villages that could not be held. Villages that surrendered were to be garrisoned and their inhabitants allowed to remain. The plan also provided for counter-offensive thrusts into Arab-held areas and defensive preparations against invading regular armies.

Implementation

From early April 1948, the Haganah executed offensives associated with the plan's framework: Operation Nachshon opened the road to besieged Jewish Jerusalem; Operations Harel, Yevusi, Yiftach (in the Galilee), Ben-Ami (western Galilee), and others captured mixed and Arab towns including Tiberias, Haifa, Safed, Jaffa and Acre. During and after these operations, large numbers of Palestinian Arabs fled or were expelled — a central episode of the Nakba and the 1948 Palestinian expulsion and flight, in which more than 700,000 Palestinian Arabs left their homes over the course of the war.

Historiography

Plan Dalet is among the most debated documents of the 1948 war. Historians differ sharply over its intent and role. Some, notably Benny Morris, regard it as a primarily military-strategic plan whose expulsion clauses were conditional and localised, and deny it was a master plan for ethnic cleansing. Others, such as Walid Khalidi and Ilan Pappé, argue it constituted a blueprint for the systematic depopulation of Palestinian villages. The debate turns on the plan's wording, the pattern of operations carried out under it, and the intentions of the Zionist leadership, and it remains central to the wider historiography of the war.
