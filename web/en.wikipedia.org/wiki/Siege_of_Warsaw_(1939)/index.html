Siege of Warsaw (1939)

The siege of Warsaw in 1939 was fought between the Polish Warsaw Army (Armia Warszawa) garrisoned in the Polish capital and the German Wehrmacht during the invasion of Poland that opened the Second World War in Europe. The city was bombed from the first day of the war, 1 September 1939, besieged from 8 September, and surrendered on 28 September after heavy artillery and air bombardment, including the notorious "Black Monday" raid of 25 September.

Background

Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939. Warsaw, a city of about 1.3 million, was defended by General Walerián Czuma's garrison, later reinforced by retreating field armies. The city's civilian defence was organised by its mayor, Stefan Starzyński, whose radio broadcasts became a symbol of resistance.

The siege

German armoured spearheads reached the suburbs on 8–9 September and were repelled in fighting around Ochota. Thereafter the Germans settled into a siege, subjecting Warsaw to continuous artillery fire and massed air attacks. On 25 September, "Black Monday", the Luftwaffe flew some 1,150 sorties and dropped hundreds of tonnes of bombs, including incendiaries; hospitals and waterworks were hit, fires raged across the city, and civilian casualties were enormous — total civilian deaths during the siege are estimated at 18,000–25,000. With food, water and ammunition exhausted, and no relief possible after the Soviet invasion of eastern Poland on 17 September, the garrison capitulated on 27–28 September; some 140,000 Polish soldiers were taken prisoner.

Aftermath

German troops entered Warsaw on 1 October 1939. The siege set the pattern for the brutal occupation that followed, which culminated in the destruction of the city in 1944.
