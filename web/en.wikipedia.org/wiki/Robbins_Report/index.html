Robbins Report

Robbins Report
Full title: Higher Education: Report of the Committee appointed by the Prime Minister under the Chairmanship of Lord Robbins
Published: 1963
Chair: Lionel Robbins
Subject: Expansion of British higher education

The Robbins Report is the 1963 report of the Committee on Higher Education in the United Kingdom, chaired by the economist Lionel Robbins. The committee had been appointed in 1961 to review the pattern of full-time higher education in Britain and to advise on its long-term development. Its report is regarded as a landmark in British education policy.

The Robbins principle

The report is famous for the Robbins principle: that "courses of higher education should be available for all those who are qualified by ability and attainment to pursue them and who wish to do so." The committee argued that the pool of talent in the population was far larger than the number of university places then available, and recommended a rapid expansion of student numbers, from about 216,000 full-time students in 1962–63 to some 558,000 by 1980–81.

Recommendations and effects

The government accepted the report's main recommendations almost immediately. The Colleges of Advanced Technology became universities, and a wave of new "plate glass" universities — including Sussex, York, East Anglia, Essex, Kent, Lancaster, and Warwick — was founded or expanded in the 1960s. The report also led to the creation of the Council for National Academic Awards to validate degrees in the non-university sector, reinforcing the "binary system" of universities and polytechnics that lasted until 1992.

Legacy

The Robbins Report set the framework for the mass expansion of British higher education in the second half of the 20th century, and its principle of admission by ability and attainment remains a reference point in debates over university access and funding.
