Axis mundi

The axis mundi (Latin for "axis of the world"), in certain beliefs and philosophies, is the world centre, or the connection between Heaven and Earth. As the celestial pole and the geographical pole connect, the axis mundi is the point where the four compass directions converge, and the place where communication between lower and higher realms is possible.

Manifestations

The historian of religion Mircea Eliade popularised the concept, identifying the axis mundi in sacred mountains (Mount Meru, Mount Olympus, Mount Fuji), world trees (Yggdrasil of Norse myth, the Tree of Life), pillars, ladders, and in built forms such as temples, ziggurats, stupas, church spires, minarets and totem poles. The axis is often sited at the omphalos, the "navel" of the world, as at Delphi. In shamanic traditions the axis is the route by which the shaman travels between worlds; in the human body it has been compared with the spinal column or the central channel of some yogic systems.

Interpretation

For Eliade, establishing an axis mundi sacralises a place and orients human habitation of the cosmos; the motif is one of the most widespread in comparative mythology, though later scholars have cautioned against flattening distinct traditions into a single universal pattern.
