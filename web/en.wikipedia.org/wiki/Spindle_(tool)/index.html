Spindle (tool)

A spindle is a rotating shaft or axis used in tools and machines to hold and drive a workpiece or a cutting tool. The term applies across many crafts: in textile work, a spindle is the slender rod on which thread is wound during spinning; in machine tools, the spindle is the precision rotating assembly of a lathe, mill, drill, or grinder.

Textile spindles

One of humanity's oldest tools, the hand spindle is a straight spike, often fitted with a whorl — a weight of clay, stone, or wood — that acts as a flywheel. The spinner drafts fibres from a supply held on a distaff or in the hand and twists them into yarn as the spindle rotates, either supported in a bowl or dropped to hang from the forming thread (the drop spindle). Spindles were later mounted in the spinning wheel and in mechanised spinning frames such as the spinning mule.

Machine tool spindles

In a lathe, the spindle in the headstock rotates the workpiece against a stationary cutting tool; in milling machines and drills, the spindle rotates the cutter. Machine spindles run in precision bearings (ball, roller, hydrostatic, or air bearings) and are driven by belts, gears, or built-in electric motors. Toolholders are secured in tapered sockets, such as Morse, ISO/BT, and HSK tapers. Spindle speed, runout, stiffness, and balance determine the accuracy and surface finish of machining; high-speed spindles in modern machining centres can exceed 20,000 revolutions per minute.

Other uses

The word also denotes the rotating pin of a hard disk drive or turntable, the axis of a gyroscope, and the turned vertical balusters ("spindles") of a staircase — reflecting the common theme of a slender rod about which something turns or is arranged.
