Pentaerythritol tetranitrate (PETN), also known as PENT, pentyl, penthrite, or nitropenta, is an explosive material. It is the nitrate ester of pentaerythritol, and is structurally very similar to nitroglycerin. Penta refers to the five carbon atoms of the neopentane skeleton. PETN is one of the most powerful high explosives known, with a relative effectiveness factor of 1.66.

PETN is also used as a vasodilator drug to treat certain heart conditions, such as for the management of angina.

History

Pentaerythritol tetranitrate was first prepared and patented in 1894 by the explosives manufacturer Rheinisch-Westfälische Sprengstoff A.G. of Cologne, Germany. It was introduced as a military explosive and saw use in both world wars. Between the wars it was used, among other things, as the filler of detonating cord and in small-calibre ammunition.

Properties

PETN is practically insoluble in water (0.04 g/100 mL at 25 °C), weakly soluble in ethanol and methanol, and soluble in acetone, methyl acetate, and dimethylformamide. Its melting point is 141.3 °C. The sensitivity of PETN to shock and friction is relatively high compared with explosives such as TNT and RDX; it ranks between primary and secondary explosives in ease of initiation, which is why it is widely used in detonators and detonating cords, generally desensitized with wax or mixed with a plasticizer or another explosive.

Production

The preparation of PETN involves the nitration of pentaerythritol with a mixture of concentrated nitric and sulfuric acids, or with white fuming nitric acid alone. The product is precipitated in water, purified by recrystallization, and dried. The pentaerythritol feedstock is produced industrially by the condensation of acetaldehyde and formaldehyde in an alkaline medium.

Uses

As an explosive

As a pure explosive PETN is used in detonating cord (Primacord), where it is enclosed in a textile or plastic sheath; mixed with about 8% wax it forms the composition used in some boosters. Plasticized with nitrocellulose it forms Semtex-H together with RDX; mixed with oils and waxes it is used in plastic-bonded formulations. It is also the explosive fill in many detonators and in the explosive lenses of some designs.

Medical use

Like nitroglycerin, PETN is used medically as a vasodilator in the treatment of angina pectoris. The drug, marketed under names such as Pentalong, is taken orally and has a longer duration of action than nitroglycerin. Its active metabolite, pentaerythritol trinitrate, contributes to its sustained effect.

Detection

Because PETN has a very low vapour pressure, it is harder to detect with vapour-sniffing instruments than TNT or nitroglycerin, and trace detection typically relies on swabbing surfaces followed by ion mobility spectrometry or mass spectrometry. Trace amounts of PETN have been used in several attempted terrorist attacks, which led to increased research into its detection.
