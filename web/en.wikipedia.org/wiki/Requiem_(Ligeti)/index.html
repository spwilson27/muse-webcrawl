Requiem (Ligeti)

ComposerGyörgy Ligeti
Year1963–1965
ScoringSoprano, mezzo-soprano, mixed choir, orchestra

György Ligeti

Requiem is a large-scale choral and orchestral composition by the Hungarian composer György Ligeti, composed between 1963 and 1965. It is one of the works that established Ligeti's international reputation and is famous for its use of micropolyphony, in which densely woven individual vocal and instrumental lines fuse into slowly shifting masses of sound.

Background

Ligeti left Hungary after the failed revolution of 1956 and settled in the West, where works such as Apparitions (1958–59) and Atmosphères (1961) had already made his name. The Requiem was commissioned by Swedish Radio and was first performed in Stockholm in 1965.

Music

The work sets four sections of the Latin requiem mass: the Introitus, Kyrie, Dies irae, and Lacrimosa. The Kyrie became particularly celebrated after Stanley Kubrick used it in the film 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968), alongside Atmosphères and Lux Aeterna. Ligeti's writing avoids traditional counterpoint in favour of clustered canons at the unison, producing textures in which individual lines are imperceptible but their aggregate motion is clearly audible.

Reception

The Requiem is regarded as one of the central choral works of the post-war avant-garde and remains one of Ligeti's most frequently performed large-scale compositions.
