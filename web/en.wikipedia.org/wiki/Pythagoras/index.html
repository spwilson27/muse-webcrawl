Pythagoras

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Marble bust of Pythagoras wearing a headband, Roman copy of a Greek original]

Pythagoras of Samos (c. 570 – c. 495 BC) was an ancient Greek philosopher and the eponymous founder of Pythagoreanism. He is credited in tradition with the Pythagorean theorem relating the sides of a right triangle — a relationship known earlier in Babylon and elsewhere — though no writings by Pythagoras survive and much about him is legend.

Life and school

Pythagoras is said to have travelled widely before settling in Croton in southern Italy, where he founded a religious and philosophical community whose members, the Pythagoreans, pursued mathematics, music theory and a disciplined communal way of life with secrecy and dietary rules.

Influence

Pythagorean ideas linked number with the structure of the cosmos and musical harmony, influencing Plato and later Western mathematics and philosophy. The discovery traditionally attributed to his school that the square root of 2 is irrational challenged the doctrine that all is number (whole numbers and their ratios).
