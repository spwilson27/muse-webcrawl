The Vistula Lagoon (Polish: Zalew Wiślany; Russian: Калининградский залив, Kaliningradskiy zaliv; German: Frisches Haff) is a brackish water lagoon on the Baltic Sea roughly 56 miles (90 km) long, 6 to 15 miles (10 to 19 km) wide, and up to 17 feet (5.2 m) deep, separated from the Gulf of Gdańsk by the Vistula Spit. The lagoon is shared between Poland and Russia, with the border between the two countries running across its waters.

Geography

The lagoon receives the waters of several rivers, including branches of the Vistula delta such as the Nogat, the Elbląg River, and the Pregolya, which flows past the city of Kaliningrad (formerly Königsberg). Historically, the only connection to the open sea was the Strait of Baltiysk in the Russian part of the lagoon. In September 2022, Poland opened the Vistula Spit canal, a shipping channel through the spit that gives Polish ports such as Elbląg direct access to the Baltic without passing through Russian waters.

History

Known in German as the Frisches Haff, the lagoon was part of East Prussia until 1945. In the final months of the Second World War, hundreds of thousands of German civilians fled across the frozen lagoon during the evacuation of East Prussia, and many perished when Soviet aircraft broke the ice. After the war the northern part passed to the Soviet Union and the southern part to Poland.

Economy and ecology

Fishing is an important local activity, with catches including pike-perch, perch, roach and eel. The lagoon's shallow, reed-fringed shores are an important habitat for water birds and form part of several protected areas.
