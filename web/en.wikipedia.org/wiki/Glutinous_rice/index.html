Glutinous rice (also called sticky rice or sweet rice) is a type of rice grown mainly in Southeast and East Asia. It is characterised by opaque grains and a very low amylose content; its starch consists almost entirely of amylopectin, which makes the cooked rice sticky and clumping. Despite its name, glutinous rice contains no gluten — "glutinous" refers to its glue-like texture.

Cultivation and use

Glutinous rice is the staple food of Laos and of northeastern Thailand (Isan), where it is eaten by hand with grilled meats and papaya salad. It is soaked before cooking and traditionally steamed in bamboo baskets rather than boiled, since boiling makes it mushy.

Dishes

  • China: zongzi (rice dumplings wrapped in bamboo leaves), nian gao (New Year cake), tangyuan (sweet filled balls), and eight-treasure rice.
  • Japan: mochi, made by pounding steamed glutinous rice; also sekihan with azuki beans.
  • Korea: tteok (rice cakes) and chapssal-based sweets and porridges.
  • Thailand and Laos: mango sticky rice (khao niao mamuang), khao tom mat, and sticky rice as a daily staple.
  • Vietnam: xôi (savoury and sweet sticky rice dishes) and bánh chưng for Tết.

Other uses

Glutinous rice flour is used as a thickener and in gluten-free baking; fermented glutinous rice gives rice wines such as Japanese amazake ferments and Korean makgeolli. Historically, sticky rice lime mortar — lime mixed with glutinous rice soup — was used in Chinese construction, including parts of city walls and tombs, and is credited with unusual durability.
