Operation Himmler was a series of false flag operations carried out by Nazi Germany on the night of 31 August 1939, designed to make it appear that Poland had attacked German targets and thus provide a pretext for the German invasion of Poland the following day. It is named after Heinrich Himmler, head of the SS, who directed the operation. The most famous incident of the operation was the staged attack on the radio station at Gleiwitz (now Gliwice, Poland).

Planning

The operation was conceived by Himmler and Reinhard Heydrich and executed by SS and SD personnel. Alfred Naujocks, an SS-Sturmbannführer, led the Gleiwitz team. The overall scheme included staged incidents at a number of locations along the German–Polish border, including attacks on a forestry station and a customs post. To lend authenticity, the SS used concentration camp prisoners dressed in Polish uniforms, killed them, and left their bodies at the scenes as supposed Polish casualties; the prisoners were contemptuously referred to in SS code as "canned goods" (Konserve).

The Gleiwitz incident

On the evening of 31 August 1939, Naujocks and a small team seized the radio station at Gleiwitz. A Polish-speaking member of the team broadcast a brief anti-German message in Polish, claiming the station was in Polish hands and calling on Poles to take up arms. Shots were fired to simulate an attack. The body of Franciszek Honiok, a Silesian farmer arrested by the Gestapo the previous day for his pro-Polish sympathies, was left at the station as a supposed attacker; Honiok is sometimes described as the first victim of the Second World War.

Aftermath

German propaganda trumpeted the "Polish attacks" as justification for the invasion launched at dawn on 1 September 1939. Adolf Hitler referred to border incidents in his speech to the Reichstag that day. The deception was exposed publicly after the war, notably through Naujocks's affidavit at the Nuremberg trials, which described the operation in detail. The Gleiwitz radio tower, the tallest wooden structure in Europe at the time, still stands in Gliwice and houses a museum commemorating the incident.
