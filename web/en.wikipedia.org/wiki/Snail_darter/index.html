Snail darter

The snail darter (Percina tanasi) is a small species of freshwater ray-finned fish in the darter subfamily, endemic to the Tennessee River drainage in the southeastern United States. It grows to about 9 cm (3.5 in) in length and feeds primarily on snails, from which it takes its name.

Discovery and the Tellico Dam controversy

The fish was discovered in 1973 in the Little Tennessee River by ichthyologist David Etnier. Shortly afterward it was listed as endangered under the newly enacted Endangered Species Act of 1973. Its listing triggered a famous legal battle over completion of the Tennessee Valley Authority's Tellico Dam, whose reservoir would inundate the fish's only known habitat.

In Tennessee Valley Authority v. Hill (1978), the U.S. Supreme Court ruled that the Endangered Species Act required halting the dam. Congress subsequently exempted the Tellico Dam from the Act, and the dam was completed in 1979. Snail darters were transplanted to other rivers, and additional populations were later discovered.

Recovery

Thanks to the discovery of further populations and successful translocations, the snail darter was downlisted from endangered to threatened in 1984, and in 2022 it was removed from the endangered species list entirely, having been declared recovered.
