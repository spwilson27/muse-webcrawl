Autograph (manuscript)

MeaningA document written entirely in the author's own hand

An autograph or autograph manuscript is a manuscript written by its author, in the author's own handwriting. The term is used especially in textual criticism, musical scholarship and the antiquarian book trade to distinguish an author's original from copies (apographs) made by scribes, copyists or later editors.

Significance

Autograph manuscripts are of primary importance to scholars because they preserve an author's readings, revisions and working process before editorial intervention. In music, the autograph score — for example Beethoven's heavily corrected manuscripts — is normally the most authoritative source for a critical edition, though a composer's final intentions may also appear in first editions corrected by hand. Surviving autographs of major literary works, such as Charlotte Brontë's juvenilia or the autograph draft of Handel's Messiah held by the British Library, command very high prices at auction.

Autograph versus signature

The word is also used more loosely for a person's signature — the sense dominant in the modern collecting hobby of autographed memorabilia. Strictly, however, a signed document is not necessarily an autograph document: a letter written by a secretary and merely signed by its author is termed an "autograph letter signed" (ALS) only if the body is in the author's hand; otherwise it is a "letter signed" (LS) or document signed (DS), distinctions carefully maintained by manuscript dealers.
