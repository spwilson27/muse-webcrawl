House of Orléans

The House of Orléans is a cadet branch of the French royal House of Bourbon, descended from Philippe I, Duke of Orléans, the younger brother of Louis XIV. Several lines of the family held the dukedom of Orléans and stood close to the throne.

History

Philippe II, Duke of Orléans, served as Regent of France during the minority of Louis XV. His descendant Louis Philippe Joseph, known as Philippe Égalité, voted for the death of Louis XVI in 1793 and was himself guillotined later that year.

July Monarchy

Philippe Égalité's son Louis Philippe I became King of the French after the July Revolution of 1830, ruling the constitutional "July Monarchy" until the Revolution of 1848. His descendants form the Orléanist line of pretenders to the French throne, currently headed by Jean, Count of Paris.

Other branches

A Brazilian branch descends from Gaston, Count of Eu, who married Isabel, Princess Imperial of Brazil; the Orléans-Braganza family remains prominent in Brazilian monarchist circles.
