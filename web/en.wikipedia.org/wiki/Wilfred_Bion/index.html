Wilfred Bion

Wilfred Ruprecht Bion (8 September 1897 – 8 November 1979) was a British psychoanalyst who became the president of the British Psychoanalytical Society from 1962 to 1965. His theories, which emphasised group dynamics, thinking, and the containment of emotion, extended the work of Melanie Klein and have been influential in psychoanalysis, organisational consulting, and psychotherapy.

Life

Bion was born in India and educated in England. He served as a tank commander in the First World War, winning the Distinguished Service Order, and the traumatic experience of combat informed his later work on groups and psychosis. He studied medicine at University College London, trained as a psychoanalyst, and during the Second World War pioneered group therapy with shell-shocked soldiers at Northfield Military Hospital.

Ideas

Bion's Experiences in Groups (1961) analysed the unconscious "basic assumptions" — dependency, fight–flight, and pairing — that undermine a group's work. His later "theory of thinking" (in Learning from Experience, 1962) proposed that thoughts precede the thinker: the infant's raw emotional experiences ("beta elements") must be transformed by a caregiver's "alpha function" into tolerable thoughts ("alpha elements"), a relationship he called container and contained. Later works — Elements of Psycho-analysis (1963), Transformations (1965), and Attention and Interpretation (1970) — developed an increasingly abstract, mathematical style of exposition that remains demanding and controversial.
