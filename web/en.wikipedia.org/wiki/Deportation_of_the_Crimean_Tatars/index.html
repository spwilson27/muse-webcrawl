The deportation of the Crimean Tatars (Crimean Tatar: Qırımtatar sürgünligi; the event is remembered as the Sürgünlik, "exile") was the ethnic cleansing and cultural genocide of the Crimean Tatars by the Soviet regime under Joseph Stalin. On 18 May 1944, the entire Crimean Tatar population of the Crimean Peninsula — around 190,000 to 230,000 people — was forcibly transferred by the NKVD to the Uzbek SSR and other distant regions of the USSR, officially as collective punishment for alleged collaboration with Nazi Germany during the occupation of Crimea.

Course of events

Within three days, NKVD troops rounded up virtually the entire ethnic group, loading them onto freight trains bound mainly for Uzbekistan. Thousands died en route from thirst, hunger and disease, and mortality in exile during the following years was severe — estimates of deaths range from roughly one fifth to nearly half of the deported population. Crimean Tatar soldiers serving in the Red Army were demobilised and sent to join their families in exile.

Aftermath

Crimea was repopulated largely with ethnic Russians and Ukrainians, Tatar place names were replaced, and in 1954 the peninsula was transferred to the Ukrainian SSR. Although a 1967 decree lifted the accusation of treason, Crimean Tatars were not permitted to return en masse until the late 1980s; some 260,000 returned after Ukrainian independence. Ukraine's parliament recognised the deportation as genocide in 2015, as have several other states. 18 May is commemorated annually as a day of remembrance; the singer Jamala, whose song "1944" won the Eurovision Song Contest 2016, brought the event to international attention.
