Rail Baltica is an ongoing greenfield railway infrastructure project to integrate the Baltic states into the European rail network. Its purpose is to provide a link between Helsinki and the capitals of Estonia, Latvia, and Lithuania, continuing to Warsaw and the rest of the European standard-gauge network. The project is a priority of the European Union's Trans-European Transport Network (TEN-T) North Sea–Baltic corridor.

Route and design

The main line runs approximately 870 km from Tallinn in Estonia through Pärnu, Riga in Latvia, Panevėžys and Kaunas in Lithuania, to the Polish border and onward to Warsaw. Unlike the 1,520 mm broad gauge inherited from the Russian Empire and Soviet period, Rail Baltica uses the 1,435 mm standard gauge, is electrified, and is double-tracked for most of its length. A connection to Helsinki via a proposed undersea tunnel under the Gulf of Finland has been studied but is not part of the current project.

History and construction

The three Baltic states coordinate the project through the joint venture RB Rail AS, founded in 2014. Feasibility studies began in the 2000s; construction of stations and sections started in the late 2010s and early 2020s, with major works under way at Riga Central Station, Tallinn Ülemiste terminal, and Kaunas. Costs have risen substantially from early estimates of around €5.8 billion to much higher figures in later assessments, and completion is targeted for around 2030 for an initial single-track configuration, after earlier targets slipped.

Significance

The line is intended to reduce dependence on Russian-gauge infrastructure, shorten travel times dramatically (Tallinn–Riga in about two hours), carry freight between northern and central Europe, and provide strategic military mobility for NATO's eastern flank — a dimension that gained prominence after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.
