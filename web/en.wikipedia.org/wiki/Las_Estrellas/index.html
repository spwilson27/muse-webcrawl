Las Estrellas

Las Estrellas ("The Stars") is a Mexican free-to-air television network owned by Televisa. It is Televisa's flagship network and one of the main Spanish-language broadcasters in the world, distributed throughout Mexico by affiliated stations and internationally via cable and satellite.

History

The network began broadcasting on 21 March 1951 as XEW-TV channel 2 in Mexico City, founded by Emilio Azcárraga Vidaurreta as part of Telesistema Mexicano, the forerunner of Televisa. Known for decades simply as El Canal de las Estrellas (The Channel of the Stars), it was officially rebranded as Las Estrellas in 2016. Its Mexico City flagship station remains XEW-TDT.

Programming

Las Estrellas is best known for its prime-time telenovelas, which have been exported across Latin America and beyond, as well as national newscasts (En Punto and morning programmes), comedy and variety shows, and football coverage, including Liga MX and the Mexico national team. Long-running programmes have included Siempre en Domingo and the news magazine tradition anchored from its studios by journalists such as Jacobo Zabludovsky, who fronted the flagship newscast 24 Horas for nearly three decades.
