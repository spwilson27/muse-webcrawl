Arc welding is a family of fusion welding processes that use an electric arc between an electrode and the workpiece to melt metals at the joint. The arc, with temperatures of several thousand degrees Celsius, forms a molten pool that solidifies into a weld, usually protected from oxidation by shielding gas or flux.

Processes

Shielded metal arc welding (SMAW, "stick") uses a flux-coated consumable electrode. Gas metal arc welding (GMAW, MIG) feeds wire continuously under inert or active gas, while gas tungsten arc welding (GTAW, TIG) uses a non-consumable tungsten electrode for precise work. Flux-cored and submerged arc welding serve heavy industry and shipbuilding.

Safety

Hazards include ultraviolet "arc eye", electric shock, fumes and burns, requiring helmets with dark filters, gloves and ventilation.
