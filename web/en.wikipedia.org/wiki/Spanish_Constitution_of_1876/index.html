Spanish Constitution of 1876

The Spanish Constitution of 1876 was the constitution of Spain during the Bourbon Restoration. Promulgated on 30 June 1876 under King Alfonso XII, it was the longest-lasting constitution in Spanish history, remaining formally in force until 1931, although it was suspended during the dictatorship of Primo de Rivera (1923–1930).

Origins

The constitution was drafted under the leadership of Antonio Cánovas del Castillo, the architect of the Restoration settlement. It sought to provide stability after decades of coups, civil wars, and short-lived constitutions by establishing a constitutional monarchy with shared sovereignty between the King and the Cortes.

Provisions

The text established a bicameral Cortes (a Senate partly appointed and partly elected, and an elected Congress of Deputies), strong executive powers for the Crown, Catholicism as the state religion with limited tolerance of private worship, and a declaration of rights whose exercise was left to ordinary laws. Electoral laws determined the franchise in practice: censitary suffrage initially, and universal male suffrage from 1890.

Political system

Under the constitution operated the turno pacífico, the managed alternation in power of the Conservative and Liberal parties, sustained by electoral manipulation through local bosses (caciques). The system delivered stability but was increasingly criticized for corruption and unrepresentativeness, and it collapsed with the proclamation of the Second Spanish Republic in 1931.
