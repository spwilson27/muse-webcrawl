Weekly Shōnen Sunday (Japanese: 週刊少年サンデー, Hepburn: Shūkan Shōnen Sandē) is a weekly shōnen manga magazine published in Japan by Shogakukan since 1959. Despite its name, it is released on Wednesdays. Along with Weekly Shōnen Jump (Shueisha) and Weekly Shōnen Magazine (Kodansha), it is one of the three historic flagship weekly boys' manga anthologies of Japan, and its collected volumes are published under the Shōnen Sunday Comics imprint.

History

The magazine launched in March 1959, the same week as its rival Weekly Shōnen Magazine, as manga publishing shifted toward weekly anthologies. Early contributors included Osamu Tezuka and the duo Fujiko Fujio. Circulation peaked during the manga boom of the 1980s and 1990s, when Rumiko Takahashi's Urusei Yatsura and Ranma ½ and Mitsuru Adachi's baseball romance Touch were flagship titles. Gosho Aoyama's Case Closed (Detective Conan), serialised since 1994, became the magazine's longest-running and best-selling anchor series, joined by Takahashi's Inuyasha. Like its competitors, Sunday's print circulation has declined steeply in the digital era, from several million copies weekly at its peak to a few hundred thousand, though hits such as Frieren: Beyond Journey's End (2020–) show its continuing influence.
