Solar power

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Rows of blue photovoltaic panels in a solar farm in the desert under a clear sky]
A utility-scale solar farm.

Solar power is the conversion of energy from sunlight into electricity, either directly using photovoltaics (PV) or indirectly using concentrated solar power. Solar technologies include rooftop and utility-scale solar farms, solar water heating and solar architecture. By the mid-2020s PV had become, in many regions, the cheapest source of new electricity in history, according to the IEA, and solar is the fastest-growing energy source worldwide.

2. Concentrated solar and other uses

Photovoltaics

PV cells, usually made from silicon, convert light directly into electricity via the photovoltaic effect. Falling costs — driven by mass manufacture, mostly in China — saw cumulative global PV capacity pass 1 terawatt in 2022 and grow rapidly thereafter. Challenges include intermittency (addressed with storage and grids) and land use.

Concentrated solar and other uses

Concentrated solar power uses mirrors to focus sunlight, heating a fluid to drive a turbine, and can store heat for evening generation. Solar energy also heats water and buildings and powers small devices from calculators to spacecraft.
