Taras Shevchenko

Taras Shevchenko (1814–1861), Ukrainian poet, writer and artist

Taras Hryhorovych Shevchenko (Ukrainian: Тарас Григорович Шевченко; 9 March 1814 – 10 March 1861) was a Ukrainian poet, writer, artist, public and political figure, folklorist and ethnographer. Regarded as the founder of modern Ukrainian literature and a national hero of Ukraine, his literary heritage — above all the poetry collection Kobzar (1840) — played a central role in the development of the modern Ukrainian language and national consciousness.

Early life and freedom

Shevchenko was born a serf in the village of Moryntsi, in the Kyiv Governorate of the Russian Empire (now Cherkasy Oblast, Ukraine). Orphaned young, he became a household servant of his landlord Pavel Engelhardt, who took him to Vilnius and then Saint Petersburg. There his artistic talent was recognised; the painters Karl Bryullov and others raised the sum to buy his freedom,, Bryullov's portrait of the poet Vasily Zhukovsky was raffled to raise the 2,500 roubles paid for Shevchenko's manumission in 1838. He then studied at the Imperial Academy of Arts.

Career and exile

The publication of Kobzar in 1840 brought him fame. His poems combined lyrical and epic forms with themes of Ukrainian history, the suffering of serfs, and longing for freedom. In the 1840s he travelled in Ukraine, joined the secret Brotherhood of Saints Cyril and Methodius, and wrote increasingly political verse. Arrested in 1847, he was exiled as a private to military service in the Orenburg region and later at Novopetrovskoye (now Fort-Shevchenko, Kazakhstan), under an order forbidding him to write or draw. He was released in 1857 but his health was broken; he died in Saint Petersburg in 1861.

Legacy

Shevchenko's remains were reburied on Chernecha Hora near Kaniv, in accordance with his poem "Zapovit" ("Testament"), which became a Ukrainian national anthem-like text. Monuments to him stand in cities worldwide, Kyiv's national university bears his name, and his portrait appears on the Ukrainian 100-hryvnia note. He also left a substantial body of paintings, drawings and etchings, including his series of works from the Aral Sea expedition of 1848–49.
