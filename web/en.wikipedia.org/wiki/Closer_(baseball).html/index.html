Closer (baseball)

A closer is a relief pitcher who specialises in getting the final outs in a close game when his team is leading. The role is often assigned to a team's best reliever. Closer roles arose in Major League Baseball in the 1960s and became standard in the 1970s and 1980s.

Role

A closer is generally brought in for the final inning (usually the ninth) when his team leads by three runs or fewer — a "save situation". When a closer records the final outs while preserving the lead he is credited with a save. Mariano Rivera, the longtime New York Yankees closer, holds the major league record with 652 saves and is regarded as the greatest closer of all time; he was inducted into the Baseball Hall of Fame unanimously in 2019.

Usage and criticism

Analysts have debated whether reserving the best reliever for the ninth inning is optimal, arguing that relief aces might be better used in higher-leverage situations earlier in games. Nonetheless the closer remains a fixture of modern bullpen construction.
