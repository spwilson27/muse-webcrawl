Dynein

Dynein
Type: motor protein (AAA+ ATPase)
Track: microtubules, toward minus end
Functions: intracellular transport, ciliary beating, cell division

Dynein is a family of cytoskeletal motor proteins that move along microtubules in cells, converting the chemical energy of ATP hydrolysis into mechanical work. Dyneins move toward the minus end of microtubules — opposite to most kinesins — and are among the largest and most complex molecular motors known.

Types and functions

Cytoplasmic dynein, working with its activator dynactin and cargo adaptors, transports vesicles, organelles, proteins, and mRNA toward the cell centre, positions the nucleus and mitotic spindle, and helps organise the Golgi apparatus. Axonemal dyneins, permanently attached in rows along the microtubules of cilia and flagella, slide adjacent doublets to produce the bending waves that propel sperm and move fluids across ciliated epithelia.

Clinical significance

Defects in axonemal dyneins cause primary ciliary dyskinesia, with chronic respiratory disease and situs inversus (Kartagener syndrome). Mutations in cytoplasmic dynein heavy chain are linked to neurodegenerative and neurodevelopmental disorders, reflecting the motor's importance in long-distance transport within neurons.
