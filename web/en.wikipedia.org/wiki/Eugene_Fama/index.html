Eugene Fama

Eugene Francis Fama (born 14 February 1939) is an American economist, best known for his empirical work on portfolio theory, asset pricing, and the efficient-market hypothesis. He shared the 2013 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences with Robert J. Shiller and Lars Peter Hansen.

Born14 February 1939, Boston, Massachusetts
EducationTufts University; University of Chicago (MBA, PhD)

AwardsNobel Prize in Economics (2013)

Work

Fama's 1965 and 1970 work formalised the idea that stock prices are hard to predict in the short run because they incorporate new information quickly. With Kenneth French he developed the Fama–French three-factor model (1992–93) and later a five-factor model, adding size, value, profitability and investment factors to explain stock returns. He spent his career at the University of Chicago Booth School of Business and is associated with Dimensional Fund Advisors.
