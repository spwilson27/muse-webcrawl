Academy of Television Arts & Sciences

The Academy of Television Arts & Sciences, commonly known as the Television Academy, is a professional honorary organisation dedicated to the advancement of the television industry in the United States. It was founded in 1946 by animator and producer Syd Cassyd and is best known for presenting the Primetime Emmy Awards.

History

The Academy was founded in Los Angeles in 1946. A New York faction later split to form the National Academy of Television Arts and Sciences, which administers the Daytime, Sports and News & Documentary Emmy Awards, while the Los Angeles-based Television Academy retained the Primetime Emmys. The two organisations also jointly oversee other Emmy ceremonies.

Activities

The Academy has tens of thousands of members organised into peer groups covering disciplines from directing and writing to animation and interactive media. In addition to the Primetime Emmy Awards, it maintains the Television Academy Hall of Fame, founded in 1984, and runs educational and archival programmes through the Television Academy Foundation, including the Archive of American Television interviews.
