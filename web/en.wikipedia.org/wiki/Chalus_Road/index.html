Chalus Road

Chalus Road (Persian: جاده چالوس), officially Road 59 in the Iranian national highway network, is a mountain road in northern Iran linking Karaj, near Tehran, with Chalus on the Caspian Sea coast. Running through the Alborz mountains via the Kandovan Tunnel and the valley of the Karaj River, it is regarded as one of the most scenic drives in Iran and one of the country's most important and heavily travelled tourist routes, particularly busy during Nowruz and summer holidays.

Route

The road leaves Karaj and follows the Karaj River gorge past the Amir Kabir Dam (Karaj Dam), whose reservoir, Lake Karaj, is a popular recreation area. It then climbs in a series of hairpin bends through mountain villages and forests to the Kandovan Tunnel, a tunnel about 1,884 metres long cut through the Alborz watershed at an elevation of roughly 2,700 metres. North of the tunnel the road descends through the lush, humid forests of Mazandaran to the Caspian coastal plain, ending at Chalus.

History

The road was built by decree of Reza Shah Pahlavi and opened in 1933 to provide a direct link between the capital region and the Caspian littoral, replacing an older mule track. The Kandovan Tunnel was completed a few years later, in 1938, giving the road its year-round character, although heavy snow and avalanches still close it in winter. Today the Tehran–North Freeway (Freeway 3) is being built as a faster alternative, with sections of tunnel and viaduct progressively opening, but the old Chalus Road remains a beloved touring route with roadside restaurants, tea houses and picnic spots along the river.

Hazards

The combination of steep gradients, sharp bends, rockfall and avalanche risk, and very heavy holiday traffic makes Chalus Road one of Iran's most accident-prone highways. Authorities sometimes convert it to one-way traffic during peak holiday periods to ease congestion.
