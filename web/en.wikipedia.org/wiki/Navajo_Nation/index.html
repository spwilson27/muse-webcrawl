Navajo Nation

Capital: Window Rock, Arizona
Area: about 27,000 sq mi (71,000 km²)
Population: roughly 400,000 enrolled members
Languages: Navajo (Diné bizaad), English
Government: President, Vice President, Navajo Nation Council

The Navajo Nation (Navajo: Naabeehó Bináhásdzo) is a Native American tribal nation and Indian reservation covering about 27,000 square miles (71,000 km²) of northeastern Arizona, southeastern Utah, and northwestern New Mexico in the United States. It is the largest reservation in the country by land area, and the Navajo are one of the largest federally recognised tribes by enrollment.

History

The Navajo (Diné, "the People") are an Athabaskan-speaking people who migrated into the Southwest centuries before Spanish contact. After conflicts with the United States, the Navajo were forcibly removed in 1864 on the "Long Walk" to internment at Bosque Redondo in New Mexico, where many died. The Treaty of 1868 allowed their return to a portion of their homeland and established the original reservation, which was expanded repeatedly in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

During the Second World War, Navajo Code Talkers used their language as an unbreakable battlefield code for the U.S. Marine Corps in the Pacific. Uranium mining on Navajo land during the Cold War left lasting health and environmental legacies that remain the subject of compensation claims.

Government

The Navajo Nation is governed from its capital at Window Rock, Arizona, by an elected president and vice president and the Navajo Nation Council, whose delegates are chosen from 110 local chapters. The tribal government administers its own courts, police, and programmes in health, education, and natural resources. In 2023 Buu Nygren became president of the Navajo Nation.

Culture and economy

The Navajo language remains one of the most widely spoken Indigenous languages north of Mexico, though transmission to children has declined. Livestock raising, weaving, silversmithing, energy resources, and tourism — including Monument Valley and Antelope Canyon — shape the economy, alongside persistent challenges in water access, infrastructure, and employment.
