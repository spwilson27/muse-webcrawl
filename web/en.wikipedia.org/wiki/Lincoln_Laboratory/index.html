Lincoln Laboratory

Established: 1951
Location: Lexington, Massachusetts, United States
Operator: Massachusetts Institute of Technology
Type: Federally funded research and development center (FFRDC)
Sponsor: U.S. Department of Defense

MIT Lincoln Laboratory, located in Lexington, Massachusetts, is a United States Department of Defense research and development center chartered to apply advanced technology to problems of national security. It is a federally funded research and development center (FFRDC) administered by the Massachusetts Institute of Technology and funded primarily by the Department of Defense, with a staff of roughly 4,000.

History

The laboratory was founded in 1951 at the recommendation of the Project Charles study on air defense, to develop the continental air defense system against Soviet bombers. Its early work produced the SAGE (Semi-Automatic Ground Environment) air defense network, driving advances in real-time computing (the Whirlwind computer and the TX-0 and TX-2), magnetic-core memory, and radar. In 1958 the first Lincoln Laboratory satellite communications experiments followed; the lab went on to develop the Lincoln Experimental Satellites and long-running work in space surveillance at the Lincoln Space Surveillance Complex in Westford, Massachusetts.

Research areas

Research spans air, missile and maritime defense; space control; communications; cyber security; air traffic control (including weather sensing for aviation); advanced electronics; and systems prototyping. The laboratory operates the Lincoln Near-Earth Asteroid Research (LINEAR) program credited with discovering many asteroids and comets, and develops laser communication systems such as those demonstrated on lunar and deep-space missions.
