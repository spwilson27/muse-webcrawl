Birka, on the island of Björkö in Lake Mälaren, Sweden, was an important Viking Age trading centre that handled goods from Scandinavia, Finland, the Baltic region and beyond, including wares that arrived via Russia from the Byzantine Empire and the Islamic world. Founded around AD 750, it is regarded as Sweden's first town, and at its height it had a population of roughly 700–1,000 people. The town was abandoned in the late tenth century, its role passing to Sigtuna.

History

Birka is described in written sources including Rimbert's Life of Ansgar, which records the missionary Ansgar's visits in 829 and the 850s to preach Christianity to the Swedes. The town was protected by a semicircular defensive rampart, a hillfort, and a harbour with jetties. Trade goods excavated at the site include Arabic silver dirhams, silk, glassware, and Rhenish pottery.

Archaeology

The "Black Earth" of Birka — the dark occupation soil of the town site — and its surrounding cemeteries contain around 3,000 graves, including rich chamber graves filled with weapons, jewellery, horses and imported luxuries. Excavations began in the seventeenth century; major campaigns by Hjalmar Stolpe in the nineteenth century and by the Swedish History Museum in the 1990s have greatly expanded knowledge of the town. A celebrated 2017 DNA study confirmed that the occupant of the high-status warrior grave Bj 581 was a woman, sparking wide discussion about women warriors in Viking society.

Legacy

Birka, together with the royal estate at Hovgården on the neighbouring island of Adelsö, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1993. A museum on Björkö displays finds and reconstructions of Viking houses and boats.
