Port Arthur is a town and former convict settlement on the Tasman Peninsula, in Tasmania, Australia. It is located approximately 97 kilometres (60 mi) southeast of Hobart. The site forms part of the Australian Convict Sites, a World Heritage property consisting of eleven remnant penal sites originally built within the British Empire during the 18th and 19th centuries on fertile Australian coastal strips.

History as a penal colony

Port Arthur was founded in 1830 as a timber-getting station and from 1833 became a punishment station for repeat offenders transported from elsewhere in Australia. At its peak in the 1840s it held more than 1,100 convicts and was an industrial settlement producing ships, furniture, bricks, and other goods, connected to Hobart by one of Australia's earliest railways pushed by convicts. The station pioneered use of the "Separate Prison" (modelled on Pentonville), where solitary confinement and silent reflection replaced physical punishment, and a Point Puer boys' prison was established in 1834 for juvenile convicts. Escapes were deterred by the narrow isthmus at Eaglehawk Neck, guarded by soldiers and a line of dogs. The prison closed in 1877.

After closure

After closure the town was renamed Carnarvon, but the name Port Arthur was restored in 1927 as tourism to the ruins grew. Bushfires in the 1890s destroyed many buildings, leaving the sandstone penitentiary, church, and hospital as dramatic ruins that became one of Tasmania's leading visitor attractions.

1996 massacre

On 28 April 1996, Port Arthur was the site of the worst mass shooting in modern Australian history, in which gunman Martin Bryant murdered 35 people and wounded 23 at and around the historic site. The massacre prompted sweeping national gun law reforms, including a buyback scheme and strict licensing under the National Firearms Agreement. A memorial garden and pool of reflection commemorate the victims.
