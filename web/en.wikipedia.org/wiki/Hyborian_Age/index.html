The Hyborian Age is a fictional historical period created by American writer Robert E. Howard as the setting for his sword and sorcery stories of Conan the Barbarian. Howard placed the age between the sinking of Atlantis and the beginnings of recorded history — roughly 32,500 to 10,000 years before the present in his timeline, with Conan's career set around 10,000 BC. Howard elaborated the setting in his essay "The Hyborian Age" (written in the 1930s, published posthumously), which traced the migrations and kingdoms of its peoples.

The world

The Hyborian world broadly resembles Eurasia and Africa reshaped: the kingdoms of the age include mighty Aquilonia, feudal Nemedia, decadent Stygia (an analogue of Egypt), Corinthia, Ophir, Argos, Zingara, Koth, Shem, Zamora with its evil towers, snowy Cimmeria (Conan's homeland), the Pictish Wilderness, the Viking-like lands of Asgard and Vanaheim, and the steppe empire of Turan. To the east lie Khitai, Hyrkania and the Vilayet Sea; to the south, Kush, Darfar, Keshan and the Black Kingdoms. Many names are borrowed from real ancient and medieval geography to give the setting a familiar-but-mythic flavour.

History within the fiction

Following the cataclysm that destroyed Atlantis and Lemuria, wandering tribes founded new civilisations; the Hyborian peoples migrated southward, founding the kingdoms that dominate the age. Conan, a Cimmerian mercenary, thief, pirate and soldier, eventually seized the throne of Aquilonia. Howard's essay closes with the Hyborian kingdoms' fall before new migrations, foreshadowing the real ancient world.

Use by later authors

After Howard's death in 1936, writers including L. Sprague de Camp and Lin Carter edited, completed and expanded the Conan canon, systematising the Hyborian chronology. The setting has been used in comics, films, role-playing games and the MMORPG Age of Conan.
