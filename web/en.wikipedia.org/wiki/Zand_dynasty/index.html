Zand dynasty

The Zand dynasty (Persian: سلسله زندیه) was an Iranian dynasty founded by Karim Khan Zand, a member of the Zand tribe of the Laks, which ruled Iran from 1751 to 1794. After the assassination of Nader Shah in 1747 threw the Afsharid empire into civil war, Karim Khan emerged as the dominant power, ruling from Shiraz not as shah but under the modest title Vakil ol-Ra'aya ("Deputy of the People"), nominally as regent for a Safavid puppet. His reign brought a generation of peace and prosperity; after his death in 1779, succession struggles weakened the dynasty until its last ruler, Lotf Ali Khan, was defeated by Agha Mohammad Khan Qajar.

Reign of Karim Khan

By 1760 Karim Khan had defeated his rivals and controlled all of Iran except Khorasan. He made Shiraz his capital and rebuilt it, erecting the Vakil mosque, bazaar and bathhouse that remain landmarks of the city, and constructing the citadel of the Arg. He encouraged agriculture and foreign trade, inviting the British East India Company to set up a factory at Bushehr, and in 1776 captured Basra from the Ottomans. Unusually for an 18th-century conqueror, he is remembered for clemency and for refusing the royal title.

Decline and fall

Karim Khan's death in 1779 triggered a brutal contest among his sons and brothers; five Zand rulers held Shiraz in a decade. Lotf Ali Khan, who came to power in 1789, proved a capable and popular ruler but could not match the relentless Agha Mohammad Khan Qajar. Betrayed at Kerman in 1794 after a famous siege and massacre, Lotf Ali Khan was captured, blinded and executed, ending the dynasty; the Qajars reunified Iran under their own rule.
