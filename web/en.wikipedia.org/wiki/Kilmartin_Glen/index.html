Kilmartin Glen

Kilmartin Glen is a valley in Argyll, on the west coast of Scotland, celebrated as one of the richest prehistoric landscapes in Britain. Within a few square miles around the village of Kilmartin lie more than 800 ancient monuments dating back some 5,000 years — standing stones, stone circles, chambered cairns, rock carvings and later hillforts — including a famous "linear cemetery" of Bronze Age burial cairns running down the valley floor.

Monuments

The glen's central complex includes the stone circles of Temple Wood, the Neolithic chambered cairn of Nether Largie South, and the line of Bronze Age cairns — Nether Largie Mid and North, Glebe and Ri Cruin — stretching for over two kilometres and interpreted as a ritual linear cemetery. At Ballymeanoch stand stone rows, a henge and a kerbed cairn, while the valley's rocks bear hundreds of cup-and-ring carvings, including the elaborate panels at Achnabreck.

At the south end of the glen rises Dunadd, a rocky hillfort that was the capital of the early medieval Gaelic kingdom of Dál Riata, where kings were inaugurated at a footprint carved in the rock. Kilmartin Museum, in the village, displays finds from the glen and interprets the landscape.

Significance

Kilmartin is unusual for the density and continuity of its monuments: communities built, reused and reinterpreted the same ritual landscape from the first farmers around 3700 BC through the Bronze Age to the kings of Dál Riata, making it a key site for understanding prehistoric Scotland.
