Document

A document is a written, drawn, presented, or memorialised representation of thought, often the manifestation of non-fictional, as well as fictional, content. The word originates from the Latin documentum, which denotes a "teaching" or "lesson". In the past, the word was usually used to denote written proof useful as evidence of a truth or fact. In the Computer Age, "document" usually denotes a primarily textual computer file, including its structure and format.

Definitions and uses

Documents take many forms: letters, contracts, certificates, reports, essays, manuscripts, maps, official records and digital files. A document may be intended to record information, to communicate, to serve as legal evidence, or to instruct. In law, documents are a principal category of evidence, and rules of procedure govern their disclosure and admissibility. Library and information science treats the concept broadly: the French documentation pioneer Paul Otlet and later Suzanne Briet argued that a document is any physical or symbolic sign preserved to represent or prove a fact — Briet famously proposed that even an antelope in a zoo could function as a document.

History

Historically, documents were inscribed on clay tablets, papyrus, parchment and paper. Monastic and chancery traditions developed diplomatics, the critical study of the form and authenticity of official documents. The printing press standardised document production; the twentieth century added carbon copies, photocopies and typewritten forms, and the digital era introduced word-processed documents, PDFs and structured data files whose boundaries as "documents" are defined by file formats and software.

Digital documents

In computing, a document is typically a file created by an application — a text document, spreadsheet or slide deck — characterised by content, structure and metadata (author, dates, revision history). Document management systems organise, store and retrieve such files for organisations, and standards such as PDF aim to preserve a document's appearance independent of the platform used to view it.
