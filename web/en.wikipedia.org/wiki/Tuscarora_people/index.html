The Tuscarora people (Tuscarora: Skarū·ręʔ) are an Indigenous people of the Northeastern Woodlands, speaking a Northern Iroquoian language. Historically based in what is now eastern North Carolina, they migrated north after the Tuscarora War of 1711–1715 and became the Sixth Nation of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois Confederacy) in 1722. Today the federally recognised Tuscarora Nation is based near Lewiston, New York.

History

At European contact the Tuscarora lived in settled agricultural towns along the Roanoke, Tar, and Neuse rivers. Mounting settler encroachment and slave raiding led to the Tuscarora War (1711–1715), after which most surviving Tuscarora migrated north under Oneida sponsorship, settling among the Five Nations in New York. Admitted as the Sixth Nation in 1722, they took part in the confederacy's councils while maintaining their own chiefs and clans.

During the American Revolutionary War, most Tuscarora sided with the Americans. In the 19th century the New York Tuscarora received a reservation in Niagara County; land purchased with the help of the Holland Land Company forms the core of the present Tuscarora Indian Reservation.

Language and culture

The Tuscarora language is critically endangered, though revitalisation efforts include school programmes and documentation at Six Nations in Ontario, where many Tuscarora also live. Traditional government rests with clan mothers and chiefs of the nation's clans (including Turtle, Wolf, Bear, Beaver, and Snipe).
