Dental floss is a cord of thin filaments used in interdental cleaning to remove food and dental plaque from between teeth, or places a toothbrush has difficulty reaching or is unable to reach. Its regular use is recommended in developing and maintaining good oral hygiene, reducing the risk of gum disease and tooth decay in the interdental areas.

History

The New Orleans dentist Levi Spear Parmly is credited as the inventor of the first form of dental floss, recommending in 1819 that people run a waxed silk thread "through the interstices of the teeth ... to dislodge that irritating matter which no brush can remove". Floss was commercially available in 1882, when the Codman and Shurtleff company began producing unwaxed silk floss. Johnson & Johnson patented dental floss in 1898.

Use

Dental professionals recommend flossing once per day, ideally before brushing. A length of about 45 cm (18 in) is wound around the fingers, and a short segment is guided between the teeth with a gentle rocking motion, then curved around each tooth in a C-shape and moved up and down below the gumline.

Types

Floss is sold waxed or unwaxed, flavoured or unflavoured, as multifilament nylon or as single-filament PTFE tape. Alternatives include floss picks, interdental brushes and water flossers.
