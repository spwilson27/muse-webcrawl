Cleisthenes of Sicyon

Cleisthenes (Greek: Κλεισθένης) was the tyrant of Sicyon, a city in the northern Peloponnese, from about 600 to 570 BC, and a member of the Orthagorid dynasty. He was the grandfather of his more famous namesake, Cleisthenes of Athens, the founder of Athenian democracy, through his daughter Agariste.

Reign

Herodotus describes Cleisthenes as an enemy of Argos and of Dorian influence in Sicyon. He renamed the Dorian tribes of the city with insulting names — "Swine-men", "Ass-men", and "Pig-men" — while giving his own tribe the name "Archelaoi" ("Rulers of the People"); he transferred tragic choruses from the hero Adrastus to Dionysus and reassigned Adrastus's sacrifices to the hero Melanippus, whom he imported from Thebes. He also waged war against Cirrha in the First Sacred War (c. 595–585 BC) on the side of the Amphictyonic League.

The marriage of Agariste

Cleisthenes is best known for the elaborate contest he staged to marry off his daughter Agariste, one of the most celebrated stories in Herodotus. After winning a victory with the four-horse chariot at the Olympic Games, Cleisthenes proclaimed that any Greek who thought himself worthy should come to Sicyon to compete for her hand. Thirteen suitors stayed for a full year of trials in wrestling, racing, music, and conversation. The favourite, Hippocleides of Athens, lost the bride by drunkenly dancing on his head at the final banquet — giving rise to the proverb "Hippocleides doesn't care" — and Agariste was instead married to Megacles of the Alcmaeonid family. Their son was Cleisthenes of Athens.

Other achievements

Cleisthenes himself won the chariot race at the Pythian Games and at Olympia, and was credited with building works at Sicyon, including a stoa and the prytaneum. His reign represents the height of Orthagorid power; the dynasty ended shortly after his death.
