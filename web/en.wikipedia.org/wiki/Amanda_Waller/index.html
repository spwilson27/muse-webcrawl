Amanda Waller

Amanda Blake Waller is a fictional character in DC Comics publications. A ruthless, high-ranking United States government official, she is best known as the creator and director of the modern Suicide Squad (Task Force X), a black-ops team of imprisoned supervillains compelled to undertake covert missions in exchange for reduced sentences. Waller first appeared in Legends #1 (1986), created by John Ostrander, Len Wein and John Byrne, and has no superpowers — her influence derives from political authority, intelligence networks and an iron will that has made even superheroes wary of crossing her.

Nicknamed "the Wall", Waller is one of DC's most prominent morally ambiguous authority figures. In various continuities she has headed the Agency, served as Secretary of Metahuman Affairs, run the covert organization Checkmate as its White Queen, and maintained files and countermeasures against the Justice League itself.

Fictional character biography

Waller's backstory, developed in the Suicide Squad series and Secret Origins, portrays her as a widow from Chicago's Cabrini-Green housing projects who, after the murders of one of her sons and one of her daughters and the death of her husband, put her surviving children through college and earned a doctorate in political science. Working as a congressional aide, she uncovered the defunct Task Force X files and persuaded Congress to revive the program under her command, staffing it with inmates from Belle Reve Penitentiary controlled by explosive implants.

Characterization

Writers consistently depict Waller as pragmatic to the point of brutality: willing to sacrifice her own agents, threaten superheroes, and commit or sanction crimes in the name of national security, yet motivated by a genuine belief that governments must be able to answer metahuman power. This tension has made her an antagonist to heroes such as Batman while remaining, formally, on the side of the state.

In other media

Waller has been portrayed in live action by Pam Grier in Smallville, Cynthia Addai-Robinson in Arrow, and Viola Davis in the films Suicide Squad (2016) and The Suicide Squad (2021) and related DC productions. Voiced versions include CCH Pounder's acclaimed portrayal in the DC animated universe beginning with Justice League Unlimited.
