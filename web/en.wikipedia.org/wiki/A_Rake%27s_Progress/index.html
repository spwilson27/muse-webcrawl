A Rake's Progress

ArtistWilliam Hogarth
Year1732–1734 (paintings); engravings published 1735
MediumOil on canvas; engravings
LocationSir John Soane's Museum, London

A Rake's Progress is a series of eight paintings and engravings by the English artist William Hogarth, produced in the 1730s. The series depicts the decline and fall of Tom Rakewell, the spendthrift son and heir of a rich miser, who comes to London, squanders his fortune on luxurious living, prostitution and gambling, and ends his days in Bethlem Royal Hospital, the notorious madhouse known as Bedlam.

The scenes

  1. The Heir
  2. The Levée
  3. The Orgy
  4. The Arrest
  5. The Marriage
  6. The Gaming House
  7. The Prison
  8. The Madhouse

Legacy

The series is one of Hogarth's celebrated "modern moral subjects". It inspired Igor Stravinsky's 1951 opera The Rake's Progress, with a libretto by W. H. Auden and Chester Kallman, as well as later adaptations in ballet, film and popular art.
