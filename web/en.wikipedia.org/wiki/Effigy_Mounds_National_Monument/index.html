Effigy Mounds National Monument

LocationAllamakee and Clayton counties, Iowa, U.S.
Nearest cityMarquette, Iowa
Area2,526 acres (1,022 ha)
EstablishedOctober 25, 1949
Governing bodyNational Park Service
FeaturesMore than 200 Native American burial and ceremonial mounds

Effigy Mounds National Monument preserves more than 200 Native American earthworks along the bluffs of the Upper Mississippi River in northeastern Iowa, including 31 mounds shaped as animals — bears and birds — built by peoples of the Woodland culture between about 450 BCE and 1300 CE. The rest are conical burial mounds, linear mounds, and compound forms. President Harry S. Truman established the monument on October 25, 1949.

The mounds

The monument lies in two main units along the bluffs overlooking the Mississippi River, plus the remote Sny Magill unit. Its most celebrated group, the Marching Bear Group, comprises ten bear effigies and three bird effigies following a bluff edge. The Great Bear Mound, the largest bear effigy in the park, measures 137 feet long and 70 feet wide. The effigy tradition — earthen animal shapes raised between roughly 700 and 1300 CE — is unique to the Upper Midwest, centred on southern Wisconsin, and is generally interpreted as expressing clan identities and cosmological relationships between people, animals, and the landscape.

Preservation

Of the thousands of mounds once present along the Upper Mississippi, most were destroyed by farming, development, and looting before protected status came in 1949. The National Park Service consults with 12 federally recognised tribes affiliated with the site, and the mounds are treated as sacred burial places; trails allow visitors to view them from the ground while protecting the fragile earthworks. The monument also protects oak savanna, tallgrass prairie remnants, and dramatic river bluffs in the Driftless Area.
