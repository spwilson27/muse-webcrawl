Predatory lending

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Predatory lending refers to unethical or abusive practices by lenders during the loan origination process that impose unfair, deceptive, or high-cost terms on borrowers, often those with low income, poor credit, or limited financial sophistication. Such practices can lead to unaffordable debt, stripping of home equity, and foreclosure, and they played a widely discussed role in the subprime mortgage crisis of 2007–2008.

Practices

  • Equity stripping — making loans based on the borrower's home equity rather than ability to repay, with the expectation of seizing the property.
  • Loan flipping — repeatedly refinancing a borrower's loan, charging fees each time.
  • Packing — adding unwanted or undisclosed products, such as credit insurance, into the loan.
  • Balloon payments and teaser rates — structures that hide the true cost of credit until payments sharply rise.
  • Steering — directing borrowers who qualify for mainstream loans into more expensive subprime products.

Regulation

In the United States, the Home Ownership and Equity Protection Act (HOEPA, 1994) imposed special rules on high-cost mortgages; many states enacted anti-predatory-lending statutes in the 2000s. The Dodd–Frank Act of 2010 created the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau and ability-to-repay rules for mortgages. Related high-cost products, such as some payday loans and rent-to-own contracts, are regulated separately and remain debated.
