The Battle of Monterrey (20–24 September 1846) was a battle of the Mexican–American War in which United States forces under General Zachary Taylor fought their way into the fortified northern Mexican city of Monterrey, defended by General Pedro de Ampudia's Army of the North. After several days of hard street fighting, Ampudia negotiated a withdrawal, surrendering the city in exchange for an eight-week armistice — terms that drew criticism in Washington.

Background

After victories at Palo Alto and Resaca de la Palma in May 1846, Taylor advanced into northern Mexico. Monterrey, the capital of Nuevo León, was ringed by strongpoints including the Citadel (the "Black Fort"), Independence Hill with the Bishop's Palace, and Federation Hill.

The battle

Taylor attacked from two directions. William J. Worth's division swung around the city to cut the road to Saltillo and stormed Federation Hill and then Independence Hill, seizing the Bishop's Palace. On the eastern side, Taylor's troops fought costly house-to-house actions, using picks to break through adobe walls — a technique later called "mouse-holing" — while Mexican defenders fired from rooftops and forts. By 24 September US troops had fought into the central plaza's edge.

Terms and aftermath

Ampudia proposed terms, and Taylor allowed the Mexican army to evacuate with its arms and colours under an armistice. The Polk administration repudiated the armistice, but Monterrey gave the Americans a firm base in northern Mexico and made "Old Rough and Ready" Taylor a national hero.
