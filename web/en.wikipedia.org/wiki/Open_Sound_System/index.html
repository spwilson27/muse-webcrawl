Open Sound System

The Open Sound System (OSS) is an interface for making and capturing sound in Unix and Unix-like operating systems. It was the standard sound architecture for Linux and several other systems through the 1990s and remains available on a number of platforms.

History

OSS was created by Hannu Savolainen and was distributed by 4Front Technologies. It originated as the sound driver for Linux in the early 1990s and was included in the Linux kernel for many years. The system presented audio through device files such as /dev/dsp and a mixer device, with a programming API based on ioctl calls.

Decline on Linux

Although parts of OSS were free software, later versions included proprietary components, which hampered its adoption in the Linux community. The Advanced Linux Sound Architecture (ALSA) was developed as a fully free replacement and became the default in the Linux kernel from the 2.6 series onward; ALSA included an OSS emulation layer. In 2007, 4Front released most of OSS version 4 under free licenses (GPL and BSD/CDDL variants). OSS remains the native sound system of FreeBSD and is available for other Unix systems, where its simpler API is still appreciated by some developers and users.

Characteristics

OSS provides a portable, operating-system-independent API across supported platforms, per-application volume controls and software mixing in its later versions, and support for both playback and recording from a wide range of audio hardware.
