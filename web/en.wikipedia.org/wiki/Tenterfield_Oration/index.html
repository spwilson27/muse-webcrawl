Tenterfield Oration

The Tenterfield Oration was a speech delivered by Sir Henry Parkes, Premier of New South Wales, on 24 October 1889 at the Tenterfield School of Arts in the town of Tenterfield, New South Wales. In it, Parkes called on the Australian colonies to unite into a federal union with a national government and army. The speech is widely regarded as the starting point of the formal federation process that culminated in the creation of the Commonwealth of Australia on 1 January 1901.

Background

By 1889 the six Australian colonies governed themselves separately, with their own tariffs, railway gauges, and defence arrangements. Parkes had long supported federation, and following correspondence with the Governor of New South Wales, Lord Carrington, and a report by Major-General James Bevan Edwards criticising the colonies' fragmented defences, he travelled to Tenterfield — a town near the Queensland border plagued by cross-border trade friction — to make his appeal.

Content

Parkes declared that "the crimson thread of kinship runs through us all" and argued that Australia's population of some three million was sufficient to form a nation. He proposed a convention of representatives from each colonial parliament to draft a federal constitution. He also cited defence, noting that Australia needed a united military commanded by a single authority.

Legacy

The oration prompted Parkes to convene the Federation Conference in Melbourne in February 1890, followed by the National Australasian Convention in Sydney in 1891, which drafted a federal constitution. Parkes died in 1896 and did not live to see federation achieved. The Tenterfield Oration remains one of the most celebrated speeches in Australian political history, and the town of Tenterfield is commemorated as the "birthplace of federation".
