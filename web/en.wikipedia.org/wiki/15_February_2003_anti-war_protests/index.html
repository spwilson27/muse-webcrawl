15 February 2003 anti-war protests

LocationWorldwide, ~600+ cities

CauseOpposition to the impending Iraq War

On 15 February 2003, a coordinated day of protests was held across the world against the imminent invasion of Iraq by the United States and its allies. It is widely considered the largest protest event in human history, with millions marching in more than 600 cities.

Scale

Rome saw the largest single demonstration, with around three million people — recognised by Guinness World Records as the largest anti-war rally ever. London's march drew an estimated one to two million, the largest political demonstration in British history, while New York, Madrid, Berlin, Paris and Sydney also saw crowds in the hundreds of thousands.

Organisation and impact

The protests were coordinated by an international network of peace groups following the European Social Forum, amplified by early internet organising. Although they failed to stop the invasion, which began on 20 March 2003, the day was described by the New York Times as evidence of a second global superpower: public opinion.
