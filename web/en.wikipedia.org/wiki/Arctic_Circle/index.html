Overview

The Arctic Circle is the southernmost latitude in the Northern Hemisphere at which the Sun can remain continuously above or below the horizon for a full 24 hours. It lies at about 66.56° north of the Equator — exactly 90° minus Earth's axial tilt.

At the June solstice, places on the circle experience one day when the Sun never fully sets (the midnight sun); at the December solstice, one day when it never rises (polar night). Closer to the pole, these seasons of continuous day and night grow longer, reaching six months at the pole itself.

Geography

The circle crosses Norway, Sweden, Finland, Russia, the United States (Alaska), Canada and Greenland, and clips the northern tip of Iceland at the island of Grímsey. A large majority of the land it crosses is Russian. Well-known towns on or near the line include Rovaniemi in Finland — marketed as the home of Santa Claus — and Murmansk, the largest city within the Arctic, in Russia.

Because Earth's axial tilt is slowly decreasing, the Arctic Circle is currently moving northward by roughly 15 metres per year, so monuments marking it periodically have to be moved.
