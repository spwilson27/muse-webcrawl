The Betelgeuse incident (also called the Whiddy Island disaster) was an explosion and fire aboard the French oil tanker Betelgeuse at the Gulf Oil terminal on Whiddy Island in Bantry Bay, County Cork, Ireland, in the early hours of 8 January 1979. Fifty-one people were killed, making it one of the worst disasters in Irish history.

The disaster

The Betelgeuse, a 121,000-tonne tanker built in 1968, was discharging a cargo of Saudi Arabian crude oil at the Whiddy Island terminal when, during ballasting operations after unloading, a series of explosions tore through the vessel. The ship broke in two and burned fiercely; the jetty also caught fire. All 42 crew members aboard the tanker and seven terminal employees on the jetty were killed, along with one other person; the intense heat prevented any rescue. The wreck's stern section sank at the berth while the bow section remained afloat.

Inquiry and aftermath

A tribunal of inquiry under Mr Justice Declan Costello found the disaster resulted from a chain of failures: the vessel was poorly maintained, with corroded and weakened ballast tanks, and improper ballasting procedures allowed explosive gases to accumulate; the terminal operator was also criticised for inadequate safety systems and an emergency response hampered by inoperative fire-fighting equipment. Unusually, the tribunal could not establish the precise ignition source. The disaster devastated the Bantry Bay oil terminal, then one of the largest in Europe, which never fully recovered its former role. A memorial to the victims stands on Whiddy Island, and commemorations are held on each anniversary.
