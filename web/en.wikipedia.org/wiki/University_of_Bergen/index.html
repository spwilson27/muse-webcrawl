University of Bergen

University
Founded: 1946
Location: Bergen, Norway
Students: about 19,000
Type: Public research university

The University of Bergen (Norwegian: Universitetet i Bergen, UiB) is a public research university in Bergen, Norway. Founded in 1946, it is the country's second-oldest university after the University of Oslo in terms of formal establishment, though academic activity in Bergen dates to the Bergen Museum of 1825.

History

Scientific life in Bergen grew around the Bergen Museum, whose natural history and later marine research became internationally known — Fridtjof Nansen conducted early neuroscience research there. The university was formally created by an act of the Storting in 1946 and opened in 1948. Bergen's geophysical institute pioneered modern meteorology through the "Bergen School" of Vilhelm Bjerknes.

Faculties

UiB has seven faculties: Humanities, Law, Mathematics and Natural Sciences, Medicine, Psychology, Social Sciences, and Fine Art, Music and Design. The university is Norway's leading institution for marine research and has strong programs in climate research (including the Bjerknes Centre), petroleum geology, and development studies.

Campus

The main campus occupies the Nygårdshøyden hill in central Bergen, close to the University Museum and the Geophysical Institute, with medical faculties centered at Haukeland University Hospital.
