Jiahu

Jiahu (賈湖, Jiǎhú) is a Neolithic site in the Huai River plain of Wuyang County, Henan province, China, occupied between about 7000 and 5800 BC. It is one of the earliest and richest known settlements of the Peiligang culture, associated with some of the earliest domesticated rice farming in the Yellow River region. The site was excavated in several campaigns between 1983 and 2013 by the Henan Provincial Institute of Cultural Relics and Archaeology.

Discoveries

Excavations uncovered houses, pits, kilns, burials and large quantities of pottery, polished stone tools and animal remains. Jiahu's inhabitants cultivated rice, raised pigs and dogs, and hunted and fished extensively. Remarkable finds include:

Bone flutes — more than thirty flutes made from the ulna bones of the red-crowned crane, some still playable and tuned to pentatonic and even heptatonic scales, among the oldest playable musical instruments in the world (c. 7000–5700 BC).

Early signs — tortoise shells and bones bearing incised marks, sometimes called Jiahu symbols; some scholars have proposed them as a very early form of proto-writing, though a direct link to later Chinese characters is unproven.

Fermented drink — chemical analysis of pottery residues revealed a fermented beverage of rice, honey and fruit (hawthorn and/or grape), among the earliest known alcoholic drinks, dating to about 7000–6600 BC.

Significance

Jiahu demonstrates that complex culture — music, ritual use of tortoise shells in divination, brewing and possibly symbolic notation — accompanied the very beginnings of sedentary farming in China.
