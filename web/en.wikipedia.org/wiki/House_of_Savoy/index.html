The House of Savoy (Italian: Casa Savoia) is a royal dynasty that was established in 1003 in the historical region of Savoy. Through gradual expansion, the family grew in power from ruling a small Alpine county to, by 1861, ruling the Kingdom of Italy. At the height of its power the family is considered one of the oldest royal houses in Europe, having produced kings of Italy, Sardinia, Sicily, and—briefly, in the person of Amadeo I—Spain.

History

Origins

The dynasty was founded by Humbert I, known as Humbert the White-Handed (Umberto Biancamano), who became Count of Savoy in 1003. The counts consolidated control over the Alpine passes between France and Italy, including the Mont Cenis and Great St Bernard routes, which gave the family strategic and commercial importance far beyond the size of its territory.

Rise to kingship

Amadeus VIII was raised to Duke of Savoy in 1416. Through the Treaty of Utrecht (1713), Victor Amadeus II acquired Sicily and the royal title; in 1720 Sicily was exchanged for Sardinia, and the dukes became Kings of Sardinia. The capital was Turin in Piedmont.

Italian unification

Under King Charles Albert and his son Victor Emmanuel II, Piedmont-Sardinia became the engine of the Risorgimento. With the help of Prime Minister Camillo Benso, Count of Cavour, and the campaigns of Giuseppe Garibaldi, the peninsula was unified, and Victor Emmanuel II was proclaimed King of Italy on 17 March 1861. Rome became the capital in 1870.

Twentieth century and fall

Victor Emmanuel III reigned through both world wars. The monarchy's association with Mussolini's Fascist regime fatally damaged its standing, and in the institutional referendum of 2 June 1946 Italians voted for a republic. King Umberto II, who had reigned for only about a month, went into exile in Portugal. Male members of the house were barred from Italian soil until the ban was lifted in 2002.

Branches

The senior line (Savoy) ruled Italy until 1946. Cadet branches include Savoy-Aosta, Savoy-Genoa (extinct 1996), Savoy-Carignano (from which the Italian kings descended after 1831), Savoy-Soissons, Savoy-Nemours, Savoy-Achaia, and Savoy-Villafranca. Since 2019–2021 the headship of the house has been disputed between the Aosta line and the line of Vittorio Emanuele, Prince of Naples (died 2024) and his son Emanuele Filiberto.
