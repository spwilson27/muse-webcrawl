State of the Presidi

The Presidi comprised Orbetello, Talamone, Porto Ercole, Porto Santo Stefano and Porto Longone (Porto Azzurro).

The State of the Presidi (Italian: Stato dei Presidi; Spanish: Estado de los Presidios) was a small state — a collection of Spanish garrison territories (presidios) — on the coast of Tuscany in Italy. It existed from 1557 to 1801 and was governed from Naples, serving Spain as a strategic link between its Italian possessions and as a means of controlling the Tyrrhenian Sea and watching the Papal States and Tuscany.

History

The state was created by the Treaty of London (1557) between Philip II of Spain and the Republic of Siena's conquerors: while the bulk of Sienese territory passed to Cosimo I de' Medici and became part of the Grand Duchy of Tuscany, Spain retained the coastal strongpoints of Orbetello, Talamone, Porto Ercole, Porto Santo Stefano, and Porto Longone (modern Porto Azzurro) on the island of Elba.

Administratively dependent on the Spanish (later Bourbon) viceroys and kings of Naples, the Presidi changed hands several times during the European wars of succession: Austrian forces occupied them in the early eighteenth century, and they were confirmed to Naples by the Treaty of Vienna (1738). Napoleon occupied the territory in 1801 and it was annexed to the Kingdom of Etruria; in 1815 the Congress of Vienna assigned the former Presidi to the Grand Duchy of Tuscany, ending their separate existence.
