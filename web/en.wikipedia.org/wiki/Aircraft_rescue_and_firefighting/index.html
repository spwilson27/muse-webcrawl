Aircraft rescue and firefighting

Aircraft rescue and firefighting (ARFF) is a specialised category of firefighting involving the emergency response, mitigation, evacuation and rescue of passengers and crew of aircraft involved in ground emergencies. Due to the mass-casualty potential of an aircraft emergency, the speed with which emergency response equipment and personnel arrive at the scene is of paramount importance. Airport fire services are usually required to reach any part of the operational area within two to three minutes of an alarm.

An ARFF crash tender applying foam in a live-fire training exercise.

Equipment

ARFF vehicles, known as crash tenders, are large, powerful foam tenders such as the Rosenbauer Panther and Oshkosh Striker. They carry large quantities of water and aqueous film-forming foam (AFFF), are fitted with roof and bumper turrets that can discharge while moving, and often have snozzle-type piercing nozzles able to penetrate an aircraft fuselage to apply agent inside the cabin. Crews wear proximity suits of aluminised fabric to withstand radiant heat from fuel fires.

Standards and training

International standards are set by ICAO Annex 14, which classifies aerodromes into ten categories by aircraft size and prescribes minimum quantities of extinguishing agent and numbers of vehicles; in the United States the FAA applies Part 139 index requirements and the NFPA publishes ARFF standards. Firefighters train on mock aircraft fuselages with controlled fuel fires, and in aircraft familiarisation, evacuation assistance and triage of survivors. See also aerial firefighting and firefighting.
