Gendai budō

[Image placeholder: Modern budō training]

Gendai budō (現代武道), meaning "modern martial way", is a term for Japanese martial arts founded after the Meiji Restoration (1868), in contrast to koryū, the classical schools founded before that era. Gendai budō arts emphasise personal development, sport, or practical self-defence rather than battlefield application, and they typically employ the dan/kyū ranking system and standardised forms suited to mass instruction.

Examples

Well-known gendai budō include judo (founded 1882 by Kanō Jigorō), kendo, iaido, jōdō, karate-dō (as systematised in mainland Japan), aikido (developed by Morihei Ueshiba in the early 20th century), kyūdō, naginata, and shorinji kempo (founded 1947). Several of these are governed by federations connected with the Japanese Budō Association and are demonstrated annually at events such as the Nippon Budōkan demonstrations.

Philosophy

The shift from bujutsu ("martial technique") to budō ("martial way") reflects a reframing of training as a path of character cultivation — the suffix -dō parallels the use in sadō (tea ceremony) and shodō (calligraphy). Kano's principles of maximum efficiency (seiryoku zen'yō) and mutual welfare and benefit (jita kyōei) exemplify the educational philosophy common to many modern arts, and helped martial arts enter school curricula and international sport, culminating in judo's inclusion in the 1964 Tokyo Olympics.
