Sprouting

Sprouting is the natural process by which seeds or spores germinate and put out shoots, and already established plants produce new leaves or buds, or other structures experience further growth. In the field of nutrition, the term signifies the practice of germinating seeds (for example, mung beans or sunflower seeds) to be eaten raw or cooked, which is considered more nutritious.

Process

Sprouting is a process where seeds are soaked in water for a period of time, then drained and rinsed at regular intervals until they begin to form roots and shoots. The process can take anywhere from a few days to a week depending on the seed variety and conditions. Commercial sprouting operations must carefully control temperature, humidity, and sanitation to produce safe, high-quality sprouts.

Nutritional aspects

The germination process increases the bioavailability of many nutrients. Sprouted grains and legumes often show increased levels of certain vitamins, including B vitamins and vitamin C, and enzymes activated during germination partially break down starches and proteins, which can make the foods easier to digest. Sprouting also reduces levels of antinutrients such as phytic acid.

Food safety

Because sprouts are grown in warm, moist conditions that are also ideal for bacterial growth, raw sprouts have been associated with outbreaks of foodborne illness caused by Salmonella and E. coli. Health authorities often advise that young children, the elderly, pregnant women, and people with weakened immune systems avoid eating raw sprouts, and that sprouts be cooked thoroughly.

Common sprouted foods

Popular sprouts include mung bean sprouts, soybean sprouts, alfalfa sprouts, radish sprouts, broccoli sprouts, and sprouted lentils and chickpeas. Sprouted grains are also used in breads and in the malting process for brewing beer and distilling spirits.
