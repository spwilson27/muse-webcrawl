A samba school (Portuguese: escola de samba) is a Brazilian organisation devoted to practising and competing in the performance of samba, above all in the spectacular parades of the Rio Carnival. Despite the name, a samba school is not a teaching institution but a large community association — often rooted in a particular neighbourhood or favela — that may field three to five thousand performers, dancers, drummers, and elaborate floats in a single parade.

History

The first samba school, Deixa Falar, was founded in 1928 in the Estácio district of Rio de Janeiro by musicians including Ismael Silva, who coined the term "escola" because the group rehearsed near a teacher-training school. In 1929 Deixa Falar's members founded other pioneering schools, among them Mangueira and Portela, which remain giants of the carnival. Competitive parades were formalised in the 1930s under mayor Pedro Ernesto and later absorbed into the official carnival programme. Since 1984 the Rio Special Group parades have been held in the Sambadrome designed by Oscar Niemeyer.

The parade

Each school presents an enredo (theme) developed by its carnival designer (carnavalesco) through a newly composed samba-enredo song, floats (alegorias), and thousands of costumes organised in wings (alas). Required elements include the flag-bearing couple (mestre-sala e porta-bandeira), the front commission (comissão de frente), the baianas wing of spinning women in traditional dress, and the drum corps (bateria) of several hundred percussionists, directed by the mestre de bateria and fronted by a drum queen. Judges score categories such as harmony, evolution, drums, costumes, and floats; the lowest-ranked Special Group school is relegated each year and the leading school is crowned champion on Ash Wednesday.

Beyond Rio

Samba schools flourish in São Paulo, whose carnival at the Anhembi Sambadrome has grown to rival Rio's, and in cities across Brazil; exile and diaspora communities have founded schools as far afield as London, Helsinki, and Tokyo.
