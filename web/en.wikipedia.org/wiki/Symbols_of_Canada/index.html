Symbols of Canada

[Image placeholder: symbols of Canada]

The symbols of Canada are the visual and emblematic representations of the country and its identity. Chief among them is the maple leaf, used as a Canadian symbol since the 18th century and featured on the national flag adopted in 1965, in which a single red maple leaf stands between two red bars.

Principal symbols

The coat of arms of Canada, proclaimed in 1921 and revised since, combines the arms of England, Scotland, Ireland, and France with maple leaves. The national anthem is "O Canada," officially adopted in 1980. The beaver — long associated with the fur trade — was granted official status as an emblem in 1975, and the Canadian horse and the maple tree are also recognised national symbols. The national colours are red and white, proclaimed in 1921.

Other symbols

The monarch of Canada is represented by the Royal Union Flag and royal standards. Sporting symbols include lacrosse (the official summer sport) and ice hockey (the official winter sport), recognised under the National Sports of Canada Act of 1994. The tartan known as the Maple Leaf Tartan was adopted as an official symbol in 2011.

Provincial and territorial symbols

Each province and territory maintains its own flag, coat of arms, flower, bird, tree, and other emblems, administered through the Canadian Heraldic Authority, established in 1988.
