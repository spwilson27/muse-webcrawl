John Frederick Kensett

John Frederick Kensett (March 22, 1816 – December 14, 1872) was an American landscape painter and engraver, a leading figure of the second generation of the Hudson River School and a central exponent of Luminism.

Career

Born in Cheshire, Connecticut, Kensett trained as an engraver before travelling to Europe in 1840 with Asher Durand and others, studying in England, France, and Italy. Returning in 1847, he established himself in New York, painting the scenery of the Catskills, the Adirondacks, New England's coast, and especially Lake George, whose calm, luminous waters became his signature subject.

Style

Kensett's mature work is marked by spare composition, smooth surfaces, still water, and subtle atmospheric light — paintings such as Eaton's Neck, Long Island (1872) rank among the finest Luminist canvases. He was a founder of the Metropolitan Museum of Art and a generous supporter of fellow artists; his sudden death from pneumonia in 1872, after rescuing a drowning woman, was widely mourned.
