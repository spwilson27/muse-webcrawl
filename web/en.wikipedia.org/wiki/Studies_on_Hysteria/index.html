Studies on Hysteria

Studies on Hysteria (German: Studien über Hysterie) is an 1895 book by Sigmund Freud and Josef Breuer, considered the founding text of psychoanalysis. It presents case histories of five hysterical patients and introduces the "talking cure": the idea that symptoms arise from repressed traumatic memories and can be relieved when the patient recalls and verbalizes them, discharging the associated affect (catharsis).

Contents

The book opens with a jointly written preliminary communication on the psychical mechanism of hysterical phenomena, followed by case histories including Breuer's famous case of "Anna O." (Bertha Pappenheim) — who herself coined the terms "talking cure" and "chimney sweeping" for the treatment — and Freud's cases of Emmy von N., Lucy R., Katharina, and Elisabeth von R. It closes with Freud's chapter on the psychotherapy of hysteria, describing the pressure technique and the emerging method of free association.

Significance

Studies on Hysteria marks the transition from purely physical treatments of nervous illness to psychological ones and the starting point of Freud's subsequent development of psychoanalytic theory.
