Superior highly composite number

Superior highly composite number
Introduced bySrinivasa Ramanujan (1915)
First terms2, 6, 12, 60, 120, 360, 2520, 5040, 55440, 720720, …
Notationd(n) = number of divisors of n

In number theory, a superior highly composite number is a positive integer that has a sharper maximum ratio between its number of divisors and powers of the number itself than any smaller number. Formally, n is superior highly composite if there exists ε > 0 such that for all k > 1, d(n) / nε ≥ d(k) / kε, where d denotes the divisor function. The concept was defined by Srinivasa Ramanujan in his 1915 paper on highly composite numbers.

Properties

Every superior highly composite number is also a highly composite number and a colossally abundant number, but not vice versa. They are all products of primorials raised to non-increasing powers: the primes appear in order with descending exponents. The first few are 2, 6, 12, 60, 120, 360, 2520, 5040, 55440, and 720720. Because 60 and 360 are in the sequence, superior highly composite numbers relate to the traditional sexagesimal units of time and angle.
