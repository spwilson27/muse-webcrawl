John Shaw Billings

John Shaw Billings (April 12, 1838 – March 11, 1913) was an American librarian, physician and surgeon. He is best known for developing the Library of the Surgeon General's Office — which became the National Library of Medicine — and for his role in designing the Johns Hopkins Hospital. He was also the first director of the New York Public Library.

Medical library

After service as a Union Army surgeon in the Civil War, Billings took charge of the Surgeon General's library and built it into the world's foremost medical collection. He launched the Index Medicus (1879) and the monumental Index-Catalogue, transforming access to medical literature.

Hospitals and public health

Billings advised on the construction of Johns Hopkins Hospital, pioneering modern ideas of hospital design, ventilation and medical education. He also worked on vital statistics, helping to organise census data collection and collaborating with Herman Hollerith on the punched-card tabulating system used in the 1890 census.

New York Public Library

In 1895 Billings was chosen to consolidate the Astor, Lenox and Tilden foundations into the New York Public Library, whose landmark main building he helped plan. He directed the library until his death in 1913.
