Harry Houdini

Harry Houdini (born Erik Weisz; March 24, 1874 – October 31, 1926) was a Hungarian-American escape artist, illusionist, and stunt performer, noted for his escape acts. His pseudonym is a reference to his spiritual master, French magician Robert-Houdin (1805–1871).

Born: Erik Weisz, March 24, 1874, Budapest, Austria-Hungary

Died: October 31, 1926 (aged 52), Detroit, Michigan, U.S.

Occupation: Magician, escape artist, stunt performer

Spouse: Bess Houdini (Wilhelmina Beatrice Rahner)

Early life

Weisz was born in Budapest to a Jewish family and emigrated to the United States with his family in 1878, settling first in Appleton, Wisconsin, where his father was a rabbi, and later in New York City. He took numerous jobs and began performing magic as a teenager, adopting "Houdini" in homage to Jean-Eugène Robert-Houdin. He met his wife Bess, who became his stage assistant, in 1893; they remained partners for the rest of his life.

Escape career

Houdini struggled in vaudeville until 1899, when manager Martin Beck advised him to concentrate on escapes. His handcuff challenge act — inviting police and audiences to secure him with any restraints — made him a star in America and then Europe, where he toured triumphantly from 1900. His signature escapes included the Milk Can Escape (1908), the suspended straitjacket escape performed hanging from cranes before huge crowds, the Chinese Water Torture Cell, in which he was lowered upside-down into a locked glass tank of water, and escaping from a sealed crate submerged in New York's East River in 1912.

Houdini also made several films, flew as an aviation pioneer (making the first controlled powered flight in Australia in 1910), and campaigned against fraudulent spirit mediums, demonstrating their methods and testifying before Congress. He served as president of the Society of American Magicians and greatly expanded it.

Death

In October 1926, after a performance tour in Canada during which a student famously punched him in the abdomen (whether this contributed to his illness remains debated), Houdini collapsed with appendicitis and peritonitis. He died in Detroit on October 31, 1926 — Halloween. Before his death, he and Bess agreed on a code by which he would attempt to contact her from beyond; she held annual séances for a decade without success. The "Official Houdini Séance" is still held each Halloween.
