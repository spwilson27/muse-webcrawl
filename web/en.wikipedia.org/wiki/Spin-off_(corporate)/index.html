Spin-off (corporate)

A corporate spin-off, also known as a spin-out or starburst, is a type of corporate action where a company "spins off" a section as a separate business, or creates a second, distinct company. In a classic spin-off, the parent company distributes shares of the new entity to its existing shareholders on a pro-rata basis, so the same investors own both companies, but the businesses are legally and operationally independent and separately traded.

Reasons for spin-offs

Companies spin off divisions to unlock value when the market values a conglomerate at less than the sum of its parts, to give a fast-growing unit its own management and stock currency, to separate businesses with different regulatory or capital needs, or to remove an underperforming or unwanted unit. Notable examples include eBay's separation of PayPal (2015) and General Electric's break-up into GE HealthCare, GE Vernova and GE Aerospace (2023–2024).

Distinguishing features

A spin-off differs from a split-off (in which shareholders choose between parent and subsidiary shares) and from an equity carve-out, in which the parent sells a minority stake in a subsidiary through an IPO while retaining control. In the United States, qualifying spin-offs under Section 355 of the Internal Revenue Code can be completed tax-free to both the parent and its shareholders.
