Gazprom (Russian: Газпром) is a Russian majority state-owned multinational energy corporation headquartered in the Lakhta Center in Saint Petersburg. The name is a portmanteau of the Russian words gazovaya promyshlennost ("gas industry"). Gazprom is the largest company in Russia by revenue and one of the world's largest producers of natural gas, holding the world's largest natural gas reserves.

History

Gazprom was created in 1989 when the Soviet Ministry of Gas Industry was converted into a state corporation, retaining control over exploration, production, transmission, and export of Soviet gas assets in Russia. Following the dissolution of the Soviet Union it was partly privatized in the 1990s, though the state retained a large stake; under President Vladimir Putin the government restored majority control in 2005. Former chancellor of Germany Gerhard Schröder served as chairman of the shareholders' committee of the Nord Stream pipeline consortium.

Operations

Gazprom's core business is the extraction of natural gas from giant fields in western Siberia, such as Urengoy, Yamburg, and Bovanenkovo on the Yamal Peninsula, and its delivery through one of the world's largest pipeline networks, largely controlled through its export arm. Through subsidiaries such as Gazprom Neft it is also a major oil producer, and it owns power generation assets and the Gazprombank financial group. Its notable pipeline projects include Nord Stream and Nord Stream 2 across the Baltic Sea, TurkStream across the Black Sea, and Power of Siberia to China.

Role in Europe and controversies

Before 2022, Gazprom supplied roughly 40 percent of the European Union's imported natural gas, and pricing and supply disputes with Ukraine in 2006 and 2009 led to interruptions affecting European customers. Following Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Gazprom sharply reduced pipeline deliveries to Europe, most European markets eliminated or drastically cut Russian gas imports, and the Nord Stream pipelines were damaged by explosions in September 2022. These events marked the collapse of the decades-old energy relationship between Gazprom and its largest export market.
