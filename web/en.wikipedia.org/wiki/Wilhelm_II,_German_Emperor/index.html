Wilhelm II, the last German Emperor and King of Prussia.

Wilhelm II (27 January 1859 – 4 June 1941) was the last German Emperor (Kaiser) and King of Prussia, reigning from 1888 until his abdication in 1918, shortly before Germany's defeat in the First World War.

Reign

The eldest grandchild of Queen Victoria, Wilhelm came to the throne in the "Year of the Three Emperors" after the deaths of his grandfather Wilhelm I and his father Frederick III. He dismissed Chancellor Otto von Bismarck in 1890 and pursued an assertive foreign policy (Weltpolitik), expanding the German colonial empire and building a High Seas Fleet that provoked an arms race with Britain. His erratic diplomacy, including the dismissal of Bismarck's alliance system, contributed to Germany's isolation before 1914.

First World War and abdication

During the July Crisis of 1914 Wilhelm supported Austria-Hungary against Serbia, helping to precipitate the First World War. As the war turned against Germany, real power passed to the High Command of Hindenburg and Ludendorff. In November 1918, amid revolution, his abdication was announced and he fled into exile in the Netherlands.

Exile and death

Wilhelm lived at Huis Doorn in the Netherlands for the rest of his life, writing memoirs and hoping in vain for a restoration that never came. He died in 1941 during the German occupation of the Netherlands.
