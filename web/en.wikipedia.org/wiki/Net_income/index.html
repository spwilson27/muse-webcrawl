Net income

In business and accounting, net income (also total comprehensive income, net earnings, net profit, or informally the "bottom line") is an entity's income minus the cost of goods sold, expenses, depreciation and amortization, interest, and taxes for an accounting period. It is computed as the residual of all revenues and gains less all expenses and losses for the period, and appears as the final line of the income statement.

Calculation

A multi-step income statement derives net income in stages: revenue minus cost of goods sold gives gross profit; subtracting operating expenses, depreciation, and amortization gives operating income; adjusting for interest and other non-operating items gives income before tax; subtracting income tax expense yields net income. For a corporation, net income is then divided between dividends to shareholders and retained earnings added to equity on the balance sheet.

Use

Net income is a key measure of profitability and the basis for earnings per share (EPS), the most widely quoted figure in financial reporting. Analysts compare it across periods and between companies, but net income is an accrual-accounting figure — it can diverge substantially from cash flow because it includes non-cash items such as depreciation and provisions. For individuals, "net income" or "take-home pay" informally means wages after deductions for tax and other withholdings.

Adjustments

Companies also report variants such as net income attributable to common shareholders (after preferred dividends), and adjusted or "non-GAAP" earnings that exclude items management considers non-recurring. Net income can be negative, in which case it is reported as a net loss.
