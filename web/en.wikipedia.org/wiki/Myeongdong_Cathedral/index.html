Myeongdong Cathedral

Location: Myeongdong, Jung-gu, Seoul, South Korea
Denomination: Roman Catholic
Consecrated: 1898
Style: Gothic Revival
Height: 23 m (spire 45 m)

The Cathedral Church of the Virgin Mary of the Immaculate Conception, known as Myeongdong Cathedral, is the national cathedral of the Archdiocese of Seoul and a landmark of the Myeongdong neighbourhood in Jung District, Seoul, South Korea. It is widely regarded as a symbol of the Catholic Church in Korea.

History

The cornerstone was laid in 1892 and the cathedral was consecrated on 29 May 1898, on a site associated with the earliest Catholic community in Korea, where the first Korean Catholic community had gathered in the 1780s. Built of red and grey brick in the Gothic Revival style, it was the first Gothic building and one of the earliest brick churches in Korea.

Role in modern Korea

During the 1970s and 1980s the cathedral became a centre of the pro-democracy movement, sheltering student protesters and serving as a venue for prayer meetings against the authoritarian government. It remains an important site for public gatherings and ceremonies in Seoul.
