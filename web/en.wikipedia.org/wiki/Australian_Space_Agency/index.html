The Australian Space Agency is an agency of the Australian Government responsible for the development of Australia's commercial space industry, coordinating domestic activities, identifying opportunities and facilitating international space engagement. It was established on 1 July 2018, replacing the role of the long-defunct Australian Space Office (1986–1996) as the country's civil space coordination body.

The agency's headquarters opened in Adelaide at Lot Fourteen, the former Royal Adelaide Hospital site, in February 2020. Its first head was Megan Clark (2018–2020), followed by Enrico Palermo. The agency's purpose, set out in the Australian Civil Space Strategy 2019–2028, is to triple the size of the Australian space sector to $12 billion and create up to 20,000 jobs by 2030.

Activities

The agency regulates Australian space and high-power rocket activities under the Space (Launches and Returns) Act 2018, issues launch permits and facility licences, and represents Australia internationally, including as a founding signatory of the Artemis Accords in 2020. Programs it has supported include the Moon to Mars initiative with NASA — under which an Australian-designed semi-autonomous rover is to fly on a NASA lunar mission — the National Space Mission for Earth observation, smart-satellite (SmartSat CRC) research, and grants for Australian launch sites such as those in South Australia and Queensland.

History

Australia was an early space player — the Woomera test range hosted ELDO and British launches, and Australia became the third country to launch a satellite from its own territory (WRESAT, 1967) — but civil coordination lapsed after the Australian Space Office was abolished in 1996. Reviews in 2017 led the government to announce a national agency in September 2017, formally established the following July.
