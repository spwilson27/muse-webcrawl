Jewish mythology

Jewish mythology is the body of myths and legendary narratives associated with Judaism, drawn from the Hebrew Bible, rabbinic literature (Midrash and Talmud), medieval writings and folklore. It includes creation stories, tales of angels and demons, and legends about biblical figures expanded far beyond the scriptural text.

Themes and beings

Major motifs include the creation of the world and of Adam and Eve, the Flood, giants (Nephilim), the sea monster Leviathan, the land beast Behemoth and the bird Ziz; Lilith, Adam's legendary first wife in medieval tradition; angels such as Metatron; demons such as Ashmedai; and the dybbuk of later folklore. The golem — an animated clay figure, most famously the Golem of Prague attributed to Rabbi Judah Loew — became one of the most influential Jewish legendary creations.

Sources

Much of this material is collected in works such as the Babylonian Talmud's aggadic passages, Sefer ha-Aggadah (compiled by Bialik and Ravnitzky), Louis Ginzburg's multi-volume The Legends of the Jews, and Howard Schwartz's anthologies of Jewish folklore and mysticism.

References

Louis Ginzburg, The Legends of the Jews; Howard Schwartz, Tree of Souls: The Mythology of Judaism (2004).
