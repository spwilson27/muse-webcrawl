The Kronos Quartet is an American string quartet based in San Francisco. It was founded in 1973 by violinist David Harrington in Seattle. For more than five decades, Kronos has combined a spirit of fearless exploration with a commitment to continually reimagine the string quartet experience, commissioning and premiering over a thousand works and recording more than 70 albums.

History

Harrington founded the quartet after hearing George Crumb's Black Angels, a work for amplified string quartet written in response to the Vietnam War; the experience convinced him of the string quartet's potential for urgent contemporary expression. The quartet relocated to San Francisco in the late 1970s and took up residency at Mills College. Long-serving members included violinist John Sherba and violist Hank Dutt (both joined 1978) and cellist Joan Jeanrenaud (1978–1999), followed by Jennifer Culp, Jeffrey Zeigler, and Sunny Yang; cellist Paul Wiancko joined in 2023.

Repertoire and collaborations

Kronos performs works by leading composers — including Philip Glass, Steve Reich, Terry Riley, Arvo Pärt, Henryk Górecki, Sofia Gubaidulina, and Osvaldo Golijov — alongside music drawn from many world traditions. Notable recordings include Reich's Different Trains (which won a Grammy Award), Gorecki's quartets, the early-music and global projects developed with the Kronos Performing Arts Association, and collaborations with artists from Wu Man and Asha Bhosle to Laurie Anderson and the Afghan National Institute of Music. The quartet's "Fifty for the Future" project commissioned 50 new works, released freely as scores and recordings for teaching the contemporary quartet repertoire.

Awards

Kronos has received numerous honours, including a Grammy Award for Best Chamber Music Performance (2004, for Alban Berg's Lyric Suite), the Polar Music Prize (shared with Patti Smith, 2011), a MacArthur "genius" grant to Harrington, and the Avery Fisher Prize.
