Royal Canadian Mint

Headquarters of the Royal Canadian Mint in Ottawa

The Royal Canadian Mint (French: Monnaie royale canadienne) is the Crown corporation responsible for the minting and distribution of all of Canada's circulation coins. It also produces coinage for foreign countries and manufactures bullion products, including gold, silver, palladium and platinum bars and coins.

History

For the first fifty years of Canadian Confederation, circulation coins were struck in London or Birmingham, England. In 1901 Parliament authorized a mint in Ottawa. The Ottawa branch of the Royal Mint was officially opened on 2 January 1908 by the Governor General, Earl Grey. In 1931, under the Royal Canadian Mint Act, the institution became a department of the Government of Canada; in 1969 it became a Crown corporation.

A high-volume production facility was opened in Winnipeg, Manitoba, in 1976 to produce circulation coinage, while the Ottawa plant specialized in collector and bullion coins. The Mint introduced the bimetallic two-dollar coin (the "toonie") in 1996 and multi-ply plated steel coinage technology in 2001.

Products

The Mint is best known internationally for its bullion products, including the Canadian Maple Leaf gold and silver coins, first issued in 1979 and 1988 respectively. Its .99999 fine gold Maple Leaf was among the purest gold coins offered by any mint. The Mint also produces commemorative circulation coins marking national anniversaries, Olympic Games and other events.
