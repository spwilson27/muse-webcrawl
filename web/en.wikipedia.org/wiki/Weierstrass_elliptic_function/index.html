Weierstrass elliptic function

Weierstrass ℙ-function
DomainComplex plane minus lattice points
PropertiesDoubly periodic, meromorphic, even
Named afterKarl Weierstrass

In mathematics, the Weierstrass elliptic functions are elliptic functions that take a particularly simple form. They are named for Karl Weierstrass. This class of functions is also referred to as ℙ-functions and they are usually denoted by the symbol ℙ, a uniquely fancy script "p". They play an important role in the theory of elliptic functions, and a ℙ-function together with its derivative can be used to parameterise elliptic curves and they generate the field of elliptic functions with respect to a given period lattice.

Definition

Let ω1, ω2 ∈ C be two complex numbers that are linearly independent over R and let Λ := Zω1 + Zω2 be the lattice generated by those numbers. Then the ℙ-function is defined as

ℙ(z) = 1/z2 + ∑(m,n)≠(0,0) [ 1/(z − mω1 − nω2)2 − 1/(mω1 + nω2)2 ]

This series converges absolutely and uniformly on every compact set that does not contain any lattice point, so that the function defined by it is meromorphic on the whole complex plane, with a double pole at each lattice point and no other poles.

Differential equation

With the above choice of the invariants, the ℙ-function satisfies the differential equation

ℙ′(z)2 = 4ℙ(z)3 − g2ℙ(z) − g3,

where g2 and g3 are the Eisenstein-series invariants of the lattice. This is precisely the equation of an elliptic curve in Weierstrass normal form, y2 = 4x3 − g2x − g3.

Properties

The ℙ-function is an even elliptic function of order two: ℙ(−z) = ℙ(z), and ℙ(z + ω) = ℙ(z) for all ω ∈ Λ. Its derivative ℙ′ is an odd elliptic function of order three. Every elliptic function with respect to the lattice Λ can be expressed rationally in terms of ℙ and ℙ′.
