Manteca is a city in San Joaquin County, California, in the northern San Joaquin Valley, about 18 miles (29 km) southeast of Stockton and about 60 miles (97 km) east of San Francisco. The name is Spanish for "lard" — according to local legend, a mistaken rendering of manteca when the town's founders had intended Monteca (associated with cream and butter); the name stuck and was adopted by the railroad.

History

The community grew up around a Southern Pacific Railroad station established in the 1870s (originally known as Calla). Agriculture — dairy, almonds, melons, and pumpkins — long dominated; Manteca became known for its watermelons and hosted an annual Watermelon Street Fair. The city was incorporated in 1918. In recent decades Manteca has grown rapidly as a comparatively affordable bedroom community for workers commuting to the Bay Area and Sacramento, served by State Routes 99 and 120 and the Altamont Corridor Express commuter rail.

Geography and economy

Manteca sits on flat valley floor at low elevation. Retail and logistics have joined agriculture as major employers; the city is home to the Great Wolf Lodge Northern California water park resort (opened 2021), a Bass Pro Shops, and large distribution centers. Local institutions include Doctors Hospital of Manteca and Manteca Unified School District.

Notable people

Notable residents have included astronaut José M. Hernández, who grew up in nearby Stockton and was raised in a migrant farmworker family in the area, and Milwaukee Brewers manager Pat Murphy, who was born in Syracuse but has ties to the area; singer-songwriter Ernie Wasson is among the city's musicians.
