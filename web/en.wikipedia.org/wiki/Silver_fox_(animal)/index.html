Silver fox (animal)

Silver fox
SpeciesVulpes vulpes (red fox colour morph)

The silver fox is a melanistic colour form of the red fox (Vulpes vulpes). Silver foxes display a great deal of variation in their coats: some are completely black except for white at the tip of the tail, while others are bluish-grey or black with long silver-tipped guard hairs that give the pelt a silvery sheen. Historically, their pelts were among the most valuable in the North American and Eurasian fur trade.

A silver fox, the melanistic form of the red fox prized for its frosted black pelt.

Distribution and genetics

In the wild, silver foxes occur mainly in the northern parts of North America and in Siberia, where they make up a small percentage — often estimated at under ten percent — of red fox populations; litters of mixed red, cross, and silver pups can be born to the same parents. The silver coat is inherited as a partially dominant trait, and foxes may pass through intermediate "cross fox" patterns with a dark stripe along the back and shoulders.

Fur trade and farming

Silver fox pelts commanded extraordinary prices in the nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, at times exceeding the value of beaver or sable. This led to the founding of fox farms on Prince Edward Island, Canada, in the 1890s, when breeders such as Charles Dalton and Robert Oulton first successfully raised silver foxes in captivity — an innovation that launched the modern fur-farming industry.

Domestication experiment

Silver foxes are the subjects of the famous Russian farm-fox experiment begun in 1959 by Dmitry Belyayev and continued by Lyudmila Trut at Novosibirsk. By selectively breeding only the tamest foxes, researchers produced foxes that sought human contact and developed dog-like traits — floppy ears, piebald coats, and wagging tails — within a few dozen generations, providing a celebrated case study in the genetics of domestication.
