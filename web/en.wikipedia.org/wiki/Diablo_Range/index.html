The Diablo Range is a mountain range in the California Coast Ranges, in west-central California, United States. It stretches roughly 180 miles (290 km) from the Carquinez Strait and Mount Diablo in the north, southeastward along the eastern side of San Francisco Bay and the Santa Clara Valley, to the Salinas Valley area in Monterey County. It forms the eastern wall of the San Francisco Bay Area and separates it from California's Central Valley.

Geography

The range's best-known summit is Mount Diablo (3,849 ft), famous for the exceptionally wide views from its peak. Other high points include Mount Hamilton (4,196 ft, actually the range's highest), site of the Lick Observatory, and peaks in Henry W. Coe State Park, the largest state park in Northern California. The range is traversed by few roads; Interstate 580 crosses it at Altamont Pass, dotted with wind turbines, and State Route 130 climbs to Mount Hamilton.

Geology and ecology

The range consists largely of uplifted sedimentary rocks of the Franciscan Complex and Great Valley Sequence, deformed by the San Andreas and Calaveras fault systems. Its dry eastern slopes support oak woodland, chaparral, and grassland, providing habitat for tule elk, golden eagles, and the endangered San Joaquin kit fox. Large ranchlands dominate land use, and the range is a focus of conservation efforts to maintain a wildlife corridor between the Bay Area and the southern Coast Ranges.
