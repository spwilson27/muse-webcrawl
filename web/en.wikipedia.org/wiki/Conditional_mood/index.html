Conditional mood

The conditional mood (abbreviated cond) is a grammatical mood used to express a proposition whose validity is dependent on some condition, possibly counterfactual. It typically occurs in the apodosis (main clause) of a conditional sentence, whose protasis expresses the condition, often introduced by a word meaning "if".

Conditional constructions pair a condition clause with a consequence clause.

In various languages

English has no fully inflected conditional mood; it forms conditional constructions periphrastically with the modal verb would (and could, should, might) plus the infinitive, as in "I would go". Many languages have a dedicated conditional verb form: French je parlerais ("I would speak"), formed from the future stem with imperfect endings; Spanish hablaría; Italian parlerei; and comparable inflected conditionals in Romanian, Polish, Finnish, Turkish and other languages. In Romance languages the conditional also serves as the "future in the past" in reported speech.

Uses

Beyond hypothetical consequences, conditional forms commonly soften requests and assertions as a politeness strategy ("I would like..."), express wishes, and report future events from a past viewpoint.
