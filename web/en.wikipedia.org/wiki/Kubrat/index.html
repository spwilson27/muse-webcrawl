Kubrat (Greek: Κουβρᾶτος; Bulgarian: Кубрат) was the khan who united the Bulgar tribes of the Pontic steppe and founded Old Great Bulgaria (Byzantine Greek: Παλαιὰ Μεγάλη Βουλγαρία), a powerful state between the Danube, the Black Sea and the Volga, around 632. He belonged to the Dulo clan and is regarded as one of the founding figures of Bulgarian statehood.

Life

According to Byzantine sources such as John of Nikiu and the chronicles of Theophanes and Patriarch Nikephoros, Kubrat spent part of his youth at the imperial court in Constantinople, where he was baptised (c. 619) and formed a friendship with the emperor Heraclius, receiving the title of patrikios. After the death of his uncle Organa, Kubrat threw off the overlordship of the Western Turkic Khaganate and the Avars, uniting the Onogur and other Bulgar tribes into Great Bulgaria, with its centre probably at Phanagoria on the Taman Peninsula.

The legend of the twigs and his sons

Byzantine and Bulgarian tradition recounts that on his deathbed Kubrat admonished his five sons — Batbayan, Kotrag, Asparukh, Kuber and Alcek — never to separate, illustrating the lesson with a bundle of twigs that could not be broken while bound together. The sons nevertheless divided: Batbayan remained in the homeland and soon submitted to the Khazars; Kotrag led the Kotrags north to the middle Volga (the future Volga Bulgaria); Asparukh crossed the Danube and founded the First Bulgarian Empire (681); Kuber settled in Macedonia; and Alcek migrated as far as Italy. A major hoard found at Malaya Pereshchepina in Ukraine in 1912, including a gold ring with a monogram read as Kubrat's, is often identified as his burial treasure.
