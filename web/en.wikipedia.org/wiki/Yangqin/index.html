The yangqin (Chinese: 揚琴; pinyin: yángqín) is a Chinese hammered dulcimer: a trapezoidal box zither whose strings are struck with thin bamboo hammers. Originally introduced to China from the West — its alternative name 洋琴 means "foreign zither" — it developed in Guangdong and the Jiangnan region and spread throughout China, becoming a staple of Cantonese music, Jiangnan sizhu, Chinese orchestra, and accompaniment for narrative singing and opera.

Construction and playing

The instrument's soundboard carries many courses of steel and brass strings (modern concert yangqin have over a hundred strings, spanning four to five octaves) stretched over movable bridges arranged in rows. The player strikes, plucks, or damps the strings with two flexible bamboo hammers, and advanced techniques include glissando, harmonics, and striking with the hammer reversed. Its bright, percussive-yet-resonant tone makes it function in ensembles as both a melodic and a chordal/rhythmic "glue" instrument, and it is a standard solo instrument with its own conservatory repertoire.
