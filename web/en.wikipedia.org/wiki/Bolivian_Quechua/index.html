Bolivian Quechua

Bolivian Quechua
Native name: Qhichwa / Runasimi
Region: Bolivia (also northern Argentina)
Speakers: about 2 million
Family: Quechuan
ISO code: quh (South Bolivian)

Bolivian Quechua is a variety of the Quechua language family spoken in Bolivia, where it is an official language alongside Spanish and other indigenous languages. It is generally divided into North Bolivian Quechua and South Bolivian Quechua, the latter being the more widely spoken and closely related to the Quechua of northern Argentina.

Characteristics

Like other Southern Quechua varieties, Bolivian Quechua is an agglutinative language with subject–object–verb word order, extensive suffixation, and evidential markers that indicate the source of a speaker's information. Its phonology typically has a simplified vowel system of three vowels /a, i, u/, though contact with Spanish has influenced pronunciation and vocabulary in many communities.

Status and use

Quechua in Bolivia is spoken especially in the departments of Cochabamba, Potosí, Chuquisaca, Oruro and La Paz. Since Bolivia's 2009 constitution recognized 36 indigenous languages as official, Quechua has been promoted in bilingual intercultural education, public media, and government services, though Spanish remains dominant in urban and formal contexts and many speakers are shifting toward Spanish.
