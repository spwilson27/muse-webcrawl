States and territories of Australia

The states and territories of Australia are the federated administrative divisions of the Commonwealth of Australia. There are six states — New South Wales, Queensland, South Australia, Tasmania, Victoria, and Western Australia — and ten federal territories, of which the mainland Northern Territory and Australian Capital Territory are the largest and most populous.

Overview

Each state has its own constitution, parliament, government, and courts, retaining powers not granted to the Commonwealth at Federation in 1901. Territories are self-governing to varying degrees but remain subject to federal override, unlike the states.

External territories

Australia also administers external territories including Norfolk Island, Christmas Island, the Cocos (Keeling) Islands, and the Australian Antarctic Territory. Jervis Bay Territory, carved from New South Wales, houses naval facilities and is administered as part of the ACT for many purposes.
