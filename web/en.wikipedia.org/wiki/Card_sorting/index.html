Card sorting

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Card sorting is a technique in user experience design in which participants organise topics into groups that make sense to them, in order to design or evaluate the information architecture of a website or application. It is used to understand users' mental models — how they expect content to be grouped and labelled — rather than imposing the designer's or organisation's own categories.

Methods

In an open card sort, participants receive cards bearing content items and create and name their own groups, revealing the vocabulary and structure users naturally use. In a closed card sort, the categories are fixed in advance and participants assign cards to them, testing whether an existing structure works. Hybrid sorts combine both. Sessions may use physical index cards with small groups in a lab, or online tools that recruit many participants and generate similarity matrices and dendrograms from the aggregate data. A typical study uses 30–50 cards and 15 or more participants.

Use and limitations

Card sorting is a standard early step in designing navigation menus, website hierarchies, and documentation structures. Its results are qualitative or semi-quantitative: they can suggest groupings and labels but are often followed by tree testing, in which users must find items within a proposed hierarchy, to validate the design. Critics note that card sorting shows how users group content out of context, which may differ from how they search for it within a real interface.
