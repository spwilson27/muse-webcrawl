Alaotra grebe

The Alaotra grebe, known only from Lake Alaotra

The Alaotra grebe (Tachybaptus rufolavatus), also known as Delacour's little grebe or the rusty grebe, was a small waterbird endemic to Lake Alaotra and surrounding ponds in the Alaotra-Mangoro region of Madagascar. Declared officially extinct in 2010, it is considered the first bird extinction confirmed in the 21st century by BirdLife International and the IUCN.

Description and habits

The Alaotra grebe was a small grebe, about 25 cm long, with a dark cap, rusty-yellow patches on the cheeks and throat, and pale eyes. It had tiny wings and was a very poor flier, apparently never leaving the Lake Alaotra basin. It fed mainly on small fish and aquatic invertebrates in the lake's reed-fringed shallows.

Decline and extinction

The species declined through the 20th century as its habitat degraded: shore vegetation was cleared for rice cultivation, nylon monofilament gill nets drowned birds, pollution and siltation increased, and introduced carnivorous fish such as the snakehead and largemouth bass preyed on chicks and competed for food. Hybridisation with the little grebe further diluted the population. The last confirmed sighting was in 1982; searches in the 1990s found none, and it was formally declared extinct in 2010.
