Management consulting

Management consulting is the practice of helping organisations improve their performance through the analysis of existing problems and the development of plans for improvement. Management consulting firms provide external, objective advice and access to specialised expertise, and have become a large global industry, with major firms including McKinsey & Company, Boston Consulting Group and Bain & Company (the "MBB" firms), alongside the consulting arms of the Big Four accounting firms.

History

The industry grew out of the scientific management movement of the early 20th century. James O. McKinsey founded his firm in Chicago in 1926; Arthur D. Little (founded 1886) is often considered the earliest management consultancy. The field expanded rapidly after the Second World War, and from the 1980s strategy consulting and IT-related advisory work drove further growth.

Services

Consultancies advise on corporate strategy, operations, organisational design, marketing, mergers and acquisitions, technology implementation and, increasingly, sustainability and digital transformation. Engagements are typically staffed by mixed teams of analysts, associates, managers and partners, billed by the day or by project, using a leveraged "pyramid" model of many juniors supervised by few seniors.

Criticism

The industry is frequently criticised for high fees relative to value delivered, generic or recycled recommendations, conflicts of interest when advising both regulators and regulated industries, and its role in controversial client work — for example, advice connected to the opioid crisis, which led McKinsey to pay settlements exceeding $600 million in the United States. Defenders argue that consultancies diffuse best practice across industries and provide surge capacity governments and firms cannot maintain in-house.
