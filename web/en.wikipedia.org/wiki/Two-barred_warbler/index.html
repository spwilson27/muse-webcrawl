Two-barred warbler

The two-barred warbler (Phylloscopus plumbeitarsus) is a species of leaf warbler (family Phylloscopidae). It was formerly included in the "Old World warbler" assemblage. It breeds in Siberia, northern Mongolia and northeastern China, and winters in southern China and Southeast Asia, including Thailand, Laos, Vietnam and Myanmar.

Description

The two-barred warbler is a medium-sized leaf warbler, about 11–12 cm long. It has greenish-olive upperparts, whitish underparts, a prominent pale supercilium (eyebrow stripe), dark olive legs — the source of its scientific name plumbeitarsus, "lead-coloured tarsus" — and, most distinctively, two white wing bars, of which the front one is sometimes faint. It closely resembles the greenish warbler (Phylloscopus trochiloides), of which it was long considered a subspecies; the two-barred warbler tends to show a brighter supercilium, cleaner white underparts and the stronger second wing bar. Its song is a rapid, jerky series of notes, differing noticeably from that of the greenish warbler.

Taxonomy

The species was described by Robert Swinhoe in 1861. It forms part of the greenish warbler ring-species complex around the Tibetan Plateau, a famous case study in speciation by distance. The two-barred warbler is now generally treated as a full species, separate from P. trochiloides, on the basis of song, plumage and genetic differences, following studies of the complex published in the early 2000s.

Distribution and status

Breeding habitat is coniferous and mixed taiga forest and mountain woodland. A rare vagrant to western Europe, with a small number of records in Britain and elsewhere in autumn, it is assessed as Least Concern on the IUCN Red List given its very large range and population.
