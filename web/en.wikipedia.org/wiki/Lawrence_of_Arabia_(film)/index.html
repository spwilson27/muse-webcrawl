Lawrence of Arabia is a 1962 British epic historical drama film based on the life of T. E. Lawrence and his 1926 book Seven Pillars of Wisdom. It was directed by David Lean and produced by Sam Spiegel. The film stars Peter O'Toole as Lawrence, with Alec Guinness playing Prince Faisal and Omar Sharif, in his English-language debut, as Sherif Ali.

Plot

The film begins with Lawrence's death in a motorcycle accident in 1935 and a memorial service at St Paul's Cathedral, then flashes back to the First World War. Lawrence, an eccentric British Army officer stationed in Cairo, is sent to Arabia to assess the prospects of Prince Faisal in his revolt against the Ottoman Turks. Defying orders, he leads a daring crossing of the Nefud desert and captures Aqaba from the landward side. He becomes a legendary figure to the Arabs and the press, but is tormented by the violence of guerrilla warfare and by the conflicting promises Britain has made to its Arab allies. After the fall of Damascus, where the Arab council collapses into factionalism, a disillusioned Lawrence returns to Britain.

Production

The film was shot on location in Jordan, Morocco, and Spain on Super Panavision 70 film. O'Toole, a virtual unknown, was cast after Albert Finney declined the role following extensive screen tests. Maurice Jarre's score, including the famous main theme, was composed in only a few weeks. The celebrated mirage shot introducing Omar Sharif's character was filmed with a special 482mm lens.

Reception and legacy

The film was a critical and commercial triumph. At the 35th Academy Awards it won seven Oscars from ten nominations, including Best Picture and Best Director. It is widely regarded as one of the greatest films ever made: it placed fifth on the British Film Institute's list of the 100 greatest British films, and the American Film Institute ranked it among the top ten American epics. A restored version overseen by Lean was released in 1989, and a 4K restoration marked the film's 50th anniversary in 2012.
