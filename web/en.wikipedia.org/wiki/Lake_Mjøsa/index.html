Lake Mjøsa (Norwegian: Mjøsa) is the largest lake in Norway and one of the deepest lakes in Europe. It is located in the southern part of the country, about 100 km (62 mi) north of Oslo, in Innlandet and Akershus counties. The lake stretches some 117 km (73 mi) from Lillehammer in the north to Eidsvoll in the south, and its main tributary is the river Gudbrandsdalslågen in the north; the lake drains south through the Vorma river to the Glomma.

Geography and ecology

Mjøsa has a surface area of about 369 km² and a maximum depth of 449 m, making its bottom well below sea level. Its largest island is Helgøya. The lake has been heavily monitored since the eutrophication problems of the 1970s, when algal blooms (including the cyanobacterium Tychonema bourrellyi) threatened its use as a drinking water source for some 100,000 people; a major cleanup campaign (the "Mjøsaksjonen") restored water quality. The lake supports some 20 fish species, most famously the large Mjøsa trout.

Transport

The lake has long been a transport artery. The paddle steamer Skibladner, launched in 1856, is the world's oldest paddle steamer still in scheduled service and is popularly known as the "White Swan of Mjøsa". The main railway line north from Oslo (the Dovre Line) and the E6 highway follow its eastern shore.

Shore towns

The three traditional "Mjøsa towns" are Hamar on the eastern shore — a medieval bishopric whose cathedral ruins are preserved under a glass structure — Gjøvik on the western shore, and Lillehammer at the northern end, host of the 1994 Winter Olympics. Brumunddal and Moelv also lie on the lake.

Name

The name derives from Old Norse Mjǫrs, possibly related to a verb meaning to shine or glitter.
