In mathematics, the Bernoulli numbers Bn are a sequence of rational numbers with deep connections to number theory. They were introduced by Jacob Bernoulli in connection with formulas for sums of powers of consecutive integers, published posthumously in his Ars Conjectandi (1713). The Japanese mathematician Seki Takakazu discovered them independently at about the same time.

Definition

The Bernoulli numbers are defined by the exponential generating function x/(ex − 1) = Σ Bn xn/n!. The first few are B0 = 1, B1 = −1/2, B2 = 1/6, B4 = −1/30, B6 = 1/42, B8 = −1/30 and B10 = 5/66; all odd Bernoulli numbers beyond B1 are zero.

Applications

Bernoulli numbers appear in the Euler–Maclaurin formula, in the Taylor series expansions of trigonometric and hyperbolic functions, and in the values of the Riemann zeta function at even positive integers: ζ(2n) is a rational multiple of π2n involving B2n. Their denominators are characterised by the von Staudt–Clausen theorem, and the numerators encode subtle arithmetic information studied through Kummer's work on Fermat's Last Theorem and the notion of regular primes. Ada Lovelace's famous 1843 notes describe an algorithm for computing Bernoulli numbers with Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine, often cited as the first computer program.
