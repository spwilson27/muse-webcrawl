Premiership of Liz Truss

The premiership of Liz Truss began on 6 September 2022, when Truss accepted Queen Elizabeth II's invitation to form a government, succeeding Boris Johnson, and ended on 25 October 2022 following her resignation. At 49 days in office (50 days by some counts), it was the shortest premiership in British history.

Leadership election and appointment

Truss, foreign secretary since 2021, defeated Rishi Sunak in the Conservative Party leadership election, winning 57% of the members' vote. She was appointed prime minister by Elizabeth II at Balmoral Castle on 6 September — the last prime minister appointed by the Queen, who died two days later.

The mini-budget crisis

On 23 September 2022, Chancellor Kwasi Kwarteng delivered a "mini-budget" announcing large unfunded tax cuts, including abolition of the 45p top rate of income tax. The statement triggered a sharp fall in the pound, surging gilt yields, and emergency intervention by the Bank of England to stabilise pension funds. Kwarteng was dismissed on 14 October and replaced by Jeremy Hunt, who reversed nearly all the measures. Truss's authority collapsed, and after chaotic parliamentary scenes she resigned on 20 October.

Aftermath

Rishi Sunak succeeded her unopposed on 25 October. The Truss premiership became a byword for political and economic turmoil, and the episode defined debates about "Trussonomics" and fiscal credibility for years afterwards.
