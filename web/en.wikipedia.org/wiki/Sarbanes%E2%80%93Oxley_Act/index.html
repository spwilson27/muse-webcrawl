Sarbanes–Oxley Act

Sarbanes–Oxley Act of 2002
Enacted30 July 2002
SponsorsSen. Paul Sarbanes; Rep. Michael Oxley
PurposeCorporate governance and financial disclosure reform

The Sarbanes–Oxley Act of 2002 is a United States federal law that mandates certain practices in financial record keeping and reporting for corporations. It was enacted in response to a series of accounting scandals, most prominently those of Enron and WorldCom.

Provisions

The act created the Public Company Accounting Oversight Board (PCAOB) to oversee auditors, required chief executives to certify financial statements, and in its Section 404 required companies to assess and report on the effectiveness of internal controls over financial reporting.

Impact

Supporters credit the law with restoring investor confidence and improving the accuracy of financial reporting, while critics argue its compliance costs fall disproportionately on smaller public companies.
