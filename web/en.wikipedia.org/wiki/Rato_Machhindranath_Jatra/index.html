Rato Machhindranath Jatra

Rato Machhindranath Jatra
Type: Hindu/Buddhist chariot festival
Location: Lalitpur (Patan), Nepal
Duration: about one month
Deity: Rato Machhindranath (Bunga Dyah)


The towering chariot of Rato Machhindranath in Patan.

Rato Machhindranath Jatra is a chariot festival held annually in Lalitpur (Patan), Nepal, in honour of Rato Machhindranath, the god of rain and harvest, worshipped by Hindus as a form of Shiva/Matsyendranath and by Buddhists as an incarnation of Avalokiteśvara. It is considered the longest chariot festival in Nepal, lasting about a month.

The procession

A towering chariot, some 32 cubits high, is constructed at Pulchok from wood and cane and draped with greenery. The image of the deity, brought from the temple at Bungamati, is installed in the chariot, which is then pulled by hundreds of devotees through the streets of Patan in stages, resting at traditional halting points.

Bhoto Jatra

The festival culminates in the Bhoto Jatra at Jawalakhel, where a jewel-studded black vest (bhoto) is displayed to the crowd from the chariot — a ceremony tied to a legend about a lost vest and a dispute settled by the deity. Traditionally the head of state attends the showing of the bhoto.

Significance

Legend holds that the festival was instituted in the 7th century by King Narendra Deva to end a severe drought, after the sage Machhindranath was brought to the Kathmandu Valley. The jatra remains a major ritual seeking timely monsoon rains.
