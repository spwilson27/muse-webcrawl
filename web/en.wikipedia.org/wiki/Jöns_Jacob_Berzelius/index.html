Jöns Jacob Berzelius (1779–1848) was a Swedish chemist who, with Dalton and Lavoisier, is regarded as a founder of modern chemistry. He introduced the current system of chemical symbols (1813–14) — letters for elements and numbers for proportions — and the terms catalysis, polymer, isomer, protein and allotrope.

Berzelius determined atomic weights with unmatched precision for his time, discovered silicon, selenium, thorium and cerium, and classified minerals by composition. His annual reports on chemistry's progress made Stockholm, briefly, the capital of the science.

Electrochemical dualism

Berzelius held that compounds form by attraction of electropositive and electronegative parts — a theory that worked well for salts but strained against organic substitution reactions, where his authority delayed acceptance of newer views.

Symbols

Before Berzelius, alchemists' signs and Dalton's circles cluttered formulas; his H₂O-style notation is essentially what chemists still write today.
