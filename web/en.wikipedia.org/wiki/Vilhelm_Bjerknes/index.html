Vilhelm Bjerknes

Vilhelm Friman Koren Bjerknes (14 March 1862 – 9 April 1951) was a Norwegian physicist and meteorologist who did much to found the modern practice of weather forecasting. He formulated the primitive equations of atmospheric motion and founded the Bergen School of Meteorology, whose model of mid-latitude cyclones and weather fronts remains foundational in synoptic meteorology.

Early life and physics

Bjerknes was born in Christiania (now Oslo), the son of Carl Anton Bjerknes, a professor of applied mathematics who worked on hydrodynamics. Vilhelm assisted his father's research as a young man and studied in Paris and Bonn, working with Heinrich Hertz on electromagnetic waves. He became professor at Stockholm in 1895 and in 1898 published his celebrated circulation theorem, generalising Kelvin's theorem to rotating, baroclinic fluids — the key to understanding how atmospheric and oceanic circulation is generated by pressure and density gradients.

Weather forecasting as physics

In a landmark 1904 paper, Bjerknes argued that weather prediction should be treated as an initial-value problem of mechanics and physics: given observations of the atmosphere's present state, the equations of motion and thermodynamics — his primitive equations — could in principle be integrated forward. This rational programme, later taken up by Lewis Fry Richardson and realised with computers, underlies modern numerical weather prediction.

The Bergen School

Returning to Norway, Bjerknes founded the Geophysical Institute in Bergen in 1917. There, with collaborators including his son Jacob Bjerknes, Halvor Solberg and Tor Bergeron, he built a dense network of weather stations and developed the polar front theory of cyclones, introducing the vocabulary of warm and cold fronts and the cyclone life-cycle model that revolutionised practical forecasting during and after the First World War.

Legacy

Bjerknes received many honours, including the Symons Gold Medal; a crater on the Moon and one on Mars bear his name. His son Jacob became a leading meteorologist in the United States and helped establish the link between El Niño and the Southern Oscillation.
