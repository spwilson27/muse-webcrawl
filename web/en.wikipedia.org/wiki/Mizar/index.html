Mizar is a second-magnitude star in the handle of the Big Dipper asterism in the constellation of Ursa Major. It has the Bayer designation Zeta Ursae Majoris (ζ Ursae Majoris). Mizar forms a well-known naked-eye double star with the fainter Alcor, and was historically used as a test of eyesight.

Multiple star system

Mizar itself was the first telescopic binary star discovered, observed by Benedetto Castelli and Galileo in the early 17th century. Its two components, Mizar A and Mizar B, are each spectroscopic binaries, making Mizar a quadruple system; together with Alcor — itself a binary — the whole grouping comprises six stars. Mizar and Alcor lie about 83 light-years from the Sun and are members of the Ursa Major Moving Group.

Name

The name Mizar derives from the Arabic miʾzar, meaning "apron" or "girdle". In Indian astronomy the pair are associated with the sage Vashishtha and his wife Arundhati.
