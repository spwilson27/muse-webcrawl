Psychiatric rehabilitation

Psychiatric rehabilitation, also known as psychosocial rehabilitation, is the process of restoring the community functioning and well-being of a person diagnosed with a mental health condition or emotional disorder. Rather than focusing solely on reducing symptoms, it aims to help people develop the skills, supports and confidence to live, learn, work and socialise in settings of their own choosing, in line with the recovery model of mental health care.

Principles

Key principles include hope and person-centredness: rehabilitation is directed by the individual's own goals, builds on strengths rather than deficits, and treats the person as an active partner. Practitioners help clients set goals in domains such as housing, employment, education and relationships, then coach the skills and arrange the environmental supports needed to reach them.

Practices

Evidence-based practices include supported employment (notably the Individual Placement and Support model), supported housing such as Housing First, social skills training, cognitive remediation, illness self-management programmes, and clubhouse communities. Services are delivered by multidisciplinary teams in community settings rather than hospitals.

History

The field grew out of deinstitutionalisation in the mid-20th century and the recognition that discharge from asylums without community skills and supports led to relapse and homelessness. The Center for Psychiatric Rehabilitation at Boston University, founded by William Anthony, systematised the approach from the 1970s, and professional bodies now certify practitioners (CPRP) in many countries.
