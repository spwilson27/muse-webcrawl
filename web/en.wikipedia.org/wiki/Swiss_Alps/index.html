The Swiss Alps are the portion of the Alpine mountain range situated in Switzerland, covering about two-thirds of the country's territory and forming one of the defining features of its geography, climate and culture.

Geography

The Swiss Alps contain most of the highest peaks in the Alps, including the Monte Rosa massif, the Dom, the Weisshorn and the iconic pyramid of the Matterhorn (4,478 m). The range is the watershed of Europe: the Rhine, Rhône, Inn (Danube) and Ticino (Po) all rise in the Gotthard massif. Major glaciers include the Aletsch Glacier, the largest in the Alps and a UNESCO World Heritage site. The Bernese Alps' Jungfrau–Aletsch region and the Eiger's north face are landmarks of alpinism.

People and tourism

The mountains are crossed by historic passes — Gotthard, Simplon, Great St Bernard — and by engineering landmarks such as the Gotthard Base Tunnel, the world's longest railway tunnel (57 km). The Alps support alpine farming with seasonal transhumance, and tourism from winter sports at resorts like Zermatt, St. Moritz and Davos to summer hiking, contributing substantially to the Swiss economy. Climate change is visibly shrinking the glaciers and thawing permafrost, destabilising rock faces.
