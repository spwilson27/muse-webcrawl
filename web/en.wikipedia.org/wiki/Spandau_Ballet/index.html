Spandau Ballet were an English new wave band formed in Islington, London, in 1979. Inspired by the capital's post-punk underground dance scene, they emerged at the start of the 1980s — initially performing at the fashionable Blitz club — as the house band of the New Romantic movement and became one of the most successful British groups of the decade. The band's name refers to the Nazi war-crimes prison in Spandau, Berlin, and the grim "dance" of prisoners hanged there, a name suggested by a friend who saw it scrawled on a Berlin toilet wall.

Career

Their debut single "To Cut a Long Story Short" (1980) made the UK top five, and albums Journeys to Glory (1981) and Diamond (1982) fused electronic dance music with fashion-conscious pop. In 1983 the album True brought international success; its title track, a smooth soul ballad written by guitarist and songwriter Gary Kemp, reached number one in the UK and number four in the United States, and became one of the most-sampled and most-broadcast songs of its era. Parade (1984) followed, and the band performed at Live Aid in 1985.

Later albums Through the Barricades (1986) and Heart Like a Sky (1989) found diminishing success outside Europe. A bitter court case in 1999, in which singer Tony Hadley, saxophonist Steve Norman, and drummer John Keeble unsuccessfully sued Kemp for a share of songwriting royalties, embittered relations until a reunion in 2009 produced a successful tour and the album Once More. Hadley left again in 2017, and after a short period with replacement singer Ross William Wild the band ceased activity in 2019.
