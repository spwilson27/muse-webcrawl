Cardiac cycle

Cardiac cycle
The Wiggers diagram charts cycle events
Duration~0.8 s at a heart rate of 75 bpm
StagesDiastole (filling) and systole (contraction/ejection)

The cardiac cycle is the performance of the human heart from the beginning of one heartbeat to the beginning of the next. It consists of two periods: one during which the heart muscle relaxes and refills with blood, called diastole, following a period of robust contraction and pumping of blood, called systole. At a resting heart rate of about 75 beats per minute, one cycle lasts approximately 0.8 seconds — roughly 0.5 s of diastole and 0.3 s of systole.

Stages

Atrial systole

The cycle begins when the sinoatrial node fires, causing both atria to contract and push a final bolus of blood into the ventricles through the open mitral and tricuspid valves; this "atrial kick" contributes roughly the last 20–30% of ventricular filling.

Ventricular systole

Ventricular contraction first passes through isovolumetric contraction, in which rising pressure closes the AV valves (producing the first heart sound, S1, the "lub") while all valves remain shut. Once ventricular pressure exceeds that in the aorta and pulmonary artery, the semilunar valves open and blood is ejected. The volume ejected is the stroke volume.

Diastole

When ventricular pressure falls below arterial pressure, the semilunar valves close (the second heart sound, S2, the "dub"). A brief isovolumetric relaxation follows, then the AV valves open and the ventricles refill passively. Most coronary blood flow to the left ventricle occurs during diastole, which shortens disproportionately as heart rate rises — one reason very fast rates compromise cardiac output.

Pressure–volume relationships

The cycle is commonly summarised by the Wiggers diagram, which aligns ECG, heart sounds, and aortic, atrial and ventricular pressures against time, and by ventricular pressure–volume loops used in cardiology to assess contractility and loading conditions.
