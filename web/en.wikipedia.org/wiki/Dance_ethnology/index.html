Dance ethnology — also known as ethnochoreology or the anthropology of dance — is the study of dance as a form of cultural expression, examining how movement reflects and shapes the values, social structures and worldviews of the communities that practise it. Dance ethnologists conduct fieldwork among dancing communities, documenting dance forms in their social and ritual contexts rather than as abstract choreography alone.

History

The field emerged in the mid-20th century. Gertrude Prokosch Kurath, who coined much of its terminology, developed methods for analysing dance structure and relating it to culture in work among Native American peoples from the 1950s. Joann Kealiinohomoku's influential 1970 essay "An Anthropologist Looks at Ballet as a Form of Ethnic Dance" challenged the hierarchy that treated Western theatre dance as universal and other forms as "ethnic". Anya Peterson Royce's The Anthropology of Dance (1977) became a foundational text.

Methods and scope

Practitioners combine participant-observation, interview, film and notation systems (such as Labanotation) to study dance events ranging from rituals and festivals to social dancing and staged folk performance. Research questions include how dance encodes gender, ethnicity and nationalism — for example in state folk-dance ensembles — and how dance traditions change through migration, tourism and globalisation.
