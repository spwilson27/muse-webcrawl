Rock microstructure

Rock microstructure includes the texture of a rock and the small-scale rock structures. The words texture and microstructure are interchangeable, with the latter preferred in modern geological literature. However, texture is still acceptable because it is a useful means of identifying the origin of rocks, how they formed, and their appearance.

Description

Microstructure is studied in thin sections viewed under a petrographic microscope, or at higher magnification with electron microscopy. Key elements include grain size and shape, grain boundaries, crystallographic preferred orientation, foliation, lineation, and features such as subgrains, deformation lamellae, and recrystallised grains.

Deformation microstructures

Rocks deformed in the Earth's crust record their strain history in their microstructures. Processes such as dislocation creep produce undulose extinction and subgrain rotation; dynamic recrystallisation generates new, strain-free grains whose size can be used as a palaeopiezometer to estimate past stress. Brittle deformation produces microcracks, cataclasis, and fault rocks such as mylonites and pseudotachylytes.

Applications

Microstructural analysis underpins structural geology, metamorphic petrology, and materials science of natural rocks, informing interpretations of tectonic history, earthquake mechanics, and the strength of the lithosphere.
