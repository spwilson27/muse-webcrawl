Timeline of the evolutionary history of life

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

This timeline of the evolutionary history of life represents the current scientific theory outlining the major events during the development of life on Earth. Dates given are estimates based on radiometric dating, molecular clocks, and the fossil record, and are subject to revision.

Hadean and Archaean

  • c. 4.54 billion years ago (Ga): Earth forms.
  • c. 4.4 Ga: First liquid-water oceans may form.
  • c. 4.0–3.8 Ga: Self-replicating molecules arise; the last universal common ancestor (LUCA) of all living organisms lives around this interval or somewhat later.
  • c. 3.5 Ga: Bacteria and archaea diversify; stromatolites formed by microbial mats appear in the fossil record.
  • c. 3.2–2.8 Ga: Photosynthesis evolves in cyanobacteria.
  • c. 2.4 Ga: The Great Oxidation Event: oxygen accumulates in the atmosphere, likely causing a mass extinction of anaerobic life and possibly a global glaciation ("Snowball Earth").

Proterozoic

  • c. 2.1–1.6 Ga: Eukaryotic cells evolve, incorporating mitochondria through endosymbiosis; chloroplasts follow in the lineage leading to algae and plants.
  • c. 1.2 Ga: Sexual reproduction is established, accelerating evolution.
  • c. 1.5–1.0 Ga: Multicellular organisms evolve several times independently.
  • c. 635–541 million years ago (Ma): The Ediacaran biota — the first large, complex soft-bodied organisms — flourish.

Palaeozoic

  • c. 541–485 Ma (Cambrian): The Cambrian explosion: most major animal phyla appear; trilobites, early chordates (such as Pikaia), and the first vertebrates evolve.
  • c. 485–444 Ma (Ordovician): Great diversification of marine life; first jawless fishes; plants and fungi begin colonizing land.
  • c. 444–419 Ma (Silurian): Vascular plants spread on land; first jawed fishes (gnathostomes).
  • c. 419–359 Ma (Devonian): "Age of Fishes"; forests form; lobe-finned fishes give rise to the first tetrapods.
  • c. 359–299 Ma (Carboniferous): Vast coal forests; giant insects; amniotes evolve, splitting into synapsids and sauropsids.
  • c. 299–252 Ma (Permian): Pangaea forms; synapsids dominate land; the era ends with the largest mass extinction in Earth's history (~252 Ma), eliminating most marine species.

Mesozoic

  • c. 252–201 Ma (Triassic): Recovery from the end-Permian extinction; first dinosaurs, mammals, and (later) pterosaurs and turtles.
  • c. 201–145 Ma (Jurassic): Giant sauropods and theropods dominate; the first birds (such as Archaeopteryx) evolve from small feathered dinosaurs.
  • c. 145–66 Ma (Cretaceous): Flowering plants (angiosperms) radiate; social insects diversify; the era ends with the Chicxulub impact and the extinction of non-avian dinosaurs (66 Ma).

Cenozoic

  • 66–23 Ma (Paleogene): Mammals and birds diversify into vacated niches; early primates, whales, and grasses evolve.
  • 23–2.6 Ma (Neogene): Grasslands expand; apes diversify; the hominin lineage splits from chimpanzees around 7–6 Ma; bipedal australopithecines appear by ~4 Ma.
  • 2.6 Ma–present (Quaternary): Ice ages cycle; genus Homo evolves (~2.8 Ma); Homo sapiens appears in Africa by ~300,000 years ago, spreads worldwide, and by ~10,000 years ago develops agriculture, transforming ecosystems globally.
