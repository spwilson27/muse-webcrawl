Country:Georgia

Poti (Georgian: ფოთი) is a port city in western Georgia, located on the eastern Black Sea coast at the mouth of the Rioni River, in the region of Samegrelo-Zemo Svaneti. It is home to the largest seaport in Georgia and is a key gateway for trade to and from the Caucasus and Central Asia.

History

The vicinity of Poti was the site of the ancient Greek colony of Phasis, founded by Miletians in the 7th or 6th century BC, and famous in Greek legend as a destination of the Argonauts. The modern city developed after the Russian Empire developed the port in the 19th century; the Poti–Tbilisi railway, completed in 1872, was the first railway in the Caucasus. The port expanded substantially under mayor Niko Nikoladze in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

Economy

The port handles containers, bulk cargo, and ferry traffic, and Poti hosts a free industrial zone established in 2008. The city is the Black Sea terminus of the railway corridor carrying Caspian oil and goods across Georgia.
