Steenrod algebra

In algebraic topology, a Steenrod algebra is a structure intervening in the study of cohomology operations. More precisely, the Steenrod algebra (over a prime field) is the algebra of all stable natural operations on mod-p cohomology.

The Steenrod squares and powers

For p = 2, the cohomology operations are the Steenrod squares Sqi, introduced by Norman Steenrod in 1947, which raise the degree of a cohomology class by i. They satisfy Sq0 = 1, Sqn(x) = x ∪ x for a class x of degree n, and Sqi(x) = 0 for i > n. For odd primes p the analogous operations are the reduced Steenrod powers Pi together with the Bockstein homomorphism.

Adem relations and structure

The Steenrod algebra is generated by the squares (respectively, powers and Bockstein) subject to the Adem relations, found by José Adem, which express products SqaSqb with a < 2b as sums of admissible products. As a consequence, the algebra has a basis of admissible monomials. It is a graded, connected, noncommutative Hopf algebra; its dual, computed by John Milnor, is a polynomial algebra, and this duality is fundamental to the Adams spectral sequence, one of the principal tools for computing stable homotopy groups of spheres.

Applications

Steenrod operations constrain which graded rings can occur as the cohomology of a space, and they were used by Steenrod and others to attack problems such as the existence of division algebras (via the Hopf invariant one problem, solved by J. F. Adams using secondary operations) and the vector fields on spheres problem.
