Tritagonist

In literature and drama, the tritagonist (from Greek τριταγωνιστής, "third actor") is the third most important character in a story, after the protagonist and the deuteragonist.

Origins

The term comes from ancient Greek theatre, where plays originally used a single actor plus chorus. Aeschylus added a second actor (the deuteragonist), and Sophocles introduced the third (the tritagonist), allowing more complex interactions and dialogue. The tritagonist traditionally played supporting or opposing roles, sometimes including the antagonist in early tragedy.

Modern usage

In modern narrative analysis, the tritagonist is the third-ranked character by prominence — for example, in the Harry Potter series, Hermione Granger is often described as tritagonist after Harry Potter and Ron Weasley. The character typically supports the protagonist's journey while having a smaller arc than the deuteragonist.
