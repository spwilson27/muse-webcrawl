32 Battalion, nicknamed the "Buffalo Battalion" or Os Terríveis ("The Terrible Ones"), was a light infantry battalion of the South African Army founded in 1975 by Colonel Jan Breytenbach. Unusually, it was composed mainly of black Angolan soldiers — former FNLA fighters — led initially by white South African officers, and it became one of the most decorated units of the South African Border War.

History

The unit grew out of Bravo Group, formed from FNLA troops who had fought alongside South Africa during Operation Savannah, the 1975 intervention in the Angolan Civil War. Based at Buffalo in the Caprivi Strip and later at Pomfret in South Africa, the battalion conducted cross-border operations against SWAPO and fought FAPLA and Cuban forces in southern Angola, including operations around Cuito Cuanavale in 1987–88.

Disbandment

32 Battalion was disbanded in March 1993, shortly before South Africa's first democratic elections, amid controversy over the unit's future and allegations linking some members to violence. Many veterans settled at Pomfret; some later worked in private military companies, and former members were among those arrested in connection with the 2004 Equatorial Guinea coup attempt.
