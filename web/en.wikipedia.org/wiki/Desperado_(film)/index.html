Desperado is a 1995 American neo-Western action film written, produced and directed by Robert Rodriguez. It is the second part of Rodriguez's Mexico Trilogy, following El Mariachi (1992) and preceding Once Upon a Time in Mexico (2003). The film stars Antonio Banderas as El Mariachi, a former musician who carries a guitar case full of weapons and seeks revenge on the drug lord Bucho (Joaquim de Almeida), whose men killed the woman he loved and shot him through the hand.

Plot

Aided by his friend Buscemi (Steve Buscemi), who spreads tall tales of his exploits in bars, El Mariachi tracks Bucho to a border town, where he is wounded and tended by Carolina (Salma Hayek), a bookstore owner coerced into Bucho's organisation. After a series of stylised gunfights — and the arrival of hitmen sent from Colombia — El Mariachi learns that Bucho is his own brother. In the climax, he and Carolina, joined by two fellow mariachi gunfighters with rocket-launcher and machine-gun guitar cases, confront Bucho's men; he refuses to kill his brother until Bucho threatens Carolina, then rides away with her.

Production and reception

Made for about $7 million — a thousand times the budget of El Mariachi — the film was shot in Ciudad Acuña, Mexico. Its flamboyant gunplay, Los Lobos soundtrack (including the hit "Canción del Mariachi") and star-making turns by Banderas and Hayek (in her Hollywood breakthrough) made it a commercial success, grossing about $58 million worldwide, and a defining entry in 1990s independent action cinema.
