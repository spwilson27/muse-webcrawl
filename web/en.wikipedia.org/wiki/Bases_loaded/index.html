Bases loaded

In baseball, the bases are loaded when there is a runner on each of first, second and third base — a condition sometimes called "bases full" or "bases juiced". It is one of the highest-leverage situations in the game: any hit, walk or hit batsman can produce runs, while the defence can record outs at any base, including a force play at home plate.

Scoring possibilities

With the bases loaded, a walk, hit by pitch or catcher's interference forces in a run. A base hit typically scores two runs. A grand slam — a home run with the bases loaded — scores four runs, the maximum possible on a single play.

Strategy

The fielding team often plays its infield in to cut off the run at the plate, or at "double-play depth" to attempt an inning-ending double play. Pitchers may intentionally walk a batter to load the bases in order to set up force plays or a more favourable matchup.

Records

Grand slams are a celebrated rarity; the only grand slam in a World Series Game 7 was hit by Bill Mazeroski-era contemporaries' successors — most famously, walk-off grand slams in postseason play have been struck by only a handful of players. A "golden" variant, the ultimate grand slam, is a walk-off grand slam with the home team trailing by three runs.
