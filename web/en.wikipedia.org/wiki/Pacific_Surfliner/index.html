The Pacific Surfliner is a 350-mile (560 km) passenger train service in Southern California, United States, operated by Amtrak under contract to the LOSSAN Rail Corridor Agency. It is the third-busiest Amtrak route and the busiest outside the Northeast Corridor, carrying about 2.5 million passengers annually in the late 2010s.

Route

The route runs from San Luis Obispo in the north through Santa Barbara, Ventura, Los Angeles (Union Station), Anaheim, Irvine, San Juan Capistrano, and San Clemente, to San Diego in the south. Much of the line between Los Angeles and San Diego hugs the Pacific Ocean coastline, giving the train scenic ocean views. The route follows much of the former Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway "Surf Line".

History

The service descends from the San Diegan, which the Santa Fe Railway began operating between Los Angeles and San Diego in 1938. Amtrak took over the route in 1971 and gradually extended service north to Santa Barbara and San Luis Obispo. In 2000 the train was rebranded as the Pacific Surfliner, with new bilevel Surfliner cars built by Alstom. Management of the service transferred from Caltrans to the regional LOSSAN Rail Corridor Agency in 2015.

Operations

Trains typically run in push-pull configuration with a diesel locomotive and a set of bilevel Surfliner and Superliner coaches, including a business-class car and a café car. The route shares track with Metrolink and Coaster commuter trains and with freight traffic. Coastal erosion near San Clemente has repeatedly forced temporary suspensions of service in the 2020s.
