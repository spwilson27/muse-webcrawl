White House press secretary

The White House press secretary is a senior official in the government of the United States whose primary responsibility is to act as spokesperson for the executive branch, especially with regard to the president, senior aides and administration policies. The press secretary collects information about actions and events within the president's administration and issues the administration's reactions to developments nationally and internationally.

History

The role evolved from the press secretaries of Franklin D. Roosevelt, whose aide Stephen Early was the first to function in the modern role; the position was formally established in 1929 under George Akerson for Herbert Hoover. The press secretary traditionally holds daily briefings in the James S. Brady Press Briefing Room, fielding questions from the White House press corps.

Responsibilities

The press secretary must be available to report to the media in a timely fashion. Their information comes from meetings with the president and senior staff. Because of the visibility of the position, press secretaries often become well-known public figures in their own right.
