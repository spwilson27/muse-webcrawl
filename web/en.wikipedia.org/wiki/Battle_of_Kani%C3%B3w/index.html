The Battle of Kaniów was fought on 10–11 May 1918 between the Polish II Corps in Russia, commanded by General Józef Haller, and German forces near Kaniv in Ukraine, during the final phase of the First World War on the Eastern Front.

Background

After the Treaty of Brest-Litovsk, German forces occupied Ukraine and sought to disarm Polish formations that had been part of the Russian army. Haller's corps refused an ultimatum to lay down its arms.

The battle

German troops attacked the Polish positions around Kaniów on the night of 10–11 May. The Poles resisted for several hours inflicting notable casualties, but, outnumbered and short of ammunition, Haller ordered capitulation. Haller himself escaped disguised and made his way to France.

Aftermath

Most of the corps' soldiers were interned, though many escaped to join Polish forces elsewhere. The battle became a celebrated episode in Polish military tradition, and Haller went on to command the Polish "Blue Army" in France.
