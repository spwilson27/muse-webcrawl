Edward Relph

Edward Relph is a Canadian geographer known for his work in phenomenology and humanistic geography, in particular his influential book Place and Placelessness (1976). He spent much of his career at the University of Toronto, where he taught at Scarborough College, and has written widely on the character of modern landscapes and urban environments.

Place and Placelessness

In Place and Placelessness, Relph argued that modern development increasingly produces "placeless" environments — airports, suburbs, motorways, chain stores and mass-produced landscapes that lack a distinctive identity or a deep sense of belonging. Drawing on phenomenology and the work of thinkers such as Martin Heidegger, he distinguished between insideness and outsideness as modes of experiencing place, contrasting authentic attachment to place with inauthentic, placeless attitudes.

Later work

Relph's subsequent books include Rational Landscapes and Humanistic Geography (1981), The Modern Urban Landscape (1987), and studies of Toronto, including Toronto: Transformations in a City and Its Region (2014). He has also written on the visual character of cities and on the impact of large-scale redevelopment on urban identity, and has revisited his concept of placelessness in light of globalisation and the internet, on which he has published essays and maintained a long-running website of notes on place.

Influence

Together with Yi-Fu Tuan and Anne Buttimer, Relph was a founding figure of humanistic geography, and his notion of placelessness became a standard term in geography, planning, architecture and heritage studies.
