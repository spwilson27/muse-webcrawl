Sexual Offences (Scotland) Act 2009

The Sexual Offences (Scotland) Act 2009 (asp 9) is an Act of the Scottish Parliament that reformed the law relating to sexual offences in Scotland. It created a new statutory framework of offences based on the absence of free agreement (consent), replacing much of the previous common law, which had relied on offences such as the common-law crime of rape defined by case law.

Background

The Act followed a review of the law of sexual offences by the Scottish Law Commission, which reported in 2007. The Commission concluded that the existing common-law framework was outdated and incoherent, particularly the definition of rape, which applied only to non-consensual vaginal intercourse with a woman. The Act was passed by the Scottish Parliament in June 2009 and came into force on 1 December 2010.

Provisions

The Act defines consent as "free agreement" and provides a non-exhaustive list of circumstances in which free agreement is absent, including where a person is asleep or unconscious, incapable because of alcohol or other substances, or where agreement is induced by threats, deception as to the nature of the act, or impersonation.

Principal offences created by the Act include:

  • Rape (section 1) — intentional or reckless penetration of the vagina, anus or mouth by the penis without consent. The definition was widened to cover male victims and oral and anal penetration, and was later extended to surgically constructed body parts.
  • Sexual assault by penetration (section 2) — penetration with a body part or object without consent.
  • Sexual assault (section 3) — a range of non-consensual sexual touching and related acts.
  • Sexual coercion (section 4) — causing another person to participate in sexual activity without consent.
  • Coercing a person into being present at a sexual activity (section 5) and communicating indecently (section 7).
  • Voyeurism (section 9) — observing or recording another person doing a private act without consent.

The Act also created a suite of offences protecting children, distinguishing between "young children" (under 13), for whom consent is irrelevant, and "older children" (aged 13 to 15). It also addresses offences by persons in positions of trust and sexual offences committed outside the United Kingdom by UK nationals and residents.
