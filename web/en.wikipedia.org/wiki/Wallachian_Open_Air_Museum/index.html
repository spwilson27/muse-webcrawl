Wallachian Open Air Museum

The Wallachian Open Air Museum (Czech: Valašské muzeum v přírodě) is a large open-air museum in Rožnov pod Radhoštěm, in the Moravian Wallachia region of the Czech Republic. Founded in 1925, it is one of the oldest and largest open-air museums in Central Europe, preserving the folk architecture and traditions of the Moravian Wallachians, a Carpathian highlander culture.

History

The museum was founded by the Jaroňek brothers, craftsmen and artists who settled in Rožnov in 1909. Inspired by Scandinavian open-air museums such as Skansen in Stockholm, they began relocating threatened wooden buildings to a site on the edge of the spa town. The museum opened in 1925 and grew steadily throughout the 20th century.

Exhibition areas

The museum is divided into several sections. The Wooden Town (Dřevěné městečko) reconstitutes a 19th-century Wallachian market town, with a town hall, church, school, and tavern. The Wallachian Village (Valašská dědina) shows farmsteads of the region's hills and valleys. The Mill Valley (Mlýnská dolina) preserves a working watermill, sawmill, fulling mill, hammer mill, and oil press. The museum also maintains an old cemetery and hosts demonstrations of traditional crafts, folk festivals, and the Rožnov festivities.

Significance

The museum is a national cultural monument and a centre for research into Wallachian ethnography, vernacular architecture, and folk crafts. In 2018, a fire damaged one of its historic buildings, prompting a restoration programme.
