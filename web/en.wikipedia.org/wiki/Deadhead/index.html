Deadhead

A Deadhead or Dead head is a fan of the American rock band the Grateful Dead. The term appeared in print by the early 1970s, and the band itself encouraged the name, printing notices addressed to "Dead Heads" in albums and mailing lists. Deadheads are known for following the band on tour, in some cases for years on end, building a distinctive subculture around the live concerts.

Culture

Deadhead culture is associated with tie-dyed clothing, an itinerant concert economy in venue parking lots (the "Shakedown Street" vending scene), and an ethic of communal generosity. Because the Grateful Dead uniquely allowed fans to record concerts, a vast tape-trading network grew up among Deadheads; the band formalised a dedicated tapers' section in 1984. Each show's setlist differed, encouraging attendance at many concerts and preserving thousands of live recordings.

History

Many early Deadheads came from the psychedelic counterculture of San Francisco's Haight-Ashbury. After the death of guitarist Jerry Garcia in 1995 and the band's disbanding, the community persisted through successor acts (The Other Ones, The Dead, Furthur, Dead & Company) and spin-offs of the surviving members.

Notable Deadheads

Fans have included US senator Al Franken, basketball player Bill Walton, and Apple co-founder Steve Wozniak. The academic study of the subculture spans sociology, economics and musicology.
