Joseph Wedderburn


Joseph Henry Maclagan Wedderburn
Born: 2 February 1882, Forfar, Angus, Scotland
Died: 9 October 1948 (aged 66), Princeton, New Jersey, U.S.
Fields: Mathematics (algebra)
Known for: Wedderburn's little theorem, Artin–Wedderburn theorem

Joseph Henry Maclagan Wedderburn (2 February 1882 – 9 October 1948) was a Scottish mathematician who taught at Princeton University for most of his career. A pioneer of modern algebra, he is known for his work on rings, algebras and matrix theory.

Work

In 1905 Wedderburn proved that a finite division ring is necessarily commutative — and therefore a field — a result now called Wedderburn's little theorem. His structure theorem for semisimple algebras, later generalised by Emil Artin as the Artin–Wedderburn theorem, classifies such algebras as products of matrix rings over division rings. His 1934 book Lectures on Matrices became a standard reference.

Life

Educated at the University of Edinburgh, Wedderburn studied in Leipzig, Berlin and Chicago before joining Princeton in 1909. He served with distinction in the First World War, and for many years edited the Annals of Mathematics. He was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1933.
