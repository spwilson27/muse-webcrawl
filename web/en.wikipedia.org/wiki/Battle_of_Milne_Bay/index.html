The Battle of Milne Bay (25 August – 7 September 1942), also known as Operation RE or the Battle of the Milne Bay, was a battle of the Pacific campaign of World War II. Japanese naval infantry troops, known as Kaigun Tokubetsu Rikusentai (Special Naval Landing Forces), attacked the Allied airfields at Milne Bay that had been established on the eastern tip of New Guinea. They were beaten back by a largely Australian force. The battle is often described as the first major defeat of Japanese land forces in the Pacific War.

Australian troops in the mud around Milne Bay.

Background

Milne Bay, at the eastern tip of the Territory of Papua, offered a sheltered anchorage and sites for airfields from which Allied aircraft could cover the approaches to Port Moresby. By mid-1942 the Allies had built airstrips guarded by Australian infantry of Milne Force, under Major General Cyril Clowes, together with Australian and American engineers and anti-aircraft units, supported by RAAF Kittyhawk fighters of Nos. 75 and 76 Squadrons.

The battle

On the night of 25–26 August 1942, about 2,000 Japanese marines landed east of the Allied positions, supported by two light tanks. Hampered by deep mud, disease, and faulty intelligence about the size of the garrison, the Japanese attacks were beaten back with heavy losses. RAAF fighters strafed the landing barges and supply dumps. Australian infantry counter-attacked and by 7 September the surviving Japanese were evacuated by sea. Of the landing force, more than 600 were killed; Allied losses were about 160 killed, most of them Australian.

Significance

The victory at Milne Bay raised Allied morale at a time when Japanese forces still appeared formidable, and it ended Japanese plans to outflank Port Moresby from the east. Together with the fighting on the Kokoda Track and at Guadalcanal, it marked a turning point in the New Guinea campaign.
