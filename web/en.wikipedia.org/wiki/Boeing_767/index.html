The Boeing 767 is an American wide-body airliner developed and manufactured by Boeing Commercial Airplanes. The aircraft was launched as the 7X7 program on July 14, 1978, and the prototype first flew on September 26, 1981. It was certified on July 30, 1982, and the initial 767-200 variant entered service with United Airlines on September 8, 1982. The 767 was the first wide-body twinjet produced by Boeing and its first airliner with a two-crew glass cockpit.

The airliner has a conventional tail and a supercritical wing for reduced aerodynamic drag. Its two underwing turbofan engines are offered by General Electric, Pratt & Whitney, and Rolls-Royce. The 767 can seating typically ranges from 181 to 375 passengers over a range of 3,850 to 6,385 nautical miles depending on variant.

Development

In the 1970s Boeing began studies of a new mid-size wide-body to complement the larger 747 and replace older trijets such as the DC-10 and Lockheed L-1011 TriStar on shorter routes. The resulting design shared development with the narrow-body Boeing 757, allowing pilots to qualify on both types with a common type rating. Rising fuel prices in the late 1970s pushed Boeing toward a twin-engine configuration, betting on the relaxation of ETOPS rules that then limited twinjets on long overwater routes.

Variants

767-200 and 767-200ER

The original 767-200 entered service in 1982. The extended-range 767-200ER followed in 1984, becoming popular for transatlantic services as ETOPS certification expanded to 120 and later 180 minutes.

767-300 and 767-300ER

Stretched by 6.43 m (21.1 ft), the 767-300 first flew in January 1986. The 767-300ER, with increased fuel capacity and higher gross weight, became the best-selling variant and a mainstay of transatlantic and transpacific fleets.

767-400ER

A further stretch introduced in 2000, with a redesigned flight deck based on the 777, raked wingtips, and seating for up to 375. Only Delta Air Lines and United (via Continental) ordered the passenger version.

Freighter and military versions

The 767-300F production freighter entered service in 1995. Military derivatives include the E-767 AWACS for Japan, the KC-767 tanker, and the KC-46 Pegasus for the United States Air Force.

Operators and retirement

Major passenger operators included Delta Air Lines, United Airlines, American Airlines, All Nippon Airways, and Japan Airlines. From the 2010s the type was progressively displaced in passenger service by the Boeing 787 Dreamliner and the Airbus A330, while freighter and tanker production continued.

Accidents and incidents

Notable accidents involving the 767 include Lauda Air Flight 004 (1991), where an uncommanded thrust reverser deployment caused a fatal breakup; Ethiopian Airlines Flight 961 (1996), a hijacking that ended in a ditching near the Comoros; and EgyptAir Flight 990 (1999). Two 767s were also hijacked and flown into the World Trade Center during the September 11 attacks: American Airlines Flight 11 and United Airlines Flight 175.

Specifications (767-300ER)
