The Banditaccia necropolis (Italian: Necropoli della Banditaccia) is an ancient Etruscan burial ground at Cerveteri, in the metropolitan city of Rome, Italy. It served the Etruscan city of Caere and is one of the largest and most important necropoleis of the Mediterranean world, with thousands of tombs spanning from the Villanovan period (9th century BC) to the late Etruscan period (3rd century BC).

Description

The site covers around 400 hectares, of which a core area of about 197 hectares is fenced and open to the public, containing some 1,000 visitable tumuli. The tombs, cut into the volcanic tufa or covered by mounds, reproduce the architecture of Etruscan houses: corridors (dromoi), multiple chambers, carved beds, chairs, shields, and ceiling beams. Later tombs were laid out along streets in a grid resembling a "city of the dead", including the famous Via degli Inferi.

Notable tombs

Among the best-known burials are the Tomb of the Reliefs, with painted stucco household objects; the Tomb of the Shields and Chairs; the Tomb of the Capitals; and the Tomb of the Five Chairs. Finds from the necropolis fill museums including the Louvre, the Vatican Museums, and the National Etruscan Museum at Villa Giulia, Rome.

Heritage status

In 2004 the necropolis, together with the Monterozzi necropolis at Tarquinia, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as the "Etruscan Necropolises of Cerveteri and Tarquinia".
