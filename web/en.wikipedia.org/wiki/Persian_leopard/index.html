Persian leopard

Conservation statusEndangered (IUCN)
Scientific namePanthera pardus tulliana
RangeIran, Caucasus, Türkiye, Iraq, Afghanistan, Central Asia

The Persian leopard (Panthera pardus tulliana), also known as the Caucasian leopard, Anatolian leopard, or Central Asian leopard, is a leopard subspecies native to western Asia. It is one of the largest leopard subspecies, with adults commonly weighing 40–90 kg. It is listed as Endangered on the IUCN Red List, with a total population estimated at fewer than 1,100 mature individuals.

Distribution and habitat

The Persian leopard survives mainly in Iran, which holds the largest remaining population, particularly in the Alborz and Zagros mountain ranges and in protected areas such as Golestan National Park. Smaller populations persist in the Caucasus (Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia, Russia), eastern Türkiye, northern Iraq, Afghanistan, and parts of Turkmenistan and Kazakhstan. It inhabits rugged mountainous terrain, juniper woodland, and subalpine meadows, generally between 600 and 3,300 metres in elevation.

Behaviour and ecology

Like other leopards it is a solitary, primarily nocturnal hunter. Wild prey includes bezoar ibex, wild sheep (urial and mouflon), wild goat, goitered gazelle, wild boar, and Indian crested porcupine; where wild prey is scarce it may attack livestock, bringing it into conflict with herders.

Threats and conservation

Principal threats are prey depletion, poaching, retaliatory killing for livestock losses, habitat fragmentation by roads and development, and land mines along some borders. Conservation measures include protected areas, camera-trap monitoring, and reintroduction programmes in the Russian Caucasus, where leopards bred from zoo stock have been released in Sochi National Park.
