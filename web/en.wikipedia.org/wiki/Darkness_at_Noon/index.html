Darkness at Noon

Darkness at Noon (German: Sonnenfinsternis) is a novel by the Hungarian-born British writer Arthur Koestler, first published in English in 1940. It is regarded as one of the most influential political novels of the 20th century and a classic account of Stalinist totalitarianism.

Plot

The protagonist, Nicholas Salmanovitch Rubashov, is an ageing veteran of the Russian Revolution and a former senior official of the Communist Party who has been arrested during the Great Purge. In prison, through interrogation by his former comrades Ivanov and Gletkin, and through his own reflections, Rubashov is brought to confess publicly to crimes he did not commit, accepting that the Party's logic — that the end justifies the means and that the individual must be sacrificed to history — demands his own destruction. He is executed after his trial.

Background and reception

Koestler, a former Communist Party member who had broken with communism after the Moscow Trials, wrote the novel in German while living in France; the original German manuscript was lost, and the English translation by Daphne Hardy became the standard text (a rediscovered German typescript was published in 2015). The novel was a bestseller, won wide acclaim, and exposed the logic of the show trials to Western readers during the Cold War.
