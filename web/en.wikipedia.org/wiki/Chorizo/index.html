Chorizo (Spanish: [tʃoˈɾiθo]; Portuguese: chouriço) is a type of pork sausage originating from the Iberian Peninsula, traditionally seasoned with smoked paprika, which gives it its characteristic deep red colour and smoky flavour. In Europe, chorizo is a fermented, cured and often smoked sausage eaten sliced without cooking; in Mexico and other parts of Latin America, "chorizo" usually denotes a fresh, raw ground sausage that must be cooked.

History

Cured sausages have been made in Iberia since Roman times, but chorizo's defining ingredient — paprika, made from New World capsicum peppers — only arrived after the Columbian exchange in the 16th century. Monasteries in Extremadura, notably around La Vera, pioneered drying and smoking the peppers into pimentón, and the region remains famous for its smoked paprika, which both flavours the sausage and acts as a preservative.

Spanish chorizo

Spanish chorizo is made from coarsely chopped pork and pork fat seasoned with pimentón (sweet, bittersweet or hot), garlic and salt, stuffed into casings, and cured for weeks to months. It ranges from soft fresh chorizo fresco for cooking to hard, sliceable curado. Renowned regional varieties include chorizo de Pamplona (finely ground), chorizo de La Rioja, chorizo de Cantimpalos, and chorizo ibérico made from acorn-fed Iberian pigs. It is eaten as a tapa, in sandwiches (bocadillo de chorizo), and cooked in stews, lentils, and dishes such as huevos rotos and Asturian fabada-adjacent bean pots.

Portuguese chouriço

Portuguese chouriço is similar but often includes wine and is smoked more heavily; varieties include chouriço de sangue (blood sausage) and the flour-based farinheira. A classic presentation is chouriço assado, flamed over alcohol in a clay dish. It is essential to caldo verde soup.

Mexican and Latin American chorizo

Mexican chorizo is a fresh sausage of ground pork (or beef, or soy-based "soyrizo"), seasoned with dried chiles (such as guajillo and ancho), vinegar, garlic and spices, sold in casings or bulk and cooked crumbled out of its casing. It is a staple of tacos, huevos con chorizo, choriqueso (queso fundido with chorizo), and Toluca is especially famous for it, including a green chorizo coloured with tomatillo and herbs. In Argentina and Uruguay, chorizo criollo is a milder fresh sausage grilled for asados and served in the choripán sandwich. In the Philippines, related longganisa sausages reflect Spanish influence.
