KM3NeT

NameCubic Kilometre Neutrino Telescope
TypeNeutrino telescope (Cherenkov detector)
LocationsARCA: off Portopalo di Capo Passero, Sicily (3,500 m depth); ORCA: off Toulon, France (2,450 m depth)
OrganisationKM3NeT Collaboration (international)
StatusUnder construction; taking data

The Cubic Kilometre Neutrino Telescope, or KM3NeT, is a European research infrastructure under construction at the bottom of the Mediterranean Sea. It consists of two deep-sea neutrino detectors composed of thousands of optical sensors suspended on vertical lines anchored to the seabed, which detect the faint Cherenkov light produced when high-energy neutrinos interact in the seawater and rock below.

Detectors

ARCA (Astroparticle Research with Cosmics in the Abyss), located about 80 km off the coast of Sicily at a depth of 3,500 m, is designed to search for high-energy cosmic neutrinos from astrophysical sources such as supernova remnants, gamma-ray bursts and colliding stars. ORCA (Oscillation Research with Cosmics in the Abyss), off Toulon, France, at 2,450 m depth, is a denser array optimised for atmospheric neutrinos and the determination of the neutrino mass ordering.

The basic sensing element is the digital optical module (DOM): a glass sphere containing 31 photomultiplier tubes, which gives nearly uniform coverage and directional information. Strings of DOMs are deployed from the seabed with the help of a spherical launcher vehicle that unfurls them in the water column.

Results

In February 2025, the collaboration reported in Nature the detection of KM3-230213A, an ultra-high-energy neutrino event of an estimated 220 PeV — by far the most energetic neutrino ever observed — recorded in 2023 by the partially completed ARCA detector. The KM3NeT infrastructure also hosts instruments for earth and sea sciences, including seismometers and hydrophones studying whale sounds and seismic activity.
