Hygiene

Hygiene is a set of practices performed to preserve health. According to the World Health Organization (WHO), "Hygiene refers to conditions and practices that help to maintain health and prevent the spread of diseases." Personal hygiene includes bathing, hand washing, dental care and grooming, while environmental and food hygiene address sanitation, safe water and the prevention of contamination.

History

Ritual washing appears in many ancient cultures, and Roman baths made communal bathing a civic institution. In the 19th century, Ignaz Semmelweis demonstrated that hand disinfection dramatically reduced puerperal fever in maternity wards, and Florence Nightingale and John Snow advanced sanitation and clean water as public-health measures. Germ theory, developed by Pasteur and Lister, gave hygiene a scientific basis.

Hand hygiene

Hand washing with soap is widely regarded as one of the most cost-effective public-health interventions. Health-care guidelines also promote alcohol-based hand rubs, and the WHO's "Five Moments for Hand Hygiene" framework is used worldwide.

Other forms

Oral hygiene—brushing and flossing—prevents dental caries and periodontal disease. Food hygiene practices such as cooking to safe temperatures and avoiding cross-contamination prevent foodborne illness. Sleep hygiene and menstrual hygiene are specialized usages of the term. The "hygiene hypothesis" proposes that reduced early-life exposure to microbes may contribute to allergies and autoimmune disease.
