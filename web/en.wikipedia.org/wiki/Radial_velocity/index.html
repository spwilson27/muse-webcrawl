Radial velocity

The radial velocity of an object with respect to a given point is the rate of change of the distance between the object and the point, i.e. the component of velocity along the line of sight. In astronomy it is measured from the Doppler shift of spectral lines.

Measurement

Spectrographs compare observed wavelengths with laboratory values; redshift indicates recession, blueshift approach. Precise radial-velocity measurements (to ~1 m/s and below) are a principal method for discovering exoplanets, revealing the wobble induced in a host star. The method also maps galactic rotation and the expansion of the universe.
