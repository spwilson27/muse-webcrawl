Freedom of thought

Freedom of thought is the freedom of an individual to hold or consider a fact, viewpoint or thought, independent of anyone's viewpoint. It is closely related to, but distinct from, freedom of expression, freedom of speech, freedom of religion and freedom of conscience.

Legal protection

Article 18 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights protects freedom of thought, conscience and religion. The internal forum — the freedom to hold beliefs — is absolute under international human rights law and may not be limited, unlike manifestations of belief which may be restricted on narrow grounds. Article 9 of the European Convention on Human Rights provides similar protection.

Rationale and scope

Freedom of thought is regarded as a precondition for other freedoms and for intellectual and moral development. Contemporary debates concern coercion of belief, compelled speech, indoctrination, and emerging neurotechnologies that could detect or influence mental states, giving rise to discussion of "cognitive liberty" and neurorights.

History

The concept developed through struggles against religious persecution, thinkers such as John Milton, John Locke and John Stuart Mill, and constitutional protections in modern liberal democracies.
