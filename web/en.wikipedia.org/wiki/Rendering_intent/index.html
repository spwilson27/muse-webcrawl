Rendering intent

In color management, a rendering intent is the method by which colors that fall outside the gamut of a destination device (out-of-gamut colors) are mapped into the gamut that the device can reproduce. The International Color Consortium (ICC) specifies four rendering intents.

The four intents

  • Perceptual — compresses or expands the whole gamut so that relative relationships between colors are preserved, at the cost of absolute accuracy. Preferred for photographs.
  • Relative colorimetric — maps the source white point to the destination white point and clips out-of-gamut colors to the nearest reproducible color; in-gamut colors are reproduced exactly.
  • Absolute colorimetric — like relative colorimetric but the white point is not adapted, so the source medium's white is simulated (used in proofing).
  • Saturation — maps saturated colors to saturated colors, preserving vividness rather than hue accuracy; suited to business graphics.

Use

Rendering intents are stored in ICC profiles and chosen by applications when converting between color spaces, for instance from an RGB working space to a CMYK printer profile. The choice trades off colorimetric accuracy against smooth gradations and detail in saturated regions.
