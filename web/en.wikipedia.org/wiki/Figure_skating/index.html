Figure skating

Figure skating is a sport in which individuals, pairs, or groups perform spins, jumps, footwork, and other intricate moves on ice skates. It was the first winter sport to be included in the Olympic Games, with competitions held at the 1908 London Olympics (as part of the Summer Games) and again in 1920 before becoming a fixture of the Winter Olympics from 1924 onward. The governing body is the International Skating Union (ISU), founded in 1892.

Disciplines

  • Men's and women's singles — individual skaters perform jumps, spins, step sequences and choreographic elements.
  • Pair skating — a man and a woman perform together, including overhead lifts, throw jumps, and side-by-side elements.
  • Ice dance — pairs perform ballroom-style dancing on ice, emphasising rhythm, footwork and lifts below the shoulder in its traditional form; solo ice dance has also developed.
  • Synchronized skating — teams of 8–20 skaters perform formations in unison; not currently an Olympic discipline.

Elements

Jumps are divided into toe jumps (toe loop, flip, Lutz) and edge jumps (Salchow, loop, Axel). The Axel, which takes off from a forward edge and requires an extra half-rotation, is the hardest; the quadruple Axel was landed in competition by Ilia Malinin in 2022. Spins include the upright, sit, camel, layback, and Biellmann positions. Pairs and dance feature lifts, twist lifts, death spirals, and step sequences graded by level.

Judging

Since 2004, following the judging scandal at the 2002 Winter Olympics, the sport has used the ISU Judging System (IJS). Each element receives a base value plus a grade of execution (−5 to +5); a technical panel identifies the elements and their levels, while judges also award program component scores for skating skills, transitions, performance, composition, and interpretation of the music. Singles and pairs events consist of a short program and a free skate; ice dance consists of a rhythm dance and a free dance.

History

Skating on ice dates back centuries in northern Europe; the first skating club was founded in Edinburgh in the 1740s. American skater Jackson Haines developed the modern artistic style in the 1860s, adding dance and ballet movements. The first World Championships were held in 1896 in Saint Petersburg. Compulsory figures — tracing specific patterns on the ice — were a major part of competition until their abolition after 1990, and they give the sport its name.

Legendary champions include Ulrich Salchow, Sonja Henie, Dick Button, Katarina Witt, Brian Boitano, Michelle Kwan, Evgeni Plushenko, Yuna Kim, and Yuzuru Hanyu, the first man to win consecutive Olympic singles titles in 66 years (2014, 2018).

Equipment and rink

Figure skates have a toe pick at the front of the blade used for jumping. Competitions take place on a rink nominally 60 m by 30 m. Elite skaters train many hours daily in skating skills, off-ice conditioning, and choreography.
