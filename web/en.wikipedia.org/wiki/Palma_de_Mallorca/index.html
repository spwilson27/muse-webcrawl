Palma (Catalan: [ˈpalmə]), often known in English as Palma de Mallorca, is the capital and largest city of the autonomous community of the Balearic Islands in Spain. It is situated on the south coast of Mallorca on the Bay of Palma, and about half of the island's population lives in the city. Palma is a major Mediterranean port and one of Europe's most popular tourist destinations, served by Palma de Mallorca Airport, among the busiest in Spain.

History

Palma was founded as a Roman camp, Palma, in 123 BC on the site of earlier Talaiotic settlement after the Roman conquest of the island. After the fall of Rome it passed through Byzantine and then Islamic hands; as Madina Mayurqa it became a prosperous port of the Caliphate of Córdoba and later of a taifa kingdom. James I of Aragon conquered the city on 31 December 1229, and it became the capital of the Kingdom of Mallorca. Its medieval prosperity as a trading port is recorded in the great Gothic Lonja (Sa Llotja) and in the cartography of the Majorcan school.

The city later declined under corsair attacks and plague, reviving in the modern era through trade and, from the mid-20th century, mass tourism, which transformed the bay with resorts and marinas.

Main sights

The city's landmark is Palma Cathedral (La Seu), a vast Catalan Gothic cathedral built from 1229 on the site of a mosque, with interior features by Antoni Gaudí and a great rose window. Above the harbour stands the circular Bellver Castle (14th century), one of the few round castles in Europe, now the city's history museum. Other sights include the Almudaina Palace, the Arab Baths, the Lonja, the old town's patios, the Es Baluard museum of modern art, and the waterfront promenade of the Paseo Marítimo.

Economy and transport

Tourism dominates the economy, alongside port services, yachting and construction. Palma's airport handles tens of millions of passengers annually, and ferries connect the port with mainland Spain and the other Balearic islands. The city is also a notable winter base for the Mediterranean superyacht industry.
