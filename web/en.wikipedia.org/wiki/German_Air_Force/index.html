The German Air Force (German: Luftwaffe, lit. "air weapon") is the aerial warfare branch of the Bundeswehr, the armed forces of the Federal Republic of Germany. It was founded in 1956 during the Cold War as the air force of West Germany; on 3 October 1990 it integrated parts of the former East German Air Forces of the National People's Army.

Roles and tasks

The Luftwaffe's tasks include air defence of German and NATO airspace (including NATO Quick Reaction Alert duties), offensive and reconnaissance air operations, air transport and air-to-air refuelling, and contributions to international crisis management. Germany participates in NATO's nuclear sharing arrangement, with Tornado aircraft at Büchel Air Base assigned the delivery role.

Equipment

The backbone of the combat fleet is the Eurofighter Typhoon, complemented by the ageing Panavia Tornado in strike, reconnaissance and electronic-warfare versions. Germany has ordered F-35A fighters to replace the Tornado in the nuclear role and as part of fleet renewal. Transport is provided by the Airbus A400M and a multinational tanker fleet; helicopters include the CH-53 heavy-lift fleet, being replaced by the CH-47F.

History

The name Luftwaffe was also borne by the air force of Nazi Germany (1935–1945); the modern service stresses its break with that past and its identity as the air force of a democratic state within NATO. The famous World War I Imperial German Air Service (Luftstreitkräfte) is a separate predecessor.
