UPS Airlines

UPS Airlines is a major American cargo airline based in Louisville, Kentucky. One of the largest cargo airlines worldwide in terms of freight volume flown, UPS Airlines flies to more than 800 destinations worldwide. A wholly owned subsidiary of United Parcel Service (UPS) since its launch in 1988, UPS Airlines marked its 35th anniversary of operations in 2023.

History

UPS began air service in 1929 but for most of the twentieth century contracted its flying to other carriers. In 1988, seeking direct control over its air network, UPS obtained its own operating certificate from the Federal Aviation Administration and launched UPS Airlines, becoming one of the fastest-growing airlines in American history. The airline's principal hub, Worldport at Louisville International Airport, is among the largest automated package-handling facilities in the world.

Fleet

The fleet has included the Boeing 747-400F and 747-8F, Boeing 767-300F, McDonnell Douglas MD-11F, Airbus A300-600F, and Boeing 757-200F. UPS was the launch customer for the freighter conversion of several types and one of the largest operators of the 747-8F.

Accidents

UPS Airlines Flight 6, a Boeing 747-400F, crashed near Dubai on 3 September 2010 following an in-flight fire in the cargo hold, killing both crew members; the accident led to major changes in lithium-battery air transport rules. UPS Airlines Flight 1354, an Airbus A300, crashed on approach to Birmingham, Alabama, on 14 August 2013, killing both pilots.
