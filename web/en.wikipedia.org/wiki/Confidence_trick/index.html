A confidence trick, confidence game, or con is an attempt to defraud a person or group after first gaining their trust. Confidence tricks exploit characteristics of the human psyche, such as credulity, naïveté, compassion, vanity, confidence, irresponsibility, and greed. Researchers have defined confidence tricks as "a distinctive species of fraudulent conduct … intending to further voluntary exchanges that are not mutually beneficial", as they "benefit con operators ('con men') at the expense of their victims (the 'marks')".

Stages

Classic analyses, notably David Maurer's study of the big con, describe stages: the foundation work (preparing the scheme and victim), the approach, the build-up (giving the mark an opportunity to profit from dishonest behaviour, making them an accomplice), the pay-off or convincer (a small reward to fuel confidence), the hurrah (a sudden manufactured crisis or opportunity forcing immediate action), and the in-and-in (bringing in a co-conspirator).

Notable cons

  • The Spanish Prisoner, one of the oldest cons, claiming a wealthy prisoner needs money for release
  • The pigeon drop, involving a found sum of money to be shared
  • Advance-fee fraud, the "419" scam and its modern email descendants
  • The Salting and big store cons of the early twentieth century
  • Romance scams and pig butchering investment scams online

In legal systems, confidence tricks may be prosecuted as fraud, obtaining property by deception, or wire fraud.
