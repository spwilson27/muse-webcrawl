Infimum and supremum

In mathematics, the infimum (abbreviated inf) of a subset of a partially ordered set is its greatest lower bound: the largest element that is less than or equal to every element of the subset, if such an element exists. Dually, the supremum (abbreviated sup) is the least upper bound, the smallest element that is greater than or equal to every element of the subset.

Definitions

An element c is a lower bound of a set S if c ≤ s for every s in S. The infimum of S is the greatest such lower bound. Similarly, an upper bound of S is an element greater than or equal to every element of S, and the supremum is the least such upper bound. Unlike the minimum and maximum, the infimum and supremum of a set need not belong to the set itself; for example, the infimum of the open interval (0, 1) is 0 and its supremum is 1, even though neither endpoint lies in the interval.

Properties

The completeness of the real numbers guarantees that every non-empty subset of ℝ that is bounded above has a supremum in ℝ, and every non-empty subset bounded below has an infimum. This least-upper-bound property is a foundation of real analysis and underlies results such as the monotone convergence theorem. If a set has a maximum, that maximum equals its supremum; if it has a minimum, that minimum equals its infimum.

Examples

The supremum of {1, 2, 3} is 3. The infimum of the set of positive real numbers {x ∈ ℝ : x > 0} is 0. For the rationals {x ∈ ℚ : x² < 2}, the supremum √2 does not exist within ℚ, illustrating that the rationals are not complete.
