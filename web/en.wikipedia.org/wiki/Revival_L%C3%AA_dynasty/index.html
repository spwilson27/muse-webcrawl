Revival Lê dynasty

The Revival Lê dynasty (Vietnamese: Nhà Lê trung hưng), conventionally dated 1533–1789, was the second period of rule of the Vietnamese Lê dynasty. It began when Lê Trang Tông was crowned in exile in Laos in opposition to the usurping Mạc dynasty, and continued after the Lê were restored to the capital Đông Kinh (Hanoi) in 1592 following the Lê–Mạc War.

Trịnh–Nguyễn division

Although the Lê emperors remained the nominal sovereigns, real power in the north passed to the Trịnh lords, who ruled as de facto regents from 1545 onward, while the Nguyễn lords governed the south. The rivalry between the Trịnh and Nguyễn families produced the Trịnh–Nguyễn Civil War (1627–1672), after which the country remained divided along the Gianh River for roughly a century.

Decline and fall

By the eighteenth century both halves of Vietnam suffered corruption, famine, and popular unrest. The Tây Sơn rebellion, beginning in 1771, overthrew the Nguyễn, then defeated the Trịnh in 1786, and finally destroyed the last Lê emperor, Lê Chiêu Thống, who fled to Qing China in 1788–1789, ending the dynasty.
