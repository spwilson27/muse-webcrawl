Oenology

Oenology is the science of wine and winemaking, from fermentation to bottling.

Oenology (also spelled enology) is the science and study of wine and winemaking. A specialist in the field is an oenologist. The word derives from the Greek oinos, "wine". Oenology is distinct from viticulture, which concerns the cultivation of grapes, although the two disciplines overlap in practice.

Scope

Oenology covers the chemistry and microbiology of fermentation, in which yeasts convert grape sugars into alcohol; the management of malolactic fermentation by lactic acid bacteria; the use of sulphur dioxide and other additives; clarification and stabilisation; ageing in barrel or tank; blending; and bottling. An oenologist monitors must and wine through laboratory analysis — sugar, acidity, pH, volatile compounds — and through sensory evaluation.

History

Winemaking is ancient, but oenology as a science dates from the 19th century, when Louis Pasteur demonstrated that fermentation is caused by microorganisms and identified the bacteria responsible for wine spoilage. Modern oenology took shape in the 20th century with temperature-controlled fermentation, cultured yeast strains, and stainless steel equipment.

Education

Formal training is offered at universities in wine regions worldwide, including the University of Bordeaux, the University of Adelaide, and the University of California, Davis, whose Department of Viticulture and Enology is a leading research centre.
