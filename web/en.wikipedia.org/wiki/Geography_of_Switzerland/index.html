Geography of Switzerland

Switzerland's three geographic regions

Area: 41,285 km²

Highest point: Dufourspitze (Monte Rosa), 4,634 m

Largest lake: Lake Geneva

Switzerland is a landlocked country in Central Europe covering 41,285 square kilometres, bordered by Italy to the south, France to the west, Germany to the north, and Austria and Liechtenstein to the east. It sits at the heart of the European watershed, where the Rhine, Rhône, Po and Danube river systems originate.

Three regions

The country divides into three geographical regions. The Swiss Alps cover about 60% of the territory in the south and east, containing most of Europe's highest peaks, including the Dufourspitze (4,634 m) in the Monte Rosa massif and the Matterhorn. The Swiss Plateau (Mittelland), a band of rolling hills between the Alps and the Jura, holds most of the population and the largest cities — Zurich, Bern, Basel, Lausanne and Geneva. The Jura Mountains form a limestone range along the northwestern border with France.

Lakes and rivers

Major lakes include Lake Geneva (shared with France), Lake Constance, Lake Neuchâtel (the largest entirely in Switzerland), Lake Zurich and Lake Lucerne. The Rhine rises in the canton of Graubünden and exits at Basel; the Rhône flows west into Lake Geneva and on to France.

Climate

Climate varies sharply with altitude, from temperate conditions on the plateau to Alpine conditions in the mountains; the southern canton of Ticino has a milder, almost Mediterranean climate influenced by warm foehn winds.
