Freestyle wrestling

A freestyle wrestling match.

Freestyle wrestling is a style of amateur wrestling practised worldwide. Together with Greco-Roman wrestling, it is one of the two styles contested at the Olympic Games. In freestyle, wrestlers may use their legs in offence and defence and may hold opponents above or below the waist, distinguishing it from Greco-Roman wrestling, where holds below the waist are forbidden.

Scoring

Points are awarded for takedowns, reversals, exposures (turning an opponent's shoulders toward the mat), and push-outs. A fall (pin) ends the match immediately. Bouts consist of two three-minute periods. Under rules administered by United World Wrestling, a wrestler can also win by technical superiority with a ten-point lead.

History

Freestyle developed from catch-as-catch-can wrestling popular in Britain and the United States in the 19th century. It has been an Olympic sport since 1904 for men, and women's freestyle was added at the 2004 Athens Games. Strong national programmes include those of Iran, Russia, the United States, Japan, and Azerbaijan.
