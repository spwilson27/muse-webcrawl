Far North Queensland

Far North Queensland (FNQ) is the northernmost part of the Australian state of Queensland. It is a region of tropical rainforests, coral reefs and agricultural land, centred on the city of Cairns. The region is one of the oldest continuously inhabited parts of Australia, with Aboriginal peoples living there for tens of thousands of years before European settlement.

Geography

The region stretches from around Innisfail in the south to the tip of Cape York Peninsula in the north, and west to the Gulf of Carpentaria. It includes the Wet Tropics of Queensland World Heritage Area, home to the Daintree Rainforest, one of the oldest surviving rainforests on Earth, and provides access to the northern sections of the Great Barrier Reef.

Economy

Tourism is the dominant industry, drawing visitors to Cairns, Port Douglas and the reef. Agriculture is also significant, including sugarcane, bananas, mangoes and coffee. The Atherton Tableland inland supports dairy farming and horticulture.

Climate

Far North Queensland has a tropical climate with a wet season from roughly November to April, during which monsoonal rain and tropical cyclones can occur, and a drier, milder season for the remainder of the year.
