Minkowski–Bouligand dimension

In fractal geometry, the Minkowski–Bouligand dimension, also known as the Minkowski dimension or box-counting dimension, is a way of determining the fractal dimension of a set S in a Euclidean space Rn, or more generally in a metric space. It is named after Hermann Minkowski and Georges Bouligand.

Definition

To compute the dimension of a fractal S, cover it with a grid of cells of side length ε and count the number N(ε) of cells that contain part of S. If N(ε) grows like ε−D as ε → 0, then D is the box-counting dimension: D = lim (log N(ε) / log(1/ε)), when the limit exists. When the upper and lower limits differ, one speaks of the upper and lower box dimensions. Intuitively, the dimension measures how the apparent complexity of the set scales with resolution.

Properties and use

The box-counting dimension is easy to estimate numerically and is widely used in physics, image analysis, geology, and medicine to characterise irregular shapes such as coastlines, clouds, and tumour boundaries. It agrees with the Hausdorff dimension for many regular fractals, such as the Cantor set and the Koch snowflake, but can exceed it for some sets; it also assigns the ambient dimension to dense countable sets, which is one reason it is considered less refined than the Hausdorff dimension.
