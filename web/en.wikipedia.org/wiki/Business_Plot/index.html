Business Plot

The Business Plot, also called the Wall Street Putsch and the White House Putsch, was a political conspiracy in 1933 in the United States in which wealthy businessmen plotted a coup d'état to overthrow President Franklin D. Roosevelt and install a fascist regime, with retired Marine Corps Major General Smedley Butler as its figurehead dictator. Butler testified to the United States House Committee on Un-American Activities (the McCormack–Dickstein Committee) that a group of conspirators, led by Wall Street bond salesman Gerald C. MacGuire, had approached him in July 1933 to lead a privately funded army of veterans in a march on Washington.

Background

The plot was conceived against the backdrop of the Great Depression and business opposition to Roosevelt's New Deal. In 1932, disgruntled veterans of the Bonus Army had marched on Washington and been dispersed by the Army. Butler, the most decorated Marine of his era and a popular figure among veterans, was approached because of his influence with them and his public break with big business after his retirement.

Butler's testimony

In November 1934, Butler testified under oath before the McCormack–Dickstein Committee. He alleged that MacGuire, backed by financiers associated with J.P. Morgan and the DuPont family, offered financing from the newly founded American Liberty League circle to raise an army of 500,000 veterans, modeled on European fascist movements such as the French Croix-de-Feu, to seize the capital and install Butler as "Secretary of General Affairs".

Investigation and outcome

The committee verified parts of Butler's story, including the existence of MacGuire and his correspondence, and stated in its final report that it had received evidence of certain persons attempting to establish a fascist organization in the United States. No prosecutions followed, and the names of the alleged financiers were partly redacted from the public version of the testimony. Historians remain divided over how far the planning progressed, but most accept that a genuine proposal was made to Butler.
