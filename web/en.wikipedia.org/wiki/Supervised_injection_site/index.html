A supervised injection site (also called a supervised consumption site, safe injection facility or overdose prevention centre) is a legally sanctioned facility where people can consume pre-obtained drugs — most commonly by injection — under the supervision of trained staff. The sites provide sterile equipment, a hygienic environment, immediate intervention (including naloxone) in case of overdose, and referrals to health, addiction treatment and social services. They are a prominent harm-reduction intervention.

History

The first legally sanctioned supervised consumption facility opened in Berne, Switzerland, in 1986. The model spread to Germany, the Netherlands, Spain, Norway, Luxembourg, Denmark, France and Australia (Sydney's Medically Supervised Injecting Centre opened in 2001). In 2003, Insite opened in Vancouver's Downtown Eastside as North America's first sanctioned site; its survival was upheld by the Supreme Court of Canada in 2011. Canada subsequently authorised dozens of sites amid the opioid crisis. In the United States, the first officially recognised overdose prevention centres opened in New York City in 2021, and Rhode Island authorised a state-regulated site.

Evidence and debate

Evaluations of Insite and the Sydney facility found reductions in public injecting and discarded syringes, no recorded overdose deaths inside the facilities, increased entry into detox and treatment, and — in Vancouver studies — reductions in overdose mortality in the surrounding area and no increase in local crime. Critics argue the sites enable drug use, attract disorder, and conflict with drug prohibition law; supporters respond that they save lives and connect marginalised users to care. Evidence reviews by public-health bodies in Europe and Australia have generally been favourable, though research on some outcomes remains contested.
