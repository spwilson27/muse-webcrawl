Todd-AO

Todd-AO is an American post-production company and a widescreen film format developed in the early 1950s by Mike Todd and American Optical Company. Using 65 mm film photographed at 30 frames per second originally, then 24 fps, it produced a large, high-resolution image projected on a curved screen.

The first Todd-AO film was Oklahoma! (1955), followed by Around the World in 80 Days (1956), South Pacific, The Alamo and The Sound of Music. The format competed with Cinerama while using a single projector. Roadshow presentations were common for Todd-AO releases.
