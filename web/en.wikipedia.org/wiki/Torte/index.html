TypeCake
CourseDessert
Place of originCentral Europe (Austria, Hungary, Germany)
Main ingredientsGround nuts or breadcrumbs, eggs, sugar, chocolate or fruit

A torte is a rich, usually multilayered cake that is filled with whipped cream, buttercreams, mousses, jams, or fruits. Ordinarily, the cooled torte is glazed and garnished. Tortes are commonly baked in a springform pan. Unlike a regular cake, which is made with flour, a torte calls for ground nuts or breadcrumbs in its place, together with eggs, sugar, and flavourings, producing a denser, moister crumb.

The word derives from the Italian torta, meaning a round cake or bread, itself from Latin torta, a twisted or round loaf. Tortes are strongly associated with the café culture of Austria, Hungary, and Germany.

Well-known tortes

Among the most famous examples are the Austrian Sachertorte, a chocolate torte with apricot jam created in Vienna in 1832; the Hungarian Dobos torte, layered sponge cake with chocolate buttercream and a caramel top; the Linzertorte, a nut pastry with a lattice top and redcurrant jam; the German Schwarzwälder Kirschtorte (Black Forest gateau), made with chocolate, cream, cherries and kirsch; and the Esterházy torta, a layered hazelnut or almond meringue cake.

Characteristics

Because tortes contain little or no flour, their texture is often denser and richer than that of a sponge cake. Layers may be soaked with syrup or liqueur and assembled with fillings such as ganache, mousse, or preserved fruit. Many classic tortes were created in the nineteenth century by court confectioners in Vienna and Budapest.
