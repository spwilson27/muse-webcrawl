Alfred Lion (born Alfred Löw; 21 April 1908 – 2 February 1987) was a German-born American record producer, the founder of the jazz label Blue Note Records in 1939.

Early life

Lion was born in Berlin-Schöneberg and fell in love with jazz after seeing Sam Wooding's orchestra perform in Berlin in 1925. He emigrated to South America and later, after the Nazi rise to power, settled in New York in 1937, determined to record the music he loved.

Blue Note Records

On 6 January 1939 Lion recorded boogie-woogie pianists Albert Ammons and Meade Lux Lewis, launching Blue Note. With childhood friend and photographer Francis Wolff, who joined the company after fleeing Germany, Lion built the label into the definitive home of modern jazz. Blue Note recorded Thelonious Monk, Bud Powell, Art Blakey, Horace Silver, John Coltrane, Miles Davis, Sonny Rollins and many others.

Lion's production philosophy was to give musicians freedom, pay for rehearsal time — an unusual generosity — and encourage original compositions. Working with engineer Rudy Van Gelder and designer Reid Miles, Blue Note developed a distinctive sonic and visual identity that defined the look and sound of 1950s and 1960s jazz.

Later years

Blue Note was sold to Liberty Records in 1965; Lion retired in 1967 as musical fashions changed. He lived to see the label revived in the 1980s and died in New York in 1987. In 2023 he entered the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame's orbit of honourees through special recognition of Blue Note's founders.
