Roc (mythology)

The roc is an enormous legendary bird of prey in Middle Eastern mythology, popularised in the One Thousand and One Nights and the writings of Marco Polo. It was said to be so large that it could carry off and eat elephants, and its wingspan was said to blot out the sun.

In literature

The roc appears in the tales of Sinbad the Sailor: in his second voyage Sinbad is carried to the roc's nest valley of diamonds, and in the fifth voyage the bird destroys his ship in revenge for the breaking of its egg. Marco Polo described the "rukh" of Madagascar, reporting that the Great Khan had been presented with a feather said to be ninety spans long. Similar giant birds feature in Persian and Arabic folklore, and the roc's legend may have been influenced by sightings of the extinct elephant bird of Madagascar.
