Big Week was a sequence of Allied bombing raids during the Second World War, conducted from 20 to 25 February 1944. Officially known as Operation Argument, it formed part of the Combined Bomber Offensive and the Pointblank directive, and aimed to destroy the German aircraft industry and lure the Luftwaffe into attritional air battles in preparation for the Normandy landings.

U.S. Eighth Air Force bombers during Operation Argument.

Background

By early 1944 the Allied air commanders recognized that the invasion of France could not proceed without air superiority. The Pointblank directive prioritized the German fighter force and its supporting industry. Long-range escort fighters, above all the P-51 Mustang, now allowed American daylight bombers to be accompanied deep into Germany, changing the character of the air war.

The raids

Beginning on 20 February, the Eighth Air Force dispatched more than 1,000 heavy bombers against aircraft plants and component factories at Leipzig, Brunswick, Gotha, Regensburg, Schweinfurt, Augsburg, Stuttgart, and Steyr, among other targets. The Fifteenth Air Force, flying from Italy, joined the effort, while RAF Bomber Command attacked many of the same cities by night. Over six days the Allies flew thousands of sorties and dropped roughly 10,000 tons of bombs.

Results

German aircraft production was disrupted and dispersed, but not halted; under Albert Speer's ministry, output of fighters actually rose in subsequent months through dispersal and rationalization. The decisive effect was on the Luftwaffe's pilot force: forced to rise and defend the factories, German fighters suffered heavy losses — several hundred aircraft and, more importantly, experienced pilots who could not be replaced at the same rate. The United States lost more than 200 heavy bombers and dozens of fighters during the week, but American production and training pipelines could absorb the losses.

Big Week is generally regarded as a turning point in the daylight air war: from the spring of 1944 the Luftwaffe increasingly lost the ability to contest Allied operations over Western Europe.
