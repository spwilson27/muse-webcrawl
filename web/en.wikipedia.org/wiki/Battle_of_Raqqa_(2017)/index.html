The Battle of Raqqa was the fifth and final phase of the Raqqa campaign (2016–2017), launched by the Syrian Democratic Forces (SDF) against the Islamic State (IS) with the support of the United States-led Combined Joint Task Force. The battle began on 6 June 2017 and concluded on 20 October with the SDF taking full control of the city. Raqqa had been proclaimed the capital of the Islamic State's self-declared caliphate in 2014, so its fall was a major symbolic and strategic blow to the group.

Background

The wider Raqqa campaign began in November 2016, as SDF forces advanced through the countryside north, east and west of the city, capturing the Tabqa Dam and the town of Tabqa in May 2017. By early June the SDF had encircled Raqqa and began its assault on the city itself.

The battle

SDF fighters advanced from the east, west and south against entrenched IS defenders who used tunnels, snipers, car bombs and improvised explosive devices. Coalition airstrikes and artillery heavily damaged the city. Fighting was particularly intense around the old city walls and the national hospital. In mid-October a deal allowed remaining IS fighters and civilians to evacuate; the SDF announced the city's complete capture on 17 October and declared victory on 20 October 2017.

Aftermath

Much of Raqqa was left in ruins; monitoring groups reported large numbers of civilian deaths from airstrikes, artillery and mines. Reconstruction proceeded slowly amid unexploded ordnance. The loss of Raqqa, following the fall of Mosul in Iraq, reduced the Islamic State to scattered pockets of territory in the Euphrates valley.
