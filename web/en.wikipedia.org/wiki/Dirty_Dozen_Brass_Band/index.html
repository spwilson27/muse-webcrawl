Dirty Dozen Brass Band

OriginNew Orleans, Louisiana, US
Formed1977
GenresBrass band, funk, bebop, R&B
Notable albumMy Feet Can't Fail Me Now (1984)

The Dirty Dozen Brass Band is an American brass band from New Orleans, Louisiana, formed in 1977. The band revived the New Orleans brass band tradition by fusing it with bebop, funk, rhythm and blues and modern jazz, and is widely credited with sparking the contemporary brass band renaissance.

History

The group grew out of the Tornado Brass Band and the social scene around the Glass House in New Orleans. Its early weekly gigs built a local following, and the 1984 album My Feet Can't Fail Me Now brought national attention. The band went on to tour internationally and record for major labels.

Style and influence

Built on the traditional lineup of trumpets, trombones, saxophones, sousaphone and drums, the Dirty Dozen stretched the repertoire from funeral dirges and standards such as "St. James Infirmary" to arrangements of Charlie Parker, Thelonious Monk and Marvin Gaye. Their success paved the way for younger groups such as the Rebirth Brass Band, and members have collaborated with artists including Elvis Costello, Widespread Panic and Modest Mouse. The band also recorded the album Medicated Magic (2002) and appeared on recordings marking New Orleans' post-Katrina recovery.
