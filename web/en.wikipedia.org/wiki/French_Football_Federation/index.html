The French Football Federation (French: Fédération Française de Football, FFF) is the governing body of football in France. Founded in 1919, it is based in Paris and oversees the France national football teams, the Coupe de France, and amateur football throughout the country; professional league football is run by the Ligue de Football Professionnel under its authority.

History

The FFF was formed on 7 April 1919 from the unification of earlier bodies, notably the Comité Français Interfédéral led by Jules Rimet, who became its first president and later president of FIFA — he was instrumental in creating the FIFA World Cup, whose first trophy bore his name. France was a founding member of UEFA in 1954.

National teams and development

France's men's national team (Les Bleus) won the FIFA World Cup in 1998 and 2018 and the European Championship in 1984 and 2000; the women's team is among the world's leading sides. The federation is renowned for its national training centre at Clairefontaine (INF Clairefontaine), opened in 1988, which has produced generations of elite players. The FFF also organises the Coupe de France (founded 1917), one of the world's largest knockout competitions, open to thousands of amateur and professional clubs.

Organisation

The federation is composed of regional leagues and district committees, counts more than two million licensed players, and has been led in recent years by presidents including Noël Le Graët (2011–2023) and Philippe Diallo (from 2023).
