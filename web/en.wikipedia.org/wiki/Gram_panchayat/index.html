Gram panchayat

A gram panchayat (village council) is the basic unit of local self-government in rural India, forming the lowest tier of the Panchayati Raj system. It serves a village or a group of villages and is responsible for local administration and development.

Structure

A gram panchayat is headed by a president known as the sarpanch, elected directly by the village's adult voters, together with ward members (panches). The general body of all registered voters is the gram sabha, which approves budgets, selects beneficiaries for welfare schemes, and reviews the panchayat's work. Elections are held every five years and are conducted by State Election Commissions.

Functions

Gram panchayats maintain village infrastructure such as roads, drains, street lighting and drinking-water sources; implement rural development and poverty-alleviation programmes; maintain records of births and deaths; and support primary education, sanitation and public health. Their revenues come from state and central grants, assigned taxes, and fees.

Reservation

The 73rd Constitutional Amendment (1992) formalised the three-tier Panchayati Raj structure and mandated reservation of seats for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes and women — not less than one-third of seats and offices for women, a share several states have since raised to one-half.
