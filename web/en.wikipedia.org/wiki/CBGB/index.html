CBGB was a New York City music club opened in 1973 by Hilly Kristal in the East Village, Manhattan. The club is widely regarded as the birthplace of American punk rock and was the launching point for bands including the Ramones, Talking Heads, Blondie, Patti Smith, Television, and the Dead Boys.

The letters CBGB stood for Kristal's originally intended musical format — "Country, BlueGrass, and Blues" — while the second part of the full name, OMFUG, stood for "Other Music For Uplifting Gormandizers". The venue instead became famous for almost the opposite repertoire, helping to define the sound and aesthetic of punk and new wave music in the late 1970s.

History

Founding

Hilly Kristal opened CBGB on December 10, 1973, at 315 Bowery, beneath a flophouse, on the site of his earlier bar, Hilly's on the Bowery. Kristal imposed two rules on performers: bands had to move their own equipment, and they had to play mostly original songs — no cover-band material. The second rule proved decisive, pushing young musicians to write their own material and helping to create a genuinely new scene.

The punk era

In 1974, Television began playing regular Sunday shows at the club, followed by the Ramones, who made their CBGB debut in August 1974. Patti Smith, Talking Heads, Blondie, the Heartbreakers, the Dead Boys, and Richard Hell and the Voidoids all became fixtures. The club's gritty interior — a narrow room with a small stage, notoriously filthy bathrooms covered in graffiti and stickers — became an icon of the punk aesthetic. The Ramones' performances there directly inspired British musicians who went on to found the UK punk scene after seeing them on tour.

Later years and closure

Through the 1980s CBGB became central to the New York hardcore scene, hosting bands such as Agnostic Front, the Cro-Mags, Murphy's Law, and Gorilla Biscuits, whose Sunday matinees became legendary. In later years the club fought a protracted rent dispute with its landlord, the Bowery Residents' Committee. After a benefit campaign and legal battle, CBGB held its final concert on October 15, 2006, headlined by Patti Smith. The site later housed a John Varvatos boutique, which preserved some of the club's interior walls; the CBGB name subsequently lived on through a festival and a restaurant at Newark Liberty International Airport.

Legacy

CBGB was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame's exhibit of landmark venues, and its awning is held by the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in Cleveland. In 2013, the former club space at 315 Bowery was added to the National Register of Historic Places as part of the Bowery Historic District. The 2013 film CBGB, starring Alan Rickman as Kristal, dramatised the club's early years.
