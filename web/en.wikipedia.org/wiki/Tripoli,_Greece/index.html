Tripoli (Greek: Τρίπολη, Trípoli) is a city in the central part of the Peloponnese, Greece. It is the capital of the regional unit of Arcadia and the administrative seat of the Peloponnese region. The city stands on a high plateau at about 650 metres above sea level, ringed by mountains including Mount Mainalo, and is known for its cold winters by Greek standards.

History

The name Tripoli—"three cities"—is traditionally linked to the ancient Arcadian cities of Mantineia, Tegea, and Pallantion nearby. In the Ottoman period the town, called Tripolitsa, grew into the administrative capital of the Morea (Peloponnese) and seat of the pasha. During the Greek War of Independence, Tripolitsa was stormed by Greek forces under Theodoros Kolokotronis in September–October 1821, the first major city to fall, followed by a massacre of much of its Muslim and Jewish population—one of the war's notorious atrocities. Ibrahim Pasha of Egypt later retook and largely destroyed the town in 1825. Rebuilt in the nineteenth century, modern Tripoli was laid out with broad squares, notably Areos Square with its marble monuments.

The city today

Tripoli is a regional market and transport hub, connected by motorway to Athens and Kalamata, and hosts units of the University of the Peloponnese and Hellenic Air Force training facilities at its nearby air base. The Archaeological Museum of Tripoli, housed in a neoclassical former hospital, displays finds from ancient Arcadia. Local products include wine from the nearby Mantinia plateau, noted for its Moschofilero grapes.
