Sri Lankan Civil War

Sri Lankan Civil War Date: 23 July 1983 – 18 May 2009
Location: Sri Lanka
Belligerents: Government of Sri Lanka vs Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE)
Result: Sri Lankan government victory; LTTE defeated and its leadership killed
Casualties: estimated 80,000–100,000+ killed overall

The Sri Lankan Civil War was a civil war fought in Sri Lanka from 1983 to 2009. The government of Sri Lanka fought the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam (LTTE), a separatist militant organisation that sought an independent Tamil state, "Tamil Eelam", in the island's north and east.

The north and east of Sri Lanka, claimed by the LTTE as Tamil Eelam

Origins

Tensions between the Sinhalese majority and the Tamil minority grew after independence, through measures such as the Sinhala Only Act of 1956 and discriminatory university admission policies. The war is conventionally dated from "Black July" 1983, when anti-Tamil pogroms followed an LTTE ambush that killed 13 soldiers.

Course of the war

The war proceeded through phases (Eelam Wars I–IV) punctuated by an Indian peacekeeping intervention (1987–1990), assassinations — including of Indian premier Rajiv Gandhi (1991) and Sri Lankan president Ranasinghe Premadasa (1993) — and a Norwegian-brokered ceasefire in 2002 that collapsed by 2006. The LTTE pioneered suicide bombing through its Black Tiger unit and maintained a navy and rudimentary air force.

End and aftermath

A final government offensive from 2006 retook the east and then the north; LTTE leader Velupillai Prabhakaran was killed in May 2009 on the shore of Nanthikadal Lagoon. The final months brought heavy civilian casualties in the shrinking "no-fire zone" at Mullivaikkal, and allegations of war crimes by both sides remain politically contested. Tens of thousands of civilians died in the conflict and hundreds of thousands were displaced.
