Dunkin' Donuts, doing business as Dunkin' since 2019, is an American multinational coffee and doughnut company, as well as a quick-service restaurant chain. It was founded by William Rosenberg in Quincy, Massachusetts, in 1950 and is one of the largest coffee and doughnut shop chains in the world, with thousands of locations across dozens of countries.

History

Rosenberg began in food service with Industrial Luncheon Services and, in 1948, opened a doughnut shop called Open Kettle in Quincy. In 1950 the shop was renamed Dunkin' Donuts, offering coffee and a wide variety of doughnuts at five cents each. Franchising began in 1955, and the chain grew rapidly; its "Time to make the donuts" advertising campaign, featuring the character Fred the Baker, became a cultural fixture in the 1980s.

The company was acquired by Allied Domecq in 1990, later became part of Dunkin' Brands alongside Baskin-Robbins, and was taken public in 2011. In 2019 the chain officially shortened its name to Dunkin', reflecting the growing share of beverage sales. In December 2020, Dunkin' Brands was acquired by Inspire Brands for approximately $11.3 billion.

Products

The chain is best known for its doughnuts — historically sold in dozens of varieties — and for its coffee, including hot and iced coffee, cold brew, espresso drinks and frozen beverages. Menus also include bagels, muffins, breakfast sandwiches and the bite-sized Munchkins doughnut holes. Although doughnuts gave the company its name, beverages account for the majority of sales.

International presence

Dunkin' operates in more than 40 countries. The name Dunkin' Donuts is retained in some international markets, while the shortened Dunkin' branding has been adopted in the United States and increasingly elsewhere.
