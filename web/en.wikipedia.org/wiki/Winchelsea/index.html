Winchelsea is a small town in East Sussex, England, in the civil parish of Icklesham. It is one of the smallest towns in England and a notable example of a planned medieval town, refounded by Edward I on a grid pattern after the original settlement was destroyed by the sea.

History

Old Winchelsea stood on a shingle spit near the mouth of the River Rother and was an important member of the Cinque Ports confederation, trading especially in wine with Gascony. A great storm in 1287 destroyed the old town, and Edward I had a new Winchelsea laid out on higher ground inland, with a regular grid of streets, a large church (St Thomas the Martyr) and defensive gates, three of which survive.

Decline and present day

The harbour silted up in the later Middle Ages, French raids damaged the town, and Winchelsea dwindled into a quiet village-like settlement, though it retained a parliamentary borough until 1832 — one of the notorious rotten boroughs. Today it is a conservation area prized for its medieval cellars, church and tranquil streets, and it claims to be the smallest town in England with its own mayor.
