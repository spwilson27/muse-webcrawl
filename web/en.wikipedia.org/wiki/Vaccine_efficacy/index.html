Vaccine efficacy

Vaccine efficacy
Definition: Percentage reduction of disease incidence in a vaccinated group under controlled trial conditions
Formula: VE = (ARU − ARV) / ARU × 100%
Compared with: Vaccine effectiveness (real-world)

Vaccine efficacy is the percentage reduction of disease in a vaccinated group of people compared to an unvaccinated group, under the most favourable, controlled conditions — typically in a randomised clinical trial. It is distinct from vaccine effectiveness, which measures how well a vaccine performs in ordinary real-world use.

Calculation

Efficacy is calculated as:

VE = (ARU − ARV) / ARU × 100%

where ARU is the attack rate in unvaccinated people and ARV is the attack rate in vaccinated people. For example, if 10% of a placebo group but only 1% of a vaccinated group contract the disease, efficacy is 90% — a statement about relative risk, not a claim that 10% of vaccinated people will become ill.

Interpretation

Efficacy depends on the endpoint measured: a vaccine may show different efficacy against infection, against symptomatic disease, against severe disease and hospitalisation, and against death; efficacy against severe outcomes is usually highest. It may also vary by age group, by time since vaccination (waning), and by pathogen variant. Regulators such as the US Food and Drug Administration have generally required a point estimate of at least 50% efficacy for approval of new vaccines.

Examples

Two doses of measles vaccine are about 97% efficacious; seasonal influenza vaccines typically show 40–60% efficacy in well-matched years; the mRNA COVID-19 vaccines showed approximately 94–95% efficacy against symptomatic disease in their initial phase III trials, with lower effectiveness observed later against infection by newer variants but continued strong protection against severe disease. Measuring efficacy requires randomised, usually placebo-controlled trials with confirmed case endpoints, while effectiveness is estimated afterwards with observational designs such as test-negative studies.
