The Hudson's Bay Company (HBC; French: Compagnie de la Baie d'Hudson) was a Canadian department store chain and, for much of its history, the dominant fur-trading company in North America. Founded on 2 May 1670 by a royal charter from King Charles II of England, it is North America's oldest extant commercial corporation; at its height it exercised effective governmental control over the vast territory of Rupert's Land.

Fur trade era

French traders Médard Chouart des Groseilliers and Pierre-Esprit Radisson, rebuffed by New France, brought the scheme for a Hudson Bay trading company to London. The charter granted the "Governor and Company of Adventurers of England trading into Hudson's Bay" a monopoly of trade in all lands draining into Hudson Bay — some 3.9 million square kilometres. From bayside factories such as York Factory, Moose Factory and Fort Albany, the company traded European goods for beaver pelts supplied by Cree, Assiniboine and other Indigenous traders.

Rivalry with French traders and, after 1821, merger with the Montreal-based North West Company left HBC dominant from the Great Lakes to the Pacific and the Arctic. Under Governor George Simpson the company administered western Canada, established posts such as Fort Vancouver on the Columbia River, and sent exploration parties across the continent. Its point blankets — woollen blankets marked with coloured stripes and "points" — became an enduring Canadian icon.

From territory to retail

In 1869–70 the company surrendered Rupert's Land to the new Dominion of Canada under the Deed of Surrender (receiving £300,000 and land grants), setting the stage for Manitoba's entry into Confederation and the Red River Resistance. HBC then reoriented toward retail and real estate: its trading posts evolved into stores, and grand department stores opened in western cities in the early 20th century. The six surviving downtown flagships date from this era.

Modern era

The company diversified into energy, wholesale and shopping centres, was acquired by the Thomson family in 1979, and later by the American firm NRDC Equity Partners (2008), which also owned Lord & Taylor and Saks Fifth Avenue. HBC spun off Saks as a separate company in 2021. Its flagship Canadian chain traded as Hudson's Bay (La Baie d'Hudson in Quebec). In March 2025 HBC filed for creditor protection in Canada; its stores and the Hudson's Bay trademarks were subsequently sold, bringing the 355-year-old enterprise to an end and triggering liquidation of the chain across Canada.
