Non-pharmaceutical intervention

Non-pharmaceutical interventions or non-pharmaceutical measures (NPIs), also called community mitigation strategies, are public health measures that reduce the spread of infectious disease without medication or vaccination. They include personal protective measures, environmental measures, and social distancing measures such as school closures, travel restrictions and quarantine. NPIs are the principal tools available at the start of an epidemic of a novel pathogen, before pharmaceutical countermeasures such as vaccines or antivirals can be developed and distributed.

Types

  • Personal measures — hand hygiene, respiratory etiquette, and face masks.
  • Social distancing — stay-at-home orders, closure of schools, workplaces and venues, cancellation of mass gatherings, and limits on occupancy.
  • Case-based measures — testing, contact tracing, isolation of cases and quarantine of contacts.
  • Travel and border measures — entry screening, travel advisories and restrictions, and border closures.
  • Environmental measures — ventilation, surface cleaning and disinfection.

History and evidence

NPIs were used in the 1918 influenza pandemic — comparisons between American cities, such as St. Louis's early layered interventions versus Philadelphia's delayed response, are a classic case study — and figured in pandemic plans worldwide thereafter. They were deployed extensively during the COVID-19 pandemic from 2020 onward, where measures such as lockdowns and mask mandates substantially reduced transmission but imposed major economic and social costs, prompting continuing research and debate about the effectiveness and proportionality of individual measures and combinations. Their goal is often described as "flattening the curve": delaying and lowering the peak of cases so that healthcare systems are not overwhelmed.
