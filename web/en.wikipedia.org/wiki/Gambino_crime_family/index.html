Gambino crime family

The Gambino crime family is an Italian-American Mafia crime family and one of the "Five Families" that dominate organised crime activities in New York City. Its illicit activities include labour and construction racketeering, gambling, loansharking, extortion, drug trafficking, money laundering and prostitution.

History

The family's roots trace to the early 1900s and the gang of Salvatore "Totò" D'Aquila, who was murdered in 1928. After passing through the hands of Frank Scalice and Vincent Mangano, the family was led from 1951 by Albert Anastasia, boss of the murder-for-hire group known as Murder, Inc. When Anastasia was shot dead in a barber's chair in 1957, his underboss Carlo Gambino seized power.

Carlo Gambino, after whom the family is named, ruled until his death in 1976, building it into the most powerful Mafia family in the United States while keeping an unusually low public profile. He named his brother-in-law Paul Castellano as successor, passing over underboss Aniello Dellacroce and splitting the family into factions. Castellano — the "Howard Hughes of the Mafia" — ran the family like a corporation from his Staten Island mansion until 16 December 1985, when he and his underboss Thomas Bilotti were gunned down outside Sparks Steak House in Manhattan.

The Gotti era

The hit had been ordered by capo John Gotti, who became boss and, unlike his predecessors, courted publicity — earning the nicknames "Teflon Don" for a string of acquittals and "Dapper Don" for his tailored suits. The publicity and his underboss Sammy "the Bull" Gravano's decision to cooperate with the FBI destroyed him: Gotti was convicted of racketeering and murder in 1992 and died in prison in 2002.

Later years

Through the 1990s and 2000s the family was weakened by RICO prosecutions, including the 2008 Operation Old Bridge raids spanning New York and Sicily. Leadership passed through figures such as Peter Gotti, Domenico Cefalù and Frank Cali, who was shot dead outside his Staten Island home in 2019 — the first murder of a New York boss in decades. The family remains active, with an estimated 150–200 made members and influence across the New York metropolitan area.
