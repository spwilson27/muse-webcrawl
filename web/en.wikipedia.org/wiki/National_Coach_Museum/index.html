National Coach Museum

National Coach Museum
Portuguese: Museu Nacional dos Coches
Location: Belém, Lisbon, Portugal
Established: 1905
Type: Transport museum

The National Coach Museum (Portuguese: Museu Nacional dos Coches) is a museum in the Belém district of Lisbon, Portugal, housing one of the finest collections of historical carriages and coaches in the world. The museum was founded in 1905 by Queen Amélie of Portugal.

Collection

The collection includes coaches, berlins, litters, sedan chairs and carriages dating from the 16th to the 19th centuries, mostly from the Portuguese royal house. Highlights include the magnificent coaches made in Lisbon in 1716 for the Portuguese ambassador to Pope Clement XI, and the coach of Philip III of Spain, among the oldest in the collection. Most vehicles are richly decorated with gilded woodwork, painting and textiles.

Buildings

The museum was originally housed in the hall of the former Royal Riding School. In 2015 a new museum building, designed by the Brazilian Pritzker Prize-winning architect Paulo Mendes da Rocha, opened across the street, and the collection is now displayed between the modern pavilion and the old riding hall.
