The president of the Republic of China, commonly known as the president of Taiwan, is the head of state of the Republic of China (ROC), which has administered Taiwan and surrounding islands since 1949. The president is also commander-in-chief of the Republic of China Armed Forces.

Election and term

Since 1996 the president has been elected by direct popular vote in a single-round plurality election for a four-year term, with a limit of two terms. Candidates run jointly with a vice-presidential running mate. Lai Ching-te of the Democratic Progressive Party won the 2024 election and is the incumbent.

History

The office originated with Sun Yat-sen as provisional president in 1912. After the Chinese Civil War, the ROC government retreated to Taiwan, where presidents ruled under martial law until democratisation in the late 1980s and 1990s, culminating in the first direct election in 1996, won by Lee Teng-hui.

Powers

The president appoints the premier, promulgates laws, declares martial law with legislative approval, and directs national security and cross-strait policy.
