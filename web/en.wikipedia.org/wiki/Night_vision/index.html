Night vision

Night vision is the ability to see in low-light conditions, whether by biological or technological means. Many animals possess superior night vision to humans thanks to adaptations such as a high density of rod cells in the retina, large pupils, and a reflective layer behind the retina called the tapetum lucidum. Human scotopic vision relies on rod cells and improves through dark adaptation over roughly 20–30 minutes as the photopigment rhodopsin regenerates.

Night-vision devices

A night-vision device (NVD) is an optoelectronic device that allows visualization in near-total darkness. The most common technology is the image intensifier tube, which amplifies available light — including near-infrared — by converting photons to electrons, multiplying them across a microchannel plate, and reconverting them into a visible (usually green) image on a phosphor screen; green is used because the human eye can distinguish the most shades in that colour. Successive "generations" of tubes (Gen 1 through Gen 3) improved sensitivity, resolution, and tube life.

Thermal imaging is a related but distinct technology that detects mid-infrared radiation emitted as heat, forming images independent of visible light. Modern systems may fuse intensified and thermal imagery.

Uses

Night-vision goggles and scopes are standard military and law-enforcement equipment, enabling navigation, surveillance, and target acquisition at night. Civilian uses include wildlife observation, hunting, boating, security cameras, and automotive night-vision systems.
