United States Office of Personnel Management

The United States Office of Personnel Management (OPM) is an independent agency of the United States federal government that manages the federal civil service. Established by the Civil Service Reform Act of 1978, which abolished the United States Civil Service Commission, OPM took over most of the Commission's personnel functions when it opened on January 1, 1979.

Functions

OPM sets personnel policy for the federal workforce, oversees merit-based hiring, administers the hiring website USAJobs, manages federal employee retirement programs (including the Federal Employees Retirement System and the Civil Service Retirement System), and administers federal health and life insurance programs such as the Federal Employees Health Benefits Program and the Federal Employees' Group Life Insurance program.

Background investigations

Until 2019, OPM's National Background Investigations Bureau conducted most federal security-clearance background investigations; that function was transferred to the Department of Defense, where it became the Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency. The transfer followed the 2015 OPM data breach, in which the personnel records of more than 21 million people were compromised.

Leadership

OPM is headed by a Director appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate. Its headquarters are in the Theodore Roosevelt Building in Washington, D.C.
