The Truth and Reconciliation Commission of Canada (TRC) was a truth and reconciliation commission active in Canada from 2008 to 2015, established under the Indian Residential Schools Settlement Agreement — the largest class-action settlement in Canadian history. Its mandate was to document the history and lasting impacts of the Canadian Indian residential school system on Indigenous children and their families.

Background

For over a century, more than 150,000 First Nations, Métis and Inuit children were removed from their families and placed in church-run, government-funded residential schools, where many suffered neglect and physical, sexual and cultural abuse, and thousands died. The last school closed in 1996. In 2008 Prime Minister Stephen Harper delivered a formal apology in the House of Commons.

Work of the Commission

Chaired initially by Harry LaForme and, from 2009, by Justice Murray Sinclair, the Commission held seven national events, gathered statements from more than 6,500 survivors and witnesses, and collected millions of records. Its archive is housed at the National Centre for Truth and Reconciliation at the University of Manitoba.

Final report and Calls to Action

The Commission's multi-volume final report, released in 2015, concluded that the residential school system amounted to a policy of cultural genocide. It issued 94 Calls to Action addressed to governments, churches, schools and other institutions, covering child welfare, education, language and culture, health and justice. The report's release helped prompt the later National Inquiry into Missing and Murdered Indigenous Women and Girls, and Canada's National Day for Truth and Reconciliation (30 September) was established in 2021.
