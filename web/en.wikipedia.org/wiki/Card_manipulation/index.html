Card manipulation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Card manipulation is the branch of magic concerned with creating effects using sleight of hand techniques involving playing cards. It is one of the most practised forms of close-up magic, and manipulators may specialise in stage manipulation — card fans, productions and vanishes performed to music — or in the covert sleights of the card sharp and the close-up magician.

Techniques

Core techniques include false shuffles and cuts that preserve the order of the deck, the pass (secretly transposing two packets to control a card), palming, the double lift (displaying two cards as one), colour changes, and forces that constrain a spectator's "free" choice. Flourishes — decorative moves such as fans, spreads, springs and one-handed cuts — are performed openly rather than secretly; the modern discipline of cardistry developed out of this flourish tradition and practises flourishes as a visual art in its own right, without magical effects.

History

Card tricks appear in print as early as the sixteenth century, and the classic text The Expert at the Card Table (1902), published under the name S. W. Erdnase, remains the foundational work on both gambling sleights and magic technique. Twentieth-century masters such as Dai Vernon ("the Professor") transformed card magic into a naturalistic art. Stage manipulators including Cardini and Channing Pollock made card production acts — fans and single cards produced from thin air — a staple of variety theatre.
