Sanada Yukimura

Sanada Yukimura
Born: 1567, Shinano Province, Japan
Died: June 3, 1615 (aged 47–48), Osaka
Allegiance: Sanada clan; Toyotomi clan
Battles: Ueda Castle; Siege of Osaka

Sanada Yukimura (真田 信織, 1567 – June 3, 1615), also known as Sanada Nobushige, was a Japanese samurai warrior of the Sengoku period. He is especially known as the leading general on the defending side of the Siege of Osaka (1614–15), in which he opposed Tokugawa Ieyasu. Historically, Yukimura is often celebrated as "A Hero who may appear once in a hundred years", "Crimson Demon of War", and "The Last Sengoku Hero"; the famed military novelist Shiba Ryotaro opined that he was the best-known warrior in Japan among the general public.

Early life

Yukimura was the second son of Sanada Masayuki, lord of Ueda in Shinano Province. In 1585 he joined the Toyotomi service as his father allied with Toyotomi Hideyoshi, and married Chikurin-in, daughter of Ōtani Yoshitsugu. In 1600 he sided with Ishida Mitsunari's Western Army against the Tokugawa, while his elder brother Nobuyuki joined the Eastern side — a division arranged so the clan would survive whoever won. Yukimura and his father defended Ueda Castle against Tokugawa Hidetada's much larger army, delaying it so effectively that Hidetada arrived too late for the Battle of Sekigahara.

Exile and Osaka

After the Western defeat, Yukimura was exiled to Kudoyama on Mount Koya, where he lived in obscurity for fourteen years under surveillance. In 1614, when war between Toyotomi Hideyori and the Tokugawa shogunate loomed, Yukimura escaped exile and entered Osaka Castle. In the Winter Campaign he built the Sanada-maru, a barbican earthwork on the castle's weak southern side, from which his men repulsed Tokugawa assaults with heavy losses.

Death

In the Summer Campaign of 1615, outnumbered, Yukimura charged through the Tokugawa lines at Tennōji and nearly reached Ieyasu's headquarters before being driven back. Exhausted and wounded, he was killed near Yasui Shrine; tradition gives his last words as asking his killer to take his head. His death marked the end of the Toyotomi and of the Sengoku wars.

Legacy

Edo-period fiction and popular theatre enormously magnified Yukimura's fame, crediting him with exploits and a band of retainers (the "Sanada Ten Braves") that are legendary. His red armour and the Sanada crest of six coins — fare for the ferryman of the dead, signifying readiness to die — remain iconic in Japanese popular culture.
