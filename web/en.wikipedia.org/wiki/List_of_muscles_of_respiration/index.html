List of muscles of respiration

The muscles of respiration are the muscles that contribute to inhalation and exhalation, by aiding in the expansion and contraction of the thoracic cavity. The diaphragm and, to a lesser extent, the intercostal muscles drive respiration during quiet breathing. Additional muscles, known as accessory muscles of respiration, are recruited during forced breathing, exercise, or respiratory distress.

Primary muscles

MuscleActionInnervation
DiaphragmContracts and flattens, increasing the vertical dimension of the thoracic cavity; responsible for roughly 75% of quiet inspirationPhrenic nerve (C3–C5)
External intercostalsElevate the ribs, increasing the anteroposterior and transverse diameters of the thoraxIntercostal nerves
Internal intercostals (interosseous part)Depress the ribs during forced expirationIntercostal nerves
Innermost intercostalsAssist the internal intercostals in expirationIntercostal nerves

Accessory muscles of inspiration

  • Scalenes (anterior, medius, posterior) — elevate the first and second ribs.
  • Sternocleidomastoid — elevates the sternum.
  • Pectoralis major and minor — elevate the ribs when the arms are fixed.
  • Serratus anterior — elevates the ribs when the scapula is fixed.
  • Latissimus dorsi — can assist in deep inspiration.
  • Serratus posterior superior — elevates the upper ribs.
  • Iliocostalis — extends and stabilizes the thoracic spine.

Accessory muscles of expiration

  • Abdominal muscles (rectus abdominis, external and internal obliques, transversus abdominis) — compress the abdomen and push the diaphragm upward.
  • Quadratus lumborum — depresses the twelfth rib.
  • Serratus posterior inferior — depresses the lower ribs.
  • Transversus thoracis — depresses the ribs.

Clinical significance

Visible use of accessory muscles is a sign of respiratory distress. Paralysis of the diaphragm, as in phrenic nerve injury, produces paradoxical breathing. In chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), hyperinflation flattens the diaphragm and increases reliance on accessory muscles.
