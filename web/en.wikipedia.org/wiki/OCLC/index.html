OCLC, Inc., formerly the Online Computer Library Center, is an American nonprofit cooperative organization "dedicated to the public purposes of furthering access to the world's information and reducing information costs". It was founded in 1967 as the Ohio College Library Center by Fred Kilgour, and is headquartered in Dublin, Ohio.

WorldCat

OCLC and its member libraries cooperatively produce and maintain WorldCat, the world's largest online public access catalog, containing hundreds of millions of bibliographic records representing materials held by libraries in more than 100 countries. OCLC's shared cataloguing system went online in 1971; its records use the MARC format, and individual items are identified by OCLC numbers.

Services and acquisitions

OCLC provides a wide range of products, including the FirstSearch reference service, the Dewey Decimal Classification (acquired through its purchase of Forest Press in 1988), interlibrary loan networks, the WorldShare library services platform, and EZproxy access software. It has absorbed several library-technology vendors, including the Dutch company that produced the Wise library system. Its research arm, OCLC Research, is one of the world's leading centres for library and information science research.

Governance

OCLC is governed by a board of trustees and a global council elected by member institutions; thousands of libraries, archives, and museums participate in the cooperative.
