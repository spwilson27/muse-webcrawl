Athletics (sport)

Athletics is the centrepiece of the Summer Olympic Games.

Athletics is a group of sporting events that involve competitive running, jumping, throwing and walking. The most common types are track and field, road running, cross country running and racewalking. In North America the sport is usually called track and field, while "athletics" there can refer to sports in general. Athletics is governed internationally by World Athletics (formerly the IAAF), founded in 1912.

Events

Track events range from the 100 metres sprint through middle- and long-distance races to hurdles, steeplechase and relays. Field events comprise the jumps (long jump, triple jump, high jump, pole vault) and throws (shot put, discus, hammer, javelin). Combined events — the decathlon for men and heptathlon for women — test all-round ability. Road racing is headlined by the marathon, and cross country and racewalking complete the programme.

History

Running and jumping contests are among the oldest of all sports, depicted in ancient Egyptian and Greek art. The ancient Olympic Games, beginning in 776 BC, centred on the stadion footrace. The modern sport took shape in 19th-century England, where the first national championships were held in 1866, and athletics has been the central discipline of the modern Olympics since the first Games in Athens in 1896.

Major competitions

Besides the Olympic Games, the sport's premier events include the biennial World Athletics Championships (first held in 1983), the annual Diamond League circuit, the World Athletics Indoor Championships and continental championships. The marathon majors and championship cross country events draw mass participation worldwide.
