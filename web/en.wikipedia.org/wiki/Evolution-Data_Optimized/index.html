Evolution-Data Optimized or Evolution-Data Only (EV-DO, also rendered EvDO) is a telecommunications standard for the wireless transmission of data through radio signals, typically for broadband Internet access. It is part of the CDMA2000 family of standards maintained by 3GPP2 and was adopted by many mobile phone service providers around the world, particularly those previously employing CDMA networks, including Verizon Wireless and Sprint in the United States.

Technology

EV-DO was designed as an evolution of the cdma2000 (IS-2000) standard that would provide high-speed data services on a dedicated 1.25 MHz radio channel, separate from voice channels. It uses advanced modulation and coding, including QPSK, 8-PSK, and 16-QAM, and schedules transmissions to users based on rapidly varying channel conditions, exploiting multiuser diversity. Unlike its predecessor 1xRTT, the original EV-DO channel carried data only, with voice handled on separate channels.

Revisions

The original standard (Release 0) offered peak forward-link rates of about 2.4 Mbit/s. Revision A (2006) raised peak rates to 3.1 Mbit/s downlink and 1.8 Mbit/s uplink, reduced latency, and added support for quality of service, making voice over IP on the data channel feasible. Revision B (2007) aggregated multiple carriers, reaching peak rates of up to 14.7 Mbit/s with three 1.25 MHz channels, though deployments were limited.

Decline

From the early 2010s, EV-DO networks were progressively displaced by LTE, which offered higher speeds and an all-IP architecture. Major carriers shut down their CDMA/EV-DO networks in the late 2010s and early 2020s, including Verizon's shutdown at the end of 2022.
