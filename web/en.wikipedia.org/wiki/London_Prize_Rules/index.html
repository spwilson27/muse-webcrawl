London Prize Rules
Sport: Bare-knuckle boxing
Introduced: 1838

The London Prize Rules were a set of rules for bare-knuckle boxing published in 1838, revising earlier rules of Jack Broughton. They governed British prizefighting until superseded by the Marquess of Queensberry Rules.

Provisions

The rules specified a roped ring, rounds ending when a fighter was knocked down, 30 seconds to come up to scratch, and prohibitions on gouging, headbutting and hitting a downed opponent, though wrestling throws were allowed.

Legacy

Under these rules, bouts could last dozens of rounds. The last major London Prize Rules championship fight was between John L. Sullivan and Paddy Ryan in 1882.
