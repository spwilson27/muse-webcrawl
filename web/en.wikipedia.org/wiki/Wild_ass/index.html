Wild ass

An onager, a subspecies of the Asiatic wild ass.

Wild ass is a common name for several species of the genus Equus that live in arid regions of Africa and Asia. The term most often refers to the African wild ass (Equus africanus), the ancestor of the domestic donkey, and the Asiatic wild ass or onager (Equus hemionus).

Species and subspecies

  • African wild ass (Equus africanus) — native to the Horn of Africa; subspecies include the Nubian wild ass and the Somali wild ass. Critically endangered.
  • Asiatic wild ass (Equus hemionus) — the onager of Iran, the khulan of Mongolia, the kulan of Central Asia, the Indian wild ass (khur) of the Rann of Kutch, and the extinct Syrian wild ass.
  • Tibetan wild ass or kiang (Equus kiang) — the largest of the wild asses, inhabiting the Tibetan Plateau.

Characteristics

Wild asses are adapted to desert and steppe environments. They are swift runners, can go long periods without water, and feed on coarse grasses and shrubs. They typically live in small groups or loose herds, with territorial males defending areas around water sources. Most wild ass populations have declined sharply because of hunting, competition with livestock, and habitat fragmentation.
