Coors family

The Coors family is an American family of German descent, owners and heirs of the Coors Brewing Company and, through mergers, major shareholders of Molson Coors Beverage Company. The family's brewing enterprise was founded in Golden, Colorado, in 1873 by Adolph Coors, an immigrant from Barmen, Prussia, together with the confectioner Jacob Schueler. Members of the family have also been prominent in conservative politics, philanthropy, and Colorado civic life.

Adolph Coors and the founding generation

Adolph Herman Joseph Coors (1847–1929) arrived in the United States as a stowaway in 1868 and worked as a brewer in Illinois and Colorado before opening the Golden Brewery with Schueler in 1873, buying out his partner in 1880. The brewery survived Prohibition by making near beer, malted milk and ceramics — the origin of CoorsTek — and Adolph died in 1929 after falling from a hotel balcony in Virginia Beach. His sons Adolph Jr., Grover, and Herman expanded the business; Adolph Coors Jr. (1884–1970) led the company for decades.

Later generations

Adolph Jr.'s sons included William K. Coors (1916–2018), who led the company for decades and pushed its national expansion, and Joseph Coors (1917–2003), a prominent conservative who helped found the Heritage Foundation and funded conservative media and causes. Another son, Adolph Coors III, was kidnapped and murdered in 1960. In the fourth and fifth generations, family members such as John Coors and Pete Coors — who became president of the brewery and a candidate for the U.S. Senate from Colorado in 2004 — continued in the business.

Business interests

The Adolph Coors Company became Molson Coors through the 2005 merger with Molson, and the family retains a significant ownership and governance role. Associated family enterprises have included CoorsTek (advanced ceramics) and the Coors Foundation, a philanthropic organisation based in Colorado.
