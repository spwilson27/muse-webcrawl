Dermatitis

Dermatitis
SpecialtyDermatology
SymptomsItchiness, red skin, rash
CausesAtopic dermatitis, allergic contact dermatitis, irritant contact dermatitis, stasis dermatitis
TreatmentMoisturizers, corticosteroid creams, antihistamines

Dermatitis is inflammation of the skin, typically characterised by itchiness, redness and a rash. In cases of short duration, there may be small blisters, while in long-term cases the skin may become thickened. The area of skin involved can vary from small to covering the entire body. Dermatitis is often called eczema, and the difference between those terms is not standardised.

Types

The exact cause of dermatitis is often unclear. Cases may involve a combination of allergy and poor venous return. The type of dermatitis is generally determined by the person's history and the location of the rash. Types include:

  • Atopic dermatitis (atopic eczema) — a chronic relapsing condition often beginning in childhood, associated with asthma and hay fever.
  • Contact dermatitis — subdivided into allergic and irritant forms, caused by substances touching the skin such as nickel, fragrances, or detergents.
  • Seborrhoeic dermatitis — affecting sebaceous-gland-rich areas such as the scalp (dandruff) and face.
  • Stasis dermatitis — occurring on the lower legs in association with venous insufficiency.

Treatment

Treatment revolves around identifying and avoiding triggers, together with emollients (moisturisers) to restore the skin barrier and topical corticosteroids to suppress inflammation. For severe or widespread disease, phototherapy or systemic immunosuppressants may be used. Antihistamines are sometimes given to help with itch and sleep.
