The Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter (MRO) is a NASA spacecraft designed to study the geology and climate of Mars, scout landing sites, and relay data from surface missions. It has returned more data than all other interplanetary missions combined at the time of its early operation.

Instruments

Its HiRISE camera resolves features under a metre across from orbit; CTX provides wide context images, CRISM maps surface minerals, and the SHARAD radar sounds the subsurface for ice. Electra radios relay data from rovers such as Curiosity and Perseverance.

Findings

MRO observations revealed recurring slope lineae — dark seasonal streaks associated with briny flows — extensive buried glaciers and polar ice, fresh impact craters exposing water ice, and detailed stratigraphy informing the history of water on Mars.
