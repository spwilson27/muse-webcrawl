Tarikh-i Rashidi

The Tarikh-i Rashidi (Persian: تاریخ رشیدی, "History of Rashid") is a Persian-language history of the Moghuls of Moghulistan and Central Asia written by Mirza Muhammad Haidar Dughlat (1499/1500–1551), a Chagatai-Turkic prince, military commander and historian. Completed around 1546 while its author governed Kashmir, it is the principal primary source for the history of the eastern Chagatai Khanate in the 14th to 16th centuries.

Contents

The work is divided into two books (daftars). The first recounts the history of the khans of Moghulistan from Tughluq Timur (r. 1347/48–1362/63), the first khan of the eastern Chagatai realm, down to Abd al-Rashid Khan, to whom the book is dedicated and from whom it takes its name. The second book is largely a personal memoir of the author, describing his own campaigns and service with the rulers of Moghulistan, his cousin Babur (founder of the Mughal Empire), the Uzbeks of Shaybani Khan, and his conquest and rule of Kashmir and expeditions into Tibet.

Significance

Written by an educated participant in the events he describes, the Tarikh-i Rashidi is prized for its accounts of peoples and places otherwise poorly documented, including early information on the Kyrgyz, Kazakhs and the geography of the Tarim Basin and Tibet. It was translated into English by Ney Elias and E. Denison Ross and published in 1895 as The Tarikh-i-Rashidi of Mirza Muhammad Haidar, Dughlát: A History of the Moghuls of Central Asia.
