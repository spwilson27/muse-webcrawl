Energiewende

The Energiewende (German for "energy turnaround" or "energy transition") is the ongoing transition by Germany to a low-carbon, nuclear-free economy based on renewable energy and energy efficiency. It is one of the most ambitious national energy policies in the world and has shaped European climate policy.

Origins and goals

The term was coined around 1980 and entered policy through the governing coalition's support for renewables. The Renewable Energy Sources Act (EEG) of 2000 introduced feed-in tariffs that drove rapid growth in wind and solar power. After the 2011 Fukushima disaster, Chancellor Angela Merkel's government accelerated the phase-out of nuclear power — completed in April 2023 when the last three plants closed.

Key targets have included cutting greenhouse-gas emissions by 80–95% by 2050 relative to 1990, raising renewables to 80% of electricity consumption by 2030, and improving energy efficiency across buildings, industry, and transport.

Progress and challenges

Renewables passed 50% of German electricity consumption in 2023, with wind the largest single source. Challenges include high household electricity prices, slow expansion of transmission lines from windy northern coasts to industrial southern states, delays in the heating and transport transitions, and debate over the economic effects of ending nuclear power before coal. Germany's remaining coal phase-out is legislated for 2038, with an ambition of 2030.
