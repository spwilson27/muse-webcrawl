History of astrology

The history of astrology extends from ancient Mesopotamian sky-watching to the horoscopic astrology still practised today. Babylonian priests developed celestial divination by the second millennium BCE, compiling omen texts such as Enūma Anu Enlil and devising the zodiac of twelve signs.

Hellenistic astrology

After Alexander's conquests, Babylonian and Egyptian traditions merged with Greek thought to produce horoscopic astrology, systematized in works such as Ptolemy's Tetrabiblos (2nd century CE), which remained authoritative for over a thousand years.

Transmission and revival

Astrology flourished in the Islamic world, where scholars such as Abū Maʿshar refined its techniques, and returned to medieval and Renaissance Europe, where it informed medicine and natural philosophy. The Scientific Revolution separated astronomy from astrology, and by the 18th century astrology lost academic standing.

Modern era

A 20th-century popular revival, aided by newspaper horoscope columns and psychological approaches influenced by Carl Jung, made astrology a widespread popular practice despite scientific criticism. See also Astrology.
