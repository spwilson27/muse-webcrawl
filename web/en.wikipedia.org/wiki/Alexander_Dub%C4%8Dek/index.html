Alexander Dubček

Alexander Dubček (27 November 1921 – 7 November 1992) was a Czechoslovak and Slovak statesman who served as the First Secretary of the Presidium of the Central Committee of the Communist Party of Czechoslovakia (KSČ) from January 1968 to April 1969. He is best known for his attempt to reform the communist regime during the Prague Spring of 1968, under the banner of "socialism with a human face".

Early life

Dubček was born in Uhrovec, Slovakia (in the same house as Ľudovít Štúr). His father, a worker, had emigrated to the United States and returned to Czechoslovakia. From 1925 to 1938 the family lived in the Soviet Union, in Kyrgyzstan and Ukraine, as part of the Interhelpo cooperative. Returning to Slovakia, Dubček joined the illegal communist movement and participated in the Slovak National Uprising of 1944 against Nazi occupation.

Prague Spring

After rising through the party in Slovakia, Dubček became leader of the KSČ in January 1968, replacing Antonín Novotný. His reform programme — abolishing censorship, rehabilitating victims of Stalinist purges, economic decentralisation, and steps toward federalisation — unleashed an extraordinary period of liberalisation known as the Prague Spring. The reforms alarmed the Soviet leadership; negotiations at Čierna nad Tisou and Bratislava failed to halt them, and on the night of 20–21 August 1968, Warsaw Pact forces invaded Czechoslovakia. Dubček and other leaders were arrested and taken to Moscow, where they were forced to sign the Moscow Protocol.

Later life

Replaced by Gustáv Husák in April 1969, Dubček briefly served as ambassador to Turkey before being expelled from the party in 1970 and working in forestry administration in Slovakia, largely out of public view. In 1989, during the Velvet Revolution, he returned to politics and was elected Chairman of the Federal Assembly of Czechoslovakia. After the 1992 elections he led the Social Democratic Party of Slovakia. He died on 7 November 1992 from injuries sustained in a car crash on the Prague–Bratislava motorway.
