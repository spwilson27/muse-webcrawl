Texas hold 'em

Type: Poker variant (community card)
Origin: Robstown, Texas, early 1900s
Cards: 2 hole cards, 5 community cards

Texas hold 'em is the most popular variant of poker. Each player is dealt two private cards ("hole cards") and combines them with five community cards dealt face up in stages — the flop, turn, and river — to make the best five-card hand, betting in rounds before and after each stage.

History

The game originated in Robstown, Texas, in the early 20th century and was brought to Las Vegas in the 1960s. It became the main event format of the World Series of Poker, founded in 1970.

Popularity

Televised coverage with hole-card cameras and Chris Moneymaker's 2003 WSOP victory triggered the "poker boom," making hold 'em the dominant form in casinos and online poker worldwide. It is also a major testbed for game-playing artificial intelligence.
