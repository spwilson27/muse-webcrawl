Polysaccharide

Polysaccharide
TypeCarbohydrate polymer
MonomersMonosaccharides (e.g. glucose)
BondGlycosidic bonds
General formula(C6H10O5)n (typical)
ExamplesStarch, glycogen, cellulose, chitin
FunctionsEnergy storage; structural support

Polysaccharides are long-chain polymeric carbohydrates composed of monosaccharide units bound together by glycosidic linkages. They range in structure from linear to highly branched, and include storage polysaccharides such as starch and glycogen and structural polysaccharides such as cellulose and chitin. Polysaccharides are generally not sweet and, unlike monosaccharides and oligosaccharides, are often insoluble in water.

Structure

A polysaccharide built from a single kind of monosaccharide is a homopolysaccharide or homoglycan; one built from more than one kind is a heteropolysaccharide. Chains may contain hundreds to tens of thousands of monomer units. Linkage geometry determines properties dramatically: starch's α(1→4) glucose linkages form helices that enzymes readily digest, whereas cellulose's β(1→4) linkages form straight ribbons that hydrogen-bond into tough fibres most animals cannot digest directly.

Storage polysaccharides

Starch

Starch is the main energy store of green plants, deposited in seeds, tubers, and roots as granules. It consists of amylose, a mostly linear α(1→4) glucan, and amylopectin, a highly branched molecule with α(1→6) branch points. Animals digest starch with amylases.

Glycogen

Glycogen is the storage glucan of animals and fungi, stored mainly in liver and muscle. It is even more densely branched than amylopectin, allowing rapid mobilisation of glucose when energy is needed.

Structural polysaccharides

Cellulose

Cellulose, a linear β(1→4) polymer of glucose, is the most abundant organic polymer on Earth and the chief component of plant cell walls and of fibres such as cotton. Its chains pack into microfibrils of high tensile strength.

Chitin

Chitin is a β(1→4) polymer of N-acetylglucosamine forming the exoskeletons of arthropods and the cell walls of fungi. Partially deacetylated chitin yields chitosan, used in medicine and water treatment.

Other polysaccharides

Bacteria produce diverse capsular polysaccharides important in immunity — several vaccines consist of purified capsular polysaccharide or its conjugates. Glycosaminoglycans such as hyaluronic acid and heparin are heteropolysaccharides of animal connective tissue. Seaweed polysaccharides — agar, carrageenan, and alginate — are widely used as gelling and thickening agents in food and microbiology, as are pectins from fruit and microbial xanthan gum and dextran.

Nutrition

Nutritionally, polysaccharides divide into digestible starches and non-digestible dietary fibres. Fibre resists human digestive enzymes but is fermented to varying degrees by gut microbes, producing short-chain fatty acids beneficial to the colon.
