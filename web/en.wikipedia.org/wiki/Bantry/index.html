Bantry

Bantry (Irish: Beanntraí) is a town in County Cork in the southwest of Ireland, situated on the N71 national road at the head of Bantry Bay, one of the great natural harbours of Europe. The town is a market and tourist centre for West Cork, with a long maritime history and a large sheltered square built on reclaimed land.

History

Bantry Bay entered world history in December 1796, when a French expeditionary fleet carrying General Hoche and the United Irishman Theobald Wolfe Tone attempted to land troops to support an Irish rebellion. Storms scattered the fleet and prevented any landing; Tone wrote bitterly that England had not had such an escape since the Armada. French forces also confronted a British squadron in Bantry Bay during the earlier Battle of Bantry Bay in 1689. The town's great house, Bantry House, home of the White family (Earls of Bantry), overlooks the bay and remains a major visitor attraction.

Economy and Whiddy Island

Fishing, mussel farming, tourism, and the market serving the Beara and Sheep's Head peninsulas underpin the modern economy. Offshore lies Whiddy Island, site of a major oil terminal built in the 1960s for Gulf Oil. On 8 January 1979 the French tanker Betelgeuse exploded at the terminal, killing 50 people, one of the worst disasters in modern Irish history; the terminal was later rebuilt at reduced scale and used for oil storage and transshipment.
