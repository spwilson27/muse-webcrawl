Clinical trial registration

Clinical trial registration is the practice of recording the key features of a clinical trial in a publicly accessible registry before the trial begins enrolling participants. Registration typically includes the trial's objectives, design, eligibility criteria, interventions, outcome measures, and sponsor, and is intended to prevent selective reporting of results, reduce publication bias, and make ongoing research visible to patients, clinicians, and researchers.

History

The modern registration movement grew from concerns that trials with negative results went unpublished. ClinicalTrials.gov, maintained by the United States National Library of Medicine, opened in 2000, initially for trials of serious diseases as required by the Food and Drug Administration Modernization Act of 1997. In 2004–2005 the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE) announced that its journals would require prospective registration as a condition of publication, a policy that greatly accelerated adoption. The World Health Organization established the International Clinical Trials Registry Platform (ICTRP) and a network of primary registries, and in 2007 the FDA Amendments Act expanded registration and results-reporting requirements in the United States.

Requirements and practice

Most registries require a minimum data set — the WHO Trial Registration Data Set lists 24 items — to be submitted before enrolment of the first participant ("prospective registration"). Registrations receive a unique identifier, such as an NCT number from ClinicalTrials.gov or an ISRCTN number, which is cited in publications. Despite the rules, audits continue to find late registration, discrepancies between registered and published outcomes, and incomplete results reporting, prompting journals and regulators to strengthen enforcement.
