Short ciliary nerves

The short ciliary nerves are a group of nerves arising from the ciliary ganglion in the orbit. Usually numbering six to ten, they pierce the sclera near the optic nerve and run forward between the sclera and choroid to supply the structures of the eyeball.

Contents

They carry three types of fibres: (1) parasympathetic postganglionic fibres originating in the Edinger–Westphal nucleus and travelling with the oculomotor nerve (CN III) to the ciliary ganglion, which supply the ciliary muscle (for accommodation) and the sphincter pupillae muscle (for pupil constriction); (2) sympathetic postganglionic fibres from the superior cervical ganglion, passing through without synapsing, which supply blood vessels and contribute to the dilator pupillae; and (3) sensory fibres from the nasociliary branch of the trigeminal nerve (CN V1), conveying sensation from the cornea, iris and ciliary body.

Clinical relevance

Damage to the short ciliary nerves can produce a tonic pupil (Adie pupil), in which the pupil reacts slowly and poorly to light but better to near effort, with segmental paralysis of the iris sphincter visible at the slit lamp.
