Saarbrücken (French: Sarrebruck) is the capital and largest city of the German state of Saarland. Situated on the river Saar directly on the border with France, it forms a cross-border urban area with the French cities of Sarreguemines and Forbach and has a population of roughly 180,000, with around 700,000 in its wider metropolitan region.

History

The city grew around a castle first mentioned in 999, guarding a bridge over the Saar. It was the seat of the Counts of Saarbrücken and later of the princes of Nassau-Saarbrücken, whose baroque rebuilding under architect Friedrich Joachim Stengel gave the old town landmarks such as the Ludwigskirche (Ludwig Church) and Saarbrücken Castle. Industrialisation turned the city into a centre of coal mining and steel. After both world wars the Saar territory was placed under French administration or protection; in a 1955 referendum its inhabitants voted to join the Federal Republic of Germany, which took effect in 1957.

Economy and culture

With coal and steel in decline, Saarbrücken has reoriented towards services, trade fairs and research. Saarland University, founded in 1948 under French auspices, is noted for computer science and hosts institutes including a Max Planck Institute and a German research centre for artificial intelligence (DFKI). The city is a hub of Franco-German cooperation, with bilingual institutions and the seat of the Franco-German television channel arte's German partner structures nearby.
