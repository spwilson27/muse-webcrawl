2018 Hawaii false missile alert

On the morning of 13 January 2018, a false ballistic missile alert was issued across the U.S. state of Hawaii by the Hawaii Emergency Management Agency (HI-EMA), sending residents and tourists into panic for 38 minutes before a correction was broadcast. The incident occurred amid heightened tensions between the United States and North Korea.

Incident details
Date13 January 2018
Alert issued8:08 a.m. HST
Correction issued8:46 a.m. HST (38 minutes later)
CauseOperator error during shift-change drill

The alert

At 8:08 a.m. HST, mobile phones across Hawaii received an Emergency Alert System and Wireless Emergency Alert message:

BALLISTIC MISSILE THREAT INBOUND TO HAWAII. SEEK IMMEDIATE SHELTER. THIS IS NOT A DRILL.

The message also interrupted television and radio broadcasts. Many residents sought shelter in storm drains, bathtubs and parking structures, and jammed highways and phone lines. Although officials including Congresswoman Tulsi Gabbard quickly posted on social media that the alert was false — around 8:17 a.m. — the official correction through the same alert systems did not go out until 8:46 a.m., 38 minutes later.

Cause

State and FCC investigations found that during a shift-change internal drill, an employee misheard a recorded message and selected "missile alert" rather than the test option from a drop-down menu, then confirmed the real alert. The system had no pre-built false-alarm correction message, contributing to the delay. The employee was later fired, the HI-EMA administrator resigned, and the agency introduced two-person confirmation and a standing cancellation template. The FCC concluded the incident resulted from inadequate safeguards and poor drill procedures, and it prompted reviews of alert systems nationwide.
