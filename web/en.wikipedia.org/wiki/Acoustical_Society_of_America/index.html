Acoustical Society of America

The Acoustical Society of America (ASA) is an international scientific society dedicated to generating, disseminating, and promoting knowledge of acoustics and its practical applications. Founded in 1929, it is the premier international society in the field of acoustics, with members in the United States and throughout the world.

History

The society was organized in 1929, following a meeting of acoustical scientists and engineers at Bell Telephone Laboratories in New York. Its founding reflected the rapid growth of acoustics as a discipline in the early twentieth century, driven by advances in telephony, sound recording, architectural design, and underwater sound. Early leaders in the field, including Harvey Fletcher, helped shape the society's scientific program.

Activities

The ASA holds semiannual meetings that bring together researchers in physical acoustics, speech communication, noise control, architectural acoustics, psychological and physiological acoustics, musical acoustics, underwater acoustics, animal bioacoustics, and structural acoustics. Technical committees coordinate work in these specialty areas.

The society publishes The Journal of the Acoustical Society of America (JASA), the leading journal in the field, along with JASA Express Letters, Acoustics Today magazine, and proceedings volumes. Through its standards program, the ASA also develops and publishes American National Standards on acoustical topics, covering subjects such as hearing conservation, noise measurement, and room acoustics.

Awards

The society's highest honor is the Gold Medal, awarded for long-standing contributions to acoustics. Other awards include the Silver Medals in the various technical areas, the R. Bruce Lindsay Award for young scientists, and the Wallace Clement Sabine Medal in architectural acoustics.
