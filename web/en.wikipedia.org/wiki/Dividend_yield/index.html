Dividend yield

The dividend yield is a financial ratio that shows how much a company pays out in dividends each year relative to its stock price. It is expressed as a percentage and is one of the main measures used by income-oriented investors to compare the cash return of different shares, funds, and market indices.

Dividend yield
[Image: chart of dividend yields over time]
TypeFinancial ratio
FormulaAnnual dividends per share ÷ price per share
VariantsTrailing yield, forward yield

Calculation

Dividend yield = (annual dividends per share) / (current share price)

For example, a company paying $2 per share annually with a share price of $50 has a dividend yield of 4%. The trailing yield uses dividends actually paid over the past twelve months, while the forward yield uses the most recent dividend payment annualised.

Interpretation

A high yield may reflect generous distributions, a depressed share price (sometimes signalling distress and an unsustainable dividend), or both. Yields rise when prices fall even if the dividend is unchanged, which is why index yields spiked during market crashes. Investors seeking income often examine yield together with the payout ratio and the company's record of dividend growth; yields must also be compared with interest rates, as in the so-called Fed model.

Related measures

The dividend yield is the inverse of the price-to-dividend ratio and is related to the earnings yield and the total return (dividends plus capital gains). For funds, the SEC yield standardises the calculation in the United States.
