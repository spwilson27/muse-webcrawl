Stockbroker

A stockbroker is a regulated broker, broker-dealer, or other agent who buys and sells stocks and other securities for both retail and institutional clients through a stock exchange or over the counter, in return for a fee or commission.

Historically, only wealthy individuals could afford a broker with access to an exchange; the rise of discount brokerages and online trading platforms from the 1970s onward greatly reduced costs and opened direct share dealing to the general public. Stockbrokers today may be full-service, offering investment advice, research and wealth management, or execution-only (discount), simply carrying out the client's instructions.

Stockbrokers are licensed and regulated in most jurisdictions. In the United States, they must pass examinations such as the FINRA Series 7 and be registered with a broker-dealer; in the United Kingdom they are regulated by the Financial Conduct Authority. The term derives from the brokers who traded shares in London coffee houses and were formally organised at the London Stock Exchange.
