Cape Verde, officially the Republic of Cabo Verde, is an island country and archipelagic state in the central Atlantic Ocean, about 570 kilometres off the coast of West Africa. The archipelago consists of ten volcanic islands divided into the northern Barlavento (Windward) group and the southern Sotavento (Leeward) group. The capital and largest city is Praia, on the island of Santiago.

History

The islands were uninhabited when Portuguese mariners reached them around 1456–1460. They became an important station in the transatlantic slave trade and a provisioning stop for shipping; the settlement of Ribeira Grande (now Cidade Velha, a UNESCO World Heritage Site) was among the first European colonial towns in the tropics. Severe droughts and famine periodically devastated the islands under colonial rule.

Cape Verde gained independence from Portugal on 5 July 1975 under the leadership of the PAIGC and its Cape Verdean successor, the PAICV, closely associated with the struggle in Guinea-Bissau led by Amílcar Cabral. Initially governed as a one-party state, the country adopted multiparty democracy in 1991 and has since been noted for political stability, peaceful transfers of power and strong democratic institutions relative to the region.

Geography and climate

The islands are volcanic in origin; the highest point is Mount Fogo (Pico do Fogo, 2,829 m), an active volcano on the island of Fogo whose eruptions have periodically destroyed villages, most recently in 2014–15. The climate is arid to semi-arid, with scarce rainfall concentrated in a short season, making water scarcity a persistent challenge.

Economy and culture

With few natural resources, Cape Verde's economy is service-oriented, centred on tourism (especially on Sal and Boa Vista), transport, remittances from a large diaspora, and fisheries. The country graduated from the UN's least-developed-country category in 2007. Cape Verdean culture blends African and Portuguese influences; the morna music of Cesária Évora, the "Barefoot Diva", is internationally renowned and was inscribed on UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage list. Portuguese is the official language, while Cape Verdean Creole (Kriolu) is spoken universally in everyday life.
