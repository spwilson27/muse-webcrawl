Via Maris

Via Maris is one modern name for an ancient trade route dating from the early Bronze Age, linking Egypt with the northern empires of Syria, Anatolia and Mesopotamia — modern-day Iran, Iraq, Israel, Palestine, Jordan, Syria, Lebanon and Turkey. Together with the King's Highway, it was one of the principal routes through the Levant.

Via Maris
RouteEgypt – Gaza – coastal Levant – Megiddo/Jezreel – Damascus
Other namesWay of the Philistines, Way of the Sea (Isaiah), Trunk Road
PeriodBronze Age onward

Route

From the Nile Delta the road ran east across northern Sinai to Gaza, then north along the Mediterranean coast through Ashkelon, Joppa and the Yarkon, turning inland through the Jezreel Valley past Megiddo, then via Hazor to Damascus, where it connected to routes east and north.

History and significance

Egyptian pharaohs including Thutmose III marched this way to battles such as Megiddo. The road carried trade, armies and messengers for Egyptians, Philistines, Israelites, Assyrians, Persians, Greeks, Romans and Crusaders. Isaiah's phrase "the way of the sea" (via maris in the Vulgate) gave the modern name. In Roman times it was paved and linked to the port cities and the Decapolis.
