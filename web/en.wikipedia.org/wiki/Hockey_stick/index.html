Hockey stick

A hockey stick is a piece of sports equipment used by players in various forms of hockey to move a ball or puck. This article focuses mainly on the ice hockey stick: a long shaft with a flat blade at one end, used to handle, pass and shoot the puck.

Ice hockey stick

Traditional ice hockey sticks are made of wood, historically ash or hickory; since the 1990s most elite sticks are one-piece composites of carbon fibre and fibreglass, prized for their light weight and tuned "flex" — the shaft's stiffness rating, roughly matched to the player's weight. The blade's curve (its pattern or "lie") affects puck handling and shot trajectory; rules limit blade curvature, a restriction famously enforced against Marty McSorley's illegal stick in the 1993 Stanley Cup Finals. Goaltenders use a wider-bladed stick with a bent shaft.

Field hockey stick

A field hockey stick is shorter, with a hooked head (the "toe") used to strike a hard ball; only the flat face of the stick may touch the ball. Modern field hockey sticks are made from composite materials with a maximum length of about 105 cm and strictly regulated bow.

Other forms

Related implements include the bandy stick, the short sticks of floor hockey and roller hockey, and the hurley used in the Irish sport of hurling, whose players strike a sliotar with a broad flat stick (camán). The earliest hockey sticks were simply shaped branches; Mi'kmaq craftsmen in Nova Scotia were noted makers of early ice hockey sticks in the 19th century.
