Handicap (Go)

In the game of Go, a handicap is a system for equalising a game between players of different strengths. The weaker player, taking Black, places a number of stones on the board before the game begins, and White then makes the first move.

Traditionally, the number of handicap stones equals the difference in the players' ranks: one stone per rank (dan or kyu) of difference, up to nine stones on a 19×19 board. The handicap stones are placed on fixed points (the hoshi, or star points) in a prescribed pattern.

Placement

On a 19×19 board, handicap stones occupy the corner, side, and centre star points in a set order, beginning with the corners. Smaller boards use different standard placements: up to nine stones on a 13×13 board and fewer on a 9×9 board, where large handicaps are impractical.

Compensation

When a handicap is used, White typically receives no komi, or a nominal half point to break ties. In even games, by contrast, komi compensates White for Black's first-move advantage.

Purpose

Handicaps allow meaningful, competitive games across a wide range of skill, and are a distinctive feature of Go culture, where the ranking system is designed so that an appropriate handicap produces a roughly even contest.
