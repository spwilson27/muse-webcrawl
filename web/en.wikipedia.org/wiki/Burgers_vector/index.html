Burgers vector

In materials science, the Burgers vector, named after Dutch physicist Jan Burgers, is a vector, often denoted b, that represents the magnitude and direction of the lattice distortion resulting from a dislocation in a crystal. It is measured by constructing a Burgers circuit: an atom-to-atom path around the dislocation that encloses it; in a perfect crystal the circuit would close, and the vector needed to close the circuit in the dislocated crystal is the Burgers vector.

Types of dislocation

For an edge dislocation, the Burgers vector is perpendicular to the dislocation line; for a screw dislocation, it is parallel to the line. Mixed dislocations have intermediate character. The Burgers vector is conserved along a dislocation line, and it determines how the dislocation moves under stress, producing plastic deformation of the crystal.

Notation

Burgers vectors are usually expressed in crystallographic notation as a fraction of a lattice translation vector, for example b = a/2⟨110⟩ in face-centred cubic metals.
