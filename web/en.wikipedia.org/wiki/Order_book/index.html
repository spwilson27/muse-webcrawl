Order book

A depth-of-market display of an order book

An order book is the list of orders (manual or electronic) that a trading venue — such as a stock exchange — uses to record the interest of buyers and sellers in a particular financial instrument. Buy orders (bids) and sell orders (asks or offers) are organised by price level and, within each level, usually by time priority. The order book is the central mechanism of a continuous double auction market.

How it works

When a new order arrives, the exchange's matching engine checks whether it can be executed against orders resting on the opposite side of the book. A buy order is matched against the lowest available ask; a sell order against the highest available bid. Orders that are not immediately executable remain in the book until they are filled, cancelled, or expire. The difference between the best bid and best ask is the bid–ask spread.

Order types

Participants may submit limit orders, which specify a maximum purchase price or minimum sale price and rest in the book, or market orders, which execute immediately against the best available prices. Variations include stop orders, iceberg orders (of which only a portion is displayed), and fill-or-kill orders.

Market data

Exchanges disseminate order book data at different levels of detail: Level 1 shows only the best bid and ask, while Level 2 (depth of market) shows multiple price levels and the aggregate volume at each. Deep order book data is a key input to algorithmic and high-frequency trading strategies.
