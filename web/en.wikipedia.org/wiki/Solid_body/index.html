Solid body


A solid body electric guitar has a solid, non-hollow wooden body.

Solid body refers to a type of electric guitar whose body is made of solid wood (or occasionally other solid materials) rather than being hollow or semi-hollow. Without a resonating sound chamber, a solid-body guitar depends almost entirely on its pickups and amplifier for sound, which greatly reduces feedback at high volumes and increases sustain.

History

Early experiments included the Rickenbacker "Frying Pan" lap steel of the 1930s. Les Paul built his solid-body prototype "The Log" in 1940, and Leo Fender's Broadcaster/Telecaster of 1950–51 became the first mass-produced solid-body electric guitar, followed by the Gibson Les Paul (1952) and Fender Stratocaster (1954).

Characteristics

Solid-body designs dominate rock and popular music. Their dense bodies produce a bright, sustained tone with minimal acoustic feedback, and they allow shapes that would be impractical in a hollow instrument.
