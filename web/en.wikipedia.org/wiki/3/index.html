3 (three) is a number, numeral and digit. It is the natural number following 2 and preceding 4, and is the smallest odd prime number and the only prime number preceding a square number. It has religious and cultural significance in many societies.

In mathematics

Three is the second-smallest prime number and the first odd prime. It is the only number that equals the sum of all the numbers below it (3 = 2 + 1), and the only number whose sum of its digits raised appropriately reproduces classic arithmetic curiosities. A triangle, the polygon with three sides, is the simplest polygon and the only rigid one, which is why trusses and tripods rely on triangular stability. Three is a triangular number, a Fibonacci number, and an approximate value of π (3.14159...) and of e (2.718...) to one significant figure. The three-body problem — predicting the motion of three mutually gravitating bodies — is a classic example of chaotic dynamics with no general closed-form solution.

In science

Lithium has atomic number 3. Humans perceive colour through three types of cone cells, and ordinary matter is described as existing in three spatial dimensions. DNA encodes proteins using triplet codons, and the elementary particles of the Standard Model fall into three generations of quarks and leptons.

In religion and culture

Three recurs throughout world religions: the Christian Holy Trinity, the Hindu Trimurti, the Three Jewels of Buddhism, and the three patriarchs of Judaism. In folklore, wishes, trials, and brothers often come in threes, a pattern known as the rule of three, also used in rhetoric and writing to make groupings memorable — as in "life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness".
