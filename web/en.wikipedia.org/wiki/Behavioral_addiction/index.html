Behavioral addiction

Behavioral addiction
Addictive behaviours engage the brain's reward system
SpecialtyPsychiatry, addiction medicine
ExamplesGambling disorder, gaming disorder, compulsive buying

Behavioral addiction is a form of addiction that involves a compulsion to engage in a rewarding non-substance-related behavior despite negative consequences to the person's physical, mental, social, or financial well-being. Such behaviors — sometimes termed natural rewards — include gambling, eating, shopping, playing video games, and using the internet, and they can activate the brain's reward system in ways comparable to addictive drugs.

Classification

The American Psychiatric Association's DSM-5 (2013) reclassified gambling disorder under "Substance-Related and Addictive Disorders" — the first behavioral addiction to be formally included — and listed Internet gaming disorder as a condition warranting further study. The World Health Organization's ICD-11 (adopted 2019) includes gambling disorder and gaming disorder under disorders due to addictive behaviors. Other proposed behavioral addictions, such as compulsive sexual behavior disorder, are classified elsewhere in ICD-11 as impulse control disorders.

Mechanisms

Research implicates the mesolimbic dopamine pathway, particularly the nucleus accumbens, in both substance and behavioral addictions. Genetic factors can play a role, as can dysregulation of neurotransmitters such as dopamine and serotonin. A gene transcription factor, ΔFosB, has been identified as a common factor involved in both behavioral and drug addictions, which are associated with the same set of neural adaptations in the reward system.

Treatment

Treatment typically involves psychotherapy, especially cognitive behavioral therapy, motivational interviewing, and twelve-step or peer-support groups adapted from substance-use programs (such as Gamblers Anonymous). Some medications used for substance dependence have been trialled for behavioral addictions, though no drug is broadly approved specifically for them.
