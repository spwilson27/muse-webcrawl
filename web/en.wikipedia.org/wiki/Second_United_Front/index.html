The Second United Front was the alliance between the Chinese Nationalist Party (Kuomintang, KMT) and the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) formed in 1937 to resist the Japanese invasion of China during the Second Sino-Japanese War. It followed the collapse of the First United Front in 1927 and a decade of civil war between the two parties.

Formation

Pressure for a united resistance grew after Japan seized Manchuria in 1931 and advanced into northern China. The CCP, following the Comintern's popular-front line and its own August 1 Declaration of 1935, called for a national united front against Japan. The decisive event was the Xi'an Incident of December 1936, in which Nationalist commander Zhang Xueliang detained Chiang Kai-shek and compelled him to agree to end the civil war and resist Japan. After the Marco Polo Bridge Incident of July 1937 opened full-scale war, the alliance was formalised in September 1937: the Communist Red Army was nominally incorporated into the National Revolutionary Army as the Eighth Route Army and the New Fourth Army, while the Communists pledged to suspend land revolution and accept the leadership of the central government, in return for legal status and subsidies.

Wartime cooperation and friction

Cooperation was real in the war's first phase — Communist units fought alongside Nationalist forces at Pingxingguan and in guerrilla operations behind Japanese lines — but mutual suspicion never disappeared. The Nationalists blockaded the Communist base area at Yan'an, and clashes between the two sides multiplied from 1939. The New Fourth Army Incident of January 1941, in which Nationalist troops annihilated a New Fourth Army column and imprisoned its commander, effectively ended the alliance in practice, although its formal shell was preserved until 1945 to avoid the appearance of disunity.

Aftermath

During the war the Communists used the breathing space to expand their base areas and armies enormously, growing from some tens of thousands of troops in 1937 to over a million regulars by 1945. When Japan surrendered, the two parties raced to occupy formerly Japanese-held territory, and full-scale civil war resumed in 1946, ending in the Communist victory of 1949.
