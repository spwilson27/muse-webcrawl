World Aquatics, formerly known as FINA (French: Fédération internationale de natation), is the international federation recognised by the International Olympic Committee (IOC) for administering international competitions in aquatic sports. It is one of several international federations which administer a given sport or discipline for both the IOC and the international community. Its headquarters are in Lausanne, Switzerland.

History

FINA was founded on 19 July 1908 in the Manchester Hotel in London, at the end of the 1908 Summer Olympics, by representatives of eight European swimming federations (Belgium, Denmark, Finland, France, Germany, Great Britain, Hungary, and Sweden). Its remit expanded from swimming to standardise rules for swimming, diving, and water polo. Membership grew from 14 nations in 1928 to over 200 by the 21st century. In June 2022 the federation's bureau voted to rename it World Aquatics, a change that took effect in January 2023, ahead of the 2024 Paris Olympics; the FINA name had been in use for 114 years.

Disciplines and events

World Aquatics governs six aquatic sports: swimming (pool and open water marathon swimming), diving, high diving, water polo, and artistic swimming (formerly synchronised swimming, renamed in 2017). Its flagship event is the biennial World Aquatics Championships (first held in Belgrade in 1973), which combine all disciplines and rank among the largest multi-sport events in the world. Other competitions include the Swimming World Cup, the World Swimming Championships (25 m), the World Junior Championships, and the water polo World Leagues and Cups.

Governance and controversies

The federation is led by a president and bureau elected by its congress of national federations. Long-serving president Julio Maglione (2009–2021) was succeeded by Husain Al-Musallam of Kuwait in 2021. World Aquatics has faced criticism over governance and finances, including US federal indictments of senior officials, and introduced reforms such as an independent Aquatics Integrity Unit (2023). In 2022 it adopted a policy restricting transgender women who had undergone male puberty from women's elite events, announcing plans for an "open" category that was trialled, with limited uptake, at the 2023 Swimming World Cup in Berlin. Following Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, Russian and Belarusian athletes were barred, with a later pathway for neutral individual participation.

Records and rules

World Aquatics ratifies world records in long-course (50 m) and short-course (25 m) pools and sets technical rules for facilities, officiating, and equipment — including the controversial 2008–2009 era of full-body polyurethane swimsuits, whose records prompted a ban on non-textile suits from 2010.
