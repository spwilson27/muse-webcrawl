Sri Lanka national cricket team

The Sri Lanka men's national cricket team, nicknamed The Lions, represents Sri Lanka in men's international cricket. It is a full member of the International Cricket Council (ICC) with Test, One-Day International (ODI) and Twenty20 International (T20I) status. The team first played international cricket (as Ceylon) in 1926–27 and was awarded Test status in 1981, which made Sri Lanka the eighth Test cricket playing nation.

History

Cricket was introduced to the island by the British in the early 19th century. As Ceylon, the side played representative matches against touring teams for decades before gaining Test status in 1981. Sri Lanka played its inaugural Test match against England at the P. Sara Oval, Colombo, in February 1982.

The team's greatest triumph came at the 1996 Cricket World Cup, which Sri Lanka co-hosted with India and Pakistan. Captained by Arjuna Ranatunga, Sri Lanka defeated Australia in the final at Lahore to win the tournament, revolutionising one-day batting with aggressive opening play by Sanath Jayasuriya and Romesh Kaluwitharana. Sri Lanka later won the 2014 ICC World Twenty20 and was a co-champion of the 2002 ICC Champions Trophy.

Players

Sri Lanka has produced some of cricket's most distinctive players, including Muttiah Muralitharan, the highest wicket-taker in both Test and ODI cricket; batsmen Kumar Sangakkara, Mahela Jayawardene, Aravinda de Silva and Tillakaratne Dilshan; and fast bowler Lasith Malinga, famed for his sling-action yorkers. The team is administered by Sri Lanka Cricket.
