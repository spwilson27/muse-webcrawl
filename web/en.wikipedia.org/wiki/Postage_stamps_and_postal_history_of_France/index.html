Postage stamps and postal history of France

The postal history of France can be traced from the royal courier services of the late Middle Ages to the modern La Poste, and France was among the first countries to issue adhesive postage stamps. France's first stamps appeared on 1 January 1849, bearing the head of Cérès, goddess of agriculture, a symbol of the French Second Republic.

Early postal services

Louis XI established a royal postal relay service in 1477. Under Henri IV and his minister Maximilien de Béthune, and then under Cardinal Richelieu's postal administrators, the service was opened to the public and farmed out. The modern system dates from the revolution of 1848 and the postal reform championed by Étienne Arago, director of posts, which introduced uniform prepaid postage modelled on the British reform of 1840.

The classic issues

The Cérès issue of 1849–1850 comprised values from 10 centimes to 1 franc; the carmine 1 franc ("un franc Cérès") is a celebrated rarity, including the tête-bêche pairs known as the "Vervins" variety. After Louis-Napoléon's coup, Cérès was replaced by stamps inscribed "République" bearing first the Prince-President and then, from 1852, the Emperor Napoleon III, initially inscribed "Empire français". The laureated issues of the 1860s and the Bordeaux provisional issue of 1870–71, printed during the Franco-Prussian War while Paris was besieged, are classics of philately. The Third Republic restored Cérès in 1870 and issued the "Sage" (Peace and Commerce) designs from 1876.

Twentieth century and modern issues

Famous French designs include the Sower (Semeuse) of Oscar Roty, used from 1903 and revived on coins, and the Marianne definitives that succeeded her from 1944 onward, each generation choosing its own representation of the Republic. France is famed for its engraved stamps and for the work of designers and engravers such as Pierre Gandon. Alongside definitives, La Poste issues commemorative and art stamps reproducing major works of French painting, the annual Red Cross booklet, and the popular "Fête du Timbre". French colonies issued their own stamps or used overprinted French issues until decolonization, and the French postal administrations in foreign countries used French stamps overprinted for places such as Alexandria and Zanzibar.

Collecting

French philately is deep and specialized: collectors study the numbered postmarks (the losange cancellation with office numbers), the star cancels of Paris, mixed frankings of the siege, and the many types of the Napoleon and Cérès issues. The standard catalogue references include Yvert et Tellier, Dallay and Maury.
