Cantons of the Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina

Image placeholder — the ten cantons of the Federation

The Federation of Bosnia and Herzegovina, one of the two entities of Bosnia and Herzegovina, is divided into ten cantons (Bosnian: kantoni, Croatian: županije). The cantons were established by the 1994 Washington Agreement and the Federation's constitution, and function as federal units with their own governments, assemblies and constitutions, responsible for areas such as policing, education, health and housing.

Governance

Each canton has an assembly elected by proportional representation and a government headed by a prime minister. Five cantons have a Bosniak majority, three a Croat majority, and two (Central Bosnia and Herzegovina-Neretva) are ethnically mixed with special power-sharing arrangements. The cantonal structure has been criticised as costly and administratively heavy — the Federation has eleven governments and parliaments including the federal level — and reform proposals recur in Bosnian politics. The numbering dispute over "Canton 10", whose name is contested between the Federation and local authorities, was addressed by the Constitutional Court.
