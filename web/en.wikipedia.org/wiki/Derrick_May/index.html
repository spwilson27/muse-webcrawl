Derrick May (born 6 April 1963), also known as Mayday and Rhythim Is Rhythim, is an American electronic musician from Belleville, Michigan, United States. May is credited with pioneering techno music in the 1980s along with collaborators Juan Atkins and Kevin Saunderson, together known as the Belleville Three.

Career

May met Atkins and Saunderson at Belleville High School and was influenced by the eclectic Detroit radio shows of The Electrifying Mojo and by European electronic acts such as Kraftwerk, alongside funk artists like Parliament. Recording as Rhythim Is Rhythim, he produced "Nude Photo" (1987) and the landmark "Strings of Life" (1987, with pianist Michael James), one of the most famous tracks of the early techno era, whose energetic piano riff helped ignite the British rave scene.

May founded the Transmat label in 1986, releasing his own work and records by artists including Carl Craig. His productions, such as "Kaos", "The Dance" and "Icon", emphasised rhythm, strings and futuristic atmosphere; he famously described techno as "George Clinton and Kraftwerk stuck in an elevator". Though his recorded output slowed after the early 1990s, May remained an influential international DJ and remixer, and his early catalogue has been repeatedly reissued, including the 1995 collection Innovator.

Legacy

Along with Atkins and Saunderson, May is regarded as one of the originators of Detroit techno, a foundational form of electronic dance music that shaped house, rave and club culture worldwide.
