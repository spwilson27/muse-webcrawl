County-level city

County-level cities are the third tier of administration in mainland China.

A county-level city (Chinese: 县级市; pinyin: xiànjí shì) is a county-level administrative division of the People's Republic of China. County-level cities are nominally governed by prefecture-level administrations (and are therefore sometimes called "sub-prefectural"), but many — in particular those designated as directly administered by their province in matters of economics and finance — enjoy substantial autonomy. There are several hundred county-level cities in mainland China; examples include Kunshan, Yiwu, Cixi and Jinjiang, several of which are major manufacturing centres with populations over a million.

Characteristics

Despite the name "city", a county-level city is a large administrative area typically comprising a central urban core (the former county town), smaller towns, and extensive surrounding rural areas and villages. County-level cities are usually created from counties whose urbanisation and economic output have met official criteria; in recent years the conversion of counties into county-level cities has been encouraged as part of urbanisation policy, though approvals were frozen for about two decades before resuming in the 2010s.

Comparison with other divisions

Within the hierarchy of the PRC, county-level cities rank below prefecture-level cities and alongside counties, autonomous counties, districts, banners and autonomous banners. They should not be confused with prefecture-level cities, which are far larger administrative units, or with the four direct-controlled municipalities (Beijing, Shanghai, Tianjin and Chongqing).
