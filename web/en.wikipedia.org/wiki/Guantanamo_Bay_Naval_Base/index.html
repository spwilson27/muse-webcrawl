Guantanamo Bay Naval Base (also called Naval Station Guantanamo Bay, or GTMO) is a United States military base occupying 45 square miles of land and water at Guantánamo Bay, Cuba. It is the oldest overseas U.S. naval base.

History

The United States took control of the bay during the Spanish–American War in 1898. Under the Cuban–American Treaty of 1903, Cuba leased the area to the United States in perpetuity for use as a naval station; a 1934 treaty reaffirmed the lease, which the Cuban government since 1959 has rejected as illegitimate. The base became internationally prominent after 2002, when the Guantanamo Bay detention camp was established there to hold detainees captured in the "war on terror".

Facilities

The base includes an airfield (Leeward Point Field), a harbour, and support facilities for a community of several thousand personnel and dependents. It is entirely surrounded by Cuban territory and a mined "no man's land" along its perimeter fence line.
