Rabat is the capital city of Morocco and its administrative centre, located on the Atlantic coast at the mouth of the Bou Regreg River, opposite its twin city Salé. It is Morocco's political capital — seat of the king, government and foreign embassies — while Casablanca is the economic capital. Rabat's population is under 600,000 in the city proper, making it only the seventh-largest city in the country, but it anchors a growing metropolitan area.

History and sights

The Almohad caliph Yaqub al-Mansur founded Rabat in the 12th century as a fortress (ribat) for campaigns in Iberia; he began the Hassan Tower, an enormous minaret of a mosque never completed, which still dominates the skyline beside the mausoleum of Mohammed V. The Kasbah of the Udayas, with its blue-and-white Andalusian lanes, overlooks the river mouth; the Chellah necropolis layers Roman Sala Colonia beneath a Marinid royal burial ground. After Morocco became a French protectorate in 1912, Resident-General Lyautey made Rabat the capital, and it remained so after independence in 1956. Expelled Muslims and Jews from Spain (Moriscos) settled the twin cities in the 1600s — the short-lived corsair Republic of Salé operated from across the river — and the city's medina preserves that Andalusian heritage.
