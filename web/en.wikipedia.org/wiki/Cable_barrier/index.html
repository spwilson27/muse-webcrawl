Cable Barrier

A cable barrier, also called a wire rope safety barrier, is a roadside or median safety barrier consisting of steel wire ropes mounted on weak posts. When a vehicle strikes it, the posts collapse and the cables deflect, absorbing kinetic energy and redirecting or capturing the vehicle with lower deceleration forces than rigid barriers.

Design

Modern high-tension systems use three or four pre-stretched cables at heights of roughly 460–760 mm, tensioned between anchor terminals and supported by posts set in sockets so they can be replaced quickly after an impact. Low-tension systems, common in earlier decades, relied on cable sag alone. Because the cables deflect substantially — a working width of 2.5 m or more is typical — cable barriers require wider medians or clear zones than concrete barriers.

Performance and use

Studies of median installations, notably in Washington State and Sweden (where wire-rope median barriers are widespread on 2+1 roads), show large reductions in cross-median fatal crashes. Cable barriers are cheaper to install than concrete or W-beam guardrail, cause less severe injuries in many impacts, and are comparatively transparent to snow and drainage, but they require frequent repair after strikes and are less suitable where deflection space is limited.
