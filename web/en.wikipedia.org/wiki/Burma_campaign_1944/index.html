Burma campaign 1944

The fighting in the Burma campaign in 1944 was among the most severe in the South-East Asian theatre of World War II. It took place along the borders between Burma and British India, and Burma and China, and involved the British Fourteenth Army under General William Slim, American and Chinese forces in the north, and the Japanese Fifteenth Army.

Major operations

In early 1944 the Japanese launched Operation U-Go, an invasion of India, which led to the battles of Imphal and Kohima — often called "Britain's greatest battle" — where the Japanese offensive was decisively defeated with enormous losses. In Arakan, the Battle of the Admin Box saw Allied forces hold out using air supply. In northern Burma, Chinese and American forces under Joseph Stilwell advanced to capture Myitkyina, while the Chindits operated behind Japanese lines. By the end of the year, the Fourteenth Army had begun its advance into central Burma, crossing the Chindwin River.

Significance

The defeats of 1944 destroyed Japanese strength in Burma and set the stage for the Allied reconquest of the country in 1945.
