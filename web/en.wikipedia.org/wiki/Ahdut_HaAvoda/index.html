Ahdut HaAvoda

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Labour Zionist pioneers in Palestine (image placeholder)

Ahdut HaAvoda (Hebrew: אחדות העבודה, "Unity of Labour") was a Zionist labour party and movement in Mandate Palestine and later Israel, existing in several incarnations between 1919 and 1968. It championed Labour Zionism: the building of a Jewish national home through the labour of Jewish workers, agricultural settlement, and the trade-union federation, the Histadrut.

First incarnation (1919–1930)

Ahdut HaAvoda was founded in 1919 at Petah Tikva by the union of the Palestine branch of Poale Zion with non-party labour groups. Its leading figures included David Ben-Gurion, Yitzhak Ben-Zvi, and Berl Katznelson. It founded the Histadrut in 1920 and the Haganah defence organization. In 1930 it merged with Hapoel Hatzair to form Mapai, the dominant party of the Yishuv and early Israel.

Second incarnation (1944–1968)

In 1944 the left wing of Mapai (Faction B) split away and revived the name Ahdut HaAvoda, closely associated with the kibbutz movement HaKibbutz HaMeuhad and its Palmach-linked leadership, including Yitzhak Tabenkin, Yisrael Galili, and Yigal Allon. In 1948 it joined with Hashomer Hatzair to form Mapam. Ahdut HaAvoda seceded from Mapam in 1954 over attitudes toward the Soviet Union, and thereafter ran as an independent party, winning seats in the Knesset. In 1965 it entered an Alignment with Mapai, and in 1968 the two merged (with Rafi) into the Israeli Labor Party.

Ideology

The movement combined socialism with activist Zionism, favouring large-scale immigration, land settlement, and a strong Jewish defence force. Its later incarnation was notably hawkish on security and sceptical of concessions, and its kibbutz base gave it an unusually disciplined organization.
