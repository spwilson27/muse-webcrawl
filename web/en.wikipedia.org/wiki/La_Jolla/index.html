La Jolla

La Jolla is a hilly, seaside community within the city of San Diego, California, occupying about seven miles of curving coastline along the Pacific Ocean. Known for its picturesque beaches, rugged coves, and affluent neighborhoods, La Jolla is also a major center of scientific research, home to the University of California, San Diego, the Scripps Institution of Oceanography, and numerous biotechnology institutes.

LocationSan Diego, California, United States
Population~46,000
Named forSpanish la joya, "the jewel" (disputed)

Geography

The community wraps around Mount Soledad and fronts beaches including La Jolla Cove, La Jolla Shores, Windansea Beach, and Black's Beach. The La Jolla Underwater Park, a marine reserve, protects kelp forests and a submarine canyon popular with divers and kayakers; seals and sea lions haul out at the Children's Pool, a small beach protected by a seawall built in 1931.

Institutions

La Jolla's research cluster includes UC San Diego (founded 1960), the Salk Institute for Biological Studies, Scripps Research, the Sanford Burnham Prebys Medical Discovery Institute, and the Birch Aquarium at Scripps. The Museum of Contemporary Art San Diego and the La Jolla Playhouse are leading cultural institutions. Tourism, finance, software, and real estate round out the local economy.

History

The Kumeyaay people inhabited the area, which they called Mat Kulaaxuuy. La Jolla was incorporated into the City of San Diego in 1850 and developed as a resort in the 1890s with the arrival of the railroad; publisher and philanthropist Ellen Browning Scripps became its great benefactor in the early 20th century.
