Knowledge is a central concept of Epistemology: most basically, a true relation between a subject and reality, whether as propositional knowledge (knowing that Paris is in France), know-how (knowing how to ride a bicycle), or acquaintance (knowing a person or a city). Plato's Theaetetus already canvassed the definition 'true belief with an account'; the formula 'justified true belief' reigned until Gettier's 1963 counterexamples.

Contemporary work

Post-Gettier epistemology branches into externalism (knowledge as reliably produced belief), virtue epistemology, and the knowledge-first programme (Timothy Williamson) that treats knowledge as a basic mental state, unanalysable into belief plus conditions. Social epistemology studies testimony, expertise and disagreement — how nearly all of what we know we owe to others.
