Financial Accounting Standards Board

Financial Accounting Standards Board Abbreviation: FASB
Formation: 1973
Headquarters: Norwalk, Connecticut, United States
Type: Non-profit standard-setting body
Parent: Financial Accounting Foundation
Purpose: Establishing U.S. GAAP for private-sector entities

The Financial Accounting Standards Board (FASB) is a private, non-profit organization whose primary purpose is to establish and improve Generally Accepted Accounting Principles (GAAP) within the United States in the public's interest. It was created in 1973, replacing the Accounting Principles Board of the American Institute of Certified Public Accountants.

Structure

The FASB operates under the oversight of the Financial Accounting Foundation (FAF), which appoints its seven full-time board members and funds its activities, largely through accounting support fees paid by publicly traded companies under the Sarbanes–Oxley Act. The Securities and Exchange Commission recognizes FASB standards as authoritative for public companies.

Codification

In 2009 the FASB launched the Accounting Standards Codification, which reorganized thousands of individual pronouncements into a single structured source of authoritative GAAP. Updates are issued as Accounting Standards Updates (ASUs), which amend the codification rather than creating new standalone standards.

Convergence with IFRS

Beginning with the 2002 Norwalk Agreement, the FASB worked with the International Accounting Standards Board (IASB) toward convergence of U.S. GAAP and International Financial Reporting Standards. Joint projects produced substantially converged standards on revenue recognition and leases, though full convergence was not achieved.
