A hackerspace (also referred to as a hacklab, hackspace, or makerspace) is a community-operated, often not-for-profit, workspace where people with common interests — often in computers, electronics, technology, science, digital art and making — can meet, socialise and collaborate. Hackerspaces typically offer shared tools and equipment (such as 3D printers, laser cutters, machine tools and electronics benches) too costly or bulky for individuals, and host workshops, talks and social events.

History

The movement grew out of the German hacker scene around the Chaos Computer Club; c-base in Berlin (founded 1995) is often cited as one of the first independent hackerspaces, followed by venues such as Metalab in Vienna (2006). North American spaces including NYC Resistor, HacDC and Noisebridge opened in 2007–2008 after European tours by American hackers, and the site hackerspaces.org coordinated a global directory of over a thousand active spaces. Related but distinct facilities include fab labs (originating at MIT) and commercial workshop chains.

Organisation and activities

Most hackerspaces are funded by membership dues, governed by consensus or elected boards, and kept open to members around the clock. Activities range from electronics and robotics projects, software development and lock-sport, to repair cafés, amateur radio, brewing and crafts. Many spaces emphasise open-source values, knowledge sharing and "do-ocracy" — decisions made by those who do the work.
