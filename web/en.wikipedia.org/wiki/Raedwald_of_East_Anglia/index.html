Rædwald (Old English: Rædwald), also written Raedwald or Redwald, was a 7th-century king of East Anglia, a long-lived Anglo-Saxon kingdom that included the present-day English counties of Norfolk and Suffolk. He reigned from about 599 until his death around 624.

Reign

Rædwald was the son of Tytila and a member of the Wuffingas dynasty, which claimed descent from Woden. During his reign East Anglia rose to become the most powerful kingdom in southern England: the Venerable Bede lists Rædwald as a bretwalda, an overlord of the southern Anglo-Saxon kingdoms. In 616 or 617 he sheltered the exiled Edwin of Deira and defeated and killed Æthelfrith of Northumbria at the Battle of the River Idle, installing Edwin on the Northumbrian throne.

Religion

Rædwald was baptised in Kent, but on returning home — influenced, Bede says, by his wife — he maintained in the same temple an altar for Christian worship alongside one for sacrifices to the old gods, a syncretism Bede strongly disapproved of.

Sutton Hoo

The great ship burial at Sutton Hoo (Mound 1), excavated in 1939 and containing a wealth of treasure including the famous helmet, is widely believed to be Rædwald's burial, though no inscription names him and the identification cannot be proven.
