Piling

A piling is a long structural element of timber, steel, or concrete driven, drilled, or jetted into the ground to form part of the deep foundation of a building, bridge, or marine structure. The process of installing pilings is called pile driving.

Function

Piles transfer structural loads through weak surface soils to stronger strata below, working either by end bearing on a firm layer or by skin friction along the shaft. They are used where shallow foundations would settle excessively, and for wharves, piers, and offshore structures where the foundation must be built through water.

Types and installation

Timber piles are among the oldest foundation elements; modern practice also uses steel H-piles and pipe piles, precast concrete piles, and cast-in-place piles formed in bored holes. Driven piles are installed with drop, diesel, hydraulic, or vibratory hammers, while bored piles reduce noise and vibration in urban sites.
