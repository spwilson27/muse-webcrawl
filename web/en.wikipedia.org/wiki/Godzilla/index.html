Godzilla (Japanese: ゴジラ, Gojira) is a fictional monster, or kaiju, that debuted in the 1954 Japanese film Godzilla, directed by Ishirō Honda and produced by Toho Co., Ltd. Godzilla has since become a worldwide pop culture icon, appearing in dozens of films, television programs, video games, novels, comic books, and other media.

Overview

Godzilla is depicted as an enormous, destructive, prehistoric sea monster awakened and empowered by nuclear radiation. With the 1954 film, Godzilla was conceived as a metaphor for nuclear weapons and the destruction they caused in Japan at the end of the Second World War, including the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki and the contamination of the fishing vessel Daigo Fukuryū Maru. The character's design combines features of a tyrannosaur, an iguanodon, stegosaurus-like dorsal plates, and rough, keloid-scarred skin.

Film history

The Toho films are conventionally divided into eras: the Shōwa era (1954–1975), in which Godzilla gradually evolved from villain to hero; the Heisei era (1984–1995), which returned the character to a destructive force; the Millennium era (1999–2004); and the Reiwa era, beginning with Shin Godzilla (2016) and continuing with Godzilla Minus One (2023), which won the Academy Award for Best Visual Effects. American productions include the 1998 film Godzilla and Legendary Pictures' MonsterVerse series, beginning with Godzilla (2014) and continuing through Godzilla x Kong: The New Empire (2024). The franchise, with more than three dozen films, is recognised by Guinness World Records as the longest continuously running film franchise.

Abilities and portrayal

Godzilla's signature weapon is its "atomic breath", a beam of radioactive energy emitted from its mouth. The monster has typically been portrayed by an actor in a latex suit (suitmation), a technique pioneered by Eiji Tsuburaya, though later films use animatronics, computer-generated imagery, and motion capture. Godzilla's iconic roar was created by composer Akira Ifukube, who produced the sound by rubbing a resin-coated leather glove along the loosened strings of a double bass.

Cultural impact

Godzilla is widely regarded as the "King of the Monsters" and is one of the most recognisable symbols of Japanese popular culture worldwide. The character has a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame and has influenced generations of filmmakers and the giant-monster genre as a whole.
