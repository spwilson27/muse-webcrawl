Roaring Forties

LocationSouthern Hemisphere, 40°S–50°S
DirectionWesterly (west to east)
RelatedFurious Fifties, Screaming Sixties

The Roaring Forties are strong westerly winds that occur in the Southern Hemisphere, generally between the latitudes of 40° and 50° south. The strong west-to-east air currents are caused by the combination of air being displaced from the Equator towards the South Pole, the Earth's rotation, and the scarcity of landmasses to serve as windbreaks at those latitudes.

Sailing

The Roaring Forties were a major aid to ships travelling the clipper route from Europe to Australia and New Zealand, and later to the west coast of the Americas via Cape Horn. Clipper captains would dive south into the forties to pick up the winds for the eastward run, accepting severe weather in exchange for speed. The zones further south are known, by analogy, as the Furious Fifties and the Screaming Sixties.

Climate

The winds are strongest over the Southern Ocean south of the Indian Ocean and around New Zealand. They drive the Antarctic Circumpolar Current and strongly influence the climates of southern New Zealand, Tasmania, southern South America and South Africa's Cape region. In recent decades the belt of westerlies has tended to shift poleward, a trend associated with changes in Southern Hemisphere circulation.
