Asymptotic giant branch

The asymptotic giant branch (AGB) is a region of the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram populated by evolved cool luminous stars. It is a period of stellar evolution experienced by all low- to intermediate-mass stars (about 0.6–10 solar masses) late in their lives, after the red giant branch and horizontal branch phases. AGB stars are characterised by an inert carbon–oxygen core, helium- and hydrogen-burning shells, deep convection, and strong stellar winds.

Evolution

On the AGB, energy comes mainly from a thin shell of helium fusion around the carbon–oxygen core, subject to periodic instabilities called thermal pulses. Between pulses, the third dredge-up mixes newly formed carbon and s-process elements to the surface; stars that dredge up enough carbon become carbon stars. Mira variables are pulsating AGB stars.

Mass loss and end state

AGB stars lose mass rapidly through dusty winds — the "superwind" phase can remove the entire envelope — enriching the interstellar medium with carbon, nitrogen, and heavy elements essential to later generations of stars and planets. The expelled envelope becomes a planetary nebula, and the exposed core cools as a white dwarf. The Sun is expected to pass through the AGB in roughly 7–8 billion years.
