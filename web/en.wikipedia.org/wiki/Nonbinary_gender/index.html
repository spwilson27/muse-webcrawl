Nonbinary gender

Nonbinary (also spelled non-binary) is an umbrella term for gender identities that are not solely male or female — that is, identities outside the gender binary. Nonbinary people may identify as having two or more genders (bigender or trigender), having no gender (agender), moving between genders (genderfluid), or a gender that is partly one gender and partly another (demigender), among other experiences. The term genderqueer has a similar meaning and an older history of use.

Pronouns and expression

Many nonbinary people use gender-neutral pronouns, most commonly singular they/them in English, though usage varies by individual and language. Nonbinary identity is distinct from sexual orientation and from being intersex; some nonbinary people consider themselves transgender and some do not, and medical or social transition needs vary widely.

History and recognition

Identities outside a strict male/female classification have existed in many cultures, including hijra communities in South Asia, Two-Spirit people in some Indigenous North American cultures, and fa'afafine in Samoa. The specific term "nonbinary" entered wide use in the 2000s and 2010s. A growing number of jurisdictions allow a nonbinary or "X" gender marker on official documents, and nonbinary options appear in censuses and surveys in some countries.

Discrimination

Surveys in several countries report that nonbinary people experience significant rates of discrimination, harassment and difficulty accessing services that assume binary gender, and legal protections vary widely by jurisdiction.
