The Dream Team was the nickname of the United States men's national basketball team that won the gold medal at the 1992 Summer Olympics in Barcelona. The first American Olympic team to feature active NBA players, it is widely regarded as the greatest basketball team ever assembled and one of the most famous teams in sports history, credited with massively increasing the global popularity of basketball.

Background

FIBA rules had long barred NBA professionals from Olympic play while other nations used state-supported "amateur" stars. After the US college team lost the 1988 Olympic semi-final to the Soviet Union, FIBA voted in 1989 to open the Games to professionals, and USA Basketball assembled a squad of the NBA's greatest names.

Roster and tournament

Coached by Chuck Daly, the team included Michael Jordan, Magic Johnson, Larry Bird, Charles Barkley, Karl Malone, John Stockton, Scottie Pippen, Patrick Ewing, David Robinson, Clyde Drexler, Chris Mullin and college star Christian Laettner; eleven of the twelve later entered the Hall of Fame as individuals. The Americans won their eight games by an average of 43.8 points without calling a single timeout, beating Croatia 117–85 in the final. Opposing players famously asked for photographs and autographs, and the team's celebrity drew enormous crowds throughout the tournament.

Legacy

The Dream Team globalised basketball: international participation, investment and talent pipelines surged, and within a generation international players such as Dirk Nowitzki, Manu Ginóbili and later Giannis Antetokounmpo and Nikola Jokić starred in the NBA. The 1992 squad was inducted collectively into the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame in 2010, and later US teams — including the 2008 "Redeem Team" — have measured themselves against its standard.
