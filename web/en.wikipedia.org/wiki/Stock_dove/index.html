The stock dove (Columba oenas) is a species of bird in the family Columbidae, the doves and pigeons. It is widely distributed in western and central parts of the Palearctic, from Iceland and the British Isles east to Central Asia, and south to North Africa. It is partly migratory: northern populations move south and west in winter, while birds in milder regions are resident.

Description

The stock dove is similar in size and plumage to the common wood pigeon, but is smaller and darker, with a shorter tail and only partial black bars on the folded wing. It has a blue-grey head and underparts, iridescent green and pink patches on the sides of the neck, a pinkish breast, and a pale yellowish bill. In flight it shows black wingtips and two short dark wing bars. Its song is a series of short, deep, disyllabic cooing notes, quite different from the familiar five-note phrase of the wood pigeon.

Habitat and behaviour

Unlike most pigeons, the stock dove nests in holes — typically in old trees, cliff faces, or nest boxes — which gave the species its name, from the "stocks" or trunks of trees. It frequents open woodland edges, parkland, and farmland, feeding on seeds, grain, green leaves, and invertebrates. It usually lays two white eggs in the nest hole, and several broods may be raised in a long breeding season.
