The South Eastern main line is a major railway line in south-east England, linking London with Dover and the Kent coast. Built by the South Eastern Railway (SER), it runs from the London termini of Charing Cross and Cannon Street through Sevenoaks, Tonbridge and Ashford to Folkestone and Dover Priory, a distance of about 77 miles (124 km) from Charing Cross.

History

The line was constructed in stages during the early 1840s. The SER reached Ashford in 1842, Folkestone in 1843 and Dover in 1844. Because of the expense of tunnelling through the North Downs, the route took an indirect course via Redhill, sharing track with the London, Brighton and South Coast Railway between London Bridge and Redhill — an arrangement that caused decades of operational friction. A more direct route via Orpington and Sevenoaks, including the Sevenoaks and Polhill tunnels, opened in 1868 and became the principal main line.

The line was famously constrained by the narrow tunnels at Hastings (on the connected Hastings line) and by the restrictive loading gauge of the SER's early construction, which required specially built rolling stock for some services. Boat trains to Dover and Folkestone for cross-Channel ferries were a prestigious feature of the line for over a century, until the opening of the Channel Tunnel in 1994 shifted traffic to the new route via Ashford International.

Services and electrification

The line is electrified with the 750 V DC third-rail system inherited from the Southern Railway, which completed electrification of the route in the 1960s as part of the Kent Coast electrification scheme. Since 2009, domestic high-speed services using Class 395 trains have run over the line between Ashford and London via High Speed 1 from Ebbsfleet. The principal operator is Southeastern, running suburban, main-line and high-speed services.
