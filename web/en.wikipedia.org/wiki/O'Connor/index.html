O'Connor

O'Connor is an Irish surname, derived from the Gaelic Ó Conchobhair, meaning "descendant of Conchobhar" (a personal name often glossed as "lover of canines" or "wolf-lover"). Several distinct O'Connor septs arose in different regions of Ireland, most famously the O'Connors of Connacht, who claimed descent from Conchobar mac Nessa's namesake ancestors and produced the last High Kings of Ireland, Tairrdelbach Ua Conchobair and his son Ruaidrí Ua Conchobair.

History

The O'Connors of Connacht traced their line to the legendary Conn of the Hundred Battles. Their kings ruled Connacht for centuries from Cruachan and later Clonalis; the family seat, Clonalis House near Castlerea, County Roscommon, remains the home of the O'Conor Don, recognised as the senior descendant of the last High King. Other major septs included the O'Connors of Kerry (Ó Conchobhair Ciarraí), of Offaly (Ó Conchobhair Failghe), and of Corcomroe in County Clare. The name spread worldwide with the Irish diaspora and is now common throughout the English-speaking world.

Notable people

Notable bearers include the author Flannery O'Connor, the singer Sinéad O'Connor, Sandra Day O'Connor, the first woman to serve on the United States Supreme Court, and Feargus O'Connor, the Chartist leader.
