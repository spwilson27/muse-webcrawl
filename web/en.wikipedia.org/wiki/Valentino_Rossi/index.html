Valentino Rossi (born 16 February 1979) is an Italian former professional motorcycle road racer and nine-time Grand Prix world champion. Widely regarded as one of the greatest motorcycle racers of all time, he is the only rider in history to win world championships in four different classes: 125cc, 250cc, 500cc and MotoGP. His career-long race number 46 and nickname "The Doctor" became among the most recognisable brands in world sport.

Early life

Rossi was born in Urbino and grew up in Tavullia, in the Marche region of Italy. His father Graziano Rossi was himself a Grand Prix motorcycle racer. Valentino began racing in go-karts and minimoto, winning the Italian minimoto championship, before moving to the Italian 125cc championship, which he won in 1995, and the European championship.

125cc and 250cc

Rossi entered Grand Prix racing in 1996 with Aprilia. After a debut season including his first win at Brno, he dominated the 1997 125cc championship, winning 11 of 15 races. Moving to the 250cc class in 1998, he finished second in his first season and won the 1999 title with nine victories.

500cc and MotoGP

Joining Honda for the 2000 season, Rossi finished runner-up in his debut 500cc campaign and won the final 500cc world championship in 2001. He then won the first two MotoGP titles of the four-stroke era in 2002 and 2003 with Honda. In a move considered a major gamble, he switched to the unfancied Yamaha team for 2004 and won the title in his first season with them, repeating the feat in 2005. After two injury- and misfortune-hit seasons, he reclaimed the championship in 2008 and 2009.

Rossi moved to Ducati for 2011 and 2012, but the partnership produced no victories, and he returned to Yamaha in 2013. He won races again and finished championship runner-up in 2014, 2015 and 2016, most controversially losing the 2015 title amid his clash with Marc Márquez. His final Grand Prix victory came at Assen in 2017. He retired from motorcycle racing at the end of the 2021 season, with 115 Grand Prix wins and 235 podiums.

Car racing and team ownership

After retiring from MotoGP, Rossi pursued a second career in GT car racing, competing in the GT World Challenge Europe and the FIA World Endurance Championship with Team WRT, including appearances at the 24 Hours of Le Mans. He also owns the VR46 Racing Team, which competes in MotoGP, and the VR46 Riders Academy in Tavullia, which has trained several Italian Grand Prix winners.
