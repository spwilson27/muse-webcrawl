Rugby Football League
Founded:1895 (as Northern Rugby Football Union)
Headquarters:Leeds, England
Sport:Rugby league

The Rugby Football League (RFL) is the governing body for professional rugby league in England. Based at Sportcity in Manchester, it administers the England national rugby league team, the Challenge Cup, Super League, and the leagues that make up the British rugby league system. The RFL also administers the amateur and junior game across Great Britain in association with the British Amateur Rugby League Association (BARLA).

History

The RFL was founded on 29 August 1895 as the Northern Rugby Football Union at a meeting of 21 clubs at the George Hotel, Huddersfield, which broke away from the Rugby Football Union over the issue of "broken time" payments to working-class players. The name was changed to the Rugby Football League in 1922.

Competitions

The RFL organises the Challenge Cup, one of the oldest knock-out competitions in sport, first held in 1896–97, and oversees the league structure headed by Super League, below which sit the Championship and League 1.

International

The RFL was a founding member of the International Rugby League and fielded the Great Britain team until 2007, after which the home nations — England, Scotland and Wales — competed separately, with England managed by the RFL.
