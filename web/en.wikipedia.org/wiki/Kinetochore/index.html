Kinetochore

The kinetochore is a disc-shaped protein structure associated with duplicated chromatids in eukaryotic cells. It assembles on the centromere and links the chromosome to microtubules of the mitotic spindle during cell division, so that sister chromatids can be pulled to opposite poles of the cell in mitosis and meiosis.

Structure

The kinetochore is a large multi-protein assembly containing more than a hundred different proteins in many species. It is conventionally divided into an inner kinetochore, which binds centromeric chromatin containing the histone variant CENP-A, and an outer kinetochore, which interacts with spindle microtubules. The KMN network — composed of the KNL1, Mis12 and Ndc80 complexes — forms the principal microtubule-binding interface.

Function

Kinetochores mediate attachment of chromosomes to spindle microtubules, harness the forces of microtubule depolymerisation to move chromosomes, and generate the "wait anaphase" signal of the spindle assembly checkpoint. This checkpoint delays the onset of anaphase until every kinetochore is properly attached under tension, preventing chromosome missegregation and aneuploidy.

Kinetochores in disease

Defects in kinetochore function contribute to chromosomal instability, a hallmark of many cancers, and errors in kinetochore–microtubule attachment are a major source of the aneuploidy seen in ageing oocytes.
