Bethnal Green is an area in the East End of London, in the London Borough of Tower Hamlets, about 3 miles (5 km) northeast of Charing Cross. Historically a hamlet in the parish of Stepney, it grew into a densely populated working-class district during the 19th century and has long been known for successive waves of immigration and for industries such as furniture-making, weaving and tailoring.

History

The name derives from the Anglo-Saxon Blithehale, meaning "happy hall" or "blithe corner". Huguenot silk weavers settled in the area in the 17th and 18th centuries, and Bethnal Green became notorious in the Victorian period for overcrowded slums. The Boundary Estate, built around Arnold Circus at the end of the 19th century, was one of London's first council housing schemes. During the Second World War, the unfinished Bethnal Green tube station was used as an air-raid shelter; on 3 March 1943, 173 people died in a crush on the station staircase in the worst civilian disaster of the war in Britain.

Culture and landmarks

Local landmarks include the V&A Museum of Childhood (now Young V&A), Bethnal Green Working Men's Club, and the green itself, associated with the legend of the Blind Beggar of Bethnal Green. Since the late 20th century the area has had a large British Bangladeshi community, and its western edges around Shoreditch have become associated with London's art and nightlife scenes.
