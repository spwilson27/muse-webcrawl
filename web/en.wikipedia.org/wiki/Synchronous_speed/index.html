Synchronous speed

QuantityRotational speed
UnitRevolutions per minute (rpm)
Applies toAC electric motors and generators

The synchronous speed of an alternating-current electric motor is the speed at which the magnetic field rotates in the stator. It is determined entirely by the frequency of the supply and the number of magnetic poles of the machine, and it sets the reference speed against which motor operation is described.

Formula

Synchronous speed is given by ns = 120f / P, where f is the supply frequency in hertz and P is the number of poles. Thus a two-pole motor on a 50 Hz supply has a synchronous speed of 3,000 rpm, a four-pole motor 1,500 rpm, and a six-pole motor 1,000 rpm; on a 60 Hz supply the corresponding figures are 3,600, 1,800, and 1,200 rpm.

Application

A synchronous motor's rotor turns exactly at synchronous speed. An induction (asynchronous) motor must run slightly slower — the difference, expressed as a percentage of synchronous speed, is called slip and is what induces rotor currents and produces torque. Under load, a typical induction motor's slip is a few percent. Synchronous speed is therefore fundamental to the design, selection, and speed control of AC machines, including the use of variable-frequency drives.
