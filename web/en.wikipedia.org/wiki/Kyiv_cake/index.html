Kyiv cake

Kyiv cake (Ukrainian: торт «Київський», tort Kyivskyi) is a dessert cake produced in Kyiv, Ukraine, since 6 December 1956 by the Karl Marx Confectionery Factory, now part of the Roshen corporation, which holds a patent for the cake. It has become one of the symbols of Kyiv and is a popular souvenir.

Composition

The cake consists of two light, airy layers of nut meringue made with a fermented egg-white mixture and a blend of nuts—traditionally hazelnuts, though cashews and other nuts have also been used—sandwiched and coated with a chocolate buttercream known as Charlotte cream. The top is decorated with cream ornamentation, often including a design featuring chestnut leaves and blossom, referencing Kyiv's emblematic chestnut trees.

History

According to the factory's account, the recipe arose partly from an accident: confectioners forgot to refrigerate a batch of egg whites intended for sponge cake, and the chief confectioner, Konstantin Petrenko, later experimented with the resulting solidified foam. The cake's recipe and production technology were refined over years and kept as a trade secret. Kyiv cake became a signature product presented by Ukrainian delegations on official visits and remains widely sold across Ukraine and abroad.
