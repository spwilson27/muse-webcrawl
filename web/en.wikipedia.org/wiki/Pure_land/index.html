A pure land is the celestial realm of a buddha or bodhisattva in Mahayana Buddhism. The term is a translation of the Sanskrit buddhakṣetra ("buddha field"), a realm purified by a buddha's merit and vows where beings may be reborn and train towards enlightenment under ideal conditions, free from the obstacles of the ordinary world.

Notable pure lands

The best known is Sukhāvatī, the Western Pure Land of the buddha Amitābha, described in the Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtras. Other pure lands include Abhirati, the eastern land of Akshobhya; Vaidūryanirbhāsa, the land of the Medicine Buddha Bhaiṣajyaguru; and the Tuṣita heaven associated with Maitreya.

Pure Land Buddhism

Around these realms developed Pure Land Buddhism, one of the most widely practised traditions of East Asian Buddhism, centred on devotion to Amitābha and the recitation of his name (nianfo / nembutsu) in the hope of rebirth in Sukhāvatī. Major schools include the Chinese Pure Land tradition and the Japanese Jōdo-shū and Jōdo Shinshū founded by Hōnen and Shinran.
