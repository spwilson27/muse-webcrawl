The 1928 Winter Olympics, officially the II Olympic Winter Games, were held in St. Moritz, Switzerland, from February 11 to 19, 1928. They were the first Winter Games held as a proper Olympic event in their own right, following the 1924 Chamonix "International Winter Sports Week", which was only retroactively designated an Olympic Games.

Events and highlights

Skeleton returned to the Olympic programme for the first time since 1928's predecessor Games — it would not reappear until 1948, again at St. Moritz. Norway's Johan Grøttumsbråten won the Nordic combined and the 18 km cross-country race, while Swedish skier Per-Erik Hedlund won the 50 km classic in extraordinary weather; the 50 km race began in a blizzard and finished in warm spring weather with a temperature swing of about 25 °C. American Jennison Heaton won the skeleton, and his brother John Heaton took silver. Norwegian figure skater Sonja Henie, aged only 15, won her first of three consecutive Olympic gold medals. Canada dominated ice hockey, winning gold without conceding a goal.

Weather problems

A warm Föhn wind plagued the Games, melting ice and forcing events to be rescheduled or held early in the morning; the 10,000 m speed skating event had to be abandoned entirely because of the poor ice. Despite this, Norway topped the medal table with six golds and 15 medals overall.
