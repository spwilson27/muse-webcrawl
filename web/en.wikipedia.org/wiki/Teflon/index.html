Teflon

Product type: Fluoropolymer brand
Owner: Chemours
Introduced: 1945 (trademark)
Material: PTFE

Teflon is a registered trademark of the Chemours company (a spin-off of DuPont) used for a family of products based primarily on polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE), a synthetic fluoropolymer of tetrafluoroethylene. The name has become a generic term for non-stick cookware coatings.

Discovery

PTFE was accidentally discovered in 1938 by Roy J. Plunkett, a chemist working for DuPont in New Jersey, when a sample of tetrafluoroethylene gas polymerized spontaneously into a waxy white solid. The material proved remarkably inert, heat-resistant, and slippery — it has one of the lowest coefficients of friction of any solid.

Applications

Teflon coatings are used in non-stick cookware, industrial gaskets and seals, electrical insulation, fabric protectors, and medical devices. Gore-Tex is a related expanded PTFE product.

Health and environment

Historically, PTFE manufacture used the processing aid PFOA, a persistent pollutant associated with health concerns near production sites; it has been phased out in the United States. Overheated PTFE coatings can decompose above roughly 260 °C, releasing fumes that cause polymer fume fever in humans and are lethal to birds.
