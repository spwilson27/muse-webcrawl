Bounty (reward)

A bounty is a payment or reward offered by a government, organisation or individual as an incentive for performing a specified task — classically the capture or killing of a fugitive or enemy, the destruction of pests, or the achievement of some difficult feat. The word derives through Old French from Latin bonitas, "goodness".

[ Image: Nineteenth-century American "Wanted" poster offering a cash bounty for a fugitive's capture ]

Law-enforcement bounties

Rewards for fugitives underpin the American institution of the bounty hunter (bail enforcement agent), who recovers defendants who have skipped bail in exchange for a percentage of the bond — a system abolished or restricted in many countries and some U.S. states. Governments also post rewards for information: the U.S. Rewards for Justice Program has offered sums of up to tens of millions of dollars for information on terrorists, and historical "wanted dead or alive" posters advertised bounties on outlaws of the American West.

Other forms

Agricultural authorities have paid bounties on pests — rats, wolves, coyotes and invasive species — sometimes with perverse results: under the French colonial rat bounty in Hanoi, and the British colonial cobra bounty in India which gives the "cobra effect" its name, people bred the very animals the scheme sought to eliminate. Maritime and military bounties include enlistment bounties for recruits and "head money" paid to warship crews for enemy vessels sunk. In the digital era, bug bounty programmes pay security researchers who responsibly disclose software vulnerabilities, and open-source projects post bounties on desired features or fixes.
