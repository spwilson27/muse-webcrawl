Middle Mongol

Middle Mongol (also called Middle Mongolian) is the stage of the Mongolic language family spoken roughly from the 13th to the early 15th century, spanning the rise of the Mongol Empire under Genghis Khan through the early Yuan dynasty period. It is the language of the most important early Mongolian documents, above all The Secret History of the Mongols.

Classification

Middle Mongol belongs to the Mongolic language family. It is directly ancestral to Classical Mongolian (the literary language from the 17th century onward) and, through spoken dialects, to the modern Mongolic languages including Khalkha Mongolian, Buryat, Oirat and Kalmyk.

Sources

The language is attested in several scripts, reflecting the multilingual administration of the empire:

  • The Secret History of the Mongols (c. 1240), preserved in Chinese characters used phonetically.
  • Documents written in the traditional Mongolian script derived from Uyghur, including imperial edicts and letters.
  • 'Phags-pa script inscriptions, commissioned by Kublai Khan in 1269.
  • Chinese sources such as the Hua-Yi Yiyu glossary.
  • Arabic-script materials, including the Muqaddimat al-Adab Mongolian–Arabic–Persian glossary.
  • Armenian and Persian sources, including the account of Kirakos Gandzaketsi.

Features

Middle Mongol retains the initial consonant h- in many words (later lost in most modern dialects, e.g. Middle Mongol haran "people" vs modern aran). It preserves vowel harmony based on a pharyngeal (ATR-like) distinction and a system of seven vowels. Its consonant inventory lacked initial p- (which became f- then h-), and it showed the beginnings of the shift of s to sh before i.

Morphology is agglutinative, with case suffixes for genitive, accusative, dative-locative, ablative, instrumental and comitative, similar to but in some respects distinct from modern Mongolian forms.
