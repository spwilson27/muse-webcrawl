Lingula (brachiopod)

Scientific classification
Kingdom:Animalia
Phylum:Brachiopoda
Class:Lingulata
Order:Lingulida
Family:Lingulidae
Genus:Lingula

Lingula is a genus of brachiopods within the class Lingulata. It is one of the oldest known animal genera, with fossils dating back to the early Cambrian period, roughly 540 million years ago. Because its shell morphology has changed so little over that immense span, Lingula is frequently cited as a classic example of a "living fossil", although genetic studies show that its species have continued to evolve.

A living Lingula showing the glossy, tongue-shaped shell and muscular stalk (pedicle).

Description

The shell is elongated and tongue-shaped (the name Lingula is Latin for "little tongue"), composed not of calcium carbonate like most brachiopods but of calcium phosphate interlayered with organic material, giving it a smooth, glossy, often greenish or brown surface. Unlike most brachiopods, Lingula lives in a vertical burrow in soft intertidal sediment, anchored by a long muscular pedicle, and feeds by filtering plankton with a ciliated lophophore.

Distribution and habitat

Living species inhabit shallow marine and estuarine waters, mainly in the Indo-Pacific region, including Japan, China, the Philippines, Indonesia, northern Australia and the Pacific coast of the Americas. The best-known species, Lingula anatina, is harvested as food in parts of Asia.

Species

Recognised living species include:

  • Lingula adamsi
  • Lingula anatina
  • Lingula parva
  • Lingula reevei
  • Lingula rostrum
  • Lingula translucida
  • Lingula tumidula

Hundreds of fossil species have at times been assigned to the genus, though many have since been reassigned to other lingulid genera.
