Regulation SHO

Regulation SHO is a body of rules issued by the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) governing short selling in the United States. Adopted in 2004 and effective from 2005, it is intended to reduce failures to deliver securities and to curb abusive "naked" short selling.

Key provisions

Regulation SHO introduced a uniform "locate" requirement, under which a broker-dealer must have reasonable grounds to believe a security can be borrowed before executing a short sale, and close-out requirements for positions that result in failures to deliver. It established the threshold securities list — securities with aggregate fails to deliver of 10,000 shares or more for five consecutive settlement days and equal to at least 0.5% of the issuer's shares outstanding — and imposed mandatory close-outs for persistent fails. Later amendments, including Rule 105 and the 2009 alternative uptick rule (Rule 201), further restricted abusive short selling during market declines.
