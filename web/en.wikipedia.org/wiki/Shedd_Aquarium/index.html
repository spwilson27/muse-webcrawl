Shedd Aquarium (formally the John G. Shedd Aquarium) is an indoor public aquarium in Chicago, Illinois, in the United States. Opened on May 30, 1930, the aquarium contains over 22,000 fish and more than 1,500 species overall. Shedd Aquarium was the first inland aquarium with a permanent saltwater fish collection. It is part of Chicago's Museum Campus, which includes the Field Museum and the Adler Planetarium, on the shore of Lake Michigan.

The Shedd Aquarium, a National Historic Landmark, on Chicago's Museum Campus.

History

The aquarium was endowed by retail magnate John G. Shedd, a protégé of Marshall Field, who gave $3 million to build a world-class aquarium for Chicago. Designed by the firm of Graham, Anderson, Probst & White in the Beaux-Arts style, with aquatic motifs by sculptor Thomas Hibben, the building is octagonal, topped by a dome and cupola, and was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1987. Walter Chute served as its first director from 1928 to 1964.

Exhibits

The original permanent exhibits, arranged in galleries around the central Caribbean Reef rotunda tank, include Waters of the World. Later additions include the Abbott Oceanarium (opened 1991), a marine mammal pavilion overlooking Lake Michigan housing beluga whales, Pacific white-sided dolphins, sea otters, and sea lions; Amazon Rising, a flooded-forest exhibit; Wild Reef (2003), a Philippine coral reef with sharks; and the special-exhibit galleries. The Caribbean Reef exhibit features a diver who feeds the animals and interacts with visitors.

Conservation and research

Shedd operates the Daniel P. Haerther Center for Conservation and Research, conducting field studies on species including iguanas in the Bahamas, seahorses, and Great Lakes fishes, and runs a great ape and marine mammal welfare programme. In the 21st century the aquarium launched an experience master plan ("Centennial Commitment") to renovate its galleries ahead of its 2030 centenary.
