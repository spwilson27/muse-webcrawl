Sodium citrate

Sodium citrate may refer to any of the sodium salts of citric acid: monosodium citrate, disodium citrate and trisodium citrate. The trisodium salt, Na3C6H5O7, is the most widely used and is commonly meant by the name. It is a white crystalline powder with a slightly tart, salty taste.

Food use

As a food additive (E331), sodium citrate is used as a flavouring agent, acidity regulator, emulsifier and preservative. It is a key ingredient in processed cheese and cheese sauces: by chelating calcium it lets casein melt smoothly without separating, a trick popularised for modernist "sodium citrate cheese sauce". It is also used in soft drinks and as the buffering agent in some gelatin desserts.

Medical use

Sodium citrate is an anticoagulant: citrate binds calcium ions in blood, preventing clotting, so it is used in blood collection tubes and in apheresis and dialysis circuits. It is also given as an alkalinising agent and antacid, and in solution (for example in oral rehydration and pre-anaesthetic prophylaxis against aspiration).

Chemistry

The citrates are salts of the weak triprotic citric acid, and mixtures act as buffers around mildly acidic to neutral pH. Sodium citrate is prepared by neutralising citric acid with sodium carbonate or sodium hydroxide.
