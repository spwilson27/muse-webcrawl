The Ceneri Base Tunnel (Italian: Galleria di base del Monte Ceneri) is a Swiss railway base tunnel in the canton of Ticino. It passes under Monte Ceneri between Camorino in the Magadino Plain and Vezia near Lugano; it bypasses the former high-altitude Monte Ceneri Tunnel and connects the Gotthard railway with the Lugano–Chiasso line. The tunnel comprises two single-track tubes 15.4 km (9.6 mi) in length, 40 metres apart, linked by cross passages.

Background

The Ceneri Base Tunnel is the third major tunnel of the Swiss NRLA (New Railway Link through the Alps) project, after the Lötschberg Base Tunnel and the Gotthard Base Tunnel. Together with the Gotthard tunnel it forms a level "flat route" through the Alps, allowing longer, heavier freight trains to travel between northern and southern Europe without helper locomotives, and significantly shortening passenger journey times between Zürich and Milan. The old Monte Ceneri line, opened in 1882 with steep gradients and a summit tunnel, was a bottleneck on the route south of Bellinzona.

Construction and opening

Construction began in 2006 from the main access at Sigirino, where most excavation proceeded by drill and blast through gneiss and other Alpine rock. Breakthrough in the two tubes took place in early 2016. The tunnel was officially opened on 4 September 2020 and entered regular service with the timetable change on 13 December 2020. Its completion cut the journey time between Bellinzona and Lugano to about 12 minutes by through trains and reduced Zürich–Lugano travel to under two hours.

Impact

Within Ticino, the tunnel, together with regional services, created a greatly improved commuter network between Bellinzona, Lugano, and Mendrisio/Chiasso, sometimes described as a metropolitan "Città Ticino" link. For freight, the Ceneri completes the flat transalpine corridor intended to shift lorry traffic from road to rail across Switzerland.
