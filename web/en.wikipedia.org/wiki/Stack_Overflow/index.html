Stack Overflow

Stack Overflow is a question-and-answer website for professional and enthusiast programmers. Launched in 2008 by Jeff Atwood and Joel Spolsky, it is the flagship site of the Stack Exchange network. Users ask and answer technical questions, primarily about programming, and can vote submissions up or down, edit them wiki-style, and earn reputation points and badges for participation. The site's name is a pun on the stack overflow error in computing, chosen by Atwood's blog readers in a poll.

History

Atwood and Spolsky founded the site partly in frustration with existing forums and with the paywalled Experts Exchange. Stack Overflow launched publicly in September 2008 and grew extremely rapidly, becoming one of the most visited websites in the world and the de facto reference point for programming questions. The company, Stack Overflow Inc. (later Stack Exchange, Inc.), raised venture funding and expanded into products such as Stack Overflow for Teams and an annual Developer Survey, whose results are widely cited in industry reporting. In June 2021 the company was acquired by the technology investor Prosus for $1.8 billion.

How it works

Questions are tagged by topic and answers are ranked by community voting; the asker may "accept" the answer that solved their problem. Reputation unlocks privileges, from commenting to moderation tools, and high-reputation users effectively curate the site. Content is user-contributed under Creative Commons licenses, and periodic data dumps were long made available to researchers. Duplicate or off-topic questions are closed to keep signal high.

Impact

Stack Overflow transformed how programmers find help: error messages pasted into a search engine almost invariably lead there. Studies have documented both its productivity benefits and concerns, including gender participation gaps, uneven civility toward newcomers, and the reusability (sometimes uncritical) of its code snippets — research has found insecure snippets copied into production software. From 2023 onward, traffic and question volume declined markedly as large language model chatbots such as ChatGPT became programmers' first resort; the 2023 edition of the Developer Survey documented rapid adoption of AI tools, and the company launched its own AI-assisted products in response.
