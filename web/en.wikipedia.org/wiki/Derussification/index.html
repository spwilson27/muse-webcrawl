Derussification

Derussification is a process or public policy aimed at restoring a country's national identity, language, culture, and history by reducing or removing the influence of Russian imperial, Soviet, or Russian Federation dominance. It is distinct from Russification, the process it reverses.

The term is applied to policies in several post-Soviet and post-communist states, including Ukraine, the Baltic states, Kazakhstan, and Georgia. Measures associated with derussification include promoting the titular national language in education and government, renaming streets and settlements that bore Russian or Soviet names, and removing monuments associated with Russian or Soviet rule.

Derussification received renewed attention following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, after which Ukraine accelerated the removal of Russian-language signage, renamed thousands of streets and settlements associated with Russia, and restricted Russian cultural products. Critics of such policies argue they can marginalize Russian-speaking minorities, while supporters frame them as decolonization and a response to Russian aggression.
