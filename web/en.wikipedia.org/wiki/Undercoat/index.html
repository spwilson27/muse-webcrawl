Undercoat

An undercoat is the layer of short, dense, soft hair found beneath the longer guard hairs in the coats of many mammals, especially double-coated dog breeds and other animals adapted to cold climates. Together with the outer coat (topcoat), it forms a double coat.

Function

The undercoat insulates the animal by trapping air close to the skin, retaining body heat in cold weather and, because the trapped air also slows heat transfer inward, providing some protection in heat. Its soft, downy or woolly hairs also repel water to a degree and cushion the skin. In many breeds the undercoat is shed seasonally — "blowing coat" in spring and fall — and regrows with the changing seasons and daylight hours (photoperiod).

In dogs

Breeds with prominent undercoats include northern and spitz types such as the Siberian Husky, Alaskan Malamute, Samoyed, and Chow Chow, as well as retrievers, shepherds, and many working breeds. Grooming of double coats usually involves brushes and undercoat rakes that remove loose hairs without cutting the guard hairs; groomers generally advise against shaving a healthy double coat because the guard hairs may regrow slowly or unevenly, the texture can change permanently, and the dog loses insulation and sun protection.

Other uses of the term

"Undercoat" also refers in painting and vehicle finishing to a layer of paint applied beneath the topcoat (a primer or rustproofing layer), and in automotive care to the protective coating sprayed on a vehicle's underbody.
