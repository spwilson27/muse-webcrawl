Gastroenterology

Gastroenterology is the branch of medicine concerned with the digestive system and its disorders. Physicians who practise in the field, gastroenterologists, diagnose and treat diseases of the gastrointestinal tract and associated organs — the oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, colon and rectum, pancreas, gallbladder, bile ducts and liver. The study of the liver, hepatology, is often treated as a subspecialty.

Scope

Common conditions managed by gastroenterologists include gastro-oesophageal reflux disease, peptic ulcers, inflammatory bowel disease (Crohn's disease and ulcerative colitis), irritable bowel syndrome, coeliac disease, gastrointestinal bleeding, pancreatitis, viral hepatitis and cirrhosis, and cancers of the digestive organs. Gastroenterologists also run population screening programmes, notably colonoscopy screening for colorectal cancer.

Procedures

The specialty is heavily procedural. Upper endoscopy (gastroscopy) and colonoscopy allow direct inspection, biopsy and therapy within the gut; endoscopic retrograde cholangiopancreatography (ERCP) treats bile-duct and pancreatic-duct disease; endoscopic ultrasound combines imaging with fine-needle sampling; and capsule endoscopy images the small intestine. Therapeutic techniques include polypectomy, haemostasis of bleeding lesions, dilatation of strictures and stent placement.

History

Ancient physicians studied digestion, but the specialty took shape with the invention of endoscopes: Philipp Bozzini's light conductor (1805), the semi-flexible gastroscope of Rudolf Schindler in the 1930s, and the fibre-optic endoscopes developed in the 1950s–60s following Basil Hirschowitz's work. The discovery of Helicobacter pylori by Barry Marshall and Robin Warren in 1982 transformed the understanding and treatment of peptic ulcer disease and earned the 2005 Nobel Prize.

Training

In most countries gastroenterology training follows general internal medicine residency, with a further fellowship of two to three years covering endoscopy and clinical gastroenterology. Advanced fellowships exist in hepatology, advanced endoscopy and inflammatory bowel disease.
