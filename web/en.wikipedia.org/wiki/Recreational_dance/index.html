Recreational dance

Recreational dance is dancing done primarily for enjoyment, social interaction, exercise and community rather than for performance or competition. It spans folk dancing, social partner dances, line dancing, square dancing, contra dancing and recreational forms of ballroom and street dance, practised in community halls, dance studios, schools and clubs by participants of widely varying skill.

Features

Recreational dance is characterised by accessibility: steps are taught in the session itself, no partner or prior experience is usually required, and the emphasis is on participation rather than technique. Organisations such as folk dance societies, callers' associations and community dance federations sustain repertoires ranging from English country dance and Scottish country dance to international folk dance drawn from Balkan, Scandinavian and other traditions.

Benefits

Studies associate regular recreational dancing with cardiovascular fitness, balance and coordination, and with social and cognitive benefits, particularly for older adults. Because instruction is embedded in the activity, recreational dance is also a common vehicle for transmitting traditional music and dance culture.
