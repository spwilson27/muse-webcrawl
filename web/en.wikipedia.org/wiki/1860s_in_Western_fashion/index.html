1860s in Western fashion

Fashion in the 1860s in Western Europe and North America was dominated by the full skirt supported by the cage crinoline, which reached its maximum circumference early in the decade before flattening at the front and sweeping toward the back, foreshadowing the bustle.

Women's fashion

The steel-hooped crinoline, patented in 1856, freed women from layers of heavy petticoats while producing an enormous bell-shaped skirt. By the later 1860s the silhouette became elliptical, with fullness concentrated behind. Garibaldi blouses, zouave jackets, and pagoda sleeves were popular, and new aniline dyes produced vivid colours such as magenta and solferino.

Men's fashion

The frock coat declined in favour of the shorter sack coat, worn with matching or contrasting trousers and waistcoat — the beginnings of the modern lounge suit. Shirts with detachable collars, four-in-hand ties, and bowlers or top hats completed the look.

Context

Charles Frederick Worth founded his Paris house in 1858, inaugurating haute couture, while the American Civil War influenced military trims and mourning dress conventions.
