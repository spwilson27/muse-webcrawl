The World Atlas of Language Structures (WALS) is a large database of structural (phonological, grammatical and lexical) properties of languages, gathered from reference grammars and descriptive materials. It was first published in book form by Oxford University Press in 2005, accompanied by a CD-ROM, and is freely available online through the Max Planck Institute.

Contents

WALS organises data by feature: each chapter, written by one or more specialists, defines a structural variable (such as word order, consonant inventories, or the presence of tone) and codes languages for its values, displayed on a world map. The print edition covered 142 features and up to 2,560 languages; the online edition expanded this to 192 features and 2,676 languages.

Editors and contributors

The atlas was edited by Matthew S. Dryer and Martin Haspelmath, with contributions from several hundred linguists. Its data have been widely used in linguistic typology and in statistical studies of language universals and areal patterns.
