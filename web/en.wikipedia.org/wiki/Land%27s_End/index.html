Land's End

Land's End (Cornish: Penn an Wlas) is a headland and tourist complex in western Cornwall, England, on the Penwith peninsula about eight miles west of Penzance. It is the most westerly point of mainland Cornwall and of mainland England.

Geography and tourism

Land's End is the traditional starting or finishing point of journeys to or from John o' Groats in Scotland, the longest journey in Great Britain at about 874 miles (1,407 km) by road. The headland offers views over the Atlantic to the Isles of Scilly and the Longships lighthouse. The site has a visitor complex with exhibitions, shops and the famous signpost indicating distances to John o' Groats and New York.

History

The area has been visited by travellers since the 18th century. The First and Last Inn at nearby Sennen is a well-known landmark. The headland and surrounding cliffs form part of an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty and a Site of Special Scientific Interest, noted for rare plants and sea birds.
