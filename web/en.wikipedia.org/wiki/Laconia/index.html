Laconia

Laconia (Greek: Λακωνία, Lakonía) is a historical and administrative region of Greece located on the southeastern part of the Peloponnese peninsula. Its administrative capital is Sparta. The region is known in particular as the home of the ancient Spartans, and its name gave rise to the English word "laconic" — describing speech that is blunt, terse, and pithy, in the manner associated with the Spartans.

Geography

Laconia is bordered by Messenia to the west and Arcadia to the north. The Eurotas River, the longest river in the region, flows through its central valley, running southward from the mountains near Sparta to the Laconian Gulf. Two mountain ranges frame the valley: Taygetus to the west — the highest mountain in the Peloponnese, rising to 2,407 metres at Profitis Ilias — and Parnon to the east. Between them, the Eurotas valley is a fertile plain historically dominated by Sparta. South of Sparta the land narrows into the Mani Peninsula, a rugged, tower-dotted landscape reaching Cape Matapan (Taenarum), the southernmost point of mainland Greece and of the Balkan Peninsula.

The Laconian Gulf lies to the south, and off the southeastern coast sits the island of Elafonisos, administratively part of Laconia. The region's principal towns include Sparta, Gytheio (the ancient port of Sparta), Monemvasia, Areopoli, Neapoli, and Molaoi.

History

Ancient period

In antiquity, Laconia was the territory of the Spartans, one of the most powerful city-states of ancient Greece. The region, often called Lacedaemon in ancient sources, was settled by Dorian Greeks around the end of the Bronze Age. Sparta's society was organised around the agoge, a rigorous system of military training, and its land was worked by the helots, a subjugated population. Sparta led the Peloponnesian League and defeated Athens in the Peloponnesian War (431–404 BC), but its power declined sharply after its defeat by Thebes at the Battle of Leuctra in 371 BC. Under Roman rule Laconia became part of the province of Achaea.

Byzantine and later periods

In the Byzantine era, Laconia was home to the fortified port of Monemvasia, a major commercial and military centre, and to the last Byzantine despotate at Mystras, near Sparta, where the final Byzantine emperor, Constantine XI, was crowned in 1449. The Mani Peninsula retained a semi-autonomous clan-based society under Ottoman rule and played a leading role in the outbreak of the Greek War of Independence in 1821.

Administration

Laconia is one of the regional units of the Peloponnese region. It is subdivided into five municipalities: East Mani, Elafonisos, Evrotas, Monemvasia, and Sparta. The modern town of Sparta was founded in 1834 by decree of King Otto on the site of the ancient city.
