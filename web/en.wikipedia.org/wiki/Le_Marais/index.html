Le Marais

CountryFrance
CityParis
Arrondissements3rd and 4th

Le Marais ("The Marsh") is a historic district in Paris, France, spread across parts of the 3rd and 4th arrondissements on the Right Bank of the Seine. Originally marshy land outside the city, it was developed from the 13th century onward and became the favoured quarter of the French aristocracy in the 17th century.

History

Religious orders drained the marshes in the Middle Ages, and in 1605 Henri IV laid out the Place Royale (now the Place des Vosges), the oldest planned square in Paris, around which nobles built grand hôtels particuliers such as the Hôtel de Sully and the Hôtel Carnavalet. After the court moved to Versailles, the quarter declined and filled with workshops and a thriving Jewish community centred on the Rue des Rosiers — the Pletzl — which remains the heart of Jewish Paris. The Marais also preserves the city's oldest house and much of pre-Haussmann Paris, protected since 1962 by André Malraux's preservation law.

Present day

Today the Marais is one of Paris's most fashionable districts, known for its museums — including the Musée Picasso, the Musée Carnavalet and the Centre Pompidou at its edge — its art galleries, boutiques, falafel shops, and its long-established gay village centred on the Rue Sainte-Croix-de-la-Bretonnerie. The Memorial de la Shoah stands at its southern edge.
