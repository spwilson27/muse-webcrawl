Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young (CSNY) were a folk-rock supergroup formed in Los Angeles in 1968, comprising David Crosby (formerly of the Byrds), Stephen Stills (Buffalo Springfield), Graham Nash (the Hollies), and — from 1969 — Canadian singer-songwriter Neil Young (also of Buffalo Springfield). Renowned for intricate vocal harmonies and politically charged songs, they were dubbed "the voice of a generation" of the counterculture era.

History

The trio's debut album, Crosby, Stills & Nash (1969), featured "Suite: Judy Blue Eyes" and "Marrakesh Express" and won the Grammy Award for Best New Artist. Joined by Young, the quartet played Woodstock in August 1969 — only their second live performance — and released Déjà Vu (1970), which topped the US charts and included "Teach Your Children", "Our House", "Helpless", and "Woodstock". In response to the Kent State shootings, they rapidly recorded and released the protest single "Ohio" (1970).

The double live album 4 Way Street (1971) also reached number one. Internal tensions and solo careers repeatedly dissolved the group; notable reunions produced CSN (1977, with "Just a Song Before I Go"), Daylight Again (1982), American Dream (1988, with Young), Looking Forward (1999), and the 2006 "Freedom of Speech" tour protesting the Iraq War.

Legacy

CSN were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1997, and all four members are two-time inductees through their other bands or solo work. Following Crosby's death in January 2023, the remaining members have performed tribute appearances but no full reunion.
