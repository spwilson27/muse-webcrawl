Eastern Hemisphere

The Eastern Hemisphere is the half of Earth east of the prime meridian (which crosses Greenwich, London) and west of the 180th meridian, the antipode of Greenwich. The term is also used to refer specifically to Europe, Asia, Africa, and Oceania (the "Old World"), in contrast to the Western Hemisphere, which comprises the Americas (the "New World").

Geographically, the Eastern Hemisphere contains more land and the majority of the world's population. Portions of the hemisphere's boundary run through land — the prime meridian crosses the United Kingdom, France, Spain, Algeria, Mali, Burkina Faso, Ghana, and Antarctica — so in the cultural sense the division is drawn instead along the Atlantic and Pacific Oceans, often using the 20°W and 160°E meridians to keep continents whole.
