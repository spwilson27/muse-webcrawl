The European mink (Mustela lutreola), also known as the Russian mink or Eurasian mink, is a semiaquatic species of mustelid native to Europe. It is critically endangered, having declined severely across most of its former range during the twentieth century.

Description

The European mink is a small, elongated mustelid with dark brown fur, short limbs and partially webbed feet suited to its waterside life. It is distinguished from the similar American mink by white markings on both the upper and lower lips, and by its smaller size.

Decline

Causes of the decline include over-hunting for fur, habitat loss and degradation of watercourses, and competition and aggression from the introduced American mink, which escaped or was released from fur farms. Disease transmission and hybridisation pressures have also been proposed. The species now survives in a few fragmented populations in northern Spain, southwestern France, the Danube Delta, Ukraine and Russia.

Conservation

Captive-breeding and reintroduction programmes have been carried out, notably on the Estonian island of Hiiumaa, where American mink were removed before releases began, and in Germany and Spain. The species is protected under the Bern Convention and the EU Habitats Directive.
