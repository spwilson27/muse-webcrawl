Lake Pamvotida

Lake Pamvotida or Pamvotis (Greek: Λίμνη Παμβώτιδα/Παμβώτης), commonly called the Lake of Ioannina, is the largest lake of Epirus in north-western Greece and one of the oldest lakes in Europe. The city of Ioannina stands on its western shore. The lake's name means "she who feeds everyone", from the Greek pan (all) and bous.

Geography

The lake covers about 19.4 km² and lies at an elevation of roughly 470 metres, surrounded by the mountains of Epirus, including Mount Mitsikeli to the northeast. It is a shallow karstic lake with a maximum depth of about 11 metres, fed by springs and small streams and drained underground through sinkholes (katavothres) toward the Louros river system. The lake has shrunk considerably from its ancient extent owing to the reclamation of the Laista and other marshlands for agriculture.

The island

Lake Pamvotida contains one of the few inhabited lake islands in Greece, the Island of Ioannina (Nisi), reached by ferry from the city. The island hosts several Byzantine and post-Byzantine monasteries, including the Monastery of St Panteleimon, where the Ottoman-Albanian ruler Ali Pasha of Ioannina was assassinated in 1822. Its tavernas are locally famous for dishes of lake fish and frog legs.

Ecology

The lake is an important wetland habitat protected under the Natura 2000 network, supporting fish species such as carp and the endemic tsima, as well as significant bird populations. It suffers from eutrophication and pollution from urban and agricultural runoff, and water quality has been a persistent concern for Ioannina.
