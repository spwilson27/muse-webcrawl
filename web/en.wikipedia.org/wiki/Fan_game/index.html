Fan game

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A fan game is a video game created by fans of an existing game, franchise, or fictional universe, rather than by the original developers or rights holders. Fan games are usually distributed free of charge and are developed as expressions of enthusiasm, as portfolio pieces, or to continue or reimagine series their creators love. They range from small demos to full-length releases rivalling commercial games in scope.

Development

Fan games are built with accessible tools such as RPG Maker, GameMaker, Unity, Godot, Clickteam Fusion, and dedicated engines for particular genres (for example, MUGEN for fighting games or Sonic Worlds for platformers). Communities organise around forums and sites that host completed works and works in progress. Some notable fan games, such as the Pokémon fan games Uranium and the Mega Man fan scene, attracted millions of downloads; others, like Another Metroid 2 Remake (AM2R), received critical praise.

Legal status

Fan games typically infringe the intellectual property of the franchise owners, since they reuse characters, settings, music, or assets. Rights holders respond inconsistently: some tolerate or even encourage fan games under published guidelines (Sega famously hired fan-game developers to create the commercial Sonic Mania), while others issue cease-and-desist letters and takedown notices — Nintendo is particularly known for shutting down high-profile projects, including Pokémon Uranium and AM2R shortly after release. Because fan games are non-commercial, disputes rarely proceed beyond takedowns.

Related forms

Closely related fan productions include ROM hacks (modifications of original game code), total conversion mods, fan remakes, and demakes. Some fan game developers later transition to original commercial titles.
