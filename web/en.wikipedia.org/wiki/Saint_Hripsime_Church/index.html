Saint Hripsime Church (Armenian: Սուրբ Հռիփսիմե եկեղեցի) is a seventh-century Armenian Apostolic church in the city of Vagharshapat (Etchmiadzin), Armenia. Completed in 618 by Catholicos Komitas, it is one of the oldest surviving churches in the country and a masterpiece of early medieval Armenian architecture; it is listed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site together with the churches of Etchmiadzin.

Architecture

The church is a domed tetraconch — a central-plan building with four apses — enclosed within a square exterior. Its ribbed, umbrella-shaped dome rises on a tall drum over the central bay. The plan, sometimes called the "Hripsime type", influenced later Armenian and Georgian church architecture. The interior preserves later wall paintings, and a small museum holds relics of Saint Hripsime.

History

The church stands on the site where Saint Hripsime (Rhipsime), a Roman nun who fled Diocletian's persecution, was martyred around 290 AD by the Armenian king Tiridates III. A martyrium was built over her tomb in the 4th–5th centuries; Komitas rebuilt it in 618. The complex was fortified in the 17th century and the belfry added in 1653. Excavations beneath the church revealed an earlier pagan fire temple at the site.
