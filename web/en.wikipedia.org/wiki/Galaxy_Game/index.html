Galaxy Game

DesignerBill Pitts, Hugh Tuck
PlatformPDP-11 (DEC)
Release1971
GenreSpace combat
ModeTwo-player
SignificanceEarly coin-operated video game

Galaxy Game is a space combat video game created in 1971 by Bill Pitts and Hugh Tuck at Stanford University. It is one of the earliest video games and, together with Computer Space created the same year, one of the first coin-operated video games.

The game is an adaptation of Spacewar! (1962): two players each control a spaceship orbiting a central star whose gravity pulls on both ships; each player attempts to torpedo the other's ship while avoiding falling into the star. Pitts and Tuck built the game around a Digital Equipment Corporation PDP-11 minicomputer with vector displays, housed in a walnut-veneer cabinet.

History

The prototype was installed in the Tresidder student union at Stanford in September 1971, charging ten cents per game or 25 cents for three games. It proved popular with students, and a second, expanded version supporting up to eight consoles was installed in the Stanford coffee house in 1972, where it remained in operation for several years. The original hardware suffered wear from intensive use; the surviving unit was restored and is exhibited at the Computer History Museum in Mountain View, California.
