Carreira da Índia

The Carreira da Índia ("India Run") was the annual Portuguese maritime route linking Lisbon to Goa and other Portuguese possessions in India, established after Vasco da Gama's first voyage of 1497–1499. For more than a century it was the backbone of Portugal's Asian empire and of the European spice trade.

The route

Fleets typically left Lisbon in March or April, rounded the Cape of Good Hope, called at Mozambique, and crossed the Indian Ocean with the monsoon to reach India by September. The return voyage left India in December or January, arriving back in Lisbon the following summer. The great carracks and later galleons of the run carried pepper, spices, silk, porcelain and other Asian goods westward, and silver, wine and manufactured goods eastward.

Decline

The run declined in the seventeenth century under competition from Dutch and English East India companies and the loss of Portuguese strongholds in Asia, though sailings between Lisbon and Goa continued into the nineteenth century.
