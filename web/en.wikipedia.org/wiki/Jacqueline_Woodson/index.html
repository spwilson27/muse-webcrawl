Jacqueline Woodson

Born: February 12, 1963, Columbus, Ohio, U.S.

Occupation: Author

Notable works: Brown Girl Dreaming, Another Brooklyn, Red at the Bone

Jacqueline Woodson (born February 12, 1963) is an American writer of books for young people. She is best known for her verse memoir Brown Girl Dreaming (2014), which won the National Book Award for Young People's Literature, and for novels including Miracle's Boys, Another Brooklyn, and Red at the Bone.

Early life

Woodson was born in Columbus, Ohio, and spent her childhood between Greenville, South Carolina, and Brooklyn, New York — a divided upbringing she later chronicled in Brown Girl Dreaming. She graduated from Barnard College with a degree in English.

Career

Woodson's works explore themes of race, gender, class, family, and identity, and frequently center Black girlhood. Her books have received numerous honors, including multiple Newbery Honors and Coretta Scott King Awards. She served as the National Ambassador for Young People's Literature from 2018 to 2019.

Awards

In 2015 she received the Astrid Lindgren Memorial Award, one of the largest prizes in children's literature, and in 2020 she was named a MacArthur Fellow. She won the Hans Christian Andersen Award for writing in 2020.
