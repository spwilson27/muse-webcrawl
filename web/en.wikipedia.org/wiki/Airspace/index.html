Airspace

Airspace is the portion of the atmosphere controlled by a country above its territory, including its territorial waters, and the system of classification and rules governing flight within it. Under the Chicago Convention of 1944, every state has complete and exclusive sovereignty over the airspace above its territory. There is no internationally agreed upper boundary, though the Kármán line at 100 km is often cited as the edge of space.

Classification

ICAO defines classes A through G. In controlled airspace (classes A–E), air traffic control provides services and may require clearances, flight plans, and radio contact; the strictness increases from E to A, where only instrument flight rules (IFR) traffic is permitted. Classes F and G are uncontrolled, where pilots are responsible for their own separation under visual or instrument rules subject to basic requirements. Airspace structures include control zones around airports, terminal control areas, airways, and upper flight information regions.

Special-use airspace

States designate prohibited areas (no flight), restricted areas (flight limited, e.g. for military operations), danger areas, military training routes, and temporary flight restrictions — for example around major events or, in the US, wherever the president travels. Foreign state aircraft require permission to enter national airspace; violations can trigger interception and have caused incidents such as the 1983 destruction of Korean Air Lines Flight 007 by the USSR.

Management

Air navigation service providers (such as the FAA, NATS, and the members of Eurocontrol) manage traffic flow. Initiatives like the Single European Sky and NextGen aim to modernize airspace design, while drones have prompted new low-altitude frameworks such as U-space in Europe and UTM in the United States.
