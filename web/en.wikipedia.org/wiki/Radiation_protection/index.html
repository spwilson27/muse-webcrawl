Radiation protection

Radiation protection, also known as radiological protection, is the science and practice of protecting people and the environment from the harmful effects of ionizing radiation. It is applied in medicine, nuclear power, industry and research wherever radioactive materials or radiation-generating equipment are used.

Principles

The system of radiation protection recommended by the International Commission on Radiological Protection (ICRP) rests on three principles: justification (any exposure should do more good than harm), optimisation (doses should be kept as low as reasonably achievable — ALARA — taking economic and social factors into account), and dose limitation (individual doses from planned exposures must not exceed prescribed limits).

Protection methods

Practical protection against external radiation follows the rules of time (minimising exposure duration), distance (exploiting the inverse-square law) and shielding (interposing absorbing material such as lead, concrete or water). Against internal contamination, measures include containment, ventilation, protective clothing and monitoring of intakes. Workers are routinely monitored with personal dosimeters such as film badges and thermoluminescent dosimeters.

Quantities and units

Absorbed dose is measured in gray (Gy); equivalent and effective dose, which account for the biological effectiveness of different radiation types and tissue sensitivities, are measured in sievert (Sv). Typical occupational limits are 20 mSv per year averaged over five years, while the public limit is 1 mSv per year above natural background.
