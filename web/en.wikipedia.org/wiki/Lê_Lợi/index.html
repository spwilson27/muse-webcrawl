Lê Lợi

Born10 September 1385, Lam Sơn, Thanh Hóa, Đại Việt
Died5 October 1433 (aged 48), Thăng Long
Reign1428–1433 (as Emperor Lê Thái Tổ)
DynastyLê dynasty (founder)

Lê Lợi (Vietnamese: [le lə̂ːjˀ]; 10 September 1385 – 5 October 1433), posthumously known by his temple name Lê Thái Tổ, was the founder of the Later Lê dynasty of Vietnam. He led the Lam Sơn uprising (1418–1427) that expelled the Ming Chinese occupation and restored Vietnamese independence, and is revered as one of Vietnam's greatest national heroes.

The Lam Sơn uprising

China's Ming dynasty annexed Vietnam in 1407 after destroying the Hồ dynasty, ruling it as the province of Jiaozhi with heavy taxation and forced cultural assimilation. Lê Lợi, a wealthy landowner from Lam Sơn in Thanh Hóa, raised the banner of revolt at the beginning of 1418, declaring himself the "Prince of Pacification" (Bình Định vương). Aided by the scholar-strategist Nguyễn Trãi, whose Bình Ngô đại cáo (Great Proclamation upon the Pacification of the Wu, 1428) became a celebrated declaration of Vietnamese sovereignty, the Lam Sơn forces waged a ten-year guerrilla and then conventional campaign.

After early defeats and a desperate siege in the mountains, the rebels won decisive victories at Tốt Động–Chúc Động (1426) and Chi Lăng–Xương Giang (1427), annihilating Ming relief armies. The Ming commander Wang Tong negotiated a withdrawal, and in 1428 Lê Lợi took the throne in Thăng Long, founding the Lê dynasty and re-establishing the state of Đại Việt.

Reign and legacy

As emperor, Lê Thái Tổ reformed land distribution and taxation, revived Confucian administration and examinations, issued a law code, and demobilised his army into farming colonies. Nearly every Vietnamese city has a major street named after him. Legend associates him with the magical sword Thuận Thiên ("Heaven's Will") and its return to the Golden Turtle God (Kim Quy) in the lake of Hanoi thereafter called Hồ Hoàn Kiếm, the "Lake of the Returned Sword."
