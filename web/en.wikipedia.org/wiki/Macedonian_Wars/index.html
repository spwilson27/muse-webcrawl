Macedonian Wars

The Macedonian Wars (214–148 BC) were a series of conflicts fought by the Roman Republic and its Greek allies against the Kingdom of Macedon and, in the final war, a Macedonian pretender. They resulted in Roman control of the Hellenistic east and the end of Macedon as an independent power; Macedon was eventually annexed as a Roman province.

First Macedonian War (214–205 BC)

During the Second Punic War, Philip V of Macedon allied with Hannibal's Carthage, prompting Rome — already stretched in Italy — to contain him with the aid of the Aetolian League and Pergamum. Fighting was inconclusive and ended with the Treaty of Phoenice in 205 BC.

Second Macedonian War (200–196 BC)

Appeals from Pergamum and Rhodes against Philip's expansion led Rome to declare war. The consul Titus Quinctius Flamininus decisively defeated Philip at the Battle of Cynoscephalae in 197 BC, where the Roman legion's flexibility overcame the Macedonian phalanx. At the Isthmian Games of 196 BC Flamininus proclaimed the freedom of the Greeks; Philip was confined to Macedon and fined.

Third Macedonian War (171–168 BC)

Philip's son Perseus rebuilt Macedonian influence, and Rome declared war in 171 BC. After early Roman setbacks, Lucius Aemilius Paullus crushed Perseus at the Battle of Pydna in 168 BC. The kingdom was abolished and split into four republics; Perseus was paraded in a Roman triumph and enormous booty flowed to Rome.

Fourth Macedonian War (150–148 BC)

The pretender Andriscus, claiming to be a son of Perseus, briefly reunited Macedon before being defeated by Quintus Caecilius Metellus in 148 BC. Macedon was then organised as a Roman province, the first in the Greek east.
