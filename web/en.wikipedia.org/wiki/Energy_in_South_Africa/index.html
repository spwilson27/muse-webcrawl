Energy in South Africa

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Energy in South Africa is dominated by coal, which supplies roughly 70–80 percent of the country's primary energy and the great majority of its electricity. South Africa possesses abundant coal reserves, concentrated in Mpumalanga, and relatively little domestic oil or natural gas, making it one of the most coal-dependent large economies in the world and one of the world's top fifteen greenhouse-gas emitters. Most electricity is generated and transmitted by the state utility Eskom. Since 2007 the sector has been defined by an electricity supply crisis, with rolling blackouts — known locally as load shedding — becoming a central economic and political issue before easing markedly from 2024.

Kusile Power Station in Mpumalanga, one of Eskom's newest coal plants.

Electricity sector

Eskom, founded in 1923, generates around 85–90 percent of South Africa's electricity, which is produced overwhelmingly by a fleet of large coal-fired stations in the northeast of the country, close to the collieries that feed them. The fleet includes the 4,800 MW Medupi and Kusile plants, completed in the early 2020s after long delays and cost overruns, and the 1,860 MW Koeberg nuclear power station near Cape Town — Africa's only commercial nuclear plant. Peaking capacity comes from pumped-storage schemes such as Ingula and from open-cycle gas turbines burning diesel. Municipalities and, increasingly, private generators distribute and supply the remainder.

The load-shedding crisis

South Africa's electricity shortage emerged in late 2007, when Eskom began implementing scheduled rolling blackouts because generating capacity could no longer meet demand. Causes commonly cited include a 1990s government decision not to build new capacity, deferred maintenance, corruption and mismanagement during the state-capture era, coal-supply problems, and the breakdown-prone condition of ageing plants. Load shedding returned at escalating severity from 2018, reaching record levels in 2023, when outages occurred on more than 300 days and cost the economy an estimated hundreds of billions of rand. A combination of intensive maintenance, reduced demand, growth in rooftop solar, and management reforms produced a sustained improvement from March 2024, with long uninterrupted periods of supply.

Renewable energy

The Renewable Energy Independent Power Producer Procurement Programme (REIPPPP), launched in 2011, procured several gigawatts of wind and solar photovoltaic capacity from private developers at rapidly falling prices and is regarded internationally as a model auction scheme. South Africa has excellent solar irradiation, particularly in the Northern Cape, and good wind resources along its coasts. Rooftop solar expanded explosively during the worst load-shedding years, adding several gigawatts of behind-the-meter capacity.

Other fuels

South Africa imports most of its crude oil; its notable domestic feature is Sasol's coal-to-liquids plant at Secunda, one of the largest point sources of carbon dioxide on Earth, which converts coal into synthetic liquid fuels. Natural gas plays a small role, supplied by pipeline from Mozambique. The country's Just Energy Transition Partnership, announced at COP26 in 2021 with an initial pledge of $8.5 billion from wealthy nations, aims to finance the shift away from coal while protecting affected workers and communities.

Policy and reform

Long-term planning is set out in the Integrated Resource Plan. Major reforms since 2021 include raising and then removing the licensing threshold for private generation, unbundling Eskom into generation, transmission and distribution subsidiaries — with the National Transmission Company of South Africa operational from 2024 — and legislation to create a competitive wholesale electricity market.
