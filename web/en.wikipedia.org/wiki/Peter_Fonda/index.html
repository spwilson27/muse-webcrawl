Peter Henry Fonda (February 23, 1940 – August 16, 2019) was an American actor, director, and screenwriter. He was a son of actor Henry Fonda, the younger brother of actress Jane Fonda, and the father of actress Bridget Fonda.

Fonda became a prominent figure of the counterculture of the 1960s, most famously as co-writer, producer, and star of the road film Easy Rider (1969), in which he played the character Wyatt, nicknamed "Captain America". The film, made for a small budget, became a landmark of the New Hollywood era.

Career

Fonda made his film debut in Tammy and the Doctor (1963) and gained attention in Roger Corman's biker film The Wild Angels (1966) and the psychedelic film The Trip (1967), written by Jack Nicholson. Following Easy Rider, he directed and starred in the Western The Hired Hand (1971).

He won the Golden Globe Award for Best Actor for his performance in Ulee's Gold (1997), for which he was also nominated for the Academy Award for Best Actor, and received a second Golden Globe for the television film The Passion of Ayn Rand (1999).

Death

Fonda died of respiratory failure caused by lung cancer at his home in Los Angeles on August 16, 2019, at the age of 79.
