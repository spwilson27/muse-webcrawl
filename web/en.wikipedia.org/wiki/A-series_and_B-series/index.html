A-series and B-series

A-series and B-series paper

Standard: ISO 216 (A series); B series from ISO 216 and JIS P 0138

Aspect ratio: 1 : √2

Base sizes: A0 = 841 × 1189 mm (1 m²); B0 = 1000 × 1414 mm

Common sizes: A4 = 210 × 297 mm; B5 = 176 × 250 mm (ISO) / 182 × 257 mm (JIS)

The A-series and B-series are families of paper sizes defined by the ISO 216 standard, used throughout most of the world (the notable exception being North America's Letter and Legal sizes). Both series are built on an aspect ratio of 1 : √2 (approximately 1 : 1.4142), so that cutting a sheet in half across its long side produces two smaller sheets with exactly the same proportions.

A series

The A series starts with A0, which has an area of exactly one square metre (841 × 1189 mm). Each subsequent size is half the area of the previous: A1 is half of A0, A2 half of A1, and so on. The most widely used size is A4 (210 × 297 mm), the standard for letters, documents, and home and office printing. Smaller sizes include A5 (notebooks) and A6 (postcards).

B series

B-series sizes are geometric means between successive A sizes, providing intermediate dimensions. B0 is defined as 1000 × 1414 mm. ISO B sizes are used for items such as posters and books (B5), while Japan's JIS standard defines slightly different B sizes (JIS B5 is 182 × 257 mm), common in Japanese books and notebooks.

History

The √2 proportion was described in a 1786 letter by the German scientist Georg Christoph Lichtenberg. France adopted similar sizes during the Revolution, and the German DIN 476 standard of 1922 — the basis of ISO 216 (1975) — was developed under Walter Porstmann.
