Edenton is a town in, and the county seat of, Chowan County, North Carolina, United States. Situated on Edenton Bay where the Chowan River meets Albemarle Sound, it was founded in the early eighteenth century and served as the capital of the Province of North Carolina from 1722 to 1766, making it one of the state's oldest incorporated towns. It is named for Charles Eden, governor of the province.

History

Edenton played a notable part in the American Revolution. In October 1774, fifty-one women led by Penelope Barker signed a petition supporting a boycott of British goods — an episode famous in Britain as the satirised "Edenton Tea Party". The town's remarkably intact colonial fabric includes the Chowan County Courthouse of 1767, considered among the finest Georgian courthouses in the South, St Paul's Episcopal Church (begun 1736), and the Cupola House (c. 1758), a National Historic Landmark. Formerly a busy port and later a centre for fishing, cotton and peanut processing, Edenton today promotes heritage tourism around its large historic district.
