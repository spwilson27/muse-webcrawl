Prime Day is an annual sales event created by Amazon, offering exclusive discounts to members of its Amazon Prime subscription program. It was launched on 15 July 2015 to mark the 20th anniversary of Amazon's founding, and has since grown into one of the largest online shopping events in the world, rivaling Black Friday and Cyber Monday in sales volume.

History

The first Prime Day lasted 24 hours and was held in nine countries; despite some customer complaints about the quality of deals and rapid sell-outs of promoted items, Amazon reported that order volume surpassed the previous Black Friday. The event was extended to 30 hours in 2017 and to 48 hours from 2019 onward. In 2020, because of the COVID-19 pandemic, Prime Day was postponed to October. Beginning in 2022, Amazon added a second Prime-branded sale in the autumn, known as Prime Early Access Sale and later Prime Big Deal Days. In 2025 the main event was extended to four days.

Sales and strategy

Prime Day regularly sets company records: Amazon reported that the 2019 event sold more than 175 million items worldwide, and subsequent events have exceeded that in gross merchandise value, with independent estimates placing sales in the tens of billions of dollars across the event. The sale is also a major driver of Prime membership sign-ups and promotes Amazon's own devices, such as Echo, Fire TV, and Kindle products, which are heavily discounted. Competing retailers including Walmart, Target, and Best Buy run concurrent sales events to compete for the same shopping traffic.

Criticism

Prime Day has drawn criticism over the working conditions of warehouse employees during peak periods; workers and labor groups have staged protests and strikes timed to the event in several countries. Critics also note that some "discounts" are measured against inflated list prices.
