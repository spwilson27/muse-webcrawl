Shaving brush

A shaving brush or shave brush is a small brush with a handle parallel to the bristles, used to apply shaving soap or shaving cream to the face when shaving. Shave brushes are often decorative; antique handles are made of materials such as ivory, gold, silver, tortoiseshell, crystal, and porcelain, though the more common handles today are made of wood, plastic, resin, or metal. The bristles may be made of natural materials such as badger hair, boar hair, or horse hair, or of synthetic fibres, typically nylon.

A modern badger-hair shaving brush.

History

The modern shaving brush can be traced to France during the 1750s, when brushes began to accompany the rise of the straight razor and the barber's trade. Brushes remained expensive luxury items through much of the 19th century because handles were turned from ivory and bristles came from fine badger hair. The mass-produced, inexpensive brush emerged in the early 20th century alongside shaving soap sticks and creams, and use declined after the Second World War with the spread of aerosol shaving foams, though traditional wet shaving has enjoyed a revival in the 21st century.

Types of bristle

  • Badger hair — regarded as the finest material because of its water retention, softness, and ability to produce a rich lather. Grades include pure badger, best badger, super badger, and silvertip, the softest and most expensive.
  • Boar bristle — stiffer and cheaper; brushes soften with use as the bristle tips split.
  • Horse hair — a balance between badger and boar in stiffness.
  • Synthetic fibres — nylon and newer "SynBad"-type fibres that dry quickly, are vegan, and have become comparable to natural hair in performance.

Use

The brush is soaked in warm water, loaded with soap (often from a puck of shaving soap), and worked in a bowl or directly on the face in circular motions to build a lather and lift the whiskers. After use the brush is rinsed and typically stored bristles-down in a stand so water drains from the knot.
