The Culinary Institute of America (CIA) is an American private college and culinary school specializing in culinary, baking, and pastry arts education. Its main campus is in Hyde Park, New York, at the former St. Andrew-on-Hudson Jesuit novitiate overlooking the Hudson River. The school was founded in 1946 in New Haven, Connecticut, as the New Haven Restaurant Institute to train veterans returning from World War II.

History

Founded by Katharine Angell and Frances Roth, the institute grew rapidly and took the name the Culinary Institute of America in 1951. In 1972 it moved to Hyde Park. The college offers associate, bachelor's, and master's degrees, as well as certificate and continuing-education programs for professionals. Students complete an externship and work in the college's public restaurants.

Campuses and restaurants

In addition to Hyde Park, the CIA operates campuses in San Antonio, Texas (at the Pearl Brewery district), at Greystone in St. Helena, California, and in Singapore. Student-staffed restaurants at Hyde Park include the American Bounty Restaurant, the Bocuse Restaurant (named for Paul Bocuse), Caterina de' Medici, and the Apple Pie Bakery Café. The CIA is considered one of the most influential culinary schools in the world, and its alumni include Anthony Bourdain, Grant Achatz, Cat Cora, Roy Choi, and Duff Goldman.
