Grand Bazaar

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Grand Bazaar
Location: Fatih district, Istanbul
Founded: 1455–1461, under Mehmed II
Scale: Over 60 covered streets and several thousand shops

The Grand Bazaar (Kapalıçarşı, "Covered Market") in Istanbul is one of the largest and oldest covered markets in the world. Construction began in 1455, shortly after the Ottoman conquest of Constantinople, when Mehmed II ordered a stone bedesten for the trade in textiles and valuables.

The market grew over centuries into a small roofed city: more than 60 streets, several thousand shops, and its own mosques, fountains, cafés, police station and banks, organised traditionally by trade — goldsmiths in one street, carpets in another, leather, ceramics, antiques and spices elsewhere. Earthquakes and fires repeatedly damaged it (notably in 1894), and it was rebuilt in stone and brick.

Today the Grand Bazaar draws tens of thousands of visitors daily — figures of a quarter of a million on busy days are commonly cited — and remains a working market as well as a tourist landmark, famous for jewellery, carpets, ceramics and the ritual of bargaining over tea.
