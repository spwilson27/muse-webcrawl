Red blood cell

A red blood cell (erythrocyte) is the most common cell in human blood, specialised to carry oxygen from the lungs to tissues and carbon dioxide back. There are about 5 million per microlitre and ~25 trillion in an adult — roughly 2.5 million are made and destroyed every second.

Structure and life

Mammalian red cells are biconcave discs without nuclei, packed with ~270 million molecules of Haemoglobin each — the iron protein that binds oxygen. They are made in red bone marrow (see Skeleton) under the hormone erythropoietin from the Kidney, live about 120 days, and are dismantled by the spleen and liver; their haem becomes bilirubin, excreted in Bile.

Disorders

Too few or too little haemoglobin is Anaemia; distorted shape causes Sickle cell disease. Antigens on red cells define the blood groups used in transfusion.
