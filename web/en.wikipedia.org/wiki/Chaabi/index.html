Chaabi

Chaabi (Arabic: شعبي, shaʿbī, "popular" or "of the people") is a term applied to several popular music genres of North Africa, most prominently in Algeria and Morocco, and also to a traditional popular music of Egypt. In Algeria, chaabi is a distinct genre of urban folk music that developed in the Casbah of Algiers in the early 20th century, drawing on the Arabo-Andalusian classical tradition.

Algerian chaabi

Algerian chaabi emerged from the medh, poetry in praise of the Prophet Muhammad sung in colloquial Algerian Arabic, combined with elements of Andalusian classical music (san'a). The genre is credited principally to El Hadj M'Hamed El Anka (1907–1978), known as the "Cardinal" of chaabi, who popularised the music, gave it a structured form, and founded an influential school of performance. Its lyrics, sung in dialect, address everyday life, morality, love, exile, and the hardships of ordinary people, which gave the genre its name: music "of the people".

Other major figures include Dahmane El Harrachi, composer of the widely covered song "Ya Rayah" ("You Who Are Leaving"), and Boudjemaâ El Ankis. Typical instruments include the Algerian mandole (a metal-stringed instrument central to the genre), banjo, violin played upright on the knee, piano, and hand percussion such as the derbouka and tar.

Moroccan chaabi

Moroccan chaabi is a popular dance music performed especially at weddings and celebrations, characterised by driving rhythms derived from Amazigh (Berber) and Gnawa traditions, call-and-response singing, and instruments such as the bendir and electric keyboards. Groups such as Nass El Ghiwane (in a related folk-revival style) and popular chaabi orchestras made the style a mainstay of Moroccan festivities.

Egyptian chaabi

In Egypt, shaabi denotes working-class popular music that arose in Cairo in the 1970s, associated with singers such as Ahmed Adaweya. Sung in colloquial Egyptian Arabic, it voices the concerns of the urban poor. From the 2000s, a faster, electronic street style known as mahraganat ("festivals") developed from shaabi in Cairo's informal neighbourhoods.
