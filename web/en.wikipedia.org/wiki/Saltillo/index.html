Saltillo (Spanish pronunciation: [salˈtiʝo]) is the capital and largest city of the northeastern Mexican state of Coahuila and the municipal seat of the municipality of the same name. Located about 400 km south of the Texas border and 90 km southwest of Monterrey, it sits at roughly 1,600 metres in the foothills of the Sierra Madre Oriental, giving it a cooler, drier climate than much of the region. Saltillo is one of the oldest cities in northern Mexico, founded in 1577.

History

Founded by the conquistador Alberto del Canto in 1577, Saltillo became a base for Spanish expansion into Texas and the north; in 1591 Tlaxcaltec settlers established the neighbouring town of San Esteban de Nueva Tlaxcala, which later merged with the city. During the Mexican–American War, the Battle of Buena Vista in 1847 was fought nearby. Saltillo briefly served as capital of Coahuila y Texas. The city gave its name to the brightly patterned woven blanket known as a sarape de Saltillo, a classic of Mexican textile art.

Economy and culture

Modern Saltillo is a major industrial centre, especially for the automotive industry—General Motors and Stellantis operate large plants in nearby Ramos Arizpe—earning it a reputation as the "Detroit of Mexico". Cement, ceramics and textiles are also important. Cultural landmarks include the 18th-century cathedral, the Museo del Desierto dedicated to the region's deserts and palaeontology, and the Museo de las Aves de México bird museum. The city's signature dish is pan de pulque, a bread leavened with fermented agave sap.
