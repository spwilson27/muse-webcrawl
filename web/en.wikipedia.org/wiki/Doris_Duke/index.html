Doris Duke

Doris Duke (November 22, 1912 – October 28, 1993) was an American billionaire tobacco heiress, philanthropist, art collector, and patron of the arts. The only child of tobacco and electric-power tycoon James Buchanan Duke, she inherited a vast fortune at the age of twelve and was dubbed by the press "the richest girl in the world".

Life

Duke was born in New York City. On her father's death in 1925 she inherited the bulk of his estate, largely centred on the American Tobacco Company and Duke Power. As an adult she travelled widely, worked briefly as a newspaper correspondent during the Second World War, and pursued interests in competitive surfing (under the tutelage of Duke Kahanamoku), jazz piano, art collecting, and historic preservation. She was married twice, to James H. R. Cromwell and to the Dominican diplomat and playboy Porfirio Rubirosa.

Homes and collections

Her principal residences included Duke Farms in New Jersey, Rough Point in Newport, Rhode Island, Falcon's Lair in Beverly Hills, and Shangri La, the Islamic-art-filled estate she built in Honolulu, Hawaii. She founded the Newport Restoration Foundation, which preserved dozens of colonial buildings in Newport, and assembled a major collection of Islamic art.

Death and legacy

Duke died at Falcon's Lair in 1993. Controversy followed over her will, which left control of her estate largely to her butler, Bernard Lafferty; after litigation, the Doris Duke Charitable Foundation was established with assets of about $1.3 billion, supporting medical research, environmental causes, the arts, and child welfare.
