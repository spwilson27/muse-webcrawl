Atropates

Atropates (Greek: Ἀτροπάτης; from Old Persian *Ātṛpāta, "protected by fire"; c. 370 BCE – after 321 BCE) was a Persian nobleman who served as satrap of Media under the last Achaemenid king, Darius III, and then under Alexander the Great. After Alexander's death he retained north-western Media as an effectively independent kingdom, which from his name became known as Atropatene — the ancestor, through Middle Persian Ādurbādagān, of the modern name Azerbaijan.

Career under Darius and Alexander

Atropates commanded the Median, Cadusian, Albanian and Sacesinian contingents in Darius III's army at the Battle of Gaugamela (331 BCE). After the Persian defeat he submitted to Alexander, who confirmed him as satrap of Media. In 324 BCE Atropates suppressed a local revolt by the pretender Baryaxes and presented Alexander at Ecbatana with a troupe of a hundred women said to be Amazons. His daughter married Alexander's general and later regent Perdiccas at the mass weddings at Susa in 324 BCE, binding the house of Atropates to the Macedonian leadership.

Kingdom of Atropatene

In the Partition of Babylon after Alexander's death (323 BCE), Media was divided: the larger southern part went to Peithon, while the north-western region remained with Atropates, who no longer behaved as a subordinate satrap. At the settlement of Triparadisus (321 BCE) he was confirmed in this territory. His descendants, the Atropatids, ruled the mountainous land south-west of the Caspian Sea for some three centuries, preserving Iranian and Zoroastrian traditions while the rest of Media fell to the Seleucids; Atropatene survived as a client kingdom into the Parthian and early Sasanian periods.

Legacy

Atropates is chiefly remembered through the name he gave his kingdom. Greek Atropatēnē, rendered in Middle Persian as Ādurbādagān and in Arabic as Ādharbayjān, is the direct etymological source of "Azerbaijan", now borne both by the Republic of Azerbaijan and by the Azerbaijan region of north-western Iran — the very territory of the ancient kingdom.
