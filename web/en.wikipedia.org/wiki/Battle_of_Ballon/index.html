The Battle of Ballon was fought on 22 November 845 between the Breton forces of Nominoe, ruler of Brittany, and the Frankish army of Charles the Bald, king of West Francia. The battle, fought in marshy ground near the abbey of Saint-Sauveur at Redon, close to the confluence of the Oust and Vilaine rivers, ended in a decisive Breton victory and is regarded as a founding moment of the Breton kingdom.

Background

Nominoe had been appointed by Louis the Pious as the emperor's representative in Brittany, but after Louis's death in 840 he moved towards independence. When Charles the Bald attempted to assert control over Brittany and replaced the bishop of Dol in defiance of Breton wishes, Nominoe openly revolted, renouncing his allegiance and allying with Lambert II of Nantes and the Viking leader Hastein.

The battle

Charles advanced into Brittany in the autumn of 845 with a relatively small army. Nominoe, familiar with the terrain, drew the Franks into the marshes near Ballon, where the Frankish heavy cavalry was hampered by the wet ground. The Bretons attacked and routed the Frankish force; Charles himself fled the field and, according to some accounts, was nearly captured. Frankish losses included senior nobles, and Charles was forced to abandon his baggage.

Aftermath

In 846 Charles recognised Nominoe's effective control of Brittany. Nominoe went on to extend Breton power, capturing Rennes and Nantes, and his son Erispoe would defeat Charles again at the Battle of Jengland in 851, after which Charles recognised Erispoe as king. The victory at Ballon is still commemorated in Brittany as a symbol of Breton resistance and nationhood.
