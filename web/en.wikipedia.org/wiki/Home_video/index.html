Home video

Home video is recorded media sold or rented for home viewing. The term originates from the era of analogue videocassette recorders and tapes — principally VHS and Betamax in the 1970s and 1980s — and has since encompassed LaserDisc, DVD, Blu-ray and Ultra HD Blu-ray, as well as direct-to-video releases produced specifically for the home market rather than for cinemas or broadcast.

History

Consumer video recording arrived in the mid-1970s with Sony's Betamax (1975) and JVC's VHS (1976); after a format war, VHS became dominant. Pre-recorded cassettes and the video rental store — exemplified by Blockbuster, founded in 1985 — created a major new market: by the late 1980s, home video revenues surpassed box office receipts for the film industry. The legality of home recording was settled in the United States by the Supreme Court's 1984 Betamax decision, Sony Corp. of America v. Universal City Studios, which held that recording programmes for later viewing ("time shifting") was fair use.

Optical disc and decline of physical media

DVD, launched in 1996–97, rapidly replaced tape thanks to better quality, random access and special features; it became the most successful consumer-electronics product of its time. Blu-ray won a second format war against HD DVD in 2008. Since the 2010s, however, streaming services and digital downloads have largely displaced physical home video, leading to the collapse of rental chains and shrinking retail sections, though a collectors' market persists for Blu-ray and boutique-label releases. The home video market also created the category of direct-to-video (or direct-to-streaming) productions and gave older films and television series a long commercial afterlife.
