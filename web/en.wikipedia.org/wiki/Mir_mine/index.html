Mir mine

The Mir mine (Russian: Кимберлитовая алмазная трубка «Мир», "Peace") is an open-pit diamond mine located in Mirny, Sakha Republic, in eastern Siberia, Russia. The pit is about 525 metres deep and 1,200 metres in diameter, making it one of the largest excavated holes in the world and the first developed industrial diamond mine in the Soviet Union.

History

The kimberlite pipe was discovered in 1955 by a Soviet geological expedition led by Ekaterina Elagina, whose coded radio message — "I am smoking the pipe of peace" — announced the find. Mining began in 1957 in extreme conditions: Siberian winters last seven months, the permafrost ground turns to mud in summer, and jet engines and dynamite were used to thaw and blast the ore. The mine supplied a large share of Soviet diamonds. Open-pit work ended in 2001, and underground mining continued until a catastrophic water inrush in August 2017 flooded the mine, trapping 151 miners; 143 were rescued, eight were lost, and the mine has remained closed.

Folklore

The pit is the subject of a persistent urban legend that its size creates downdrafts capable of pulling helicopters into it; airspace above the pit is in fact restricted.
