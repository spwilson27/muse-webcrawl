Bani River

The Bani River is a river in Mali, West Africa, and the principal tributary of the Niger River. Formed by the confluence of the Baoulé and Bagoé rivers in southern Mali, it flows northeast for roughly 430 kilometres (270 mi) before joining the Niger at Mopti. Its name means "little river" in the Bambara language.

Course and hydrology

The Bani drains a basin of about 101,000 square kilometres. Its flow is strongly seasonal: during the rainy season the river rises and, together with the Niger, inundates the Inner Niger Delta, a vast floodplain of lakes, marshes and channels north of Segou and around Mopti that supports fisheries, rice cultivation and pastoralism of regional importance. Average discharge at Douna is on the order of 500 cubic metres per second, but annual volumes vary greatly with rainfall in the Guinean and Malian headwaters. The Talo Dam, completed in the 2000s near San, regulates part of the flow for irrigation, a scheme that drew debate over effects on downstream flooding and fisheries.

Settlements

The historic town of Djenné, famous for its Great Mosque built of mud brick, lies between the Bani and the Niger near their confluence zone, and the port city of Mopti stands at the junction of the two rivers.
