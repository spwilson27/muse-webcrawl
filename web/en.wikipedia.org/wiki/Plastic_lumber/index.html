Plastic lumber

Deck boards made from recycled plastic lumber, a common application of the material.

Plastic lumber is a plastic form of lumber (timber) made of virgin or recycled plastic. It is mostly made of plastic and binders, but a small amount of wood product can be present; in wood-plastic composites the wood fraction may be much larger. It is made of 100% plastic when described as "plastic lumber" strictly, and is widely used for outdoor deck boards, mouldings and trim, and park furniture such as benches and picnic tables.

Properties

Plastic lumber does not rot, absorb water, or suffer insect attack, and it does not require painting or preservative treatment, unlike treated timber. It can be worked with ordinary woodworking tools, though it is heavier, less stiff and more prone to creep and thermal expansion than wood, and it tends to be more expensive. Because it is not biodegradable, it also raises questions about end-of-life disposal, though most products are themselves recyclable.

Types and manufacture

Most plastic lumber is made from polyethylene — high-density polyethylene (HDPE) recovered from milk jugs and other post-consumer waste is typical — coloured with pigments and often textured to resemble wood grain. It is produced by extrusion or continuous moulding into standard lumber dimensions. Wood-plastic composite (WPC) products add wood flour or fibre to improve stiffness and reduce cost. Structural plastic lumber, reinforced with fibreglass, is used for heavier applications such as railway sleepers (ties), marine pilings, and bridge components, where its resistance to rot and marine borers is valuable.

Applications

Common uses include decks, boardwalks, playground equipment, fencing, garden furniture, landscaping timbers and bollards. Demand grew from the 1990s as recycled-plastic manufacturers sought high-volume outlets for recovered HDPE and as consumers sought low-maintenance alternatives to preservative-treated wood.
