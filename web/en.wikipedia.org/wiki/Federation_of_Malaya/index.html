Federation of Malaya

The Federation of Malaya (Malay: Persekutuan Tanah Melayu) was a federation of eleven states of the Malay Peninsula — the nine Malay states and the British settlements of Penang and Malacca — established on 1 February 1948 and granted independence from the United Kingdom on 31 August 1957. In 1963 it combined with Singapore, Sabah and Sarawak to form the larger federation of Malaysia.

Formation

The federation replaced the short-lived Malayan Union (1946), whose liberal citizenship provisions and reduction of the Malay rulers' sovereignty provoked widespread Malay opposition and led to the founding of the United Malays National Organisation (UMNO). Under the Federation of Malaya Agreement of 1948, sovereignty was restored to the rulers, citizenship was tightened, and a federal government was created under a British High Commissioner.

Independence

The federation's early years were dominated by the Malayan Emergency, a counter-insurgency campaign against the Malayan Communist Party. In the 1955 federal elections the Alliance Party of Tunku Abdul Rahman won decisively and pressed for self-government; after constitutional talks in London, the federation became independent on 31 August 1957, with Tunku Abdul Rahman as its first prime minister and Abdul Rahman of Negeri Sembilan as its first Yang di-Pertuan Agong (king), a rotating elective monarchy drawn from the nine hereditary rulers.

Legacy

The 1957 federal constitution remained, with amendments, the basis of Malaysia's constitution after 1963, and "Malaya" survives as the common name for the peninsular part of the country (Peninsular Malaysia).
