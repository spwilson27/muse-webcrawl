Pygmalion was a king of Tyre, the great Phoenician city-state on the coast of the Levant, who reigned in the late 9th century BC. He is best known from classical tradition as the brother of Dido (Elissa), the legendary founder of Carthage, whose flight from Tyre was said to have been provoked by Pygmalion's greed and the murder of her husband.

Historical sources

The principal source for Pygmalion's reign is the lost history of Menander of Ephesus, whose chronology of Tyrian kings was quoted by the Jewish historian Josephus in Against Apion. According to Josephus's citation, Pygmalion succeeded his father Mattan I and reigned for 47 years. In the seventh year of his reign, his sister Elissa fled Tyre and founded Carthage in North Africa. Josephus used this synchronism to date the founding of Carthage, and by extension the reign of Hiram I and the building of Solomon's Temple, making Pygmalion's regnal dates an important anchor in ancient chronology.

The legend of Dido

In the account retold by later authors, notably the Roman historian Justin and, most famously, Virgil in the Aeneid, Pygmalion was consumed by avarice. Dido's husband Acerbas (also called Sychaeus), a priest of Melqart and the richest man in Tyre after the king, was murdered by Pygmalion out of greed for his wealth. Warned in a dream by her husband's ghost, Dido loaded the treasure onto ships, gathered a company of Tyrian nobles who opposed Pygmalion, and sailed away, eventually reaching the coast of modern Tunisia, where she founded Carthage.

Chronology

Modern scholars generally place Pygmalion's reign at approximately 831–785 BC, following the framework derived from Josephus. The seventh year of his reign, and hence the traditional founding of Carthage, is accordingly dated to about 825 BC, somewhat later than the date of 814 BC given by Timaeus of Tauromenium.
