Community gardening

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Community gardening is the practice of gardening a single piece of land collectively by a group of people, usually divided into individual or family plots alongside shared areas, on land that may be publicly or privately owned. Community gardens provide fresh produce and plants, neighbourhood improvement, a sense of community, and connection to the environment, and are found in cities and rural areas worldwide.

History

Precursors include the allotment gardens of industrial Europe, given statutory footing in Britain by the Allotments Acts of the early twentieth century. In the United States, waves of collective gardening accompanied economic crises and wars: the "potato patches" of the 1890s depression, relief gardens of the 1930s, and the victory gardens of both world wars, which at their Second World War peak produced a large share of America's vegetables. The modern community gardening movement took shape in the 1970s, when residents of cities such as New York reclaimed vacant lots — the Green Guerrillas, founded in 1973, famously converted a derelict lot in Manhattan into the Liz Christy Garden — prompting cities to create leasing programmes for garden groups.

Organisation and benefits

Gardens are typically run by associations or nonprofits that allocate plots for a small fee, set rules on maintenance and organic practice, and organise shared work, tools, water, and composting. Research and practitioner reports associate community gardens with improved access to fresh food, physical activity, mental health, social cohesion, reduced crime around garden sites, urban greening and biodiversity, and higher nearby property values — the last a double-edged effect in gentrifying districts, where gardens' insecure tenure on borrowed land is a perennial vulnerability.

Forms

Related forms include allotment gardens, school gardens, therapeutic and hospital gardens, guerrilla gardening on unauthorised land, and rooftop gardens. Worldwide, community gardening is part of the urban agriculture movement.
