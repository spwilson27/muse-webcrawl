Lintel

A lintel is a structural horizontal support spanning the space or opening between two vertical supports, such as the top of a door or window. It is often a load-bearing member, though it may also be a decorative architectural element. Together with posts or columns, lintels form the ancient post-and-lintel system of construction used in structures from Stonehenge to Greek temples.

Materials and mechanics

Lintels are made of stone, timber, steel, or reinforced concrete. A beam in bending, the lintel's top surface is compressed and its bottom stretched; stone, being weak in tension, limits stone lintels to relatively short spans, one reason ancient builders favored closely spaced columns and later developed the arch.

Ornament

In classical and later architecture, lintels often carry inscriptions, reliefs, or moldings, from Egyptian temple doorways to the carved tympanum-framing lintels of Romanesque churches.
