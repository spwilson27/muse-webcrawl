Bundesrat (Germany)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Bundesrat ("Federal Council") is the upper house of the German legislature, through which the sixteen states (Länder) participate in federal legislation and administration. It is not elected: its members are members of the Land governments, delegated and recallable by them, and its votes are cast as Land blocs weighted by population (69 votes in total, three to six per Land).

Functions

Legislation affecting the Länder — historically a large share of federal law, since the Länder administer most of it — requires Bundesrat consent ("consent laws"); for other laws the Bundesrat's objection can be overridden by the Bundestag with a corresponding majority. When opposition parties control a Bundesrat majority through state governments, Germany experiences its version of divided government, pushing legislation into the mediation committee and grand bargaining (the dynamic that drove labour-market and tax reforms of the 2000s). The Basic Law's design — executive federalism, in which state governments rather than a separately elected senate form the second chamber — is unique among federations and explains both Germany's strong cooperative federalism and its recurrent "joint-decision trap" reform debates.
