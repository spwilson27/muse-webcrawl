British Parliamentary Style (BP) is a common format in competitive debating that has its roots in the debating traditions of the United Kingdom and is the standard format at the World Universities Debating Championship and most international debating competitions.

Format

In BP, four teams of two speakers compete: two in "Government" (proposition) and two in "Opposition", arranged into Opening and Closing halves. Speaking order alternates between the sides. Each speech lasts between five and seven minutes, and speakers may accept points of information from opposing teams. After the motion is announced, typically 15 minutes of preparation time is given. The format is modelled on a parliamentary chamber, with teams split into front and back benches.

Roles of speakers

The first speaker defines the case; subsequent speakers extend and rebut. The Closing team's first speaker (the "extension speaker") must introduce a new substantive argument, and the final speaker on each side (the "summary" or "whip" speaker) concludes the team's case. In the last tournament rounds, the closing team's extension argument is critical to their success.

History

BP developed at the University of Glasgow and the UK universities' debating circuit in the 1980s and 1990s and has since become the default format for schools and universities in many countries, including Australia and Canada. Variants include American parliamentary debate and the Asian parliamentary style used in some Asian schools' competitions.
