Shimadzu Corporation

Shimadzu Corporation (株式会社島津製作所) is a Japanese public company, founded in 1875 in Kyoto, that manufactures precision instruments, measuring instruments, and medical and industrial equipment. Its products include analytical instruments such as chromatographs and mass spectrometers, medical imaging systems, and aircraft equipment.

History

The company was founded by Genzo Shimadzu Sr., who began manufacturing educational physics and chemistry instruments in Kyoto in 1875. His son, Genzo Shimadzu Jr., expanded the business considerably; among the company's early achievements was the first X-ray photograph taken in Japan in 1896, shortly after Röntgen's discovery. Shimadzu also developed early lead-acid batteries and became a major maker of scientific instruments in Japan.

Shimadzu scientist Koichi Tanaka shared the 2002 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the development of soft laser desorption ionization methods for mass spectrometry of biological macromolecules, an honor the company celebrated widely.

Products

Shimadzu's principal lines include analytical and measuring instruments (liquid and gas chromatographs, mass spectrometers, spectrophotometers, and materials testing machines), medical systems (X-ray, angiography, and diagnostic imaging), and industrial machinery and aircraft equipment such as flight control systems and pilot displays.
