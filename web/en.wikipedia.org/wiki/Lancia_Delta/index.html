The Lancia Delta is a small family car produced by the Italian automobile manufacturer Lancia in three generations. The first generation (1979–1994), designed by Giorgetto Giugiaro, was a five-door hatchback that won the European Car of the Year award in 1980 and achieved enduring fame in rallying: the four-wheel-drive Delta HF Integrale won six consecutive World Rally Championship constructors' titles between 1987 and 1992, a record that still stands.

First generation

Launched at the Frankfurt Motor Show in 1979, the Delta was initially offered with 1.3- and 1.5-litre engines. Sportier HF versions followed, and in 1986 the Delta S4's road-going successor, the four-wheel-drive HF 4WD, began the rally programme that produced the Integrale in 1987. Successive evolutions — the Integrale 8v, 16v and finally the wide-arched Evoluzione and Evoluzione II — became icons of Group A rallying, driven by champions such as Juha Kankkunen, Miki Biasion, Markku Alén and Didier Auriol, and remain highly collectible.

Later generations

A second-generation Delta (1993–1999) offered hatchback, saloon (Prisma-derived Dedra aside) and estate bodies but never repeated the original's sporting success. The name was revived for a third-generation model (2008–2014), a larger semi-luxury hatchback also sold briefly as a Chrysler in some markets. Lancia announced that the Delta name would return again on an electric model as part of the brand's relaunch in the 2020s.
