Fundamental rights in India

The Fundamental Rights in India are a group of rights guaranteed by Part III of the Constitution of India (Articles 12–35). Enforceable by the courts, they include the rights to equality, freedom, freedom from exploitation, freedom of religion, cultural and educational rights, and constitutional remedies.


Source: Constitution of India, Part III
Enforcement: Supreme Court (Art. 32), High Courts (Art. 226)

The rights

Articles 14–18 secure equality before law and abolish untouchability and titles; Articles 19–22 secure freedoms of speech, assembly, movement and protections in criminal cases; Articles 23–24 prohibit human trafficking and child labour in hazardous work; Articles 25–28 protect religion; Articles 29–30 protect minorities' culture and educational institutions.

Evolution

The right to property was removed as a fundamental right in 1978. Landmark cases such as Kesavananda Bharati (1973) held fundamental rights part of the constitution's basic structure; Justice K. S. Puttaswamy (2017) recognised privacy as a fundamental right. B. R. Ambedkar called Article 32, the right to move the Supreme Court, the "heart and soul" of the Constitution.
