FIA

The Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA; International Automobile Federation) is the governing body for many world motor sport series, including Formula One, and a global organisation promoting road safety and mobility. Founded in 1904 as the Association Internationale des Automobile Clubs Reconnus, it is headquartered in Paris.

FIA
Founded: 20 June 1904
Headquarters: Paris, France
President: Mohammed Ben Sulayem (from 2021)
Sport: Motor racing governance

Role

The FIA sanctions world championships — Formula One, World Rally, World Endurance, Formula E, Rallycross and Karting — sets technical and safety regulations, and licenses drivers and circuits. Its mobility arm represents motoring organisations and campaigns such as road-safety action. Notable past presidents include Jean-Marie Balestre and Max Mosley (1993–2009), followed by Jean Todt.
