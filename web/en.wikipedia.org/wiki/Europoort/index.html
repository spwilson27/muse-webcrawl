Europoort (English: Eurogate) is an area of the Port of Rotterdam and the adjoining industrial area in the Netherlands. It was built in the 1960s to accommodate very large crude carriers that could not reach the older port installations closer to the city.

Development

Post-war growth in oil imports and the increasing size of tankers led Rotterdam to expand westward toward the North Sea. Europoort was excavated on the island of Rozenburg, with deep-water basins able to receive the largest supertankers of the era. Refineries, oil storage terminals and petrochemical plants were established around the basins, connected by pipeline to refineries in the Netherlands, Belgium and Germany.

Today

Europoort remains one of the world's largest concentrations of crude-oil handling and refining capacity. Together with the later Maasvlakte extensions further west, it forms the seaward end of the Rotterdam port complex, for many years Europe's busiest port by cargo tonnage.
