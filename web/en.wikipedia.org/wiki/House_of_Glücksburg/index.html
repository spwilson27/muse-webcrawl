The House of Glücksburg (Danish: Lyksborg; German: Glücksburg) is a collateral branch of the House of Oldenburg, named after Glücksburg Castle in Schleswig-Holstein. Its members have reigned in Denmark, Norway, Greece, and Iceland, and the British royal family belongs to the house in the male line.

History

The line descends from Christian III of Denmark. In 1825 Friedrich Wilhelm took the title Duke of Glücksburg. His son Christian was chosen in 1863 as King Christian IX of Denmark—later nicknamed the "father-in-law of Europe" because his children married into many royal houses: his son became George I of Greece, his daughter Alexandra married Edward VII of Britain, and his daughter Dagmar became Empress Maria Feodorovna of Russia.

Reigning branches

The Danish line's senior branch now reigns in Norway (since 1905, beginning with Haakon VII) and, following the 2024 abdication of Margrethe II, Frederik X reigns in Denmark. The Greek branch ruled from 1863 until the monarchy's abolition in 1974. Prince Philip, Duke of Edinburgh, was born a prince of Greece and Denmark of this house; Charles III is thus a patrilineal member, though the British royal house retains the name Windsor.
