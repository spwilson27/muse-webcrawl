Aldous Huxley

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Aldous Huxley: tall, thin, heavy-lidded eyes behind round glasses, photographed in California in the 1950s]

Aldous Leonard Huxley (26 July 1894 – 22 November 1963) was an English writer and philosopher. His bibliography spans nearly 50 books, including novels and non-fiction works, as well as essays, narratives and poems. By the end of his life, Huxley was acknowledged as one of the outstanding intellectuals of his time, nominated for the Nobel Prize in Literature nine times.

Grandson of T. H. Huxley ('Darwin's bulldog') and brother of the biologist Julian Huxley, he was nearly blinded by illness as a teenager. His early satires (Crome Yellow, Point Counter Point) led to Brave New World (1932). Emigrating to Los Angeles in 1937, he turned toward mysticism and psychedelic exploration (The Doors of Perception, 1954, on mescaline) and wrote his utopian counterpart to his dystopia, Island (1962). He died on the same day as C. S. Lewis and John F. Kennedy.
