Romanian Rhapsodies (Enescu)

The Romanian Rhapsodies, Op. 11, are two orchestral rhapsodies composed by George Enescu in 1901, when he was nineteen and twenty years old. They are his best-known works and among the most popular pieces of Romanian classical music.

The rhapsodies

Rhapsody No. 1 in D major is the more famous of the two. It is a lively, dance-like work built from Romanian folk melodies, including the tune "Am un leu şi vreau să-l beu" ("I have a coin and I want to drink"), and builds to a whirlwind conclusion in the manner of a horă accelerating into a frenzy. Rhapsody No. 2 in D major is more reflective, opening with the folk song "Pe o dunga de răzor" and quoting the medieval ballad of Master Manole; its middle section introduces a more animated dance before a quiet close.

Reception

Both rhapsodies were premiered in Bucharest in 1903 under the composer's direction. The First Rhapsody in particular became an international success and a concert favourite, sometimes to Enescu's frustration, as its popularity overshadowed his more ambitious later works such as the opera Œdipe. The rhapsodies were popularised in the United States by conductors including Leopold Stokowski.
