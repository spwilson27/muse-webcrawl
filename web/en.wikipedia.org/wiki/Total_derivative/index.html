Total derivative

In mathematics, the total derivative of a function of several variables is its derivative with respect to one variable taking into account that the other variables may themselves depend on that variable. It contrasts with a partial derivative, which holds the other variables constant.

Formula

If f depends on x and y, and both depend on t, then the chain rule gives: df/dt = (∂f/∂x)(dx/dt) + (∂f/∂y)(dy/dt). More generally, for a function of variables x1…xn each depending on t, the total derivative sums the partials weighted by each variable's rate of change. In differential form, the total differential df = Σ (∂f/∂xi) dxi captures the linear approximation to the change in f.

Applications

Total derivatives are fundamental in thermodynamics (relating state functions), in Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics, in material derivatives in fluid dynamics, and in economics, where the total derivative of a utility or production function captures indirect effects along a constraint; the related notion of total differentiation underlies the implicit function theorem.
