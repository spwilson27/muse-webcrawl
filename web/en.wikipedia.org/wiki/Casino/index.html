A casino is a facility for gambling in which patrons wager money on games of chance, sometimes combined with skill, typically in a setting that also offers hotels, restaurants, entertainment, and shopping. The word comes from the Italian casino, a small house or pavilion used for pleasure. Casinos profit from the house edge built into their games, which guarantees the house a statistical advantage over time.

History

The first known European gambling house was the Ridotto, opened in Venice in 1638 to provide controlled gambling during carnival. The 19th century saw famous establishments at spa towns such as Baden-Baden and the Monte Carlo Casino (1863) in Monaco. In the United States, Nevada legalised casino gambling in 1931, and Las Vegas grew into the world's best-known casino city; Atlantic City followed in 1978. In the 21st century, Macau — where casinos were legalised under Portuguese administration and expanded after 2002 — surpassed Las Vegas in gaming revenue, and Singapore and other jurisdictions opened integrated resorts.

Games and operations

Casinos offer table games (blackjack, roulette, baccarat, craps, poker variants) run by dealers, and electronic gaming machines — slot machines typically generate the largest share of revenue. Layouts, lighting, free drinks, loyalty programmes ("comps"), and the absence of clocks are designed to encourage continued play. Operations are heavily surveilled ("eye in the sky") and licensed, and casinos must guard against cheating, advantage play such as card counting, money laundering, and problem gambling, for which most jurisdictions impose responsible-gambling measures.

Online casinos

Since the mid-1990s, internet casinos have offered the same games remotely, with regulation varying widely by country; many jurisdictions license online operators while others prohibit them.
