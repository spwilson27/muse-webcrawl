The Audi RS 7 Sportback is a high-performance version of the Audi A7 developed and produced by Audi Sport GmbH, the high-performance subsidiary of the German automobile manufacturer Audi AG. It was first unveiled at the 2013 North American International Auto Show in Detroit.

The RS 7 combines the coupé-like styling of the A7 Sportback with a powerful twin-turbocharged V8 engine, Audi's quattro permanent all-wheel drive, and a luxurious interior, placing it in competition with cars such as the BMW M6 Gran Coupé, the Mercedes-AMG CLS 63, and the Porsche Panamera Turbo.

First generation (C7, 2013–2018)

The first-generation RS 7 is powered by a 4.0-litre TFSI twin-turbocharged V8 engine producing 560 PS (412 kW; 552 hp) and 700 N·m (516 lb·ft) of torque. An optional Dynamic Plus package increased output to 605 PS (445 kW; 597 hp) in the RS 7 Performance model introduced for 2016. Power is transmitted through an eight-speed Tiptronic automatic transmission to all four wheels.

Audi quoted a 0–100 km/h (0–62 mph) acceleration time of 3.9 seconds for the standard car and 3.7 seconds for the Performance version, with a top speed of up to 305 km/h (190 mph) when equipped with the Dynamic Plus package. The engine features cylinder on demand technology, deactivating four cylinders under light loads to improve fuel efficiency.

Second generation (C8, 2019–present)

The second-generation RS 7 Sportback debuted at the 2019 Frankfurt Motor Show. It uses the same 4.0-litre twin-turbo V8, now augmented by a 48-volt mild-hybrid system, producing 600 PS (441 kW; 592 hp) and 800 N·m (590 lb·ft) of torque. Audi quotes 0–100 km/h in 3.6 seconds. The car is based on the MLB Evo platform and features a wider body than the standard A7, along with standard air suspension and optional dynamic all-wheel steering.

For the 2023 model year, Audi introduced the RS 7 Performance, with output increased to 630 PS (463 kW; 621 hp) and 850 N·m of torque, achieved through larger turbochargers and increased boost pressure.
