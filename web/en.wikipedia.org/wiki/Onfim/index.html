Onfim (Old Novgorodian: Онѳиме, Onfime) was a boy who lived in Novgorod (present-day Veliky Novgorod, Russia) in the 13th century. He left a remarkable corpus of homework exercises and drawings scratched into soft birch bark, which were preserved in the clay soil of Novgorod and rediscovered by archaeologists in the 20th century. Historians estimate Onfim was about six or seven years old when he made them.

The writings

Seventeen birch bark items by Onfim survive: twelve are writings, mostly alphabets and syllable exercises copied as handwriting practice, and five include drawings. His exercises include psalm fragments and standard formulae such as "Lord, help your servant Onfim." One note reads "I am a beast", beside a drawing of a fantastic animal; another depicts Onfim himself as a mounted warrior striking down an enemy, captioned with his name. His spelling provides valuable evidence for the Old Novgorod dialect and for everyday literacy in medieval Rus'.

Birch bark documents

Onfim's work forms part of the corpus of birch bark manuscripts excavated at Novgorod since 1951, numbering over a thousand items. Because birch bark was cheap, the documents reveal that literacy was widespread among ordinary townspeople, including children and women — unusual for medieval Europe.
