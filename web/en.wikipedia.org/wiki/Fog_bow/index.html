Fog bow

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A fog bow, sometimes called a white rainbow or cloud bow, is a natural optical phenomenon similar to a rainbow, but produced by sunlight interacting with the very small water droplets of fog, mist or cloud rather than with larger raindrops. Because fog droplets are so tiny (often under 0.05 mm), diffraction broadens and overlaps the refracted colours, washing them out; the bow therefore appears as a broad, faint white arc, sometimes with a slight reddish outer edge and bluish inner edge.

Formation and appearance

Like a rainbow, a fog bow is centred on the antisolar point, opposite the sun, and its angular radius (about 42° in principle) is somewhat smaller than a rainbow's because of diffraction effects. Fog bows are best seen when the sun is low and shines into a thin bank of fog with the observer's shadow falling toward the fog. They are observed from hillsides, coasts and aircraft, and are related to the glory and the Brocken spectre, which may appear at the same point in the sky.
