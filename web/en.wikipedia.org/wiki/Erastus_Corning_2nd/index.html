Erastus Corning 2nd

Born: October 7, 1909, Albany, New York
Died: May 28, 1983 (aged 73), Boston, Massachusetts
Office: Mayor of Albany, 1942–1983

Erastus Corning 2nd (October 7, 1909 – May 28, 1983) was an American politician and the longest-serving mayor of Albany, New York, holding office from 1942 until his death in 1983 — a tenure of 41 years. A Democrat, he was the scion of the Corning family: his great-grandfather Erastus Corning had also been mayor of Albany and a founder of the New York Central Railroad; his father was Lieutenant Governor of New York Edwin Corning.

Career

Educated at the Groton School and Yale University, Corning served in the New York State Assembly and State Senate before winning the Albany mayoralty in 1941. He enlisted in the U.S. Army during the Second World War, serving in Iceland and Europe, while retaining his office. Working with Democratic county chairman Daniel P. O'Connell, Corning presided over one of the last big-city political machines in the United States, which dominated Albany politics for decades.

Legacy

His administration saw the construction of the Empire State Plaza under Governor Nelson Rockefeller, with whom Corning had a famously courteous but adversarial relationship. The Corning Tower, the tallest building in Albany, is named for him, as are Corning City Preserve and other sites. Paul Grondahl's biography Mayor Corning: Albany Icon, Albany Enigma (2007) examines his era.
