Bass (voice type)

Bass
Vocal rangeTypically E2–E4
SubtypesBasso profondo, basso buffo, basso cantante, bass-baritone

A bass is a type of classical male singing voice whose vocal range lies below the baritone, typically from about E2 to E4. It is the lowest of the standard voice types. The term derives from the Italian basso, "low".

Subtypes and roles

The basso profondo reaches the lowest notes and often portrays solemn authority figures, such as Sarastro in Mozart's The Magic Flute. The basso buffo specialises in comic roles requiring agility and patter, such as Bartolo in Rossini's The Barber of Seville. The basso cantante is a lighter, more lyrical bass, and the bass-baritone overlaps the baritone range; famous bass roles include Boris Godunov, King Philip in Don Carlos, and Baron Ochs in Der Rosenkavalier.

In choral music

In four-part choral writing (SATB), the basses sing the lowest line and provide the harmonic foundation. Russian Orthodox choral music is celebrated for its exceptionally deep oktavists, who sing an octave below the normal bass line.
