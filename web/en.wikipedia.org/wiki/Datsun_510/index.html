Datsun 510

ManufacturerNissan (Datsun)
Production1967–1973
ClassCompact car
LayoutFront-engine, rear-wheel drive

The Datsun 510 is a series of compact cars built by Nissan under the Datsun brand from 1967 to 1973, sold in Japan as the third-generation Bluebird. It was offered as a four-door sedan, two-door sedan, five-door station wagon and two-door coupé. Influenced by contemporary European sedans, the 510 combined a overhead-cam engine, four-wheel independent suspension on sedan models, front disc brakes and unibody construction at an affordable price.

The 510 earned a reputation as a "poor man's BMW" for its handling and specification.

Engineering

Export models were powered mainly by the 1.6-litre L16 inline-four engine producing about 96 hp, paired with a four-speed manual or three-speed automatic transmission. MacPherson-strut front suspension and, on sedans, a semi-trailing-arm independent rear suspension gave the car handling comparable to more expensive European rivals.

Motorsport

The 510 achieved major success in North American racing. Prepared by Brock Racing Enterprises (BRE) under Peter Brock, 510s driven by John Morton won the SCCA Trans-Am 2.5 Challenge championships in 1971 and 1972 against Alfa Romeo and BMW opposition. The car also won its class in the East African Safari Rally. Its racing record and tunability made it a lasting favourite in amateur motorsport and classic-car circles.
