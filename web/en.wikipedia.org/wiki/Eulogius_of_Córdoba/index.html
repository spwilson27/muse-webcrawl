Eulogius of Córdoba

Saint Eulogius of Córdoba (Spanish: San Eulogio; died 11 March 859) was a Spanish priest and scholar in Muslim-ruled Córdoba and the principal chronicler of the Martyrs of Córdoba, a group of Christians executed in the 850s for publicly insulting Islam or apostatising from it. He encouraged and documented the movement in his writings and was himself executed in 859.

Life

Eulogius came from a senatorial family of Córdoba, then the capital of the Umayyad Emirate of Córdoba. Educated at the monastery of St Zoilus, he became a priest and head of the principal ecclesiastical school of the city, known for his learning and asceticism. In the 850s a voluntary martyrdom movement arose among Córdoba's Christians: dozens publicly denounced Muhammad before Muslim judges, an offence punishable by death. Eulogius, though he did not seek death himself at first, championed the martyrs and composed his Memoriale sanctorum, the Documentum martyriale, and the Liber apologeticus martyrum to defend their sanctity against critics, including many fellow Christians who considered the deaths self-inflicted.

Martyrdom and legacy

Imprisoned more than once, Eulogius was elected archbishop of Toledo in 858 but could not take up the see. He was arrested for sheltering Leocritia, a young woman who had converted from Islam to Christianity, and — refusing to recant and openly professing his faith before the judge — was beheaded on 11 March 859. His works, preserved through copies carried to northern Spain, are the main source for the religious tensions of 9th-century al-Andalus and for Latin Christian culture under Islamic rule. His feast day is 11 March.
