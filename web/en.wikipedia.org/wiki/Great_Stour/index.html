Great Stour

The River Great Stour is a river in Kent, England, which rises near the village of Lenham and flows for approximately 58 miles (93 km) before reaching the English Channel at Pegwell Bay near Sandwich. It is the second-longest river in Kent, after the River Medway, and is noted for its passage through the historic city of Canterbury.

Course

The Great Stour rises from springs on the greensand ridge near Lenham, west of Ashford. It flows eastward through Ashford, where it is joined by the East Stour, then continues northeast through the North Downs gap near Wye and Chilham. From Canterbury the river flows on to Fordwich — once its port and the smallest town in England by population — and then past the former Roman shore fort at Richborough. Beyond Sandwich the river reaches the sea at Pegwell Bay.

Tributaries

The principal tributaries of the Great Stour include the East Stour, which rises near Hythe, the Little Stour, which joins it below Littlebourne, and the Nailbourne, an intermittent stream (a bourne) that rises at Lyminge and flows through the Elham Valley before joining the Little Stour.

History and use

The river was navigable as far as Fordwich in Roman and medieval times, and Canterbury's prosperity as a port depended upon it before the silting of the Wantsum Channel in the 15th and 16th centuries. The Stour valley carried corn mills, iron working in the Weald, and later paper mills. Today the river supports coarse fishing, an abstraction source for public water supply, and flood management works along its course.
