Gameplay of Pokémon

A turn-based battle between two Pokémon in the core series games.

The gameplay of the Pokémon video game series revolves around catching, training, and battling fictional creatures called Pokémon. In the core role-playing games, developed by Game Freak and published by Nintendo and The Pokémon Company, players assume the role of a Pokémon Trainer travelling through a fictional region, capturing wild Pokémon with devices called Poké Balls, raising a team, and challenging other Trainers, culminating in battles against the region's Elite Four and Champion.

Exploration and capture

Players explore an overworld of towns, routes, and dungeons. Wild Pokémon appear in tall grass, caves, and water, and are encountered in random battles (or, in later games, visible in the overworld). During a wild encounter the player may fight, flee, use an item, or attempt a capture by throwing a Poké Ball; the success of a capture depends on the species' catch rate, the Pokémon's remaining health and status condition, and the type of ball used. Captured Pokémon are stored in a personal computer system once the party of six is full.

Battles

Battles are turn-based. Each Pokémon knows up to four moves, which may deal damage, inflict status conditions such as paralysis, poison, burn, sleep, or freeze, or alter statistics. Every species belongs to one or two of eighteen elemental types (such as Fire, Water, Grass, Electric, and Dragon); type match-ups form a rock-paper-scissors system of strengths, weaknesses, and immunities. Individual Pokémon are further distinguished by abilities, natures, and individual and effort values that shape their statistics as they gain experience and level up.

Progression and trading

Defeating Trainers and Gym Leaders earns badges that mark story progress. Many species evolve into stronger forms upon reaching a certain level, or through stones, trading, friendship, or other conditions. Trading Pokémon with other players—originally via the Game Boy link cable, later wirelessly and online—is a central mechanic, since some species are exclusive to particular versions and some evolve only when traded. Competitive battling, breeding for optimal statistics, and completing the Pokédex (the in-game encyclopedia) provide long-term goals beyond the main story.
