Cispadane Republic

The Cispadane Republic (Italian: Repubblica Cispadana) was a short-lived French client republic founded in northern Italy in 1796 during the Italian campaigns of Napoleon Bonaparte. Its name means "on this side of the Po" — the territory lay south of the river Po.

History

After the French victory at the Battle of Lodi in May 1796, the cities of Modena, Reggio, Ferrara and Bologna threw off their old rulers — the Este dukes and the Papal legations — and formed a confederation. In October 1796 delegates met at Modena, and in December the Cispadane Republic was formally proclaimed at Reggio Emilia. It was at the Reggio congress, on 7 January 1797, that the red, white and green tricolour was first adopted as a national flag — the origin of the modern flag of Italy.

In 1797 the republic was merged with the Transpadane Republic (Lombardy) to form the larger Cisalpine Republic, of which it became a core part.

Legacy

Although it existed for less than a year, the Cispadane Republic is remembered chiefly for the birth of the Italian tricolour at Reggio Emilia, an event celebrated in Italy to this day.
