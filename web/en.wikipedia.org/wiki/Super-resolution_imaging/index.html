Super-resolution imaging

Super-resolution reconstructs a higher-resolution image from one or more low-resolution observations.

Super-resolution imaging (SR) is a class of techniques that enhance the resolution of an imaging system beyond the limits imposed by its hardware. In optical imaging, the relevant limit is often the diffraction limit; in digital imaging, super-resolution combines information from multiple low-resolution frames — shifted by sub-pixel amounts — or exploits prior knowledge about the image to reconstruct detail that no single frame contains.

Motivation

High-resolution images are desirable in satellite and aerial observation, medical imaging, surveillance, microscopy, and consumer photography, but sensor and lens costs, physical size, and noise constraints limit the resolution that hardware alone can deliver. Computational super-resolution offers a cheaper route to improved detail, trading computation for optics.

Methods

Multi-frame super-resolution

Multi-frame methods register several low-resolution images of the same scene, each sampled on a slightly different grid because of camera or scene motion, and fuse them into a single high-resolution image. Because each frame aliases high frequencies differently, the combined measurements can, in principle, disambiguate detail beyond the Nyquist limit of any individual frame.

Single-image super-resolution

Single-image methods infer plausible high-resolution content from one low-resolution input. Early approaches used interpolation and edge-directed filtering; modern methods use machine learning, particularly convolutional neural networks and generative adversarial networks trained on large image datasets, to hallucinate statistically likely detail. Such methods can produce convincing textures but may invent detail that was not present in the scene, which matters in forensic and scientific use.

Applications

  • Earth observation and remote sensing
  • Medical image enhancement (MRI, ultrasound, microscopy)
  • Surveillance and licence-plate recognition
  • Smartphone computational photography
