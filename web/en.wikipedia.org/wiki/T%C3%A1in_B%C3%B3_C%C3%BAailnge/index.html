Táin Bó Cúailnge

Táin Bó Cúailnge (the "Cattle Raid of Cooley") is an epic from early Irish literature, the central text of the Ulster Cycle of Irish mythology. Set in the Iron Age Ireland of heroic legend, it tells of the war waged by Queen Medb of Connacht to seize the great bull Donn Cúailnge from Ulster, and of the lone defence of Ulster by the boy hero Cú Chulainn.

Plot

After a quarrel with her husband Ailill over whose wealth is greater, Queen Medb learns that Ailill owns the magnificent white-horned bull Finnbhennach while she lacks an equal; she therefore seeks to obtain the Donn Cúailnge, the great brown bull of Cooley. When negotiation fails, she invades Ulster, whose warriors lie incapacitated by the curse of Macha. Only Cú Chulainn, still a youth, stands in her way, fighting a series of single combats at fords, including the tragic duel with his foster-brother Fer Diad. The tale ends with the battle of the two bulls, in which Donn Cúailnge kills Finnbhennach and then dies himself.

Manuscripts

The Táin survives in several recensions, principally in the twelfth-century Book of Leinster and the earlier Lebor na hUidre. Thomas Kinsella's 1969 English translation, illustrated by Louis le Brocquy, brought the epic to a wide modern readership.
