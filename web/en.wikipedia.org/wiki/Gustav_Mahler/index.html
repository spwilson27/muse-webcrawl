Gustav Mahler

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Gustav Mahler conducting the Vienna Philharmonic: dark hair, glasses, baton mid-gesture]

Gustav Mahler (7 July 1860 – 18 May 1911) was an Austro-Bohemian Romantic composer and one of the leading conductors of his generation. His output, relatively small for its scale, bridged the 19th-century Austro-German tradition and the modernism of the early 20th century: ten symphonies (the last unfinished) of vast scope and the song cycles Das Lied von der Erde and Kindertotenlieder.

As director of the Vienna Court Opera (1897–1907) Mahler was a demanding reformer; antisemitism and press campaigns dogged his tenure. He composed in summer huts in the Alps, remarking that a symphony 'must be like the world — it must embrace everything'. His music, neglected after his death and banned under the Nazis, was revived from the 1960s, notably by Leonard Bernstein, and now stands at the centre of the repertoire.
