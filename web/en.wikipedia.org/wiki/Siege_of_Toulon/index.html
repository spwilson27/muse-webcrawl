Siege of Toulon

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Siege of Toulon (18 September – 18 December 1793) took place during the French Revolutionary Wars, when royalist inhabitants of the great naval port of Toulon handed the city and the French Mediterranean fleet to an Anglo-Spanish force, and Republican armies besieged them. The siege is famous for bringing the young artillery officer Napoleon Bonaparte to prominence.

Background

In August 1793, amid the federalist revolts against the revolutionary government in Paris, Toulon's royalists admitted a British fleet under Admiral Lord Hood, which occupied the forts commanding the harbour and seized some thirty French warships. The Convention sent successive generals — Carteaux, then Doppet, then Dugommier — to recover the port.

The siege

Bonaparte, appointed to command the Republican artillery, argued that the key to Toulon was not the city walls but the forts on the heights of l'Eguillette and Balaguier dominating the anchorage. After heavy fighting, French forces stormed the key position of "Little Gibraltar" on the night of 16–17 December; guns emplaced on the captured heights made the Allied fleet's position untenable.

Aftermath

The Allies evacuated Toulon on 18–19 December, taking thousands of royalist refugees and burning or removing much of the fleet and arsenal; some 14,000–15,000 Toulonnais fled or faced reprisals, and the Convention ordered mass executions and temporarily renamed the city Port-la-Montagne. Bonaparte was promoted to brigadier general at the age of twenty-four.
