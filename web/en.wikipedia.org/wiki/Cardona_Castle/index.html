Cardona Castle

Cardona Castle (Catalan: Castell de Cardona) is a historic fortress in the town of Cardona, in the comarca of Bages, Catalonia, Spain. It stands on a hill overlooking the river valley of the Cardener and the town's famous salt mines.

History

The castle was built in 886 by Wilfred the Hairy, count of Barcelona, and became the centre of the viscounty, later county and duchy of Cardona. The Cardona family, enriched by the salt trade, became one of the most powerful houses of the Crown of Aragon, sometimes described as "kings without a crown".

Within the walls stands the collegiate church of Sant Vicenç, begun in 1019 and consecrated in 1040, a masterpiece of Catalan Romanesque architecture with one of the earliest ribbed vaults in Europe.

War of the Spanish Succession

Cardona was the last stronghold in Catalonia to surrender to the Bourbon forces of Philip V, holding out until 18 September 1714, a week after the fall of Barcelona. Despite this, its fortifications were only partly demolished.

Today

The castle now houses a parador (state-run hotel) and museum, and is regarded as one of the most important medieval fortresses in Catalonia.
