Chihuahua (state)

Chihuahua (Spanish pronunciation: [tʃiˈwawa]), officially the Free and Sovereign State of Chihuahua (Spanish: Estado Libre y Soberano de Chihuahua), is one of the 31 states which, along with Mexico City, comprise the 32 federal entities of Mexico. It is located in northwestern Mexico and is bordered by the states of Sonora to the west, Sinaloa to the southwest, Durango to the south, and Coahuila to the east. To the northeast it borders the US state of Texas, and to the north and northwest it borders the US states of New Mexico and Texas.

Geography

Chihuahua is the largest state in Mexico by area, larger than the entire United Kingdom. Its terrain divides into three regions: the Sierra Madre Occidental in the west, with pine-oak forests and the dramatic Copper Canyon system — a network of gorges collectively larger and in places deeper than the Grand Canyon; the central grasslands and transitions; and the Chihuahuan Desert in the east, which extends into Texas and New Mexico. The state's climate ranges from alpine in the mountains to hot desert in the lowlands.

History

The region was inhabited in pre-Columbian times by peoples including the builders of Paquimé (Casas Grandes), a major trading centre that flourished between roughly 1130 and 1450 CE. Spanish colonisation followed the discovery of silver near Santa Bárbara in 1567; the city of Chihuahua was founded in 1709. The priest Miguel Hidalgo, father of Mexican independence, was executed in Chihuahua City in 1811.

During the Mexican Revolution, Chihuahua was the home base of Pancho Villa and his División del Norte. In the later twentieth century the border city of Ciudad Juárez grew into a major manufacturing (maquiladora) centre, and in the 2000s–2010s it was severely affected by drug-cartel violence, from which it has partially recovered.

Economy and demographics

The economy rests on manufacturing in Juárez and Chihuahua City (automotive and electronics), mining (silver, lead, zinc), agriculture and cattle ranching, and forestry in the Sierra. The state is home to the indigenous Rarámuri (Tarahumara), Tepehuán, Guarijío and Pima peoples, as well as Mennonite communities established in the 1920s around Cuauhtémoc, noted for their cheese production. The state lends its name to the Chihuahua dog breed, though the breed's precise origin is debated.
