Play (activity)

Children at play

Play is a range of voluntary, intrinsically motivated activities typically associated with pleasure and enjoyment, undertaken by children and adults alike. Play may consist of amusing and fun interactions with toys, games, sports or other people, and it is found not only in humans but in many animals, especially young mammals and birds.

Functions

Play is widely regarded as essential to healthy development. Through play, children practise motor skills, social negotiation, language and imagination, and rehearse adult roles in a low-risk setting. Ethologists observe similar functions in animal play, which hones fighting, hunting and escape skills. Psychologists and educators from Vygotsky to Piaget have emphasised play's role in cognitive growth; Vygotsky argued that in play a child behaves "a head taller" than in everyday life.

Definitions and theory

The Dutch historian Johan Huizinga's Homo Ludens (1938) famously treated play as a primary, irreducible element of culture, preceding and generating cultural forms such as law, war, poetry and sport. Later scholars, including Roger Caillois, developed typologies of play ranging from competition and chance to mimicry and vertigo. Modern research also studies adult play and its links to creativity, stress relief and workplace culture, while "serious games" and gamification apply play structures to education and training.
