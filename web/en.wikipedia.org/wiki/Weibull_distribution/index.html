Weibull distribution

Parametersλ > 0 scale; k > 0 shape
Supportx ∈ [0, ∞)
PDFf(x) = (k/λ)(x/λ)k−1 e−(x/λ)k
CDFF(x) = 1 − e−(x/λ)k
Meanλ Γ(1 + 1/k)

In probability theory and statistics, the Weibull distribution is a continuous probability distribution named after Swedish mathematician Waloddi Weibull, who described it in detail in 1951, although it was first identified by Fréchet in 1927 and applied by Rosin and Rammler in 1933. It is a versatile two-parameter family used extensively in reliability engineering and survival analysis.

Definition

With scale parameter λ > 0 and shape parameter k > 0, the probability density function is f(x; λ, k) = (k/λ)(x/λ)k−1 exp(−(x/λ)k) for x ≥ 0, and the cumulative distribution function is F(x) = 1 − exp(−(x/λ)k). Special cases include the exponential distribution (k = 1) and the Rayleigh distribution (k = 2).

Applications

The shape parameter controls the failure rate: k < 1 gives a decreasing failure rate (early failures), k = 1 a constant rate, and k > 1 an increasing rate (wear-out), which lets one distribution model the whole "bathtub curve" of product lifetimes. Weibull analysis is standard for modelling component life data, wind speeds in energy engineering, and material strength, where the distribution also arises from weakest-link extreme value theory. Related to the weak formulations of extreme value statistics, the Weibull is one of the three extreme value distribution types.
