Metallica

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Metallica on stage in the round: four musicians in black before a stadium crowd, the snake pit of fans at the centre]

Metallica is an American heavy metal band formed in Los Angeles in 1981 by drummer Lars Ulrich and guitarist-vocalist James Hetfield. The leading band of thrash metal's 'Big Four' (with Slayer, Megadeth and Anthrax), they became the best-selling metal act in history by wedding underground speed and aggression to steadily widening songcraft.

Kill 'Em All (1983) and Ride the Lightning (1984) defined thrash; Master of Puppets (1986) is widely ranked metal's greatest album; the 1991 self-titled 'Black Album' — 'Enter Sandman', 'The Unforgiven', 'Nothing Else Matters' — sold in the tens of millions. Bassist Cliff Burton's death in a 1986 tour-bus crash in Sweden nearly broke the band; successors Jason Newsted (1986–2001) and Robert Trujillo (from 2003) complete the classic lineups. Song titles cited only.

Mainstream and controversy

The Black Album's Bob Rock production took Metallica to stadiums and to a purist backlash that has never fully abated. The 2000 lawsuit against Napster made them the face of the industry's file-sharing war; the documentary Some Kind of Monster (2004) exposed the band in group therapy during the troubled St. Anger sessions — an act of self-exposure few stars would risk.

Standing

Metallica's M72-era stadium tours remain metal's biggest live draw; their foundation All Within My Hands funds workforce education and food banks. In 2018 they received Sweden's Polar Music Prize, the citation treating metal as Sweden's folk music of export. They are the only band to have played all seven continents (Antarctica, 2013).
