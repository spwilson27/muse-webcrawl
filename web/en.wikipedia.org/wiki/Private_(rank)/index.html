Private (rank)

Private is the lowest enlisted rank in many armies.

A private is a soldier of the lowest military rank (equivalent to NATO OR-1 to OR-3 grades depending on the country and grade). The term originates in the 16th century, when soldiers were hired or mustered as "private soldiers" — men serving as individuals rather than as part of a lord's retinue or a formed unit's officer corps.

Usage by country

United Kingdom

In the British Army, private (Pte) is the lowest rank after completing training; many regiments use traditional titles — fusilier, guardsman, kingsman, rifleman, signaller, sapper (Royal Engineers), gunner (Royal Artillery), trooper (cavalry), and craftsman (REME). The next rank is lance corporal.

United States

The US Army uses a ladder of private grades: Private E-1 (PV1, "recruit"), Private E-2 (PV2), and Private First Class (PFC, E-3). The Marine Corps uses Private and Private First Class. Privates are sometimes colloquially the butt of the catchphrase "the lowly private" but form the bulk of the enlisted force.

Other countries

Most Commonwealth armies follow the British pattern. In Germany the equivalent basic rank is Soldat (with arm-specific variants); in France, soldat de deuxième classe. Some armies, such as Russia's, use ryadovoy.

History

Early modern "private men" (as in the New Model Army) stood below the officers and "non-commissioned" ranks; "private" signalled the absence of any particular office or commission. The word entered general English usage for the rank as standing armies standardised their hierarchies in the 18th and 19th centuries.
