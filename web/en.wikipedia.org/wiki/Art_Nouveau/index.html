Art Nouveau

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
A lithograph poster in the Art Nouveau style: a woman in flowing robes framed by sinuous whiplash curves and floral ornament

Art Nouveau (French for 'new art') is an international style of art, architecture and applied art, especially the decorative arts, that was most popular between about 1890 and 1910. Inspired by natural forms — plants, flowers and curved 'whiplash' lines — it sought to break down hierarchies between fine and applied art and to create a total, modern style for the industrial age.

Leading figures included the Czech poster artist Alphonse Mucha, the Belgian architect Victor Horta, the Spanish architect Antoni Gaudí, the Scottish designer Charles Rennie Mackintosh, and the Austrian painter Gustav Klimt, whose Vienna Secession was a close cousin of the movement. In Germany it was called Jugendstil, in Italy Stile Liberty, and in Spain Modernisme.

Architecture and design

Horta's Hôtel Tassel in Brussels (1893) and Hector Guimard's Paris Métro entrances are landmark works. Art Nouveau embraced new materials — cast iron, glass, ceramics — and influenced everything from jewellery (René Lalique) and glassware (Louis Comfort Tiffany, Émile Gallé) to typography and furniture.
