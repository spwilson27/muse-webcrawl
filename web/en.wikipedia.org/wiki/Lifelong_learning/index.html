Lifelong learning

Lifelong learning
DefinitionThe ongoing, voluntary and self-motivated pursuit of knowledge for personal or professional reasons
StagesFormal, non-formal and informal learning across the lifespan

Lifelong learning is the "ongoing, voluntary, and self-motivated" pursuit of knowledge for either personal or professional reasons. It not only enhances social inclusion, active citizenship and personal development, but also self-sustainability, competitiveness and employability. The concept recognises that learning is not confined to childhood and the classroom but takes place throughout life and in a range of situations.

The term recognises that learners change over their lifetime: the learning of an adult is different from that of a child, and the traditional model of a front-loaded education followed by work has been challenged by longer lives, technological change and shifting labour markets.

Origins

The idea has roots in the work of educators such as Eduard Lindeman, who wrote in The Meaning of Adult Education (1926) that "the whole of life is learning". The 1972 UNESCO Faure report, Learning to Be, popularised "lifelong education" as a master concept for educational policy, and the 1996 Delors report Learning: The Treasure Within restated it around four pillars: learning to know, learning to do, learning to live together and learning to be.

Forms

Lifelong learning includes formal learning (degree and certificate programmes), non-formal learning (structured courses not leading to formal qualifications, such as workplace training and adult education classes) and informal learning (self-directed learning from experience, reading, media and online resources). Adult education, continuing professional development, second-chance schooling, community learning and universities of the third age are all part of the landscape.

Policy

Governments and international bodies — including UNESCO, the OECD and the European Union — promote lifelong learning as a response to globalisation, automation and ageing populations. The EU has set participation targets for adult learning, and the United Nations' Sustainable Development Goal 4 calls for inclusive, equitable quality education and lifelong learning opportunities for all.

Technology

Massive open online courses (MOOCs), open educational resources, podcasts and mobile learning have lowered the cost of continued study, making self-directed learning practical at any age, though digital divides in access remain a concern.
