Powwow (folk magic)


A powwow practitioner's charm book

Powwow, also spelled pow-wow, is a system of American folk religion and folk magic associated with the Pennsylvania Dutch (Deutsch) communities of Pennsylvania and neighbouring states. In Pennsylvania German the practice is called Brauche or Braucherei, and its practitioners are sometimes known as powwow doctors.

Origins

Powwow blends elements of German folk magic, Christian prayer and scripture, and grimoire traditions brought by German-speaking immigrants in the 17th and 18th centuries. Its best-known text is John George Hohman's Long Lost Friend, first published in 1820, a collection of charms, remedies and prayers.

Practices

Practitioners treat ailments of people and livestock, remove warts and hexes, and provide protection against witchcraft, often through spoken charms accompanied by hand movements and the use of scripture verses.
