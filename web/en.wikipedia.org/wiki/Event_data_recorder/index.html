Event data recorder

An event data recorder (EDR) is a device installed in motor vehicles that records technical information about the vehicle's status and operation for a short period before, during and after a crash. Often described as an automotive "black box", the EDR function is usually performed by the airbag control module. Recorded data typically include vehicle speed, brake application, throttle position, steering input, seat-belt use, longitudinal and lateral acceleration, and whether the airbags deployed, generally covering about five seconds before the triggering event.

Use and regulation

EDR data are used by crash investigators, insurers, manufacturers and courts to reconstruct collisions and improve vehicle safety design. In the United States, federal regulation (49 CFR Part 563) standardises the data elements recorded by voluntarily installed EDRs, and most new cars sold there have included them since the 2010s. Since July 2024, the European Union has required EDRs in all newly registered cars and vans under its General Safety Regulation. Access to the data usually requires a special crash data retrieval tool connected to the vehicle.

Privacy

EDRs have raised privacy questions about who owns the recorded data and when police or insurers may use it. Many US states have enacted laws treating the data as the vehicle owner's property and restricting access without consent or a court order; the federal Driver Privacy Act of 2015 confirmed owner rights at the national level.
