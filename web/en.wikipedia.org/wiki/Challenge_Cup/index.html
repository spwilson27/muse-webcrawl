Challenge Cup

The Challenge Cup is a knockout rugby league cup competition organised by the Rugby Football League, held annually since 1896. It is one of the most prestigious competitions in the sport and is open to clubs from across the British rugby league system, together with invited amateur, student, armed-forces and overseas teams. The final has traditionally been held at Wembley Stadium in London, and the competition is known for giant-killing victories by lower-division sides over elite clubs.

History

Following the schism of 1895 that created the Northern Union (later rugby league), the new governing body instituted a challenge cup for its member clubs, modelled on association football's FA Cup. The first final was played in 1897, when Batley defeated St Helens at Headingley. From 1929 the final was staged at Wembley, where it became a centrepiece of the sporting calendar; wartime finals were held at regional grounds. The competition's most famous upset came in 1998, when Sheffield Eagles defeated the all-conquering Wigan side 17–8 at Wembley.

Format

The cup is contested over a series of rounds beginning in the winter with amateur and community clubs, with Championship and League 1 sides entering next and Super League clubs joining in the later rounds. Ties are decided in a single match, with drawn games resolved by extra time and, if necessary, golden-point. The semi-finals are often played as a double-header at a neutral venue, and the final at Wembley traditionally shares the day with the final of the schools' Champion Schools tournament and, more recently, the Women's Challenge Cup final.

Lance Todd Trophy

The man of the match in the Challenge Cup Final is awarded the Lance Todd Trophy, instituted in 1946 in memory of Lance Todd, the New Zealand player and Salford manager who was killed in a road accident during the Second World War. The award is voted for by members of the Rugby League Writers' Association.

Records

Wigan Warriors are the most successful club in the competition's history, with more cup wins than any other side, including eight consecutive victories between 1988 and 1995. Leeds Rhinos, St Helens and Hull FC are among the other multiple winners. The record attendance for a final was set in 1985, when 99,801 watched Wigan beat Hull FC at Wembley.
