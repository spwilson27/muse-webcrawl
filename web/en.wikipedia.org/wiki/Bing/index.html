Microsoft Bing, commonly referred to as Bing, is a web search engine owned and operated by Microsoft. The service has its origins in Microsoft's previous search engines: MSN Search, Windows Live Search, and later Live Search. Bing was unveiled by Microsoft CEO Steve Ballmer on May 28, 2009, at the All Things Digital conference in San Diego, California, and was fully online on June 3, 2009.

Notable changes made by Microsoft include the listing of search suggestions in real time as queries are typed and a list of related searches. Bing also powers search results for Yahoo! Search (under a 2009 partnership), and for a time for Facebook and other partners. In October 2020 the service was renamed Microsoft Bing as part of a rebranding of Microsoft's search offerings.

Features

Bing presents a daily changing background image of locations around the world. Its features include web, image, video, news, and maps search; instant answers and computational answers in partnership with WolframAlpha; flight and shopping search; and a rewards program (Microsoft Rewards) that grants points for searching. Bing also provides search services through an API to developers and powers results in Microsoft products including Windows search, Edge, and Cortana.

AI integration

In February 2023, Microsoft announced a new version of Bing integrating a large language model from OpenAI (the technology behind ChatGPT), offering a conversational chat experience, later branded as part of Microsoft Copilot. The announcement sparked a renewed "search war" with Google, which responded with its own AI offerings. Chat features included chat, compose, and image creation powered by DALL·E.

Market share

Bing is the second most used search engine in the United States desktop market, typically holding a share in the range of roughly 6–10% globally (and higher in US desktop search), far behind Google. It is the default search engine in Microsoft Edge and has benefited from integration into Windows.
