The "Dear Boss" letter was a message allegedly written by the serial killer known as Jack the Ripper, who murdered and mutilated at least five women in the Whitechapel district of London in the autumn of 1888. The letter was received on 27 September 1888 by the Central News Agency and forwarded to Scotland Yard on 29 September.

Content

The letter, written in red ink, begins "Dear Boss" and taunts the police, promising to continue killing and to clip the ears of the next victim. It is signed "Jack the Ripper"—the first known use of that name, which the letter's publicity fixed permanently to the unidentified killer. A postscript jokes about saving the "red ink" until the writer has done more work.

Authenticity

Police at the time received hundreds of purported Ripper letters, most considered hoaxes. The "Dear Boss" letter and the related "Saucy Jacky" postcard were publicised by police in hopes that someone would recognise the handwriting, without success. Several senior officers later expressed the belief that the letter was a journalistic hoax, possibly written by a Central News Agency employee to boost circulation; journalist Tom Bulling and agency head Charles Moore were named in later accounts as suspects for its authorship.

The original letter disappeared from police files for many years before being returned anonymously in 1988, with the envelope and other effects, and is now held in the National Archives at Kew.
