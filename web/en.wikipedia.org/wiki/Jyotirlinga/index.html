Jyotirlinga

A Jyotirlinga (Sanskrit: ज्योतिर्लिङ्ग, "lingam of light") is a devotional representation of the Hindu god Shiva. The word is a compound of jyoti ("radiance") and linga (the aniconic symbol of Shiva). There are traditionally twelve Jyotirlinga shrines in India, said to be the places where Shiva appeared as a fiery column of light.

Legend

According to the Shiva Purana, Brahma and Vishnu disputed which of them was supreme. Shiva appeared between them as an infinite pillar of fire and challenged each to find its end; neither could. A verse in the Shiva Purana and the Dvadasha Jyotirlinga Stotra enumerate the twelve sites, and pilgrimage to them is considered among the holiest acts of Shaiva devotion.

The twelve Jyotirlingas

  • Somnath — Gir Somnath, Gujarat
  • Mallikarjuna — Srisailam, Andhra Pradesh
  • Mahakaleshwar — Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh
  • Omkareshwar — Khandwa district, Madhya Pradesh
  • Kedarnath — Rudraprayag, Uttarakhand
  • Bhimashankar — Pune district, Maharashtra
  • Vishvanatha (Kashi Vishwanath) — Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh
  • Trimbakeshwar — Nashik district, Maharashtra
  • Vaidyanath — Deoghar, Jharkhand
  • Nageshwar — Dwarka, Gujarat
  • Rameshwaram — Ramanathapuram, Tamil Nadu
  • Grishneshwar — Aurangabad district, Maharashtra
