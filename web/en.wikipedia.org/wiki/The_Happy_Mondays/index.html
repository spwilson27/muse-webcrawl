Happy Mondays were central figures of the Madchester scene.

Happy Mondays are an English rock band formed in Salford in 1980. The original line-up was Shaun Ryder (vocals), his brother Paul Ryder (bass), Gary Whelan (drums), Paul Davis (keyboards), and Mark Day (guitar), with Mark "Bez" Berry later joining as dancer and percussionist. Signed to Tony Wilson's Factory Records, they fused indie rock with house music and became leading figures of the "Madchester" and rave scenes centered on the Haçienda.

Career

Their debut album Squirrel and G-Man Twenty Four Hour Party People Plastic Face Carnt Smile (White Out) appeared in 1987, produced by John Cale. Bummed (1988), produced by Martin Hannett, won critical acclaim. Commercial success came with the Madchester Rave On EP (1989) and Pills 'n' Thrills and Bellyaches (1990), which reached number one in the UK and included the hits "Step On" and "Kinky Afro." The chaotic, expensive recording of Yes Please! (1992) in Barbados flopped and contributed to Factory Records' collapse; the band split in 1993. Shaun Ryder and Bez then formed Black Grape, and the Mondays have reformed several times for touring and recording.

Legacy

Happy Mondays' loose funk grooves, drug-fuelled image, and Ryder's surreal lyrics made them emblematic of the baggy era; the phrase "24 Hour Party People" inspired the title of the 2002 film about Factory Records.
