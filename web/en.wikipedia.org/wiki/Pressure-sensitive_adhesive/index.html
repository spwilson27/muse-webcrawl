Pressure-sensitive adhesive (PSA, also self-adhesive or self-stick adhesive) is a type of non-reactive adhesive which forms a bond when pressure is applied to bond the adhesive with a surface. No solvent, water, or heat is needed to activate the adhesive. PSAs are used in pressure-sensitive tapes, labels, sticky notes, automobile trim, road-safety reflectors, and many medical products. The strength of the bond depends on the amount of pressure applied, the dwell time, and the properties of both the adhesive and the adherend.

Properties

Pressure-sensitive adhesives are permanently tacky at room temperature. Performance is described by three interrelated properties: tack (initial stickiness on light contact), peel adhesion (force needed to remove the adhesive at an angle), and shear resistance (holding power against sliding under load, reflecting the adhesive's internal cohesion). The Dahlquist criterion states that a material will show tack if its storage modulus at the bonding timescale is below roughly 3 × 105 Pa at room temperature — soft enough to flow and wet the surface, yet cohesive enough to resist removal.

Chemistry

Common PSA chemistries include:

  • Rubber-based — natural rubber, styrene-butadiene rubber (SBR) and styrene block copolymers (SIS, SBS), usually blended with tackifying resins; economical and high-tack but prone to oxidation.
  • Acrylic — copolymers of acrylate esters such as 2-ethylhexyl acrylate; excellent UV and ageing resistance, widely used in tapes and labels.
  • Silicone — used for high-temperature tapes and skin-contact medical adhesives because of their biocompatibility and performance across temperature extremes.
  • Polyurethane and others — used in speciality applications.

Applications

The best-known PSA product is the Post-it Note, whose weak, repositionable adhesive — developed at 3M by Spencer Silver and applied by Art Fry in the late 1970s — results from a low-tack acrylic formulation containing microspheres. Other major uses include masking tape and duct tape, double-sided foam tapes that replace mechanical fasteners in construction and electronics, price labels and shipping labels, wound dressings and ECG electrodes, and transdermal drug-delivery patches.

Removability

Adhesives are formulated on a spectrum from permanent (structural bonding, labels intended never to be removed) to removable and ultra-removable (sticky notes, protective films). A poorly chosen PSA may leave residue ("legging" and transfer) or, conversely, lose adhesion over time as plasticisers migrate or the polymer oxidises.
