Carangiformes is an order of ray-finned fishes within the percomorph clade Carangimorpha. Its best-known members are the jacks, trevallies, pompanos, and scads of the family Carangidae, but modern classifications based on molecular phylogenetics group a diverse assemblage of families into the order.

Classification

Depending on the classification used, Carangiformes includes the families Carangidae (jacks), Coryphaenidae (dolphinfishes/mahi-mahi), Echeneidae (remoras), Nematistiidae (roosterfish), Rachycentridae (cobia), and — in broader schemes — groups such as the billfishes (swordfish and marlins), moonfishes, and others. The precise composition has shifted as DNA-based studies have rearranged the traditionally recognised Perciformes.

Biology

Carangiform fishes are predominantly fast-swimming marine predators of tropical and temperate oceans. Many are silvery, streamlined schooling fish of great importance to commercial and recreational fisheries; remoras have evolved a sucking disc from a modified dorsal fin, while dolphinfishes are noted for their brilliant colours and rapid growth.
