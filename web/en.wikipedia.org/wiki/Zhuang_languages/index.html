Zhuang languages

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Zhuang languages

Native to: Guangxi and Yunnan, China

Speakers: about 15–19 million (largest minority language of China)

Family: Kra–Dai (Tai) — Northern and Central Tai

Standard form: Standard Zhuang, based on the Yongbei dialect of Wuming

Scripts: Latin-based Zhuang alphabet; historical Sawndip (characters)

The Zhuang languages are a group of Tai languages spoken by the Zhuang people, China's largest ethnic minority, mainly in the Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region and in Wenshan Prefecture, Yunnan. With roughly 15 to 19 million speakers collectively, they form the most widely spoken non-Chinese language of China, though the varieties are not all mutually intelligible and are increasingly displaced by Mandarin in daily life.

Classification and varieties

Linguists divide Zhuang into Northern Zhuang varieties (north of the Yong River), on which the official Standard Zhuang is based, and Southern (Central Tai) varieties, which are more closely related to languages across the border in Vietnam, such as Nùng and Tày. Well-known varieties include Yongbei, Youjiang, Guibei, Liujiang, Hongshuihe, and the Wenshan speech of Yunnan.

Writing

For centuries Zhuang was written with Sawndip, a script of Chinese characters and locally coined characters used for folk songs, opera, and ritual texts. In 1957 the People's Republic of China adopted a Latin-based alphabet for Standard Zhuang, revised in 1982. Zhuang appears on Chinese banknotes, in regional law (Guangxi law provides for its use), in broadcasting, and in bilingual schooling, though literacy in the standard remains limited compared with Mandarin.
