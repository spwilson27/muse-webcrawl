Mucormycosis

Broad, ribbon-like hyphae of the Mucorales fungi

Mucormycosis, also known as zygomycosis and colloquially as "black fungus", is a serious fungal infection caused by fungi of the order Mucorales, most commonly species of Rhizopus, Mucor and Lichtheimia. It generally affects people whose immune systems are compromised, including those with uncontrolled diabetes, organ transplants, or neutropenia.

Signs and symptoms

The most common form is rhinocerebral mucormycosis, beginning in the nose and sinuses after inhalation of spores and potentially spreading to the eyes and brain; symptoms include facial pain and swelling, a black eschar in the nasal cavity or palate, and vision changes. Other forms include pulmonary, gastrointestinal, cutaneous and disseminated disease. The fungi invade blood vessels, causing thrombosis and tissue necrosis, and the disease can progress rapidly and is often fatal without treatment.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on clinical suspicion, imaging, and biopsy showing characteristic broad, sparsely septate hyphae branching at right angles in tissue. Treatment combines urgent surgical debridement of infected tissue, correction of underlying conditions such as hyperglycaemia, and antifungal therapy with amphotericin B, with posaconazole or isavuconazole as step-down or salvage agents.

Epidemiology

Mucormycosis is rare but globally distributed, with a notably higher burden in India. In 2021, during the COVID-19 pandemic in India, tens of thousands of cases were reported among people recovering from COVID-19, many of whom had diabetes and had received corticosteroids, prompting India to declare the disease notifiable.
