S&P Global Ratings

ParentS&P Global (formerly McGraw Hill Financial)
PredecessorsStandard Statistics (1906); Poor's Publishing (1860)
ProductsCredit ratings, research and analytics

S&P Global Ratings, formerly Standard & Poor's, is an American credit rating agency and a division of S&P Global. It issues credit ratings for issuers of debt — corporations, governments and structured-finance vehicles — expressed in a letter scale running from AAA (highest quality) through BBB (lowest investment grade) to BB and below ("speculative grade") and D (default).

History

The firm traces its roots to Henry Varnum Poor's History of Railroads and Canals in the United States (1860) and to Luther Lee Blake's Standard Statistics Bureau (1906). Poor's and Standard merged in 1941 to form Standard & Poor's, which was acquired by McGraw-Hill in 1966. The agency is, with Moody's and Fitch, one of the "Big Three" rating agencies and a designated nationally recognized statistical rating organization (NRSRO) in the United States.

Criticism and the financial crisis

S&P and its peers were widely criticised for assigning high ratings to mortgage-backed securities that collapsed in the 2007–2008 financial crisis. In 2015 S&P paid about $1.5 billion to settle U.S. federal and state claims over its crisis-era ratings. Its 2011 downgrade of U.S. sovereign debt from AAA to AA+ was a historic first.
