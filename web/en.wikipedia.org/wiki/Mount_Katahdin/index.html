Mount Katahdin

LocationBaxter State Park, Maine, USA
Elevation1,606 m
Known forNorthern terminus of Appalachian Trail
[Image: Mount Katahdin and the Knife Edge]
Mount Katahdin and the Knife Edge

Mount Katahdin is the highest mountain in Maine, USA, at 1,606 m, the centrepiece of Baxter State Park and the northern terminus of the Appalachian Trail. Its name, from the Penobscot language, means "the greatest mountain".

Description

Katahdin is a granite massif with a broad, flat summit plateau — the Tableland — and several peaks, of which Baxter Peak is the highest. The Knife Edge, a narrow arête between Baxter and Pamola peaks, is its most famous climb. Percival Baxter, governor of Maine, bought the mountain and donated it to the state in the 1930s on condition it remain "forever wild", which still shapes the park's strict rules. Thru-hikers finishing the Appalachian Trail climb Katahdin as their final ascent, usually between June and October.
