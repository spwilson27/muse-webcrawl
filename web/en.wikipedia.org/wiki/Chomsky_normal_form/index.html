Chomsky normal form

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In formal language theory, a context-free grammar G is said to be in Chomsky normal form (named after Noam Chomsky) if all of its production rules are of the form:

  • A → BC,
  • A → a, or
  • S → ε,

where A, B and C are nonterminal symbols, a is a terminal symbol, S is the start symbol, and ε denotes the empty string. Also, neither B nor C may be the start symbol, and the third production rule can only appear if ε is in the language generated by G.

Conversion

Every context-free grammar can be transformed into an equivalent grammar in Chomsky normal form. The conversion proceeds in stages: introducing a new start symbol, removing ε-productions, removing unit productions, and breaking long productions into chains of binary productions. This property makes the form useful for proving theorems, for example in the proof of the pumping lemma for context-free languages.

Applications

Besides its theoretical importance, Chomsky normal form is used by the CYK algorithm, a parsing algorithm for context-free grammars with worst-case complexity O(n3 · |G|). A related normal form is the Greibach normal form.
