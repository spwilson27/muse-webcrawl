Constitution of Kenya

The Constitution of Kenya is the supreme law of the Republic of Kenya. It was adopted after being approved by 67% of Kenyan voters in a referendum on 4 August 2010 and was promulgated on 27 August 2010 at a ceremony at Uhuru Park in Nairobi, replacing the independence constitution of 1963.

Background

The independence constitution had been amended many times during the presidencies of Jomo Kenyatta and Daniel arap Moi, concentrating power in the executive. Demands for a new constitution grew through the 1990s and 2000s. A draft produced under President Mwai Kibaki (the "Wako draft") was rejected in a 2005 referendum. Following the disputed 2007 election and the ensuing post-election violence, constitutional reform became a central part of the national accord and reconciliation process. A Committee of Experts produced a harmonised draft, which was reviewed by a parliamentary select committee and approved in 2010.

Key features

  • Devolution: power and resources are devolved to 47 counties, each with a governor and county assembly, under Chapter Eleven.
  • Bill of Rights: an expanded Chapter Four guarantees civil, political, economic, social and cultural rights, including rights to health, housing, food and water.
  • Executive: the President is directly elected, must win both a national majority and at least 25% of the vote in more than half of the counties, and is limited to two five-year terms.
  • Legislature: a bicameral Parliament consisting of the National Assembly and a revived Senate.
  • Judiciary: an independent judiciary headed by the Chief Justice, with a Supreme Court as the highest court.
  • Land and environment: Chapter Five classifies land as public, community or private and establishes the National Land Commission.
  • Representation: constitutional seats are reserved to advance the representation of women, youth and persons with disabilities.

Structure

The constitution has a preamble and 18 chapters, covering sovereignty, the republic, citizenship, the Bill of Rights, land and environment, leadership and integrity, representation of the people, the legislature, the executive, the judiciary, devolved government, public finance, the public service, national security, commissions and independent offices, and amendment procedures.
