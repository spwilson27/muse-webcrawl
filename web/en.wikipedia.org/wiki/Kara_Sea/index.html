Kara Sea

Location: Arctic Ocean, north of Siberia
Area: about 926,000 km²
Average depth: 131 m
Max. depth: 620 m
Inflows: Ob, Yenisey, Pyasina

The Kara Sea (Russian: Ка́рское мо́ре, Karskoye more) is a marginal sea of the Arctic Ocean, located north of Siberia. It is separated from the Barents Sea to the west by the Kara Strait and Novaya Zemlya, and from the Laptev Sea to the east by the Severnaya Zemlya archipelago. It is named after the Kara River, which flows into Baydaratskaya Bay.

Geography

The Kara Sea covers an area of roughly 926,000 square kilometres and is comparatively shallow, with an average depth of about 131 metres. Its main islands include Bely Island, Dikson Island, the Kamennyye Islands, and the southern islands of Novaya Zemlya along its western boundary. The Ob and Yenisey — two of the largest rivers in the world — discharge enormous volumes of fresh water into the sea through the Gulf of Ob and the Yenisey Gulf, strongly affecting its salinity and ice cover.

Climate and ice

The Kara Sea is one of the coldest seas of the Arctic. It is frozen for most of the year, with ice usually breaking up only in the south in July and August. Fog and storms are frequent. Because of the great river inflow, the surface waters are relatively fresh and freeze readily.

History and navigation

Explored by Russian and Dutch navigators from the 16th century, the Kara Sea formed the difficult middle leg of the Northern Sea Route along Russia's Arctic coast. The port of Dikson, founded in 1915, became an important station on the route. Today the sea is used by ice-class vessels serving Arctic ports and the Yamal LNG project, and navigation is supported by nuclear icebreakers.

Environment

The Kara Sea was used during the Soviet era as a dumping ground for nuclear waste, including reactor compartments and the submarine K-27, raising long-term environmental concerns. It also holds large offshore oil and natural gas prospects, including the Pobeda field discovered in the Universitetskaya-1 well area in 2014.
