Baked Alaska

Baked Alaska is a dessert consisting of ice cream placed in a pie dish lined with slices of sponge cake or Christmas pudding and topped with meringue. The entire dessert is then placed in an extremely hot oven for a brief time, long enough to firm and caramelize the meringue but not so long as to melt the ice cream.

CourseDessert
Place of originUnited States / France (disputed)
Main ingredientsIce cream, sponge cake, meringue
VariationsFlambéed, Bombe Alaska

History

Desserts of ice cream insulated by pastry were made possible by knowledge, articulated by Benjamin Thompson (Count Rumford) in the 1800s, that whipped egg white is a poor conductor of heat. French versions appeared as omelette norvégienne. The name "Baked Alaska" is usually credited to chef Charles Ranhofer at Delmonico's in New York in 1867, honouring the recent Alaska Purchase from Russia. February 1 is celebrated as National Baked Alaska Day in the United States.

Preparation

Ice cream is frozen hard in a mould, set on sponge cake, and completely covered with meringue, whose air pockets insulate the ice cream. The dish is briefly baked at very high temperature or torched until the peaks brown; a flambéed version is doused with a warmed spirit and set alight at the table.
