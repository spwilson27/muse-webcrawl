Operation Unified Protector was a NATO operation in Libya in 2011 enforcing United Nations Security Council Resolution 1973, which authorized member states to impose an arms embargo and a no-fly zone and to take "all necessary measures" to protect civilians during the First Libyan Civil War. NATO assumed command of all military operations on 31 March 2011 from the initial coalition effort (France's Opération Harmattan, Britain's Operation Ellamy, Canada's Operation Mobile, and the U.S.-led Operation Odyssey Dawn) and ran the mission until 31 October 2011.

Mandate and forces

The operation had three components: a naval arms embargo, a no-fly zone, and air and naval strikes to protect civilians and civilian-populated areas from attack. Operational headquarters was Allied Joint Force Command Naples, under Canadian Lieutenant-General Charles Bouchard. Participating air forces included those of France, the United Kingdom, the United States, Italy, Canada, Denmark, Norway, Belgium, the Netherlands, Spain, and—beyond NATO—Qatar, the United Arab Emirates, Jordan, and Sweden. Over the campaign NATO flew about 26,500 sorties, including some 9,700 strike sorties, and hailed over 3,100 vessels for the embargo.

Course of the operation

Coalition strikes had begun on 19 March 2011, halting the regime's advance on Benghazi. Under NATO command the campaign destroyed Libyan air defences and progressively degraded Gaddafi's forces, enabling rebel advances on Tripoli in August and the final assault on Sirte in October. Gaddafi was captured and killed on 20 October; the UN Security Council terminated the mandate effective 31 October 2011.

Criticism and aftermath

The operation was praised for preventing a massacre in Benghazi but criticized—by Russia, China, the African Union, and later commentators—for exceeding the civilian-protection mandate in pursuit of regime change, and for contributing to Libya's subsequent instability, including the second civil war. A 2016 British parliamentary report concluded that the intervention rested on flawed intelligence and drifted into an opportunistic policy of regime change.
