Unit 1 of the Bushehr plant, Iran's first nuclear power station.

The Bushehr Nuclear Power Plant is a nuclear power plant in Iran, located about 17 kilometres southeast of the city of Bushehr on the Persian Gulf. Its first unit is the first civilian nuclear power reactor in the Middle East.

History

Construction began in 1975 under the government of Shah Mohammad Reza Pahlavi, with the German firm Kraftwerk Union (Siemens) building two pressurised water reactors. Work stopped after the 1979 Iranian Revolution, and the unfinished plant was damaged by Iraqi air strikes during the Iran–Iraq War. In 1995 Iran signed a contract with Russia's Atomstroyexport to complete Unit 1 using a Russian VVER-1000 reactor fitted into the German-built containment — a unique hybrid of German and Russian technology.

Operation

The reactor was fuelled in 2010, connected to the national grid in September 2011, and reached full power in 2012; formal handover to Iran followed in 2013. Russia supplies the fuel and takes back spent fuel under the agreement. Construction of additional VVER units at the site began in the late 2010s. The plant has been a focus of international non-proliferation diplomacy, though unlike Iran's enrichment facilities it operates under IAEA safeguards with Russian fuel arrangements designed to limit proliferation concerns.
