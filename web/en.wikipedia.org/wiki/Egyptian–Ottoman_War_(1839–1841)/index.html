Egyptian–Ottoman War (1839–1841)

Date: 1839 – 1841

Location: Syria, Lebanon, Anatolia, eastern Mediterranean

Belligerents: Egypt under Muhammad Ali vs. the Ottoman Empire, supported by Britain, Austria, Prussia and Russia

Result: Ottoman–European victory; Convention of London (1840)

The Second Egyptian–Ottoman War (1839–1841), also called the Second Turco-Egyptian War, was a military conflict between the Ottoman Empire and Egypt under Muhammad Ali, whose forces under his son Ibrahim Pasha had occupied Syria since the first war of 1831–1833. The conflict triggered the Oriental Crisis of 1840 and ended with European intervention that forced Muhammad Ali to give up Syria while confirming his family's hereditary rule in Egypt.

Course of the war

Sultan Mahmud II, seeking to recover Syria, sent an army across the Euphrates in the spring of 1839. Ibrahim Pasha crushed it at the Battle of Nezib on 24 June 1839, days before the Sultan's death; shortly afterwards the entire Ottoman fleet defected to Alexandria. The Ottoman Empire appeared on the verge of collapse, but Britain, Austria, Prussia and Russia — determined to preserve it and to exclude French influence, which favoured Muhammad Ali — imposed a settlement.

Convention of London and campaign of 1840

Under the Convention of London (15 July 1840) the four powers offered Muhammad Ali hereditary rule of Egypt if he withdrew from Syria and returned the fleet; when he refused, a British-Austrian-Ottoman force campaigned in the Levant. Beirut was bombarded and taken, and on 3 November 1840 a combined fleet under Admiral Sir Robert Stopford bombarded Acre, whose magazine exploded, forcing its surrender. Ibrahim's army, harassed and cut off, evacuated Syria.

Settlement

By the firman of 1841 Muhammad Ali accepted hereditary governorship of Egypt for his line, an Egyptian army limited to 18,000 men, and the application of Ottoman treaties, in return for abandoning Syria, Crete and the Hejaz. The London Straits Convention of 1841 then closed the Turkish Straits to foreign warships in peacetime, settling the wider crisis.
