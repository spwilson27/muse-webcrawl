Food court

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A food court is an area, usually within a shopping mall, airport, university or other public building, where several food vendors or fast-food outlets share a common seating area. Customers order at counters — often belonging to competing chains — and eat at shared tables, typically using trays and disposable serviceware.

History

The concept emerged with the enclosed suburban shopping mall in the United States in the 1970s; one of the first successful mall food courts opened at the Paramus Park mall in New Jersey in 1974. Food courts proved a powerful way to lengthen shoppers' visits and were rapidly adopted worldwide. Earlier precedents include market halls, railway station refreshment rooms and the hawker centres of Singapore and Malaysia, which apply the same shared-seating model to street-food stalls.

Design and economics

Landlords lease small counter spaces to vendors and provide centralised seating, cleaning and tray return. The model offers shoppers variety — important for groups with different tastes — and low start-up costs for small food businesses, though vendors commonly pay a share of turnover as well as rent. Seating areas are deliberately comfortable enough to encourage a stop, but not so comfortable as to encourage lingering without buying.

Variations

Upscale versions, rebranded as food halls, feature artisan and local vendors; they became a retail trend in the 2010s as malls sought experiences that online shopping could not replicate. Food courts are also standard in airports, motorway service areas, hospitals, office complexes and large railway stations, and are a fixture of everyday eating in much of Asia.
