Solar concentrator

A solar concentrator is an optical device that gathers sunlight over a large area and focuses it onto a much smaller area, increasing the intensity (flux) of the light. Concentrators use mirrors or lenses arranged as parabolic dishes, parabolic troughs, Fresnel reflectors, or heliostat fields aimed at a central tower. The concentrated sunlight is converted to heat — for steam generation and electricity in concentrated solar power (CSP) plants, or for industrial process heat, solar cooking, and materials testing — or directed onto high-efficiency photovoltaic cells in concentrator photovoltaics (CPV).

Types

  • Parabolic trough — a long curved mirror focuses light onto a receiver tube along its focal line; the dominant commercial CSP design, used at plants such as the SEGS stations in California.
  • Parabolic dish — a point-focus dish, often coupled to a Stirling engine; achieves the highest concentration ratios and temperatures.
  • Solar power tower — a field of tracking mirrors (heliostats) focuses light on a receiver atop a tower, as at Ivanpah and Gemasolar.
  • Linear Fresnel reflector — flat or slightly curved mirror strips approximate a trough at lower cost.
  • Non-imaging concentrators — compound parabolic concentrators used for solar thermal collectors and some CPV modules, accepting light over a wide angle without tracking at low concentration ratios.

Principles

The concentration ratio is the ratio of aperture area to receiver area. Thermodynamics (étendue conservation) limits the maximum concentration for a source subtending the Sun's angular diameter to roughly 46,000× for point focus. Concentrators capture only direct (beam) sunlight, not diffuse light, so they perform best in high-insolation desert climates and require one- or two-axis tracking to follow the Sun.

Applications

Beyond electricity, solar furnaces such as the one at Odeillo, France, reach temperatures above 3,000 °C for materials research. Smaller concentrators power solar cookers and water pasteurizers in off-grid settings.
