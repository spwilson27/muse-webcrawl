Cricket in India

Cricket is the most popular sport in India. It is played almost everywhere in the country and enjoys an enormous television audience. The Board of Control for Cricket in India (BCCI), founded in 1928, is the governing body for the sport and one of the richest sporting organisations in the world.

History

Cricket was introduced to India by British sailors and soldiers in the early eighteenth century; the first recorded match was played in 1721, and the Calcutta Cricket Club was founded in 1792. The game was adopted by Indian communities, notably the Parsis, Hindus and Muslims, whose communal tournaments in Bombay (the Bombay Quadrangular and later Pentangular) became major events. India played its first Test match at Lord's in 1932 and recorded its first Test series victory in 1952.

National team

The India men's national cricket team has won the Cricket World Cup twice, in 1983 under Kapil Dev and in 2011 under M. S. Dhoni, as well as the ICC T20 World Cup in 2007 and 2024 and the ICC Champions Trophy. The women's team has grown rapidly in prominence, reaching World Cup finals and winning the inaugural Women's Premier League era audiences. Iconic players include Sunil Gavaskar, Sachin Tendulkar — widely regarded as one of the greatest batsmen of all time — Rahul Dravid, Virat Kohli and Jasprit Bumrah.

Domestic cricket and the IPL

Domestic first-class cricket is contested in the Ranji Trophy, founded in 1934, alongside the Duleep Trophy, Vijay Hazare Trophy and Syed Mushtaq Ali Trophy. The Indian Premier League (IPL), launched in 2008, is a franchise-based Twenty20 league that has become the most lucrative cricket competition in the world, transforming player salaries and sports broadcasting in India.

Culture

Cricket permeates Indian popular culture, with matches against Pakistan drawing audiences in the hundreds of millions and leading players attaining celebrity comparable to film stars. Informal street and gully cricket is played throughout the country.
