Right to housing

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The right to housing is the economic, social and cultural right to adequate housing and shelter. It is recognized in Article 25 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948) as part of the right to an adequate standard of living, and in Article 11 of the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (1966), which obliges states parties to take appropriate steps toward the realization of everyone's right to adequate housing.

Elements of adequacy

The UN Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights, in its General Comment No. 4 (1991), held that adequate housing requires more than a roof: it includes legal security of tenure; availability of services, materials, facilities and infrastructure; affordability; habitability; accessibility; location; and cultural adequacy. General Comment No. 7 addresses forced evictions, which the Committee considers prima facie incompatible with the Covenant.

Implementation

Some constitutions make the right judicially enforceable. South Africa's Constitutional Court, in Government of the Republic of South Africa v Grootboom (2000), required the state to adopt a reasonable programme providing relief for people in desperate need. Other states, such as France with its droit au logement opposable (2007), have created statutory enforceable rights, while many governments implement the right through social housing, rent regulation and homelessness services rather than litigation.

Debates

Discussion continues over whether the right entails an individual, immediately enforceable claim or a programmatic obligation subject to progressive realization within available resources, and over the roles of markets, land-use planning and public investment in fulfilling it.
