Torino Football Club is an Italian professional football club based in Turin, Piedmont. Founded in 1906, the club plays in the distinctive granata (maroon) shirt and is nicknamed Il Toro, "the Bull," after the symbol of the city. Torino play their home matches at the Stadio Olimpico Grande Torino and contest the Derby della Mole with city rivals Juventus.

Grande Torino

The club's greatest era came in the 1940s, when the side known as the Grande Torino dominated Italian football, winning five consecutive league titles (1942–43 and 1946–49, interrupted by the Second World War) and supplying the backbone of the Italian national team — on one occasion ten of the eleven Azzurri were Torino players. Captained by Valentino Mazzola, the team is still regarded as one of the finest in the history of the Italian game.

Superga tragedy and after

On 4 May 1949, the aircraft carrying the team home from a friendly in Lisbon crashed into the retaining wall of the Basilica of Superga on the hills above Turin in fog, killing all 31 people on board, including virtually the entire first-team squad. The tragedy devastated Italian football. Torino won further league titles only once more, in 1975–76, and later endured relegations and financial crises, including bankruptcy and refounding in 2005. The club won the Coppa Italia most recently in 1993 and reached the final of the UEFA Cup in 1992, losing to Ajax. The 4th of May remains a day of commemoration for the club and the city.
