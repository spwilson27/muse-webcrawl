Maria Theresa (13 May 1717 – 29 November 1780) was the ruler of the Habsburg monarchy from 1740 until her death and the only woman to hold the position in her own right. She was sovereign of Austria, Hungary, Croatia, Bohemia, Transylvania, Milan, Galicia and the Austrian Netherlands, and Holy Roman Empress by marriage to Francis I. Her forty-year reign began with the War of the Austrian Succession and was marked by sweeping administrative, military, educational and economic reforms.

Maria Theresa, Archduchess of Austria and Queen of Hungary and Bohemia

Early life and accession

Maria Theresa was the eldest daughter of Emperor Charles VI, whose Pragmatic Sanction of 1713 attempted to secure her succession to the Habsburg hereditary lands. On his death in October 1740 she acceded, but Prussia's Frederick the Great invaded Silesia, triggering the War of the Austrian Succession (1740–1748). She retained nearly all her inheritance except Silesia, and her husband Francis was elected Holy Roman Emperor in 1745.

Reforms

Guided by advisers such as Count Haugwitz and Wenzel Anton von Kaunitz, Maria Theresa centralised the administration, created a standing army and a modern bureaucracy, reformed taxation so that nobility and clergy no longer escaped it, and promoted commerce and agriculture. Her 1774 General School Ordinance made elementary education compulsory — a landmark in European public education. The Theresian Military Academy and medical reforms under Gerard van Swieten were among her other foundations. In the Diplomatic Revolution of 1756 she allied with France against Prussia, though the Seven Years' War failed to recover Silesia.

Family

She married Francis Stephen of Lorraine in 1736 and had sixteen children, including the emperors Joseph II and Leopold II, and Marie Antoinette, queen of France. Her dynastic marriages shaped European politics for a generation. After Francis's death in 1765 she ruled jointly with Joseph, with whom she often clashed over the pace of reform.

Death and legacy

Maria Theresa died in Vienna in 1780 and was buried in the Imperial Crypt. She remains one of the most admired Habsburg rulers, remembered as an enlightened absolutist who preserved and modernised her monarchy.
