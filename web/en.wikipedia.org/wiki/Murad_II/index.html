Murad II (Ottoman Turkish: مراد ثانى, Murād-ı sānī; June 1404 – 3 February 1451) was twice the sultan of the Ottoman Empire, reigning from 1421 to 1444 and again from 1446 to 1451. His reign saw the consolidation of Ottoman power in the Balkans and Anatolia after the chaos of the Ottoman Interregnum, and the defeat of the last major crusades against the Ottomans.

The Muradiye Complex in Bursa, where Murad II is buried.

First reign

Murad succeeded his father Mehmed I in 1421 at the age of seventeen, immediately facing the rival claim of his uncle Mustafa Çelebi, backed by Byzantium; Mustafa was defeated and executed in 1422. Murad besieged Constantinople unsuccessfully in 1422, recovered territory in Anatolia from the Karamanids and other beyliks, and fought a long war with Venice (1423–1430), capturing Thessalonica in 1430. In the Balkans his armies campaigned against Serbia and Hungary, clashing with the Hungarian commander John Hunyadi, whose victories in 1442–1443 forced Murad into the Treaty of Edirne (1444).

Abdication and return

In 1444 Murad abdicated in favour of his twelve-year-old son Mehmed II and retired to Manisa — an unprecedented step. When a crusading army under Hunyadi and Władysław III of Poland broke the peace and marched on Varna, Murad was recalled to command; the crusaders were crushed at the Battle of Varna on 10 November 1444, and King Władysław was killed. A Janissary revolt in 1446 persuaded Murad to resume the throne formally, relegating Mehmed to Manisa.

Second reign and death

Murad campaigned in Greece, storming the Hexamilion wall across the Isthmus of Corinth, and decisively defeated Hunyadi again at the Second Battle of Kosovo in 1448, securing Ottoman rule in the Balkans for generations. He died at Edirne on 3 February 1451 and was succeeded by Mehmed II, who would conquer Constantinople two years later. Murad was also a notable patron: he built the Üç Şerefeli Mosque in Edirne, supported scholars and poets, and encouraged the translation of Persian works into Turkish.
