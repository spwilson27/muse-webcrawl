Kanto Plain

The Kantō Plain (Japanese: 関東平野, Kantō heiya) is the largest lowland in Japan, covering roughly 17,000 square kilometres in the Kantō region of Honshū. It includes the Tokyo Metropolis and the prefectures of Gunma, Tochigi, Ibaraki, Saitama, Chiba, and Kanagawa in whole or in part.

Geography

The plain is bordered by mountain ranges to the north and west, including the Abukuma Highlands and the Kantō Mountains, and faces the Pacific Ocean and Tokyo Bay to the east and south. Major rivers crossing the plain include the Tone River, the Arakawa River, and the Tama River. Much of the surface is covered by volcanic ash deposits (the Kantō Loam) derived from Mount Fuji and other volcanoes, laid down over older marine and river sediments.

Population and economy

The Kantō Plain contains the Greater Tokyo Area, the largest metropolitan economy in the world, with a population of over 40 million people. It is the political, economic, and cultural centre of Japan, containing the capital Tokyo as well as major cities such as Yokohama, Kawasaki, Saitama, Chiba, and Sagamihara.

History

The plain became the political centre of Japan during the Edo period, when Tokugawa Ieyasu established his shogunate at Edo (modern Tokyo) in 1603. Extensive river engineering, including the diversion of the Tone River eastward, reshaped the plain's hydrology in the 17th century. The plain suffered catastrophic damage in the 1923 Great Kantō earthquake.
