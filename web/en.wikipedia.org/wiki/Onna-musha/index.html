Onna-musha

An onna-musha in armour, armed with a naginata.

Onna-musha (Japanese: 女武者) is a term referring to female warriors in pre-modern Japan. These women belonged to the bushi (warrior) class and were trained in the use of weapons to protect their households, families and honour in times of war. They contrast with the stereotypical image of the passive samurai wife: onna-musha actively took part in battle, often alongside male samurai.

History

Long before the emergence of the samurai class, Japanese women were trained to wield weapons, most characteristically the naginata, a polearm whose length compensated for a smaller fighter's lesser reach and strength, as well as the kaiken dagger and the bow. Female warriors appear in the earliest Japanese chronicles; the semi-legendary Empress Jingū was said to have led an invasion of Korea in the third century.

During the Heian and Kamakura periods, women such as Tomoe Gozen, who fought in the Genpei War (1180–1185), and Hangaku Gozen became celebrated in war tales. In the Sengoku period (1467–1603), as warfare became near-constant, women of castle households were expected to defend the castle in their lord's absence; some, like Ii Naotora, even became daimyō in their own right. During the Boshin War of 1868–1869, the women of Aizu fielded the Jōshitai (娘子隊, "women's army"), led by Nakano Takeko, who died fighting Imperial troops.

Weapons and training

The naginata became so strongly associated with women of the samurai class that it remains a symbol of the female martial artist in Japan; the modern martial art of atarashii naginata is practised predominantly by women. Training also covered archery, knife fighting and, later, the martial disciplines expected of a castle's last line of defence.

Notable onna-musha

  • Tomoe Gozen — companion of Minamoto no Yoshinaka in the Genpei War
  • Hangaku Gozen — archer of the Taira clan
  • Nakano Takeko — leader of the Aizu women's unit in the Boshin War
  • Tsuruhime — warrior of Ōhōri, Ehime
  • Empress Jingū — legendary early sovereign and warrior
