Local Government Act 1888

The Local Government Act 1888 (51 & 52 Vict. c. 41) was an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that established county councils in England and Wales. Coming into force on 1 April 1889, it was a landmark in the development of democratic local government in Britain.

Provisions

The Act created elected county councils for each administrative county, taking over the administrative and fiscal functions previously exercised by unelected magistrates in quarter sessions: responsibility for roads and bridges, county buildings, asylums, and the police among other matters. Sixty-one counties in England and Wales were constituted, many based on the historic counties. Larger boroughs with populations over 50,000 were made county boroughs, independent of the county councils. The Act also created the County of London, governed by the new London County Council, out of parts of Middlesex, Surrey, and Kent.

Context

The reform followed the extension of the parliamentary franchise in 1884 and the reform of municipal corporations earlier in the century; the counties were the last major area of local administration still run by appointed justices of the peace. The elected councils were chosen by ratepayers, and further Acts in 1894 created urban and rural district councils and parish councils, completing the structure of elected local government.

Legacy

The system created in 1888 lasted, with modifications, until the reorganisation of local government in 1974 (1965 in London). Many of the administrative counties it defined still shape modern ceremonial and administrative geography.
