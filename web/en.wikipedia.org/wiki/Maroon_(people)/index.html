Maroon (people)

Maroons are descendants of Africans in the Americas who escaped from slavery and formed independent settlements. The word derives from the Spanish cimarrón, originally used for escaped livestock and then for fugitive slaves.

History

Wherever slavery existed in the Americas, escapees formed maroon communities in swamps, mountains and forests. Famous groups include the Jamaican Maroons, who fought the British to treaties in 1739 under leaders such as Queen Nanny and Cudjoe; the Bushinengue of Suriname and French Guiana; the Palenqueros of Colombia, whose village San Basilio de Palenque preserved an African-influenced creole language; and the quilombos of Brazil, notably Palmares.

Culture

Maroon societies blended African, European and Indigenous elements into distinctive languages, religions, crafts and systems of governance, and many retain recognised autonomy and cultural heritage today.
