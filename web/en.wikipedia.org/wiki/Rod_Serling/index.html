Rod Serling

Rodman Edward Serling (December 25, 1924 – June 28, 1975) was an American screenwriter, playwright, television producer and narrator, best known as creator and host of the anthology series The Twilight Zone (1959–1964).

Born in Syracuse, New York, Serling served as a paratrooper in the Pacific in World War II, an experience that marked his writing. He won Emmys for scripts such as Patterns (1955) and Requiem for a Heavyweight (1956). Frustrated by network censorship of social themes, he turned to science fiction and fantasy allegory in The Twilight Zone, whose twist endings and moral tales became iconic.

Serling also co-wrote the screenplay for Planet of the Apes (1968) and created Night Gallery (1969–1973). He died aged 50 following heart surgery. He won six Emmy Awards in total.
