Picturephone

Picturephone was a videophone service developed by Bell Telephone Laboratories and introduced commercially by AT&T in the early 1970s. It was a pioneering but commercially unsuccessful attempt to bring two-way video calling to businesses and the public.

History

AT&T demonstrated picture-telephone prototypes from the 1950s, and unveiled the Picturephone at the 1964 New York World's Fair, where visitors could make video calls to a booth at Disneyland. Commercial service began in Pittsburgh in 1970, with sets leased at high monthly rates.

Technology and failure

The system transmitted a small black-and-white image over specially conditioned lines. High cost, bulky equipment, and limited demand caused AT&T to withdraw the service by the mid-1970s after investing hundreds of millions of dollars; it became a famous case study of a technology introduced before its market existed. Video calling only became mass-market decades later with webcams and smartphones.
