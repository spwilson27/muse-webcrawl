Item response theory

Item response theory (IRT), also known as latent trait theory or modern mental test theory, is a paradigm for the design, analysis, and scoring of tests, questionnaires, and similar instruments measuring abilities, attitudes, or other variables. Rather than scoring a test as the sum of correct answers, IRT models the probability of a particular response to each item as a function of the respondent's latent trait level (θ) and item parameters.

Item characteristic curves: the probability of a correct response rises with ability θ, more steeply for higher-discrimination items

Item characteristic curve

The fundamental IRT object is the item characteristic curve (ICC), which plots the probability of a correct response against ability. The common three-parameter logistic (3PL) model is:

P(θ) = c + (1 − c) / (1 + e−a(θ − b))

where b is item difficulty, a is discrimination, and c is the lower asymptote representing guessing.

Models

  • Rasch model (1PL) — difficulty only; developed by Georg Rasch.
  • 2PL model — adds discrimination (Birnbaum).
  • 3PL model — adds a pseudo-guessing parameter.
  • Graded response and partial credit models — for polytomous (e.g. Likert-scale) items.

Applications

IRT underlies computerized adaptive testing (used by the GRE and GMAT), large-scale educational assessment (NAEP, PISA score linking), patient-reported outcome measurement, and test equating across forms and populations. Key theoretical contributions came from Frederic Lord, Melvin Novick, and Georg Rasch.
