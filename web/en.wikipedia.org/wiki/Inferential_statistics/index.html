Inferential statistics

Branch of: Statistics

Key tools: Estimation, hypothesis testing, confidence intervals

Inferential statistics is the branch of statistics that draws conclusions about a population from a sample of data, quantifying the uncertainty of those conclusions. It contrasts with descriptive statistics, which only summarizes the data at hand.

Main approaches

Core methods include estimation (point estimates and confidence intervals), hypothesis testing (using significance levels and p-values), and regression modelling. Philosophical approaches include the frequentist framework, Bayesian inference, and likelihood-based methods.

Assumptions

Valid inference generally requires that the sample be representative — ideally randomly drawn — and that model assumptions about the data-generating process hold approximately.
