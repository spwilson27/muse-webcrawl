Lexicalization

Lexicalization is the process in language change by which a phrase, sequence of words, or grammatical construction becomes a single lexical item — a word or fixed expression with its own dictionary entry whose meaning may no longer be fully predictable from its parts. Examples in English include forget-me-not (from a whole clause), nevertheless (from "never the less"), and idioms such as kick the bucket, whose meaning cannot be derived from the individual words. Lexicalization is the counterpart of grammaticalization, in which lexical items become grammatical markers; the two processes are often studied together in historical linguistics.

Mechanisms

Lexicalization typically proceeds through frequent use and gradual loss of internal structure. Contributing mechanisms include:

  • Univerbation — two or more words fuse into one, as in cupboard (formerly "cup board") or into.
  • Idiomatization — a compositional phrase acquires a non-compositional meaning, as in red herring or spill the beans.
  • Demorphologization — a formerly regular derivational formation becomes a simple, unanalysable word, as in business, no longer perceived as "busy + -ness".
  • Instantial lexicalization — a speaker coins a one-off formation (a "nonce" usage) that catches on, a route common in journalism and advertising.

As forms lexicalize they often undergo phonological erosion, semantic drift, and loss of syntactic flexibility: a lexicalized phrase can no longer be freely modified internally (compare kick the bucket, where the bucket cannot be pluralised or described).

Lexicalization patterns

The term is also used in typology and semantics, following Leonard Talmy, for the characteristic ways different languages "lexicalize" — pack into their verbs and constructions — components of a semantic event. In Talmy's classic example, English manner-of-motion verbs such as run, float, and slide conflate motion with manner ("The bottle floated into the cave"), while Spanish typically expresses path in the verb and manner in an adjunct ("La botella entró a la cueva flotando" — "the bottle entered the cave, floating"). On this typology, languages are classed as verb-framed or satellite-framed according to where the path component is lexicalized.

Lexicalization in lexicography

For dictionaries, lexicalization marks the point at which a formation is treated as a headword in its own right rather than a predictable product of grammar. It is relevant to decisions about lexemes, spelling (solid, hyphenated, or open), and the treatment of multiword expressions in both human and computational lexicography.
