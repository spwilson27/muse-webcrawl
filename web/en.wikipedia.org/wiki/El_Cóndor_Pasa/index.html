El Cóndor Pasa

El Cóndor Pasa (Spanish for "The Condor Passes") is an orchestral musical piece from the zarzuela of the same name, composed by the Peruvian composer Daniel Alomía Robles in 1913. It is based on traditional Andean music of Peru, and the melody is the best-known Peruvian song worldwide. In 2004, Peru declared the piece part of its national cultural patrimony.

The zarzuela

Robles composed El cóndor pasa as a zarzuela — a Spanish-language lyric-dramatic work — with a libretto by Julio de La Paz (the pen name of Julio Baudouin). The plot concerns the exploitation of Indigenous miners by a foreign mining company in the Andes, and the condor, a symbol of freedom, contrasts with the oppression of the workers. Robles drew on folk melodies he had collected during his travels in the Peruvian highlands; the famous final melody is associated with the kashua dance tradition of Cusco.

Simon & Garfunkel version

The American folk-rock duo Simon & Garfunkel brought the song to international fame with their 1970 version, "El Cóndor Pasa (If I Could)", on the album Bridge over Troubled Water. Paul Simon had heard the melody performed by the Andean group Los Incas in Paris and, believing it to be a traditional folk song, wrote English lyrics and recorded the track over the group's arrangement. Robles's son later established the composer's authorship, and Robles was credited. The song has since been covered in many languages and by many artists, becoming a standard of Andean music ensembles around the world.

Cultural significance

The piece is regarded as an unofficial second national anthem of Peru and a symbol of Andean identity. Its melody, often played on quena flute and charango, is one of the most recognisable musical emblems of South America.
