Sebastián Vizcaíno

Sebastián Vizcaíno
Born: c. 1548, Huelva, Spain
Died: 1627, Mexico City, New Spain
Occupation: Explorer, soldier, diplomat

Sebastián Vizcaíno (c. 1548–1627) was a Spanish soldier, explorer and ambassador, best known for his expeditions along the Pacific coast of North America and for a voyage to Japan. He served in the Philippines and New Spain, surviving the wreck of the Manila galleon Santa Ana in 1587.

California expedition

In 1602–1603 Vizcaíno commanded three ships charged with charting the coast of Alta California and finding a safe harbour for returning Manila galleons. He named and described many features of the coast — including San Diego Bay and Monterey Bay, whose advantages he praised (renaming it for the viceroy, the Count of Monterrey) — and his charts and place names were used for the next two centuries, although his glowing account of Monterey contributed to its overestimation by later navigators.

Japan

In 1611–1613 he travelled as ambassador to Japan, meeting Tokugawa Hidetada and the retired shogun Ieyasu, and surveyed part of the Japanese coast. He died in Mexico City in 1627.
