Yerofey Pavlovich Khabarov (Russian: Ерофей Павлович Хабаров; c. 1603 – c. 1671) was a Russian explorer and adventurer best known for exploring and attempting to colonize the Amur River basin in the mid-seventeenth century, during the early Russian conquest of Siberia. The major Far Eastern city of Khabarovsk and the surrounding Khabarovsk Krai are named after him.

Early life

Khabarov came from the Russian North, likely the region of Veliky Ustyug, and became a promyshlennik — a fur-trading entrepreneur — in eastern Siberia. He traded and farmed around the Lena River and was imprisoned for a time in Yakutsk after disputes with the authorities, an experience typical of the unruly frontier world of Cossacks and tribute collectors.

The Amur expeditions

Drawn by reports of the fertile, grain-producing lands of the Daur people along the Amur, Khabarov organized an expedition that entered the river system in 1649–1650, following earlier reconnaissance by Vasily Poyarkov. He descended the Amur, seized the Daur town of Albazin, and imposed tribute (yasak) on local peoples, often by violence. In 1651–1653 his detachments fought a series of engagements with Daur, Ducher, and ultimately Manchu Qing forces, culminating in clashes that foreshadowed the later Sino-Russian border conflicts. Complaints about his brutality and his seizure of supplies led the Yakutsk authorities to arrest him and strip him of his command; a new commander, and eventually the diplomat Fyodor Baykov's mission, followed.

Later life and legacy

Khabarov was sent to Moscow, tried, and largely exonerated, receiving a minor noble rank and land near the Lena. He died around 1671. Although criticized even by contemporaries for cruelty, he is commemorated in Russia as a pioneer of the Far East; a major railway station on the Trans-Siberian line, Yerofey Pavlovich, also bears his name.
