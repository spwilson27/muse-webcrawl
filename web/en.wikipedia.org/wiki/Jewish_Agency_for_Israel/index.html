The Jewish Agency for Israel (Hebrew: הסוכנות היהודית) is the largest Jewish non-profit organisation in the world and the primary organisation fostering aliyah — Jewish immigration to Israel — and immigrant absorption. It is the modern continuation of the Jewish Agency established in 1929 under the British Mandate.

Work since 1948

After Israeli independence, the Agency's status was formalised by the 1952 Status Law. It organised mass immigration waves, including airlifts from Yemen, Iraq, Ethiopia (Operations Moses and Solomon), and over a million immigrants from the former Soviet Union in the 1990s. Through emissaries (shlichim) in diaspora communities, it runs Jewish and Zionist education programmes, and with partners such as the World Zionist Organization and United Israel Appeal it links world Jewry with Israel.

Notable chairmen

Chairmen have included David Ben-Gurion (of the predecessor body), Avraham Burg, Natan Sharansky (2009–2018), and Isaac Herzog (2018–2021), who left to become President of Israel.
