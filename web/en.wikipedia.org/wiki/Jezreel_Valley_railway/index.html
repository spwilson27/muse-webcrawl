The Jezreel Valley railway (Hebrew: מסילת העמק, Mesilat HaEmek) is a railway line in northern Israel running from Haifa through the Jezreel Valley to Beit She'an. The name refers both to the historic Ottoman-era line, a branch of the Hejaz railway that operated from 1904 to 1948, and to the modern Israel Railways line opened in 2016 broadly following the same route.

The historic line

Built by the Ottoman Empire as the Haifa–Daraa branch of the Hejaz railway, the line opened in stages between 1904 and 1905. It ran from Haifa eastward through Afula and Beit She'an, crossed the Jordan River and climbed to Daraa in Syria, where it joined the main Hejaz line to Damascus and Medina. The 1,050 mm narrow-gauge line served agriculture in the valley and became fondly remembered in Israeli culture ("the train of the valley," celebrated in song). Passenger service wound down during the British Mandate, and the line ceased operation during the 1948 Arab–Israeli War; the bridges over the Yarmouk and Jordan had been blown up in the 1946 Night of the Bridges operation.

The new line

Plans to rebuild the railway were advanced in the 2000s. The new standard-gauge line, about 60 km long with stations at Haifa, Yokneam–Kfar Yehoshua, Migdal HaEmek–Kfar Baruch, Afula and Beit She'an, opened on 16 October 2016. It is designed for speeds up to 160 km/h and serves both passenger and freight traffic, with long-term ambitions of a connection eastward to Jordan and the Irbid area.
