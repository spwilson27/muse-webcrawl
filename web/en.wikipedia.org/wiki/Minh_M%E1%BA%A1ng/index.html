SuccessorThiệu Trị
Born25 May 1791, Saigon

Minh Mạng (25 May 1791 – 20 January 1841) was the second emperor of the Nguyễn dynasty of Vietnam, reigning from 1820 until his death. The fourth son of Emperor Gia Long, he is remembered as a vigorous, conservative Confucian ruler who centralised administration and expanded Vietnamese territory.

Reign

Minh Mạng reorganised the bureaucracy on Chinese models, created provinces, and suppressed rebellions including the Lê Văn Khôi revolt in the south. Under his rule Vietnam annexed parts of Cambodia and Laos, bringing the empire to its greatest extent. He restricted foreign trade and persecuted Catholic missionaries and converts, worsening relations with France.

Legacy

His tomb near Huế is regarded as a masterpiece of Nguyễn royal architecture. Historians debate his reign as both a peak of dynastic power and a period whose isolationism left Vietnam ill-prepared for Western pressure.
