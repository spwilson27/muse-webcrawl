Screen time

Screen time is the amount of time spent using a device with a screen, such as a smartphone, computer, television, or video game console. The concept became prominent in public health and parenting discussions in the 2000s and 2010s as portable screens became widespread, and it is the subject of a large body of research on physical and mental health, development, sleep and education.

Health guidance

Paediatric and public-health bodies have issued guidance on children's screen time. The American Academy of Pediatrics historically recommended strict limits for young children, later shifting toward family media plans emphasising content quality and balance. The World Health Organization recommends that children aged 2–4 have no more than one hour of sedentary screen time per day, and none for one-year-olds. Guidance typically also discourages screens before bed because of effects on sleep.

Research findings

Research links heavy screen use with shorter sleep, sedentary behaviour and obesity, while findings on mental health are mixed and often show small effect sizes once other factors are controlled. Some researchers argue that the type of use — passive consumption versus creative, social or educational use — matters more than raw duration, and that the "screen time" concept itself is too crude to capture these distinctions.

Management tools

Operating systems and apps now include built-in screen-time dashboards and limits, such as Apple's Screen Time and Google's Digital Wellbeing, which report usage and allow users or parents to set restrictions.
