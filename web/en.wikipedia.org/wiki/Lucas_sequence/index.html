In mathematics, the Lucas sequences Un(P, Q) and Vn(P, Q) are certain constant-recursive integer sequences named after the French mathematician Édouard Lucas (1842–1891). They generalize the Fibonacci numbers, the Lucas numbers, the Pell numbers, and the Jacobsthal numbers.

Definition

Given two integer parameters P and Q with discriminant D = P² − 4Q, the Lucas sequences are defined by the recurrence xn = P·xn−1 − Q·xn−2, with initial values U0 = 0, U1 = 1 for the first kind, and V0 = 2, V1 = P for the second kind.

Important examples include: the Fibonacci numbers Un(1, −1); the Lucas numbers Vn(1, −1); the Pell numbers Un(2, −1); and the Mersenne numbers Un(3, 2).

Properties and applications

Lucas sequences satisfy many identities analogous to those of the Fibonacci numbers, including divisibility properties used in primality testing. They underpin the Lucas primality test and the Lucas–Lehmer test for Mersenne numbers, and appear in proofs involving primitive divisors (Bang–Zsigmondy theorem).
