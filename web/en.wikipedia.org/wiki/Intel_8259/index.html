Intel 8259

The Intel 8259 is a programmable interrupt controller (PIC) chip designed for use with the Intel 8085 and 8086 microprocessors. It manages up to eight vectored priority interrupts and can be cascaded to handle up to 64 interrupt requests.

Use in PCs

The original IBM PC used a single 8259 to manage its hardware interrupts (IRQs). The IBM PC/AT added a second 8259 cascaded through the first, giving 15 usable IRQ lines. The 8259 pair remained part of PC architecture for decades, later integrated into southbridge chipsets, before being largely superseded by the Advanced Programmable Interrupt Controller (APIC).

Operation

Peripherals signal interrupts on the chip's request lines; the controller prioritises them, signals the CPU, and supplies the corresponding interrupt vector. Modes include fully nested, rotating priority and polled operation.
