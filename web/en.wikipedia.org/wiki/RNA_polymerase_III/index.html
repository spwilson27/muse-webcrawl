RNA polymerase III

Placeholder image: structure of RNA polymerase III.

RNA polymerase III (also called RNA Pol III) is one of the three nuclear RNA polymerases found in eukaryotic cells. It transcribes DNA to synthesise small RNAs, principally transfer RNAs (tRNAs), the 5S ribosomal RNA, and other small non-coding RNAs such as the U6 spliceosomal RNA, 7SL RNA of the signal recognition particle, and various microRNAs and snoRNAs. These products are typically short, abundant, and stable.

Pol III is the largest of the eukaryotic nuclear polymerases, with 17 subunits and a mass of about 700 kDa in yeast. Several of its subunits are shared with or homologous to those of RNA polymerase I and polymerase II.

Types of transcription

Pol III promoters fall into three main types. Type 1 promoters, found in 5S rRNA genes, and type 2 promoters, found in tRNA and adenovirus VA genes, lie within the transcribed region and are recognised by the assembly factor TFIIIA (for 5S genes) together with TFIIIC and TFIIIB. Type 3 promoters, such as that of the U6 snRNA gene, lie upstream of the gene and contain elements resembling polymerase II promoters, including a TATA box. TFIIIB, containing TBP, Brf1 and Bdp1, is the key initiation factor that recruits Pol III to all its genes.

Termination and regulation

Pol III terminates transcription at simple runs of thymidines on the non-template DNA strand, without requiring additional factors. The enzyme can recycle efficiently on the same template, supporting the very high output needed for tRNA and 5S rRNA synthesis. Its activity is repressed by the tumour suppressor proteins p53 and RB, and by the Maf1 repressor under stress and nutrient-starvation conditions; conversely, elevated Pol III transcription is common in transformed and cancer cells.
