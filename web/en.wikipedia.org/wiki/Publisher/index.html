Publisher

A publisher is a person or organisation responsible for making content available to the public — traditionally books, newspapers, magazines and sheet music, and now also digital media, software and video games. Publishing involves acquiring or commissioning content, editing and designing it, financing and organising its production, and marketing and distributing the finished work, usually in return for a share of revenue and under rights granted by the creator.

Roles

In trade book publishing, the publisher commissions or accepts manuscripts from authors (often through literary agents), provides editorial development and copy-editing, arranges design, typesetting, printing and binding, sets the price, and sells through wholesalers and retailers. Advances against royalties are paid to authors, and the publisher usually owns or licenses the copyright in the published edition. Newspaper and magazine publishers oversee periodical publication and derive revenue from both readers and advertisers. In scholarly publishing, publishers organise peer review and produce journals and monographs, increasingly in digital and open-access forms.

History

The publisher as a distinct figure emerged with the printing press in the fifteenth century, when printers such as Aldus Manutius combined manufacture with editorial selection. Copyright law, beginning with the Statute of Anne (1710), separated authors' rights from publishers' interests and shaped the modern trade. The nineteenth and twentieth centuries saw the rise of large publishing houses, paperback formats and international conglomerates; the largest trade groups today include Penguin Random House, Hachette, HarperCollins, Simon & Schuster and Macmillan.

New media

Digital technology has blurred the role: self-publishing platforms let authors act as their own publishers; software and game "publishers" finance, market and distribute developers' products; and in advertising technology a "publisher" is any website or app owner selling advertising space. Desk-top and print-on-demand tools have lowered the cost of entry, while discoverability and platform gatekeeping have become central challenges.
