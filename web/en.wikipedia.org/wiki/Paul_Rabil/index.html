Paul Rabil (born 14 December 1985) is an American former professional lacrosse player and entrepreneur. Widely regarded as one of the greatest lacrosse players of all time, Rabil was a star midfielder at Johns Hopkins University, a two-time Major League Lacrosse (MLL) Most Valuable Player, and a two-time world champion with the United States national team. In 2018 he co-founded the Premier Lacrosse League (PLL) with his brother Mike Rabil, transforming the economics and media profile of the professional sport.

College career

Rabil grew up in Maryland and attended DeMatha Catholic High School before playing at Johns Hopkins, where he helped the Blue Jays win NCAA Division I championships in 2005 and 2007. A three-time first-team All-American, he finished his college career as Hopkins' all-time leader in points by a midfielder and won the McLaughlin Award as the nation's top midfielder in 2007.

Professional career

The Boston Cannons selected Rabil first overall in the 2008 MLL draft. He won the league's MVP award in 2009 and 2011, led Boston to the 2011 MLL championship, and in 2015 was traded to the New York Lizards, with whom he won another title. In indoor lacrosse he played in the National Lacrosse League for the San Jose/Washington Stealth, winning the 2010 Champion's Cup. Internationally, Rabil won gold with Team USA at the 2010 and 2018 World Lacrosse Championships and was named to the All-World team. His shot was once clocked at 111 mph, then a world record.

Premier Lacrosse League

Frustrated with low player pay in the MLL, the Rabil brothers launched the PLL in 2019 as a tour-based league in which players received salaries, benefits and equity. The league drew major media deals, first with NBC and later ESPN, and in 2023 acquired and merged the MLL brand. Rabil played two PLL seasons with Atlas LC and Cannons LC before retiring as a player in 2021 to focus on running the league as its president. He is also one of the sport's most prominent online personalities, building a large social-media following that helped popularise lacrosse with younger audiences.
