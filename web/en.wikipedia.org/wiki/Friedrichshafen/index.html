Friedrichshafen is a city on the northern shore of Lake Constance (Bodensee) in southern Germany, near the borders of Austria and Switzerland. It is the district capital of the Bodenseekreis district in the state of Baden-Württemberg.

History

The town was founded in 1811 by King Frederick I of Württemberg, after whom it is named, by merging the town of Buchhorn with the village of Hofen. It became famous in the early 20th century as the home of Count Ferdinand von Zeppelin's airship works, which built the rigid Zeppelin airships. The city was heavily bombed during the Second World War because of its aircraft and engine industries.

Economy and transport

Friedrichshafen remains an industrial centre: ZF Friedrichshafen, a major automotive supplier, has its headquarters there, along with aviation-related firms. The city has a regional airport, a ferry connection across the lake to Romanshorn in Switzerland, and a long lakeside promenade popular with visitors.
