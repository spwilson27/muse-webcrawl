Synonymous substitution

A synonymous substitution (also called a silent substitution) is an evolutionary change in a DNA sequence that alters a codon without changing the amino acid it encodes, owing to the redundancy of the genetic code. Because the resulting protein sequence is unchanged, synonymous substitutions are often treated as selectively neutral.

Role in molecular evolution

The rate of synonymous substitution, commonly denoted dS or Ks, is used as an approximate neutral baseline against which the rate of nonsynonymous substitution (dN) is compared; the dN/dS ratio is a standard test for natural selection on protein-coding genes. Synonymous changes accumulate roughly in proportion to time and are used in molecular clock analyses.

Non-neutral effects

Research has shown that synonymous mutations are not always silent: they can affect codon usage bias, mRNA folding and stability, splicing, and translation speed, and some have been implicated in human disease.
