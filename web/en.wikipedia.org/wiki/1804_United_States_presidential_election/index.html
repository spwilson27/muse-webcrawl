The 1804 United States presidential election was the fifth quadrennial presidential election, held from Friday, November 2, to Wednesday, December 5, 1804. Incumbent Democratic-Republican President Thomas Jefferson defeated Federalist Charles Cotesworth Pinckney of South Carolina in a landslide. It was the first presidential election conducted under the Twelfth Amendment to the United States Constitution, which separated the electoral votes for president and vice president.

Background

Jefferson's first term was marked by the Louisiana Purchase of 1803, which doubled the size of the United States, and by a strong economy. The Federalist Party, weakened by internal divisions and its association with the controversial Alien and Sedition Acts, nominated Pinckney, a former minister to France and hero of the Quasi-War. Vice President Aaron Burr, disgraced after killing Alexander Hamilton in a duel in July 1804, was dropped from the Democratic-Republican ticket and replaced by New York governor George Clinton.

Results

Jefferson won 162 of 176 electoral votes and carried 15 of the 17 states, losing only Connecticut and Delaware. His share of the electoral vote was among the largest in American history, and he also won the popular vote decisively where it was held. Pinckney carried only Connecticut and Delaware, the last strongholds of Federalism in New England and the mid-Atlantic.

Significance

The election confirmed the dominance of the Democratic-Republican Party and the continuing decline of the Federalists, who would never again seriously contest the presidency after 1816. It also inaugurated the so-called "Virginia dynasty": Jefferson, James Madison, and James Monroe — all Virginians — would hold the presidency for 24 consecutive years.
