Hōnen

Born1133, Mimasaka Province, Japan
Died29 February 1212, Kyoto
SchoolJōdo-shū (Pure Land Buddhism)

Hōnen (法然, 1133–1212) was a Japanese Buddhist monk and the founder of Jōdo-shū, the first independent school of Japanese Pure Land Buddhism. He taught that rebirth in Amida Buddha's Pure Land could be attained simply by reciting the nembutsu — "Namu Amida Butsu" — with sincere faith.

Life

Ordained as a Tendai monk on Mount Hiei, Hōnen was influenced by the Chinese Pure Land master Shandao and in 1175 embraced exclusive nembutsu practice. His teaching attracted followers from all classes, including women and outcasts, but provoked hostility from established schools; in 1207 he was exiled to Shikoku and some disciples were executed. Pardoned in 1211, he died in Kyoto the following year.

Legacy

His principal work is the Senchaku Hongan Nembutsushū (1198). His disciple Shinran went on to found Jōdo Shinshū, which became one of Japan's largest Buddhist traditions.
