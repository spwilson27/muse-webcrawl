Arthur Ashe Stadium is a tennis stadium at the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center in Flushing Meadows, Queens, New York City, and the main court of the US Open. Opened in 1997 to replace Louis Armstrong Stadium as the tournament's centre court, it seats 23,771 spectators, making it the largest tennis-specific stadium in the world. It is named for Arthur Ashe, the African American champion who won the inaugural Open Era US Open in 1968.

Features and history

The stadium's scale long made a roof seem impractical, and rain delays plagued finals in the late 2000s; a lightweight retractable roof was finally installed for the 2016 tournament as part of a wider transformation of the grounds, which also produced a new Louis Armstrong Stadium in 2018. Ashe's night sessions — late, loud and often attended by celebrities — are a signature of the US Open and of New York's sporting summer. The court has staged the tournament's finals since 1997, from the Williams sisters' era through the Djokovic, Nadal and Alcaraz years.
