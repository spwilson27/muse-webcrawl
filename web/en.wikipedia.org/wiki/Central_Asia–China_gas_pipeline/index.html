Central Asia–China gas pipeline

Central Asia–China gas pipeline
Route: Turkmenistan – Uzbekistan – Kazakhstan – China
Length: about 1,833 km (lines A/B, outside China)
Commissioned: 2009 (Line A)
Capacity: up to 55 bcm/year (lines A–C)

The Central Asia–China gas pipeline is a natural gas pipeline system that carries gas from Central Asia to China. It begins near Gedaim on the Turkmenistan–Uzbekistan border, crosses Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan, and enters China at Khorgos in Xinjiang, where it connects to China's West–East Gas Pipeline.

Lines

The twin lines A and B opened in 2009–2010, followed by line C in 2014, raising total capacity to about 55 billion cubic metres per year. A fourth line D, routed through Tajikistan and Kyrgyzstan, has been planned and intermittently under construction. Most of the gas comes from Turkmenistan, with additional volumes from Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan.

Significance

The pipeline was China's first major overland gas import route and a cornerstone of energy cooperation under the Belt and Road Initiative.
