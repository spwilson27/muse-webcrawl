Béla Alexander Balassa (6 April 1928 – 10 May 1991) was a Hungarian economist who became a professor at Johns Hopkins University and a long-serving consultant to the World Bank. He is best known for the Balassa–Samuelson effect, which explains international differences in price levels by productivity differences between traded and non-traded sectors.

Work

Balassa's 1961 book The Theory of Economic Integration defined the stages of regional integration — free trade area, customs union, common market, economic union and total integration — that became standard vocabulary in the field. He was a prominent advocate of export-oriented development strategies, based on extensive comparative studies of developing countries.

Life

Balassa left Hungary after the 1956 uprising, earned his PhD at Yale University, and advised governments and the World Bank on trade policy for three decades.
