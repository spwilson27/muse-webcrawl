Blackfoot Confederacy

The Blackfoot Confederacy, or Niitsitapi, is a historic collective name for the closely related Indigenous peoples of the northern Great Plains who speak Blackfoot, an Algonquian language. The confederacy traditionally comprised four nations: the Siksika, the Kainai (Blood), and the two Piikani (Peigan) divisions, whose territory stretched from the North Saskatchewan River in present-day Alberta to the Yellowstone River in Montana.

History

Before colonisation the Niitsitapi were nomadic buffalo hunters whose culture centred on the buffalo hunt, communal pounds and jumps such as Head-Smashed-In Buffalo Jump. Horses and firearms, acquired in the 18th century, made them a dominant military power on the northwestern plains. Epidemics, the destruction of the buffalo herds by the early 1880s, and treaties — Treaty 7 (1877) in Canada and a series of agreements in the United States — confined the nations to reserves and reservations.

Modern nations

Today the confederacy's members are the Siksika Nation, Kainai Nation and Piikani Nation in Alberta, and the Blackfeet Nation in Montana. The Blackfoot language is endangered but is the subject of revitalisation efforts, and the nations cooperate through the Blackfoot Confederacy Tribal Council.
