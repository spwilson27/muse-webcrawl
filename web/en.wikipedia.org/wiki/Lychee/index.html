Lychee

Lychee
Litchi chinensis
Family: Sapindaceae
Genus: Litchi
Native to: South China

The lychee (Litchi chinensis; Chinese: 荔枝) is a tropical tree native to South China, Malaysia, and northern Vietnam. It is the sole member of the genus Litchi in the soapberry family, Sapindaceae. The tree has been cultivated in China since the 11th century and is prized for its fragrant, sweet fruit.

Description

The lychee is an evergreen tree growing 10–28 m tall, with pinnate leaves of two to eight leaflets. The fruit is a drupe about 3–4 cm long, covered by a pink-red, roughly textured rind that is inedible. Inside is a layer of sweet, translucent white flesh rich in vitamin C, surrounding a single glossy brown seed.

Cultivation and uses

Lychees are grown commercially in China, India, Thailand, Vietnam, and other subtropical regions. They are eaten fresh, canned in syrup, dried (sometimes called "lychee nuts"), or made into juice and wine. Cultivars include 'Mauritius', 'Brewster', and 'Emperor'. Fresh lychees are highly perishable and are often air-freighted to distant markets.

History

Cultivation began in southern China, where lychees were a favoured delicacy of the imperial court. The fruit was famously delivered by fast courier to the Tang dynasty consort Yang Guifei. Lychees reached the West in the 17th century and were later introduced to Hawaii, Florida, and California.
