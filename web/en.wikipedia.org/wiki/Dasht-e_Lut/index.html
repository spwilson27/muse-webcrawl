Dasht-e Lut

The Dasht-e Lut, also the Lut Desert, is a large salt desert in the provinces of Kerman and Sistan-Baluchestan, Iran. It is among the world's hottest and driest places and is inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site.


Location: Southeastern Iran
Area: c. 51,800 km²
Designation: UNESCO World Heritage Site (2016)

Geography and climate

The desert features yardangs, massive dunes, salt plains, and lava fields around Gandom Beryan. NASA satellite measurements recorded land surface temperatures up to 70.7°C (159.3°F), among the highest ever measured, though air temperatures are lower. Strong seasonal winds sculpt its formations.

Ecology and heritage

Despite extreme aridity, surveys found insects, reptiles, and foxes sustaining a surprising ecosystem, partly fed by wind-blown debris. Archaeological sites on its margins show ancient settlement.
