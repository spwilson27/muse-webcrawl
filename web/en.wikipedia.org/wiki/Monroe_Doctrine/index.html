The Monroe Doctrine is a United States foreign policy position that opposes European colonialism in the Western Hemisphere. Announced by President James Monroe in his annual message to Congress on 2 December 1823, it declared the Americas closed to future European colonisation, stated that any European interference in the political affairs of the independent American states would be viewed as dangerous to the peace and safety of the United States, and pledged that the United States would not interfere in European affairs or in Europe's existing colonies.

Background

The doctrine arose from anxiety that the conservative Holy Alliance of European monarchies might help Spain reconquer its rebellious colonies in Latin America, and from Russian expansion on the Pacific Northwest coast. The British foreign secretary George Canning proposed a joint Anglo-American declaration; Secretary of State John Quincy Adams persuaded Monroe that a unilateral American statement would better serve the young republic's dignity and freedom of action. In practice, it was the British Royal Navy that enforced the doctrine for most of the 19th century, British interests coinciding with American ones.

Application and corollaries

Largely dormant for decades, the doctrine was invoked against French intervention in Mexico in the 1860s and was asserted forcefully by Secretary of State Richard Olney during the Venezuelan boundary crisis of 1895. The Roosevelt Corollary of 1904, announced by President Theodore Roosevelt, went further: it claimed for the United States an "international police power" to intervene in Latin American states guilty of "chronic wrongdoing", and was used to justify repeated interventions in the Caribbean and Central America. The Clark Memorandum of 1928, published by the State Department, repudiated the Corollary and held that the doctrine concerned European, not American, intervention. The doctrine nonetheless remained a reference point through the Cold War — when the United States treated communist influence in the hemisphere as outside interference — and in 2013 Secretary of State John Kerry declared that "the era of the Monroe Doctrine is over", though officials have periodically revived its language since.
