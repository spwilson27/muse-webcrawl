Jerry Siegel

Jerome Siegel (October 17, 1914 – January 28, 1996) was an American comic book writer, best known as co-creator, with artist Joe Shuster, of Superman, published by DC Comics. The character debuted in Action Comics #1 (1938) and launched the superhero genre. Siegel and Shuster's lifelong disputes over ownership and compensation became emblematic of creators' rights in comics.

Born: October 17, 1914, Cleveland, Ohio
Died: January 28, 1996, Los Angeles, California
Area: Writer

Early life and creation of Superman

Siegel, son of Lithuanian Jewish immigrants, met Shuster at Glenville High School in Cleveland, where they published a science-fiction fanzine. After Siegel's father died in a robbery in 1932, the pair developed Superman — first a villain (1933), then a hero in a cape drawing on circus strongmen, Douglas Fairbanks films and Siegel's daydreams. Rejected for years as a newspaper strip, Superman was sold to Detective Comics, Inc. in 1938 for $130, signing away the rights.

Career and legal battles

Siegel wrote most early Superman stories, co-created Superboy, the Spectre and Doctor Occult, and served in the U.S. Army writing for Stars and Stripes. In 1947 he and Shuster sued for the rights and were fired; they settled out of court in poverty. Siegel returned to DC in 1959, writing Legion of Super-Heroes stories, before a second dismissal. A public campaign by artists Neal Adams and Jerry Robinson in 1975, ahead of the Superman film, won Siegel and Shuster lifetime pensions and the "created by" credit. Copyright termination claims by his heirs continued into the 21st century.

Legacy

Siegel was inducted into the Will Eisner Comic Book Hall of Fame. His Cleveland home and Shuster's are marked as historic sites where Superman was created.
