Diphthong

A diphthong is a glide from one vowel position to another within a single syllable.

A diphthong (from Greek díphthongos, "two sounds") is a complex speech sound that begins as one vowel and glides toward another within the same syllable — as in the English words coin /ɔɪ/, loud /aʊ/ and face /eɪ/. It contrasts with a monophthong, a pure, steady vowel sound.

Types

Diphthongs are classified by the direction of the glide. In a falling (or descending) diphthong the first element is stronger and more prominent, as in most English diphthongs; in a rising diphthong the second element is the syllable peak. Centring diphthongs glide toward the central schwa /ə/, as in the traditional Received Pronunciation pronunciations of near /ɪə/ and square /eə/. Diphthongs also differ as closing or opening according to whether the tongue moves toward a closer (higher) or more open (lower) position.

In English

Standard varieties of English typically have three closing diphthongs — /aɪ/ (price), /aʊ/ (mouth) and /ɔɪ/ (choice) — plus /eɪ/ (face) and /əʊ/ or /oʊ/ (goat). The Great Vowel Shift largely created the long-vowel diphthongs of modern English from earlier pure vowels.
