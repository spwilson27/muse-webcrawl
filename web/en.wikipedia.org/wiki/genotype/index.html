Genotype

The genotype of an organism is its complete set of genetic material. However, the term is often used to refer to a single gene or set of genes, such as the genotype for eye color. The genes partly determine the observable characteristics of an organism, called its phenotype.

Genotype and phenotype

The distinction between genotype and phenotype was proposed by Danish botanist Wilhelm Johannsen in 1911. An organism's phenotype results from the expression of its genotype together with environmental influences and the interplay between the two.

Genotyping

Genotyping is the process of determining an individual's genotype, using techniques such as polymerase chain reaction, DNA sequencing, and microarrays. It is used in genetic testing, forensics, agriculture, and research into inherited disease.

Inheritance

For a diploid organism, the genotype at a locus consists of two alleles, inherited from each parent; the combination determines whether traits are expressed as dominant or recessive.
