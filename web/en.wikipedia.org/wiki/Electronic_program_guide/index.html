Electronic program guide

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Electronic program guides (EPGs) and interactive program guides (IPGs) are menu-based systems that provide users of television, radio and other media devices with continuously updated menus displaying broadcast programming or scheduling information for current and upcoming programming.

Typically displayed as an on-screen grid of channels against time, an EPG allows viewers to browse schedules, read programme descriptions, set reminders, and — in systems connected to a digital video recorder — schedule recordings with one button press.

Technology

EPG data is delivered by various means: multiplexed within digital television signals (such as DVB-SI or ATSC PSIP metadata), over the internet to IP-connected receivers, or historically over the vertical blanking interval of analogue signals. Metadata typically includes titles, start and end times, genre, synopsis and parental ratings.

History

Early precursors included scrolling on-screen guide channels of the 1980s, such as the Prevue Guide. Interactive guides became standard with digital cable and satellite television in the 1990s and 2000s; the intellectual property surrounding on-screen guides was the subject of extensive patent litigation involving companies such as Gemstar-TV Guide and Rovi.
