Alain Prost

Alain Marie Pascal Prost (born 24 February 1955) is a French former racing driver and team owner, and a four-time Formula One World Drivers' Champion (1985, 1986, 1989 and 1993). Nicknamed "The Professor" for his cerebral, calculated approach, he won 51 Grands Prix — a record at the time of his retirement — and is remembered for his fierce rivalry with Ayrton Senna.

Career

Prost rose through karting and French junior formulae, winning the 1979 European Formula Three title before debuting in Formula One with McLaren in 1980. After three seasons at Renault (1981–83), where he came close to the 1983 championship, he returned to McLaren and won back-to-back titles in 1985 and 1986, the latter snatched in a famous three-way decider in Adelaide. Joined by Senna in 1988, the pair dominated but their relationship collapsed into open hostility; their collision at Suzuka decided the 1989 title for Prost. He moved to Ferrari in 1990, losing that year's championship to Senna after another Suzuka collision, and was sacked late in 1991 after criticising the team. After a sabbatical year, he returned with Williams in 1993, won his fourth title comfortably, and retired rather than be joined at the team by Senna in 1994.

Later life

Prost bought the Ligier team in 1997 and ran Prost Grand Prix until its collapse in 2002. He later raced in the Andros Trophy ice series, served as a special adviser to the Renault/Alpine Formula One team, and co-founded involvement in Formula E as a team co-owner.
