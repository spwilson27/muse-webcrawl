Crete

Crete is the largest and most populous of the Greek islands, and the fifth-largest island in the Mediterranean Sea, with a population of about 625,000. It lies south of the Aegean Sea and is the largest region of Greece by area, with its capital at Heraklion.

Crete was the centre of the Minoan civilisation (c. 3000-1100 BCE), Europe's first advanced civilisation, whose palaces at Knossos, Phaistos and Malia featured in the myths of King Minos, the Minotaur, the Labyrinth, Daedalus and Icarus. The island later passed through Roman, Byzantine, Arab, Venetian and Ottoman hands before joining Greece in 1913. The Samaria Gorge, the Ida (Psiloritis) range and the Lasithi plateau, Cretan cuisine (a model of the Mediterranean diet) and mantinades music define its character; the Battle of Crete (1941) was a famous airborne assault of WWII.
