Columbia (supercontinent)

Columbia, also known as Nuna or Hudsonland, is one of Earth's oldest known hypothesized supercontinents. It was first proposed by Rogers and Santosh (2002) and is thought to have existed approximately 1,800 to 1,500 million years ago in the Paleoproterozoic era. Columbia consisted of proto-cratons that made up the cores of the continents of Laurentia, Baltica, Ukrainian Shield, Amazonia, Australia, Siberia, North China, and Kalahari.

Size and location

Columbia is estimated to have been approximately 12,900 kilometres (8,000 mi) from north to south at its broadest part, and some 4,800 km (3,000 mi) across at its widest section. The east coast of India was attached to western North America, while Australia lay against western Canada. Most of South America was rotated such that the western edge of modern Brazil lined up with the eastern United States.

Growth and breakup

The assembly of Columbia was associated with globally distributed collisional events between about 2.1 and 1.8 billion years ago, including the Trans-Hudson, Penokean, Taltson–Thelon, and Wopmay orogens of North America. Columbia began fragmenting about 1.6 billion years ago and had substantially broken up by about 1.3 billion years ago, partly through widespread mafic magmatic events. Its fragments were later reassembled into the supercontinent Rodinia.
