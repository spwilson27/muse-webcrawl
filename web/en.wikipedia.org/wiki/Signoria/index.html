Signoria

Signoria (Italian, from signore, "lord") was a form of government in the Italian city-states of the late Middle Ages and Renaissance, in which a single lord (signore) held power — de facto and often hereditary — over a city and its territory, superseding the republican institutions of the commune. The word also denotes the governing council in some republics, most famously the Signoria of Florence, the executive body of the Florentine Republic, and the Signoria of Venice.

Rise of the signorie

From the later 13th century, chronic factional conflict between Guelphs and Ghibellines and between noble and popular parties led many communes to entrust extraordinary authority to a single strongman, initially for a term and then for life. Dynasties of signori emerged across northern and central Italy: the Visconti and later Sforza in Milan, the Este in Ferrara and Modena, the Gonzaga in Mantua, the Della Scala in Verona, the Da Carrara in Padua, the Malatesta in Rimini and the Montefeltro in Urbino. Signori frequently sought legitimation through imperial or papal titles — vicariates, then marquisates and duchies — while retaining communal magistracies in form. Their courts became brilliant centres of Renaissance art and learning, even as republican writers condemned the regime as tyranny.

The Florentine Signoria

In Florence the Signoria was not a lordship but the republic's chief executive: eight Priors of the Guilds plus the Gonfaloniere of Justice, chosen by lot from eligible guild members for terms of only two months, residing during their term in the Palazzo della Signoria (Palazzo Vecchio). Created in 1282, the institution governed Florence — with interruptions and manipulations, including the ascendancy of the Albizzi and the Medici — until the republic's fall in the 1530s.

Venice

In the Republic of Venice the Signoria comprised the Doge, his six ducal councillors (the Minor Council) and the three heads of the Council of Forty; together with the College it formed the effective steering body of the Venetian state.
