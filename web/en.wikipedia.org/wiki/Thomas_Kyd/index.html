Thomas Kyd (1558–1594) was an English playwright, the author of The Spanish Tragedy, and one of the most important figures in the development of English drama. Though little is known of his life, Kyd is credited with having initiated the popularity of the revenge tragedy in the late 16th century, profoundly influencing his contemporary William Shakespeare, among others. He is also thought to have been the author of an earlier, lost play of Hamlet — the so-called Ur-Hamlet — which may have served as a major source for Shakespeare's famous tragedy.

Life

Kyd was baptised in the church of St Mary Woolnoth in Lombard Street, London, on 6 November 1558. He was the son of Francis Kyd, a scrivener. He was educated at the Merchant Taylors' School, where the headmaster was Richard Mulcaster; among his probable schoolmates were the poets Edmund Spenser and Thomas Lodge. Unlike many university-educated playwrights of his generation (the so-called "University Wits"), Kyd apparently did not attend university, and he may have followed his father into the scrivener's trade before turning to writing.

By the late 1580s Kyd was writing for the theatre and sharing a chamber with Christopher Marlowe. In May 1593 he was arrested after "atheistical" papers were found among his possessions during a search connected with anti-immigrant riots in London; Kyd maintained that the papers had belonged to Marlowe. He was released but apparently never recovered his health or reputation, and he died the following year, aged 35 or 36. He was buried on 15 August 1594 in St Mary Colechurch, London.

Works

Kyd's best-known work, The Spanish Tragedy, or Hieronimo is Mad Again, was written in the late 1580s and became one of the most popular plays of the Elizabethan stage. Its protagonist, Hieronimo, driven mad by the murder of his son Horatio, helped establish the conventions of revenge tragedy: the ghost, the soliloquising revenger, feigned or real madness, and the play-within-a-play. The play was frequently reprinted and was quoted throughout the period.

Other works attributed to Kyd include the tragedy Cornelia (1594), a translation of a French play by Robert Garnier; Soliman and Perseda; and a translation of Torquato Tasso's Padre di Famiglia, published as The Householders Philosophie (1588). The Ur-Hamlet attribution, though unproven, rests on contemporary references to a Hamlet play performed before Shakespeare's version was written.

Legacy

Kyd's influence on the shape of English tragedy is now widely acknowledged. The success of The Spanish Tragedy created a vogue for revenge plays that culminated in Hamlet, and his use of intrigue, ghosts and metatheatrical devices became permanent features of the early modern stage.
