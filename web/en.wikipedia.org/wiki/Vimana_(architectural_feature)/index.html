Vimana (architectural feature)

A vimana is the structure over the garbhagriha (inner sanctum) in Hindu temples of South India. It is typically a pyramidal tower, composed of progressively receding storeys (talas), and crowned by a cupola-like element called the shikhara or stupika. The vimana is the defining vertical element of the Dravidian style of temple architecture.

Terminology and contrasts

In North Indian (Nagara) architecture, the tower over the sanctum is called a shikhara, whereas in the Dravidian south the whole tower is the vimana and its crowning dome is the shikhara. The entrance gateway towers of southern temples, the gopurams, in later temples often grew taller than the vimana itself — a famous example being the Brihadeeswarar Temple at Thanjavur, whose vimana rises about 66 metres and remained among the tallest temple towers of its era.

Form and symbolism

The vimana is usually square in plan. Its storeys are ornamented with miniature shrines (kudu and sala motifs), pilasters and sculpted deities. Symbolically the vimana represents Mount Meru, the cosmic mountain and axis of the world, and serves as the dwelling of the deity enshrined in the sanctum below. Texts on architecture, the vastu shastras and shilpa shastras, prescribe its proportions relative to the sanctum and the temple as a whole.

Examples

Notable vimanas include those of the Shore Temple at Mahabalipuram (an early structural example of the Pallava period), the Kailasanathar Temple at Kanchipuram, the Brihadeeswarar Temple at Thanjavur, and the temples of Hampi.
