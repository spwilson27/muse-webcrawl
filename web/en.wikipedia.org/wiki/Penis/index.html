Penis

SystemMale reproductive and urinary systems
PartsRoot, body (shaft), glans
TissueCorpora cavernosa, corpus spongiosum

The penis (pl. penises or penes) is the male external sex organ of many animals, including humans, that serves as an intromittent organ for sexual reproduction and, in mammals, also carries the urethra for urination.

Anatomy

The human penis consists of a root, a body (shaft) and the glans, covered in part by the foreskin unless removed by circumcision. Internally it contains three columns of erectile tissue: the paired corpora cavernosa and the single corpus spongiosum, through which the urethra runs. During sexual arousal, parasympathetic signals cause these tissues to fill with blood, producing an erection.

Function and health

The penis delivers sperm into the female reproductive tract during intercourse and directs urine from the body. Conditions affecting it include erectile dysfunction, Peyronie's disease, infections and cancer, which is rare. Size and appearance vary considerably among individuals, a common source of cultural and psychological attention out of proportion to medical significance.
