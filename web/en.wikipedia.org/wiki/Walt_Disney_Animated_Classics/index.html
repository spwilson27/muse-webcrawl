Walt Disney Animated Classics

The Walt Disney Animated Classics series (also known as the Disney Animated Canon) is the official list of animated feature films produced by Walt Disney Animation Studios, the animation division of the Walt Disney Studios. The series began in 1937 with Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs and is widely considered the defining canon of American feature animation.

History

Walt Disney gambled the future of his studio on Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs (1937), the first cel-animated feature film in English. Its success enabled a run of classics: Pinocchio (1940), Fantasia (1940), Dumbo (1941), and Bambi (1942). After harder postwar years, the studio returned to fairy tales with Cinderella (1950) and continued through The Jungle Book (1967), the last feature Walt Disney personally supervised.

The Disney Renaissance

The period from 1989 to 1999, known as the Disney Renaissance, revived the canon with The Little Mermaid, Beauty and the Beast (the first animated film nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture), Aladdin, and The Lion King, which became one of the highest-grossing animated films of its era.

The CGI era

The studio transitioned to computer animation with Chicken Little (2005) and found a new commercial peak with films such as Tangled (2010), Frozen (2013), Zootopia (2016), and Moana (2016). Frozen and its 2019 sequel each grossed over $1 billion worldwide. The 60th film in the canon, Encanto, was released in 2021; subsequent entries include Strange World (2022), Wish (2023), and Moana 2 (2024).

Numbering and regional differences

The numbering of the canon differs slightly between North America and some other regions: certain films are counted in the official canon in one territory and not another, owing to release arrangements and studio reorganisations. Films produced by other Disney units, such as Pixar or DisneyToon Studios, are not part of the Animated Classics canon.
