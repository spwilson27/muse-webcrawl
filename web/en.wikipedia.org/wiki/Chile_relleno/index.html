The chile relleno (Spanish for "stuffed chile") is a dish in Mexican cuisine that originated in the city of Puebla. It consists of a roasted poblano pepper (or, regionally, other chiles such as Hatch, Anaheim, or jalapeño) stuffed with cheese — traditionally queso Chihuahua or Oaxaca cheese — or sometimes with a sweet-savoury minced meat mixture called picadillo, then coated in an egg batter or corn masa and fried, and served in a mild tomato sauce.

Preparation

The peppers are first roasted until their skins blister, then peeled and slit open to remove the seeds. After stuffing, the chile is dredged in flour, dipped in a fluffy beaten-egg batter (yolks and whites beaten separately), and fried until golden and puffed. It is typically served bathed in a tomato-based salsa roja and accompanied by rice and beans.

Variations

In New Mexico and the US Southwest, rellenos are made with New Mexico green chiles, usually dipped in batter or breadcrumbs. The chile relleno burrito wraps the fried stuffed chile inside a flour tortilla. A distinguished seasonal relative is the chile en nogada — a poblano stuffed with picadillo and covered in walnut sauce and pomegranate seeds — traditionally served in Puebla from late summer into September. Fast-food and Tex-Mex adaptations substitute other peppers and coatings, and jalapeño poppers are a bite-size bar-food descendant of the concept.
