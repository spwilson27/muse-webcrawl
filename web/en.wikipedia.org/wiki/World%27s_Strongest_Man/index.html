The World's Strongest Man is an international strongman competition held annually, first organised in 1977 at Universal Studios in California. Competitors contest a series of events testing whole-body strength—such as the Atlas Stones, vehicle pulls, log lifts, deadlifts for repetitions and loading races—through qualifying heats leading to a final.

History and champions

Early years featured athletes from powerlifting, wrestling and field events; winners included Bruce Wilhelm, Don Reinhoudt and Bill Kazmaier. Icelanders Jón Páll Sigmarsson and Magnús Ver Magnússon each won four titles in the 1980s–90s, as did Žydrūnas Savickas of Lithuania. Poland's Mariusz Pudzianowski holds the record with five titles (2002–2008). Other champions include Brian Shaw, Hafþór Júlíus Björnsson, Eddie Hall, Oleksii Novikov and Tom Stoltman. The contest is produced for television and broadcast widely, traditionally around the New Year period in the UK.

Format

Around thirty athletes compete in heats, with the top finishers advancing to a ten-man final. Events vary by year but the five Atlas Stones have become the traditional closing spectacle.
