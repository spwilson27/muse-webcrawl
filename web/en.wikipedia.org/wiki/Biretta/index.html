Biretta
TypeSquare clerical cap
Worn byCatholic, Anglican, and some Lutheran clergy
ColorsBlack (priests), purple (bishops), scarlet (cardinals)

A biretta is a square cap with three or four peaks or horns, sometimes surmounted by a tuft or pompon, worn by clergy of the Catholic Church and by some Anglican and Lutheran clergy. It is traditionally worn outdoors and in processions, but not during Mass.

Design and colors

The color of the biretta indicates the wearer's rank: black for priests, purple for bishops, and scarlet silk for cardinals, who receive their biretta from the pope at a consistory. Doctors of theology and canon law may wear a biretta with a tuft of a color corresponding to their faculty.

History

The biretta developed from the soft caps worn by academics and clergy in the late Middle Ages; the word derives from the Latin birretum, a small cap. Its use declined after the Second Vatican Council but remains common in traditional liturgical contexts, where it is put on and removed at prescribed points in ceremonies such as solemn Mass and benediction.
