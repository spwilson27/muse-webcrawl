Reichenau Abbey

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
The former abbey church on Reichenau Island

Reichenau Abbey (German: Kloster Reichenau) was a Benedictine monastery on Reichenau Island in Lake Constance in southern Germany, within the modern state of Baden-Württemberg. Founded in 724 by the itinerant bishop Saint Pirmin, it was one of the great intellectual and artistic centres of the Carolingian and Ottonian empires, renowned for its scriptorium, library and school of manuscript illumination.

History

Pirmin founded the abbey with the support of Charles Martel and Alemannic nobles; under abbots such as Waldo, Heito and Walahfrid Strabo — the poet and horticultural writer whose Hortulus describes the abbey garden — Reichenau became a hub of learning on the route between Italy and the Frankish north. Its library held some 450 volumes by the 9th century, an extraordinary collection for the time. From the late 10th to the early 11th century the Reichenau school produced some of the masterpieces of Ottonian illumination, including Gospel books made for Otto III and Henry II, such as the Pericopes of Henry II and the Reichenau Gospels in Munich.

The abbey's fortunes declined from the 11th century as reform movements and rival centres rose. It was incorporated into the Diocese of Constance in 1540, its community reduced to a priory; the last monks left in the 18th century and the monastery was dissolved in the secularisation of 1802–1803. The island's three Romanesque churches — St Mary and St Mark (the abbey church), St Peter and St Paul at Niederzell, and St George at Oberzell, the last famous for its Ottonian wall paintings — survive, and the "Monastic Island of Reichenau" was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2000. A small Benedictine community, the Cella St. Benedikt, was re-established on the island in 2001.
