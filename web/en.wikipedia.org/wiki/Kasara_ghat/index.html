Kasara ghat

The Kasara ghat, also known as the Thal Ghat, is a mountain pass in the Western Ghats of Maharashtra, India, near the town of Kasara. It carries the main road and rail routes between Mumbai and Nashik, forming part of the historic corridor linking the Konkan coast with the Deccan plateau and north India.

Railway

The railway over the ghat, part of the Great Indian Peninsula Railway's north-east line, was completed in the 1860s and was a celebrated feat of colonial engineering, with steep gradients of about 1 in 37, reversing stations and viaducts climbing from Kasara to Igatpuri. For many years trains required additional banker locomotives to surmount the grade. The section was later electrified and is operated by Central Railway; Kasara is today also the northern terminus of Mumbai's suburban railway services, from which long-distance trains climb the ghat toward Igatpuri and Nashik.

Road

The old Mumbai–Agra road and its successor, National Highway 160 (formerly NH 3), ascend the ghat with numerous hairpin bends. The pass is notorious for heavy monsoon rain, fog and landslides, and for congestion on the busy Mumbai–Nashik corridor. The nearby hill station of Igatpuri stands at the top of the ascent.
