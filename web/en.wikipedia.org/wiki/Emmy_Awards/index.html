Emmy Awards

The Emmy Awards, or simply Emmys, are awards for excellence in the television industry. They are presented in various annual award ceremonies held throughout the year, each celebrating achievements in a particular sector of the industry. The two ceremonies that receive the most media coverage are the Primetime Emmy Awards, which honour primetime entertainment programming, and the Daytime Emmy Awards, which honour daytime entertainment programming.

History

The first Emmy Awards ceremony was held on 25 January 1949 at the Hollywood Athletic Club. The name "Emmy" derives from "Immy", a nickname for the image orthicon camera tube that was instrumental in the development of early television. The statuette, designed by television engineer Louis McManus, depicts a winged woman holding an atom, symbolising television's fusion of art and science.

Organisations

Three related organisations present the awards: the Television Academy (Academy of Television Arts & Sciences), which administers the Primetime Emmys; the National Academy of Television Arts and Sciences, which administers the Daytime, Sports, News & Documentary and other national awards; and the International Academy of Television Arts and Sciences, which administers the International Emmy Awards.

Categories

Primetime categories include Outstanding Drama Series, Outstanding Comedy Series, Outstanding Limited or Anthology Series, and acting categories for lead and supporting performers. Separate Creative Arts ceremonies recognise technical achievements such as cinematography, editing, casting and costume design.

Records

The sketch comedy programme Saturday Night Live is among the most nominated and awarded programmes in Emmy history. In drama, series such as Game of Thrones and The West Wing have won multiple Outstanding Drama Series awards.
