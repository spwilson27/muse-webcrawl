TORCH complex

TORCH complex (also known as TORCH syndrome or the TORCH infections) is a medical acronym for a set of congenital infections that can be transmitted from a pregnant woman to her fetus and cause significant birth defects and developmental problems. The acronym stands for:

T – Toxoplasmosis; O – Other agents (including syphilis, varicella-zoster, parvovirus B19 and others); R – Rubella; C – Cytomegalovirus; H – Herpes simplex.

Signs and symptoms

Although each infection has its own characteristic features, the TORCH infections share many findings in the newborn: growth restriction, microcephaly, intracranial calcifications, rash, jaundice, enlargement of the liver and spleen, anaemia and low platelets, eye abnormalities such as chorioretinitis, and sensorineural hearing loss. Cytomegalovirus is the most common congenital infection and a leading non-genetic cause of childhood hearing loss.

Diagnosis and prevention

Diagnosis relies on serological testing of mother and infant, and on PCR testing of blood, urine or amniotic fluid. Prevention includes rubella vaccination before pregnancy, hygiene measures to avoid toxoplasma and cytomegalovirus exposure, and screening and treatment of syphilis during pregnancy.
