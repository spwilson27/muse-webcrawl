Ascending aorta

SourceLeft ventricle
BranchesLeft and right coronary arteries
Continues asAortic arch
Lengthabout 5 cm

The ascending aorta is the first portion of the aorta, the largest artery in the human body. It begins at the aortic valve, at the level of the lower border of the third costal cartilage, and ascends for about 5 centimetres before continuing as the aortic arch.

Anatomy

At its origin, the ascending aorta presents three small dilatations, the aortic sinuses (sinuses of Valsalva), corresponding to the cusps of the aortic valve. Two of these sinuses give origin to the right and left coronary arteries, the only branches of the ascending aorta, which supply the heart muscle itself. The segment is largely enclosed within the pericardium together with the pulmonary trunk.

Clinical significance

Dilatation of the ascending aorta (aortic root aneurysm) and aortic dissection involving this segment (Stanford type A) are surgical emergencies. Widening is associated with hypertension, bicuspid aortic valve and connective-tissue disorders such as Marfan syndrome.
