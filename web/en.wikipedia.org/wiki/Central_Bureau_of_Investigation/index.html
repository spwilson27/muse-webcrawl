Central Bureau of Investigation

The Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI) is the premier investigating police agency of India. It operates under the jurisdiction of the Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions of the Government of India, and is headed by a Director appointed by a high-powered committee. The agency investigates major crimes including corruption in the public services, economic offences, bank fraud, and serious cases referred to it by state governments and the courts.

History

The CBI traces its origins to the Special Police Establishment, set up in 1941 during the Second World War to investigate corruption in the war and supplies departments. It was formally established under its present name in 1963 and derives its investigative powers from the Delhi Special Police Establishment Act of 1946.

Jurisdiction and work

The CBI investigates offences notified under the Prevention of Corruption Act and the Indian Penal Code, and routinely handles high-profile murder cases, financial scandals, and cases transferred by the Supreme Court of India and the High Courts. Because policing is primarily a state subject, the CBI generally requires state consent to investigate within a state, a requirement that has periodically been the subject of political controversy.
