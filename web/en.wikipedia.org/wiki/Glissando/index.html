Glissando

In music, a glissando is a glide from one pitch to another. It is an Italianised form of the French glissant, "sliding". A glissando is indicated by a wavy line or a straight line joining the initial and final notes, sometimes with the abbreviation gliss.

Glissando and portamento

Some authorities distinguish the glissando — a stepped glide through discrete notes, as on a piano or harp — from the portamento, a continuous slide possible on string instruments, trombone, and the voice. On the piano a glissando is played by sliding a fingernail or fingertips along the white or black keys.

Notable uses

Famous examples include the clarinet glissando opening Gershwin's Rhapsody in Blue, trombone slides in jazz, and harp glissandi in orchestral works by Debussy and Ravel.
