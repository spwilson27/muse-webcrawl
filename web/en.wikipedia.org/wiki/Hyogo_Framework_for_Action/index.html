The Hyogo Framework for Action 2005–2015: Building the Resilience of Nations and Communities to Disasters was a global blueprint for disaster risk reduction adopted by 168 governments at the World Conference on Disaster Reduction, held in Kobe, Hyōgo, Japan, from 18 to 22 January 2005. The conference took place shortly after the devastating 2004 Indian Ocean earthquake and tsunami, and near the tenth anniversary of the Great Hanshin earthquake that struck Kobe in 1995.

Goals and priorities

The Framework's strategic goals were the integration of disaster risk reduction into sustainable development policies, the development of institutions and capacities to build resilience, and the incorporation of risk reduction into emergency preparedness, response, and recovery. It set five priorities for action:

  • Ensure that disaster risk reduction is a national and local priority with a strong institutional basis for implementation.
  • Identify, assess and monitor disaster risks and enhance early warning.
  • Use knowledge, innovation and education to build a culture of safety and resilience at all levels.
  • Reduce the underlying risk factors.
  • Strengthen disaster preparedness for effective response at all levels.

Implementation and legacy

Implementation was coordinated by the United Nations International Strategy for Disaster Reduction (UNISDR, now UNDRR), with progress reviewed in biennial Global Assessment Reports and through the HFA Monitor, a voluntary national reporting tool. In March 2015 the Framework was succeeded by the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction 2015–2030, adopted at the third World Conference on Disaster Risk Reduction in Sendai, Japan, which added global targets and a stronger emphasis on understanding disaster risk and on the role of non-state stakeholders.
