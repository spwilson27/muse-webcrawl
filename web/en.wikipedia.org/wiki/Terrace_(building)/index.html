Terrace (building)

A terrace in architecture is an exterior, raised, flat area adjoining a building — commonly a rooftop platform, a raised patio, or a paved area next to a house used for outdoor living, dining, and recreation. Terraces may be open to the sky or partly covered by a pergola or roof.

Types and history

Rooftop terraces are characteristic of Mediterranean and modern urban architecture, providing private outdoor space in dense cities. Terraced gardens and terraces appear from ancient Persian and Roman building onward, and the terrace became a signature element of modernist houses, such as those of Le Corbusier, whose "five points" included the roof garden. In everyday usage the term overlaps with patio, deck, balcony, and veranda, though a balcony is smaller and projects from an upper floor, while a terrace generally sits at ground level or on a roof.

Terraced housing

In British usage, a "terrace" can also denote a row of attached houses sharing side walls (terraced housing), a distinct usage from the outdoor platform.
