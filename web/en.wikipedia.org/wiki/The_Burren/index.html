The Burren

The Burren (Irish: Boireann, meaning "great rock") is a karst landscape in County Clare, on the west coast of Ireland, covering roughly 250–350 square kilometres of glaciated limestone pavement, terraced hills, and underground rivers. One of Ireland's six national parks, the Burren National Park, protects part of the southeastern Burren. The region is famed for its extraordinary flora, its dense concentration of archaeological monuments, and its stark, moonlike scenery.

Geology

The limestone was laid down as sediment in a tropical sea some 350 million years ago, during the Carboniferous period, and later uplifted and scoured by glaciers. Rainwater, slightly acidic, dissolved the rock along joints to form the distinctive pavement of blocks (clints) separated by fissures (grikes), as well as caves (such as Aillwee Cave and Pol an Ionain, home to a celebrated free-hanging stalactite), disappearing streams, and seasonal lakes called turloughs.

Flora and fauna

The Burren is botanically unique: Mediterranean and Alpine-Arctic plants grow side by side — bloody cranesbill, spring gentian, orchids, and mountain avens — sheltered in the warm, lime-rich grikes. Roughly 70% of Ireland's native plant species are found here. The famous remark attributed to a Cromwellian officer described a country where there was "not a tree to hang a man, water enough to drown him, nor earth enough to bury him" — yet the fertile pockets between the rocks supported farming for millennia, and cattle are still wintered on the uplands in the distinctive practice of "winterage".

Archaeology and history

Humans have lived in the Burren since the Neolithic. Monuments include portal tombs such as Poulnabrone (about 5,800 years old), hundreds of wedge tombs, ring forts (cahers), the great stone fort of Cahercommaun, early churches, and the round tower and high crosses of Kilfenora. Tower houses and the settled villages of Ballyvaughan, Lisdoonvarna, and Doolin — a centre for traditional Irish music — serve today's visitors. Together with the Cliffs of Moher, the Burren forms a UNESCO Global Geopark.
