Isadora Duncan

BornAngela Isadora Duncan, 26 May 1877, San Francisco, US
Died14 September 1927 (aged 50), Nice, France
OccupationDancer, choreographer

Angela Isadora Duncan (26 May 1877 – 14 September 1927) was an American dancer and choreographer who was a pioneer of modern contemporary dance and performed to great acclaim throughout Europe and the United States.

Art

Rejecting the rigidity of classical ballet, Duncan danced barefoot in flowing Greek-inspired tunics, drawing movement from nature, classical art and music — she performed to Chopin, Gluck, Beethoven and Wagner at a time when "interpretive" dancing to concert music was radical. She located the source of movement in the solar plexus and believed dance should express emotion and the liberation of the body, especially the female body.

Life

Born in San Francisco, Duncan found little success in America and moved to Europe in 1899, performing in London, Paris, Berlin and St. Petersburg, where she influenced the Ballets Russes circle. She founded schools for girls in Germany, France and, after the Russian Revolution, in Moscow at the invitation of the Soviet government. Her personal life was marked by scandal and tragedy: both her children drowned in a car accident in the Seine in 1913; she married the Russian poet Sergei Yesenin in 1922.

Death and legacy

Duncan died in Nice in 1927 when her long scarf caught in the wheel of a car, breaking her neck. Her autobiography My Life appeared the same year. She is remembered as the "Mother of Modern Dance", whose free style opened the way for modern dance worldwide.
