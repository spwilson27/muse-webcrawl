Mechanical equilibrium

A standard definition of mechanical equilibrium in classical mechanics is that a particle is at rest, or moving with constant velocity, when the net force acting on it is zero. A system is in mechanical equilibrium when the net force and the net torque on each part of the system are zero.

Types

Equilibrium may be stable, unstable or neutral (indifferent). In stable equilibrium a small displacement produces restoring forces that return the system toward equilibrium, as with a ball in a bowl; in unstable equilibrium a displacement grows, as with a ball balanced on a hilltop; in neutral equilibrium the system simply rests in its new position.

Statics

The study of systems in mechanical equilibrium is called statics and underpins the design of structures such as bridges and buildings, where forces and torques from loads must balance at every joint.
