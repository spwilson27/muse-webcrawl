International Business Machines Corporation, nicknamed Big Blue, is an American multinational technology and consulting corporation headquartered in Armonk, New York. Founded in 1911 as the Computing-Tabulating-Recording Company and renamed IBM in 1924, it is one of the oldest surviving technology companies. IBM dominated the mainframe computer era, built the systems that helped put Apollo astronauts on the Moon, launched the IBM Personal Computer in 1981, and today focuses on hybrid cloud computing, artificial intelligence (the watsonx platform) and enterprise consulting, competing in cloud with Amazon Web Services and Microsoft Azure.

History

Under Thomas J. Watson Sr. and his son Thomas Watson Jr., IBM became the central company of the punch-card and then the electronic computing industry; its System/360 family of mainframes, launched in 1964 after a bet-the-company investment, defined enterprise computing for decades. IBM survived a 13-year US antitrust suit (dropped in 1982) and near-collapse in the early 1990s, when Lou Gerstner refocused it on services. IBM sold its personal computer division to Lenovo in 2005 and its low-end server business in 2014.

Notable demonstrations include the Deep Blue chess computer, which beat world champion Garry Kasparov in 1997, and Watson, which won the quiz show Jeopardy! in 2011. In 2019 IBM made its largest acquisition, the open-source software company Red Hat, for about $34 billion, anchoring its hybrid-cloud strategy; in 2021 it spun off its managed-infrastructure business as Kyndryl.

Research

IBM Research operates laboratories worldwide; its scientists have won Nobel Prizes and Turing Awards, and IBM has led US patent rankings for many years. The company is also a leader in quantum computing, offering cloud access to quantum processors.
