Nusinersen

Nusinersen, sold under the brand name Spinraza, is a medication used to treat spinal muscular atrophy (SMA), a genetic disorder caused by loss of the SMN1 gene. It was the first approved treatment for SMA, authorized by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in December 2016. Nusinersen is an antisense oligonucleotide given by intrathecal injection into the cerebrospinal fluid.

Mechanism

People with SMA lack sufficient survival motor neuron (SMN) protein because of mutations in SMN1. A nearly identical gene, SMN2, produces mostly a truncated, unstable protein because exon 7 is usually skipped during splicing. Nusinersen binds to a splicing silencer site in SMN2 pre-mRNA and promotes inclusion of exon 7, so that SMN2 yields full-length, functional SMN protein.

Use and cost

Treatment begins with four loading doses over about two months, followed by maintenance doses every four months for life. Clinical trials in infants (ENDEAR) and children (CHERISH) showed improved survival and motor milestones compared with untreated controls, with the greatest benefit when treatment starts before symptoms — one reason newborn screening for SMA has expanded. The drug's very high list price (initially about US$750,000 for the first year and $375,000 per year thereafter) made it a prominent case in debates over the pricing of rare-disease therapies. Alternatives that followed include the gene therapy onasemnogene abeparvovec (Zolgensma) and the oral small molecule risdiplam (Evrysdi).
