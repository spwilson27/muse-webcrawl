Agra Fort is a historical fort in the city of Agra in Uttar Pradesh, India. It was the main residence of the emperors of the Mughal Empire from 1565 until 1638, when the capital was shifted to Delhi. The massive red sandstone fortress stands on the west bank of the Yamuna River, about 2.5 km northwest of the Taj Mahal.

History

The fort occupies the site of an older brick fort, Badalgarh. After the Mughal emperor Akbar made Agra his capital, he rebuilt the fort in red sandstone between about 1565 and 1573, employing some 4,000 builders over eight years. His grandson Shah Jahan later demolished many of the sandstone buildings and replaced them with palaces of white marble inlaid with precious stones.

Near the end of his life Shah Jahan was deposed and confined in the fort by his son Aurangzeb; tradition holds that he died in the Musamman Burj, a marble tower with a view of the Taj Mahal, the tomb he had built for his wife Mumtaz Mahal. The fort later passed to the Jats, the Marathas, and finally the British, and it was a scene of fighting during the Indian Rebellion of 1857.

Layout and buildings

The fort forms a roughly semicircular walled city, its 70-foot-high double ramparts running about 2.5 km in circumference, with four gates — the Delhi Gate, the Amar Singh Gate (now the public entrance), and gates on the river side. Notable structures inside include the Jahangiri Mahal, the Khas Mahal, the Diwan-i-Khas (hall of private audience), the Diwan-i-Am (hall of public audience), the Sheesh Mahal (mirrored palace), the Pearl Mosque (Moti Masjid), and the Musamman Burj.

Recognition

Agra Fort was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1983. It is sometimes called the "Red Fort of Agra" — not to be confused with the Red Fort of Delhi.
