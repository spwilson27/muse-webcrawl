HathiTrust

HathiTrust is a partnership of research libraries and academic institutions that operates a shared digital repository of digitised books and journals. Founded in 2008, its collection comprises tens of millions of volumes, many digitised through the Google Books Library Project and the Internet Archive. The name derives from hathi, the Hindi and Urdu word for elephant, an animal noted for its long memory.

Services

The HathiTrust Digital Library provides bibliographic search across the full collection and full-text search across the corpus. Works in the public domain can be read online in full, while in-copyright works are preserved and made available for computational research, accessibility services for print-disabled users, and limited search display. The partnership also runs the HathiTrust Research Center, which supports text mining of the corpus, and a shared print programme coordinating retention of physical copies.

Legal significance

HathiTrust was a defendant in Authors Guild v. HathiTrust, in which the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Second Circuit held in 2014 that the libraries' mass digitisation for full-text search and accessibility was a fair use. The decision, together with the parallel Google Books ruling, established important precedent for digitisation by libraries.
