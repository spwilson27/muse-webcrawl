Truce of Yam-Zapolsky
Signed15 January 1582
PartiesPolish–Lithuanian Commonwealth and the Tsardom of Russia
MediatorAntonio Possevino (papal legate)
EffectEnded the Livonian War; Russia ceded Livonia and Polotsk to the Commonwealth

The Truce of Yam-Zapolsky, signed on 15 January 1582, was a ten-year truce between the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth and the Tsardom of Russia that ended the Livonian War (1558–1583). It was negotiated at the village of Yam-Zapolsky near Pskov with the mediation of the papal legate Antonio Possevino.

Terms

Following King Stephen Báthory's successful campaigns and the siege of Pskov, Ivan the Terrible agreed to surrender all of Livonia still in Russian hands, together with the city of Polotsk, to the Commonwealth. Russia retained its other territories and abandoned its claims to Livonia and the Duchy of Courland. A separate settlement with Sweden, the Truce of Plussa (1583), ended the war on the northern front on similarly unfavourable terms for Russia.

Significance

The truce confirmed the Commonwealth as the dominant power in the eastern Baltic and ended Russia's bid for direct access to the Baltic Sea for more than a century, until the Great Northern War.
