Personal finance

Personal finance is the financial management that an individual or family performs to budget, save, and spend money over time, taking into account financial risks and future life events. It covers how people earn, allocate, and protect their resources, and is closely tied to financial literacy and long-term planning.

Key areas

Personal finance is typically organised around several areas: income (wages, business earnings, pensions); spending (budgeting and controlling expenses); saving (emergency funds, deposits); investing (stocks, bonds, mutual funds, real estate, retirement accounts such as 401(k)s and IRAs); and protection (insurance against illness, disability, liability, and death, plus estate planning).

Planning process

Financial planning generally involves assessing one's current net worth and cash flow, setting goals (education, home purchase, retirement), creating and following a budget, managing and repaying debt, and periodically reviewing the plan. Compound interest makes early saving particularly powerful, while high-interest consumer debt is a common obstacle. Concepts such as diversification, risk tolerance, tax-advantaged accounts, and inflation feature throughout.

Education and advice

Personal finance is taught in schools in some jurisdictions and is the subject of a large popular literature. Professional advice is provided by certified financial planners; in many countries advisers are regulated and may owe clients a fiduciary duty. Behavioural finance research shows that biases and habits strongly affect everyday money decisions.
