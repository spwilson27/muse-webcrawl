Blizzard Entertainment, Inc. is an American video game developer and publisher based in Irvine, California. Founded in 1991 as Silicon & Synapse by Michael Morhaime, Frank Pearce and Allen Adham, the company was renamed Blizzard Entertainment in 1994. It is a subsidiary of Activision Blizzard, which became part of Microsoft in 2023.

History and games

After early work on ports and titles such as The Lost Vikings and Rock n' Roll Racing, Blizzard achieved major success with the real-time strategy game Warcraft: Orcs & Humans (1994). Its franchises became industry landmarks: Diablo (1997) in action role-playing, StarCraft (1998) in strategy and esports, and the massively multiplayer World of Warcraft (2004), which became the most successful MMORPG of its era. Later titles include Hearthstone (2014), Heroes of the Storm (2015), Overwatch (2016), Diablo IV (2023) and Overwatch 2.

Blizzard operates the Battle.net online gaming service, launched in 1996 with Diablo, and hosted the annual BlizzCon fan convention from 2005. The company has been noted for a "when it's ready" development philosophy and for extensive post-launch support, as well as for controversies including a 2021 California lawsuit alleging workplace harassment and discrimination, which preceded Microsoft's acquisition of Activision Blizzard.
