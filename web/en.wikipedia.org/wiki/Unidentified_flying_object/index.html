Unidentified flying object

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An unidentified flying object (UFO), or unidentified anomalous phenomenon (UAP), is any perceived aerial or celestial object or phenomenon that cannot be immediately identified or explained. The term "flying saucer" entered popular culture in 1947 after pilot Kenneth Arnold's sighting of crescent-shaped objects over Washington state, followed weeks later by the Roswell incident in New Mexico. The U.S. Air Force coined "UFO" in 1952 and investigated reports through Projects Sign, Grudge, and Blue Book (1952–1969), which catalogued over 12,000 sightings; the 1968 Condon Report concluded that further scientific study was unlikely to be productive.

Explanations

Studies consistently find that the overwhelming majority of reports resolve into known causes: Venus and other bright astronomical objects, weather balloons, aircraft (including classified prototypes), satellites and rocket re-entries, drones, birds, atmospheric phenomena such as lenticular clouds and sprites, temperature inversions affecting radar, and deliberate hoaxes. A small residue remains formally unidentified, usually because the data are too poor to analyse rather than because the objects demonstrably defy known physics. The extraterrestrial hypothesis — that some UFOs are alien spacecraft — lacks any confirmed physical evidence, though it dominates popular discussion.

Recent developments

Interest revived after 2017 reporting on the Pentagon's Advanced Aerospace Threat Identification Program and the release of U.S. Navy infrared videos (FLIR1, Gimbal, GoFast). The Office of the Director of National Intelligence assessed 144 military UAP reports in 2021 without settling their nature; NASA convened an independent study team in 2022, and the Pentagon's All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office (AARO), established in 2022, stated in its 2024 historical review that it found no evidence of extraterrestrial technology or hidden reverse-engineering programmes. UAP remains, officially, a data-quality and air-safety problem as much as a scientific one.
