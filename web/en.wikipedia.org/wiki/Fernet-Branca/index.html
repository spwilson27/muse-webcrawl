Fernet-Branca

Fernet-Branca is an Italian brand of fernet, a style of bitter herbal liqueur (amaro). It was formulated in Milan in 1845 by Bernardino Branca and is produced by the Fratelli Branca Distillerie using a secret recipe said to contain 27 herbs, roots and spices, aged for twelve months in oak casks.

Characteristics

Fernet-Branca is exceptionally bitter and aromatic, with prominent notes of gentian, chamomile, saffron, myrrh and aloe. It is bottled at around 39% alcohol by volume and is traditionally served as a digestivo, neat after meals, though it is also used in cocktails such as the Hanky Panky.

Popularity in Argentina

The drink enjoys extraordinary popularity in Argentina, where it is commonly mixed with cola as fernet con coca. The country accounts for a large share of world consumption, and the Branca distillery in Buenos Aires supplies much of the South American market.
