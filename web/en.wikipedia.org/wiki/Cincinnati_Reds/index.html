The Cincinnati Reds are an American professional baseball team based in Cincinnati, Ohio, competing in Major League Baseball as a member club of the National League Central division. The franchise traces its lineage to the Cincinnati Red Stockings of 1869, the first openly all-professional baseball team.

History

The original Red Stockings went 57–0 in 1869. The present club, founded in 1881 and a charter presence in the American Association before joining the National League in 1890, won its first World Series in 1919 over the Chicago White Sox — the series later tainted by the Black Sox scandal. The Reds won again in 1940, and reached their peak as the "Big Red Machine" of the 1970s, when the lineups of Johnny Bench, Joe Morgan, Tony Pérez and Pete Rose, managed by Sparky Anderson, won back-to-back championships in 1975 and 1976, the 1975 Series against Boston being widely ranked among the greatest ever played. A fifth title followed in 1990, a wire-to-wire season capped by a sweep of the Oakland Athletics.

Ballparks

The Reds played at Riverfront Stadium (later Cinergy Field) from 1970 to 2002 and since 2003 at Great American Ball Park on the Ohio River, beside the National Underground Railroad Freedom Center.
