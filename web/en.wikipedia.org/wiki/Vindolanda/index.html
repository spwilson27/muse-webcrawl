Vindolanda

Vindolanda
Location: Northumberland, England, just south of Hadrian's Wall
Occupied: c. AD 85 – 370
Famous for: Vindolanda tablets

Vindolanda was a Roman auxiliary fort (castrum) in northern England, guarding the Stanegate road just south of Hadrian's Wall. Garrisoned by cohorts from Gaul and the Low Countries, it stood near modern Bardon Mill in Northumberland and was occupied from about AD 85 into the late 4th century.

Vindolanda tablets

The site's anaerobic soil preserved thousands of wooden writing tablets — Britain's oldest surviving handwritten documents (AD 85–130), voted Britain's top treasure — ink-written letters and reports revealing frontier daily life: supply lists, strength reports, and Claudia Severa's birthday invitation, among the earliest known Latin written by a woman. Preserved shoes, textiles and a possible gladiator's remains add to the picture.

Excavation

Ongoing excavations by the Vindolanda Trust since the 1970s, associated with the Birley family, have uncovered successive timber and stone forts, a large extramural settlement (vicus), temples and bath houses; finds are displayed at the on-site museum.
