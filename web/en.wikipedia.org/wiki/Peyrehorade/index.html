Peyrehorade

Country: France

Region: Nouvelle-Aquitaine

Department: Landes

Population: Approximately 3,600

Peyrehorade is a commune in the Landes department in Nouvelle-Aquitaine in southwestern France. It lies at the confluence of the Gave de Pau and the Gave d'Oloron, which join to form the Gaves réunis, a short distance before flowing into the Adour river. The town sits at the meeting point of the historic regions of Gascony, Béarn, and the French Basque Country.

Geography and economy

Peyrehorade is a small market town serving the surrounding agricultural area. The local economy is known in particular for kiwi fruit production — the Adour valley around Peyrehorade is one of France's principal kiwi-growing areas — as well as maize, duck farming, and salmon fishing in the gaves. The town is connected by rail on the line between Bayonne and Pau/Toulouse.

Sights

Landmarks include the Château de Montréal, a fortified house rising above the town whose towers date in part to the medieval period and which served as a residence of the viscounts of Orthé, the church of Saint-Martin, and riverside quays along the Gaves réunis. The town hosts traditional markets and festivals linked to its Gascon and Béarnais heritage.

Sport

The local rugby union club, Peyrehorade Sports Rugby, reflects the Southwest's strong rugby culture. The commune is also a starting point for canoeing and rafting on the Gave d'Oloron.
