Film crew

A film crew is a group of people hired by a film production company to produce a film or motion picture. The crew is distinguished from the cast, the actors who appear in the production, and is organized into departments, each responsible for a particular aspect of filmmaking.

Departments

The director leads the creative work of the crew, supported by assistant directors who manage the set and schedule. Major departments include production (producers, production managers, and coordinators), camera (headed by the director of photography, with camera operators, focus pullers, and loaders), lighting and grip (gaffers, best boys, grips, and riggers), sound (production sound mixers and boom operators), art (production designers, set decorators, and prop masters), costume, make-up and hair, and editorial and post-production.

Size and organization

Crew size varies enormously: an independent short film may be shot by a handful of people, while a major studio production can employ hundreds, divided between pre-production, the shooting unit (sometimes with second and additional units), and post-production. In unionized industries, crew roles, working hours, and credits are governed by guild and union agreements.
