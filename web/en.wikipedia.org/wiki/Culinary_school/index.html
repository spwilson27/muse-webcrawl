Culinary school

A culinary school is an institution devoted to education in the art and science of cooking and food preparation. Culinary schools offer certificate, diploma and degree programmes in culinary arts, baking and pastry, and hospitality management, combining classroom instruction in food science, sanitation and costing with extensive hands-on work in teaching kitchens and training restaurants.

History

Formal cookery education grew out of the professionalization of the kitchen in 19th-century France. The Boston Cooking School (1879) was among the first in the United States; Le Cordon Bleu was founded in Paris in 1895; and the Culinary Institute of America, founded in 1946 in New Haven and now based at Hyde Park, New York, became the best-known American school. Apprenticeship remains an alternative path, and in France and Germany culinary training is often delivered through formal vocational systems.

Curriculum

Programmes typically cover knife skills, stocks and sauces, butchery, garde manger, pastry, nutrition, food safety (such as HACCP), menu engineering and kitchen management, followed by an externship in a working restaurant or hotel.

Notable schools

  • Le Cordon Bleu (Paris, 1895)
  • The Culinary Institute of America (1946)
  • Institut Paul Bocuse (Lyon)
  • École Ferrandi (Paris)
