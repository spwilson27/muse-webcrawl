Balkan sprachbund

The Balkan sprachbund or Balkan language area is a group of languages spoken in the Balkans that, although belonging to different branches of Indo-European (and in the case of Turkish, a different family entirely), have developed striking similarities in grammar, syntax, vocabulary and phonology through centuries of multilingual contact. The core members are Albanian, Greek, Romanian, Bulgarian, Macedonian and the Torlakian dialects of Serbian, with Balkan Romani and Balkan Turkish also participating.

Shared features (Balkanisms)

  • Postposed definite article — the article is suffixed to the noun (Romanian omul "the man," Bulgarian čovekǎt, Albanian njeriu).
  • Loss or reduction of the infinitive, replaced by subjunctive clauses ("I want that I go" rather than "I want to go").
  • Future tense formed from a verb meaning "want" plus the subjunctive.
  • Merger of genitive and dative cases.
  • Analytic comparison of adjectives and extensive use of clitic doubling of objects.
  • Evidential verb forms (renarrative mood) in Bulgarian, Macedonian, Albanian and Turkish.
  • A central vowel (ə/ɨ) and shared vocabulary of several hundred common words.

History of study

The convergence was noted as early as the 19th century; Jernej Kopitar suggested in 1829 that Albanian, Romanian and Bulgarian shared features not explained by descent. The concept became foundational to the study of linguistic areas (Sprachbünde), and scholars have debated whether the features radiated from a single source (often proposed to be Greek, or a substrate language such as Thracian or Illyrian) or arose through prolonged mutual contact under Byzantine and Ottoman rule.
