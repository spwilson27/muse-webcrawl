Hamilton (Scots: Hamiltoun; Scottish Gaelic: Baile Hamaltan) is a town in South Lanarkshire, in the central Lowlands of Scotland. It is the administrative headquarters of South Lanarkshire Council and lies about 19 kilometres (12 mi) southeast of Glasgow, on the south bank of the River Clyde at its confluence with the Avon Water.

History

Originally known as Cadzow, the town grew around the seat of the Hamilton family, whose palace in Hamilton Park was once among the grandest houses in Scotland before its demolition in the 1920s due to subsidence from coal mining. The town expanded with the coal, steel and textile industries of the Clyde valley, and later became a commuter town for Glasgow.

Landmarks

Landmarks include Hamilton Town House, which houses a library and theatre; the Hamilton Mausoleum, built for the 10th Duke of Hamilton, noted for its extraordinary echo and once holding the record for the longest echo in a man-made structure; the surviving palace hunting lodge at Chatelherault Country Park; and the ruins of Cadzow Castle above the Avon Gorge. The Low Parks Museum occupies the former palace riding school.

Sport and transport

The town's football club, Hamilton Academical, plays at New Douglas Park. Hamilton has two railway stations, Hamilton Central and Hamilton West, on lines to Glasgow, and lies near the M74 motorway.
