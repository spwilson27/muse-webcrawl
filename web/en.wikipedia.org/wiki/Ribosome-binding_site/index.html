Ribosome-binding site

A ribosome-binding site (RBS) is a sequence of nucleotides upstream of the start codon of an mRNA transcript that is responsible for the recruitment of a ribosome during the initiation of translation. The term chiefly refers to bacterial sites, though analogous mechanisms operate in archaea and, by different means, in eukaryotes.

Prokaryotic ribosome-binding sites

In bacteria the RBS contains the Shine–Dalgarno sequence, a purine-rich stretch (consensus AGGAGG) located about 8 base pairs upstream of the AUG start codon. It base-pairs with a complementary pyrimidine-rich region at the 3′ end of the 16S ribosomal RNA of the small ribosomal subunit, positioning the ribosome correctly. The spacing between the Shine–Dalgarno sequence and the start codon, and the strength of the pairing, strongly influence how efficiently a gene is translated — a fact exploited in synthetic biology, where engineered RBS sequences are used to tune protein expression.

Eukaryotic initiation

Eukaryotic ribosomes generally do not bind directly at an internal site; instead the small subunit, recruited at the 5′ cap, scans along the mRNA until it finds a start codon in a favourable context, the Kozak consensus sequence. Some viral and cellular mRNAs bypass scanning using internal ribosome entry sites (IRES), structured RNA elements that recruit the ribosome internally.
