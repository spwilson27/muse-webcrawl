Matsya

Devanagariमत्स्य
Meaning"Fish"
AffiliationAvatar of Vishnu; Dashavatara

Matsya (Sanskrit: मत्स्य, "fish") is the fish avatar of the Hindu god Vishnu, and the first of the ten principal avatars in the Dashavatara list. Matsya is associated with the rescue of the sage Manu — and, in later versions, of the Vedas themselves — from a great deluge, a flood narrative with parallels in many world mythologies.

Legend

In the earliest version, in the Shatapatha Brahmana, a small fish asks Manu for protection and grows miraculously large; in gratitude it warns him of a coming flood and instructs him to build a boat. When the flood arrives, Manu ties the boat to the fish's horn and is towed to safety on a mountain. In the Mahabharata and Puranic versions, the fish is identified with Vishnu, the demon Hayagriva steals the Vedas, and Matsya recovers them after slaying the demon. In some tellings the fish also carries the seeds of all life and the seven sages (Saptarishi).

Iconography and worship

Matsya is depicted either as a giant fish or as a half-human, half-fish figure — a human torso with a fish's lower body. Temples dedicated to Matsya are rare; the best known is the Shankhodhara temple at Bet Dwarka in Gujarat. The Matsya Purana, one of the eighteen major Puranas, bears his name and is framed as his teaching to Manu.
