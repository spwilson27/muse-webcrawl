Cadillac Motor Car Division, or simply Cadillac, is a division of the American automobile manufacturer General Motors (GM) that designs and builds luxury vehicles. Its major markets are the United States, Canada and China. Cadillac models are distributed in 34 additional markets worldwide.

History

Cadillac was formed from the remnants of the Henry Ford Company in 1902, when Henry Leland was brought in to appraise the plant and instead persuaded the directors to continue in business using his single-cylinder engine. It was named after the French explorer Antoine de la Mothe Cadillac, founder of Detroit. General Motors bought the company in 1909, and within six years Cadillac had laid the foundation of modern mass production of automobiles through its championship of interchangeable parts — winning the Dewar Trophy in 1908 — and it introduced the first production V8 engine in 1914, the electric starter (1912), and the first mass-produced car with a fully enclosed body.

Through the mid-20th century "Cadillac" became a byword for the best of its kind. Post-war models such as the 1959 Eldorado, with their towering tailfins, became icons; later decades saw downsizing, a reputation slump, and competition from European and Japanese luxury marques. In the 21st century GM has repositioned Cadillac with rear-wheel-drive performance cars, the Escalade SUV, and an all-electric flagship strategy led by the Lyriq and the hand-built Celestiq.

Motorsport and culture

Cadillac competes in top-level endurance racing with the V-Series.R in IMSA and the FIA World Endurance Championship, and announced a Formula One entry with Andretti for the 2026 season as a works team project approved by the FIA and Formula One Group. The brand's name is embedded in the language — "the Cadillac of…" — and its cars have carried US presidents, with the presidential state car nicknamed "The Beast."
