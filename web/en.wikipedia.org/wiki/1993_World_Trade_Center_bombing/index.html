The 1993 World Trade Center bombing was a terrorist attack on the World Trade Center in New York City on February 26, 1993. A van bomb packed with roughly 1,300 pounds of urea nitrate exploded in the underground parking garage beneath the North Tower at 12:18 p.m., killing six people and injuring more than a thousand. The plot, led by Ramzi Yousef, intended to collapse one tower into the other and kill tens of thousands; the tower survived, but the attack exposed the complex as a target years before the September 11 attacks.

The attack

The explosion carved a crater about 30 metres wide through several levels of the garage, cut power—including the emergency lighting and public-address systems—and filled the towers with smoke, forcing a slow, hours-long evacuation of tens of thousands of people down darkened stairwells. Six victims, all Port Authority employees, died, one of them a pregnant woman.

Investigation and trials

Investigators quickly recovered a vehicle identification number from the wreckage of the rented Ryder van, leading to Mohammad Salameh, who was arrested when he tried to reclaim the rental deposit. Further arrests followed, including Nidal Ayyad, Mahmud Abouhalima, and Ahmad Ajaj. Four men were convicted in 1994 and sentenced to 240 years each. Ramzi Yousef, the mastermind, fled abroad, plotted the Bojinka airliner scheme with his uncle Khalid Sheikh Mohammed, and was captured in Pakistan in 1995; he is serving a life sentence in federal supermax prison. Eyad Ismoil, who drove the van, was convicted in 1997.

Legacy

The bombing prompted major security upgrades at the World Trade Center, including a hardened command center and improved evacuation systems—measures later overtaken by the towers' destruction on September 11, 2001. A memorial fountain listing the six victims' names was destroyed in 2001; their names are now inscribed at the National September 11 Memorial.
