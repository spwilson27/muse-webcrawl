The Five Elders (Chinese: 少林五祖; pinyin: Shàolín wǔ zǔ), also known as the Five Shaolin Elders, are legendary figures in Chinese martial arts folklore. According to popular tradition, they were the five survivors of the destruction of the Shaolin Temple by forces of the Qing dynasty, and went on to found or inspire several of the major southern Chinese martial arts.

The legend

In the most common version of the story, Qing troops (in some tellings aided by traitors within the temple) burned the Shaolin Monastery — placed in Fujian as a “Southern Shaolin” temple in many accounts — to suppress its monks' support for Ming loyalist resistance. Only five masters escaped. The five are usually named as:

  • Jee Sin Sim See (至善禅師), the abbot, associated with the transmission of skills that later fed into Hung Gar and Choy Li Fut traditions;
  • Ng Mui (五媒), a nun, credited in Wing Chun legend as the creator of that art and teacher of Yim Wing-chun;
  • Bak Mei (白眉, “White Eyebrow”), the Taoist priest Pai Mei, founder of the Bak Mei style — and in many versions the traitor whose knowledge helped the Qing destroy the temple;
  • Fung Dou Dak (震道德), associated with the Bak Fu Pai (White Tiger) tradition;
  • Miu Hin (苗風), a lay master and father of Miu Tsui Fa, linking the lineage toward the Five Family styles and Choy Gar.

Historical status

Historians treat the Five Elders as legend rather than documented history. The stories circulated widely in late Qing and Republican-era martial arts novels, secret-society (Tiandihui) mythology, and oral lineages of southern kung fu, where claiming descent from a Shaolin survivor conferred prestige and legitimacy. No contemporary Qing records corroborate the burning of a southern Shaolin temple or the five figures as described, and individual elders — particularly Bak Mei and Ng Mui — are portrayed inconsistently across traditions, sometimes as heroes and sometimes as villains.

In popular culture

The elders appear throughout wuxia fiction and Hong Kong cinema. Bak Mei (Pai Mei) became an iconic villain in films such as Executioners from Shaolin (1977) and, in a homage by Quentin Tarantino, Kill Bill: Volume 2 (2004). The Five Elders narrative also underlies the origin stories taught in Wing Chun, Bak Mei, and several Fujian White Crane lineages.
