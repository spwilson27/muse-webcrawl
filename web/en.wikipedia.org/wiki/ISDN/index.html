ISDN

Integrated Services Digital Network (ISDN) is a set of communication standards for simultaneous digital transmission of voice, video, data, and other network services over the digitalised circuits of the public switched telephone network. ISDN was designed in the 1980s and standardised by the CCITT (now ITU-T) in 1988; it was intended to replace analogue telephone service with an all-digital network reaching the subscriber.

Interfaces

The Basic Rate Interface (BRI) provides two 64 kbit/s bearer (B) channels and one 16 kbit/s signalling (D) channel (2B+D), typically delivered over a single copper pair, allowing simultaneous voice calls and data. The Primary Rate Interface (PRI) provides 23 B channels plus one 64 kbit/s D channel on a T1 line in North America and Japan, or 30 B channels plus a D channel on an E1 line in Europe and Australia.

Services and uses

ISDN supported digital voice telephony with fast call setup, circuit-switched data at 64 or 128 kbit/s, videoconferencing (notably with the H.320 standard), and was widely used for Internet dial-up access and backup links in the 1990s. It was particularly popular in Germany and other European countries, and in Japan under NTT's INS-Net service.

Decline

Broadband technologies such as DSL and cable modems, which offered far higher speeds over the same copper plant, overtook ISDN from the late 1990s. Many carriers have since retired or scheduled the retirement of ISDN as part of the migration to all-IP networks, though it lingered in niches such as broadcast audio contribution and backup connections.
