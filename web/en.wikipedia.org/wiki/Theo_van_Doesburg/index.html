Overview

Theo van Doesburg (born Christian Emil Marie Küpper; 30 August 1883 - 7 March 1931) was a Dutch artist, architect, poet, and art theorist, best known as the founder and driving force of De Stijl ('The Style'), the Dutch movement devoted to pure abstraction through straight lines, right angles, and primary colours. In 1917 he founded the journal De Stijl, which brought together artists including Piet Mondrian, Vilmos Huszár, and Bart van der Leck.

Career and theory

Van Doesburg tirelessly promoted De Stijl across Europe, lecturing, exhibiting, and collaborating with figures of the Bauhaus and Dada — he also published Dada poetry under the pseudonym I. K. Bonset. From the mid-1920s he broke with Mondrian by introducing the diagonal into his compositions, a doctrine he called Elementarism, which Mondrian rejected.

His major late project was the interior design of the Café Aubette in Strasbourg (1926-1928, with Sophie Taeuber-Arp and Jean Arp), a landmark of abstract environmental design. In 1930 he issued the manifesto of Concrete art. He died of a heart attack in Davos in 1931, having shaped the course of abstract art and modern architecture across Europe.
