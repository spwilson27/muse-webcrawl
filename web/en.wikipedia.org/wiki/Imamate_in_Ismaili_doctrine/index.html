Imamate in Ismaili doctrine

The doctrine of the imamate in Ismaili Islam holds that the imam is the divinely appointed, infallible spiritual leader of the Muslim community, designated by explicit appointment (nass) from the preceding imam. Ismailis, a branch of Shia Islam, believe the line of imams descends from Ali ibn Abi Talib and Fatimah through Ismail ibn Jafar, the son of Imam Jafar al-Sadiq.

Concept

In Ismaili theology, the imam is the inheritor of the Prophet Muhammad's esoteric knowledge and the sole authoritative interpreter (ta'wil) of the Qur'an. The imam is considered to be in possession of a divine light (nur) passed down through the lineage, and his guidance is essential for the spiritual salvation of believers.

Branches

Historical splits within Ismailism, often over succession to the imamate, produced branches including the Nizari Ismailis — whose present imam is the Aga Khan — the Musta'li (Bohra) communities, the Druze, and the now-extinct Qarmatians. The Fatimid Caliphate (909–1171) was ruled by Ismaili imam-caliphs.

Periods of concealment

Ismaili history is conventionally divided into periods of concealment (satr), when the imams lived hidden from public view, and periods of manifestation, such as the Fatimid era and the Alamut period of the Nizari imams in Persia.
