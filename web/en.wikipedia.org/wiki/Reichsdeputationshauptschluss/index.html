Reichsdeputationshauptschluss

The Reichsdeputationshauptschluss (formally the Hauptschluss der außerordentlichen Reichsdeputation, "Principal Conclusion of the Extraordinary Imperial Deputation"), ratified on 27 April 1803, was the last significant law enacted by the Holy Roman Empire. It authorised a sweeping redistribution of territory within the Empire to compensate German princes for lands west of the Rhine annexed by revolutionary France under the Treaty of Lunéville.

Effects

The settlement secularised nearly all ecclesiastical states, transferring bishoprics and abbeys to secular rulers, and mediatised most of the free imperial cities, of which only six (including Hamburg, Bremen, Lübeck and Frankfurt) survived. More than a hundred small states disappeared, reshaping the map of Germany and greatly strengthening larger states such as Prussia, Bavaria, Baden and Württemberg. It paved the way for the dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire in 1806.
