Heisler locomotive

The Heisler locomotive is one of the three major types of geared steam locomotive, the others being the Shay and Climax types. Designed by Charles L. Heisler, the locomotive was produced by the Stearns Manufacturing Company of Erie, Pennsylvania, beginning in 1891.

Design

The Heisler's distinguishing feature is a V-twin two-cylinder steam engine mounted centrally under the boiler, driving a longitudinal drive shaft along the center line of the locomotive. The drive shaft is connected by bevel gears to the axles of the powered trucks (bogies), so all wheels are driven. Because the pistons drive a crankshaft, the gearing ratio gives the locomotive high tractive effort at low speed, making it well suited to steep grades, sharp curves, and the rough temporary track found in logging operations.

Use

Heislers were used extensively in the logging industry of North America, as well as in mining and other industrial service where standard rod-driven locomotives could not operate. Although generally slower than the Shay, the Heisler was the fastest of the geared types because of its two-cylinder engine arrangement. Production continued until 1941; a number of examples survive in preservation.
