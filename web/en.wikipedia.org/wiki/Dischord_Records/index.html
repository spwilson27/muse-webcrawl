Dischord Records is an independent record label based in Washington, D.C., specializing in the independent and punk music of the D.C. area. It was founded in 1980 by Ian MacKaye and Jeff Nelson, members of the Teen Idles, initially to release their own band's record, the Minor Disturbance EP.

History

Dischord became the central label of the Washington, D.C. hardcore punk scene, releasing records by Minor Threat, Government Issue, Faith/Void, Scream, and Marginal Man. In the mid-1980s the label documented the "Revolution Summer" era with bands such as Rites of Spring, Embrace, and Dag Nasty, helping to define post-hardcore and early emo. From 1987 onward MacKaye's band Fugazi became the label's best-known act, famous for its low ticket prices and independent ethics.

Ethos

Dischord is noted for its strictly do-it-yourself approach: it is owned and run by its founders, keeps prices low, does not sign contracts with its bands (relying on handshake agreements), and splits profits evenly with artists. The label has never relied on major-label distribution and remains a model for independent labels worldwide.
