Strike (baseball)

In baseball, the strike zone is the volume of space through which a pitch must pass in order to count as a strike, if the batter does not swing. A strike is a legal pitch that the batter swings at and misses, that the batter does not offer at but which passes through the strike zone, that the batter hits into foul territory (with exceptions), or that the batter bunts into foul territory.

The strike zone

Under the official rules of Major League Baseball, the strike zone is the area over home plate whose upper limit is a horizontal line at the midpoint between the top of the batter's shoulders and the top of the uniform pants, and whose lower limit is a line at the hollow beneath the kneecaps, determined from the batter's stance as the batter is prepared to swing.

Types of strikes

  • Swinging strike — the batter swings and misses.
  • Called strike — the pitch passes through the zone without a swing, as judged by the home plate umpire.
  • Foul ball — counts as a strike unless the batter already has two strikes; a foul bunt with two strikes is a strikeout.
  • Foul tip — a ball that goes sharply and directly to the catcher's glove and is legally caught counts as a strike at any count.

Consequences

Three strikes retire the batter in a strikeout. An exception is the uncaught third strike rule: if a third strike is not caught cleanly by the catcher (and first base is unoccupied, or there are two outs), the batter may attempt to run to first base.
