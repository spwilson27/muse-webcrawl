Benjamin Jowett

Born15 April 1817, Camberwell, London, England
Died1 October 1893, Headley Park, Hampshire, England
OccupationClassical scholar, translator, educator
Known forTranslations of Plato; Master of Balliol College, Oxford

Benjamin Jowett (15 April 1817 – 1 October 1893) was an English classical scholar, tutor and reforming Master of Balliol College, Oxford, and Regius Professor of Greek. He is best known for his translations of Plato and Thucydides, which remained standard English versions for generations, and for transforming Balliol into one of Oxford's leading colleges.

Career at Oxford

Jowett won a scholarship to Balliol in 1836 and became a fellow in 1838. As a tutor he pioneered an intensive, personal style of teaching that made Balliol famous. His contribution to Essays and Reviews (1860), a volume of liberal theology, brought him charges of heresy and a prosecution in the vice-chancellor's court, which failed. He became Regius Professor of Greek in 1855 and, after years of obstruction by opponents, Master of Balliol in 1870.

Translations and influence

His Plato translation (1871, with extensive introductions) presented Plato as a living thinker to the Victorian reading public; his Thucydides followed in 1881. As Master he widened Balliol's intake, championed university reform, and taught a generation of statesmen and administrators of the British Empire, earning the sobriquet "the great tutor". He was vice-chancellor of Oxford from 1882 to 1886. The students' rhyme about him ("My name is Benjamin Jowett...") is among the most famous pieces of Oxford doggerel.
