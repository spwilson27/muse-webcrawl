Placoid scale

Placoid scales, also called dermal denticles, are the tooth-like scales covering the skin of sharks, rays, and other cartilaginous fishes (chondrichthyans). They give shark skin its characteristic rough, sandpaper-like texture.

Structure

Each placoid scale has essentially the same structure as a tooth: a base embedded in the dermis, a core of dentine surrounding a pulp cavity, and an outer cap of enamel-like vitrodentine. Unlike the scales of bony fish, placoid scales do not grow as the fish grows; instead, new scales are added between the old ones.

Function

The ridged, backward-pointing denticles reduce drag and turbulence as water flows over the body, improving swimming efficiency — an effect that has inspired biomimetic swimsuit and hull-coating designs. They also protect the skin against abrasion and parasites. Shark skin covered in denticles was historically used as a sandpaper-like material called shagreen.
