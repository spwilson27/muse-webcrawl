Chowder

Chowder
TypeSoup / stew
Place of originFrance and coastal North America
Main ingredientsSeafood or vegetables, potatoes, onions, stock or milk/cream
VariationsNew England, Manhattan, Rhode Island clam chowder; corn chowder

Chowder is a thick soup or stew, traditionally made with seafood and vegetables in a base of stock, milk, or cream, and typically thickened with potatoes, crackers, or a roux. The name is generally traced to the French chaudière, a cauldron used by fishermen in Brittany, whose fish stews influenced the chowders of French and British settlers in North America.

History

Fish chowders were common in the coastal villages of western France and Cornwall, where returning fishermen contributed part of the catch to a communal pot. The dish took root in New England and the Canadian Maritimes in the 18th century; the earliest printed American recipes date to the mid-1700s, when chowder was often layered with ship's biscuit. By the 19th century clam chowder had become a signature dish of New England, served at celebrations such as the Fourth of July.

Varieties

  • New England clam chowder — the best-known style: milk- or cream-based, with clams, potatoes, onions, and salt pork, traditionally served with oyster crackers.
  • Manhattan clam chowder — tomato-based and red, a New York style that famously provoked a Maine legislator in 1939 to propose (unsuccessfully) a law against putting tomatoes in chowder.
  • Rhode Island clam chowder — a clear-broth style with quahogs, potatoes, and onions, without dairy or tomato.
  • Corn chowder — a vegetarian or bacon-laced chowder built on sweet corn, common across the United States.
  • Seafood and fish chowders — mixed fish, shrimp, or smoked haddock (as in Scottish cullen skink).
  • Bermuda fish chowder — spiced with sherry peppers and black rum.

Preparation

A typical chowder begins by rendering salt pork or bacon, sweating onions and celery in the fat, then adding potatoes and stock (often clam juice), simmering until tender, and finishing with the seafood and dairy. Chowders are usually kept just below a hard boil once cream is added so the base does not break.
