Cyclonic separation


Airflow in a cyclone separator (placeholder image)

Cyclonic separation is a method of removing particulates from an air, gas or liquid stream without the use of filters, through vortex separation. Rotational effects and gravity are used to separate mixtures of solids and fluids. A high-speed rotating (air)flow is established within a cylindrical or conical container called a cyclone.

Operation

The stream enters tangentially and spirals downward; centrifugal force throws denser particles to the wall, where they slide to a collection hopper, while the cleaned gas reverses into an inner vortex and exits the top. Cyclones are cheap, rugged and have no moving parts, so they are widely used for dust collection in sawmills, cement and grain handling, as pre-cleaners before filters, and (as hydrocyclones) for liquid–liquid and liquid–solid separation in mineral processing and oil production. Their efficiency falls for very fine particles, and domestic "bagless" vacuum cleaners such as Dyson's apply the same principle at small scale.
