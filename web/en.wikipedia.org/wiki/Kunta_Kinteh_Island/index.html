Kunta Kinteh Island, formerly known as James Island, is an island in the Gambia River, 30 km (19 mi) from the river mouth and near Juffureh in the Republic of The Gambia. Fort James is located on the island. It lies less than 200 metres from the mainland village of Albreda.

History

The first European settlers on the island were Baltic German merchants from the Duchy of Courland and Semigallia, who established a presence in 1651. The island was subsequently held at various times by the Dutch and the English; the English built Fort James in 1661, naming the island after James, Duke of York. Control of the island and its fort changed hands repeatedly between Britain and France during their colonial wars, and the fort was rebuilt several times.

The island was an important entrepôt in the West African slave trade. Enslaved people were held there before being transported across the Atlantic. The site gained widespread recognition through Alex Haley's 1976 novel Roots: The Saga of an American Family, whose protagonist Kunta Kinte was taken from nearby Juffureh. In 2011 the island was renamed from James Island to Kunta Kinteh Island.

Heritage status

In 2003 the island, together with related sites including Albreda, Juffureh and Fort Bullen, was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List. The island suffers from severe erosion: it is now roughly one-sixth of its size during the period when the fort was active, and several ruins stand close to the water's edge.
