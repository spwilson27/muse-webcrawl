Video game console

A video game console is a computer system that outputs a video signal or image to display a video game that one or more people can play through a game controller. Consoles are distinguished from personal computers by being dedicated primarily to gaming and by using standardised hardware within each generation.

History

The first home console was the Magnavox Odyssey (1972). The Atari 2600 (1977) popularised interchangeable cartridges. After the North American video game crash of 1983, Nintendo revived the market with the Nintendo Entertainment System (1985). Subsequent generations brought Sega, Sony's PlayStation (1994), Microsoft's Xbox (2001), and motion and hybrid systems such as the Wii (2006) and Nintendo Switch (2017). The ninth generation, led by PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S (2020), emphasised solid-state storage and ray tracing.

Types

Home consoles connect to a television; handheld consoles integrate screen and controls; hybrid systems such as the Switch function as both. Microconsoles and retro mini-consoles form smaller niches.

Industry

Consoles are typically sold near or below cost, with profit from game licensing, subscriptions and accessories — the "razor and blades" model.
