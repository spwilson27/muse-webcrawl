Hydrogen fluoride

Hydrogen fluoride (fluorane) is a chemical compound with the formula HF. This colourless gas or liquid is the principal industrial source of fluorine. Its aqueous solution, hydrofluoric acid, is a weak acid but is exceptionally corrosive and toxic, famously able to dissolve glass and many oxides.

Properties

Hydrogen fluoride boils at 19.5 °C, just below room temperature, and is strongly hydrogen-bonded, forming zigzag chains in the liquid and cyclic oligomers in the vapour. Commercial production is by treating the mineral fluorspar (calcium fluoride) with concentrated sulfuric acid in rotary kilns.

Uses

Most HF is converted to fluorocarbons (including PTFE and refrigerants), aluminium fluoride and synthetic cryolite for aluminium smelting, and uranium hexafluoride in nuclear fuel processing. It is also a catalyst in petroleum alkylation units and, as hydrofluoric acid, is used for etching glass, pickling stainless steel and cleaning silicon wafers.

Safety

HF is uniquely dangerous: contact may initially be painless while fluoride ions penetrate tissue, bind calcium and magnesium, and can cause deep burns, bone damage and life-threatening hypocalcaemia. Calcium gluconate gel is the standard first-aid treatment. Accidental releases, such as industrial leaks, have caused mass-casualty incidents, making HF handling heavily regulated.
