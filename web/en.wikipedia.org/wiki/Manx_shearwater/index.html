Manx shearwater

Manx shearwater
Scientific name: Puffinus puffinus
Family: Procellariidae
Wingspan: 76–89 cm
Conservation status: Least Concern

The Manx shearwater (Puffinus puffinus) is a medium-sized shearwater in the seabird family Procellariidae. It is black above and white below, flying with stiff wings low over the waves in the gliding-and-flapping flight typical of shearwaters. The species' name reflects its former abundance around the Isle of Man, though its scientific name is the source of confusion with the Atlantic puffin (Fratercula arctica), which was originally named Puffinus after these birds.

Breeding and migration

Manx shearwaters breed in burrows and rock crevices on islands around Britain and Ireland — notably Skomer, Skokholm and Rum, which hold a large share of the world population — as well as in Iceland, the Faroes and elsewhere in the North Atlantic. They visit colonies only at night, giving eerie calling choruses. A single egg is laid; the chick is deserted when fat and fledges alone.

The species undertakes one of the longest migrations of any bird, wintering off the coasts of Brazil and Argentina, a round trip of some 14,000 km. Individuals are long-lived — a bird ringed on Copeland Island was recaptured over 50 years later, among the oldest known wild birds.

Diet

They feed on small fish such as herring and sprats, squid and crustaceans, caught by pursuit-diving or from the surface.
