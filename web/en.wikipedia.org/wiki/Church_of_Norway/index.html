Church of Norway

Church of Norway
Norwegian: Den norske kirke
Classification: Protestant (Lutheran)
Presiding Bishop: the Bishop of Nidaros
Headquarters: Oslo, Norway
Members: roughly two-thirds of the population

The Church of Norway (Norwegian: Den norske kirke) is an Evangelical Lutheran denomination of Christianity that is the largest church in Norway and was historically the country's established state church. Christianity was introduced to Norway around the year 1000, notably under King Olaf II (Saint Olaf), and the Lutheran Reformation was imposed in 1537 under Danish rule.

Structure

The church is divided into 11 dioceses, each led by a bishop, and some 1,200 parishes. The Bishop of Nidaros, seated at Nidaros Cathedral in Trondheim where Saint Olaf is buried, serves as presiding bishop (Preses) of the Bishops' Conference. The General Synod (Kirkemøtet) is the church's supreme governing body.

Separation from the state

Constitutional amendments in 2012 ended most remaining elements of the state-church system: clergy ceased to be civil servants appointed by the king, and the Church of Norway became a separate legal entity from 1 January 2017, while remaining described in the constitution as "Norway's national church" supported by the state. Baptised members still make up a majority of the population, though regular attendance is low.

Worship and life

The church conducts baptisms, confirmations, weddings, and funerals for a large share of Norwegians, and maintains historic buildings including stave churches and cathedrals. It is a member of the Lutheran World Federation and the Porvoo Communion, which links it with Anglican churches.
