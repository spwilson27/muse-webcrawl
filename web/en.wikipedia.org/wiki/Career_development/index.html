Career development

[Image placeholder: Diagram of career stages from exploration to establishment and maintenance]

Career development is the process of managing life, learning and work over the lifespan, involving the ongoing acquisition of skills, experience and qualifications, and the planning of transitions between education, employment, roles and organisations. It is studied within organisational behaviour, human resource management and vocational psychology.

Theories

Donald Super's life-span, life-space theory describes career development as unfolding through stages — growth, exploration, establishment, maintenance and disengagement — and emphasises the development and implementation of a self-concept. John Holland's theory of vocational personalities matches people to work environments of six types (Realistic, Investigative, Artistic, Social, Enterprising, Conventional). More recent approaches, such as the protean and boundaryless career concepts, stress self-direction and mobility across organisations.

Organisational practice

Employers support career development through training, mentoring, coaching, succession planning, internal mobility and performance appraisal. Individual development plans and career counselling services are common tools. Critics note that responsibility for development has shifted from organisations to individuals as stable long-term employment has declined.
