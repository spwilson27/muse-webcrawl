The Universal Negro Improvement Association and African Communities League (UNIA-ACL) is a Black nationalist and Pan-African organization founded in 1914 by Marcus Garvey in Kingston, Jamaica. It advocated the unification and uplift of people of African descent worldwide and promoted the "Back to Africa" movement.

Growth in the United States

After Garvey moved to Harlem in 1916, the UNIA expanded rapidly, claiming millions of members in hundreds of divisions across the United States, the Caribbean, Africa, and elsewhere. Its newspaper, Negro World, circulated internationally, and its 1920 international convention at Madison Square Garden adopted the Declaration of the Rights of the Negro Peoples of the World and elected Garvey Provisional President of Africa.

Enterprises

The UNIA founded businesses including the Black Star Line shipping company and the Negro Factories Corporation, intended to build Black economic independence. Financial difficulties and Garvey's 1923 mail fraud conviction and subsequent deportation in 1927 weakened the organization, though branches of the UNIA survive to the present day.

Legacy

The UNIA's red, black, and green flag became an enduring symbol of Pan-Africanism, and the movement influenced later civil rights, Black Power, and African independence movements.
