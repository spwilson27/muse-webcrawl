1970s energy crisis

Period1973–1974 and 1979
Main causesOPEC embargo; Iranian Revolution
EffectsHigh inflation, fuel shortages, rationing
Policy responsesEfficiency standards, strategic reserves, speed limits

The 1970s energy crisis was a period of fuel shortages, sharp price rises and economic disruption in many industrial countries, centred on the oil shocks of 1973–1974 and 1979. It exposed the dependence of the post-war economy on cheap imported petroleum and reshaped energy policy, automobile design and geopolitics.

1973 oil embargo

After the Yom Kippur War began in October 1973, Arab members of OPEC embargoed oil exports to countries supporting Israel and production cuts drove prices upward. In the United States and parts of Europe, drivers queued for petrol, governments imposed odd-even rationing or speed limits, and inflation accelerated while growth stalled, producing “stagflation.”

1979 shock and aftermath

The Iranian Revolution disrupted oil output and triggered panic buying, causing a second price spike. Consumers shifted toward smaller cars, insulation and alternative fuels; governments created strategic petroleum reserves and efficiency rules. Although prices fell in the 1980s, the crisis left lasting effects on energy security thinking and on the political power of oil-exporting states.
