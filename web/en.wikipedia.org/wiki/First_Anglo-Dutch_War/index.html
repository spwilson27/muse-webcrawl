First Anglo-Dutch War

The First Anglo-Dutch War (1652–1654) was fought between the Commonwealth of England and the Dutch Republic, primarily at sea, over trade rivalry and England's Navigation Act of 1651, which restricted Dutch carrying trade. It was the first of four Anglo-Dutch wars.

Date1652–1654
LocationEnglish Channel, North Sea, Mediterranean
ResultTreaty of Westminster (1654)
CommandersRobert Blake, George Monck; Maarten Tromp, Michiel de Ruyter

Causes

Both nations competed for global commerce; the Navigation Act required goods imported to England to be carried in English ships or ships of the producing country, striking at Dutch entrepôt trade. An encounter off Dover in May 1652 between Robert Blake and Maarten Tromp escalated into war.

Course

Major battles included Kentish Knock, Dungeness (a Dutch victory), Portland, the Gabbard and Scheveningen (1653), where Tromp was killed. England's larger purpose-built warships and line-ahead tactics gradually prevailed, blockading the Dutch coast and crippling trade, while Dutch forces fought in the Mediterranean at Elba and Leghorn.

Peace

The Treaty of Westminster (1654) largely restored the status quo but upheld the Navigation Act; a secret annex, the Act of Seclusion, barred the young William III of Orange from the stadtholderate — a demand of Oliver Cromwell.
