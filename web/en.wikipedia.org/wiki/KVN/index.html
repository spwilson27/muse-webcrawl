KVN
Russian: КВН (Клуб весёлых и находчивых)
First broadcast: 1961
Genre: Humour competition
Long-time host: Alexander Maslyakov

KVN (Russian: КВН, an abbreviation of Klub vesyolykh i nakhodchivykh, "Club of the Funny and Inventive") is a Russian and formerly Soviet comedy competition in which teams of performers — traditionally students — compete in humorous skits, improvisation and musical numbers before a jury.

History

KVN first aired on Soviet television in 1961, was suspended in 1972, and was revived in 1986 during perestroika. Under its long-serving host Alexander Maslyakov it grew into a vast league system spanning schools, universities and cities across Russia and the former Soviet republics, as well as émigré communities abroad.

Format

Teams perform prepared and improvised rounds — greetings, warm-ups, musical contests and homework sketches — scored by a panel of judges. The Major League, televised from Moscow, is the pinnacle of a pyramid of regional and student leagues.

Cultural impact

KVN has been a training ground for generations of Russian-language comedians, writers and television personalities, and its style of team sketch comedy remains a fixture of popular culture in the Russian-speaking world.
