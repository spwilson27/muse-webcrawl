Gorkhaland movement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Darjeeling hills, the heartland of the proposed state of Gorkhaland.

The Gorkhaland movement is a campaign to create a separate state of Gorkhaland within India, in the Gorkha-populated hill districts of Darjeeling, Kalimpong, and parts of the Dooars and Siliguri in northern West Bengal. Its supporters argue that the Indian Gorkhas — Nepali-speaking Indians — have a distinct ethnic, linguistic, and cultural identity that is not adequately represented within West Bengal.

History

Demands for separate administrative arrangements for the Darjeeling hills date to the early twentieth century. The demand for a full state was first raised in the 1980s by Subhash Ghisingh and the Gorkha National Liberation Front (GNLF), whose agitation of 1986–1988 turned violent and cost more than a thousand lives. The movement subsided with the creation of the Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council in 1988.

The demand revived in 2007 under Bimal Gurung's Gorkha Janmukti Morcha (GJM) after Ghisingh's support for Sixth Schedule status alienated many supporters. Following negotiations, a tripartite agreement in 2011 created the Gorkhaland Territorial Administration (GTA), a semi-autonomous body. Dissatisfaction with the GTA's limited powers led to renewed agitation, including a 104-day general strike and shutdown in 2017 that paralysed the hills and their tea and tourism industries.

Arguments

Supporters cite a sense of alienation from Kolkata, underdevelopment of the hills, and the desire of Indian Gorkhas to distinguish their identity from citizens of Nepal. Opponents, including successive West Bengal governments, argue against further division of the state and point to the autonomy granted through the GTA.
