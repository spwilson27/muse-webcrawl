Badme (Tigrinya: ባድመ, Arabic: بادم) is a town in the Gash-Barka Region of Eritrea, on the border with Ethiopia. It became internationally known as the flashpoint of the Eritrean–Ethiopian War of 1998–2000.

Border dispute

The status of Badme and surrounding border areas was contested between Eritrea and Ethiopia, whose colonial-era treaties left the boundary ambiguous. Fighting that began around Badme in May 1998 escalated into a full-scale war that killed an estimated 70,000–100,000 people. The Algiers Agreement of December 2000 ended the war and created a boundary commission, which in 2002 awarded Badme to Eritrea. Ethiopia long declined to withdraw from the area, leaving the border in a state of "no war, no peace" until the 2018 Eritrea–Ethiopia summit and peace agreement, after which Ethiopia accepted the commission's ruling.

Geography

Badme lies on an arid plain near the Mareb river basin, in a region farmed by Irob and Kunama communities and administered before the war as part of Ethiopia's Tigray Region.
