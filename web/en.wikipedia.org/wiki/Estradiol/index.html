Estradiol

ClassEstrogen; steroid hormone
FormulaC18H24O2
SourcesOvaries (granulosa cells); also placenta, adipose tissue, testes

Estradiol (E2), also spelled oestradiol, is the principal and most potent estrogen steroid hormone in humans. It is produced mainly by the ovarian follicles in women during the reproductive years, and in smaller amounts by the testes in men and by peripheral aromatization of androgens in adipose tissue in both sexes. Estradiol regulates the menstrual cycle, the development of female secondary sexual characteristics, and the maintenance of reproductive tissues, bone, and cardiovascular and brain function.

Biological role

Estradiol acts mainly through nuclear estrogen receptors ERα and ERβ, which function as ligand-activated transcription factors, and through membrane receptors such as GPER mediating rapid signalling. Its levels fluctuate across the menstrual cycle, rising during the follicular phase to trigger the luteinizing hormone surge and ovulation, and fall sharply after menopause. During pregnancy the placenta produces large quantities of estrogens, with estriol predominating.

Medical use

Pharmaceutical estradiol is used in menopausal hormone therapy (as oral tablets, transdermal patches and gels, and vaginal preparations), in combined hormonal contraceptives (often as the synthetic analog ethinylestradiol), in treatment of hypoestrogenism, and as part of feminizing hormone therapy. Esterified forms such as estradiol valerate and estradiol cypionate are used as long-acting injections. Risks associated with estrogen therapy include venous thromboembolism, stroke, and hormone-sensitive cancers, varying with dose, route, and progestogen co-administration.

Chemistry

Estradiol is an estrane steroid with hydroxyl groups at the 3 and 17β positions. It is synthesized from testosterone by the enzyme aromatase, and interconverts with the weaker estrogen estrone.
