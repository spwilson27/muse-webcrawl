Tomb of Shah Rukn-e-Alam

The Tomb of Shah Rukn-e-Alam is a mausoleum in Multan, Punjab, Pakistan, built between 1320 and 1324 for the Sufi saint Sheikh Rukn-ud-Din Abul Fateh (Shah Rukn-e-Alam). It is considered one of the masterpieces of pre-Mughal funerary architecture in South Asia.

Architecture

The tomb stands within the Multan Fort on a raised platform. The structure is an octagon about 15.8 metres across internally, built of red brick with massive sloping (battered) walls reminiscent of Tughluq military architecture, and crowned by a large hemispherical dome about 17.7 metres in diameter. Bands of blue, azure, and white glazed tile, carved brickwork, and wooden canopies richly decorate the exterior; second-storey corner turrets and merlons complete the silhouette. Originally built by Ghiyath al-Din Tughluq, likely as his own tomb while governor of the region, it was given to the saint's family by Muhammad bin Tughluq.

Significance

The shrine remains a major pilgrimage centre; the saint's annual urs draws over 100,000 devotees. Hundreds of family members and followers are buried around the mausoleum. The tomb won an Aga Khan Award for Architecture after conservation work, and is on Pakistan's tentative UNESCO World Heritage list.
