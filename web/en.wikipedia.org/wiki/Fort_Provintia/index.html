Fort Provintia (Chinese: 赤崁樓; historically also written Fort Providentia) was a Dutch outpost established in 1653 in what is now Tainan, Taiwan, during the period of Dutch colonisation of the island. Built on the site of the aboriginal village of Sakam, it stood across the bay from the Dutch headquarters at Fort Zeelandia and served as an administrative and trading centre for the surrounding plain.

History

The fort was constructed after a major Han Chinese uprising against Dutch rule in 1652 (the Guo Huaiyi rebellion), which exposed the need for a defensive strongpoint amid the Chinese farming settlements around Sakam. It was a modest square brick fort with a residence, warehouse and church. In 1661–62 the Ming loyalist commander Koxinga (Zheng Chenggong) besieged Dutch Taiwan; Fort Provintia, commanded by Valentijn, surrendered in April 1661 after its water supply was cut, while Fort Zeelandia held out until February 1662, ending Dutch rule.

Later history

Under Qing rule the site decayed through earthquakes and storms; in the 19th century Chinese temples and pavilions were built over the Dutch foundations, creating the present-day Chihkan Tower (Chihkan Lou), one of Tainan's best-known landmarks. The surviving Dutch-era foundations and walls are incorporated into the historic site, which displays artefacts including the nine imperial stone tortoise stelae of the Qianlong era.
