Cowl (chimney)

A chimney cowl is a hood-shaped covering fitted to the top of a chimney, usually made of galvanised iron, copper, stainless steel or terracotta. Its purpose is to improve the draught of the chimney and prevent downdraughts caused by wind.

Function

Cowls prevent wind from blowing smoke back down the flue into the room below, reduce rain entry, and discourage birds from nesting in the chimney. Some designs rotate or use the Venturi effect to increase updraught.

Types

Common types include the H-pot, the lobster-back cowl, rotating cowls, and anti-downdraught cowls with louvred or conical tops. Oast houses in Kent traditionally used large rotating cowls to draw air through the kiln for drying hops.
