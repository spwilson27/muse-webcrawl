The Georgia Institute of Technology, commonly referred to as Georgia Tech, is a public research university and institute of technology in Atlanta, Georgia. Founded in 1885 as the Georgia School of Technology during Reconstruction, it has grown into one of the leading research universities in the United States, particularly known for its engineering, computing, science, business, design and liberal arts colleges.

Academics

Georgia Tech enrols more than 45,000 students across its Atlanta campus, international campuses in France (Georgia Tech Europe, Metz) and China (Shenzhen), and a large online master's programme in computer science. Its College of Engineering is among the largest in the United States, and its computing programme is consistently ranked among the nation's best. The institute operates the Georgia Tech Research Institute, an applied research arm founded in 1934.

Campus and student life

The 400-acre campus in Midtown Atlanta hosted the Olympic Village during the 1996 Summer Olympics. Traditions include the Ramblin' Wreck, a 1930 Ford Model A sport coupe that serves as an official mascot, and the fight song "Ramblin' Wreck from Georgia Tech." Georgia Tech's athletic teams, the Yellow Jackets, compete in the Atlantic Coast Conference.

Notable alumni

Alumni include President Jimmy Carter (who attended briefly), Nobel laureate Kary Mullis, architect John Portman, and numerous astronauts and CEOs.
