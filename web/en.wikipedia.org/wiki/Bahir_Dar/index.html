Bahir Dar (Amharic: ባሕር ዳር, "sea shore") is the capital city of the Amhara Region of Ethiopia, situated on the southern shore of Lake Tana, the source of the Blue Nile. It is one of Ethiopia's leading tourist destinations, known for its palm-lined avenues, lakeside setting, the island monasteries of Lake Tana, and the nearby Blue Nile Falls (Tis Issat, "smoking water").

Geography

The city lies where the Blue Nile flows out of Lake Tana at an elevation of about 1,800 metres. Its climate is tropical highland, with a rainy season from June to September. The Zege Peninsula and some 37 islands in the lake hold medieval churches and monasteries, several founded in the 14th–17th centuries, preserving illuminated manuscripts and wall paintings.

History

A Jesuit presence existed near the site in the 17th century, and a settlement developed around the lake port. Bahir Dar grew substantially in the 20th century: it was briefly occupied by Italian forces during the Second Italo-Ethiopian War, was developed as an administrative and military centre, and after 1991 became the capital of the Amhara Region. Bahir Dar University, founded in 2001 from earlier polytechnic and education colleges, is one of Ethiopia's largest universities.

Economy and tourism

Tourism, fishing, agriculture and light industry underpin the economy. Visitors take boats to the lake's island monasteries — including Ura Kidane Mehret on the Zege Peninsula — and to the Blue Nile Falls some 30 km south-east of the city, where the river drops about 45 metres. Bahir Dar International Airport connects the city with Addis Ababa and other destinations.
