Nanophotonics

Nanophotonics or nano-optics is the study of the behaviour of light on the nanometre scale, and of the interaction of nanometre-scale objects with light. It is a branch of optics, optical engineering, electrical engineering, and nanotechnology. It often involves dielectric structures such as nanoantennas, or metallic components, which can transport and focus light via surface plasmon polaritons.

Overview

Normal optical components, like lenses and microscopes, cannot normally focus light to nanometre scales, because of the diffraction limit (Rayleigh criterion), which prevents focusing light to spots much smaller than about half its wavelength. Nanophotonics seeks to circumvent this limit using near-field effects, plasmonic resonances, photonic crystals, and metamaterials, enabling optical devices and imaging at scales far below the wavelength of light.

Subfields and techniques

  • Plasmonics – exploiting surface plasmons at metal–dielectric interfaces to confine light to nanometre volumes.
  • Photonic crystals – periodic nanostructures that control light through photonic band gaps.
  • Optical nanoantennas – metallic or dielectric structures that convert freely propagating light into localised fields and vice versa.
  • Near-field scanning optical microscopy (NSOM) – imaging with resolution beyond the diffraction limit.
  • Metamaterials and metasurfaces – engineered structures with optical properties not found in nature.
  • Silicon photonics – nanoscale waveguides and resonators integrated on chips.

Applications

Applications include super-resolution microscopy, nanoscale light sources and lasers, high-density optical data storage, sensors capable of detecting single molecules, improved solar cells and LEDs, quantum information processing with single-photon devices, and on-chip optical interconnects.
