Nicolas Bourbaki

Nicolas Bourbaki is the collective pseudonym under which a changing group of mathematicians — founded in 1934–35 by, among others, Henri Cartan, Claude Chevalley, Jean Delsarte, Jean Dieudonné and André Weil — has published an influential series of textbooks presenting modern mathematics in a unified, rigorous, structural style.

History

The founders, young lecturers dissatisfied with the available analysis textbooks, met in Paris in December 1934 and resolved to write their own treatise. The invented name "Bourbaki" — borrowed from a 19th-century French general of Greek descent — became an elaborate group joke, complete with a fictional biography and membership in an imaginary academy. The group holds regular private congresses, drafts are read aloud and revised collectively, and members traditionally retire at fifty.

Éléments de mathématique

The Éléments de mathématique (note the singular) appeared from 1939 onward, covering set theory, algebra, general topology, functions of a real variable, topological vector spaces, integration, Lie groups, commutative algebra and spectral theory. Bourbaki popularised the axiomatic "structural" view of mathematics, coined terminology and notation still in use (the empty-set symbol ∅, the terms injective, surjective and bijective), and profoundly influenced the "New Math" school reforms of the 1960s — and the backlash against them.

Influence

Though its dominance has waned, Bourbaki's insistence on rigour and abstraction permanently reshaped twentieth-century pure mathematics, especially in France.
