Gloucester College, Oxford

Founded1283
Dissolved1539
Named forGloucester (founding benefaction)
SuccessorGloucester Hall; Worcester College

Gloucester College was a monastic college of the University of Oxford founded in 1283 for Benedictine monks. Established through a gift of land by Sir John Giffard of Brimpsfield, the college provided a place at Oxford where monks from Benedictine abbeys could study theology. Individual abbeys contributed chambers and lodgings around a quadrangle, each house maintaining its own buildings within the shared college, and the college drew students from monasteries across the southern province of the English Benedictine congregation.

History

The college grew through the fourteenth and fifteenth centuries, with houses maintained by abbeys including St Peter's, Gloucester; Malmesbury; Westminster; and St Augustine's, Canterbury. Its most famous early member was John Wycliffe, who is traditionally associated with the college in the 1360s, though the details of his residence are debated. Gloucester College was dissolved with the monasteries in 1539 during the English Reformation, and its property passed first to the newly founded see of Oxford and then to the Crown.

Legacy

The site was refounded in 1560 as Gloucester Hall, an academic hall of the university, and in 1714 it was refounded again, through the benefaction of Sir Thomas Cookes, as Worcester College. Several of the medieval monastic chamber blocks survive within Worcester College, notably the row of fifteenth-century cottages along the south side of the main quadrangle, giving the modern college one of the most distinctive ranges of medieval buildings in Oxford.
