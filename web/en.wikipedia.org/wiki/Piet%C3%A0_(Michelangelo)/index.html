The Pietà is a Renaissance marble sculpture by Michelangelo Buonarroti, housed in St. Peter's Basilica in Vatican City. Carved in 1498–1499 when the artist was in his early twenties, it depicts the Virgin Mary cradling the dead body of Jesus after the Crucifixion, and is regarded as one of the masterpieces of Renaissance art. It is the only work Michelangelo ever signed — his name is carved on the sash across Mary's chest.

Commission and composition

The sculpture was commissioned by the French cardinal Jean de Bilhères as a funerary monument. Michelangelo selected a single flawless block of Carrara marble and arranged the figures in a pyramidal composition: Mary, depicted serenely youthful, supports Christ's body across her lap, her drapery fanning out to form the broad base of the group. The idealization of Mary's youth drew comment even in the Renaissance; Michelangelo defended it as a symbol of her purity.

Damage and display

On 21 May 1972, a mentally disturbed geologist, Laszlo Toth, attacked the sculpture with a hammer, striking off Mary's left arm, breaking her nose and damaging an eyelid. Restorers reassembled the fragments using original marble pieces, and the Pietà is now displayed behind bulletproof acrylic glass in the first chapel on the right of St. Peter's. Michelangelo returned to the subject in later, unfinished works, the Florentine and Rondanini Pietàs.
