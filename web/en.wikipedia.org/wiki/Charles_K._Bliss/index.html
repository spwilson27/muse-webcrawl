Charles Kasiel Bliss AM (5 September 1897 – 13 July 1985), born Karl Kasiel Blitz, was an Austrian-Australian chemical engineer and semiotician who invented Blissymbols, a system of ideographic writing intended as a universal language that could end misunderstanding between peoples.

Life

Bliss was born in Czernowitz in the Austro-Hungarian Empire and trained as a chemical engineer in Vienna. After the Anschluss he was imprisoned in the Dachau and Buchenwald concentration camps; released through his wife's efforts, he fled to Greece and then to Shanghai, where he encountered Chinese characters and became convinced that a writing system of meaning-symbols, independent of any spoken language, could promote peace. After the Second World War he emigrated to Australia, settling in Sydney, where he worked in industry and developed his system in his spare time.

Blissymbols

He published his system as Semantography in 1949 (later editions appearing as Blissymbolics). Composed of a limited set of basic ideographs that combine into new meanings, it was largely ignored as a world language — but in 1971 it was adopted by the Ontario Crippled Children's Centre in Toronto as a communication aid for children with cerebral palsy who could not speak. Bliss initially celebrated, then quarrelled with, this augmentative and alternative communication (AAC) application, insisting on the purity of his original design. Blissymbols remain in use in AAC today.

The 2007 documentary Mr Symbol Man recounts his life and disputes with the Canadian therapists.
