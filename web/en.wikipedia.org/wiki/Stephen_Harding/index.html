Stephen Harding

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Stephen Harding (French: Étienne Harding; c. 1060 – 28 March 1134) was an English-born monk and abbot who was one of the founders of the Cistercian Order. He served as the third abbot of Cîteaux Abbey and is credited with producing the Carta Caritatis ("Charter of Charity"), the constitutional document that organized the order's growing family of monasteries. He is venerated as a saint in the Roman Catholic Church, with a feast day of 28 March (formerly 17 April).

Early life

Harding was born in England, probably at Sherborne in Dorset, and was educated at Sherborne Abbey. As a young man he travelled in Scotland and France, and visited Rome before entering the abbey of Molesme in Burgundy, where Robert of Molesme was abbot. When Robert and a group of monks left Molesme in 1098 to found a stricter community in the forest of Cîteaux, Harding was among the founding party.

Abbot of Cîteaux

Harding became prior under Abbot Alberic and succeeded him as abbot in 1109. The young order struggled at first: recruits were few and deaths many, and by some accounts Harding wondered whether the venture would survive. In 1112 the young Bernard of Clairvaux arrived with thirty companions, transforming the community's fortunes. Under Harding, Cîteaux founded its first daughter houses, including La Ferté, Pontigny, Clairvaux, and Morimond — the "four eldest daughters" through which the order spread across Europe.

Harding was a scholar as well as an administrator. He oversaw the production of illuminated manuscripts at Cîteaux, including a corrected edition of the Latin Bible (the "Stephen Harding Bible"), for which texts were checked against Hebrew sources with the help of Jewish scholars. His Carta Caritatis, approved by Pope Callixtus II in 1119, established the order's system of annual general chapters and visitation, balancing unity of observance with the independence of each abbey.

Death and veneration

Aged and going blind, Harding resigned the abbacy in 1133 and died on 28 March 1134. He was buried at Cîteaux beside his predecessor Alberic. The Cistercian order flourished for centuries on the constitutional foundations he laid.
