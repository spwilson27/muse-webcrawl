Arethusa (Greek: Ἀρέθουσα) was a nymph in Greek mythology, a follower of the goddess Artemis. She is best known for the myth in which she fled the river god Alpheus and was transformed into a freshwater spring on the island of Ortygia in Syracuse, Sicily.

Myth

According to Ovid's Metamorphoses, Arethusa, a huntress nymph in Achaea, bathed in the river Alpheus after a hunt. The river god rose from the waters and pursued her. Exhausted, she called upon Artemis, who hid her in a cloud and then transformed her into a spring. Her waters flowed beneath the earth and sea to emerge on Ortygia. Alpheus, however, mingled his river waters with her spring, and in Sicilian tradition the two waters were said to join.

The spring and coinage

The Fountain of Arethusa on Ortygia still exists and was famous in antiquity. Arethusa's head, surrounded by dolphins, became the celebrated design of the decadrachm coins of Syracuse, among the most admired coins of the ancient world, including the issues signed by the engraver Euainetos.

In literature

The myth inspired works by Shelley, who wrote the lyric "Arethusa," and many other poets and artists. The tale is retold by Ovid and mentioned by Virgil, Pausanias, and others.
