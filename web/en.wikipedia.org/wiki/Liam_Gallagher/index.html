William John Paul Gallagher (born 21 September 1972) is an English singer and songwriter who achieved fame as the lead vocalist and frontman of the rock band Oasis from 1991 to 2009. One of the figureheads of the Britpop movement of the 1990s, he is known for his distinctive singing style — a snarling delivery delivered leaning forward with hands clasped behind his back — and for his abrasive public persona and long-running feud with his elder brother, guitarist and songwriter Noel Gallagher.

Oasis

Gallagher formed the band that became Oasis in Manchester in 1991, inviting his brother Noel to join on condition that Noel write the songs. Albums Definitely Maybe (1994) and (What's the Story) Morning Glory? (1995) made Oasis the biggest British band of their era, with Liam's voice defining hits such as "Live Forever", "Wonderwall", and "Champagne Supernova". The band's two nights at Knebworth in 1996 drew 250,000 people. Oasis split in August 2009 after a backstage fight in Paris, when Noel quit shortly before a festival performance.

Beady Eye and solo career

The remaining members continued as Beady Eye (2009–2014), releasing two albums. Gallagher launched a solo career with As You Were (2017), which debuted at number one in the UK, followed by Why Me? Why Not. (2019) and C'mon You Know (2022); his 2022 Knebworth solo shows echoed the band's 1996 peak. In August 2024 the Gallagher brothers announced an Oasis reunion, with a stadium tour beginning in 2025.
