Megaraptora

Megaraptora is a clade of carnivorous tetanuran theropod dinosaurs with controversial relationships. Megaraptorans lived from the late Early Cretaceous to the end of the Late Cretaceous (roughly 130–66 million years ago), on the former southern supercontinent Gondwana — South America, Australia, and Antarctica — with a possible additional genus (Rapator) Australian and debated Asian records. They are characterised by large, sickle-shaped claws on the hands.

Discovery and classification

The group is named after Megaraptor from Argentina, described in 1998; its huge claw was first mistaken for a dromaeosaur-like foot claw before more complete remains (including the juvenile specimen described in 2014) showed it was on the thumb. Other genera include Australovenator (Australia), Fukuiraptor (Japan, sometimes excluded), Aerosteon, Orkoraptor, Murusraptor, Tratayenia, Maip (one of the largest, from Argentina), and Aoniraptor. The family Megaraptoridae was named by Fernando Novas and colleagues in 2013.

Megaraptorans were initially considered allosauroids (within Neovenatoridae), but more recent phylogenetic analyses increasingly place them as coelurosaurs, possibly as basal tyrannosauroids or as their own branch near the base of Coelurosauria. Their pneumatic, lightweight skeletons and long, slender skulls support a coelurosaurian identity.

Description

Megaraptorans ranged from about 4–5 m (Fukuiraptor) to over 9–10 m in length (Maip). They had long, low skulls with many small serrated teeth, long arms with enormous curved claws on digits I and II, small hindlimb "sickle" traces absent, highly pneumatised vertebrae, and slender, bird-like bodies suggesting fast, agile predators.
