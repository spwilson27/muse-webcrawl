The 2012–13 Premier League was the 21st season of the Premier League, the top English professional league for association football clubs. Manchester United won their 13th Premier League title and a record-extending 20th English league championship, clinching the title with four matches to spare after a 3–0 win over Aston Villa on 22 April 2013, in which Robin van Persie scored a hat-trick. United finished 11 points ahead of defending champions Manchester City.

Summary

The season was the last in charge for Manchester United manager Sir Alex Ferguson, who announced his retirement in May 2013 after more than 26 years at the club; his final match was a remarkable 5–5 draw at West Bromwich Albion. Van Persie, signed from Arsenal in the summer of 2012, finished as the league's top scorer with 26 goals and won the PFA Players' Player of the Year award, while Tottenham's Gareth Bale won both the PFA and FWA player of the year honours.

The title race proved one-sided after the turn of the year, but the battle for Champions League places went to the final day, with Arsenal edging Tottenham Hotspur for fourth place. At the bottom, Queens Park Rangers and Reading were relegated with games to spare, and Wigan Athletic joined them despite winning the FA Cup three days before their relegation was confirmed — becoming the first club to win the cup and be relegated in the same season.

League table (top six)

1. Manchester United — 89 points; 2. Manchester City — 78; 3. Chelsea — 75; 4. Arsenal — 73; 5. Tottenham Hotspur — 72; 6. Everton — 63. United, City and Chelsea qualified directly for the Champions League group stage, with Arsenal entering the play-off round.
