Soroban

The soroban (算盤, そろばん) is an abacus developed in Japan, derived from the Chinese suanpan, which was imported to Japan in the 14th century. It consists of a frame with an odd number of columns (rods), each with beads: one bead above a horizontal divider bar (worth five) and four beads below it (each worth one) in the modern form.

History

The soroban evolved from the Chinese suanpan, which originally had two beads above and five below. By the mid-20th century the Japanese version was standardised to the faster one-above, four-below arrangement. Despite the spread of electronic calculators, the soroban remains in use in Japanese elementary education and in mental-calculation (anzan) training and competitions.

Use

Numbers are set by moving beads toward the divider bar. Addition and subtraction are performed with complement techniques, and skilled users can multiply, divide, and extract roots remarkably quickly. In flash anzan competitions, contestants mentally visualise a soroban and sum sequences of numbers flashed on a screen.
