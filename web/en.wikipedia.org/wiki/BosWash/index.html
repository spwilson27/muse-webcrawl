BosWash is a name for the chain of metropolitan areas along the northeastern seaboard of the United States, running from Boston in the north to Washington, D.C. in the south. The geographer Jean Gottmann studied the region in his 1961 book Megalopolis, and the portmanteau "BosWash" was popularised by the futurist Herman Kahn. Often called the Northeast megalopolis or the Northeast Corridor, it is the most populous and economically productive megalopolis in the United States.

[ Image: Satellite view at night of the continuous urban corridor from Boston to Washington ]

Description

The corridor extends through Massachusetts, Rhode Island, Connecticut, New York, New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Delaware, Maryland and the District of Columbia. Although it occupies about 2% of the land area of the contiguous United States, it holds roughly 17% of the national population and produces around a fifth of U.S. GDP. It concentrates finance (New York), the federal government (Washington), higher education (Boston and the Ivy League), pharmaceuticals and media.

Transport

BosWash is bound together by Interstate 95, U.S. Route 1 and Amtrak's Northeast Corridor — the busiest passenger rail line in the country, hosting the Acela high-speed service — together with a chain of major airports. Gottmann's insight was that the cities function increasingly as a single interdependent urban system while retaining distinct identities and governments.
