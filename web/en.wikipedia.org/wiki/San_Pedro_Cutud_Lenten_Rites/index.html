San Pedro Cutud Lenten Rites

The San Pedro Cutud Lenten Rites are a re-enactment of Christ's Passion held every Good Friday in San Pedro Cutud, a barangay of San Fernando, Pampanga, in the Philippines. The event, part of the Maleldo ("Holy Week") observances of the city, is famous — and controversial — because volunteer penitents are actually nailed to wooden crosses in imitation of the crucifixion, a practice that draws thousands of tourists and devotees each year.

The rites

The centrepiece is a street passion play, the Via Crucis, written in Kapampangan and performed since the 1950s. The play culminates on a man-made hill called Burol, where three crosses are erected. The actor playing Christ — most famously Ruben Enaje, a sign painter who has played the role since 1986 — and other penitents have stainless-steel nails driven through their palms (and sometimes ropes supporting their weight) and remain on the cross for several minutes before being taken down and given first aid. Around the crucifixions, hundreds of flagellants (magdarame) process through the streets whipping their bare, bloodied backs as acts of penance or in fulfilment of vows (panata).

History

The passion play in Cutud dates to 1955, and the first actual crucifixion is generally dated to 1962, when Artemio Anonuevo volunteered to be nailed in fulfilment of a vow. The rites grew with media attention and tourist interest, and the city government incorporated them into its Holy Week programme.

Reception

The Roman Catholic Church in the Philippines has expressed disapproval of literal crucifixion and self-flagellation, urging devotees toward more orthodox expressions of faith, while health authorities warn of infection risks. Devotees maintain that the practices are profound personal vows made in gratitude for healings or in petition for blessings.
