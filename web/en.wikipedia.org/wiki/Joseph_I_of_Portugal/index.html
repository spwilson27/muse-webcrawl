Joseph I (Portuguese: José I; 1714–1777), nicknamed "the Reformer", was King of Portugal from 1750 until his death. Largely content to leave government to his powerful secretary of state, Sebastião José de Carvalho e Melo, Marquis of Pombal, his reign was dominated by Pombal's sweeping reforms.

Reign

After the catastrophic Lisbon earthquake of 1755, Pombal directed an energetic reconstruction of the capital. The reign saw the suppression of the Jesuits in Portugal (1759), abolition of slavery in metropolitan Portugal, reform of education and the army, and the curtailing of the Inquisition. In 1758 the king survived an assassination attempt, which was followed by the Távora affair and the destruction of that noble family. Joseph was succeeded by his daughter Maria I.
