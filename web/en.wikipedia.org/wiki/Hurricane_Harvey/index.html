Hurricane Harvey

Hurricane Harvey was a devastating Category 4 hurricane that made landfall in Texas in August 2017, causing catastrophic flooding in the Houston metropolitan area. It is tied with Hurricane Katrina (2005) as the costliest tropical cyclone on record, inflicting an estimated $125 billion in damage, primarily from flooding.

Meteorological history

Harvey formed from a tropical wave east of the Lesser Antilles on 17 August 2017, weakened over the Caribbean, then rapidly intensified over the Gulf of Mexico. It made landfall near Rockport, Texas, on 25 August with winds of 130 mph (215 km/h), the first major hurricane to strike the United States since Wilma in 2005. The storm then stalled over southeastern Texas, dropping up to 60.58 inches (1,539 mm) of rain at Nederland, Texas — a United States record for a tropical cyclone.

Impact

At least 107 people died in storm-related incidents. Hundreds of thousands of homes were flooded in the Houston area, tens of thousands of residents were rescued, and major industrial sites, including petrochemical plants along the Gulf Coast, were shut down or damaged. The name Harvey was retired by the World Meteorological Organization in 2018.
