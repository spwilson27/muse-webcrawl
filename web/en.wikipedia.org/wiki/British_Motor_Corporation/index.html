The British Motor Corporation (BMC) was a British vehicle manufacturer formed in 1952 by the merger of the Austin Motor Company and the Morris-based Nuffield Organisation. It was one of the largest car makers in Britain in the 1950s and 1960s, producing the Mini, the Morris Minor, the MGB and a host of badge-engineered saloons. In 1966 BMC merged with Jaguar to form British Motor Holdings, which two years later joined Leyland to create British Leyland.

Formation

The merger was brokered by Leonard Lord of Austin, who became BMC's first chairman, combining Austin's Longbridge plant with Morris's Cowley works at Oxford and the MG factory at Abingdon. The new company rationalised production but continued to sell overlapping models under the Austin, Morris, MG, Riley and Wolseley badges.

Models and legacy

BMC's most famous product is the Mini, designed by Alec Issigonis and launched in 1959. The Morris Minor, also Issigonis's work, sold more than 1.3 million units. The MGB, launched in 1962, became the best-selling sports car of its era. BMC's commercial vehicles — the Austin and Morris trucks and the ubiquitous J4 and JU vans — were equally common on British roads. Despite the company's later collapse, the Mini marque remains one of the world's best-known automotive brands.
