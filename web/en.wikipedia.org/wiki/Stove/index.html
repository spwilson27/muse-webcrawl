Stove

A domestic stove

A stove or range is a device that burns fuel or uses electricity to generate heat for cooking or for heating a space. Kitchen stoves are among the most common household appliances, typically combining a cooktop with burners or heating elements and an enclosed oven. Heating stoves, by contrast, are designed to warm rooms and range from simple wood-burning boxes to elaborate masonry heaters.

History

Early cooking was done over open fires and hearths. Enclosed stoves appeared in various forms in China and Europe; the cast-iron stove was developed in Europe in the late 17th and 18th centuries, with Benjamin Franklin's "Pennsylvania fireplace" (1742) and Benjamin Thompson's (Count Rumford's) designs improving efficiency. The modern kitchen range took shape in the 19th century as cast-iron coal and wood ranges became common, followed by gas stoves in the later 1800s and electric stoves in the early 20th century.

Types

Cooking stoves may be fuelled by natural gas, liquefied petroleum gas, electricity, wood, coal, or induction. Induction cooktops heat the vessel directly through electromagnetic induction and are valued for speed and control. Heating stoves include wood stoves, pellet stoves, and coal stoves. Portable stoves, such as camping stoves, burn liquid fuel, gas canisters, or solid fuel tablets.

Efficiency and safety

Open fires convert only a small fraction of fuel energy into useful heat, whereas modern enclosed stoves can reach efficiencies above 70 percent. Incomplete combustion produces carbon monoxide, so stoves require proper venting and maintenance. Indoor air pollution from solid-fuel cooking remains a significant health issue in much of the developing world, and improved cookstoves are a focus of public-health efforts.
