Yehud (Persian province)

Yehud was a province of the Achaemenid (Persian) Empire in the southern Levant, roughly corresponding to the territory of the earlier Babylonian province and, before it, the Iron Age kingdom of Judah. Established after Cyrus the Great conquered Babylon in 539 BCE, it endured for about two centuries until Alexander the Great's conquest of the Persian Empire in the 330s BCE.

History

After Cyrus issued his edict permitting exiled peoples to return, waves of Judean returnees from Babylonia repopulated the small province. Under figures such as Zerubbabel, Ezra, and Nehemiah, Jerusalem's Second Temple was completed (c. 515 BCE) and its walls rebuilt. The province was governed by officials bearing the title pehah (governor) — Nehemiah is the best known — and issued its own small silver coins stamped "Yehud" in Aramaic script.

Territory and population

Yehud was small — at its greatest extent covering the Judean hills from around Bethel in the north to Hebron in the south, and at times parts of the coastal plain including Lod and Ono. Its population was modest, probably in the tens of thousands, with Jerusalem itself a small town of perhaps a few thousand inhabitants for much of the period.

Significance

The Persian period in Yehud is regarded as formative for Judaism: much of the Hebrew Bible was edited or composed during this era, the Torah was publicly promulgated, and the community's identity was consolidated around the Jerusalem temple and its law.
