The World Karate Federation (WKF) is the largest international governing body for sport karate, recognised by the International Olympic Committee. It organises the World Karate Championships and the Karate1 Premier League circuit, and oversees competition rules for kata (forms) and kumite (sparring).

History

The WKF was founded in 1970 as the World Union of Karate-do Organizations (WUKO), with the first World Championships held in Tokyo the same year. After a long campaign for unity in the sport, and recognition disputes with rival bodies, the organisation adopted the name World Karate Federation in the 1990s and received full IOC recognition in 1999. Its long-serving president is Antonio Espinós of Spain.

Competitions

The senior World Championships are held every two years, with events in male and female individual and team kata and kumite across weight classes. The Karate1 series — Premier League, Series A, and Youth League events — provides a world ranking circuit. Karate made its Olympic debut at the Tokyo 2020 Games (held in 2021), where kata and kumite medals were contested, but the sport was not retained for Paris 2024.

Rules

In WKF kumite, bouts last three minutes for seniors; techniques score ippon (3 points), waza-ari (2 points), or yuko (1 point) depending on the target and technique, and controlled contact is required — excessive contact is penalised. Kata is judged by panels scoring technical and athletic performance. Para-karate events for athletes with disabilities were added to the World Championships in the 2010s.
