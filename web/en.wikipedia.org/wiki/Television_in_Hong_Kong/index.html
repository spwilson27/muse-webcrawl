Television in Hong Kong

Television in Hong Kong began in 1957 with the cable service Rediffusion Television. Free-to-air terrestrial broadcasting is dominated by TVB (Television Broadcasts Limited), which launched in 1967, alongside the public broadcaster RTHK and newer entrants such as HKTVE (ViuTV). Hong Kong television drama and variety programming have been culturally influential across the Chinese-speaking world.

History

Rediffusion (later ATV) began as a subscription cable service in 1957 and became a free-to-air broadcaster in 1973. TVB Jade, broadcasting in Cantonese, quickly became the market leader. A third station, Commercial Television, failed in 1978. ATV, the world's first Chinese-language broadcaster, lost its licence and closed in 2016 after years of decline. ViuTV launched the same year, targeting younger viewers.

Broadcasters

  • TVB — Jade (Cantonese) and Pearl (English), plus digital channels.
  • RTHK — the public broadcaster, operating TV channels RTHK 31–33 since 2014.
  • HKTVE — ViuTV (Cantonese) and ViuTVsix (English).
  • HOY TV — free-to-air channels operated by Fantastic Television.

Pay television includes the Now TV and i-Cable platforms, and satellite signals from the region are widely received.

Regulation

The industry is regulated by the Communications Authority. Broadcasters operate under the Broadcasting Ordinance; licensing decisions have at times been controversial, notably the 2013 refusal of a licence to HKTV, which triggered mass protests.
