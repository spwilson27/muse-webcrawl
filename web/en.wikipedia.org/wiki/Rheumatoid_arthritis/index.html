Overview

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA) is a long-term autoimmune disorder that primarily affects joints. It typically results in warm, swollen and painful joints — most commonly the small joints of the hands and feet, symmetrically — with stiffness worst in the morning and after rest. Untreated, synovial inflammation (pannus) erodes cartilage and bone, deforming joints.

Course and treatment

RA affects roughly 0.5–1% of adults, women more than men, with onset usually in mid-life. It is a systemic disease: rheumatoid nodules, lung and eye inflammation, accelerated cardiovascular disease and fatigue accompany the arthritis. Smoking and genetic factors (the HLA "shared epitope") contribute; citrullinated-protein antibodies (anti-CCP) are highly specific. Treatment aims at remission with disease-modifying drugs — methotrexate first-line, then biologics (TNF, IL-6 and B-cell targeted) and JAK inhibitors — a revolution that has made severe deformity far less common where treatment is accessible.
