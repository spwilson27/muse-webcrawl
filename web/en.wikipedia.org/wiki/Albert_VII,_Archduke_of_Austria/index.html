Albert VII (German: Albrecht VII; 13 November 1559 – 13 July 1621) was an Austrian archduke who, with his wife Isabella Clara Eugenia, jointly ruled the Spanish Netherlands from 1598 until his death. Earlier in life he had been a cardinal and Archbishop of Toledo, Viceroy of Portugal, and Governor-General of the Habsburg Netherlands.

Early life and church career

Albert was the fifth son of Maximilian II, Holy Roman Emperor, and Maria of Spain, and was raised at the court of his uncle Philip II in Spain. Destined for the church, he was made a cardinal in 1577 at the age of eighteen, though he was never ordained a priest. In 1594 he became Archbishop of Toledo, one of the richest sees in Christendom.

Viceroy and governor

From 1583 to 1593 Albert served as Viceroy of Portugal, which had recently come under Spanish rule. In 1595 Philip II appointed him Governor-General of the Spanish Netherlands, where he campaigned against the Dutch Republic and France; he captured Calais and Hulst but was defeated by Maurice of Nassau at the Battle of Nieuwpoort in 1600.

Co-sovereign of the Netherlands

Released from his cardinalate, Albert married his cousin Isabella Clara Eugenia in 1599. By the Act of Cession of 1598, Philip II transferred sovereignty over the Spanish Netherlands to the couple, on condition that if they died without children the provinces would revert to Spain. Their court at Brussels presided over a period of relative peace and cultural flowering, marked by the patronage of Peter Paul Rubens. After the long Siege of Ostend (1601–1604), Albert and Isabella concluded the Twelve Years' Truce with the Dutch Republic in 1609.

Death

Albert died in Brussels on 13 July 1621, leaving no surviving children; sovereignty over the Netherlands accordingly reverted to the Spanish crown under Philip IV, while Isabella remained as governess until her death in 1633.
