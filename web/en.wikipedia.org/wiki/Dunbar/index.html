Dunbar

Dunbar (Scots: Dunbaur; Scottish Gaelic: Dùn Barra) is a town in East Lothian, Scotland, on the North Sea coast about 30 miles (48 km) east of Edinburgh. It was a royal burgh and is known for Dunbar Castle, two harbours, and as the birthplace of the naturalist and conservationist John Muir.

History

Dunbar Castle, stronghold of the Earls of Dunbar and March, was one of the most important fortresses in Scotland; Mary, Queen of Scots was brought here after her abduction by the Earl of Bothwell in 1567. The town gave its name to two battles of Dunbar (1296 and 1650); in the second, Oliver Cromwell defeated the Scottish army, one of his most celebrated victories.

The town today

Dunbar has a population of roughly 9,000. Attractions include the John Muir Birthplace museum, the harbours, and the Dunbar lifeboat station. The town enjoys a notably sunny microclimate and brands itself "Sunny Dunbar". The nearby Belhaven Bay and John Muir Country Park are popular with walkers and birdwatchers.
