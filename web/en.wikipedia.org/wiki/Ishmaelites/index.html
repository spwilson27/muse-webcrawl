Ishmaelites

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Ishmaelites in biblical tradition

The Ishmaelites are, in the Hebrew Bible and later traditions, the descendants of Ishmael, the firstborn son of Abraham by Hagar, Sarah's Egyptian handmaid. Genesis 25 lists Ishmael's twelve sons, described as princes of twelve tribes, and associates the Ishmaelites with the desert regions from Havilah to Shur, east of Egypt toward Assyria, including the northern Arabian Peninsula.

In biblical narrative

Ishmaelites appear as traders to whom Joseph was sold and who carried him to Egypt (Genesis 37), and as camel-riding raiders associated with Midianites and Amalekites in the Book of Judges. Their portrayal reflects the world of north Arabian camel nomads of the early first millennium BCE.

In later tradition

In the Qur'an, Ishmael (Isma'il) is a prophet and ancestor of Arab tribes; Islamic tradition holds that he and Abraham raised the foundations of the Kaaba in Mecca. In Jewish and Christian interpretation, Ishmaelites came to be associated broadly with Arabs and, later, sometimes with Muslims generally.
