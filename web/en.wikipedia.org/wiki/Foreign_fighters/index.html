Foreign fighters

DefinitionNon-citizens who join an armed conflict abroad, usually in an insurgency
Historic examplesInternational Brigades (Spain), Afghan Arabs, fighters in Syria/Iraq and Ukraine

Foreign fighters are individuals who leave their country of origin or residence to join an armed conflict abroad, typically with a non-state armed group, though sometimes with a foreign state's forces. Motivations studied by researchers include ideology, religion, ethnic kinship, adventure, identity and personal crisis.

History

Notable waves include the International Brigades who fought for the Spanish Republic in the 1930s, foreign volunteers in Israel's 1948 war, and the Arab volunteers who fought the Soviets in Afghanistan in the 1980s, some of whom later formed the core of al-Qaeda. In the 2010s, the Syrian Civil War and the Islamic State drew an estimated 40,000 foreign fighters from over 100 countries — the largest mobilisation of its kind — prompting UN Security Council Resolution 2178 (2014) and domestic laws criminalising travel to join designated groups. After Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, foreign volunteers joined Ukraine's International Legion, while foreigners also fought on the Russian side.

Legal and security issues

Returnees pose policy dilemmas: prosecution, rehabilitation and monitoring of battle-hardened fighters, the status of captured fighters under international humanitarian law, and the citizenship-stripping measures adopted by some states.
