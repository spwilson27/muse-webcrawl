Capital allowance

Capital allowance
JurisdictionUnited Kingdom (and similar Commonwealth systems)
Governing lawCapital Allowances Act 2001
PurposeTax relief for capital expenditure

A capital allowance is a form of tax relief in the United Kingdom tax system (with equivalents in several other jurisdictions) that allows a business to deduct the cost of qualifying capital expenditure — such as plant, machinery, vehicles and certain buildings — from its taxable profits. Because ordinary accounting depreciation is not deductible for tax purposes, capital allowances are the statutory mechanism by which businesses obtain relief for the wearing-out and obsolescence of assets.

Main categories

Expenditure is generally allocated to pools attracting writing-down allowances at prescribed rates, historically 18% per year on a reducing-balance basis for the main pool and 6% for the special-rate pool (which includes integral features of buildings and long-life assets). Cars are allocated according to their CO2 emissions.

Annual investment allowance and full expensing

The annual investment allowance (AIA) gives 100% relief for qualifying plant and machinery expenditure up to a capped amount (set at £1 million). In 2023 the UK introduced "full expensing", allowing companies to deduct 100% of the cost of qualifying new main-rate plant and machinery in the year of purchase. Structures and buildings allowances give relief for construction costs at a flat rate over a long period (3% per year over 33⅓ years).

First-year allowances

First-year allowances give accelerated relief for specified environmentally beneficial expenditure, such as zero-emission vehicles and energy-saving equipment.
