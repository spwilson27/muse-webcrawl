The National People's Congress (NPC) is the national legislature of the People's Republic of China. With nearly 3,000 members, it is the largest parliamentary body in the world, and the constitution designates it the highest organ of state power.

Structure

Deputies are elected for five-year terms by provincial-level people's congresses and by the People's Liberation Army. The NPC meets in full session roughly once a year, usually in March in the Great Hall of the People in Beijing, in tandem with the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference — the "Two Sessions".

Powers

The NPC elects the President and Vice President of China, the Premier, and other leading state officials; it amends the constitution and enacts basic laws. Between sessions, its roughly 175-member Standing Committee exercises legislative functions, interprets the constitution and laws, and can enact most legislation.

History

The first NPC convened in 1954 under the new constitution of the People's Republic. The body, along with its Standing Committee chaired in recent decades by senior Communist Party figures, forms the formal apex of China's state structure.
