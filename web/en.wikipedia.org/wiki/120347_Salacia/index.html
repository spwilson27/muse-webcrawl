120347 Salacia, provisional designation 2004 SB60, is a large trans-Neptunian object in the Kuiper belt, approximately 846 kilometres (526 mi) in diameter. It was discovered on 22 September 2004 by American astronomers Henry Roe, Michael Brown, and Kristina Barkume at the Palomar Observatory in California. Salacia is a possible dwarf planet and is orbited by a natural satellite named Actaea.

Discovery and naming

Salacia was discovered on 22 September 2004 during a survey for large Kuiper belt objects. It is named after Salacia, the Roman goddess of salt water and the female counterpart of Neptune. Its moon Actaea is named after one of the Nereids of Greek mythology. The names were approved by the International Astronomical Union in 2011.

Orbit and classification

Salacia orbits the Sun at an average distance of about 42 astronomical units with an orbital period of roughly 274 years. Its orbit is moderately eccentric and inclined relative to the ecliptic. It is classified as a classical Kuiper belt object, or cubewano, meaning its orbit is not controlled by an orbital resonance with Neptune.

Physical characteristics

Observations with the Hubble Space Telescope and thermal measurements indicate that Salacia is one of the largest known trans-Neptunian objects, with a diameter near 846 km. Its surface is known to contain water ice, and its density, derived from the orbit of its moon, is relatively low for an object of its size, suggesting a substantial fraction of porous ice in its interior.

Satellite

Salacia has one known moon, Actaea, discovered on 21 July 2006 from Hubble Space Telescope observations. Actaea is roughly 286 kilometres in diameter and orbits Salacia at a distance of about 5,700 kilometres with a period of about 5.5 days. The pair form one of the most tightly bound binary systems known among large trans-Neptunian objects, and their mutual orbit has been used to measure the system's total mass.
