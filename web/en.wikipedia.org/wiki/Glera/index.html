Glera is a white variety of grape of Slovenian and Italian origin, long grown in the hills of Friuli-Venezia Giulia and the Veneto in north-eastern Italy. It is the principal grape used to make Prosecco sparkling wine.

Name change

The variety was historically called "Prosecco", after the village of Prosecco near Trieste. In 2009, to protect the Prosecco denomination and prevent producers elsewhere from using the name as a grape variety, Italy renamed the grape Glera — an old synonym — while "Prosecco" became a protected geographical designation (DOC/DOCG). Under EU rules, Prosecco wine must now be made from at least 85% Glera.

Viticulture and wine

Glera is a vigorous, late-ripening variety with pale golden berries, valued for its high acidity and delicate aromas of apple, pear, white peach and blossom. It is usually vinified into sparkling or semi-sparkling (frizzante) wines by the Charmat (tank) method, in which the second fermentation takes place in pressurised stainless steel tanks, preserving its fresh fruit character.
