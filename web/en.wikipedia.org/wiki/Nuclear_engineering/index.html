Nuclear engineering

Nuclear engineering is the branch of engineering concerned with the application of atomic nuclei and subatomic physics, particularly nuclear fission and nuclear fusion, and with the design, construction, operation and maintenance of systems that exploit nuclear processes. Its best-known application is the generation of electricity in nuclear power plants, but it also covers nuclear propulsion, nuclear medicine, radiation detection and the management of radioactive waste.

Branches

Major areas of the discipline include reactor physics and reactor core design; thermal hydraulics, the study of heat removal from reactor cores; radiation shielding and radiation protection; nuclear fuel cycle technology, from enrichment to reprocessing and waste disposal; materials science for components exposed to intense radiation; instrumentation and control; and nuclear safety analysis, including probabilistic risk assessment. Research also extends to fusion engineering for experimental devices such as tokamaks and stellarators, and to applications such as radioisotope production for medicine and industry.

History

The field emerged from nuclear physics discoveries of the 1930s and 1940s: the discovery of fission in 1938, Fermi's first controlled chain reaction in 1942, and the wartime Manhattan Project. Civil nuclear power followed in the 1950s with experimental reactors and, in 1957, the Shippingport Atomic Power Station in the United States. Nuclear engineering became established as a university discipline, and professional practice is now closely regulated because of the safety and security implications of the technology.
