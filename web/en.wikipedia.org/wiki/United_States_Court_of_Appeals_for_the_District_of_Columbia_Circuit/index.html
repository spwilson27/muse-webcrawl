United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit

The United States Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit (the D.C. Circuit) is one of the thirteen United States Courts of Appeals. It hears appeals from the United States District Court for the District of Columbia and, because it sits in Washington, D.C., it decides an exceptionally large share of cases involving federal administrative law.

D.C. Circuit
Established: 1893
Court: E. Barrett Prettyman U.S. Courthouse, Washington, D.C.
Judges: 11 authorized
Chief Judge: Sri Srinivasan (from 2020)

History

The court traces its origins to 1893. Its jurisdiction expanded greatly with the growth of the federal regulatory state in the 20th century. Because statutes often provide for direct review of agency rules in the D.C. Circuit, its decisions shape environmental, telecommunications, energy, securities, and labor regulation nationwide.

Composition and procedure

Cases are normally heard by three-judge panels, with en banc rehearing by the full court in exceptional cases. Judges are nominated by the President of the United States and confirmed by the Senate, serving during good behaviour.

Notable judges

Many D.C. Circuit judges have later joined the Supreme Court, including Ruth Bader Ginsburg, Antonin Scalia, Clarence Thomas, John Roberts, and Brett Kavanaugh. It is often described as the second most important court in the United States.
