Schur polynomial

In mathematics, Schur polynomials, named after Issai Schur, are certain symmetric polynomials in n variables, indexed by partitions, that constitute a basis for the space of all symmetric polynomials. They are special cases of several other families, including Jack polynomials and (via specialisation) characters of irreducible representations of the general linear group.

Schur polynomials are indexed by partitions, drawn as Young diagrams.

Definition

Given a partition λ = (λ1 ≥ λ2 ≥ … ≥ λn), the Schur polynomial can be defined by the bialternant formula: the quotient of the determinant of the matrix (xiλj+n−j) by the Vandermonde determinant. Though defined as a quotient of polynomials, the result is a polynomial by a divisibility argument.

Combinatorial description

Schur polynomials also admit a combinatorial formula as a sum over semistandard Young tableaux of shape λ, each tableau contributing a monomial recording its entries. The coefficients in the expansion of products of Schur polynomials are the Littlewood–Richardson coefficients, which also count certain tableaux and describe tensor product decompositions of representations.

Properties

Schur polynomials satisfy Jacobi–Trudi identities expressing them as determinants in the complete homogeneous or elementary symmetric polynomials. They appear in Schubert calculus, where they represent cohomology classes of Schubert varieties in Grassmannians.
