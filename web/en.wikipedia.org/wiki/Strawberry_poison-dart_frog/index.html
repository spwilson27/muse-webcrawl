Strawberry poison-dart frog


Scientific name: Oophaga pumilio
Family: Dendrobatidae
Conservation status: Least Concern

The strawberry poison frog, strawberry poison-dart frog, or blue jeans poison frog (Oophaga pumilio, formerly Dendrobates pumilio) is a species of small poison dart frog found in Central America. It is common throughout its range, which extends from eastern central Nicaragua through Costa Rica and northwestern Panama.

Description

The species is small, roughly 17.5–22 mm in snout–vent length. Although the classic colouration is a bright red body with blue-purple limbs — the "blue jeans" morph of Costa Rica — the species is famous for extreme colour polymorphism: some fifteen or more colour morphs occur, especially in the Bocas del Toro archipelago of Panama, ranging from entirely red or blue to green with black spots. The bright colours are aposematic, advertising skin alkaloids sequestered from the mites and ants the frogs eat.

Breeding behaviour

Oophaga pumilio shows remarkable maternal care. Males call and defend small territories; after mating, the male keeps the eggs moist, and when the tadpoles hatch the female carries them one by one on her back to small pools of water held in bromeliads. She then returns to feed each tadpole with unfertilised eggs — hence the genus name Oophaga, "egg-eater".
