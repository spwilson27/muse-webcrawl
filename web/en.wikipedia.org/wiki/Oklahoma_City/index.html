Oklahoma City, often shortened to OKC, is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of Oklahoma. The county seat of Oklahoma County, it ranks among the largest cities in the United States by both population and land area. Its economy centres on government, energy, aviation (including Tinker Air Force Base and the FAA's Mike Monroney Aeronautical Center), health care and biotechnology.

History

The city was founded literally overnight during the Land Run of 22 April 1889, when some 10,000 settlers claimed lots in the "Unassigned Lands" of Indian Territory; it became the state capital in 1910 after a popular vote moved it from Guthrie. Oil discovered within the city limits in 1928 — including a well on the capitol grounds — made it a petroleum centre. On 19 April 1995 the city suffered the deadliest act of domestic terrorism in U.S. history, the bombing of the Alfred P. Murrah Federal Building, which killed 168 people; the site is now commemorated by the Oklahoma City National Memorial.

Geography and climate

Oklahoma City lies in the Sandstone Hills region of central Oklahoma, on the North Canadian River (partly dammed as the Oklahoma River through downtown). It has a humid subtropical climate with hot summers, mild winters and a pronounced severe-weather season: the city sits in "Tornado Alley" and was struck by devastating tornadoes in 1999 and 2013 in the suburb of Moore.

Culture and sports

Attractions include the National Cowboy & Western Heritage Museum, the Bricktown entertainment district and the Myriad Botanical Gardens. The city's major-league team is the NBA's Oklahoma City Thunder, which arrived in 2008 and plays at the Paycom Center. Since 1993, voter-approved MAPS sales-tax programmes have funded successive rounds of public projects, from canals and arenas to parks and streetcars.
