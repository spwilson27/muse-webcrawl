Citizen of the United Kingdom and Colonies

Citizen of the United Kingdom and Colonies (CUKC) was the principal form of British nationality under the British Nationality Act 1948, in force from 1 January 1949 until 31 December 1982. It replaced the previous common status of British subject with a single citizenship covering the United Kingdom and its colonies, while other Commonwealth countries created their own citizenships.

Acquisition

CUKC status was generally acquired by birth in the United Kingdom or a colony, by descent from a CUKC father, by registration, or by naturalisation. Originally, CUKCs enjoyed free movement to and settlement in the United Kingdom, but immigration legislation beginning with the Commonwealth Immigrants Act 1962 progressively restricted this right, culminating in the Immigration Act 1971's concept of the "right of abode", which was limited to CUKCs with a close connection to the UK itself (so-called patrials).

Abolition

The British Nationality Act 1981 abolished CUKC status from 1 January 1983, replacing it with three categories: British citizen (for those with the right of abode), British Dependent Territories citizen, and British Overseas citizen. CUKCs without the right of abode generally became British Overseas citizens, a status that does not carry the right to live or work in the UK.
