Sakurajima (Japanese: 桜島, literally "cherry blossom island") is an active stratovolcano in Kagoshima Prefecture on the island of Kyushu, Japan, and the symbol of the city of Kagoshima, which lies just across the bay. It is one of Japan's most active volcanoes, with near-continuous activity since 1955 centred on the Minamidake crater. Sakurajima forms a peninsula on the northern shore of Kagoshima Bay, within the Aira Caldera.

1914 eruption

The volcano's largest historical eruption began on 11 January 1914, after more than a century of dormancy. Earthquakes preceded the eruption, and lava flows eventually filled the strait between the island and the Ōsumi Peninsula, joining Sakurajima to the mainland. The eruption killed 58 people and produced enormous volumes of lava; the subsidence of the caldera floor was later used by scientists to understand magma chamber behaviour. A secondary peak, Shōwa crater, formed in 1946.

Activity and life with the volcano

Sakurajima typically produces hundreds of small explosive eruptions each year, raining volcanic ash on Kagoshima, where residents routinely carry umbrellas against ashfall and schoolchildren wear protective helmets. Despite the hazard, several thousand people live on the peninsula, farming giant Sakurajima daikon radishes and komikan mandarins in the volcanic soil. Ferries connect the peninsula to Kagoshima city around the clock. Volcanic activity increased at the Shōwa crater from 2006, and in 2015 the alert level was briefly raised amid fears of a larger eruption.
