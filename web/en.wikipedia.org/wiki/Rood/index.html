Rood

A rood or rood cross, sometimes known as a triumphal cross, is a large crucifix, often with attendant statues of the Virgin Mary and St John, set up above the entrance to the chancel of a medieval church. In British churches it typically stood on the rood screen, supported by a rood beam spanning the chancel arch, and might be reached by a rood stair for the tending of candles and the covering of the cross during Lent.

Etymology and use

The word derives from the Old English rōd, "pole" or "cross" — the same word used of the Holy Cross itself, as in the poem The Dream of the Rood. The term also survives in "Holyrood" (holy cross). By extension, "rood" came to name related features: the rood screen, rood loft (a gallery above the screen, used by singers or for readings), and rood stairs. In German the equivalent fixture is the Triumphkreuz, often a monumental carved group, as in Halberstadt and Lübeck cathedrals.

History

Great crosses over the chancel entrance appear from the early medieval period; the Anglo-Saxon rood at St Laurence's Church, Bradford-on-Avon, and surviving figures such as the Langford rood are rare early examples. Most medieval roods were destroyed in England during the Reformation and Civil War iconoclasm — virtually every English parish church once had one, but complete medieval roods are now extremely rare, though many screens survive. The Gothic Revival restored roods to many Anglican and Catholic churches in the 19th century, and new roods, such as George Gilbert Scott's at various cathedrals, were widely installed. In Catholic usage after the Second Vatican Council, the rood was sometimes removed or repositioned as sanctuary arrangements changed.

Surviving examples

Notable surviving medieval roods include the triumphal cross groups in German churches, the painted rood at St Fiacre in Brittany, and reconstructed roods such as that in the church of St John the Baptist, Cirencester, based on fragments. Fragments of English rood figures survive in museums, including the South Cerney rood.
