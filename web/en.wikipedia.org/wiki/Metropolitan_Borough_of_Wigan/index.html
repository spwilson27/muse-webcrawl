The Metropolitan Borough of Wigan is a metropolitan borough of Greater Manchester in North West England. It is named after the town of Wigan, its largest settlement and administrative centre, and also includes the towns of Leigh, Ashton-in-Makerfield, Hindley, Ince-in-Makerfield, Atherton, Tyldesley, Golborne, Standish, Aspull, and Orrell, among others. The borough was created on 1 April 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972, merging the former county borough of Wigan with a number of neighbouring Lancashire districts.

History and economy

The area developed rapidly during the Industrial Revolution on the Lancashire Coalfield, with coal mining, cotton mills, and engineering as the historic industries; Wigan Pier on the Leeds and Liverpool Canal became nationally famous through George Orwell's The Road to Wigan Pier (1937). Mining and textiles declined in the later 20th century, and the borough's economy shifted toward manufacturing, food production (notably Heinz's large factory at Wigan), logistics, and services.

Governance

Wigan Council provides local government services across 75 seats in 25 wards; the borough is also part of the Greater Manchester Combined Authority. It returns Members of Parliament for constituencies including Wigan, Leigh and Atherton, and Makerfield.

Sport and culture

The borough is known for rugby league (Wigan Warriors) and football (Wigan Athletic, FA Cup winners in 2013), and for the Northern soul scene centred historically on Wigan Casino.
