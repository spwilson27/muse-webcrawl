Coup d'état of December Twelfth

The coup d'état of December Twelfth (Korean: 12·12 군사 반란) was a military coup d'état that took place in South Korea on 12 December 1979. It was carried out by Major General Chun Doo-hwan and a group of officers belonging to the Hanahoe faction within the army, following the assassination of President Park Chung-hee on 26 October 1979.

Background

After Park's assassination, Prime Minister Choi Kyu-hah became acting president under the Yushin Constitution, and martial law was declared. General Jeong Seung-hwa, the martial law commander and Army Chief of Staff, was responsible for investigating the assassination, in which the director of the Korean Central Intelligence Agency, Kim Jae-kyu, was implicated. Chun Doo-hwan, head of the Defense Security Command, was placed in charge of the investigation.

The coup

On the night of 12 December 1979, Chun ordered the arrest of General Jeong on charges of involvement in Park's assassination, without the authorisation of President Choi. Troops from the 9th Division and other units loyal to Hanahoe moved into Seoul, and after a night of confrontations and gunfire around the Army headquarters and the Ministry of Defense, Jeong was arrested and the rebels prevailed. The following morning, Chun and his allies effectively controlled the military.

Aftermath

The coup consolidated Chun Doo-hwan's power within the armed forces and paved the way for the subsequent imposition of full martial law in May 1980, the suppression of the Gwangju Uprising, and Chun's accession to the presidency later in 1980. In the post-democratisation period, Chun and his successor Roh Tae-woo were prosecuted for their roles in the coup; in 1996 Chun was convicted of mutiny and treason arising from the events of December 1979 and May 1980.
