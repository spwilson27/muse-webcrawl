Slow movement (culture)

The slow movement is a cultural initiative that advocates a shift away from the fast pace of modern life, arguing for quality, sustainability and local tradition over speed and standardisation. Its best-known branch is Slow Food, founded in Italy in 1986 by Carlo Petrini in protest at a McDonald's restaurant planned near the Spanish Steps in Rome, and formally launched in Paris in 1989 with a manifesto signed by delegates from 15 countries.

Branches

From food, the philosophy spread: Cittaslow (Slow Cities, founded 1999 in Orvieto) certifies small towns that protect local character and pace of life; Slow Travel favours depth over itineraries; Slow Fashion counters fast fashion; Slow Media, slow parenting, slow scholarship and slow cinema apply the critique of acceleration to their fields. Carl Honoré's 2004 bestseller In Praise of Slow popularised the umbrella term in English.

Philosophy

"Slow" in this sense does not mean doing everything at a snail's pace, but doing things at the right speed — savouring, making connections, and resisting the industrial logic that treats time purely as a resource to be minimised. Slow Food organises around food that is "good, clean and fair", defends biodiversity through its Ark of Taste catalogue of endangered heritage foods, and convenes the Terra Madre network of food communities.

Criticism

Critics have characterised slow living as an affluent lifestyle choice, since slowness often costs money and time that lower-income people lack; defenders reply that its policy goals (local food systems, humane urban design) are broadly egalitarian.
