Order of the Rising Sun

Order of the Rising Sun
Japanese: 旭日章 (Kyokujitsu-shō)
Established: 10 April 1875
Classes: Six, from Grand Cordon to Silver Rays

The Order of the Rising Sun (旭日章, Kyokujitsu-shō) is a Japanese order, established in 1875 by Emperor Meiji. It was the first national decoration awarded by the Government of Japan and is conferred for distinguished achievements in international relations, promotion of Japanese culture, public service, and other fields.

Design

The badge depicts rays of sunlight emanating from the rising sun, executed in white enamel with a red cabochon garnet at the centre representing the sun, suspended from paulownia leaves and flowers. The design reflects the order's name and Japan's epithet as the "Land of the Rising Sun".

Classes and awards

The order originally had nine grades; since reforms in 2003 it has six classes, the highest being the Grand Cordon. Until 2003 the order was generally awarded to men, with women receiving the Order of the Precious Crown instead; after the reforms both orders were opened regardless of gender. Foreign nationals receive it for contributions to relations with Japan, and awards are announced in spring and autumn conferment ceremonies.
