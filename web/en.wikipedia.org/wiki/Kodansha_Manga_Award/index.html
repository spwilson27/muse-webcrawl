The Kodansha Manga Award (講談社漫画賞, Kōdansha Manga Shō) is an annual award for serialized manga published in the previous year, sponsored by the Japanese publisher Kodansha. It has been awarded since 1977 and is, together with the Shogakukan Manga Award and the Tezuka Osamu Cultural Prize, one of Japan's major manga honours.

Categories and history

The award began in 1977 with categories for shōnen and shōjo manga. A general (seinen/overall) category was added in 1982 and a children's category in 2003; the categories were reorganised again in 2015, with the children's prize folded into the demographic categories. Winners receive a bronze statuette and a cash prize of one million yen. Eligible works are generally those published by Kodansha, though the general category has on occasion honoured works from other publishers.

Notable winners

Past winning works include Candy Candy, Akira, Sailor Moon, Slam Dunk-era rivals in the shōnen field, Parasyte, Shōwa-shi by Shigeru Mizuki, Vinland Saga, March Comes in Like a Lion, Attack on Titan, The Quintessential Quintuplets, Blue Period and Frieren: Beyond Journey's End (from Shogakukan's magazine, in the general category).
