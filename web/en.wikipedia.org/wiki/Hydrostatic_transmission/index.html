Hydrostatic transmission

A hydrostatic transmission (HST) is a type of hydraulic drive system in which a positive-displacement pump, powered by a prime mover, drives a hydraulic motor; varying the pump's displacement varies the motor speed continuously, giving a form of continuously variable transmission. Power is transmitted by fluid pressure rather than by fluid momentum, distinguishing hydrostatic drives from hydrodynamic drives such as torque converters.

Operation

In the common closed-loop configuration, a variable-displacement axial-piston pump (controlled by tilting its swashplate) circulates fluid directly to a fixed- or variable-displacement motor and back. Passing through zero displacement reverses the output, so the transmission provides forward, neutral and reverse without clutches or gears. A small charge pump replenishes leakage and supplies control pressure and cooling flow.

Applications

Hydrostatic transmissions are standard in garden and lawn tractors, zero-turn mowers, skid-steer loaders, combine harvesters, forklifts, and many construction and agricultural machines, where precise low-speed control and frequent direction changes matter more than peak efficiency. They were also used in some diesel-hydraulic locomotives and in the Sundstrand-type drives of military vehicles. Their main drawbacks are cost and efficiency losses as heat.
