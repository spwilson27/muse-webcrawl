Maecenas

BornGaius Cilnius Maecenas, c. 70 BC
Died8 BC, Rome
OccupationAdvisor to Augustus, patron of the arts

Gaius Cilnius Maecenas (c. 70 – 8 BC) was a friend and political advisor to Octavian, the future emperor Augustus, and a celebrated patron of Roman poets. His name has become a byword for a wealthy patron of culture — a 'maecenas' — in many languages.

Patronage

Of Etruscan equestrian stock, Maecenas never sought senatorial office but wielded great informal influence. His circle included Virgil (whose Georgics are dedicated to him), Horace, to whom he gave the Sabine farm celebrated in Horace's poems, and Propertius. His gardens on the Esquiline Hill in Rome were famed. He fell out of favour in his later years but left his estate to Augustus on his death in 8 BC, the same year as Horace.
