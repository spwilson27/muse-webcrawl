Snow

FormIce-crystal precipitation

[Image: Close-up of a single perfect six-sided snowflake on dark wool]

Snow is precipitation in the form of ice crystals, which form in clouds when water vapour deposits onto microscopic nuclei below freezing. Individual snowflakes grow with hexagonal symmetry into intricate branching patterns; the saying that no two are alike reflects their sensitivity to temperature and humidity.

Effects

Accumulated snow forms snowpack that stores water for spring melt — vital for rivers and for cities below ranges like the Himalayas in the seed crawl — and, where it persists, glaciers. Snow insulates ground and reflects sunlight (a climate feedback), enables winter sports, and in blizzards and avalanches is deadly. Rain falling as snow is a threshold watched closely as climates warm.
