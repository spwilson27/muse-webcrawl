One Day International

A One Day International (ODI) is a form of limited overs cricket, played between two teams with international status, in which each team faces a fixed number of overs, currently 50. ODI matches are played in a single day and are governed by the International Cricket Council (ICC).

History

The first ODI was played on 5 January 1971 between Australia and England at the Melbourne Cricket Ground, improvised after the first three days of the Third Test were washed out; Australia won the 40-over match. The format grew rapidly in popularity, leading to the first Cricket World Cup in England in 1975. Innovations associated with ODIs include coloured clothing, white balls, day/night matches played under floodlights, and fielding restrictions (Powerplays).

Rules and status

Teams with ODI status include the ICC's full members and certain associate teams. Each bowler may bowl at most ten overs. Results in rain-shortened matches are commonly decided using the Duckworth–Lewis–Stern method. ODI records are kept separately from Test and Twenty20 records; notable records include India's and other teams' 400-plus totals and individual double centuries, first achieved by Sachin Tendulkar in 2010.
