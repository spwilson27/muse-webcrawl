Cyclic voltammetry

Cyclic voltammetry (CV) is a type of potentiodynamic electrochemical measurement. In a CV experiment, the working electrode potential is ramped linearly versus time; when the set potential is reached, the ramp is reversed, producing a cyclic potential waveform.

Method

The experiment uses a three-electrode cell (working, reference, and counter electrodes) controlled by a potentiostat. The current at the working electrode is plotted against the applied voltage to give a cyclic voltammogram, whose peaks reveal the redox potentials, reversibility, and diffusion behaviour of electroactive species.

Applications

CV is widely used in chemistry to study electron transfer kinetics and mechanisms, to measure formal potentials and diffusion coefficients, and to characterise materials for batteries, catalysts, and sensors. The Randles–Ševčík equation relates peak current to scan rate and analyte concentration.
