Crayfish

Scientific classification
Kingdom: Animalia
Phylum: Arthropoda
Class: Malacostraca
Order: Decapoda
Superfamily: Astacoidea / Parastacoidea

Crayfish are freshwater crustaceans belonging to the infraorder Astacidea, which also includes lobsters. Taxonomically, they are members of the superfamilies Astacoidea and Parastacoidea. They breathe through feather-like gills and are found in bodies of water that do not freeze to the bottom. They are able to tolerate low levels of dissolved oxygen.

In some locations, they are also known as crawdads, crawdaddies, crawfish, crawldads, freshwater lobsters, mountain lobsters, mudbugs, or yabbies. The term "crayfish" is the more common spelling in the northern United States and in much of the world, while "crawfish" predominates in the southern United States, particularly Louisiana.

Anatomy

The body of a decapod crustacean, such as a crab, lobster, or prawn (shrimp), is made up of twenty body segments grouped into two main body parts: the cephalothorax and the abdomen. Attached to the thorax are the walking legs and the large claws (chelae), used for defence and for capturing and manipulating food. Crayfish feed on animals and plants, either living or decomposing, and detritus.

Distribution and ecology

More than 600 species of crayfish are recognised. The Astacoidea are found in North America and Europe, while the Parastacoidea live in the Southern Hemisphere — South America, Madagascar, Australia and New Zealand. Australia is home to the world's largest freshwater invertebrate, the Tasmanian giant freshwater crayfish.

Some species, such as the red swamp crayfish Procambarus clarkii, have become invasive outside their native ranges, spreading through the aquarium and food trades and displacing native species.

As food

Crayfish are eaten all over the world. Like other edible crustaceans, only a small portion of the body of a crayfish is edible. In most prepared dishes, such as soups, bisques and étouffées, only the tail portion is served. In Louisiana, a crayfish boil is a traditional festive gathering at which the animals are boiled with spices, potatoes and corn.
