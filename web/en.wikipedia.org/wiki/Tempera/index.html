Tempera
Medium: Pigment mixed with egg yolk (egg tempera)
Known from: Ancient Egypt, Byzantium, early Renaissance
Notable users: Botticelli, Duccio, Andrew Wyeth

Tempera, also known as egg tempera, is a permanent, fast-drying painting medium consisting of pigments mixed with a water-soluble binder, usually glutinous material such as egg yolk. It was the primary panel-painting medium in Europe until oil paint supplanted it after 1500.

History

Tempera was used in ancient Egyptian sarcophagi, Fayum mummy portraits, Byzantine icons, and medieval and early Renaissance panels by artists such as Botticelli and Fra Angelico.

Technique

Applied in thin, hatched layers on gessoed wood, tempera dries quickly to a matte, durable finish but cannot easily achieve oil's blended colour transitions.
