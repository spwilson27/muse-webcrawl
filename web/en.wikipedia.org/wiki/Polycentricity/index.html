Polycentricity

Polycentricity
FieldsUrban studies, political science, governance
MeaningMany centres of decision-making or activity
Key scholarsVincent and Elinor Ostrom; Peter Hall
AntonymMonocentricity

Polycentricity is the principle of organisation of a region around several political, social or financial centres. The term is used in several disciplines with related meanings: in urban geography it describes metropolitan regions with multiple nuclei rather than one dominant core; in political science it describes governance systems with many independent, overlapping centres of decision-making.

Governance and the Ostroms

The concept was introduced into political science by Vincent Ostrom, Charles Tiebout and Robert Warren (1961) in a study of metropolitan governance, arguing that a patchwork of overlapping jurisdictions could deliver public services efficiently. Elinor Ostrom later made polycentric governance central to the study of common-pool resources: nested, semi-autonomous decision centres at multiple scales can monitor one another, experiment, and adapt — a framework she applied extensively to climate policy, where action by cities, states and firms supplements international agreements.

Urban and regional studies

In spatial planning, a polycentric urban region — such as the Randstad in the Netherlands or the Rhine-Ruhr in Germany — contains several cities of comparable size linked by dense flows of commuters and services. European spatial policy (notably the 1999 European Spatial Development Perspective) promoted polycentric development to spread growth more evenly and counter primate-city dominance. Critics note the concept's slipperiness: morphological polycentricity (several centres) does not guarantee functional integration, and celebrated "polycentric" regions sometimes operate as loose collections of mono-functional centres.

Other uses

In international relations, a polycentric world order denotes one with several centres of power. In organisation theory it describes structures with multiple semi-autonomous hubs.
