Naturhistorisches Museum, Vienna

The Naturhistorisches Museum Wien (NHM, Museum of Natural History) is a large natural history museum located in Vienna, Austria. Together with its architectural twin, the Kunsthistorisches Museum, it faces Maria-Theresien-Platz on the Ringstraße. The museum holds some 30 million specimens and artefacts and is one of the most important natural history museums in the world.

History

The collections descend from the imperial collections of the Habsburgs, founded from 1750 when Emperor Francis I Stephen purchased one of the largest natural history collections of the age. Emperor Franz Joseph I commissioned the present building as part of the Ringstraße project; designed in Renaissance Revival style by Gottfried Semper and Karl von Hasenauer, it opened on 10 August 1889.

Collections

Highlights include the Venus of Willendorf, a roughly 29,500-year-old figurine found in 1908; the skeleton and mount of a Steller's sea cow; extensive meteorite holdings, including the Hraschina meteorite; the gemstone and mineral collections assembled for Maria Theresa; and large zoological, botanical, geological and anthropological departments. The museum's prehistory department curates material from the Hallstatt salt mines, and its digital planetarium and rooftop tours are popular visitor attractions.

Research

Around 60 staff scientists conduct research in fields from taxonomy and mineralogy to archaeology and meteoritics. The museum publishes scientific journals and participates in international biodiversity and planetary-science programmes.
