Phife Dawg

Born: Malik Izaak Taylor, November 20, 1970, Queens, New York City, U.S.
Died: March 22, 2016 (aged 45), Oakley, California, U.S.
Occupation: Rapper
Genres: Hip hop, jazz rap

Phife Dawg was a founding member of A Tribe Called Quest.

Malik Izaak Taylor (November 20, 1970 – March 22, 2016), known professionally as Phife Dawg (or simply Phife), was an American rapper and a member of the hip hop group A Tribe Called Quest with Q-Tip and Ali Shaheed Muhammad. Known for his high-pitched, quick-witted delivery and sports references, he was a central voice on the group's landmark albums The Low End Theory (1991) and Midnight Marauders (1993).

Life and career

Taylor was born in St. Albans, Queens, New York, to Trinidadian parents. He met Q-Tip in childhood and the two formed the group that became A Tribe Called Quest in the late 1980s, part of the Native Tongues collective alongside De La Soul and the Jungle Brothers. Phife contributed memorable verses to tracks such as "Can I Kick It?", "Scenario", and "Electric Relaxation".

Solo work and health

Phife released the solo album Ventilation: Da LP in 2000. He struggled for many years with type 1 diabetes and kidney failure, receiving a kidney transplant from his wife in 2008. He died on March 22, 2016, from complications relating to diabetes. A Tribe Called Quest released their final album, We Got It from Here... Thank You 4 Your Service, later that year, completed with his recorded contributions, and a posthumous solo album, Forever, followed in 2022. A street corner in Queens was co-named Malik "Phife Dawg" Taylor Way in his honour.
