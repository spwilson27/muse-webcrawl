Convergent thinking is a term coined by psychologist Joy Paul Guilford (1956) for the type of thinking that focuses on coming up with the single, well-established answer to a problem. It emphasises speed, accuracy, and logic.

Many lines of thought converge on one correct answer.

Definition

Convergent thinking applies existing knowledge and standard rules to find a unique solution: for example, answering a multiple-choice question or solving an arithmetic problem.

Versus divergent thinking

Divergent thinking moves outward to many possible answers and is associated with creativity. Guilford's Structure of Intellect model treated both as distinct operations; effective problem solving generally requires both.

In education

Most standardised testing rewards convergent thinking, leading to criticism that schools undervalue divergent, creative responses.
