Cluster headache
Specialty: Neurology
Symptoms: Severe unilateral head pain, tearing, nasal congestion, restlessness
Duration: 15 minutes to 3 hours per attack
Frequency: 1 in 1,000 people

Cluster headache is a neurological disorder characterized by recurrent severe headaches on one side of the head, typically around the eye. There may be associated tearing, nasal congestion, or swelling around the eye on the affected side. Attacks last from 15 minutes to three hours and occur in clusters lasting weeks or months, followed by periods of remission. It is one of the trigeminal autonomic cephalgias.

Signs and symptoms

The pain is excruciating — often described as one of the most severe pains known — and sufferers are typically restless or agitated during attacks, unlike patients with migraine, who prefer to lie still. Attacks may occur up to eight times a day and often strike at the same time of day, suggesting a role for the hypothalamus and circadian rhythms.

Treatment

Acute attacks are treated with inhaled high-flow oxygen or injectable or nasal sumatriptan. Preventive treatments include verapamil, corticosteroids as transitional therapy, and the monoclonal antibody galcanezumab for episodic cluster headache.
