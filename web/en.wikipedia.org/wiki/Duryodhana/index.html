Duryodhana (Sanskrit: दुर्योधन) is a major figure and the principal antagonist of the Hindu epic Mahabharata. He was the eldest of the hundred Kaurava brothers, sons of the blind king Dhritarashtra and Queen Gandhari, and the chief rival of his cousins the Pandavas for the throne of Hastinapura. His envy and refusal to share the kingdom lead to the disastrous Kurukshetra War.

Life in the epic

From youth Duryodhana hated the Pandavas, attempting to kill Bhima by poisoning and to burn the brothers in the lacquer house of Varanavata. When Yudhishthira lost his kingdom, brothers, and wife Draupadi in the rigged dice game engineered by Duryodhana's uncle Shakuni, Duryodhana refused all later settlements, famously declaring he would not give the Pandavas even land enough to hold a needle's point. He bound the great warrior Karna to him with friendship and the gift of the kingdom of Anga.

Death

On the final day of the Kurukshetra War, after all his brothers and allies had fallen, Duryodhana fought Bhima in a mace duel. On Krishna's signal, Bhima struck him below the belt — against the rules of mace combat — shattering his thighs. Dying, Duryodhana was comforted by Ashvatthama's night raid on the Pandava camp, and he died at dawn, ascending to heaven in some tellings because he had lived as a warrior.

Character

Although the epic's villain, Duryodhana is portrayed as brave, loyal to friends, and devoted to Karna; some regional traditions, including temples in Uttarakhand and the Poruvazhy Peruviruthy Malanada temple in Kerala, worship him as a just king.
