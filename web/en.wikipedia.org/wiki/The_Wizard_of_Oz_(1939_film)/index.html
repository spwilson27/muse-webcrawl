The Wizard of Oz (1939 film)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Dorothy in her blue gingham dress with Toto, the Scarecrow, Tin Woodman and Cowardly Lion on the yellow brick road, the Emerald City ahead]

The Wizard of Oz is a 1939 American musical fantasy film produced by Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer, adapted from L. Frank Baum's 1900 novel The Wonderful Wizard of Oz. Directed principally by Victor Fleming, it stars Judy Garland as Dorothy Gale, swept by a Kansas tornado to the Land of Oz, following the yellow brick road with a Scarecrow, a Tin Woodman and a Cowardly Lion to find the Wizard — and learning there is no place like home, in the film's universally known refrain (cited here by reputation).

A modest success in 1939 (it recouped only over decades), the film became immortal through television: CBS broadcasts from 1956 made it an annual ritual for generations of American children, and its songs by Harold Arlen and Yip Harburg — 'Over the Rainbow' above all — are standards. It was among the first 25 films in the US National Film Registry.

Production

MGM bought the rights after Disney's Snow White proved fantasy could pay. The sepia Kansas opening bursts into Technicolor on Dorothy's arrival in Munchkinland — cinema's most famous transition. Production was famously troubled: director Richard Thorpe was fired, George Cukor advised, Fleming left for Gone with the Wind, King Vidor finished; Buddy Ebsen, the original Tin Woodman, was hospitalised by the aluminium makeup and replaced by Jack Haley; Margaret Hamilton (the Wicked Witch) was burned in a pyrotechnic accident.

Legacy

The ruby slippers (silver in Baum's book, red for Technicolor) sit in the Smithsonian; pairs have sold at auction for millions, one stolen pair recovered by the FBI in 2018 after 13 years. 'Over the Rainbow' topped the AFI's list of film songs. The film's meanings — queer icon (the 'friend of Dorothy' phrase), political allegory readings, pure childhood dream — keep multiplying; few works are so completely the common property of a culture.
