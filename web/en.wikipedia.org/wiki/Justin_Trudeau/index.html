Justin Pierre James Trudeau (born 25 December 1971) is a Canadian politician who served as the 23rd prime minister of Canada from 2015 to 2025 and as leader of the Liberal Party from 2013 to 2025. He is the eldest son of Pierre Trudeau, the 15th prime minister of Canada, making theirs the first father–son pair of Canadian prime ministers. Born at the prime ministerial residence during his father's tenure, Trudeau worked as a teacher before entering Parliament in 2008 as the member for Papineau. Early life and career

Trudeau was born in Ottawa on Christmas Day 1971, the son of Prime Minister Pierre Trudeau and Margaret Trudeau (née Sinclair). He was educated at Collège Jean-de-Brébeuf in Montreal, studied literature at McGill University (BA, 1994), and later completed a bachelor of education at the University of British Columbia. He taught French and mathematics in Vancouver and became a public advocate for youth service and avalanche safety after his younger brother Michel died in an avalanche in 1998. His televised eulogy for his father in 2000 brought him to national attention.

Leader of the Liberal Party

Elected to Parliament in 2008, Trudeau won the Liberal leadership in April 2013 by an overwhelming margin. In the 2015 federal election he led the Liberals from third place to a majority government — the first time a party had moved from third to first in a Canadian election — defeating the incumbent Conservatives of Stephen Harper.

Prime Minister of Canada

Trudeau's governments legalised recreational cannabis (2018), introduced a national carbon-pricing framework and the Canada Child Benefit, pursued an explicitly feminist foreign policy, presided over the renegotiation of NAFTA as the USMCA/CUSMA, and responded to the COVID-19 pandemic with large-scale income supports and vaccine procurement. His tenure was also marked by controversies, including the SNC-Lavalin affair, ethics findings over a family vacation, and images of him in blackface as a young man, which he apologised for during the 2019 campaign. The Liberals were reduced to minority governments in 2019 and 2021.

By 2024 Trudeau faced deep unpopularity amid cost-of-living pressures and a housing crisis. In January 2025, after the resignation of his finance minister Chrystia Freeland and a collapse in caucus support, he announced his intention to resign. Mark Carney won the ensuing leadership election and succeeded him as prime minister on 14 March 2025.

Resignation

Trudeau formally left office in March 2025 and did not seek re-election to Parliament in the 2025 federal election, ending nearly a decade as prime minister — the third-longest consecutive tenure of any Canadian prime minister at the time.

Personal life

Trudeau married television host Sophie Grégoire in 2005; they have three children and announced their separation in 2023. He is known for interests in snowboarding, boxing — he won a televised charity match against Senator Patrick Brazeau in 2012 — and for being the first Canadian prime minister with a visible tattoo.
