Euthyphro dilemma

The Euthyphro dilemma is found in Plato's dialogue Euthyphro, in which Socrates asks: "Is the pious loved by the gods because it is pious, or is it pious because it is loved by the gods?" In modern discussions of monotheism it is usually restated as: "Is what is morally good commanded by God because it is morally good, or is it morally good because it is commanded by God?"

The two horns

If good is good independently of God's commands, then morality appears to be a standard external to God, limiting divine omnipotence and making God's commands merely informative rather than constitutive of morality. If, on the other hand, actions are good simply because God commands them, morality seems arbitrary: had God commanded cruelty, cruelty would be good. This second horn is a classic objection to divine command theory.

Responses

Theologians and philosophers have offered many replies. A common one identifies goodness with God's unchanging nature, so that God's commands are neither arbitrary nor subject to an external standard: God commands what is good because goodness is grounded in his character. Others, following Leibniz and Robert Adams, have developed modified divine command theories, while defenders of natural law argue that moral truths are built into creation and apprehended by reason. The dilemma remains a standard starting point in metaethics and the philosophy of religion.
