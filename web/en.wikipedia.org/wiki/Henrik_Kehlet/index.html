Born: 1942, Copenhagen, Denmark
Nationality: Danish

Henrik Kehlet

Henrik Kehlet (born 1942) is a Danish surgeon and professor known as the founder of the concept of fast-track surgery, also known as Enhanced Recovery After Surgery (ERAS). His work at the University of Copenhagen and Rigshospitalet demonstrated that a multimodal, evidence-based approach to perioperative care could dramatically reduce complications, hospital stay, and recovery time after major surgery.

Career

Kehlet trained in surgery and physiology in Copenhagen and became professor of surgery at the University of Copenhagen and head of surgery at Rigshospitalet. In the 1990s he began systematically questioning traditional perioperative practices — prolonged fasting, routine bowel preparation, liberal opioid use, and enforced bed rest — and replaced them with coordinated interventions.

Fast-track surgery

The fast-track programme combines preoperative patient information, optimised anaesthesia and analgesia (including epidural and regional techniques that minimise opioids), early oral nutrition, and early mobilisation. Applied first to colonic surgery, the approach cut typical hospital stays from more than a week to two or three days while reducing medical complications. The principles were later generalised through the ERAS Society, founded in 2010, which publishes guidelines for many surgical specialties.

Research and recognition

Kehlet has published hundreds of papers on surgical pathophysiology, pain, and perioperative medicine, and has received numerous international honours for transforming postoperative care into a recognised scientific discipline.
