Alexander I of Epirus (Greek: Ἀλέξανδρος Αʹ; c. 370 BC – 331 BC), known as Alexander the Molossian, was a king of Epirus of the Aeacid dynasty. He was the son of Neoptolemus I, brother of Olympias, and thus the maternal uncle of Alexander the Great. He is sometimes seen as a western counterpart to his nephew: while Alexander the Great conquered the Persian East, Alexander of Epirus campaigned in Italy.

Reign

Philip II of Macedon placed Alexander on the Molossian throne in 342 BC, displacing his cousin Arybbas. In 337 BC Alexander married Philip's daughter Cleopatra — the wedding at Aegae at which Philip was assassinated. Although relations with Alexander the Great were initially cordial, they later cooled amid rivalry between the two courts.

Italian campaign and death

Around 334 BC the Greek city of Tarentum in southern Italy, threatened by the Lucanians and other Italic peoples, appealed to Alexander for help, just as it had earlier called on Archidamus III of Sparta. Alexander crossed the Adriatic, won a series of victories over the Lucanians and Bruttians, and took several cities, including Heraclea, Sipontum and Consentia. But his success alarmed Tarentum, and alliances shifted against him. In 331 BC he was killed at the battle of Pandosia, reportedly fulfilling — in an unexpected place — an oracle that had warned him of death at Pandosia and the river Acheron.
