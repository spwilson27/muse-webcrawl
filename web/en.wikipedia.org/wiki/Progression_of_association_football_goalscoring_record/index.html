Progression of association football goalscoring record

This article traces the progression of the world goalscoring record in association football — the player who has scored the most goals in official top-level matches at any given time. Because historical records differ in what counts as an official match, several tallies are debated, notably those of Josef Bican, Romário and Pelé.

Overview

Early record holders included English and Scottish forwards of the nineteenth century. In the twentieth century the mark passed through prolific scorers such as Imre Schlosser of Hungary, Arthur Friedenreich of Brazil, and Josef Bican, the Austrian-Czech striker whose total of more than 800 official goals was long regarded as the benchmark. Pelé's officially recognised tally surpassed 750 goals for Santos, New York Cosmos and Brazil.

Modern era

In the twenty-first century, Cristiano Ronaldo overtook Bican's recognised total (under FIFA/IFFHS criteria) and became the first player credited with more than 900 career goals in official matches, as well as the all-time top scorer in men's international football with well over 100 goals for Portugal. Lionel Messi holds the record for goals scored for a single club, with 672 for Barcelona.

Counting controversies

Different bodies count friendlies, tour matches and wartime games inconsistently, which is why rival claims — for example Romário's self-declared 1,000 goals including unofficial matches — remain contested.
