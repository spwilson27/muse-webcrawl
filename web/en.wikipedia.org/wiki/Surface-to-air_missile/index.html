Surface-to-air missile

A mobile surface-to-air missile system

Type: Anti-aircraft missile
Guidance: Radar, infrared, command, semi-active
Range: From a few km to over 400 km

A surface-to-air missile (SAM), also known as a ground-to-air missile (GTAM) or surface-to-air guided weapon (SAGW), is a missile designed to be launched from the ground or a ship to destroy aircraft, helicopters, cruise missiles, or other missiles in flight. SAMs form the backbone of modern layered air defence networks.

History

The first serious development of surface-to-air missiles began during the Second World War, when Germany experimented with weapons such as the Wasserfall and Rheintochter in response to Allied bombing. After the war, the United States, the Soviet Union, and Britain invested heavily in guided air defence. The Soviet S-75 Dvina (NATO reporting name SA-2 Guideline) became famous in 1960 when it shot down Francis Gary Powers' U-2 spy plane over the Soviet Union. SAMs proved decisive in the Vietnam War and the Yom Kippur War of 1973, forcing major changes in air tactics, electronic warfare, and suppression of enemy air defences (SEAD).

Guidance systems

SAMs use several guidance methods. Command guidance steers the missile by radio from the launch site. Semi-active radar homing missiles ride a radar beam reflected from the target by a ground radar. Active radar homing missiles carry their own radar seeker. Infrared homing missiles, common in short-range and man-portable systems (MANPADS) such as the FIM-92 Stinger and the Soviet 9K38 Igla, home on the heat of an aircraft's engines. Modern long-range systems such as the Russian S-400 and the American MIM-104 Patriot combine several guidance modes.

Classes

Systems are commonly divided by range and mobility: man-portable air-defence systems (MANPADS), short-range systems such as the British Rapier, medium-range systems such as the Norwegian-American NASAMS, and long-range strategic systems such as the S-400 and the naval Aegis/SM-6 combination. Some SAMs, including Patriot PAC-3 and the Russian S-500, are also designed for ballistic missile defence.
