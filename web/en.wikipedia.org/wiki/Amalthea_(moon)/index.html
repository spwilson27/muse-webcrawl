Amalthea (moon)

Amalthea
Discovery: 9 September 1892, by E. E. Barnard
Orbital period: 0.498 days
Dimensions: 250 × 146 × 128 km
Mean radius: 83.5 km

Amalthea is a moon of Jupiter. It has the third closest orbit around Jupiter among known moons and was the fifth moon of Jupiter to be discovered, so it is also known as Jupiter V. It was discovered on 9 September 1892 by Edward Emerson Barnard, and was the last moon discovered through direct visual observation.

Physical characteristics

Amalthea is a small, irregularly shaped body, deeply red in colour, a tint attributed to sulfur originating from Io. It is porous and icy in composition, with a very low density. Its surface is heavily cratered, with bright patches and large impact structures such as the craters Pan and Gaea. Despite its small size, Amalthea radiates more heat than it receives from the Sun, probably because of tidal heating and Jupiter's magnetospheric currents.

Orbit and rings

Amalthea orbits Jupiter at a distance of about 181,000 km in just under 12 hours, inside the orbit of Io. Its orbit is the source of the Amalthea Gossamer Ring, one of the faint components of Jupiter's ring system, formed from dust knocked off the moon by micrometeorite impacts.
