Microsurgery

Microsurgery is a general term for surgery requiring an operating microscope and specialized precision tools. It enables surgeons to operate on structures as small as blood vessels and nerves less than a millimetre in diameter, using sutures finer than a human hair.

Applications

Microsurgical techniques are used for free flap transfer in reconstructive surgery, replantation of amputated digits and limbs, peripheral nerve repair, vasectomy reversal and tubal reconstruction in fertility surgery, lymphoedema surgery (lymphaticovenular anastomosis), and in ophthalmology, neurosurgery, and otolaryngology.

History

Otolaryngologists first used microscopes in surgery in the 1920s; major advances came in the 1960s with microvascular anastomosis pioneered by Harry Buncke and others, making free tissue transfer and replantation practical. Robotic assistance is an emerging extension of the field.

Training

Microsurgery demands extensive laboratory training on models and small animals to master hand stability, instrument handling, and suturing under magnification.
