Stroke (engine)

In a reciprocating engine, the stroke is the distance travelled by the piston between its two extreme positions, top dead centre and bottom dead centre. Together with the bore (the diameter of the cylinder), the stroke determines the engine's displacement: the volume swept by one piston is π/4 × bore² × stroke.

Stroke phases

In a four-stroke engine, the cycle consists of four piston strokes: intake, compression, power (combustion/expansion) and exhaust. Two-stroke engines complete the cycle in a single crankshaft revolution. The stroke length is set by the throw of the crankshaft crankpins.

Bore/stroke ratio

Engines with bore larger than stroke are called oversquare and favoured for high rotational speeds; long-stroke (undersquare) engines tend to produce greater torque at lower speeds and can have better thermal efficiency. Steam engines likewise define stroke as the piston travel between dead centres.
