Stephen Shore (born 8 October 1947) is an American photographer known for his deadpan colour images of banal scenes and landscapes in the United States, and for his pivotal role in establishing colour photography as a legitimate artistic medium. His books American Surfaces (photographed 1972–73, published 1999) and Uncommon Places (1982) are landmarks of the New Topographics generation.

Career

A prodigy, Shore sold photographs to the Museum of Modern Art at fourteen and spent his late teens photographing Andy Warhol's Factory scene. In 1971, aged 24, he had a solo exhibition at the Metropolitan Museum of Art — almost unheard of for a living photographer. In 1972 he began a series of road trips across America, recording meals, motel rooms, streets and parking lots with a snapshot aesthetic in American Surfaces, then re-photographing the vernacular landscape with a large-format 8×10 view camera for Uncommon Places, producing coolly descriptive images that profoundly influenced later photographers, notably the Düsseldorf school of Andreas Gursky and Thomas Struth.

Teaching and later work

Since 1982 Shore has directed the photography programme at Bard College in New York. His textbook The Nature of Photographs (1998) is a widely used primer on photographic seeing. Later projects include work in Ukraine, Israel and Abu Dhabi, experiments with digital photography and print-on-demand publishing, and the retrospective exhibitions that toured from 2017.
