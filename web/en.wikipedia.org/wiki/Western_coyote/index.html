Western coyote

The western coyote refers to the coyote (Canis latrans) populations of western North America, sometimes treated as the subspecies Canis latrans latrans (the plains coyote) and related western subspecies. Coyotes are medium-sized canids native to North America, closely related to wolves and domestic dogs but generally smaller, with long legs, a narrow snout and a bushy tail.

Description and behaviour

Western coyotes typically weigh 8–20 kg, with grizzled grey, tan and reddish fur. They are opportunistic omnivores, hunting rodents, rabbits and deer as well as eating carrion, insects and plant matter. Social organisation ranges from solitary animals and mated pairs to family groups; they are famous for their vocalisations — yips, barks and howls — especially at dawn and dusk.

Distribution

Originally animals of the western plains and deserts, coyotes expanded across the whole continent during the 20th century, aided by the removal of wolves and landscape change. Western populations remain abundant in open country, deserts and mountains from Canada to Mexico, and coyotes are now also common in western cities such as Los Angeles and San Francisco.
