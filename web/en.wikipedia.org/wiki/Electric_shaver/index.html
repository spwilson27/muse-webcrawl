An electric shaver (also known as an electric razor or dry shaver) is a razor with an electrically powered oscillating or rotating blade assembly, protected by a perforated metal screen so that the blades do not contact the skin directly. Unlike conventional razors, it can be used without water, shaving cream or soap, though "wet/dry" models also permit shaving in the shower.

How it works

Hairs pass through holes or slots in a thin outer foil or rotary head and are sheared by cutters moving rapidly beneath it. Two architectures dominate: foil shavers, in which cutters oscillate side-to-side under a straight or curved foil (pioneered by Braun), and rotary shavers, in which circular cutters spin beneath round slotted heads (pioneered by Philips under the Philishave/Norelco names). Foil models are generally favoured for close shaves on flat surfaces; rotary models follow facial contours.

History

The first patent for an electric shaver was filed by John Francis O'Rourke in 1898, but the first practical device came from Colonel Jacob Schick, who patented his design in 1928 and marketed the Schick Dry Shaver in 1931. Remington introduced the first dual-head model in 1937 and the first women's model shortly after. Philips launched its rotary Philishave in 1939; the design matured into the triple-head configuration of 1951. Cordless rechargeable models became widespread from the 1960s onward.

Use and care

Electric shavers require no lather and reduce cuts, but typically shave slightly less closely than a blade and work best on shorter stubble; some users apply pre-shave lotions to raise hairs. Heads and foils are consumables, usually replaced every 12–18 months, and modern premium models use cleaning-and-charging stations. Related devices include hair clippers and beard trimmers, which use open comb-like cutters rather than foils.
