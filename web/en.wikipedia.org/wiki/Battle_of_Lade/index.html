Battle of Lade

The Battle of Lade was a naval battle that occurred in 494 BC, during the Ionian Revolt, in which the fleets of the rebellious Ionian city-states were decisively defeated by the Persian fleet off the island of Lade, near Miletus in Ionia. It was the turning point of the revolt, and led directly to the fall of Miletus and the end of the rebellion.

Background

By 494 BC the Ionian Revolt had been rolled back on land, and the Persians concentrated on Miletus, attacking it by land while a Phoenician fleet moved against the Ionian fleet assembled at Lade. The Ionian commander Dionysius of Phocaea attempted to impose rigorous training and discipline, but his harsh regime bred resentment among the crews.

The battle

When battle was joined, the Samians — secretly approached by the Persians promising leniency — deserted in mid-battle, followed by the Lesbians and others. Only the Chians fought on with heavy losses. The allied fleet disintegrated, and Dionysius fled to Sicily, where he turned to piracy against Carthaginian and Etruscan shipping.

Aftermath

Miletus fell after a siege and was sacked; its population was deported to Mesopotamia. Persian reprisals across Ionia were severe, though within a few years Persian policy softened under Mardonius. The suppression of the revolt paved the way for Darius's punitive expeditions against mainland Greece, culminating in the campaigns of Marathon (490 BC) and the Greco-Persian Wars.

Source: the battle is described by Herodotus, Histories, Book VI.
