European colonization of the Americas

The European colonization of the Americas began after Christopher Columbus reached the Caribbean in 1492 under the Spanish Crown and continued for more than three centuries, transforming the peoples, ecology and politics of two continents. The first sustained contact, however, was Norse: Vikings from Greenland and Iceland settled briefly at L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland around the year 1000, five centuries before Columbus.

Spanish and Portuguese empires

Spain rapidly conquered the Aztec Empire (1519–21) under Hernán Cortés and the Inca Empire (1532–33) under Francisco Pizarro, then built vast colonial administrations in Mexico and Peru centred on silver mining at Potosí and Zacatecas. The Treaty of Tordesillas (1494) divided the non-European world between Spain and Portugal; Portugal's share became Brazil, colonized along the Atlantic coast and expanded inland by bandeirante expeditions. Both empires established plantation economies worked by enslaved Africans transported across the Atlantic in growing numbers.

Northern European colonies

France colonized Canada and Louisiana, England established colonies along the Atlantic seaboard of North America beginning with Jamestown (1607) and Plymouth (1620), and the Dutch founded New Netherland before losing it to England. Caribbean islands changed hands repeatedly as sugar colonies of extraordinary profitability worked by enslaved labour.

Consequences

The Columbian exchange transferred crops, animals and technologies between the hemispheres, but Old World diseases such as smallpox and measles devastated Indigenous populations, which in many regions fell by half or more within generations of contact. European settlement, African slavery and Indigenous displacement created new mixed societies in the Americas. Most mainland colonies won independence between 1776 and the 1820s, beginning with the United States and Haiti and continuing with the Spanish American wars of independence.
