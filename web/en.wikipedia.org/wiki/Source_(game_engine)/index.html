Source is a 3D game engine developed by Valve, first released in October 2004 alongside Counter-Strike: Source and Half-Life 2. It succeeded the GoldSrc engine and was noted at launch for its advanced physics (using the Havok physics engine), facial animation, and scalable rendering across a wide range of hardware.

Features

Source introduced a shader-based renderer, high-dynamic-range rendering (added in 2005 with Half-Life 2: Lost Coast), realistic rigid-body physics, blended skeletal character animation with detailed facial expressions, and networked multiplayer supporting large maps. Valve updated the engine continuously in branches, adding multicore support, the "Orange Box" improvements, and Mac and Linux support.

Notable games

Beyond Valve's own titles — Half-Life 2 and its episodes, Portal and Portal 2, Team Fortress 2, Left 4 Dead, and Counter-Strike: Source and Global Offensive — Source was licensed to other developers, producing games such as Vampire: The Masquerade – Bloodlines, Titanfall and Titanfall 2 (heavily modified), Garry's Mod, and Dear Esther. The engine's modding tools (the Source SDK and Hammer editor) supported a large community of user-created modifications.

Source 2

Valve's successor engine, Source 2, debuted with Dota 2 in 2015 and powered Half-Life: Alyx (2020) and Counter-Strike 2 (2023).
