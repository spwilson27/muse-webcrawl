Golden Week (Japan)

Golden Week (Japanese: ゴールデンウィーク, Gōruden Wīku) is a cluster of Japanese national holidays in late April and early May: Shōwa Day (29 April), Constitution Memorial Day (3 May), Greenery Day (4 May) and Children's Day (5 May). Together with adjoining weekends — and bridge holidays when a weekday falls between two holidays — it forms one of Japan's longest holiday periods and one of its busiest travel seasons, alongside New Year and Obon.

History

The concentration arose from Japan's 1948 Public Holiday Law, which established several holidays in this window. The name "Golden Week" is credited to the film industry: in 1951 the Daiei Film executive Hidesaburō Matsuyama noted that the holiday week produced record box-office takings, analogous to radio's "golden time" for peak listening, and the term spread. In 2019, the imperial transition produced an exceptional ten-day Golden Week when the abdication and accession days became holidays.

Observance

Many companies close for the whole period, granting one of the few extended breaks in the Japanese working year. Transport networks, resorts and tourist sites become heavily congested; many Japanese travel domestically or overseas, while others use the week for leisure at home. Schools and public offices close on the holidays themselves.
