Ordinary Time

Ordinary Time (Latin: Tempus per annum) is the part of the liturgical year in the Roman Rite of the Catholic Church — and in similar calendars of other Western Christian churches — that falls outside the major seasons of Advent, Christmas, Lent, and Easter. Its name comes from the Latin ordinalis, referring to the numbered (ordinal) weeks, rather than meaning "common" or "unremarkable".

Structure

Ordinary Time comprises two periods totalling 33 or 34 weeks. The first begins on the Monday after the Feast of the Baptism of the Lord (the Sunday after Epiphany) and runs until the day before Ash Wednesday. The second resumes on the Monday after Pentecost and continues until the First Sunday of Advent. Sundays are numbered, for example the "Fourteenth Sunday in Ordinary Time".

Liturgical practice

The liturgical colour for Ordinary Time is green, symbolising hope and growth. The readings in this season follow a semi-continuous reading of the Synoptic Gospels in the three-year Sunday cycle (Matthew in Year A, Mark in Year B, Luke in Year C), while weekday readings follow a two-year cycle. Feasts of saints and solemnities such as Trinity Sunday, Corpus Christi, and Christ the King punctuate the season.
