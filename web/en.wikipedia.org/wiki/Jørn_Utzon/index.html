Jørn Oberg Utzon (Danish: [ˈjɶɐ̯n ˈutsʌn]; 9 April 1918 – 29 November 2008) was a Danish architect, most notable for designing the Sydney Opera House in Australia. He was awarded the Pritzker Architecture Prize, architecture's highest honour, in 2003.

Early life and education

Utzon grew up in Aalborg, Denmark, where his father was a naval engineer and director of a shipyard. Sailing and the forms of ships influenced his imagination from an early age. He studied at the Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts in Copenhagen (1937–1942), and later worked with Gunnar Asplund in Sweden and with Alvar Aalto in Finland, absorbing the Scandinavian tradition of careful craft and organic form. Travels in the United States, Mexico, China, Japan, India, and Australia exposed him to the platform architecture of the Maya, the roofs of Japanese temples, and Islamic building traditions, all of which fed his mature design approach of "additive architecture".

Sydney Opera House

In 1957 Utzon unexpectedly won the international competition for a national opera house at Bennelong Point in Sydney Harbour with what has been described as a sketch-like entry of soaring, shell-like roofs. The engineering of the shells defeated several initial approaches until Utzon arrived at his celebrated solution: all the shells are derived from sections of a single sphere, allowing the ribs to be mass-produced as precast concrete segments. Construction began in 1959 under enormous difficulty and political pressure; costs ballooned, and after a change of government and disputes with the new minister for public works, Utzon resigned in 1966 and left Australia, never to see the finished building. A team led by Peter Hall completed the interiors. The Opera House opened in 1973 and was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2007 — one of very few sites recognised during its architect's lifetime.

Later work and recognition

In Denmark, Utzon designed the Kingohusene housing at Helsingør, the Bagsværd Church (1976) — whose undulating concrete ceiling evokes clouds above a restrained brick exterior — and his own houses on Mallorca, Can Lis (1971) and Can Feliz. He also won the competition for the Kuwait National Assembly Building, completed in 1982. For decades his reputation was centred on Sydney, but late in life there was a reconciliation: Utzon was engaged to guide renovations of the Opera House, including a reception hall renamed the Utzon Room in 2004, and in 2003 he received the Pritzker Prize, whose jury called the Opera House "one of the great iconic buildings of the 20th century". Utzon died in his sleep in Helsingør on 29 November 2008, aged 90.
