Bon Echo Provincial Park

ProvinceOntario
FeatureMazinaw Rock pictographs
LakeMazinaw Lake
[Image: Mazinaw Rock from the water]
Mazinaw Rock from the water

Bon Echo Provincial Park is a provincial park in eastern Ontario, Canada, centred on Mazinaw Lake and the 100-metre Mazinaw Rock, a cliff inscribed with over 260 Indigenous pictographs — one of the largest visible collections in Canada.

Overview

The park's name ("good echo") comes from the rock's natural acoustics across the narrows. The red-ochre pictographs, painted over centuries by Algonquin and earlier peoples, are viewed from canoes and a viewing platform; the cliff was also a pilgrimage for the Group of Seven and for Flora MacDonald Denison, whose Bon Echo Inn (1910s) hosted artists and suffragists and carved Walt Whitman's words into the rock — a well-meant defacement now part of its layered history. Abes and Essens trails cross the Canadian Shield behind the cliff.
