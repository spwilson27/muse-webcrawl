Société de transport de Montréal

The Société de transport de Montréal (STM; English: Montreal Transit Corporation) is the public transport agency that operates transit services across the Island of Montreal, Quebec, Canada. It runs the Montreal Metro subway system and an extensive bus network, as well as paratransit services.

History

Public transit in Montreal dates to horse-drawn tramways in 1861 and electric streetcars from 1892. The city took over the private Montreal Tramways Company in 1951, creating the Montreal Transportation Commission. The Metro opened in 1966, ahead of Expo 67. The agency became the Société de transport de la Communauté urbaine de Montréal (STCUM) in 1970 and adopted its current name in 2002 following municipal mergers.

Services

The Metro has four lines and 68 stations, entirely underground, running on rubber-tyred rolling stock derived from the Paris Métro. The bus network comprises several hundred routes, including all-night services and express routes to Trudeau International Airport. Fares are integrated regionally through the OPUS smart card, under the planning authority of the Autorité régionale de transport métropolitain (ARTM). The STM is among the largest transit operators in Canada, carrying well over a million trips on a typical weekday.
