The 1970 FIFA World Cup was the ninth FIFA World Cup, held in Mexico from 31 May to 21 June 1970. It was the first World Cup staged in North America and the first held outside Europe and South America. Brazil won the tournament, beating Italy 4–1 in the final at the Estadio Azteca in Mexico City, and — as the first nation to win three World Cups — kept the Jules Rimet Trophy permanently. The Brazilian side captained by Carlos Alberto and starring Pelé, Jairzinho, Rivellino, Tostão and Gérson is widely regarded as the greatest national team of all time.

Innovations

The 1970 tournament introduced several lasting features. It was the first World Cup broadcast in colour, the first to use substitutions (up to two per team), and the first to use red and yellow cards — although no player was sent off during the tournament itself. The official ball was the Adidas Telstar, whose black-and-white panelled design became the iconic image of a football.

Tournament summary

Highlights included Peru's comeback from 0–2 down to beat Bulgaria 3–2, England's group-stage clash with Brazil — remembered for Gordon Banks's "save of the century" from Pelé's header — and the semi-final between Italy and West Germany at the Azteca, dubbed the "Game of the Century", which Italy won 4–3 after extra time with five goals scored in the added period. In the final, goals from Pelé, Gérson, Jairzinho and Carlos Alberto — the last culminating a celebrated team move — gave Brazil a 4–1 win over a tired Italian side.

Legacy

Mexico 1970 is frequently voted the greatest World Cup ever. Brazil's attacking football, the colour television spectacle and an average of nearly three goals per game — with no goalless draws in the knockout rounds — set a template for the modern tournament. Pelé became, and remains, the only player to win three World Cups.
