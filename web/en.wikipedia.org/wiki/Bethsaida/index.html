Bethsaida (from Hebrew/Aramaic בית צידא, beth-tsaida, "house of hunting" or "house of fishing") is a place mentioned in the New Testament. It was a fishing village on the northern shore of the Sea of Galilee, in the region of Gaulanitis, and is traditionally identified with the archaeological site of et-Tell.

In the New Testament

Bethsaida was the home town of the apostles Peter, Andrew and Philip (John 1:44). According to the Gospels it was the scene of several of Jesus's miracles, including the healing of a blind man (Mark 8:22–26) and, in some traditions, the feeding of the five thousand. Along with Chorazin and Capernaum, Bethsaida is named among the cities rebuked for not repenting despite the miracles performed there (Matthew 11:21).

History

Around 30 CE the tetrarch Philip, son of Herod the Great, raised the village to the status of a city and renamed it Julias, in honour of Julia, the daughter of Augustus (according to Josephus, for Livia-Julia, wife of Augustus). Philip was buried there in 34 CE. The site declined after the Roman period and was largely abandoned.

Archaeology

Excavations at et-Tell, begun in 1987 under Rami Arav, uncovered remains of a Hellenistic and Roman fishing village, including fishermen's houses with fishing gear, a Roman-era house with a wine cellar, and evidence of an earlier Iron Age city gate complex that some associate with the biblical kingdom of Geshur. The exact identification remains debated, with an alternative candidate at el-Araj on the shoreline.
