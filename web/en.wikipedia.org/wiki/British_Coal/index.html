British Coal
Founded: 1947 (as National Coal Board)
Defunct: 1997
Industry: coal mining

The British Coal Corporation, known as British Coal, was a nationalised industry responsible for coal mining in Great Britain. It was created as the National Coal Board in 1947 when the coal industry was taken into public ownership, and renamed British Coal in 1987. Its remaining deep mines were privatised in 1994, and the corporation was wound up in 1997.

History

At nationalisation the NCB took over more than 950 collieries employing about 700,000 miners. Mechanisation and pit closures steadily reduced the industry; the 1984–85 miners' strike, fought against a programme of closures, ended in defeat for the NUM and accelerated the rundown. By privatisation only a small number of deep mines remained.

Legacy

British Coal's liabilities, including subsidence damage and miners' health compensation schemes, passed to the Coal Authority and the government.
