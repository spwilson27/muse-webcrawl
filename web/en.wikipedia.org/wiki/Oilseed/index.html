Oilseeds are seeds grown primarily for the production of edible and industrial oils. They form one of the pillars of world agriculture: the oil is used for cooking, food processing, cosmetics and increasingly biofuels, while the residual meal — rich in protein — is the principal protein source in livestock feed worldwide.

Major oilseeds

The soybean is by far the most important oilseed by volume, grown chiefly in the United States, Brazil and Argentina. Rapeseed (in its low-erucic-acid form, canola) is the main oilseed of Canada, Europe, China and India. Sunflower seed dominates in Ukraine, Russia and Argentina. Other significant oilseeds include peanuts, cottonseed, palm kernels, copra (coconut), linseed (flax), sesame and castor beans.

Processing

Oil is extracted by mechanical pressing, solvent extraction (usually with hexane) or a combination of both. Crude oil is then refined — degummed, neutralised, bleached and deodorised — for food use. Global vegetable oil output exceeds 200 million tonnes annually, and oilseed markets are among the most actively traded agricultural commodities, with benchmark prices set on exchanges such as the Chicago Board of Trade.
