AFL–CIO

American Federation of Labor and Congress of Industrial Organizations
Founded: 5 December 1955
Headquarters: Washington, D.C., US
Membership: about 12.5 million (affiliated unions)
First president: George Meany

The American Federation of Labor and Congress of Industrial Organizations (AFL–CIO) is the largest federation of unions in the United States, made up of dozens of national and international unions, together representing millions of workers.

History

The AFL–CIO was formed in 1955 by the merger of the American Federation of Labor (AFL), founded in 1886, and the Congress of Industrial Organizations (CIO), founded in 1935 as a rival federation organising workers by industry rather than by craft. The merger reunited the American labour movement after two decades of competition.

Activities

The federation coordinates political action, lobbying, and organizing campaigns on behalf of affiliated unions, and takes positions on issues such as wages, workplace safety, trade, and labour law reform. It does not itself bargain collectively; that is done by its member unions.
