German Revolution of 1918–1919

DateNovember 1918 – August 1919
LocationGermany
OutcomeWeimar Republic proclaimed; monarchy abolished

The German Revolution of 1918–1919 (German: Novemberrevolution) was the sequence of events that brought an end to the German Empire in the final days of the First World War and replaced it with the democratic Weimar Republic.

Origins

Military defeat, food shortages, and war weariness radicalised the population. When naval commanders ordered a final sortie of the High Seas Fleet in late October 1918, sailors mutinied at Wilhelmshaven and Kiel, and workers' and soldiers' councils formed throughout the country.

November 1918

Chancellor Max von Baden announced the Kaiser's abdication on 9 November; Philipp Scheidemann proclaimed a republic from the Reichstag, while Karl Liebknecht proclaimed a socialist republic from the Berlin Palace. Friedrich Ebert's Council of the People's Deputies governed until elections.

Consolidation

The Spartacist uprising of January 1919 and the short-lived Bavarian Soviet Republic were suppressed by government forces and Freikorps. The Weimar Constitution, adopted in August 1919, formally completed the transition.
