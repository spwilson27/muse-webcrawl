The coyote (Canis latrans) is a species of canine native to North America. It is smaller than its close relative, the gray wolf, and slightly larger than its other close relatives, foxes. The coyote fills much of the same ecological niche as the golden jackal does in Eurasia. Its name comes from the Nahuatl word coyōtl, and its scientific name means "barking dog", a reference to its wide repertoire of vocalisations — yips, howls and barks — heard most often at dusk and dawn.

Description

Coyotes typically weigh 8–20 kg, with males slightly larger than females; northern subspecies are larger than southern ones. The fur is long and coarse, generally a grizzled buff and gray, though colour varies from almost white in snowy regions to reddish in desert ones. The tail is bushy with a black tip, and the ears are relatively large in proportion to the head.

Distribution and habitat

Originally confined largely to the arid plains and deserts of western North America, the coyote has expanded dramatically over the past two centuries, aided by the decline of wolves and by human landscape change. It now ranges from Alaska and Canada through the continental United States and Mexico into Central America as far south as Panama, and inhabits deserts, prairies, forests, mountains and increasingly urban and suburban areas, including large cities such as Los Angeles and Chicago.

Behaviour and ecology

The coyote is a highly flexible omnivore and opportunist. Its diet consists mainly of small mammals — rabbits, rodents and carrion — supplemented by deer (often fawns or scavenged), snakes, insects, fruit and vegetable matter. Social organisation is flexible: coyotes may live in family packs centred on a mated pair, or hunt alone or in loose pairs. They are generally monogamous. Pups, usually four to seven per litter, are born in spring in an underground den.

Coyotes occasionally interbreed with wolves and domestic dogs, producing hybrids sometimes called coywolves or coydogs. Despite intensive persecution, including government eradication campaigns that killed hundreds of thousands, the species remains abundant and is classified as Least Concern.

In culture

The coyote is a prominent figure in the mythology of many Native American peoples, where Coyote appears as a trickster, creator or culture hero. In modern popular culture it is familiar as Wile E. Coyote, the perpetually unsuccessful pursuer of the Road Runner in Warner Bros. cartoons.
