Karkonosze National Park

Karkonosze National Park (Polish: Karkonoski Park Narodowy) is a national park in the Karkonosze (Giant Mountains) of south-western Poland, along the border with the Czech Republic. Created in 1959, it protects the highest and most distinctive part of the Sudetes range, including Śnieżka (1,603 m), the highest peak of the Czech Republic and of the range. Together with the adjoining Czech Krkonoše National Park, it forms a cross-border protected area and a UNESCO biosphere reserve.

Landscape

The park is known for its glacial cirques (such as Śnieżne Kotły, the "Snowy Cirques"), granite rock formations and tors, alpine tundra vegetation, subalpine dwarf pine scrub, and raised peat bogs on the summit plateau. Waterfalls including Kamieńczyk and Szklarka are popular destinations. The Elbe river rises on the Czech side of the range near the park boundary.

Nature

The Karkonosze support relict arctic-alpine plant species left from the Ice Ages, along with mouflon (introduced in the 19th century), red deer, and birds such as the alpine accentor and bluethroat. Spruce forests in the park were severely damaged by air pollution and acid rain in the 1970s–80s — the so-called "Black Triangle" ecological disaster — and are now the subject of long-term restoration.

Tourism

The towns of Karpacz and Szklarska Poręba are the main gateways. A dense network of marked trails crosses the park, and a chairlift from Karpacz serves the slopes below Śnieżka, whose summit carries a meteorological observatory and the disc-shaped refuge buildings that are icons of the range.
