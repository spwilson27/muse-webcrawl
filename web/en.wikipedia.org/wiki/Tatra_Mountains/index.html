The Tatra Mountains (Slovak and Polish: Tatry), or simply the Tatras, are a mountain range that forms a natural border between Slovakia and Poland. They are the highest mountain range in the Carpathian Mountains. The Tatras are distinct from the Low Tatras (Slovak: Nízke Tatry), a separate Slovak mountain range further south.

Approximately 785 square kilometres (303 sq mi) of the Tatras lie within Slovakia and about 278 square kilometres (107 sq mi) within Poland, with roughly three-quarters of the range in Slovakia and one-quarter in Poland. The highest peak, called Gerlachovský štít (2,655 m or 8,711 ft), is located north of Poprad, entirely in Slovakia. The highest point in Poland, Rysy (2,499 m or 8,199 ft), is located near Morskie Oko lake, on the Polish–Slovak border.

Geology

The Tatras were formed during the Alpine orogeny. Their core consists mainly of granitic rocks, raised and exposed by uplift and glacial erosion during the Pleistocene ice ages, which carved the sharp ridges, deep valleys, and numerous tarn lakes — such as Morskie Oko and Štrbské pleso — that characterise the range today.

Divisions

The range is conventionally divided into the Western Tatras, the High Tatras (the highest and most visited portion, lying along the border), and the Belianske Tatras in the north-east. The Low Tatras, although a separate range to the south, are sometimes loosely associated with the Tatras as a whole.

Nature protection

Both countries protect the range through paired national parks: Slovakia's Tatra National Park (TANAP, established 1949) and Poland's Tatra National Park (TPN, established 1954). Together they form a UNESCO transboundary biosphere reserve. The parks protect endemic species such as the Tatra chamois (Rupicapra rupicapra tatrica) and the Tatra marmot, as well as dwarf pine, spruce forests, and alpine meadows.

Tourism

The Tatras are a major centre for hiking, climbing, and skiing in Central Europe. On the Slovak side, resorts include Štrbské Pleso, Starý Smokovec, and Tatranská Lomnica; on the Polish side the principal resort town is Zakopane, the "winter capital of Poland". A network of mountain huts and marked trails serves hikers on both sides of the border.
