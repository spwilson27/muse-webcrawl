The Lorax is a children's book written and illustrated by Theodor Seuss Geisel under the pen name Dr. Seuss, published in 1971. It chronicles the plight of the environment and the Lorax, a small, orange, mustachioed creature who "speaks for the trees" and confronts the Once-ler, a businessman who destroys the environment to manufacture a versatile garment called a Thneed.

Plot

A young boy living in a polluted area visits the Once-ler, who recounts how he arrived in a lush valley of Truffula trees, began chopping them down to knit Thneeds despite the Lorax's warnings, and expanded his factory until the last tree fell. The pollution drove away the Bar-ba-loots, Swomee-Swans and Humming-Fish, and the Lorax departed, leaving only a pile of rocks inscribed "UNLESS". The Once-ler gives the boy the last Truffula seed, urging him to plant it and restore the valley.

Themes

The book is one of Seuss's most explicitly political works, addressing deforestation, industrial pollution and corporate greed. Its closing message — "Unless someone like you cares a whole awful lot, nothing is going to get better. It's not." — has become a widely quoted environmental slogan.

Adaptations

The story was adapted as an animated television special in 1972 and as a computer-animated feature film, Dr. Seuss' The Lorax, released by Illumination Entertainment in 2012 with Danny DeVito voicing the Lorax. A stage musical premiered at the Old Vic in London in 2015.
