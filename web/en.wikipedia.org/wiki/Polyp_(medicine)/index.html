Polyp (medicine)

In medicine, a polyp is an abnormal growth of tissue projecting from a mucous membrane. Polyps may be attached by a narrow stalk (pedunculated) or grow directly on the surface (sessile).

Sites

Polyps occur in the colon and rectum, nose and sinuses, stomach, uterus (endometrial polyps), cervix, vocal cords, and gallbladder. Colorectal polyps are especially significant because adenomatous polyps can develop into colorectal cancer; they are commonly removed during colonoscopy (polypectomy) and examined histologically.

Classification

Colorectal polyps are classified as neoplastic (adenomas, with malignant potential) or non-neoplastic (hyperplastic, inflammatory, hamartomatous). Hereditary conditions such as familial adenomatous polyposis cause hundreds of polyps and a very high lifetime risk of cancer.
