Salve Regina


Text: attributed to Hermann of Reichenau (traditionally)
Language: Latin
Type: Marian antiphon

The Salve Regina (Latin: [ˈsalve reˈdʒina], "Hail Queen"), also known as the Hail, Holy Queen, is a Marian hymn and one of four Marian antiphons sung at different seasons within the Christian liturgical calendar of the Catholic Church. It is traditionally sung at the end of a priest's day in the Divine Office (Compline) from the Saturday before Trinity Sunday until the Friday before the first Sunday of Advent.

Text and origins

The antiphon traditionally attributed to the eleventh-century monk Hermann of Reichenau addresses Mary as queen and mother of mercy: "Salve, Regina, mater misericordiae: vita, dulcedo, et spes nostra, salve" — "Hail, Queen, mother of mercy: our life, sweetness, and hope, hail." The prayer describes the faithful as "exiled children of Eve" (exules filii Evae) sighing and weeping "in this valley of tears" (in hac lacrimarum valle), and asks Mary to turn her eyes of mercy toward them and show them Jesus, "the blessed fruit of thy womb."

Usage

Tradition relates that Saint Bernard of Clairvaux added the concluding invocation "O clemens, O pia, O dulcis Virgo Maria" ("O clement, O loving, O sweet Virgin Mary"). The Salve Regina is prayed at the conclusion of the Rosary and is commonly sung at funerals and during processions. It has been set to music by numerous composers across the centuries, and it serves as the namesake and school song of Salve Regina University in Newport, Rhode Island.
