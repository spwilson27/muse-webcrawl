LocationPortrack House, near Dumfries, Scotland
Area30 acres (12 ha)

DesignersCharles Jencks and Maggie Keswick Jencks

The Garden of Cosmic Speculation is a 30-acre sculpture garden created by landscape architect and theorist Charles Jencks and his wife Maggie Keswick Jencks at Portrack House, near Dumfries in south-west Scotland. Beginning in 1989, it is one of the most ambitious private gardens of the modern era.

Design

Unlike conventional gardens, the Garden of Cosmic Speculation uses the forms of nature and mathematics to explore fundamental scientific and cosmological ideas: black holes, fractals, DNA double helixes, string theory, the Big Bang, and the quasicrystal. Sweeping sculpted landforms, spiralling mounds, artificial lakes and aluminium sculptures are arranged as a microcosm of the universe.

Features include the Universe Cascade, a staircase of 25 landings charting the history of the universe from the Big Bang to the present; the Snail Mound and the Fractal Terrace; and the Garden of the Six Senses.

Access

The garden is private and traditionally opened to the public only one day a year, raising money for Maggie's Centres, the cancer-care charity founded by Maggie Keswick Jencks before her death in 1995. Charles Jencks also published a book of the same name describing the garden.
