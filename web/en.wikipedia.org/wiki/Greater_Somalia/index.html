Greater Somalia (Somali: Soomaaliweyn) is an irredentist concept that seeks to unite in one state all ethnic Somali-inhabited regions: Somalia itself, Djibouti, the Ogaden region of Ethiopia, the North Eastern Province of Kenya, and sometimes the island of Socotra. It was a central goal of Somali nationalism at independence in 1960 and is symbolised by the five-pointed star on the Somali flag, each point representing one of these territories.

History

When British Somaliland and Italian Somaliland united to form the Somali Republic on 1 July 1960, the new state adopted the goal of uniting all Somalis. This brought it into conflict with Ethiopia and Kenya. The Shifta War in Kenya (1963–1967) and clashes with Ethiopia culminated in the Ogaden War of 1977–1978, in which Somalia invaded Ethiopia to seize the Ogaden but was defeated after the Soviet Union and Cuba intervened on Ethiopia's side.

Legacy

The defeat in the Ogaden War weakened the Somali state and the Greater Somalia project. Following the collapse of the Somali government in 1991, irredentist ambitions largely faded as an official policy, though the five-pointed star remains on the national flag.
