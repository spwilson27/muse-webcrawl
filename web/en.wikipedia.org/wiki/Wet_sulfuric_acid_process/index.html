Wet sulfuric acid process

The wet sulfuric acid process (WSA process) is a technology for producing concentrated sulfuric acid from sulfur-containing gases, most notably the hydrogen sulfide (H2S) off-gases of oil refineries and sour-gas plants. It was commercialised by the Danish company Haldor Topsoe in the 1980s.

Process description

Unlike the conventional contact process, which requires the feed gas to be dried, the WSA process works with wet gas. Sulfur compounds are first combusted to sulfur dioxide (SO2) at around 1,000 °C. The gas then passes over a vanadium pentoxide catalyst, converting SO2 to sulfur trioxide (SO3). The SO3 reacts with water vapour already present in the gas to form gaseous sulfuric acid, which is condensed in a glass-tube condenser cooled by air, yielding commercial-grade acid of 93–98% strength.

Advantages and applications

The process is highly exothermic and exports surplus energy as steam, needs no gas drying or acid circulation for absorption, and captures essentially all the sulfur, making it attractive for environmental compliance. It is used in refineries, coke ovens, metallurgical plants, and for regenerating spent sulfuric acid from alkylation units, commonly under the trade name SNOX/WSA variants marketed by Topsoe (now Topsoe A/S).
