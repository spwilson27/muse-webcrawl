Acetaldehyde

FormulaC2H4O (CH3CHO)
Molar mass44.05 g/mol
AppearanceColourless liquid
Boiling point20.2 °C
CAS number75-07-0

Acetaldehyde (systematic name ethanal) is an organic chemical compound with the formula CH3CHO. It is a colourless, volatile liquid with a pungent, fruity odour, and is one of the most important aldehydes, occurring widely in nature and produced on a large scale in industry.

Occurrence and metabolism

Acetaldehyde occurs naturally in coffee, bread, and ripe fruit. In the human body it is the first metabolite of ethanol: the enzyme alcohol dehydrogenase converts ethanol to acetaldehyde, which is then broken down to acetate by aldehyde dehydrogenase. Acetaldehyde is more toxic than ethanol itself and is responsible for many hangover symptoms; it is classified as a carcinogen in the context of alcoholic beverages.

Production

Industrially, acetaldehyde is produced mainly by the Wacker process, the palladium/copper-catalysed oxidation of ethylene. It was historically also made by hydration of acetylene and by partial oxidation or dehydrogenation of ethanol.

Uses

Acetaldehyde is a precursor to acetic acid, pyridine derivatives, pentaerythritol, and numerous other chemicals. It is also used in the synthesis of fragrances and as an intermediate in the manufacture of resins and disinfectants.
