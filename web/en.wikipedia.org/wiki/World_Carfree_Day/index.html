World Carfree Day

DateSeptember 22
First observed2000 (as a global event)
PurposePromote alternatives to car dependence: walking, cycling, public transport
RelatedEuropean Mobility Week

World Carfree Day is an annual event held on September 22 in cities around the world, on which motorists are encouraged to give up their cars for the day. Organized events take place in thousands of cities in dozens of countries, often involving the temporary closure of streets to motor traffic so that residents can experience their neighborhoods on foot, by bicycle and by public transport.

History

Car-free days began as responses to the 1970s oil crisis, when several European countries restricted Sunday driving. National car-free days were organized in the 1990s in Iceland, the United Kingdom and France — France's "En ville, sans ma voiture!" launched in 1998 — and the European Commission incorporated them into European Mobility Week. In 2000 the World Carfree Network promoted September 22 as a self-organized global day of action, and the date has since been observed annually, with large events in cities such as Bogotá (which pioneered its weekly Ciclovía and a citywide car-free day) and Paris.

Goals and criticism

Supporters aim to highlight air and noise pollution, road danger, and the amount of urban space devoted to cars, and to demonstrate the feasibility and benefits of alternatives. Critics argue that one-off closures can inconvenience residents and businesses and may have only symbolic effect unless paired with lasting infrastructure and policy changes. Nevertheless, some cities have used car-free days as a step toward permanent pedestrianization measures.
