Procedural drama

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A procedural drama is a genre of television programming in which a problem is introduced, investigated, and solved within the same episode, in contrast to the serial drama's continuing story arcs. These dramas are defined by an emphasis on the procedures and technical detail of a professional field — most often police work, law, medicine, or forensic science — and by largely self-contained episodes that make them well suited to syndication and casual viewing.

Subgenres

  • Police procedurals: from early examples such as Dragnet to the Law & Order, CSI, and NCIS franchises.
  • Legal dramas: series built around cases of the week in courtrooms and law firms.
  • Medical procedurals: hospital series in which a case is diagnosed and resolved per episode, such as House.
  • Forensic and military variants: combining investigative procedure with laboratory or military settings.

Format

The classic procedural episode follows a stable structure: a crime or crisis is discovered (often before the title sequence), the professional team investigates with characteristic tools and jargon, false leads are pursued, and the case is resolved by the end of the episode, restoring order. Character development and personal storylines tend to accumulate slowly in the background. Although characteristically episodic, many modern procedurals incorporate serialised elements and season-long arcs, a hybrid sometimes called the "procedural-serial".
