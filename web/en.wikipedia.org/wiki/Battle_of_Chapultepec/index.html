Battle of Chapultepec

The Battle of Chapultepec was fought on 13 September 1847 during the Mexican–American War, at Chapultepec Hill and its castle on the western approach to Mexico City. United States forces under General Winfield Scott stormed the position, defended by Mexican troops and cadets of the Military Academy, and opened the way to the occupation of the Mexican capital the following day. The battle is remembered in Mexico for the six teenage cadets, the Niños Héroes ("Boy Heroes"), who died defending the castle.

Background

After victories at Contreras, Churubusco and Molino del Rey in August and early September 1847, Scott's army stood before Mexico City. The western gates were guarded by Chapultepec, a fortified hill topped by a castle that housed the Mexican Military Academy. Scott chose to attack from the west rather than force the southern causeways. Following a day-long artillery bombardment on 12 September, American infantry assaulted the hill at dawn on 13 September.

The battle

Storming parties from the divisions of Gideon Pillow and John Quitman scaled the steep slopes under fire, carrying scaling ladders over the walls. The fighting was fierce and often hand-to-hand; among the U.S. officers wounded was Lieutenant George Pickett, and Second Lieutenant Ulysses S. Grant served a howitzer in a church tower during the advance toward the city gates. The defenders, outnumbered, were driven from the castle by about 9:30 a.m. General Nicolás Bravo was captured. According to Mexican tradition, six cadets — Juan de la Barrera, Juan Escutia, Agustín Melgar, Vicente Suárez, Fernando Montes de Oca and Francisco Márquez — refused to retreat and died at their posts; legend holds that Escutia wrapped himself in the Mexican flag and leapt from the roof to keep it from capture. Santa Anna watched the defeat from a distance, remarking that if the position had been defended as it should have been, the outcome would have differed.

Aftermath

With Chapultepec taken, U.S. troops advanced along the causeways and fought through the Belén and San Cosme gates; Scott entered Mexico City on 14 September. The occupation lasted until the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo ended the war in 1848. In the United States the battle is commemorated in the opening line of the Marines' Hymn, "From the Halls of Montezuma", referring to the castle; in Mexico the Niños Héroes remain national symbols, honoured by a major monument at the foot of Chapultepec Hill, and 13 September is a civic holiday.
