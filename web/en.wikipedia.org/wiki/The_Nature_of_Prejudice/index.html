The Nature of Prejudice

Author: Gordon W. Allport
Published: 1954
Publisher: Addison-Wesley
Subject: Social psychology

The Nature of Prejudice is a 1954 book by American psychologist Gordon W. Allport, one of the most influential works in the social psychology of intergroup relations. Drawing on the research of the previous decades, Allport systematized the causes, forms, and reduction of prejudice.

Main ideas

Allport defined prejudice as an antipathy based on faulty and inflexible generalization. He proposed a five-point scale of prejudiced action, from antilocution (hostile talk) through avoidance, discrimination, and physical attack to extermination. He analysed the roles of categorization, scapegoating, conformity, and personality — including his earlier work on the authoritarian personality — and examined prejudice against many targets, not only racial groups.

The contact hypothesis

The book's most famous legacy is the contact hypothesis: under conditions of equal status, common goals, intergroup cooperation, and institutional support, contact between groups tends to reduce prejudice. Decades of subsequent research, including large meta-analyses, have broadly supported the theory, and it underpins many desegregation and reconciliation programs.
