Shō

TypeFree-reed mouth organ
OriginJapan
Used inGagaku

The shō (笙) is a Japanese free-reed mouth organ that is a key instrument in gagaku, the ancient court music of Japan. It produces a soft, ethereal cluster of tones and is said to imitate the cry of the mythical phoenix.

Construction

The shō consists of 17 bamboo pipes of differing lengths set into a lacquered wooden wind chest. Fifteen of the pipes carry metal free reeds; originally two pipes were silent and included for symmetry, although modern instruments may make all 17 functional. The player's breath passes through a mouthpiece, and holes on the pipes are covered to select which reeds sound.

Technique and repertoire

Unlike most wind instruments, the shō sounds on both inhalation and exhalation, allowing an unbroken tone. In gagaku ensembles the shō plays sustained chord clusters called aitake, which shift gradually to colour the harmony above the melody instruments. Contemporary composers such as Tōru Takemitsu have also written for the instrument outside the traditional repertoire.
