Old Faithful is a cone geyser in Yellowstone National Park in Wyoming, United States. It was named in 1870 during the Washburn–Langford–Doane Expedition and was the first geyser in the park to be named. It is Yellowstone's most famous feature and one of the most predictable large geysers in the world.

Eruptions

Eruptions occur roughly every 90 minutes, though the interval has lengthened from about 65 minutes in earlier decades, partly owing to earthquakes and subterranean changes. Each eruption expels 3,700 to 8,400 US gallons of boiling water to a height of 106 to 185 feet, lasting 1.5 to 5 minutes. Park rangers predict eruption times within a window of about ten minutes, based on the duration of the previous eruption.

History and setting

The geyser stands in the Upper Geyser Basin, which contains the world's largest concentration of geysers. The Old Faithful Inn, built in 1903–1904 from local lodgepole pine and stone, is a landmark of rustic "parkitecture" and one of the largest log buildings in the world. Despite the name, Old Faithful is neither the tallest nor the largest geyser in the park — that distinction belongs to Steamboat Geyser — but its regularity made it an icon of Yellowstone and of the national park idea.
