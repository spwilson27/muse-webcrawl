Reflexology


A reflexology chart of the foot.

Reflexology, also known as zone therapy, is an alternative medicine practice involving the application of pressure to specific points on the feet and hands. It is based on a pseudoscientific system of zones and reflex areas that purportedly reflect an image of the body on the feet and hands, with the premise that such work on the feet and hands causes a physical change to the supposedly related areas of the body.

History

Modern reflexology was developed in the United States in the early 20th century. Physician William H. Fitzgerald proposed "zone therapy," dividing the body into ten longitudinal zones; Eunice Ingham refined the approach in the 1930s and 1940s, mapping the whole body onto the feet. Similar foot-massage practices have older roots in China and Egypt, though the continuity claimed by practitioners is disputed.

Evidence and regulation

Systematic reviews have found no convincing scientific evidence that reflexology is effective for any medical condition, beyond possible short-term relaxation or placebo effects. There is no consensus among reflexologists on how the practice is supposed to work; a common explanation invokes the flow of "energy" (qi). Regulation varies widely: in some jurisdictions reflexologists are licensed, while elsewhere the field is unregulated. Medical bodies generally caution that reflexology should not replace conventional treatment.
