SubjectPedigrees of Thoroughbred horses in Britain and Ireland

The General Stud Book is the official register of Thoroughbred broodmares and stallions in Great Britain and Ireland, published by Weatherbys since the late 18th century. It is the foundation stud book of the Thoroughbred breed: a horse is generally recognised as a Thoroughbred only if its pedigree can be traced to stock recorded in the General Stud Book or an approved foreign equivalent.

An early volume of the General Stud Book.

History

James Weatherby, secretary to the Jockey Club, published An Introduction to a General Stud Book in 1791, followed by the first volume of the General Stud Book proper in 1793, recording the pedigrees of 387 mares. Subsequent volumes appeared at intervals of several years, documenting the breeding stock from which all modern Thoroughbreds descend. The register famously traces the breed's ancestry to three foundation sires imported into England around the turn of the 18th century: the Byerley Turk, the Darley Arabian, and the Godolphin Arabian.

Role in the breed

The General Stud Book established the "closed stud book" model, in which registration requires both parents to be registered. This closed population, selected intensively for racing speed, produced the modern Thoroughbred. National stud books in other racing countries operate on the same principle under the oversight of the International Stud Book Committee.
