Timișoara

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Timișoara is a city in western Romania, the capital of Timiș County and the historical centre of the Banat region. It is one of the country's largest cities, with a population of roughly 250,000–300,000, and an important industrial, university and cultural centre near the borders with Serbia and Hungary.

History

The settlement grew around a fortress on the Bega River, passing through Hungarian, Ottoman and Habsburg rule. Under Austrian administration in the 18th century it was rebuilt as a fortified city. Timișoara was the first city in mainland Europe to have electric street lighting, in 1884, and one of the first to introduce horse-drawn and later electric trams.

The 1989 revolution

Timișoara is best known internationally as the starting point of the Romanian Revolution of December 1989. Protests in defence of the Reformed pastor László Tőkés grew into a general uprising; the army fired on demonstrators, and on 20 December 1989 Timișoara declared itself the first city in Romania free of communism. The revolution spread to Bucharest and led within days to the fall and execution of Nicolae Ceaușescu.

Culture

The city's landmarks include Victory Square (Piața Victoriei), flanked by the Orthodox Metropolitan Cathedral and the Opera House; Union Square with its baroque palaces; and the Bastion Theresia, a remnant of the Habsburg fortress. Timișoara held the title of European Capital of Culture in 2023. Its multicultural heritage — Romanian, Hungarian, German, Serbian and Jewish — is reflected in its architecture, theatres and universities, including the West University and the Polytechnic University.
