The New York Yacht Club (NYYC) is a private social club and yacht club based in New York City and Newport, Rhode Island. Founded in 1844, it is widely regarded as one of the most prestigious yacht clubs in the world. The club is famous for having held the America's Cup trophy for 132 years, the longest winning streak in the history of international sport, from 1851 until 1983.

History

The club was founded on July 30, 1844, aboard the schooner Gimcrack, anchored off the Battery in New York Harbor, by John Cox Stevens and eight other gentlemen. Stevens became the club's first commodore. In 1851, the club's syndicate-owned schooner America crossed the Atlantic and won a race around the Isle of Wight against a fleet of British yachts, winning the trophy that was subsequently named the America's Cup. The club successfully defended the Cup in a series of challenges until 1983, when the Australian challenger Australia II, representing the Royal Perth Yacht Club, won the series and ended the streak.

Clubhouses

The club's main clubhouse at 37 West 44th Street in Midtown Manhattan, completed in 1901 to designs by Warren and Wetmore, is a celebrated Beaux-Arts building famous for its model room, which houses a large collection of ship models, including the half-hull models of America's Cup yachts. The building is a designated New York City landmark. The club also maintains Harbour Court, a waterfront property in Newport, Rhode Island, acquired in the 1980s, which serves as its on-the-water clubhouse for regattas.

Regattas

The NYYC organizes numerous regattas, including the New York Yacht Club Annual Regatta, first held in 1845, Race Week at Newport, and the club has been involved in events such as the Transatlantic Race. The club has also fielded entries in modern America's Cup campaigns under the name American Magic.
