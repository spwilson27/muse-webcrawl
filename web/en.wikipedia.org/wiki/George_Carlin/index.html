George Carlin

George Denis Patrick Carlin (May 12, 1937 – June 22, 2008) was an American stand-up comedian, actor, author, and social critic.

Born: New York City, U.S.
Died: June 22, 2008, Santa Monica, California
Occupation: Comedian, actor, author
Years active: 1956–2008

George Carlin was regarded as one of the most important and influential stand-up comedians of all time. He was known for his dark comedy and his reflections on politics, the English language, psychology, religion, and taboo subjects. His "seven dirty words" routine was central to the 1978 United States Supreme Court case F.C.C. v. Pacifica Foundation, which affirmed the government's power to regulate indecent material on public airwaves.

Early life

Carlin was born in Manhattan, the son of Mary and Patrick John Carlin. He grew up in the Morningside Heights neighborhood and attended Cardinal Hayes High School, from which he was expelled. He joined the United States Air Force as a radar technician, stationed at Barksdale Air Force Base in Louisiana, where he began working as a disc jockey at a local radio station.

Career

Carlin formed a comedy team with Jack Burns in the late 1950s, performing in coffee houses and on television. After the duo split amicably, Carlin reinvented himself in the early 1970s as a counterculture comedian, growing his hair and beard and adopting the routines that made him famous. His album FM & AM (1972) won a Grammy Award, and Class Clown (1972) introduced the "Seven Words You Can Never Say on Television."

He hosted the first episode of Saturday Night Live in 1975 and performed 14 HBO comedy specials between 1977 and 2008. He also acted in films including Bill & Ted's Excellent Adventure (1989), in which he played Rufus, and voiced characters in animated works. He narrated the children's series Thomas & Friends in the United States.

Style and themes

Carlin's comedy centered on wordplay, language and euphemism, and a skeptical, often cynical view of institutions, religion, and American politics. Later specials such as Jammin' in New York (1992) and It's Bad for Ya (2008) mixed observational humor with philosophical monologues. He frequently remarked that his job was merely to point out that "the planet is fine — the people are fouled up."

Death and legacy

Carlin died of heart failure on June 22, 2008, in Santa Monica, California, at age 71. He had a long history of heart problems, including three heart attacks. In 2008 he was posthumously awarded the Mark Twain Prize for American Humor. His memoir Last Words was published in 2009.
