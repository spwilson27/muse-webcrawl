Caps (party)

Image placeholder: Swedish political life in the Age of Liberty.

The Caps (Swedish: Mössorna) were a political faction during Sweden's Age of Liberty (1719–1772), rivals of the Hats. Their name derived from the nightcap — a jibe by opponents suggesting they were sleepy and unwarlike, in contrast to the martial "hats".

Policies

The Caps favoured a peaceful foreign policy and rapprochement with Russia, as opposed to the Hats' revanchist, pro-French orientation aimed at recovering territory lost to Russia. Domestically, the Caps drew support from the lower estates, peasants, and parts of the clergy and nobility hostile to the Hat regime's costly wars and mercantilist privileges; they advocated fiscal restraint and criticised the Hats' secretive foreign-policy decision-making.

History

The Caps first gained power at the Riksdag of 1738–39 only briefly being displaced, and returned to govern after 1765, following the Hats' disastrous involvement in the Seven Years' War and a financial crisis. The Cap government of 1765–1769 pursued retrenchment and passed reforms. Both factions received subsidies from foreign powers — Britain and Russia backed the Caps; France backed the Hats. The era of party rule ended when Gustav III seized power in his coup of 1772, restoring royal authority.
