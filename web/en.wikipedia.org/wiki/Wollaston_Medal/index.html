Wollaston Medal

The Wollaston Medal is a scientific award for geology, the highest award granted by the Geological Society of London. It is named after William Hyde Wollaston (1766–1828), the English chemist and physicist, and was first awarded in 1831. The medal was originally made of palladium, a metal discovered by Wollaston.

History

Wollaston bequeathed funds to the Geological Society to promote geological research, and the society used the donation to establish the medal and the Wollaston Fund. The first recipient, in 1831, was William Smith, the geologist famous for producing the first geological map of England and Wales. Charles Darwin received the medal in 1859, and Charles Lyell in 1858.

Notable recipients

Recipients include many of the most influential figures in the history of geology, including Roderick Murchison, Adam Sedgwick, Louis Agassiz, James Hall, Eduard Suess, and Grove Karl Gilbert, as well as modern geoscientists recognised for contributions across all fields of the Earth sciences.
