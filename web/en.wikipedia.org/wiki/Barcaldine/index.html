Barcaldine is a rural town and locality in the Barcaldine Region of Central West Queensland, Australia. The town is celebrated as the spiritual birthplace of the Australian labour movement: during the 1891 Australian shearers' strike, striking shearers camped beneath a ghost gum — the Tree of Knowledge — outside the railway station, and meetings held there contributed to the formation of the Australian Labor Party.

Tree of Knowledge

The heritage-listed eucalypt under which the strikers gathered became a national symbol. The tree was poisoned in 2006 and died; the site was redeveloped as a memorial, opened in 2009, in which the dead trunk is sheltered by a dramatic timber structure. The Australian Workers' Heritage Centre in Barcaldine documents the history of working Australians.

Geography

Barcaldine sits at the junction of the Landsborough and Capricorn highways, roughly 520 kilometres west of Rockhampton. The surrounding district is sheep and cattle country on the edge of the Mitchell grass downs, and the town takes its name from Barcaldine in Argyll, Scotland.
