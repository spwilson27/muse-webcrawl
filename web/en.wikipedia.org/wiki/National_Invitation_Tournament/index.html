The National Invitation Tournament (NIT) is an American college basketball tournament operated by the NCAA. Founded in 1938 by the Metropolitan Basketball Writers Association, it predates the NCAA Tournament by one year and was long considered the more prestigious of the two. Today it serves as a secondary postseason tournament for Division I men's and women's teams that do not receive NCAA Tournament bids.

History

The first NIT, played at Madison Square Garden in New York in 1938, was won by Temple. In the 1940s and 1950s the NIT field included the nation's best teams — champions such as Kentucky, Utah, and San Francisco — and in 1950 City College of New York won both the NIT and NCAA tournaments in the same season, a unique "grand slam". As the NCAA Tournament expanded and imposed exclusivity rules, the NIT declined in stature. In 2005, following an antitrust lawsuit, the NCAA purchased the NIT and has operated it since.

Format

The men's NIT selects 32 teams, with automatic bids historically given to conference regular-season champions that lose their conference tournaments; qualification rules were revised repeatedly, most notably in 2024. Early rounds are played at campus sites, and the semifinals and final were traditionally held at Madison Square Garden, though in recent years they have rotated to other venues including Las Vegas and Indianapolis. A separate Preseason NIT (now the NIT Season Tip-Off) is held each November.

Notable champions

St. John's has won the most NIT titles (six, including a vacated 2003 championship), and players such as Walt Frazier and Connie Hawkins used the tournament as a showcase. The women's NIT counterpart for teams outside the NCAA field is the WNIT, run separately.
