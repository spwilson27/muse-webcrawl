Lake Thun

Lake Thun (German: Thunersee) is an Alpine lake in the Bernese Oberland in the canton of Bern, Switzerland. It takes its name from the city of Thun at its western end. The lake is fed principally by the river Aare, which flows into it from Lake Brienz near Interlaken at its eastern end, and drains westward through Thun toward Bern.

Geography

The lake has a surface area of about 48.3 km², is roughly 17.5 km long and up to 3.5 km wide, and reaches a maximum depth of 217 metres. Its surface lies 558 metres above sea level. Settlements on its shores include Thun, Spiez, Hilterfingen, Oberhofen, Gunten, Merligen and Faulensee. Since the Kander river was diverted into the lake in 1714 — one of the earliest major river corrections in Switzerland — the Kander delta on the southern shore has grown steadily.

Sights and tourism

The shores of Lake Thun are dotted with castles, including those at Thun, Schadau, Oberhofen, Hünegg and Spiez, most now museums. The St. Beatus Caves above the northern shore are a popular attraction, and scheduled passenger boats operated by BLS connect the lakeside towns, with onward connections to Interlaken and Lake Brienz. The lake is also known for sailing, windsurfing and its population of whitefish and pike.

Sunken munitions

After the Second World War, the Swiss Army dumped thousands of tonnes of surplus munitions into Lake Thun (as well as Lakes Brienz and Lucerne). The deposits remain on the lake bed and are periodically monitored; studies have generally found that disturbing them would pose greater risks than leaving them in place.
