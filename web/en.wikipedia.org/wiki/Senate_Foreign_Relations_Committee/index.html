United States Senate Committee on Foreign Relations

The United States Senate Committee on Foreign Relations is a standing committee of the U.S. Senate charged with leading foreign-policy legislation and debate in the Senate. It is among the oldest and most prestigious Senate committees, established in 1816, and generally serves as the counterpart to the House Committee on Foreign Affairs.

Jurisdiction

The committee oversees foreign aid programs, arms sales, treaties, and nominations for positions in the Department of State and the foreign service, which by the Constitution the Senate must confirm. It has played a central role in major episodes of American foreign policy, including debates over the League of Nations after the First World War, the Marshall Plan, and the Vietnam War, when hearings chaired by J. William Fulbright became a national forum for dissent.

Subcommittees and leadership

Much of the committee's work is conducted through regional and functional subcommittees covering areas such as Europe, East Asia, the Western Hemisphere, Africa, international development, and multilateral institutions. The chair is traditionally a leading voice on foreign policy in the Senate; notable past chairs include Arthur Vandenberg, J. William Fulbright, Jesse Helms, and Joseph Biden.
