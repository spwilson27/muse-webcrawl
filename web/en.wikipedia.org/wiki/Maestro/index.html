Maestro

Maestro (Italian for "master" or "teacher") is an honorific title of respect given to a master musician, most commonly a conductor, composer or music teacher. The feminine form is maestra. The word derives from the Latin magister, meaning a master or chief.

Usage in music

In classical music the title is used for distinguished conductors and for composers of stature; it is also applied in opera houses to the senior musical staff, such as the maestro di cappella (master of the chapel) and the maestro sostituto, who prepares singers. In orchestral rehearsal etiquette, players traditionally address the conductor as "maestro".

Other uses

The term has been extended to masters in other fields: a chess grandmaster may be called maestro in Spanish-speaking countries, and the word is used generally in Italian and Spanish for a schoolteacher or master craftsman. It has also been adopted as a product name in technology and finance, including a well-known brand of debit card.
