Ginans

Ginans (from Sanskrit jñāna, "knowledge") are devotional hymns and scriptures of the Satpanth tradition of Nizari Ismaili Islam, composed in South Asia — chiefly Gujarat, Sindh and Punjab — between roughly the 13th and 19th centuries. Attributed to pirs (missionary saints) such as Pir Shams, Pir Sadruddin and Pir Hasan Kabiruddin, and to later sayyids and authors, the ginans form the principal religious literature of the Khoja Ismaili communities.

Content and themes

Written in vernacular languages including Gujarati, Sindhi, Punjabi, Hindi and Multani/Saraiki, and traditionally recorded in the Khojki script, the ginans convey Ismaili doctrine through the idioms of Indic devotion (bhakti). Themes include the coming of the tenth avatara (Naklanki/Kalki), identified with the Imam; the saving role of the Satguru or Pir; cosmology; moral instruction; meditation on the divine Name; and narratives of souls, salvation and the afterlife. Famous compositions include Pir Shams's Bujh Niranjan, the Salokas, and the eschatological Dasa Avatara.

Transmission and use

Ginans are recited daily in Jamatkhanas (Ismaili prayer houses) and at religious gatherings, set to distinctive ragas. In the 20th century, under the guidance of the Aga Khans, the Khojki manuscripts were transcribed and printed in Gujarati and other scripts, standardising the corpus. Scholars value the ginans as a major source for the study of religious synthesis in medieval India, though aspects of their interpretation — and the place of their Hindu imagery within an Ismaili and (for related traditions) Sikh milieu, where some compositions overlap with the Granth tradition — have been debated.
