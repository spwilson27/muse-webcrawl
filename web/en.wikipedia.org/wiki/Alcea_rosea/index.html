Alcea rosea, the common hollyhock, is an ornamental dicot flowering plant in the family Malvaceae. It was imported into Europe from southwestern China during, or possibly before, the 15th century. William Turner, a herbalist of the time, gave it the name "holyoke" from which the English name derives.

Description

Alcea rosea is variously described as a biennial (having a two-year life cycle), as an annual, or as a short-lived perennial. It frequently self-sows, which may create a perception that the plants are perennial. The plant may flower during its first year when sown early. It will grow in a wide range of soils, and can easily reach heights of about 8 ft (2.4 m).

The flowers are in a range of colours from white to dark red, including pink, yellow and orange. Different colours prefer different soils. The darker red variety seems to favour sandy soils, while the lighter colour seems to favour clay soils.

Cultivation

Hollyhocks are popular garden flowers and are easily grown from seed. They are particularly associated with cottage gardens. The plants are susceptible to the rust fungus Puccinia malvacearum, which can be controlled with fungicides or by removing infected leaves.

Uses

The flowers have been used in herbal medicine, and the plant has a long history of use in traditional Chinese medicine. The edible flowers are sometimes used in salads and teas.
