Uji (宇治市, Uji-shi) is a city on the southern outskirts of the city of Kyoto, in Kyoto Prefecture, Japan. It lies on the Uji River and is famous throughout Japan for the production of high-quality green tea, particularly matcha, and for the UNESCO World Heritage temple Byōdō-in.

The Phoenix Hall of Byōdō-in, depicted on the Japanese 10-yen coin.

History

Uji was an important river crossing and aristocratic retreat during the Heian period. The final ten chapters of The Tale of Genji are set in Uji and are known as the "Uji chapters". In 1052 the Fujiwara villa at Byōdō-in was converted into a Buddhist temple; its Phoenix Hall (Hōō-dō), completed in 1053, is one of the few surviving buildings of the Heian period and appears on the reverse of the 10-yen coin.

Tea cultivation was promoted in Uji from the Kamakura period, encouraged by the monk Eisai, and Uji tea came to be prized by the Ashikaga shogunate and by tea master Sen no Rikyū. Uji matcha remains one of Japan's most prestigious teas.

Sights

  • Byōdō-in — UNESCO World Heritage site, part of the Historic Monuments of Ancient Kyoto
  • Ujigami Shrine — among the oldest Shinto shrines in Japan, also a World Heritage site
  • The Tale of Genji Museum
  • Omotesandō tea shops and the Uji Bridge, first built in 646
