Maiden Tower (Baku)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Maiden Tower (Azerbaijani: Qız qalası) is a 12th-century monument in the Old City, Baku, Azerbaijan. Along with the Shirvanshahs' Palace, dated to the 15th century, it forms a group of historic monuments listed in 2001 under the UNESCO World Heritage List of Historical Monuments as cultural property.

Description

The tower is a massive cylindrical stone structure about 29.5 metres high, with walls up to five metres thick at the base, surmounted by a projecting wing-like buttress on its eastern side whose purpose remains debated. It contains eight storeys connected by a stone staircase built into the wall. The tower's date and original function are unresolved: theories identify it as a defensive fortress, a Zoroastrian fire temple or dakhma, an astronomical observatory, or some combination of these. Archaeological evidence suggests the foundations may be considerably older than the 12th-century upper structure.

Legend

The name is explained by a popular legend in which a maiden — in some versions a princess threatened with a forced or incestuous marriage — threw herself from the top of the tower into the Caspian Sea, which once lapped at its base. The legend has inspired poems, plays and the first Azerbaijani ballet, Maiden Tower (1940) by Afrasiyab Badalbeyli.

Today

The tower houses a museum and is one of Azerbaijan's most recognisable national symbols, appearing on the Azerbaijani manat banknotes and official emblems.
