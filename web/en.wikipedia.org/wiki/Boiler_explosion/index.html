Boiler explosion
TypeMechanical failure / explosion
CauseOverpressure, corrosion, low water, material failure
Energy sourceSuperheated water flashing to steam

A boiler explosion is a catastrophic failure of a boiler, typically of the steam type, in which the pressure vessel bursts and its contents are released explosively. Because the water inside a steam boiler is superheated, a rupture lets it flash almost instantly into steam occupying roughly 1,600 times its liquid volume, releasing enormous energy.

Causes

Explosions result from overpressure, weakened plates from corrosion or grooving, low-water conditions that overheat the crown sheet, defective safety valves, or poor repairs. In the 19th century, boiler explosions on steamboats, locomotives and in factories killed thousands; disasters such as the steamboat Sultana (1865) drove regulation.

Prevention

The disasters led to inspection regimes, standards such as the ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code (first issued 1914–15), fusible plugs, and mandatory safety valves, which have made explosions rare in regulated industries.
