Nassau is a town in the Rhein-Lahn district of Rhineland-Palatinate, Germany, situated on the river Lahn in the Lahn valley between Koblenz and Limburg an der Lahn. It is the ancestral seat of the House of Nassau, the noble dynasty that produced the counts and dukes of Nassau and, through its Orange-Nassau branch, the monarchs of the Netherlands and grand dukes of Luxembourg.

History

Nassau was first mentioned in a document of 915 in connection with the Villa Nassov. Around 1100 the counts of Laurenburg built Nassau Castle on a rock above the Lahn, and by the mid-12th century the family took its name—Counts of Nassau—from the castle. The town below received town rights in 1348 from Count Adolf of Nassau, who later became King Adolf of Germany (reigned 1292–1298).

Over the centuries the town was divided between different branches of the Nassau family and their co-heirs. In 1806 the area was incorporated into the Duchy of Nassau, and after the Austro-Prussian War of 1866 it passed to Prussia along with the duchy. Nassau Castle was largely destroyed by bombing in the Second World War; its keep was later restored and the ruins remain a landmark above the town.

Sights and transport

Landmarks include the ruins of Nassau Castle and Stein Castle, the town hall, and the birthplace traditions associated with the imperial reformer Heinrich Friedrich Karl vom und zum Stein, whose family seat stood in the town. Nassau lies on the Lahntal railway between Koblenz and Gießen and on the Lahn cycle path; the river is popular for canoeing and the surrounding Taunus and Westerwald hills for hiking.
