Yukos

Founded: 1993
Defunct: 2007
Industry: Oil and gas

Yukos Oil Company (Russian: ЮКОС, IPA: [ˈjukəs]) was an oil and gas company based in Moscow, Russia. Assembled in the 1990s from state oil assets, it became Russia's largest oil producer and one of the world's biggest non-state oil companies under its chief executive, Mikhail Khodorkovsky. The company was dismantled after Khodorkovsky's arrest in 2003 and a series of huge tax claims, and it was declared bankrupt in 2006.

History

Yukos was created by presidential decree in 1993, combining the Yuganskneftegaz production unit with refineries. In the controversial loans-for-shares privatisations of 1995, Khodorkovsky's Bank Menatep acquired control cheaply. After the 1998 Russian financial crisis, Yukos adopted Western accounting and management practices and its market value soared; by 2003 it was producing about a fifth of Russia's oil and negotiating a merger and possible foreign investment.

Downfall

In October 2003 Khodorkovsky was arrested on charges of fraud and tax evasion, which his supporters and many observers described as politically motivated by his funding of opposition parties and his challenge to President Vladimir Putin. Tax claims totalling tens of billions of dollars followed; Yukos's main production unit was auctioned to a state-linked shell company and quickly passed to the state oil company Rosneft. Yukos was liquidated in 2007. In 2014 an arbitral tribunal in The Hague awarded Yukos's former shareholders $50 billion in damages from Russia, the largest arbitral award in history, though its enforcement remained contested; the European Court of Human Rights also found violations in the handling of the case.
