Microtia

Microtia
Specialty: Medical genetics, otorhinolaryngology
Incidence: roughly 1 in 8,000–10,000 births
Related: Aural atresia, anotia
Grades of microtia, from mild (I) to anotia (IV)

Microtia is a congenital deformity in which the outer ear (pinna) is underdeveloped. It ranges from a slightly small ear with subtle structural changes (grade I) through a rudimentary, peanut-like remnant of cartilage (grade III, the most common form) to complete absence of the external ear, known as anotia (grade IV). Microtia usually affects one ear — most often the right — and occurs more frequently in males. It is frequently accompanied by aural atresia, the absence or closure of the external ear canal, which causes conductive hearing loss.

Causes

The condition arises from disrupted development of the first and second branchial arches early in pregnancy. Most cases are sporadic, though genetic syndromes such as Treacher Collins syndrome and oculo-auriculo-vertebral spectrum (Goldenhar syndrome) can include microtia, and environmental factors such as maternal diabetes and certain medications have been implicated. Reported incidence is higher in Hispanic, Asian and Indigenous American populations.

Treatment

Management includes hearing assessment and support — bone-conduction hearing devices can be fitted in infancy — and, where desired, surgical reconstruction of the outer ear. Reconstruction options include carving a framework from the patient's rib cartilage (usually from about age 6–10), using a porous polyethylene (Medpor) implant, or fitting a prosthetic ear. Canal surgery for atresia is considered only in favourable anatomical cases.
