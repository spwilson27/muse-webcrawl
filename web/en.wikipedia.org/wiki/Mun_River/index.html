Mun River

Mun River (Thai: แม่น้ำมูล)
Country: Thailand
Length: approx. 673 km
Mouth: Mekong River, Ubon Ratchathani
Basin: Khorat Plateau

The Mun River (Thai: แม่น้ำมูล, RTGS: Maenam Mun) is a major river of northeastern Thailand (Isan) and the largest tributary of the Mekong within Thailand. It flows about 673 km eastward across the Khorat Plateau.

Course

The Mun rises in the Sankamphaeng Range near Khao Yai National Park in Nakhon Ratchasima Province and flows east through Buriram, Surin, Sisaket, and Ubon Ratchathani provinces. At Khong Chiam it joins the Mekong near the Lao border, where the two differently coloured waters are a noted sight. Its principal tributary is the Chi River, which joins it in Sisaket Province.

Human use and controversy

The basin is a rice-growing heartland and has important prehistoric sites, including Ban Chiang-era settlements. The Pak Mun Dam, completed in 1994 near the river's mouth, became internationally controversial for its impact on fisheries and local livelihoods, prompting long-running protests by the Assembly of the Poor and experiments with seasonally opening the dam gates.
