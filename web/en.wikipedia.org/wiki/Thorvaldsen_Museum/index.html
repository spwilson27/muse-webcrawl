Thorvaldsen Museum

Thorvaldsens Museum
Location: Bertel Thorvaldsens Plads 2, Copenhagen, Denmark
Coordinates: 55.6766° N, 12.5783° E
Type: Art museum
Established: 18 September 1848
Architect: Michael Gottlieb Bindesbøll

The Thorvaldsen Museum (Danish: Thorvaldsens Museum) is a single-artist museum in Copenhagen, Denmark, dedicated to the art of Danish neoclassical sculptor Bertel Thorvaldsen (1770–1844), who lived and worked in Rome for most of his life. The museum is located on the small island of Slotsholmen in central Copenhagen, next to Christiansborg Palace.

The museum building, with Jørgen Sonne's frieze depicting Thorvaldsen's return to Copenhagen in 1838.

History

Thorvaldsen bequeathed his entire collection of his own works, together with his extensive collection of paintings, antiquities and other objects, to the city of Copenhagen. The museum building, designed by Michael Gottlieb Bindesbøll and constructed between 1839 and 1848 on the site of the former Royal Wagon Yard, was the first public museum building in Denmark. Thorvaldsen died in 1844, four years before the museum opened, and he is buried in its central courtyard.

Collections

The museum contains virtually all of Thorvaldsen's original plaster models, which were used to carve the marble versions of his sculptures found in museums and churches across Europe, including the famous Jason with the Golden Fleece, Ganymede, the Three Graces, and the colossal Christ statue (Christus) and the twelve apostles made for Copenhagen Cathedral. The museum also displays Thorvaldsen's personal collections of contemporary Danish and European painting, Egyptian and Greek antiquities, books, and personal effects.

Architecture

The building is strongly influenced by antique architecture, with a striking exterior frieze by painter Jørgen Sonne depicting Thorvaldsen's homecoming in 1838, when he was received as a national hero. The interior is richly decorated with coloured walls and mosaic floors that set off the white plaster sculptures.
