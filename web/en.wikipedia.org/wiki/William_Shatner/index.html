William Shatner (born March 22, 1931) is a Canadian actor, author and cultural figure whose career spans more than seven decades. He achieved worldwide fame as Captain James T. Kirk of the starship Enterprise in the Star Trek franchise, a role he played in the original television series (1966–1969), the animated series, and seven feature films between 1979 and 1994.

Career

Born in Montreal and trained at the Stratford Shakespeare Festival, Shatner moved into American television and film in the 1950s. After Star Trek he starred as the police officer T.J. Hooker (1982–1986) and hosted the rescue-docudrama series Rescue 911. His portrayal of Denny Crane in The Practice and Boston Legal (2004–2008) won him two Emmy Awards and a Golden Globe. He is also known for a recording career of spoken-word albums beginning with The Transformed Man (1968), for numerous memoirs and science-fiction novels including the TekWar series, and for competitive horse breeding and showing.

Spaceflight

On October 13, 2021, aged 90, Shatner flew to the edge of space aboard Blue Origin's New Shepard capsule on the NS-18 mission, becoming the oldest person to travel to space. He described the experience — the view of Earth against the blackness above the atmosphere — as profoundly moving, later writing about it as an experience of grief and awe.
