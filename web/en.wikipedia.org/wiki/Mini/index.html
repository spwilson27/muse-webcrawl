Mini

The Mini is a small car produced by the British Motor Corporation (BMC) and its successors from 1959 until 2000. Designed by Alec Issigonis, it used a transverse engine and front-wheel drive in a two-box layout that gave remarkable passenger space for its size.

Design and success

Launched in response to fuel rationing after the Suez Crisis, the Mini seated four adults in a car just over three metres long. The sporty Mini Cooper, developed with John Cooper, won the Monte Carlo Rally in 1964, 1965 and 1967. About 5.3 million classic Minis were built, and the car became an icon of 1960s British culture.

Legacy

In 2001 BMW launched the modern MINI hatch as a premium successor. The classic Mini was voted the second most influential car of the 20th century, behind the Ford Model T.
