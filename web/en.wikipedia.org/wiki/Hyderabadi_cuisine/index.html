Hyderabadi cuisine, also known as Deccani cuisine, is the native cooking style of Hyderabad and the wider Deccan region of India. It developed in the kitchens of the Nizams of Hyderabad, blending local Telugu and Marathwada ingredients with Mughlai, Persian, Arab and Turkish traditions brought by the court. Its hallmark is careful use of souring agents (especially tamarind and raw mango), roasted spices, herbs and slow-cooking methods such as dum (sealed-pot steaming).

History

The cuisine took shape under the Qutb Shahi dynasty and reached its height under the Asaf Jahi Nizams, whose royal kitchens employed khansamas who refined dishes for court banquets. Hyderabad's position as a princely state open to traders and scholars gave its food a cosmopolitan character distinct from both North Indian Mughlai and South Indian cooking.

Notable dishes

  • Hyderabadi biryani — the city's emblematic dish, in kachchi (raw marinated meat layered with rice) and pakki (pre-cooked) forms, sealed and cooked on dum; usually served with mirchi ka salan and raita. It holds a Geographical Indication tag.
  • Haleem — a slow-cooked stew of wheat, lentils, barley and pounded meat, especially associated with Ramadan; Hyderabadi haleem also has GI status.
  • Mirchi ka salan — large green chillies in a peanut, sesame and coconut curry.
  • Pathar ka gosht — meat cooked on a heated stone slab.
  • Double ka meetha — bread pudding with saffron and nuts; qubani ka meetha — stewed apricot dessert.
  • Irani chai and Osmania biscuits — staples of Hyderabad's Irani cafés.
