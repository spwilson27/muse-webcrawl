Ulpiana

Location: Near Gračanica, Kosovo
Region: Dardania, Moesia Superior
Founded: 2nd century AD (refounded as Iustiniana Secunda, 6th century)
Type: Roman and Byzantine city

Ulpiana was an ancient Roman city in the province of Moesia Superior (later Dardania), whose remains lie near the modern town of Gračanica in Kosovo, about 10 kilometres southeast of Pristina.

History

Ulpiana was founded in the 2nd century AD, apparently taking its name from the emperor Trajan (Marcus Ulpius Traianus). Situated near the rich mining districts of Dardania and on routes crossing the central Balkans, it grew into a flourishing municipium with temples, baths, a forum and defensive walls. The city suffered in the Gothic invasions of late antiquity. After an earthquake in 518, the emperor Justinian I rebuilt it and renamed it Iustiniana Secunda. The city was abandoned amid the Slavic invasions of the late 6th and early 7th centuries.

Archaeology

Excavations since the mid-20th century have uncovered stretches of the city wall, an early Christian basilica with mosaics, baptistery, necropolises and rich finds of sculpture and inscriptions. The archaeological park preserves the layout of one of the most important Roman urban centres in the region.
