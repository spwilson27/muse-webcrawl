The Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō

The Fifty-three Stations of the Tōkaidō (Japanese: 東海道五十三次, Tōkaidō Gojūsan-tsugi) is a series of ukiyo-e woodcut prints by the Japanese artist Utagawa Hiroshige, published in 1833–1834. It depicts the 53 post stations of the Tōkaidō road between Edo (Tokyo) and Kyoto, plus scenes of the two terminal cities.

ArtistUtagawa Hiroshige
Year1833–1834
MediumWoodblock print (ukiyo-e)
Prints55 (53 stations plus start and end points)

The series

The Hōeidō edition, named after its publisher, is the most famous of more than 30 Tōkaidō series Hiroshige produced. Celebrated prints include the departure from Nihonbashi, snow at Kambara, rain at Shōno, and travellers crossing the Ōi River at Shimada. The series made Hiroshige's reputation and helped define the landscape print in Japan.

Influence

Hiroshige's compositions influenced European artists including Van Gogh, who painted copies of two prints from the series. The stations themselves correspond to the rest stops established by the Tokugawa shogunate along the great highway.
