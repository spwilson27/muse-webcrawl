Earl of Howth

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Creation date: 1767

Created by: George III

Peerage: Peerage of Ireland

First holder: Thomas St Lawrence, 1st Earl of Howth

Last holder: William Ulick Tristram St Lawrence, 4th Earl of Howth

Extinct: 1909

Family seat: Howth Castle, County Dublin

Earl of Howth was a title in the Peerage of Ireland. It was created in 1767 for Thomas St Lawrence, 14th Baron Howth, of the St Lawrence family that had held Howth, north of Dublin, since the Anglo-Norman invasion of Ireland in the twelfth century.

History

The St Lawrences claimed descent from Almeric Tristram, a knight who landed in Ireland in 1177 and was granted Howth. The family were raised to the Irish peerage as Barons Howth around 1489. The 14th Baron, Thomas St Lawrence (1712–1801), was created Viscount St Lawrence and Earl of Howth in 1767. His son William, the 2nd Earl, sat in the House of Lords as an Irish representative peer. Thomas, the 3rd Earl, served as Lord Lieutenant of the County of Dublin.

Extinction

The earldom became extinct on the death of the 4th Earl, William Ulick Tristram St Lawrence, in 1909, when the barony of Howth also became dormant or extinct. Howth Castle passed by descent through the female line to the Gaisford-St Lawrence family, who remained at the castle into the twenty-first century. The earls are commemorated in the street names and the St Lawrence heritage of Howth village.

Earls of Howth (1767)

Thomas St Lawrence, 1st Earl of Howth (1712–1801); William St Lawrence, 2nd Earl of Howth (1754–1822); Thomas St Lawrence, 3rd Earl of Howth (1803–1874); William Ulick Tristram St Lawrence, 4th Earl of Howth (1827–1909).
