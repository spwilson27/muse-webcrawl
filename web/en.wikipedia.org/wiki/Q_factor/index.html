Q factor

In physics and engineering, the quality factor or Q factor is a dimensionless parameter that describes how underdamped an oscillator or resonator is. It is approximately defined as the ratio of the initial energy stored in the resonator to the energy lost in one radian of the cycle of oscillation. Equivalently,

Q = 2π × (energy stored) / (energy dissipated per cycle) = ω₀ / Δω

where ω₀ is the resonant angular frequency and Δω is the bandwidth of the resonance.

Significance

A high Q factor indicates a low rate of energy loss and sharp resonance: a tuning fork, a quartz crystal oscillator, and a superconducting radio-frequency cavity all have very high Q values and oscillate for a long time. A low Q indicates a heavily damped system. Q factor is used to characterise inductors and capacitors, RLC circuits, filters, lasers, and mechanical resonators such as pendulums and AFM cantilevers.
