Interbreeding between archaic and modern humans

Interbreeding between archaic and modern humans occurred during the Middle Paleolithic and early Upper Paleolithic as anatomically modern humans dispersed out of Africa and encountered archaic groups such as Neanderthals and Denisovans.

Genomic studies since 2010 have shown that non-African modern populations carry roughly 1–4% Neanderthal ancestry, while some Oceanian populations carry an additional 4–6% Denisovan ancestry. Gene flow also occurred in the other direction and between archaic groups themselves.

Introgressed genes have influenced modern human immunity, skin and hair traits, and adaptation to high altitude — for example, the EPAS1 variant aiding Tibetans derives from Denisovans.
