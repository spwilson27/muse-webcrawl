The Izz ad-Din al-Qassam Brigades (Arabic: كتائب الشهيد عز الدين القسام) are the military wing of the Palestinian Islamist organisation Hamas. Created in 1991 and named after the Syrian-born preacher and militant Izz ad-Din al-Qassam, killed by British forces in 1935, they became the largest and best-equipped armed group in the Gaza Strip.

History

The Brigades emerged during the First Intifada as Hamas's armed apparatus, carrying out attacks against Israeli military and civilian targets, including suicide bombings during the Second Intifada. Bomb-makers such as Yahya Ayyash ("the Engineer", killed in 1996) became central figures in the group's identity. After Hamas won the 2006 Palestinian legislative elections and took control of Gaza in 2007, the Brigades developed into a semi-regular force organised in regional brigades and specialised units, with an arsenal of domestically produced rockets — the Qassam rockets named after the group — mortars, anti-tank missiles, drones, naval commandos, and an extensive tunnel network under Gaza.

Gaza war (2023– )

On 7 October 2023 the Brigades led the cross-border assault on southern Israel (Operation Al-Aqsa Flood) that killed about 1,200 people and took some 250 hostages, triggering a major Israeli invasion of Gaza. During the ensuing war Israel killed much of the Brigades' senior leadership, including, by its account, military chief Mohammed Deif in July 2024, and Hamas leader Yahya Sinwar in October 2024, and severely degraded the organisation's organised battalions, though the group continued guerrilla operations.

Structure and capabilities

Pre-war estimates by Israeli and Western analysts put the Brigades' strength at roughly 25,000–40,000 fighters organised by geographic brigades in Gaza, supported by rocket production workshops and smuggling and attack tunnels. The group is regarded as distinct from, though subordinate to, Hamas's political leadership.
