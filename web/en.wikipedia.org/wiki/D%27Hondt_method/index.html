D'Hondt method

D'Hondt method
Type: Party-list proportional representation
Named after: Victor d'Hondt (1841–1901)
Used in: Many countries, incl. Spain, Netherlands, Belgium, European Parliament elections

The D'Hondt method is a method for allocating seats in a legislature among parties in party-list proportional representation. It is named after the Belgian mathematician Victor d'Hondt, who described it in 1878; the same method was devised earlier by Thomas Jefferson and is known in the United States as the Jefferson method.

Procedure

Each party's vote total is divided successively by 1, 2, 3, … and seats are awarded to the highest resulting quotients in turn. Compared with the Sainte-Laguë method, D'Hondt slightly favours larger parties, encouraging stable majorities.

Use

It is used in numerous countries in Europe, Latin America and elsewhere, and for allocating seats in the European Parliament among member states' delegations.
