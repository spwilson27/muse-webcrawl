John Stuart, 3rd Earl of Bute (May 25, 1713 – March 10, 1792) was a Scottish nobleman and politician who served as Prime Minister of Great Britain from 1762 to 1763 under George III. He was the first Scotsman to hold the office after the Acts of Union, and his closeness to the king made him a target of intense public hostility.

Career

Bute became tutor and confidant to the young Prince George, and on George's accession in 1760 he rose rapidly: Secretary of State, then First Lord of the Treasury in May 1762 on the fall of the Duke of Newcastle. His ministry negotiated the Treaty of Paris (1763) ending the Seven Years' War. Beset by attacks from the press — notably John Wilkes's The North Briton — and unpopular measures such as the cider tax, he resigned in April 1763, though rumours of his continuing influence behind the scenes persisted for years.

Later life and interests

In retirement Bute devoted himself to botany and patronage of the arts and sciences; he was a major benefactor of the University of Glasgow and helped Princess Augusta found the gardens at Kew. He died in 1792.
