4-Dimethylaminopyridine

4-Dimethylaminopyridine (DMAP) is a derivative of pyridine bearing a dimethylamino group at the 4-position. It is a colourless solid widely used as a nucleophilic catalyst in organic synthesis, particularly in esterification and acylation reactions.

DMAP is far more effective than pyridine itself as an acylation catalyst because the dimethylamino group donates electron density into the ring, stabilising the N-acylpyridinium intermediate formed when DMAP reacts with an acylating agent such as an anhydride or acid chloride. The alcohol then attacks this activated intermediate, regenerating DMAP.

It is classically used in the Steglich esterification, in which a carboxylic acid and an alcohol are coupled with a carbodiimide such as DCC in the presence of a catalytic amount of DMAP. DMAP is toxic and is absorbed through the skin, so it must be handled with care.
