Miami Beach is a coastal resort city in Miami-Dade County, Florida, United States. It is located on a series of natural and man-made barrier islands between the Atlantic Ocean and Biscayne Bay, across the bay from the city of Miami. The population was 82,890 at the 2020 census. Miami Beach is one of America's best-known beach resorts, famous worldwide for its South Beach neighbourhood and pastel-coloured Art Deco architecture.

History

The island was originally covered in mangrove swamp. In the 1870s a coconut plantation was established by the Lum family and later developers, but the town took shape after 1912, when entrepreneur Carl G. Fisher financed development, clearing mangroves and building the Collins Bridge across the bay in 1913. The city was incorporated in 1915. The Florida land boom of the 1920s filled the island with hotels and winter homes; after the 1926 hurricane and the Depression, smaller Art Deco and Streamline Moderne hotels rose along Ocean Drive and Collins Avenue in the 1930s and 1940s.

After World War II Miami Beach became a premier destination for Jewish retirees and tourists, later declining in the 1970s before a renaissance: the Miami Design Preservation League, founded by Barbara Capitman, won National Register listing for the Miami Beach Architectural District in 1979, protecting more than 800 Art Deco buildings. Fashion shoots, nightlife, and events such as Art Basel Miami Beach (from 2002) restored its global glamour.

Geography and culture

The city stretches about 18 miles along the barrier island chain, with neighbourhoods including South Beach (SoBe) at the southern tip, Mid-Beach, and North Beach. Lincoln Road is a celebrated pedestrian shopping street. South Beach's nightlife, modelling industry, and LGBTQ scene made it an icon of 1980s and 1990s popular culture — the look of the television series Miami Vice was strongly associated with it. The low-lying city, averaging only about 4.4 feet above sea level, faces acute challenges from sea-level rise and "sunny day" tidal flooding, and has invested heavily in raised roads and pump systems.
