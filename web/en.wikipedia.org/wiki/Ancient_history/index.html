Ancient history

[Image: cuneiform clay tablets from ancient Mesopotamia arranged in a museum case]

Ancient history is the study of the recorded human past from the earliest written records (about 3300 BC) to the Early Middle Ages. It covers the first complex societies of Mesopotamia and Egypt, the classical civilisations of Greece and Rome, ancient Persia, India and China, and the pre-Columbian Americas, among many others. The preceding period is prehistory, known only through archaeology rather than texts.

The discipline rests on the evidence of surviving writings — cuneiform tablets, papyri, inscriptions and manuscripts — combined with archaeology, numismatics and the sciences of dating and climate reconstruction.

Scope and periodisation

Ancient history conventionally begins with the invention of writing: cuneiform in Sumer and hieroglyphs in Egypt around the late fourth millennium BC. In South Asia the Indus Valley Civilisation flourished (c. 2600–1900 BC), and in China the earliest dynasties and oracle-bone writing date to the second millennium BC.

The traditional Western endpoint is the fall of the Western Roman Empire in AD 476, though many historians now prefer a longer 'late antiquity' extending into the seventh or eighth century, or end with the early Islamic conquests.

Themes

Key themes include the origins of states and cities, the development of law, money and bureaucracy, the interaction of empires with their neighbours, slavery and labour systems, and the transmission of technologies, religions and ideas along routes such as the Silk Road.

Because literacy was always restricted to minorities, ancient history is as much about reconstructing the lives of the illiterate majority — farmers, soldiers, women, enslaved people — from material remains as it is about kings and texts.
