Miaphysitism

Miaphysitism is the Christological doctrine held by the Oriental Orthodox Churches, which teaches that in the one person of Jesus Christ, divinity and humanity are united in one (μία, mia) nature (physis), the two being united without separation, without confusion, and without alteration. The doctrine is drawn from Cyril of Alexandria's formula "one nature of God the Word incarnate" (mia physis tou Theou Logou sesarkōmenē).

History

After the Council of Chalcedon (451), which defined Christ as one person in two natures, many Christians in Egypt, Syria and Armenia rejected its definition — partly from loyalty to Cyril's teaching, partly from fear that "two natures" smuggled in the Nestorian division of Christ into two persons. Their opponents, both Chalcedonian and hostile chroniclers, labelled them "Monophysites", a term Oriental Orthodox Christians reject as implying the heresy of Eutyches, who taught that Christ's humanity was absorbed into his divinity. Imperial attempts at compromise, such as Zeno's Henotikon (482), failed to heal the schism, and under leaders such as Severus of Antioch and Jacob Baradaeus the non-Chalcedonian churches organised separate hierarchies that persist today: the Coptic, Syriac (Jacobite), Armenian, Ethiopian, Eritrean and Malankara Orthodox churches.

Theology

Miaphysitism affirms that Christ is fully God and fully man, consubstantial with the Father in divinity and consubstantial with us in humanity. The union is a single, composite nature after the incarnation, while the difference between divinity and humanity remains perceptible "in contemplation only" (theōria). Modern ecumenical dialogues — including agreed statements between the Oriental Orthodox and Eastern Orthodox (1989–1990) and with the Roman Catholic Church — have concluded that the two families confess the same faith in different terminology, the ancient quarrel being largely over words and politics rather than substance.

Terminology

Scholars and the churches themselves prefer miaphysite to the polemical monophysite. The related term dyophysitism describes the Chalcedonian position of two natures in one person (hypostasis).
