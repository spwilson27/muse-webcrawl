Grande Torino

The Grande Torino squad

The Grande Torino ("Great Torino") was the celebrated Torino Football Club team of the 1940s, one of the strongest sides in Italian football history. The team won five consecutive Serie A titles (1942–43 and 1945–46 to 1948–49; the championship was suspended for two seasons during the Second World War) and formed the backbone of the Italian national team, on one occasion supplying all ten outfield players for an international match.

The team

Built under club president Ferruccio Novo, the side played an attacking variant of the Sistema and was captained by Valentino Mazzola, father of later star Sandro Mazzola. Key players included goalkeeper Valerio Bacigalupo, defenders Aldo Ballarin and Virgilio Maroso, and forwards Ezio Loik, Guglielmo Gabetto and Romeo Menti.

Superga air disaster

On 4 May 1949 the entire first team was killed in the Superga air disaster, when the Fiat G.212 aircraft carrying them home from a friendly match against Benfica in Lisbon crashed into the retaining wall of the Basilica of Superga on the hills above Turin in thick fog. All 31 people on board died, including 18 players, club officials, coaches and journalists. Torino were declared champions of the 1948–49 season at the request of their rivals, and the tragedy remains one of the defining events in Italian sporting memory; the club has never since matched the dominance of the Grande Torino era.
