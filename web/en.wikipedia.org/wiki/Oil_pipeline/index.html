Oil pipeline

An oil pipeline is a system of pipes for transporting petroleum and refined products over long distances. Pipeline transport is the long-distance transportation of a liquid or gas through a system of pipes—a pipeline—typically to a market area for consumption. The latest data from 2014 gives a total of slightly less than 3.5 million kilometres (2.2 million miles) of pipeline in 120 countries.

Oil pipelines are made from steel or plastic tubes, usually buried underground, with pumping stations along their length. Major examples include the Trans-Alaska Pipeline System and the Druzhba pipeline. Pipelines are generally the most economical way to transport large quantities of oil over land, though spills and environmental concerns are significant issues.
