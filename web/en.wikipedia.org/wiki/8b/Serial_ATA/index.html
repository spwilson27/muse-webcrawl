Serial ATA (Serial Advanced Technology Attachment, abbreviated SATA) is a computer bus interface that connects host bus adapters to mass storage devices such as hard disk drives, optical drives, and solid-state drives. It replaced Parallel ATA (PATA) as the dominant storage interface in desktop and laptop computers during the 2000s.

Advantages over PATA

SATA cables are much thinner than PATA ribbon cables—seven conductors instead of forty or eighty—improving airflow inside computer cases and allowing longer cables. Each device connects on its own point-to-point link, eliminating the master/slave jumper configuration of PATA, and SATA supports native hot swapping when the controller and operating system allow it.
