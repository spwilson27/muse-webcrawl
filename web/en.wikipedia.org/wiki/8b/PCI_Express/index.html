PCI Express (Peripheral Component Interconnect Express), officially abbreviated as PCIe or PCI-e, is a high-speed serial computer expansion bus standard, designed to replace the older PCI, PCI-X, and AGP standards. It is the most common motherboard interface for graphics cards, sound cards, network adapters, and solid-state storage.

Architecture

Unlike the shared parallel bus of PCI, PCIe uses point-to-point serial links called lanes. Each lane consists of two differential signaling pairs—one for transmitting and one for receiving—so devices communicate in full duplex. Links can aggregate 1, 2, 4, 8, or 16 lanes (×1 through ×16), with bandwidth scaling accordingly. Data is sent in packets, with flow control and error checking handled by the link and transaction layers.
