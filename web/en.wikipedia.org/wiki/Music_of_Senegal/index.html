Music of Senegal

The music of Senegal is among the most influential in Africa. Senegal's position as a crossroads of West African cultures, and the Wolof, Serer, and Mandinka traditions of its people, produced the energetic dance music known as mbalax, which brought Senegalese music to international attention through superstar Youssou N'Dour.

Traditional music

Like neighbouring Mali, Senegal has a griot (gewel in Wolof) tradition of hereditary praise-singers accompanying themselves on the kora, xalam (lute), and balafon. The sabar drum ensemble — a family of drums played with one hand and one stick — accompanies dances at weddings, wrestling matches, and naming ceremonies, and its complex rhythms form the rhythmic foundation of mbalax.

Mbalax

Mbalax (Wolof for "rhythm/accompaniment") developed in the 1970s by fusing traditional sabar drumming and griot singing with Afro-Cuban music, jazz, soul, and rock. The band Étoile de Dakar and its young singer Youssou N'Dour pioneered the modern form; N'Dour's own group, Super Étoile de Dakar, refined it. N'Dour achieved global fame through collaborations with Peter Gabriel and the 1994 hit "7 Seconds" with Neneh Cherry, becoming arguably Africa's most famous living singer and later Senegal's Minister of Tourism and Culture.

Other styles

The pioneering group Xalam and the band Touré Kunda (the Touré brothers, from Casamance) gained international followings in the 1980s. Orchestra Baobab, formed in 1970, blended Afro-Cuban son with Wolof and Mandinka melodies and enjoyed a celebrated revival in the 2000s. Baaba Maal, a Fula singer from Podor, fuses traditional pulaar music with pop. Senegalese hip hop, performed in Wolof and French by groups such as Daara J and Positive Black Soul, is a major voice for youth political expression, and played a role in civic movements such as Y'en a Marre.
