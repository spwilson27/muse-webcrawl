Shang dynasty

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: A Shang dynasty bronze ritual vessel (ding) with taotie decoration]

The Shang dynasty (c. 1600–1046 BC) was the second dynasty of traditional Chinese history and the first for which contemporary written records survive. Ruling from capitals in the Yellow River valley, most famously at Yin (near modern Anyang), the Shang kings presided over a Bronze Age society skilled in bronze casting, chariot warfare and divination. Their oracle bone inscriptions — questions to royal ancestors carved on bone and shell — are the earliest known form of Chinese writing.

Society and religion

Shang kings were priest-kings who mediated with the high god Di and the royal ancestors through oracle-bone divination; cracks in heated bones were read as answers. Elite tombs at Yinxu contained bronze ritual vessels of superb quality, chariots, and victims of human sacrifice. Society was hierarchical, with aristocratic clans, artisans, farmers growing millet and wheat, and war captives used as labour and sacrifice.

Fall and legacy

The last king, Di Xin, was remembered by later tradition as a decadent tyrant; in 1046 BC (by the traditional chronology) the Zhou defeated the Shang at the Battle of Muye and founded the Zhou dynasty, justifying the conquest with the doctrine of the Mandate of Heaven. Oracle bones, unearthed only from 1899, confirmed the historicity of the Shang king list preserved in much later histories.
