Rostra

The Rostra (Italian: Rostri) was a large platform built in the city of Rome that stood in the Forum Romanum and served as the stage from which orators addressed the assembled people. Its name derives from the rostra, the bronze prows (rams) of captured enemy warships, which decorated its front.

History

The original Rostra stood in the Comitium. It was decorated in 338 BC with the prows of ships captured from the Volscians of Antium in the Latin War. Julius Caesar relocated and rebuilt it at the western end of the Forum (the Rostra Vetera side); the Augustan completion was inaugurated in 29 BC with prows from the Battle of Actium. Speakers such as Cicero addressed crowds from the platform; Cicero's severed head and hands were notoriously displayed on the Rostra after his proscription in 43 BC.

Legacy

The word "rostrum" in modern English for a speaker's platform descends from the Rostra. Related monuments include the Rostra Augusti and the column of Gaius Duilius (Columna Rostrata).
