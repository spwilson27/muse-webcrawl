Cleveland Clinic

Cleveland Clinic is a nonprofit American academic medical center based in Cleveland, Ohio. Founded in 1921 by four physicians — including George Washington Crile and William E. Lower, who had served together in a military medical unit in the First World War — it pioneered the model of group practice in which physicians are salaried employees rather than fee-for-service practitioners.

Reputation and specialties

Cleveland Clinic is consistently ranked among the best hospitals in the United States, and its heart program was ranked number one in the country by U.S. News & World Report for more than two decades. Its main campus near downtown Cleveland covers a large complex of hospitals and research buildings, and the system operates regional hospitals in Ohio, facilities in Florida, and international sites including Abu Dhabi, Toronto, and London.

Research and education

The Lerner Research Institute is the Clinic's research arm, and the Cleveland Clinic Lerner College of Medicine, affiliated with Case Western Reserve University, trains physician-scientists. Notable achievements associated with the Clinic include advances in coronary artery bypass surgery and the first successful larynx transplant.

History

In 1929 a fire caused by nitrocellulose X-ray films killed 123 people at the Clinic, one of the deadliest hospital disasters in American history; the institution rebuilt and expanded greatly after the Second World War under the leadership of figures such as cardiac surgeon René Favaloro, who worked there in the 1960s.
