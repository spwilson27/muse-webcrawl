Harold Arlen
Born: Hyman Arluck, 15 February 1905, Buffalo, New York
Died: 23 April 1986, New York City
Occupation: Composer
Notable works: "Over the Rainbow", "Stormy Weather", "Blues in the Night"

Harold Arlen (born Hyman Arluck; February 15, 1905 – April 23, 1986) was an American composer of popular music, having written over 500 songs. He is best known for composing "Over the Rainbow" for The Wizard of Oz (1939), which won the Academy Award for Best Original Song.

Career

The son of a cantor, Arlen sang in his father's synagogue choir before becoming a jazz pianist and band vocalist in Buffalo and New York. At the Cotton Club he wrote revue songs with lyricist Ted Koehler, including "Stormy Weather" and "I've Got the World on a String".

Collaborations

He worked with lyricists E. Y. Harburg, Johnny Mercer and Ira Gershwin on Broadway shows such as Bloomer Girl, St. Louis Woman and House of Flowers, and on standards including "Blues in the Night", "That Old Black Magic" and "Come Rain or Come Shine".
