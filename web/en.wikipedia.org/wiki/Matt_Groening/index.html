Matthew Abram Groening (born February 15, 1954) is an American cartoonist, writer, producer, and animator. He is best known as the creator of the television series The Simpsons (1989–present), Futurama (1999–2003, 2008–2013, 2023–present), and Disenchantment (2018–2023), and of the comic strip Life in Hell (1977–2012). The Simpsons is the longest-running American animated series, longest-running sitcom, and longest-running scripted primetime series in US television history.

Early life and Life in Hell

Groening was born in Portland, Oregon; his father, Homer Groening, was a filmmaker and cartoonist of German-Canadian descent, and several Simpson family members share names with his relatives (his parents Homer and Margaret, sisters Lisa and Maggie). After attending The Evergreen State College, he moved to Los Angeles in 1977 and began self-publishing the comic strip Life in Hell, featuring the rabbit Binky, which was picked up by the Los Angeles Reader and then syndicated nationally.

The Simpsons

In 1987 producer James L. Brooks commissioned Groening to create animated shorts for The Tracey Ullman Show; Groening hastily sketched the Simpson family in Brooks's office waiting room, naming the characters after his own family (substituting "Bart", an anagram of "brat", for himself). The shorts became a half-hour series in December 1989 and grew into a global cultural phenomenon, spawning a 2007 feature film and earning dozens of Emmy Awards.

Futurama and Disenchantment

With David X. Cohen, Groening created Futurama, a science-fiction comedy about a delivery boy cryogenically frozen and revived in the year 3000, which premiered on Fox in 1999 and was revived multiple times, most recently by Hulu in 2023. For Netflix he created the medieval fantasy Disenchantment (2018–2023), starring Princess Bean of Dreamland.
