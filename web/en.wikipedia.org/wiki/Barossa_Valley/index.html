The Barossa Valley is a valley in South Australia, about 60 kilometres north-east of Adelaide, formed by the North Para River. It is one of Australia's oldest and most famous wine regions, settled from 1842 by German Lutheran immigrants from Prussia and Silesia whose language, food, churches, and building traditions still shape the valley's character.

Wine

The Barossa is best known for powerful Shiraz made in part from some of the world's oldest vines — ungrafted Shiraz and Grenache plantings dating to the 1840s that survived because phylloxera never reached South Australia. Major producers founded in the valley include Penfolds (1844), Seppeltsfield (1851), Yalumba (1849), and Jacob's Creek. The adjoining, cooler Eden Valley is noted for Riesling. The Barossa is a Geographical Indication zone encompassing both valleys.

Heritage

Barossa German was once widely spoken, and Lutheran churches, bakeries selling Streuselkuchen and mettwurst, and vintage festivals preserve the region's distinctive German-Australian culture.
