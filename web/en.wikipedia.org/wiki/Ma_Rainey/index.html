Gertrude "Ma" Rainey (born Gertrude Pridgett; 1886–1939) was an American blues singer regarded as the "Mother of the Blues". One of the first generation of blues singers to record, she bridged the worlds of vaudeville and the authentic rural blues of the American South, and her powerful contralto voice and theatrical stage presence made her one of the most celebrated Black entertainers of the 1920s.

Early life and career

Born in Columbus, Georgia, Rainey performed from her teens in talent shows and minstrel troupes. After marrying Will "Pa" Rainey in 1904, she toured with tent shows and the Rabbit Foot Minstrels. She claimed to have first heard blues music around 1902 and became one of the performers who brought the form to wide audiences, mentoring the younger Bessie Smith, who briefly sang in her troupe.

Recordings

Between 1923 and 1928 Rainey recorded over 90 sides for Paramount Records, including "Bo-Weevil Blues", "See See Rider Blues" (with Louis Armstrong among her accompanists), "Black Bottom" and "Prove It on Me Blues", notable for its frank treatment of lesbian themes. She performed with her Georgia Jazz Band and recorded with musicians including Tampa Red and Blind Blake.

Later life and legacy

The decline of vaudeville and the classic blues market during the Great Depression ended her touring career by the mid-1930s; she retired to Columbus, where she ran local theatres, and died of a heart attack in 1939. Rainey was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1990, and August Wilson's 1982 play Ma Rainey's Black Bottom — adapted into a 2020 film starring Viola Davis — dramatises her 1927 recording session in Chicago.
