President's Hundred tab

The President's Hundred tab is a badge of the United States Army awarded to the top 100 competitors in the President's Match, a rifle and pistol shooting competition held annually at Camp Perry, Ohio, as part of the Civilian Marksmanship Program's National Matches. It is one of the most prestigious marksmanship awards in the US military and one of only a handful of tabs authorised for permanent wear on the Army uniform by soldiers who earn it, similar in status to the Ranger and Sapper tabs.

History

The President's Match dates to 1878, and the "President's Hundred" designation for the top hundred marksmen was formalised in the early 20th century. Competitors include military personnel and civilians; soldiers finishing in the top hundred may wear the tab. The distinctive feature of the award is the congratulatory letter historically associated with it — the top-scoring competitors received a letter from the President of the United States, a tradition dating to 1904.

The tab

The full-colour tab is a rectangular patch edged in gold, worn on the left sleeve of the dress uniform, bearing the words "PRESIDENT'S HUNDRED". A subdued version exists for field uniforms. Because membership is limited to 100 per year yet lasts for the wearer's career, the tab is rare and highly coveted among competitive service shooters.
