Afroalpine

Afroalpine describes the high-mountain ecosystems found on the isolated peaks of eastern and northeastern Africa, above the treeline at roughly 3,500–4,000 metres. Major afroalpine areas include the Ethiopian Highlands, Mount Kenya, Kilimanjaro, the Rwenzori Mountains, and Mount Elgon.

Climate

The afroalpine climate has been described as "summer every day, winter every night": intense equatorial sun raises temperatures by day, while nights regularly fall below freezing at any time of year. Plants must therefore tolerate daily freeze–thaw cycles rather than a long winter season.

Flora and fauna

The vegetation is famous for giant rosette plants — giant groundsels (Dendrosenecio) and giant lobelias — whose forms evolved independently on separate mountains as protection against frost. Characteristic animals include the gelada and Ethiopian wolf in the Ethiopian highlands, and sunbirds and chameleons elsewhere. Below the afroalpine zone lies the afro-montane belt of forest and bamboo.
