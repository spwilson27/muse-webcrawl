Noise-cancelling headphones

Noise-cancelling headphones are headphones that reduce unwanted ambient sound. Active noise control (ANC) uses microphones to sample ambient noise and electronics to generate an "anti-noise" signal with the opposite phase, which destructively interferes with the noise at the ear. Passive noise isolation instead relies on the physical seal and materials of the earcups, like ordinary ear protection.

How active cancellation works

A feedforward microphone outside the earcup, or a feedback microphone inside it, picks up ambient sound. A processor inverts the waveform and mixes it into the audio played by the driver, so the residual noise reaching the eardrum is greatly reduced. The method works best on steady, low-frequency sounds such as aircraft engine drone and is less effective on sudden, irregular sounds.

History

The principle was patented by Paul Lueg in the 1930s. Practical headsets were developed in the 1980s for pilots; Amar Bose, inspired by a 1978 flight, founded research that led to the Bose aviation headsets of 1989 and the consumer QuietComfort line in 2000. ANC is now common in premium consumer headphones and earbuds from many manufacturers.

Applications

Noise-cancelling headsets are standard equipment for airline pilots and are widely used by travellers and office workers. They can reduce listener fatigue and allow lower listening volumes, though they do not block all sound and the electronics require battery power.
