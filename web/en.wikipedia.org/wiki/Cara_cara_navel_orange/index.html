Cara cara navel orange

The Cara Cara navel orange, or red-fleshed navel, is a cultivar of navel orange (Citrus × sinensis) distinguished by its salmon-pink to deep red flesh. It originated as a spontaneous bud mutation on a Washington navel orange tree discovered in 1976 at the Hacienda Cara Cara in Valencia, Venezuela — hence its name. Cara Cara oranges were introduced to the United States in the 1980s and reached wider retail distribution in the 1990s and 2000s, marketed as a premium winter citrus.

Description

Externally the fruit resembles an ordinary navel orange, with a smooth orange rind and the characteristic "navel" at the blossom end. Inside, the flesh is pink-red owing to the carotenoid lycopene — the pigment also found in tomatoes and pink grapefruit — rather than the anthocyanins that colour blood oranges. The flavour is sweet with lower acidity than a standard navel and often described as having notes of cranberry, blackberry, or rose. Like other navels, the fruit is seedless and easy to peel.

Nutrition

Cara Cara oranges provide vitamin C, folate, potassium, and fibre, and contain more lycopene than conventional navel oranges. A medium fruit has roughly 70–80 calories.

Cultivation

The cultivar is grown commercially in Venezuela, the United States (California, Florida, Texas), Spain, South Africa, and Australia, among other citrus regions, allowing nearly year-round supply. Trees are propagated by grafting budwood onto rootstocks, as the mutation does not breed true from seed.
