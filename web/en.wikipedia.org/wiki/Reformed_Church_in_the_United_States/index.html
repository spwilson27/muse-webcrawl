Reformed Church in the United States


Abbreviation: RCUS
Orientation: Reformed (Calvinist)
Headquarters: Shafter, California

The Reformed Church in the United States (RCUS) is a conservative Reformed Protestant denomination in the United States. It descends from the German Reformed churches founded by immigrants in the colonial period, and is not to be confused with the historically Dutch Reformed Church in America.

History

German Reformed congregations were organised in Pennsylvania in the 1720s, aided by the missionary Michael Schlatter, sent in 1746. The German Reformed Church grew through the 19th century and renamed itself the Reformed Church in the United States in 1869. In 1934 the majority of the denomination merged with the Evangelical Synod of North America to form the Evangelical and Reformed Church, which in 1957 joined the United Church of Christ. Congregations of the Eureka Classis in the Dakotas refused the 1934 merger and continued the RCUS as an independent body.

Beliefs and practice

The RCUS holds to the Three Forms of Unity—the Heidelberg Catechism, the Belgic Confession, and the Canons of Dort—as its doctrinal standards. It maintains conservative positions on Scripture, worship, and church order, with government by consistories, classes, and a general synod.
