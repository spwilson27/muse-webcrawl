Vehicle registration

Vehicle registration is the official recording of a motor vehicle with a government authority, usually for purposes of identification, taxation, and enforcing legal responsibility for the vehicle. In most jurisdictions a vehicle must be registered before it may be driven on public roads, and the registration is evidenced by a registration certificate and by vehicle registration plates (number plates or license plates) affixed to the vehicle displaying a unique registration mark.

Purpose

Registration establishes a link between a vehicle and its registered keeper or owner, enabling authorities to identify vehicles involved in offences, accidents, or tolling; to collect vehicle excise duties and registration fees; and to maintain records for safety recalls and emissions or roadworthiness testing. Registered details normally include the vehicle identification number (VIN), make and model, and the keeper's name and address.

Systems by country

In the United States, registration is administered at state level, typically by a Department of Motor Vehicles, and must usually be renewed annually or biennially, evidenced by a sticker on the plate. In the United Kingdom, the Driver and Vehicle Licensing Agency (DVLA) maintains the register, issues V5C registration certificates, and assigns registration marks whose format indicates the vehicle's age and region of first registration. In the European Union, member states operate their own registers while plate designs share the common blue EU band.

Registration plates

Plates serve as the visible identifier of registration. Many systems allow personalised or "vanity" plates for an additional fee. Cross-border recognition of plates is governed by international conventions, and vehicles travelling abroad may also display a distinguishing sign of the state of registration, such as an oval country code sticker.
