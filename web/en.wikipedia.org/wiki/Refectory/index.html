Refectory


The refectory of a monastery, with long communal tables.

A refectory is a communal dining hall, especially in monasteries, boarding schools, and academic institutions. The word derives from the Latin refectorium, from reficere, "to restore," and in monastic use it denotes the room where monks or nuns take their meals together.

Monastic refectories

In medieval monasteries the refectory typically stood on the south side of the cloister, opposite the church. Meals were eaten in silence while one brother read aloud from a small pulpit built into the wall, following the Rule of Saint Benedict. The room usually contained a lavabo for washing hands before meals. Many famous refectories are decorated with paintings of the Last Supper; the refectory of Santa Maria delle Grazie in Milan houses Leonardo da Vinci's The Last Supper.

Modern usage

Colleges, universities, seminaries, and boarding schools retain the term for their dining halls, notably the older colleges of Oxford and Cambridge, where the refectory may be called a hall. Cafeteria-style dining rooms in institutions are also sometimes called refectories.
