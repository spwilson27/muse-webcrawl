Figure of the Earth

The figure of the Earth is the size and shape used to model the Earth in geodesy and related sciences. Although the Earth's surface is rugged and irregular, it can be approximated to increasing levels of accuracy: as a sphere, as an oblate spheroid (ellipsoid of revolution), and as the geoid, the surface of equal gravitational potential that coincides with mean sea level.

History

The spherical Earth was established in ancient Greek astronomy; Eratosthenes measured its circumference around 240 BC. In the 17th and 18th centuries the question of whether the Earth was flattened at the poles (Newton) or elongated (Cassini) was settled by French geodetic expeditions to Lapland and Peru, confirming an oblate figure. Modern satellite geodesy measures the shape with centimetre precision.

Models

Reference ellipsoids such as WGS 84 — equatorial radius 6,378.137 km, flattening about 1/298.257 — underpin global positioning and mapping. For precise work, the geoid, with undulations of up to about ±100 m relative to the ellipsoid, is used to define heights above sea level. The equatorial radius exceeds the polar radius by roughly 21.4 km.
