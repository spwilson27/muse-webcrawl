2020s in fashion

The fashion of the 2020s was shaped by the COVID-19 pandemic, the dominance of social media platforms such as TikTok and Instagram, and rapid trend cycles driven by online micro-aesthetics. The decade saw a revival of early-2000s ("Y2K") styles, the mainstreaming of athleisure and comfort wear, and continuing growth of second-hand and sustainable fashion alongside ultra-fast-fashion retailers.

Pandemic era (2020–2021)

Lockdowns and remote work pushed demand toward loungewear, athleisure and comfortable "work from home" dressing, while face masks became an ubiquitous accessory. Fashion weeks went digital, supply chains were disrupted, and brands accelerated e-commerce. As restrictions eased, "revenge dressing" — bold, celebratory outfits — gained attention.

Mid-decade trends

Social-media microtrends proliferated: cottagecore, dark academia, Barbiecore (spurred by the 2023 Barbie film), "quiet luxury" associated with the television series Succession, mob-wife aesthetics and blokecore. Wide-leg and baggy silhouettes displaced skinny jeans, low-rise cuts and cargo trousers returned from the 2000s, and vintage/thrift shopping became a mainstream identity marker for younger consumers. At the same time, ultra-fast-fashion platforms such as Shein drew criticism over labour and environmental practices, fuelling EU and US regulatory debate.

Industry developments

Resale platforms (Depop, Vinted, Vestiaire Collective) grew rapidly; major houses rotated creative directors at an unusually brisk pace; and artificial intelligence entered design, marketing and virtual try-on. Sustainability claims faced scrutiny under new greenwashing rules, while the second-hand market was projected to keep outpacing conventional retail growth.
