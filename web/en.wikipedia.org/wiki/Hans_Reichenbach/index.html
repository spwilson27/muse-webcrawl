Hans Reichenbach

Hans Reichenbach (26 September 1891 – 9 April 1953) was a leading philosopher of science, educator, and proponent of logical empiricism. He is best known for founding the Berlin Circle (Gesellschaft für empirische Philosophie) alongside the Vienna Circle, for his philosophical analysis of the theory of relativity and quantum mechanics, and for his probabilistic theory of causality. His 1938 work Experience and Prediction is regarded as a classic of the movement.

Life

Reichenbach studied engineering, mathematics, physics and philosophy at Berlin, Erlangen, Göttingen and Munich; his teachers included Max Planck, Max Born, David Hilbert and Ernst Cassirer. During the First World War he served as a radio engineer on the Russian front. After the war he completed a dissertation on the theory of probability in 1920 and joined Einstein's relativity seminars in Berlin. In 1926 he became professor of the philosophy of physics at the University of Berlin. Dismissed by the Nazi regime in 1933 because of his Jewish ancestry and socialist sympathies, he emigrated to Turkey, where he taught at Istanbul University, and in 1938 moved to the University of California, Los Angeles, where he remained until his death.

Philosophy

Reichenbach's early works — The Theory of Relativity and A Priori Knowledge (1920) and The Philosophy of Space and Time (1928) — argued that Kant's synthetic a priori must be abandoned, while preserving a role for "coordinative definitions" that link mathematical structures to physical reality. In The Theory of Probability (1935) he defended a frequency interpretation of probability. His later work The Direction of Time (1956, posthumous) sought to ground the asymmetry of time in causal and statistical processes, developing the principle of the common cause and the notion of conjunctive forks. In logic he proposed a three-valued logic for quantum mechanics; in ethics and epistemology he advocated a volitional, pragmatic justification of induction grounded in his "pragmatic vindication": if any method works for prediction, induction will.

Legacy

Reichenbach trained several generations of philosophers of science at UCLA, where his students included Hilary Putnam and Wesley Salmon. The Hans Reichenbach Prize is awarded in his honour, and his collected works have been published by the University of Pittsburgh Press.
