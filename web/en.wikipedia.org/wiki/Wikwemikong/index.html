Wikwemikong, officially the Wiikwemkoong Unceded Territory, is a First Nation reserve on the eastern end of Manitoulin Island in Lake Huron, Ontario, Canada. It is home to the Wiikwemkoong First Nation, an Anishinaabe people of Ojibwe, Odawa and Potawatomi heritage — the Council of Three Fires. "Wiikwemkoong" means "bay of the beavers" in Anishinaabemowin.

Unceded status

Wikwemikong is notable as the largest unceded First Nation territory in Canada: its people never signed away their land in the treaties that covered the rest of Manitoulin Island. When the island was surrendered by other chiefs in the Manitoulin Treaty of 1862, the Wikwemikong chiefs refused to sign, and their peninsula was excluded from the surrender; the community has defended that status ever since, including in litigation and negotiation with the Crown. The reserve lands include the Wikwemikong peninsula and numerous islands in Lake Huron recognised in an 1836 arrangement, aspects of which have been the subject of long-running claims.

Community and culture

The main settlement is the village of Wikwemikong on Smith Bay. The community operates its own schools, health centre, radio station and the Wikwemikong Development Commission, and is a centre for Anishinaabe language and cultural revitalisation. It hosts the Wiikwemkoong Cultural Festival, held annually on the August civic holiday weekend since 1961 and among the oldest and largest powwows in Canada, and is home to Debajehmujig Storytellers, one of Canada's first Indigenous theatre companies, and to noted artists including painter Daphne Odjig, a founder of the "Indian Group of Seven", who grew up in the community.

History

The area has been an Anishinaabe centre for centuries, and in the 17th and 19th centuries it was an important mission site for Roman Catholic (Jesuit) missionaries among the Odawa and Ojibwe of the upper Great Lakes. The mission church at Wikwemikong, rebuilt after fires, remains a landmark of the village.
