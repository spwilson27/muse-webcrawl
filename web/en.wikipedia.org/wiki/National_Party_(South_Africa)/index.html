The National Party (Afrikaans: Nasionale Party, NP) was a political party in South Africa that governed the country from 1948 to 1994. It is best known for implementing and enforcing the system of racial segregation known as apartheid. Founded in 1914 by J. B. M. Hertzog, the party championed Afrikaner nationalism and the interests of the white Afrikaner population.

History

The party first came to power in 1924 under Hertzog as part of the Pact Government with the Labour Party. After a period in opposition during the Fusion government of the 1930s, the "purified" National Party under D. F. Malan won the 1948 general election in coalition with the Afrikaner Party, beginning 46 years of uninterrupted rule.

Under prime ministers Malan, J. G. Strijdom, Hendrik Verwoerd, B. J. Vorster, and P. W. Botha, the NP formalised racial segregation through laws governing residence, marriage, education, and political representation. Verwoerd, often called the "architect of apartheid", led South Africa to become a republic in 1961 and leave the Commonwealth.

End of apartheid and decline

In 1989 F. W. de Klerk became party leader and State President. He initiated negotiations to end apartheid, unbanning the African National Congress and releasing Nelson Mandela in 1990. The NP came second in the first democratic elections of 1994 and joined a Government of National Unity, but withdrew in 1996. Renamed the New National Party in 1997, it steadily declined, dissolved in 2005, and many of its members joined the ANC.
