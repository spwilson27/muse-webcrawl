The 1922 Committee, formally the Conservative Private Members' Committee, is the parliamentary group of the Conservative Party in the House of Commons of the United Kingdom, consisting of all backbench Conservative Members of Parliament. It meets weekly while Parliament is in session and plays a central role in the election — and removal — of the party's leader.

History

The committee was formed in 1923 by Conservative MPs first elected in the 1922 general election, initially as a social and informational forum for new members. Its name is often incorrectly associated with the Carlton Club meeting of 19 October 1922, at which Conservative MPs voted to end the coalition government of David Lloyd George. Over time membership was extended to all Conservative backbenchers.

Role in leadership contests

The 1922 Committee's executive sets the rules for Conservative leadership elections. Under rules adopted in the 2000s, a vote of confidence in the leader is triggered if 15 percent of Conservative MPs write to the chairman of the committee requesting one. The committee's chairman — for many years Graham Brady (2010–2024) — receives the confidential letters, and its meetings in its traditional venue, Committee Room 14 of the Palace of Westminster, are a focal point of political drama during crises. The committee administered the leadership processes that removed or replaced leaders including Iain Duncan Smith, Theresa May (resignation), Boris Johnson (resignation), and Liz Truss in 2022, when its chairman's visit to Downing Street preceded her announcement that she would resign after 45 days in office.

Structure

The committee elects an executive of 18 members plus officers. Ministers and frontbenchers are not members, though they may attend by invitation. In opposition, all Conservative MPs other than the leader are generally included.
