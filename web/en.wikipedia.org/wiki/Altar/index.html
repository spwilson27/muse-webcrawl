Altar

[Image: A stone altar inside a church]
A church altar.

An altar is a table or platform used as a centre of worship for presentation of religious offerings, for sacrifices, or other ritual purposes. Altars are found at shrines, temples, churches, and other places of worship in most religions, and have existed since antiquity — ranging from simple earthen mounds and flat stones to elaborate carved structures such as the ancient Greek and Roman altars and the monumental altarpieces of medieval and Renaissance churches.

In antiquity

In the ancient world, altars stood at the heart of sacrificial ritual. Animal sacrifices in Greek and Roman religion were performed at outdoor altars before temples — great examples include the Ara Pacis commissioned by Augustus and the enormous Pergamon Altar. In the Hebrew Bible, altars of earth, unhewn stone, bronze, and gold are described in detail, including the altar of burnt offering in the Tabernacle and Temple in Jerusalem. Fire altars are central to Zoroastrian worship, and stone altars crowned Mesoamerican pyramids and temples.

In Christianity

In Christian churches, the altar is the table upon which the Eucharist is consecrated. Early Christians celebrated on portable tables; as church buildings developed, fixed stone altars came to occupy the sanctuary at the east end. Roman Catholic altars traditionally contained relics of saints and were consecrated with elaborate rites; following the Second Vatican Council, altars were commonly arranged so the priest celebrates facing the congregation (versus populum). Eastern Orthodox altars, behind the iconostasis, hold the antimension and Gospel book. Protestant traditions vary: Anglicans retain altars or communion tables, while many Reformed churches use a simple table, reflecting differing Eucharistic theologies.

In other religions

Hindu worship uses altars both in temples and in homes, where images of deities receive offerings of flowers, food, and lamps. Buddhist shrines present offerings before images of the Buddha. In modern Neopagan and Wiccan practice, an altar is a personal or ritual workspace bearing symbols of the elements and deities invoked. Altars dedicated to ancestors and the dead, such as those of the Mexican Día de los Muertos (ofrendas), are also widespread.
