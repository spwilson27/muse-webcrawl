JAK-STAT signaling pathway

JAK-STAT signaling pathway
TypeSignal transduction pathway
ComponentsCytokine receptor, Janus kinase (JAK), STAT transcription factor
ActivatorsInterferons, interleukins, growth factors
RegulatorsSOCS proteins, PIAS proteins, phosphatases

The JAK-STAT signaling pathway is a chain of interactions between proteins in a cell, and is involved in processes such as immunity, cell division, cell death and tumour formation. It communicates information from chemical signals outside of a cell to the cell nucleus, resulting in the activation of genes through the process of transcription.

Mechanism

The pathway has three main components: a cell-surface receptor, a Janus kinase (JAK), and a signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) protein. When a ligand such as an interferon or interleukin binds the receptor, the receptor dimerises and associated JAKs phosphorylate each other and the receptor's intracellular tail. STAT proteins are recruited, phosphorylated by JAK, and dimerise; the STAT dimer then translocates to the nucleus, where it binds DNA and activates transcription of target genes.

Mammals have four JAKs (JAK1, JAK2, JAK3 and TYK2) and seven STATs (STAT1–4, STAT5A, STAT5B and STAT6).

Clinical significance

Dysregulation of the pathway is implicated in autoimmune and inflammatory diseases, allergies, and cancers; a gain-of-function mutation in JAK2 (V617F) is found in most patients with polycythemia vera and many with other myeloproliferative neoplasms. Drugs that inhibit JAKs — including ruxolitinib, tofacitinib and upadacitinib — are used to treat myelofibrosis, rheumatoid arthritis, atopic dermatitis and other immune-mediated conditions. The pathway is negatively regulated by SOCS (suppressor of cytokine signaling) proteins and by protein tyrosine phosphatases.
