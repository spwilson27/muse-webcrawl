Alexander Mikhaylovich Butlerov (Russian: Александр Михайлович Бутлеров; 15 September 1828 – 17 August 1886) was a Russian chemist and one of the founders of the theory of chemical structure, the conceptual foundation of modern organic chemistry.

Life and career

Butlerov was born at Chistopol in the Kazan Governorate and studied chemistry at Kazan University under Nikolay Zinin and Karl Ernst Claus. He became a professor at Kazan, where he built a leading school of organic chemistry, and later moved to Saint Petersburg University. He was elected to the Russian Academy of Sciences.

Chemical structure theory

In 1861, in a paper presented at Speyer, "On the Chemical Structure of Substances", Butlerov argued that the properties of a compound are determined by the definite arrangement of atoms in its molecule — its chemical structure — and that each compound has a single, unique structure which can in principle be deduced from its reactions. His ideas explained isomerism and made possible the systematic prediction and synthesis of new compounds. Butlerov synthesised several new substances, including isobutylene, and discovered formaldehyde (1859) and the formose reaction, in which formaldehyde polymerises to form sugars.

Legacy

Butlerov is regarded, alongside Archibald Scott Couper and August Kekulé, as a founder of structural chemistry. The lunar crater Butlerov is named in his honour.
