Autism spectrum

The autism spectrum — clinically diagnosed as autism spectrum disorder (ASD) — is a neurodevelopmental condition characterised by persistent differences in social communication and interaction, alongside restricted and repetitive patterns of behaviour, interests, or activities, and often atypical sensory responses. The term "spectrum" reflects the wide variation in presentation and support needs among autistic people.

History and classification

Leo Kanner described "early infantile autism" in 1943 and Hans Asperger described a related profile in 1944. The DSM-5 (2013) merged earlier separate diagnoses — autistic disorder, Asperger syndrome, childhood disintegrative disorder, and PDD-NOS — into the single diagnosis of ASD, with severity specified by level of support required. The ICD-11 adopted a similar framework.

Characteristics

Autistic people may communicate and socialise differently, develop intense focused interests, prefer routine, and experience sensory hypersensitivity or hyposensitivity. Co-occurring conditions are common, including ADHD, anxiety, epilepsy, and intellectual disability, though many autistic people have average or above-average intelligence.

Causes

Research indicates a strong genetic component, with heritability estimates commonly around 70–90%, involving many common and rare genetic variants; prenatal and perinatal environmental factors may also contribute. Claims linking autism to vaccines have been thoroughly debunked and the original 1998 study retracted as fraudulent.

Support and perspectives

There is no cure; support focuses on education, speech and occupational therapy, and accommodations. The neurodiversity movement, led by autistic self-advocates, frames autism as a natural form of human variation and emphasises acceptance and accessibility, sometimes in tension with parent- and clinician-led approaches aimed at treatment of associated difficulties.
