Kandovan

CountryIran
ProvinceEast Azerbaijan
DistrictOsku County, near Tabriz
Known forRock-cut cave dwellings

Kandovan is an ancient village in East Azerbaijan province, northwestern Iran, roughly 60 km southwest of Tabriz at the foot of Mount Sahand. It is famous for its troglodyte dwellings: homes carved into cone-shaped volcanic rock formations, called karaan, which resemble giant beehives or termite mounds and give the village its name (from kando, "beehive").

Description

The cones are the weathered remains of volcanic ash and debris from ancient eruptions of Mount Sahand, compacted into tuff soft enough to excavate. Villagers have hollowed them into multi-storey houses, stables and storerooms for centuries — local tradition holds the settlement to be some 800 years old, with roots perhaps much older — and roughly a hundred families still live in them year-round, the thick rock walls insulating against both summer heat and winter cold.

Tourism

Kandovan is one of Iran's best-known rural attractions, noted for its mineral spring water, local honey and handicrafts, and a cave hotel built within the rock cones. It is often compared to Cappadocia in Turkey, though unlike most such sites it remains fully inhabited.
