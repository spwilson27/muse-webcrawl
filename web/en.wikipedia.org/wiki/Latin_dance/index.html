Latin dance

Latin dance is a general label for a broad family of social and competitive dances that originated in Latin America, the Caribbean, and the Latin diaspora, together with their associated music. The term is used in two main senses: as a category of ballroom dance — the "International Latin" dances of DanceSport competition (cha-cha-cha, samba, rumba, paso doble, and jive) — and as the wider world of club and street dances such as salsa, bachata, merengue, cumbia, mambo, and Argentine tango. Latin dances are typically danced with a partner and are characterised by expressive hip movement (often called "Latin hip action"), rhythmic footwork, and interplay between the dancers.

History

Latin dances grew out of the blending of Indigenous American, African, and European traditions under colonialism. African rhythms and call-and-response structures, carried by enslaved people to Cuba, Brazil, and elsewhere in the Americas, combined with European couple dances and instruments to produce new forms: in Cuba, the danzón, son, mambo, and cha-cha-cha; in Brazil, samba; in the Dominican Republic, merengue and bachata; in Argentina and Uruguay, tango, born in the working-class port districts of Buenos Aires and Montevideo. In the twentieth century, recording, radio, film, and migration — especially to New York City, where salsa coalesced from Cuban and Puerto Rican traditions in the 1960s and 1970s — carried these dances around the world.

Ballroom Latin

In International Style ballroom dancing, five dances form the Latin-American category:

  • Cha-cha-cha — lively Cuban dance in 4/4 time, developed in the 1950s.
  • Samba — a ballroom adaptation of Brazilian samba, with a characteristic bounce action.
  • Rumba — a slow, romantic dance derived from the Cuban bolero-son tradition.
  • Paso doble — a dramatic dance modelled on Spanish bullfighting.
  • Jive — a fast, energetic dance descended from American swing and rock and roll.

The American Rhythm category of ballroom uses a partly different set (cha-cha, rumba, East Coast swing, bolero, and mambo). Latin dances contrast with the "Standard" (ballroom) dances such as waltz and tango, in which partners maintain continuous body contact; in Latin they use more open positions and separate turns.

Social and club styles

Beyond competition ballrooms, Latin dance thrives in social settings: salsa clubs and congresses, bachata festivals, Brazilian zouk, cumbia in Mexico and Central America, and milongas for Argentine tango. Zumba and other fitness programmes have further popularised Latin rhythms. Styles continue to evolve through fusion genres such as bachatango, reggaeton-influenced dance, and sensual bachata.
