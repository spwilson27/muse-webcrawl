The Jay Pritzker Pavilion, also known as the Pritzker Pavilion or Pritzker Music Pavilion, is a bandshell in Millennium Park in the Loop community area of Chicago, Illinois, United States. Located on the south side of Randolph Street and east of the Chicago Landmark Historic Michigan Boulevard District, the pavilion was designed by architect Frank Gehry and named after Jay Pritzker, whose family is known for owning Hyatt Hotels and for the Pritzker Architecture Prize.

Constructed between June 1999 and July 2004, the pavilion opened on July 16, 2004, along with the rest of Millennium Park. It serves as the home of the Grant Park Symphony Orchestra and Chorus and the Grant Park Music Festival, the country's only remaining free outdoor classical music series, and hosts a wide range of other music performance series and annual performing arts events.

Design

The pavilion is Gehry's first concert-hall design to be completed. Its stage is framed by a proscenium clad in brushed stainless steel, exploding outward into a headdress of curved, ribbon-like stainless steel panels resembling billowing sails. A trellis of crisscrossing steel pipes arches 115 feet (35 m) over the 250-by-400-foot (76 by 122 m) Great Lawn, supporting the pavilion's distributed sound system and visually tying the listening area together.

The backstage area connects to the Harris Theater for Music and Dance, which shares loading docks and dressing rooms. A curving BP Pedestrian Bridge, also designed by Gehry, crosses Columbus Drive and links the pavilion to the rest of the park; its serpentine form and steel cladding are stylistically matched to the pavilion.

The structure was built above the two-level Millennium Park parking garage on the former Illinois Central rail yard site. Funding came largely from the Pritzker family and other private donors, after the city declined to use Gehry's original costlier design for a simpler bandshell.

Sound system

The pavilion's distinctive steel trellis supports an LARES-type sound reinforcement system — an electronic architecture system with a network of loudspeakers distributed across the trellis and the proscenium. The system attempts to replicate the ambience and acoustic clarity of an indoor concert hall across the entire outdoor seating area, compensating for the absence of reflective surfaces outdoors. The network is often described as the first permanent system of its kind in the United States.

Events

The pavilion hosts the Grant Park Music Festival each summer, with open rehearsals and free concerts. Other regular events have included the Chicago Gospel Music Festival, the Chicago Blues Festival (formerly held in Grant Park), world-music series, film screenings on a large LED screen, and free morning yoga and workout sessions on the Great Lawn. The pavilion has also hosted ticketed rock and pop concerts by acts including Wilco, The Decemberists, and Tori Amos, as well as the 2012 Chicago stop of tours promoted with the adjacent Harris Theater.

Reception

Critics have praised the pavilion as the visual centerpiece of Millennium Park and an iconic piece of Chicago architecture, describing it as Gehry's most important Chicago work. The New York Times' Herbert Muschamp and Chicago Tribune critic Blair Kamin both lauded the design. The pavilion contributed to Millennium Park receiving numerous design awards and becoming one of Chicago's most visited attractions.
