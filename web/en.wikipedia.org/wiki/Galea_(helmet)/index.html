Galea (helmet)


A galea was the metal helmet worn by Roman soldiers.

A galea (from Latin galea, "helmet") was a Roman soldier's helmet. Some gladiators, specifically the myrmillones, also wore a bronze galea with face mask and crest.

Design

The galea typically consisted of a bronze or iron bowl with a broad neck guard flaring out at the back to protect the neck and shoulders, hinged cheek pieces (bucculae) that protected the face while leaving openings for the eyes, nose, and mouth, and often a knob or spike on top to hold a crest (crista) of horsehair or feathers. Crests are thought to have been worn in battle by some ranks and on parade, possibly to make the soldier appear taller and more imposing and to help officers identify their men.

Types

Archaeologists classify Roman helmets into types including the Montefortino, Coolus, and the Imperial Gallic and Imperial Italic series. The Montefortino, derived from Celtic designs, was used from the Republic into the early Empire. The Imperial Gallic type, influenced by Gaulish workshops, added reinforcing bands across the skull, embossed "eyebrows," and a deeper neck guard. Over the course of the Principate the galea was gradually modified, and by late antiquity it was largely replaced by ridge helmets of the Intercisa and Berkasovo types.

Materials and manufacture

Helmets were made from bronze or iron, sometimes tinned to give a silvery appearance. Surviving examples have been found across the former Roman world, including in rivers and at battle sites. The galea, together with the lorica segmentata, scutum, gladius, and pilum, formed the classic equipment of the Roman legionary.
