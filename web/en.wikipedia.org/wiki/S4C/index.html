S4C (Welsh pronunciation: [ˌɛs ˈpɛdwar ɛk]; from Sianel Pedwar Cymru, meaning "Channel Four Wales") is a Welsh-language free-to-air television channel broadcast in Wales. It was the first television channel to be aimed specifically at a Welsh-language audience and, alongside the English-language Channel 4, one of the first channels launched in the United Kingdom after BBC Two.

History

S4C launched on the evening of 1 November 1982, one day before Channel 4 in the rest of the UK. Its creation followed a long campaign for a Welsh-language television service, including a threat by former Plaid Cymru leader Gwynfor Evans to go on hunger strike in 1980, which persuaded the Conservative government of Margaret Thatcher to reverse its opposition to the channel.

Before the digital switchover in 2010, S4C broadcast a mix of Welsh-language programmes and English-language programmes from Channel 4 during off-peak hours. Since the completion of switchover in Wales, S4C has been an entirely Welsh-language channel, while Channel 4 is available separately.

Programming

S4C is known for the soap opera Pobol y Cwm, one of the longest-running Welsh-language television series, the children's strand Cyw, and drama productions such as Y Gwyll (Hinterland). Many of its programmes are produced by independent Welsh production companies, and content is also available through the BBC iPlayer and the S4C Clic streaming service.
