Malayan campaign

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Date8 December 1941 – 15 February 1942
LocationBritish Malaya and Singapore
BelligerentsBritish Empire (British, Indian, Australian, Malayan forces) vs. Empire of Japan
CommandersArthur Percival (Allies); Tomoyuki Yamashita (Japan)
ResultJapanese victory; fall of Singapore

The Malayan campaign was a military campaign fought by the Empire of Japan and the Allies during the Second World War for control of British Malaya and the strategic base of Singapore. It began with Japanese landings in northern Malaya and southern Thailand on 8 December 1941 and ended with the surrender of Singapore on 15 February 1942.

Course of the campaign

Japanese forces under General Tomoyuki Yamashita advanced rapidly down the Malay Peninsula, using light tanks, air superiority and troops moving on bicycles to outflank successive British defensive lines. On 10 December 1941 Japanese aircraft sank the British battleship HMS Prince of Wales and battlecruiser HMS Repulse — Force Z — removing Allied naval power from the theatre. Kuala Lumpur fell on 11 January 1942, and by the end of January the defenders had withdrawn across the Johore Strait to Singapore Island.

Fall of Singapore

After crossing the strait on 8–9 February, Japanese troops overwhelmed the island's defences within a week. On 15 February 1942 Lieutenant-General Arthur Percival surrendered some 80,000 troops — the largest surrender of British-led forces in history. Winston Churchill called the fall of Singapore "the worst disaster and largest capitulation in British history."

Aftermath

Malaya and Singapore remained under harsh Japanese occupation until 1945. The defeat shattered the prestige of the British Empire in Asia and encouraged independence movements across the region.
