Italdesign Giugiaro

Italdesign Giugiaro S.p.A. is an Italian design and engineering company based in Moncalieri, near Turin. Founded in 1968 by Giorgetto Giugiaro and Aldo Mantovani, it became one of the most influential automotive design studios in the world, styling production cars for dozens of manufacturers as well as industrial products ranging from cameras to trains. Since 2010 the company has been majority-owned by the Volkswagen Group through Lamborghini.

History

The firm began on 13 February 1968 as Studi Italiani Realizzazione Prototipi, soon renamed Italdesign. Giorgetto Giugiaro, who had previously worked at Bertone and Ghia, brought instant credibility, and the company combined styling with engineering and prototyping services, allowing manufacturers to outsource complete vehicle development. In 2010 Volkswagen acquired a 90.1% stake; the Giugiaro family sold its remaining shares in 2015, and Giorgetto and his son Fabrizio departed to found GFG Style.

Notable designs

Italdesign's production credits include the Volkswagen Golf Mk1 (1974), the Lotus Esprit (1976), the BMW M1 (1978), the Lancia Delta (1979), the DeLorean DMC-12 (1981), the Fiat Panda (1980) and Uno (1983), the Saab 9000, the Subaru SVX, and the Daewoo Matiz. Its concept cars — the Alfa Romeo Alfasud Caimano, the wedge-shaped Maserati Boomerang, and the 1988 Aztec — helped define the angular "folded paper" style of 1970s car design, for which Giugiaro was named Car Designer of the Century in 1999. Beyond cars, the studio has designed Nikon camera bodies, Seiko watches, high-speed trains, and agricultural machinery.

Recent work

Under Volkswagen ownership Italdesign has worked on group models and limited-run specials, launching its own Automobili Speciali division with the limited-production Zerouno supercar in 2017. The company continues to offer styling, engineering, and prototype construction from Moncalieri.
