Kung fu (Chinese: 功夫; pinyin: gōngfu) is an umbrella term for the Chinese martial arts. In Chinese, the term originally means any skill or discipline achieved through hard work and practice — a master calligrapher or chef can be said to have good kung fu — but in English it has come to denote the martial arts of China specifically. Closely related terms are wushu (武術, "martial arts"), the formal name of the modern sport, and quanfa (拳法, "fist method").

History

Chinese martial arts developed over millennia from military training, hunting, and ritual dance. The Shaolin Monastery became famed for the martial monks who defended it, and a legend credits the Indian monk Bodhidharma with teaching them exercises ancestral to Shaolin kung fu. During the Ming and Qing dynasties, distinctive styles crystallised, often organised by region ("northern legs, southern fists"), family, or philosophy, and intertwining with Daoist and Buddhist ideas of health and meditation (qigong). After 1949 the People's Republic codified modern wushu as a performance and competition sport, while traditional styles survived in China, Taiwan, and overseas Chinese communities.

Styles

Hundreds of styles exist. They are commonly divided into external (hard, muscular) and internal (soft, qi-focused) schools. Major external styles include Shaolinquan, Changquan, Nanquan, Wing Chun, Hung Ga, Choy Li Fut, and animal styles such as White Crane, Praying Mantis, and Monkey. The best-known internal styles are Taijiquan (tai chi), Baguazhang, and Xingyiquan. Training typically combines basic stances, forms (taolu), weapons practice (staff, spear, broadsword, straight sword), application drills, and sparring (sanshou/sanda).

Popular culture

Kung fu cinema — from Bruce Lee and the Shaw Brothers studio to Jackie Chan, Jet Li, and films such as Crouching Tiger, Hidden Dragon and the animated Kung Fu Panda series — globalised the term and inspired martial artists worldwide. Bruce Lee's philosophy of Jeet Kune Do explicitly drew on the broad idea of kung fu as cultivated skill.
