Honey bee

A honey bee is a eusocial flying insect within the genus Apis of the bee clade. Honey bees are known for their construction of perennial colonial nests from wax, the large size of their colonies, and their production and storage of honey — distinguishing their hives as prized targets for beekeepers (apiculture) for thousands of years. Only eight species of honey bee are currently recognised.

Colony structure

A colony consists of a single queen, tens of thousands of female workers, and, seasonally, male drones. Workers progress through tasks — nursing, building comb, guarding, foraging — as they age. Foragers communicate the location of food sources through the waggle dance.

Importance to humans

The western honey bee (Apis mellifera) has been domesticated worldwide for honey and beeswax and as a pollinator of crops, contributing an enormous fraction of global agricultural pollination services.

Threats

Honey bees face parasites such as the Varroa mite, pathogens, pesticides, habitat loss and colony collapse disorder, in which most worker bees abandon a colony. Conservation and improved husbandry are widely studied responses.
