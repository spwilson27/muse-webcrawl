Katoomba Scenic World

Scenic World
Location: Katoomba, New South Wales, Australia
Opened: 1945 (as Scenic World)
Attractions: Scenic Railway, Scenic Skyway, Scenic Cableway, Scenic Walkway
Owner: Hammons family

Scenic World is a tourist attraction at Katoomba in the Blue Mountains of New South Wales, Australia, overlooking the Jamison Valley. It operates four attractions: the Scenic Railway, the Scenic Skyway, the Scenic Cableway and the Scenic Walkway.

Attractions

The Scenic Railway, opened in 1945 on the line of an 1878 coal-mine tramway built to haul coal and kerosene shale from the valley floor, descends 310 metres through a cliff-side tunnel at a gradient of 52 degrees — recognised as the steepest passenger railway in the world. The Scenic Skyway cable car, opened in 1958, crosses the gorge above Katoomba Falls at a height of about 270 metres. The Scenic Cableway, the steepest aerial cable car in the Southern Hemisphere, descends into the valley, where the Scenic Walkway offers 2.4 km of elevated boardwalk through temperate rainforest.

History

The site was developed by Harry Hammon, who bought the coal-mine lease in 1945 and converted the colliery railway for passengers; the business remains family-owned by the Hammons. The attractions were progressively rebuilt, including a new Skyway cabin in 2017 and redeveloped railway trains in 2013.
