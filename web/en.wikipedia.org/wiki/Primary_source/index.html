Primary source

In the study of history as an academic discipline, a primary source (also called an original source) is an artifact, document, diary, manuscript, autobiography, recording, or any other source of information that was created at the time under study. It serves as an original source of information about the topic.

In other fields, such as library science, primary sources may also be distinguished from secondary sources, which cite, comment on, or build upon primary sources. The classification of sources as primary or secondary depends on how the source is used: the same source can be primary for one inquiry and secondary for another.

Examples

Examples of primary sources include:

  • Letters, diaries, and memoirs
  • Official records such as census data and court records
  • Newspaper and magazine articles from the period
  • Photographs, films, and audio recordings
  • Artifacts such as tools, clothing, and furniture
  • Speeches, interviews, and oral histories
  • Original research data and scientific reports

Use in historical research

Historians use primary sources as closely as possible to the original event. Working with such sources requires source criticism: evaluating the authenticity, authorship, purpose, and reliability of a document. A primary source is not necessarily more truthful than a secondary source; it may reflect the biases or limited knowledge of its creator.
