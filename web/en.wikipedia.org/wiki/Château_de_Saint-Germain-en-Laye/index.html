Château de Saint-Germain-en-Laye
Location: Saint-Germain-en-Laye, Yvelines, France
Built: From 12th century; rebuilt under Francis I
Now: Musée d'Archéologie nationale

The Château de Saint-Germain-en-Laye is a former royal palace west of Paris, in the commune of Saint-Germain-en-Laye. It was a favourite residence of French kings from the Middle Ages until Louis XIV moved the court to Versailles.

History

A fortress was founded under Louis VI in the 12th century; the Sainte-Chapelle was built under Saint Louis (Louis IX) in the 1230s. Francis I rebuilt the château from 1539. Henri IV added terraces and gardens overlooking the Seine. Louis XIV was born here in 1638 and made it his principal residence before 1682. It later sheltered the exiled court of James II of England after 1688.

Later use

The Treaty of Saint-Germain-en-Laye (1919) with Austria was signed here after the First World War — one of the Paris Peace Conference treaties. Since 1862 the château has housed the National Archaeology Museum.
