The Adda is a river in North Italy, a tributary of the Po. It rises in the Alps near the border with Switzerland and flows through Lake Como. The Adda joins the Po a few kilometres upstream of Cremona. It is 313 kilometres (194 mi) long, making it one of the longest rivers entirely within Italy.

Course

The river rises at about 2,237 metres (7,339 ft) above sea level at Val Alpisella in the Rhaetian Alps, flows through the Valtellina valley past Sondrio and Tirano, and enters Lake Como at its northern end. It leaves the lake at Lecco and flows south through Trezzo sull'Adda, Cassano d'Adda, and Lodi before joining the Po.

History

The Adda valley was the scene of fighting in ancient Roman campaigns and later formed a contested frontier between Milan and Venice. Leonardo da Vinci studied the river's hydraulics and designed canal works while in Milanese service. The Battle of Lodi in 1796 was fought at the Adda crossing.
