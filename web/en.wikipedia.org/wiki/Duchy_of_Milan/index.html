Duchy of Milan

Duchy of Milan (Ducato di Milano)
Status: Imperial duchy, later Spanish and Austrian possession
Existed: 1395–1796
Capital: Milan

The Duchy of Milan was an Italian state of the Holy Roman Empire centred on Milan and covering much of Lombardy. Created in 1395 for Gian Galeazzo Visconti, it became one of the great powers of Renaissance Italy.

Visconti and Sforza

Under the Visconti the duchy expanded aggressively across northern Italy; Gian Galeazzo came close to uniting the peninsula before his death in 1402. After the brief Ambrosian Republic (1447–1450), the condottiere Francesco Sforza seized Milan and founded a new dynasty. Under Ludovico Sforza, Milan's court, adorned by Leonardo da Vinci and Bramante, was among the most splendid in Europe.

Foreign rule

Ludovico's appeals to France triggered the Italian Wars; Milan became the prize contested by France and Habsburg Spain. The last Sforza died in 1535, and the duchy passed to Spain, remaining a Spanish possession for nearly 170 years. In the War of the Spanish Succession it passed to Austria, whose rule (interrupted by Napoleon's conquest in 1796) shaped Lombard administration into the 19th century.

Culture

The duchy produced Milan Cathedral's completion campaigns, the Certosa di Pavia, Leonardo's Last Supper, and a distinctive Lombard Renaissance blending Gothic tradition with classical innovation.
