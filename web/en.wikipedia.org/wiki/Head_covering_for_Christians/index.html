Head covering for Christians

Christian head covering, also known as Christian veiling, is the traditional practice of women covering their heads in public and especially during prayer and worship, based primarily on 1 Corinthians 11 in the New Testament. Coverings have included veils, scarves, hats, and mantillas.

History

Head covering was near-universal among Christian women for most of church history and was taught by early Church Fathers such as Tertullian and John Chrysostom. It remains customary in Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox, and many Anabaptist and conservative Protestant communities, and is still widely practised in parts of Eastern Europe, Africa, and Asia. In Roman Catholic practice, chapel veils were common until the mid-20th century and have seen a revival of interest among some traditionalists.

Interpretation

Communities differ over whether the practice is a permanent ordinance, a cultural custom of the first century, or a symbol whose substance is preserved in other ways. Churches that practise veiling typically cite headship, modesty, and reverence in worship as its rationale.
