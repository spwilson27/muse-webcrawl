Self-supervised learning

Self-supervised learning is a paradigm of machine learning in which a model is trained on data without human-provided labels, using supervisory signals that are generated from the data itself. A model learns representations by solving a "pretext" task — predicting some withheld, corrupted, or transformed part of the input — and the resulting representations are then transferred to downstream tasks, often with little labelled data.

Methods

In natural language processing, the dominant pretext tasks are masked language modelling, as in BERT, where random tokens are hidden and predicted from context, and next-token (autoregressive) prediction, as in the GPT family; these underpin modern large language models. In computer vision, methods include predicting image rotations, solving jigsaw puzzles, colorising greyscale images, and, most successfully, contrastive learning (such as SimCLR and MoCo), which trains a model to pull representations of augmented views of the same image together and push apart views of different images, and masked image modelling (MAE), which reconstructs heavily masked images. Related non-contrastive methods such as BYOL, DINO, and Barlow Twins avoid trivial collapse of representations through architectural asymmetries, stop-gradients, or redundancy-reduction objectives.

Significance

Self-supervised learning addresses the bottleneck of labelled data: unlabelled text, images, audio, and video are abundant, and pretext training at scale produces general-purpose "foundation models" that can be fine-tuned, prompted, or used as feature extractors. The term gained prominence in the late 2010s, championed by researchers such as Yann LeCun, who argued that self-supervision — predicting parts of the world from other parts — is the likely route to more general machine intelligence, since most human and animal learning is of this kind. Challenges include the cost of large-scale pretraining, understanding why particular pretext tasks produce transferable representations, and biases inherited from unfiltered web data.
