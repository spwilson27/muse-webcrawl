Pomp and Circumstance

The Pomp and Circumstance Marches, Op. 39, are a set of five marches for orchestra composed by Edward Elgar between 1901 and 1930. The title is taken from Act III of Shakespeare's Othello: "Pride, pomp, and circumstance of glorious war!"

March No. 1 and "Land of Hope and Glory"

The first march, in D major, premiered in Liverpool in October 1901. Its famous trio melody was later fitted with words by A. C. Benson to become "Land of Hope and Glory", the centrepiece of Elgar's Coronation Ode of 1902, and it remains an unofficial English national anthem sung at the Last Night of the Proms.

Graduation use

In the United States and several other countries, the trio of March No. 1 is played as the processional at high-school and university graduation ceremonies — a tradition dating from 1905, when the march was played at Yale University as Elgar received an honorary doctorate.

The other marches

March No. 2 in A minor (1901) is the shortest; No. 3 in C minor (1904), No. 4 in G major (1907) — whose trio was adapted during the Second World War as "Song of Freedom" — and No. 5 in C major (1930) complete the published set. Sketches for a sixth march were elaborated by Anthony Payne in 2006.
