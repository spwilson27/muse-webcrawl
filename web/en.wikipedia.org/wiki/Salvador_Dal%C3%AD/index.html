Salvador Dalí

Salvador Domingo Felipe Jacinto Dalí i Domènech (11 May 1904 – 23 January 1989) was a Spanish surrealist artist renowned for striking, dreamlike images and flamboyant public persona. His best-known work, The Persistence of Memory (1931), with its melting pocket watches, is an icon of Surrealism.

Career

Dalí developed a "paranoiac-critical method" to access the subconscious, producing paintings such as Metamorphosis of Narcissus and Lobster Telephone (sculpture). He collaborated with Luis Buñuel on the film Un Chien Andalou (1929), worked in the US during World War II, designed for Hitchcock's Spellbound, and later pursued "nuclear mysticism" and optical illusions.

Legacy

The Dalí Theatre-Museum in Figueres and the Salvador Dalí Museum in Florida house major collections. His commercialism drew criticism from fellow surrealists (Breton anagrammed his name as "Avida Dollars"), yet his influence on pop art and popular culture is immense.
