Rough Wooing

The Rough Wooing (December 1543 – March 1551), also known as the Eight Years' War, was a war between Scotland and England. Following England's break with Rome, it was fought in an attempt by England to force a marriage between Mary, Queen of Scots (aged five months at the start of the war) and the son of the ageing King Henry VIII, Edward, Prince of Wales, and to obtain Scotland's agreement to an alliance. The expression "rough wooing" derives from a remark attributed to the Earl of Hertford, and was popularised by Sir Walter Scott.

Background

In 1542 King James V of Scotland died shortly after the Scottish defeat at the Battle of Solway Moss, leaving his infant daughter Mary as queen. In July 1543 the Treaties of Greenwich proposed that Mary marry Edward, but the Scottish Parliament repudiated the treaties in December 1543 and renewed the Auld Alliance with France. Henry VIII responded with punitive invasions of Scotland.

Course of the war

In May 1544 an English army under the Earl of Hertford landed near Leith and burned Edinburgh. The Scots won a notable victory at the Battle of Ancrum Moor in February 1545. After Henry's death in 1547, the Duke of Somerset, Protector for Edward VI, invaded again and won the Battle of Pinkie Cleugh in September 1547, the last great pitched battle between the two kingdoms; English garrisons were planted in the Scottish Lowlands, most notably at Haddington. Scotland, however, refused to submit: in 1548 the Treaty of Haddington betrothed Mary to the French Dauphin Francis, and the young queen was sent to France. French troops reinforced Scotland, and the English garrisons were progressively starved out. The Treaty of Norham in 1551 formally ended the war.

Consequences

The war devastated the Scottish Borders and the Lothians but failed in its object: Mary married the Dauphin in 1558, briefly uniting the Scottish and French crowns' claims. The conflict hardened Scottish resistance to English interference and shaped the politics that led to the Scottish Reformation of 1560.
