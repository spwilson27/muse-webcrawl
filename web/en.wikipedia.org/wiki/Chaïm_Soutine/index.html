Chaïm Soutine

Chaïm Soutine (13 January 1893 – 9 August 1943) was a Belarusian-born painter of Jewish origin who made a major contribution to Expressionism while living and working in Paris. Associated with the School of Paris, he is known for his turbulent portraits, landscapes and still lifes — above all his series of hanging beef carcasses and portraits of pastry cooks and choirboys — painted with agitated brushwork and intense colour.

Life

Soutine was born in Smilavičy, near Minsk, in the Russian Empire (now Belarus), the tenth of eleven children of a tailor. Against his family's wishes he studied art in Minsk and at the Academy of Fine Arts in Vilnius. In 1913 he emigrated to Paris, studied briefly at the École des Beaux-Arts under Fernand Cormon, and settled at La Ruche, the artists' residence in Montparnasse, where he became a close friend of Amedeo Modigliani, who painted his portrait several times.

For years Soutine lived in poverty, painting in a frenzy and sometimes destroying his own canvases. His fortunes changed in 1923 when the American collector Albert C. Barnes bought some sixty of his works on sight, bringing him sudden wealth and fame. His dealer Léopold Zborowski and later buyers in America sustained a successful career, though Soutine remained anxious, reclusive and plagued by stomach ulcers.

Work

Between 1920 and 1929, much of it painted at Céret and Cagnes in the south of France, Soutine produced his best-known work: swirling, distorted landscapes; portraits of hotel staff, valets and pastry chefs in their whites; and the Carcass of Beef series (c. 1925), inspired by Rembrandt's Slaughtered Ox, painted from real carcasses kept in his studio. His mature style uses thick impasto, slashing strokes and a palette of deep reds, yellows and greens to convey emotional intensity rather than observed accuracy.

Death and legacy

After the German invasion of France in 1940, Soutine, as a Jew, fled Paris and lived in hiding in the countryside, forced to move frequently to avoid arrest. His health collapsed; he was smuggled to Paris for an emergency operation for a perforated ulcer but died on 9 August 1943 and was buried in Montparnasse Cemetery.

Soutine's work profoundly influenced later painters, including Willem de Kooning and the Abstract Expressionists, and Francis Bacon and the School of London. His paintings are held by the Barnes Foundation, the Musée de l'Orangerie, the National Gallery of Art in Washington and other major collections.
