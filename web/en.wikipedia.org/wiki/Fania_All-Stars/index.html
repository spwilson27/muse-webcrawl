Fania All-Stars

The Fania All-Stars is a supergroup of salsa musicians formed in 1968 by Fania Records, the New York label founded by Johnny Pacheco and Jerry Masucci. Drawing together the label's leading artists, the All-Stars became the flagship ensemble of the 1970s salsa boom and helped carry salsa music from New York's Latin dance halls to concert stadiums around the world.

Origin:New York City, United States
Genres:Salsa, Latin jazz
Years active:1968–present (intermittently)
Label:Fania Records
Key members:Johnny Pacheco, Celia Cruz, Héctor Lavoe, Willie Colón, Ray Barretto, Rubén Blades

History

The group's first performance, in 1968 at the Red Garter club in Greenwich Village, was released as the Live at the Red Garter albums. Their breakthrough came on 26 August 1971 with a concert at the Cheetah nightclub in Manhattan, filmed and recorded for the album and film Our Latin Thing (Nuestra Cosa), widely credited with igniting the salsa explosion. In 1973 the All-Stars played before a crowd of some 40,000 at Yankee Stadium, and in 1974 they performed in Kinshasa, Zaire, as part of the Zaire 74 festival accompanying the "Rumble in the Jungle" boxing match.

Legacy

With singers such as Celia Cruz, Héctor Lavoe, Ismael Miranda, Cheo Feliciano and Pete "El Conde" Rodríguez, and instrumentalists including Ray Barretto, Willie Colón, Larry Harlow and Bobby Valentín, the Fania All-Stars defined the classic New York salsa sound — tight brass, driving percussion and extended improvisational descarga jams. Their albums for Columbia Records in the late 1970s pursued crossover success, and the group has reunited periodically for anniversary tours.
