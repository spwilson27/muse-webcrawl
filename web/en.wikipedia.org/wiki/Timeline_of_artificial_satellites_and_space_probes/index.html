Timeline of artificial satellites and space probes

This timeline of artificial satellites and space probes records the launch dates of satellites and space probes, beginning with the first artificial satellite, Sputnik 1, launched by the Soviet Union on 4 October 1957. It covers Earth-orbiting satellites and interplanetary probes in chronological order.

1950s

Sputnik 1 (1957) was followed by Sputnik 2, carrying the dog Laika, and by the first American satellite, Explorer 1 (1958), which discovered the Van Allen radiation belts. Luna 1, 2, and 3 in 1959 were the first probes to reach the Moon and photograph its far side.

1960s–1970s

The 1960s saw the first weather, communications, and navigation satellites, and probes to Venus and Mars, including Mariner and Venera. The 1970s brought Pioneer and Voyager missions to the outer planets, the Skylab and Salyut space stations, and the Viking landers on Mars.

Later decades

Subsequent entries cover the space shuttle era, the Hubble Space Telescope, the International Space Station, commercial satellite constellations, and missions such as Cassini, New Horizons, and the Mars rovers, through to the present era of megaconstellations and sample-return missions.
