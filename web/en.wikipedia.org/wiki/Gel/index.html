Gel

A gel is a soft, solid-like material consisting of a cross-linked three-dimensional network of solid (often polymer) dispersed within a liquid, which it immobilises. By weight, gels are mostly liquid, yet they behave like solids because the network resists flow. The process of forming a gel is called gelation, and the point at which it occurs is the gel point.

Structure and properties

Gels are a class of colloid. In a hydrogel the liquid phase is water, held in networks of materials such as gelatin, agar, pectin, alginate or synthetic polymers; organogels use organic solvents, while aerogels are produced by replacing the liquid with gas, giving extremely low-density solids. The network may be held together by chemical (covalent) cross-links or by physical associations such as hydrogen bonding and crystallites, the latter producing thermo-reversible gels that melt on heating — the familiar behaviour of gelatin desserts.

Gels typically exhibit viscoelasticity, a finite yield stress, and phenomena such as swelling, shrinking in response to temperature, pH or solvent (as in smart hydrogels), and syneresis, the slow expulsion of liquid.

Applications

Gels are used in foods (jellies, gums), cosmetics and hair gel, pharmaceuticals (topical gels, drug delivery), medicine (wound dressings, contact lenses, tissue engineering), photography and laboratory techniques such as gel electrophoresis, and industry (silica gel desiccant, gel fuels, and gel batteries).
