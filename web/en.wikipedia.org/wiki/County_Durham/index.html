County Durham

Ceremonial county in North East England; county town Durham.

County Durham is a ceremonial county in North East England. The county town is the city of Durham, and the largest settlements include Darlington, Hartlepool, and Stockton-on-Tees. The county borders Tyne and Wear and Northumberland to the north, the North Sea to the east, North Yorkshire to the south, and Cumbria to the west.

History

The county grew out of the lands of the community of St Cuthbert, governed for centuries by the prince-bishops of Durham, who exercised palatine powers—raising armies, minting coinage, and administering their own law—until these were curtailed in the 16th century and finally abolished in 1836. Durham Cathedral, founded in 1093 to house the shrine of St Cuthbert, and the adjoining castle form a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

From the 19th century, the Durham coalfield powered heavy industry: coal mining, iron and steel at Consett, shipbuilding, and engineering shaped the landscape and communities. The Stockton and Darlington Railway, the world's first public steam railway, opened in 1825. The decline of mining in the later 20th century, culminating in the miners' strike of 1984–85 and subsequent pit closures, brought severe economic change.

Geography and governance

The west of the county rises into the North Pennines, an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty, including High Force, one of England's largest waterfalls, on the River Tees. Since 2009 the former county council area has been governed by the unitary Durham County Council, with Darlington, Hartlepool, and parts of Stockton-on-Tees forming separate unitary authorities within the ceremonial county.

Culture

The Durham Miners' Gala, held annually since 1871, celebrates the county's mining heritage with brass bands and union banners. Durham University, founded in 1832, is one of England's oldest universities. The county cricket club, based at Chester-le-Street, won the County Championship in 2008, 2009, and 2013.
