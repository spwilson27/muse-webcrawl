Empire State Trail

The Empire State Trail is a 750-mile (1,210 km) multi-use trail system in the U.S. state of New York, completed in December 2020. It is the longest multi-use recreational trail in the United States, running from New York City north to the Canadian border at Rouses Point, and from Albany west to Buffalo. The trail links existing off-road segments with newly built sections and on-road connections.

Route

The trail comprises three principal legs forming a "T" shape. The Hudson Valley Greenway runs from Manhattan north through the Hudson Valley to Albany. The Erie Canalway Trail runs west from Albany to Buffalo largely along the historic Erie Canal towpath. The Champlain Valley Trail continues north from Albany through the Lake Champlain region to Canada. About 85 percent of the route is off-road.

History

Governor Andrew Cuomo announced the Empire State Trail initiative in 2017, with state funding to close gaps between existing trails including the Hudson River Valley Greenway, the Erie Canalway, and local rail trails. Construction of the remaining segments proceeded over three years; the trail was declared complete on 31 December 2020, with new welcome centers, signage, and trailheads along the route.

Use

The trail is open to cyclists, walkers, runners, and, in winter on some segments, cross-country skiers and snowmobilers. It passes through dozens of communities and state parks and is marketed as both a recreational resource and an engine of heritage tourism along the canal corridor and Hudson Valley.
