Ikshvaku

Ikshvaku (Sanskrit: इक्ष्वाकु, IAST: Ikṣvāku) is a legendary king in Hindu mythology. He is described to be the first king of the Ikshvaku dynasty, also known as the Suryavamsha (solar dynasty), and of the kingdom of Kosala in ancient India. According to the Ramayana and Mahabharata, Ikshvaku was the son of Vaivasvata Manu, and the grandson of the sun god Surya.

Legend

Ikshvaku is credited with establishing the city of Ayodhya as his capital. His descendants include the kings Sagara, Bhagiratha, Raghu and, most famously, Rama, the hero of the Ramayana. The name is said to derive from the Sanskrit word ikṣu, meaning sugarcane, in connection with a legend of the king's birth.

Other traditions

Ikshvaku also appears in Jain and Buddhist traditions; in Buddhism he (as Okkāka) is considered an ancestor of the Shakya clan, to which Gautama Buddha belonged.
