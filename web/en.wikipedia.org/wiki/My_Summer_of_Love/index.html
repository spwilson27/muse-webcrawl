My Summer of Love is a 2004 British drama film directed by Paweł Pawlikowski and co-written by Pawlikowski and Michael Wynne. It is loosely based on the novel of the same name by Helen Cross. The film stars Natalie Press as Mona, a working-class teenager in a Yorkshire village, and Emily Blunt, in her feature film debut, as Tamsin, a wealthy and manipulative girl with whom Mona becomes infatuated over one summer. Paddy Considine plays Mona's brother Phil, an ex-convict turned born-again Christian.

Plot

Mona lives above a pub with her brother Phil, who has renounced his former life of violence and petty crime and embraced evangelical Christianity, much to Mona's irritation. She meets Tamsin, who is home from boarding school for the summer and claims her sister Sadie has died of anorexia. The two girls form an intense friendship that deepens into a love affair. Tamsin enchants Mona with stories, Edith Piaf records, and a glamorous, decadent world. When Phil attempts to exorcise and reform the girls, the affair reaches a crisis, and Mona discovers that many of Tamsin's stories — including the death of her sister — were fabrications. The film ends with a confrontation in which the fantasy collapses and Mona leaves Tamsin behind.

Reception

The film received critical acclaim for its performances and atmosphere. It won the Alexander Korda Award for Best British Film at the 2005 BAFTA Awards, and Pawlikowski won the award for best director at several festivals. Emily Blunt's performance launched her international career.
