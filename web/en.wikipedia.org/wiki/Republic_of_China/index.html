Republic of China

The Republic of China (ROC), commonly known as Taiwan, is a country in East Asia. Founded in 1912 in mainland China as the successor to the Qing dynasty, the ROC government retreated to the island of Taiwan in 1949 after losing the Chinese Civil War to the Chinese Communist Party, which established the People's Republic of China (PRC) on the mainland. The ROC now effectively administers Taiwan and a number of smaller islands, including Penghu, Kinmen and Matsu.

History

The republic was proclaimed on 1 January 1912 with Sun Yat-sen as provisional president. Its early decades were marked by warlordism, the Northern Expedition, war with Japan (1937–1945) and renewed civil war. Following the government's relocation to Taipei in 1949, Taiwan remained under martial law until 1987, after which a process of democratisation produced competitive multi-party elections; the first direct presidential election was held in 1996.

Political status

The political status of Taiwan is disputed. The PRC claims Taiwan as part of its territory and considers the ROC defunct, while the ROC maintains that it is a sovereign state. Most countries recognise the PRC and maintain unofficial relations with Taiwan; a small number of states maintain formal diplomatic relations with the ROC. Taiwan participates in many international organisations under names such as "Chinese Taipei".

Geography and economy

Taiwan island is mountainous in its eastern two-thirds, with densely populated plains in the west. With a population of about 23.5 million, the ROC has a highly developed, export-oriented economy, prominent in semiconductors — the Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) is the world's largest contract chipmaker — as well as electronics, petrochemicals and machinery. Its capital and largest city is Taipei.
