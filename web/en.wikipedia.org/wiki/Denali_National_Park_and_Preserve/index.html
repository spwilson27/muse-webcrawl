Overview

Denali National Park and Preserve is a US national park in the Alaska Range, centred on Denali, at 20,310 feet (6,190 m) the highest mountain in North America. Established in 1917 largely through the efforts of naturalist Charles Alexander Sheldon to protect Dall sheep, it protects over six million acres of taiga, tundra and glaciers.

Visiting

A single 92-mile road crosses the park; since 1972 most visitors travel by park buses beyond mile 15, making wildlife viewing — grizzly bears, caribou, moose, wolves, Dall sheep — a signature of the Denali experience. The mountain shows itself on only about a third of summer days, a statistic rangers repeat with pride ("the 30% club"). The park was known as Mount McKinley National Park until 1980; the mountain's federal name was restored to Denali in 2015 (a 2025 executive order re-designated the peak Mount McKinley while the park kept the Denali name).
