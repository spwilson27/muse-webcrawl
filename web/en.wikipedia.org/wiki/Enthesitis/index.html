Enthesitis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Enthesitis is inflammation of the entheses, the sites where tendons or ligaments insert into the bone. It is a hallmark feature of the spondyloarthropathies, a group of inflammatory rheumatic diseases that includes ankylosing spondylitis, psoriatic arthritis, reactive arthritis, and enteropathic arthritis.

Signs and symptoms

Enthesitis typically causes pain, tenderness, stiffness, and sometimes swelling at the insertion site. Common sites include the Achilles tendon insertion at the heel, the plantar fascia insertion on the calcaneus, the patellar tendon, and the insertions around the elbow. Pain is often worse with activity and after periods of rest.

Causes

In the spondyloarthropathies, enthesitis is driven by immune-mediated inflammation and is strongly associated with the HLA-B27 genetic marker. Mechanical stress at entheses may contribute to the localization of inflammation. Enthesitis-like pain can also occur from overuse injuries, though this mechanical enthesopathy is distinct from inflammatory enthesitis.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is primarily clinical, supported by imaging such as ultrasound and MRI, which can show thickening, erosions, and bone marrow edema at the enthesis. Treatment includes nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs), physical therapy, and, in persistent cases associated with spondyloarthritis, biologic agents such as TNF inhibitors and IL-17 inhibitors.
