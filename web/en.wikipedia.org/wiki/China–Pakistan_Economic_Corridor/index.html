China–Pakistan Economic Corridor

Countries: China, Pakistan

Launched: 2015

Announced investment: Over US$60 billion

Key sites: Gwadar Port, Kashgar, Karakoram Highway

The China–Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) is a collection of infrastructure and energy projects under construction throughout Pakistan, launched in 2015 as the flagship project of China's Belt and Road Initiative. It aims to modernise Pakistan's transport networks and energy infrastructure and to connect China's Xinjiang region with the Arabian Sea port of Gwadar in Balochistan.

Projects

CPEC's first phase concentrated on energy — coal, wind, solar and hydroelectric power plants built to relieve Pakistan's chronic electricity shortages — and on roads, including upgrades to the Karakoram Highway and new motorways linking Karachi and Lahore to the north. The port of Gwadar, operated by a Chinese state company, is being developed with an adjacent free trade zone. Later phases emphasise industrial cooperation through special economic zones, agriculture and the ML-1 railway upgrade between Karachi and Peshawar.

Strategic significance

For China, the corridor offers a shorter overland route for energy imports from the Middle East, bypassing the Strait of Malacca. For Pakistan, it represents the largest foreign investment programme in its history. The project has also drawn criticism over debt sustainability, transparency, security attacks on Chinese workers, and India's objection to routes passing through Gilgit-Baltistan in territory it claims.
