Sodium borohydride

Sodium borohydride, also known as sodium tetrahydridoborate, is an inorganic compound with the formula NaBH4. This white crystalline solid, usually encountered as a powder, is a reducing agent that finds application in papermaking and dye industries. It is also used in organic synthesis as a versatile reagent for the reduction of aldehydes and ketones.

Discovery and production

Sodium borohydride was discovered in the 1940s by H. I. Schlesinger's team, which was searching for volatile uranium compounds for the Manhattan Project. It is manufactured by the Bayer process, reacting trimethyl borate with sodium hydride at high temperature.

Chemistry

NaBH4 is a more selective reducing agent than lithium aluminium hydride: it reduces aldehydes and ketones to alcohols but usually leaves esters, carboxylic acids, nitriles and epoxides untouched. It reacts with water, slowly at neutral pH and rapidly in acid, releasing hydrogen gas. In protic solvents such as methanol and ethanol it is commonly used at 0 °C to room temperature. It also reduces many metal salts to the metals or borides, which is used to prepare nanoparticle catalysts.

Applications

Beyond organic chemistry, sodium borohydride is used for bleaching wood pulp, for recovering precious metals from waste streams, and in experimental hydrogen storage and direct borohydride fuel cells.
