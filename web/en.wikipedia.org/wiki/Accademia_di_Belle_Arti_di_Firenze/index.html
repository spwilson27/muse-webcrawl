Accademia di Belle Arti di Firenze

The Accademia di Belle Arti di Firenze (Academy of Fine Arts of Florence) is an art academy in Florence, Italy, rooted in the Accademia delle Arti del Disegno founded in 1563 by Cosimo I de' Medici at the instigation of Giorgio Vasari — generally regarded as the first academy of art in Europe. The school's former premises house the Galleria dell'Accademia, home to Michelangelo's David.

History

The Accademia e Compagnia delle Arti del Disegno was founded in January 1563 with Michelangelo as its nominal head; it allowed artists to organise outside the guild system and study anatomy, perspective and drawing. In 1784 Grand Duke Pietro Leopoldo of Tuscany reorganised Florentine art teaching into the Accademia di Belle Arti, uniting schools of painting, sculpture, architecture and music. Michelangelo's David was moved to the Academy's gallery in 1873 to protect it from the elements, and the Galleria dell'Accademia became a separate museum institution in the twentieth century.

The school today

The Academy offers degree-equivalent diplomas in painting, sculpture, scenography, graphic arts, decoration and museology, and remains one of Italy's principal state art academies. Its historic library and collection of plaster casts preserve works by Lorenzo Bartolini, Luigi Pampaloni and other Tuscan artists.
