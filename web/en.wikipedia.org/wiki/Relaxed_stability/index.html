Relaxed stability

Relaxed stability, also known as relaxed static stability, is an aircraft design approach in which the aircraft is intentionally made aerodynamically unstable — or nearly so — in pitch, and relies on a computerised fly-by-wire flight control system to provide artificial stability.

Advantages

Moving the centre of gravity aft, close to or behind the aerodynamic centre, reduces the download the tailplane must produce to trim the aircraft. This lowers trim drag, allows a smaller tailplane, improves fuel efficiency and can greatly increase manoeuvrability, which is especially valuable in fighter aircraft.

Applications

The General Dynamics F-16 Fighting Falcon was the first production aircraft designed with relaxed static stability, using quadruplex fly-by-wire controls to make thousands of corrective inputs per second. Many later fighters, such as the Eurofighter Typhoon, and some airliners use milder forms of the concept.
