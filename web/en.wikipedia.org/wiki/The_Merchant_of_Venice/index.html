The Merchant of Venice

The Merchant of Venice is a play by William Shakespeare, believed to have been written between 1596 and 1598. Classified among Shakespeare's comedies, it is famous for its dramatic scenes and for the character Shylock, one of Shakespeare's most complex creations.

Plot

Bassanio, a young Venetian gentleman, borrows money from his friend Antonio, the merchant of the title, to woo the heiress Portia of Belmont. Antonio, whose wealth is all at sea, borrows 3,000 ducats from the Jewish moneylender Shylock, agreeing to a bond of a pound of his flesh if he defaults. When Antonio's ships are reported lost, Shylock demands the bond. At the trial, Portia, disguised as a young lawyer, first appeals for mercy ("The quality of mercy is not strained") and then defeats Shylock on a legal technicality: he may take the flesh but shed no blood. Shylock loses his fortune and is forced to convert to Christianity; meanwhile Bassanio wins Portia by choosing the lead casket in her father's test, and Antonio's ships are revealed to have arrived safely after all.

Shylock and the antisemitism debate

Shylock's portrayal — including the "Hath not a Jew eyes?" speech — has been debated for centuries. He can be played as a comic villain, as Elizabethan audiences likely saw him, or as a wronged, dignified victim of Christian prejudice, an interpretation that has dominated since the nineteenth century and deepened after the Holocaust. The play remains one of Shakespeare's most discussed works on questions of justice, mercy, prejudice and identity.

Notable speeches

  • "The quality of mercy is not strained" — Portia
  • "Hath not a Jew eyes?" — Shylock
  • "All that glisters is not gold" — from the scroll in the golden casket

Adaptations

The play has inspired films (including the 2004 version with Al Pacino as Shylock), operas, and countless stage productions. It is frequently studied in schools.
