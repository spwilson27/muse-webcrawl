The Band

OriginToronto, Ontario, Canada
GenresRoots rock, Americana, folk rock
Years active1967–1977; 1983–1999
MembersRobbie Robertson, Rick Danko, Levon Helm, Richard Manuel, Garth Hudson

The Band was a Canadian-American rock band formed in Toronto, Ontario, in 1967. The group consisted of Canadians Robbie Robertson (guitar, piano, lead vocals), Rick Danko (bass, guitar, vocals, fiddle), Garth Hudson (keyboards, accordion, saxophone) and Richard Manuel (piano, drums, vocals) and American Levon Helm (drums, mandolin, guitar, vocals). The Band combined elements of Americana, folk, rock, jazz, country and rhythm and blues, influencing musicians such as George Harrison, Elton John and Eric Clapton.

History

The members first came together as the Hawks, the backing band of rockabilly singer Ronnie Hawkins. In 1965 they were recruited by Bob Dylan, touring with him in 1965–66 and recording the celebrated Basement Tapes with him in Woodstock, New York, in 1967. Their debut album, Music from Big Pink (1968), introduced their distinctive sound, followed by the eponymous The Band (1969), featuring songs such as "The Night They Drove Old Dixie Down" and "Up on Cripple Creek".

The group disbanded after a celebrated farewell concert on Thanksgiving Day 1976, documented in Martin Scorsese's film The Last Waltz (1978). Robertson's memoir and the group's induction into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1994 cemented their reputation. Levon Helm's song "The Weight", first released on Music from Big Pink, remains one of the most covered songs in rock history.
