Thomond (Irish: Tuadhmhumhain, "North Munster") was a kingdom of Gaelic Ireland, associated politically with the O'Brien dynasty (Uí Bhriain) and roughly corresponding to modern County Clare, together with parts of north County Tipperary and County Limerick. It emerged in the early twelfth century when the ancient province of Munster was divided between Thomond in the north and Desmond in the south.

History

The kingdom of Thomond was created in 1118 by the Treaty of Glanmire, which divided Munster between the MacCarthy dynasty in the south (Desmond) and the O'Briens in the north. The O'Briens were descendants of the legendary High King Brian Boru, who had been king of the Dál gCais and died at the Battle of Clontarf in 1014.

Following the Anglo-Norman invasion of Ireland in the late twelfth century, Thomond contracted west of the River Shannon but remained largely independent under its own kings for several centuries. The O'Briens successfully resisted Norman encroachment, decisively defeating the de Clares at the Battle of Dysert O'Dea in 1318, which ended Norman ambitions in the region.

Surrender and regrant

In 1543 the last king of Thomond, Murrough O'Brien, submitted to Henry VIII under the policy of surrender and regrant. He surrendered the kingdom and received it back as the first Earl of Thomond, bringing the medieval kingdom to an end and integrating it into the Tudor Kingdom of Ireland.

Legacy

The name Thomond survives in several Irish place names and institutions, including Thomond Park, the famous rugby stadium in Limerick, home of Munster Rugby, and Thomond College of Education. The Diocese of Killaloe roughly corresponds to the old kingdom's territory.
