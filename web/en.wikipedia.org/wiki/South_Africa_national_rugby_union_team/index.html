The South Africa national rugby union team, commonly known as the Springboks, represents South Africa in men's international rugby union. Governed by SA Rugby, the team plays in green and gold jerseys and is one of the most successful national sides in the sport, having won the Rugby World Cup four times — more than any other nation.

History

South Africa played its first test in 1891 against a touring British Isles side. Early Springbok teams established a fierce rivalry with New Zealand's All Blacks, regarded for much of the 20th century as rugby's defining contest. During the apartheid era the team's racially exclusive selection led to protests and, eventually, international isolation; South Africa was excluded from the first two World Cups in 1987 and 1991.

World Cup triumphs

Readmitted to world rugby, South Africa hosted and won the 1995 World Cup, a victory freighted with symbolism as President Nelson Mandela, wearing a Springbok jersey, presented the trophy to captain Francois Pienaar. The Springboks won again in 2007 under Jake White, defeating England in the Paris final; in 2019, coached by Rassie Erasmus and captained by Siya Kolisi — the team's first Black captain — they beat England again in Yokohama; and in 2023 they retained the title in France, beating New Zealand 12–11 in the final to become the first team to win four World Cups.

Competitions

South Africa competes annually in The Rugby Championship (formerly the Tri Nations) against New Zealand, Australia, and Argentina, and hosts touring sides from the Northern Hemisphere. The Springboks have frequently held the number one position in the World Rugby rankings.
