Doñana National Park (Spanish: Parque Nacional de Doñana) is a natural reserve in Andalusia, southern Spain, at the mouth of the Guadalquivir River where it meets the Atlantic Ocean. Its marshes, dunes and scrubland form one of Europe's most important wetland reserves.

Wildlife

The park lies on the East Atlantic flyway and hosts millions of migratory birds, including greater flamingos, spoonbills and wintering geese. It is a stronghold for the endangered Iberian lynx and the Spanish imperial eagle, and is home to red and fallow deer, wild boar and the retuerta wild horses.

Threats

Doñana faces severe water stress from illegal wells serving strawberry farms around the park, which have lowered the aquifer and dried ponds; drought in the 2020s intensified the crisis. The park was also threatened by the 1998 Aznalcóllar mine tailings spill, which was halted just short of its boundaries. In 2023 the European Court of Justice ruled against Spain over illegal water abstraction.
