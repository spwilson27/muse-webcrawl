Antony Charles Robert Armstrong-Jones, 1st Earl of Snowdon (7 March 1930 – 13 January 2017), known professionally as Lord Snowdon, was a British photographer, designer and documentary filmmaker. He married Princess Margaret, the younger sister of Queen Elizabeth II, in 1960 — the first commoner to marry a king's daughter in over 400 years — and was created Earl of Snowdon in 1961.

Career

After studying architecture at Cambridge and contracting polio as a teenager, Armstrong-Jones became a fashion and society photographer for Vogue and The Sunday Times, noted for informal, naturalistic portraits of royalty, actors, artists and politicians. His body of portraiture is now held by the National Portrait Gallery, London.

Design and film work

Snowdon co-designed the Snowdon Aviary at London Zoo (1965) and worked in furniture and industrial design, championing design for disabled people — a lifelong cause. He made award-winning documentary films, including Don't Count the Candles (1968), about ageing.

Later life

His marriage to Princess Margaret ended in divorce in 1978; he later remarried and, in 1999, was made a life peer as Baron Armstrong-Jones, retaining his seat in the House of Lords after the hereditary peers' reform. He died at his Kensington home in January 2017.
