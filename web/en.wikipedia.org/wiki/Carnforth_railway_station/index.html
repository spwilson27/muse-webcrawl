Carnforth railway station is a railway station serving the town of Carnforth in Lancashire, England. It stands on the West Coast Main Line and is the junction for the Furness line to Barrow-in-Furness and the Leeds–Morecambe line. The station is famous worldwide as the principal filming location of David Lean's 1945 film Brief Encounter.

History

The station opened in 1846 on the Lancaster and Carlisle Railway and became an important junction when the Furness Railway and the Midland Railway lines arrived. Under the London, Midland and Scottish Railway it grew into a major interchange with extensive facilities, and its adjacent motive power depot (Carnforth MPD) became, after the end of main-line steam in 1968, the Steamtown museum and later the base of West Coast Railways. The main-line platforms closed in 1970 when long-distance trains ceased to stop, and the station was served only by local trains on the secondary platforms thereafter.

Brief Encounter and the heritage centre

David Lean filmed the station scenes of Brief Encounter, starring Celia Johnson and Trevor Howard, at Carnforth early in 1945, choosing it because it was far enough from London to be safe from wartime blackout restrictions. The station clock, the refreshment room and the subway all feature prominently. A heritage centre and the restored "Brief Encounter Refreshment Room" opened in the station buildings in 2003, and the clock — restored after an appeal — hangs again over the platform.

Services

Northern Trains operates local services to Lancaster, Barrow-in-Furness, Morecambe and Leeds; the station is a short distance south of the junction where the Furness and Leeds lines diverge from the West Coast Main Line.
