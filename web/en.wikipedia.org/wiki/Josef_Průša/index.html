Josef Průša

Josef Průša (born 23 February 1990) is a Czech developer and entrepreneur, founder of Prusa Research, and a leading figure in the open-source 3D printing movement. His "Prusa i3" printer design, developed within the RepRap project, became one of the most copied and widely used desktop 3D printer designs in the world.

Early work and RepRap

Průša grew up in Havlíčkův Brod, in the Czech Republic. While studying at university in Prague he became involved in the RepRap project, the open-source initiative to build self-replicating 3D printers, contributing designs including the simplified "Prusa Mendel". In 2012 he left his studies to found Prusa Research, selling kits of his Prusa Mendel iteration 3 — the "Prusa i3" — whose aluminium-frame, moving-bed layout became a template imitated by manufacturers worldwide.

Prusa Research

The company grew rapidly on the success of the Original Prusa i3 MK2 (2016), widely praised in reviews and notable for features such as automatic mesh bed levelling and a removable magnetic print surface in later models. Prusa Research expanded from its Prague base to employ several hundred people, shipping tens of thousands of printers monthly at its peak, and extended its range to SLA resin printers (the SL1), the larger CORE One and XL multi-toolhead machines, and its own Prusament filament line. The company also runs the Printables.com model-sharing platform.

Průša has been a prominent advocate of open-source hardware, releasing his printer designs and firmware under free licences, while also voicing criticism of manufacturers who clone open designs without contributing back. He and Prusa Research have received numerous Czech and international business awards, and Průša appears regularly in discussions of European manufacturing and maker culture.
