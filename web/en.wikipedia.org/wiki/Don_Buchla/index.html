Donald Buchla (17 April 1937 – 14 September 2016) was an American pioneer of electronic musical instruments, inventor of the voltage-controlled modular synthesizer in parallel with Robert Moog, and founder of Buchla & Associates. His instruments, favoured by experimental composers of the San Francisco Bay Area, defined the "West Coast" approach to synthesis, emphasising wavefolding, low-pass gates and touch-plate controllers rather than keyboards.

Career

Trained in physics and music, Buchla was commissioned in 1963 by composers Ramon Sender and Morton Subotnick of the San Francisco Tape Music Center to build a modular electronic music system, funded by a Rockefeller grant. The resulting Buchla 100 Series (1965) used touch-sensitive plates instead of a conventional keyboard, which Buchla considered a limitation. His later designs included the Buchla 200 Series "Electric Music Box", the microtonal Marimba Lumina MIDI controller, the Lightning infrared controller, and the Buchla 200e system (2004), which married analogue modules to digital preset management.

Legacy

Buchla's vactrol-based "lowpass gate" gives his instruments a characteristically plucky, acoustic-like decay prized by performers such as Suzanne Ciani. The revival of modular synthesis in the 2000s, especially the Eurorack format, drew heavily on his design philosophy. His instruments are held in museum collections, and the company he founded continues under new ownership.
