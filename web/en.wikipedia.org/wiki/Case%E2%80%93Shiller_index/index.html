Case–Shiller index

The Case–Shiller indices are a family of repeat-sales house price indices for the United States, developed by economists Karl Case and Robert Shiller and now produced as the S&P CoreLogic Case-Shiller U.S. National Home Price Index and related metropolitan area indices.

Methodology

The indices are calculated from repeated sales of the same single-family houses: because the same properties are tracked over time, changes in the index reflect changes in market value rather than differences in the mix of houses sold. The national index is published monthly with a two-month lag, and individual indices cover major metropolitan areas such as New York, Los Angeles and Chicago, along with 10-city and 20-city composites.
