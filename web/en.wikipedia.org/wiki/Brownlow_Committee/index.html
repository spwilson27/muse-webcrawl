Brownlow Committee

Official namePresident's Committee on Administrative Management
Appointed1936, by Franklin D. Roosevelt
Report issued1937
MembersLouis Brownlow, Charles Merriam, Luther Gulick

The Brownlow Committee, formally the President's Committee on Administrative Management, was a presidentially commissioned panel of political science and public administration experts who in 1937 recommended sweeping changes to the executive branch of the United States federal government.

Recommendations

Chaired by Louis Brownlow, with Charles Merriam and Luther Gulick, the committee famously declared that "the President needs help." It recommended expanding the White House staff, creating what became the Executive Office of the President, strengthening presidential control over the sprawling independent agencies, and modernizing personnel and fiscal management.

Legacy

Congressional resistance, heightened by Roosevelt's simultaneous "court-packing" plan, defeated the full reorganization bill in 1938, but a weaker Reorganization Act passed in 1939. Using its authority, Roosevelt created the Executive Office of the President, including the Bureau of the Budget (now the Office of Management and Budget), institutionalizing the modern presidency.
