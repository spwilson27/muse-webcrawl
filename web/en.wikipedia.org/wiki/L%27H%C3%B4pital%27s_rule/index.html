L'Hôpital's rule

L'Hôpital's rule (also called Bernoulli's rule) is a theorem in calculus that provides a technique to evaluate limits of indeterminate forms, such as 0/0 or ∞/∞, by replacing the limit of a quotient with the limit of the quotient of the derivatives.

Statement

If functions f and g are differentiable near a point c, and the limit of f/g at c is of the form 0/0 or ±∞/±∞, then the limit of f/g equals the limit of f′/g′ provided the latter exists (or is infinite). The rule may be applied repeatedly as long as the conditions continue to hold.

History

The rule first appeared in Guillaume de l'Hôpital's 1696 textbook Analyse des Infiniment Petits, the first textbook on differential calculus, though the result itself is credited to Johann Bernoulli.
