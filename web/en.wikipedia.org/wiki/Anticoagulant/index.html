Anticoagulant

Anticoagulants
Examples: Warfarin, heparin, apixaban, rivaroxaban
Use: Prevent thrombosis
Risk: Bleeding

Anticoagulants, commonly called blood thinners, are chemical substances that prevent or reduce coagulation of blood, prolonging clotting time. They are used for thrombosis, atrial fibrillation, and pulmonary embolism.

Types

Vitamin K antagonists

Warfarin and coumarins inhibit vitamin K epoxide reductase, reducing factors II, VII, IX, X. Monitoring uses INR.

Heparins

Unfractionated heparin and low-molecular-weight heparins such as enoxaparin potentiate antithrombin.

Direct oral anticoagulants

DOACs include direct thrombin inhibitor dabigatran and factor Xa inhibitors apixaban, rivaroxaban, edoxaban.

Adverse effects

Major risk is haemorrhage. Reversal agents include vitamin K, protamine, idarucizumab, and andexanet alfa.
