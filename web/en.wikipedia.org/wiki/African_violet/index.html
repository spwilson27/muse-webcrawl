African violet

African violets are plants of the genus Streptocarpus section Saintpaulia, in the family Gesneriaceae. Native to the cloud forests of the Eastern Arc Mountains of Tanzania and south-eastern Kenya, they are among the world's most popular flowering houseplants, prized for their rosettes of velvety leaves and clusters of violet, pink, white, or bicoloured flowers.

Description and history

The plants were brought to scientific attention in 1892 by Baron Walter von Saint-Paul-Illaire, a German colonial official in Tanganyika, for whom the former genus Saintpaulia was named; the first species was described as Saintpaulia ionantha. Modern taxonomy places them within Streptocarpus. Thousands of cultivars have been bred, ranging from miniature trailing types to large standards with variegated foliage and frilled double flowers.

Cultivation

African violets prefer bright indirect light, consistently moist (not wet) soil, and watering from below, since cold water on the leaves can cause spotting. They are readily propagated from leaf cuttings. In the wild, several species are threatened by habitat loss in their narrow mountain range.
