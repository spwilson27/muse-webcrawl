Puerto Morelos

Puerto Morelos is a town in Quintana Roo, Mexico, on the Caribbean coast of the Yucatán Peninsula, roughly 36 km (22 mi) south of Cancún and north of Playa del Carmen. It is the seat of Puerto Morelos Municipality, created in 2016, and is known for its comparatively quiet fishing-village atmosphere within the Riviera Maya resort corridor.

Geography and reef

The town is divided by Federal Highway 307 into a coastal section (the old port area, with the zócalo and pier) and an inland residential section known as Colonia Joaquín Zetina Gasca. Just offshore lies the Puerto Morelos Reef National Park, established in 1998, protecting a well-preserved section of the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef System only a few hundred metres from shore; snorkeling and diving on the reef are the town's principal attractions. A leaning lighthouse, tilted by Hurricane Beulah in 1967, is a local landmark on the main beach.

History

The settlement developed in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries as a port for shipping chicle, cedar, and other products of the Yucatán's interior forests. With the growth of Cancún from the 1970s onward, Puerto Morelos acquired resort facilities and an expanding population, while local planning rules long limited building heights in the port area, preserving a lower-key character than its neighbours.
