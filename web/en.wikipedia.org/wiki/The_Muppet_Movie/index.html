The Muppet Movie

The Muppet Movie is a 1979 musical road comedy film and the first theatrical film featuring the Muppets. Directed by James Frawley and produced by Jim Henson, it was written by The Muppet Show writers Jerry Juhl and Jack Burns. A meta-narrative framed as the Muppets watching a film of their own origin story, it follows Kermit the Frog's journey from a swamp in Florida to Hollywood.

Plot and music

Encouraged by a talent agent, Kermit travels cross-country to audition for a Hollywood studio, collecting Fozzie Bear, Gonzo, Miss Piggy, Rowlf, and the band Dr. Teeth and the Electric Mayhem along the way. Pursuing them is Doc Hopper, a French-fried frog-leg restaurant magnate who wants Kermit as his spokesman. The score by Paul Williams and Kenny Ascher includes "Rainbow Connection", which was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Original Song, "Movin' Right Along", and "Can You Picture That?". Celebrity cameos include Steve Martin, Mel Brooks, Richard Pryor, Bob Hope, Milton Berle, and Orson Welles.

Reception and legacy

The film was a commercial success and was followed by several sequels, beginning with The Great Muppet Caper (1981). In 2009 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry as being "culturally, historically, or aesthetically significant".
