Itch.io is an American website for users to host, sell and download indie video games, game assets, and other creative work. Launched in 2013 by programmer Leaf Corcoran, the platform distinguishes itself by letting creators design their own store pages, set their own prices (including pay-what-you-want), and choose how much revenue to share with the platform under its "open revenue sharing" model.

Features

Any registered user may upload a project without an approval process or listing fee. Games can be downloadable or playable in the browser via HTML5; developers can run game jams — themed competitions with deadlines and community voting — of which thousands are hosted each year. The site also functions as a major hub for independent tabletop role-playing games, zines and asset packs, and its desktop app provides a library and auto-updating for downloaded titles.

Charity bundles

Itch.io has become prominent for large charity bundles: the Bundle for Racial Justice and Equality (June 2020) offered over 1,700 works and raised more than US$8 million, while a 2022 bundle for Ukraine raised over US$6 million. These bundles are frequently cited as demonstrations of the platform's flexible pricing infrastructure.

Reception and controversies

The platform is praised within the indie community for low barriers to entry and creator control, but the absence of curation also means uneven quality and discoverability challenges. Its permissive content policies have periodically brought it into conflict with payment processors; in July 2025, itch.io deindexed adult NSFW content under processor pressure, prompting debate about financial intermediaries' influence over online speech.
