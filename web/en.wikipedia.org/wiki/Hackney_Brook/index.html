Hackney Brook

The Hackney Brook is one of the subterranean rivers of London. Once a significant tributary of the River Lea, it rose in the area of Holloway and Highbury in north London and flowed southeast through Stoke Newington, Hackney, and Homerton to join the Lea near Old Ford. As London urbanised in the nineteenth century the brook was culverted and incorporated into the sewer network, and it now runs almost entirely underground.

Course

The brook had two principal sources, one near the site of Holloway Prison in Islington and another in Highbury. From there it flowed through Stoke Newington, where it supplied ponds and watercress beds, crossed Mare Street in Hackney, and continued through Hackney Wick and Victoria Park's vicinity to the Lea. The low-lying land along its course, including Hackney Marshes, was historically flood-prone.

History

In the medieval and early modern periods the brook powered mills and marked parish and manorial boundaries. Like other London streams, it became increasingly polluted as the population grew, serving as an open sewer, and during the nineteenth century it was progressively covered over as its valley was built up. The last open stretches disappeared as the area around Hackney Wick and the marshes was developed, and the brook's waters were diverted into the Northern Outfall Sewer system. Its course is still traced by local roads, low ground, and occasional flooding, and it is remembered in street and place names along its route.
