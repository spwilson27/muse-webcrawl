Nainsukh

Nainsukh (c. 1710 – 1778) was an Indian painter of the Pahari school, regarded as one of the greatest miniature painters of the eighteenth century. The younger brother of Manaku and son of the painter Pandit Seu, he came from Guler in the Punjab hills.

Life and work

Nainsukh spent the central part of his career at the court of Raja Balwant Singh of Jasrota, whose intimate daily life he documented in a celebrated series of small, exquisitely observed paintings: the raja writing, having his beard trimmed, examining a painting, watching a performance, or hawking. These works break with the formality of earlier court painting in favour of a quiet, naturalistic intimacy.

His style is noted for spare, uncluttered compositions, broad expanses of empty space, muted colour, and a precise, feathery line. After Balwant Singh's death in exile in 1763, Nainsukh appears to have worked for other patrons in the hills, including at Basohli and for Amrit Pal.

Legacy

Long overlooked, Nainsukh was brought to international attention by the art historian B. N. Goswamy, whose research reconstructed his career largely from inscriptions on the paintings themselves. His life inspired the 2010 film Nainsukh by Amit Dutta. Major collections of his work are held by the Los Angeles County Museum of Art, the Museum Rietberg in Zurich and Indian museums.
