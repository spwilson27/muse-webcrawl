The Graian Alps (French: Alpes Grées; Italian: Alpi Graie) are a mountain range in the western part of the Alps, on the border of France, Italy, and Switzerland. The name "Graian" derives from the Graioceli, a Celtic tribe that inhabited the area, and is also linked to the legendary Graius, a companion of Hercules.

Geography

The range extends from the Little St Bernard Pass in the north to the Col de l'Iseran region in the south, drained by the Isère and Arc rivers on the French side and by the Orco, Stura di Lanzo, and Dora Baltea on the Italian side. Its highest summit is the Gran Paradiso (4,061 m), the centrepiece of Italy's oldest national park, established in 1922. Other major peaks include the Grande Casse (3,855 m) in the Vanoise massif and Monte Viso is sometimes grouped nearby in the Cottian Alps to the south.

Subdivisions

The Graian Alps are commonly divided into the northern Graian (including the Mont Blanc area margins in some classifications), the central group around Gran Paradiso, and the southern or eastern group including the Lanzo and Alta Moriana ranges. The Vanoise National Park in France and Gran Paradiso National Park in Italy protect much of the high country, home to ibex and chamois.

Passes and tourism

Historic passes include the Little St Bernard and the Great St Bernard (often assigned to the Pennine Alps), and the Col de l'Iseran, famous from the Tour de France. Major resorts include Val d'Isère, Tignes, Courmayeur's hinterland, and Cogne.
