Single-ended signalling

Single-ended signalling — the British-English spelling of "single-ended signaling" — denotes the same electrical transmission method described at Single-ended signaling: a signal is carried on one conductor as a varying voltage measured against a shared reference, usually ground (earth). The double-"l" spelling signalling is standard in British, Commonwealth and international (IEC/ITU) technical writing, and the topic is indexed under this spelling in those sources.

Terminology note

Engineering literature varies by region: American standards (such as those of the IEEE and JEDEC) typically use "signaling", whereas British and European documents use "signalling". Both spellings refer to one and the same technique and to the same contrast with differential signalling, in which a pair of complementary conductors is used and only their voltage difference is read, giving strong rejection of common-mode interference.

Where the term appears

The expression occurs in specifications for unbalanced audio connections, railway and automotive control wiring, logic families and printed-circuit-board design guidance, where designers must weigh the low cost of single-ended links — one wire per signal plus a common return — against their susceptibility to earth-potential differences and coupled noise on longer runs.
