Spanish cuisine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Tapas dishes and a paella in Spain]

Spanish cuisine is the cooking of Spain, a collection of strong regional cuisines united by Olive oil, garlic, saffron, cured ham (jamón) and the culture of tapas — small dishes eaten in bars, often standing, with wine or beer.

Its most famous dish is Paella from Valencia; others include gazpacho, tortilla española (potato omelette), gambas al ajillo, churros, and the avant-garde creations of Ferran Adrià's elBulli, which reshaped world gastronomy in the 2000s. Spain holds some of the world's most decorated restaurants in the Basque Country and Catalonia.

Contents
1. History

History

Roman, Visigothic and Moorish rule layered ingredients — rice, saffron, citrus and almonds from the Islamic period — while the Columbian exchange added tomatoes, peppers and potatoes, now essential. Regional identities remain proud: Galician seafood, Castilian roasts, Catalan sauces, Basque pintxos.
