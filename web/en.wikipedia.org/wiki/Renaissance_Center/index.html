The Renaissance Center (commonly called the RenCen) is a group of seven connected skyscrapers in downtown Detroit, Michigan, United States, situated on the International Riverfront along the Detroit River. The central tower, the Detroit Marriott at the Renaissance Center, is the second-tallest building in Michigan and was the tallest building in the state from 1977 until 2024. Since 1996 the complex has served as the world headquarters of General Motors.

History

The project was conceived beginning in 1970 by Henry Ford II and other Detroit business leaders as a privately financed catalyst for the revival of the city's downtown, following the 1967 unrest. Designed by architect John Portman, the complex consisted in its first phase (opened in 1977) of a central 73-story cylindrical glass hotel tower surrounded by four 39-story octagonal office towers, all fronted in dark reflective glass. Two additional 21-story towers were added in 1981. The inward-looking design, with its maze-like interior atrium, was intended to create a secure "city within a city", but it was criticized for turning its back on downtown and confusing visitors.

General Motors purchased the complex in 1996 for a reported $72 million and undertook a $500 million renovation completed in the 2000s, which opened up the riverfront side, added the glass Wintergarden atrium, removed concrete berms that had separated the complex from Jefferson Avenue, and created a promenade along the river.

Layout and features

The complex contains about 5.5 million square feet of space, including offices, retail, restaurants, and the 1,298-room Marriott hotel in the central tower. It has its own station on the Detroit People Mover elevated transit loop. The GM World display in the atrium showcases the company's vehicles, and the Coach Insignia restaurant, once located on the 71st and 72nd floors, was among the highest restaurants in the United States.

Significance

The Renaissance Center is the most recognizable feature of the Detroit skyline and frequently appears as a symbol of the city. In 2024, General Motors announced plans to relocate its headquarters to the new Hudson's Detroit development, and GM and Bedrock proposed a redevelopment of the RenCen that would demolish two of the office towers and reconfigure the complex.
