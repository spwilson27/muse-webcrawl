Oversampling

In signal processing, oversampling is the process of sampling a signal at a rate significantly higher than the Nyquist rate — more than twice the highest frequency present in the signal.

Benefits

Oversampling eases the design of anti-aliasing filters, since the transition band between the passband and the aliasing region becomes wider. It also reduces quantisation noise within the band of interest: when combined with filtering and decimation, oversampling improves the effective resolution of an analogue-to-digital converter, a principle exploited in sigma-delta converters. In audio and imaging, oversampling is used to improve quality and to move distortion artefacts outside the range of interest.

In statistics

The term is also used in survey methodology and machine learning, where oversampling means deliberately sampling a minority class or population at a higher rate than its share, then correcting for the bias in analysis or training.
