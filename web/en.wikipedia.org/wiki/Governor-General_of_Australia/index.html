The governor-general of Australia is the representative of the monarch of Australia, currently King Charles III. The governor-general performs the monarch's constitutional and ceremonial duties at the federal level, including appointing the prime minister, giving royal assent to legislation, and serving as commander-in-chief of the Australian Defence Force.

Appointment and role

The governor-general is appointed by the monarch on the advice of the prime minister of Australia, and by convention has been an Australian citizen since the 1960s; the first Australian-born holder was Sir Isaac Isaacs (1931). Constitutional powers include summoning, proroguing, and dissolving parliament, issuing writs for elections, and acting on the advice of the Federal Executive Council in most matters.

Reserve powers and 1975

The governor-general retains reserve powers exercisable without ministerial advice in exceptional circumstances. Most famously, on 11 November 1975 Sir John Kerr dismissed Prime Minister Gough Whitlam during the supply crisis and commissioned Malcolm Fraser to form a caretaker government — an event still debated in discussions of Australian republicanism.
