The cubit is an ancient unit of length based on the distance from the elbow to the tip of the middle finger. It was used in the ancient Near East, Egypt, Greece, Rome and many other cultures, and is frequently mentioned in the Bible — for example in the dimensions of Noah's Ark and the Tabernacle.

Standards

Because forearms vary, cubits were standardized against official rods. The Egyptian royal cubit (about 52.5 cm) was divided into 7 palms of 4 digits each; the common or short cubit had 6 palms. Surviving wooden and stone cubit rods from Egypt date back to the 3rd millennium BC. The Roman cubitum was 1.5 Roman feet (about 44.4 cm), and the Greek pēchys about 46 cm. In medieval and early modern Europe, local cubits and the related ell persisted in cloth measurement.

Uses

Cubit measures determined the proportions of temples, ships and monuments across the ancient world; the Great Pyramid of Giza was laid out as 440 royal cubits per side. Biblical and rabbinic measurements are conventionally converted using values between about 44 and 56 cm, a range that matters in archaeology and in halakhic (Jewish legal) practice today.
