Gregg Allman

Gregory LeNoir Allman (December 8, 1947 – May 27, 2017) was an American musician, singer and songwriter. He was known for performing in the Allman Brothers Band with his elder brother Duane Allman. An originator of Southern rock, he was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1995 as a member of the band, and his soulful, blues-drenched voice and Hammond B-3 organ playing were central to its sound. He is also remembered for his distinguished solo career and autobiography, My Cross to Bear (2012).

Early life

Allman was born in Nashville, Tennessee, and raised in Daytona Beach, Florida, after his father was murdered by a hitchhiker while on leave from the Korean War. He and Duane formed a series of garage bands in the 1960s, including the Escorts, the Allman Joys and Hour Glass, before founding the Allman Brothers Band in Jacksonville, Florida, in 1969.

Career

The Allman Brothers Band found success in the early 1970s with albums such as At Fillmore East (1971) and Brothers and Sisters (1973), the latter featuring "Ramblin' Man". Allman wrote or co-wrote many of the band's best-known songs, including "Whipping Post", "Melissa" and "Midnight Rider". The band survived the deaths of Duane Allman (1971) and bassist Berry Oakley (1972), and Allman continued with various line-ups until the group's final shows in 2014. His solo albums include Laid Back (1973), I'm No Angel (1987) and Low Country Blues (2011). His final studio album, Southern Blood, was released posthumously in 2017.

Personal life and death

Allman was married seven times, including to the singer and actress Cher (1975–1979), with whom he had a son, Elijah Blue Allman. He struggled for decades with alcohol and drug abuse and was diagnosed with hepatitis C, undergoing a liver transplant in 2010. He died of complications from liver cancer at his home in Richmond Hill, Georgia, on 27 May 2017.
