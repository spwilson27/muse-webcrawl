Alternative hip hop

Stylistic origins: Hip hop, jazz rap, funk, psychedelic, indie
Cultural origins: Late 1980s, United States
Other names: Alt-rap, underground hip hop

Alternative hip hop (also known as alternative rap) is a subgenre of hip hop music that is defined as much by its distance from mainstream commercial rap as by any single sound. Alternative hip hop artists typically reject the gangsta imagery and materialism associated with mainstream rap in favour of eclectic influences — jazz, funk, soul, rock and psychedelia — and lyrics dealing with social commentary, introspection and abstract themes.

History

The subgenre emerged in the late 1980s with groups such as De La Soul, whose 1989 album 3 Feet High and Rising introduced a playful, sample-rich "Daisy Age" aesthetic, and A Tribe Called Quest and the Jungle Brothers, who formed the Native Tongues collective. In the 1990s, acts such as Arrested Development, The Pharcyde, Digable Planets and The Roots pushed the style further, while groups like Outkast brought alternative sensibilities to commercial success.

During the 2000s, alternative hip hop flourished through independent labels such as Definitive Jux and Rhymesayers, home to artists including Aesop Rock, MF DOOM, Atmosphere and El-P. The rise of internet distribution allowed later artists — among them Tyler, the Creator, Kendrick Lamar in his early work, Brockhampton and Childish Gambino — to blur the line between alternative and mainstream entirely.

Characteristics

Alternative hip hop is characterised by unconventional sampling, live instrumentation, jazz and rock influences, and lyricism that favours wordplay, storytelling and social or philosophical themes over boasts about wealth and violence. Because the category is defined by its opposition to commercial norms, it encompasses a wide range of sounds, from the jazz rap of A Tribe Called Quest to the industrial production of Death Grips.
