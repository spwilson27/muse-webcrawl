Gonbad-e Qabus (tower)

LocationGonbad-e Kavus, Golestan Province, Iran
Built1006–1007 AD
Height53 m (174 ft), 72 m including the hill
UNESCOWorld Heritage Site (2012)

Gonbad-e Qabus is a monumental brick tower in the city of Gonbad-e Kavus, Iran, built in 1006–1007 AD as the tomb tower of Qabus ibn Wushmgir, the Ziyarid ruler and scholar. Rising 53 metres (174 ft) on a ten-pointed star plan with a conical roof, it is one of the tallest and oldest surviving brick tomb towers in the Islamic world.

Architecture

The tower is built entirely of fired brick in a decagonal, star-shaped plan, its ten fluted sides tapering slightly as they rise. A single band of Kufic inscription near the top records the founder and the date — 375 solar years after the Hijra. The bare, geometrically pure form, essentially unchanged for a thousand years, is regarded as a masterpiece of early Islamic architecture and mathematics in Iran.

History and legend

Local legend holds that Qabus's body was suspended in a glass coffin from the tower's dome. The monument became the namesake of the modern city and inspired the famous visit described in Robert Byron's travel classic The Road to Oxiana. In 2012 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.
