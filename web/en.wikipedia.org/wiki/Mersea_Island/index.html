Mersea Island is an island in Essex, England, in the Blackwater and Colne estuaries to the south-east of Colchester. Its name comes from the Old English word meresig, meaning "island of the pool", and it is the most easterly inhabited island in the United Kingdom. The island has been inhabited since pre-Roman times and is famed for its oysters, which have been harvested since Roman times.

Geography

The island is divided into West Mersea, where most of the population lives, and the smaller village of East Mersea. It is joined to the mainland by a causeway, the Strood, which floods at high spring tides — making Mersea, at those times, a true tidal island. A passenger ferry links East Mersea to Point Clear and Brightlingsea across the Colne.

Oysters

Colchester Native oysters from Mersea are protected by a European designation and are celebrated each September at the Colchester Oyster Feast. The island's oyster fishery dates back to Roman occupation, and oyster pits are still visible along the shore.

History

Evidence of Romano-British occupation includes the Barrowfields burial mound in West Mersea. The island was raided by Vikings and in the 11th century the manor was granted to the Abbey of St Ouen in Rouen. During the Second World War the island was fortified as part of Britain's anti-invasion defences.
