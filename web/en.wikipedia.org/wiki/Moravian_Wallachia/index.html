Moravian Wallachia (Czech: Moravské Valašsko, or simply Valašsko) is a mountainous cultural and ethnographic region in the far east of Moravia, Czech Republic, in the Western Carpathians around the towns of Vsetín, Rožnov pod Radhoštěm, and Valašské Meziříčí. It takes its name from the Vlachs (Wallachians), Romance-speaking shepherds who colonised the Carpathian highlands in the 14th–17th centuries, bringing a pastoral culture of sheep-herding, mountain meadows (paseky), and distinctive dialect words of Romanian origin.

History and culture

Colonisation under "Wallachian law" settled the Beskid foothills with shepherds who grazed sheep and goats on cleared ridges, producing cheeses and wool. The settlers were gradually assimilated linguistically into the surrounding Slavic population, but Wallachian identity remained strong, expressed in timber log architecture, folk costume, bagpipe and fiddle music, and a reputation for stubborn independence — the region saw Wallachian uprisings during the Thirty Years' War and strong partisan resistance in the Second World War.

Sights and traditions

The Wallachian Open Air Museum in Rožnov pod Radhoštěm, founded in 1925, is the oldest and largest open-air museum in Central Europe, preserving wooden town, village, and mill buildings. Regional specialities include frgál, a large round fruit-topped cake; plum brandy (slivovice); and bryndza-style cheeses. The rolling, partly forested landscape of pastures and scattered farmsteads is protected in part by the Beskydy Protected Landscape Area.
