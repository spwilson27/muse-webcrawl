Athos

Athos (Greek: Ἄθως) may refer to several things. Most prominently it is the name of Mount Athos, a mountain and peninsula in north-eastern Greece, home to the autonomous monastic community of the Holy Mountain, an important centre of Eastern Orthodox monasticism for more than a thousand years.

Other uses

In Greek mythology, Athos was one of the Gigantes, who hurled a massive rock at Poseidon that became the mountain. Athos is also a character in Alexandre Dumas's novel The Three Musketeers (1844), the eldest and most reserved of the musketeers, modeled on the historical Armand d'Athos. The name also appears in astronomy, botany and numerous place names around the Greek world.

Mount Athos

The monastic community comprises 20 monasteries under the spiritual jurisdiction of the Ecumenical Patriarch of Constantinople. By a charter tradition known as the avaton, women are prohibited from entering the peninsula. Mount Athos is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
