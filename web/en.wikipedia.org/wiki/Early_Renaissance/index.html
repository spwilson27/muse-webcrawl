Early Renaissance

The Early Renaissance is a period in European art and culture, roughly 1400 to 1495, centred in Italy, in which medieval traditions gave way to a revival of classical antiquity, linear perspective, naturalism and humanism.

Early Renaissance
Period: c. 1400-1495
Centre: Florence, Italy
Figures: Brunelleschi, Donatello, Masaccio, Botticelli

Origins in Florence

Wealthy Florentine patronage, especially by the Medici, funded innovations: Filippo Brunelleschi's perspective and dome for Florence Cathedral, Donatello's sculpture, and Masaccio's frescoes in the Brancacci Chapel.

Spread and characteristics

Artists such as Piero della Francesca, Andrea Mantegna and Sandro Botticelli developed balanced composition, anatomy and classical mythology. The period culminated in the High Renaissance of Leonardo, Michelangelo and Raphael.
