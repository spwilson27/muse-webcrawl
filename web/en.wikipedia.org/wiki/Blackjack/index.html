Blackjack (also called twenty-one) is a casino banking game in which players compete against the dealer rather than each other, aiming to accumulate cards with a total closer to 21 than the dealer's without going over ("busting"). It is the most widely played casino banking game in the world, and unusual among casino games in that skilled play can reduce the house edge to well under one percent.

Rules

Cards 2 through 10 count at face value, face cards count 10, and aces count 1 or 11. Each player receives two cards; one of the dealer's cards is dealt face up. A two-card total of 21 — an ace with a ten-value card — is a "blackjack" or "natural," typically paying 3 to 2. Players may "hit" (take another card), "stand," "double down" (double the bet and take exactly one more card), "split" a pair into two hands, or, in some casinos, "surrender." The dealer must hit until reaching at least 17. If the dealer busts, all remaining players win.

History

Blackjack descends from the French game vingt-un ("twenty-one"), documented in the early 18th century and mentioned by Cervantes in a precursor form (veintiuna) as early as 1601. The game spread to the United States in the 19th century; the name "blackjack" arose from an early American bonus payout for a hand consisting of the ace of spades and a black jack. Nevada legalized casino gambling in 1931, and blackjack became a fixture of Las Vegas.

Strategy and card counting

In 1956 Roger Baldwin and colleagues published a mathematically derived "basic strategy," later popularized and refined by Edward O. Thorp, whose 1962 book Beat the Dealer demonstrated that card counting — tracking the ratio of high to low cards remaining — could give players an edge. Casinos responded with multi-deck shoes, frequent shuffling, and barring suspected counters. Teams such as the MIT Blackjack Team famously exploited the game in the 1980s and 1990s.

House edge

Under typical rules and perfect basic strategy, the house edge is about 0.5%. Rule variations — such as paying 6 to 5 on blackjack, or requiring the dealer to hit on soft 17 — increase the edge substantially.
