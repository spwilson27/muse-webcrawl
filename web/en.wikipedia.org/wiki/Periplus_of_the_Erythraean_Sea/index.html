The Periplus of the Erythraean Sea (Greek: Periplous tēs Erythras Thalassēs) is a Greek periplus — a sailing and trading manual — describing navigation routes and commercial opportunities along the coasts of the Red Sea, the Horn of Africa, the Arabian Peninsula, Persia and India. The "Erythraean Sea" of the title comprised what are now the Red Sea, the Gulf of Aden, the Persian Gulf and the Indian Ocean.

Content

Written by an anonymous Greek-speaking merchant based in Egypt, the text gives a port-by-port account from the Egyptian Red Sea harbours of Myos Hormos and Berenice south along the African coast to Rhapta (probably in modern Tanzania), and eastward past Arabia and the Persian Gulf to India, naming ports such as Barbarikon near the Indus delta, Barygaza (Bharuch) in Gujarat, and Muziris and Nelcynda on the Malabar Coast. For each port it notes what is imported and exported — Roman wine, glass, copper, coin, ivory, tortoiseshell, spices, pepper, silk and slaves — along with notes on local rulers, customs and sailing seasons exploiting the monsoon winds.

Dating and significance

Internal references, including the reign of a Nabataean king and a ruler identified with the Satavahana dynasty, date the work to the mid-first century AD, roughly AD 40–70. It is one of the most important surviving sources for the ancient Indian Ocean trade and for Roman commerce with India, for the geography of East Africa and Arabia, and for early references to places as distant as the Ganges and "Thina" (China), from which silk reached the West.

Textual history

The work survives in a single tenth-century Byzantine manuscript, now at Heidelberg University Library, which came to scholarly attention in the sixteenth century. Lionel Casson's 1989 translation and commentary is the standard modern edition.
