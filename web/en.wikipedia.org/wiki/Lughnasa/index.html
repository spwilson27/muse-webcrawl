Lughnasa

Lughnasa (Irish: Lúnasa; also spelled Lughnasadh) is a Gaelic festival marking the beginning of the harvest season, traditionally celebrated on 1 August, about halfway between the summer solstice and the autumn equinox. It is one of the four Gaelic seasonal festivals, along with Samhain, Imbolc and Beltane, and is named for the god Lugh.

Origins and mythology

According to Irish tradition, Lugh instituted the festival as funeral games in honour of his mother (or foster-mother) Tailtiu, who was said to have died of exhaustion after clearing the plains of Ireland for agriculture. The great Óenach Tailten (Tailtin Fair) became the most famous of the Lughnasa assemblies, featuring games, contests, marriages and markets.

Customs

Historical celebrations included climbing hills and mountains — a custom that survived in Christianised form as pilgrimages on "Reek Sunday" to peaks such as Croagh Patrick — visiting holy wells, offering the first fruits of the harvest, feasting on new potatoes and bilberries (hence "Fraughan Sunday"), fairs such as the Puck Fair in Killorglin, and ritual dancing and bonfires. In modern Ireland Lúnasa is simply the Irish word for the month of August.

Modern observance

Lughnasa is celebrated by Celtic neopagans and reconstructionists as one of the eight sabbats of the Wheel of the Year (in the Wiccan tradition usually spelled Lammas, from the Anglo-Saxon "loaf-mass"). Brian Friel's 1990 play Dancing at Lughnasa, set during the festival season of 1936, brought the name to international audiences.
