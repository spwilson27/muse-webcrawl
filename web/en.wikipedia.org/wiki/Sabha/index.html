Sabha or Sebha (Arabic: سبها) is an oasis city in southwestern Libya, the capital of the Sabha District and the principal city of the Fezzan region, roughly 770 km south of Tripoli. Historically a hub of the trans-Saharan caravan trade, it remains the main urban and transport centre of southern Libya.

History

Sabha grew around a desert oasis on caravan routes linking the Mediterranean coast with sub-Saharan Africa, trading in goods including slaves, ivory and gold. The old town is overlooked by a fort — known in the Italian colonial period as Fortezza Margherita — built on a hill above the settlement. Italy occupied the Fezzan during its colonisation of Libya in the early 20th century. After independence, oil-era development and the city's position on the road to the south made it an administrative and military centre, home to a major air base and the Sabha International Airport.

Geography and climate

The city lies at the edge of the Ubari sand sea in a hot desert climate, with extremely hot summers, mild winters, and very low rainfall. Agriculture around the oasis depends on groundwater, including fossil water from the Nubian Sandstone Aquifer.

Contemporary issues

Since the 2011 Libyan civil war, Sabha has been contested by rival local communities — including Tebu, Tuareg and Arab tribes — and armed factions aligned with the competing governments in Tripoli and the east. Its location has also made it a major transit point on migration routes from sub-Saharan Africa toward the Mediterranean coast; reports by international agencies have documented trafficking and detention of migrants in and around the city.
