Gilead Sciences

Type: Public (Nasdaq: GILD)
Founded: 1987
Headquarters: Foster City, California, US
Industry: Biotechnology / pharmaceuticals

Gilead Sciences, Inc. is an American biopharmaceutical company headquartered in Foster City, California. Founded in 1987 by physician Michael L. Riordan, the company focuses on antiviral drugs — particularly treatments for HIV/AIDS, viral hepatitis B and C, and influenza — and has expanded into oncology through acquisitions.

History and products

Gilead's name refers to the biblical Balm of Gilead. Its early success centred on antivirals developed with chemistry originating in Eastern European research institutes, including tenofovir, which became a backbone of HIV therapy in products such as Viread, Truvada (for treatment and later pre-exposure prophylaxis), and the single-tablet regimens Atripla, Genvoya and Biktarvy. Tamiflu (oseltamivir), licensed to Roche, brought substantial royalties during influenza outbreaks.

The 2011 acquisition of Pharmasset for about $11 billion brought sofosbuvir, marketed as Sovaldi (2013) and in the combination Harvoni (2014) — the first highly effective, curative oral therapies for hepatitis C. Their high launch prices (Sovaldi famously at about $1,000 per pill in the US) triggered wide debate over drug pricing, even as the drugs were cost-effective compared with the consequences of chronic hepatitis C. Later hepatitis C products include Epclusa and Vosevi.

Remdesivir and oncology

During the COVID-19 pandemic, Gilead's remdesivir (Veklury) became the first antiviral approved in the United States for treatment of the disease. Through the $21 billion acquisition of Immunomedics (2020) and the $11.9 billion purchase of Kite Pharma (2017), Gilead moved into oncology, gaining the antibody–drug conjugate Trodelvy and the CAR-T cell therapies Yescarta and Tecartus. Daniel O'Day became chief executive in 2019.
