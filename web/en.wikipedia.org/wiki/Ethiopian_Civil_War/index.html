Ethiopian Civil War

The Ethiopian Civil War, fought from 1974 to 1991, pitted the Ethiopian military government — the Provisional Military Government (Derg) under Mengistu Haile Mariam — against ethnic and political rebel groups including the Eritrean People's Liberation Front, the TPLF, and the forces that became the EPRDF.

Date12 September 1974 – 4 June 1991
LocationEthiopia (incl. Eritrea)
ResultRebel victory; Derg overthrown; Eritrean independence; EPRDF government

Course

The war began when the Derg deposed Emperor Haile Selassie in 1974. The regime's Red Terror of 1976–78 killed tens of thousands. Ethiopia fought Somalia in the Ogaden War (1977–78) with Soviet and Cuban support, while insurgencies grew in Eritrea and Tigray, aggravated by the catastrophic 1983–85 famine.

End

As Soviet support collapsed, EPRDF forces advanced and took Addis Ababa on 28 May 1991; Mengistu fled to Zimbabwe. Eritrea became independent in 1993 after a referendum. Total deaths are estimated from several hundred thousand to over a million, including famine victims.
