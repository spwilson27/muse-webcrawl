The Two Towers is the second volume of J. R. R. Tolkien's epic novel The Lord of the Rings, first published in 1954. Tolkien was never entirely happy about which two towers the title denotes; his own cover drawing shows Orthanc and Minas Morgul, though the pairing of Orthanc with Barad-dûr, or Minas Tirith with Minas Morgul, has also been argued.

Contents

The volume is divided into two "books". Book III follows the scattered Fellowship: Aragorn, Legolas and Gimli pursue the Orcs who captured Merry and Pippin; the hobbits escape, meet the Ent Treebeard, and witness the Ents' destruction of Isengard; Gandalf, returned as the White, rallies Rohan to victory at Helm's Deep. Book IV follows Frodo and Sam toward Mordor, guided — and dogged — by Gollum, through the Dead Marshes and Ithilien, where Faramir spares them, to the stairs of Cirith Ungol and the lair of the giant spider Shelob, who stings Frodo as the volume ends.

Adaptation

Peter Jackson's film The Lord of the Rings: The Two Towers (2002) won two Academy Awards and expanded the Battle of Helm's Deep into its climax. It restructured much of the book's interleaved narrative and altered Faramir's character, changes debated by readers.
