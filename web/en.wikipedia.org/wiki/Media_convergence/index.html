Media convergence


A smartphone exemplifies technological convergence

Media convergence is the merging of media through digitisation and networked computing. As media content is encoded in a common digital form, previously distinct media — print, radio, television, film, and telecommunications — can be produced, distributed, and consumed through the same devices and platforms.

Dimensions

Scholars distinguish several overlapping forms of convergence. Technological convergence refers to devices combining functions, such as the smartphone absorbing the camera, music player, and newspaper. Economic or corporate convergence describes mergers and cross-ownership among media and technology firms. Cultural convergence, a term popularised by Henry Jenkins in Convergence Culture (2006), describes how audiences engage across platforms, participate in fan communities, and contribute user-generated content, with stories flowing across media in transmedia franchises.

Effects

Convergence has disrupted traditional business models for news and entertainment, enabled streaming services and social media, and blurred the line between producers and consumers ("prosumers"). It also raises concerns about media concentration, platform gatekeeping, filter bubbles, and the decline of local journalism. Countervailing "divergence" persists as media fragment into niches and specialised formats.

History of the concept

The idea was anticipated by Ithiel de Sola Pool in Technologies of Freedom (1983), who described the "convergence of modes" eroding the boundaries between media. The spread of the World Wide Web in the 1990s and of smartphones after 2007 made convergence a mass phenomenon.
