Doctor of Osteopathic Medicine

The Doctor of Osteopathic Medicine (DO or D.O.) is a professional doctoral degree in medicine offered by osteopathic medical schools in the United States. DO graduates receive full physician licensure equivalent to that of MD graduates, and may practise in all medical specialties.

Training and philosophy

Osteopathic medicine was founded by Andrew Taylor Still in 1874, emphasising a whole-person approach, the interrelationship of structure and function, and the body's capacity for self-healing. In addition to the standard medical curriculum, DO students receive several hundred hours of training in osteopathic manipulative treatment (OMT), hands-on techniques used to diagnose and treat musculoskeletal problems.

Osteopathic physicians make up a growing share of the US physician workforce — roughly a quarter of American medical students now attend osteopathic schools — and since 2020 DO and MD graduates have matched into a single residency accreditation system.
