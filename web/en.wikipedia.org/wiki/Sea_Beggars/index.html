Sea Beggars

The capture of Brielle by the Sea Beggars, 1 April 1572.

The Sea Beggars (Dutch: Watergeuzen) were a confederacy of Dutch privateers and adventurers who fought Spanish rule during the Eighty Years' War (1568–1648). Operating under letters of marque from William of Orange, they harassed Spanish shipping and coastal towns, and their capture of Brielle in 1572 ignited the Dutch Revolt in Holland and Zeeland.

Active: 1569–1572 (as an independent confederacy)

Allegiance: William of Orange; Dutch rebels

Opponents: Habsburg Spain

Notable action: Capture of Brielle (1572)

Leaders: William van der Marck, Lord of Lumey

Origins

The name derived from the Dutch nobles who in 1566 presented a petition to the regent Margaret of Parma and were dismissed as "beggars" (gueux). Exiled Dutch Calvinists and displaced seamen took to the sea as privateers, based at first in English and German ports such as Dover, Emden and La Rochelle. Their ranks mixed committed Calvinists with fortune-seekers, and discipline was often loose.

Capture of Brielle

Expelled from English ports by Queen Elizabeth I in March 1572 under Spanish pressure, the fleet under William van der Marck, Lord of Lumey, seized the weakly defended port of Brielle (Den Briel) on 1 April 1572. The capture — commemorated in the Dutch saying "Op 1 april verloor Alva zijn bril" ("On April 1, Alba lost his glasses", a pun on Brielle) — gave the revolt its first permanent foothold, and town after town in Holland and Zeeland declared for the rebels.

Later history

William of Orange, initially embarrassed by their piracy and their massacre of Catholic clergy at Gorinchem, gradually brought the Sea Beggars under state control; they formed the nucleus of the Dutch navy that later won victories such as the Battle on the Zuiderzee (1573).
