Golgi tendon organ

The Golgi tendon organ (GTO), also called the tendon organ or neurotendinous organ, is a proprioceptive sensory receptor located at the origin and insertion of skeletal muscle fibres into tendons. It provides the sensory component of the Golgi tendon reflex (the inverse myotatic reflex), monitoring the tension generated by the muscle and protecting it from excessive force.

The organ is named after the Italian physician Camillo Golgi, who described it in 1880. It consists of a capsule of connective tissue enclosing braided collagen fibres intertwined with the terminals of a large type Ib sensory nerve fibre. When the muscle contracts, the collagen bundles straighten and compress the nerve endings, which fire in proportion to the force of contraction. In contrast to muscle spindles, which lie in parallel with muscle fibres and sense length, tendon organs lie in series with the fibres and sense tension.

Function

Signals from Golgi tendon organs travel via Ib afferents to the spinal cord, where they inhibit (through interneurons) the motor neurons supplying the same muscle, reducing its contraction when tension becomes dangerously high, while exciting antagonist muscles. This feedback allows fine regulation of muscle force — contributing, for example, to the steady grip needed to hold a fragile object — and plays a role in the "clasp-knife" response.
