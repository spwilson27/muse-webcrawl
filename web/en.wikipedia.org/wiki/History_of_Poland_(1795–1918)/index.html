History of Poland (1795–1918)

Period1795 – 1918
Key eventsThird Partition (1795), Duchy of Warsaw, November Uprising (1830), January Uprising (1863)

The history of Poland from 1795 to 1918 covers the period when the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth had been erased from the map by partition between Russia, Prussia and Austria, and Poles struggled to preserve their nation and regain independence.

The partitions' aftermath

The Third Partition (1795) completed the division begun in 1772 and 1793. Polish legions fought alongside Napoleon, who created the Duchy of Warsaw (1807); after 1815 the Congress of Vienna made it the Russian-ruled Congress Poland, while Prussia held the Grand Duchy of Posen and Austria held Galicia.

Uprisings

The November Uprising (1830–31) against Russia failed and brought harsher Russification and mass exile. The January Uprising (1863–64) likewise ended in defeat, abolition of Congress Poland's remaining autonomy, and intensified Germanisation in the Prussian partition. Galician Poles enjoyed comparatively broad cultural autonomy.

Towards independence

"Organic work" — economic and educational self-improvement — sustained national identity, while figures such as Józef Piłsudski and Roman Dmowski built rival political movements. The First World War, in which the partitioning powers fought on opposite sides, created the opening: Poland regained independence on 11 November 1918.
