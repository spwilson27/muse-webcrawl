History of Google

The history of Google dates to January 1996, when Larry Page and Sergey Brin, PhD students at Stanford University, began a research project that became the world's most popular search engine. Google was incorporated in September 1998 and grew from a garage start-up in Menlo Park, California, into one of the world's most valuable companies, reorganized in 2015 as a subsidiary of the holding company Alphabet Inc.

Origins and founding (1996–1998)

Page and Brin developed a search engine originally nicknamed "BackRub", which analysed the web's link structure to rank pages. Its ranking algorithm, PageRank, treated a link from one page to another as a vote of importance — an insight drawn from academic citation analysis. The name "Google" is a play on "googol", the mathematical term for 10100. In August 1998, Sun Microsystems co-founder Andy Bechtolsheim wrote the founders a cheque for $100,000, and Google Inc. was incorporated on 4 September 1998. The company first operated from the garage of Susan Wojcicki in Menlo Park.

Early growth (1999–2004)

Google moved to Mountain View, California — the "Googleplex" — and rapidly overtook rival search engines such as AltaVista and Yahoo's own search. Its clean homepage, speed, and relevant results made it the dominant search engine by the early 2000s. The company launched AdWords (2000), its advertising auction platform, which became its principal revenue source, followed by products such as Google News (2002), Gmail (2004), and Google Maps (2005). Eric Schmidt joined as CEO in 2001. Google's initial public offering took place on 19 August 2004, raising $1.67 billion.

Expansion and acquisitions (2005–2014)

Google acquired the video-sharing site YouTube in 2006 for $1.65 billion and the advertising company DoubleClick in 2007. It launched the Chrome web browser (2008) and backed the Android mobile operating system, which became the world's most widely used smartphone platform. Other milestones included Google Docs, Street View, the self-driving car project (later Waymo), and Google Glass. Larry Page became CEO again in 2011. By this period Google faced growing scrutiny over privacy, competition, and tax practices in the United States and Europe.

Alphabet and beyond (2015–present)

In October 2015, Google restructured under a new parent company, Alphabet Inc., with Sundar Pichai becoming CEO of Google (and, in 2019, of Alphabet as well). The company pivoted toward artificial intelligence, declaring itself "AI-first", launching products such as the Google Assistant, the Pixel phones, and large language models including BERT and the Gemini family. Antitrust cases in the US and EU — including a 2024 US court ruling that Google held an illegal monopoly in search — became a central challenge. Google remains the world's dominant search engine and one of the largest technology companies by market value.
