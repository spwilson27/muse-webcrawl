Dorothy Stuart Hamill (born July 26, 1956) is a retired American figure skater. She is the 1976 Olympic champion and 1976 World champion in ladies' singles. Hamill became one of the most popular athletes in the United States in the 1970s; her wedge haircut started a fashion craze, and the spin she popularised — a camel spin that transitions into a sit spin — is known as the "Hamill camel."

Hamill's gold medal at the 1976 Innsbruck Olympics made her "America's sweetheart."

Career

Hamill grew up in Riverside, Connecticut, and began skating on a pond near her home. Coached by Carlo Fassi in Denver, she won three consecutive U.S. national titles (1974–1976). At the 1976 Winter Olympics in Innsbruck, she won the gold medal with an athletic, graceful free skate, and followed it with the world title in Gothenburg a month later.

Turning professional, Hamill headlined the Ice Capades, which she later owned with her then-husband. She won the World Professional Championship five times, was inducted into the United States Figure Skating Hall of Fame and the World Figure Skating Hall of Fame, and appeared on television for decades, including as a mentor on skating shows. Her autobiographies include On and Off the Ice (1983) and A Skating Life (2007), in which she wrote candidly about depression and financial hardship.
