Orient

Orient
EtymologyLatin oriens, "rising (sun)", the east
AntonymOccident

"Orient" derives from the Latin word for the rising sun — the east.

The Orient is a term for the East, traditionally comprising the societies and countries of Asia — including the Near East, Middle East, and Far East — in contrast to the Occident (the West). It derives from the Latin oriens, the rising sun (the east), as its counterpart occidens, the setting sun, gave the Occident.

Usage and history

The boundaries of "the Orient" shifted over time: in antiquity it might mean lands east of Greece; in the 19th century, European usage ranged from the Ottoman Near East through Persia and India to China and Japan. The term is embedded in institutional names such as the School of Oriental and African Studies, the Oriental Institute, and the Orient Express, and in disciplines such as Oriental studies.

Criticism

Since Edward Said's Orientalism (1978), the term has been criticized for lumping diverse cultures together and framing them through a Western, colonial lens as exotic and static. As a result, "Orient" and especially "Oriental" for people have fallen out of polite use in much of the English-speaking world, replaced by specific regional terms such as East Asia, South Asia, and West Asia; adjectival uses for objects (e.g., an Oriental rug) persist.
