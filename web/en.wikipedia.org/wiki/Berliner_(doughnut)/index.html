Berliner (doughnut)

A Berliner is a German jam doughnut with no central hole, made from sweet yeast dough fried in fat or oil, filled with jam, marmalade, or other fillings, and usually topped with icing, powdered sugar, or granulated sugar. In Berlin and much of northern Germany it is called Pfannkuchen, while in most of the rest of Germany it is called Berliner; in southern regions it is known as Krapfen.

Traditions

Berliners are traditionally eaten on New Year's Eve and during carnival season, including Rosenmontag and Fat Tuesday. A playful tradition fills some Berliners in a batch with mustard instead of jam, without telling the eater.

"Ich bin ein Berliner"

United States President John F. Kennedy's 1963 speech in West Berlin, in which he said "Ich bin ein Berliner", gave rise to an urban legend that he had called himself a jam doughnut. In fact, the phrase was grammatically correct and understood as intended — "I am a Berliner" — and the doughnut sense was only a later embellishment of the story.
