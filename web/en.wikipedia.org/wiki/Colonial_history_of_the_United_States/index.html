Colonial history of the United States

Period: c. 1492 / 1607 – 1776
Key events: Jamestown 1607, Plymouth 1620, French and Indian War, American Revolution begins 1775

The colonial history of the United States covers the period of European colonisation of North America from the early 17th century — after earlier Spanish, French and Dutch ventures — until the American Revolution and independence in 1776.

Early colonisation

Spain established St. Augustine, Florida (1565), the oldest continuously inhabited European-founded city in the continental U.S. England's first permanent settlement was Jamestown, Virginia (1607), followed by Plymouth (1620) by Pilgrims, and the Puritan Massachusetts Bay Colony (1630). The Dutch founded New Netherland, seized by England in 1664. Colonies developed distinct regional societies: plantation economies based on enslaved labour in the South, mixed farming and trade in the Middle Colonies, and maritime and farming communities in New England.

Native peoples and slavery

Colonisation displaced Indigenous peoples through disease, war and land seizure. Enslaved Africans were first brought to Virginia in 1619, and chattel slavery became central to colonial economies, especially for tobacco, rice and later cotton.

Imperial wars and revolution

Britain's victory over France in the French and Indian War (1754–1763) removed France from mainland North America but left war debts. New taxes and measures — the Stamp Act, Townshend duties, Tea Act — provoked colonial resistance under the slogan of no taxation without representation, leading to the Boston Tea Party (1773), the First Continental Congress (1774), fighting at Lexington and Concord (1775), and the Declaration of Independence on 4 July 1776 establishing the United States.
