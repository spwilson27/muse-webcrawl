Floodgate

Floodgates, also called stop gates, are adjustable gates used to control water flow in flood barriers, reservoir, waterways, river, levee and stormwater management systems. They may be designed to set spillway crest heights in dams, to adjust flow rates in sluices and canals, or they may be designed to stop water flow entirely as part of a levee or storm surge system.

Types

Common types include radial (tainter) gates, vertical-lift gates, mitre gates used in locks, flap gates that open under pressure on one side, and needle gates. Since most of these devices operate by raising or lowering into the water, the thickness of the panel is small relative to the other dimensions of the gate.

Uses

Major floodgate systems include the Thames Barrier in London and the Maeslantkering in the Netherlands. Floodgates also feature in irrigation systems, hydropower intakes, and municipal drainage.
