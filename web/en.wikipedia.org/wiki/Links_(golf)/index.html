Links (golf)

A links is the oldest style of golf course, first developed in Scotland. Links courses are generally built on sandy coastland that offers a firmer playing surface than parkland or heathland courses, and are characterised by undulating fairways, deep bunkers and an absence of trees. The word "links" comes via the Scots language from the Old English word hlinc ("rising ground, ridge") and traditionally refers to an area of coastal sand dunes.

A classic links course along a sandy coastline.

Characteristics

Because links land is often unsuitable for farming, the terrain was left to grazing and, in Scotland, to golf. Links courses feature firm, fast fairways that reward the low, running "bump-and-run" shot, pot bunkers with revetted faces, and large, contoured greens. Exposure to coastal wind is a defining hazard, and the weather can change rapidly, making course management and shot-making rather than raw distance the prime skills.

Famous links courses

The Old Course at St Andrews is the archetype and is regarded as the oldest golf course in the world. Other celebrated links include Royal St George's, Royal Birkdale, Muirfield, Carnoustie, Royal County Down, Royal Portrush and Ballybunion. The Open Championship is always contested on a links course in the United Kingdom.

Links-style courses

Many modern courses outside Britain and Ireland imitate links features—firm turf, dunes, pot bunkers and few trees—even when built inland or on artificial terrain; these are commonly called "links-style" courses, though purists reserve the term for true seaside links on sandy soil.
