Brigadier general

Brigadier general is a senior military rank, the lowest rank of general officer in many countries. In the United States it is the first of the general officer grades — a one-star rank (pay grade O-7) senior to a colonel and junior to a major general — whose holder typically commands a brigade or serves in senior staff appointments. Its NATO code is OF-6.

History

The rank originates with the officer commanding a brigade. In France the brigadier des armées du roi dated from the 17th century, and the revolutionary rank of général de brigade passed into many other armies. In Britain the equivalent appointment was held by a brigadier, which in the British Army remains a rank senior to colonel but is not classed as a general officer rank.

United States

In the U.S. Army, Air Force, Marine Corps and Space Force, brigadier generals wear a single silver star. The rank was first used in the Continental Army during the American Revolutionary War and was held by officers such as Benedict Arnold before his defection. Brigadier generals commonly serve as deputy commanders of divisions, commanders of brigades and installations, and as directors on joint staffs.
