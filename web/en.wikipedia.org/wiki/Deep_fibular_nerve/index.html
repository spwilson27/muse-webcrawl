Deep fibular nerve

From: Common fibular nerve

Innervates: Anterior compartment of the leg; dorsum of the foot

The deep fibular nerve (also called the deep peroneal nerve) is a branch of the common fibular (peroneal) nerve that supplies the anterior compartment of the leg and the dorsum of the foot. It carries fibres principally from spinal segments L4 and L5.

Structure

The common fibular nerve winds around the neck of the fibula and divides into the superficial and deep fibular nerves. The deep fibular nerve pierces the anterior intermuscular septum and descends on the interosseous membrane accompanied by the anterior tibial vessels, lying between the tibialis anterior and extensor digitorum longus muscles, then between tibialis anterior and extensor hallucis longus. It passes deep to the extensor retinaculum at the ankle onto the dorsum of the foot.

Function

Motor branches supply tibialis anterior, extensor hallucis longus, extensor digitorum longus, fibularis tertius, and, on the dorsum of the foot, extensor digitorum brevis and extensor hallucis brevis — muscles responsible mainly for dorsiflexion of the ankle and extension of the toes. Its cutaneous contribution is small: skin of the web space between the first and second toes.

Clinical significance

Injury to the deep fibular nerve, or to its parent common fibular nerve at the fibular neck, causes weakness of dorsiflexion known as foot drop. The deep fibular nerve may also be compressed in anterior tarsal tunnel syndrome beneath the extensor retinaculum at the ankle, producing pain and paraesthesia on the dorsum of the foot.
