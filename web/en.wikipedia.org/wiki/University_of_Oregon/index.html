The University of Oregon (UO) is a public research university in Eugene, Oregon, United States. Founded in 1876, it is the flagship institution of the University of Oregon System and a member of the Association of American Universities. Its 295-acre campus lies along the Willamette River.

History

The Oregon Legislative Assembly established the university in 1872, and it opened in 1876 after Eugene residents raised funds by selling produce and holding strawberry festivals to avert foreclosure. Deady Hall, named for the first president of the board of regents, was the first building. The university grew through the 20th century under presidents such as Prince Lucien Campbell and acquired a strong reputation in architecture, journalism, and the sciences.

Academics

The UO is organized into colleges and schools including the College of Arts and Sciences, the Lundquist College of Business, the College of Education, the School of Journalism and Communication, the School of Law, the College of Design, and the School of Music and Dance. It is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities – Very high research activity." The Knight Campus for Accelerating Scientific Impact, funded by a major gift from Phil Knight, opened in 2020.

Athletics

The Oregon Ducks compete in the Big Ten Conference. The university is renowned for track and field — Eugene is nicknamed "TrackTown USA" — and Hayward Field has hosted multiple U.S. Olympic Trials and the 2022 World Athletics Championships. Phil Knight, co-founder of Nike, is an alumnus, and Nike's origins trace to the UO track program under coach Bill Bowerman.
