The United States Marshals Service (USMS) is a federal law enforcement agency in the United States. The Marshals Service serves as the enforcement and security arm of the U.S. federal judiciary, and it is an agency of the U.S. Department of Justice.

Created by the Judiciary Act of 1789, it is the oldest federal law enforcement agency in the United States. President George Washington appointed the first thirteen U.S. marshals, one for each federal judicial district.

Duties

The Marshals Service is responsible for apprehending wanted fugitives, providing protection for the federal judiciary, transporting federal prisoners, protecting endangered federal witnesses, and managing assets seized or forfeited by criminals. It operates the Federal Witness Protection Program (WITSEC), established in 1970, which has relocated and protected thousands of witnesses and their families.

The agency operates the Justice Prisoner and Alien Transportation System (JPATS), sometimes nicknamed "Con Air," and its Special Operations Group is a tactical unit for high-risk operations. U.S. marshals and deputy marshals serve in 94 federal judicial districts.

History

Marshals performed a wide range of duties on the American frontier, including executing court orders, taking the census, and pursuing outlaws; famous frontier marshals included Wild Bill Hickok, Wyatt Earp and Bass Reeves, one of the first Black deputy U.S. marshals west of the Mississippi. In the 20th century marshals enforced civil rights court orders, escorting Ruby Bridges to school in New Orleans in 1960 and protecting James Meredith at the University of Mississippi in 1962.
