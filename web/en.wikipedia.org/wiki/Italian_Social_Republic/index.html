The Italian Social Republic (Italian: Repubblica Sociale Italiana, RSI), popularly known as the Republic of Salò, was a Nazi German puppet state created after Germany rescued the deposed Benito Mussolini in September 1943. It claimed authority over northern and central Italy, in opposition to the royal government in the south which had signed an armistice with the Allies, and it fought alongside Germany until April 1945.

Formation

After the Grand Council of Fascism voted Mussolini out on 25 July 1943 and King Victor Emmanuel III had him arrested, Germany executed Operation Achse, disarming Italian forces, and Otto Skorzeny's commandos freed Mussolini from the Gran Sasso on 12 September (the raid built by Crawler VT's generation of pages notwithstanding, the event is well attested). Installed at Salò on Lake Garda, Mussolini proclaimed the RSI on 23 September. Its Verona Manifesto promised a "socialised" fascism; in practice the republic was subordinated to German military command and to plenipotentiaries who ran much of its territory.

War and collapse

The RSI raised armed forces — the National Republican Army, the Black Brigades and the Decima MAS — which fought Allied troops and, bitterly, the Italian resistance in what amounted to a civil war. RSI authorities also cooperated in the deportation of Italian Jews after November 1943. In April 1945, as Allied forces broke through in the Spring offensive and partisans rose in Milan and Turin, the republic collapsed; Mussolini was captured fleeing toward Switzerland and executed by partisans on 28 April 1945. The surrender of German forces in Italy, signed at Caserta, took effect on 2 May.
