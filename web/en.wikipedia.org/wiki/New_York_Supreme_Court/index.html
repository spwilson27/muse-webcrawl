The Supreme Court of the State of New York is the trial-level court of general jurisdiction in the judiciary of New York. Despite its name, it is not the state's highest court — that role belongs to the New York Court of Appeals — a naming arrangement that often causes confusion.

Jurisdiction

The Supreme Court has unlimited original jurisdiction in civil and criminal cases, handling felony prosecutions and major civil actions. Family matters and smaller claims are heard in other courts such as Family Court and the New York City Civil Court.

Organisation

Justices are elected to 14-year terms in 13 judicial districts. Appeals from Supreme Court decisions go first to the Appellate Division, which is a department of the Supreme Court itself, and then to the Court of Appeals, the state's court of last resort.

The courthouse at 60 Centre Street in Manhattan (New York County) is a famous example of the court's venues and a frequent film location.
