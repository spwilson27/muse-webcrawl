White House Director of Speechwriting

The White House Director of Speechwriting is a senior staff position in the Executive Office of the President of the United States, responsible for overseeing the research and writing of the President's speeches. The director leads a small team of staff speechwriters within the White House Office and works closely with the communications staff and policy councils.

Role

The speechwriting office drafts major addresses such as the State of the Union address, inaugural addresses, and remarks for state visits, ceremonies, and policy announcements. Drafts typically circulate among senior staff and policy experts before the President reviews them. Judson Welliver, speechwriter to Presidents Warren G. Harding and Calvin Coolidge, is often described as the first presidential speechwriter in the modern sense.

Notable holders

Notable directors and chief speechwriters have included Theodore Sorensen (for John F. Kennedy), Peggy Noonan (for Ronald Reagan), Jon Favreau (for Barack Obama) and Cody Keenan, and Vinay Reddy (for Joe Biden).
