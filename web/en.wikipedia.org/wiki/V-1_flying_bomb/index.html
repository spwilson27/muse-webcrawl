V-1 flying bomb

The V-1 flying bomb (German: Vergeltungswaffe 1, "Retribution Weapon 1"), also known to the Allies as the buzz bomb or doodlebug, was an early cruise missile developed by Nazi Germany and used in the Second World War. Powered by a simple pulsejet engine and guided by an autopilot, the V-1 was launched against London and other targets from June 1944, a week after the D-Day landings. More than 30,000 were built; roughly 9,500 were fired at England, of which about 2,400 reached London.

Design

The V-1 was a mid-wing monoplane built largely of plywood and welded sheet steel. Its Argus pulsejet, firing about 45–50 times per second, gave it its distinctive buzzing drone. Guidance was by a gyroscopic autopilot controlling altitude and course, while a small propeller-driven air log in the nose measured the distance flown; when the preset range was reached, the controls were locked to send the missile into a steep dive. The sudden silence of the engine as it dived became a feared signal to those below.

Operational history and countermeasures

Launches began on 13 June 1944 from ski-shaped ramps in northern France. The attacks killed over 6,000 people in Britain and injured many more. British defences — fast fighters such as the Hawker Tempest and Spitfire XIV, anti-aircraft guns directed by radar with proximity-fuzed shells, and barrage balloons — progressively destroyed a large share of incoming V-1s. Deception operations feeding false impact reports through double agents also misled German targeting. The campaign against England ended in early 1945; V-1s were also launched at Antwerp and other continental targets, and a piloted version, the Fieseler Fi 103R Reichenberg, was developed but never used operationally.
