Executive protection
Also calledClose protection
ClientsCorporate executives, celebrities, dignitaries, wealthy families
Core tasksThreat assessment, advance work, secure transportation, residential security

Executive protection (EP), also known as close protection, refers to security and risk-mitigation measures taken to ensure the safety of very important persons (VIPs) or other individuals who may be exposed to elevated personal risk because of their employment, celebrity status, wealth, associations, or geographical location. Unlike the popular image of the "bodyguard", modern executive protection emphasises advance planning, intelligence, and avoidance of risk over physical intervention.

Scope of work

A protective programme typically includes threat and vulnerability assessment of the principal and their family, advance work at sites to be visited (checking routes, venues, and emergency facilities), secure ground transportation with trained drivers, residential and office security, travel logistics, and coordination with law enforcement and venue security. Protective agents may work singly or in details of several agents, and in low-profile assignments deliberately blend in as aides or staff rather than presenting a visible security presence.

Training and industry

Practitioners commonly come from military, law-enforcement, and government protective-service backgrounds (such as the US Secret Service or military police), supplemented by specialist training in protective operations, emergency medicine, defensive driving, and firearms, and by licensing where required by local law. Corporate security departments and specialist firms provide executive protection for senior business leaders, a practice that expanded after high-profile attacks and kidnappings of executives in the 1970s and remains common for CEOs of large companies, whose protective costs are often disclosed as a business expense.
