Monoterpenes are a class of terpenes that consist of two isoprene units and have the molecular formula C10H16. They may be linear (acyclic) or contain rings, and oxygenated derivatives — alcohols, aldehydes, ketones and ethers such as linalool, menthol, camphor and eucalyptol — are collectively called monoterpenoids. Monoterpenes are major constituents of the essential oils of many plants and are responsible for much of the aroma of herbs, spices, citrus fruits and conifers.

Biosynthesis

In plants, monoterpenes are produced mainly in plastids via the MEP (methylerythritol phosphate) pathway, which condenses isopentenyl pyrophosphate and dimethylallyl pyrophosphate to form geranyl pyrophosphate (GPP), the universal C10 precursor. Terpene synthase enzymes then cyclise or rearrange GPP into the hundreds of known monoterpene skeletons.

Examples and uses

α-Pinene and β-pinene dominate turpentine from pine resin; limonene gives citrus peel its scent; menthol flavours mint; camphor has long been used medicinally; and carvone accounts for the contrasting flavours of spearmint and caraway. Monoterpenes serve plants as insect repellents, pollinator attractants and antimicrobial defences, and enter the atmosphere in vast quantities — emissions from forests contribute to aerosol formation and the characteristic blue haze of mountain ranges such as the Great Smoky Mountains.
