Kardar–Parisi–Zhang equation

The Kardar–Parisi–Zhang (KPZ) equation is a non-linear stochastic partial differential equation introduced by Mehran Kardar, Giorgio Parisi and Yi-Cheng Zhang in 1986 to describe the dynamics of growing interfaces, such as a flame front, a bacterial colony edge, or a thin film deposited atom by atom. It reads:

∂h/∂t = ν∇²h + (λ/2)(∇h)² + η(x, t)

where h(x, t) is the height of the interface, ν is a smoothing (surface tension) coefficient, λ measures the strength of lateral growth, and η is a noise term, usually taken to be Gaussian white noise.

Background

Without the non-linear term, the equation reduces to the Edwards–Wilkinson equation, which describes a roughening interface whose width grows with a characteristic exponent. The (∇h)² term — arising physically because growth tends to occur normal to the local surface — changes the scaling behaviour fundamentally. Surfaces governed by KPZ dynamics grow laterally as well as upward, producing fluctuations whose statistics depend strongly on dimension.

KPZ universality class

The equation defines the KPZ universality class: a broad family of growth models sharing the same scaling exponents and fluctuation statistics. In one spatial dimension the exponents are known exactly (the roughness exponent α = 1/2 and growth exponent β = 1/3), and height fluctuations are governed by the Tracy–Widom distributions of random matrix theory, a connection confirmed experimentally in turbulent liquid crystals and other systems. In higher dimensions the exponents are known only approximately, and the strong-coupling behaviour of KPZ remains a major open problem in statistical physics. Through the Cole–Hopf transformation, the KPZ equation is equivalent to the noisy Burgers equation of fluid dynamics and to a diffusion equation with multiplicative noise describing a directed polymer in a random medium.
