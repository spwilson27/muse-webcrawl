The Motorcycle Diaries

AuthorErnesto "Che" Guevara
Original titleDiarios de Motocicleta
LanguageSpanish
GenreMemoir, travel writing
Published1993 (posthumously)

The Motorcycle Diaries (Spanish: Diarios de motocicleta) is a posthumously published memoir of the Marxist revolutionary Ernesto "Che" Guevara. It recounts his early travels as a 23-year-old medical student with his friend Alberto Granado, a biochemist, traveling 8,000 miles through South America on a 500cc Norton motorcycle they called La Poderosa ("The Mighty One").

The journey

Leaving Buenos Aires in January 1952, Guevara and Granado crossed Argentina, Chile, Peru, Colombia, and Venezuela over nine months. The motorcycle broke down in Chile, and the two continued by foot, boat, and hitchhiking. They visited the Chuquicamata copper mine in Chile, the Incan ruins of Machu Picchu, and a leper colony on the Amazon River at San Pablo, Peru, where they volunteered as doctors. The encounters with poverty, indigenous communities, and exploitation along the way profoundly transformed Guevara's worldview.

Contents and themes

The diary chronicles Guevara's developing identity, from a middle-class Argentine adventurer to a man increasingly committed to the idea of a unified Latin America and social revolution. A famous passage describes his swim across the Amazon on his twenty-fourth birthday, separating the two sides of the leper colony. Scholars note that the published text was edited later by Guevara himself, and its literary shaping reflects hindsight about his revolutionary destiny.

Film adaptation

In 2004 the memoir was adapted into an acclaimed film, also titled The Motorcycle Diaries (Diarios de motocicleta), directed by Walter Salles, starring Gael García Bernal as Guevara and Rodrigo de la Serna as Granado. The film premiered at the Sundance Film Festival and won the Academy Award for Best Original Song ("Al otro lado del río").
