Christopher Guest

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Christopher Haden-Guest, 5th Baron Haden-Guest (born 5 February 1948), known professionally as Christopher Guest, is a British-American actor, comedian, screenwriter and director. He is best known for writing, directing and starring in a series of improvisational mockumentary comedy films.

Career

Guest first gained attention as a writer and performer on The National Lampoon Radio Hour and as a cast member of Saturday Night Live (1984–85). He co-wrote and starred in Rob Reiner's This Is Spinal Tap (1984), playing guitarist Nigel Tufnel of the fictional heavy metal band Spinal Tap — a band he performed with in reality, alongside Michael McKean and Harry Shearer.

His directed mockumentaries, largely improvised from detailed outlines with a regular ensemble cast, include Waiting for Guffman (1996), Best in Show (2000), A Mighty Wind (2003), For Your Consideration (2006) and Mascots (2016). As an actor he appeared in films such as The Princess Bride (as Count Rugen) and A Few Good Men.

Personal life

Guest was born in New York City to a British father and American mother; he succeeded his father as Baron Haden-Guest and sat in the House of Lords until 1999. He is married to actress Jamie Lee Curtis. He is also a musician, playing guitar and mandolin.
