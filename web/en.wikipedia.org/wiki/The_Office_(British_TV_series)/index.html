The Office (British TV series)

The Office is a British television mockumentary sitcom created, written and directed by Ricky Gervais and Stephen Merchant. First broadcast on BBC Two in 2001, it follows the everyday lives of office employees at the Slough branch of the fictional Wernham Hogg paper company.

Overview

Regional manager David Brent (Gervais) fancies himself a beloved entertainer and philosopher, but is in fact a deluded, needy and incompetent boss who constantly embarrasses his staff. Around him revolve salesman Tim Canterbury (Martin Freeman), stuck in a dead-end job; receptionist Dawn Tinsley (Lucy Davis), with whom Tim shares a will-they-won't-they romance; and Brent's sycophantic assistant Gareth Keenan (Mackenzie Crook). The series charts redundancies, a merger with the Swindon branch, Brent's own sacking, and — in the acclaimed 2003 Christmas specials — a quietly hopeful resolution for Tim and Dawn.

Reception and influence

Made in a fly-on-the-wall documentary style with no laugh track and long, excruciating silences, the show won Golden Globes and BAFTAs and transformed television comedy, helping popularise "cringe comedy" and the mockumentary format. It is regularly ranked among the greatest British sitcoms of all time.

Remakes

The format was remade in many countries, most famously the American version (The Office, NBC, 2005–2013) starring Steve Carell, as well as versions in France, Germany, Canada, Chile, Israel, India, Australia and elsewhere.
