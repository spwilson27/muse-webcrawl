Diane von Fürstenberg

Diane von Fürstenberg
Born: Diane Simone Michele Halfin, (1946-12-31) December 31, 1946, Brussels, Belgium
Occupation: Fashion designer
Known for: Wrap dress

Diane von Fürstenberg (born Diane Simone Michele Halfin; 31 December 1946) is a Belgian fashion designer best known for her wrap dress. She initially rose to prominence in 1969 when she married into the German princely House of Fürstenberg, becoming Princess Diane of Fürstenberg; the couple separated in 1972 and divorced in 1983.

Career

Von Fürstenberg began her fashion career in New York in the early 1970s. Her jersey wrap dress, introduced in 1974, became an enduring symbol of women's liberation and independence; by 1976 she had sold millions of the dresses and appeared on the cover of Newsweek, which declared her "the most marketable woman since Coco Chanel". After a period away from the business, she relaunched her company in 1997 (as DVF), reviving the wrap dress for a new generation, and the brand expanded into a global luxury lifestyle label.

Later work and philanthropy

She served as president of the Council of Fashion Designers of America (CFDA) from 2006 to 2019, after which she became its chair. With her husband Barry Diller, she established the Diller–von Fürstenberg Family Foundation and the DVF Awards, honouring women leaders. Her memoirs include A Signature Life (1998) and The Woman I Wanted to Be (2014).
