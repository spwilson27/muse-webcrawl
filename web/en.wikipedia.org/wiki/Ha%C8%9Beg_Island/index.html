Hațeg Island

Hațeg Island was a large island in the Tethys Sea during the Late Cretaceous period, roughly 80 to 66 million years ago, whose deposits are now exposed in the Hațeg Basin of present-day Romania. It is famous for its endemic dinosaur fauna, in which many species show insular dwarfism — sauropods such as Magyarosaurus were dramatically smaller than their mainland relatives — while giant pterosaurs such as Hatzegopteryx, one of the largest flying animals of all time, occupied the apex-predator niche.

Location (modern)Hațeg Basin, Hunedoara County, Romania
AgeMaastrichtian, Late Cretaceous (~72–66 Ma)
Key fossilsMagyarosaurus, Zalmoxes, Hatzegopteryx, Balaur bondoc
Named forTown of Hațeg

Palaeontology

The island's fossils were first studied by the Austro-Hungarian palaeontologist Franz Nopcsa in the early 20th century; Nopcsa himself proposed the theory of insular dwarfism to explain the small size of its dinosaurs. The fauna included the dwarf titanosaur Magyarosaurus dacus, the ornithopods Zalmoxes and Telmatosaurus, the two-clawed dromaeosaur Balaur bondoc, and the gigantic azhdarchid Hatzegopteryx thambema. Mammals such as the multituberculate Kogaionon, turtles, crocodilians and frogs round out a rich island ecosystem preserved in the Sânpetru and Densuș-Ciula formations.
