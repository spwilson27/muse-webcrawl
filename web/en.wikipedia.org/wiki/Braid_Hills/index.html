The Braid Hills are an area of open hill ground in the south of Edinburgh, Scotland, forming one of the city's characteristic groups of hills. They lie between the districts of Morningside, Comiston and Liberton, south of Blackford Hill and across the Braid Burn valley from the Pentland Hills to the southwest. The highest point, Buckstone Snab, reaches about 213 metres above sea level, and the hills offer wide views over the city to the Firth of Forth.

Geology

Like Arthur's Seat and Blackford Hill, the Braid Hills are formed of Lower Carboniferous volcanic rocks — basaltic lavas and intrusions associated with the ancient volcanic complex centred on Arthur's Seat — which resist erosion better than the surrounding sedimentary strata, leaving the hills standing above the city.

Recreation

Much of the area is occupied by public golf courses: the Braid Hills courses, laid out in the late 19th and early 20th centuries on common grazing land, are among Edinburgh's most popular municipal courses, together with the Wee Braid nine-hole course and the nearby Liberton and Lothianburn courses. The hills are crossed by footpaths and are popular with walkers and sledgers in winter. The Braid Hills Hotel (now the Braid Hills Hotel on Braid Road) and the Hermitage of Braid, a wooded nature reserve along the Braid Burn below Blackford Hill, lie nearby.
