Dili (Portuguese/Tetum: Díli) is the capital and largest city of Timor-Leste (East Timor). It lies on the northern coast of the island of Timor, on the Ombai Strait, at the foot of dry, scrub-covered hills. Dili is the chief port and commercial centre of the country and home to roughly a quarter of its population.

History

Dili was settled in 1520 by the Portuguese, who made it the capital of Portuguese Timor in 1769, moving the seat of government from Lifau. During the Second World War, Dili was occupied by Australian and Dutch forces and then by the Japanese from 1942 to 1945, and was heavily bombed.

In 1975, after a brief civil war, the Fretilin party declared East Timor independent in Dili, but Indonesia invaded nine days later and annexed the territory. During the Indonesian occupation Dili grew as an administrative centre. Following the 1999 referendum in which East Timorese voted for independence, militia violence destroyed much of the city; Dili became the capital of the restored independent Democratic Republic of Timor-Leste on 20 May 2002.

Geography and climate

Dili has a tropical savanna climate with a pronounced dry season from roughly May to November. The city spreads along a narrow coastal plain backed by mountains, and its bay is sheltered by Atauro Island to the north.

Landmarks

Landmarks include the 27-metre Cristo Rei statue on a cape east of the city, inaugurated in 1996; the Motael Church, the oldest church in the country; the Santa Cruz cemetery, site of the 1991 massacre; the Resistance Museum; and the Palacio de Cinzas and the Government Palace on the waterfront. Presidente Nicolau Lobato International Airport serves the city from the western suburb of Comoro.
