RMS Olympic

RMS Olympic was a British ocean liner and the lead ship of the White Star Line's trio of Olympic-class liners, which also included Titanic and Britannic. Unlike her younger sisters, Olympic enjoyed a long and illustrious career spanning 24 years, from 1911 to 1935, including service as a troopship during the First World War, which earned her the nickname "Old Reliable".

Career

Built by Harland & Wolff in Belfast and launched on 20 October 1910, Olympic made her maiden voyage to New York in June 1911 under Captain Edward Smith, who later commanded Titanic. In September 1911 she collided with the cruiser HMS Hawke, sustaining substantial damage. After Titanic sank in April 1912, Olympic was refitted with improved safety features, including a raised double hull and watertight bulkheads extended higher.

Requisitioned in 1915 as HMT Olympic, she carried hundreds of thousands of troops, and in May 1918 rammed and sank the German submarine U-103 — the only known instance of a merchant vessel sinking an enemy submarine in the war. Postwar refits returned her to luxury service; she remained a flagship of the North Atlantic run through the 1920s.

Retirement

Declining passenger numbers during the Great Depression and the merger of White Star with Cunard made the aging liner uneconomical. Olympic was withdrawn in 1935 and scrapped at Jarrow and Inverkeithing; many of her fittings were auctioned and survive in hotels and private collections.
