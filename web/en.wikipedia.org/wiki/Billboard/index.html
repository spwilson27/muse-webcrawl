Billboard

Billboard
TypeRoadside advertising structure
Typical sizesBulletin (14 × 48 ft), poster (12 × 24 ft)
IndustryOut-of-home advertising

A billboard (also called a hoarding in the United Kingdom and many other parts of the world) is a large outdoor advertising structure typically found in high-traffic areas such as alongside busy roads. Billboards present large advertisements to passing pedestrians and drivers, and are a major component of out-of-home advertising.

History

Large-format outdoor advertising grew rapidly in the late nineteenth century with improvements in lithographic printing, which allowed posters to be produced in large sections and pasted onto wooden boards. The rise of the automobile in the early twentieth century made roadside billboards a dominant advertising medium, and standardized sizes — notably the 24-sheet poster and the larger "bulletin" format — became industry norms.

Types

Traditional billboards use printed vinyl or paper posters. Digital billboards, which use LED displays, can rotate multiple advertisements and change messages remotely. Mobile billboards are mounted on trucks, while painted wall displays and three-dimensional or inflatable extensions are used for added visual impact.

Regulation

Billboards are regulated in many jurisdictions for reasons of traffic safety and visual amenity. In the United States, the Highway Beautification Act of 1965 restricted billboard placement along federal highways, and some states and cities, such as Vermont and São Paulo, Brazil, have imposed broad bans.
