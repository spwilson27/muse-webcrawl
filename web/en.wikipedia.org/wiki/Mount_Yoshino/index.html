Mount Yoshino (吉野山, Yoshinoyama) is a mountain in the town of Yoshino in Nara Prefecture, Japan. It is renowned as Japan's most famous site for viewing cherry blossoms (hanami): some 30,000 cherry trees, planted over centuries in groves at four elevations — Shimo Senbon, Naka Senbon, Kami Senbon and Oku Senbon, the "thousand trees" at the bottom, middle, top and deep interior — bloom in rolling waves up the mountainside each spring.

Religious significance

Yoshino is sacred in Shugendō, the Japanese mountain ascetic tradition. The mountain is associated with En no Gyōja, the legendary 7th-century founder of Shugendō, who is said to have carved images of the deity Zaō Gongen there. The great hall of Kinpusen-ji, the Zao-dō, is among the largest wooden buildings in Japan and enshrines towering statues of Zaō Gongen. Yoshino also contains Yoshimizu Shrine and numerous other temples and shrines along the pilgrimage route to Mount Ōmine.

History

Yoshino was celebrated in classical Japanese poetry from the earliest anthologies onward, becoming a byword for cherry blossoms in the poetic tradition. In the 14th century, during the Nanboku-chō period, Emperor Go-Daigo established his Southern Court at Yoshino after fleeing Kyoto, and the mountain served as its seat for decades. The warlord Toyotomi Hideyoshi held a grand blossom-viewing party there in 1594, attended by thousands of guests.

Visiting

The blossom season, usually from late March to mid-April, draws enormous crowds to the narrow mountain town. A ropeway and hiking paths ascend through the four groves. The site is listed together with Mount Kōya and Kumano as part of the UNESCO World Heritage Site inscribed in 2004.
