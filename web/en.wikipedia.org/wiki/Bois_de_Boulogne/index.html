The Bois de Boulogne is a large public park on the western edge of Paris, in the 16th arrondissement. Covering 845 hectares — about two and a half times the area of New York's Central Park — it is, with the Bois de Vincennes, one of the "lungs" of the city.

History

The wood is a remnant of the ancient oak forest of Rouvray. Under Napoleon III, who had admired London's Hyde Park, it was transformed between 1852 and 1858 by Baron Haussmann and engineer Jean-Charles Alphand into a landscaped park with lakes, cascades, lawns and winding paths.

Features

The park contains the Lac Inférieur and Lac Supérieur, the Longchamp and Auteuil racecourses, the Roland Garros tennis stadium, the Jardin d'Acclimatation amusement park, the Parc de Bagatelle rose garden, and the Fondation Louis Vuitton museum, opened in 2014.
