Laws in Wales Acts 1535 and 1542

The Laws in Wales Acts 1535 and 1542 (Welsh: Deddf Uno Cymru a Lloegr) were acts of the Parliament of England, during the reign of Henry VIII, which legally annexed Wales to the Kingdom of England and imposed English law and administration across the country. They are often collectively referred to as the Acts of Union.

Background

Before the acts, Wales was divided between the Principality of Wales, ruled directly by the Crown, and the Marcher lordships, semi-independent fiefs along the border. Welsh law (Cyfraith Hywel) survived in parts of the country alongside English law, and Wales sent no representatives to Parliament.

Provisions

The 1535 act declared Wales incorporated into England, divided the Marcher lordships into English-style counties, extended English common law to the whole country, and required English to be the sole language of the courts and of officialdom. The 1542 act completed the settlement, establishing the Court of Great Sessions and granting Wales representation in the English Parliament. Monmouthshire's status was left ambiguous for centuries.

Effects and legacy

The acts unified the administration of England and Wales, a constitutional arrangement that persisted until Welsh devolution in the late 20th century. They marginalized the Welsh language in public life, though Welsh remained the language of the great majority of the population. The legislation was formally repealed in stages, with remaining provisions repealed in 1993.
