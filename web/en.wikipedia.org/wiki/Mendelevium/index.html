Mendelevium is a synthetic chemical element with the symbol Md and atomic number 101. It is a metallic radioactive actinide, named in honour of Dmitri Mendeleev, creator of the periodic table — the first element named after a person who was neither a discoverer of it nor (at naming) mythological, a choice that caused Cold War controversy because Mendeleev was Russian. Mendelevium was first created in 1955 at Berkeley by bombarding einsteinium-253 with alpha particles; the initial experiment produced only 17 atoms, and it was the first element synthesised one atom at a time.

Properties and significance

All mendelevium isotopes are radioactive; the longest-lived, Md-258, has a half-life of about 51 days, and only trace quantities have ever been made, so its chemistry is inferred from minute samples. Studies established that, unusually for the later actinides, mendelevium favours the +2 oxidation state — chemical detective work from handfuls of atoms. It has no applications outside basic research, but it is a landmark in the synthesis of superheavy elements and a fitting tribute within the table Mendeleev built, whose gaps once predicted elements like gallium.
