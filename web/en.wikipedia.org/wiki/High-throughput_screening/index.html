High-throughput screening

High-throughput screening (HTS) is a method for scientific experimentation especially used in drug discovery. Using robotics, data processing software, liquid handling devices and sensitive detectors, HTS allows a researcher to quickly conduct millions of chemical, genetic or pharmacological tests, rapidly identifying active compounds ("hits") that modulate a particular biomolecular pathway.

Methods

Experiments are carried out in microtiter plates — commonly 384- or 1536-well formats — where each well serves as a miniature test tube. Assays may be biochemical (purified target protein) or cell-based (reporter genes, fluorescence readouts, high-content imaging). The quality of an assay is commonly quantified by the Z-factor. Hit compounds are retested in dose-response curves and secondary assays before becoming lead compounds in medicinal chemistry.

Automation and scale

Fully automated screening systems integrate plate stackers, robotic arms, incubators and plate readers, and can process over 100,000 compounds per day (ultra-high-throughput screening). Results feed compound libraries and databases such as PubChem, and HTS data are now widely used to train machine-learning models for virtual screening.
