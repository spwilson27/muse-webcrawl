GnuCOBOL

GnuCOBOL (formerly OpenCOBOL, and for a short time known as GNU Cobol) is a free implementation of the COBOL programming language. GnuCOBOL is a transcompiler which translates COBOL programs into C and compiles them using a native C compiler; it can also act as an interpreter through its runtime library.

The project was originally started by Keisuke Nishida as OpenCOBOL, and was later developed under the leadership of Roger While and subsequently Simon Sobisch. It became part of the GNU Project and was renamed GnuCOBOL in 2014–2015. The compiler supports much of the COBOL 85 and COBOL 2002 standards along with many extensions present in commercial compilers such as those from IBM and Micro Focus.

GnuCOBOL runs on Linux, Windows, macOS and mainframe environments, and is used to modernise legacy COBOL applications by organisations lacking commercial compiler licences.
