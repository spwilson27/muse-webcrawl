Thujone is a ketone and a monoterpene that occurs naturally in two diastereomeric forms: (−)-α-thujone and (+)-β-thujone. It is found in a number of plants, such as arborvitae (genus Thuja, hence the derivation of the name), clary sage, tansy, and wormwood, most notably Artemisia absinthium, usually as a mix of isomers in a 1:2 ratio.

Pharmacology

Thujone acts on GABA receptors in the brain; in large doses it is a convulsant. It achieved notoriety as the compound blamed for the supposed psychoactive and toxic effects of the spirit absinthe, a reputation modern analyses consider greatly exaggerated: historical absinthes contained far less thujone than early estimates, and their principal pharmacological agent was ethanol.

Regulation

Because of its toxicity in concentrated form, thujone content in food and drink is regulated in many jurisdictions; the European Union limits it to 35 mg/kg in absinthe-style spirits, and the United States requires thujone-containing foods and spirits to be effectively "thujone-free" (under 10 ppm).
