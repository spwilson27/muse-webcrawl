Bumba Meu Boi
OriginMaranhão, Brazil
OccasionJune festivals (Festas Juninas), esp. São João
RecognitionUNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage (2019)

Bumba Meu Boi is a Brazilian folk festival and interactive dance-drama originating in the state of Maranhão, performed during the June festivals. It retells the legend of the death and resurrection of a bull, blending African, indigenous, and Portuguese elements in music, dance, theatre, and elaborate costume.

The story

In the standard plot, the enslaved worker Pai Francisco kills his master's prize bull to satisfy his pregnant wife Mãe Catirina's craving for the bull's tongue (or liver). The enraged landowner orders the animal restored; healers and shamans are summoned, and the bull is miraculously resurrected, prompting celebration. The tale satirises colonial plantation society and the relations between masters and enslaved people.

Performance styles

Groups (batalhões) perform in distinct rhythmic styles called sotaques — including matraca (with wooden rattles), zabumba (bass drums), orquestra (with wind instruments), and baixada — each with characteristic costumes. The centrepiece is the bull itself, a lightweight frame covered in embroidered velvet and sequins, animated by a dancer (the miolo do boi) hidden beneath it.

Recognition

In 2019 UNESCO inscribed the Bumba Meu Boi cultural complex of Maranhão on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. Related bull-festival traditions, such as the Boi Bumbá of Parintins in Amazonas, are performed elsewhere in Brazil.
