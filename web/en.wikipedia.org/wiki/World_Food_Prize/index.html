The World Food Prize is an international award recognising the achievements of individuals who have advanced human development by improving the quality, quantity or availability of food in the world. It has been referred to as the "Nobel Prize for food and agriculture".

History

The prize was conceived by Nobel Peace Prize laureate Norman Borlaug, the agronomist credited with launching the Green Revolution, and established in 1986 with the support of General Foods. The first laureate, in 1987, was M. S. Swaminathan of India. In 1990 the prize was taken over by Iowa businessman and philanthropist John Ruan, and its headquarters moved to Des Moines, Iowa. The award ceremony is held each October at the Iowa State Capitol, around World Food Day, alongside the Borlaug Dialogue international symposium.

Laureates

Laureates have included plant scientists, economists, policymakers and humanitarian leaders from many countries, such as Muhammad Yunus, George Snell (co-laureate), Gurdev Khush, Catherine Bertini, David Beckmann and Jo Luck, Gebisa Ejeta, and the nutrition scientist Shakuntala Haraksingh Thilsted. The monetary award is US$500,000.
