Richard Peacock

Born: 9 April 1820, Swaledale, Yorkshire
Died: 3 March 1889 (aged 68)
Occupation: Locomotive engineer, politician
Known for: Co-founder of Beyer, Peacock and Company

Richard Peacock (9 April 1820 – 3 March 1889) was an English engineer and locomotive manufacturer, a co-founder of the firm Beyer, Peacock and Company, and later a Liberal Member of Parliament.

Early life and career

Born in Swaledale, Yorkshire, the son of a lead-mine manager, Peacock was apprenticed at Fenton, Murray and Jackson in Leeds. He worked on the Leeds and Selby Railway and became locomotive superintendent of the Manchester and Leeds Railway and then of the Manchester, Sheffield and Lincolnshire Railway, where he supervised the construction of Gorton Foundry.

Beyer, Peacock and Company

In 1854 he founded Beyer, Peacock and Company with Charles Beyer at Gorton, Manchester. The firm became one of the world's leading builders of steam locomotives, exporting engines across the British Empire; its Garratt articulated locomotives, built from 1909, were used worldwide.

Politics

Peacock, a Unitarian and philanthropist, was elected MP for the Gorton division in the 1885 general election and held the seat until his death.
