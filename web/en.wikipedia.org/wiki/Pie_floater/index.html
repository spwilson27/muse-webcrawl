Pie floater

The pie floater is an Australian dish particularly associated with Adelaide, South Australia. It consists of a meat pie "floating" in a bowl of thick pea soup, usually topped with tomato sauce and sometimes mint sauce.

History

The dish dates to the pie carts that served Adelaide from the 1870s, where it became a late-night staple for workers, theatre-goers, and revellers. Variants served the pie inverted or partially submerged in the soup. In 2003 the pie floater was recognised as a South Australian heritage icon by the National Trust of Australia.

Preparation

The soup is made from blue boiler peas cooked until thick. A hot meat pie is placed in the soup and finished with a generous amount of tomato (ketchup) sauce. Pie carts in Adelaide, notably those outside the General Post Office and Adelaide railway station, were famous purveyors of the dish.
