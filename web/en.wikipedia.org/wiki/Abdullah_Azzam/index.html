Abdullah Yusuf Azzam (1941 – 24 November 1989) was a Palestinian Islamic scholar and militant who played a central role in organising Arab volunteers for the Afghan mujahideen during the Soviet–Afghan War. He co-founded the Maktab al-Khidamat (Services Bureau) in Peshawar in 1984 and was a mentor of Osama bin Laden.

Early life and education

Born in the village of Silat al-Harithiya near Jenin, Azzam studied agriculture in Jordan and later Islamic law, earning a doctorate from Al-Azhar University in Cairo in 1973. He taught at the University of Jordan until 1980, then moved to Saudi Arabia, where he lectured at King Abdulaziz University in Jeddah; among his students was Osama bin Laden.

Afghanistan

After the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan, Azzam issued the fatwa Defence of the Muslim Lands, declaring jihad in Afghanistan an individual duty for Muslims. From Peshawar he raised funds and recruited foreign fighters through the Maktab al-Khidamat and its magazine Al-Jihad, working alongside bin Laden. His writings, including Join the Caravan, framed participation in the Afghan war as a religious obligation.

Death and legacy

Azzam was assassinated by a car bomb in Peshawar on 24 November 1989, together with two of his sons; the perpetrators were never conclusively identified. After his death, bin Laden's faction developed the network that became al-Qaeda, and Azzam's ideas continued to influence global jihadist movements, while also being debated and criticised by other Islamists.
