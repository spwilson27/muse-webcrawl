Establishing shot

An establishing shot in filmmaking and television production sets up, or establishes, the context for a scene by showing the relationship between its important figures and objects. It is generally a long or extreme-long shot at the beginning of a scene indicating where, and sometimes when, the remainder of the scene takes place.

Establishing shots were more common during the classical era of filmmaking than they are now, when the expense of a shot that adds little narrative information has led filmmakers to omit them or to compress them into very brief images. Nevertheless, they remain a standard tool for orienting the audience in space and time.

Use

An establishing shot might show a city skyline, a sign identifying a location, or the exterior of a building before cutting to action inside it. Aerial photography and drone footage are frequently used for modern establishing shots because they can survey a large area quickly and spectacularly. In serialised television, recurring establishing shots of familiar landmarks — such as the exterior of a courthouse or a police precinct — help viewers identify the setting instantly.

The establishing shot relates to the concept of the master shot, which records the entire action of a scene in a single take, and contrasts with the close-up, which isolates a detail or face.
