Chuuk State is one of the four states of the Federated States of Micronesia (FSM), located in the Caroline Islands of the western Pacific Ocean. It consists of several island groups, most importantly the atoll of Chuuk Lagoon (formerly Truk Lagoon), a large, sheltered lagoon ringed by a barrier reef and dotted with high volcanic islands, of which Weno (formerly Moen) hosts the state capital. The state's land area is only about 127 square kilometres, but it accounts for roughly half of the population of the FSM, with some 48,000 inhabitants, most speaking Chuukese.

History

The islands were first sighted by Europeans in the sixteenth century and came under Spanish, then German colonial rule. After the First World War, Japan administered Chuuk under a League of Nations mandate and developed Truk Lagoon into a major naval base — the "Gibraltar of the Pacific" — anchoring the Imperial Japanese Navy's Combined Fleet. In February 1944, the United States Navy's Operation Hailstone devastated the anchorage, sinking dozens of warships and auxiliaries and destroying hundreds of aircraft; the wrecks make the lagoon one of the world's most famous wreck-diving destinations, its "Ghost Fleet" lying in shallow, warm water.

After the Second World War Chuuk became part of the US-administered Trust Territory of the Pacific Islands. In 1979 it joined the other districts in forming the Federated States of Micronesia, which entered into a Compact of Free Association with the United States in 1986.

Geography and economy

Besides the lagoon islands, the state includes outlying atolls and island groups such as the Mortlock Islands and the Hall Islands. The economy rests on subsistence farming and fishing, copra, public-sector employment supported by Compact funding, tourism centred on wreck diving, and remittances from Chuukese living abroad.
