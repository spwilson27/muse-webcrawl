French Army

French Army personnel during the Bastille Day parade in Paris.

The French Army (French: Armée de terre, lit. "Land Army") is the land-based and largest component of the French Armed Forces. It is responsible to the Government of France, alongside the French Navy, the French Air and Space Force, and the National Gendarmerie. The Army has roughly 118,000 personnel, making it the largest land force in the European Union.

History

The French Army traces its origins to the standing armies created by Charles VII in the 15th century. It was the dominant land force of Europe under Louis XIV and Napoleon, whose Grande Armée conquered much of the continent during the Napoleonic Wars. The Army bore the brunt of French fighting in both World Wars, including the battles of Verdun and the Somme, and after 1945 fought colonial wars in Indochina and Algeria. Conscription was suspended in 1996 and the Army became fully professional by 2001.

Modern organisation and equipment

The Army is organised into combined-arms brigades under a single operational command (CFT). Principal equipment includes Leclerc main battle tanks, VBCI and Griffon armoured vehicles, CAESAR self-propelled howitzers, and Tiger attack helicopters. It maintains the French Foreign Legion and deploys forces in French overseas territories and on operations in Africa, the Middle East, and Eastern Europe.
