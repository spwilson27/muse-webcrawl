Leeds Cathedral, formally the Cathedral Church of St Anne, is a Roman Catholic cathedral in Leeds, West Yorkshire, England. It is the mother church of the Diocese of Leeds and the seat of the Bishop of Leeds. The cathedral stands in the city centre, on Great George Street, near Leeds Town Hall and the city's civic quarter. It should not be confused with Leeds Minster, the Anglican parish church dedicated to St Peter.

History

The first St Anne's church on the site was built in the 1830s to serve the growing Catholic population of industrial Leeds, much of it Irish in origin. When the Diocese of Leeds was erected in 1878, St Anne's became its cathedral. At the end of the nineteenth century the old building was found to be in poor condition — and, awkwardly, the city corporation needed part of the site for road widening — so the church was demolished and a new cathedral built on the remainder of the plot.

The present cathedral was designed by John Henry Eastwood (1843–1912), a Leeds-born architect, with construction beginning in 1901 and the building opening in 1904. Because the site was cramped and hemmed in by streets, Eastwood produced a comparatively compact church, with the sanctuary deliberately placed to one side of the usual axis. The building is executed in Weldon stone with details in an early Gothic style enriched by Arts and Crafts influences.

Architecture and restoration

The cathedral is a Grade II* listed building. Its most striking features include a tall, light interior with stone vaulting implied by plasterwork, an elaborately carved reredos, and a baptistery with a distinctive font. Following the liturgical reforms of the Second Vatican Council the sanctuary was reordered in the 1970s, not entirely sympathetically.

A major restoration programme was undertaken in the mid-2000s with support from the Heritage Lottery Fund. The works, completed in 2007, restored the interior decoration, installed new liturgical furnishings, created a new baptistery area, improved access, and returned the high altar toward the east end. The cathedral reopened to widespread praise for its warm, richly coloured interior scheme.

Music

Leeds Cathedral maintains a noted choral tradition. Its choirs — including boys', girls' and adult ensembles — sing for the weekly round of Masses and offices and form part of the Diocese of Leeds Schools Singing Programme, one of the largest church music education initiatives in the United Kingdom.
