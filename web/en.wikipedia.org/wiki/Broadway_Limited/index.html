The Broadway Limited was a passenger train operated by the Pennsylvania Railroad (PRR) and its successors between New York City and Chicago. Introduced in 1902 as the Pennsylvania Special and renamed in 1912, it was the Pennsylvania's flagship — an all-Pullman, extra-fare luxury train — and the principal rival of the New York Central Railroad's 20th Century Limited on the premier New York–Chicago route. Contrary to popular belief, its name referred not to theatre but to the "broad way" of the PRR's four-track main line across Pennsylvania.

History

The Pennsylvania Special began service on June 15, 1902, cutting the New York–Chicago journey to 20 hours; renamed Broadway Limited on November 24, 1912, the train was accelerated to 18 hours in the 1920s and to 16 hours, then 15½, as competition with the 20th Century Limited intensified. In June 1938 both trains were re-equipped with streamlined lightweight cars: the PRR train's cars were designed by Raymond Loewy in the "Fleet of Modernism" style, hauled by streamlined steam locomotives and GG1 electrics. Unlike the 20th Century, the Broadway retained its name and premium status through the diesel era, adding coaches only in 1952 as patronage declined. It was one of the last two all-Pullman trains in the United States. The Broadway outlived the Pennsylvania itself: after the 1968 Penn Central merger and takeover by Amtrak on May 1, 1971, it remained Amtrak's flagship New York–Chicago service via the former PRR main line. Amtrak discontinued the name on September 9, 1995, replacing the train with the Three Rivers, itself discontinued in 2005.

Services

In its prime the Broadway offered private rooms, a barber and valet, a train secretary, shower baths, and celebrated dining-car cuisine. In its Amtrak years the train carried sleeping cars, a dining car and coaches, with sections serving Washington, D.C. via the Port Road Branch at various times.
