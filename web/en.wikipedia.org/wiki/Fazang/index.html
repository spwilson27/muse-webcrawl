Fazang

Fazang (643–712) was a Chinese Buddhist monk and scholar, traditionally regarded as the third patriarch of the Huayan school of Mahayana Buddhism. Of Sogdian descent, he became an influential adviser at the court of Empress Wu Zetian.

Life and work

Fazang systematized Huayan doctrine, most famously in his classification of teachings (panjiao) and in the treatise On the Golden Lion, written to explain Huayan metaphysics to Empress Wu using the example of a golden lion statue: gold (principle, li) and lion (phenomena, shi) are mutually interpenetrating.

Doctrine

His works elaborate the Huayan views of interpenetration, the dharmadhatu, and the "six characteristics" of phenomena. Fazang also translated and commented on the Avatamsaka Sutra tradition, leaving a lasting influence on East Asian Buddhism.
