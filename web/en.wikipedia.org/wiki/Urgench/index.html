Urgench, regional capital of Khorezm

Urgench (Uzbek: Urganch) is a city in western Uzbekistan, the capital of the Xorazm (Khorezm) Region. It lies on the Amu Darya delta near the border with Turkmenistan and serves as the main transport gateway to the museum-city of Khiva, some 35 kilometres to the southwest, to which it is connected by one of the world's few intercity trolleybus lines.

History

The modern city should not be confused with its famous namesake, Old Urgench (Kunya-Urgench), the medieval capital of Khwarazm, whose ruins lie in present-day Turkmenistan. That city — known to medieval geographers as Gurganch — was one of the great trading cities of the Silk Road before its destruction by the Mongols under Genghis Khan in 1221, an event infamous for the diversion of the Amu Darya and mass slaughter of its inhabitants.

Present-day Urgench (originally called New Urgench, Yangi Urganch) was founded by Russians in 1643 as a trading post on the road to Khiva. After the Russian conquest of the Khanate of Khiva in 1873 the town grew as an administrative and cotton-trading centre. In the Soviet period it became the regional capital, a status it retained after Uzbek independence in 1991.

Geography and transport

Urgench stands on the Shavat canal in the irrigated oasis of the lower Amu Darya, surrounded by the Kyzylkum and Karakum deserts. The city has an airport with flights to Tashkent and other destinations, a railway station, and the trolleybus connection to Khiva (Ichan-Kala, a UNESCO World Heritage Site), making it the usual entry point for tourists visiting Khorezm.
