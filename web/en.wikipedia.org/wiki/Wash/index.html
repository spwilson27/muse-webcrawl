The Wash

The Wash is a broad, roughly rectangular estuary and bay on the east coast of England, where the counties of Norfolk and Lincolnshire meet the North Sea. It is one of the largest estuaries in the United Kingdom, fed by the rivers Witham, Welland, Nene and Great Ouse, and is bordered by the ports of King's Lynn, Boston and Skegness.

Geography and wildlife

The Wash is characterized by extensive intertidal sandbanks and mudflats, salt marshes, and deep channels. It is of international importance for wildlife: hundreds of thousands of wading birds and wildfowl overwinter on its mudflats, and it supports one of England's largest colonies of common seals. Much of the shore is protected as a national nature reserve and a Site of Special Scientific Interest.

History

According to legend, King John lost part of his baggage train — in some versions including the Crown Jewels — to the incoming tide while crossing the Wash in 1216. The surrounding Fens were progressively drained and reclaimed from the Middle Ages onward. In modern times the Wash has been studied for tidal barrage and water-storage schemes, none of which has been built. RAF bombing ranges occupy parts of its shores, and the estuary supports important shellfisheries for cockles, mussels and shrimp.
