Country rock

Country rock is a genre of music which fuses rock and country music. It developed in the late 1960s when rock musicians, influenced by country and honky-tonk traditions, began incorporating pedal steel guitars, close vocal harmonies and country songwriting into rock arrangements.

History

The genre emerged as artists such as Bob Dylan explored country sounds on albums like John Wesley Harding (1967) and Nashville Skyline (1969). The Byrds, joined by Gram Parsons, released Sweetheart of the Rodeo (1968), widely regarded as the first major country rock album. Parsons went on to form the Flying Burrito Brothers with Chris Hillman.

In the 1970s country rock reached its commercial peak with the Eagles, whose blend of country harmonies and rock production produced some of the best-selling albums of all time, alongside acts such as Poco, Linda Ronstadt, the Nitty Gritty Dirt Band and Pure Prairie League. The genre influenced Southern rock, heartland rock and, later, the alternative country movement of the 1990s.

Characteristics

Country rock typically features twangy lead guitar, pedal steel, fiddle or banjo alongside a standard rock rhythm section, multi-part vocal harmonies, and lyrics dealing with rural life, love and loss.
