Hook length formula

Hook length formula
A Young diagram with the hook length of each cell
FieldAlgebraic combinatorics, representation theory
Proved byJ. S. Frame, G. de B. Robinson, R. M. Thrall (1954)

In combinatorics, the hook length formula is a formula for the number of standard Young tableaux whose shape is a given Young diagram. It has applications to representation theory: it gives the dimension of the irreducible representation of the symmetric group Sn corresponding to a partition of n, and it also computes dimensions of irreducible representations of the general linear group.

Statement

For a cell of a Young diagram, its hook consists of the cell itself, the cells to its right in the same row, and the cells below it in the same column; the hook length is the number of cells in the hook. If λ is a partition of n and fλ is the number of standard Young tableaux of shape λ, then:

fλ = n! / ∏ (hook lengths of all cells of λ)

For example, for the shape (3,2) the hook lengths are 4, 3, 1 in the first row and 2, 1 in the second, giving 5!/(4·3·1·2·1) = 120/24 = 5 standard tableaux.

History

The formula was found in 1954 by Frame, Robinson, and Thrall by inspecting cases, and it long lacked a proof felt to be "combinatorial" in spirit. Greene, Nijenhuis, and Wilf (1979) gave a celebrated probabilistic proof using a random "hook walk" and showed that a uniformly random insertion process reaches each standard tableau with equal probability.

Related results

The hook-content formula generalises the result to semistandard tableaux, and the formula is a special case of determinant and product formulas arising from the representation theory of the symmetric and general linear groups, such as the Weyl dimension formula.
