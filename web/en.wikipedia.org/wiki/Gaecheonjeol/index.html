Gaecheonjeol (Korean: 개천절; Hanja: 開天節; lit. 'Festival of the Opening of Heaven'; [kɛtɕʰʌndʑʌl]) is a public holiday in South Korea on 3 October. Also known by the English name National Foundation Day, this holiday celebrates the legendary formation of the first Korean state of Gojoseon in 2333 BC. This date has traditionally been regarded as the date for the founding of the Korean people by the god-king Dangun.

Gaecheonjeol is also recognised and celebrated in North Korea, although it is not a public holiday there; the North marks the legend at the Tomb of King Tongmyong and the Mausoleum of Tangun near Pyongyang.

Etymology and legend

Gaecheon ("Opening of Heaven") refers to 3 October 2457 BC, the date when Hwanung, son of Hwanin (the Lord of Heaven), descended to Earth to live with mankind. According to the founding myth, Hwanung descended to the summit of Mount Taebaek with 3,000 followers, establishing the "Divine City". His son Dangun Wanggeom later founded Gojoseon — the first Korean kingdom — in 2333 BC.

History

The celebration of the founding legend predates the modern holiday: Daejongists and other followers of Daejonggyo, the religion worshipping Dangun, marked the date on the lunar calendar. In 1909, Gaecheonjeol was established as a national holiday by the Korean government-in-exile in Shanghai. After the establishment of the Republic of Korea in 1948, it was fixed to 3 October of the Gregorian calendar and made an official public holiday in 1949.

Celebration

An official ceremony is held at the Sejong Center for the Performing Arts in Seoul, attended by political leaders. Households and public buildings fly the South Korean national flag, the Taegukgi. Activities associated with the day include memorial rites at Dangun shrines and the distribution of songpyeon and other traditional foods in some communities.
