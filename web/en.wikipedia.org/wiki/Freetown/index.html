Freetown is the capital and largest city of Sierra Leone. It is a major port city on the Atlantic Ocean and is located in the Western Area of Sierra Leone. Freetown is Sierra Leone's major urban, economic, financial, and cultural centre, as well as the seat of the Government of Sierra Leone.

History

Freetown was founded on 11 March 1792 by the Sierra Leone Company as a settlement for freed African American, Afro-Caribbean, and Liberated African slaves, following an earlier "Province of Freedom" settlement at Granville Town in 1787. Its settlers — the Nova Scotians, Maroons from Jamaica, and later recaptives freed from slave ships by the Royal Navy — formed the Krio people, whose Krio language became the lingua franca of Sierra Leone. The city became a British crown colony in 1808 and the base of the West Africa Squadron suppressing the Atlantic slave trade.

During the Sierra Leone Civil War (1991–2002), Freetown was attacked and occupied by Revolutionary United Front rebels in January 1999 before being retaken with Nigerian-led ECOMOG and later British support. The city also suffered severely in the West African Ebola epidemic of 2014–2016 and in the 2017 mudslides, which killed more than a thousand people on the city's outskirts.

Geography and landmarks

Freetown sits on a peninsula backed by forested hills at the mouth of the Sierra Leone River estuary, forming one of the world's largest natural harbours. For centuries its most famous landmark was a giant cotton tree under which the first settlers prayed; the centuries-old tree was toppled by a storm in May 2023. Other landmarks include the Law Courts, the National Museum, Fourah Bay College (founded 1827, the first Western-style university in sub-Saharan Africa), and the beaches of Lumley.
