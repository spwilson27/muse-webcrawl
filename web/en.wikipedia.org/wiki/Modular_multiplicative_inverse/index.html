Modular multiplicative inverse

In mathematics, particularly in the area of modular arithmetic, the modular multiplicative inverse of an integer a modulo m is an integer x such that ax ≡ 1 (mod m). It is the analogue, within modular arithmetic, of the reciprocal 1/a in ordinary arithmetic.

Existence

The inverse exists if and only if a and m are coprime — that is, gcd(a, m) = 1. When it exists, it is unique modulo m. If m is prime, every non-zero residue has an inverse, and by Fermat's little theorem it can be computed as am−2 mod m.

Computation

The standard method is the extended Euclidean algorithm, which finds integers x and y satisfying Bézout's identity ax + my = 1; reducing modulo m shows that x is the inverse of a. Euler's theorem gives another formula using Euler's totient function when the numbers are coprime.

Applications

Modular inverses are fundamental in public-key cryptography: in the RSA cryptosystem, the private decryption exponent is computed as the modular inverse of the public exponent modulo a totient. They are also used in solving linear congruences and in the Chinese remainder theorem.
