Althaea (plant)

Scientific classification
KingdomPlantae
OrderMalvales
FamilyMalvaceae
SubfamilyMalvoideae
GenusAlthaea L.

Althaea is a genus of herbaceous perennial plants native to Europe, North Africa and western Asia. It includes Althaea officinalis, also known as the marsh mallow (or marshmallow), from whose mucilaginous root the original confection of that name was made. The genus belongs to the mallow family, Malvaceae, which also includes hibiscus, cotton and okra.

Althaea species are erect herbs with softly hairy, lobed leaves and five-petalled flowers in shades of white, pink or lilac, arranged in the leaf axils or in terminal clusters. Depending on the taxonomic treatment, the genus contains roughly nine to twelve accepted species; some species formerly placed here, including the hollyhock, are now assigned to the related genus Alcea.

Description

Plants of the genus are generally hairy with star-shaped (stellate) hairs, a hallmark of the mallow family. Their flowers have an epicalyx of several bracts beneath the calyx, and the fruit is a schizocarp that splits into many one-seeded segments. They typically grow in damp places — salt marshes, riverbanks and wet meadows — which is reflected in the marsh mallow's common name.

Species

Accepted species include Althaea officinalis (marsh mallow), A. cannabina (palm-leaf marshmallow), A. hirsuta (hairy marshmallow), A. armeniaca and A. broussonetiifolia. The generic name derives from the Greek altho, "to heal", in reference to the plants' long-standing medicinal reputation.

Uses

The root of A. officinalis yields a soothing mucilage used since ancient Egyptian and Greek times for coughs and sore throats, and beaten with sugar and egg white it formed the pâté de guimauve, the forerunner of the modern marshmallow sweet — though today's confection is made with gelatin and starch instead. Leaves and flowers are also used in herbal medicine as a demulcent.
