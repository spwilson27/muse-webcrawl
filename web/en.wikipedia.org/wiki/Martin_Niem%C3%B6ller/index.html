Martin Niemöller (14 January 1892 – 6 March 1984) was a German Lutheran pastor and theologian. A former U-boat commander and nationalist who initially welcomed aspects of the Nazi rise, he became a leading figure in the Confessing Church's resistance and was imprisoned in concentration camps from 1937 to 1945. He is famous for the postwar quotation 'First they came for …'.

Resistance and imprisonment

Niemöller co-founded the Pastors' Emergency League in 1933 to oppose Nazi interference in the Protestant churches. Arrested in 1937, he was held in Sachsenhausen and Dachau as Hitler's personal prisoner until liberated in 1945.

Postwar life

After the war Niemöller helped draft the Stuttgart Declaration of Guilt and became a prominent pacifist and critic of nuclear weapons and the Vietnam War. His confessional poem about the failure to speak out against persecution became one of the most widely quoted texts of the twentieth century.
