Guido d'Arezzo
Born: c. 991, Arezzo (or Pomposa), Italy
Died: after 1033
Occupation: Benedictine monk, music theorist

Guido d'Arezzo (c. 991 – after 1033) was an Italian Benedictine monk and music theorist of the Medieval era. He is regarded as the inventor, or by some the codifier, of modern staff notation, which replaced neumatic notation and revolutionized how music was taught, learned, and transcribed.

Life and works

Guido was a monk at the abbey of Pomposa and later taught singing at Arezzo cathedral. His treatise Micrologus (c. 1026) set out his system of a four-line staff with lines and spaces representing definite pitches, using letters and colours to identify F and C. Pope John XIX summoned him to Rome to demonstrate his method.

Solmization

Guido is credited with the solmization syllables ut–re–mi–fa–sol–la, derived from the initial syllables of successive lines of the hymn "Ut queant laxis" in honour of John the Baptist. The mnemonic "Guidonian hand" mapped notes onto the joints of the hand. His innovations allowed singers to sight-read unknown chants, drastically shortening training.
