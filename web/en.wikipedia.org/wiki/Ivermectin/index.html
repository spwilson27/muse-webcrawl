Ivermectin

[Image: Skeletal formula of ivermectin B1a]

RoutesOral, topical, veterinary formulations

Ivermectin is a medication used to treat many types of parasite infestations. It is a derivative of the avermectins, natural products of the bacterium Streptomyces avermitilis discovered in the 1970s by Satoshi Ōmura's group in collaboration with Merck; Ōmura and William C. Campbell shared part of the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for its development.

Medical uses

In humans, ivermectin is approved for intestinal strongyloidiasis and onchocerciasis (river blindness) taken orally, and topically for rosacea and head lice; it is also used off-label for scabies. Mass drug administration programmes using donated ivermectin (Mectizan) dramatically reduced river blindness and lymphatic filariasis across Africa and Latin America. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Mechanism

Ivermectin binds to glutamate-gated chloride channels in invertebrate nerve and muscle cells, increasing membrane permeability to chloride ions, causing paralysis and death of the parasite. Mammals are largely unaffected at therapeutic doses because the channels are absent and the drug does not readily cross the blood–brain barrier.

Veterinary use

The drug is very widely used in livestock and companion animals for heartworm prevention and treatment of mites and gastrointestinal worms.

COVID-19 controversy

During the COVID-19 pandemic, ivermectin was widely promoted as a treatment despite clinical-trial evidence failing to show benefit; health authorities including the FDA, EMA and WHO recommended against its use for COVID-19 outside trials.
