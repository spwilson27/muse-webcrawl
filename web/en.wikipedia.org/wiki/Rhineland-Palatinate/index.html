Rhineland-Palatinate

Rhineland-Palatinate (German: Rheinland-Pfalz) is a western state of Germany. It covers an area of 19,846 square kilometres (7,663 sq mi) and has a population of about 4.1 million, making it the seventh-largest German state by area and the sixth-most populous. Its capital and largest city is Mainz. Other large cities include Ludwigshafen, Koblenz, Trier, Kaiserslautern and Worms. It is bordered by the German states of North Rhine-Westphalia, Hesse, Baden-Württemberg and the Saarland, and by France, Luxembourg and Belgium.

History

The state was established on 30 August 1946 by ordinance No. 57 of the French military government in the aftermath of the Second World War. It was formed from the northern part of the French occupation zone, combining the historic regions of the Rhineland (the southern districts of the Prussian Rhine Province), the Bavarian Palatinate and parts of the People's State of Hesse. A referendum held in May 1947 approved the state's constitution, and Franz Altmeier became its first minister-president.

The region itself has a long history: Trier, founded by the Romans as Augusta Treverorum, is often described as Germany's oldest city, and the cathedrals of Speyer, Worms and Mainz testify to the importance of the Rhine corridor in the Holy Roman Empire.

Geography

Rhineland-Palatinate is characterised by the Rhine valley and the uplands of the Rhine Slate Mountains, including the Eifel, Hunsrück, Westerwald and Taunus ranges, divided by the Rhine and its tributaries the Moselle, Lahn and Nahe. The Upper Middle Rhine Valley, between Bingen and Koblenz, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site famous for its castles, steep vineyards and the Lorelei rock. The highest peak is the Erbeskopf (816 m) in the Hunsrück. The Palatinate Forest (Pfälzerwald) in the south is the largest contiguous forest area in Germany.

Economy

The state is a centre of the German chemical industry: Ludwigshafen hosts the headquarters of BASF, the world's largest chemical company. Wine growing is prominent along the Rhine and Moselle; Rhineland-Palatinate's six wine regions (Ahr, Mittelrhein, Moselle, Nahe, Rheinhessen and Pfalz) produce the majority of German wine. Rheinhessen and the Pfalz are the two largest German wine-growing regions by vineyard area. Tourism, agriculture and the large American military presence around Ramstein Air Base and Kaiserslautern are also significant.

Politics

The state parliament is the Landtag of Rhineland-Palatinate, meeting in Mainz. Since 1991 the state has been led continuously by the Social Democratic Party (SPD), under minister-presidents Rudolf Scharping, Kurt Beck, Malu Dreyer and, since July 2024, Alexander Schweitzer.
