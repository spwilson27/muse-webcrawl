Paul Barras

Paul François Jean Nicolas, vicomte de Barras (30 June 1755 – 29 January 1829) was a French politician of the Revolutionary period and the dominant figure of the Directory regime that governed France from 1795 to 1799.

Revolutionary career

From a Provençal military family, Barras served as an officer in India before returning to France. Elected to the National Convention, he voted for the death of Louis XVI, helped suppress royalist and federalist revolts in the south — where he first patronised the young Napoleon Bonaparte — and played a key part in the Thermidorian Reaction that overthrew Robespierre in 1794. He commanded the forces defending the Convention against the royalist rising of 13 Vendémiaire (1795), again using Napoleon.

The Directory and fall

Barras was the only Director to serve continuously through the Directory's four years, notorious for corruption and for his salon presided over by his former lover Joséphine de Beauharnais, later Napoleon's wife. Napoleon's coup of 18 Brumaire (1799) forced his resignation, and he lived in internal exile and retirement under the Empire and Restoration.
