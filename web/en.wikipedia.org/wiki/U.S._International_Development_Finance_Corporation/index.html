U.S. International Development Finance Corporation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The U.S. International Development Finance Corporation (DFC) is the development finance institution of the United States federal government. It partners with the private sector to finance projects in low- and middle-income countries in sectors such as energy, healthcare, infrastructure, agriculture and financial services.

History

DFC was established by the BUILD Act of 2018 and began operations in December 2019, succeeding the Overseas Private Investment Corporation (OPIC) and absorbing the Development Credit Authority of USAID. The BUILD Act doubled its investment cap to $60 billion and gave it new tools, including equity investment authority and local-currency financing.

Tools and priorities

DFC provides debt financing, equity, political risk insurance, and technical assistance. Its portfolio spans dozens of countries, with strategic priorities including climate finance, health security, digital infrastructure, and supporting supply chains as an element of U.S. foreign policy and an alternative to rival development lenders.

Governance

The corporation is led by a chief executive officer and a board of directors chaired by the U.S. Secretary of State, with representation from other federal agencies and private-sector members.
