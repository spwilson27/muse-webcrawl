Richard Cameron (c. 1648 – 22 July 1680) was a Scottish preacher and leader of the radical Covenanters who opposed the attempts of Charles II and James VII to impose episcopacy and royal supremacy over the Church of Scotland. His followers, the Cameronians, formed the nucleus of the later Reformed Presbyterian Church, and a Scottish regiment later took the name Cameronians in his memory.

Life

Cameron was born at Falkland in Fife and worked as a schoolmaster. Initially inclined toward the episcopal settlement, he was converted to the Presbyterian cause by the field preachers (conventiclers) and was licensed to preach by the outlawed presbyterians around 1678. He preached at conventicles in the south-west of Scotland, denouncing the "indulged" Presbyterian ministers who had accepted royal licences as well as the government itself.

Sanquhar Declaration and death

In June 1680 Cameron rode into the burgh of Sanquhar with a small armed party and publicly read a declaration disowning Charles II as king, on the grounds of his tyranny and breach of the Covenants — an act of open rebellion. A price was placed on his head, and on 22 July 1680 he and a small band of followers were overtaken by a troop of dragoons under Bruce of Earlshall at Airds Moss in Ayrshire. Cameron was killed in the skirmish; his head and hands were cut off and displayed in Edinburgh.

Legacy

Cameron's uncompromising followers maintained a separate "United Societies" organisation, refusing allegiance to William of Orange after 1689 because he was an uncovenanted king. They eventually formed the Reformed Presbyterian Church. The Cameronians (Scottish Rifles) regiment, raised in 1689 from his followers, bore his name until its disbandment in 1968.
