Korean Wave

The Korean Wave (Korean: 한류, Hallyu) is the global increase in the popularity of South Korean culture since the 1990s, driven first by the spread of Korean television dramas (K-dramas) and popular music (K-pop) across East and Southeast Asia, and later, via the internet and streaming platforms, to a worldwide audience. The term was coined by journalists in China in the late 1990s.

Development

After the 1997 Asian financial crisis, South Korea lifted restrictions on cultural exchange and treated cultural exports as a strategic industry. Dramas such as Winter Sonata (2002) became sensations in Japan and beyond, while Jewel in the Palace (2003) aired in dozens of countries. In music, acts such as H.O.T., BoA and TVXQ built Asian fanbases in the 2000s; in the 2010s PSY's "Gangnam Style" (2012) became the first YouTube video to reach a billion views, and BTS and BLACKPINK achieved sustained chart success in the United States and Europe.

Film, food and beyond

Korean cinema won global acclaim with Bong Joon-ho's Parasite (2019), the first non-English-language film to win the Academy Award for Best Picture, and the Netflix series Squid Game (2021) became the platform's most-watched show. The Wave has expanded to Korean cuisine, beauty products (K-beauty), fashion, webtoons, video games and Korean-language study, and is actively promoted by the South Korean government as soft power, with measurable effects on tourism and exports.

Impact and criticism

Hallyu has been credited with improving South Korea's international image and generating billions of dollars in related exports. It has also provoked backlash at times — including Chinese restrictions after the 2016 THAAD missile-defence dispute — and debate about the idol industry's gruelling training system and the standardisation of its output.
