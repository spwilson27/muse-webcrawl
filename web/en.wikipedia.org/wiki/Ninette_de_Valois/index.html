Ninette de Valois

BornEdris Stannus, 6 June 1898, County Wicklow, Ireland
Died8 March 2001, London, England

Dame Ninette de Valois (born Edris Stannus; 6 June 1898 – 8 March 2001) was an Irish-born British dancer, teacher, choreographer, and director of classical ballet. She founded the Vic-Wells Ballet, which became The Royal Ballet, and its associated school, and is regarded as the founder of English ballet.

Early career

Stannus trained in London and adopted the stage name Ninette de Valois. From 1923 to 1925 she danced with Sergei Diaghilev's Ballets Russes. Returning to London, she opened the Academy of Choreographic Art in 1926, teaching pupils who formed the nucleus of her company. In 1931, in partnership with Lilian Baylis, she established the Vic-Wells Ballet at Sadler's Wells Theatre.

The Royal Ballet

De Valois built a repertory combining the 19th-century classics with new British works by choreographers such as Frederick Ashton, and trained successive generations of dancers including Margot Fonteyn and Rudolf Nureyev's generation of guests. The company received a Royal Charter in 1956, becoming The Royal Ballet, and moved to the Royal Opera House, Covent Garden. She retired as director in 1963 but remained an influential presence—"Madam" to everyone in the ballet world—until her death at the age of 102.
