Cura Annonae

Cura Annonae ("care for the grain supply") was the term used in ancient Rome for the importation and distribution of grain to the residents of the cities of Rome and, after its foundation, Constantinople. The term also came to describe the grain dole itself — the free or subsidized distribution of grain to poor citizens — and the administrative apparatus that managed it.

History

Rome grew far beyond the ability of its immediate hinterland to feed it, and from the Republic onward the city depended on grain imported by sea from Sicily, Sardinia, North Africa, and above all Egypt. Securing the supply became a central political concern: interruptions could spark riots, and politicians such as the Gracchi made subsidized grain a weapon of popular politics. In 123 BC, Gaius Gracchus passed a law providing grain to citizens at a fixed, below-market price. In 58 BC, Publius Clodius Pulcher made the distribution free.

Julius Caesar reduced the number of recipients, and Augustus regulated the system, eventually taking personal responsibility for the supply during shortages. Under the Empire, the praefectus annonae, an equestrian official, supervised the whole operation: the grain fleets, the warehouses (horrea) at Ostia and later Portus, and the monthly distributions in Rome.

The dole

Eligible male citizens received a monthly ration of about five modii of grain (roughly 33 kilograms), enough to form the basis of a family's diet when milled and baked into bread. Recipients were registered and issued tokens (tesserae frumentariae). At its height, perhaps 200,000–320,000 residents of Rome benefited. Under Aurelian in the 270s AD, the dole of unmilled grain was converted into distributions of baked bread, and pork and wine were added at various times.

Constantinople and decline

When Constantine founded Constantinople, he created a parallel annona for the new capital, supplied chiefly by Egypt. The system in Rome survived in reduced form into late antiquity; the loss of North Africa to the Vandals in the 5th century crippled it, and distributions faded during the Gothic Wars of the 6th century.
