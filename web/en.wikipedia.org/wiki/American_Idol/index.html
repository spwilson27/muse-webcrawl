American Idol is an American singing competition television series created by Simon Fuller, produced by Fremantle and 19 Entertainment. It aired on Fox from 11 June 2002 to 7 April 2016 for 15 seasons, before being revived by ABC in 2018. Hosted by Ryan Seacrest, the show searches for unsigned singing talent, with the winner chosen by viewers' votes and receiving a recording contract.

Format

Contestants audition before a panel of judges, with successful singers advancing to Hollywood rounds and then to live shows, where the public votes by phone, text and online. The original judging panel — record executive Simon Cowell, singer Paula Abdul and producer Randy Jackson — became famous for its chemistry and Cowell's blunt critiques.

Impact

At its mid-2000s peak, American Idol was the most-watched programme on American television for a record eight consecutive years. It launched the careers of Kelly Clarkson, Carrie Underwood, Jennifer Hudson, Chris Daughtry, Adam Lambert and others, and its format was exported around the world as part of the Idol franchise. It is also credited with popularising interactive television voting.

Revival

After Fox ended the show in 2016, ABC revived it in March 2018 with judges Katy Perry, Lionel Richie and Luke Bryan, with Seacrest returning as host. The revival has continued into subsequent seasons with some changes to the judging panel.
