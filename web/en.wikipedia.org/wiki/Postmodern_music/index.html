Postmodern music

Postmodern music is music following the modernist era, characterized by a pluralism of styles, quotation and borrowing from earlier music, irony, and the blurring of boundaries between "high" and "low" art and between popular and classical traditions.

Characteristics

Musicologists associate postmodernism in music with features such as eclecticism, polystylism, collage, the reuse of historical styles (sometimes called neo-tonality or new romanticism), scepticism toward grand narratives of progress, and an emphasis on surface, pastiche, and intertextuality. Composers such as Alfred Schnittke (with his "polystylism"), George Rochberg, and John Adams combined modernist techniques with tonal and popular materials. Sampling and remix culture in popular music are frequently cited as postmodern practices.

History

The term gained currency in the 1970s and 1980s as composers reacted against the perceived dogmatism of postwar serialism and the avant-garde. Minimalism, initially a modernist reaction, was later read as an early postmodern current. By the end of the twentieth century the "postmodern condition" in music was widely debated, with some critics arguing the term described the entire musical culture rather than a single style.

Criticism

Critics have questioned whether postmodernism, a concept developed in architecture and literary theory, maps cleanly onto music, and whether eclecticism alone distinguishes the period from earlier stylistic mixing.
