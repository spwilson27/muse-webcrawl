Leather carp

The leather carp is a domesticated variety of the common carp (Cyprinus carpio) characterized by having very few scales—typically a sparse row along the dorsal line and lateral line—or an almost completely smooth, leathery skin. It was selectively bred in Europe from scaled carp, in parallel with the mirror carp, whose scales are reduced to a few large, irregular plates.

Characteristics

Leather carp owe their reduced scalation to the same scale-cover genes that produce mirror carp; the fully naked "leather" form results from particular gene combinations historically selected because scaleless fish were easier to prepare for the table in Central European fish farming. They grow as large as other common carp, commonly reaching 5–15 kg in ponds and lakes.

Cultivation and angling

Leather carp are stocked in carp ponds in Germany, Poland, and the Czech Republic, and are popular with coarse anglers in Britain and continental Europe. Because of their sparse scales they can be more susceptible to skin damage and parasites, and careful handling is recommended in catch-and-release fishing.
