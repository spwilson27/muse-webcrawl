Osama bin Laden, the founder and first leader of the Islamist militant group al-Qaeda, was killed in Pakistan on 2 May 2011 (local time) by United States Navy SEALs of the Naval Special Warfare Development Group (DEVGRU) in Operation Neptune Spear, a covert raid authorised by President Barack Obama and conducted with the Central Intelligence Agency. Bin Laden had been hunted since the September 11 attacks of 2001, which he had ordered.

The hunt

Analysts at the CIA identified one of bin Laden's couriers, whose movements led them by 2010 to a large, unusually secure compound in Abbottabad, a garrison town near Pakistan's military academy. Surveillance of the compound and of a tall resident who never left it ("the Pacer") convinced officials the occupant was probably bin Laden. In early 2011 Obama authorised planning for a raid rather than a bombing, to confirm the identity of the target.

The raid

In the early hours of 2 May 2011, two stealth-modified Black Hawk helicopters carrying SEALs flew from Afghanistan into Pakistan. One helicopter clipped the compound wall and crash-landed, but the assault force completed the mission: the courier and his brother, a son of bin Laden, and a woman were killed, and bin Laden was shot dead on the third floor of the main house. The raid lasted about 40 minutes. No American personnel were killed. The wrecked helicopter was destroyed by the SEALs before extraction.

Aftermath

Bin Laden's body was flown to Afghanistan, identified by DNA comparison, and buried at sea within 24 hours in accordance with Islamic practice, aboard USS Carl Vinson in the North Arabian Sea. President Obama announced the death on US television late on 1 May (Washington time), prompting celebrations in the United States. The raid sharply strained US–Pakistan relations and raised debates over the legality of the targeted killing and the non-disclosure of photographs.
