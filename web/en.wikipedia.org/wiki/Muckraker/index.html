Muckraker

[Image: Covers of McClure's Magazine from its muckraking heyday]

Muckrakers were American investigative journalists of the Progressive Era (roughly 1900–1912) who exposed corruption, corporate abuses and social ills in mass magazines such as McClure's. The name comes from Theodore Roosevelt's 1906 speech invoking Bunyan's 'man with the muck rake' — half criticism, half badge of honour.

Figures and impact

Ida Tarbell's dismantling of Standard Oil, Lincoln Steffens' city corruption series, Upton Sinclair's The Jungle (1906) and Ida B. Wells' earlier anti-lynching journalism fed directly into reforms: antitrust enforcement, food and drug laws, and municipal change. The style was revived by later investigative traditions.
