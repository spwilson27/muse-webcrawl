Marc Russell Benioff (born 25 September 1964) is an American internet entrepreneur and philanthropist. He is the co-founder, chairman and chief executive officer of Salesforce, the cloud computing company that pioneered software as a service for customer relationship management, and since 2018 the owner of Time magazine.

Early life and career

Benioff was raised in the San Francisco Bay Area and sold his first software, for the Atari 8-bit computer, as a teenager; he founded Liberty Software at age 15. After graduating from the University of Southern California in 1986, he joined Oracle Corporation, where he spent 13 years and became the company's youngest vice president, working directly with Larry Ellison.

Salesforce

In 1999 Benioff co-founded Salesforce.com in a rented San Francisco apartment, championing "the end of software" — business applications delivered over the internet by subscription. The company grew into one of the world's largest enterprise-software firms, acquiring companies including Tableau, Slack (for $27.7 billion, completed 2021) and MuleSoft. Benioff promotes the "1-1-1 model" of corporate philanthropy, donating 1 percent of equity, product and employee time, an approach formalised through the Salesforce Foundation and the Pledge 1% movement.

Other activities

Benioff and his wife Lynne bought Time magazine for $190 million in 2018. He has been a major donor to children's hospitals in San Francisco and Hawaii, to ocean conservation, and to efforts addressing homelessness in San Francisco, while also drawing criticism for his support of a business tax to fund homeless services and, at times, for his prominence in Salesforce Tower-dominated San Francisco politics.
