Commonwealth troops on the hills above the Kapyong River, April 1951.

The Battle of Kapyong (Korean: 가평전투), fought from 22 to 25 April 1951 during the Korean War, was a defensive action in which troops of the 27th British Commonwealth Brigade helped blunt a major Chinese offensive aimed at Seoul. The stand of the 3rd Battalion, Royal Australian Regiment (3 RAR), on Hill 504 and of the 2nd Battalion, Princess Patricia's Canadian Light Infantry (2 PPCLI), on Hill 677 became two of the most celebrated actions fought by Australian and Canadian forces in the war, and both battalions, along with the United States 72nd Heavy Tank Battalion's A Company, received United States Presidential Unit Citations.

Background

In April 1951 the Chinese People's Volunteer Army launched its Fifth Phase Offensive, the largest Communist attack of the war, intending to recapture Seoul. In the Kapyong valley, northeast of the capital, the attacking Chinese 118th Division shattered the South Korean 6th Division, opening a gap. The 27th British Commonwealth Brigade — 3 RAR, 2 PPCLI, the 1st Battalion Middlesex Regiment, supported by New Zealand artillery of the 16th Field Regiment and American tanks — was rushed into blocking positions astride the valley as the last reserve of the US IX Corps.

The battle

On the night of 23 April, Chinese forces attacked the Australians on Hill 504. In fierce close-quarters fighting, including bayonet charges, 3 RAR held through the night but was forced to withdraw on 24 April after ammunition ran low and the position became untenable; Australian casualties were 32 killed and 59 wounded. The Chinese then turned on the Canadians dug in on Hill 677. Surrounded and cut off, 2 PPCLI repelled repeated assaults through the night of 24–25 April; at one point the battalion called down New Zealand artillery fire directly on its own positions to break up attacks forming among its trenches. Airdrops replenished Canadian ammunition, and by dawn on 25 April — Anzac Day — the Chinese had withdrawn, their offensive in the valley spent. Canadian losses were 10 killed and 23 wounded.

Aftermath and legacy

The Kapyong stand, together with the concurrent British stand at the Imjin River, prevented the Chinese from breaking through to Seoul on that axis and is credited by historians with helping to stall the entire offensive. The battle is commemorated in Australia and Canada as an exemplar of small-unit defensive fighting: the Kapyong Battle Commemorative monument stands in the valley, both battalions carry the battle honour "Kapyong", and the action is remembered annually by the veterans' associations of both countries.
