Kaladan River

The Kaladan River (Burmese: ကုလားတန်မြစ်), known upstream in India as the Chhimtuipui River, is a river in eastern India and western Myanmar. It rises in the Chin Hills of Mizoram, flows southward through Myanmar's Chin State and Rakhine State, and empties into the Bay of Bengal at Sittwe. The river is about 450 kilometres long.

Course

Originating in the mountains of Mizoram near the Myanmar border, the river flows south into Myanmar, where it is joined by tributaries such as the Tiau and Tuipui rivers. It forms part of the border between Chin State and Rakhine State for a stretch before reaching its estuary at Sittwe, the capital of Rakhine State.

Kaladan Multi-Modal Transit Transport Project

The river is central to the Kaladan Multi-Modal Transit Transport Project, an India–Myanmar initiative intended to connect the Indian port of Kolkata with Sittwe seaport, then via the Kaladan River to Paletwa, and by road to Mizoram. The project aims to provide India's landlocked northeastern states with an alternative route to the sea, bypassing the narrow Siliguri Corridor.
