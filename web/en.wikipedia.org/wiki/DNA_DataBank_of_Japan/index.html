The DNA DataBank of Japan (DDBJ) is a biological database that collects DNA sequences. It is located at the National Institute of Genetics (NIG) in the Shizuoka prefecture of Japan. It is a member of the International Nucleotide Sequence Database Collaboration (INSDC) together with the European Nucleotide Archive (ENA) at the European Bioinformatics Institute and GenBank at the NCBI in the United States; the three databases exchange data daily, so that a sequence submitted to any one of them appears in all three.

The National Institute of Genetics, home of DDBJ.

History

DDBJ began its activities in 1986 at the National Institute of Genetics and has operated as Japan's national nucleotide sequence archive since then. It was established in response to the growing international effort to share DNA sequence data publicly, joining the collaboration that had been formed earlier in the decade by EMBL's database in Europe and GenBank in the United States.

Activities

DDBJ accepts submissions of nucleotide sequences from researchers — principally, though not exclusively, in Japan — issues accession numbers, and provides data retrieval, search and analysis services. It operates the DDBJ Sequence Read Archive (DRA) for high-throughput sequencing data, the BioProject and BioSample databases in coordination with INSDC partners, and the Japanese Genotype-phenotype Archive (JGA) for controlled-access personal genome data. DDBJ also provides pipeline and cloud analysis services (such as the DDBJ Pipeline and its successor services) and runs the NIG supercomputer for large-scale sequence analysis.
