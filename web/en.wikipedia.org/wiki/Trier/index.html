Trier is a city on the banks of the Moselle in Germany. It lies in a valley between low vine-covered hills in the west of the state of Rhineland-Palatinate, near the border with Luxembourg. Founded by the Romans in late antiquity, Trier claims to be Germany's oldest city, dating to around 16 BC as Augusta Treverorum.

History

Under the Roman Empire, Trier grew into a major city and, during the Tetrarchy, became an imperial residence—sometimes called the "second Rome". Its Roman monuments include the Porta Nigra gate, the Imperial Baths, the Aula Palatina basilica and an amphitheatre. In the Middle Ages the Archbishops of Trier were powerful prince-electors of the Holy Roman Empire.

Notable people

Trier is the birthplace of Karl Marx (1818), whose childhood home is now a museum, and of Saint Ambrose of Milan according to some traditions.

Sights

The Roman Monuments, the Cathedral of St Peter and the Church of Our Lady were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1986. The Hauptmarkt, the Electoral Palace and the Karl Marx House are also major attractions.
