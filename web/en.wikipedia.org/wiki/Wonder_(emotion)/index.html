Wonder (emotion)

Wonder is an emotion comparable to surprise that people feel when perceiving something rare or unexpected—especially something inexplicably beautiful or new. It has historically been seen as an important aspect of human nature, linked specifically with curiosity and the drive behind intellectual exploration. Wonder is also often compared to the emotion of awe, but awe implies fear or respect rather than joy.

In philosophy

Plato and Aristotle both held that philosophy begins in wonder (thaumazein): it is the experience of astonishment at the world that prompts inquiry. In the early modern period, René Descartes listed wonder (admiration) as the first of the passions in his Passions of the Soul, describing it as the soul's sudden surprise that disposes us to attend to and remember novel objects.

In psychology and science

Modern psychology treats wonder as a response to vastness or novelty that challenges existing mental schemas, often prompting "accommodation"—the revision of one's understanding. Wonder is associated with curiosity, learning and creativity, and is studied alongside awe in research on self-transcendent emotions. Natural phenomena such as eclipses, the night sky and childbirth are classic triggers of wonder.

In religion and culture

Wonder features prominently in religious experience, where it may be directed at the divine or the miraculous. In literature and art, a sense of wonder is a defining quality attributed to fantasy and science fiction, whose readers are said to seek the pleasurable astonishment of imagined worlds.
