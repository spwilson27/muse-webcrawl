Brighton Beach

Brighton Beach is a neighbourhood in the southern part of the New York City borough of Brooklyn, on the Coney Island peninsula facing the Atlantic Ocean. It is bounded by Coney Island to the west, Manhattan Beach to the east and Gravesend Bay to the north. The neighbourhood is famous for its large community of Russian-speaking immigrants, earning it the nicknames "Little Odessa" and "Little Russia".

History

Brighton Beach developed as a beach resort in the late 19th century, named after the English seaside town of Brighton, with grand hotels served by rail from the rest of Brooklyn. After the Second World War it became home to many Jewish survivors and refugees; from the 1970s, and especially after the fall of the Soviet Union, large numbers of immigrants from Ukraine, Russia and other former Soviet republics settled there.

Culture

Brighton Beach Avenue, beneath the elevated B and Q subway lines, is lined with Russian and Ukrainian shops, bakeries, restaurants and theatres. The neighbourhood is the setting of Neil Simon's play Brighton Beach Memoirs and has featured in many films and television series. Its beach and boardwalk, shared with Coney Island, draw crowds throughout the summer.
