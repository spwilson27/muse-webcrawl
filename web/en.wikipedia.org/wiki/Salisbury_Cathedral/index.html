Salisbury Cathedral, formally the Cathedral Church of Saint Mary, is an Anglican cathedral in Salisbury, Wiltshire, England. Built almost entirely within a single generation, from 1220 to 1258, it is a masterpiece of the Early English Gothic style, notable for the unity of its design and for its soaring spire, at 123 metres (404 ft) the tallest church spire in the United Kingdom. The cathedral also houses the best surviving of the four original copies of Magna Carta and the world's oldest working mechanical clock, dated to about 1386.

History

The cathedral was founded when the see was transferred from Old Sarum, an exposed hilltop fortress site with a cramped Norman cathedral, to the new city laid out in the valley below under Bishop Richard Poore. Foundation stones were laid on 28 April 1220, and the main body was completed and consecrated in 1258 — remarkable speed that accounts for its stylistic consistency. The cloisters and octagonal chapter house followed, and the great spire was added around 1320, raised on a slender crossing tower that visibly strained under the load: later centuries added tie rods, including works supervised by Christopher Wren, who surveyed the spire in 1668.

The cathedral's treasures survived the Reformation, Civil War (during which the close was attacked), and Victorian restoration, including the controversial interventions of James Wyatt in the 1790s and the more sympathetic work of George Gilbert Scott.

Architecture and treasures

The cathedral is set in the largest cathedral close in England, an 80-acre walled precinct of lawns and historic houses. Unusually, its cruciform plan and skyline are best appreciated from outside, across the water meadows of the River Avon — the view immortalised in John Constable's Salisbury Cathedral from the Bishop's Garden and related paintings.

  • The spire rests on a tower built for a much smaller load; its piers bend visibly, yet it has stood since the 14th century.
  • The clock, dating to about 1386, is the oldest surviving working mechanical clock; rediscovered in 1929, it has neither face nor hands and strikes the hours on a bell.
  • Magna Carta: Salisbury's copy of the 1215 charter, brought to the cathedral in the Middle Ages, is displayed in the chapter house, whose walls carry a carved frieze of scenes from Genesis and Exodus.
  • The Prisoners of Conscience window (1980) and other modern stained glass complement medieval glass survivals.
  • The Trinity Chapel contains the tomb of St Osmund, canonised in 1457, the last English cathedral saint.
