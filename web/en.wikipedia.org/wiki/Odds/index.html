Odds

Odds express the ratio of the probability that an event occurs to the probability that it does not.

In probability theory, odds provide a measure of the likelihood of a particular outcome. They are calculated as the ratio of the number of events that produce the outcome to the number that do not. For an event with probability p, the odds are p / (1 − p).

Examples

Rolling a three with a fair six-sided die has probability 1/6. The odds in favour are therefore (1/6) / (5/6) = 1/5, expressed as "1 to 5" or "5 to 1 against". An event certain to happen has infinite odds; an impossible event has odds of zero.

Gambling usage

In gambling, odds are the ratio used to determine payouts on bets. Common formats include fractional odds (e.g. 5/1, traditional in the United Kingdom), decimal odds (e.g. 6.0, common in Europe and Australia), and moneyline odds (common in the United States). Bookmakers set odds that include a margin (the overround) so that the implied probabilities sum to more than 100 percent.

Statistical usage

The odds ratio compares the odds of an event in two groups and is widely used in epidemiology and logistic regression, where the natural logarithm of the odds (the logit) is modelled as a linear function of explanatory variables. In Bayesian statistics, posterior odds equal prior odds multiplied by the Bayes factor.
