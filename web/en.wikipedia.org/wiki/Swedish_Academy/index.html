Swedish Academy

The Swedish Academy (Swedish: Svenska Akademien), founded in 1786 by King Gustav III, is one of the Royal Academies of Sweden. Its 18 members — known as "the Eighteen" — are elected for life and include writers, linguists, and historians. The Academy works to promote the Swedish language and literature, publishes the authoritative dictionary Svenska Akademiens ordbok and the word list SAOL, and is best known internationally for selecting the laureate of the Nobel Prize in Literature each year.

The Swedish Academy meets in the Börshuset (Stock Exchange Building) in Stockholm.

History

Gustav III modelled the Academy on the Académie française, charging it with cultivating the purity, vigour, and majesty of the Swedish language. Its statutes, said to have been written by the king himself, fixed the membership at eighteen. In 1901 the Academy was entrusted, under Alfred Nobel's will, with awarding the Nobel Prize in Literature, a task it performs through its Nobel Committee. The Academy also awards numerous other prizes, including the Dobloug Prize and the Nordic Prize.

2018 crisis

In 2017–2018 the Academy was engulfed in scandal when Jean-Claude Arnault, husband of member Katarina Frostenson, was accused of sexual assault and of leaking Nobel laureates' names; he was later convicted of rape. Disputes over his wife's position led to a wave of resignations and withdrawals, leaving the Academy unable to reach quorum to elect new members until the king amended the statutes. The 2018 Nobel Prize in Literature was postponed — two prizes were awarded in 2019 — and the Academy underwent significant reform and renewal of its membership.
