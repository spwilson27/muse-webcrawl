Beemster (Dutch pronunciation: [ˈbeːmstər]) is a former municipality in the Netherlands, in the province of North Holland. On 1 January 2022 it merged into the municipality of Purmerend. The name also refers to the famous Beemster polder, the first polder in the Netherlands reclaimed from a lake — the Beemster Lake — using windmills, between 1609 and 1612.

History

Around 800 AD the area was covered with peat. The name Beemster derives from "Bamestra", the name of a small river in the area. Storms between 1150 and 1250 enlarged the inland lake until, in the early 17th century, Amsterdam merchants led by Dirck van Oss, with the engineer Jan Adriaanszoon Leeghwater among those involved in its drainage, invested in reclaiming it. Drainage was completed in 1612, producing about 7,200 hectares of fertile land laid out in a rational Renaissance grid of roads and canals.

The well-preserved polder landscape was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1999 as the "Droogmakerij de Beemster (Beemster Polder)", recognised as a masterpiece of Dutch land reclamation. Villages in the former municipality include Middenbeemster, Noordbeemster, Westbeemster and Zuidoostbeemster.

Beemster cheese

The region gives its name to Beemster cheese, a Gouda-style cheese produced by the CONO cooperative (founded 1901) from milk of cows grazing on the polder's clay soil. Aged varieties such as Beemster Classic (18 months) and XO (26 months) are exported worldwide.
