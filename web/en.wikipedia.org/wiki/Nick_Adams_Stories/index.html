The Nick Adams Stories

The Nick Adams Stories is a 1972 collection of short stories by American author Ernest Hemingway (1899–1961). The collection, edited by Philip Young, brings together all of the stories Hemingway wrote about Nick Adams, a semi-autobiographical character who appears in more stories than any other Hemingway figure, arranged roughly in the order of Nick's life from boyhood to adulthood.

Contents and themes

The stories follow Nick Adams from his childhood in northern Michigan — fishing, hunting and camping with his father, a doctor — through his wounding as a soldier in the First World War, to his return home and his uneasy attempts to recover through sport and solitude. Eight of the pieces were previously unpublished or uncollected at the time of the volume's appearance. Celebrated entries include "Indian Camp", "The Killers", "Big Two-Hearted River", "The Battler", "Cross-Country Snow" and "A Way You'll Never Be".

Significance

Read together, the stories form a loose bildungsroman and a key document of Hemingway's "iceberg theory" of understated prose. The Michigan settings draw on Hemingway's own summers at Walloon Lake, while the war stories reflect the wounds he suffered on the Italian front in 1918. Critics regard the sequence as among the purest expressions of Hemingway's early style and of the initiation theme in American literature.
