Bureau of American Ethnology

The Bureau of American Ethnology (BAE) was a research unit of the Smithsonian Institution in the United States. It was founded in 1879 by an act of Congress, with the anthropologist John Wesley Powell as its first director. The bureau's purpose was to transfer archives, records and materials relating to Native American peoples from the Department of the Interior to the Smithsonian, and to conduct systematic anthropological research.

Work

Under Powell and his successors, including William Henry Holmes and Jesse Walter Fewkes, the BAE sponsored extensive fieldwork on the languages, cultures, archaeology and physical anthropology of Indigenous peoples of the Americas. Its staff included pioneering ethnologists such as James Mooney, Franz Boas (as a collaborator), Alice Fletcher and Frances Densmore. The bureau published an influential series of Annual Reports and Bulletins that remain standard references.

Legacy

In 1965 the BAE merged with the Smithsonian's Department of Anthropology to form the Smithsonian Office of Anthropology, now part of the National Museum of Natural History; its collections form the core of the National Anthropological Archives.
