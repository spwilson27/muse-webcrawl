Casserole

A casserole (French: diminutive of casse, from Provençal cassa, 'pan') is a kind of large, deep pan or bowl used for cooking a variety of dishes in the oven; also a category of foods cooked in such a vessel.

Casserole
Type: Baked dish / cookware
Serving temperature: Hot
Variations: Hotdish, tuna casserole, green bean casserole

History

Slow-cooked one-pot dishes date to prehistory. The modern casserole became especially popular in the United States in the 20th century, particularly after the Second World War, when canned soups and convenience ingredients made dishes such as tuna noodle casserole and green bean casserole household staples and popular potluck contributions.

Characteristics

A casserole typically combines a protein, vegetables, a starchy binder and a sauce, baked until tender, often with a cheese or breadcrumb topping. Examples worldwide include French cassoulet, Greek moussaka, Italian lasagne-type bakes, and the Minnesota hotdish.
