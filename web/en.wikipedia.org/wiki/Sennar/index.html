Sennar

Sennar (Arabic: سنار) is a town on the Blue Nile in Sudan and the capital of the state of Sennar. It was for several centuries the capital of the Funj Sultanate of Sennar, one of the most important states of the Nile valley. The modern town lies near the Sennar Dam, completed in 1925 to irrigate the Gezira Scheme.

History

The Funj Sultanate was founded around 1504 by Amara Dunqas, and Sennar became its capital, prospering from trade in gold, slaves, and ivory along the Nile and with routes to Ethiopia and the Red Sea. Travellers such as James Bruce, who visited in 1772, described a populous royal city, though the sultanate by then was in decline under its powerful Hamaj regents. In 1821 the last Funj sultan submitted to the forces of Muhammad Ali of Egypt, ending the sultanate and incorporating the region into Turco-Egyptian Sudan.

Modern town

The Sennar Dam, built by the British in 1925, impounds the Blue Nile to supply the Gezira irrigation scheme, one of the largest gravity-fed irrigation projects in the world. The modern town is a regional market and transport centre, distinct from the site of the old royal city nearby.
