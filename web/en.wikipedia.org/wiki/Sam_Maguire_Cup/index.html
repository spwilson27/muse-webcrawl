Sam Maguire Cup

Awarded for: Winning the All-Ireland Senior Football Championship

Presented by: Gaelic Athletic Association (GAA)

First awarded: 1928

Named after: Sam Maguire (1877–1927)

The Sam Maguire Cup (Irish: Corn Sam Mhic Uidhir), often shortened to Sam, is the trophy awarded to the winners of the All-Ireland Senior Football Championship, the premier competition in Gaelic football organised by the Gaelic Athletic Association.

History

The cup is named after Sam Maguire, a native of Dunmanway, County Cork, who played a leading role in the GAA in London and captained London in All-Ireland finals. After Maguire's death in 1927, friends commissioned a trophy in his memory. The first cup, modelled on the Ardagh Chalice, was made by Hopkins and Hopkins of Dublin and was first presented in 1928, when Kildare were the inaugural winners.

The original and replica cups

The original 1928 cup was retired in 1987 and is displayed in the GAA Museum at Croke Park. A replica, produced by silversmith Desmond Byrne, has been presented since 1988. The cup is made of sterling silver and stands approximately two feet high.

Winners

Kerry have won the cup more times than any other county, followed by Dublin. The winning captain traditionally lifts the cup in the Hogan Stand at Croke Park on All-Ireland final day, amid celebrations in the team's county colours.
