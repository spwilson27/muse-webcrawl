Norris–LaGuardia Act

Norris–LaGuardia Act
Long titleAn Act to amend the Judicial Code and to define and limit the jurisdiction of courts sitting in equity, and for other purposes
EnactedMarch 23, 1932
SponsorsSen. George W. Norris; Rep. Fiorello H. LaGuardia
Codification29 U.S.C. §§ 101–115

The Norris–LaGuardia Act (also known as the Anti-Injunction Bill) is a 1932 United States federal law that curtailed the power of federal courts to issue injunctions against labor unions engaged in strikes, boycotts and picketing, and that outlawed so-called "yellow-dog contracts" — agreements by which employees promised not to join a union.

Sponsored by Senator George W. Norris of Nebraska and Representative Fiorello La Guardia of New York, the act was a landmark in American labor law and a forerunner of the National Labor Relations Act of 1935.

Background

In the decades before 1932, federal judges routinely intervened in labor disputes on the side of employers, issuing sweeping injunctions that effectively criminalized strikes and union organizing. Labor leaders condemned "government by injunction," and reformers sought to remove the federal courts from private labor conflicts unless violence or fraud was involved.

Provisions

The act declared that employees should have full freedom of association and self-organization without employer interference; it made yellow-dog contracts unenforceable in federal court; and it sharply limited the circumstances in which federal courts could enjoin strikes, picketing and union publicity, requiring clear proof of unlawful acts, irreparable injury and the failure of local authorities to protect property before an injunction could issue. It also guaranteed jury trials for certain contempt charges arising from labor disputes.

Legacy

The Supreme Court upheld the act's narrowing of judicial power, and its policy declarations shaped the Wagner Act three years later. Although later statutes such as the Taft–Hartley Act restored some grounds for injunctions, the Norris–LaGuardia Act remains a foundational statute of U.S. labor law.
