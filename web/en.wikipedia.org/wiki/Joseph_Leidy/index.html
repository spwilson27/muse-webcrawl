Joseph Leidy

Joseph Mellick Leidy
Born: September 9, 1823, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Died: April 30, 1891 (aged 67), Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Fields: Palaeontology, anatomy, parasitology, natural history

Joseph Mellick Leidy (September 9, 1823 – April 30, 1891) was an American palaeontologist, parasitologist, and anatomist, often called the father of American vertebrate palaeontology. A polymath of extraordinary range, he was a leading figure of nineteenth-century American natural history.

Career

Leidy was born in Philadelphia and studied medicine at the University of Pennsylvania, where he later became professor of anatomy. He also held chairs in natural history at Swarthmore College and the Wagner Free Institute of Science, and was associated with the Academy of Natural Sciences of Philadelphia for most of his career.

Palaeontology

Leidy described many of the first dinosaurs and fossil mammals known from North America, including Hadrosaurus foulkii (1858), the first reasonably complete dinosaur skeleton, which he recognised as a bipedal animal — a landmark in dinosaur science. Working with specimens sent from western expeditions, he monographed the extinct mammalian faunas of the Dakota and Nebraska territories and named numerous genera of fossil horses, camels, rhinoceroses, and other mammals. His careful, non-speculative style contrasted with, and was eventually overshadowed by, the publicity-driven rivalry of Edward Drinker Cope and Othniel Charles Marsh, both of whom he had mentored and supplied with identifications.

Other work

Leidy founded American parasitology as a discipline, demonstrated that trichinosis could be transmitted through undercooked pork, pioneered forensic microscopy in criminal cases, and wrote the classic monograph Fresh-water Rhizopods of North America (1879), notable for its beautiful illustrations of amoebae. He was among the early American scientists to accept Darwin's theory of evolution and helped introduce Darwinian thinking into American biology.

Legacy

Leidy is commemorated in the names of many species and in awards of the Academy of Natural Sciences. His breadth — spanning anatomy, protozoology, parasitology, and palaeontology — has rarely been matched.
