Casanova

Giacomo Girolamo Casanova (2 April 1725 – 4 June 1798) was an Italian adventurer and author from Venice. His autobiography, Histoire de ma vie (Story of My Life), written in French, is a vivid source for 18th-century European social life, and his name became synonymous with the art of seduction: a "Casanova" is a famous womaniser.

Life

Educated at Padua, Casanova variously worked as a church lawyer, violinist, magician, lottery organiser and spy for Venetian inquisitors. Imprisoned in 1755 in the Leads prison under the Doge's Palace for offences against religion, he made a celebrated escape in 1756, fleeing to Paris, where he helped establish the state lottery. He travelled across Europe meeting Voltaire, Frederick the Great, Catherine the Great and Mozart's circle, and may have contributed to the libretto milieu of Don Giovanni.

Later years

He spent his final years as librarian to Count Waldstein at Dux Castle in Bohemia, writing his memoirs. See also Venice.
