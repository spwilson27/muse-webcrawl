Sex offense

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A sex offense (or sexual offence) is a category of criminal conduct involving sexual acts committed without the consent of another person, against a person unable to consent, or otherwise prohibited by law. The precise definition and range of offences vary substantially between jurisdictions, but the category commonly includes rape and sexual assault, sexual activity with minors, indecent exposure, voyeurism, and certain offences involving pornography.

Terminology

In the United States, "sex offense" is the umbrella term used in statutes governing registration and sentencing, and a person convicted of such an offence is commonly called a "sex offender". In England and Wales, the Sexual Offences Act 2003 consolidated and reformed the law, defining offences including rape, assault by penetration, sexual assault and offences against children. Other jurisdictions use terms such as "sexual offences", "crimes against sexual integrity" or specific offence names.

Registration and notification

Many jurisdictions maintain sex offender registries requiring convicted offenders to register their addresses with authorities, sometimes for life. In the United States, the federal Adam Walsh Child Protection and Safety Act (2006) and state "Megan's Law" statutes provide for public notification. Similar registers exist in Canada, the United Kingdom (the Violent and Sex Offender Register) and elsewhere. These schemes are debated: supporters credit them with aiding public safety, while critics question their effectiveness and their collateral consequences for housing, employment and reintegration.

Prevalence and reporting

Sexual offences are widely considered to be substantially under-reported to police. Victimisation surveys consistently estimate higher prevalence than official crime statistics, and attrition between report, prosecution and conviction is a persistent concern in many legal systems.
