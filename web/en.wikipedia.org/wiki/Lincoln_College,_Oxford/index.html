Lincoln College (formally, The College of the Blessed Mary and All Saints, Lincoln) is one of the constituent colleges of the University of Oxford, situated on Turl Street in central Oxford. It was founded in 1427 by Richard Fleming, the then Bishop of Lincoln, making it the ninth-oldest college in Oxford.

Fleming founded the college to combat the Lollard heresy, intending it as a "little college of true students of theology who would defend the mysteries of Scripture against those ignorant laymen who profaned with swinish snouts its most holy pearls." It remained small and relatively poor until benefactions in the 17th and 18th centuries enlarged it.

History

John Wesley, the founder of Methodism, was a fellow of Lincoln from 1726 and held his early meetings of the "Holy Club" there; his room and chapel pulpit are part of college legend. In 1958 Lincoln became one of the first men's colleges joined in a graduate society with other colleges; women were first admitted in 1979. The college claims to have invented the drink "Spritzer" connections aside, it is known for a close-knit, unpretentious ethos.

Buildings

The college occupies a compact site between Turl Street and Brasenose Lane. Its 15th-century Front Quad is among Oxford's most picturesque; the chapel (1629–31) is a fine example of late Gothic survival with notable stained glass by Abraham van Linge. All Saints Church on the High Street, redundant since 1971, serves as the college library, its Baroque tower a landmark of the Oxford skyline.

Traditions

On Ascension Day, Lincoln invites neighbouring Brasenose College members to drink ivy beer — laced, legend says, in memory of a Brasenose man who died after Lincoln's gate was shut against a pursuing mob in 1386 — through a small door between the colleges, the only time it opens each year. The college grace is in Latin.

Notable alumni

Alumni include John Wesley; the children's author Dr. Seuss (Theodor Geisel), who studied (without taking a degree) in the 1920s; the illustrator Edward Gorey briefly; former British politician Rishi Sunak, who read PPE at Lincoln and became Prime Minister; the novelist John le Carré (David Cornwell); the mathematician and broadcaster; and the athlete; as well as Nobel laureate Howard Florey, co-developer of penicillin, and the physicist Edward Teller (research fellow).
