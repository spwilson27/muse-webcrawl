JN-25 was the principal operational code used by the Imperial Japanese Navy during World War II. The name was the designation given by Allied cryptanalysts: "JN" for "Japanese Navy", the 25th such system identified.

Structure

JN-25 was a superenciphered code: words and phrases were represented by five-digit code groups drawn from a codebook of roughly 45,000 entries, and the groups were then enciphered by adding (digit-by-digit, without carry) a sequence taken from a separate additive book. The system was introduced on 1 June 1939 and went through a series of editions and additive changes, notably JN-25a and JN-25b in late 1940 and further changes in 1942.

Breaking JN-25

United States Navy codebreakers — principally the OP-20-G unit in Washington and Station HYPO at Pearl Harbor under Joseph Rochefort — progressively recovered the codebook and additives from intercepted traffic. Partial decrypts contributed to American success at the Battle of the Coral Sea in May 1942, and, most famously, to Admiral Nimitz's ambush of the Japanese carrier force at the Battle of Midway in June 1942, after the codebreakers helped confirm "AF" as the target designator for Midway by means of a ruse message about a water-desalination plant.

JN-25 intelligence later contributed to operations including the interception and death of Admiral Isoroku Yamamoto in April 1943 (Operation Vengeance), which relied on a decrypted itinerary transmitted in JN-25.
