Mauritshuis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The Mauritshuis in The Hague: an elegant Dutch classical palace beside the Hofvijver pond, reflections in still water]

The Mauritshuis is an art museum in The Hague, Netherlands, housing the Royal Cabinet of Paintings, which consists of 841 objects, mostly Dutch Golden Age paintings. The collections include works by Johannes Vermeer, Rembrandt van Rijn, Jan Steen, Paulus Potter and Frans Hals. The museum is located in a 17th-century Dutch classicist building beside the Hofvijver pond, built as the residence of Count John Maurice of Nassau-Siegen.

Its star attractions are Johannes Vermeer's Girl with a Pearl Earring and View of Delft, Rembrandt's late self-portraits, and Potter's life-size The Young Bull. Small, jewel-like and intensely visited, it reopened in 2014 after a renovation that doubled its space with an underground link to a neighbouring art deco building.
