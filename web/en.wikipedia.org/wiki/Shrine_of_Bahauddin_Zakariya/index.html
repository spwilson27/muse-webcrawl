The Shrine of Bahauddin Zakariya (Urdu: مقبرہ بہاؤ الدین زکریا) is a 13th-century shrine located in the city of Multan, in Pakistan's Punjab province. The tomb is dedicated to the Muslim mystic and saint Bahauddin Zakariya (c. 1170–1262), founder of the Suhrawardiyya order of Sufism in South Asia. It is considered one of the most important shrines in southern Punjab and a landmark of Multan, a city famed for its shrines.

History

Bahauddin Zakariya, a grandson of Shahab al-Din Suhrawardi, settled in Multan and established a large khanqah (hospice) that drew disciples from across the region. Upon his death in 1262, his tomb was built at the site. The original structure was largely destroyed during the Siege of Multan in 1848, when British forces bombarded the Multan Fort; the shrine was subsequently rebuilt in the later 19th century in its present form.

Architecture

The shrine is one of the earliest examples of the distinctive Multan style of funerary architecture. The tomb has a two-tiered plan: a square base that transitions through an octagonal drum to a hemispherical dome covered in blue glazed tiles. The exterior is richly decorated with blue and white glazed tile work with geometric and floral patterns, and the interior features carved woodwork and fresco remnants. The building stands within the raised courtyard of the Multan Fort area, near the shrines of Zakariya's disciple Shamsuddin Sabzwari and his grandson Shah Rukn-e-Alam.

Devotion

The saint's annual urs festival, held in Safar, draws large numbers of devotees from across Pakistan. The shrine is maintained by descendants of the saint and the Auqaf Department, and qawwali and devotional gatherings are held at the site.
