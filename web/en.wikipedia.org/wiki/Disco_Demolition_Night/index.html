Disco Demolition Night was a Major League Baseball promotion held on 12 July 1979 at Comiskey Park in Chicago, Illinois, that ended in a riot and the forfeiture of a game. Between the two games of a doubleheader between the Chicago White Sox and the Detroit Tigers, radio disc jockey Steve Dahl blew up a crate filled with disco records on the field — a stunt aimed at the genre's dominance of the charts.

Background

Dahl had been fired from a Chicago station when it switched to an all-disco format, and built a following at WLUP with his "Disco Sucks" campaign and an army of fans called the Insane Coho Lips. White Sox promotions man Mike Veeck, son of owner Bill Veeck, offered admission for 98 cents to fans who brought a disco record to be destroyed. Organisers expected perhaps 20,000 people; roughly 50,000 or more came, many climbing fences to get in.

The riot

After the first game, Dahl detonated the records in centre field, tearing a hole in the playing surface. Thousands of fans streamed onto the field, tearing up the turf, stealing bases and setting fires. The field was left unplayable, and the second game was forfeited to the Tigers — only the sixth forfeiture in major league history at that time and a permanent stain on the White Sox' season.

Legacy

The event is often cited as a symbolic end of the disco era in the United States and remains one of the most infamous promotions in sports history. Later commentators have also debated the extent to which the anti-disco backlash carried racial and homophobic undertones, given disco's roots in Black, Latino and gay club culture. Dahl and the White Sox marked the promotion's anniversaries with wry retrospectives, and the incident has been the subject of documentaries and books.
