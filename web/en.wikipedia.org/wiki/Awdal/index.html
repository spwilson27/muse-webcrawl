Awdal (Somali: Awdal) is an administrative region in northwestern Somalia, corresponding to part of the former British Somaliland protectorate. Its capital is Borama. The region borders Djibouti to the northwest, Ethiopia to the southwest, the Somali region of Woqooyi Galbeed (Maroodi Jeex) to the east, and the Gulf of Aden to the north. Since 1991 Awdal has been administered as part of the self-declared Republic of Somaliland, although its status is contested.

History

Awdal takes its name from the medieval Adal Sultanate, whose heartland lay in this area. The coastal town of Zeila was for centuries one of the principal ports of the Horn of Africa, a major centre of trade with Arabia and the wider Islamic world and an early centre of Islam in the region. The ruined city preserves mosques and other remains from this period.

Under British rule, the area formed part of the British Somaliland protectorate. After Somali independence in 1960 it became part of the Somali Republic. Following the collapse of the Somali central government in 1991, Awdal was incorporated into Somaliland, whose declaration of independence Somalia does not recognise.

Geography

The region encompasses coastal plains along the Gulf of Aden, rising southwards through the Guban lowlands to the foothills and highlands around Borama, which sits at an elevation of roughly 1,400 metres. The climate ranges from the extremely hot and arid coast to the cooler, semi-agricultural plateau in the south.

Demographics and economy

The population is predominantly Somali, principally of the Gadabursi (Dir) clan, with Issa communities in the coastal areas around Zeila and Lughaya. The economy is based on pastoralism, small-scale agriculture in the southern escarpment zone, fishing, and trade through Zeila and Borama. Borama is a significant educational centre, home to Amoud University, founded in 1998 and regarded as a symbol of post-war reconstruction in the region.

Districts

Awdal is subdivided into districts including Borama, Baki, Lughaya and Zeila districts. The town of Zeila, historically the region's principal port, has declined in importance relative to Berbera and Djibouti but retains significant archaeological and historical interest.
