Metropolitan Borough of Wirral

The Metropolitan Borough of Wirral is a metropolitan borough of Merseyside, in North West England. It has a population of about 320,000 (2021 census). The borough is named after and covers most of the Wirral Peninsula, a roughly rectangular peninsula between the River Mersey and the River Dee. Its largest town is Birkenhead, where the council has its headquarters at Wallasey Town Hall. Other settlements include Wallasey, Bebington, Heswall, Hoylake and West Kirby.

History

The borough was created on 1 April 1974 under the Local Government Act 1972, merging the county boroughs of Birkenhead and Wallasey with the municipal borough of Bebington and the urban districts of Hoylake and Wirral. Historically the whole peninsula lay within Cheshire, and the area retains the CH postcode district; it forms part of the Liverpool City Region combined authority.

Geography

The peninsula is bounded by the Irish Sea to the north-west, the River Dee to the west and the River Mersey to the east. Much of the eastern side is urban and industrial, historically dominated by the Cammell Laird shipyards at Birkenhead, while the west is more affluent and rural, with coastal resorts at West Kirby and Hoylake — home to the Royal Liverpool Golf Club, a regular venue for The Open Championship. Hilbre Island, off West Kirby, is a tidal island and local nature reserve.

Transport

The Wirral line of Merseyrail links the borough to Liverpool through the Mersey Railway Tunnel, and the M53 motorway runs down the peninsula's spine. The famous Mersey Ferries connect Birkenhead and Wallasey (Seacombe) with Liverpool's Pier Head.
