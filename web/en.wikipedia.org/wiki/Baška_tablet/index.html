Baška tablet

Created: c. 1100
Discovered: 1851, Jurandvor near Baška, island of Krk, Croatia
Script: Glagolitic
Present location: Croatian Academy of Sciences and Arts, Zagreb

The Baška tablet (Croatian: Bašćanska ploča) is one of the first monuments containing an inscription in the Croatian recension of the Church Slavonic language written in the Glagolitic script. Dating from around 1100, it records a donation of land by the Croatian king Zvonimir to a Benedictine abbey, and it is the first document to mention a Croatian ruler by name in the Croatian language.

Discovery and content

The large stone slab — about 99.5 × 199 cm and weighing roughly 800 kg — was found in 1851 forming part of the floor of the early Romanesque church of St. Lucy in Jurandvor near Baška on the island of Krk. Its thirteen-line inscription, partly damaged, names Abbot Držiha as the writer, records King Zvonimir's gift of land to the church of St. Lucy, lists witnesses, and invokes a curse on anyone who would annul the donation.

Significance

The tablet is a cornerstone source for Croatian history, language, and palaeography: it shows the Glagolitic script in transition, mixes Chakavian dialect features with Church Slavonic, and provides the oldest mention of the title of Croatian king in a domestic document. The original is kept in the Croatian Academy of Sciences and Arts in Zagreb; a replica remains in the church of St. Lucy. Elements of the tablet appear on Croatia's former 100-kuna banknote.
