Simbang Gabi

Simbang Gabi (Tagalog for "Night Mass" or "Evening Mass", also called Misa de Gallo) is a devotional series of nine Masses celebrated by Catholics in the Philippines in anticipation of Christmas. The Masses are traditionally held in the early hours of the morning — typically from 3:00 to 5:00 a.m. — from 16 to 24 December, though in recent decades many parishes also hold anticipated evening Masses for those who cannot attend at dawn.

History

The practice derives from the Spanish colonial period, when farmers asked to hear Mass before going to the fields. The nine-day novena of Masses parallels other Advent devotions in the Hispanic world; the pre-dawn timing was a practical accommodation to agricultural labour, sanctioned by the Church. Churches fill with lantern (parol) light and carols, and the last Mass, on the morning or evening of 24 December, shades into the Christmas Eve Misa de Gallo proper.

Traditions

Completing all nine Masses is popularly believed to grant a special wish or petition. After Mass, vendors outside church doors sell traditional delicacies such as bibingka (rice cake cooked in clay pots) and puto bumbong (purple rice cake steamed in bamboo), eaten with hot salabat (ginger tea) or tsokolate. The devotion is also kept by Filipino diaspora communities worldwide.
