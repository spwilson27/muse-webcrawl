Situational crisis communication theory

FieldPublic relations
Developed byW. Timothy Coombs
PurposeCrisis response strategy

Situational crisis communication theory (SCCT) is a framework in public relations, developed by W. Timothy Coombs, for matching an organisation's crisis response to the level of reputational threat posed by a crisis. It holds that managers should assess how much stakeholders attribute responsibility for the crisis to the organisation, then choose response strategies accordingly.

Crisis clusters

SCCT groups crises into three clusters by attributed responsibility: victim crises (weak attribution, e.g. natural disasters and rumours), accidental crises (minimal attribution, e.g. technical-error accidents) and preventable crises (strong attribution, e.g. organisational misconduct and human-error accidents).

Response strategies

Recommended responses range from denial strategies (attacking the accuser, denial, scapegoating) for victim crises, through diminish strategies (excuses and justification) for accidental crises, to rebuild strategies (compensation and apology) for preventable crises. Base responses in all cases include instructing information (how to stay safe) and adjusting information (helping stakeholders cope).

Evidence

Experimental research supporting SCCT shows that matching response accommodation to responsibility attribution better protects reputation, and that prior reputation and crisis history intensify or buffer the threat.
