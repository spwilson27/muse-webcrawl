Instant messaging

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: Screenshot-style image of a phone messaging app with a conversation of short bubbles]

Instant messaging (IM) is a type of online chat allowing immediate transmission of messages over the Internet or another computer network. Messages are typically transmitted between two or more parties in real time, and modern IM adds voice and video calls, file sharing, reactions and group chats of thousands.

IM's ancestors are the talk programs of 1970s timesharing systems and Internet Relay Chat (1988). The mass-market era began with ICQ (1996, from Israeli company Mirabilis — 'I seek you'), which gave users UIN numbers and a flower icon, and AOL Instant Messenger (1997), whose buddy list, away messages and door-slam sounds defined a generation of American adolescence. Microsoft's MSN/Windows Live Messenger and Yahoo! Messenger fought the 'messenger wars', refusing to interoperate.

The centre of gravity then moved to phones: WhatsApp (2009) used the phone number as identity and end-to-end encryption as a feature, passing two billion users; WeChat in China fused messaging with payments and mini-apps into a 'super-app'; and iMessage, Telegram, Signal and Discord each carved out territory. IM displaced SMS, and in much of the world it simply is the Internet's front door — the endpoint of the line that began with two mainframes exchanging 'LO' in 1969.
