Whist

Whist
Type: Trick-taking
Players: 4 (2 partnerships)
Cards: 52
Origin: England, 16th century

Whist is a classic English trick-taking card game played by four players in two partnerships with a standard 52-card deck. It was widely played in the 18th and 19th centuries and is the ancestor of contract bridge and of spades.

Rules

Cards rank from ace high to two low. The dealer deals 13 cards to each player, and the last card is turned up to determine the trump suit. Players must follow suit if able; the highest card of the suit led wins the trick unless trumped. A partnership scores one point for each trick taken beyond six ("book"), and the first side to reach five points wins the game, subject to honours scoring in the classic form.

History

Whist developed from the older game of ruff and honours. Edmond Hoyle's A Short Treatise on the Game of Whist (1742) codified its rules and etiquette, and whist became the dominant intellectual card game of the English-speaking world until it was supplanted by bridge around the turn of the 20th century.
