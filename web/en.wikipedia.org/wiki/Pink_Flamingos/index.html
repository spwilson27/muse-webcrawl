Pink Flamingos

Pink Flamingos is a 1972 American black comedy film written, produced, and directed by John Waters. Starring the drag performer Divine, it is a notorious exercise in deliberate bad taste and a landmark of underground and midnight-movie culture. The film forms part of what Waters later called his "Trash Trilogy", along with Female Trouble (1974) and Desperate Living (1977).

Plot

Notorious criminal Divine, living in a trailer under the alias Babs Johnson, is celebrated in the tabloids as "the filthiest person alive". Jealous rivals Connie and Raymond Marble scheme to usurp the title, setting off an escalating contest of depravity that culminates in revenge and a famously shocking closing scene.

Reception and legacy

Made for about $10,000 and filmed in Baltimore with Waters's ensemble, the Dreamlanders, the film became a long-running midnight movie and a touchstone of camp and cult cinema. Initially denounced by critics and banned or censored in several countries, it was reassessed as a satire of celebrity and media notoriety. In 2021 it was selected for preservation in the United States National Film Registry.
