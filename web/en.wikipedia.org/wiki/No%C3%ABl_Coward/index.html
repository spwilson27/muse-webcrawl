Noël Coward

BornDecember 16, 1899, Teddington, Middlesex, England
DiedMarch 26, 1973, Blue Harbour, Jamaica
OccupationPlaywright, composer, actor, singer, director
Notable worksHay Fever, Private Lives, Blithe Spirit, Brief Encounter

Sir Noël Peirce Coward (December 16, 1899 – March 26, 1973) was an English playwright, composer, director, actor and singer, known for his wit, flamboyance and what Time magazine called 'a sense of personal style, a combination of cheek and chic, pose and poise'. He achieved enduring success as the author of comedies such as Hay Fever, Private Lives and Blithe Spirit, and as a songwriter, cabaret performer and film-maker.

Career

A child actor from the age of eleven, Coward had his first major success as a playwright with The Vortex (1924), a scandal-tinged drama that made him famous at twenty-five. Through the late 1920s and 1930s he produced a string of hit comedies and revues, often acting in them alongside Gertrude Lawrence, and Design for Living, Cavalcade and Tonight at 8:30 cemented his position among the most popular dramatists in the English-speaking world.

During the Second World War Coward toured entertaining troops and made the patriotic films In Which We Serve (1942) and This Happy Breed; his screenplay for Brief Encounter (1945), directed by David Lean, became a classic of British cinema. He also performed intelligence-related work for the British government.

Later years and legacy

After a dip in popularity in the early post-war years, Coward reinvented himself as a cabaret performer in Las Vegas and London, and his plays returned to fashion in the 1960s in what he called 'Dad's Renaissance'. He was knighted in 1970.

Coward wrote more than 50 plays and composed hundreds of songs, including 'Mad Dogs and Englishmen' and 'A Room with a View'. He divided his later life between homes in Switzerland and Jamaica, where he died in 1973. His diaries and letters, published posthumously, revealed a private life discreetly kept from the public during his lifetime.
