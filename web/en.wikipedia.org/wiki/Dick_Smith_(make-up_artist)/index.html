Dick Smith (make-up artist)

BornRichard Emerson Smith, June 26, 1922, Larchmont, New York, U.S.
DiedJuly 30, 2014 (aged 92), Los Angeles, California
OccupationMake-up artist
Known forThe Godfather, The Exorcist, Amadeus

Richard Emerson Smith (June 26, 1922 – July 30, 2014), known as Dick Smith, was an American special make-up effects artist, nicknamed "The Godfather of Make-Up". He pioneered techniques such as multi-piece foam latex appliances and realistic ageing and gore effects that transformed film makeup.

Career

Smith became the first makeup artist at NBC television in 1945 and spent years experimenting there with flexible materials. For film he aged Dustin Hoffman by some 100 years in Little Big Man (1970), turned Marlon Brando into Don Corleone in The Godfather (1972), created Linda Blair's demonic look and its shocking effects in The Exorcist (1973), and aged F. Murray Abraham and Tom Hulce in Amadeus (1984), for which he shared the Academy Award for Best Makeup. His correspondence course and mentorship trained a generation of effects artists, including Rick Baker, who called him his idol. In 2011 Smith received an Academy Honorary Award for his life's work.
