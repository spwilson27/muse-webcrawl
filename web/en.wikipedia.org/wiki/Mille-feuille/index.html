Mille-feuille

A mille-feuille ("thousand leaves"), also called a Napoleon, vanilla slice or custard slice in various countries, is a French pastry made of puff pastry layered with crème pâtissière (pastry cream). Its modern form was influenced by improvements made by the 19th-century chef Marie-Antoine Carême.

Composition

Traditionally a mille-feuille consists of three layers of puff pastry alternating with two layers of pastry cream, topped with icing, fondant, or a dusting of confectioners' sugar, sometimes decorated with a marbled or feathered pattern. Variations use whipped cream, jam, or fruit.

Names around the world

In Britain it is a vanilla slice or custard slice; in Australia a vanilla slice (sometimes with passionfruit icing); in North America, a Napoleon. Similar layered pastries exist across Europe, including the Dutch tompouce.
