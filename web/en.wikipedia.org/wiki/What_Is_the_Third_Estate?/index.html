What Is the Third Estate?

What Is the Third Estate? (French: Qu'est-ce que le tiers état?) is a political pamphlet written in January 1789 by the French clergyman and political theorist Emmanuel Joseph Sieyès. It became one of the most influential texts of the French Revolution.

The argument

The pamphlet opens with three famous questions and answers:

  1. What is the Third Estate? Everything.
  2. What has it been until now in the political order? Nothing.
  3. What does it ask? To become something.

Under the Old Regime, French society was divided into three estates: the clergy (First Estate), the nobility (Second Estate), and the commoners (Third Estate). The Third Estate made up roughly 98 percent of the population and did nearly all the productive work — farming, trade, manufacturing, and the professions — yet it was politically subordinate and taxed heavily while the privileged orders were largely exempt.

Sieyès argued that the Third Estate was the nation: it performed all the useful functions of society, and the privileged orders were a useless excrescence upon it. The nation, he claimed, does not need the privileged orders, while the privileged orders cannot exist without the nation.

Influence

The pamphlet provided the ideological justification for the Third Estate's deputies to declare themselves the National Assembly in June 1789, the act that launched the Revolution. Sieyès's distinction between the nation and its privileged minority, and his argument that political legitimacy derives from the nation as a whole, shaped revolutionary doctrine and modern democratic theory.
