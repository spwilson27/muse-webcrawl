Wiedergutmachung
Meaning:"Making good again" — German reparations to victims of Nazism
Key instrument:Luxembourg Agreements (1952) between West Germany, Israel and the Claims Conference
Individual law:Federal Compensation Act (BEG, 1953/1956)
Total paid:Tens of billions of Deutschmarks/euros over subsequent decades

Wiedergutmachung (German for "making good again") refers to the compensation and reparations paid by the Federal Republic of Germany (West Germany) to victims of Nazi persecution, principally Jewish survivors of the Holocaust and the State of Israel. Chancellor Konrad Adenauer declared in 1951 that unspeakable crimes had been committed in Germany's name, obliging the country to make moral and material amends.

Luxembourg Agreements

The agreements signed in Luxembourg on 10 September 1952 between West Germany, Israel and the Conference on Jewish Material Claims Against Germany (Claims Conference) committed the Federal Republic to pay 3 billion Deutschmarks to Israel — mostly in goods — and 450 million to the Claims Conference for victims outside Israel. The deal was fiercely debated in both countries; in Israel, Menachem Begin led violent protests against accepting "blood money", while Adenauer pushed ratification through the Bundestag in 1953.

Individual compensation

The Federal Compensation Act (Bundesentschädigungsgesetz, 1953, revised 1956) created pensions and payments for individuals persecuted on political, racial or religious grounds — for imprisonment, injury to health, loss of property, career and education. By the early 21st century Germany had paid out well over €70 billion in total under the various Wiedergutmachung programmes.

Criticism and later programmes

Many victims — including Roma, forced labourers, homosexuals and victims in Eastern Europe — were long excluded or restricted by residence rules and deadlines. Later foundations addressed gaps: the 2000 "Remembrance, Responsibility and Future" Foundation compensated former forced labourers, and hardship funds negotiated with the Claims Conference continue to expand eligibility and home-care funding for ageing survivors.
