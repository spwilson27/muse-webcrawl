Don Giovanni

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia
[Image: The finale of Don Giovanni: the stone statue of the Commendatore at the banquet table, flames rising as the Don refuses to repent]

Don Giovanni (Italian pronunciation: [don dʒoˈvanni]), K. 527, is an opera in two acts with music by Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart and Italian libretto by Lorenzo Da Ponte. It is based on the legends of Don Juan, a fictional libertine and seducer, and it is considered one of the greatest operas of all time. It premiered at the Estates Theatre in Prague on 29 October 1787.

The opera follows the last day of the rake Don Giovanni: murdering the Commendatore, he pursues Donna Elvira, Donna Anna and the peasant Zerlina, catalogued by his servant Leporello ('Madamina, il catalogo è questo'), until the Commendatore's statue accepts his mocking dinner invitation and drags the unrepentant Don to Hell. Kierkegaard devoted a famous essay to it in Either/Or.
