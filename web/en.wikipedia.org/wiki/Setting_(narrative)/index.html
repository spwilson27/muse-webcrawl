Setting (narrative)

Setting
Setting establishes the time, place and atmosphere of a story
FieldNarratology, literary studies
Related elementsPlot, character, theme, point of view

A setting (or backdrop) is the time and geographic location within a narrative, either non-fiction or fiction. It is a literary element. The setting initiates the main backdrop and mood for a story. The setting can be referred to as the story world or milieu to include a context (especially society) beyond the immediate surroundings of the story. Elements of setting may include culture, historical period, geography, and hour.

Types of setting

Settings can be broadly classified as backdrop settings, which are relatively interchangeable and serve mainly as a stage for the action, and integral settings, in which time and place are essential to the story's meaning — as in regional novels set in a particular landscape and community, or historical novels anchored in a specific period. A setting may also be a fictional world with its own geography, history and rules, as in fantasy and science fiction.

Function

Setting establishes mood and atmosphere, grounds characters' behaviour in a social and physical environment, can act as an antagonist (for example, a hostile wilderness), and can symbolise a story's themes. Writers convey setting through description, exposition, and the details of characters' speech, dress and customs.
