Horse trainer

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A horse trainer is a person who tends to horses and teaches them different disciplines. Some responsibilities trainers have are caring for the animals' physical needs, as well as teaching them good behaviour and/or coaching them for events, which may include contests and other riding purposes. The level of education and the yearly salary they can earn for this profession may differ depending on where the person is employed.

History

Horse domestication by the Botai culture in Kazakhstan dates to about 3500 BCE. Written records of horse training as a pursuit have been documented as early as 1350 BC, by Kikkuli, the Hurrian "master horse trainer" of the Hittite Empire. Another source of early recorded horse training is Xenophon, in his work On Horsemanship written about 350 BC. Writing in about 30 AD, Columella advised that a colt should be taken in hand at four years old; his views on training methods differ from some of those held today in that he made an allowance for the animal's feelings but also recommended beating.

Responsibilities

In horse racing, a trainer prepares a horse for races, with responsibility for exercising it, getting it race-ready and determining which races it should enter. A good trainer can make a mediocre horse perform well, and keep a good horse in peak condition. Trainers are normally responsible for day-to-day care of the horses in their charge, including feeding, grooming, exercise, and stable management.

In other equestrian disciplines, trainers teach horses to respond to commands for specific activities, such as dressage, show jumping, western riding, or driving. Trainers may also work with young horses to accustom them to being handled, ridden, and to basic ground manners before they are old enough to be ridden under saddle — a process often called "breaking in" or "starting" a horse.

Education and licensing

Requirements vary by discipline and jurisdiction. In many forms of horse racing, trainers must hold a licence issued by the governing racing authority. Licensing usually involves examinations on stable management, horse care, and the rules of racing. Trainers in other disciplines may obtain certification through breed or discipline organisations, or develop reputations through apprenticeship and competitive success.
