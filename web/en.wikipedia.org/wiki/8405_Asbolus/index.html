8405 Asbolus is a centaur orbiting the Sun between the orbits of Jupiter and Neptune. It was discovered on 5 April 1995 by James V. Scotti and Robert Jedicke of the Spacewatch program at Kitt Peak National Observatory. It was named after Asbolus, a centaur from Greek mythology who was a diviner and soothsayer.

Orbit and characteristics

Asbolus follows a highly eccentric, unstable orbit typical of centaurs, crossing the orbits of the giant planets; such orbits persist only for a few million years before planetary perturbations eject the body or send it into the inner Solar System. Its orbit has a semi-major axis of about 17.9 AU and a period of roughly 76 years.

In 2000, observations with the Hubble Space Telescope revealed a fresh, bright impact crater on Asbolus, which appeared as a bright icy spot on one hemisphere—an unusual finding, since most centaurs are dark, reddened bodies. The discovery suggested that fresh water ice had been exposed by a collision within roughly the last 10 million years, offering a rare glimpse of unweathered interior material.
