Overall efficiency

Overall efficiency is the ratio of useful output to total input for a complete system, taking into account every stage of conversion and loss between the original energy or resource supplied and the final useful effect obtained.

Definition

Where a system consists of several stages in series — for example a boiler, a turbine and a generator in a power station — the overall efficiency is the product of the individual stage efficiencies. A chain of stages each 90% efficient, for instance, yields an overall efficiency well below 90% after only a few steps, which is why system-level figures are often far lower than component ratings.

Applications

The concept is used in thermodynamics and power engineering (overall thermal efficiency of a plant), in lighting (wall-plug efficiency from electricity to light), in transport (well-to-wheel efficiency) and in economics and operations, where overall equipment effectiveness is a related but distinct measure.
