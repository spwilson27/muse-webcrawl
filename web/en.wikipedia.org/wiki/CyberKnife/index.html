CyberKnife

Placeholder image: CyberKnife

The CyberKnife system is a robotic radiosurgery device manufactured by Accuray Incorporated, used for treating benign tumors, malignant tumors, and other medical conditions. It delivers stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS) and stereotactic body radiation therapy (SBRT) using a compact linear accelerator mounted on a robotic arm, guided by real-time imaging.

History

The system was developed by John R. Adler Jr., a neurosurgeon at Stanford University, together with Russell Adler and colleagues, drawing inspiration from the Gamma Knife concept but using a frameless, robotic delivery platform. The first patient was treated at Stanford in 1994, and the system received FDA clearance for treatment of tumors in the head and neck in the 1990s, with clearance for treatment anywhere in the body following in 2001.

Technology

Unlike frame-based systems, CyberKnife uses image guidance and fiducial markers or direct tumor tracking (such as the Synchrony system for respiratory motion) to locate the target continuously during treatment. The robotic arm positions the accelerator at hundreds of orientations, allowing many non-coplanar beams to converge on the tumor with submillimeter accuracy while sparing surrounding healthy tissue. Treatments are non-invasive — despite the name, no incision is made — and are typically delivered in one to five sessions on an outpatient basis.

Clinical uses

CyberKnife is used for intracranial lesions, spinal tumors, lung cancer, prostate cancer, liver and pancreatic tumors, and trigeminal neuralgia, among other indications. Later versions include the CyberKnife VSI and M6 systems with multileaf collimation.
