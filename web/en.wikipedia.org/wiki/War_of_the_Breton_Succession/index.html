War of the Breton Succession

The War of the Breton Succession was a conflict between the counts of Blois and the Montforts of Brittany for control of the Duchy of Brittany between 1341 and 1364. It formed a major theatre of the Hundred Years' War between England and France: the Blois faction was supported by the French crown, the Montfort faction by England.

Background

When Duke John III of Brittany died childless in 1341, the succession was contested between his niece Joan of Penthièvre, who claimed through her husband Charles of Blois, and his half-brother John of Montfort. King Philip VI of France recognised Charles of Blois, while John of Montfort sought the support of Edward III of England.

The war

The war was marked by sieges, raids and a handful of pitched battles, and by the celebrated exploits of figures such as Joanna of Flanders, wife of John of Montfort, who defended Hennebont in 1342, and Bertrand du Guesclin. The Combat of the Thirty in 1351, a staged fight between thirty knights and squires of each side, became famous in chivalric literature. Charles of Blois was killed at the Battle of Auray in 1364, ending effective Blois resistance.

Settlement

By the Treaty of Guérande in 1365, John of Montfort's son was recognised as Duke John IV of Brittany, while the Penthièvre family retained their estates and a residual claim. Brittany remained within the French orbit but with considerable autonomy.
