Jeong (philosophy)

Jeong (Korean: 정, Hanja: 情) is a Korean philosophical and cultural concept describing a deep, strong feeling of attachment, affection, and emotional bond that develops between people over time. It is often compared, imperfectly, with concepts such as love, compassion, sympathy, and community, but it is distinguished by its emphasis on accumulated shared experience rather than on a sudden or romantic emotion.

Characteristics

Jeong is said to grow gradually through repeated contact, shared meals, mutual care, and the weathering of difficulties together. Unlike a feeling that is chosen or declared, jeong is understood to "seep in" naturally. Once formed, it is believed to be extremely durable: a common saying holds that even people who dislike each other can develop jeong through sustained proximity, and that jeong, once established, is very hard to break.

The concept encompasses a range of related feelings, including affection toward family members, loyalty between friends, warmth toward neighbours, and even attachment to places and objects associated with one's life. It also has a communal dimension, being associated with the Korean concepts of uri ("we" or "us") and collective solidarity.

Cultural significance

Jeong is frequently cited by Koreans as a defining feature of Korean emotional life and social relations. It influences everyday behaviour, including generosity in sharing food, reluctance to cut ties, and a strong sense of obligation toward those with whom one shares jeong. Scholars have discussed jeong in relation to Confucian values of relational harmony, while also noting that it can complicate boundaries between personal relationships and professional or public life.

The opposite of jeong is sometimes given as han, a feeling of sorrow, resentment, and unresolved longing; the two concepts are often discussed together in Korean cultural commentary.
