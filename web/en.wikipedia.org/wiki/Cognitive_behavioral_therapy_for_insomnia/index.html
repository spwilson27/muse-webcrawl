Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia

[Image placeholder: A sleep diary and clock used in CBT-I]

Cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia (CBT-I) is a structured, evidence-based technique for treating insomnia without the use of sleeping medication. It is recommended as the first-line treatment for chronic insomnia by the American College of Physicians and other medical bodies.

Components

  • Stimulus control: re-associating the bed with sleep by using it only for sleep, leaving when unable to sleep, and keeping a fixed wake time.
  • Sleep restriction: temporarily limiting time in bed to actual sleep time to build sleep pressure, then gradually extending it.
  • Cognitive therapy: identifying and restructuring unhelpful beliefs and worries about sleep.
  • Sleep hygiene education: guidance on caffeine, alcohol, exercise, light and the bedroom environment.
  • Relaxation training (sometimes included).

Evidence

Meta-analyses show CBT-I produces durable improvements in sleep onset latency, wake after sleep onset and sleep efficiency, with benefits persisting after treatment ends — unlike hypnotic medications. It is typically delivered over four to eight sessions, in person, in groups, or through validated digital programmes.
