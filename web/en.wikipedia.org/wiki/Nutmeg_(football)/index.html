Nutmeg (football)

A nutmeg (or meg) is a skill in association football in which a player kicks, rolls, or dribbles the ball through an opponent's legs, usually while keeping possession on the other side. It is considered one of the most humiliating moves a defender can suffer and is celebrated by crowds as a display of close control and cheek.

Technique

The nutmeg is usually performed at close quarters, when a defender approaches square-on with legs apart. The attacker plays the ball through the gap with the inside of the foot and accelerates past to collect it. Timing is critical: the move works best when the defender is committed to a tackle or moving in a predictable direction. Skilled dribblers such as Ronaldinho, Lionel Messi, and Neymar have made the nutmeg a signature element of their play.

Etymology

The origin of the term is debated. One theory links it to Cockney rhyming slang, in which "nutmegs" means legs. Another points to the Victorian nutmeg trade, where to be "nutmegged" was slang for being deceived or made a fool of, after exporters were tricked with wooden imitations of the spice. In Spanish-speaking football the move is called a caño (pipe), and in Brazilian Portuguese a caneta.

In other sports

Equivalent moves exist in ice hockey, where a player can slide the puck between an opponent's legs or skates, and in basketball, where the ball may be bounced through a defender's legs.
