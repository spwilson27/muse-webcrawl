Shah Rukn-e-Alam

Shah Rukn-e-Alam (Urdu: شاہ رکن عالم; 1251 – 1335), formally Sheikh Rukn-ud-Din Abul Fath, was a 13th–14th century Punjabi Sufi saint of the Suhrawardiyya order, based in Multan, in present-day Pakistan. He is one of the most venerated saints of the Indian subcontinent, and his tomb in Multan is one of the most important and architecturally distinguished Sufi shrines in South Asia.

Life

Rukn-e-Alam was born in Multan in 1251, the grandson of the celebrated saint Bahauddin Zakariya, founder of the Suhrawardi order in Multan, and son of Sheikh Sadruddin Arif. He succeeded his father as head of the Suhrawardi hospice (khanqah) in Multan and became renowned for his learning, generosity, and spiritual authority. Tradition holds that he enjoyed cordial relations with the Tughluq sultans of Delhi; Ghiyath al-Din Tughluq is said to have consulted him, and the saint is credited with predicting the sultan's death. He died in 1335.

Tomb

The mausoleum of Shah Rukn-e-Alam stands in the Multan Fort, built originally between 1320 and 1324 by Ghiyath al-Din Tughluq, who is said to have intended it for himself before giving it to the saint. It is considered the earliest surviving example of Tughluq architecture: a massive octagonal structure of red brick with sloping (battered) walls, wooden banding, and glazed blue tile decoration, crowned by a large dome. The shrine draws tens of thousands of devotees annually, particularly during the saint's urs festival, and the tomb is depicted on Pakistan's five-rupee note.
