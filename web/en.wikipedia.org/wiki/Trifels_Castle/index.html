Trifels Castle

Trifels Castle (German: Reichsburg Trifels) is a reconstructed medieval castle near the small town of Annweiler in the Palatinate region of Rhineland-Palatinate, Germany. It stands on a red sandstone rock 500 metres above sea level on the Sonnenberg hill, split into three peaks — hence its name, "triple rock". As an Imperial castle (Reichsburg) of the Salian and Hohenstaufen emperors it guarded the treasury of the Holy Roman Empire.

History

First mentioned in 1081, Trifels came into imperial hands in the twelfth century. It is most famous as the prison of Richard the Lionheart: captured on his way home from the Third Crusade, Richard was held at Trifels in 1193 until a huge ransom — some 100,000 pounds of silver — was negotiated. The Imperial Regalia of the Holy Roman Empire were kept at the castle at times, guarded (according to tradition) by the monks of a nearby abbey.

Decline and reconstruction

The castle decayed after the Thirty Years' War and the wars of Louis XIV, and was struck by lightning and largely ruined. Romantic-era interest led to excavations, and in the late 1930s–40s the Nazi regime began rebuilding it as a monument; reconstruction was completed in a more measured style by 1966. Today the castle is a popular tourist attraction, housing a museum with replicas of the Imperial Regalia and exhibitions on the Staufer period.
