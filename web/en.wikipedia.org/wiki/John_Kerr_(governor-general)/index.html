Sir John Robert Kerr (24 September 1914 – 24 March 1991) was an Australian barrister and judge who served as the 18th Governor-General of Australia, in office from 1974 to 1977. He is best known for his role in the 1975 Australian constitutional crisis, in which he dismissed the government of Prime Minister Gough Whitlam and appointed opposition leader Malcolm Fraser as caretaker prime minister—the only dismissal of an Australian government by a governor-general.

Legal career

Kerr was admitted to the New South Wales bar and developed a prominent practice, becoming a Queen's Counsel and president of the NSW Bar Association. He served as a judge of the Commonwealth Industrial Court and other tribunals, and in 1972 became Chief Justice of New South Wales.

The Dismissal

In late 1975 the opposition-controlled Senate blocked the government's supply bills, creating a deadlock. On 11 November 1975, after Whitlam declined to advise an election for the House of Representatives, Kerr withdrew his commission as prime minister and commissioned Fraser on condition that supply be passed and a double dissolution election called. Labor supporters reacted with outrage, and Kerr faced public protest for the remainder of his term; he resigned in 1977 and moved overseas for a period. The extent of any consultation between Kerr and others, including suggestions of contact with foreign intelligence services, has been debated by historians ever since; the release of the "palace letters" in 2020 renewed public discussion.

Later life

Kerr published a memoir, Matters for Judgment (1978), defending his actions. He died in Sydney in 1991.
