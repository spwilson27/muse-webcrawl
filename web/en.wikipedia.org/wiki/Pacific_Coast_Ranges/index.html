The Pacific Coast Ranges (sometimes also called the Pacific Coast Range or Pacific Mountain System) are the series of mountain ranges that stretch along the West Coast of North America from Alaska south to Northern and Central Mexico. They are part of the North American Cordillera (sometimes known as the Western Cordillera), the essentially continuous sequence of mountain ranges that form the western "backbone" of North, Central and South America.

Geography

The system runs roughly 5,300 kilometers (3,300 miles) from the Alaska Range foothills and the Saint Elias Mountains of Alaska and Yukon, through the Coast Mountains of British Columbia — the largest single range of the system — and down through the Olympic Mountains, Oregon Coast Range, Klamath Mountains, California Coast Ranges, Transverse Ranges and Peninsular Ranges of California, ending in the Sierra de la Laguna of Baja California Sur. The ranges are characteristically rugged, heavily forested in the north, and rise abruptly from the Pacific shoreline, often with no intervening coastal plain.

Geology and climate

The ranges were built by the accretion of oceanic terranes and by subduction and strike-slip faulting along the active western margin of the North American Plate, including the Queen Charlotte, San Andreas and related fault systems; many of the higher peaks of Alaska and British Columbia are heavily glaciated volcanoes or plutonic massifs, and the tallest, Mount Fairweather in the Saint Elias range, reaches 4,653 meters. The ranges force moist Pacific air upward, producing some of the wettest temperate rainforests on Earth along the British Columbia and Alaskan coasts, strong rain shadows inland, and Mediterranean and chaparral climates in California. Coast redwoods and Sitka spruce forests occupy the foggy coastal belts.

Major constituent ranges

  • Saint Elias Mountains and Coast Mountains (Alaska, Yukon, British Columbia)
  • Insular Mountains of Vancouver Island and Haida Gwaii
  • Olympic Mountains (Washington)
  • Oregon Coast Range and Klamath Mountains
  • California Coast Ranges, Transverse Ranges (San Gabriel, San Bernardino) and Peninsular Ranges
  • Sierra de la Laguna and related Baja California ranges
