Lotus Software

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Lotus Software Formerly: Lotus Development Corporation
Founded: 1982, Cambridge, Massachusetts, U.S.
Founders: Mitch Kapor, Jonathan Sachs
Fate: Acquired by IBM (1995); brands sold to HCL (2018)

Lotus Software (called Lotus Development Corporation before its acquisition) was an American software company founded in 1982 by Mitch Kapor and Jonathan Sachs. Its spreadsheet program Lotus 1-2-3, released in January 1983, was the IBM PC's first major "killer application" and for several years the dominant software product of the personal computer era.

History

Lotus 1-2-3 combined spreadsheet calculation with database functions and charting, and was the first product supported by a television advertising campaign. The company grew explosively, becoming one of the largest independent software vendors of the 1980s. Lotus later acquired the groupware platform Lotus Notes, developed from Ray Ozzie's work and released in 1989, which pioneered collaborative client-server software and became the company's flagship enterprise product alongside the SmartSuite office applications.

IBM and after

In 1995 IBM acquired Lotus for about US$3.5 billion, principally to obtain Notes. The Lotus brand persisted within IBM's software division for products including Notes/Domino, Sametime and Connections. In 2018 IBM sold the Notes/Domino portfolio and other former Lotus products to HCL Technologies, ending the brand's use by IBM; development continues under HCLSoftware.
