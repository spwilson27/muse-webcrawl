Apache Point Observatory (APO) is an astronomical observatory in the Sacramento Mountains in Sunspot, New Mexico, United States, adjacent to the National Solar Observatory's Sacramento Peak site. It is owned and operated by the Astrophysical Research Consortium (ARC).

Telescopes

The observatory's principal instruments are the ARC 3.5-metre telescope, used for a broad range of imaging and spectroscopy, and the 2.5-metre Sloan Foundation telescope, built for the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS), which has mapped large fractions of the sky and obtained spectra of millions of galaxies, quasars and stars. Smaller instruments include the NMSU 1-metre telescope and the ARCSAT 0.5-metre telescope.

APOLLO

APO hosts the Apache Point Observatory Lunar Laser-ranging Operation (APOLLO), which uses the 3.5 m telescope to time laser pulses reflected from retroreflectors left on the Moon by Apollo missions and Soviet Lunokhod rovers. APOLLO's millimetre-precision measurements test general relativity, including the strong equivalence principle and any time variation of the gravitational constant.
