Scone Palace is a Category A listed historic house and 200-acre public-access estate near the village of Scone, in Perth and Kinross, Scotland. It is the seat of the Earl of Mansfield and stands on the site of Scone Abbey, the traditional crowning place of the Kings of the Scots.

Scone Palace, Perthshire.

History

Scone was the ancient coronation site of Scottish monarchs, who were inaugurated on the Moot Hill seated upon the Stone of Scone (Stone of Destiny). Kenneth MacAlpin is traditionally said to have become king of a united Scotland here in the 9th century; Robert the Bruce was crowned at Scone in 1306, and Charles II was crowned there in 1651, the last coronation held at Scone. The abbey was sacked by a mob in 1559 during the Scottish Reformation.

The palace

The present red sandstone palace, in late Georgian Gothic style, was completed in 1808 for the Murray family, Earls of Mansfield. Its state rooms contain collections of furniture, porcelain, ivory and clocks, and the grounds include gardens, a pinetum and a maze. A replica of the Stone of Scone sits on the Moot Hill, topped by the palace chapel; the original stone was returned to Scotland in 1996 and is displayed in Perth Museum.
