Private military company

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A private military company (PMC) or private military and security company (PMSC) is a private company providing armed combat or security services for financial gain. PMCs refer to their personnel as "security contractors" or "private military contractors". The services and expertise offered by PMCs are typically similar to those of governmental security, military, or police forces, most often on a smaller scale.

Services

While PMCs provide services to various clients, in the areas of armed security, the services offered include armed guarding and protection of buildings and persons, convoy escort, training of local forces, military advice and intelligence, maintenance and operation of weapons systems, detention and interrogation support, and logistical support. Clients include governments, corporations (especially extractive industries operating in unstable regions), shipping companies guarding against piracy, and humanitarian organisations.

History

The modern industry emerged after the end of the Cold War, when demobilised soldiers and surplus equipment entered the market and states reduced standing armies. Companies such as Executive Outcomes and Sandline International fought directly in African civil wars in the 1990s. The industry expanded enormously during the wars in Iraq and Afghanistan, when contractors at times outnumbered uniformed troops in theatre; the 2007 Nisour Square shooting by Blackwater employees in Baghdad, in which 17 Iraqi civilians were killed, became the defining scandal of the industry. In the 2010s the Russian-aligned Wagner Group operated as a state-linked PMC in Syria, Ukraine and several African states.

Legal status and regulation

PMC personnel occupy an ambiguous position in international law: they are generally civilians under the Geneva Conventions unless formally incorporated into armed forces, and most are not legally "mercenaries" under the narrow definition of the 1989 UN Mercenary Convention. Regulation has proceeded through instruments such as the Montreux Document (2008), which restates states' obligations regarding PMCs, and the voluntary International Code of Conduct for Private Security Service Providers (2010).
