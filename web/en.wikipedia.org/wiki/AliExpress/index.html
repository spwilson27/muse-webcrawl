AliExpress (Chinese: 全球速卖通) is an online retail service based in China and owned by the Alibaba Group. Launched in 2010, it is a marketplace made up of small businesses in China and elsewhere offering products to international online buyers, and it has become one of the most visited e-commerce websites in the world.

Business model

AliExpress began as a business-to-business buying and selling portal and expanded to business-to-consumer and consumer-to-consumer sales. Unlike Alibaba.com, which connects manufacturers and wholesalers, AliExpress lets individual sellers — mostly Chinese small businesses — sell directly to overseas consumers, often in single units and with low-cost shipping. The platform does not itself sell goods to consumers, and it differs from Amazon in that it acts only as an e-commerce platform and does not hold inventory.

Reach and regulation

The site is offered in numerous languages including English, Russian, Spanish, Portuguese, and French, and is especially popular in Russia, Brazil, Spain, and other markets outside China; it does not sell to customers in mainland China itself. Payments are held in escrow until buyers confirm receipt. AliExpress has faced scrutiny in the European Union, where in 2023 the European Commission designated it a Very Large Online Platform under the Digital Services Act, and regulators have investigated sales of counterfeit and unsafe goods on the platform.
