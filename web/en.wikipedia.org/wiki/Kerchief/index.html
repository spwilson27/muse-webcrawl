Kerchief

A woman wearing a kerchief

A kerchief (from the French couvre-chef, "cover the head"), also known as a bandana or bandanna, is a triangular or square piece of cloth tied around the head, face, or neck for protective or decorative purposes. The popularity of head kerchiefs may vary by culture or religion, and may vary among Orthodox Christian, Jewish, and Muslim women, among others.

History

Kerchiefs have been worn since ancient times as protection from sun, wind, and dust. The word "bandana" derives from the Hindi bandhnū, a tie-dyeing technique, and entered English through Portuguese in the 18th century for colourful silk and cotton handkerchiefs imported from India.

Handkerchief

A related item, the handkerchief or "pocket square," is a smaller square of thin fabric carried in a pocket or handbag and used for personal hygiene purposes such as wiping one's hands or face, or blowing one's nose. Decorative pocket squares are worn with suits as a fashion accessory.

In culture

Coloured bandanas have served as signals and symbols for many groups, from cowboys and railway workers to motorcycle riders and, in the 1970s and 1980s, as colour-coded signals in some subcultures. The image of "Rosie the Riveter" wearing a polka-dot kerchief became an emblem of women's wartime work.
