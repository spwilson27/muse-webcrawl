Alpha Group

Alpha Group (Russian: Группа «Альфа»), officially the Directorate "A" of the FSB Special Purpose Centre, is an elite Russian special forces and counter-terrorism unit of the Federal Security Service (FSB). Founded in 1974 within the KGB of the Soviet Union, it is one of the best-known Spetsnaz formations, alongside its sister unit Vympel (Directorate "V").

History

Alpha was created on 29 July 1974 by order of KGB chairman Yuri Andropov, partly in response to the 1972 Munich Olympics massacre, to conduct counter-terrorist operations. Its first major operations included the storming of the Amin palace in Kabul in December 1979 (Operation Storm-333), which opened the Soviet–Afghan War. Alpha personnel served throughout the Afghan war and in Soviet internal crises, including the January 1991 events in Vilnius, Lithuania, where its participation in the assault on the television tower remains controversial.

During the August 1991 coup attempt in Moscow, Alpha officers famously refused orders to storm the Russian White House. After the dissolution of the USSR, the unit passed to the Russian security services, eventually the FSB in 1998.

Operations in Russia

Alpha has taken part in Russia's major hostage crises, including the Budyonnovsk hospital crisis (1995), the Moscow theatre siege (2002), and the Beslan school siege (2004), in which several Alpha operators were killed. The unit also conducts counter-terrorism operations in the North Caucasus and protection and arrest missions for the FSB.

Organisation

Alpha is headquartered in Moscow with regional detachments in cities including Krasnodar, Yekaterinburg, and Khabarovsk. Recruitment is from experienced officers, and training emphasises marksmanship, hostage rescue, explosive breaching, and close protection.
