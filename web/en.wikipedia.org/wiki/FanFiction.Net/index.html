FanFiction.Net (often abbreviated as FF.net or FFN) is an automated fan fiction archive website. It was founded in 1998 by Los Angeles-based programmer Xing Li, who also runs the sister site FictionPress, for original fiction. It became one of the largest and most popular fan fiction archives in the world, hosting millions of stories in dozens of languages across anime, book, film, game, television, and other fandom categories.

History

Xing Li created the site in 1998 as a hobby project. Early growth was rapid: writers could upload stories in chapters, readers could review each chapter, and the site organised works by fandom, genre, rating, and characters. In 2002 the site removed NC-17-rated works, and over the years it removed categories of content at the request of some copyright holders — for a time, works based on books by certain authors who objected to fan fiction, such as Anne Rice, were prohibited. In 2001 the site banned "choose your own adventure" and second-person interactive stories.

Features and culture

Stories are rated using a FictionRatings.com scale (K, K+, T, M). Registered users can follow and favourite stories and authors, post reviews, join communities, and use forums. The site hosts some of the longest works of fiction ever written in any medium, including multi-million-word fan novels. FanFiction.Net played a central role in popularising fan fiction terminology and large-scale online fandom; many published authors began by posting there.

Criticism and decline

The site has been criticised for intrusive advertising, infrequent design updates, and episodes of mass content purges (the 2012 removal of explicit content, sometimes called the "Great Purge"). From the 2010s, the younger nonprofit-run Archive of Our Own (AO3), launched by the Organization for Transformative Works, drew many writers away, though FanFiction.Net remains one of the largest archives by total number of stories.
