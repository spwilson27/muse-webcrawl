Palestine (region)

Palestine is a geographic region in the southern Levant, in Western Asia, conventionally bounded by the Jordan River to the east, the Mediterranean Sea to the west, and stretching from the area of the Litani River in the north to the Negev and Sinai in the south. The name derives from the "Sea Peoples" known as the Philistines, who settled its southern coast in the twelfth century BCE, and was formalised in Greek and Roman usage — as Palaestina — for the Roman province of Syria Palaestina. Across three millennia the region has been ruled by Canaanites, Israelites, Assyrians, Persians, Greeks, Romans, Byzantines, Arab caliphates, Crusaders, Mamluks, Ottomans and the British, and it is sacred to Judaism, Christianity and Islam. Today the region comprises the State of Israel and the Palestinian territories of the West Bank and Gaza Strip.

Name and history of the term

Egyptian records of the thirteenth century BCE mention the Peleset among the Sea Peoples; Herodotus used Palaistine in the fifth century BCE for the coastal district of Syria. After quelling the Bar Kokhba revolt in 135 CE, Rome renamed the province of Judaea as Syria Palaestina. The name persisted through Byzantine and Islamic administration — the early caliphate organised the military district of Jund Filastin — and in European usage, while "the Holy Land" was the common Christian designation. Under the British Mandate (1920–1948), "Palestine" named the whole mandated territory, and the term was applied to its Arab and Jewish inhabitants alike.

Geography

From west to east the region falls into four bands: a fertile coastal plain; the central hill country of Galilee, Samaria and Judaea; the deep Jordan Rift Valley, containing the Sea of Galilee and the Dead Sea — the lowest land on Earth; and the eastern plateau of Transjordan in some broader definitions. The climate is Mediterranean, with hot dry summers and cool wet winters, grading to desert in the Negev and Jordan Valley.

History in brief

The region lay at the crossroads of empires throughout antiquity and the Middle Ages. It saw the kingdoms of Israel and Judah, Hellenistic and Roman rule, the birth of Christianity and the ministry of Jesus, the Jewish revolts against Rome, and — after the seventh-century Muslim conquest — inclusion in successive caliphates, interrupted by the Crusader states (1099–1291). Ottoman rule lasted from 1517 to the First World War. The British Mandate, the 1948 war and the creation of Israel, and the 1967 occupation of the West Bank and Gaza define its modern political geography and the continuing Israeli–Palestinian conflict.
