Goodness of fit

The goodness of fit of a statistical model describes how well it fits a set of observations. Measures of goodness of fit typically summarise the discrepancy between observed values and the values expected under the model in question. Such measures can be used in statistical hypothesis testing, for example to test for normality of residuals, to test whether two samples are drawn from identical distributions, or to test whether outcome frequencies follow a specified distribution.

Fit of distributions

In assessing whether a given distribution fits a data set, common tests include:

  • Pearson's chi-squared test — compares observed and expected frequencies in categories, testing whether sample data are consistent with a hypothesised distribution.
  • Kolmogorov–Smirnov test — based on the maximum difference between the empirical and hypothesised cumulative distribution functions.
  • Anderson–Darling test — a modification of Kolmogorov–Smirnov that weights the tails of the distribution more heavily.
  • Shapiro–Wilk test — specifically tests whether a sample comes from a normally distributed population.
  • Likelihood-ratio test and information criteria such as AIC and BIC, which compare nested or rival models while penalising complexity.

Regression analysis

In regression, goodness of fit is summarised by quantities such as the coefficient of determination (R²), which gives the proportion of variance in the dependent variable accounted for by the model, and by residual diagnostics. In generalised linear models, deviance and Pearson residuals are used, and the Hosmer–Lemeshow test is applied to logistic regression.

Interpretation

A goodness-of-fit test takes the model as its null hypothesis; a small p-value indicates that the observed data would be unlikely if the model were correct. Failing to reject the model does not prove it is true — only that the data do not provide strong evidence against it. Because any model is an approximation, assessments of fit are usually combined with subject-matter judgement, residual plots and out-of-sample validation.
