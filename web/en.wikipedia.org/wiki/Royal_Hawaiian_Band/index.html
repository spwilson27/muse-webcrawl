Royal Hawaiian Band

Origin: Honolulu, Hawaii
Founded: 1836
Type: Municipal concert band

The Royal Hawaiian Band is a municipal band based in Honolulu, Hawaii. Founded in 1836 by King Kamehameha III, it is the oldest and only full-time municipal band in the United States.

History

Kamehameha III established the band in 1836, and it performed for Hawaiian monarchs for the rest of the kingdom era. Its most famous bandmaster was Heinrich (Henry) Berger, a Prussian musician sent by Kaiser Wilhelm I, who led the band from 1872 and arranged hundreds of Hawaiian songs, earning the title "Father of Hawaiian Music". The band famously refused to swear allegiance after the overthrow of the monarchy in 1893, an episode remembered in the song and story of the "Band Boys".

Today

Now an agency of the City and County of Honolulu, the band gives regular free concerts, including weekly performances at the ʻIolani Palace bandstand and at the Kapiʻolani Park Bandstand, and plays Hawaiian standards such as "Hawaiʻi Ponoʻī" and "Aloha ʻOe".
