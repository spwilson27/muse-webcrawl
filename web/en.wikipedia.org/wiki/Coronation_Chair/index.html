Coronation Chair

The Coronation Chair, also known as St Edward's Chair or King Edward's Chair, is an ancient wooden chair on which British monarchs sit when they are invested with regalia and crowned at their coronations. It was commissioned in 1296 by King Edward I to contain the Stone of Scone, which he had captured from the Scots. The chair is kept in Westminster Abbey, London.

Description

The high-backed Gothic chair is made of oak and was originally painted and gilded, with four gilt lions forming its feet (renewed in 1727). Beneath the seat is a cavity that held the Stone of Scone until 1996, when the Stone was returned to Scotland; it is now brought to London for coronations, as at the coronation of Charles III in 2023. The chair bears extensive graffiti, much of it carved by visitors and Westminster schoolboys in the 18th and 19th centuries.

History

Every English and British monarch since 1308 is understood to have been crowned in the chair, with a few exceptions. It was damaged by a suffragette bomb attack in 1914 and narrowly survived the Second World War in hiding at Gloucester Cathedral. In 1950 Scottish nationalists removed the Stone from the Abbey; it was recovered in 1951.
