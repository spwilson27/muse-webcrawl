Data rate units

In telecommunications and computing, bit rate is the number of bits conveyed or processed per unit of time. Data rates are commonly expressed in bits per second (bit/s, bps) or bytes per second (B/s), with SI prefixes used for larger multiples. This article summarizes the units used to express data transfer rates.

One byte equals eight bits, so a rate of 1 megabyte per second (MB/s) corresponds to 8 megabits per second (Mbit/s). Network speeds are usually quoted in bits per second, while file transfer speeds are often shown in bytes per second, a frequent source of confusion.

Decimal and binary prefixes

In telecommunications, prefixes are decimal: a kilobit is 1,000 bits and a megabit is 1,000,000 bits. In some computing contexts, binary prefixes (kibi-, mebi-, gibi-) denoting powers of 1,024 are used for quantities of bytes, e.g. 1 MiB = 1,048,576 bytes. Standards bodies such as the IEC and IEEE recommend using decimal prefixes for data rates.

Examples

Typical figures include: dial-up modems at 56 kbit/s; ADSL broadband at a few to tens of Mbit/s; Fast Ethernet at 100 Mbit/s; Gigabit Ethernet at 1 Gbit/s; and modern Wi-Fi and fiber-optic links reaching multiple Gbit/s.
