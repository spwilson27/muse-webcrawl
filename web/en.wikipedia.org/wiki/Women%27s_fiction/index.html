Women's fiction

Women's fiction is an umbrella term for books that are marketed to female readers, and includes many mainstream novels, romantic fiction, "chick lit", and other subgenres. The term is principally a marketing category rather than a description of content or authorship: women's fiction is generally written for women, frequently by women, and often centres on women's emotional lives and relationships.

Debate

The label is controversial. Critics note that equivalent categories do not exist for books aimed at men, and that shelving work by women under "women's fiction" can marginalise or trivialise it. Others argue the category helps readers find books and recognises the commercial importance of women readers, who buy the majority of fiction. The Romance Writers of America defines women's fiction as a commercial genre focused on a woman's emotional journey, distinct from romance in that a romantic plot need not be central or end happily.

Related genres

Related categories include chick lit, domestic fiction, book-club fiction and upmarket fiction, as well as the long tradition of the domestic novel and sensation fiction in the nineteenth century.
