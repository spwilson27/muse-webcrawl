Overview

Sir Andrew Fielding Huxley (1917–2012) was an English physiologist, half-brother of the writer Aldous Huxley and grandson of the biologist T. H. Huxley. As an undergraduate he began working with Alan Hodgkin on the squid giant axon, recording the first intracellular action potentials in 1939.

Work

After war work on radar and gunnery, Huxley rejoined Hodgkin for the 1952 papers that produced the Hodgkin–Huxley equations of the action potential, sharing the 1963 Nobel Prize. He then turned to muscle, developing interference microscopy and, with Rolf Niedergerke, proposing (independently of Hugh Huxley and Jean Hanson) the sliding filament theory of muscle contraction in 1954. He was President of the Royal Society (1980–85) and Master of Trinity College, Cambridge.
