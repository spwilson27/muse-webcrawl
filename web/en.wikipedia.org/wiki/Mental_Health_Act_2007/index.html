Mental Health Act 2007

Mental Health Act 2007
United Kingdom statute
Royal assent: 19 July 2007
Amends: Mental Health Act 1983

The Mental Health Act 2007 (c. 12) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom. It amended the Mental Health Act 1983 and the Mental Capacity Act 2005. It applies to England and Wales. The Act received royal assent on 19 July 2007, and most of its provisions came into force in November 2008.

Main provisions

The 2007 Act made several major changes to mental health law:

  • Definition of mental disorder: it replaced the 1983 Act's categories with a single broad definition—"any disorder or disability of the mind"—removing exclusions while continuing to exclude dependence on alcohol or drugs alone.
  • Appropriate treatment test: it replaced the "treatability" test with a requirement that appropriate medical treatment be available for a patient before detention on long-term powers can be imposed.
  • Community treatment orders (CTOs): it introduced supervised community treatment, allowing certain patients discharged from hospital detention to be treated in the community subject to conditions and recall.
  • Professional roles: it broadened the range of professionals who can act as approved mental health professionals (AMHPs) and responsible clinicians, roles previously restricted largely to social workers and doctors respectively.
  • Nearest relative: it allowed patients to apply to displace a nearest relative and extended the category to include civil partners.
  • Safeguards: it introduced independent mental health advocates (IMHAs) and reduced the automatic renewal periods, while amending the Mental Capacity Act to add deprivation-of-liberty safeguards (DoLS).

Debate

The bill was controversial and took years of negotiation. Critics, including professional bodies and service-user groups, raised concerns about community treatment orders and the broadened definition of mental disorder, while the government argued the reforms balanced public safety with patients' rights.
