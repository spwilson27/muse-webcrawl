John Maynard Smith

John Maynard Smith FRS (6 January 1920 – 19 April 2004) was a British theoretical and mathematical evolutionary biologist and geneticist. Originally an aeronautical engineer during the Second World War, he later trained in zoology at University College London under J. B. S. Haldane, and went on to become one of the most influential evolutionary theorists of the twentieth century.

Maynard Smith is best known for applying game theory to evolution, developing the concept of the evolutionarily stable strategy (ESS) with George R. Price. His books, including The Evolution of Sex (1978) and Evolution and the Theory of Games (1982), helped found the field of evolutionary game theory.

Early life and education

Born in London, Maynard Smith was educated at Eton College and studied engineering at Trinity College, Cambridge. During the war he worked on aircraft design. After becoming disillusioned with engineering, he studied zoology at University College London, where Haldane became his mentor. He spent most of his academic career at the University of Sussex, where he was a founding member of the School of Biological Sciences and served as its dean.

Research

His research spanned the evolution of ageing, the evolution of sex and recombination, animal signalling, and the major transitions in evolution. With Eörs Szathmáry he wrote The Major Transitions in Evolution (1995), which examined how new levels of biological organisation arise. He was awarded the Darwin Medal in 1986, the Kyoto Prize in 2001, and the Copley Medal in 1999.
