Rosas (dance company)

Founded: 1983, Brussels, Belgium

Founder: Anne Teresa De Keersmaeker

Type: Contemporary dance company

Rosas is a Brussels-based contemporary dance company founded in 1983 by the Belgian choreographer Anne Teresa De Keersmaeker. One of the most influential dance companies in Europe, Rosas takes its name from its breakthrough production Rosas danst Rosas ("Rosas dances Rosas"), premiered in 1983, a rigorously structured work for four women set to music by Thierry De Mey and Peter Vermeersch.

History

De Keersmaeker formed the company after the success of Fase (1982), a duet to the phase-shifting music of Steve Reich. Rosas danst Rosas established her signature style: repetitive, mathematically patterned movement interwoven with everyday gestures, performed to minimalist scores. Through the 1980s and 1990s the company produced landmark works including Bartók/Mikrokosmos (1987), Stella (1990), Amor constante más allá de la muerte (1994), and Drumming (1998), again set to Reich.

From 1992 to 2007 Rosas was company-in-residence at the Brussels opera house La Monnaie. In 1995 De Keersmaeker founded the dance school P.A.R.T.S. (Performing Arts Research and Training Studios) in Brussels, which has trained generations of contemporary dancers and choreographers. Later productions include Rain (2001), En Atendant and Cesena (2010–11), created with early-music ensembles, Vortex Temporum (2013) to the music of Gérard Grisey, and Mitten wir im Leben sind/Bach6Cellosuiten (2017), performed with cellist Jean-Guihen Queyras.

Influence

In 2011 a public controversy arose when elements of the choreography of Rosas danst Rosas appeared in Beyoncé's music video "Countdown"; De Keersmaeker responded by publishing the choreography's movement material openly, and a worldwide participatory project, Re:Rosas, invited anyone to learn and film the famous chair sequence. The company continues to tour internationally and remains a central institution of Flemish and European contemporary dance.
