Acid jazz

Stylistic originsJazz, funk, soul, hip hop, acid house, disco
Cultural originsLate 1980s, London, United Kingdom
Typical instrumentsTurntables, keyboards, brass, bass, drums

Acid jazz is a music genre that combines elements of jazz, soul, funk, disco, and hip hop. It emerged in the London club scene of the mid-to-late 1980s, driven by DJs such as Gilles Peterson, and developed into a commercially successful movement in the 1990s through acts including Jamiroquai, the Brand New Heavies, Incognito, and US3.

History

The term is often credited to DJ Gilles Peterson, who co-founded the Acid Jazz Records label in 1987; a popular story holds that he coined it jokingly in response to the acid house craze. Rare-groove DJs had been playing 1970s jazz-funk records at high tempo in clubs; producers began sampling those records and combining live instrumentation with hip hop beats and electronic production.

Characteristics

Acid jazz typically features looped grooves, jazz harmony and improvisation, horn sections, Fender Rhodes and Hammond organ textures, and sometimes rapped or soulful sung vocals. Unlike jazz fusion, its forms are usually groove- and dancefloor-oriented rather than virtuosic.

Notable artists and labels

Key acts include Jamiroquai, the Brand New Heavies, Incognito, Galliano, Guru (Jazzmatazz), and US3, whose "Cantaloop (Flip Fantasia)" sampled Herbie Hancock. Labels such as Acid Jazz Records and Talkin' Loud, and the related Japanese and European scenes, sustained the style, whose influence persists in nu-jazz and broken beat.
