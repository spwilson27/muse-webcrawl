Acoustic absorption

Acoustic absorption is the process by which a material, structure or object takes in sound energy when sound waves are encountered, converting part of the energy into heat or transmitting it, rather than reflecting it. Absorption is a key concept in acoustics and in the design of quiet, intelligible indoor spaces.

Mechanism

Porous absorbers such as mineral wool, foam and fibreglass dissipate sound energy through friction as air molecules oscillate within their pores. Panel (membrane) absorbers and Helmholtz resonators absorb sound by resonant vibration, and are effective at particular low frequencies. The effectiveness of an absorber is expressed by its absorption coefficient, ranging from 0 (total reflection) to 1 (total absorption), and by the Noise Reduction Coefficient (NRC).

Applications

Absorptive materials are used in concert halls, recording studios, offices, classrooms and industrial noise control to reduce reverberation time and improve speech intelligibility. In anechoic chambers, deep absorptive wedges reduce reflections to near zero.
