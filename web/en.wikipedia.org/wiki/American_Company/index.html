American Company

Founded: c. 1752
Founder: David Douglass
Type: Theatre company
Significance: First professional theatre company in the American colonies

The American Company was a professional theatre company active in the British American colonies and the early United States from about 1752 into the early nineteenth century. Descended from the company brought to Virginia in 1752 by Lewis Hallam under the name the London Company of Comedians, it became known as the American Company under the management of David Douglass, and it is credited with establishing professional theatre in America.

History

Hallam's company landed at Yorktown, Virginia, in 1752 and performed in Williamsburg and then in New York, Philadelphia and Charleston, facing colonial suspicion of the stage and, in some colonies, outright bans. After Hallam's death in Jamaica, his widow married David Douglass, who reorganised the troupe, built theatres — including the Southwark Theatre in Philadelphia (1766) and the John Street Theatre in New York (1767) — and presented a repertoire of Shakespeare and contemporary London plays.

The first American play

On 16 April 1767 the company performed Thomas Godfrey's The Prince of Parthia in Philadelphia, the first professionally produced play written by an American. The company withdrew to the West Indies during the Revolutionary War, when the Continental Congress discouraged theatre, and returned after independence as the Old American Company under John Henry and Lewis Hallam Jr., performing in New York and Philadelphia until its leading members merged into newer companies around the turn of the century.
