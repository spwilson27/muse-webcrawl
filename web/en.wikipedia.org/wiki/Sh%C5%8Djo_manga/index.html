Shōjo manga

Shōjo manga (少女マンガ) is an editorial category of Japanese comics targeting an audience of adolescent females and young adult women. It traditionally features romance and relationships, with distinctive visual conventions such as large, sparkling eyes, floral motifs and fluid panel layouts.

History

Shōjo manga arose in pre-war girls' magazines. In the 1970s the Year 24 Group — women artists including Moto Hagio and Keiko Takemiya — revolutionised the genre with psychologically complex stories and works such as The Rose of Versailles by Riyoko Ikeda.

Notable works

Influential titles include Candy Candy, Sailor Moon, Fruits Basket, Nana and Cardcaptor Sakura. Dedicated magazines such as Nakayoshi, Ribon and Margaret serialise the genre.

Style

The category is defined by its target readership rather than content alone; stories range from school romance to fantasy, horror and science fiction, often emphasising interiority and emotion.
