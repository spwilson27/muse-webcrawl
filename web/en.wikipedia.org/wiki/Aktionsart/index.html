Aktionsart (German: [ʔakˈtsi̯oːnsˌʔaːɐ̯t], plural Aktionsarten) is a term from linguistics meaning the inherent temporal structure of a verb — the way the action or state it denotes unfolds in time. It is also called lexical aspect, to distinguish it from grammatical aspect, which is marked by verb forms. The concept entered general linguistic discussion through the study of Slavic and Germanic languages, in which prefixes and other derivational devices systematically modify a verb's actional character.

Vendler's classification

Zeno Vendler's influential 1957 classification divides verb meanings into four kinds: states (e.g. know, love), which are static and homogeneous; activities (e.g. run, push a cart), which proceed in time without a built-in endpoint; accomplishments (e.g. build a house), which have duration and a natural conclusion; and achievements (e.g. reach the summit, win), which are near-instantaneous transitions. Later work (Dowty and others) decomposed these classes into semantic features such as dynamicity, duration and telicity.

Interaction with grammatical aspect

A verb's Aktionsart constrains how it combines with grammatical aspect: states resist progressive forms in English ("*I am knowing"), and achievements combine differently with duration adverbials ("in an hour" versus "for an hour"). Languages differ in whether actionality is expressed lexically, derivationally (as with Slavic verbal prefixes such as Russian pere- or German ver-), or contextually.
