Orchomenus (Boeotia)

Orchomenus (Greek: Ὀρχομενός) was an ancient city of Boeotia, Greece, and is now a municipality in the Boeotia regional unit. In the Bronze Age it was one of the richest and most powerful cities of Greece, the centre of the Minyan people, famed in myth for the wealth of its treasury.

History

The site was inhabited from Neolithic times. In the Mycenaean period (c. 1600–1200 BCE) Orchomenus controlled the drainage works of Lake Copais, whose reclamation — attributed to vast dikes and canals — created rich farmland. Pausanias described its tholos tomb, the "Treasury of Minyas", as a wonder comparable to the pyramids; the tomb, excavated by Heinrich Schliemann in the 1880s, featured an elaborately carved ceiling in its side chamber.

In historical times Orchomenus was a member of the Boeotian League but often at odds with Thebes; Thebans destroyed the city in 364 BCE, and it was later rebuilt under Macedonian patronage. A famous battle was fought nearby in 86 BCE, when Sulla defeated the forces of Mithridates VI of Pontus during the First Mithridatic War.

Archaeology

Remains include the tholos tomb, the theatre, the Hellenistic tower, and the church of the Dormition of the Theotokos (Panagia), built in 874 over an ancient temple and preserving notable early Byzantine sculpture.
