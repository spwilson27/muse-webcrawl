William C. Campbell (scientist)

Born: 28 June 1930, Ramelton, County Donegal, Ireland
Known for: Discovery of avermectin / ivermectin
Awards: Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (2015)
Field: Parasitology

William Cecil Campbell (born 28 June 1930) is an Irish-American parasitologist who shared the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for his role in the discovery of avermectin, the basis of ivermectin, a drug that has dramatically reduced the incidence of river blindness (onchocerciasis) and lymphatic filariasis.

Early life and education

Campbell was born in Ramelton, County Donegal, Ireland. He studied zoology at Trinity College Dublin, graduating in 1952, and earned a PhD from the University of Wisconsin–Madison in 1957.

Career

From 1957 to 1990 Campbell worked at the Merck Institute for Therapeutic Research in New Jersey. In the 1970s his screening programme identified, in a soil sample from Japan supplied by Satoshi Ōmura, a Streptomyces culture whose product — avermectin, later modified to ivermectin — proved extraordinarily potent against parasitic worms. Merck donated ivermectin for the treatment of river blindness in Africa, transforming control of the disease.

Nobel Prize

In 2015 Campbell and Ōmura jointly received one half of the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine "for their discoveries concerning a novel therapy against infections caused by roundworm parasites"; the other half went to Tu Youyou for artemisinin.
