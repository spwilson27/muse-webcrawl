Inborn errors of metabolism

Inborn errors of metabolism
FieldMedical genetics, metabolic medicine
Term coinedArchibald Garrod, 1908
ExamplesPhenylketonuria, alkaptonuria, Tay–Sachs disease, Gaucher disease
InheritanceUsually autosomal recessive
DetectionNewborn screening (e.g., tandem mass spectrometry)

Inborn errors of metabolism are a large class of genetic disorders in which a single gene defect causes a clinically significant block in a metabolic pathway, resulting in accumulation of a substrate, deficiency of a product, or production of toxic by-products. Individually rare, they collectively affect roughly 1 in 1,000 to 1 in 2,500 births.

History

The concept originated with the British physician Archibald Garrod, who studied alkaptonuria and, in his 1908 Croonian Lectures and 1909 book Inborn Errors of Metabolism, proposed that inherited "chemical individuality" underlies such disorders — one of the founding insights of human genetics.

Classification and presentation

Disorders are grouped by the affected pathway: amino acid disorders (phenylketonuria, maple syrup urine disease), organic acidaemias, urea cycle defects, lysosomal storage diseases (Gaucher, Tay–Sachs, Fabry), peroxisomal and mitochondrial disorders, and defects of carbohydrate metabolism (galactosaemia, glycogen storage diseases). Symptoms range from acute neonatal encephalopathy, vomiting and hypoglycaemia to developmental delay and organ damage.

Diagnosis and treatment

Expanded newborn screening using tandem mass spectrometry allows presymptomatic detection of dozens of conditions from a dried blood spot. Treatments include dietary restriction (the low-phenylalanine diet for PKU), cofactor supplementation, enzyme replacement therapy, substrate reduction, and in some cases liver or bone-marrow transplantation; gene therapies are under development. Early treatment dramatically improves outcomes for many disorders.
