Intrathecal administration

Intrathecal administration is a route of administration for drugs via injection into the spinal canal, more specifically into the subarachnoid space (the intrathecal space) so that the drug reaches the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF). It is useful for applications that need to act directly on the central nervous system or to bypass the blood–brain barrier.

Uses

Spinal anaesthesia is commonly given intrathecally, as are some chemotherapy agents for leukaemia or lymphoma involving the central nervous system (for example, methotrexate), certain antibiotics for meningitis, and analgesics. Implantable intrathecal pumps deliver drugs such as baclofen for severe spasticity or morphine for chronic pain at very low doses with fewer systemic side effects. Antisense therapy for spinal muscular atrophy, nusinersen, is also administered intrathecally.

Technique and risks

The injection is usually performed by lumbar puncture below the level of the conus medullaris to avoid injuring the spinal cord. Formulations must be preservative-free, since preservatives and some drugs are neurotoxic in the CSF: accidental intrathecal administration of vincristine, for example, is almost invariably fatal. Other risks include post-dural-puncture headache, infection, and bleeding.
