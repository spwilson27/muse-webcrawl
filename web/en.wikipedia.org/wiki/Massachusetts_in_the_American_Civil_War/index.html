Massachusetts in the American Civil War

The Commonwealth of Massachusetts played a significant role in national events leading up to and during the American Civil War (1861–1865). A centre of abolitionist activism, the state was among the first to respond to President Abraham Lincoln's call for troops. Massachusetts furnished roughly 159,000 men to the Union Army and Navy, organised into 62 regiments of infantry, six regiments of cavalry, 16 batteries of artillery, and other units.

First blood

On April 19, 1861 — the anniversary of Lexington and Concord — the 6th Massachusetts Militia was attacked by a pro-Confederate mob while passing through Baltimore, Maryland, in the Baltimore riot of 1861. Four soldiers were killed, making them among the first Union combat deaths of the war, and the regiment was celebrated in the North as the "first to fall".

Notable units and contributions

Massachusetts raised the 54th Massachusetts Infantry Regiment, one of the first African American regiments in the Union Army, led by Colonel Robert Gould Shaw and immortalised for its assault on Fort Wagner in 1863. The state was also a shipbuilding and manufacturing centre: the Boston Navy Yard and private yards produced warships, and mills and factories supplied uniforms, arms, and equipment. Leading political figures included Governor John Albion Andrew, a staunch abolitionist who energetically organised the state's war effort, and Senator Charles Sumner. Clara Barton, founder of the American Red Cross, came from Massachusetts, and the state's women organised extensive relief work.
