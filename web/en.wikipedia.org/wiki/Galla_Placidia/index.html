Aelia Galla Placidia (388–392 – 27 November 450) was a Roman empress and one of the most powerful women of the late Roman world. Daughter of the emperor Theodosius I and half-sister of the emperors Arcadius and Honorius, she was successively a hostage and queen of the Visigoths, wife of the emperor Constantius III, and regent of the Western Roman Empire for her son Valentinian III.

Life

Placidia was raised in Constantinople and then at the western court. Taken captive by Alaric's Visigoths during the sack of Rome in 410, she travelled with them into Gaul and in 414 married their king, Ataulf, at Narbonne in a Roman ceremony; Ataulf was assassinated the following year, and after a period of humiliating treatment under his successor Sigeric she was ransomed back to the Romans. In 417 she married the general Constantius, who briefly reigned as Constantius III in 421. Widowed again and exiled after a breach with her brother Honorius, she returned from Constantinople in 425 with an eastern army that overthrew the usurper Joannes and placed her young son Valentinian III on the western throne.

Regency

As Augusta and regent, Placidia dominated the western government during her son's minority and remained a central political figure until her death, balancing the rival generals of the age — Bonifatius, Aëtius and Felix — and maintaining close ties with the eastern court. A devout Christian, she was a noted church builder, responsible for churches in Ravenna including San Giovanni Evangelista.

Legacy

The small cruciform building in Ravenna known as the Mausoleum of Galla Placidia, with its luminous blue and gold mosaics of the Good Shepherd and starry heavens, is one of the masterpieces of early Byzantine art, though she was in fact buried in Rome. Placidia's long career at the centre of power makes her life a lens on the final decades of the Western Empire.
