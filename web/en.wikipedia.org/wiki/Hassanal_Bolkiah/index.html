Hassanal Bolkiah Mu'izzaddin Waddaulah (born 15 July 1946) is the Sultan of Brunei, reigning since his father Sultan Omar Ali Saifuddien III abdicated in 1967. He is the 29th sultan of a dynasty claiming descent from the 14th century, head of state and head of government of an absolute monarchy, and was for decades routinely listed among the richest individuals on Earth, his fortune built on Brunei's oil and gas (Shell's Seria field) and managed through the Brunei Investment Agency.

Reign

Educated at Sandhurst, the Sultan led Brunei to full independence from Britain in 1984 and shaped its ideology of MIB — Melayu Islam Beraja (Malay Islamic Monarchy) — under which Bruneians enjoy free healthcare and education, subsidised housing and no income tax. The 1997–98 Asian financial crisis and the collapse of his brother Prince Jefri's Amedeo conglomerate cost the state billions and produced a famous family legal war. The Sultan's palaces, hotels (the Dorchester collection), polo and a car collection reported in the thousands are global bywords for royal opulence. In the 2010s he phased in a strict Syariah penal code — the final phase (2019), including stoning provisions for adultery and homosexuality, triggered international boycotts of his hotels; a moratorium on the death penalty was announced within weeks and Brunei has not carried out an execution since 1957. With no elected legislature and a state of emergency in force since 1962, succession planning for Crown Prince Al-Muhtadee Billah is the monarchy's quiet central question.
