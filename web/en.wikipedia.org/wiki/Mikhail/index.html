Mikhail

Mikhail (Russian: Михаил) is a masculine given name of Hebrew origin, the East Slavic form of Michael, meaning "Who is like God?". It is common in Russia and other countries influenced by Orthodox Christian naming traditions.

Usage

The name was borne by numerous Russian rulers and notables, including Mikhail I, the first Romanov tsar (reigned 1613–1645), Mikhail Gorbachev, the last leader of the Soviet Union, cosmonaut-era figures, scientists and artists. Diminutives include Misha.

Related names

Cognates include Michael (English), Michel (French), Miguel (Spanish and Portuguese), Mikkel (Scandinavian) and Mikael. The feminine form in some traditions is Mikhaila.
