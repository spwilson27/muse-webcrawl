Novartis

Novartis AG is a Swiss multinational pharmaceutical corporation headquartered in Basel, Switzerland. It is one of the largest pharmaceutical companies in the world by revenue and market capitalisation. Novartis was formed in 1996 from the merger of Ciba-Geigy and Sandoz, two Basel chemical and pharmaceutical firms with roots in the 19th-century dye industry—at the time the largest corporate merger in history.

Business

After a series of divestments—including the spin-off of the eye-care division Alcon in 2019 and of its generics and biosimilars division Sandoz in 2023—Novartis has concentrated on innovative prescription medicines. Its major therapy areas include cardiovascular and metabolic disease, immunology, neuroscience, and oncology. Well-known products include the heart-failure drug Entresto (sacubitril/valsartan), the psoriasis and arthritis biologic Cosentyx (secukinumab), the multiple sclerosis drug Kesimpta (ofatumumab), and the cancer therapy Kisqali (ribociclib). Novartis was an early mover in cell and gene therapy, winning the first FDA approval for a CAR-T cell therapy, Kymriah, in 2017, and markets the gene therapy Zolgensma for spinal muscular atrophy.

Research

Research is carried out chiefly by the Novartis Institutes for BioMedical Research (now Novartis Biomedical Research), headquartered in Cambridge, Massachusetts, with major sites in Basel and elsewhere. The company spends on the order of US$10 billion annually on research and development. See also Sandoz, pharmaceutical industry, and Basel.
