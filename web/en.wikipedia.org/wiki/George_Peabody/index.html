George Peabody

George Peabody (February 18, 1795 – November 4, 1869) was an American financier and philanthropist.

Born: South Danvers (now Peabody), Massachusetts
Died: London, England
Known for: Father of modern philanthropy; Peabody Trust; Peabody Institute

George Peabody was an American banker, merchant, and philanthropist who founded the banking firm of Peabody & Co. and is widely regarded as the father of modern philanthropy. His charitable trusts in Britain and the United States became models for later benefactors such as Andrew Carnegie and John D. Rockefeller.

Life and business

Born in Massachusetts to a poor family, Peabody had little schooling and rose through the dry-goods trade in Baltimore before moving into finance. Settling in London in 1837, he built George Peabody & Co. into a leading merchant bank dealing in American securities; in 1854 he took on Junius Spencer Morgan as a partner, and the firm is an ancestor of both JPMorgan Chase and Morgan Stanley. During the Panic of 1857 he famously propped up confidence in American credit in London.

Philanthropy

Peabody gave away most of his fortune during his lifetime. In London he founded the Peabody Donation Fund (1862, now the Peabody Trust), which built model housing for the capital's working poor and still provides social housing today. In the United States he founded the Peabody Institute and library in Baltimore, endowed museums at Harvard and Yale, supported libraries in his home town (renamed Peabody, Massachusetts, in his honour), and established the Peabody Education Fund (1867) to promote education in the war-devastated South.

Death and legacy

Peabody died in London in 1869 and was temporarily buried in Westminster Abbey — a rare honour for an American — before his body was returned to Massachusetts aboard HMS Monarch. Congress awarded him a gold medal in 1867; Queen Victoria reportedly offered him a baronetcy, which he declined. The Peabody Awards for broadcasting, administered by the University of Georgia, also bear his name.
