Epcot

Epcot, stylised in all uppercase as EPCOT, is a theme park at the Walt Disney World Resort in Bay Lake, Florida. It is owned and operated by The Walt Disney Company through its Parks, Experiences and Products division. Inspired by an unrealised concept developed by Walt Disney, the park opened on 1 October 1982 as EPCOT Center, and was the second of four theme parks built at Walt Disney World.

Concept and history

Walt Disney's original EPCOT — the "Experimental Prototype Community of Tomorrow" — was to be a planned city of some 20,000 residents showcasing innovation in urban living. After Disney's death in 1966, the company shelved the city concept but retained the name for its second Florida park. EPCOT Center opened in 1982 at a cost of roughly $1.4 billion, one of the largest construction projects of its time. Its icon is Spaceship Earth, an 18-storey geodesic sphere.

Areas

The park spans about 305 acres and is divided into two main sections:

  • World Celebration, World Discovery, and World Nature (formerly the single area Future World) — pavilions devoted to technology, science, energy, the seas, the land, and space, including attractions such as Test Track, Mission: SPACE, Soarin', and Guardians of the Galaxy: Cosmic Rewind.
  • World Showcase — eleven pavilions arranged around a lagoon, representing the cultures of Mexico, Norway, China, Germany, Italy, the United States, Japan, Morocco, France, the United Kingdom, and Canada.

Festivals and attendance

Epcot hosts popular annual festivals, including the International Flower and Garden Festival, the International Food & Wine Festival, the International Festival of the Arts, and the International Festival of the Holidays. It regularly ranks among the most-visited theme parks in the world, drawing roughly 10–12 million guests per year in recent years.
