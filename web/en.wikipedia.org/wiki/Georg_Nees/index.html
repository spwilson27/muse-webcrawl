Georg Nees (23 June 1926 – 3 January 2016) was a German mathematician, physicist and philosopher who became one of the founders of computer art. Working at Siemens in Erlangen, he produced some of the earliest computer-generated graphics ever exhibited as art.

Life and work

Nees studied mathematics and physics at the universities of Erlangen and Tübingen, earning his doctorate in 1951. He later studied philosophy and wrote a dissertation on the semiotics of aesthetics under the influence of Max Bense's information aesthetics. From 1955 he worked at Siemens, where he gained access to early computers and a Zuse Graphomat flatbed plotter.

Computer graphics

In February 1965 Nees held the exhibition Generative Computergraphik at the Studiengalerie of the Technische Hochschule Stuttgart — widely regarded as the first exhibition of computer art, predating the better-known New York shows of the same year. His plotter drawings, generated by programs that combined deterministic structures with pseudo-random parameters, included works such as 23-Ecke (23-Corner) and Schotter (Gravel), in which a grid of squares dissolves into disorder.

Nees published the book Generative Computergraphik in 1969, based on his second dissertation, and helped shape the terminology of generative aesthetics. His work influenced later generations of generative and algorithmic artists.
