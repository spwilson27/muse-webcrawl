Cereblon

Cereblon
GeneCRBN (chromosome 3p26.2 in humans)
FunctionSubstrate receptor of the CRL4CRBN E3 ubiquitin ligase
Known ligandsThalidomide and other immunomodulatory drugs (IMiDs)
Clinical relevanceThalidomide embryopathy; multiple myeloma therapy

Cereblon is a protein that in humans is encoded by the CRBN gene. It is the substrate-recognition subunit of the CRL4CRBN E3 ubiquitin ligase complex (with DDB1, CUL4A/B, and RBX1), which tags other proteins with ubiquitin for degradation by the proteasome. Cereblon became famous as the primary direct target of thalidomide and related immunomodulatory drugs (IMiDs), a discovery that explained both the drugs' therapeutic effects and thalidomide's notorious teratogenicity.

Discovery as the thalidomide target

In 2010, a team led by Hiroshi Handa in Tokyo used thalidomide immobilised on magnetic beads to fish out its binding partners from cell extracts and identified cereblon. Mutations or knockdown of cereblon in zebrafish and chicks reproduced the limb and fin malformations characteristic of thalidomide embryopathy, establishing cereblon binding as the cause of the drug's birth defects. Mutations in the human CRBN gene had earlier been associated with autosomal recessive intellectual disability, reflecting the protein's roles in the brain.

Mechanism of IMiD action

Thalidomide and its analogues lenalidomide and pomalidomide act as "molecular glues": by binding cereblon, they reshape its surface so that it recruits proteins it would not normally target — so-called neosubstrates — for ubiquitination and destruction. In multiple myeloma, the critical neosubstrates are the zinc-finger transcription factors Ikaros (IKZF1) and Aiolos (IKZF3), whose destruction kills myeloma cells. In embryos, degradation of other substrates such as SALL4 underlies the teratogenic effect. Cereblon's normal physiological substrates include the transcription factor MEIS2 and the large-conductance potassium channel subunit involved in neuronal function.

Targeted protein degradation

Cereblon is now one of the most widely exploited E3 ligases in drug discovery. It is the recruiting element in many PROTACs (proteolysis-targeting chimeras) and a new generation of cereblon E3 ligase modulators (CELMoDs), designed to degrade disease-causing proteins that conventional inhibitors cannot reach.
