Valençay is a French goat's-milk cheese made in the town of Valençay and the surrounding Berry countryside in the Loire Valley, in the department of Indre. It is instantly recognisable by its shape — a truncated pyramid coated in wood ash, which matures to a blue-grey rind — and is protected by an appellation d'origine contrôlée (AOC) granted in 1998, making it, unusually, a product sharing both a cheese AOC and a wine AOC name (Valençay wine).

The Talleyrand legend

According to the famous story, Valençay cheese was originally moulded as a full pyramid. After Napoleon's disastrous Egyptian campaign, the cheese was served at a banquet at the Château de Valençay, home of Napoleon's foreign minister Talleyrand. Lest the shape remind the emperor of his defeat among the pyramids, Talleyrand is said to have sliced off the top with a sword — or ordered the cheeses made flat-topped thereafter — giving Valençay its enduring truncated form. Like many food legends, the tale is more often repeated than documented, but it fixed the cheese's identity.

Production and character

Valençay is made from raw whole goat's milk, coagulated slowly by lactic fermentation with a small addition of rennet, ladled into pyramid moulds, drained, then unmoulded, salted, and dusted with ash (traditionally charcoal ash, today food-grade vegetable ash). It matures for at least eleven days from renneting. Fresh, the interior is white, dense, and lemony; with age it softens beneath the rind, which develops blue-grey moulds over the ash, and the flavour deepens to a nutty, earthy tang. The ash coating, a pre-refrigeration preservative technique shared with other Loire goat cheeses, also moderates the surface acidity so the rind can form.

Valençay is served at room temperature on a cheese board, where its shape makes it a centrepiece, and pairs naturally with the Sauvignon-based white wines of its own AOC — Valençay blanc — or with neighbouring Loire whites such as Sancerre and Quincy. It belongs to the great family of Loire goat cheeses, alongside Sainte-Maure de Touraine, Selles-sur-Cher, Pouligny-Saint-Pierre, and the little Crottin de Chavignol.
