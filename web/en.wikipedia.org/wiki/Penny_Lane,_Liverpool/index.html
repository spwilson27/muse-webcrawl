Penny Lane, Liverpool


Location: Mossley Hill / Allerton, Liverpool, England
Known for: The Beatles song "Penny Lane"

Penny Lane is a street in the Mossley Hill and Allerton districts of Liverpool, England, near the childhood homes of John Lennon and Paul McCartney. It was immortalised by the Beatles' 1967 song "Penny Lane", written largely by McCartney from memories of the bus terminus and shops at the Smithdown Place end of the street.

History

The street existed by the eighteenth century and was named on maps of the period. Its name was long said to derive from a toll or "penny" charge, though later research linked it to James Penny, a Liverpool slave trader—an association that brought the street into debate during the Black Lives Matter protests of 2020, when its signs were defaced before the International Slavery Museum concluded the connection to Penny could not be firmly established and the name was retained.

The song

Released as a double A-side with "Strawberry Fields Forever" in February 1967, "Penny Lane" describes the barber shop, bank, fire station, and "shelter in the middle of the roundabout" near the street's northern end. The song reached number one in the United States. Street signs from Penny Lane became among the most stolen in Britain, prompting the city to paint signs on walls instead; a signed sign presented by McCartney in 2018 is displayed at The Beatles Story museum.

Today

Penny Lane remains a working suburban street and a major stop on Beatles tourism tours of Liverpool, with the Penny Lane Development Trust community centre near its northern end.
