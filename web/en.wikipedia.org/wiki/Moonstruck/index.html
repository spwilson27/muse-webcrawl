Moonstruck

Moonstruck is a 1987 American romantic comedy-drama film directed by Norman Jewison, written by John Patrick Shanley, and starring Cher, Nicolas Cage, Olympia Dukakis, Vincent Gardenia, and Danny Aiello. It tells the story of Loretta Castorini, a widowed Italian-American bookkeeper in Brooklyn who falls in love with her fiancé's estranged younger brother.

Plot

Loretta, 37, accepts a marriage proposal from the cautious Johnny Cammareri, who flies to Sicily to attend his dying mother. Tasked with inviting his brother Ronny — a baker who lost a hand in an accident he blames on Johnny — to the wedding, Loretta instead falls passionately for him under an unusually large full moon. Around them, her father carries on an affair, her mother discovers it and questions the meaning of love, and her aunt and uncle are caught up in the moon's spell. The film ends with a chaotic family breakfast and a double engagement.

Reception and awards

Moonstruck was a critical and commercial success, grossing about $122 million worldwide. At the 60th Academy Awards it won three Oscars — Best Actress for Cher, Best Supporting Actress for Dukakis, and Best Original Screenplay for Shanley — from six nominations. Dean Martin's recording of "That's Amore" opens the film, and Puccini's La bohème figures centrally in the romance.
