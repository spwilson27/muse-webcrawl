Amelogenin

Amelogenin
Type: Enamel matrix protein
Genes: AMELX (X chromosome), AMELY (Y chromosome)

Amelogenins are a group of protein isoforms produced by alternative splicing and proteolytic processing of the amelogenin gene. They are the most abundant proteins of developing tooth enamel — comprising up to about 90% of the enamel matrix secreted by ameloblasts — and they guide the organised growth of hydroxyapatite crystals during enamel formation (amelogenesis).

Genetics

In humans, amelogenin is encoded by genes on both sex chromosomes: AMELX on the X chromosome and AMELY on the Y chromosome. Because the X and Y copies differ slightly in sequence and size, PCR of the amelogenin locus is widely used in forensic science and prenatal testing to determine the sex of a DNA sample. Mutations in AMELX cause X-linked amelogenesis imperfecta, a disorder of enamel formation.

Function

Amelogenin self-assembles into nanospheres that space and orient enamel crystallites; the protein is progressively removed by proteases such as MMP20 and KLK4 as the enamel matures and hardens. Recombinant amelogenin and its peptide derivatives have been investigated for biomimetic enamel repair.
