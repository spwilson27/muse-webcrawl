Pietro da Cortona (born Pietro Berrettini; 1 November 1596 – 16 May 1669) was an Italian artist and architect of the Baroque era. Along with his contemporaries and rivals Gian Lorenzo Bernini and Francesco Borromini, he was one of the key figures in the emergence of Roman Baroque architecture. He was also an important designer of interior decorations.

Biography

Born in Cortona, in Tuscany, he trained in Florence and settled in Rome around 1612–13. He came to the attention of the Barberini family, and his career advanced rapidly under the patronage of Pope Urban VIII.

Painting

Cortona's most famous painting is the vast frescoed ceiling of the grand salon of the Palazzo Barberini in Rome, The Glorification of the Reign of Pope Urban VIII (also known as The Triumph of Divine Providence), executed between 1633 and 1639. It is a masterpiece of illusionistic Baroque ceiling painting, crowded with swirling figures seen di sotto in sù. He later painted extensive fresco cycles in the Pitti Palace in Florence for the Medici.

Architecture

His architectural works in Rome include the church of Santi Luca e Martina (1634–69), with its undulating façade, and the façade of Santa Maria della Pace, whose convex portico projects into its small piazza like a stage set. He also redesigned the church of Santa Maria in Via Lata.

Legacy

Cortona led the Accademia di San Luca and was a central figure in debates about the relative merits of exuberant, painterly Baroque decoration versus a more classical restraint — a controversy aired in his rivalry with the classicist painter Andrea Sacchi.
