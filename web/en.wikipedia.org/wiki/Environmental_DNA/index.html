Environmental DNA

Environmental DNA or eDNA is DNA collected from an environmental sample such as soil, seawater, snow, or air, rather than directly from an individual organism. Organisms shed DNA into their surroundings through skin, hair, scales, mucus, faeces, and other material.

Applications

Analysis of eDNA by PCR and metabarcoding allows researchers to detect species — including rare, invasive, or endangered ones — without capturing or observing them. It is widely used in aquatic ecology, biodiversity monitoring, and conservation biology. Challenges include DNA degradation, contamination, and the inability of the method by itself to reveal the size or health of a population.
