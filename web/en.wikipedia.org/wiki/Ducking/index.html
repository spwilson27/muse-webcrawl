Ducking

Ducking: the music level falls automatically when the announcer speaks

In audio engineering, ducking is an effect in which the level of one audio signal is automatically reduced by the presence of another signal. It is most commonly achieved with a compressor whose sidechain (detector) input is fed by the controlling signal: when the control signal exceeds a threshold, the compressor applies gain reduction to the main signal.

Applications

The archetypal use is in radio and television broadcasting, where background music is ducked under the announcer's voice so that speech remains intelligible, then swells back up in the pauses. The same technique is used for voice-over in film and video, in podcast production, in public-address systems (so announcements override music), and in automatic mixing in houses of worship and conference systems.

Creative use in music production

Producers in electronic dance music turned ducking into a conspicuous stylistic device: sidechaining a synthesiser pad or the whole mix to the kick drum produces the rhythmic "pumping" or "breathing" effect characteristic of French house and much modern EDM, in which the track audibly dips on every beat. A related manual technique, in which the engineer rides a fader to dip music under dialogue, predates the compressor implementation.
