Quattro (four-wheel-drive system)

quattro
Manufacturer: Audi AG
Introduced: 1980
Type: Permanent all-wheel drive

Quattro (meaning four in Italian) is the trademarked name used by the German car manufacturer Audi for its all-wheel-drive technology and for the models equipped with it. The system was first introduced in 1980 in the original Audi Quattro coupe, unveiled at the Geneva Motor Show that year.

History

The idea for a high-performance four-wheel-drive road car originated with Audi engineer Jörg Bensinger, who had observed the winter performance of the Volkswagen Iltis military vehicle during testing in Scandinavia. Development chief Ferdinand Piëch championed the project, and the resulting Ur-Quattro transformed rallying: with turbocharging and permanent all-wheel drive, Audi won the World Rally Championship manufacturers' titles in 1982 and 1984, and drivers' titles with Hannu Mikkola (1983) and Stig Blomqvist (1984).

Technology

Early quattro systems used manually lockable differentials. From 1987 the centre differential became a Torsen (torque-sensing) design that automatically varies the torque split between front and rear axles — typically 50:50 under normal conditions, able to send up to about 75 percent of torque to either axle. Later generations added electronic differential locks using the wheel brakes, and torque vectoring on the rear axle in performance models.

Transversely engined Audi models use a different system based on a Haldex-type multi-plate clutch, which is marketed as quattro but engages the rear wheels on demand rather than permanently.

Generations

The longitudinal quattro system has passed through several generations, from the original manually locked layout through successive Torsen centre differentials (T1, T2, T3), the crown-gear centre differential introduced in 2010, and systems paired with the S tronic dual-clutch and Tiptronic automatic gearboxes. The fully electric e-tron models use separate electric motors on each axle, marketed as electric quattro.
