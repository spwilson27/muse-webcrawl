Napster was a peer-to-peer (P2P) file sharing service, founded in 1999 by Shawn Fanning and Sean Parker, that pioneered the mass sharing of digital music files (MP3s) over the Internet. It allowed users to search a central index of files offered by other connected users and download them directly from one another's computers. At its peak in early 2001 it had around 80 million registered users.

History

Fanning developed Napster while a student at Northeastern University, releasing it in June 1999. The service spread virally on college campuses and quickly became one of the most popular applications on the Internet. Its ease of use — a simple search box and chat rooms organised by genre — made acquiring music dramatically easier than previous tools.

Legal challenges

In December 1999 the Recording Industry Association of America (RIAA) sued Napster for contributory and vicarious copyright infringement. Artists including Metallica and Dr. Dre also sued and demanded their works be blocked. In 2001 the U.S. Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals upheld an injunction against the service; unable to filter copyrighted material effectively, Napster shut down its network in July 2001 and filed for bankruptcy in 2002.

Legacy

Napster's brand was sold and relaunched as a paid, licensed music service in 2003, and later passed through various owners (including Best Buy and Rhapsody) as a streaming platform. Historically, Napster is credited with transforming the music industry's business model, popularising MP3s and peer-to-peer technology, and paving the way for legal download stores and streaming services.
