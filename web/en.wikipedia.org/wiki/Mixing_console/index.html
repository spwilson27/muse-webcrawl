Mixing console

Mixing console
Other namesAudio mixer, sound board, mixing desk
TypeAudio equipment
UsesRecording studios, live sound, broadcasting
Key componentsChannel strips, faders, EQ, buses, master section

A mixing console (or audio mixer, sound board, or mixing desk) is an electronic device for mixing audio signals. Inputs to the console are typically microphones and line-level sources such as instruments and playback devices. The console allows the operator to adjust each signal's level, timbre, dynamics, and stereo position, and to combine ("mix") them into one or more outputs. Mixing consoles are used in recording studios, public address and live concert sound, broadcasting, and film post-production.

A large-format analogue mixing console in a recording studio.

Structure

Channel strips

Each input channel is laid out as a vertical strip, typically containing: an input gain (trim) control and microphone preamplifier, often with phantom power for condenser microphones; equalization (EQ) controls; auxiliary sends feeding monitor mixes or external effects; a pan control; mute and solo buttons; and a sliding fader that sets the channel's contribution to the mix.

Buses and outputs

Signals are routed onto buses — the main stereo mix bus, subgroups (for processing several channels together), and auxiliary buses. The master section controls overall output level and feeds the main loudspeakers, recording device, or broadcast chain. Metering, from simple LED ladders to VU meters, displays signal levels to prevent overload and distortion.

Analogue and digital consoles

Analogue consoles process signals as continuous voltages through physical circuitry, prized by many engineers for their sound and hands-on layout. Digital consoles convert inputs with ADCs and mix in the digital domain using DSP, offering total recall of settings, onboard effects and dynamics processing on every channel, motorized faders, and remote control via tablet apps. Modern digital audio workstations replicate the console layout on screen as a "virtual mixer."

Applications

  • Recording studios — tracking and mixing music; classic large consoles by Neve and Solid State Logic (SSL) are strongly associated with the sound of countless hit records.
  • Live sound — front-of-house and monitor consoles mixing bands and events for audiences.
  • Broadcast — radio and television production, where consoles integrate with intercom and playout systems.
