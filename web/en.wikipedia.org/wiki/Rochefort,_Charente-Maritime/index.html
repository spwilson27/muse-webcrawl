Rochefort, Charente-Maritime

Rochefort is a commune in southwestern France, in the Charente-Maritime department in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region. It lies on the River Charente, about 30 km southeast of La Rochelle.

Founded in 1666 by Jean-Baptiste Colbert as a naval arsenal and dockyard for the French Navy, Rochefort was laid out on a grid plan. Its royal rope factory, the Corderie Royale (completed 1669), was for centuries among the longest buildings in Europe. The arsenal closed in 1926.

History and sights

The town preserves extensive 17th- and 18th-century naval architecture, including the dry docks, the Hôtel de la Marine, and the reconstructed frigate Hermione, a replica of the ship that carried Lafayette to America in 1780. Rochefort was the setting for Jacques Demy's 1967 musical film The Young Girls of Rochefort. The writer Pierre Loti lived here, and his house is now a museum.

Population is roughly 23,000. The commune is connected by bridge to the Île d'Aix and is a gateway to the Charente estuary marshes.
