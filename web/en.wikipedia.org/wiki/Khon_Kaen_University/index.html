Khon Kaen University (Thai: มหาวิทยาลัยขอนแก่น; abbreviated KKU) is a public research university in Khon Kaen, Thailand. Founded in 1966, it was the first university established in northeastern Thailand (Isan) and remains the largest university in the region, serving as a major center of education and research for the Greater Mekong Subregion.

History

Plans for a university in the northeast began in 1941, but were interrupted by the Second World War. In 1964 the Thai government approved the establishment of the university, initially as an institute of engineering and agriculture, and in 1966 it formally opened as the University of North East Thailand before being renamed Khon Kaen University. King Bhumibol Adulyadej presided over graduation ceremonies and the university's development from its earliest years.

Academics

The university comprises numerous faculties, including medicine, engineering, agriculture, science, humanities and social sciences, dentistry, pharmacy, nursing, veterinary medicine, and public health, together with Srinagarind Hospital, its teaching hospital. KKU is especially known for research in tropical medicine and parasitology, including the liver fluke Opisthorchis viverrini, which is endemic to the region, as well as agricultural science and Mekong-region development studies.

Campus

The main campus covers about 900 acres near the center of Khon Kaen city and includes lecture buildings, dormitories, the Golden Jubilee Convention Hall, and large reservoirs that supply the campus.
