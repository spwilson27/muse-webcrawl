Harlech Castle

Harlech Castle (Welsh: Castell Harlech) is a medieval fortification in Harlech, Gwynedd, Wales. Built by Edward I during his invasion of Wales between 1282 and 1289, it stands on a rocky crag overlooking the Irish Sea and is a Grade I listed building and a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the Castles and Town Walls of King Edward in Gwynedd.

History

Designed by Master James of St George, the castle cost about £8,190 to build — one of the least expensive of Edward's great Welsh castles. Its concentric design features a massive twin-towered gatehouse and a defensive "way from the sea", a fortified stairway down the cliff that allowed the garrison to be supplied by ship.

During the rising of Madog ap Llywelyn in 1294–95 the castle was besieged but held out thanks to seaborne supplies. In 1404 it fell to Owain Glyndŵr and became his residence and headquarters until it was retaken by English forces under the future Henry V in 1409. During the Wars of the Roses, Lancastrian forces held Harlech for seven years (1461–1468); its resistance inspired the song "Men of Harlech". In the English Civil War it was the last Royalist fortress to surrender, in March 1647.

Architecture

The castle is built of local sandstone in a concentric plan: an inner ward with four great round towers and the gatehouse, surrounded by a lower outer wall hugging the rock. The sea has since retreated, leaving the castle some distance inland from the modern shoreline.
