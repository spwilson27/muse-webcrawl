Yaroslavl (Russian: Ярославль) is a city in Russia, the administrative centre of Yaroslavl Oblast, located about 250 kilometres northeast of Moscow at the confluence of the Volga and Kotorosl rivers. Founded in 1010 by Prince Yaroslav the Wise of Kievan Rus', it is one of the oldest Russian cities and part of the Golden Ring of historic towns; its historical centre is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

Yaroslavl grew wealthy as a river port on the Volga trade route and, as a principality, was absorbed into Moscow in 1463. In 1612, during the Time of Troubles, it served for several months as the de facto capital of Russia, where the militia of Minin and Pozharsky gathered before liberating Moscow from Polish occupation. In the 17th century the city's merchants funded dozens of richly frescoed churches, including the Church of Elijah the Prophet, masterpieces of Russian architecture.

Economy and culture

Modern Yaroslavl is an industrial centre with oil refining, tyre and diesel-engine production, and a major Volga river port and railway junction. It is home to the Volkov Theatre, founded in 1750 and regarded as the first professional theatre in Russia. The city suffered tragedy in 2011 when a plane crash killed the entire Lokomotiv Yaroslavl ice hockey team.
