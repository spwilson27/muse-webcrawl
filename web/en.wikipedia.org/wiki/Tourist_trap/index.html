Tourist trap

A tourist trap is an establishment (or a group of establishments) created or re-purposed with the aim of attracting tourists and their money. Tourist traps typically provide overpriced services, entertainment, food, souvenirs, and other products for purchase. While some may be genuinely entertaining or historically significant, many are regarded as exploitative or kitsch.

Characteristics

Tourist traps are often found along major highways and near famous landmarks. Common features include oversized or novelty architecture (mimetic architecture), aggressive advertising on billboards, gift shops selling mass-produced souvenirs unrelated to local culture, and prices substantially higher than those at comparable establishments elsewhere. In some cases, the term is applied to an entire district that has become dominated by such businesses.

Examples

Famous American examples include Wall Drug in South Dakota, which grew from a small pharmacy into a sprawling roadside attraction through free ice water advertising, and South of the Border in South Carolina. In many older cities, areas immediately surrounding major attractions develop concentrations of souvenir shops and restaurants aimed almost exclusively at visitors, sometimes driving out businesses serving local residents — a phenomenon related to overtourism.

Debate

The label is subjective: some "tourist traps" are beloved institutions or preserve genuine folk history, and employment at such attractions can be an important part of local economies. Travel writers often advise visitors to venture a few streets away from major sights to find better value and more authentic experiences.
