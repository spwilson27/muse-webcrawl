Human calculator

A human calculator, also called a mental calculator or lightning calculator, is a person with a prodigious ability to perform complex arithmetic calculations mentally — such as multiplying large numbers, extracting roots, or determining the day of the week for a given date — rapidly and accurately, often faster than a mechanical or electronic calculator could be operated.

History

Mental calculators were figures of scientific curiosity and stage entertainment from at least the early 19th century. Performers such as Zerah Colburn and George Parker Bidder demonstrated their abilities before learned societies, and mathematicians studied their methods. Jedediah Buxton, an 18th-century English farm labourer, performed feats of computation despite being barely literate. In the 19th and early 20th centuries, "lightning calculators" were popular vaudeville and music-hall acts.

Notable mental calculators

  • Shakuntala Devi (1929–2013) — Indian calculator known as the "human computer," who in 1980 multiplied two 13-digit numbers in 28 seconds, a feat recorded in the Guinness Book of Records.
  • Wim Klein (1912–1986) — Dutch calculator who worked at CERN and extracted high-order roots of large numbers.
  • Truman Henry Safford (1836–1901) — American child prodigy who became an astronomer.
  • Daniel Tammet — British savant known for reciting pi to over 22,000 digits and for calendar calculation.
  • Rüdiger Gamm — German mental calculator known for computing high powers.
  • Alexis Lemaire — French calculator noted for 13th-root calculations.

Methods

Mental calculators use a variety of techniques, including memorized multiplication tables extended to large ranges, factorization, digit-by-digit algorithms, and cross-multiplication methods. Calendar calculators determine weekdays using congruences such as Zeller's congruence or the Doomsday rule. Competitive mental calculation is organized in events such as the Mental Calculation World Cup and Memoriad, where contestants perform tasks including multi-digit multiplication, square roots, and calendar dates under time pressure.

Calendar calculation

A common feat is naming the weekday of any given date. Practitioners anchor on known reference days and apply modular arithmetic for years, months, and leap years. The skill is also associated with autistic savants, some of whom can answer such questions for dates thousands of years in the past or future almost instantaneously.
