Electromagnetic coil

Electromagnetic coil
Type: passive electrical component
Forms: solenoid, toroid, winding
Property: inductance (henries)

An electromagnetic coil is a conductor, typically insulated copper wire, wound in the shape of a coil, spiral, or helix. When an electric current passes through it, the coil generates a magnetic field; conversely, a changing magnetic field through the coil induces a voltage in it.

Properties

The coil's inductance depends on the number of turns, the cross-sectional area, the coil's length, and the permeability of the core material. Concentrating turns around a ferromagnetic core, as in an electromagnet, greatly strengthens the field.

Forms and uses

A long cylindrical coil is a solenoid, used in valves, actuators, and electromagnets. Coils serve as the windings of transformers, motors, and generators; as inductors in electronic circuits; as ignition coils in petrol engines; and as the field and voice coils in loudspeakers and relays.

Inductive effects

By Lenz's law, a coil opposes changes in current through it, storing energy in its magnetic field. This property is exploited in filters, chokes, and switch-mode power supplies.
