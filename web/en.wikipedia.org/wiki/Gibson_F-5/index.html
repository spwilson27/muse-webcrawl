Gibson F-5

ManufacturerGibson
Introduced1922
TypeFlorentine-style carved-top mandolin
DesignerLloyd Loar

The Gibson F-5 is a carved-top mandolin model introduced by the Gibson Mandolin-Guitar Company in 1922 as the flagship of its "Master Model" line. Designed under acoustic engineer Lloyd Loar, the F-5 features f-shaped sound holes (rather than the oval hole of earlier models), a longer neck joint, a scroll-shaped "Florentine" body and a carved spruce top with maple back and sides, finished with elaborate binding and inlay.

Loar-era instruments

F-5s built and signed by Loar between 1922 and 1924 are among the most sought-after mandolins ever made, valued for their volume, clarity and balance. Fewer than 300 signed examples are known, and prime specimens sell for well into six figures.

Bill Monroe and bluegrass

The F-5 became the defining instrument of bluegrass music through Bill Monroe, the genre's founder, who played a July 9, 1923 Loar-signed F-5 for most of his career. Monroe's percussive "chop" rhythm and virtuoso lead playing on the model made it the archetype copied by nearly every later bluegrass mandolinist; his F-5 is now displayed at the Country Music Hall of Fame.

Later production

Gibson has produced F-5 variants almost continuously since 1922, including the modern F-5G and signature models for players such as Sam Bush and Adam Steffey.
