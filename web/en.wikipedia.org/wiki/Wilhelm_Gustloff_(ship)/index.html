Wilhelm Gustloff (ship)

MV Wilhelm Gustloff
Launched: 5 May 1937
Tonnage: 25,484 GRT
Fate: Sunk 30 January 1945

MV Wilhelm Gustloff was a German cruise ship built by Blohm & Voss in Hamburg and launched in 1937. Named after Wilhelm Gustloff, the assassinated leader of the Swiss branch of the Nazi Party, she was the flagship of the Strength Through Joy (KdF) leisure fleet before being requisitioned as a hospital ship and later a naval accommodation ship.

Sinking

On 30 January 1945, during Operation Hannibal — the evacuation of German troops and civilians from East Prussia ahead of the advancing Red Army — the ship left Gotenhafen (Gdynia) grossly overloaded with an estimated 10,000 people aboard. The Soviet submarine S-13, commanded by Alexander Marinesko, torpedoed her in the Baltic Sea off the Pomeranian coast. She sank in under an hour. Approximately 9,000 people died, making it the deadliest single ship sinking in maritime history.
