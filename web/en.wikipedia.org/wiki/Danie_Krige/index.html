Danie Krige

Danie Gerhardus Krige (26 August 1919 – 3 March 2013) was a South African mining engineer and statistician who pioneered the field of geostatistics. The estimation technique known as kriging is named after him.

Career

Krige was born in Bothaville in the Orange Free State and graduated in mining engineering from the University of the Witwatersrand in 1942. Working on the Witwatersrand gold fields, he confronted a systematic problem: gold grades estimated from borehole samples consistently misjudged the actual value of ore blocks — rich blocks were overestimated and poor ones underestimated, a statistical regression effect.

Kriging

In his 1951 paper published in the Journal of the Chemical, Metallurgical and Mining Society of South Africa, Krige showed how the relationship between a block's true grade and its sample estimates could be corrected using the spatial correlation among samples. The French engineer Georges Matheron later formalised Krige's empirical methods mathematically within his theory of regionalised variables, coining the name "kriging" in Krige's honour. Kriging became the standard tool for ore-reserve estimation and spread to petroleum geology, hydrology, meteorology, environmental science and machine learning (as Gaussian process regression).

Later life

Krige spent his career with the Government Mining Engineer's department and Anglo American, and held a chair at the University of the Witwatersrand. He received numerous awards, including membership of the Royal Society of South Africa, and died in Johannesburg in 2013, aged 93.
