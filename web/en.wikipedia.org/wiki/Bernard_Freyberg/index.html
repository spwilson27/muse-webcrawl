Bernard Freyberg

Lieutenant-General Bernard Freyberg, VC, commander of the 2nd New Zealand Division.

Bernard Cyril Freyberg, 1st Baron Freyberg, VC, GCMG, KCB, KBE, DSO & Three Bars (21 March 1889 – 4 July 1963) was a British-born New Zealand soldier and Victoria Cross recipient who commanded the 2nd New Zealand Division through much of the Second World War and later served as Governor-General of New Zealand. One of the most highly decorated soldiers of the British Empire, he was wounded many times in both world wars yet repeatedly led from the front.

Early life and First World War

Freyberg was born in Richmond, London, and emigrated with his family to Wellington, New Zealand, as a small child. A champion swimmer and qualified dentist, he travelled to Britain in 1914 and, through a meeting with Winston Churchill, obtained a commission in the Royal Naval Division. He won the Distinguished Service Order at Gallipoli — swimming ashore alone at Bulair to light flares as a diversion — and the Victoria Cross on the Somme in November 1916 at Beaucourt, where despite four wounds he led his battalion's attack and held the captured village. Rising to command a brigade at 29, he ended the war with the DSO and three Bars and the French Croix de Guerre; legend held that he had been wounded more times than any other British general.

Second World War

In 1939 Freyberg, by then a British major-general, accepted command of New Zealand's expeditionary force. He led the 2nd New Zealand Division in Greece (1941), Crete — where he was the Allied commander in the losing battle for the island — the North African campaign, including El Alamein, and Italy, including the controversial battles of Monte Cassino. His charter from the New Zealand government, allowing him to refer orders endangering his division to Wellington, made his position unusual; Churchill called him "the salamander", for seeming to live through fire unharmed.

Later life

Freyberg served as Governor-General of New Zealand from 1946 to 1952, the first with a New Zealand upbringing, and was raised to the peerage as Baron Freyberg in 1951. He sat in the House of Lords and became Deputy Constable and Lieutenant-Governor of Windsor Castle, dying in 1963 after an old war wound ruptured.
