LINEAR

The Lincoln Near-Earth Asteroid Research (LINEAR) project is a collaboration of the United States Air Force, NASA, and MIT's Lincoln Laboratory for the systematic discovery and tracking of near-Earth objects. Operating from the Lincoln Laboratory Experimental Test Site at White Sands Missile Range in Socorro, New Mexico, LINEAR was for many years the most productive asteroid discovery programme in the world.

History

The project began in 1996 using a 1.0-metre telescope equipped with a large charge-coupled device (CCD) camera developed at Lincoln Laboratory, and a second 1.0-metre telescope was added in 1999. From 1998 onward LINEAR discovered the majority of all newly found near-Earth asteroids, and it is credited with the discovery of tens of thousands of asteroids in total, along with numerous comets — including several periodic comets named for the project, such as 176P/LINEAR.

Method

LINEAR's telescopes sweep large areas of sky repeatedly each night; software compares successive images to detect faint moving points of light against the fixed star background. Candidate detections are reported to the Minor Planet Center for confirmation and orbit determination, which prioritises potentially hazardous asteroids whose orbits approach Earth's.

Legacy

LINEAR transformed near-Earth object surveying in the late 1990s and 2000s before other surveys, notably the Catalina Sky Survey and later Pan-STARRS, overtook its discovery rate. Its observatory code is 704.
