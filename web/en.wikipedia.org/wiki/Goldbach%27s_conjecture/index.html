Goldbach's conjecture

Goldbach's conjecture is one of the oldest and best-known unsolved problems in number theory. It states that every even natural number greater than 2 is the sum of two prime numbers — for example, 4 = 2 + 2, 6 = 3 + 3, 8 = 3 + 5, and 10 = 3 + 7 = 5 + 5.

History

The conjecture originated in a 1742 letter from Prussian mathematician Christian Goldbach to Leonhard Euler. Goldbach's original formulation concerned odd numbers as sums of three primes; the modern "strong" or "binary" version for even numbers implies the "weak" version.

Progress

The weak Goldbach conjecture — every odd number greater than 5 is the sum of three odd primes — was proved by Harald Helfgott in 2013, building on Vinogradov's 1937 result for sufficiently large numbers. Chen Jingrun proved in 1966 that every sufficiently large even number is the sum of a prime and a semiprime. The strong conjecture has been verified computationally for all even numbers up to at least 4 × 10¹⁸, but a general proof remains elusive.
