Mirrlees Review

The Mirrlees Review was a comprehensive review of the United Kingdom tax system carried out by the Institute for Fiscal Studies (IFS), chaired by Nobel laureate Sir James Mirrlees. Launched in 2006 and funded by the Nuffield Foundation, it reported in 2010–2011 with two volumes: Dimensions of Tax Design (2010), a collection of analytical studies, and Tax by Design (2011), which set out recommendations for reform. It is widely regarded as the most authoritative modern blueprint for tax reform in the UK, following in the tradition of the Meade Report of 1978.

Main recommendations

  • Tax the tax system as a whole coherently rather than piecemeal, aiming overall for neutrality, simplicity, and stability.
  • Apply VAT to a much broader base — removing most zero-rating and reduced rating, including on food and children's clothes — while compensating poorer households through the benefit system.
  • Integrate income tax and employee National Insurance contributions into a single tax on earnings.
  • Introduce a progressive consumption-style system: exempt the normal return to saving (a rate-of-return allowance), with taxation of returns above that.
  • Replace fuel duties and vehicle excise duty over time with road pricing (congestion charging) as motoring taxes erode.
  • Reform property taxation: replace council tax with a proportional property tax and reform business rates; replace stamp duty land tax, which discourages mobility.
  • Set environmental taxes, especially a consistent carbon price, to meet emissions targets efficiently.

Reception and influence

Tax by Design was praised by economists and commentators across the political spectrum as a rigorous, evidence-based agenda. Its recommendations have influenced subsequent policy debate in areas including the integration of income tax and National Insurance, VAT base broadening, pension taxation, and carbon pricing, even though governments have adopted its proposals only selectively.
