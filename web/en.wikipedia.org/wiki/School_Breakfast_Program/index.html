School Breakfast Program

School Breakfast Program
Country: United States
Established: 1966
Administered by: USDA, Food and Nutrition Service

The School Breakfast Program (SBP) is a federally assisted meal program operating in public and nonprofit private schools and residential child care institutions in the United States. Established as a pilot project under the Child Nutrition Act of 1966 and made permanent in 1975, it provides cash assistance to states to operate nonprofit breakfast programs in schools. It is administered at the federal level by the Food and Nutrition Service of the U.S. Department of Agriculture.

Operation

Children from families with incomes at or below 130 percent of the federal poverty level are eligible for free meals, and those between 130 and 185 percent qualify for reduced-price meals. Schools receive reimbursement for each meal served, with higher rates for schools serving a large proportion of low-income children ("severe need" schools). Meals must meet federal nutrition standards based on the Dietary Guidelines for Americans.

Scale and research

The program serves well over ten million children each school day. Research associates school breakfast participation with improved attendance, behaviour, and academic performance. Provision methods such as breakfast in the classroom and the Community Eligibility Provision — which allows high-poverty schools to serve free meals to all students — have expanded participation.
