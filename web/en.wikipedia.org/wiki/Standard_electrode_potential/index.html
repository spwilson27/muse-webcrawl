Standard electrode potential

In electrochemistry, standard electrode potential (E⦵) is the measure of the individual potential of a reversible electrode at standard state with ions at an effective concentration of 1 mol dm−3 at a pressure of 1 bar and a temperature of 298.15 K (25 °C). It is measured relative to the standard hydrogen electrode (SHE), whose potential is defined as 0.00 V.

Significance

The standard electrode potential of a half-cell indicates its tendency to gain electrons (be reduced). A more positive value indicates a stronger oxidising agent in the oxidised form of the couple, while a more negative value indicates a stronger reducing agent in the reduced form. Standard potentials are used to calculate the standard cell potential of an electrochemical cell, predict the direction of redox reactions, and determine equilibrium constants.

Measurement

Because absolute single-electrode potentials cannot be measured directly, potentials are measured with the electrode of interest connected to a reference electrode, conventionally the standard hydrogen electrode. In practice, more convenient reference electrodes such as the saturated calomel electrode or the silver/silver chloride electrode are used and their results converted to the SHE scale.
