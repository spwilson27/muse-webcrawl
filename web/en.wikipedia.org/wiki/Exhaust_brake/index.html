Exhaust brake

Image placeholder: Exhaust brake valve fitted in a diesel exhaust system

An exhaust brake is a means of slowing a diesel engine by closing off the exhaust path from the engine, causing the exhaust gases to be compressed in the exhaust manifold and in the cylinder, thus increasing the drag on the engine and slowing the vehicle. It is a form of engine braking widely used on heavy trucks and buses as a supplement to the friction service brakes.

Operation

An exhaust brake works by restricting the flow of exhaust gases leaving the engine. Most designs use a butterfly valve installed in the exhaust pipe downstream of the turbocharger. When the driver activates the brake, the valve closes, creating back pressure in the exhaust manifold. During the exhaust stroke the piston must push against this pressure, so the engine acts as an energy-absorbing air compressor and the vehicle is slowed without wearing the wheel brakes.

Exhaust brakes are most effective at high engine speeds, and drivers typically use them to control speed on long downhill grades, reducing brake fade and extending the life of the service brakes. They are distinct from, but often confused with, compression release engine brakes (such as the "Jake Brake"), which open the exhaust valves near top dead centre of the compression stroke to release compressed air.

Applications

Exhaust brakes are standard or optional equipment on many medium- and heavy-duty diesel trucks, coaches, and motorhomes. Smaller versions are also offered on diesel pickup trucks for use while towing.
