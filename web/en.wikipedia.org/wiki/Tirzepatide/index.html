Tirzepatide, sold under the brand names Mounjaro and Zepbound, is an antidiabetic and weight-loss medication developed by Eli Lilly and Company. It is a dual glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide (GIP) and glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonist, administered as a once-weekly subcutaneous injection.

Medical uses

The US Food and Drug Administration first approved tirzepatide in May 2022 for improving blood-sugar control in adults with type 2 diabetes. In November 2023 it was approved under the name Zepbound for chronic weight management in adults with obesity or overweight with weight-related conditions. In clinical trials (SURMOUNT-1), participants lost on average up to roughly 20% of body weight at the highest dose, among the largest reductions achieved by any medication at approval. It has also been approved for treating moderate-to-severe obstructive sleep apnea in adults with obesity.

Mechanism and side effects

By activating both GIP and GLP-1 receptors, tirzepatide increases insulin secretion, suppresses glucagon, slows gastric emptying, and reduces appetite. Common adverse effects are gastrointestinal—nausea, vomiting, diarrhoea, and constipation—especially during dose escalation. The drug carries a boxed warning regarding thyroid C-cell tumours observed in rodents.

Society and culture

Demand for tirzepatide and the related drug semaglutide surged in the early 2020s, producing global shortages and making Lilly one of the world's most valuable pharmaceutical companies.
