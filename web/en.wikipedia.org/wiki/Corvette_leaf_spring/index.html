Corvette leaf spring

A Corvette leaf spring is a type of leaf spring used in the suspension systems of Chevrolet Corvette sports cars. Unlike a traditional multi-leaf steel spring, the Corvette spring consists of a single transverse composite leaf — originally steel (from the C2 generation, transverse at the rear from 1963), and from the 1981 model year and the C4 generation onwards made of glass-fibre-reinforced plastic (fibreglass monoleaf) — mounted transversely, serving both wheels on an axle.

Design

The transverse leaf acts like two separate springs joined in the middle, and also serves partly as an anti-roll bar. Because a single leaf spans both sides, forces from one wheel can interact slightly with the other ("cross-talk"). Advantages include low weight (the fibreglass monoleaf weighs far less than two coil springs or a steel spring pack), compact packaging that frees luggage space, and durability.

History

The transverse leaf layout was used at the rear of the Corvette from 1963. The fourth-generation (C4, 1984) extended composite transverse leaves to both front and rear axles, a layout that continued through the C5, C6 and C7 generations. The mid-engined C8 Corvette (2020) switched to coil springs, ending the design's long run.
