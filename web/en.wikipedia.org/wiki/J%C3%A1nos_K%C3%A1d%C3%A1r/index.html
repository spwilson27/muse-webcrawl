János Kádár

János Kádár (26 May 1912 – 6 July 1989) was a Hungarian communist leader who ruled Hungary from 1956 until 1988, the longest-serving leader of socialist Hungary. Installed after the Soviet suppression of the Hungarian Revolution of 1956, he later presided over the relatively liberalised system known as "Goulash Communism".

Born26 May 1912, Fiume, Austria-Hungary
Died6 July 1989, Budapest, Hungary
OfficeGeneral Secretary of the Hungarian Socialist Workers' Party (1956–1988); Prime Minister (1956–1958, 1961–1965)

Rise and 1956

A communist activist imprisoned under both the Horthy regime and, in the Rákosi purges, by his own party, Kádár joined Imre Nagy's revolutionary government in October 1956, then defected to the Soviets and headed the puppet government that followed the invasion of 4 November. The ensuing reprisals included Nagy's execution in 1958.

Kádár era

From the early 1960s Kádár relaxed repression ("whoever is not against us is with us"), amnestied political prisoners, and introduced the New Economic Mechanism of 1968, blending planning with market elements and raising living standards. Removed as party leader in May 1988 as the system unravelled, he died on 6 July 1989—the day the Supreme Court formally rehabilitated Imre Nagy.
