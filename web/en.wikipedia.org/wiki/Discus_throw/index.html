Discus throw

EquipmentDiscus (2 kg men, 1 kg women)
VenueThrowing circle, 2.5 m diameter
OlympicMen since 1896; women since 1928
World recordsMen 75.48 m — Mykolas Alekna (2025); women 76.80 m — Gabriele Reinsch (1988)

The discus throw (pronounced /ˈdɪskəs/), also known as the disc throw, is a track and field sport in which the athlete throws a heavy disc — called a discus — in an attempt to mark a farther distance than other competitors. It is an ancient sport, as demonstrated by the fifth-century-BC Myron statue Discobolus. Although not part of the modern pentathlon, it was one of the events of the ancient Greek pentathlon, attested from 708 BC.

Technique and rules

The athlete stands in a circle 2.5 metres (8 ft 2¼ in) in diameter with a raised rim, spins about one and a half turns to build momentum, and releases the discus from the front of the circle; the throw is a foul if the athlete touches the top of the rim or steps outside before the discus lands. The discus must land within a sector of about 34.92 degrees. In major competitions each athlete receives three attempts, with the top eight receiving three more. The men's discus weighs 2 kilograms and is 22 centimetres in diameter; the women's weighs 1 kilogram and is 18–18.2 centimetres in diameter.

History

The event was part of the first modern Olympic Games in Athens in 1896 and has featured at every Summer Olympics since; the women's event was added in 1928. American Al Oerter won four consecutive Olympic gold medals (1956–1968), a feat matched in the modern era by few throwers. Jürgen Schult of East Germany set a long-standing men's world record of 74.08 m in 1986 that survived until Mykolas Alekna of Lithuania threw 74.35 m in 2024 and improved it further in 2025. The women's record of 76.80 m, set by Gabriele Reinsch in 1988, remains among the oldest in athletics.
