Koinobori strung across a river for Children's Day

Koinobori (鯉のぼり), meaning "carp streamer" in Japanese, are windsock-like banners in the shape of koi (carp) flown in Japan to celebrate Tango no sekku, the traditional festival now observed as Children's Day (Kodomo no hi) on 5 May. From late April to early May, households and public spaces raise poles hung with carp streamers that billow and appear to swim in the wind.

Symbolism and arrangement

The carp symbolises courage, strength and perseverance, after the Chinese legend of a carp that swims upstream and leaps the falls at Longmen to become a dragon; parents fly the streamers hoping their children will grow up healthy and determined. A typical set is topped by a fukinagashi streamer and sacred arrow-wheel, followed by a large black carp (magoi) for the father, a red carp (higoi) for the mother, and progressively smaller carp in blue, green and other colours for each child. The custom descends from samurai families' Edo-period practice of flying banners and iris-decorated streamers for the boys' festival; paper and cloth carp became widespread in the Meiji era, and since 1948 the day has honoured all children.

Culture

Koinobori feature in the well-known children's song of the same name ("Koinobori", 1931). Large displays — hundreds of streamers strung across rivers and valleys — are held in many towns, and the banners are a universal emblem of spring and childhood in Japan.
