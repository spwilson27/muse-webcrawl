Arkady and Boris Strugatsky

Arkady (1925–1991) and Boris (1933–2012) Strugatsky, the most famous Soviet science fiction writers.

Arkady Natanovich Strugatsky (28 August 1925 – 12 October 1991) and Boris Natanovich Strugatsky (14 April 1933 – 19 November 2012) were Soviet and Russian science fiction authors who generally wrote together. Their books later influenced many other writers and filmmakers and are considered classics of the genre.

Life and career

The brothers were born in Leningrad (Arkady in Batumi, Georgia, and raised in Leningrad). Arkady worked as a translator of English and Japanese; Boris was an astronomer at Pulkovo Observatory. They began publishing science fiction in the late 1950s, initially with optimistic stories set in a shared future universe known as the Noon Universe (Mir Poludnia).

Their most famous novel, Roadside Picnic (1972), imagines the aftermath of an alien visitation that left behind mysterious "Zones" full of dangerous artefacts. It inspired Andrei Tarkovsky's film Stalker (1979) and later a series of video games. Other celebrated works include Hard to Be a God (1964), Monday Begins on Saturday (1965), The Ugly Swans, The Snail on the Slope, and The Doomed City.

Themes and reception

The Strugatskys' work blends adventure with philosophical and political questions: progress and its costs, the ethics of intervention in less developed societies (the "Progressor" theme), and the limits of human knowledge. Several of their later works faced censorship difficulties in the Soviet Union and were published abroad or in samizdat. After Arkady's death in 1991, Boris wrote two novels under the pen name S. Vititsky.

Legacy

The asteroid 3054 Strugatskia is named after them. Their works have been translated into many languages, and new English translations in the 2010s renewed international interest in their fiction.
