The Army of the Czech Republic (Czech: Pozemní síly Armády České republiky, the Land Forces of the Armed Forces of the Czech Republic) is the land warfare branch of the armed forces of the Czech Republic. Together with the Czech Air Force it forms the main combat arm of the country's military. The modern army dates from the division of Czechoslovakia in 1993, which split the Czechoslovak People's Army between the Czech Republic and Slovakia.

History

Czech military traditions trace to the Czechoslovak Legions of the First World War and the interwar Czechoslovak Army. After 1948 the army was integrated into the Warsaw Pact as the Czechoslovak People's Army. Following the Velvet Revolution of 1989 and the dissolution of Czechoslovakia on 1 January 1993, the Army of the Czech Republic was established. The Czech Republic joined NATO in 1999, and conscription was abolished at the end of 2004, making the force fully professional.

Structure and equipment

The Land Forces are organized around two mechanized brigades: the 7th Mechanized Brigade ("Dukelská") headquartered in Hranice, and the 4th Rapid Deployment Brigade ("Obrany vlasti") in Žatec, supported by artillery, engineer, logistics, reconnaissance and CBRN regiments. Principal equipment has included T-72M4CZ tanks (later supplemented by Leopard 2A4s donated by Germany), BVP-2 infantry fighting vehicles, Pandur II armored vehicles, and DANA self-propelled howitzers, with modernization programs underway.

Deployments

Czech soldiers have served in the Balkans (KFOR), Iraq, and extensively in Afghanistan (ISAF and Resolute Support), as well as in Mali and NATO enhanced Forward Presence battlegroups in the Baltic states and Slovakia.
