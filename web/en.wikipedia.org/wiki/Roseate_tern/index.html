Roseate tern

The roseate tern (Sterna dougallii) is a seabird of the tern family, found in coastal regions of the Atlantic, Indian and Pacific Oceans. It is named for the delicate rosy flush that colours the breast of breeding adults.

Description

The roseate tern is a medium-sized tern, 33–36 cm (13–14 in) long with long, deeply forked tail streamers in breeding plumage. It is exceptionally pale, almost white above, with a black cap, a long black bill that develops a red base in the breeding season, and a distinctive, more musical call than the similar common tern and Arctic tern, with which it often nests.

Behaviour and diet

It feeds almost entirely by plunge-diving for small schooling fish, especially sand eels, often in association with predatory fish or other terns that drive prey to the surface. Nests are scrapes in sand or among rocks, usually concealed under vegetation or debris, with one to three eggs.

Conservation

North Atlantic populations collapsed in the late 19th century because of hunting for the millinery trade; although protected since, the northeastern North American population remains listed as Endangered under the US Endangered Species Act and depends heavily on managed colonies in New England and Long Island. Threats include predation by gulls and mammals, loss of nesting islands to erosion and sea-level rise, and reduced food supplies.
