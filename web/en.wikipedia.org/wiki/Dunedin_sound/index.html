Dunedin sound

The Dunedin sound is a style of indie pop music that emerged in the early 1980s in Dunedin, New Zealand, characterized by jangling guitars (often droning or played through cheap amplifiers and effects), minimal bass lines, loose drumming and echoed, often buried vocals. The term was coined by The Clean's David Kilgour in 1982.

Characteristics and origins

Bands such as The Clean, The Chills, The Verlaines, Sneaky Feelings, Straitjacket Fits, The Bats and Look Blue Go Purple combined influences from 1960s garage rock, psychedelia, The Velvet Underground and punk with a distinctive lo-fi aesthetic shaped by isolation, cheap equipment and the damp southern climate. Many of the key records were released by the Christchurch-founded label Flying Nun Records, established by Roger Shepherd in 1981, beginning with The Clean's "Tally Ho!" single.

Influence

The Dunedin sound became one of New Zealand's most internationally recognized musical exports, influencing American indie and college rock acts such as Pavement, R.E.M. and Yo La Tengo, and later generations of jangle pop. Martin Phillipps of The Chills achieved an international hit with "Heavenly Pop Hit" (1990).
