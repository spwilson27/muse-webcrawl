The Florence Baptistery, also known as the Baptistery of St. John (Italian: Battistero di San Giovanni), is a religious building in Florence, Italy, and has the status of a minor basilica. The octagonal baptistery stands in both the Piazza del Duomo and the Piazza San Giovanni, across from Florence Cathedral.

History

Built between 1059 and 1128 in the Florentine Romanesque style, it is one of the oldest buildings in the city. Florentines once believed it had been a Roman temple of Mars. Until the nineteenth century all Florentine Catholics were baptised here; Dante Alighieri was among them.

The doors

The baptistery is renowned for its three sets of bronze doors. Andrea Pisano made the south doors (1330s); Lorenzo Ghiberti won the famous 1401 competition for the north doors; and his east doors (1425–1452), depicting scenes from the Old Testament, were dubbed the "Gates of Paradise" by Michelangelo.
