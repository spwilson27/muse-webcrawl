Storyspace

Storyspace is a hypertext writing environment first released in 1987, designed for creating, editing and publishing hypertext fiction and other complex linked documents. It is widely regarded as the authoring system of the first wave of literary hypertext published by Eastgate Systems, and pioneered concepts — such as the map view and guard fields — that remain influential in hypertext research.

Storyspace
Developer: Eastgate Systems
Initial release: 1987
Creators: Jay David Bolter, Michael Joyce, John B. Smith
Genre: Hypertext authoring software

History

Storyspace grew out of research by Jay David Bolter, Michael Joyce and John B. Smith in the mid-1980s; an early version was demonstrated at the first ACM Hypertext conference in 1987. Eastgate Systems of Watertown, Massachusetts, published it commercially and used it to issue pioneering works of hypertext fiction, including Michael Joyce's afternoon, a story (1990), Stuart Moulthrop's Victory Garden and Shelley Jackson's Patchwork Girl (1995). Mark Bernstein, Eastgate's chief scientist, led later versions; Storyspace 3 appeared in 2015, sharing its file format heritage with the note-taking application Tinderbox.

Features

Authors write in "writing spaces" (nodes) arranged on a visual map, and link them by path. Guard fields are Boolean conditions attached to links that control navigation — a link may become available only after the reader has visited certain other spaces — enabling plots shaped by the reader's history. Readers explore through links in the text, maps, and default reading paths. The environment supports export to the web and stand-alone editions.

Legacy

Storyspace shaped the first academic field of hypertext literary studies; critics such as George Landow and Espen Aarseth used works written in it as central examples. Its map-based approach influenced later tools for writers and knowledge workers.
