Hedenbergite

Hedenbergite is a calcium–iron pyroxene mineral with the formula CaFeSi2O6. It forms a complete solid-solution series with diopside (the calcium–magnesium endmember) and is closely related to johannsenite (the calcium–manganese analogue). The mineral is named after the Swedish mineralogist and chemist Magnus (or Anders Ludvig) Hedenberg, a collaborator of Jöns Jacob Berzelius, who analysed the original material from Tunaberg, Sweden.

Occurrence

Hedenbergite occurs characteristically in calcium-rich skarns formed where iron-bearing fluids metasomatise limestone, and in metamorphosed iron formations and manganese deposits. It is also found in some igneous rocks and in meteorites. Notable localities include Tunaberg in Södermanland, Sweden; Broken Hill, Australia; and Dalnegorsk, Russia. Crystals are typically prismatic and dark green to nearly black, often radiating or acicular.

Related minerals

Together with diopside and augite, hedenbergite is one of the most important calcic clinopyroxenes. The manganese-rich variety is sometimes called manganhedenbergite, and zinc-bearing material grades toward petedunnite.
