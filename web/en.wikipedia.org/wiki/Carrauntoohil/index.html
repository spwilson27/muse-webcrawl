Carrauntoohil

Carrauntoohil (Irish: Corrán Tuathail, "Tuathal's sickle") is the highest mountain in Ireland, at 1,038.6 metres (3,407 feet). It lies in the MacGillycuddy's Reeks mountain range in County Kerry, in the south-west of the island. The summit is marked by a large metal cross, erected in 1976 and replaced after being cut down in 2014.

Geology and geography

The mountain is composed of Devonian Old Red Sandstone, sculpted by glacial erosion into arêtes, corries and sharp ridges. Its neighbours in the Reeks include Beenkeragh (1,008 m) and Caher (1,001 m), the only other Irish peaks above 1,000 m. Carrauntoohil's north-east face drops into the Hag's Glen, where the Devil's Ladder — a steep, eroded rocky gully — provides the most popular ascent route.

Climbing

Some 25,000–30,000 people climb Carrauntoohil each year. Common routes include the Devil's Ladder from Cronin's Yard, the multi-summit Coomloughra Horseshoe, and the full MacGillycuddy's Reeks ridge traverse. Weather on the mountain changes rapidly, and the Kerry Mountain Rescue Team is frequently called out; several fatalities have occurred, prompting advice to use experienced local guides.
