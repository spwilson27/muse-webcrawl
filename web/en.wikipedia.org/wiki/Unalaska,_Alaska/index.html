Unalaska, Alaska

Unalaska (Aleut: Iluulux̂) is a city in the Aleutians West Census Area of Alaska, United States. It encompasses part of Unalaska Island and neighbouring Amaknak Island in the Aleutian chain, about 1,300 kilometres southwest of Anchorage. The population was 4,254 at the 2020 census. Its port, Dutch Harbor, is one of the busiest fishing ports in the United States by volume of seafood landings.

History

The Unangan (Aleut) people have inhabited the island for thousands of years. Russian fur traders arrived in the mid-eighteenth century, founding a trading post and bringing the Russian Orthodox Church, whose Church of the Holy Ascension (1825) remains a landmark. In June 1942, during the Second World War, Japanese aircraft bombed Dutch Harbor, and the native population was evacuated to camps in southeast Alaska for the remainder of the war. The area became a significant naval base during the war.

Economy

Since the 1970s, and especially after the development of the Bering Sea pollock and crab fisheries, Dutch Harbor has repeatedly ranked as the top US fishing port by tonnage landed. The population swells seasonally with processing-plant and fishing-crew workers. The port also serves as a supply and refuge harbour on the Great Circle route between North America and East Asia. The television series Deadliest Catch is based around crab boats operating from Dutch Harbor.

Geography and climate

The city straddles Unalaska Bay and Iliuliuk Harbor, connected by the Bridge to the Other Side between Unalaska and Amaknak islands. The climate is subpolar oceanic: cool, windy and wet, with frequent storms, modest snowfall and narrow temperature variation. The active volcano Makushin (2,036 m) rises on the island's western side.
