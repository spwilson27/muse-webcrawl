Downward entailment

In formal semantics, a downward-entailing context is one that licenses inferences from general terms to more specific ones — the reverse of ordinary upward entailment. Negation is the classic example: 'I don't eat vegetables' entails 'I don't eat spinach'.

Properties

Downward-entailing environments license negative polarity items such as 'any' and 'ever', a connection formalised by Gilles Fauconnier and Frans Zwarts, who ordered contexts by monotonicity strength.

Examples

Negation, the antecedent of conditionals, questions, and quantifiers like 'few' and 'at most n' create downward-entailing contexts.
