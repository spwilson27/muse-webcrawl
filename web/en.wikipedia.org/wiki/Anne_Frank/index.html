Annelies Marie Frank (12 June 1929 – c. February 1945) was a German-born Jewish diarist. She gained posthumous fame with the publication of The Diary of a Young Girl, in which she documented her life in hiding from 1942 to 1944, during the German occupation of the Netherlands in World War II. It is one of the world's best-known books and has been translated into more than 70 languages.

Early life

Anne Frank was born in Frankfurt, Germany, to Otto and Edith Frank. The family emigrated to Amsterdam in 1934 after the Nazis came to power. Anne attended the 6th Montessori School and later the Jewish Lyceum. For her thirteenth birthday in June 1942 she received a red-and-white checked autograph book, which she used as a diary.

Life in hiding

In July 1942, after her sister Margot received a call-up notice for a work camp, the family went into hiding in rooms behind Otto Frank's business premises at Prinsengracht 263 — the "Secret Annex" — together with the van Pels family and dentist Fritz Pfeffer. Aided by former colleagues including Miep Gies, the eight people remained hidden for over two years. On 4 August 1944 the annex was raided and its occupants arrested.

Deportation and death

The Frank family was sent to Auschwitz; Anne and Margot were later transferred to Bergen-Belsen, where both died of typhus in early 1945, weeks before the camp's liberation. Otto Frank was the annex's only survivor. After the war, Miep Gies returned Anne's preserved writings to him, and the diary was published in 1947 as Het Achterhuis.

Legacy

The Anne Frank House in Amsterdam opened as a museum in 1960. The diary has been adapted for stage and screen many times and is regarded as a central literary testimony of the Holocaust.
