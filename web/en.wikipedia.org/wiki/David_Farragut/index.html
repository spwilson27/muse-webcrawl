David Farragut

David Glasgow Farragut (July 5, 1801 – August 14, 1870) was a flag officer of the United States Navy during the American Civil War. He was the first rear admiral, vice admiral, and admiral in the history of the United States Navy. He is remembered for his order at the Battle of Mobile Bay, usually paraphrased as "Damn the torpedoes, full speed ahead."

Early life and career

Born near Knoxville, Tennessee, Farragut went to sea at age nine and was appointed a midshipman while still a child. He served in the War of 1812 aboard the frigate Essex and spent more than five decades in naval service, including duty in the Mediterranean and in the Mexican–American War.

Civil War

Remaining loyal to the Union, Farragut captured New Orleans in April 1862 after running his fleet past Forts Jackson and St. Philip, a victory that gave the Union control of the lower Mississippi River. He later helped take Vicksburg's approaches and, in August 1864, won the Battle of Mobile Bay, closing one of the Confederacy's last major ports.

Legacy

Farragut died in Portsmouth, New Hampshire, in 1870. Numerous ships, schools, and places, including Farragut Square in Washington, D.C., bear his name.
