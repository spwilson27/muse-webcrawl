Wollemi National Park

Wollemi National Park is a protected national park in New South Wales, Australia, about 130 kilometres north-west of Sydney. At roughly 500,000 hectares it is the second-largest national park in the state and part of the UNESCO Greater Blue Mountains World Heritage Area.

Geography and ecology

The park protects rugged sandstone plateaus, deep gorges and wilderness areas drained by the Colo and Wolgan rivers. It is famous as the discovery site, in 1994, of the Wollemi pine (Wollemia nobilis), a 'living fossil' tree previously known only from fossils.

Heritage

The area contains many Aboriginal sites, including rock art, and is valued for remote bushwalking and canyoning. Large parts are declared wilderness, with limited access to protect fragile ecosystems and the secret location of the Wollemi pines.
