The SAG-AFTRA Foundation is an American nonprofit organisation that provides programmes and emergency financial assistance to members of the SAG-AFTRA performers' union and promotes children's literacy. It was founded in 1985 as the Screen Actors Guild Foundation and adopted its present name after the 2012 merger of the Screen Actors Guild (SAG) and the American Federation of Television and Radio Artists (AFTRA). The Foundation is governed by its own board and is independent of the union, although it works closely with it.

Programmes

  • Emergency assistance — financial aid to performers facing health crises, injury or other hardship, including assistance funds activated during events such as the COVID-19 pandemic and industry strikes.
  • Educational programming — free workshops, panels, screenings with cast Q&As ("Conversations"), casting access programmes and computer labs for members in Los Angeles, New York and online, as well as voiceover labs (the Don LaFontaine Voiceover Lab and the EIF Voiceover Lab).
  • Storyline Online — an award-winning children's literacy website on which actors read illustrated children's books aloud on video.
  • Scholarships — the John L. Dales Scholarship Fund and related awards for union members and their dependants.

Facilities

The Foundation operates screening rooms and computer labs in Los Angeles (the SAG-AFTRA Foundation Screening Room at its mid-Wilshire headquarters) and New York, offering members free access to audition self-tape facilities, classes and casting-director workshops.
