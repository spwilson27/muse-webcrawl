Growth factor

A growth factor is a naturally occurring substance, usually a protein or a steroid hormone, capable of stimulating cell proliferation, wound healing, and sometimes cellular differentiation. Growth factors act as signalling molecules between cells, binding to specific receptors on the cell surface and activating intracellular pathways.

Types

Important classes include epidermal growth factor (EGF), platelet-derived growth factor (PDGF), fibroblast growth factors (FGFs), transforming growth factors (TGF-α and TGF-β), vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), insulin-like growth factors (IGFs), and nerve growth factor (NGF), the first growth factor discovered, by Rita Levi-Montalcini and Stanley Cohen.

Clinical use

Recombinant growth factors are used in medicine: erythropoietin stimulates red blood cell production, granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) is used to treat neutropenia, and PDGF preparations aid wound healing. Blockade of growth factor signalling—especially VEGF and HER2/EGF receptors—is a major strategy in cancer therapy.
