Steamer Point

Steamer Point (Arabic: التواهي) is a district and peninsula area of Aden, Yemen, forming part of the Tawahi district at the western side of Aden harbour. The name derives from the anchorage used by steamships calling at Aden after the opening of the Suez Canal route, when Aden became a major coaling and bunkering station on the route between Europe and Asia.

History

After the British captured Aden in 1839, the harbour developed into an important port and refuelling station. Steamer Point grew as the commercial and passenger landing area, with piers, hotels, shops and the offices of shipping agents serving travellers on liners between Britain, India and the Far East. It was where passengers disembarked to visit the bazaars of nearby Crater and Tawahi while their ships took on coal and supplies.

Landmarks

The area is associated with the Crescent Hotel, built to accommodate first-class passengers, and with the Prince of Wales Pier, where arriving dignitaries were received. The district overlooks the entrance to Aden's natural harbour, guarded by the historic fortifications on Sira Island nearby.
