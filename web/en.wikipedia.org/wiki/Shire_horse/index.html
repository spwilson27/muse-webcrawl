Shire horse

The Shire is a British breed of draught horse. It is among the largest horse breeds in the world: stallions typically stand 17 hands (173 cm) or more at the withers and may weigh 850–1,100 kg. The breed is characterised by a broad, powerful build, a long head with a convex profile, and abundant feathering — long silky hair — on the lower legs. Coat colours are usually black, bay, brown or grey.

History

The Shire descends from the medieval English "great horse" used in war and later for heavy agricultural and haulage work. Through the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, breeders in the English Midlands — Leicestershire, Derbyshire, Staffordshire and Lincolnshire — refined the type. The Shire Horse Society was founded in 1878 and published its first stud book in 1884. At the breed's peak in the early twentieth century, tens of thousands of Shires pulled brewery drays, carts and ploughs across Britain, and many were exported, particularly to the United States.

Decline and conservation

Mechanisation after the Second World War caused numbers to collapse, and by the 1960s the breed was near extinction. A revival followed, driven by show breeders, brewers who kept dray teams for promotion, and enthusiasts of heavy horse work. The Rare Breeds Survival Trust nevertheless still lists the Shire as at risk.

Records

A Shire gelding named Sampson, foaled in 1846 in Bedfordshire and later renamed Mammoth, stood about 21.2½ hands (219 cm) and is regarded as the tallest horse ever recorded.
