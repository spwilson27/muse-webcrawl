Seto Inland Sea

The Seto Inland Sea (Japanese: 瀬戸内海, Seto Naikai), sometimes shortened to the Inland Sea, is the body of water separating Honshū, Shikoku and Kyūshū, three of the four main islands of Japan. It serves as a waterway connecting the Pacific Ocean to the Sea of Japan.

Geography

The sea extends roughly 450 kilometres east to west and contains some 3,000 islands, including Awaji Island, Shōdoshima and the art islands of Naoshima and Teshima. Much of it lies within the Setonaikai National Park, one of Japan's oldest national parks, designated in 1934. Major coastal cities include Kobe, Okayama, Hiroshima, Iwakuni and Matsuyama.

History and economy

Historically the Inland Sea was Japan's most important internal trade route, and its shores preserve landmarks such as the floating shrine of Itsukushima. In the twentieth century its coast became a major industrial belt, bringing heavy pollution that has since been partly remediated. Today the region is noted for fisheries and aquaculture (including oysters and yellowtail), ferry routes, and a network of great bridges, among them the Great Seto Bridge and the Shimanami Kaidō.
