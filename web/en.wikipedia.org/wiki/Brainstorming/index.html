Brainstorming

Brainstorming is a group creativity technique by which efforts are made to find a conclusion or solutions for a specific problem by gathering a list of ideas spontaneously contributed by its members.

Brainstorming
Method: Group discussion
Originator: Alex F. Osborn
First described: 1940s-1953

The term was popularized by advertising executive Alex Faickney Osborn in the classic work Applied Imagination (1953). Osborn claimed brainstorming was more effective than individuals working alone in generating ideas, although more recent research has challenged this conclusion.

Origin

Osborn developed the method while working at BBDO advertising agency. He frustrated by employees' inability to develop creative ideas, began hosting group sessions where quantity was encouraged.

Osborn's rules

Four general rules were intended to reduce social inhibitions, stimulate idea generation, and increase overall creativity: go for quantity, withhold criticism, welcome wild ideas, and build on the ideas of others.

Procedure and variations

A facilitator records ideas, often on whiteboards or sticky notes. Variations include nominal group technique, brainwriting, electronic brainstorming, and mind mapping. See also Creativity, Design thinking, and Mind map.

Criticism and research

Studies show production blocking, evaluation apprehension, and social loafing can make interactive groups less productive than nominal groups, though brainstorming remains widely used in business, education, and product design.
