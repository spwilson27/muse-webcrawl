Kings of Connacht

Rathcroghan (Cruachan) was the traditional royal seat of the kings of Connacht.

The kings of Connacht ruled the western Irish province of Connacht from late antiquity until the 15th century. In the early medieval period the kingship was held by branches of the Uí Briúin dynasty, descendants of Brión, a legendary brother of Niall of the Nine Hostages, and later by the Uí Briúin Seóla and the Ó Conchubhair (O'Conor) family.

History

Notable kings include Cathal mac Conchobair (973–1010), Tairrdelbach Ua Conchobair (Toirdelbach O'Conor, 1106–1156), who became High King of Ireland and rebuilt Connacht's power, and his son Ruaidrí Ua Conchobair, the last High King before the Anglo-Norman invasion of 1169. After the invasion, the O'Conors fought a long rearguard action against the de Burgh (Burke) lordship; Cathal Crobhdearg's descendants held a shrinking kingdom east of the Shannon until the last effective king, Feidhlimidh Ó Conchubhair, died in 1464.

The list of kings is preserved in the Irish annals and in genealogical tracts such as those in the Book of Ballymote and the Book of Lecan.
