Carlota of Mexico (born Princess Marie Charlotte Amélie Augustine Victoire Clémentine Léopoldine of Belgium; 7 June 1840 – 19 January 1927) was a Belgian princess who became Empress of Mexico as the wife of Emperor Maximilian I during the Second Mexican Empire (1864–1867).

Early life and marriage

Charlotte was the only daughter of King Leopold I of Belgium and Queen Louise of Orléans. In 1857 she married Archduke Ferdinand Maximilian of Austria, younger brother of Emperor Franz Joseph I. The couple first lived at Miramare Castle near Trieste, where Maximilian served as governor-general of Lombardy–Venetia.

Empress of Mexico

In 1864, under the sponsorship of Napoleon III of France, Maximilian accepted the Mexican crown offered by Mexican conservatives. Charlotte, who took the Spanish name Carlota, was an ambitious and capable partner in the imperial project: she served as regent during Maximilian's absences, promoted public works, education, and charity, and presided over the court at Chapultepec Castle.

As the French-backed empire collapsed under republican resistance led by Benito Juárez and the withdrawal of French troops, Carlota travelled to Europe in 1866 to plead for support from Napoleon III and Pope Pius IX. Her mission failed, and during it she suffered a severe mental breakdown, becoming convinced she was being poisoned. She never returned to Mexico. Maximilian was captured and executed at Querétaro on 19 June 1867.

Later life

Carlota spent the remaining sixty years of her life in seclusion in Italy and then at Bouchout Castle in Belgium, suffering from mental illness. She died in 1927, having outlived the empire by six decades.
