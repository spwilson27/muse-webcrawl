Thermodynamic equilibrium

Thermodynamic equilibrium
BranchThermodynamics
ConditionsThermal, mechanical and chemical equilibrium simultaneously
Characterized byUniform intensive properties; no net macroscopic flows; extremum of a thermodynamic potential

Thermodynamic equilibrium is a state of a thermodynamic system in which no net macroscopic flows of matter or energy occur and all macroscopic properties are unchanging in time. Equilibrium is the foundational concept of classical thermodynamics: a system in equilibrium can be completely described by a small number of state variables such as temperature, pressure and composition.

Conditions of equilibrium

Full thermodynamic equilibrium requires three conditions simultaneously. Thermal equilibrium means temperature is uniform throughout the system, with no net heat flow — the content of the zeroth law of thermodynamics. Mechanical equilibrium means pressure (more generally, all generalized forces) is balanced, with no net work or bulk motion. Chemical equilibrium means the chemical potentials of each species are uniform, so no net chemical reaction or diffusion proceeds. At equilibrium the appropriate thermodynamic potential is extremized: for an isolated system entropy is maximal; at constant temperature and pressure the Gibbs free energy is minimal.

Local equilibrium and non-equilibrium

Many real systems are not in global equilibrium but can be treated by the hypothesis of local thermodynamic equilibrium, in which each small region is approximately equilibrated and state variables vary smoothly in space and time — the basis of continuum fluid mechanics and heat transfer theory. Systems maintained away from equilibrium by external flows, from convection cells to living organisms, are the subject of non-equilibrium thermodynamics, which studies steady states, entropy production and dissipative structures.

Examples

A sealed, insulated container of gas left undisturbed settles into thermodynamic equilibrium. Phase equilibrium — ice melting in water at 0 °C, or saturated vapour over a liquid — and chemical equilibrium in reacting mixtures are special cases studied in physical chemistry and engineering thermodynamics.
