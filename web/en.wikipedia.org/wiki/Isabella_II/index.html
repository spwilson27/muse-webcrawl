Isabella II

Isabella II (Spanish: Isabel II; 10 October 1830 – 9 April 1904) was Queen of Spain from 1833 until her deposition in 1868. She was the daughter of King Ferdinand VII and Maria Christina of the Two Sicilies. Her accession as an infant, made possible by her father's Pragmatic Sanction repealing the Salic law, was disputed by supporters of her uncle Don Carlos and plunged Spain into the Carlist Wars.

Early reign

During Isabella's minority, her mother Maria Christina acted as regent (1833–1840), followed by General Baldomero Espartero (1840–1843). The First Carlist War (1833–1840) ended with a liberal victory, but the question of the succession shaped Spanish politics for decades. Isabella was declared of age at thirteen and crowned in 1843. In 1846 she was married, against her wishes, to her cousin Francisco de Asís, Duke of Cádiz.

Reign as adult

Isabella's reign was marked by chronic political instability, military pronunciamientos, and the alternation in power of the Moderate and Progressive parties. Governments rose and fell rapidly; generals such as Ramón María Narváez, Leopoldo O'Donnell and Espartero dominated public life. Spain intervened abroad in Morocco, Cochinchina and Mexico, and briefly re-annexed Santo Domingo. The queen's personal conduct, her reliance on court favourites, and rumours about the paternity of her children progressively undermined the prestige of the monarchy.

Deposition and exile

In September 1868 the "Glorious Revolution" forced Isabella into exile in France. In 1870 she abdicated in favour of her son Alfonso, who was restored to the throne as Alfonso XII in 1874. Isabella lived mainly in Paris, at the Palacio Castilla, making only occasional visits to Spain. She died in Paris in 1904 and was buried at El Escorial.

Legacy

Historians generally regard Isabella II's reign as a period of turbulent transition from absolutism to a liberal, if unstable, constitutional monarchy. Her reign saw the construction of Spain's first railways, the disentailment of church and common lands, and the consolidation of a two-party system dominated by the army.
