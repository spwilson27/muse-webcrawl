Quartz arenite

Quartz arenite in thin section.

Quartz arenite or quartzarenite is a variety of arenite (sandstone) composed overwhelmingly of quartz grains. In most classification schemes (e.g. those of Folk and of Pettijohn) a quartz arenite contains more than 90–95% quartz, with less than a few percent feldspar and lithic fragments and little matrix. It is the most mineralogically and texturally "mature" end-member of sandstone composition.

Formation

Such purity requires prolonged or repeated weathering, transport, and reworking, which destroys the less resistant feldspar and rock fragments and winnows out clay, leaving rounded, well-sorted quartz grains. Many quartz arenites are the products of multiple sedimentary cycles — sands eroded from older sandstones — or of long residence in high-energy environments such as beaches, shallow seas, and deserts. They often form extensive sheet deposits on stable continental cratons, such as the Ordovician St Peter Sandstone of North America.

Properties and uses

Quartz arenites are typically white or light-coloured, cemented by silica or calcite, and hard and resistant to erosion. Because of their purity, some are mined as glass sand and foundry sand, and porous examples are important aquifer and hydrocarbon reservoir rocks.
