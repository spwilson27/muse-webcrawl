Humboldt Bay

Humboldt Bay
Location: Humboldt County, California
Coordinates: approx. 40.75° N, 124.2° W
Area: about 25 sq mi (65 km²) at high tide
Cities: Eureka, Arcata

Humboldt Bay is a natural bay and a multi-basin, bar-built coastal lagoon located on the rugged North Coast of California, United States, in Humboldt County. It is the largest protected body of water on the West Coast between San Francisco Bay and Puget Sound, and the second-largest natural bay in California.

Geography

The bay consists of three main basins — Arcata Bay (North Bay), the Entrance Bay, and South Bay — connected by channels and surrounded by extensive tidal mudflats and salt marshes. The narrow, treacherous entrance, improved by jetties, gives access to the Port of Humboldt Bay, serving the cities of Eureka and Arcata.

Ecology

Humboldt Bay supports the largest eelgrass beds in California, important habitat for Pacific brant geese, migratory shorebirds on the Pacific Flyway, and commercially farmed oysters; most of California's oysters are cultivated in its northern basin. Parts of the bay are protected within the Humboldt Bay National Wildlife Refuge.

History

The bay is the traditional homeland of the Wiyot people, centred on Tuluwat Island; the Wiyot massacre of 1860 took place there during Euro-American settlement. Euro-American ships entered the bay in 1806 and again in 1849–50 during the Gold Rush, after which Eureka was founded as a timber port. The bay was named for the naturalist Alexander von Humboldt, who never visited it.
