Hard-edge painting

Hard-edge painting is painting in which abrupt transitions are found between colour areas. Colours are often constant from one edge to the other, and the style is related to geometric abstraction, Op art, and colour field painting.

The term was coined in 1959 by the Los Angeles art critic Jules Langsner to describe the work of California painters such as Karl Benjamin, Lorser Feitelson, Frederick Hammersley, and John McLaughlin, whose crisp geometry contrasted with the gestural brushwork of Abstract Expressionism.

Characteristics

Hard-edge works feature sharply defined shapes, flat colour, clean surfaces, and an overall economy of form. The style flourished in the 1960s, with artists including Ellsworth Kelly, Kenneth Noland, and Frank Stella producing influential examples.

Exhibitions

Langsner's 1959 exhibition "Four Abstract Classicists" at the Los Angeles County Museum of Art introduced the term; a later British showing was retitled "West Coast Hard Edge". The style became a broad movement within post-painterly abstraction.
