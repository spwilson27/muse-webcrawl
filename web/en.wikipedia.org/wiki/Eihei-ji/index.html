Eihei-ji (永平寺, "Temple of Eternal Peace") is one of two main temples of the Sōtō school of Zen Buddhism, the other being Sōji-ji. Located in the mountains near Eiheiji town, Fukui Prefecture, Japan, it was founded in 1244 by Eihei Dōgen, who introduced Sōtō Zen from China.

History

Dōgen founded the monastery after leaving Kyoto, accepting an invitation from the Hatano clan. Originally named Daibutsu-ji, it was renamed Eihei-ji in 1246. Dōgen served as abbot until his death in 1253. The temple burned and was rebuilt several times; its present buildings largely date to restorations under subsequent abbots.

Practice

About 150 trainee monks live at Eihei-ji at any time, following a strict regimen centered on shikantaza ("just sitting") zazen, manual labor, and communal rituals laid down in Dōgen's Eihei Shingi. Lay visitors may join short training stays (sanzen).

Buildings

The complex of over 70 buildings is connected by covered walkways among ancient cedars. Key halls include the Sanmon (gate), Butsuden (Buddha hall), Hatto (Dharma hall), Sodo (monks' hall), and the Yokushitsu bathhouse.
