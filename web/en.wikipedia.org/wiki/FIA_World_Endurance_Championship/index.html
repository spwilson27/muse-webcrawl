The FIA World Endurance Championship, abbreviated as WEC, is an auto racing world championship organized by the Automobile Club de l'Ouest (ACO) and sanctioned by the Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA). The series superseded the ACO's Intercontinental Le Mans Cup in 2012 and revived the name of the former World Sportscar Championship. It features multiple classes of cars competing in endurance races of between six and twenty-four hours, centred on the 24 Hours of Le Mans.

Format

The championship awards world titles to drivers and manufacturers. Races are typically six or eight hours long, with the Le Mans 24 Hours as the centrepiece and other classic events including the 6 Hours of Spa-Francorchamps, the 1000 Miles of Sebring and later the 1812 km of Qatar among the calendar's fixtures. Cars are shared by crews of two or three drivers, making strategy, reliability, and traffic management as important as outright speed.

Classes

The original top class, LMP1, was contested by factory hybrid prototypes from Audi, Toyota, and Porsche. It was replaced in 2021 by the Hypercar class, accepting both Le Mans Hypercars (LMH) and, from 2023, LMDh cars, drawing manufacturers such as Toyota, Ferrari, Porsche, Cadillac, BMW, Lamborghini, Alpine, and Peugeot. The LMP2 class served as the secondary prototype category before being removed from the regular WEC grid in 2024. Production-based grand tourers raced as LMGTE Pro and Am until those classes were replaced by LMGT3 in 2024.

History

The championship was announced in 2011 and launched in 2012 with an eight-race calendar. Audi won the first manufacturers' titles before Porsche's return in 2014 produced three straight Le Mans wins and championships (2015–2017). Toyota dominated the LMP1 twilight and the early Hypercar era, while Ferrari's return to the top class with the 499P in 2023 delivered consecutive Le Mans victories in 2023 and 2024.
