The Geysers is the largest complex of geothermal power plants in the world.

The Geysers is a geothermal field in the Mayacamas Mountains of Northern California, located about 72 miles (116 km) north of San Francisco in Sonoma and Lake counties. It is the largest complex of geothermal power plants in the world, drawing steam from more than 350 wells across roughly 30 square miles (78 km²).

Geology and history

The field is a vapor-dominated reservoir heated by a young magma body, the Clear Lake volcanic field. Hot springs in the area were known to Indigenous peoples and were developed into a resort, The Geysers Resort, in the 19th century, visited by figures such as Ulysses S. Grant and Mark Twain. Commercial power generation began in 1960, when Pacific Gas and Electric's Unit 1 (11 MW) became the first geothermal power plant in the United States of significant scale.

Production

Output peaked in the late 1980s at around 2,000 MW as many plants were built, then declined as steam pressure fell from overproduction. Since the 1990s, treated wastewater from Santa Rosa and Lake County has been piped to the field for injection, recharging the reservoir and stabilizing generation. The field now produces roughly 850–950 MW, enough to supply hundreds of thousands of homes, with most plants operated by Calpine. Induced seismicity, mostly small earthquakes, is monitored in the area.
