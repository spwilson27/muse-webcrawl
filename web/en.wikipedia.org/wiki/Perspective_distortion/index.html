Perspective distortion

Perspective distortion
FieldPhotography, optics
TypesExtension (wide-angle) and compression (telephoto)
CauseCamera-to-subject distance relative to subject depth

In photography and cinematography, perspective distortion is a warping or transformation of an object and its surrounding area that differs significantly from what the object would look like with a normal focal length. In fact it is determined not by the lens itself but by the relative distances between the camera and the objects photographed: objects closer to the camera appear larger relative to objects farther away in proportion to those distances.

Extension distortion

Extension or wide-angle distortion occurs when close objects are photographed from very near — as in selfies or wide-angle portraits — so that features nearest the lens (a nose, a forehead) loom disproportionately large. Wide-angle lenses exhibit this strongly because they permit close working distances, and because their wide field of view includes objects at very different distances.

Compression distortion

Compression, or telephoto distortion, is the converse: distant objects photographed from far away with a long lens appear flattened, with depth compressed and background elements looming large relative to the subject — as in photographs of a huge moon rising behind a city skyline. The layers of a scene appear stacked because the difference in distances is small relative to the total distance.

Optics and perception

Strictly, perspective depends only on viewpoint; cropping or changing focal length from the same position does not change it. What changes with distance is the ratio of distances to near and far parts of the subject. Viewed at the "correct" distance for the perspective captured, images appear undistorted; discomfort arises when the viewing geometry mismatches the capture geometry.
