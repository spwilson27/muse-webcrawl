Yule log (cake)

Yule log
Alternative namesBûche de Noël
TypeChristmas cake
CourseDessert
Place of originFrance
Main ingredientsGenoise sponge, chocolate buttercream

A Yule log or bûche de Noël (French pronunciation: [byʃ də nɔɛl]) is a traditional Christmas cake, often served as a dessert near Christmas, especially in France, Belgium, Luxembourg, Switzerland, and several former French colonies such as Vietnam. Made of sponge cake, it is baked and decorated to resemble a miniature traditional Yule log.

History

The cake emerged in the 19th century as Parisian bakers popularised a rolled sponge cake filled and frosted with chocolate buttercream, its surface scored with a fork to imitate tree bark and dusted with powdered sugar to suggest snow. The dessert commemorates the ancient custom of burning a large Yule log in the hearth during the Christmas season, a tradition dating to pre-Christian winter solstice celebrations in Europe.

Preparation

A thin genoise sponge is baked in a shallow Swiss roll pan, spread with filling, and rolled into a cylinder. The outside is coated with chocolate buttercream or ganache, textured to look like bark, and often decorated with meringue mushrooms, marzipan holly leaves, and a dusting of icing sugar. Variations include coffee, chestnut, praline, and Grand Marnier flavours.
