Kingdom of Afghanistan

Years: 1926 – 1973

Capital: Kabul

First king: Amanullah Khan

Last king: Mohammed Zahir Shah

The Kingdom of Afghanistan (Pashto: د افغانستان شاهي دولت; Dari: پادشاهی افغانستان) was a monarchy in Central Asia that existed from 1926, when Amanullah Khan proclaimed himself king (having ruled as emir since 1919), until the coup of 1973 that deposed Mohammed Zahir Shah and proclaimed a republic.

History

Amanullah pursued rapid modernisation, provoking conservative revolts that briefly brought the bandit-turned-ruler Habibullah Kalakani to power in 1929 before Mohammed Nadir Shah restored the dynasty. Nadir was assassinated in 1933 and succeeded by his son Zahir Shah, whose forty-year reign (1933–1973) was the longest in Afghan history. Under Zahir Shah, Afghanistan remained neutral in the Second World War and the Cold War, accepting aid from both the United States and the Soviet Union. A 1964 constitution introduced a constitutional monarchy and parliamentary elections.

End of the monarchy

On 17 July 1973, while Zahir Shah was abroad in Italy, his cousin and former prime minister Mohammed Daoud Khan seized power in a bloodless coup, abolishing the monarchy and declaring the Republic of Afghanistan. The king abdicated the following month. He returned to Kabul in 2002 after the fall of the Taliban and died in 2007, honoured as "Father of the Nation" but without restoration of the throne.
