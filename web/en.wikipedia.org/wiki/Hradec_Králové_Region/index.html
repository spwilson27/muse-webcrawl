Hradec Králové Region

The Hradec Králové Region (Czech: Královéhradecký kraj) is an administrative unit of the Czech Republic, located in the north-eastern part of the historical region of Bohemia. It is named after its capital, the city of Hradec Králové. The region borders Poland to the north and east.

Geography

The region covers an area of about 4,759 km² and has a population of roughly 540,000. Its northern part is mountainous, containing much of the Krkonoše (Giant Mountains) — the highest range in the Czech Republic, including Sněžka (1,603 m) — and the Orlické Mountains along the Polish border. The south is lowland along the Elbe (Labe) and Orlice rivers. The river Hron flows in neighbouring Slovakia.

Districts

The region is divided into five districts: Hradec Králové, Jičín, Náchod, Rychnov nad Kněžnou and Trutnov. Major towns besides the capital include Trutnov, Náchod, Jičín, Dvůr Králové nad Labem and the spa town of Janské Lázně.

Economy and tourism

The Krkonoše resorts of Špindlerův Mlýn and Pec pod Sněžkou are among the most popular skiing and hiking destinations in the country. The region also contains the Adršpach–Teplice Rocks sandstone formations, the baroque hospital at Kuks, and Josefov Fortress near Jaroměř. Agriculture, engineering and the manufacture of musical instruments (Petrof pianos, in Hradec Králové) are important industries.
