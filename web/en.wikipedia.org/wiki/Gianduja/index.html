Gianduja

Gianduja, the hazelnut chocolate paste of Turin

Gianduja or gianduia (Piedmontese: giandoja) is a sweet chocolate spread containing about 30% hazelnut paste, invented in Turin during Napoleon's regency (1796–1814). It takes its name from Gianduja, a mask in the Italian commedia dell'arte representing the archetypal Piedmontese.

History

During the Napoleonic Wars, the British blockade and Napoleon's Continental System sharply restricted supplies of cocoa to continental Europe. Turinese chocolatiers stretched the limited chocolate available by mixing it with paste of the hazelnuts abundant in the Langhe hills of Piedmont. The result became a speciality of the city. The chocolate maker Michele Prochet is often credited, in 1852, with refining the recipe; the firm Caffarel produced gianduia commercially, and the wrapped chocolate known as the gianduiotto was introduced for the Turin Carnival of 1865, taking its name from the Gianduja mask.

Products

Gianduja exists as solid chocolate (the gianduiotto), spreadable paste, and filling. The modern hazelnut-chocolate spread Nutella, created by the Ferrero company in 1964, descends from an earlier Ferrero product called Pasta Gianduja (1946) and Supercrema Gianduja.
