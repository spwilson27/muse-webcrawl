Immanuel

Immanuel (Hebrew: עִמָּנוּאֵל, romanized: ʿĪmmānūʾēl, meaning "God [is] with us") is a Hebrew name that appears in the Book of Isaiah (7:14) as a sign given to King Ahaz of Judah. In Christian theology the prophecy is read as foretelling the birth of Jesus, and "Immanuel" — spelled Emmanuel in the Greek-based form used in the Gospel of Matthew — became one of the titles of Christ.

The sign of Immanuel is given in Isaiah 7 during the Syro-Ephraimite War.

The sign in Isaiah

In Isaiah 7, Jerusalem is threatened by the allied kings of Aram (Syria) and Israel during the Syro-Ephraimite War (c. 735–732 BCE). The prophet Isaiah offers the reluctant King Ahaz a sign: "the young woman (almah) shall conceive and bear a son, and shall name him Immanuel." Before the child knows how to refuse evil and choose good, the lands of the two threatening kings are to be deserted. In its eighth-century context, most scholars read the sign as concerning a child of Isaiah's own time — possibly a royal child such as Hezekiah — whose early years would mark the timeframe of Judah's deliverance and subsequent Assyrian devastation.

Christian interpretation

The Greek Septuagint translated almah as parthenos ("virgin"), and Matthew 1:22–23 quotes Isaiah 7:14 in that form, applying it to the virgin birth of Jesus: "they shall name him Emmanuel, which means, 'God is with us'." The name thus functions in Christian tradition less as Jesus's given name than as a theological statement about the Incarnation. It appears prominently in Advent and Christmas liturgy and hymnody, notably the Advent chant and hymn "O come, O come, Emmanuel".

As a given name

Immanuel and its variants (Emmanuel, Manuel, Immanuil) are used as personal names in many cultures. Famous bearers include the philosopher Immanuel Kant and the Swedish mystic Emanuel Swedenborg.
