Leading-tone

In music theory, a leading-tone (also called a leading-note) is a note or pitch which resolves or "leads" to a note one semitone above or below it, typically the tonic. In a major scale, the seventh scale degree functions as a leading-tone because it lies a semitone below the tonic and strongly implies resolution to it.

Function

The leading-tone is a key element of tonal harmony: it is the third of the dominant chord (V) and creates the pull toward the tonic that defines cadences. In the natural minor scale the seventh degree is a whole step below the tonic and is called the subtonic; composers traditionally raised it in the harmonic and melodic minor scales to restore the leading-tone function.

Voice leading

In common-practice voice leading, the leading-tone generally resolves upward to the tonic, especially in outer voices, although in inner voices it may descend to the dominant when necessary for complete chords. The diminished triad and dominant seventh chord derive much of their tension from the leading-tone.
