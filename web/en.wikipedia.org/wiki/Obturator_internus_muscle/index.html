Obturator internus muscle

Obturator internus
OriginInner surface of the obturator membrane and surrounding bones of the pelvis
InsertionMedial surface of the greater trochanter of the femur
NerveNerve to obturator internus (L5, S1, S2)
ActionsLateral rotation of the extended thigh; abduction of the flexed thigh

The obturator internus is a muscle of the pelvis and hip. It originates from the inner surface of the obturator membrane and the surrounding margins of the obturator foramen on the ischium and pubis, passes out of the pelvis through the lesser sciatic foramen, and inserts on the medial surface of the greater trochanter of the femur.

Structure

The muscle's tendon turns sharply around the lesser sciatic notch, which acts as a pulley; a bursa separates the tendon from the bone at this point. After leaving the pelvis it is joined by the superior and inferior gemelli muscles, and the three together are sometimes called the triceps coxae. The obturator internus is supplied by the nerve to obturator internus, a branch of the sacral plexus.

Function

Working with the other short external rotators of the hip — the piriformis, obturator externus, gemelli, and quadratus femoris — the obturator internus laterally rotates the thigh when the hip is extended and abducts the thigh when the hip is flexed. It also helps stabilise the head of the femur in the acetabulum.

Clinical significance

The muscle gives its name to the obturator internus fascia, thickenings of which form the tendinous arch of the levator ani. Irritation or spasm of the muscle can contribute to deep gluteal pain syndromes, and its tendon is an anatomical landmark in posterior approaches to the hip joint.
