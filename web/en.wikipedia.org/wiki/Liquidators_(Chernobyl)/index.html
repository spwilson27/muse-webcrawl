Liquidators (Chernobyl)

Liquidators were the civil and military personnel in the Soviet Union who were called upon to deal with the consequences of the 1986 Chernobyl nuclear disaster. The term originates from the Soviet phrase for "liquidation of the consequences of the accident".

Work

An estimated 600,000 people took part in the cleanup between 1986 and 1990: extinguishing fires, decontaminating settlements and vehicles, clearing highly radioactive debris from the destroyed reactor's roof (in shifts of only seconds to minutes), evacuating residents, and building the sarcophagus (Shelter Object) enclosing Unit 4. Workers included firefighters, soldiers, miners who tunneled beneath the reactor, scientists, and medical staff.

Health effects

Liquidators received widely varying radiation doses; the highest went to early emergency workers, 134 of whom suffered acute radiation syndrome. Long-term studies document increased risks of leukemia, cardiovascular disease, and cataracts, and liquidators in Ukraine, Belarus, and Russia have received special benefits and commemorations, including the Chernobyl liquidator medal.
