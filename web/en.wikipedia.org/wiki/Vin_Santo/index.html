Vin Santo

Vin Santo (or Vino Santo, "holy wine") is a style of Italian dessert wine traditionally made in Tuscany from dried white grapes, most often Trebbiano and Malvasia. The grapes are left to dry — historically on straw mats or hanging from rafters — until around Holy Week or through the winter, concentrating their sugars.

A glass of amber Vin Santo with cantucci biscuits for dipping.

Production

After drying for several months, the grapes are pressed and the must is fermented and aged, traditionally in small sealed barrels called caratelli, stored in an attic room (the vinsantaia) exposed to seasonal temperature swings. Ageing lasts at least three years and often much longer. During this time a sediment of yeast known as the madre ("mother") is passed from barrel to barrel, contributing to the wine's distinctive character.

Style

Finished Vin Santo ranges from dry to very sweet, in colours from pale gold to deep amber, with flavours of apricot, honey, hazelnut, and orange peel. A dark version, Occhio di Pernice ("partridge eye"), is made from red grapes such as Sangiovese. In Tuscany the classic pairing is with cantucci, almond biscuits, which are dunked in the wine. Similar wines are made in other Italian regions and on the Greek island of Santorini, whose Vinsanto claims an even older origin for the name.
