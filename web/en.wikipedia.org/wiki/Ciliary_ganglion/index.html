Ciliary ganglion

Nerves of the orbit; the ciliary ganglion lies between the optic nerve and the lateral rectus muscle.

The ciliary ganglion is a small parasympathetic ganglion located in the posterior orbit, just behind the eyeball, between the optic nerve and the lateral rectus muscle. It is roughly the size of a pinhead (about 1–2 mm) and is one of four paired parasympathetic ganglia of the head, along with the pterygopalatine, submandibular, and otic ganglia. It gives rise to the short ciliary nerves, which carry postganglionic parasympathetic fibres to the eye.

Connections

Three roots enter the ganglion:

  • The parasympathetic (motor) root, carrying preganglionic fibres from the Edinger–Westphal nucleus via the inferior division of the oculomotor nerve (CN III). Only these fibres synapse in the ganglion.
  • The sympathetic root, carrying postganglionic fibres from the internal carotid plexus, which pass through without synapsing.
  • The sensory root, from the nasociliary nerve (a branch of the ophthalmic nerve, CN V1), also passing through.

Function

The short ciliary nerves (some six to ten fine branches) pierce the sclera around the optic nerve and supply the ciliary muscle and the sphincter pupillae muscle. Parasympathetic output from the ganglion therefore controls accommodation of the lens and constriction of the pupil (miosis). Damage to these postganglionic fibres produces a tonic pupil (Adie's pupil), in which the affected pupil reacts slowly to light but better to near effort.
