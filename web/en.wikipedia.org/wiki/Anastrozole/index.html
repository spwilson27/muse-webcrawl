Anastrozole, sold under the brand name Arimidex among others, is an antiestrogenic medication used in the treatment of breast cancer. Specifically, it is used for hormone receptor-positive breast cancer in postmenopausal women. It is also used off-label in other contexts, including the prevention of breast cancer in those at high risk and in fertility treatment.

Medical uses

Anastrozole is used in the adjuvant treatment of early-stage estrogen receptor-positive breast cancer in postmenopausal women, and as first-line treatment for locally advanced or metastatic disease. It is taken orally once per day. The ATAC trial demonstrated that anastrozole was more effective than tamoxifen in reducing recurrence in postmenopausal women, with a different side-effect profile.

Mechanism of action

Anastrozole is a nonsteroidal aromatase inhibitor. It works by reversibly binding to the aromatase enzyme and inhibiting the conversion of androgens (androstenedione and testosterone) into estrogens (estrone and estradiol) in peripheral tissues. In postmenopausal women, this reduces circulating estrogen levels by roughly 85 percent, depriving estrogen-dependent tumours of growth stimulation.

Side effects

Common side effects include hot flashes, joint pain (arthralgia), nausea, mood changes, and decreased bone mineral density, which increases the risk of osteoporosis and fractures. Compared with tamoxifen, anastrozole carries a lower risk of endometrial cancer and thromboembolism but a higher risk of musculoskeletal symptoms.

History

Anastrozole was developed by Imperial Chemical Industries (later AstraZeneca) and patented in 1987. It was approved for medical use in the United States in 1995. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines and is available as a generic medication.
