Nojpetén (also written Noh Petén, and called Tayasal by the Spanish) was the island capital of the Itza Maya kingdom, the last independent Maya polity, located on Lake Petén Itzá in the Petén region of present-day Guatemala. Its name means 'Great Island' in the Itza language. The city stood on the island now occupied by the town of Flores and survived as an independent Maya capital for more than 170 years after the Spanish conquest of the rest of Mesoamerica, until its capture in 1697.

History

The Itza migrated into the Petén lakes region by the early 15th century and made Nojpetén their capital, building temples, palaces and a large ceremonial assembly hall on the island. Hernán Cortés visited and passed through the Itza country in 1525 during his march to Honduras, leaving a lame horse that the Itza reportedly venerated after its death.

Missionary visits, notably by Franciscans in 1618–1619, were rebuffed, and the Itza king Kan Ek' alternately negotiated with and resisted Spanish demands to submit and convert. Spanish campaigns from Yucatán and Guatemala made repeated attempts on the kingdom through the 17th century.

Conquest and aftermath

In 1697 an expedition under Martín de Urzúa y Arizmendi built a road to the lake, constructed a brigantine, and launched an assault on Nojpetén on March 13; after brief fighting the city fell and its temples were destroyed. The conquest of Nojpetén is often taken as the final act of the Spanish conquest of the Maya world.

The Spanish founded the settlement of Flores on the site, where a church and plaza replaced the main temple pyramid. Archaeological investigation of the island and surrounding lake settlements has recovered Itza ceramics, architecture and evidence of the colonial mission town that followed.
