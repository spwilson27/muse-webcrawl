Scrubber

A scrubber is a device or system used to remove pollutants from an industrial exhaust stream. In a wet scrubber, the polluted gas is brought into contact with a scrubbing liquid — usually water or a reactive solution — which absorbs or reacts with contaminants such as sulfur dioxide, hydrogen chloride, particulates and odours before the cleaned gas is released.

Types

Common designs include spray towers, packed-bed scrubbers, venturi scrubbers, which use a high-velocity gas stream to atomise the liquid and capture fine particles, and dry or semi-dry scrubbers, which inject a dry sorbent. Scrubbers are standard equipment in chemical plants, incinerators, metal processing and power generation for meeting air-emission limits.

Other uses

The term also applies to marine exhaust-gas cleaning systems fitted to ships to comply with IMO sulfur rules, to CO₂ scrubbers that remove carbon dioxide from breathing gas in submarines, spacecraft and rebreathers, and, by analogy, to software and hardware that cleans unwanted material from data or signals.
