Jamatkhana

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A jamatkhana (from Persian: جماعت خانه, "gathering place") is the congregational place of worship and assembly for Ismaili Muslims, particularly the Nizari community whose spiritual leader is the Aga Khan. The word combines jama'at ("congregation" or "community") and khana ("house" or "place"). Jamatkhanas serve as the centre of the community's religious and social life.

Functions

Within a jamatkhana, Ismailis gather for daily prayers (du'a), recitation of devotional literature such as ginans, and religious festivals including Navroz and the commemorations of the Imams' birthdays and accession anniversaries. Beyond worship, jamatkhanas host educational programmes, youth and women's activities, health and welfare services, and community meetings administered by appointed volunteers and local councils.

Historically, in parts of South Asia, the jamatkhana also functioned as a place of refuge, charitable distribution and communal deliberation. Access to the prayer hall is traditionally restricted to Ismailis, reflecting the esoteric character of the community's ritual practice.

Ismaili Centres

In the modern period, the Aga Khan has commissioned a series of high-profile Ismaili Centres in cities including London, Vancouver (Burnaby), Lisbon, Dubai, Dushanbe and Toronto. These combine a jamatkhana with representative and cultural functions open to the wider public, and are noted for architecture that blends Islamic design traditions with local contexts. The first purpose-built Ismaili Centre in the Western world opened in Burnaby, British Columbia, in 1985.
