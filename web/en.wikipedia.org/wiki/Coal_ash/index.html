Coal ash

Coal ash, also called coal combustion residuals, is the solid waste left after coal is burned in power plants and industrial boilers. It includes fly ash, which rises with exhaust gases and is captured by filters, and bottom ash, which collects at the base of the furnace.

Coal ash
TypesFly ash, bottom ash, boiler slag, flue-gas desulfurisation gypsum
ContainsSilica, alumina, calcium oxide, trace heavy metals

Uses

Fly ash is widely recycled as a supplementary cementitious material in concrete, where it can improve workability and durability. Bottom ash is used in road construction and as aggregate.

Environmental concerns

Unrecycled ash is stored in landfills and surface impoundments ("ash ponds"), which can leak arsenic, mercury, lead and other contaminants into groundwater. Major spills, such as the 2008 Kingston Fossil Plant spill in Tennessee, prompted stricter regulation in the United States.
