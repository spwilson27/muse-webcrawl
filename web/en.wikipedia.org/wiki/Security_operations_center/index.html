Security operations center

A security operations center (SOC) is a facility or organizational unit that houses an information security team responsible for monitoring and analyzing an organization's security posture on an ongoing basis. The SOC team's goal is to detect, analyze, and respond to cybersecurity incidents using a combination of technology solutions and a strong set of processes. Security operations centers are typically staffed with security analysts, engineers, and managers who oversee security operations.

Functions

The central functions of a SOC include continuous monitoring of networks, servers, endpoints, databases, applications, and websites for signs of security incidents; triage and investigation of alerts; incident response and recovery; threat intelligence gathering; vulnerability management support; and compliance reporting. SOCs commonly rely on a security information and event management (SIEM) system to aggregate and correlate log data.

Staffing and tiers

SOC analysts are often organized in tiers: Tier 1 analysts perform initial triage of alerts, Tier 2 analysts conduct deeper investigations, and Tier 3 analysts and threat hunters handle advanced incidents and proactive hunting. Many organizations operate their own SOC, while others outsource the function to a managed security service provider (MSSP) or use a hybrid model.

Related concepts

A SOC differs from a network operations center (NOC), which focuses on network performance and availability rather than security. Modern SOCs increasingly integrate security orchestration, automation, and response (SOAR) tooling to speed up routine response tasks.
