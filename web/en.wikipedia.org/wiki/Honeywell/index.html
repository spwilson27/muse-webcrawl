Honeywell International Inc. is an American publicly traded, multinational conglomerate corporation headquartered in Charlotte, North Carolina. It primarily operates in four areas of business: aerospace, building technologies, performance materials and technologies, and safety and productivity solutions.

History

The company's roots trace to 1885, when Albert Butz invented the damper flapper, a thermostat-like device for coal furnaces, and founded the Butz Thermo-Electric Regulator Company in Minneapolis. Mark C. Honeywell founded the Honeywell Heating Specialty Company in Wabash, Indiana, in 1906; it merged in 1927 with the Minneapolis Heat Regulator Company to form the Minneapolis-Honeywell Regulator Company. The company became famous for its round thermostats, including the iconic T87 "Round" designed by Henry Dreyfuss in 1953.

Minneapolis-Honeywell expanded into controls, aerospace, and computing — it was one of the early mainframe computer companies (the "BUNCH" era rivalry with IBM) before selling its computer business to Groupe Bull in stages ending in 1991. In 1999, AlliedSignal acquired Honeywell Inc. in a $15 billion deal and adopted the Honeywell name for its greater brand recognition. A planned merger with General Electric in 2000–2001 was blocked by the European Commission on competition grounds.

Business segments

  • Aerospace — avionics, aircraft engines (including the TFE731 and HTF7000 families), auxiliary power units, flight management systems, and wheels and brakes.
  • Building Technologies — HVAC controls, building management systems, fire and security products.
  • Performance Materials and Technologies — process automation (Honeywell Process Solutions), UOP refining technologies, advanced materials including Solstice refrigerants and Spectra fibre.
  • Safety and Productivity Solutions — personal protective equipment, warehouse automation (Intelligrated), sensing and productivity products.

Corporate affairs

Honeywell is a Fortune 500 company and a component of the Dow Jones Industrial Average. Its headquarters moved to Charlotte, North Carolina, in 2019. In 2025 the company announced plans to separate into three independent public companies (aerospace, automation, and advanced materials), following activist investor pressure.
