Kohn Pedersen Fox Associates (KPF) is an American architectural firm founded in New York City in 1976 by A. Eugene Kohn, William Pedersen, and Sheldon Fox. It is one of the world's largest architecture practices and is especially known for supertall skyscrapers, major corporate headquarters, and large-scale urban master plans.

History

The three founders left the firm John Carl Warnecke & Associates to start their own practice. Within a decade KPF had become a leading designer of American office towers, reinterpretating the skyscraper with contextual massing and dressed stone facades in works such as 333 Wacker Drive in Chicago (1983) and the Procter & Gamble headquarters in Cincinnati (1985). From the 1990s the firm expanded aggressively into Asia and Europe, designing many of the era's signature high-rises.

Notable works

Major completed projects include the Shanghai World Financial Center (2008) — for a time China's tallest building, recognisable for its trapezoidal aperture; the International Commerce Centre in Hong Kong (2010), among the world's tallest buildings; the Lotte World Tower in Seoul (2017); One Vanderbilt in New York (2020), directly connected to Grand Central Terminal; the Heron Tower and the Scalpel in London; the master plan for Hudson Yards' eastern phase in collaboration with others; and the Petersen Automotive Museum's ribbon facade in Los Angeles. KPF has designed six of the world's twelve tallest buildings completed in its era of supertall work and has received numerous AIA and CTBUH awards.
