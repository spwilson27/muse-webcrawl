Saint Pishoy (Coptic: ⲡⲓϣⲱⲓ; 320 – 417), also spelled Bishoy or Pishoi, was an Egyptian Desert Father and monk, venerated as a saint in the Coptic Orthodox Church. He was a disciple of Saint Pambo and a contemporary of Saint Macarius the Great and Saint John the Dwarf in the monastic settlement of Scetes (Wadi El Natrun).

Life

Pishoy was the youngest of six brothers. According to the Coptic Synaxarium, his mother saw an angel who told her that one of her children was chosen by God; she pointed to the weakling Pishoy. He entered the monastery at Scetes under Saint Pambo, where he became known for extreme asceticism — he is said to have tied his hair to a rope from the ceiling to keep himself awake in prayer.

Visions and sayings

Coptic tradition holds that Pishoy saw Jesus Christ several times and washed his feet, and that angels repeatedly lifted his worn body. A celebrated saying attributed to him — that if we see our neighbour we shall see God — reflects his emphasis on charity: he is remembered for refusing to judge a sinful monk, carrying the man's burden-sack and declaring that he carried his own sins behind him, unseen.

Legacy

The Monastery of Saint Pishoy in Wadi El Natrun, founded around his community, keeps what is believed to be his incorrupt body and remains a major centre of Coptic monasticism and pilgrimage.
