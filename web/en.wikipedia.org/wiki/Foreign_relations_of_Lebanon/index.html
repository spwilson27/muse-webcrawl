Foreign relations of Lebanon

The foreign relations of Lebanon are shaped by the country's position in the Levant, its large global diaspora, and its internal sectarian balance, which has historically made Lebanese politics an arena for regional rivalries between, principally, Saudi Arabia and the Gulf states on one side and Syria and Iran on the other.

Regional relations

Lebanon is a founding member of the Arab League. It has never signed a peace treaty with Israel; the two countries remain formally at war, and the disputed Shebaa Farms area and the armed power of Hezbollah — backed by Iran — have led to repeated conflicts, including the 2006 Lebanon War and renewed hostilities in 2023–2024. Syria's influence, dominant during its 1976–2005 military presence, remains a central theme; Lebanon hosts well over a million Syrian refugees displaced by the Syrian civil war, alongside several hundred thousand Palestinian refugees.

Wider relations

France, the former mandate power, retains close cultural and political ties. The United States and European Union are major donors and supporters of the Lebanese Armed Forces. A maritime boundary agreement with Israel, brokered by the United States in 2022, opened the way for offshore gas exploration. Since 2019 Lebanon's economic collapse has deepened its dependence on foreign aid and the remittances of its diaspora.
