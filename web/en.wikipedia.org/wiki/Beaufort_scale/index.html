Beaufort scale

The Beaufort scale is an empirical measure that relates wind speed to observed conditions at sea or on land. The scale was devised in 1805 by the Irish Royal Navy officer Francis Beaufort (later Rear Admiral Sir Francis Beaufort). It originally ran from 0, "calm", to 12, "hurricane", based on the effect of wind on the sails of a man-of-war. The scale was adopted by the Royal Navy in the 1830s and later standardised internationally, with wind-speed equivalents added in the 20th century.

Modern usage

The scale remains in daily use in marine forecasts, including the BBC Shipping Forecast, where terms such as "gale force 8" are routine. Extended versions with forces up to 17 are used in parts of East Asia for tropical cyclones. Wind speeds in the standard scale correspond to a 10-minute mean measured at 10 metres above the surface.
