Grey Tuesday was a day of online protest held on 24 February 2004, organised in response to legal threats against DJ Danger Mouse's The Grey Album, a mashup album combining vocals from Jay-Z's The Black Album with instrumentals built entirely from samples of the Beatles' The White Album. EMI, which held rights in the Beatles recordings, had sent cease-and-desist letters to distributors and retailers of the unofficial album.

The protest

Coordinated by the activist site Downhill Battle, around 170 websites turned their pages grey for the day and offered The Grey Album for free download as an act of civil disobedience against what participants argued was an overbroad use of copyright law against transformative sampling. The album was downloaded more than 100,000 times that day by common estimates, drawing wide press coverage and making the episode a landmark in debates over sampling, fair use and remix culture. No lawsuits were filed against the protesters, and the publicity significantly raised Danger Mouse's profile; he went on to mainstream success as a producer and as one half of Gnarls Barkley.
