Parasports

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Parasports are sports played by people with a disability, including physical and intellectual disabilities. Some parasports are variations on existing able-bodied sports, while others have been specifically created for persons with a disability and do not have an able-bodied equivalent. Disability exists in four categories: physical, mental, permanent and temporary. At a competitive level, disability sport classifications are applied to allow people of varying abilities to face similar opposition.

History

Organised sport for athletes with a disability developed after the Second World War, partly in response to the needs of wounded veterans and civilians. In 1948, Sir Ludwig Guttmann organised the Stoke Mandeville Games for British veterans with spinal cord injuries, timing them to coincide with the London Olympics. These grew into the Paralympic Games, first held in Rome in 1960. The Special Olympics, founded in 1968, serve athletes with intellectual disabilities, and the Deaflympics, dating to 1924, serve deaf athletes.

Organisation

The International Paralympic Committee (IPC) is the global governing body of the Paralympic Movement and organises the Summer and Winter Paralympic Games. International federations govern individual sports, and classification systems group athletes by the extent of activity limitation resulting from their impairment. Major multi-sport events include the Invictus Games for wounded service personnel and regional para games such as the Parapan American Games.

Categories of parasport

  • Adapted sports — modifications of existing sports, e.g. wheelchair basketball, sitting volleyball, para swimming.
  • Purpose-created sports — sports invented for disabled athletes, e.g. goalball and boccia.
  • Deaf sports — sports organised by and for deaf athletes, largely without rules modification.
  • Special Olympics sports — for athletes with intellectual disabilities.
  • Invictus sports — adaptive sports for wounded, injured and sick military personnel.

Classification

Classification aims to ensure that success is determined by skill, fitness, power, endurance, tactical ability and mental focus rather than by degree of impairment. Athletes are assessed and assigned a sport class; medical and technical classifiers review eligibility and class status at competitions.
