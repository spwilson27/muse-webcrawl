Shirley MacLaine

Shirley MacLaine (born Shirley MacLean Beaty; April 24, 1934) is an American actress, singer, dancer and author. With a career spanning more than seven decades, she has won an Academy Award, an Emmy, two BAFTA Awards and six Golden Globes, and received the AFI Life Achievement Award in 2012 and Kennedy Center Honors in 2013.

Early life and career

MacLaine was born in Richmond, Virginia, and trained as a ballet dancer. On Broadway she was an understudy in The Pajama Game; when the star broke an ankle in 1954, MacLaine went on and was spotted by a film producer. Her screen debut came in Alfred Hitchcock's The Trouble with Harry (1955), winning her a Golden Globe for New Star of the Year.

Film highlights

She starred in Billy Wilder's The Apartment (1960) and Irma la Douce (1963), in Some Came Running (1958), Being There (1979) and Steel Magnolias (1989). She won the Academy Award for Best Actress for Terms of Endearment (1983), playing Aurora Greenway opposite Debra Winger. She also directed the documentary The Other Half of the Sky: A China Memoir (1975).

Writing and beliefs

MacLaine is a well-known advocate of New Age spirituality. Her bestselling memoir Out on a Limb (1983) described her spiritual journey and belief in reincarnation, and was adapted into a television miniseries. Her younger brother is the actor Warren Beatty.
