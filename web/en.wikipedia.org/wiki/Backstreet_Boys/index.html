The Backstreet Boys are an American vocal group formed in Orlando, Florida, in 1993, consisting of Nick Carter, Howie Dorough, AJ McLean, Brian Littrell and Kevin Richardson. They are the best-selling boy band of all time, with over 100 million records sold worldwide.

Career

Put together by impresario Lou Pearlman, the group first found success in Europe before breaking through in the United States with their 1997 American debut. Their albums Millennium (1999) and Black & Blue (2000) made them global superstars, with hits including "I Want It That Way", "Quit Playing Games (with My Heart)", "Everybody (Backstreet's Back)" and "Shape of My Heart".

Later years

After a hiatus and Richardson's temporary departure (2006–2012), the group reunited with its original line-up, recorded DNA (2019) — their first US number-one album in nearly two decades — and toured extensively. They received a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame in 2013.
