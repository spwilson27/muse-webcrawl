GAL (paramilitary group)

The GAL (Spanish: Grupos Antiterroristas de Liberación, "Antiterrorist Liberation Groups") were a paramilitary group active from 1983 to 1987, operating mainly in the French Basque Country. They were a death squad organised as part of Spain's "dirty war" against the Basque separatist organisation ETA, killing at least 27 people, about a third of whom were not ETA members.

History

From 1983, kidnappings, torture and assassinations were carried out in the French Basque Country against suspected ETA members and sympathisers. The most notorious crimes included the kidnapping of Segundo Marey (mistaken for an ETA member), and the 1985 Monbar Hotel attack in Bayonne, which killed four people.

Investigations by journalists and later by judge Baltasar Garzón revealed that GAL was financed with Spanish government slush funds and run with the involvement of senior officials in the government of Felipe González, including interior minister José Barrionuevo, who was convicted in 1998 for the Marey kidnapping. The scandal — the GAL affair — was a major blow to the PSOE government in the 1990s and a landmark case of state terrorism in a European democracy.
