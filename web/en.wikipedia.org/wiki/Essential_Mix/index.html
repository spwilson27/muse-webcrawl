The Essential Mix is a weekly radio show on BBC Radio 1 featuring a two-hour uninterrupted DJ mix by a guest DJ, producer or electronic music artist. First broadcast on 30 October 1993, it is one of the longest-running and most influential dance music programmes in the world, traditionally aired in the early hours of the weekend and presented by Pete Tong.

Format

Each episode consists of a continuous two-hour set recorded by the guest, intended to showcase their current sound, influences or a special concept. Mixes have originated from studios, clubs and festivals around the world. The show has featured virtually every major figure in electronic music, and an annual "Essential Mix of the Year" is selected from the year's broadcasts.

History

The programme was created as Radio 1 expanded its dance coverage in the early 1990s. Paul Oakenfold's 1994 "Goa Mix" broadcast is frequently cited as one of the greatest radio mixes of all time. The series has launched careers and preserved landmark sets, with archived tracklists maintained by devoted listeners.

Cultural impact

An invitation to record an Essential Mix is regarded as a career milestone for DJs. The show helped define the superstar-DJ era of the 1990s and remains a reference point for electronic music culture.
