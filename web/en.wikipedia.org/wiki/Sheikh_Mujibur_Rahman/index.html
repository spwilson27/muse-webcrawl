Sheikh Mujibur Rahman

Sheikh Mujibur Rahman
Born: 17 March 1920, Tungipara, Bengal
Died: 15 August 1975, Dhaka (assassinated)
Offices: First President and Prime Minister of Bangladesh

Sheikh Mujibur Rahman (1920–1975), often called "Bangabandhu" (Friend of Bengal), was a Bengali politician and statesman regarded as the founding father of Bangladesh. He led the Bengali nationalist movement, headed the Awami League, and proclaimed Bangladeshi independence in 1971.

Career

After his Six Points movement for autonomy, his party won Pakistan's 1970 election but was denied power, precipitating the Bangladesh Liberation War. Mujib became Bangladesh's first President and then Prime Minister (1972–1975), steering a war-ravaged country amid famine and unrest. His move to a one-party system in 1975 was controversial. On 15 August 1975 he and most of his family were assassinated by army officers. His daughter Sheikh Hasina later served as Prime Minister.
