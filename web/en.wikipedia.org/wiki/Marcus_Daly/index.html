Marcus Daly

Marcus Daly (5 December 1841 – 12 November 1900) was an Irish-born American businessman known as one of the three “Copper Kings” of Butte, Montana, United States, along with William A. Clark and F. Augustus Heinze. He was the founder of the Anaconda Copper Mining Company, one of the largest mining concerns in the world.

Early life

Daly was born in Ballyjamesduff, County Cavan, Ireland, into a poor Catholic family. At the age of fifteen he emigrated to New York City, working at various jobs before travelling west to California in 1861 to work in the gold and silver mines. He later moved to Nevada and Utah, where he gained experience in mining operations and eventually managed the Alice Mine in Walkerville, Montana.

Anaconda

In 1880, acting as an agent for the Walker brothers of Salt Lake City, Daly purchased the Anaconda silver mine in Butte. While developing it, the miners struck an enormous body of high-grade copper ore. Recognizing the value of copper for the coming electrical age, Daly persuaded George Hearst and other San Francisco investors to back the venture, and the Anaconda Mining Company was organized. The town of Anaconda, Montana, was founded by Daly in 1883 to house the smelter that processed the ore.

Later life and legacy

Daly engaged in a long and bitter rivalry with William A. Clark, spending heavily on Montana politics and newspapers to advance his interests. He died in New York City in 1900. A bronze statue of Daly stands at the entrance to the Montana Tech campus in Butte.
