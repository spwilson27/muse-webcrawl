Fair Work Commission

The Fair Work Commission (FWC) is the Australian industrial relations tribunal created by the Fair Work Act 2009. It began operating on 1 July 2009 as Fair Work Australia, and was renamed the Fair Work Commission on 1 January 2013 following amendments passed in 2012. It is the successor to bodies including the Australian Industrial Relations Commission.

Functions

The Commission is Australia's national workplace relations tribunal. Its powers include: setting and varying modern awards; conducting the annual wage review that sets the national minimum wage and award rates; approving enterprise agreements; hearing unfair dismissal and general protections claims; dealing with industrial disputes and bargaining orders; and regulating registered organisations (unions and employer associations). Decisions are made by members — a president, deputy presidents, commissioners and expert panel members — appointed by the Governor-General.

Relationship to other bodies

The FWC works alongside the Fair Work Ombudsman, which enforces compliance with workplace laws, and the Fair Work division of the Federal Court of Australia and Federal Circuit Court, which hear civil remedy matters. Appeals from the Commission lie to a Full Bench and, on questions of law, to the Federal Court.
