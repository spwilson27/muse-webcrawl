Atil (also spelled Ätil or Itil) was the capital city of the Khazar Khaganate from the mid-8th century until the fall of the khaganate in the late 10th century. Its name derives from the Turkic name for the Volga River (Atil/Itil), in whose delta the city stood.

History

After the Khazars were pushed westward by Arab armies in the Caucasus during the Arab–Khazar wars, the capital was moved from Balanjar to the lower Volga. Atil grew into a major commercial hub on the trade routes between Northern Europe, the Islamic world and Byzantium. The city was divided into sections: the island containing the khagan's palace, the eastern (commercial) part, and the western part, connected by a bridge of boats. Arabic geographers described it as a populous, multi-religious city with mosques, churches and synagogues, and a population including Muslims, Jews, Christians and pagans.

Destruction and site

Atil was destroyed by Sviatoslav I of Kiev during his campaign against Khazaria around 965–969. The city was never rebuilt to its former importance, and its exact location has long been debated. Archaeologists have proposed sites in the Volga delta near Astrakhan; in 2008, excavations at Samosdelka were announced as possibly identifying the city, though the identification remains uncertain.
