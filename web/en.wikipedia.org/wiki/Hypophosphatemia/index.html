Hypophosphatemia

Specialty: Endocrinology, nephrology
Definition: Low serum phosphate (< 2.5 mg/dL in adults)

Hypophosphatemia is an electrolyte disorder in which there is a low level of phosphate in the blood. Normal serum phosphate is roughly 2.5–4.5 mg/dL (0.81–1.45 mmol/L) in adults. Mild hypophosphatemia is common and often inconsequential, but severe depletion can cause muscle weakness, respiratory failure, hemolysis, and encephalopathy.

Causes

Mechanisms fall into three groups: redistribution of phosphate into cells (refeeding syndrome, insulin treatment of diabetic ketoacidosis, respiratory alkalosis, hungry bone syndrome after parathyroidectomy); decreased intestinal absorption (vitamin D deficiency, phosphate binders, malabsorption, alcohol use disorder); and increased renal excretion (hyperparathyroidism, Fanconi syndrome, and fibroblast growth factor 23 (FGF23)-mediated disorders such as X-linked hypophosphatemia and tumour-induced osteomalacia).

Signs and symptoms

Most patients with mild or chronic hypophosphatemia have few symptoms; chronic phosphate wasting presents with rickets in children or osteomalacia, bone pain, and fractures in adults. Acute severe hypophosphatemia impairs ATP generation and 2,3-bisphosphoglycerate production, producing proximal muscle weakness, rhabdomyolysis, diaphragmatic weakness, cardiac dysfunction, altered mental status, and red-cell fragility.

Treatment

Treatment addresses the cause and replaces phosphate, orally for mild chronic cases and intravenously for severe or symptomatic depletion, with care to avoid hypocalcemia and metastatic calcification. FGF23-mediated disease in X-linked hypophosphatemia can be treated with burosumab, a monoclonal antibody against FGF23.
