Bolt action

Bolt action is a type of manual firearm action that is operated by directly manipulating the bolt via a bolt handle. The bolt typically locks the breech, fires the cartridge, then is unlocked, extracted and ejected by rotating and sliding the handle, cocking the weapon and readying a new round. Most bolt-action firearms are rifles, though bolt-action shotguns, carbines and handguns exist.

History

The first bolt-action rifle was the Dreyse needle gun, adopted by Prussia in 1841, whose paper-cartridge "needle" ignition gave Prussian infantry a decisive rate-of-fire advantage. Metallic cartridges led to designs such as the French Chassepot and Gras, the British Lee–Metford and Lee–Enfield, the Russian Mosin–Nagant, and the German Mauser actions — the Gewehr 98's controlled-feed action is widely regarded as the classic bolt-action design. Bolt actions were the standard infantry rifle action of both World Wars before being displaced in military service by semi-automatic and automatic rifles.

Operation and characteristics

Because the action is strong, simple and mechanically locked at the moment of firing, bolt-action rifles are noted for accuracy and reliability, and they dominate precision shooting, sniping and much hunting. Variants include turn-bolt designs, in which the handle is lifted and pulled back, and straight-pull designs such as the Swiss Schmidt–Rubin and K31, in which the handle is pulled straight to the rear. Cock-on-open and cock-on-close systems differ in when the striker is cocked during the cycle.
