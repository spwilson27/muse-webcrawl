China National Highway 318 (G318) runs from People's Square in Shanghai in the east to Zhangmu on the China–Nepal border in the Tibet Autonomous Region. At 5,476 kilometres (3,403 mi) it is the longest of the China National Highways, traversing the full width of the country from the East China Sea to the Himalayas.

The highway passes through eight province-level divisions and crosses some of China's most dramatic landscapes. Its western portion comprises the Sichuan–Tibet Highway, famous for high mountain passes on the eastern Tibetan Plateau — several above 4,000 metres — deep river gorges of the Jinsha, Lancang and Nu rivers, and celebrated scenery such as the "72 bends" of the Nujiang.

Route

From Shanghai, G318 runs west through Suzhou, Huzhou, and into Anhui, passing Xuancheng and Anqing before entering Hubei, where it serves Yichang and Enshi. It continues through Chongqing municipality into Sichuan, reaching Chengdu, then climbs westward via Kangding, Litang and Markam into Tibet, passing Nyingchi before reaching Lhasa. West of Lhasa it continues through Shigatse and Tingri to Nyalam and the border town of Zhangmu, where it connects with Nepal's Araniko Highway toward Kathmandu.

Significance

The Shanghai–Lhasa section of G318 has become one of China's most famous road-trip and cycling routes, nicknamed for its sequence of landmark kilometre markers; a monument at the 5,000 km point and the concept of a "318 road trip" are fixtures of domestic tourism. Much of the route's long-haul traffic has been supplemented by parallel expressways, but the highway remains a vital artery for western Sichuan and Tibet.
