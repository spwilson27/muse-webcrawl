Hild of Whitby

Saint Hild
Born: c. 614
Died: 17 November 680
Office: Abbess of Whitby
Feast day: 17 November

Hild of Whitby (c. 614 – 17 November 680), also known as Saint Hilda, was an English abbess and a founder of the monastery at Whitby. A great-niece of King Edwin of Northumbria, she was baptised in 627 and, after a secular life at court, became a nun under the influence of Aidan of Lindisfarne.

Abbess

Hild first ruled the abbey at Hartlepool, then in 657 founded a double monastery at Streoneshalh, modern Whitby, for both men and women. Her monastery became a major centre of learning; five of its monks became bishops, and the herdsman Cædmon composed the earliest surviving Old English Christian poetry there.

Synod of Whitby

In 664 her abbey hosted the Synod of Whitby, at which King Oswiu decided that Northumbria would follow the Roman method of calculating Easter rather than the Ionan tradition.

Legacy

Bede praised Hild's wisdom, noting that kings and princes sought her counsel. She is venerated as a saint in the Roman Catholic, Anglican and Orthodox traditions.

References: Bede, Ecclesiastical History of the English People, Book IV.
