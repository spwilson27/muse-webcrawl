Urasenke (裏千家) is one of the three main schools (san-Senke) of the Japanese tea ceremony, alongside Omotesenke and Mushakōjisenke. It is the largest of the three, with the majority of tea practitioners in Japan and abroad affiliated with its tradition. The school is headquartered at the Konnichian estate in Kyoto.

History

The three Sen schools descend from Sen no Rikyū, the 16th-century tea master who perfected the wabi aesthetic of tea. Rikyū's grandson Sen Sōtan divided his Kyoto estate among his sons: the front house (Omotesenke) on one street and the rear house (Urasenke, the "rear Sen family") on the street behind. Urasenke descends from Sōtan's third son, Sen Sōshitsu I, who became tea master to the Maeda lords of Kaga.

Grand masters

The head of Urasenke bears the hereditary name Sen Sōshitsu. Sen Sōshitsu XV (Hōunsai, 1923–2025) was particularly active in promoting tea internationally under the motto "Peacefulness through a Bowl of Tea," founding Urasenke chapters and cultural centres around the world, including Urasenke Tankokai associations in many countries. He was succeeded by his son, Sen Sōshitsu XVI (Zabōsai).

Teaching and outreach

Urasenke maintains a structured curriculum of temae (tea procedures) taught through licensed instructors, and it supports academic study of tea culture through institutions such as the Urasenke Gakuen professional school in Kyoto and partnerships with universities. Its international foundation and Tankokai associations organize demonstrations, workshops and teacher training outside Japan.
