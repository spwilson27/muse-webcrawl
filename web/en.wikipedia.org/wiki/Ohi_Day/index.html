Ohi Day

Ohi Day (Greek: Επέτειος του Όχι, "Anniversary of the No") is a public holiday celebrated in Greece, Cyprus, and Greek communities around the world on 28 October each year. It commemorates the rejection, on 28 October 1940, of the ultimatum delivered by Italian dictator Benito Mussolini to Greek Prime Minister Ioannis Metaxas, which demanded that Axis forces be allowed to occupy strategic locations in Greece.

The ultimatum

In the early hours of 28 October 1940, Italian ambassador Emanuele Grazzi presented Metaxas with an ultimatum giving Greece three hours to accept occupation. According to popular tradition, Metaxas answered with a single word: "Ohi" ("No"). His recorded response in French was reportedly "Alors, c'est la guerre" ("Then it is war"). Within hours, Italian troops invaded Greece from Italian-occupied Albania, beginning the Greco-Italian War. Against expectations, Greek forces repelled the invasion and counter-attacked deep into Albania, achieving the first Allied land victories of the Second World War, before Germany intervened in April 1941.

Celebrations

Ohi Day is marked by a major military parade in Thessaloniki, attended by the President of Greece, and by student and veterans' parades throughout the country. Museums and war memorials hold commemorations, and Greek flags are displayed on public buildings and homes. The holiday, first observed during the war years, became officially established after the Second World War and remains one of Greece's two principal national days, alongside Independence Day on 25 March.
