Radha, the supreme goddess of love and devotion in Krishna tradition.

Radha (Sanskrit: राधा), also called Radharani, is a Hindu goddess and the chief consort of the god Krishna. In the Vaishnava traditions of the Braj region she is revered as the goddess of love, tenderness, compassion and devotion, and is regarded as the personification of Krishna's internal energy (hladini shakti). In many traditions, notably Gaudiya Vaishnavism and the Radha Vallabha Sampradaya, Radha is worshipped as the supreme deity alongside or even above Krishna.

Origins and literature

Radha's prominence grew in medieval devotional literature. She is a central figure in Jayadeva's 12th-century Sanskrit poem Gita Govinda, in the Bhagavata Purana (where she is traditionally identified though not named prominently), and in later Puranas such as the Brahma Vaivarta and Padma Purana. Her birthplace is given as Barsana (Rawal) and her childhood home as Vrindavan, in the Braj region of present-day Uttar Pradesh.

Theology

In Gaudiya theology, Radha and Krishna are one soul in two forms; her love for Krishna represents the highest form of bhakti (devotion), and her longing in separation (viraha) is a model of the soul's yearning for God. In the Nimbarka and Radha Vallabha traditions, the divine couple Radha-Krishna is the joint object of worship.

Worship

Radha's birthday, Radhashtami, is celebrated in Braj and by ISKCON communities worldwide. Temples dedicated to Radha-Krishna, such as Prem Mandir in Vrindavan, and festivals including Holi and Janmashtami centre on her pastimes with Krishna, including the divine dance (rasa lila).
