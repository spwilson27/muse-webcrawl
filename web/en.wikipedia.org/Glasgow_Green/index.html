Glasgow Green

Glasgow Green is a park in the east end of Glasgow, Scotland, on the north bank of the River Clyde. Established in the 15th century, it is the oldest of the city's parks and covers about 55 hectares (136 acres).

History

The Green was granted to the people of Glasgow in 1450 by Bishop William Turnbull, originally for common uses such as grazing, washing and bleaching linen, and drying fishing nets. It later became a focus for public gatherings, political rallies, fairs and sport. The Glasgow Fair was traditionally held on the Green, and it was a notable site of Radical and Chartist meetings in the 19th century.

Landmarks

The park contains the People's Palace museum and Winter Gardens, opened in 1898, the Doulton Fountain — the largest terracotta fountain in the world — and the Nelson Monument, erected in 1806. The McLennan Arch stands at the north-west entrance. The Green remains a venue for concerts, festivals and sporting events, and hosted events during the 2014 Commonwealth Games.
