Sir Geoffrey Ingram Taylor (7 March 1886 – 27 June 1975), usually cited as G. I. Taylor, was a British physicist and mathematician, and a major figure in fluid dynamics and wave theory. His grandfather was George Boole, founder of Boolean logic.

Life

Taylor studied at Trinity College, Cambridge, and remained at Cambridge for most of his career, becoming Royal Society Research Professor in 1937. An enthusiastic sailor and inventor, he designed the CQR anchor used worldwide. During the Second World War he contributed to ordnance research and was sent to the United States to share results on blast waves; his analysis of the Trinity test photographs famously allowed an estimate of the bomb's yield.

Work

Taylor's contributions span an extraordinary range: statistical theory of turbulence and turbulent diffusion, the Taylor–Goldstein equation for stratified flows, Taylor columns in rotating fluids, Rayleigh–Taylor instability, the stability of viscous flow between rotating cylinders (Taylor–Couette flow), and pioneering work on dislocations in crystal plasticity with Polanyi and Orowan. He published nearly 250 papers over six decades.

Honours

Taylor was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1919, knighted in 1944, and appointed to the Order of Merit. The Taylor–Couette apparatus, the G. I. Taylor lecture of the Institute of Physics, and a crater on the Moon bear his name.
