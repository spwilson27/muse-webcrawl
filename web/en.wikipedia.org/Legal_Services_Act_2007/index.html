Legal Services Act 2007

The Legal Services Act 2007 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that reformulated the legal services market in England and Wales, and established the Legal Services Board as an oversight regulator. The Act also allowed for new forms of legal practice known as alternative business structures (ABS), under which non-lawyers could own and invest in law firms — a reform nicknamed "Tesco law" in the press.


The UK Parliament, which enacted the Legal Services Act 2007

Background

The Act implemented many recommendations of Sir David Clementi's 2004 review of the regulatory framework for legal services. It separated the representative and regulatory functions of professional bodies such as the Law Society and the Bar Council.

Key provisions

The Act lists eight regulatory objectives, including protecting the public interest, supporting the rule of law, and improving access to justice. It defines "reserved legal activities" — such as rights of audience and the conduct of litigation — that may only be carried out by authorized persons, created the Office for Legal Complaints and the Legal Ombudsman, and provided for the licensing of alternative business structures from 2011 onward.
