Chief operating officer


Corporate offices

A chief operating officer (COO), also called a chief operations officer, is an executive in charge of the daily operations of an organization (the COO of staff). COOs are usually second-in-command immediately after the CEO. In some countries and organizations, the term president is used to designate the COO equivalent.

The COO role is often a training or proving ground for a future CEO, as it gives exposure to all facets of running a company. Not all companies or organizations have a COO position, as many CEOs prefer to share the duties amongst a wider leadership team or take on the responsibility themselves.

Responsibilities

The COO is responsible for the daily operation of the company and its office building and routinely reports to the highest-ranking executive, usually the chief executive officer (CEO). The COO is usually an executive or senior officer. The role can involve responsibilities similar to a vice president of operations.

Unlike other C-suite positions, which tend to be defined according to commonly designated responsibilities across most companies, a COO's job tends to be defined in relation to the specific CEO with whom they work, given the close working relationship of these two individuals. In many ways, the selection of a COO is similar to the selection of a vice president of the United States: the role becomes far greater than the sum of its parts depending on the resources available to the officeholder.

Relationship with CEO

Because the COO works so closely with the CEO, a transfer of responsibilities can occur if the CEO is absent or otherwise unable to perform their duties. Selecting the right COO is important because the relationship between the CEO and COO must be one of trust and mutual respect. Studies of successful CEO–COO partnerships have identified several archetypes of the COO role, including the executor, the change agent, the mentor, the "other half", the partner, the heir apparent, and the MVP.
