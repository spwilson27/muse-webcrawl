Yacht clubs combine racing organization, marina facilities and social life.

A yacht club is a sports club specifically related to yachting and sailing. Yacht clubs organize racing, cruising and training, maintain marinas and clubhouses, and provide a social centre for their members.

History

The first yacht club is generally considered to be the Neva Yacht Club in St. Petersburg, Russia, founded by Tsar Peter the Great in 1718. The Royal Cork Yacht Club in Ireland (1720) is the oldest in the British Isles, and the Royal Swedish Yacht Club (1830) and the New York Yacht Club (1844) became famous for their roles in the America's Cup, first contested in 1851.

Organization and traditions

Yacht clubs are traditionally led by a commodore, assisted by vice- and rear-commodores — titles borrowed from naval ranks. Each club has a distinctive triangular flag called a burgee, flown by members' boats, and many clubs have reciprocal visiting rights with one another. Clubs administer the racing rules of sailing, measure and rate boats, run regattas and sailing schools, and often organize junior programmes.

Modern yacht clubs

Today yacht clubs range from grand, exclusive institutions to small, volunteer-run community sailing associations open to anyone. Alongside keelboat racing, many support dinghy sailing, windsurfing and paddle sports, and act as custodians of local maritime traditions.
