Fanlight

A fanlight is a window, often semicircular or semi-elliptical in shape, with glazing bars or tracery set in a fan or sunburst pattern, placed above a door or window. The term is most commonly used in British architecture; in the United States the more general term transom window is used for windows above doors, of which the fanlight is the semicircular form. Fanlights allowed light into entrance halls before the days of electric lighting.

History

Fanlights became a characteristic feature of Georgian and Regency architecture in Britain and Ireland, and of Federal and Georgian Revival houses in North America. Set above the front door, often within a classical doorcase with columns or pilasters, the fanlight lit the hallway while preserving privacy. Early examples were simple semicircles of glass; later ones carried elaborate radiating glazing bars, leaded coloured glass and decorative motifs such as swags and husks. House numbers were often painted in gold leaf on the fanlight.

Construction

A fanlight is usually fixed, though some open for ventilation. In modern usage the term is sometimes used loosely for any glazed panel over a door, including rectangular ones. Fanlights remain a common conservation feature in historic terraces and are reproduced in period-style new construction.
