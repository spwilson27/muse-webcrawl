Fake news

Fake news is false or misleading information presented as news. It is a form of misinformation and disinformation created to damage the reputation of a person or entity, influence public opinion or politics, or generate advertising revenue through clickbait. Although false news stories are as old as the printing press, the term "fake news" rose to prominence during the 2016 United States presidential election, when fabricated stories spread widely on social media reportedly out-performed real news in engagement.

History and forms

Precursors include the yellow journalism of the 1890s, wartime propaganda and hoaxes such as the 1835 "Great Moon Hoax". Modern fake news ranges from entirely invented stories produced by content farms — famously including teenagers in Veles, North Macedonia, who profited from election stories in 2016 — to state-sponsored disinformation campaigns and manipulated images and video, including deepfakes. Scholars distinguish misinformation (false but not deliberately deceptive) from disinformation (deliberately false), and also note the term's misuse by politicians to dismiss legitimate journalism they dislike.

Responses

Responses have included fact-checking organisations, media-literacy education, platform labelling and removal policies, and legislation in some countries criminalising false news. Research suggests corrections have modest effects and that false stories often spread faster than true ones on social networks, partly because of novelty and emotional content. The phenomenon remains a central concern in debates about democracy, free expression and the business models of online platforms.
