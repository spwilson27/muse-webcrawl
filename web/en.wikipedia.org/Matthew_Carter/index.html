Matthew Carter

Matthew Carter (born 1 October 1937) is a British type designer. A 2005 New Yorker profile described him as "the most widely read man in the world", in reference to the enormous use of his typefaces, which include the screen fonts Verdana and Georgia, commissioned by Microsoft, and the Bell Centennial typeface designed for American telephone directories.

Career

The son of the typographer and historian Harry Carter, Matthew Carter learned punchcutting in the Netherlands before working at Crosfield Electronics on phototypesetting. He co-founded Bitstream Inc. in 1981, one of the first digital type foundries, and later co-founded Carter & Cone Type with Cherie Cone.

Typefaces

His designs span metal, photographic, and digital technologies: Bell Centennial (1978) for AT&T directories, ITC Galliard, and the Microsoft core fonts Verdana and Georgia (1996), engineered for legibility on low-resolution screens. In 2010 he received a MacArthur "genius" fellowship.
