Aleppo

Country: Syria
Governorate: Aleppo Governorate
Population: approx. 2 million (pre-war metropolitan estimates)
Historic site: Ancient City of Aleppo (UNESCO World Heritage Site, 1986)

Aleppo (Arabic: حلب, Ḥalab) is a city in northern Syria and the capital of the Aleppo Governorate, the most populous governorate of Syria. One of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in the world, it has stood at the crossroads of trade routes linking Mesopotamia, Anatolia, and the Mediterranean for millennia, and was the largest city in Syria before the civil war.

History

Aleppo appears in records as early as the 3rd millennium BCE as the capital of the Amorite kingdom of Yamhad. It passed through Hittite, Assyrian, Persian, Hellenistic, Roman, and Byzantine hands before the Muslim conquest in 637 CE. The city flourished under the Hamdanids, Zengids, and Ayyubids — the great Citadel and much of the old city's fabric date from these eras — survived Mongol invasions, and prospered under the Ottomans as an entrepôt of the Silk Road famous for its covered souks, khans, and soap.

Civil war

From 2012 to 2016 Aleppo was a divided battlefield between government and rebel forces, suffering immense destruction, particularly in the Ancient City, a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1986. Government forces retook the whole city in December 2016. In late 2024, opposition forces captured Aleppo again during the offensive that led to the fall of the Assad regime. Reconstruction of the old city, including the Umayyad Mosque's minaret, has proceeded slowly.

Culture

Aleppo is renowned for its cuisine, its musical traditions of qudud halabiya, its laurel (Aleppo) soap, and landmarks including the Citadel, the Great Mosque, and the Baron Hotel.
