Tabasco

Tabasco is a state in southeastern Mexico, bordering the Gulf of Mexico to the north, the states of Campeche and Chiapas to the east and south, and Veracruz to the west. Its capital and largest city is Villahermosa. Tabasco is one of the smallest Mexican states by area but is rich in water: the Grijalva and Usumacinta rivers cross its low, swampy plains, and it receives some of the heaviest rainfall in the country.

History

Tabasco was a heartland of the Olmec civilisation, whose major centre at La Venta flourished from about 900 to 400 BCE; the famous colossal heads now displayed in Villahermosa's La Venta Park come from this site. Later, Maya peoples inhabited the region, and the Chontal Maya controlled river trade. In 1519 Hernán Cortés fought the Maya at the Battle of Centla, one of the first battles of the Spanish conquest of Mexico. Tabasco became a state in 1824.

Geography and climate

Most of the state lies at low elevation on the Gulf coastal plain, laced with rivers, lagoons and wetlands. The climate is hot and humid, with annual rainfall frequently exceeding 2,000 mm. Seasonal flooding is a recurrent problem, most severely in 2007, when flooding inundated much of Villahermosa and affected around a million people.

Economy

Tabasco is one of Mexico's principal oil-producing states; petroleum extraction in its offshore and onshore fields, managed by the state company Pemex, dominates the economy. Agriculture includes cacao — Tabasco is Mexico's leading cacao producer — bananas, plantains and cattle ranching. The state's name is also lent to the tabasco pepper, the chilli variety used in the famous hot sauce produced in Louisiana, though the sauce itself is not made in the state.
