Hairstyling

Hairstyling is the occupation and craft of cutting, arranging and treating hair to achieve a desired appearance. Practitioners include hairdressers, barbers and cosmetologists, who work in salons, barbershops, spas, and in the fashion, film and theatre industries. Hairstyling services range from basic cuts and blow-drying to chemical treatments such as colouring, perming and straightening, and the fitting of extensions and wigs.

Techniques and tools

Styling tools include shears, clippers, razors, combs, brushes, blow dryers, curling irons and flat irons. Products such as shampoo, conditioner, gel, mousse, pomade and hair spray cleanse, condition and hold the hair. Chemical services alter the hair's structure or pigment: permanent waves use reducing agents to break and reform disulfide bonds, while oxidative hair dyes lift the cuticle and deposit colour.

Training and licensing

In many jurisdictions, hairstylists must complete an accredited cosmetology programme — typically 1,000 to 1,600 hours of training in the United States — and pass a state licensing examination covering cutting, chemical services, sanitation and safety. Barbers are often licensed separately, with a focus on short cuts and shaving.

Health and safety

Occupational hazards include dermatitis from wet work and chemicals, exposure to formaldehyde-releasing straightening products, and repetitive strain. Sanitation rules require disinfection of tools between clients to prevent the spread of infection.
