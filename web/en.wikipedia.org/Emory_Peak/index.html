Emory Peak, located in Big Bend National Park, is the highest peak in the Chisos Mountains and the highest in Brewster County in the U.S. state of Texas. The peak is named for William H. Emory, the chief surveyor of the U.S. Boundary Survey team of 1852.

Geography

Emory Peak rises to 7,825 feet (2,385 m) above sea level. From the Chisos Basin, the peak is visible as a prominent rocky outcrop, and although not visible from all sides, it dominates the surrounding desert landscape. The Chisos Mountains are a volcanic range isolated within the Chihuahuan Desert, and their elevation gives them cooler temperatures and woodland habitat unlike the surrounding lowlands.

Hiking

The most common route to the summit is the Emory Peak Trail, which branches off the Pinnacles Trail from the Chisos Basin. The round trip is about 10.5 miles (17 km) with roughly 2,400 feet of elevation gain; the final section involves a short rock scramble. The summit offers panoramic views across Big Bend and into Mexico.

Ecology

The slopes support pinyon pine, juniper and oak woodland, and the range is notable as the only place in the United States where the Colima warbler breeds. Black bears and mountain lions inhabit the area.
