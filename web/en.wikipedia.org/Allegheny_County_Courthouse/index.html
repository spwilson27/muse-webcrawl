Allegheny County Courthouse

Location436 Grant Street, Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, U.S.
Built1884–1888
ArchitectHenry Hobson Richardson
Architectural styleRomanesque Revival (Richardsonian Romanesque)
DesignationNational Historic Landmark (1976)

The Allegheny County Courthouse in downtown Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, is part of a complex of buildings which includes the Old Allegheny County Jail. The buildings are considered among the finest examples of the Romanesque Revival style designed by architect Henry Hobson Richardson. The complex is bordered by wide streets named for city planners and civic leaders, and is a landmark in the city.

The present courthouse, completed in 1888, replaced an earlier building destroyed by fire in 1882. Richardson's design was selected in a competition in 1883; the architect died in 1886 before the complex was finished, and the work was completed by his successors, Shepley, Rutan and Coolidge. The complex was designated a National Historic Landmark in 1976 and remains a working courthouse for Allegheny County.

Architecture

The courthouse is built of rusticated granite and light brown sandstone, rising around a central courtyard. Its most prominent feature is a main tower 76 metres (249 feet) tall. The design displays the hallmarks of the style Richardson pioneered and which bears his name: rough-hewn stone, massive round arches ("Richardsonian arches"), recessed entrances, cylindrical towers with conical caps, and strong horizontal banding. A bridge, modelled on the Bridge of Sighs in Venice and called the "Bridge of Sighs", connects the courthouse across Ross Street to the jail.

The interior features a central courtyard, large murals, and elaborately carved stone and woodwork. The jail, which closed in 1995, was partly converted to house the family division of the county court, with its courtyard redesigned by the landscape architect Daniel Urban Kiley.

History

Construction began in 1884 and the building was dedicated in 1888 at a cost of roughly 2.25 million dollars. The courthouse complex has been praised by architectural historians as one of the great American public buildings of the 19th century and is often ranked as Richardson's masterpiece. It has served as a filming location, including for the movie Mrs. Soffel (1984).
