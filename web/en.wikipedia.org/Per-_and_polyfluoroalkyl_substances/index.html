Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances

Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS) are a large group of synthetic organofluorine compounds containing carbon–fluorine bonds, in use since the 1940s in products from non-stick cookware and water-repellent fabrics to firefighting foams and food packaging. Because their carbon–fluorine bonds are exceptionally strong, many PFAS are extremely persistent in the environment, earning them the name "forever chemicals".

Health and environment

The best-studied members, PFOA and PFOS, have been linked in epidemiological studies to effects including increased cholesterol, reduced vaccine response, thyroid disease, and certain cancers. PFAS are now detected worldwide in water, soil, wildlife, and human blood. Drinking-water contamination near manufacturing plants and military bases using aqueous film-forming foam has driven major litigation and cleanup programmes.

Regulation

PFOS was listed under the Stockholm Convention in 2009 and PFOA in 2019. The US Environmental Protection Agency set enforceable drinking-water limits for several PFAS in 2024, and the European Union has proposed a broad restriction on the entire class. Industry is shifting toward short-chain and alternative chemistries, whose safety is also debated.
