American open-wheel car racing, generally and commonly known as Indy car racing, is a category of professional automobile racing in the United States. As of 2025, the top-level American open-wheel racing championship is sanctioned by IndyCar and titled the IndyCar Series. The cars are single-seaters with exposed wheels racing on a mix of ovals, road courses, and street circuits, and are among the fastest circuit-racing cars in the world, exceeding 230 mph (370 km/h) at the Indianapolis Motor Speedway.

History

Championship racing began under the American Automobile Association (AAA) in 1905. The first Indianapolis 500 was held in 1911 and became the sport's crown jewel. After the 1955 Le Mans disaster, the AAA withdrew from racing and the United States Auto Club (USAC) took over sanctioning in 1956.

In 1979 team owners formed Championship Auto Racing Teams (CART), sparking the first "split" with USAC. CART's PPG Indy Car World Series flourished in the 1980s and early 1990s with stars such as Mario Andretti, Rick Mears, Emerson Fittipaldi, and Nigel Mansell. In 1996 Indianapolis Motor Speedway president Tony George founded the rival Indy Racing League (IRL), built around the Indianapolis 500 and oval racing, dividing fans, sponsors, and teams for twelve years. CART went bankrupt in 2003; its successor, Champ Car, merged into the IRL in 2008, reunifying the sport under what became the IndyCar Series.

Cars and races

Modern Indy cars use a spec Dallara chassis with 2.2-litre twin-turbocharged V6 engines from Honda and Chevrolet producing roughly 650–700 horsepower. The schedule centres on the Indianapolis 500, held each Memorial Day weekend since 1911, and also includes the street races at Long Beach and St. Petersburg and road courses such as Road America and Mid-Ohio.

Notable drivers

A. J. Foyt, Rick Mears, and Al Unser Sr. each won the Indianapolis 500 four times. Mario Andretti is the only driver to win the Indianapolis 500, the Daytona 500, and the Formula One World Championship. Dario Franchitti, Scott Dixon, and Álex Palou are among the most successful drivers of the modern era.
