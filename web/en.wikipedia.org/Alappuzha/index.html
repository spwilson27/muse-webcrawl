Alappuzha (Malayalam: ആലപ്പുഴ), also known by its former name Alleppey, is a city in the Indian state of Kerala and the administrative headquarters of Alappuzha district. Situated on the Laccadive Sea coast between Kochi and Kollam, it is famed as the "Venice of the East" for its network of canals, lagoons and backwaters.

Geography and backwaters

Alappuzha lies at the southern end of Vembanad Lake, the longest lake in India. The region's backwaters — a maze of canals, rivers and lakes — are navigated by traditional kettuvallam houseboats, now a major tourist attraction. The nearby Kuttanad region, one of the few places in the world where farming is done below sea level, is known as the "rice bowl of Kerala".

Economy

The city is the centre of Kerala's coir industry, processing coconut fibre into mats, matting and geotextiles exported worldwide. Tourism, fishing, seafood processing and handloom weaving are also important. Alappuzha port, once a busy trading harbour for spices and timber, declined after the rise of Kochi port.

Culture and events

The Nehru Trophy Boat Race, held annually on Punnamada Lake on the second Saturday of August, features spectacular snake boats (chundan vallam) crewed by over a hundred oarsmen. The Alappuzha beach and its 19th-century pier and lighthouse are local landmarks. The city hosts people of Hindu, Christian and Muslim faiths, with notable temples, churches (including St. Mary's Forane Church, Edathua) and mosques.
