Demodulation

Demodulation is the process of extracting the original information-bearing signal from a modulated carrier wave. A demodulator is an electronic circuit (or computer program in a software-defined radio) that performs this function, reversing the modulation applied at the transmitter.

Methods

For amplitude modulation, demodulation can be performed with a simple envelope detector — a diode and a low-pass filter — which follows the amplitude of the carrier. Frequency modulation is demodulated with circuits such as the Foster–Seeley discriminator, ratio detector or phase-locked loop, which convert frequency variations into amplitude variations. Digital modulation schemes require demodulators that recover symbols, often using coherent detection with a recovered carrier reference, as in quadrature amplitude modulation used in modems and digital television.

Applications

Demodulation is used in every radio, television, telephone and data receiver: broadcast receivers, mobile phones, Wi-Fi equipment, satellite terminals and cable modems all demodulate incoming signals to recover audio, video or data.
