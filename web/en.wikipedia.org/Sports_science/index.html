Sports science

Sports science (also called sport science, sports and exercise science, or exercise science) is an interdisciplinary field that studies how the healthy human body works during exercise, and how sport and physical activity promote health and performance from cellular to whole-body perspectives. It combines physiology, biomechanics, psychology, anatomy, biochemistry, nutrition and motor learning, and applies scientific methods to coaching, rehabilitation and physical education.

Scope

Major sub-disciplines include exercise physiology (energy systems, cardiovascular and muscular adaptations), biomechanics (measurement and modelling of movement), sports psychology (motivation, anxiety, mental training), sports nutrition, strength and conditioning, performance analysis (including video and GPS tracking), and sports medicine and injury prevention. Practitioners work with elite athletes, teams, schools, hospitals and the military; typical assessments include VO2 max and lactate threshold testing, force-plate analysis and body-composition measurement.

History

Formal research began in the early 20th century with laboratories such as the Harvard Fatigue Laboratory (1927–1947) and Scandinavian exercise physiology schools; professional bodies such as the American College of Sports Medicine (founded 1954) and university degree programmes expanded the field rapidly from the 1960s. Modern sports science is a major academic discipline and an integral part of professional sport, although it also attracts criticism for hype, conflicts of interest in supplement research, and questions about how directly laboratory findings transfer to competition.
