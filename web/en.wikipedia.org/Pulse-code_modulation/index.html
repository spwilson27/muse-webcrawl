Pulse-code modulation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pulse-code modulation (PCM) is a method used to digitally represent sampled analogue signals. It is the standard form of digital audio in computers, compact discs, digital telephony, and other digital audio applications. In a PCM stream, the amplitude of the analogue signal is sampled at uniform intervals, and each sample is quantized to the nearest value within a range of digital steps.

Linear PCM (LPCM) uses uniform quantization and is used in the audio CD standard (44.1 kHz sampling rate, 16 bits per sample), in WAV and AIFF files, and in AES3 digital audio interfaces. Non-uniform quantization laws, such as the µ-law and A-law algorithms, are used in digital telephony (G.711) to give greater dynamic range for speech at 8 bits per sample and 8 kHz sampling.

History

PCM was invented by Alec Reeves at the Paris laboratories of International Telephone and Telegraph in 1937; his patent described encoding speech as binary pulses. The first practical application was the SIGSALY secure voice system used by the Allies in World War II. Commercial deployment began in the 1960s with the T-carrier system (T1, 1962) in the telephone network, and PCM became the basis of the compact disc format launched in 1982.

Process

Producing a PCM stream involves three operations: sampling (measuring the signal at discrete times, at a rate at least twice the highest frequency, per the Nyquist–Shannon sampling theorem), quantization (rounding each sample to the nearest of a finite set of levels, which introduces quantization error), and encoding (representing each level as a binary code word). At the receiver, a digital-to-analogue converter reconstructs the signal, followed by low-pass filtering.

Variants

Related techniques include differential PCM (DPCM) and adaptive DPCM (ADPCM), which encode differences between successive samples; delta-sigma modulation, used in Super Audio CD's Direct Stream Digital format; and lossy perceptual audio coding (such as MP3 and AAC), which build on PCM representations while discarding inaudible information.
