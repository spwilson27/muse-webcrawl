Latvian Song and Dance Festival

The Latvian Song and Dance Festival (Latvian: Vispārējie latviešu Dziesmu un Deju svētki) is one of the largest amateur choral and folk dance events in the world, held roughly every five years in Riga, Latvia. First held in 1873, it is a cornerstone of Latvian national identity and was inscribed by UNESCO, together with its Estonian and Lithuanian counterparts, on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

History

The first festival in 1873 gathered just over 1,000 singers in Riga during the Latvian national awakening. The tradition survived tsarist rule, independence, Soviet occupation — when massed singing became a form of quiet resistance culminating in the Singing Revolution of the late 1980s — and restored independence. The centenary-era festivals have drawn tens of thousands of participants, with massed choirs of more than 10,000 singers performing on the open-air stage at Mežaparks.

The festival

Events span about a week: opening concerts, folk dance performances at Daugava Stadium, a participants' parade through Riga, sacred music concerts, and the grand closing concert at Mežaparks. Participants wear regional folk costumes, many hand-made and passed down through families.
