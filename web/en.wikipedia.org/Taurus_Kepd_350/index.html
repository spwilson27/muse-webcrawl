Taurus Kepd 350

The Taurus Kepd 350, commonly written Taurus KEPD 350, is an air-launched cruise missile and stand-off weapon manufactured by Taurus Systems, a joint venture between MBDA Germany and Saab Dynamics of Sweden. The name Taurus derives from the target system (Target Adaptive Unitary and Dispenser Robotic Ubiquity System), while KEPD stands for Kinetic Energy Penetrator and Destroyer. It entered service with the German Luftwaffe in 2005.

Design

The missile is a stealthy, long-range precision weapon powered by a Williams P8300-15 turbofan engine. It weighs about 1,400 kg, is roughly 5 metres long, and has a range exceeding 500 km. It is guided by a combination of inertial navigation, GPS, terrain-referenced navigation using radar altimetry and stored terrain data, and image-based navigation with an infrared seeker for terminal homing, allowing it to fly autonomously at low altitude without GPS.

Warhead

The 481 kg MEPHISTO warhead is a two-stage tandem penetrator: a shaped charge first breaches hardened or buried structures, followed by a main penetrator charge with a programmable fuze that counts voids and layers within a target to detonate at the optimal depth. The system can also be used for airburst against area targets.

Operators

The KEPD 350 is carried by the Tornado and Eurofighter Typhoon in German service. Spain operates the missile on its EF-18 Hornets, and South Korea acquired it for its F-15K fleet as the KEPD 350K, with a locally developed variant (350K-2) also planned. The missile has been transferred to Ukraine by Germany and Sweden, and has been used in combat during the Russo-Ukrainian War.
