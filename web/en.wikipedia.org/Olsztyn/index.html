Olsztyn (German: Allenstein) is a city in northeastern Poland, on the Łyna River, and the capital of the Warmian-Masurian Voivodeship. It is the largest city of the historic region of Warmia and a gateway to the Masurian Lake District.

History

The Teutonic Order built a castle here in the 14th century, and the settlement that grew around it received town rights in 1353. Olsztyn's castle is famously associated with Nicolaus Copernicus, who as administrator of the Warmian chapter in 1516–1519 defended the castle against the Teutonic Knights and conducted astronomical observations there. The city passed between Polish and Teutonic/Prussian rule over the centuries, became part of East Prussia, and was heavily damaged in 1945 before being transferred to Poland after the Second World War.

Geography and sights

Olsztyn sits amid forests and more than a dozen lakes within its municipal area, the largest being Lake Ukiel. Landmarks include the Gothic Olsztyn Castle (now the Museum of Warmia and Mazury), the Old Town with its market square and High Gate, and the cathedral of St. James. The University of Warmia and Mazury, founded in 1999, is a major employer and cultural force.
