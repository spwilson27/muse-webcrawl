The Sasanian Empire (Middle Persian: Ērānshahr, "Empire of the Iranians") was the last Persian imperial dynasty before the Muslim conquest, ruling from 224 to 651 AD. Founded by Ardashir I, who overthrew the Parthian Arsacid dynasty, it was the great rival of Rome and Byzantium for over four centuries, and at its height under Khosrow II stretched from Mesopotamia and the Caucasus to the Indus and southern Arabia.

History

Ardashir I defeated the last Parthian king, Artabanus IV, at Hormozdgan in 224. His son Shapur I won famous victories over Rome, capturing the emperor Valerian in 260. After periods of internal strife, the empire was revitalised under Khosrow I (r. 531–579), whose fiscal and military reforms, palace building at Ctesiphon, and patronage of learning — including the Academy of Gondishapur — defined its classical age. Khosrow II (r. 590–628) conquered Syria, Egypt and Anatolia from Byzantium, nearly restoring Achaemenid frontiers, before Heraclius's counter-offensive and a palace coup destroyed him; the ensuing civil wars fatally weakened the state before the Arab Muslim armies invaded.

Fall

Defeats at al-Qadisiyyah (636) and Nahavand (642) opened Persia to conquest; the last shah, Yazdegerd III, was killed at Merv in 651. Sasanian administration, court culture, coinage and art profoundly influenced the early Islamic world and later Persian identity.

Culture

The empire is famed for rock reliefs at Naqsh-e Rostam and Bishapur, silverware, silk weaving, the great brick vault of Taq Kasra, and the codification of Zoroastrian scripture and Middle Persian literature.
