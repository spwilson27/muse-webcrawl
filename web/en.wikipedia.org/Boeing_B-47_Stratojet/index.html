The Boeing B-47 Stratojet (Boeing company designation Model 450) is a retired American long-range, six-engined, turbojet-powered strategic bomber designed to fly at high subsonic speeds and at high altitude to avoid enemy interceptor aircraft. It was the United States Air Force's primary medium bomber and nuclear deterrent aircraft during the 1950s, serving with Strategic Air Command (SAC).

Design and development

The B-47 grew out of a 1943–1944 requirement for a jet-powered reconnaissance/bomber aircraft. Captured German research on swept wings, examined by Boeing aerodynamicist George Schairer in 1945, led Boeing to redesign the aircraft with a 35-degree swept wing — a feature that, together with podded engines mounted on pylons beneath the wing, became a template for later jet airliners including the Boeing 707. The thin, flexible wing required bicycle landing gear in the fuselage with outrigger wheels in the inner engine pods. Because the early turbojets produced limited thrust, the B-47 used rocket-assisted take-off (RATO) bottles for heavily loaded departures, and a drag parachute to shorten landing rolls.

Operational history

The B-47 entered service in 1951 and formed the backbone of SAC's medium bomber force through the 1950s; at its peak in 1956, SAC operated over 1,300 B-47 bombers and several hundred RB-47 reconnaissance variants. RB-47s flew peripheral and, on a few occasions, overflight reconnaissance missions along and over the borders of the Soviet Union; several were attacked and at least one was shot down (an RB-47H over the Barents Sea in 1960). The type's high-altitude subsonic performance was rapidly overtaken by supersonic fighters and surface-to-air missiles, and the bomber fleet was retired by 1966 as the B-52 and B-58 took over the mission. Electronic warfare and weather reconnaissance variants served into the 1970s.

Legacy

Although it never dropped a bomb in combat, the B-47 was a landmark design: its swept wing and podded-engine layout defined the configuration of nearly every large jet aircraft that followed, military and civil.
