Missa solemnis (Beethoven)

ComposerLudwig van Beethoven
CatalogueOp. 123
Composed1819–1823
Premiere7 April 1824, St. Petersburg
ScoringSoloists, choir, orchestra, organ

The Missa solemnis in D major, Op. 123, is a setting of the Latin Mass by Ludwig van Beethoven, composed between 1819 and 1823. Beethoven considered it one of his greatest works—he inscribed the autograph of the Kyrie "Von Herzen—möge es wieder—zu Herzen gehn!" ("From the heart—may it return—to the heart!"). It is standard repertoire for large choral societies but rarely used liturgically because of its scale and difficulty.

Background

Beethoven began the Mass for the enthronement of his patron and pupil Archduke Rudolf as Archbishop of Olomouc, planned for March 1820, but the work grew far beyond its occasion and was not finished until 1823. Beethoven sold manuscript copies to European courts by subscription before publication. The first complete performance was given in St. Petersburg on 7 April 1824 under the patronage of Prince Nikolai Galitzin; a partial performance in Vienna followed on 7 May 1824, when three movements were heard on the same concert as the premiere of the Ninth Symphony.

Structure

The work sets the five movements of the Ordinary: Kyrie, Gloria, Credo, Sanctus (with the Benedictus), and Agnus Dei. Highlights include the archaic, devout Kyrie; the trumpet-and-drum "war music" of the Agnus Dei's plea for peace, interrupted by anxious recitatives; and the extended violin solo of the Benedictus. A performance lasts roughly 80 minutes. Together with Bach's Mass in B minor, it is regarded as one of the supreme achievements of the mass repertoire.
