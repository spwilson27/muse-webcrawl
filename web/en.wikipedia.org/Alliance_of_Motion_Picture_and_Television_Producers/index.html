Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers

The Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers (AMPTP) is a trade association based in Sherman Oaks, Los Angeles, California, that represents over 350 American television and film production companies in collective bargaining negotiations with entertainment industry trade unions that include, among others, the Screen Actors Guild–American Federation of Television and Radio Artists (SAG-AFTRA), the Directors Guild of America (DGA), the Writers Guild of America, West (WGA West) and the Writers Guild of America, East (WGA East), the American Federation of Musicians (AFM), and the International Alliance of Theatrical Stage Employees (IATSE).

History

The AMPTP was founded in 1924 as the Association of Motion Picture Producers. It became the Association of Motion Picture and Television Producers before taking its current name in 1982, when it merged with the Alliance of Motion Picture and Television Producers' predecessor organizations representing independent producers.

Role in labor disputes

The AMPTP negotiates the industry's basic agreements with the guilds, which cover wages, working conditions, residuals, and health and pension benefits. It was the studios' bargaining counterpart during the 2007–08 Writers Guild of America strike and the dual 2023 Writers Guild strike and SAG-AFTRA strike, which together shut down much of American film and television production for several months and centered on streaming residuals and the use of artificial intelligence.
