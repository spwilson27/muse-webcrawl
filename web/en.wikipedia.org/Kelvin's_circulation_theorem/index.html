Kelvin's circulation theorem

Kelvin's circulation theorem, published by Lord Kelvin (William Thomson) in 1869, states that in a barotropic ideal fluid with conservative body forces, the circulation around a closed curve moving with the fluid remains constant with time.

Mathematically, dΓ/dt = 0, where Γ is the circulation, the line integral of velocity around the material contour. The theorem underlies results on vortex motion and potential flow: vorticity can neither be created nor destroyed in such a flow, and irrotational flow remains irrotational. It fails when viscosity, non-conservative forces or baroclinic effects (as with temperature gradients in the atmosphere) are present.
