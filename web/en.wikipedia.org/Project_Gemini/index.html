Project Gemini

Project Gemini was NASA's second human spaceflight program, conducted between 1961 and 1966. It bridged the Mercury program and the Apollo Moon program. The Gemini spacecraft carried two astronauts and was designed to develop the techniques — long-duration flight, orbital rendezvous and docking, and extravehicular activity (spacewalks) — required for Apollo.

Objectives

Gemini aimed to test astronauts' endurance on flights of up to 14 days, to perfect rendezvous and docking with target vehicles (the Agena), to develop controlled re-entry and precision landing techniques, and to evaluate the effects of prolonged weightlessness. Gemini 4 (1965) featured the first American spacewalk, by Ed White. Gemini 6A and 7 achieved the first rendezvous of two crewed spacecraft in December 1965; Gemini 8 performed the first docking but was aborted after a dangerous stuck-thruster spin.

Flights

Ten crewed missions flew in 20 months, an intense cadence. Gemini 7 set an endurance record of nearly 14 days. The final missions in 1966, with Buzz Aldrin on Gemini 12, resolved the difficulties of working outside the spacecraft using handholds and restraints, demonstrating that useful work during EVA was possible.

Legacy

Gemini proved every major technique Apollo depended upon except landing on the Moon itself, and trained a generation of Apollo commanders, including Neil Armstrong, Jim Lovell, John Young, and Buzz Aldrin. It is often regarded as the program that put the United States ahead of the Soviet Union in the Space Race.
