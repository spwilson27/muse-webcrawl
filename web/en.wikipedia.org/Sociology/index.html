Sociology

DisciplineSocial science
Term coinedAuguste Comte, 1830s
Founding figuresÉmile Durkheim, Karl Marx, Max Weber, Georg Simmel
Major paradigmsStructural functionalism, conflict theory, symbolic interactionism

Sociology is the scientific study of human society, social behaviour and the institutions, groups and interactions that shape them. Its subject matter ranges from the micro-level analysis of face-to-face interaction to macro-level processes such as globalisation, inequality and social change. Sociologists research topics including family, education, religion, crime, health, work and culture.

Origins

Auguste Comte coined the term "sociology" and envisioned a positivist science of society. In the late 19th century Émile Durkheim established sociology as an academic discipline, founding the first European sociology department at Bordeaux and applying statistical method in Suicide (1897). Karl Marx's analysis of capitalism and class conflict, Max Weber's interpretive sociology and theory of bureaucracy, and Georg Simmel's formal sociology complete the classical canon.

Approaches and methods

Major theoretical traditions include structural functionalism (Talcott Parsons), conflict theory, symbolic interactionism (George Herbert Mead, Herbert Blumer), feminism and post-structuralism. Methods include surveys, interviews, participant observation and ethnography, historical comparison, and quantitative analysis of statistical data.

Branches

Subfields include the sociology of religion, education, law, health and medicine, science and technology, the family, deviance, organisations and urban and rural sociology.
