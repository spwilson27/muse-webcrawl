Ç

Ç (lowercase ç), called C-cedilla, is a Latin-script letter used in several languages including French, Portuguese, Catalan, Turkish, Azerbaijani, Albanian and Friulian. It originated in Old Spanish as a form of the letter z with a cedilla diacritic.

Usage

In French, Portuguese and Catalan, ç represents the voiceless alveolar fricative /s/ before the back vowels a, o and u, where a plain c would be read as /k/. In Turkish and Azerbaijani it represents the voiceless postalveolar affricate /tʃ/, the sound of English "ch".

History

The cedilla began as a small z written beneath the c in medieval Spanish manuscripts. The mark spread into French and Portuguese orthography and survives today as an integral part of the letter in many alphabets.
