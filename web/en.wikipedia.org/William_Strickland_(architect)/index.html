William Strickland (architect)

William Strickland (November 1788 – April 6, 1854) was a noted American architect and civil engineer in Philadelphia and Nashville. He is best known for the Greek Revival buildings of early nineteenth-century America, including several United States Mint and bank buildings.

Training and Philadelphia works

Born in Navesink, New Jersey, Strickland was apprenticed to Benjamin Henry Latrobe, the leading architect of the federal era. His independent masterpiece in Philadelphia is the Second Bank of the United States (1819–24), a marble temple modelled on the Parthenon and a landmark of the Greek Revival. He also designed the Merchants' Exchange, the Philadelphia Naval Asylum and the United States Mint building.

Tennessee State Capitol

In 1845 Strickland moved to Nashville to design the Tennessee State Capitol, considered one of the finest examples of Greek Revival civic architecture in the country. He died in 1854 while it was still under construction and, by his own wish, was entombed in a vault in its northeast wall; his son Francis completed the work.

Engineering work

Strickland also worked as a civil engineer, reporting for Pennsylvania on railroads and canals after a study tour of Britain in 1825, and is credited with helping introduce railroad technology to the United States. He mentored architect Thomas Ustick Walter.
