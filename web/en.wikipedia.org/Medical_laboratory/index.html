Medical laboratory

A medical laboratory (or clinical laboratory) is a laboratory where tests are performed on clinical specimens — blood, urine, tissue, and other body fluids — to obtain information about a patient's health for diagnosis, treatment, and prevention of disease. Laboratory medicine underpins a large share of clinical decisions; it is commonly estimated that around 70% of medical decisions depend on laboratory results.

Departments

Laboratories are typically organised into sections: clinical chemistry (assays of analytes in serum and plasma), haematology (blood counts and coagulation), microbiology (culture and identification of bacteria, viruses, and fungi, and antimicrobial susceptibility), immunology/serology, transfusion medicine (blood banking and cross-matching), histopathology and cytology (examination of tissues and cells), and molecular diagnostics (PCR and genetic testing).

Staff and workflow

Laboratories are directed by pathologists or clinical scientists and staffed by medical laboratory scientists/technologists and technicians. Work divides into pre-analytical (ordering, collection, transport, accessioning), analytical (testing, often on automated analysers), and post-analytical (validation and reporting) phases; most errors arise in the pre-analytical phase.

Regulation and accreditation

Laboratories are regulated under regimes such as CLIA in the United States and accredited to standards such as ISO 15189, with external quality assessment schemes verifying accuracy. Standalone reference laboratories complement hospital-based services.
