Józef Piłsudski

Józef Klemens Piłsudski (5 December 1867 – 12 May 1935) was a Polish statesman who served as the Chief of State (1918–1922) and First Marshal of Poland (from 1920), and was the de facto leader of the Second Polish Republic as Minister of Military Affairs from 1926 until his death. He is regarded as the father of the Second Polish Republic, re-established in 1918.

Early life

Born in Zułów, in the Vilnius Governorate of the Russian Empire, into a Polish-Lithuanian noble family, Piłsudski was exiled to Siberia for alleged involvement in a plot against Tsar Alexander III. On his return he became a leader of the Polish Socialist Party (PPS) and editor of the underground paper Robotnik, later building paramilitary Polish Legions that fought alongside the Central Powers in the First World War.

Independence and war

In November 1918 Piłsudski proclaimed Polish independence and became Chief of State. He led Poland in the Polish–Soviet War, winning the decisive Battle of Warsaw in 1920 — the "Miracle on the Vistula" — which halted the Red Army's westward advance. He promoted the Promethean project and the Intermarium federation concept for Central and Eastern Europe.

May Coup and final years

After retiring in 1923, Piłsudski returned to power in the May Coup of 1926 and dominated Polish politics under the Sanation movement, while formally serving mainly as Minister of Military Affairs. He died in Warsaw in 1935 and was buried in Wawel Cathedral in Kraków.
