Alan McGee

Born: 29 September 1960, East Kilbride, Scotland
Occupation: Record label owner, music manager, musician
Known for: Co-founding Creation Records

Alan McGee (born 29 September 1960) is a Scottish businessman and music industry executive. He is best known as the co-founder of the independent record label Creation Records, which he ran from 1983 to 1999, and for discovering and signing Oasis. Creation's roster included Primal Scream, My Bloody Valentine, The Jesus and Mary Chain, Teenage Fanclub, and Ride, making it one of the defining labels of British indie music and Britpop.

Early life

McGee was born in East Kilbride and grew up in Glasgow. He played in the band The Laughing Apple and later Biff Bang Pow!, named after a song by The Creation. Moving to London in the early 1980s, he promoted club nights and managed The Jesus and Mary Chain before forming Creation Records with Dick Green and Joe Foster, taking the label's name from the 1960s band The Creation.

Creation Records

Founded in 1983, Creation began with releases by McGee's own circle of bands and grew through the decade with acts such as The Weather Prophets, Primal Scream, and Felt. The label's 1991 release of My Bloody Valentine's Loveless nearly bankrupted it. In 1992 McGee sold a 49% stake to Sony. In 1993 he saw Oasis play at King Tut's Wah Wah Hut in Glasgow and signed them; the band's success made Creation one of the most successful independent labels of the decade. McGee closed the label in 1999.

Later career

After Creation, McGee founded the Poptones label, managed acts including The Libertines, and worked as a DJ and music blogger. He later launched Creation Management and, in 2018, a new label venture, It's Just Creation. His life and the label's story were dramatized in the 2021 film Creation Stories, written by Irvine Welsh and based on McGee's autobiography.
