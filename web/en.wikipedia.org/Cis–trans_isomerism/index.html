Cis–trans isomerism

Cis and trans isomers of but-2-ene.

Cis–trans isomerism, also known as geometric isomerism or configurational isomerism, is a form of stereoisomerism describing the relative orientation of functional groups within a molecule. The terms come from Latin: cis means "on this side of" and trans means "across" or "on the other side of". The phenomenon typically arises in molecules containing double bonds, which cannot rotate freely, or in ring structures, where rotation is constrained.

Organic chemistry

In an alkene of the form R–CH=CH–R, if the two R groups lie on the same side of the double bond the isomer is cis; if they lie on opposite sides it is trans. A classic example is but-2-ene: cis-but-2-ene boils at 3.7 °C, while trans-but-2-ene boils at 0.9 °C, showing that geometric isomers have distinct physical properties. In cyclic compounds, substituents can likewise be cis or trans relative to the plane of the ring.

When the two substituents on each carbon of a double bond are different, the cis/trans terminology becomes ambiguous, and the more general E–Z notation is used instead. Under that system, priorities are assigned by the Cahn–Ingold–Prelog rules: if the two higher-priority groups are on the same side the configuration is Z (zusammen, "together"); if opposite, E (entgegen, "opposite").

Inorganic chemistry

Geometric isomerism also occurs in coordination complexes. In square planar complexes such as cisplatin, Pt(NH3)2Cl2, the cis isomer has the two chloride ligands adjacent and is a widely used anticancer drug, whereas the trans isomer (transplatin) is therapeutically inactive. Octahedral complexes can show cis/trans and also fac/mer isomerism.

Biological significance

Geometric isomerism matters in biology: the visual pigment retinal changes from the 11-cis to the all-trans form when it absorbs light, triggering vision. Naturally occurring unsaturated fatty acids are mostly cis, whereas industrial partial hydrogenation produces trans fatty acids, which are associated with adverse cardiovascular effects.
