Raynald of Châtillon

Raynald of Châtillon (c. 1124 – 4 July 1187), also known as Reynald or Reginald, was a knight who came to the Holy Land on the Second Crusade and rose to become Prince of Antioch and later Lord of Oultrejordain. He is one of the most controversial figures of the Crusader states, remembered for his aggression against Muslim neighbours and caravan traffic, which helped provoke Saladin's campaign that destroyed the Crusader army at Hattin in 1187.

Prince of Antioch

A younger son from Châtillon-sur-Loire in France, Raynald arrived in the Levant in 1147 and entered the service of Constance, Princess of Antioch, whom he married in 1153. As prince he raided Byzantine Cilicia and, in a famous act of humiliation, tortured the Latin Patriarch of Antioch, Aimery of Limoges. In 1156 he joined Thoros II of Armenia in a brutal raid on Byzantine Cyprus. Captured by the Muslims while raiding near Aleppo in 1160 or 1161, he spent some fifteen years in captivity until ransomed in 1176.

Lord of Oultrejordain

Raynald then married Stephanie of Milly, heiress to the lordship of Oultrejordain, which controlled the castles of Kerak and Montreal and the roads between Egypt and Syria. From Kerak he repeatedly attacked Muslim caravans despite truces, and in 1182–1183 he launched a remarkable naval raid down the Red Sea, threatening the holy cities of Mecca and Medina and provoking Saladin's lasting enmity. In late 1186 or early 1187 he seized a rich caravan travelling from Egypt to Damascus, refusing King Guy's order to make restitution.

Death

Saladin invaded the kingdom in 1187. Raynald was captured at the Battle of Hattin on 4 July 1187 and, after refusing to convert to Islam, was executed by Saladin personally, in fulfilment of a vow. His death and the destruction of the Crusader field army led directly to the fall of Jerusalem that October.
