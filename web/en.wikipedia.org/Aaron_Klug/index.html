Aaron Klug

Sir Aaron Klug
Born: 11 August 1926, Želva, Lithuania
Died: 20 November 2018, Cambridge, England
Known for: Crystallographic electron microscopy; zinc fingers
Awards: Nobel Prize in Chemistry (1982)

Sir Aaron Klug (11 August 1926 – 20 November 2018) was a Lithuanian-born British biophysicist and chemist. He was a winner of the 1982 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for his development of crystallographic electron microscopy and his structural elucidation of biologically important nucleic acid–protein complexes.

Career

Educated in South Africa and at Cambridge, Klug worked at Birkbeck College with Rosalind Franklin on the structure of tobacco mosaic virus before moving to the Medical Research Council Laboratory of Molecular Biology in Cambridge, which he later directed (1986–1996). His methods combined X-ray diffraction and electron microscopy to reconstruct three-dimensional images of viruses and chromatin.

Zinc fingers

In the 1980s Klug's group discovered zinc finger domains, a widespread DNA-binding motif in transcription factors, work that later informed the development of engineered zinc-finger nucleases. He served as president of the Royal Society from 1995 to 2000.
