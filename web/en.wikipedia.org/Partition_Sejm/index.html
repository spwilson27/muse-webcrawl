The Partition Sejm (Polish: Sejm Rozbiorowy) was a sejm held in Warsaw from 1773 to 1775, which, under duress, ratified the First Partition of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth by Russia, Prussia and Austria. It is remembered as a symbol of the Commonwealth's subjugation to foreign powers — and, paradoxically, as the body that enacted some significant reforms, including the creation of the Commission of National Education.

Background

In 1772 Russia, Prussia and Austria agreed to annex large border territories of the Commonwealth, occupying them by force. To give the seizures legal form, the three powers demanded that a sejm formally cede the lands. Delegates were intimidated and bribed; Russian ambassador Otto Magnus von Stackelberg orchestrated proceedings, and a confederated sejm — voting by majority rather than unanimity, thus neutralising the liberum veto — was imposed under the marshal Adam Poniński, widely regarded as a Russian client.

The Rejtan protest

At the opening, the deputy Tadeusz Rejtan of Nowogródek famously protested against the confederation and the partition, at one point barring the chamber door and baring his chest, begging the deputies not to proceed — an episode immortalised in Jan Matejko's painting Rejtan (1866). Despite such protests, the sejm appointed a delegation that on 18 September 1773 signed treaties ceding roughly 30% of the Commonwealth's territory and about a third of its population.

Other legislation

The sejm also restructured the state: it established a Permanent Council (1775) as a governing executive, reorganised the army and finances, and — following the suppression of the Jesuit order — created the Commission of National Education on 14 October 1773, often described as Europe's first ministry of education, which took over the Jesuit school network and reformed Polish education.
