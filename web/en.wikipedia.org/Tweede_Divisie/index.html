Tweede Divisie

The Tweede Divisie is the third tier of Dutch football

The Tweede Divisie (Dutch pronunciation: [ˈtʋeːdə diˈvizi]; English: Second Division) is the third tier of professional football in the Netherlands. It sits below the Eredivisie and the Eerste Divisie in the Dutch football league system and is administered by the KNVB.

History

The Tweede Divisie was founded in 1956 as a professional league and existed until 1971, when it was disbanded as part of a restructuring that left only two professional divisions. It was re-established for the 2016–17 season as part of a major overhaul of the Dutch football pyramid intended to restore promotion and relegation between professional and amateur football. The new league is semi-professional, containing clubs relegated from the Eerste Divisie, top amateur clubs from the former Topklasse, and reserve ("Jong") teams of professional clubs.

Format

The league has generally consisted of 18 teams playing a double round-robin. Under the arrangements in force since its reintroduction, a limited and conditional promotion route to the Eerste Divisie exists for eligible non-reserve teams, while the lowest finishers can be relegated to the Derde Divisie, subject to licensing and the presence of reserve teams in the league.
