Oppositional defiant disorder

Oppositional defiant disorder (ODD) is a mental disorder in children and adolescents characterised by a persistent pattern of angry or irritable mood, argumentative and defiant behaviour, or vindictiveness lasting at least six months. It is listed in the DSM-5 among the disruptive, impulse-control and conduct disorders.

SpecialtyChild psychiatry, psychology
SymptomsDefiance, argumentativeness, irritability, vindictiveness
Usual onsetEarly childhood
Risk factorsFamily conflict, inconsistent discipline, temperament

Signs and symptoms

Children with ODD often lose their temper, argue with authority figures, actively refuse requests or rules, deliberately annoy others, blame others for their mistakes, and are easily annoyed, angry or spiteful. To meet diagnostic criteria the behaviours must occur with more than one person and cause significant impairment. Unlike conduct disorder, ODD does not involve serious violations of the rights of others such as aggression or theft.

Causes

No single cause is known. Genetic and temperamental factors, including difficulty regulating emotions, interact with environmental influences such as harsh or inconsistent parenting, family instability and peer rejection.

Treatment

First-line treatment is psychosocial: parent management training, problem-solving skills training and family or school-based interventions. Medication is not used for ODD itself but may treat co-occurring conditions such as ADHD, which is frequently comorbid.
