Brachycephaly

Brachycephaly (from Greek brachys, short, and kephalē, head) is a skull shape characterised by a short, broad head, with a cephalic index of 80 or above. It may occur as a normal variant, as a result of positional moulding in infants, or from premature fusion of the coronal sutures (craniosynostosis).

Positional brachycephaly became more common after public-health campaigns advised placing infants on their backs to sleep, reducing sudden infant death syndrome. It is usually cosmetic and often resolves with growth, repositioning, or helmet therapy in more pronounced cases. Syndromic forms are associated with conditions such as Apert syndrome and Down syndrome.

The term is also used in zoology for short-skulled, flat-faced dog breeds such as pugs and bulldogs, whose conformation predisposes them to brachycephalic obstructive airway syndrome.
