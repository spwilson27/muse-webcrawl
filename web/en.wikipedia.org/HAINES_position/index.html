HAINES position

The HAINES position, a modified lateral recovery position.

The HAINES position (High Arm In Endangered Spine) is a modified recovery position used in first aid for an unconscious, breathing casualty in whom a spinal injury is suspected. It was described in 1991 by Australian researcher Frank Haines.

Technique

The casualty is rolled onto their side with both arms positioned to support the head and neck in a neutral alignment: the arm nearest the ground is extended above the head, and the other arm is placed so the hand supports the head, keeping the cervical spine in line while the airway remains open and fluids can drain from the mouth. The upper leg is flexed to stabilise the body.

Rationale

The standard recovery position can involve twisting of the neck and trunk, which in theory may aggravate an unstable spinal injury. Studies using cadaveric and radiological models suggested the HAINES position allows the required airway protection of the lateral position while producing less lateral neck movement than some conventional techniques. It has been taught by some surf life saving and first aid organisations, particularly in Australia, though mainstream resuscitation guidelines continue to emphasise that airway and breathing take priority over spinal precautions in an unconscious casualty.
