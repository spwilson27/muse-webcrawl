Hawker centre

A hawker centre, or cooked food centre, is an open-air complex found in Singapore, Malaysia, Hong Kong and other parts of Southeast Asia, housing many individual stalls that sell a variety of inexpensive cooked food. They are typically found in city centres, housing estates and near markets and transport interchanges.

History and culture

Hawker centres emerged as governments moved street food vendors into hygienic, purpose-built facilities beginning in the 1950s–1970s. In Singapore, hawker culture — the practice of preparing, selling and eating food in these communal spaces — was inscribed on the UNESCO Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2020.

Stalls commonly serve dishes such as Hainanese chicken rice, char kway teow, laksa, satay, roti prata and nasi lemak. Meals are eaten at shared tables, and ordering drinks is often handled by a separate drinks stall.
