Humboldt University of Berlin

The Humboldt University of Berlin (German: Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin) is a public research university in the Mitte district of Berlin, Germany. Founded in 1810 as the University of Berlin on the initiative of Wilhelm von Humboldt, it became the model for the modern research university, uniting teaching and research.

History

King Frederick William III founded the university in 1810 in the wake of Prussia's defeat by Napoleon, with Wilhelm von Humboldt designing its structure around academic freedom and the unity of teaching and research (Einheit von Lehre und Forschung). Wilhelm's brother, the naturalist Alexander von Humboldt, is the university's second namesake; it received the Humboldt name in 1949.

People and scholarship

Its scholars have included Hegel, Fichte, Max Planck, Albert Einstein (as director of a Kaiser Wilhelm institute and lecturer), Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels as students, and 29 Nobel laureates among its professors, with 57 laureates affiliated overall by the university's count.

Division and reunification

After 1945 the university lay in the Soviet sector of Berlin; many students and staff left to found the Free University of Berlin in the west in 1948. Under East Germany the Humboldt University was reshaped along socialist lines, and after reunification in 1990 it was restructured and re-integrated into the German academic system. It is consistently ranked among Germany's leading universities and was selected in the German Universities Excellence Initiative.
