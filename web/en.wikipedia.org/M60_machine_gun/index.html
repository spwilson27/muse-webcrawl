The M60 is an American family of general-purpose machine guns chambered for the 7.62×51mm NATO cartridge. Adopted in 1957, it became the standard infantry machine gun of the United States military during the Vietnam War, where it earned the nickname "the Pig" for its bulk.

Design

The M60 drew on German Second World War designs, combining the belt-feed mechanism of the MG 42 with features of the FG 42. It is gas-operated, air-cooled and fires from an open bolt, with a quick-change barrel intended to be swapped by a gunner wearing an asbestos mitt — one of several ergonomic shortcomings that plagued early versions. Early guns suffered reliability and durability problems, addressed progressively through variants such as the M60E3 and M60E4/Mk 43.

Service

The M60 served on infantry bipods and tripods, as a door gun on helicopters, and as vehicle armament. From the 1980s it was largely replaced in US infantry service by the M240 (FN MAG), though variants remain in use with US special operations and many allied militaries worldwide.
