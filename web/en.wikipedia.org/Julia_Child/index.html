Julia Carolyn Child (née McWilliams; 1912 – 2004) was an American chef, author and television personality recognized for bringing French cuisine to the American public through her debut cookbook, Mastering the Art of French Cooking (1961, with Simone Beck and Louisette Bertholle), and her long-running television series The French Chef, which premiered in 1963.

Early life and war service

Child grew up in Pasadena, California, graduated from Smith College, and during the Second World War worked for the Office of Strategic Services (OSS), serving in Washington, Ceylon (Sri Lanka) and China, where she met her husband, Paul Child. The couple married in 1946 and moved to Paris in 1948, where Julia's first meal of sole meunière sparked her passion for French cooking.

Career

Child studied at Le Cordon Bleu and with private tutors, then taught cooking with Beck and Bertholle at their school, L'École des Trois Gourmandes, while spending a decade testing recipes for their cookbook. Mastering the Art of French Cooking became a bestseller, and a promotional appearance making an omelette on Boston public television led to The French Chef, whose relaxed, mistake-tolerant style and Child's distinctive voice made her a beloved figure. Further series included Julia Child & Company, Dinner at Julia's and Baking with Julia. Her Cambridge, Massachusetts kitchen is preserved at the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History.

Legacy

Child received the Presidential Medal of Freedom in 2003 and was inducted into the National Women's Hall of Fame. Her memoir My Life in France (2006, with Alex Prud'homme) inspired the 2009 film Julie & Julia. She is widely credited with transforming American home cooking and food television.
