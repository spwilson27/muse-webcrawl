Department of Veterans Affairs

The United States Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) is a Cabinet-level executive branch department of the federal government charged with providing lifelong healthcare services to eligible military veterans at VA medical centers and outpatient clinics located throughout the country. Non-healthcare benefits include disability compensation, vocational rehabilitation, education assistance, home loans, and life insurance. The VA also provides burial and memorial benefits to eligible veterans and family members at national cemeteries.

The department employs more than 400,000 people at hundreds of VA medical facilities, clinics, and benefits offices, and is headed by the Secretary of Veterans Affairs. It is the second-largest department of the U.S. federal government, after the Department of Defense.

History

Federal provision for veterans dates to the Revolutionary War era, when the Continental Congress provided pensions for disabled soldiers. The Veterans Administration was established in 1930 by executive order, consolidating several bureaus. In 1989 it was elevated to Cabinet status and renamed the Department of Veterans Affairs.

Organization

The VA is organized into three main administrations: the Veterans Health Administration (VHA), the largest integrated health care system in the United States; the Veterans Benefits Administration (VBA), which administers compensation, pensions, education, and loan programs; and the National Cemetery Administration (NCA).

Motto

The VA motto quotes Abraham Lincoln's second inaugural address: "To care for him who shall have borne the battle and for his widow, and his orphan."
