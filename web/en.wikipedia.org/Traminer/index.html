Traminer

Traminer grapes

Traminer is a family of wine grape varieties named after the village of Tramin (Termeno) in South Tyrol, northern Italy. The family includes the relatively neutral Traminer itself and its aromatic, pink-skinned mutation Savagnin rose, of which the best-known form is Gewürztraminer. DNA profiling has shown that Traminer is essentially identical to the variety known in the Jura as Savagnin (Savagnin blanc), one of the oldest cultivated grape varieties in Europe.

History and characteristics

Grapes of the Traminer/Savagnin family have been grown around Tramin since at least the early Middle Ages and possibly Roman times; ampelographers regard Savagnin as a "founder variety" ancestral to many modern cultivars, with genetic links to Pinot and to varieties of northeastern France and central Europe. The berries of the original Traminer are green-yellow, while Savagnin rose has pink skin; Gewürztraminer ("spiced Traminer") was selected from the rose form for its intense lychee, rose-petal and spice aromas. The family is known for moderate acidity, high potential sugar and susceptibility to spring frost and coulure.

Wine regions

Today the name Traminer is most often encountered in Alsace, Germany, Austria, northern Italy and Eastern Europe, though most celebrated "Traminer" wines are in fact Gewürztraminer. Savagnin proper survives most famously in the Jura, where it produces the oxidative vin jaune of Château-Chalon, while Traminer-labelled wines are produced in Alto Adige/Südtirol, Slovenia, Croatia, Romania and Moldova.
