Properties of water

Water
Formula: H2O
Molar mass: 18.015 g/mol
Melting point: 0 °C
Boiling point: 100 °C
Density: ~1 g/cm³

Water (H2O) is an inorganic compound with a wide range of unusual physical and chemical properties that arise from its polar covalent bonding and extensive hydrogen bonding. It is the most abundant substance on Earth's surface and is essential to all known forms of life.

Physical properties

Water is a tasteless, odorless liquid at standard temperature and pressure. Owing to hydrogen bonding, it has anomalously high melting and boiling points, a high specific heat capacity, and high heats of vaporization and fusion. Unusually, its solid form, ice, is less dense than the liquid, so ice floats; liquid water reaches its maximum density at about 4 °C.

Chemical properties

The water molecule is bent, with an H–O–H angle of about 104.5°, producing a strong molecular dipole. Water is amphoteric, undergoing self-ionization to hydronium and hydroxide ions, and it is an excellent solvent for polar and ionic substances, earning it the description "universal solvent". It participates in hydrolysis and hydration reactions and is a product of acid–base neutralization.

Biological significance

Water's solvent properties, thermal buffering, cohesion, and adhesion make it the medium in which biochemical reactions occur and a major constituent of cells.
