Dodge Charger Daytona

The Dodge Charger Daytona is a high-performance, aerodynamically modified version of the Dodge Charger built for NASCAR racing in 1969. It is famous for its pointed nose cone and towering rear wing, and was the first NASCAR stock car to exceed 200 mph.

Development

After the Charger 500 proved insufficiently aerodynamic, Dodge engineers added an extended sheet-metal nose, a flush rear window and a 23-inch-tall rear wing mounted high to reach undisturbed air. The changes cut drag dramatically. Buddy Baker broke the 200 mph barrier at Talladega in March 1970 in a Daytona.

Production and legacy

NASCAR rules required street versions be sold to the public; about 503 road cars were built in 1969, fitted with 440 Magnum or 426 Hemi V8s. Dominance by the "winged warriors" — the Daytona and its Plymouth Superbird sibling — led NASCAR to restrict aero cars for 1971. Original Daytonas are now among the most valuable American muscle cars. Dodge later revived the Daytona name for trim packages and, in 2024, for its electric Charger Daytona.
