Fred Hampton

DiedDecember 4, 1969 (aged 21), Chicago, Illinois, U.S.

Fred Hampton Sr. (August 30, 1948 – December 4, 1969) was an American activist and Marxist revolutionary. He rose to prominence as chairman of the Illinois chapter of the Black Panther Party and deputy chairman of the national party, and he founded the multiracial Rainbow Coalition of working-class organisations in Chicago. He was killed at age 21 in a pre-dawn police raid on his apartment.

Activism

Hampton grew up in Maywood, a Chicago suburb, and was active in the NAACP youth council while still a student. Joining the Black Panthers in 1968, he proved a gifted organiser and orator, helping to establish community programs including free breakfasts for children and a free medical clinic. His signature achievement was brokering alliances among the Panthers, the Young Lords, the Young Patriots, and other groups — the original "Rainbow Coalition" — uniting Black, Puerto Rican, and poor white organisations around shared class interests.

Death

In the early morning of December 4, 1969, officers attached to the Cook County State's Attorney raided Hampton's apartment, using a floor plan supplied by FBI informant William O'Neal under the Bureau's COINTELPRO program. Panther Mark Clark was killed, and Hampton was shot dead in his bed; survivors maintained he had been drugged or incapacitated. Ballistics evidence indicated police fired the overwhelming majority of shots. No officer was convicted over the raid; civil litigation ended in 1982 with a settlement of $1.85 million paid to survivors and the families of Hampton and Clark.

Legacy

Hampton's speeches, organising, and death made him an enduring figure in American protest movements. The 2021 film Judas and the Black Messiah dramatised his life and the informant's role in the raid.
