Adil Shahi dynasty

The Adil Shahi dynasty ruled the Sultanate of Bijapur in the western Deccan region of South India from 1490 to 1686. The dynasty was founded by Yusuf Adil Shah, a governor of the Bahmani Sultanate of probable Georgian or Turkic origin, who declared independence as Bijapur broke away from the declining Bahmani state.

History

Bijapur was one of the five Deccan sultanates that succeeded the Bahmani kingdom. The Adil Shahis alternately fought and allied with their neighbours — the Nizam Shahis of Ahmadnagar, the Qutb Shahis of Golconda, the Portuguese at Goa, and the Hindu empire of Vijayanagara, whose defeat at the Battle of Talikota (1565) was achieved by a confederacy of the Deccan sultanates. Under Ibrahim Adil Shah II (1580–1627), a noted patron of music and painting, Bijapur reached its cultural zenith.

Architecture and legacy

The dynasty left major monuments at Bijapur (now Vijayapura), including the Gol Gumbaz — the mausoleum of Mohammed Adil Shah, whose dome is among the largest in the pre-modern world — and the Ibrahim Rauza. The sultanate was finally annexed by the Mughal emperor Aurangzeb in 1686 after a long siege.
