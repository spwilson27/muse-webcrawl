Air National Guard

Active: 18 September 1947 – present
Country: United States
Type: Air force militia, reserve component
Size: over 100,000 personnel

The Air National Guard (ANG), also known as the Air Guard, is a federal military reserve force of the United States Air Force, and the air militia of each state, the District of Columbia, and the territories of Puerto Rico, Guam and the U.S. Virgin Islands. Together with the Army National Guard, it forms the National Guard of the United States.

Dual status

Air National Guard units have a unique dual role. Under state status (Title 32 or State Active Duty), they answer to the governor of their state and respond to natural disasters, civil emergencies and homeland defence tasks. When federalised under Title 10, they come under the President and the Air Force and can deploy worldwide. Most members serve part-time – typically one weekend a month and two weeks a year – while a cadre of full-time Active Guard and Reserve (AGR) personnel and air reserve technicians maintains daily operations.

History

The ANG was established under the National Security Act of 1947, alongside the independent Air Force, inheriting state aviation units that dated back to the 29th Observation Squadron organised in New York before the First World War. Guard air units served in Korea, the Berlin crisis, Vietnam, the Gulf War, and extensively in the post-2001 operations in Iraq and Afghanistan, in addition to flying the air defence alert mission over the United States.

Organisation

There are Air National Guard flying wings and groups in every state, operating fighters (F-15, F-16, F-22, F-35), tankers (KC-135, KC-46), airlifters (C-130, C-17) and specialised units for rescue, intelligence, space operations, cyber operations and remotely piloted aircraft. The ANG is administered federally by the National Guard Bureau, whose chief is a member of the Joint Chiefs of Staff.
