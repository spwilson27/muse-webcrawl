Creighton Abrams

Born: September 15, 1914, Springfield, Massachusetts
Died: September 4, 1974, Washington, D.C.
Rank: General, U.S. Army
Commands: MACV; Chief of Staff of the U.S. Army

Creighton Williams Abrams Jr. (September 15, 1914 – September 4, 1974) was a United States Army general who commanded U.S. forces in the Vietnam War from 1968 to 1972 and then served as Chief of Staff of the Army until his death in office. The M1 Abrams battle tank is named in his honour.

Early career

A 1936 West Point graduate, Abrams won renown in the Second World War as commander of the 37th Tank Battalion in Patton's Third Army, spearheading the relief of Bastogne during the Battle of the Bulge; Patton called him the best tank commander in the Army. He later served in Korea and commanded an armoured division in Germany.

Vietnam

As deputy, then successor to General William Westmoreland at Military Assistance Command, Vietnam, Abrams shifted emphasis from large search-and-destroy sweeps toward pacification and protection of the population, and oversaw "Vietnamization" and the drawdown of U.S. troops from over 535,000 to under 30,000. His tenure included the Cambodian incursion of 1970 and the repulse of the 1972 Easter Offensive.

Chief of Staff

As Chief of Staff from 1972, Abrams rebuilt an Army demoralized by Vietnam, championed the all-volunteer force, and created the modern Ranger battalions. He died of complications from lung cancer surgery in 1974 and is buried at Arlington National Cemetery.
