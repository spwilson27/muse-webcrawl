HMS Prince of Wales (pennant number R09) is the second Queen Elizabeth-class aircraft carrier of the Royal Navy. She was built by the Aircraft Carrier Alliance, floated out in 2017 at Rosyth and commissioned in December 2019. She is the eighth Royal Navy ship to bear the name Prince of Wales.

Design

The ship displaces about 65,000 tonnes, is 280 metres long, and operates short take-off but arrested recovery (STOVL) aircraft — principally F-35B Lightning II jets launched via a ski-jump — together with helicopters such as the Merlin and Wildcat. Her twin-island design separates ship navigation from flying control. She has a crew of about 700, rising to about 1,600 with an air group embarked.

Service

Prince of Wales served as NATO's command ship for the Maritime High Readiness Force in 2022. In August 2022 she suffered a propeller shaft coupling failure shortly after leaving Portsmouth for trials in North America and required lengthy repairs in dry dock at Rosyth, returning to sea in 2023. She subsequently assumed duties including deputising for her sister ship HMS Queen Elizabeth and major multinational exercises, and in 2025 led the UK Carrier Strike Group deployment to the Indo-Pacific (Operation Highmast).
