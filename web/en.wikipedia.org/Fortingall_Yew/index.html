Fortingall Yew

Fortingall Yew
Species: European yew (Taxus baccata)
Location: Fortingall, Perthshire, Scotland
Estimated age: 2,000–5,000+ years (traditionally ~5,000)
Status: One of Britain's oldest trees

The Fortingall Yew is an ancient European yew (Taxus baccata) in the churchyard of the village of Fortingall in Perthshire, Scotland. It is often cited as one of the oldest trees in Britain, and possibly the oldest in Europe, with age estimates ranging from 2,000 to as much as 5,000 years or more.

Age and condition

In the 18th century the trunk had a girth of over 16 metres, but the once-single stem has split into separate hollow stems, making age estimation by ring counts impossible. A low stone wall now protects the tree.

History

Local tradition associates the site with pre-Christian worship and early Christian missionaries; a persistent legend claims Pontius Pilate was born in Fortingall and played under the yew as a child, though this is unsupported. In 2015 researchers noted the tree, long considered male, had produced berries — a sex change occasionally observed in yews.
