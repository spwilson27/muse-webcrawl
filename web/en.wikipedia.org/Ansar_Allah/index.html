Ansar Allah (Arabic: أنصار الله, "Supporters of God"), commonly known as the Houthi movement, is a Zaydi Shia Islamist political and military organisation that emerged from the Saada Governorate of northern Yemen in the 1990s. The movement takes its popular name from its founding leader, Hussein Badreddin al-Houthi, who was killed by Yemeni government forces in 2004; it is subsequently led by his brother Abdul-Malik al-Houthi.

History

The movement grew out of the "Believing Youth" (Muntada al-Shabab al-Mu'min), a Zaydi revivalist network founded in 1992 to promote Zaydi religious education in the face of growing Salafi influence and perceived marginalisation of the northern highlands. Between 2004 and 2010 the group fought six wars against the government of President Ali Abdullah Saleh.

In 2014, amid fuel-subsidy unrest, Houthi forces seized the capital Sanaa, and in early 2015 President Abdrabbuh Mansur Hadi fled, prompting a Saudi-led coalition to intervene militarily in March 2015. The ensuing Yemeni civil war produced one of the world's worst humanitarian crises. The Houthis governed much of northern Yemen, including Sanaa, through the following decade.

Red Sea attacks

Beginning in November 2023, during the Gaza war, Ansar Allah forces launched missile and drone attacks on commercial shipping in the Red Sea and Bab-el-Mandeb strait, stating they targeted vessels linked to Israel in solidarity with Palestinians. The attacks severely disrupted global shipping and prompted United States and United Kingdom airstrikes on Houthi targets in Yemen from January 2024, together with a US-led maritime protection mission.

Beliefs and designation

The movement's slogan (the sarkha) includes denunciations of the United States and Israel. The Houthis maintain close ties with Iran, though the extent of Iranian command influence is debated. The United States designated the group a Specially Designated Global Terrorist entity in January 2024 (a Foreign Terrorist Organization designation was briefly applied in 2021, revoked, and later reimposed in 2025).
