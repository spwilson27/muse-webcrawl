County Cork (Irish: Contae Chorcaí) is the largest and the southernmost county of Ireland, situated in the province of Munster and named after the city of Cork, Ireland's second-largest city. It is known as the "Rebel County", a name from its support for the Yorkist pretender Perkin Warbeck in 1491 and its prominent role in the Irish War of Independence.

Geography

The county has a long Atlantic coastline with peninsulas such as Beara, Sheep's Head, and Mizen Head, and towns including Cobh, Kinsale, Bantry, Skibbereen, Youghal, and Midleton. Cork Harbour is one of the largest natural harbours in the world. The River Lee flows through the county to the sea at Cork city; the Blackwater flows east to Youghal.

History and culture

County Cork was a centre of the War of Independence — the Kilmichael and Crossbarry ambushes took place there, and Michael Collins was born near Clonakilty and killed at Béal na Bláth in 1922. The county is a stronghold of Gaelic games, with Cork teams among the most successful in hurling and Gaelic football. Blarney Castle, home of the Blarney Stone, is near Cork city.
