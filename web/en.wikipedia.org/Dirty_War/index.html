The Dirty War (Spanish: Guerra sucia) is the name used for the campaign of state terrorism carried out in Argentina from around 1974 — and systematically after the military coup of 24 March 1976 — until the return of democracy in 1983. Under the military dictatorship known as the National Reorganization Process, the armed forces and security services abducted, tortured, and murdered thousands of people suspected of left-wing sympathies, most of whom "disappeared" (desaparecidos) into a network of clandestine detention centres.

Background and course

The repression began amid the political violence of the early 1970s, including the right-wing death squads of the Argentine Anticommunist Alliance (Triple A) under the government of Isabel Perón. After the 1976 coup, the junta led successively by Jorge Rafael Videla, Roberto Viola, and Leopoldo Galtieri waged a secret war against guerrilla organizations such as Montoneros and the ERP, but also against trade unionists, students, journalists, clergy, and relatives of suspects. Victims were held in centres such as the ESMA (Navy School of Mechanics) in Buenos Aires; many were killed in "death flights" — thrown drugged from aircraft into the Río de la Plata or the Atlantic. Babies born to detainees were in hundreds of cases given to military families under false identities.

Aftermath and accountability

Following Argentina's defeat in the Falklands War (1982), the junta fell and President Raúl Alfonsín established the National Commission on the Disappearance of Persons (CONADEP), whose 1984 report Nunca Más ("Never Again") documented 8,961 disappearances. In the 1985 Trial of the Juntas, Videla and other junta members were convicted. Later amnesty laws and pardons were annulled in the 2000s, and trials resumed, resulting in hundreds of convictions. The term "Dirty War" is criticized by many victims' groups because it implies a war between two symmetric sides; the Mothers and Grandmothers of the Plaza de Mayo have kept the search for the disappeared and for stolen grandchildren alive for decades.
