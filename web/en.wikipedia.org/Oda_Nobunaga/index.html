Oda Nobunaga

Oda Nobunaga (23 June 1534 – 21 June 1582) was a Japanese daimyō and one of the leading figures of the Sengoku period. He was the initiator of the unification of Japan under the rule of the shogunate, a process completed by his successors Toyotomi Hideyoshi and Tokugawa Ieyasu. Nobunaga is regarded as one of the three great unifiers of Japan.

Early life and rise

Born in Owari Province, Nobunaga inherited his father's position in 1551 and gradually subdued rival branches of the Oda clan. His victory over the much larger army of Imagawa Yoshimoto at the Battle of Okehazama in 1560 brought him national attention.

Unification

From his alliance with Tokugawa Ieyasu, Nobunaga advanced on Kyoto in 1568, installing Ashikaga Yoshiaki as shogun before later expelling him. He defeated the Asakura and Azai clans, destroyed the warrior monks of Mount Hiei, crushed the Takeda at Nagashino in 1575 with massed arquebus volleys, and built Azuchi Castle as his seat. His forces made extensive use of the officer-led ashigaru infantry and firearms units that transformed Japanese warfare.

Death

In June 1582, while resting at Honnō-ji temple in Kyoto with a small retinue, Nobunaga was attacked by his retainer Akechi Mitsuhide in the Honnō-ji Incident. Facing certain capture, he died in the burning temple, by suicide or in the flames; his remains were never found.
