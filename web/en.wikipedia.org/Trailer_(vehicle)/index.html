Trailer (vehicle)


A small utility trailer

A trailer is an unpowered vehicle towed by a powered vehicle. Trailers are used for the transport of goods and materials, for carrying boats, vehicles and livestock, and as mobile dwellings (travel trailers or caravans). Unlike a semi-trailer, a full trailer supports its own weight entirely on its own axles and is coupled to the towing vehicle by a drawbar.

Types

Common types include general-purpose utility trailers; enclosed cargo trailers; flatbed trailers; boat trailers with rollers and bunks; horse trailers and livestock trailers; dump trailers; and travel trailers fitted with living accommodation. Large freight combinations use semi-trailers supported at the front by the tractor unit's fifth wheel, or full trailers behind trucks in road trains.

Coupling and safety

Trailers attach via a tow hitch — commonly a ball-and-socket coupling on light vehicles — and are secured with safety chains. Larger trailers have their own braking systems (overrun or electric brakes) and lighting connected to the towing vehicle. Safe towing depends on correct nose weight, balanced loading, adequate towing capacity and mirror visibility; excessive speed or poor loading can cause dangerous trailer sway ("snaking"). Licensing rules in many jurisdictions impose additional categories or tests for towing heavier trailers.
