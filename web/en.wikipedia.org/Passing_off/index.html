Passing off

Passing off is a common law tort which can be used to enforce unregistered trade mark rights. The tort of passing off protects the goodwill of a trader from misrepresentation. The law of passing off prevents one trader from misrepresenting goods or services as being the goods and services of another, and also prevents a trader from holding out their goods or services as having some association or connection with another when this is not true.

The classic trinity

In the leading case Reckitt & Colman Ltd v Borden Inc (the "Jif Lemon" case), Lord Oliver summarised the elements a claimant must prove: (1) goodwill or reputation attached to the goods or services; (2) a misrepresentation by the defendant to the public leading or likely to lead the public to believe the goods or services are those of the claimant; and (3) damage suffered or likely to be suffered by reason of that misrepresentation.

Extended passing off

The tort has been extended to protect shared goodwill in generic terms associated with a class of products, as in the "Champagne" and "Advocaat" cases, and to cases of false endorsement by celebrities, as in Irvine v Talksport.
