Bird intelligence

Bird intelligence refers to the cognitive abilities of birds, including problem solving, tool use, learning, communication and social cognition. Corvids (crows, ravens, jays) and parrots show abilities comparable in some respects to primates, despite very different brain organization.

Evidence

New Caledonian crows manufacture hooked tools; ravens plan for future tool use and bartering; scrub-jays remember what food they cached, where and when (episodic-like memory) and adjust for pilferers. African grey parrots such as Alex learned labels for objects, colours and numbers. Pigeons categorize images and learn sequences; many species learn songs culturally.

Brain basis

Birds lack a layered neocortex, but their pallium — especially the nidopallium caudolaterale — supports executive functions, and bird brains pack neurons at very high density. Avian cognition is a case of convergent evolution of intelligence.

Social and other abilities

Studies report cooperation, social learning, individual recognition, play, and possibly elements of theory of mind in corvids, alongside navigation feats like migration using magnetic and celestial cues in many species.
