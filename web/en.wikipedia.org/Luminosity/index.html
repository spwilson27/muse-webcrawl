Luminosity

Luminosity is the total amount of electromagnetic energy emitted per unit time by a star, galaxy, or other astronomical object. In SI units it is measured in watts; astronomers often express it in solar luminosities, L☉ ≈ 3.828 × 1026 W.

Measurement

Luminosity follows from apparent brightness and distance via the inverse-square law. Bolometric luminosity integrates over all wavelengths; corrections account for emission outside the observed band. For stars, L = 4πR²σTeff⁴, linking luminosity, radius, and effective temperature.

Stellar luminosity

Main-sequence stars obey a mass–luminosity relation, roughly L ∝ M3.5. The Sun defines 1 L☉; the most luminous stars reach millions of solar luminosities, approaching the Eddington limit where radiation pressure balances gravity.

Other uses

In particle physics, luminosity measures collision rates in accelerators. In photometry, related quantities include luminous intensity and luminance, weighted by human visual sensitivity.
