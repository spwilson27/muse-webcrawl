Schwarzschild metric

Flamm's paraboloid: the embedding of the Schwarzschild geometry.

In Einstein's theory of general relativity, the Schwarzschild metric is an exact solution of the Einstein field equations that describes the gravitational field outside a spherical mass, under the assumptions that the mass is non-rotating and uncharged. It was found by Karl Schwarzschild in 1915, shortly after Einstein published general relativity, and it provides an excellent approximation to the slowly rotating fields of planets and stars.

The metric

In Schwarzschild coordinates, the line element contains the factor (1 − rs/r), where rs = 2GM/c² is the Schwarzschild radius. At r = rs the metric has a coordinate singularity marking the event horizon; at r = 0 there is a genuine curvature singularity.

Consequences

The Schwarzschild solution predicts gravitational time dilation, the gravitational redshift, the bending of light by the Sun, and the anomalous precession of Mercury's perihelion—all subsequently confirmed by observation. A body compressed within its Schwarzschild radius forms a Schwarzschild black hole.

History

Schwarzschild derived the solution while serving on the Eastern Front in the First World War; Einstein presented it to the Prussian Academy of Sciences in January 1916. By Birkhoff's theorem, the Schwarzschild metric is the unique spherically symmetric solution of the vacuum field equations.
