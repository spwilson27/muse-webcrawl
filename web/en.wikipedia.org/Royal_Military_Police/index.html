The Royal Military Police (RMP) is the corps of the British Army responsible for the policing of army personnel and for providing a military police presence in support of military operations at home and overseas. Its members are instantly recognisable by their scarlet peaked caps, which give them the nickname "Redcaps".

Role

The RMP investigates crime involving service personnel — from theft and assault to serious offences handled by its Special Investigation Branch (SIB) — guards prisoners and detainees, enforces discipline and service law, controls routes and traffic for military movements, and provides close protection for senior officers and VIPs. In operations it supports commanders with law-and-order advice, detention handling, and policing in the rear area and among displaced populations. Since 1992 the corps has been part of the Adjutant General's Corps while retaining its separate identity, cap badge and traditions.

History

The office of Provost Marshal dates to 1511, making the provost function the oldest in the British Army, but organised military police units date from the nineteenth century: the Military Mounted Police was formed in 1885 and the Military Foot Police in 1885–86. The two merged as the Corps of Military Police in 1926. The corps served in both world wars — directing traffic behind the lines, rounding up stragglers and, in the Second World War, staffing the new Special Investigation Branch — receiving the prefix "Royal" in 1946 in recognition of its wartime service.

More recently the RMP deployed extensively in Northern Ireland, the Balkans, Iraq and Afghanistan. In 2003 six RMP soldiers were killed by a crowd in Majar al-Kabir, Iraq, one of the corps' darkest days.

Organisation

The corps is organised into regiments aligned with army formations, including 1, 3 and 4 Regiment RMP, plus specialist units such as the Military Corrective Training Centre staff at Colchester, the Close Protection Unit and the Service Police Crime Bureau. The Provost Marshal (Army) heads the corps and is responsible to the Adjutant General.
