Ying Chen (born 1961) is a Chinese-born Canadian novelist who writes in French. She emigrated to Quebec in 1989 and has lived in Montreal and later in Vancouver.

Works

Her novels, spare and meditative, explore memory, exile, reincarnation, and the instability of identity. They include La mémoire de l'eau (1992), Les lettres chinoises (1993), L'ingratitude (1995), which won the Prix Québec-Paris, Le champ dans la mer (2002), and Espèces (2010).

Style and themes

Chen's fiction is noted for its minimalist prose and cyclical structures, in which narrators drift between lives and places. Writing in an adopted language, she has reflected on the experience of living between cultures and languages.
