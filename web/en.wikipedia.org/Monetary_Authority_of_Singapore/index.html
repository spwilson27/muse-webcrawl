The Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) is the central bank of Singapore and its integrated financial regulatory authority. Established on 1 January 1971 under the Monetary Authority of Singapore Act, it administers the statutes governing money, banking, insurance, securities, and the financial sector in general, and manages Singapore's official foreign reserves.

Monetary policy

Unlike most central banks, the MAS does not set interest rates. Instead, since 1981 it has conducted monetary policy through the exchange rate, managing the Singapore dollar against an undisclosed trade-weighted basket of currencies within an adjustable policy band (the Singapore dollar nominal effective exchange rate, S$NEER). The MAS announces its policy stance twice a year, typically in April and October, and can adjust the slope, width, and centre of the band.

The exchange-rate-centred framework reflects Singapore's very open economy, in which imports form a large share of domestic consumption, making the exchange rate a powerful lever on imported inflation.

Financial supervision

As an integrated regulator, the MAS supervises banks, insurers, capital-market intermediaries, exchanges, and payment service providers, and it is responsible for financial stability surveillance. It also develops Singapore as an international financial centre and issues the country's banknotes and coins, which are fully backed by external assets held by the Currency Fund.
