The Cahn–Ingold–Prelog priority rules (CIP rules) are a standard process used in organic chemistry to name a stereoisomer of a molecule unambiguously. Developed by Robert Sidney Cahn, Christopher Kelk Ingold and Vladimir Prelog, the rules assign priorities to the substituents of a stereocentre or double bond, enabling the assignment of descriptors such as R and S for chirality and E and Z for alkene geometry.

The rules

Rule 1: The atoms directly attached to the stereocentre are ranked by atomic number; the higher the atomic number, the higher the priority.

Rule 2: If two substituents begin with the same atom, the lists of atoms attached to each are compared in descending order, working outward sphere by sphere, until a difference is found.

Rule 3: Multiple bonds are treated as if the multiply bonded atom were duplicated or triplicated — for example, a C=O double bond is treated as carbon bonded to O, O.

Rule 4: When all else is equal, isotopes are ranked by mass number, and lone pairs are treated as atoms of atomic number zero.

Assigning R and S

With the substituents ranked 1 to 4, the molecule is oriented so that the lowest-priority group points away from the viewer. If the remaining three groups decrease in priority clockwise, the centre is R (rectus); if counterclockwise, it is S (sinister). The same prioritisation scheme underlies the E/Z notation for double bonds and descriptors for axial and planar chirality.

History

The rules were first published by Cahn and Ingold in 1951, expanded with Prelog in 1956, and codified in a definitive 1966 paper in Angewandte Chemie. They were later incorporated into the IUPAC nomenclature recommendations and remain part of the "Blue Book" of organic nomenclature.
