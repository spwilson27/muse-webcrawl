The Battle of Prokhorovka was fought on 12 July 1943 near Prokhorovka, 87 kilometres southeast of Kursk, during the Battle of Kursk on the Eastern Front of the Second World War. It is often described as one of the largest tank battles in military history, pitting the German II SS Panzer Corps against the Soviet 5th Guards Tank Army.

Background

In July 1943 Germany launched Operation Citadel, an offensive against the Kursk salient. On the southern face of the salient, the 4th Panzer Army and Army Detachment Kempf drove north toward Prokhorovka. The Soviet high command committed the 5th Guards Tank Army under Lieutenant General Pavel Rotmistrov, which had force-marched some 400 kilometres to reach the battlefield.

The battle

On the morning of 12 July, Soviet armoured formations counterattacked the II SS Panzer Corps in a series of frontal assaults across open ground southwest of Prokhorovka. Fighting was close-range and costly: the Soviets lost far more vehicles, but the German advance was checked, and the sheer exhaustion of the attackers combined with the Allied landing in Sicily led Hitler to call off Citadel days later.

Historiography

Post-war accounts long exaggerated the scale of a single day's tank clash and the number of German Tiger tanks destroyed. Modern research, drawing on archival records and aerial photographs, gives a more measured picture: several hundred armoured vehicles were engaged on 12 July, with Soviet losses markedly higher, yet the strategic initiative passed permanently to the Red Army.
