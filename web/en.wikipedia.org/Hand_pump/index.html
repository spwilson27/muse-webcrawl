Hand pump

TypeManually operated pump
UsesWater wells, bicycle tyres, air mattresses
MechanismReciprocating piston or lever action

A hand pump is a pump operated by human muscle power, used to move liquids or gases. Hand pumps are widely used in developing countries for drawing drinking water from wells and boreholes, and in portable forms such as bicycle pumps for inflating tyres.

Types

Common designs include suction pumps, which rely on atmospheric pressure and can lift water only about 7–8 metres, and deep-well force pumps with a piston in the cylinder below ground, such as the India Mark II and Afridev pumps, standardised for village-level maintenance. Direct-action and rope pumps are simpler designs used where water tables are shallow.

Development importance

Reliable hand pumps are a key technology for rural water supply; the concept of Village Level Operation and Maintenance (VLOM) guided designs that local mechanics can repair with locally available parts. Hundreds of millions of people depend on hand pumps for daily water, though sustainability of maintenance remains a major challenge.
