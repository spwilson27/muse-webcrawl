Eightfold Way (physics)

Eightfold Way
Proposed1961
ProponentsMurray Gell-Mann, Yuval Ne'eman
Based onSU(3) flavour symmetry

In particle physics, the Eightfold Way is a classification scheme for subatomic particles — hadrons — proposed independently in 1961 by Murray Gell-Mann and Yuval Ne'eman. It organises baryons and mesons into multiplets of the approximate SU(3) flavour symmetry acting on the up, down and strange quarks. The name, from the Buddhist Noble Eightfold Path, was given by Gell-Mann.

Multiplets and predictions

Under the scheme, mesons fall into octets and singlets (explaining the η and η′ mesons), spin-½ baryons form an octet (nucleons, Λ, Σ, Ξ), and spin-³⁄₂ baryons form a decuplet. Nine members of the decuplet were known; the scheme predicted a tenth, the Ω⁻, with specific mass and quantum numbers — its discovery at Brookhaven in 1964 was a triumph for the model.

Legacy

The Eightfold Way's success led Gell-Mann (and independently George Zweig) to propose in 1964 that the multiplets arise because hadrons are composed of quarks. It is now understood as an approximate symmetry of quantum chromodynamics, broken by the heavier strange quark mass.
