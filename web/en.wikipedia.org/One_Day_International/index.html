A One Day International (ODI) is a form of limited overs cricket, played between two teams with international status, in which each team faces a fixed number of overs, currently 50, and the game lasts up to 9 hours. The Cricket World Cup, generally held every four years, is played in this format. One Day International matches are also called Limited Overs Internationals (LOI), although that term may also refer to Twenty20 International matches.

ODI teams traditionally wear coloured clothing and use a white ball.

History

The first ODI was played on 5 January 1971 between Australia and England at the Melbourne Cricket Ground, improvised to fill the days left after the scheduled Test match was washed out; Australia won the 40-eight-ball-over match. The format grew out of domestic limited-overs competitions in England in the 1960s and led to the first Cricket World Cup in 1975. Innovations popularized in one-day cricket include coloured clothing, white balls, day/night matches under floodlights, and fielding restrictions.

Rules

Each side bats for a maximum of 50 overs unless bowled out sooner. Bowlers may bowl at most 10 overs each. During the first 10 overs (the powerplay), no more than two fielders may stand outside the 30-yard circle; fielding restrictions relax in later phases. In rain-affected matches, targets are recalculated using the Duckworth–Lewis–Stern method. ODI status is granted by the International Cricket Council to its full members and selected associate teams.

Records

Sachin Tendulkar of India holds the records for most ODI runs (18,426) and most matches played. Sri Lanka's Muttiah Muralitharan took the most ODI wickets (534). The highest team total in an ODI is England's 498 for 4 against the Netherlands in 2022.
