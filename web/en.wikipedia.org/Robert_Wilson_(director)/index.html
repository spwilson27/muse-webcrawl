Robert Wilson (4 October 1941 – 31 July 2025) was an American experimental theatre and opera director, playwright, choreographer, and visual artist. Described by The New York Times as "the world's foremost avant-garde 'theatre artist'", Wilson developed a signature style of extreme slowness, sculptural lighting, formalized gesture, and architectural staging, usually devised in collaboration with performers rather than from a conventional dramatic text.

Career

Wilson founded the performance company the Byrd Hoffman School of Byrds in New York in the late 1960s, creating early "operas" such as Deafman Glance (1970), developed with the deaf-mute boy Raymond Andrews, and the week-long KA MOUNTAIN AND GUARDenia TERRACE (1972). His breakthrough came with Einstein on the Beach (1976), a five-hour opera created with composer Philip Glass and choreographer Lucinda Childs, which premiered at the Avignon Festival and was performed at the Metropolitan Opera House.

His sprawling multinational project the CIVIL warS: a tree is best measured when it is down was conceived for the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics; only sections were completed, though the Rome section won acclaim and Wilson received a Pulitzer Prize nomination for drama. He directed canonical operas and plays at major houses worldwide, often in collaboration with artists such as Tom Waits, William S. Burroughs (The Black Rider, 1990), David Byrne, Jessye Norman, and Lady Gaga. In 1992 he founded the Watermill Center on Long Island as a laboratory for interdisciplinary performance. He received the Golden Lion for sculpture at the Venice Biennale in 1993.

Style

Wilson's productions are characterized by precisely choreographed slow movement, striking tableaux, silence, and a lighting design treated as the most important element of the work—"It is light that allows us to hear and see." Text might be fragmented, repeated, or displaced by image and sound, making his theatre closer to visual art and architecture than to psychological drama.
