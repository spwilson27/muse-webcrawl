Hood Canal

Hood CanalLocation: Puget Sound, Washington, USA
Type: fjord
Length: approx. 110 km (68 mi)
Named for: Lord Samuel Hood

Hood Canal is a fjord forming the western lobe of Puget Sound in the U.S. state of Washington, separating the Olympic Peninsula from the Kitsap Peninsula. Despite its name, it is a natural waterway, not an artificial canal. It stretches about 110 km from Admiralty Inlet, bending sharply at the "Great Bend" near Union, and connects to the rest of Puget Sound near the Hood Canal Bridge.

Geography and ecology

Carved by glaciers during the last ice age, the canal is deep but has a shallow sill at its mouth that restricts water exchange; its southern reaches are prone to low dissolved oxygen events that have caused fish kills. The canal supports shellfish farming — oysters, geoduck and Manila clams — and salmon runs in rivers such as the Skokomish, Hamma Hamma and Dosewallips.

Human use

Naval Base Kitsap–Bangor, home port to ballistic-missile submarines, lies on its eastern shore. The floating Hood Canal Bridge (SR 104), first opened in 1961, is one of the longest floating bridges over saltwater; its western half sank in a 1979 storm and was rebuilt. The canal's shores, facing the Olympic Mountains, are lined with small communities and state parks prized for recreation.
