The San Gabriel Mountains (Spanish: Sierra de San Gabriel) are a mountain range located in northern Los Angeles County and western San Bernardino County, California. Part of the Transverse Ranges, the range forms a formidable barrier between the Los Angeles Basin to the south and the Mojave Desert to the north.

The highest summit is Mount San Antonio, commonly called Mount Baldy, at 10,064 feet (3,069 m). Other notable peaks include Mount Wilson, home of the historic Mount Wilson Observatory, and Mount Baden-Powell.

Geology

The range consists largely of uplifted Precambrian and Mesozoic crystalline basement rocks along the San Andreas fault system. Rapid uplift over the past several million years produced steep, deeply incised canyons; the range is still rising and is subject to intense erosion, debris flows and flooding during winter storms.

Ecology

Lower slopes are covered in chaparral, while higher elevations support oak woodlands and mixed conifer forest of ponderosa pine, incense cedar and white fir. The mountains are habitat for bighorn sheep, black bears, mountain lions and the California condor. Much of the range lies within the Angeles National Forest and the San Gabriel Mountains National Monument, designated in 2014 and expanded in 2023 to cover nearly 700,000 acres together with surrounding areas.

Recreation

The mountains are a major recreational resource for metropolitan Los Angeles, offering hiking, skiing at Mount Baldy and Mountain High, the scenic Angeles Crest Highway, and the San Gabriel River bicycle trail. The Pacific Crest Trail crosses the range near Wrightwood.
