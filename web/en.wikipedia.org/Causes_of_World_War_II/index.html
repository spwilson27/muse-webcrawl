Causes of World War II

The causes of World War II have been given a great deal of attention by historians, from the immediate consequences of World War I to the rise of Nazism, fascism, and Japanese militarism in the 1930s. The war is generally considered to have begun on 1 September 1939 with the invasion of Poland by Nazi Germany, though conflict in Asia began earlier with Japan's invasion of Manchuria in 1931 and full-scale war with China in 1937.

Aftermath of World War I

The Treaty of Versailles imposed heavy reparations and territorial losses on Germany, fostering resentment that nationalist movements exploited. The League of Nations, intended to preserve peace, lacked enforcement power and the participation of the United States. The Great Depression after 1929 destabilized democracies and strengthened extremist parties across Europe and Japan.

Rise of the Axis

In Germany, Adolf Hitler and the Nazi Party came to power in 1933, repudiated Versailles, rearmed, and pursued Lebensraum in the east. Italy under Mussolini invaded Ethiopia in 1935; Japan expanded in China. The Western democracies responded with appeasement, most famously at the Munich Agreement of 1938, which ceded the Sudetenland to Germany without preventing further aggression.

Immediate causes

The Molotov–Ribbentrop Pact of August 1939 between Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union divided Eastern Europe into spheres of influence and removed the obstacle of a two-front war. Germany invaded Poland on 1 September 1939; Britain and France declared war on 3 September.
