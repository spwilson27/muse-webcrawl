Waveform

Common typesSine, square, triangle, sawtooth
Related quantitiesFrequency, amplitude, phase
Displayed byOscilloscope

A waveform is the shape and form of a signal such as a wave moving in a physical medium or an abstract representation of a varying quantity. In electronics and signal processing, the term usually refers to the path traced by a voltage or current plotted against time. Many waveforms are periodic and repeat at a fixed frequency.

Common waveforms

The sine wave is the most fundamental waveform: a smooth periodic oscillation described by a sinusoid, of which all other periodic waveforms can be built by superposition (Fourier analysis). The square wave alternates between two levels at regular intervals and is rich in odd harmonics. The triangle wave has a linear rise and fall, and the sawtooth wave ramps in one direction before dropping abruptly. In music synthesis, each waveform has a distinctive timbre: sine waves sound pure, square waves hollow, and sawtooth waves bright and buzzy.

Measurement and display

Waveforms are displayed by instruments such as the oscilloscope, which shows voltage against time, and the spectrum analyser, which shows the signal's frequency content. In digital audio workstations, recorded audio is conventionally drawn as a waveform whose amplitude envelope shows the loudness contour of the sound, allowing precise editing.
