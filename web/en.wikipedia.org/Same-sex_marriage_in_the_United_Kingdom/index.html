Same-sex marriage in the United Kingdom

Same-sex marriage is legal throughout the United Kingdom, having been introduced at different times in its constituent countries: England and Wales in 2014, Scotland in 2014, and Northern Ireland in 2020. Before marriage, same-sex couples could enter civil partnerships, introduced UK-wide in 2005, which granted most of the same rights and responsibilities.

England, Wales and Scotland

The Marriage (Same Sex Couples) Act 2013, passed by the UK Parliament in July 2013, legalized same-sex marriage in England and Wales; the first ceremonies took place on 29 March 2014. The legislation allowed religious organizations to opt in to solemnizing such marriages, while protecting the Church of England and the Church in Wales from being compelled to do so. In Scotland, the Scottish Parliament passed the Marriage and Civil Partnership (Scotland) Act in February 2014, and the first marriages took place in December 2014, with the Church of Scotland and Scottish Episcopal Church later permitting clergy to conduct them.

Northern Ireland

Northern Ireland was the last part of the UK where same-sex marriage remained unavailable, after the devolved Assembly's votes on the issue were blocked by a petition of concern. In July 2019 the UK Parliament legislated to legalize same-sex marriage in Northern Ireland if the Assembly was not restored by 21 October 2019; the change took effect on 13 January 2020, and the first marriage was celebrated in February 2020.
