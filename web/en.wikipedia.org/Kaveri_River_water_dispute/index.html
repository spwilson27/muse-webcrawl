Kaveri River water dispute

The Kaveri River water dispute is a long-running conflict over the sharing of the waters of the Kaveri (Cauvery) River in southern India, principally between the states of Tamil Nadu and Karnataka, with Kerala and the union territory of Puducherry also parties. The river rises in Karnataka and flows through Tamil Nadu to the Bay of Bengal, supporting intensive irrigation in both states.

Origins

The dispute dates to agreements of 1892 and 1924 between the Madras Presidency and the princely state of Mysore, which governed dam construction and irrigation for fifty years. After Indian independence and the reorganisation of states in 1956, the 1924 agreement's lapse in 1974 set off decades of negotiation, litigation, and periodic unrest whenever monsoon failures forced Karnataka to restrict releases from its reservoirs, notably at Krishnaraja Sagar, above the Mettur Dam in Tamil Nadu.

Tribunal and court decisions

The Government of India constituted the Cauvery Water Disputes Tribunal in 1990 under the Inter-State River Water Disputes Act. The tribunal's final award, notified in 2013, allocated shares of the river's assessed yield among the four parties, awarding the largest share to Tamil Nadu and Karnataka. In February 2018 the Supreme Court of India modified the award, slightly increasing Karnataka's share (to supply drinking water to Bengaluru) and reducing Tamil Nadu's, and directed the creation of the Cauvery Water Management Authority to implement the scheme. Disputes over monthly releases in deficit years, and over Karnataka's proposed Mekedatu dam project, continue.
