The Dniester (Romanian: Nistru; Ukrainian: Дністер, Dnister) is a transboundary river in Eastern Europe. It runs first through Ukraine and then through Moldova (from which it more or less forms the border with the breakaway region of Transnistria), finally flowing into the Black Sea on Ukrainian territory again. At approximately 1,362 km it is one of the major rivers of Europe.

Course

The Dniester rises in the Ukrainian Carpathians near the village of Vovche, close to the Polish border, and flows southeast through western Ukraine (past Lviv Oblast and the historic city of Halych), then forms a winding canyon-like valley. In Moldova it passes near Bender (Tighina) and Tiraspol. It ends in the shallow Dniester Estuary (liman) southwest of Odesa.

History

Known to the ancient Greeks as the Tyras, the river was a frontier for much of its history — between Dacia and Sarmatia, later between empires including the Ottoman Empire, the Russian Empire and Romania, and during the Second World War as a defensive line. The river's name derives from a Scythian/Sarmatian word for "near river", paired with the Dnieper, the "far river".

Use and ecology

The Dubăsari and Novodnistrovsk hydroelectric stations generate power and regulate flow. The lower Dniester wetlands are internationally important for birds (a Ramsar site). Disputes over water management and pollution involve Ukraine, Moldova and Transnistria; treaties on the Dniester basin were signed in 2012, though implementation has been partial.
