Last Judgment

The Last Judgment, also called the Final Judgment, Day of Judgment, or Judgment Day, is an eschatological concept found in the Abrahamic religions. It is the judgment of all humanity (and in some traditions all creation) at the end of the world, when the dead are resurrected and every person is judged according to their deeds, receiving eternal reward or punishment.

In Christianity

In Christian eschatology, the Last Judgment follows the Second Coming of Christ and the resurrection of the dead. The New Testament describes the Son of Man seated on a throne of glory, separating the nations "as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats" (Matthew 25). Medieval theology distinguished the particular judgment of each soul at death from the general judgment at the end of time. The doctrine is affirmed in the Nicene Creed: Christ "will come again in glory to judge the living and the dead."

In Islam

In Islam, Yawm al-Qiyāmah (the Day of Resurrection) and Yawm ad-Dīn (the Day of Judgment) are central beliefs. After the signs of the Hour, the dead are raised, deeds are weighed in the balance (mīzān), and each person crosses the bridge of Siraṭ toward paradise or falls to hellfire. Belief in the Last Day is one of the six articles of faith in Sunni Islam.

In Judaism and other traditions

Jewish tradition speaks of a future day of judgment associated with the coming of the Messiah and the resurrection of the dead, while Zoroastrianism teaches a final judgment (Frashokereti) far earlier in history, in which a river of molten metal tests the righteous and the wicked — a likely influence on later Abrahamic eschatology.

In art

The Last Judgment is one of the most common subjects of Christian art, from Romanesque tympana to Michelangelo's vast fresco on the altar wall of the Sistine Chapel (1536–1541), which shows Christ as judge, the blessed ascending at his right hand and the damned dragged down at his left.
