Operation Galvanic

Operation Galvanic was the code name for the United States landings on Makin (Butaritari) and Tarawa in the Gilbert Islands during the Pacific War in November 1943, part of Operation Galvanic and the larger Central Pacific drive toward Japan.

The operation, beginning 20 November 1943, paired the U.S. Army's 27th Infantry Division on Makin with the 2nd Marine Division on Betio, Tarawa Atoll. The Battle of Tarawa became notorious for its ferocity: nearly 1,000 Marines were killed and over 2,000 wounded in 76 hours, against almost the entire Japanese garrison of about 4,700, while misjudged tides left landing craft stranded on reefs under fire.

Significance

Galvanic provided airfields to support the subsequent Marshall Islands campaign and taught hard lessons on amphibious assault, naval gunfire preparation, reef reconnaissance, and amphibious tractors that shaped later landings at Kwajalein, Saipan and beyond. Graphic newsreel and photographs shocked the American public.
