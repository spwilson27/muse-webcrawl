Council housing
CountryUnited Kingdom
ProvidersLocal authorities (councils)
Key legislationHousing Act 1919; Housing Act 1980 (Right to Buy)

Council housing is a form of social housing in the United Kingdom, built and managed by local authorities (councils) and let to tenants at below-market rents. A dwelling provided this way is commonly called a council house, and large developments are known as council estates.

History

Although councils built some housing in the nineteenth century, large-scale construction began after the First World War with the Housing Act 1919 — the "Homes Fit for Heroes" act — which subsidised council building. Construction peaked after the Second World War, when council housing formed a central part of reconstruction and slum clearance, and millions of homes were built.

Right to Buy

The Housing Act 1980 introduced the Right to Buy, allowing sitting tenants to purchase their homes at substantial discounts. More than two million council homes were sold, and the stock was not replaced at the same rate, sharply reducing the sector's size.

Today

Councils remain significant landlords, though much stock has transferred to housing associations. Demand for social housing far exceeds supply in most areas, with long waiting lists a persistent feature of the system.
