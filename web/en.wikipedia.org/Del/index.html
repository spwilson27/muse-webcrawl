Del, or nabla, is a vector differential operator, denoted by the inverted triangle symbol ∇. Applied to scalar and vector fields, del generates three fundamental operations of vector calculus: the gradient (del followed by a scalar field), the divergence (del dot a vector field), and the curl (del cross a vector field).

Definition

In three-dimensional Cartesian coordinates, del is written
∇ = i ∂/∂x + j ∂/∂y + k ∂/∂z,
where i, j, k are unit vectors. It behaves in many algebraic manipulations like a vector, although it is an operator and does not commute with the fields it acts on.

Uses

  • Gradient: ∇f — points in the direction of greatest increase of the scalar field f.
  • Divergence: ∇ · F — measures the net outflow of the vector field F from a point.
  • Curl: ∇ × F — measures the local rotation of F.
  • Laplacian: ∇2 = ∇ · ∇ — central to potential theory, heat conduction, and wave equations.
  • Directional derivative: (u · ∇)f along a unit vector u.

Coordinate systems

In cylindrical, spherical, and other curvilinear coordinates, del acquires extra terms because the basis vectors vary with position. Formulae for the gradient, divergence, curl, and Laplacian in these systems are standard tools in electromagnetism and fluid dynamics.

History and notation

The symbol ∇ was introduced by William Rowan Hamilton, who called it "nabla" after an ancient harp of similar shape; the operator formalism was developed into modern vector calculus by Josiah Willard Gibbs and Oliver Heaviside in the 1880s. The name "del" is used particularly in American usage.
