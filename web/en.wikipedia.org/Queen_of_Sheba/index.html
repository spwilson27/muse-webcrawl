Queen of Sheba

The Queen of Sheba (Hebrew: מַלְכַּת־שְׁבָא) is a figure first mentioned in the Hebrew Bible, in which she visits King Solomon in Jerusalem bearing great gifts of spices, gold and precious stones, having heard of his fame and wisdom. In the biblical account (1 Kings 10 and 2 Chronicles 9), she tests Solomon with hard questions, is overwhelmed by his wisdom and the glory of his court, and exchanges gifts with him before returning to her own land.

Traditions

The queen is unnamed in the Bible. In Ethiopian tradition she is called Makeda, and the national epic Kebra Nagast recounts that she bore Solomon a son, Menelik I, who became the first emperor of Ethiopia and, in the legend, carried the Ark of the Covenant to Axum. The Solomonic dynasty of Ethiopia claimed descent from this union. In Islamic tradition she is Bilqis, queen of Saba in Yemen, and the Quran (Surah An-Naml) tells of Solomon's hoopoe bringing news of her kingdom and of her eventual submission to God. Jewish tradition, in works such as the Targum Sheni, elaborates her riddles and her visit.

Historical questions

Sheba is generally identified with the South Arabian kingdom of Saba, centred in modern Yemen, wealthy from the incense trade; some scholars have instead linked the queen's realm with the Horn of Africa. No contemporary inscription names such a queen, and historians debate whether the biblical story preserves a memory of trade contacts between Israel and South Arabia or is a purely literary construction.
