Gloire ("Glory") was a French Navy armoured frigate and the world's first oceangoing ironclad warship. Launched at Toulon in 1859, she was designed by the naval architect Henri Dupuy de Lôme during the reign of Napoleon III, as France sought a technological edge over the wooden fleets of its rivals.

Design

Unlike the floating batteries used in the Crimean War, Gloire was a true seagoing warship. Her wooden hull was sheathed along the waterline and upper works with wrought-iron armour plates 120 mm (4.7 inches) thick, proof against the explosive shell guns of the day. She carried a single-cylinder steam engine driving one propeller, supplemented by a full sailing rig, and mounted 36 rifled muzzle-loading guns on a single gun deck.

Impact

Gloire rendered every existing wooden ship of the line obsolete at a stroke and triggered an ironclad arms race. Britain, unwilling to rely on timber hulls, replied within a year with HMS Warrior, built with an iron hull and even more powerful machinery. Two sister ships, Invincible and Normandie, followed Gloire into service.

Rapid technological change overtook the design; iron hulls, breech-loading guns and turrets soon superseded her. Gloire was struck from the navy list in 1879 and scrapped in 1883.
