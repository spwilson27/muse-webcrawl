Prilep

Prilep (Macedonian: Прилеп [ˈpriːlɛp]) is the third-largest city in North Macedonia, situated in the northern part of the Pelagonia plain. Known as the "city under Marko's Towers" after the medieval Serbian prince Marko who ruled from the fortress above the town, it is also nicknamed "tobacco city" for its long-standing role in tobacco production.

History

The site was settled in antiquity near the ancient town of Styberra. In the Middle Ages Prilep passed between Byzantine, Bulgarian, and Serbian rule; Prince Marko (King Marko) made it his capital in the late 14th century, and after his death at the Battle of Rovine in 1395 the city came under Ottoman rule, which lasted until the Balkan Wars of 1912–1913. Prilep is also remembered as the birthplace of the Macedonian revolutionary movement figures and suffered during both World Wars.

Economy and culture

Prilep's economy centres on tobacco—grown in the surrounding region and processed in the city, home to a tobacco research institute and the Tutunski Kombinat combine—as well as marble quarrying (the white Sivec marble used in famous buildings) and food processing. Each year the city hosts a beer festival, Pivofest, and the Monastery of Treskavec, reached by mountain paths above the city, is a major pilgrimage site.
