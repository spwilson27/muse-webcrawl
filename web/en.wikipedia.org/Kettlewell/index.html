Kettlewell village in Upper Wharfedale, Yorkshire Dales.

Kettlewell is a village and civil parish in Upper Wharfedale in the Yorkshire Dales National Park, North Yorkshire, England. It lies beside the River Wharfe, upstream of Grassington and downstream of Buckden, and is a popular base for walkers exploring the dales.

History

The village's name derives from Old Norse, roughly "Kettil's spring," reflecting the Norse settlement of northern England. Kettlewell appears in the Domesday Book of 1086. In the 17th and 18th centuries the village grew with the local lead-mining industry, and its market cross and stone cottages date largely from these periods. St Mary's Church stands on a site used since Anglo-Saxon times; the present church is Victorian.

Present day

Kettlewell is known for its annual Scarecrow Festival, founded in the 1990s, which draws thousands of visitors each August. The village has pubs, tea rooms, a youth hostel, and a campsite, and hosts the finish of fell races over nearby Great Whernside, which rises to 704 metres east of the village.
