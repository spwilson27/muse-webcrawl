Dogen

Born19 January 1200, Kyoto, Japan
Died22 September 1253 (aged 53), Kyoto, Japan
SchoolSōtō Zen Buddhism
Notable workShōbōgenzō

Dogen (Japanese: 道元), also known as Dōgen Zenji and Eihei Dōgen (1200–1253), was a Japanese Buddhist priest, writer and philosopher who founded the Sōtō school of Zen in Japan. He is regarded as one of the most important Japanese philosophers, noted for his masterwork the Shōbōgenzō ("Treasury of the True Dharma Eye") and for promoting seated meditation, zazen — specifically shikantaza, "just sitting" — as the core of practice.

Life

Born in Kyoto, possibly into an aristocratic family, Dōgen was orphaned young and ordained as a Tendai monk on Mount Hiei at 13. Dissatisfied with the doctrine that enlightenment is innate yet practice unnecessary, he sought instruction from Myōzen and in 1223 travelled to China, where he studied under the Caodong master Rujing at Tiantong Temple and attained awakening. Returning to Japan in 1227, he taught first near Kyoto (founding Kōshō-ji) and in 1243 moved to remote Echizen Province, where he established Eihei-ji, still a head temple of Sōtō Zen.

Teachings

Dōgen taught the oneness of practice and enlightenment (shushō ittō): zazen is not a means to Buddhahood but its expression. The Shōbōgenzō essays explore time and existence ("Uji", "being-time"), Buddha-nature in all beings including mountains and rivers, and language's capacity to express truth. His Eihei Shingi set out monastic regulations, and the Tenzo Kyōkun instructs the temple cook.

Legacy

Largely studied within Sōtō monasteries for centuries, Dōgen's writings gained broad attention in 20th-century Japan and the West, influencing philosophy, literature and the global spread of Zen.
