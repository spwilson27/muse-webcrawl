Counter-Strike (also known as Half-Life: Counter-Strike or Counter-Strike 1.6) is a tactical first-person shooter video game developed by Valve. It was initially developed and released in 1999 as a modification to Half-Life by Minh Le and Jess Cliffe, before Valve acquired the rights and the developers. The game was released for Microsoft Windows in November 2000 and became one of the most popular online shooters of its era.

Gameplay

Players join either the terrorist or counter-terrorist team and compete across round-based objective scenarios, principally bomb planting and defusal, hostage rescue, and assassination. Players earn money for completing objectives and eliminating opponents, which can be spent on weapons and equipment at the start of each round. Dead players wait until the next round. Iconic maps include Dust II, Inferno, Nuke, and Aztec.

Development

Le and Cliffe released the first beta of the mod in June 1999. Its rapid popularity drew Valve's attention; the company hired the pair and acquired the intellectual property, releasing the game commercially as version 1.0 in 2000. Subsequent updates culminated in version 1.6, bundled with Valve's Steam platform in 2003.

Legacy

Counter-Strike spawned a series including Counter-Strike: Condition Zero, Counter-Strike: Source (2004), Counter-Strike: Global Offensive (2012), and Counter-Strike 2 (2023). It is regarded as a foundational esports title and one of the most influential multiplayer shooters ever made.
