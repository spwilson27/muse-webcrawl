The United States Department of Commerce is an executive department of the US federal government concerned with creating the conditions for economic growth and opportunity. Its tasks include promoting American business and trade, gathering economic and demographic data for business and government decision-making, issuing patents and trademarks, and helping to set industrial standards. The department is headed by the Secretary of Commerce, a member of the president's Cabinet.

History

The department was created on February 14, 1903, as the United States Department of Commerce and Labor. On March 4, 1913, it was split into separate departments of Commerce and Labor. Herbert Hoover, Secretary of Commerce from 1921 to 1928, greatly expanded the department's role, and its headquarters building in the Federal Triangle is named for him. Over time, agencies have been added and transferred out; the Patent and Trademark Office, the Census Bureau, and the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) are among its best-known components.

Organisation

Bureaus and offices of the department include:

  • Bureau of the Census — conducts the decennial census and other statistics
  • Bureau of Economic Analysis — produces GDP and national accounts
  • National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA) — including the National Weather Service and National Marine Fisheries Service
  • United States Patent and Trademark Office
  • International Trade Administration and Bureau of Industry and Security
  • National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)
  • National Telecommunications and Information Administration (NTIA)
  • Minority Business Development Agency
