Constitution Act, 1982

Enacted17 April 1982 (proclaimed)
Enacted byParliament of the United Kingdom (Canada Act 1982), at Canada's request
Key contentsCanadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms; amending formula; Aboriginal rights (s. 35)

The Constitution Act, 1982 (French: Loi constitutionnelle de 1982) is a part of the Constitution of Canada, enacted as Schedule B to the Canada Act 1982 passed by the Parliament of the United Kingdom. It patriated the Canadian constitution — ending the role of the British Parliament in amending it — and introduced the Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms and a domestic amending formula.

Contents

Canadian Charter of Rights and Freedoms

Part I of the Act is the Charter, guaranteeing fundamental freedoms, democratic, mobility, legal and equality rights, and official-language and minority-language education rights. Section 1 permits reasonable limits; section 33, the notwithstanding clause, allows Parliament or a provincial legislature to declare that a law operates notwithstanding certain Charter rights, renewable every five years.

Aboriginal rights

Section 35 recognises and affirms "the existing aboriginal and treaty rights of the aboriginal peoples of Canada" — the Indian, Inuit and Métis peoples — deliberately left to be defined by negotiation and litigation.

Amending formula

Part V established general procedures for amendment, ordinarily requiring the Senate, the House of Commons and at least two-thirds of the provinces representing at least 50% of the population (the "7/50 formula"), with unanimity for certain matters such as the monarchy and some language rights.

Other provisions

The Act also includes an equalisation commitment (s. 36), declares the Constitution the supreme law of Canada (s. 52), and renamed the British North America Act, 1867 as the Constitution Act, 1867.

History

The Act crowned decades of failed attempts to agree a domestic amending formula. Prime Minister Pierre Trudeau's government negotiated a package in November 1981 with nine provinces; Quebec's government under René Lévesque did not assent, a grievance that fed later failed accords (Meech Lake and Charlottetown). Queen Elizabeth II proclaimed the Act in Ottawa on 17 April 1982.
