Mount Meru is a dormant stratovolcano in Arusha Region, Tanzania, about 70 km west of Mount Kilimanjaro. At 4,562 metres it is Tanzania's second-highest mountain and one of Africa's highest peaks. It lies within Arusha National Park.

Geology

Meru began forming about a million years ago. A major eruption roughly 7,800 years ago blew out its eastern wall, creating the horseshoe-shaped caldera seen today, and a subsidiary ash cone grew inside the crater. Minor activity was last recorded in 1910.

Climbing and wildlife

The mountain is a popular acclimatization climb for Kilimanjaro and a rewarding trek in its own right, passing through forest rich in wildlife — giraffe, buffalo, colobus monkeys — on the Momella route, with Socialist Peak as the summit. In Hindu, Jain and Buddhist cosmology, the sacred Mount Meru is a distinct mythological mountain sharing the name.
