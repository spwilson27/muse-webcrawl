Ska is a music genre that originated in Jamaica in the late 1950s and was the precursor to rocksteady and reggae. It combined elements of Caribbean mento and calypso with American jazz and rhythm and blues, and is characterised by a walking bass line, rhythms on the offbeat, and prominent horn sections.

Characteristics

Ska's signature sound is the skank — guitar or piano chords struck on the offbeats of the bar — over a propulsive walking bass. Early recordings were made for Jamaica's sound systems, portable discos that were the centre of the island's dance culture. The Skatalites, formed in 1964, became the defining session band of the era.

Waves

Music historians commonly divide ska into three waves. The first wave is the original Jamaican music of the 1960s, by artists such as Prince Buster, Desmond Dekker and the Wailers. The second wave, or 2 Tone movement, began in late-1970s England, where bands including the Specials, Madness and the Selecter fused ska with punk energy and multiracial line-ups. The third wave emerged in the 1980s and 1990s, especially in the United States, where ska punk bands such as the Mighty Mighty Bosstones and No Doubt reached mainstream success.

Culture

Ska is associated with the rude boy subculture in Jamaica and with mod and skinhead scenes in Britain. The black-and-white checkerboard pattern of the 2 Tone label became its most recognisable visual symbol.
