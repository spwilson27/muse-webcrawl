The Administrator of the Government of Canada is the official who exercises the powers of the Governor General of Canada when that office is vacant, or when the Governor General is absent or unable to act. The role arises under the Letters Patent, 1947, which authorize the appointment of an administrator by commission under the Great Seal of Canada.

Office holders

By convention the Chief Justice of Canada serves as Administrator when the need arises. Notable examples include Chief Justice Lyman Duff, who acted after the death of Lord Tweedsmuir in 1940; Chief Justice Robert Taschereau; and Chief Justice Richard Wagner, who served as Administrator for several months in 2021 between the resignation of Julie Payette and the appointment of Mary Simon — one of the longest such periods in Canadian history.

Powers and limits

An Administrator may perform the ordinary functions of the Governor General, including granting royal assent, summoning and dissolving Parliament on ministerial advice, and appointing officials. The Administrator acts in the name of the Crown but is a temporary substitute; the position carries no additional title after the commission ends.
