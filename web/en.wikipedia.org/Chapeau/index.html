Chapeau

Chapeau is the French word for "hat", and in English usage it has acquired several specific technical meanings:

In heraldry

In heraldry, the chapeau (also called a cap of maintenance) is a cap of crimson velvet lined with ermine, worn by peers and forming part of certain coats of arms. A chapeau is borne as a crest by some families, and in Scottish heraldry it denotes feudal baronial status. The cap of maintenance is also one of the symbols of state that may precede the sovereign at the State Opening of Parliament in the United Kingdom, carried alongside the Sword of State.

In parliamentary and diplomatic usage

Chapeau is the term for the introductory text of a document, resolution or treaty article that sets the context for the operative clauses that follow — used in United Nations resolutions, WTO agreements and legislative drafting. In archaeology, a chapeau de fer ("iron hat") is a medieval helmet type, a wide-brimmed war hat of iron or steel.

Other uses

"Chapeau!" is used in French — and in cycling culture internationally — as an exclamation of respect, literally "hats off", equivalent to tipping one's hat to an impressive achievement. The word also appears in the names of places, such as the municipality of Chapeau in Quebec, Canada.
