Salty liquorice

Salty liquorice, or salmiak liquorice, is a variety of liquorice flavoured with ammonium chloride (salmiak), giving it a sharp, salty, and somewhat astringent taste. It is especially popular in the Nordic countries, the Netherlands, and northern Germany, and comes in forms ranging from mildly salted to extremely strong. To palates unaccustomed to it, the flavour is famously polarizing.

Description

Despite the name, salty liquorice typically contains little or no table salt; its characteristic taste comes from ammonium chloride (NH4Cl), historically called sal ammoniac. Products range from black hard candies and soft chews to salted coatings and powders, with the saltiness graded from mild to "extra strong". Finnish salmiakki, Dutch drop (especially zoute drop), Swedish saltlakrits, and Icelandic saltlakkrís are the best-known forms.

History

Ammonium chloride was long used in apothecary preparations — as a cough remedy and expectorant — and liquorice pastilles with salmiak were sold by pharmacies in Finland and elsewhere from the early twentieth century. Commercial confectionery followed: Finnish salmiakki production is commonly dated to the 1930s, when pharmacist Yrjö Kallio and the Hottinger company developed salty liquorice products that became a national staple.

Regional varieties

Finland is the heartland of salmiakki, which flavours ice cream, chocolate, sauces, and even a strong koskenkorva-based liqueur (Salmiakki Koskenkorva). The Netherlands consumes more liquorice per capita than any other country, with salty varieties a large share. Swedish brands popularized "super salty" candies, while Iceland favours strong salty liquorice combined with chocolate. German Salmiakpastillen are a milder apothecary tradition.

Health considerations

Like other liquorice, salty liquorice contains glycyrrhizin, whose excessive consumption can cause hypertension and hypokalemia; high ammonium chloride intake can also be harmful, and strong products sometimes carry advisory labels in their home markets. Moderate consumption is generally considered safe.
