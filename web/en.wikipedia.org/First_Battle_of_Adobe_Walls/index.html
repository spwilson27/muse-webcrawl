First Battle of Adobe Walls

The First Battle of Adobe Walls was fought on November 25, 1864, in the vicinity of the ruins of William Bent's abandoned adobe trading post and fort, Adobe Walls, in what is now Hutchinson County, Texas. It was the largest battle of the American Civil War era fought on the Great Plains, pitting a United States expedition under Colonel Kit Carson against a large force of Kiowa, Comanche and Plains Apache warriors.

Background

With the Civil War drawing Union troops east, Plains tribes had increased raids on the Santa Fe Trail and settlements in New Mexico and Texas. General James Carleton ordered Carson to lead about 400 soldiers of the 1st California and New Mexico cavalry and infantry regiments, with two mountain howitzers and some Ute and Jicarilla Apache scouts, against winter encampments along the Canadian River.

The battle

Carson's troops attacked a Kiowa village of some 150 lodges at dawn, capturing and burning it, but then faced counterattacks from an estimated 1,000 to 3,000 warriors from nearby, much larger Comanche and Kiowa encampments. Using his howitzers to keep the attackers at bay, Carson conducted a fighting retreat back to the Adobe Walls ruins and then to his supply wagons, and withdrew during the night. The expedition suffered only a handful of fatalities, while Native losses were estimated in the dozens. Strategically, the Army had shown it could strike deep into the southern plains in winter, a foretaste of the campaigns of the following decade.

A second, more famous battle at the same site was fought in 1874, when a small party of buffalo hunters held off a large attacking force.
