Health technology assessment

Health technology assessment (HTA) is the systematic evaluation of the properties and effects of a health technology, addressing the direct and intended effects of the technology as well as its indirect and unintended consequences, and aimed mainly at informing decision-making about the adoption and use of health technologies. "Health technology" is interpreted broadly to include medicines, medical devices, vaccines, procedures, and systems of care.

Scope

An HTA typically examines a technology's safety, clinical effectiveness, and cost-effectiveness, and may also consider ethical, legal, social, and organisational implications. Economic evaluation commonly compares costs and outcomes against existing alternatives using measures such as the incremental cost-effectiveness ratio and cost per quality-adjusted life year (QALY).

Organisations

HTA is carried out by national and regional agencies in many countries, including the National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE) in England, the Canadian Agency for Drugs and Technologies in Health (CADTH), the Pharmaceutical Benefits Advisory Committee in Australia, and Germany's IQWiG. Their appraisals inform reimbursement and coverage decisions — for example, whether a new drug will be publicly funded. International collaboration occurs through networks such as EUnetHTA and the professional society HTAi.

History and issues

The term arose in the 1970s as medical technology proliferated and costs rose, with the U.S. Office of Technology Assessment an early institutional home. Debate continues over the thresholds used for cost-effectiveness, the treatment of rare diseases and end-of-life care, and how patient and public values should enter appraisals.
