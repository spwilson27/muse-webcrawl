Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca (Turkish: Küçük Kaynarca Antlaşması) was signed on 21 July 1774 in Küçük Kaynarca (today Kaynardzha, Bulgaria) between the Russian Empire and the Ottoman Empire, concluding the Russo-Turkish War of 1768–1774. It was one of the most consequential treaties in Ottoman history, marking the first major cession of territory by the empire to a Christian power.

Terms

The Ottomans ceded the fortresses of Kerch and Yenikale in Crimea and territory between the Dnieper and Southern Bug rivers, giving Russia direct access to the Black Sea. The Crimean Khanate was declared independent of the Porte (Russia annexed it in 1783). Russian merchant ships gained free passage through the Bosphorus and Dardanelles, and Russia received an indemnity of 4.5 million roubles. Ambiguous clauses on the protection of Orthodox Christians in the empire later served Russia as a pretext for intervention in Ottoman affairs.

Significance

The treaty shifted the Black Sea balance decisively toward Russia and opened the "Eastern Question" that would preoccupy European diplomacy until the First World War.
