Ergative–absolutive alignment

Ergative–absolutive alignment
CasesErgative, absolutive
Example languagesBasque, Georgian, many Australian and Mayan languages
ContrastNominative–accusative alignment

In linguistic typology, ergative–absolutive alignment is a type of morphosyntactic alignment in which the single argument of an intransitive verb behaves the same as the object of a transitive verb, and differently from the subject of a transitive verb.

How it works

The shared case is called the absolutive, and the special case used only for transitive subjects is the ergative. In English-like (nominative–accusative) languages, by contrast, the intransitive subject groups with the transitive subject.

Distribution

Ergativity appears in Basque, Georgian and other Caucasian languages, Kurdish varieties, many Australian Aboriginal languages, Mayan languages, and Eskimo–Aleut languages, among others. Some languages show split ergativity, using ergative marking only in certain tenses, aspects, or classes of nouns.
