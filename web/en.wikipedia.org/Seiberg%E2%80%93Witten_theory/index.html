Seiberg–Witten theory

Seiberg–Witten theory is a body of results in theoretical physics and mathematics arising from the work of Nathan Seiberg and Edward Witten in 1994 on the exact low-energy solution of N = 2 supersymmetric Yang–Mills theory. By identifying the correct effective description of the theory in terms of magnetic monopoles, they gave the first controlled demonstration of monopole condensation as a mechanism of quark confinement.

The Seiberg–Witten solution

The exact prepotential of the low-energy theory is determined by an elliptic curve, the Seiberg–Witten curve, whose periods give the effective couplings. Singularities in the moduli space correspond to points where monopoles or dyons become massless.

Seiberg–Witten invariants

Witten immediately applied the same ideas to topology, defining the Seiberg–Witten invariants of smooth four-manifolds. These invariants, computed from solutions of the Seiberg–Witten (monopole) equations, are far simpler to work with than Donaldson invariants and provided powerful new tools for studying exotic smooth structures in dimension four.
