Sandworm is a Russian cyber-espionage and sabotage group attributed by the United States, United Kingdom and other governments to Unit 74455 of the Russian military intelligence agency, the GRU. It is considered among the most destructive state-sponsored hacking groups in operation.

Operations

The group first attracted wide attention in 2014, when researchers disclosed its use of a Windows zero-day vulnerability (CVE-2014-4114) against Ukrainian and NATO targets. In December 2015 it carried out the first publicly acknowledged cyberattack to cause a power outage, cutting electricity to some 230,000 customers in Ukraine's Ivano-Frankivsk region using the BlackEnergy malware. A second grid attack in Kyiv in December 2016 used the modular Industroyer (CrashOverride) framework.

In June 2017 Sandworm released NotPetya, a wiper disguised as ransomware that spread from compromised Ukrainian accounting software and caused an estimated $10 billion in damage worldwide, hitting firms such as Maersk, Merck and FedEx. It also attacked the 2018 Winter Olympics opening ceremony (the Olympic Destroyer malware), attempted to disrupt the 2020 French and Ukrainian targets, and deployed successive wiper families against Ukraine following the 2022 invasion.

Indictments and name

In October 2020 the U.S. Department of Justice indicted six alleged Unit 74455 officers for the group's campaigns. The name "Sandworm" was coined by iSIGHT Partners in 2014 from references to the sandworms of Frank Herbert's novel Dune found in the group's code; Microsoft tracks the actor as Seashell Blizzard.
