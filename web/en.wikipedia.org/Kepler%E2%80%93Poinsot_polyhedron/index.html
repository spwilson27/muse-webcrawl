Kepler–Poinsot polyhedron

The small stellated dodecahedron, one of the four Kepler–Poinsot polyhedra.

In geometry, a Kepler–Poinsot polyhedron is any of four regular star polyhedra: the small stellated dodecahedron, the great dodecahedron, the great stellated dodecahedron, and the great icosahedron. They are the regular non-convex polyhedra, having pentagonal or pentagrammic faces and self-intersecting structure.

History

Johannes Kepler described the two stellated dodecahedra in his 1619 work Harmonices Mundi. In 1809 Louis Poinsot rediscovered Kepler's figures and described the two remaining polyhedra, the great dodecahedron and the great icosahedron. Augustin-Louis Cauchy later proved that the four solids complete the list of regular star polyhedra.

Characteristics

The Kepler–Poinsot polyhedra are dual to one another in pairs: the small stellated dodecahedron is dual to the great dodecahedron, and the great stellated dodecahedron is dual to the great icosahedron. Because their faces intersect, their Euler characteristic differs from that of convex polyhedra, and the density of the solids — how many times a ray from the centre crosses faces — is a key invariant in their modern treatment.
