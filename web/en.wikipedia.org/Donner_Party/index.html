Donner Party

Donner Party
Date: 1846–1847
Route: California Trail (Hastings Cutoff)
Members: 87 emigrants; 48 survived
Location: Sierra Nevada, California

The Donner Party was a group of American pioneers who migrated to California in a wagon train from the Midwest. Delayed by a series of mishaps, most notably taking the untested Hastings Cutoff, they spent the winter of 1846–1847 snowbound in the Sierra Nevada. Of the 87 members, 48 survived; some resorted to cannibalism to survive, an aspect that has fixed the episode in American memory.

Journey

The party departed Springfield, Illinois, in April 1846 under the leadership of George Donner and James F. Reed. Following Lansford Hastings's advertised shortcut through the Great Salt Lake Desert cost them weeks and supplies. Heavy early snows blocked the passes near Truckee Lake (now Donner Lake) in late October, trapping the emigrants in makeshift cabins.

Rescue and aftermath

A small group known as the Forlorn Hope snowshoed out to seek help; four relief parties reached the camps between February and April 1847. Donner Memorial State Park in California commemorates the emigrants.
