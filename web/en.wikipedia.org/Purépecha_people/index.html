Purépecha people

The Purépecha (historically called Tarascans by the Spanish) are an indigenous people of the Mexican state of Michoacán. Numbering in the hundreds of thousands, they inhabit the highlands around Lake Pátzcuaro and the Meseta Purépecha, and they speak the Purépecha language, a language isolate.

History

Before the Spanish conquest, the Purépecha formed a powerful, centralised state — the second-largest in Mesoamerica — with its capital at Tzintzuntzan. Famed for metallurgy in copper, bronze, and gold, they successfully defended their frontiers against the Aztec Empire. After 1530 the region came under Spanish rule; the bishop Vasco de Quiroga founded hospital-towns and craft communities that shaped Purépecha colonial society.

Culture

Purépecha communities are renowned for traditional crafts: copper work from Santa Clara del Cobre, pottery from Tzintzuntzan and Capula, embroidered textiles, and guitars from Paracho. Major celebrations include the Night of the Dead around Lake Pátzcuaro and the Dance of the Old Men (Danza de los Viejitos). In recent decades, Purépecha communities such as Cherán have become known for movements defending communal forests and indigenous self-government.
