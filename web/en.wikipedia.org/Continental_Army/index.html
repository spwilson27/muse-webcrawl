Continental Army

The Continental Army under George Washington won American independence.

The Continental Army was the army of the United Colonies, later the United States, formed on 14 June 1775 by resolution of the Second Continental Congress, in the wake of the outbreak of the American Revolutionary War. The Congress appointed George Washington as commander-in-chief. The Army was intended to coordinate the military efforts of the Thirteen Colonies in their revolt against the rule of Great Britain, and it was supplemented by local militias and volunteer troops.

History

After early actions around Boston and the disastrous 1776 New York campaign, Washington's victories at Trenton and Princeton revived the cause. The Army endured the winter at Valley Forge (1777–78), where Baron von Steuben instilled professional drill and discipline. French alliance from 1778 brought money, troops and naval power, and the Army, with French support, won the decisive siege of Yorktown in 1781.

Disbandment and legacy

Most of the Continental Army was disbanded in 1783 after the Treaty of Paris formally ended the war; remaining regiments formed the basis of the United States Army, which traces its birthday to 14 June 1775. On 3 June 1784 Congress resolved to maintain a small standing force, creating the First American Regiment.
