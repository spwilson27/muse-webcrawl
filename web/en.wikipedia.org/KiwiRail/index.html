KiwiRail Holdings Limited, trading as KiwiRail, is a New Zealand state-owned enterprise responsible for the country's national rail network. It operates freight trains, long-distance scenic passenger services, and the Interislander ferry services across Cook Strait. KiwiRail also owns and maintains the track infrastructure, having been established after the government repurchased the rail network and operations in 2008.

History

New Zealand's railways were privatised in 1993, passing through the hands of Tranz Rail and later Toll Holdings. After years of concern over deferred maintenance and the state of the network, the government bought back the track infrastructure in 2004, and in 2008 purchased Toll's rail and ferry operations for NZ$665 million. The new state operator was launched as KiwiRail on 1 October 2008. Urban passenger services in Auckland and Wellington were subsequently transferred to metropolitan operators, leaving KiwiRail focused on freight, long-distance passenger trains, and ferries.

Operations

Freight

Freight is KiwiRail's largest business, moving bulk commodities such as coal, logs, dairy products, steel, and containerised import/export freight between ports and inland terminals. The network uses the 3 ft 6 in (1,067 mm) Cape gauge.

Passenger

KiwiRail operates scenic long-distance trains marketed as "Great Journeys New Zealand": the Northern Explorer (Auckland–Wellington), the TranzAlpine (Christchurch–Greymouth, crossing the Southern Alps via Arthur's Pass), and the Coastal Pacific (Picton–Christchurch).

Interislander

The Interislander ferry service carries passengers, cars, and rail freight wagons across Cook Strait between Wellington and Picton, with rail-connected roll-on/roll-off ferries forming a vital link in the national freight chain.

Network

The KiwiRail network comprises roughly 3,700 kilometres of line, including the North Island Main Trunk, the Midland Line, and numerous branch and industrial lines, along with tunnels such as the Otira and Rimutaka tunnels.
