Norisring

The Norisring is a street circuit in Nuremberg, Germany. Racing has taken place around the Steintribüne, the massive stone grandstand built on the former Nazi party rally grounds, since 1947. The name is derived from Noris, an old poetic name for Nuremberg, chosen to avoid confusion with the Nürburgring.

Layout

The basic layout, used in near-unchanged form for decades, is a simple, fast loop around the Zeppelinfeld area: a long start-finish straight past the stone grandstand, the Grundig hairpin (Grundigkehre), fast esses behind the lake Dutzendteich and the Schöller-S corner returning to the straight. At only 2.3 km it is the shortest circuit on the DTM calendar, and despite its simplicity it is known for heavy braking zones that promote overtaking.

History

Motorcycle racing began at the site in 1947, followed by car racing. From 1984 the circuit became a fixture of the Deutsche Tourenwagen Meisterschaft and its successor, the DTM, and the annual Norisringrennen weekend is sometimes called the "Franconian Monaco". Mexican driver Pedro Rodríguez died in a crash at the circuit in 1971 during a sports car race.
