Shanghai

Pudong skyline from the Bund

Shanghai is a direct-administered municipality and the most populous urban area in China, with a population of roughly 29 million in the municipality (2023). Located on China's central coast where the Yangtze River meets the East China Sea, it is a global centre of finance, trade, shipping, and innovation, home to the world's busiest container port and one of its largest stock exchanges.

History

Shanghai grew from a fishing village into a market town, receiving a city wall in 1554 against wokou pirates. After the Treaty of Nanking (1842) opened it as a treaty port, British, French, and American concessions (later the International Settlement) transformed it into East Asia's greatest commercial city — for a time dubbed the "Paris of the East" — with the grand buildings of the Bund facing the Huangpu River. The Chinese Communist Party held its first congress in Shanghai in 1921. The city suffered in the Battle of Shanghai (1937) and Japanese occupation, and after 1949 its industry bankrolled much of the national budget. Designated a special economic hub and boosted by the development of Pudong from 1990, Shanghai regained global prominence; it hosted Expo 2010.

Geography and cityscape

The municipality includes Chongming Island and sits on alluvial plain, mostly only a few metres above sea level. The Huangpu River divides the historic western bank (Puxi), with the Bund, the former French Concession, and Nanjing Road, from Pudong, whose Lujiazui financial district is crowned by the Oriental Pearl Tower, the Shanghai Tower (China's tallest building at 632 m), and the Shanghai World Financial Center. Shanghai has the world's largest metro system by route length.

Economy and culture

The Port of Shanghai has been the world's busiest container port since 2010. The city is a hub for finance (the Shanghai Stock Exchange), automobile and electronics manufacturing, and increasingly for artificial intelligence and biotech. Its local culture — haipai, "sea-style" cosmopolitanism — blends Jiangnan traditions with Western influences; the local language is Shanghainese, a Wu Chinese variety. Landmarks include Yu Garden, Jing'an Temple, and the shikumen lane houses.
