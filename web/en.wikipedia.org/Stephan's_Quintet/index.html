Stephan's Quintet

Stephan's Quintet (HCG 92) imaged by the James Webb Space Telescope in 2022.

Stephan's Quintet is a visual grouping of five galaxies in the constellation Pegasus, discovered by Édouard Stephan in 1877 at the Marseille Observatory — the first compact galaxy group ever identified. It is also catalogued as Hickson Compact Group 92.

Members

Four of the five galaxies — NGC 7317, NGC 7318, NGC 7319 and NGC 7320c — form a physically associated, interacting group about 290 million light-years away. The fifth, NGC 7320, is a foreground galaxy only about 39 million light-years distant that happens to lie along the same line of sight. The group shows tidal tails and a large intergalactic shock wave, visible in X-rays and infrared, where one member is ploughing into the others at high speed.

Redshift controversy

The discordant redshift of NGC 7320 — much lower than its apparent companions — was long cited by Halton Arp and others as evidence against the cosmological interpretation of redshift. The consensus explanation is simply that NGC 7320 is far closer than the rest of the group.

In popular culture

The quintet was among the first images released from the James Webb Space Telescope in July 2022, and its appearance is often said to have inspired the angelic figures in the opening of the 1946 film It's a Wonderful Life, as the group was pictured in astronomy books of the era.
