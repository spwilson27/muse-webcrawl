Novelization

[Image placeholder: Novelization]

A novelization (or novelisation) is a derivative novel that adapts the story of a work created for another medium, such as a film, television series, stage play or video game. Novelizations became especially popular from the 1970s onward, when film tie-in books allowed audiences to revisit movies before home video.

History and practice

Early examples appeared with silent films. The modern era of the novelization is associated with blockbusters such as Star Wars (1976, ghostwritten by Alan Dean Foster under George Lucas's name) and Alien (1979), which were major bestsellers. Novelizers usually work from a screenplay, often an early draft, so novelizations may contain scenes and details absent from the finished film; they also let the author expand characters' thoughts.

Reverse adaptation

The opposite process — turning a novel into a film — is adaptation rather than novelization. Some novelizations, such as Arthur C. Clarke's 2001: A Space Odyssey, written alongside the film, have acquired independent literary standing.
