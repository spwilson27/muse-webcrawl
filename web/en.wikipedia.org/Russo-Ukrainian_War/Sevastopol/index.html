Sevastopol is a port city on the southwestern tip of the Crimean Peninsula, with one of the finest natural harbours on the Black Sea. Founded by Russia in 1783 on the site near the ancient Greek colony of Chersonesus, it became the principal base of the Russian Black Sea Fleet and the scene of two famous sieges.

History

During the Crimean War the city withstood an eleven-month Anglo-French siege (1854–1855) before falling; Tolstoy's Sevastopol Sketches reported the defence. Rebuilt as a fortress and naval base, Sevastopol endured a second epic siege in the Second World War, holding out for 250 days against German forces in 1941–1942 before its liberation in 1944. In the Soviet Union it was a closed city of special status.

Status dispute

After 1991 Sevastopol lay within independent Ukraine, while Russia leased naval facilities under agreements extended by the 2010 Kharkiv Pact. In 2014 Russia annexed Crimea, making Sevastopol a Russian federal city — a status not recognised internationally, with the UN General Assembly affirming Ukraine's territorial integrity. Since 2022 the city and its fleet have been repeatedly attacked by Ukrainian missiles and naval drones, forcing much of the Black Sea Fleet to relocate eastward.

Landmarks

Sights include the ruins of Chersonesus (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), the Panorama museum of the 1855 defence, the Monument to the Sunken Ships (1905), and the naval installations around Sevastopol Bay.
