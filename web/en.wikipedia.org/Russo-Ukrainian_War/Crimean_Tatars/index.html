The Crimean Tatars are a Turkic ethnic group and an indigenous people of Crimea. They emerged as a distinct people in the Crimean Khanate (1441–1783), a successor state of the Golden Horde ruled by the Giray dynasty, blending Kipchak steppe Tatars with the peninsula's older Greek, Goth, Genoese and other populations.

History

For three centuries the khanate, an Ottoman vassal, dominated the northern Black Sea steppe; its horsemen raided deep into Poland-Lithuania and Muscovy. Russia annexed Crimea in 1783, and waves of emigration to the Ottoman Empire reduced the Tatar share of the population. In May 1944, on Stalin's orders, the entire Crimean Tatar population — some 190,000 people — was deported to Central Asia on accusations of collective collaboration with Nazi Germany; a large fraction died in exile. The deportation is recognised by Ukraine and several other states as genocide (the Sürgünlik).

Return and recent history

Only from 1989, under perestroika, were Crimean Tatars permitted to return in large numbers, rebuilding communities and the representative bodies of the Qurultay and the Mejlis. After Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014, which most Crimean Tatar leaders opposed, the Mejlis was banned as "extremist" in 2016 and activists faced prosecutions — a central grievance in the wider Russo-Ukrainian War context in which Crimea's status remains contested.

Culture

Crimean Tatar culture combines steppe, Ottoman and Crimean traditions in its language, embroidery (örnek ornament), cuisine and music. Notable figures include the educator Ismail Gasprinsky and the singer Jamala, whose song "1944" about the deportation won the Eurovision Song Contest in 2016.
