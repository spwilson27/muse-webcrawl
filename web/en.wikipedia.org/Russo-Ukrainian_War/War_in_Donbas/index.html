The war in Donbas was an armed conflict in the Donbas region of eastern Ukraine from 2014 to 2022, forming the first phase of the Russo-Ukrainian War. Following Ukraine's Euromaidan revolution and Russia's annexation of Crimea, Russian-backed separatists seized government buildings in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts and proclaimed "people's republics," prompting Ukraine to launch an "Anti-Terrorist Operation."

Course of the conflict

Ukrainian forces regained much territory in mid-2014, but direct intervention by regular Russian units reversed the campaign at Ilovaisk (August 2014). On 17 July 2014 Malaysia Airlines Flight MH17 was shot down over the war zone, killing 298 people. The Minsk Protocol (September 2014) and Minsk II (February 2015), negotiated after the battle of Debaltseve, established ceasefires that never fully held; the front line froze into trench warfare monitored by the OSCE, with periodic shelling and sniper fire for seven years. Investigators, NATO and Ukraine documented extensive Russian supply of weapons, personnel and command to the separatist formations, which Moscow publicly denied while acknowledging Russian "volunteers."

Human cost

The UN estimated more than 14,000 deaths between 2014 and the end of 2021, including over 3,000 civilians, and some 1.5 million people were displaced. Large areas were contaminated by mines and unexploded ordnance.

Escalation

On 21 February 2022 Russia formally recognised the DPR and LPR; three days later it launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine, absorbing the Donbas front into a much larger war. Russia announced the annexation of both oblasts in September 2022, a move condemned as illegal by the UN General Assembly.
