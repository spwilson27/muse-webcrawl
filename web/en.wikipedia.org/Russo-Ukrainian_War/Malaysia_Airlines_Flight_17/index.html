Malaysia Airlines Flight 17 (MH17) was a scheduled international passenger flight from Amsterdam to Kuala Lumpur that was shot down on 17 July 2014 while flying over eastern Ukraine, during the war in Donbas. All 283 passengers and 15 crew were killed, making it one of the deadliest shoot-downs of a commercial airliner in history. The passengers included 193 Dutch nationals, many travelling to an international AIDS conference in Melbourne, including the researcher Joep Lange.

Investigation

The Dutch Safety Board concluded in 2015 that the aircraft was destroyed by a 9N314M warhead from a Buk surface-to-air missile, which detonated just above and to the left of the cockpit. The criminal investigation by the Dutch-led Joint Investigation Team (JIT) determined that the Buk launcher had been transported into Ukraine from Russia's 53rd Anti-Aircraft Missile Brigade, fired from separatist-held territory near Pervomaiskyi, and driven back into Russia afterwards.

Trial and aftermath

In November 2022, the District Court of The Hague convicted three men — Igor Girkin, Sergey Dubinskiy, and Leonid Kharchenko — in absentia of murder and of causing the crash, sentencing them to life imprisonment; a fourth defendant was acquitted. Russia has consistently denied responsibility. In 2025 the UN's International Civil Aviation Organization council formally held the Russian Federation responsible for the downing. The disaster led to sweeping changes in how civil aviation assesses and avoids conflict zones.
