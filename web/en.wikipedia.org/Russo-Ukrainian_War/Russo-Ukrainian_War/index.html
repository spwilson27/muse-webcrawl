Russo-Ukrainian War

The Russo-Ukrainian War is an ongoing war between Russia (together with Russian-backed separatists and, since 2024, North Korean troops) and Ukraine. It began in February 2014 following Ukraine's Revolution of Dignity, with Russia's annexation of Crimea and the outbreak of war in the Donbas. On 24 February 2022, Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine, beginning the largest war in Europe since the Second World War.

Background

After the Revolution of Dignity removed pro-Russian president Viktor Yanukovych in February 2014, unmarked Russian troops seized Crimea, which Russia annexed following a disputed referendum in March 2014. In April, Russian-backed separatists proclaimed "people's republics" in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts, igniting the war in Donbas. The Minsk agreements of 2014 and 2015 failed to end the fighting, which claimed more than 14,000 lives by 2022.

Full-scale invasion

After a months-long military build-up, President Vladimir Putin recognised the separatist republics and on 24 February 2022 launched a multi-axis invasion aimed at Kyiv. Ukrainian resistance, led by President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, forced Russia to abandon the Kyiv axis by April. Ukraine retook parts of Kharkiv and Kherson oblasts in autumn 2022 counteroffensives. Russia illegally annexed four Ukrainian oblasts, mobilised reserves, and settled into a war of attrition along a fortified front line in the east and south, while striking Ukrainian cities and infrastructure and occupying roughly a fifth of Ukrainian territory.

Consequences

The war has caused hundreds of thousands of military casualties and tens of thousands of civilian deaths, displaced millions of Ukrainians, and triggered global food and energy crises. Ukraine has received extensive military and financial aid from Western allies; Russia has faced sweeping sanctions. The International Criminal Court has indicted Putin for the deportation of Ukrainian children.
