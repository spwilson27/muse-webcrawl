Battle of the Dnieper

Date24 August – 23 December 1943
LocationDnieper River, Ukrainian SSR, Soviet Union
BelligerentsSoviet Union vs Nazi Germany and Romania
Strengthc. 2.6 million Soviet troops; c. 1.2 million Axis troops
ResultSoviet victory; liberation of Kyiv

The Battle of the Dnieper was a major military campaign of the Second World War fought from August to December 1943 on the Eastern Front. Following their victory at Kursk, Soviet forces of five fronts advanced on a front of some 1,400 kilometres to the Dnieper River, forced crossings at dozens of points, and liberated Kyiv on 6 November 1943. It was one of the largest operations of the war, involving around four million soldiers in total.

Background

After the Battle of Kursk ended in August 1943, the Red Army's summer offensive rolled back Army Group South. Hitler ordered the construction of the Panther–Wotan Line along the Dnieper — the German Ostwall ("Eastern Wall") — and the systematic devastation of the territory to be abandoned. German forces under Field Marshal Erich von Manstein conducted a fighting withdrawal to the river.

The crossings

Rather than pausing to regroup, Soviet commanders ordered an immediate pursuit, and assault units crossed the wide river on improvised rafts, fishing boats and barrels, often under fire, establishing bridgeheads such as those at Lyutezh and Bukryn north and south of Kyiv. An airborne operation at Bukryn on 24 September — the largest Soviet paratroop drop of the war — largely failed, with many paratroopers dropped onto German positions or into the river. Nevertheless, by late October dozens of bridgeheads had been secured and linked.

Liberation of Kyiv and outcome

General Nikolai Vatutin's 1st Ukrainian Front secretly shifted its main effort to the Lyutezh bridgehead and struck on 3 November, liberating Kyiv three days later, on the eve of the anniversary of the October Revolution. By 23 December the Soviets held a strategic bridgehead hundreds of kilometres wide on the western bank, setting the stage for the winter offensives that would clear Right-Bank Ukraine in early 1944. Nearly 3,500 soldiers received the title Hero of the Soviet Union for the crossing — more than for any other single operation.
