Kyiv (Ukrainian: Київ) is the capital and most populous city of Ukraine. Located on the Dnieper River in north-central Ukraine, it is one of the oldest cities in Eastern Europe and a major industrial, scientific, educational and cultural centre. (The older romanisation "Kiev," from Russian, has been largely replaced by "Kyiv" in English usage since 1991 and especially since 2022.)

History

According to legend, Kyiv was founded by the brothers Kyi, Shchek and Khoryv and their sister Lybid. It grew rich on the trade route "from the Varangians to the Greeks" and in the ninth century became the capital of Kyivan Rus, the great East Slavic state. Under Yaroslav the Wise (d. 1054) the city gained Saint Sophia Cathedral and the Golden Gate. Kyiv was devastated by the Mongol invasion of 1240 and passed successively under Lithuanian, Polish, Cossack and Russian rule, before becoming capital of the Ukrainian SSR and then, in 1991, of independent Ukraine.

Modern Kyiv

Maidan Nezalezhnosti (Independence Square) was the centre of the Orange Revolution (2004) and the Euromaidan protests (2013–2014). During Russia's full-scale invasion beginning in February 2022, Kyiv was the principal target of the opening offensive; Ukrainian forces repelled the attack in the Battle of Kyiv, and Russian forces withdrew from the region by April 2022, though the city has continued to face missile and drone strikes. Its two great monastic ensembles — Saint Sophia and the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra — are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

Geography and transport

Kyiv straddles both banks of the Dnieper, linked by several bridges; the historic right-bank core rises on hills above the river. The Kyiv Metro, opened in 1960, includes Arsenalna, one of the world's deepest stations.
