Revolution of Dignity

The Revolution of Dignity (Ukrainian: Революція гідності), also known as the Maidan Revolution, was a wave of protests and civil unrest in Ukraine that began in November 2013 as the Euromaidan movement and culminated in February 2014 with the ousting of President Viktor Yanukovych. The revolution followed months of demonstrations sparked by Yanukovych's refusal to sign an association agreement with the European Union, and by widespread anger over government corruption.

Background

On 21 November 2013, President Viktor Yanukovych's government announced it would suspend preparations for signing the Ukraine–European Union Association Agreement, instead seeking closer ties with Russia. Journalist Mustafa Nayyem's social media call for protest brought thousands to Kyiv's Maidan Nezalezhnosti that evening.

Escalation

A violent dispersal of student protesters by Berkut riot police on 30 November dramatically enlarged the movement; crowds of up to a million marched in Kyiv in December. Protesters occupied the square and nearby buildings, building barricades. In January 2014, parliament passed sweeping anti-protest laws, and clashes on Hrushevsky Street produced the first protester deaths. On 18–20 February a government assault on the Maidan failed; on 20 February snipers killed dozens of protesters, an event commemorated as the sacrifice of the "Heavenly Hundred".

Outcome

On 21 February Yanukovych signed an EU-brokered agreement with the opposition, but protesters rejected it and demanded his resignation. Yanukovych fled Kyiv that night and was removed by parliament the next day. Oleksandr Turchynov became acting president, and Petro Poroshenko won the May 2014 presidential election. Russia responded by annexing Crimea and fomenting war in the Donbas, beginning the Russo-Ukrainian War.
