Malaysia Airlines Berhad is the flag carrier of Malaysia, headquartered at Kuala Lumpur International Airport. Founded in 1947 as Malayan Airways and renamed after the restructurings that followed Singapore's separation, the airline grew into one of Asia's leading carriers and a member of the Oneworld alliance.

History

The airline expanded through the jet age under Malaysian Airline System (from 1972), building an extensive network across Asia-Pacific, Europe and the Americas. Following financial difficulties in the late 1990s and 2000s, the state investment fund Khazanah Nasional progressively increased its stake.

The 2014 disasters

In 2014 Malaysia Airlines suffered two catastrophic losses within five months. Flight MH370, a Boeing 777 from Kuala Lumpur to Beijing, disappeared on 8 March 2014 with 239 people aboard; its fate remains aviation's greatest mystery, with debris later washing ashore in the western Indian Ocean. On 17 July 2014, Flight MH17 from Amsterdam to Kuala Lumpur was shot down by a Buk surface-to-air missile over the war zone in eastern Ukraine, killing all 298 people aboard. International investigators and a Dutch court concluded the missile was fired from territory controlled by Russian-backed separatists using a launcher supplied by Russia — findings Russia rejects. The twin disasters devastated the airline, which was renationalised and restructured, emerging as Malaysia Airlines Berhad in 2015.

Fleet and services

The airline operates Airbus A330 and A350 aircraft on long-haul routes and Boeing 737s regionally, and is known for its Malaysian hospitality branding centred on the traditional wau kite motif.
