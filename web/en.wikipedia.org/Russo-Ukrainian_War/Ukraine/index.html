Ukraine is a country in Eastern Europe, the second-largest in Europe by area and the largest lying entirely within the continent. It borders Russia to the east and northeast, Belarus to the north, Poland, Slovakia, and Hungary to the west, and Romania and Moldova to the southwest, with coasts on the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov. Its capital and largest city is Kyiv.

History

The territory was the centre of Kyivan Rus', the medieval state whose legacy Ukraine, Russia, and Belarus all claim, converted to Christianity under Volodymyr the Great in 988. After the Mongol invasions, Ukrainian lands passed among Lithuania, Poland, the Cossack Hetmanate — whose uprising under Bohdan Khmelnytsky began in 1648 — and eventually the Russian and Habsburg empires. A short-lived Ukrainian People's Republic followed the Russian Revolution of 1917 before most of Ukraine was absorbed into the Soviet Union, where it suffered the Holodomor famine of 1932–33, in which millions died, and catastrophic destruction in the Second World War. Ukraine regained independence in 1991 following a referendum supported by over 90 percent of voters.

Modern Ukraine and the Russo-Ukrainian War

Post-independence Ukraine alternated between western- and Russian-oriented governments, punctuated by the Orange Revolution (2004) and the Euromaidan protests (2013–14). In 2014 Russia annexed Crimea and backed separatists in the Donbas war. On 24 February 2022 Russia launched a full-scale invasion, the largest war in Europe since 1945; Ukrainian resistance, supported by Western military and financial aid, halted the advance on Kyiv and recaptured substantial territory, while Russia occupied and claimed to annex parts of the east and south, annexations not recognised internationally.

Geography and economy

Much of Ukraine is fertile steppe — the "breadbasket of Europe" — and it is a major exporter of grain, sunflower oil, iron, and steel. Its industries include aerospace, shipbuilding, and a large information-technology sector. Ukrainian culture centres on the Ukrainian language, Orthodox and Greek Catholic traditions, and a rich folk and literary heritage.
