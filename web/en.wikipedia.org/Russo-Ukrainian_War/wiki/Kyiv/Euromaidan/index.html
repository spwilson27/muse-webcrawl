Euromaidan

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Date21 November 2013 – 22 February 2014
LocationMaidan Nezalezhnosti, Kyiv, Ukraine
CauseSuspension of the EU association agreement; police violence; authoritarian laws
OutcomePresident Yanukovych removed; new elections; Revolution of Dignity

Euromaidan was a wave of demonstrations and civil unrest in Ukraine that began on 21 November 2013 on Kyiv's Maidan Nezalezhnosti (Independence Square), sparked by the government of President Viktor Yanukovych suddenly suspending preparations to sign an association agreement with the European Union in favour of closer ties with Russia.

Course of the protests

A violent dispersal of student protesters by Berkut riot police on 30 November transformed the rally into a mass movement demanding the government's resignation. Hundreds of thousands occupied the Maidan behind barricades of tyres and ice through the winter, organising self-defence units, kitchens and medical services. Clashes in January 2014, after parliament passed sweeping "dictatorship laws", brought the first deaths. Between 18 and 20 February snipers and security forces killed around one hundred protesters — remembered as the Heavenly Hundred — before Yanukovych fled Kyiv on 21–22 February and parliament voted to remove him.

Aftermath

The events, known in Ukraine as the Revolution of Dignity, led to interim government, early elections won by Petro Poroshenko, and the signing of the EU association agreement. Russia responded by annexing Crimea and fuelling war in the Donbas — the opening phase of the wider Russo-Ukrainian War.
