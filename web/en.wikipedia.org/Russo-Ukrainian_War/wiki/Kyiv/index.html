Kyiv (Ukrainian: Київ) is the capital and most populous city of Ukraine, located in the north-central part of the country on the Dnieper River. It is one of the oldest cities in Eastern Europe, with a history spanning more than 1,400 years, and it was the centre of Kievan Rus', the medieval state from which Ukraine, Russia and Belarus all trace cultural origins.

History

Legend attributes Kyiv's founding to three brothers — Kyi, Shchek and Khoryv — and their sister Lybid. By the 9th and 10th centuries the city was the wealthy capital of Kievan Rus' under princes such as Volodymyr the Great, who Christianised the Rus' in 988, and Yaroslav the Wise, under whom Saint Sophia Cathedral was built. The Mongol invasion of 1240 devastated the city. Kyiv later passed through Lithuanian, Polish, Cossack and Russian imperial rule, suffered catastrophe again in the Second World War, including the Babyn Yar massacre of 1941, and became the capital of independent Ukraine in 1991.

Russo-Ukrainian War

Kyiv was the site of the Euromaidan protests of 2013–14. In February 2022 Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine, with Kyiv as its principal objective. The offensive on the capital stalled amid fierce Ukrainian resistance in battles at Hostomel Airport, Irpin and Bucha, and Russian forces withdrew from the Kyiv region in early April 2022. The city has since remained under Ukrainian control while enduring repeated missile and drone strikes on its infrastructure.

Landmarks

Major sites include Saint Sophia Cathedral and the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra (both UNESCO World Heritage Sites), the Motherland Monument, Independence Square (Maidan Nezalezhnosti), and the golden-domed St Michael's Monastery.
