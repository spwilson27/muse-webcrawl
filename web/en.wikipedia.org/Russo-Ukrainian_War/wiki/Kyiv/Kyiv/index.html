Kyiv

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Kyiv (Ukrainian: Київ) is the capital and most populous city of Ukraine. Located in north-central Ukraine along the Dnieper River, it is one of the oldest cities in Eastern Europe, with a history conventionally dated to the late 9th century, when it became the capital of Kievan Rus', the first East Slavic state. Its population is roughly 2.8–3 million people.

History

Legend attributes Kyiv's founding to the brothers Kyi, Shchek, and Khoryv and their sister Lybid. The city flourished under princes such as Volodymyr the Great, who adopted Christianity in 988, and Yaroslav the Wise. It was destroyed by the Mongol invasion of 1240 and later passed through Lithuanian, Polish, and Russian rule. Kyiv became a major industrial and cultural centre of the Russian Empire and the Soviet Union, suffered catastrophically during the Second World War — including the Babi Yar massacres of 1941 — and became the capital of independent Ukraine in 1991. The 2004 Orange Revolution and the 2013–14 Euromaidan protests centred on the city.

Russo-Ukrainian War

Following Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, Kyiv was a primary objective of the opening offensive. Russian forces advanced from Belarus toward the capital, seizing Hostomel Airport, but Ukrainian resistance and logistics failures stalled the assault; Russia withdrew from the Kyiv region in early April 2022. The city has since remained the seat of government and a symbol of Ukrainian resistance, though subject to repeated missile and drone strikes on infrastructure.

Culture and landmarks

St Sophia's Cathedral and the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra (Monastery of the Caves) are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. The city is noted for its golden-domed churches, chestnut trees, the monumental Mother Ukraine statue, and its deep metro — Arsenalna station is among the deepest in the world.
