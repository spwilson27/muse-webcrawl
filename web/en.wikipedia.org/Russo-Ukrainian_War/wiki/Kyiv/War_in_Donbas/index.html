War in Donbas
DateApril 2014 – February 2022 (phase of the Russo-Ukrainian War)
LocationDonetsk and Luhansk oblasts, eastern Ukraine
BelligerentsUkraine vs. Russian-backed "DPR" and "LPR" separatists and Russian forces
OutcomeFrozen conflict along a line of contact; merged into the full-scale Russian invasion of 2022

The war in Donbas was an armed conflict in the Donbas region of eastern Ukraine between the Ukrainian government and Russian-backed separatist forces of the self-proclaimed Donetsk People's Republic (DPR) and Luhansk People's Republic (LPR). It began in April 2014, after Russia's annexation of Crimea, when armed groups seized government buildings across Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts. The war killed roughly 14,000 people by early 2022 and displaced over a million, before merging into Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

Course of the war

Ukraine launched an "Anti-Terrorist Operation" in spring 2014 and initially regained much territory, including Sloviansk and Kramatorsk. In August 2014, regular Russian forces intervened at Ilovaisk, inflicting a heavy defeat on Ukrainian troops and reversing their gains. The Minsk Protocol (September 2014) failed to hold, and fighting for Donetsk airport and the rail hub of Debaltseve culminated in the Minsk II agreement of February 2015. Thereafter the conflict settled into positional trench warfare along a roughly 450 km line of contact, punctuated by shelling and sniping, monitored imperfectly by the OSCE.

MH17

In July 2014 the separatist-held airspace saw the conflict's most internationally notorious incident: the shoot-down of Malaysia Airlines Flight 17, killing all 298 people aboard, which Dutch investigators and courts attributed to a Russian-supplied Buk missile.

End of the phase

In February 2022 Russia recognised the DPR and LPR, signed "friendship treaties" with them, and on 24 February launched its full-scale invasion, transforming the Donbas war into a much larger conflict in which the region remained one of the principal fronts.
