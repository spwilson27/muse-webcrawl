The Dnieper (Ukrainian: Dnipro) is one of the longest rivers in Europe, rising in Russia, flowing through Belarus, and crossing Ukraine to the Black Sea. At Kyiv the river divides the historic city on the high right bank from the flat left-bank districts, and its wide floodplain — with islands such as Trukhaniv and the Hydropark — forms the green heart of the Ukrainian capital.

History

The Dnieper was the artery of the ancient trade route "from the Varangians to the Greeks", along which Kyiv grew into the capital of Kievan Rus'. The river was crossed by legendary floating bridges and, from the 19th century, by permanent spans such as the Nicholas Chain Bridge (1853). In the Second World War the Battle of the Dnieper (1943) saw Soviet forces force a crossing near Kyiv, liberating the city that November.

The river in modern Kyiv

Upstream of the city the Kyiv Reservoir (the "Kyiv Sea") regulates the flow. Bridges including the Metro Bridge, the Paton Bridge and the pedestrian Park Bridge link the banks, and the embankments are lined with beaches, marinas and the riverside park. During the 2022 Russian invasion the river at Kyiv again became a strategic barrier, with bridges across it critical to the defence of the capital in the war's opening weeks.
