Ukraine is a country in Eastern Europe, the second-largest on the continent by area, and its capital and largest city is Kyiv, on the Dnieper River in north-central Ukraine. Kyiv has been the political heart of Ukraine through its incarnations as the capital of Kievan Rus', of the Ukrainian People's Republic (1917–1921), of the Ukrainian SSR, and of independent Ukraine since 1991.

The capital's role

Kyiv hosts the Verkhovna Rada (parliament), the Office of the President on Bankova Street, the Cabinet of Ministers, the National Bank and the headquarters of most national institutions. The city is also Ukraine's chief centre of education, culture and industry, with a population of about 3 million. Its historic centre contains two UNESCO World Heritage properties: Saint Sophia Cathedral and the Kyiv-Pechersk Lavra.

Kyiv in the Russo-Ukrainian War

When Russia launched its full-scale invasion on 24 February 2022, Kyiv was the principal strategic objective of the opening offensive. Ukrainian forces defeated the assault in the Battle of Kyiv, and Russian troops withdrew from the capital region by early April 2022. The city has since endured repeated missile and drone strikes while remaining the seat of government and the destination of solidarity visits by allied leaders.
