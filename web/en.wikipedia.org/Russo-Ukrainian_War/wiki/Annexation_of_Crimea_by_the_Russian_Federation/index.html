Annexation of Crimea by the Russian Federation

The annexation of Crimea by the Russian Federation took place in February–March 2014, in the aftermath of the Ukrainian revolution of February 2014. Russian military personnel without insignia — the so-called "little green men" — seized government buildings and Ukrainian military bases across the Crimean Peninsula. A referendum held under Russian military control on 16 March 2014 officially reported a majority in favour of joining Russia, and on 18 March Russia signed a treaty of accession with the self-declared Republic of Crimea.

Background

Crimea had been transferred from the Russian SFSR to the Ukrainian SSR within the Soviet Union in 1954 and remained part of independent Ukraine after 1991, with Russia leasing the Sevastopol naval base for its Black Sea Fleet. After the Euromaidan protests forced President Viktor Yanukovych from power in February 2014, Russian forces moved to secure the peninsula.

International reaction

The annexation was widely condemned as a violation of international law and of Russia's commitments under the UN Charter, the Helsinki Accords and the 1994 Budapest Memorandum. UN General Assembly Resolution 68/262 affirmed Ukraine's territorial integrity and declared the referendum invalid. The United States, the European Union and other states imposed sanctions on Russia. Only a small number of states have recognised Crimea as part of Russia; the overwhelming majority of the international community continues to regard it as occupied Ukrainian territory.
