Kyiv is the capital and most populous city of Ukraine, located in the north-central part of the country on the Dnieper River. One of the oldest cities in Eastern Europe, it was the capital of Kyivan Rus' and is revered as a cradle of Slavic civilisation and Orthodox Christianity.

History

Legend dates Kyiv's founding to the 5th century, attributing it to the brothers Kyi, Shchek, and Khoryv and their sister Lybid. By the 9th century the city was the capital of the Rus' state; under Yaroslav the Wise in the 11th century it flourished, gaining Saint Sophia Cathedral, famed for its mosaics and frescoes, and the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra monastery. The Mongols destroyed Kyiv in 1240. The city later passed through Lithuanian, Polish, and Cossack eras before incorporation into the Russian Empire. It suffered terribly in the 20th century — revolution, the Holodomor years, and the Nazi occupation of 1941–43, including the Babyn Yar massacre of Kyiv's Jews. Kyiv became the capital of independent Ukraine in 1991 and the centre of the Orange Revolution and Euromaidan protests.

The city since 2022

In February–April 2022 Kyiv was the primary objective of Russia's invasion; Ukrainian forces defended the approaches to the city in the Battle of Kyiv, forcing a Russian withdrawal from the north. The city has since endured repeated missile and drone strikes while remaining the seat of government and a symbol of Ukrainian resistance.

Landmarks

UNESCO World Heritage Sites include Saint Sophia Cathedral and the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra. Other landmarks include the Golden Gate, St Michael's Golden-Domed Monastery, Khreshchatyk boulevard, Maidan Nezalezhnosti (Independence Square), and the monumental Mother Ukraine statue.
