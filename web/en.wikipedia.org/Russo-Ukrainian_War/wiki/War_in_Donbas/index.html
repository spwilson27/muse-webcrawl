War in Donbas

The Donbas region of eastern Ukraine
Date6 April 2014 – 24 February 2022 (phase of the Russo-Ukrainian War)
LocationDonbas (Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts), Ukraine
BelligerentsUkraine vs Russian-backed separatist forces (DPR/LPR), with Russian support
Casualtiesc. 14,000 killed by 2021 (UN estimate, incl. civilians and MH17)

The War in Donbas was an armed conflict in the Donbas region of eastern Ukraine that began in April 2014, when Russian-backed separatist groups seized government buildings in Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts and proclaimed the "Donetsk People's Republic" and "Luhansk People's Republic". It formed the opening phase of the Russo-Ukrainian War and merged into the full-scale Russian invasion of Ukraine in February 2022.

Course of the conflict

Ukraine launched an "Anti-Terrorist Operation" to retake the seized territory. Fighting escalated in the summer of 2014; in July 2014 Malaysia Airlines Flight MH17 was shot down over the conflict zone, killing all 298 people aboard — international investigators attributed the shootdown to a Buk missile supplied from Russia. Direct interventions by Russian regular forces at Ilovaisk (August 2014) and Debaltseve (early 2015) reversed Ukrainian gains. The Minsk Protocol (September 2014) and Minsk II agreement (February 2015) established ceasefires that were never fully implemented, and the front line stabilised into years of trench warfare and artillery exchanges.

Humanitarian impact

By the end of 2021 the UN estimated around 14,000 deaths connected with the conflict, including some 3,400 civilians, and more than 1.5 million people had been internally displaced. In February 2022 Russia recognised the DPR and LPR as independent states days before launching its full-scale invasion, after which the Donbas war was subsumed into the wider conflict.
