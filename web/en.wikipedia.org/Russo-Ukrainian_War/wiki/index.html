Russo-Ukrainian War
DateFebruary 2014 – present
LocationUkraine and Russia
BelligerentsUkraine (supported by NATO and EU states) vs Russia (with Belarusian support)
Key eventsAnnexation of Crimea (2014); War in Donbas (2014–2022); Full-scale invasion (2022–present)

The Russo-Ukrainian War is an ongoing war between Russia and Ukraine that began in February 2014. Following Ukraine's Revolution of Dignity, Russia occupied and annexed Crimea from Ukraine and supported pro-Russian separatists fighting the Ukrainian military in the Donbas region of eastern Ukraine, in a conflict that killed more than 14,000 people by 2022. On 24 February 2022, Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine, the largest war in Europe since the Second World War.

Background

After the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, Ukraine became independent but remained economically and culturally linked to Russia. Ukraine's moves toward closer ties with the European Union and NATO met Russian opposition. Protests in 2013–14 (Euromaidan) ousted President Viktor Yanukovych, after which Russia seized Crimea and unrest erupted in Donbas, where Russian-backed separatists proclaimed the Donetsk and Luhansk "people's republics". The Minsk agreements of 2014–15 failed to end the fighting.

Full-scale invasion (2022–present)

Russia attacked from the north, east and south, aiming initially at Kyiv, but Ukrainian resistance and logistical failures forced a Russian withdrawal from the north in April 2022. Fighting concentrated in the east and south: Ukraine liberated Kharkiv oblast and Kherson in autumn 2022 counteroffensives, while Russia captured Mariupol, Bakhmut and Avdiivka after prolonged battles and formally annexed four Ukrainian oblasts it did not fully control. The war has seen extensive use of drones and missiles, attacks on energy infrastructure, the destruction of the Kakhovka dam (2023), Ukraine's 2024 incursion into Russia's Kursk Oblast, and massive military aid to Ukraine from Western states, alongside sweeping sanctions on Russia.

Consequences

The war has caused tens of thousands of military and civilian deaths, displaced millions of Ukrainians, disrupted global food and energy markets, and driven Finland and Sweden to join NATO. The International Criminal Court issued an arrest warrant for Vladimir Putin in 2023 over the deportation of Ukrainian children. As of the mid-2020s the front line remained largely static with attritional fighting in the east, and the conflict unresolved.
