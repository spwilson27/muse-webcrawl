Crimea is a peninsula in Eastern Europe, on the northern coast of the Black Sea, connected to the mainland by the Isthmus of Perekop and, since 2018, to Russia's Taman Peninsula by the Crimean Bridge across the Kerch Strait. It has a long and layered history as a crossroads of Greek, Scythian, Gothic, Byzantine, Genoese, Tatar, Ottoman, and Russian rule. Since 2014 Crimea has been administered by the Russian Federation following its annexation, while the great majority of states continue to recognise it as part of Ukraine.

Geography

Most of the peninsula is steppe, rising in the south to the Crimean Mountains, behind which a narrow subtropical coastal strip around Yalta enjoys a Mediterranean climate long famous for resorts and vineyards. The peninsula is almost cut off from the mainland by the Syvash, a system of shallow lagoons.

History

Greek colonies such as Chersonesus were founded on the coast in antiquity. The Crimean Khanate, a successor state of the Golden Horde and an Ottoman vassal, ruled from the 15th century until the Russian Empire annexed Crimea in 1783. The peninsula was the main theatre of the Crimean War (1853–1856) and a major battleground in the Second World War. In 1944 Stalin deported the entire Crimean Tatar population to Central Asia; they were allowed to return only at the end of the Soviet period. In 1954 Crimea was transferred from the Russian SFSR to the Ukrainian SSR, and it remained in independent Ukraine after 1991, hosting Russia's Black Sea Fleet at Sevastopol under lease agreements.

2014 annexation and aftermath

Following Ukraine's Euromaidan revolution, unmarked Russian forces seized the peninsula in February–March 2014. A referendum held under occupation, unrecognised internationally, preceded Russia's formal annexation, condemned by UN General Assembly Resolution 68/262 affirming Ukraine's territorial integrity. Since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Crimea has served as a major Russian military base and has itself been repeatedly struck by Ukrainian attacks.
