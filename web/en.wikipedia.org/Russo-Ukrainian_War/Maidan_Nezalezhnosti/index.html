Maidan Nezalezhnosti

Maidan Nezalezhnosti (Ukrainian: Майдан Незалежності, literally "Independence Square") is the central square of Kyiv, the capital of Ukraine. It has been the traditional site of political rallies and protests in the country, most notably during the Orange Revolution of 2004 and the Euromaidan protests of 2013–2014 that culminated in the Revolution of Dignity.

History

The square occupies the site of the former Khreshchaty Yaroslaviv gate area and developed during the 19th century as Kyiv's commercial centre grew along Khreshchatyk street. Under the Soviet Union it was known successively as Soviet Square, Kalinin Square, and October Revolution Square. Following Ukrainian independence in 1991, it received its present name.

During the 2001 "Ukraine without Kuchma" protests, the square hosted tent encampments. In November–December 2004 it became the focal point of the Orange Revolution, when hundreds of thousands of protesters camped there demanding a re-run of the fraudulent presidential election runoff.

Euromaidan

On 21 November 2013, protests began on the Maidan after President Viktor Yanukovych suspended preparations to sign an association agreement with the European Union. The protests grew into the Euromaidan movement; after violent crackdowns, including the sniper killings of February 2014 in which around one hundred protesters (the "Heavenly Hundred") were killed, Yanukovych fled the country. A memorial to the Heavenly Hundred now stands on Instytutska Street adjoining the square.

Landmarks

The square is dominated by the Independence Monument, a 61-metre column topped by a statue of the Slavic goddess Berehynia, erected in 2001. Beneath the square lies the Globus underground shopping centre. The Hotel Ukraine and the building of the Federation of Trade Unions of Ukraine — which burned during the February 2014 clashes — stand on its northern side.
