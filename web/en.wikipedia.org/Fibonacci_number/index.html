Fibonacci number

In mathematics, the Fibonacci numbers, commonly denoted Fn, form a sequence, the Fibonacci sequence, in which each number is the sum of the two preceding ones. The sequence commonly starts from 0 and 1, although some authors omit the initial 0.

Definition

F0 = 0, F1 = 1, and Fn = Fn−1 + Fn−2 for n > 1. A closed form is given by Binet's formula.

Golden ratio

The ratio Fn+1/Fn tends to the golden ratio φ ≈ 1.6180339887 as n → ∞.

Occurrence in nature

Fibonacci numbers appear in phyllotaxis: the arrangement of leaves, the branching of trees, and the spiral of pine cones, sunflowers and pineapples. Patterns of animal breeding, first described by Fibonacci for rabbits in his Liber Abaci (1202), also follow the sequence.

Applications

Applications include computer science (Fibonacci heaps, Fibonacci search), financial analysis, and number theory.
