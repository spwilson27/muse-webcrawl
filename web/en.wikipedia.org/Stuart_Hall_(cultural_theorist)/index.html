Stuart Hall (cultural theorist)

Stuart Hall
Born: 3 February 1932, Kingston, Jamaica
Died: 10 February 2014, London
Fields: Cultural studies, sociology

Stuart McPhail Hall (1932–2014) was a Jamaican-born British cultural theorist and sociologist, a founding figure of cultural studies and of the British New Left. He directed the Centre for Contemporary Cultural Studies at the University of Birmingham (1968–1979) and later taught at the Open University.

Work

Hall's encoding/decoding model (1973) reshaped media studies by showing audiences actively interpret messages in dominant, negotiated or oppositional ways. With colleagues he wrote Policing the Crisis (1978), analysing moral panic and Thatcherism, which he named and critiqued. His essays on identity argued identities are productions, never complete.

Legacy

Hall co-founded the New Left Review, influenced studies of race, diaspora and popular culture, and remains one of the most cited scholars in the humanities.
