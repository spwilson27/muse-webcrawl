Bursitis

Bursitis is the inflammation of one or more bursae—small fluid-filled sacs that cushion bones, tendons, and muscles near joints. Symptoms include pain that worsens with movement, localized swelling, and tenderness. More than 150 bursae exist in the body; the shoulder (subacromial), elbow (olecranon), hip (trochanteric), knee (prepatellar), and heel are most commonly affected.

Causes

The most frequent cause is repetitive movement or sustained pressure: leaning on elbows, kneeling ("housemaid's knee"), overhead work, or prolonged sitting on hard surfaces. Sudden trauma, infection (septic bursitis, usually Staphylococcus aureus entering through skin breaks), gout, and inflammatory arthritis can also provoke bursitis. Risk increases with age and with occupations or sports stressing particular joints.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is usually clinical, supported by ultrasound or MRI when needed; aspiration of bursal fluid distinguishes infection or crystals. Most non-infectious bursitis resolves with rest, ice, compression, elevation, and nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory drugs. Corticosteroid injections may help persistent cases, while septic bursitis requires antibiotics and sometimes drainage. Recalcitrant cases may need surgical removal of the bursa (bursectomy). Preventive measures include padding, ergonomic changes, and gradual conditioning.
