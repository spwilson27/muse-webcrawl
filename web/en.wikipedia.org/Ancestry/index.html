Ancestry

Ancestry is the line of descent of a person or group from their forebears. It encompasses the ancestors from whom an individual is descended — parents, grandparents, and earlier generations — and is studied through genealogy, family history, and, increasingly, genetic testing. A person's complete set of ancestors doubles with each generation going back, a fact tempered by pedigree collapse, in which the same individuals recur in multiple lines.

Cultural significance

Ancestry holds religious and social importance in many cultures. Ancestor veneration is a central practice in traditional Chinese religion and Confucianism, as well as in many African and Indigenous traditions, where ancestors are honoured as guardians of the living. Systems of descent — patrilineal, matrilineal, or bilateral — determine inheritance, clan membership, and identity in different societies.

Genealogy and genetics

Genealogists reconstruct ancestry from records such as censuses, parish registers, and civil certificates. Direct-to-consumer DNA tests estimate biogeographical ancestry and identify genetic relatives, raising both scientific questions about reference populations and ethical questions about privacy and identity.
