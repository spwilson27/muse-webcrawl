An Olympic medal is awarded to successful competitors at one of the Olympic Games. There are three classes of medal: gold (for first place), silver (second place), and bronze (third place), awarded in nearly all events.

History

At the first modern Games in Athens in 1896, winners received a silver medal and an olive branch, while runners-up received bronze medals and laurel branches. The familiar gold–silver–bronze sequence began in 1904 at St. Louis. Between 1912 and 1948, gold medals were of solid gold; modern "gold" medals are in fact silver with gold plating, containing at least 6 grams of pure gold.

Design

Since 1928, Summer Olympic medals have traditionally borne an image of Nike, the Greek goddess of victory, on the obverse, though host cities now design their own versions; Winter Games medals have always had free designs. Since 2004 the obverse shows Nike flying over the Panathenaic Stadium. Medals are produced by the host nation's mint, and recent Games have incorporated recycled metals — Tokyo 2020 medals were made entirely from recycled consumer electronics.

Records

American swimmer Michael Phelps holds the record for Olympic medals with 28 (23 gold) won between 2004 and 2016.
