Dai Vernon

Dai Vernon
Born: David Frederick Wingfield Verner, 11 June 1894, Ottawa, Canada
Died: 21 August 1992, Los Angeles, United States
Occupation: Magician
Known for: Sleight of hand, close-up magic

Dai Vernon (born David Frederick Wingfield Verner; June 11, 1894 – August 21, 1992) was a Canadian magician widely regarded as one of the most influential figures in sleight-of-hand and close-up magic. Known respectfully as "The Professor", he spent much of his later life as the resident magician and mentor at the Magic Castle in Hollywood, California.

Career

Vernon built his reputation in New York during the 1920s and 1930s, performing and associating with the leading card men of the era. He is famous in magic lore as the man who fooled Harry Houdini with a card trick — an ambitious card routine that Houdini could not explain even after repeated viewings. Vernon refined classic effects such as the cups and balls and numerous card techniques, and taught the importance of naturalness, economy of motion, and strong theoretical grounding in misdirection.

Legacy

His teachings were recorded in books such as The Dai Vernon Book of Magic and in the multi-volume Revelations video series. Generations of magicians studied under him or were influenced by his philosophy, which is often summarized in maxims such as "be natural" and "confusion is not magic".
