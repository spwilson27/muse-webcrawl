Symplectic geometry

Symplectic geometry is a branch of differential geometry and differential topology that studies symplectic manifolds: smooth manifolds equipped with a closed, nondegenerate differential 2-form. The subject grew out of the Hamiltonian formulation of classical mechanics, in which the phase space of a physical system is naturally a symplectic manifold.

Overview

Unlike Riemannian geometry, symplectic geometry has no local curvature invariants: by Darboux's theorem, all symplectic manifolds of the same dimension look identical locally. The interesting questions are therefore global, concerning the shape and symmetries of phase spaces, Hamiltonian flows, and counting problems for periodic orbits. A landmark development was Mikhail Gromov's 1985 nonsqueezing theorem, which showed that symplectic maps are far more rigid than volume-preserving maps and founded modern symplectic topology through the theory of pseudoholomorphic curves.

Key topics

Central ideas include Lagrangian submanifolds, moment maps and symplectic reduction for group actions, Floer homology, the Arnold conjectures on fixed points of Hamiltonian diffeomorphisms, and mirror symmetry, which connects symplectic geometry with algebraic geometry in string theory. The name “symplectic” was coined by Hermann Weyl from the Greek symplektikos (“intertwined”), replacing the older term “complex group” for the symplectic group Sp(2n).
