Date31 May 1935

Magnitude7.7 Mw
EpicentreNear Ali Jaan, Baluchistan (British India)

Casualties30,000–60,000 dead

The 1935 Quetta earthquake struck Quetta, Baluchistan, British India (now Pakistan), in the early hours of 31 May 1935. With a magnitude of 7.7, it ranks among the deadliest earthquakes in South Asian history, killing between 30,000 and 60,000 people and largely destroying the city of Quetta.

The earthquake

The quake struck at about 2:33 a.m. local time and lasted some 30 seconds. Its epicentre lay near the town of Ali Jaan, southwest of Quetta, along the Chiltan fault. Almost every building in Quetta collapsed or was badly damaged, and the seepage of groundwater — locally called the "seepage wave" — added to the destruction in several districts.

Aftermath and relief

The British Indian administration imposed martial law, and the rescue effort was hampered by the complete destruction of infrastructure. The Royal Air Force station at Quetta was badly hit. In the weeks that followed, aftershocks continued, and the summer heat raised fears of epidemic disease. A major aftershock on a later date devastated the town of Mastung.

Reconstruction

Quetta was rebuilt under strict new building codes: low-rise, earthquake-resistant structures with timber framing replaced the heavy mud-brick architecture that had proved so lethal. The event remains a landmark in the history of seismic building regulation in the region.
