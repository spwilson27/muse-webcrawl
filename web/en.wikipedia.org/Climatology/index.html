Climatology

Climatology
Field: Atmospheric science
Focus: Long-term weather patterns
Key systems: Köppen classification
Related: Meteorology, paleoclimatology

Climatology is the scientific study of climate, defined as the statistical description of weather patterns over periods ranging from months to millions of years. Where meteorology focuses on short-term weather, climatology examines the long-term averages, variability, and extremes that characterize the climate of a place or region.

Subfields

Climatology includes paleoclimatology, which reconstructs past climates from proxies such as ice cores, tree rings, and sediments; historical climatology; physical climatology, concerned with energy and water balances; and applied climatology, which uses climate information in agriculture, engineering, public health, and planning.

Classification

Climate classification systems organize the world's climates into regions. The Köppen climate classification, developed by Wladimir Köppen and modified by Rudolf Geiger, groups climates by temperature and precipitation patterns and remains the most widely used system. The Thornthwaite system incorporates moisture and potential evapotranspiration.

Climate change

Modern climatology plays a central role in detecting and attributing climate change. Climatologists use observational records, satellite data, and global climate models to study phenomena such as global warming, El Niño–Southern Oscillation, and shifts in storm tracks, and to project future conditions under different emissions scenarios.
