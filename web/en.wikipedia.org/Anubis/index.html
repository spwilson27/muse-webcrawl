Anubis

Anubis (Ancient Greek: Ἄνουβις), also known as Inpu in Ancient Egyptian, is the god of funerary rites, protector of graves, and guide to the afterlife in ancient Egyptian religion, usually depicted as a canine or a man with a canine head.

Roles

Anubis presided over mummification and the embalming process, protected cemeteries from desecration, and escorted souls to the afterlife. In the judgment scene described in the Book of the Dead, he supervised the weighing of the heart against the feather of Maat. His black colour symbolized the colour of mummified bodies and of the fertile Nile silt, associated with regeneration.

History

Anubis was one of the most ancient Egyptian gods, appearing in the earliest mastaba tombs of the First Dynasty. His prominence in the cult of the dead was later partly eclipsed by Osiris during the Middle Kingdom, though he remained central to funerary ritual. The Greeks identified him with Hermes, giving rise to the syncretic Hermanubis.
