How to Read a Book is a 1940 book by philosopher Mortimer J. Adler that sets out a systematic method for reading difficult books intelligently. A substantially revised edition, co-written with Charles Van Doren, appeared in 1972 and remains in print; it is regarded as a classic guide to active reading.

The four levels of reading

Adler distinguishes four cumulative levels: elementary reading (basic literacy); inspectional reading (systematic skimming and superficial reading to grasp a book's structure); analytical reading (thorough, rule-governed reading that classifies the book, states its propositions and arguments, and criticises it fairly); and syntopical reading (comparative reading across many books on one subject, constructing an analysis none of them contains alone).

Method and influence

The analytical rules — find what kind of book it is, state its unity, outline its parts, define its terms, test its arguments — reflect Adler's scholastic training and his role in the Great Books movement. The book shaped reading instruction and is frequently assigned in courses on critical thinking.
