Mercury

Mercury is a name shared by several distinct subjects. This page serves as a disambiguation overview of the most prominent uses of the term.

Mercury (planet)

Mercury is the smallest planet in the Solar System and the closest to the Sun. Its orbital period of about 88 Earth days is the shortest of all the planets. Despite its proximity to the Sun, radar observations have shown evidence of water ice in permanently shadowed polar craters. NASA's MESSENGER spacecraft orbited Mercury from 2011 to 2015, and the BepiColombo mission is en route to study it further.

Mercury (element)

Mercury is a chemical element with the symbol Hg and atomic number 80. It is a heavy, silvery liquid metal — the only metallic element that is liquid at standard temperature and pressure. Mercury and its compounds are highly toxic and were historically used in thermometers, barometers, and fluorescent lamps.

Mercury (mythology)

In Roman mythology, Mercury is the god of commerce, travel, communication, and trickery, identified with the Greek Hermes. He is traditionally depicted with winged sandals and carrying a caduceus.

Other uses

The name Mercury has also been used for NASA's Project Mercury, the first United States human spaceflight program (1958–1963), an American automobile brand produced by Ford from 1938 to 2011, and numerous newspapers, ships, and musical works.
