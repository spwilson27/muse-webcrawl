White Cliffs of Dover

The White Cliffs of Dover are chalk cliffs on the Kent coast of England facing the Strait of Dover and France. Their bright white face, formed from Cretaceous chalk with streaks of black flint, has made them an enduring symbol of Britain.

Geology and setting

The cliffs reach about 110 metres at their highest and form part of the North Downs chalk system. Erosion continually renews the white surface but also causes occasional rockfalls, so paths near the edge require care.

Symbolism

For travellers arriving by sea the cliffs are a first sight of England. They featured in wartime song and imagery, notably Vera Lynn's “(There'll Be Bluebirds Over) The White Cliffs of Dover,” and remain a major visitor landscape protected in part by the National Trust.
