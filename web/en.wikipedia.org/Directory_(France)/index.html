French Directory

The Directory (French: le Directoire) was the governing five-member committee of the French First Republic from 26 October 1795 until 9 November 1799, when it was overthrown by Napoleon Bonaparte in the Coup of 18 Brumaire and replaced by the Consulate. It was the final government of the French Revolution before Napoleon's rise.

Structure

Under the Constitution of the Year III, executive power was vested in five Directors, chosen by the legislature and renewed by one member each year. The legislature itself was bicameral: the Council of Five Hundred proposed laws, and the Council of Ancients approved them. The franchise was restricted to taxpaying property owners — a deliberate reaction against the universal male suffrage of the radical phase of the Revolution.

History

The Directory inherited war with most of Europe, bankruptcy, inflation, royalist plots, and continuing unrest from the Terror. It survived through repeated coups against its own legislature — Fructidor (1797) against royalists, Floréal (1798) and Prairial (1799) against Jacobins — undermining its legitimacy. Abroad, French armies, above all Napoleon's Army of Italy, won spectacular victories and exported "sister republics", but the regime became dependent on the army it could not control.

Politically unstable and financially corrupt, the Directory is often remembered for the fashionable society of the Incroyables et Merveilleuses and the Directoire style in art and furniture. On 18 Brumaire (9 November 1799), Napoleon, with the help of the Director Emmanuel Joseph Sieyès, dissolved the government and established the Consulate, ending the revolutionary decade.
