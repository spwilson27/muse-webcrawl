Crazy Horse Memorial

Crazy Horse Memorial
Location: Black Hills, South Dakota
Sculptor: Korczak Ziolkowski
Begun: 1948
Planned height: 641 ft long, 563 ft high

The Crazy Horse Memorial is a mountain monument under construction on privately held land in the Black Hills of Custer County, South Dakota. When completed, it will depict the Oglala Lakota warrior Crazy Horse riding a horse and pointing to his tribal land.

History

Lakota chief Henry Standing Bear invited sculptor Korczak Ziolkowski, who had worked on Mount Rushmore, to create the memorial. Work began in 1948. Ziolkowski refused federal funding, and the project continues under his family through the nonprofit Crazy Horse Memorial Foundation, financed by admission fees and donations.

Scale and progress

The planned sculpture, 641 feet long and 563 feet high, would be far larger than Mount Rushmore. Crazy Horse's face, 87 feet high, was completed and dedicated in 1998. Work continues on the horse's head and the outstretched arm. The complex includes the Indian Museum of North America and a planned university and medical training center for Native American students.

Controversy

Some Lakota critics object that Crazy Horse himself opposed being photographed or depicted, and that carving the sacred Black Hills repeats the desecration associated with Mount Rushmore.
