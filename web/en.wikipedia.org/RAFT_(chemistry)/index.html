RAFT (chemistry)

Reversible addition−fragmentation chain-transfer or RAFT polymerization is a controlled radical polymerization technique reported in 1998 by researchers at CSIRO in Australia. It uses a thiocarbonylthio compound — such as a dithioester, trithiocarbonate, or xanthate — as a chain-transfer agent to mediate the growth of polymer chains, giving well-defined polymers with narrow molecular weight distributions and controlled architectures.

Mechanism

In RAFT, radicals generated by a conventional initiator add to monomer and to the C=S bond of the chain-transfer agent, forming an intermediate radical that fragments to release a new radical. This rapid, reversible exchange between active and dormant chains means all chains grow at roughly the same rate, so molecular weight increases linearly with conversion and dispersity stays low (often below 1.2).

Scope and applications

RAFT tolerates a wide range of monomers (styrenics, acrylates, methacrylates, acrylamides, vinyl acetate via xanthates) and functional groups, and can be run in water and at moderate temperatures. It is used to make block copolymers, star and graft polymers, and materials for drug delivery, coatings, and nanotechnology.

Limitations

The sulfur-containing end groups can impart colour and odour and may need removal for some applications; the choice of chain-transfer agent must be matched to the monomer class for good control.
