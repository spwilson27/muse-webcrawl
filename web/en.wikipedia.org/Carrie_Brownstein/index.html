Carrie Brownstein

Carrie Rachel Brownstein
Born: 27 September 1974, Seattle, Washington, U.S.
Occupations: Musician, actress, writer, comedian
Known for: Sleater-Kinney, Portlandia

Carrie Rachel Brownstein (born 27 September 1974) is an American musician, actress, and comedian. She is a guitarist and vocalist in the rock band Sleater-Kinney, and co-created and starred in the sketch comedy series Portlandia (2011–2018) with Fred Armisen.

Music

Raised in Redmond, Washington, Brownstein formed the band Excuse 17 in the Olympia riot grrrl scene before co-founding Sleater-Kinney with Corin Tucker in 1994. The band became one of the most acclaimed American rock groups of its era, releasing albums including Dig Me Out (1997) and The Woods (2005) before a hiatus, then reuniting for No Cities to Love (2015). Rolling Stone named her among the greatest guitarists of all time.

Television and writing

Portlandia, filmed in Portland, Oregon, won a Peabody Award and ran for eight seasons. Brownstein also acted in Transparent and the film Carol (2015), contributed music writing to NPR and The Believer, and published the memoir Hunger Makes Me a Modern Girl (2015).
