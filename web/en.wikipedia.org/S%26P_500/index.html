S&P 500

S&P 500
Stock market index
Foundation: 4 March 1957
Operator: S&P Dow Jones Indices
Constituents: approx. 500 large companies

The S&P 500 is a stock market index tracking the stock performance of about 500 leading large-cap companies listed on stock exchanges in the United States. It is one of the most commonly followed equity indices and is widely regarded as the best gauge of large-cap US equities.

Selection and weighting

Constituents are selected by a committee at S&P Dow Jones Indices based on market capitalisation, liquidity, domicile, sector representation and financial viability. The index is weighted by free-float market capitalisation, so larger companies count for more. It covers roughly 80% of available US market capitalisation.

History

The index in its modern 500-stock form was introduced on 4 March 1957, succeeding earlier Standard & Poor's indices dating back to 1923. It has become the benchmark for index funds and exchange-traded funds such as the SPDR S&P 500 ETF.
