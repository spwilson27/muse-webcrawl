CONCACAF

The Confederation of North, Central America and Caribbean Association Football, known as CONCACAF (stylised as Concacaf), is the continental governing body for association football in North America, Central America and the Caribbean. Founded in 1961, it is one of FIFA's six continental confederations and has 41 member associations.

Competitions

Concacaf organises national-team tournaments including the Gold Cup, the Nations League and the W Gold Cup, and club competitions including the Concacaf Champions Cup. It also runs World Cup qualifying for its region.

History

Concacaf formed from the merger of the North American Football Confederation and the Confederación Centroamericana y del Caribe de Fútbol. Its headquarters are in Miami, Florida. The confederation was at the centre of the 2015 FIFA corruption case, after which reforms and a rebrand followed.
