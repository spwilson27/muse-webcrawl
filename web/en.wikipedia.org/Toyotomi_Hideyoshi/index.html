Toyotomi Hideyoshi

Born1536/1537, Nakamura, Owari Province, Japan
Died18 September 1598, Fushimi Castle, Kyoto
TitleKampaku (Imperial Regent), Daijō-daijin
PredecessorOda Nobunaga (as unifier)
SuccessorTokugawa Ieyasu (de facto)

Toyotomi Hideyoshi (豊臣 秀吉, 1536/1537 – 18 September 1598), otherwise known as Kinoshita Tōkichirō (木下 藤吉郎) and Hashiba Hideyoshi (羽柴 秀吉), was a Japanese samurai and daimyō of the late Sengoku period regarded as the second "Great Unifier" of Japan. Rising from peasant stock to become one of the most powerful men in the country, Hideyoshi succeeded his former lord, Oda Nobunaga, and brought an end to the Sengoku period of civil war.

Rise to power

Hideyoshi joined Oda Nobunaga's forces around 1558 as a lowly servant and rose through ability, becoming one of Nobunaga's most trusted generals. After Nobunaga's assassination in the Honnō-ji Incident of 1582, Hideyoshi defeated the assassin Akechi Mitsuhide at the Battle of Yamazaki, then sidelined rivals within the Oda clan. By defeating the Shikoku, Kyushu, and Odawara campaigns (1585–1590), he completed the unification of Japan.

Rule

Hideyoshi became Imperial Regent (kampaku) in 1585 and received the clan name Toyotomi from the Emperor in 1586. His domestic policies included the "sword hunt" disarming peasants, the separation of warriors and farmers, a nationwide land survey (Taikō kenchi), restrictions on Christianity including the expulsion edict of 1587 and the martyrdom of the Twenty-six Martyrs of Japan in 1597, and the building of Osaka Castle and Fushimi Castle.

Invasions of Korea and death

In 1592 and again in 1597, Hideyoshi launched invasions of Korea intended ultimately to conquer Ming China. The campaigns failed against Korean and Chinese resistance, devastated Korea, and were abandoned after Hideyoshi's death in 1598. Power struggles among his regents led to the Battle of Sekigahara in 1600 and the rise of Tokugawa Ieyasu; the Toyotomi clan was destroyed at the Siege of Osaka in 1615.
