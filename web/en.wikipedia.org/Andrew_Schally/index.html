Andrew Victor Schally (30 November 1926 – 17 October 2024) was a Polish-American endocrinologist who shared the 1977 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Rosalyn Sussman Yalow and Roger Guillemin for discoveries concerning the peptide hormone production of the brain. His laboratory isolated and determined the structures of the hypothalamic hormones thyrotropin-releasing hormone (TRH) and gonadotropin-releasing hormone (GnRH, also called LHRH), founding the modern field of neuroendocrinology.

Life and career

Schally was born in Wilno, Poland, the son of a professional soldier; the family fled the German invasion in 1939, and he was educated in Scotland. He worked at the National Institute for Medical Research in London, then moved to McGill University in Montreal, earning his PhD in 1957. In the United States he worked at Baylor College of Medicine in Houston, then in 1962 established his laboratory at the Veterans Administration hospital and Tulane University in New Orleans, where he remained for decades before moving to the University of Miami's Miller School of Medicine.

Research

Schally's group processed enormous quantities of animal hypothalami — millions of pig and sheep brains — to extract minute quantities of releasing hormones. His team isolated TRH in 1969 (sharing priority in a famous rivalry with Guillemin's laboratory) and determined the structure of GnRH in 1971; he later contributed to the characterisation of somatostatin. His later work developed synthetic analogs of these hormones — agonists and antagonists of GnRH and related peptides — widely used in the treatment of prostate cancer, endometriosis, precocious puberty, and in assisted reproduction.

Legacy

Schally's work established that the brain controls the endocrine system through peptide hormones, reshaping endocrinology and yielding a large class of peptide drugs. He authored or co-authored well over two thousand publications and continued research into his nineties.
