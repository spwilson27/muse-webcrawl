Viacom (1952–2006)

The original Viacom Inc. (from "Video & Audio Communications") was an American media conglomerate that existed from 1952 to 2006. It began as CBS Television Film Sales, the syndication division of CBS, was spun off and renamed Viacom in 1971, and grew into one of the world's largest entertainment companies before splitting into CBS Corporation and a new Viacom at the end of 2005.

History

Forced to divest its syndication arm under the financial interest and syndication rules, CBS spun Viacom off to shareholders in 1971. The company expanded into cable networks, acquiring MTV Networks and building brands such as MTV, Nickelodeon, VH1, and BET. In 1987, Sumner Redstone's National Amusements won a bidding war for control. Viacom acquired Paramount Pictures in 1994 and CBS itself in 2000.

Split

Seeking to separate its slower-growing broadcast assets from its cable networks and studio, Viacom split on December 31, 2005: CBS Corporation took the CBS network, radio, and publishing assets, while the new Viacom retained MTV Networks and Paramount Pictures. The two companies recombined in 2019 as ViacomCBS, renamed Paramount Global in 2022.
