National Amusements

National Amusements, Inc. is an American privately owned movie theater operator and mass media holding company based in Norwood, Massachusetts. Founded in 1936 by Michael Redstone as the Northeast Theatre Corporation, it is best known as the controlling shareholder of Paramount Global, holding the majority of its voting shares.

History

Michael Redstone built a chain of drive-in theaters in the northeastern United States. His son Sumner Redstone took control in the 1960s, coined the term "multiplex", and transformed the company into a media empire by acquiring Viacom in 1987 and Paramount Pictures and CBS in later deals. Sumner Redstone served as executive chairman until his death in 2020, when control passed within the Redstone family, with Shari Redstone as president and non-executive chair of Paramount.

Operations

The company operates cinemas under brands including Showcase Cinemas in the United States, the United Kingdom, and Latin America, while its decisive asset is its roughly four-fifths voting stake in Paramount Global.
