CBS (originally the Columbia Broadcasting System) is an American commercial broadcast television and radio network, the flagship property of Paramount Global. Founded in 1927 as a radio network, it is one of the "Big Three" American television networks, alongside ABC and NBC.

History

William S. Paley bought the struggling radio network in 1928 and built it into a dominant broadcaster, home to Edward R. Murrow's wartime reporting. CBS television rose to prominence in the 1950s with programs such as I Love Lucy, produced by Desilu at CBS facilities, and long-running institutions including 60 Minutes (from 1968) and the CBS Evening News.

Programming

The network is known for crime procedurals such as the NCIS and CSI franchises, comedies including The Big Bang Theory, reality series like Survivor, and major sports broadcasts, notably NFL football and NCAA March Madness shared with partners.

Eye logo

The famous CBS "Eye" logo, designed by William Golden, debuted in 1951 and is among the most recognizable trademarks in television.
