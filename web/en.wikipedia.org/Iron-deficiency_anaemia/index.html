Iron-deficiency anaemia

Iron-deficiency anaemia is a common type of anaemia in which the blood lacks adequate healthy red blood cells because of insufficient iron. Without enough iron the body cannot produce haemoglobin, the protein that enables red blood cells to carry oxygen. It is the most common cause of anaemia worldwide.

Causes

Causes include blood loss (for example from menstruation, gastrointestinal bleeding or frequent blood donation), inadequate dietary iron intake, malabsorption as in coeliac disease, and increased requirements during pregnancy and growth. In older adults, iron-deficiency anaemia is an important warning sign of gastrointestinal malignancy.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms develop gradually and include fatigue, pallor, breathlessness, headache and palpitations. Distinctive features include pica (craving non-food substances), brittle or spoon-shaped nails (koilonychia), angular cheilitis and restless legs syndrome.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis is based on a full blood count showing microcytic, hypochromic red cells, and low serum ferritin. Treatment addresses the underlying cause and replenishes iron, usually with oral ferrous salts such as ferrous sulfate; intravenous iron is used when oral iron is not tolerated or absorbed. Related blood conditions include ischaemic disorders of other organs.
