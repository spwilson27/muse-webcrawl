Paris–Nice

Paris–Nice Nickname: The Race to the Sun
First held: 1933
Timing: Annually in March
Format: 8-stage road race, UCI World Tour
Finish: Promenade des Anglais, Nice
Record wins: 7 — Sean Kelly (1982–1988)

Paris–Nice is a professional cycling stage race held each year in March. The first race was run in 1933, organised by Albert Lejeune, director of the newspaper Le Petit Journal, and it has been held annually since then, apart from interruptions during the Second World War and a gap in 1940–1945. It is frequently called the Race to the Sun, because it starts in the wintry weather of the Paris region and finishes in the spring sunshine of the Mediterranean coast at Nice.

Race

Paris–Nice is one of the most prestigious week-long races on the calendar and forms part of the UCI World Tour. The route typically runs from the Paris area south through central France, crossing the hills of the Morvan and the Cévennes or Provence, and in some editions climbing major passes such as Mont Ventoux or the Col de Turini, before descending to the finish line on Nice's Promenade des Anglais. The traditional final stage is a hilly circuit around Nice, often including the Col d'Èze. Early editions and occasional modern ones include an individual or team time trial, and a mountain-top finish such as La Colmiane or Auron.

History and winners

The race was revived after the war by Jean Leulliot and for many years organised by his family; it is now run by ASO, organisers of the Tour de France. Sean Kelly won seven consecutive editions between 1982 and 1988, an unmatched streak. Other multiple winners include Jacques Anquetil, Eddy Merckx, Raymond Poulidor, and Joop Zoetemelk. In 2003 the Kazakh rider Andrei Kivilev died from injuries sustained in a crash on stage 2, a tragedy that accelerated the adoption of compulsory helmet rules in professional racing.
