Linear representation

In mathematics, a linear representation of a group G is a way of realising the elements of G as linear transformations of a vector space V, so that the group operation in G corresponds to composition of transformations. Precisely, a linear representation is a group homomorphism from G into the general linear group GL(V) of invertible linear maps on V; the space V is then called the representation space, and its dimension is the degree (or dimension) of the representation.

Motivation

Linear representations allow abstract groups to be studied through the well-developed machinery of linear algebra and matrix theory. Symmetry groups arising in geometry, physics and chemistry can be analysed in terms of how they act on functions, tensors and state spaces; in quantum mechanics, for example, the states of a system transform under representations of its symmetry group.

Basic theory

A subspace W of V invariant under all the transformations is a subrepresentation. A representation with no nontrivial subrepresentations is irreducible; one that decomposes as a direct sum of irreducibles is completely reducible. For finite groups over the complex numbers, Maschke's theorem guarantees complete reducibility, and the irreducible representations are governed by their characters — the traces of the representing matrices — which form an orthonormal set of class functions. The number of irreducible complex representations of a finite group equals its number of conjugacy classes.

Generalizations

Analogous notions exist for Lie groups and Lie algebras (where representations of the group and its algebra are closely linked), for algebras and quivers, and in the geometric theory where flat connections and local systems play the role of representations of the fundamental group. Projective representations, in which the homomorphism property holds only up to scalar multiples, are classified by group cohomology.
