Kiel

Country: Germany
State: Schleswig-Holstein
Population: about 247,000

Kiel is the capital and most populous city of the northern German state of Schleswig-Holstein, with roughly 247,000 inhabitants. It lies on the Kiel Firth, an inlet of the Baltic Sea, at the eastern end of the Kiel Canal, the world's busiest artificial waterway.

History

Founded in the 13th century, Kiel joined the Hanseatic League in 1284. It became a major naval base after German unification, and the sailors' mutiny here in November 1918 sparked the German Revolution that ended the First World War and the monarchy. The city was heavily bombed in the Second World War and rebuilt in modern style.

Maritime life

Kiel is known for Kiel Week (Kieler Woche), the world's largest sailing event, held every June, and for its shipyards, ferry links to Scandinavia, the University of Kiel (founded 1665) and the GEOMAR Helmholtz Centre for Ocean Research. Its handball team, THW Kiel, is among Europe's most successful.
