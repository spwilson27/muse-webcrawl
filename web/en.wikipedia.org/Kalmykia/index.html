Kalmykia

The Republic of Kalmykia (Kalmyk: Хальмг Таңһч) is a republic of Russia located in the North Caucasus region of Southern Russia, on the northwestern shore of the Caspian Sea. Its capital and largest city is Elista. Kalmykia is the only region in Europe where Buddhism is the predominant religion, practised by the Kalmyks, a Mongolic people descended from Oirat nomads who migrated from Central Asia in the 17th century.

Geography

The republic covers about 76,000 km² of dry steppe and semi-desert in the Caspian Depression. It borders Dagestan, Stavropol Krai, Rostov Oblast, Volgograd Oblast and Astrakhan Oblast. Much of the territory lies below sea level near the Caspian shore, and the climate is strongly continental, with hot summers and cold winters.

History

The Kalmyks formed the Kalmyk Khanate in the 17th century, a nomadic power that allied with the Russian Empire. Catherine the Great abolished the khanate in 1771, prompting much of the population to attempt a disastrous return migration to Dzungaria. In December 1943 the entire Kalmyk population was deported to Siberia by Stalin on accusations of collaboration; the autonomous republic was restored only in 1957–58, when the Kalmyks were allowed to return.

Economy and culture

The economy rests on agriculture, especially sheep and cattle herding, and on oil and gas extraction. Elista is known for its Buddhist temples, including the Golden Temple (Burkhan Bakshin Altan Sume), one of the largest Buddhist temples in Europe, and for Chess City, a complex built under former president Kirsan Ilyumzhinov, a long-serving head of the World Chess Federation (FIDE). The population is roughly 270,000, with Kalmyks forming a majority alongside Russians.
