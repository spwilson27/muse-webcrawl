Jalal Talabani (Kurdish: جەلال تاڵەبانی; 12 November 1933 – 3 October 2017) was an Iraqi Kurdish politician who served as the sixth President of Iraq from 2005 to 2014, the first non-Arab to hold the office. Affectionately known as "Mam Jalal", he founded the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan (PUK) in 1975 and was a leading figure in the Kurdish national movement for half a century.

Early life and Kurdish politics

Talabani was born in the village of Kelkan near Erbil and studied law in Baghdad, though his studies were interrupted by his political activity. He joined the Kurdistan Democratic Party (KDP) as a teenager, rising in its ranks under Mustafa Barzani, and took part in the Kurdish revolt of 1961. After disputes with Barzani's leadership and the collapse of the Kurdish movement following the 1975 Algiers Agreement, Talabani founded the PUK in Damascus, launching a new armed struggle against Baghdad. His forces fought both Saddam Hussein's regime and, during the 1990s Kurdish civil war, the rival KDP.

Presidency

Following the 2003 invasion of Iraq and the fall of Saddam Hussein, Talabani became a member of the Iraqi Governing Council and a key negotiator of Iraq's transitional arrangements. In April 2005 the National Assembly elected him President of Iraq; he was re-elected in 2006 under the new constitution and again in 2010. As a largely ceremonial but influential head of state, he styled himself a unifying figure among Iraq's Kurds, Sunni and Shia Arabs.

Later life

In December 2012 Talabani suffered a stroke and was treated in Germany; he returned to Iraq in July 2014 as his second term ended, succeeded by Fuad Masum. He died in Berlin on 3 October 2017, and was buried in Sulaymaniyah in a funeral attended by tens of thousands of mourners — the day after the Kurdish independence referendum whose aftermath he did not live to see resolved.
