Circular economy

A circular economy (also referred to as circularity or CE) is a model of production and consumption that involves sharing, leasing, reusing, repairing, refurbishing and recycling existing materials and products for as long as possible. In this way, the life cycle of products is extended, waste is reduced to a minimum, and resources are kept within the economy so they can be used again and again to create further value — in contrast to the traditional linear economy of "take, make, use, dispose".


The "butterfly diagram" popularised by the Ellen MacArthur Foundation distinguishes biological and technical cycles.

Principles and intellectual roots

The concept draws on several schools of thought, including Walter Stahel's "cradle to cradle" and performance economy, industrial ecology, biomimicry, and the "spaceship Earth" economics of Kenneth Boulding. The Ellen MacArthur Foundation, founded in 2010, popularised the framework for business and policy; its commonly cited design principles are to eliminate waste and pollution, circulate products and materials at their highest value, and regenerate nature. Products are designed for durability, disassembly and recovery, and business models such as product-as-a-service keep ownership — and responsibility for end-of-life — with manufacturers.

Policy

The European Union adopted a Circular Economy Action Plan as part of the European Green Deal, with measures including ecodesign rules, a right to repair, digital product passports and targets for recycling and waste reduction. China promoted circular economy through national legislation from 2009, and many cities and companies have adopted circularity strategies. Critics note rebound effects, thermodynamic limits to endless recycling, and the risk that "circular" claims become marketing; scholars argue circularity must be paired with absolute reductions in material throughput to deliver environmental benefit.

Strategies

  • Refuse, rethink, reduce — avoid unnecessary products and material use.
  • Reuse, repair, refurbish, remanufacture — keep products in service longer.
  • Repurpose and recycle — recover materials when products cannot be used further.
  • Design for disassembly and use of secondary raw materials.
