Lovastatin

Lovastatin, the first statin drug marketed, was isolated from the fungus Aspergillus terreus.

Lovastatin, sold under the brand name Mevacor among others, is a statin medication used to lower cholesterol and reduce the risk of cardiovascular disease. It is taken by mouth. Lovastatin was patented in 1979 and approved for medical use in 1987; it was the first statin to be marketed. It is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.

Mechanism

Lovastatin is a prodrug whose active β-hydroxy acid form competitively inhibits HMG-CoA reductase, the rate-limiting enzyme in the mevalonate pathway of cholesterol synthesis in the liver. It is naturally present in certain foods and fungi, including oyster mushrooms and red yeast rice. The related compound mevastatin (compactin) had been isolated by Akira Endo in the 1970s; lovastatin was isolated from the fungus Aspergillus terreus by scientists at Merck.

Side effects

Common side effects include headache, muscle pain, nausea, constipation and difficulty sleeping. Serious side effects may include liver problems, muscle breakdown (rhabdomyolysis) and increased blood sugar. Use during pregnancy may harm the baby. Lovastatin interacts with grapefruit juice and certain other drugs via the CYP3A4 enzyme.
