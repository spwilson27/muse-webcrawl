Hodag

Hodag
Grouping: Folklore creature / hoax
Country: United States
Region: Rhinelander, Wisconsin

The hodag is a fearsome critter in American folklore from the state of Wisconsin. It is said to inhabit the forests around Rhinelander in northern Wisconsin and has become the city's official symbol and mascot.

History

The hodag was popularized in 1893 by timber cruiser and prankster Eugene Shepard, who claimed to have captured a living creature with horns, spikes, glowing eyes, and a voracious appetite. Newspapers repeated the story, and Shepard later admitted the creature displayed at a fair was a carved and manipulated hoax.

Description

Descriptions give the hodag a frog-like head, a grinning face with fangs, stout legs with claws, a dinosaur-like back with spikes, and a long tail. Tales claimed it could only be subdued with chloroform, lemons, or dynamite, depending on the version.

Legacy

Despite its hoax origin, the hodag endures in Wisconsin festivals, school mascots, businesses, and tourism.
