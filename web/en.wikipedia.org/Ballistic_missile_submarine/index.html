Ballistic missile submarine

A ballistic missile submarine is a submarine capable of deploying submarine-launched ballistic missiles (SLBMs) armed with nuclear warheads. In US Navy hull classification these boats are designated SSBN — ship, submersible, ballistic, nuclear — nicknamed "boomers". Their purpose is nuclear deterrence: by remaining hidden in the oceans they guarantee a survivable second-strike capability, the sea-based leg of the nuclear triad.

History

The first operational ballistic missile submarines were the Soviet Golf-class diesel boats and the US Navy's USS George Washington (1960), which carried Polaris missiles. Successive generations fielded longer-range weapons — Poseidon, then Trident I and Trident II (D5) — while Soviet/Russian classes grew to the enormous Typhoon class, the largest submarines ever built. Modern classes include the American Ohio and Columbia classes, Russia's Borei class, Britain's Vanguard and Dreadnought classes, France's Triomphant class, China's Type 094, and India's Arihant class.

Operations

SSBNs conduct deterrent patrols lasting months, staying submerged and silent, navigating without surfacing and communicating via very-low-frequency radio. Two-crew rotations (such as the US Navy's Blue and Gold crews) maximise time at sea. Strict safety and authorisation procedures govern their nuclear weapons; the United Kingdom's prime minister writes secret "letters of last resort" for each boat's commanding officer.

Strategic role

Because an SSBN on patrol is effectively undetectable, it stabilises deterrence by removing any incentive for a disarming first strike. The boats' strategic importance is reflected in treaties counting their warheads, such as New START.
