Conrad III of Germany

Conrad III (1093 or 1094 – 15 February 1152) was the first king of Germany of the Hohenstaufen dynasty, reigning from 1138 until his death. The son of Frederick I, Duke of Swabia, and of Agnes, daughter of Emperor Henry IV, he was elected king in opposition to the Welf duke Henry the Proud and spent much of his reign contending with Welf resistance in Saxony and Bavaria.

The Second Crusade

Moved by the preaching of Bernard of Clairvaux, Conrad took the cross in 1146 and led a German army on the Second Crusade. His forces were largely destroyed by the Seljuk Turks near Dorylaeum in Anatolia in 1147; Conrad continued to Jerusalem and joined the unsuccessful siege of Damascus in 1148 before returning home. He never received imperial coronation in Rome, remaining "king of the Romans" rather than emperor.

Death and succession

Conrad died at Bamberg in 1152. On his deathbed he is said to have designated his nephew, Frederick Barbarossa, rather than his own young son, as his successor, a choice ratified by the princes at Frankfurt.
