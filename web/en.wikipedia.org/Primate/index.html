Primate

A primate is a mammal of the order Primates, which includes lemurs, lorises, galagos, tarsiers, monkeys, and apes — including humans. Primates are characterised by large brains relative to body size, forward-facing eyes with binocular vision, grasping hands and feet with flat nails rather than claws, and opposable thumbs. More than 500 species are recognised, ranging from Madame Berthe's mouse lemur, at about 30 grams, to the eastern gorilla, at over 200 kilograms.

Classification

Primates divide into two suborders. The Strepsirrhini ("wet-nosed" primates) include lemurs of Madagascar, lorises, and galagos. The Haplorhini ("dry-nosed" primates) include tarsiers and the simians — New World monkeys (Platyrrhini) of the Americas and Old World monkeys and apes (Catarrhini) of Africa and Asia. Humans are catarrhine apes of the family Hominidae, most closely related to chimpanzees and bonobos.

Characteristics

Most primates are arboreal and adapted to life in trees: flexible shoulder joints, grasping extremities, and acute vision at the expense of smell. Their dentition is generalised, reflecting omnivorous diets of fruit, leaves, insects, and sometimes meat. Primates have slow life histories — long gestations, usually single offspring, extended infant care, and long lifespans — matched by large, complex social groups and sophisticated cognition, including tool use, deception, and culturally transmitted traditions.

Distribution and habitat

Non-human primates live mainly in tropical rainforests, with some species adapted to savannas (baboons), mountains (Japanese macaques, which endure snow), and even deserts. Madagascar's lemurs evolved in isolation for tens of millions of years and are found nowhere else — a fauna that evolved alongside the island's distinctive wildlife as far east as Indonesia's orangutans of Borneo and Sumatra.

Relationship with humans

Primates are studied in anthropology, psychology, and medicine, feature prominently in the world's religions — the monkey god Hanuman in Hinduism, sacred monkeys at temples dedicated to Shiva — and are kept (controversially) as research subjects and pets. About 60% of primate species are threatened with extinction, chiefly through habitat loss, hunting, and the illegal wildlife trade; conservation of primates is regarded as a global priority.
