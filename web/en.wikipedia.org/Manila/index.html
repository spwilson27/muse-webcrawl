Manila (Tagalog: Maynila), officially the City of Manila, is the capital and second-most-populous city of the Philippines, after Quezon City. Located on the eastern shore of Manila Bay on the island of Luzon, it is one of the oldest and most densely populated cities in the world, with a population density exceeding 40,000 people per square kilometre.

History

Before Spanish colonization, the area at the mouth of the Pasig River was the site of Maynila, a fortified Tagalog polity ruled by Rajah Sulayman and trading with China and Southeast Asia. In 1571 Miguel López de Legazpi founded the Spanish city of Manila, and the walled district of Intramuros became the seat of Spanish colonial government for more than 300 years. Manila was the Pacific terminus of the Manila–Acapulco galleon trade, which for 250 years linked Asia with the Americas and Europe.

The city was occupied by the British in 1762–1764 during the Seven Years' War, ceded to the United States after the Spanish–American War of 1898, and devastated in the Battle of Manila in 1945, one of the most destructive urban battles of the Second World War. After independence in 1946, Manila remained the capital, except for the period 1948–1976 when Quezon City held that status.

Geography and districts

Manila is divided into 16 administrative districts, including Binondo — the world's oldest Chinatown, founded in 1594 — Ermita, Malate, Quiapo, Sampaloc, Santa Cruz, Tondo, and the historic core of Intramuros. The larger metropolitan area, Metro Manila, comprises 17 cities and municipalities and has a population of around 15 million.

Culture and landmarks

Landmarks include Rizal Park, Manila Cathedral, San Agustin Church (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), Fort Santiago, the University of Santo Tomas (founded 1611, the oldest existing university in Asia), and the Cultural Center of the Philippines. The city is a major centre of education, finance, commerce, and transport in Southeast Asia.
