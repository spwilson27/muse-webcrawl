The glomerulus (plural glomeruli) is a tuft of capillaries at the beginning of the nephron in the vertebrate kidney. Together with the surrounding Bowman's capsule it forms the renal corpuscle, where the first step of urine formation — filtration of the blood — takes place.

Structure

Blood enters the glomerulus through the afferent arteriole and leaves through the efferent arteriole. The capillary walls constitute a three-layered filtration barrier: a fenestrated endothelium, the glomerular basement membrane, and the foot processes (pedicels) of podocytes, between which lies the slit diaphragm. Mesangial cells occupy the central stalk of the tuft, providing support and contractile regulation.

Filtration

Hydrostatic pressure forces water and small solutes — ions, glucose, amino acids and urea — across the barrier, while cells and most plasma proteins are retained. In a healthy adult the kidneys filter about 180 litres of fluid per day, almost all of which is reabsorbed downstream in the tubule. The filtrate collects in Bowman's space and drains into the proximal convoluted tubule.

The juxtaglomerular apparatus, where the distal tubule contacts the glomerulus's own arterioles, senses sodium delivery and regulates filtration and renin release.

Clinical significance

Damage to the filtration barrier causes glomerulonephritis, proteinuria and haematuria. Diabetic nephropathy and hypertensive injury progressively scar glomeruli (glomerulosclerosis), leading to chronic kidney disease.
