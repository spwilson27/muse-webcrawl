The 2021 Abu Dhabi Grand Prix (officially the Formula 1 Etihad Airways Abu Dhabi Grand Prix 2021) was a Formula One motor race held on 12 December 2021 at the Yas Marina Circuit. The final round of the 2021 World Championship, it decided one of the closest title fights in history: Lewis Hamilton and Max Verstappen entered the race level on 369.5 points. Verstappen won the race — and his first World Championship — by overtaking Hamilton on the very last lap following a highly controversial safety car restart.

The race

Verstappen took pole, but Hamilton made the better start and led most of the race, surviving a first-lap run-off incident at the Turn 6 chicane that the stewards declined to investigate. Verstappen pitted under a virtual safety car for fresh tyres but could not close the roughly 11-second gap to Hamilton, whose victory — and record eighth world title — appeared assured.

On lap 53 Nicholas Latifi crashed, bringing out the safety car. Verstappen pitted for soft tyres; Hamilton stayed out on worn hard tyres. Race director Michael Masi initially stated that lapped cars would not be allowed to unlap themselves, but then permitted only the five lapped cars between Hamilton and Verstappen to pass, and restarted the race with one lap remaining. On that final lap Verstappen, on far fresher tyres, passed Hamilton at Turn 5 and held on to win the race and the championship, 397.5 points to 387.5.

Aftermath

Mercedes protested the result; the stewards dismissed both protests, and Mercedes ultimately withdrew its appeal. The FIA's subsequent inquiry concluded that Masi had acted in good faith but that "human error" had occurred — the safety car procedure had not been applied correctly — and Masi was removed from the race director role. Changes to the sporting regulations and race-control structure followed. The race remains among the most debated in Formula One history; its final lap is often compared with the dramatic 2008 finale at Interlagos.
