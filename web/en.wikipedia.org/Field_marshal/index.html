A field marshal (or field-marshal) is the most senior military rank in many armies, ranking above a full general. The title derives from the Old High German marahscalc, "master of the horse" — originally the officer in charge of a ruler's cavalry and stables — and its prestige grew as its holders became commanders of whole armies in the field. The rank is typically marked by a ceremonial baton.

History

The office developed in medieval France and the Holy Roman Empire, where the marshal (maréchal, Marschall) rose from stable-master to one of the great officers of state. By the early modern period, "field marshal" designated a commander entrusted with an independent army. In the German states the rank of Generalfeldmarschall became the summit of a military career, famously conferred — according to tradition — only in wartime and for victory.

United Kingdom

The rank of field marshal was introduced into the British Army in 1736. Holders have included the Duke of Wellington, Lord Roberts, Lord Kitchener, and in the twentieth century Bernard Montgomery and William Slim. Promotions largely ceased in peacetime in the 1990s, though the rank continued to be conferred on senior royals and retiring Chiefs of the Defence Staff for a time thereafter. British field marshals traditionally never retire, remaining on the active list for life and drawing half-pay.

Other countries

France awards the dignity of Marshal of France as a distinction rather than a rank; the last was conferred posthumously in the twentieth century. Germany created twelve field marshals in 1940 after the fall of France. The Soviet Union used the equivalent rank of Marshal of the Soviet Union. India has had two field marshals, Sam Manekshaw and K. M. Cariappa. Many Commonwealth and Middle Eastern armies retain the rank.

Equivalent ranks

The United States has never used the title "field marshal"; its nearest equivalents are General of the Army (five-star) and the special rank of General of the Armies. Naval and air force equivalents include admiral of the fleet and marshal of the air force.
