Yogi Bhajan

Yogi Bhajan
Born: Harbhajan Singh, 26 August 1929, Kot Harkarn, Punjab
Died: 6 October 2004, Española, New Mexico
Known for: Bringing Kundalini Yoga to the West; founding 3HO and the businesses Yogi Tea and Akal Security

Yogi Bhajan (born Harbhajan Singh) was an Indian-born American spiritual teacher who introduced his version of Kundalini Yoga to the United States in 1969 and built around it a global movement of organisations, businesses, and communities.

Life and movement

Raised in a Sikh family in Punjab, Harbhajan Singh worked as an Indian customs official before emigrating, via Canada, to Los Angeles in 1968. Breaking with the tradition of teaching yoga privately, he taught open classes to young seekers of the counterculture, founding the 3HO Foundation ("Healthy, Happy, Holy Organization") in 1969. His followers — many adopting Sikh dress, turbans, and the surname Khalsa — established ashrams, the Khalsa Council, Sikh Dharma International, and the Kundalini Research Institute. The movement's ventures included the Yogi Tea company, Golden Temple foods, and Akal Security, which grew into a major U.S. government contractor.

Teachings

Yogi Bhajan taught sets of exercises (kriyas) combining posture, breath (pranayama), mantra, and meditation aimed at awakening kundalini energy, along with "Humanology" teachings on lifestyle, diet, and relationships. Kundalini Yoga as taught by Yogi Bhajan spread worldwide through teacher trainings; adherents built a summer solstice gathering in New Mexico and the town community at Espanola.

Controversies

After his death, and especially following a 2020 memoir by his former secretary Pamela Saharah Dyson (Premka: White Bird in a Golden Cage) and an independent investigation commissioned by affiliated organisations, numerous allegations of sexual abuse, exploitation, and misconduct against Yogi Bhajan were publicly reported. The investigating report concluded the allegations were more likely than not true. The movement subsequently fractured, with many teachers and organisations distancing themselves, renaming bodies, and reviewing his teachings' provenance, which scholars had already questioned.
