Great Unconformity

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Great Unconformity is a major gap in the geological record in which sedimentary strata of Cambrian age (around 540–500 million years old) rest directly upon much older crystalline basement rock, typically 1.2–1.8 billion years old. More than a billion years of Earth history is missing at the contact. John Wesley Powell named the feature during his exploration of the Grand Canyon (1869–1875), where Cambrian Tapeats Sandstone lies on the Vishnu Schist and Zoroaster Granite.

Distribution

Comparable unconformities of similar age occur on several continents — in North America from Arizona to Wyoming and the Canadian Shield, and in places such as Namibia, Siberia, and China — suggesting a global erosional episode in the late Neoproterozoic or earliest Cambrian.

Proposed causes

Explaining the removal of kilometres of rock is a longstanding problem. Hypotheses include uplift and erosion during the breakup of the supercontinent Rodinia; tectonism along continental margins; and the "Snowball Earth" glaciations, whose ice sheets may have scoured the continents — a proposal revived by thermochronology studies showing widespread exhumation around 700–600 million years ago. The unconformity also coincides with the Cambrian explosion of animal life, prompting speculation that the erosion delivered nutrients to the oceans and helped precipitate the radiation of complex life, though causation remains unproven.
