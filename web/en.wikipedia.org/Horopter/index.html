Horopter

The theoretical horopter: the Vieth–Müller circle

The horopter is the volume of space containing all points that project onto corresponding retinal points in the two eyes when the eyes are fixed on a given point. Points on the horopter are seen as single (fused) rather than double, and define the plane of zero binocular disparity for a given fixation.

Theoretical horopter

For an idealized visual system, the horopter is the Vieth–Müller circle: the circle passing through the fixation point and the nodal points of both eyes. Points on this circle stimulate geometrically corresponding points on the two retinas. The empirically measured horopter, however, deviates systematically from the geometric prediction, being flatter than the circle at near distances — the Hering–Hillebrand deviation.

Panum's fusional area

Strictly, single vision is not confined to the horopter itself. Points within a small region around it, called Panum's fusional area, are also fused into a single percept, with the residual disparity providing the basis for stereoscopic depth perception (stereopsis).
