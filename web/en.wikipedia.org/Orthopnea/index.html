Orthopnea
DefinitionShortness of breath when lying flat
Common causesHeart failure, pulmonary oedema
Relieved bySitting or standing upright
RelatedParoxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea, platypnoea

Orthopnea (also spelled orthopnoea) is shortness of breath (dyspnoea) that occurs when lying flat, causing the person to have to sleep propped up on pillows or in a chair. It is a classic symptom of heart failure and other conditions in which fluid accumulates in the lungs. The word comes from Greek ortho ("straight") and pnoia ("breathing").

Causes

When a patient with left ventricular failure lies down, blood redistributes from the legs and abdomen to the chest, increasing pulmonary venous pressure; the failing heart cannot handle the extra volume, and pulmonary congestion worsens. Other causes include chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, obesity, ascites, diaphragmatic paralysis and large pleural effusions.

Assessment

Clinicians quantify orthopnea by the number of pillows needed ("two-pillow orthopnea"). It often accompanies paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea, in which the patient wakes gasping one to two hours after falling asleep. The opposite condition, breathlessness when upright relieved by lying flat, is called platypnoea.

Management

Treatment is directed at the underlying cause — for heart failure, diuretics, afterload reduction and management of fluid overload — together with sleeping in an elevated position for symptom relief.
