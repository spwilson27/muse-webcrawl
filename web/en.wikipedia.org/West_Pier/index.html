West Pier

West Pier
Location: Brighton, East Sussex, England
Opened: 1866
Closed: 1975
Designer: Eugenius Birch
Status: Ruined (pier head destroyed by fire, 2003)

The West Pier is a ruined pier in Brighton, England. Designed by Eugenius Birch and opened in 1866, it was the second pier built in the town and one of the finest pleasure piers of the Victorian era. It was extended in the 1890s with a pavilion and in 1916 with a concert hall, and it remained a popular attraction through the mid-20th century.

Decline and destruction

The pier closed in 1975 as its condition deteriorated and the unauthorised removal of landing stages isolated it from the shore. After years of decay, the pier was severely damaged by storms in December 2002, when part of the concert hall collapsed into the sea, and two fires in March and May 2003 destroyed the remaining pier-head buildings. The skeletal remains of the pier head, standing in the sea off the Brighton seafront, have become an iconic and much-photographed ruin, home to starling murmurations. The West Pier Trust preserves the site's history, and the nearby British Airways i360 tower stands close to the pier's original entrance.
