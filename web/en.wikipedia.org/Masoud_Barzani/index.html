Masoud Barzani

Masoud Barzani (Kurdish: مەسعوود بارزانی, Mesûd Barzanî; born 16 August 1946) is an Iraqi Kurdish politician who has led the Kurdistan Democratic Party (KDP) since 1979 and served as President of the Kurdistan Region of Iraq from 2005 to 2017. He is the son of Mustafa Barzani, the historic leader of the Kurdish national movement in Iraq.

Barzani led the KDP for more than four decades and the Kurdistan Region from 2005 to 2017.

Early life

Barzani was born in Mahabad, in northwestern Iran, where his father was serving as a commander of the short-lived Republic of Mahabad. After its collapse the family went into exile, spending years in the Soviet Union before returning to Iraq after the 1958 revolution. Masoud joined the Peshmerga forces and the KDP as a young man and became a close aide to his father during the Kurdish wars against Baghdad.

KDP leadership

After Mustafa Barzani's death in 1979, Masoud succeeded him as KDP leader. The party survived the Iran–Iraq War, Saddam Hussein's Anfal campaign against the Kurds, and the 1991 Kurdish uprising, after which a Western-protected safe haven allowed the establishment of a de facto autonomous Kurdistan Region. The KDP and its rival, the Patriotic Union of Kurdistan, fought a civil war in the mid-1990s before reconciling. After the 2003 US-led invasion of Iraq, Barzani served on the Iraqi Governing Council and in 2005 became President of the Kurdistan Region, a position he held beyond his second term under a controversial extension.

Independence referendum and aftermath

In September 2017 Barzani presided over a referendum on Kurdish independence, which passed overwhelmingly in the Kurdistan Region. Baghdad rejected the vote, and Iraqi forces retook Kirkuk and other disputed territories; Barzani announced he would not continue as president when his term expired on 1 November 2017. He remains KDP president and the dominant figure of the party.
