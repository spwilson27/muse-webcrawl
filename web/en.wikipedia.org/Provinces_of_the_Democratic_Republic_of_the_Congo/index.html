Provinces of the Democratic Republic of the Congo

The DRC has been divided into 26 provinces since the 2015 découpage took effect.

The Democratic Republic of the Congo is divided into 26 provinces. Article 2 of the 2006 constitution increased the number of provinces from 11 (including the city-province of Kinshasa) to 26, by subdividing six of the old provinces — a process known as découpage. Implementation was delayed for years and finally carried out in 2015.

The provinces

The provinces are: Bas-Uele, Équateur, Haut-Katanga, Haut-Lomami, Haut-Uele, Ituri, Kasaï, Kasaï-Central, Kasaï-Oriental, Kinshasa, Kongo Central, Kwango, Kwilu, Lomami, Lualaba, Mai-Ndombe, Maniema, Mongala, Nord-Kivu, Nord-Ubangi, Sankuru, Sud-Kivu, Sud-Ubangi, Tanganyika, Tshopo and Tshuapa.

Each province is headed by a governor and has a provincial assembly. The subdivision was intended to bring government closer to citizens in one of Africa's largest countries by area, though critics noted the cost of new administrations and the weakness of provincial revenue. Kinshasa, the capital, has the status of both a city and a province.

Historical divisions

Under Belgian colonial rule and in the early independence period the Congo had six provinces; their number and boundaries changed repeatedly under Mobutu's Zaire, when provinces were called regions and included Bandundu, Bas-Zaïre, Équateur, Haut-Zaïre, Kasaï-Occidental, Kasaï-Oriental, Kivu, Nord-Kivu and Shaba (later Katanga). The 26-province map largely restores and subdivides these historic units.
