Stephen Báthory

King of Poland: 1576–1586
Prince of Transylvania: 1571–1586
Born: 27 September 1533, Șimleu Silvaniei, Transylvania
Died: 12 December 1586, Grodno
Spouse: Anna Jagiellon

Stephen Báthory (Hungarian: Báthory István; 27 September 1533 – 12 December 1586) was Voivode and then Prince of Transylvania (1571–1586), and from 1576 King of Poland and Grand Duke of Lithuania, reigning jointly with his wife Anna Jagiellon. He is regarded as one of the most successful kings of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth.

Reign

Elected king after the flight of Henry of Valois, Báthory married Anna Jagiellon to legitimise his rule and overcame the rival candidacy of Emperor Maximilian II, defeating the rebellious city of Gdańsk. He reformed the Polish military, relying on the winged hussars and Hungarian-style infantry, and established the piechota wybraniecka.

Livonian War

In the Livonian War against Ivan the Terrible of Russia, Báthory conducted three successful campaigns (1579–1581), recovering Polotsk and besieging Pskov, which ended with the Truce of Yam-Zapolsky in 1582 restoring Livonia to the Commonwealth. At home he strengthened royal authority, founded the Academy of Vilnius (1579), and maintained religious toleration under the Warsaw Confederation.
