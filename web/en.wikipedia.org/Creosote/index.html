Creosote

Creosote
Type: Wood preservative / tar distillate
Source: Coal tar or wood tar
Uses: Railroad ties, utility poles

Creosote is a category of carbonaceous chemicals formed by the distillation of tars. Coal-tar creosote is the most widely used wood preservative in industrial applications, protecting railroad ties, utility poles, and marine pilings against fungi, insects, and water damage.

Composition and use

Coal-tar creosote is a complex mixture of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) and other compounds. Pressure-treating timber with creosote greatly extends its service life. Wood-tar creosote, once used medicinally, is a different product.

Health and environment

Creosote is a probable human carcinogen, and its use is restricted in many countries to industrial applications, with consumer sales banned in the European Union. Contaminated former treatment sites are common targets of environmental remediation.
