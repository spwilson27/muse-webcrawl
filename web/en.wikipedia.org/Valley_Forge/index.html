Valley Forge

Valley Forge was the winter encampment of the Continental Army under George Washington from December 1777 to June 1778, during the American Revolutionary War. Located about 18 miles northwest of British-occupied Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, the camp became a symbol of endurance and sacrifice.

The winter of 1777–78

Some 12,000 soldiers arrived after defeats at Brandywine and Germantown. Supplies were critically short — men lacked shoes, blankets and food — and disease, especially typhus and dysentery, killed roughly 2,000. Yet conditions improved through the spring as supply reforms under Nathanael Greene took effect and the army built over a thousand log huts, defences and roads.

Training and transformation

The Prussian officer Baron Friedrich Wilhelm von Steuben arrived in February 1778 and drilled the army in a simplified European system of manoeuvre and bayonet use, later codified in his "Blue Book". The encampment also saw the army celebrate news of the French alliance in May 1778.

Aftermath

When the British evacuated Philadelphia in June 1778, the reorganised army marched out and fought them to a draw at the Battle of Monmouth. Today the site is preserved as Valley Forge National Historical Park, with Washington's headquarters, reconstructed huts and the National Memorial Arch.
