FeliCa is a contactless RFID smart card system developed by Sony, used primarily in Japan and other parts of Asia for electronic money, public transportation ticketing, and access control. The name derives from "Felicity Card", reflecting the goal of making everyday transactions fast and convenient.

History

Sony developed FeliCa beginning in the late 1980s, and the technology was first deployed commercially in 1997 in Hong Kong's Octopus card system. In Japan, it became the basis for the Suica card launched by JR East in 2001, as well as the Edy electronic money service. FeliCa chips were later embedded in mobile phones as part of the "Osaifu-Keitai" (mobile wallet) service launched in 2004.

Technology

FeliCa operates at 13.56 MHz and supports communication speeds of 212 kbps and 424 kbps. Unlike many other contactless systems, FeliCa supports mutual authentication and encrypted communication. A single card can host multiple independent services, each with its own access keys and data areas, allowing one card to function simultaneously as a transit pass, e-money wallet, and employee ID. The system was standardized as NFC-F in the NFC Forum specifications, making it one of the three global NFC communication modes.

Applications

FeliCa is used in transit systems across Japan (Suica, PASMO, ICOCA and others), Hong Kong's Octopus, and Singapore's EZ-Link (in earlier generations). It is also used for convenience store payments, vending machines, loyalty programs, and building access. Apple added support for FeliCa in iPhone and Apple Watch models from 2016 onward, enabling Suica use in Apple Pay.
