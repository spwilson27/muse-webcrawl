Dardanelles Gun

TypeBombard (great cannon)
Place of originOttoman Empire
Date1464
FounderMunir Ali
Calibreabout 518 mm (20.4 in) stone balls
Present locationFort Nelson, Hampshire, England (Royal Armouries)

The Dardanelles Gun is a large Ottoman bombard cast in bronze in 1464 by the founder Munir Ali, modelled on the great bombards used in the siege of Constantinople in 1453. It defended the Dardanelles strait for centuries and is now displayed at Fort Nelson in England.

Description

The gun is about 5.2 metres long and is cast in two parts — barrel and powder chamber — which screw together for transport and loading. It fired granite stone balls of about 63 cm in diameter (sources vary slightly on calibre) weighing several hundred kilograms.

Service

Remarkably, the gun was still in active service more than 300 years after its casting: in 1807, during the Dardanelles Operation, ancient Ottoman guns including this piece fired on a British squadron under Sir John Duckworth, inflicting casualties. The sultan presented the gun to Queen Victoria in 1866, and it later passed to the Royal Armouries.
