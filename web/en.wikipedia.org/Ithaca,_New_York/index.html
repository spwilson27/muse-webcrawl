Ithaca, New York

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Ithaca is a city in and the county seat of Tompkins County, New York, United States. Situated on the southern shore of Cayuga Lake in the Finger Lakes region of Central New York, it had a population of about 32,000 at the 2020 census. The city is best known as the home of Cornell University, an Ivy League institution with over 20,000 students.

Geography

Ithaca is known for its gorges and waterfalls — more than 100 within 10 miles of downtown — inspiring the slogan "Ithaca is Gorges." The city lies in a valley carved by glacial action, with steep hills rising around Cayuga Lake.

Education

In addition to Cornell University, Ithaca is home to Ithaca College. The presence of the universities shapes the city's economy, culture, and demographics.

History

The area was originally home to the Cayuga people of the Haudenosaunee. The city was named after the Greek island of Ithaca and was incorporated as a village in 1821 and as a city in 1888. Cornell University was founded in 1865 by Ezra Cornell and Andrew Dickson White.
