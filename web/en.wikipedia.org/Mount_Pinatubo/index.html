Mount Pinatubo

Mount Pinatubo is an active stratovolcano in the Zambales Mountains on Luzon, Philippines. Its eruption on June 15, 1991 was the second largest volcanic eruption of the 20th century, surpassed only by Novarupta in 1912.

The 1991 eruption

After more than 400 years of quiet, Pinatubo awoke in April 1991. Timely monitoring by Philippine and U.S. volcanologists prompted evacuations that saved thousands of lives, including the evacuation of Clark Air Base. The climactic eruption ejected over 10 km³ of material, injected some 20 million tons of sulfur dioxide into the stratosphere, and cooled global temperatures by about 0.5 °C for several years. Ashfall combined with the rains of Typhoon Yunya to collapse roofs and kill several hundred people; lahars continued to bury lowlands for years afterward. The crater now holds Lake Pinatubo, a popular trekking destination. The mountain is sacred to the indigenous Aeta people, many of whom were displaced by the eruption.
