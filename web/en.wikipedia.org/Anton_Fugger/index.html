Anton Fugger

Born10 June 1493, Augsburg
Died14 September 1560 (aged 67), Augsburg
OccupationMerchant, banker, head of the Fugger firm
PredecessorJakob Fugger ("the Rich")

Anton Fugger (10 June 1493 – 14 September 1560) was a German merchant and banker of the Fugger family of Augsburg, who succeeded his uncle Jakob Fugger as head of the family's commercial empire in 1525. Under his direction the Fugger firm reached the height of its wealth and influence, financing the Habsburg emperors Charles V and Ferdinand I and trading in metals, spices, and textiles across Europe and overseas. He was raised to the hereditary nobility and made an Imperial Count in 1530.

Early life and succession

Anton was the third son of Georg Fugger and Regina Imhof. After the deaths of his older brothers Raymund and Hieronymus, and in the absence of a direct heir to his childless uncle Jakob, he was designated successor and trained in the firm's branches in Rome, Venice, and Hungary. Jakob's will of 1525 left the entire business to Anton and his nephews, with Anton as managing director.

Head of the Fugger firm

Anton consolidated the firm's mining interests in Tyrol, Hungary, and Spain (including the mercury mines of Almadén, essential to American silver refining), expanded its trade with the Spanish and Portuguese Indies, and acted as principal banker to Charles V, lending enormous sums for imperial elections, wars against France and the Ottomans, and the Schmalkaldic War. The firm's fortune under Anton is estimated to have more than doubled, reaching some five million guilders. He purchased extensive estates in Swabia, including Babenhausen and Kirchheim, and in 1530 Charles V made the Fuggers hereditary Imperial Counts.

Later years and legacy

The later 1550s brought strain: the Spanish crown's suspensions of payment (1557) hit the firm's Habsburg loans, and Anton began withdrawing capital into land. A famous anecdote has him burning Charles V's bond before the emperor's eyes to demonstrate his wealth — a story later retold of his uncle Jakob. Anton died in Augsburg on 14 September 1560. He continued the family's philanthropy, maintaining the Fuggerei housing settlement for the poor founded by his uncle, and his sons were unable to prevent the long decline of the firm in the following decades.
