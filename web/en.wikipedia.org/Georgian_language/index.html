Georgian (ქართული ენა, kartuli ena) is the official language of the country of Georgia and the largest of the Kartvelian (South Caucasian) languages, a family unrelated to any other established language family. It is written in its own distinctive alphabet and has a literary tradition stretching back to the 5th century AD.

History

The earliest surviving Georgian inscriptions date from the 5th century, and Old Georgian literature includes saints' lives, hymns and inscriptions. Georgian is traditionally said to have been created in connection with the Christianisation of Georgia in the 4th century. Three historical scripts have been used: Asomtavruli, Nuskhuri and the now-standard Mkhedruli, whose 33 letters correspond closely to the language's sounds.

Features

Georgian is an agglutinative language famous for its complex verb morphology — a single verb can encode subject, object and other information — and for its large consonant clusters, as in words like gvp'rtskvni ("you peel us"). It has five vowels and no grammatical gender. Its grammar includes split ergativity, in which case marking changes with tense. Related Kartvelian languages include Mingrelian, Laz and Svan.

Dialects and status

Standard Georgian is based on the Kartlian dialect of the east. The language's status was a sensitive issue under Russian imperial and Soviet rule; mass demonstrations in Tbilisi in 1978 successfully defended Georgian's constitutional status as the state language of the Georgian SSR, and 14 April is now celebrated as Georgian Language Day.
