Flypast

A flypast (known in the United States as a flyover) is a ceremonial or celebratory flight of one or more aircraft over a specific location, usually at low altitude and in formation. Flypasts are performed at state occasions, military parades, air shows, sporting events, and commemorations.

Ceremonial use

In the United Kingdom, Royal Air Force flypasts mark events such as Trooping the Colour and the monarch's official birthday, often concluding with the Red Arrows trailing red, white, and blue smoke over Buckingham Palace. In France, aircraft fly over the Champs-Élysées during the Bastille Day military parade; in Russia, large formations feature in Victory Day commemorations in Moscow.

United States flyovers

The U.S. Air Force, Navy, and other services perform flyovers at the Super Bowl and other major sporting events, at military funerals, and at national ceremonies. Teams such as the Blue Angels and Thunderbirds combine flyovers with demonstration flying. A "missing man formation," in which one aircraft pulls away from the formation, honors fallen aviators at memorial services.

Coordination

Flypasts demand precise timing so that aircraft arrive over the focal point at exactly the scheduled moment, often coordinated to the second with ground ceremonies. Aircraft typically hold at a distance and fly a carefully planned route, sometimes rehearsing days in advance.
