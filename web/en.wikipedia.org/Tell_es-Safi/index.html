Tell es-Safi

Tell es-Safi (Arabic: تل الصافي, Tall aṣ-Ṣāfī) is an archaeological site in central Israel, identified as the biblical Philistine city of Gath, one of the five cities of the Philistine pentapolis and the hometown of Goliath. The tell is recognizable by its white chalk cliffs and covers about 50 hectares.

Excavations

The site was described by explorers in the 19th century and first excavated by Frederick Bliss and R. A. S. Macalister in 1899–1900. Since 1996 it has been excavated by Aren Maeir of Bar-Ilan University. Discoveries include a 9th-century BCE inscription with names resembling "Goliath", an early alphabetic inscription, and evidence of the destruction by Hazael of Aram around 830 BCE, including a siege trench — one of the earliest known siege systems.

History

The site was occupied from the Chalcolithic period through the Bronze and Iron Ages, flourishing as Philistine Gath in the Iron Age IIA before its destruction by Hazael. Later settlements included a Crusader castle (Blanchegarde) and the Arab village of Tell es-Safi, depopulated in 1948.
