Monterey Pop Festival

Monterey International Pop Festival

Dates: June 16–18, 1967
Location: Monterey County Fairgrounds, Monterey, California
Organizers: John Phillips, Lou Adler, Derek Taylor
Attendance: est. 25,000–90,000

The Monterey International Pop Festival was a three-day music festival held June 16–18, 1967, at the Monterey County Fairgrounds in Monterey, California. It was a landmark event of the counterculture era and a defining moment of the "Summer of Love", widely regarded as one of the first major rock festivals and a template for later events such as Woodstock.

Performances

The festival featured breakout American performances by Janis Joplin (with Big Brother and the Holding Company), Jimi Hendrix, and Otis Redding, alongside The Who, Simon & Garfunkel, The Mamas & the Papas, Jefferson Airplane, The Byrds, Buffalo Springfield, Ravi Shankar, Laura Nyro, and others. Two moments became legendary: Hendrix setting his guitar on fire during "Wild Thing", and The Who smashing their instruments. Redding's soul set, backed by Booker T. & the M.G.'s, introduced him to a mainstream white audience.

Organization and legacy

The festival was planned by John Phillips of The Mamas & the Papas, producer Lou Adler, and publicist Derek Taylor, with a board that included members of The Beatles and The Beach Boys. Artists performed without fees, and proceeds went to charity. D. A. Pennebaker's concert film Monterey Pop (1968) documented the event and cemented its reputation. The festival is credited with launching the careers of Joplin, Hendrix, and Redding in the United States and with establishing the model of the large-scale rock festival.
