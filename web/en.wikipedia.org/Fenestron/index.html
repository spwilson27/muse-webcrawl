Fenestron

Fenestron
InventorSud Aviation (Airbus Helicopters)
First flight use1968 (SA 340 Gazelle prototype)
TypeDucted fan anti-torque system

A Fenestron is a protected tail rotor of a helicopter operating like a ducted fan. The housing in the tail fin is integral with the tail boom, and, like a conventional tail rotor, it counters torque on the main rotor. The term Fenestron is a trademark of Airbus Helicopters; the concept was developed by Sud Aviation under engineer Paul Fabre.

Design

Unlike a conventional open tail rotor with two to five blades, a Fenestron contains a multi-blade fan (typically 8 to 18 blades) turning within a duct built into the vertical fin. The blades are often unevenly spaced to spread noise across frequencies, and their pitch is varied collectively to control thrust.

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages include reduced risk of the rotor striking people or objects, protection from foreign-object damage, lower noise, and reduced susceptibility to vortex interference. Disadvantages include greater weight, higher construction cost, and greater power consumption in hover compared with an equivalent open rotor, which is why Fenestrons are most common on light and medium helicopters such as the Gazelle, Dauphin, EC130, and H160.
