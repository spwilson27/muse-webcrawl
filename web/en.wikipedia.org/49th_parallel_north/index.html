The 49th parallel north is a circle of latitude that is 49 degrees north of the Earth's equatorial plane. It crosses Europe, Asia, the Pacific Ocean, North America, and the Atlantic Ocean. The parallel is best known as the demarcation of a long stretch of the international border between Canada and the United States, giving rise to the expression "the 49th parallel" as a synonym for that border.

Canada–United States border

Under the Convention of 1818, the border between British North America and the United States was set along the 49th parallel from the Lake of the Woods to the Rocky Mountains. The Oregon Treaty of 1846 extended the line westward to the Strait of Georgia, though the line deviates through the waters around Vancouver Island, which remains entirely Canadian. Surveyed in the 19th century, the actual border markers deviate slightly from the geometric parallel due to surveying inaccuracies.

Around the world

Starting at the prime meridian and heading eastward, the parallel passes through the English Channel, France (just south of Paris), Germany, the Czech Republic, Poland, Ukraine, Russia, Kazakhstan, Mongolia, China, and Japan's northern seas before crossing the Pacific. At this latitude the sun is visible for about 16 hours, 12 minutes during the summer solstice and 8 hours, 14 minutes during the winter solstice.
