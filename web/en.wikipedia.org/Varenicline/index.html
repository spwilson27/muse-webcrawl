Varenicline

Varenicline
Trade names: Chantix, Champix
Use: Smoking cessation
Routes: Oral
Legal status: Prescription only

Varenicline, sold under the brand names Chantix and Champix, is a medication used for smoking cessation. It is a partial agonist at nicotinic acetylcholine receptors, reducing the rewarding effects of nicotine while easing withdrawal symptoms and cravings.

Medical use

Treatment typically begins one to two weeks before a quit date, with doses titrated over the first week and continued for about twelve weeks. Clinical trials show varenicline roughly doubles to triples the odds of quitting compared with placebo and is more effective than bupropion or single-form nicotine replacement.

Mechanism

Varenicline binds with high affinity to the α4β2 nicotinic receptor, the main subtype mediating nicotine dependence. As a partial agonist it stimulates the receptor enough to relieve craving while blocking nicotine from producing its full rewarding effect.

Adverse effects

The most common side effect is nausea; abnormal dreams, insomnia and headache also occur. Early concerns about neuropsychiatric effects led to a boxed warning in the United States in 2009, which was removed in 2016 after the large EAGLES trial found no significant increase in such events. In 2021, Pfizer recalled Chantix lots over nitrosamine impurities, temporarily halting distribution.
