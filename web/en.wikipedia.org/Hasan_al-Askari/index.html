Hasan al-Askari

Hasan ibn Ali al-Askari (c. 846 – 874) was the eleventh of the Twelve Imams in Twelver Shia Islam. He succeeded his father, Ali al-Hadi, in 868 and spent most of his life under house arrest by the Abbasid caliphs in Samarra, in the military camp district (al-ʿAskar) from which his epithet derives.

Life

Born in Medina, Hasan was brought as a small child to Samarra when the caliph al-Mutawakkil summoned his father there. Under close Abbasid surveillance, he communicated with his followers chiefly through a network of deputies (the wikala system), which collected religious taxes and conveyed questions and answers. A collection of Quranic exegesis is attributed to him, though its authorship is debated.

Death and succession

Hasan al-Askari died in 874 at about 28 years of age, reportedly poisoned on the orders of the caliph al-Mu'tamid according to Shia tradition. Twelver Shias believe he was succeeded by his young son Muhammad, the Mahdi, whose occultation began at this time. Hasan is buried beside his father in the al-Askari shrine in Samarra, a major pilgrimage site that was bombed in 2006, sparking widespread sectarian violence in Iraq.
