Pediatrician

A pediatrician (American English) or paediatrician (British English) is a medical doctor who manages the physical, behavioural and mental care of children from birth until age 18 or 21. Pediatrics differs from adult medicine because children's bodies are growing and their diseases and responses to treatment differ.

Pediatrician
Occupation: Physician
Field: Pediatrics
Training: Medical degree plus residency in pediatrics

Training

After medical school, pediatricians complete a residency of about three years in pediatrics, and may subspecialise in areas such as neonatology, cardiology, oncology, emergency medicine or developmental-behavioural pediatrics.

Scope of practice

Pediatricians provide preventive well-child visits, immunisations, developmental screening, and diagnosis and treatment of illnesses ranging from infections and genetic disorders to allergies such as lactose intolerance and conditions of malabsorption. They also counsel families on nutrition, safety and behaviour.

History

Pediatrics emerged as a specialty in the 19th century, with figures such as Abraham Jacobi in the United States, alongside the growth of children's hospitals.
