Pulmonary valve

The pulmonary valve (sometimes called the pulmonic valve) is the semilunar valve of the heart that lies between the right ventricle and the pulmonary artery. It has three cusps, or leaflets, which open when ventricular pressure exceeds arterial pressure during systole, allowing deoxygenated blood to flow to the lungs, and close in diastole to prevent backflow.

Disorders

Pulmonary valve stenosis, a narrowing often congenital, obstructs right ventricular outflow and can occur as part of tetralogy of Fallot. Pulmonary regurgitation (incompetence) is frequently seen after repair of Fallot's tetralogy and may require valve replacement, increasingly performed by transcatheter techniques.
