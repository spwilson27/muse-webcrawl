Clermont-Ferrand

Clermont-Ferrand is a city and commune in central France, the prefecture of the Puy-de-Dôme department in the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region. With a population of roughly 147,000 in the commune and over 500,000 in its metropolitan area, it is one of France's oldest cities, set on the edge of the Chaîne des Puys volcanic range.

History

The city grew from the Gallo-Roman town of Augustonemetum, capital of the Arverni, whose chief Vercingetorix won the nearby Battle of Gergovia against Julius Caesar in 52 BC. In 1095, at the Council of Clermont, Pope Urban II preached the call that launched the First Crusade. The present city results from the union of the rival towns of Clermont and Montferrand, joined by edict in 1630 and confirmed in 1731. Its gothic cathedral is built of black lava stone from the surrounding volcanoes.

Economy and culture

Clermont-Ferrand is the historic home of the tyre manufacturer Michelin, founded there in 1889 and still headquartered in the city, and hosts the Université Clermont Auvergne. The city is known for the Clermont-Ferrand International Short Film Festival, the largest of its kind in the world, and for the ASM Clermont Auvergne rugby union club.
