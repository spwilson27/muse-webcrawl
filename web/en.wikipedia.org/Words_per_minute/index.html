Words per minute

Unit ofRate (reading, speaking, typing)
Symbolwpm
Standard word5 characters, including spaces

Words per minute, commonly abbreviated WPM (sometimes wpm), is the speed at which words are processed. It is commonly used as a measurement of the speed of typing, reading, or Morse code reception and transmission. For the purposes of WPM measurement, a word is standardized to five characters or keystrokes, including spaces and punctuation.

Typing

An average professional typist types about 65 to 75 WPM; average computer users type around 40 WPM. "Gross WPM" is calculated by dividing all characters typed by five and by the minutes taken, while "net WPM" subtracts a penalty for uncorrected errors, giving a more meaningful measure of productive speed. Competitive typists regularly exceed 120 WPM, and the fastest recorded speeds approach 200 WPM in short bursts.

Reading

Adults typically read English prose at about 200–300 WPM with good comprehension. Speed-reading techniques claim rates of 700 WPM or more, usually with reduced comprehension; research suggests a natural ceiling around 400–500 WPM for full comprehension of unfamiliar material.

Speech

Conversational English is usually spoken at roughly 150 WPM. Audiobook narration often falls between 150 and 160 WPM, while auctioneers and sports commentators may exceed 250 WPM. In Morse code, speed in WPM traditionally uses the word "PARIS" as the standard, which takes 50 units of time.
