South Waziristan

South Waziristan District
Country: Pakistan
Province: Khyber Pakhtunkhwa
Headquarters: Wana
Area: about 6,600 km²

South Waziristan is a district of Pakistan's Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province, bordering Afghanistan to the west. It is the largest of the former tribal agencies of the Federally Administered Tribal Areas (FATA), which were merged into Khyber Pakhtunkhwa in 2018. Its administrative centre is Wana. The rugged, mountainous territory is inhabited mainly by Pashtun tribes, principally the Wazir and the Mehsud, long noted for their fierce independence.

History

Under British rule Waziristan was a byword for resistance: expeditions and campaigns were mounted repeatedly from the 19th century through the 1930s, and the fakir of Ipi led a guerrilla campaign against the British and later Pakistan. The region was administered through political agents and tribal jirgas under the Frontier Crimes Regulation.

Militancy and military operations

After 2001, South Waziristan became a stronghold of the Pakistani Taliban: Baitullah Mehsud, founder of the Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan, was based there until killed by a US drone strike in 2009. The Pakistan Army's Operation Rah-e-Nijat (2009) cleared much of the Mehsud territory at the cost of mass displacement, and the area has remained a focus of counter-insurgency, drone strikes and development efforts since.

Geography

The district drains through the Gomal and Tochi river systems toward the Indus plain; its valleys support pine forests, terraced farming and seasonal migration, and the historic Gomal Pass links it to Afghanistan.
