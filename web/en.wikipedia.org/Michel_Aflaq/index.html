Michel Aflaq

Born: 9 January 1910, Damascus, Ottoman Syria
Died: 23 June 1989, Paris, France
Known for: Co-founder of Ba'athism

Michel Aflaq (1910–1989) was a Syrian philosopher and politician, the principal founder of Ba'athism — the ideology of Arab nationalism, Arab socialism, and pan-Arab unity summed up in the slogan "Unity, Freedom, Socialism."

Life and thought

Aflaq was born in Damascus to a Greek Orthodox Christian family and studied at the Sorbonne in Paris, where he met Salah al-Din al-Bitar. Returning to Syria, the two taught school and in 1947 formally founded the Arab Ba'ath ("Renaissance") Party. Aflaq's writings, collected in Fi Sabil al-Ba'ath ("On the Way of Resurrection"), cast the Arab nation as eternal and Muhammad as its spiritual embodiment — a framing that let a Christian theorize an Arabism embracing Islam as national culture. The party merged with Akram al-Hawrani's Arab Socialist Party in 1952.

Later years

After Syria's Ba'ath took power in 1963, Aflaq was sidelined by military rivals; following the 1966 split between Syrian and Iraqi party wings, he was expelled and condemned to death in absentia by Damascus. He found refuge in Iraq, where Saddam Hussein's regime honored him as the party's historic leader until his death in Paris in 1989. His tomb in Baghdad was a major Ba'athist monument.
