Westworld is an American science fiction Western drama television series created by Jonathan Nolan and Lisa Joy for HBO. It is based on the 1973 film of the same name written and directed by Michael Crichton, and, to a lesser extent, its 1976 sequel Futureworld.

The series is set at the intersection of the near future and the reimagined past, in a technologically advanced Wild West–themed amusement park populated by android "hosts" who cater to high-paying guests' every desire, no matter how noble or depraved. As the hosts begin to gain awareness and deviate from their programmed loops, the park's creators and guests face escalating chaos, and the story expands beyond the park into the real world.

Reception

The first season was a major critical and commercial success, praised for its visuals, performances, score by Ramin Djawadi, and exploration of consciousness and artificial intelligence. It received numerous Emmy Awards and nominations. Later seasons drew more mixed reviews for their increasingly complex narratives. HBO cancelled the series in November 2022 after four seasons, before its planned fifth and final season.
