Balabhadra

Balabhadra is a Hindu deity, better known as Balarama, the elder brother of Krishna. He is worshipped in his own right and, most famously, as part of the Jagannath triad at Puri, Odisha, where he is venerated alongside Jagannath (Krishna) and their sister Subhadra.

Mythology

In the Sanskrit epics and Puranas, Balabhadra is the son of Vasudeva and Rohini, transferred as an embryo from Devaki's womb to Rohini's, and is famed for his immense strength, his plough (hala) and mace, and his fondness for wine. He taught the martial arts to both Bhima and Duryodhana and remained neutral in the Kurukshetra war. He is sometimes identified as an incarnation of the serpent Shesha and in some traditions as an avatar of Vishnu.

Worship

Balabhadra is associated with agriculture and strength, and his plough makes him a patron of farmers. At the Jagannath Temple in Puri, his stylised wooden image, tall and light-coloured, is carried in procession with Jagannath and Subhadra during the annual Ratha Yatra festival. His cult also survives in the Balarama temples and in the devotional traditions of the Vaishnava movements that revere Krishna's family.
