House Un-American Activities Committee

Formed1938
Abolished1975
TypeInvestigative committee of the U.S. House of Representatives

The House Un-American Activities Committee (HUAC) was an investigative committee of the United States House of Representatives, created in 1938 to investigate alleged disloyalty and subversive activities on the part of private citizens and organisations suspected of having communist ties.

History

HUAC originated as the Special Committee on Un-American Activities, chaired by Martin Dies Jr., which initially investigated both Nazi and communist sympathies. It became a standing committee in 1945. During the early Cold War, HUAC became prominent for its investigations of the motion picture industry: in 1947 it held hearings on alleged communist influence in Hollywood, leading to the blacklisting of the "Hollywood Ten" and many others who refused to testify or name names.

Criticism and decline

The committee's methods — compelling testimony about political beliefs and associations — drew criticism as violations of the First Amendment and as a form of public shaming; the term "McCarthyism" (associated chiefly with Senator Joseph McCarthy's separate activities) came to describe the era's anti-communist investigations generally. HUAC's influence waned in the 1960s as courts constrained its powers, and it was abolished in 1975, its functions transferred to the House Judiciary Committee.
