Boeing XF6B

The Boeing XF6B (Boeing Model 236) was a prototype carrier-based fighter-bomber built for the United States Navy in the early 1930s. Powered by a 625 hp Pratt & Whitney R-1535 Twin Wasp Junior radial engine, it was Boeing's last biplane fighter design for the Navy.

Development and testing

Ordered in 1931 and first flown in 1933, the XF6B combined an all-metal semi-monocoque fuselage with fabric-covered wings. Although its performance was respectable, the Navy found it offered insufficient improvement as a fighter, and its role shifted toward fighter-bomber evaluation under the designation XBFB-1. Only the single prototype was built, as naval aviation moved toward monoplanes such as the Grumman F2F.

Specifications

Single-seat biplane; length about 22 ft; maximum speed around 200 mph (320 km/h); armament two .30-calibre machine guns and provision for light bombs.
