Thérèse of Lisieux (French: Thérèse de Lisieux; born Marie Françoise-Thérèse Martin, 2 January 1873 – 30 September 1897), also known as Saint Thérèse of the Child Jesus and the Holy Face, was a French Catholic Discalced Carmelite nun who is widely venerated in modern times. She is popularly known in English as “the Little Flower of Jesus” or simply “the Little Flower”.

Life

Thérèse was born in Alençon, the youngest daughter of Louis and Zélie Martin, both later canonized themselves. Her mother died when Thérèse was four. After a childhood marked by illness and sensitivity, she experienced a conversion on Christmas night 1886 and resolved to enter the Carmelite convent at Lisieux, where two of her sisters had already professed. Granted special permission because of her youth, she entered in April 1888, aged fifteen, taking the name Thérèse of the Child Jesus. She later added “and the Holy Face”.

The Little Way

Thérèse developed a spirituality she called the “little way” (la petite voie): seeking holiness not in great deeds but in doing small things with great love and in trusting in God's mercy like a child. Ordered by her prioress to write her memoirs, she produced the notebooks published after her death as The Story of a Soul. She died of tuberculosis on 30 September 1897, aged 24, promising to “spend my heaven doing good on earth” and to let fall a “shower of roses”.

Veneration and influence

Beatified in 1923 and canonized in 1925 by Pope Pius XI, Thérèse became one of the most popular saints of the twentieth century. The Basilica of Saint Thérèse in Lisieux is a major pilgrimage site. In 1997 Pope John Paul II declared her a Doctor of the Church — the third woman so honoured — and she is a patron saint of France, missionaries, and florists. Her parents, Louis and Zélie Martin, were canonized in 2015, the first married couple canonized together.
