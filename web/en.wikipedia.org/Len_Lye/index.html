Len Lye

Len Lye (1901–1980) was a New Zealand artist known for experimental films made by drawing and scratching directly on film stock, and for large kinetic sculptures of moving steel.

Film

Working in London in the 1930s, Lye made Colour Box (1935) by painting directly on film for the British Post Office. In New York he became a central figure of avant-garde cinema, pursuing what he called an art of motion.

Sculpture

His motorised stainless-steel works — such as Wind Wand and the monumental pieces planned for a never-built Temple — move and ring in the wind. The Len Lye Centre in New Plymouth, opened in 2015 with a rippling mirrored facade, houses his work in New Zealand.
