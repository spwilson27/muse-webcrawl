Fourth derivative

The fourth derivative is the derivative of the third derivative of a function.

In calculus, the fourth derivative is the derivative of the third derivative of a function — that is, the result of differentiating a function four times in succession. In Leibniz notation it is written d4f/dx4; in Lagrange's notation, f⁗(x) or f(4)(x).

In physics

Where the function describes position as a function of time, its successive derivatives have standard names: velocity (first), acceleration (second), jerk (third), and for the fourth derivative, snap or jounce. A passenger's comfort, and the design of cams and motion profiles, depend on keeping these higher derivatives bounded: a motion with continuous snap feels smooth. Higher derivatives still — the fifth and sixth — are sometimes called crackle and pop.

Applications

The fourth derivative appears in the theory of Taylor series, in the analysis of inflection behaviour, and in beam theory: the deflection of an elastic beam under a distributed load is governed by a fourth-order differential equation relating the load to the fourth derivative of the deflection curve. Fourth-order derivatives also occur in the Cahn–Hilliard equation and other higher-order partial differential equations.
