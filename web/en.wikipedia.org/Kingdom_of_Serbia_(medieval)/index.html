Kingdom of Serbia (medieval)

The medieval Kingdom of Serbia was a Serbian monarchy in the Balkans that existed from the coronation of Stefan the First-Crowned in 1217 until the proclamation of the Serbian Empire in 1346. It developed from the Grand Principality of Serbia under the Nemanjić dynasty and became the dominant power in the southwestern Balkans.

Foundations

Stefan Nemanja (1166–1196) united the Serbian lands of Raška and Zeta, and his son Rastko — Saint Sava — secured autocephaly for the Serbian Orthodox Church in 1219. Stefan the First-Crowned received a royal crown from Pope Honorius III in 1217 and a second coronation from a Byzantine-rite legate, balancing Rome and Constantinople. The dynasty founded enduring monasteries — Studenica, Žiča, Mileševa, Sopoćani — centres of Serbian art and literacy, many now UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

Expansion

Under Stefan Milutin (1282–1321) Serbia expanded deep into Byzantine Macedonia, developed rich silver mines such as Novo Brdo worked by Saxon miners, and prospered from Ragusan trade. Stefan Dečanski won the great victory over Bulgaria at Velbazhd (1330), and his son Stefan Dušan conquered Albania, Epirus, Thessaly and most of Macedonia, crowned himself Emperor of the Serbs and Greeks at Skopje in 1346 and promulgated Dušan's Code (1349–1354). After Dušan's death in 1355 the empire fragmented, and Serbian resistance to the advancing Ottomans culminated at the Battle of Kosovo in 1389; the Serbian Despotate survived until 1459.

Culture

The medieval kingdom produced a distinctive Raška school of church architecture and fresco painting blending Romanesque and Byzantine styles, and its monasteries preserved Serbian manuscripts and identity through centuries of Ottoman rule.
