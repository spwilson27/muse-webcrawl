Wiener filter

In signal processing, the Wiener filter is a filter used to produce an estimate of a desired or target random process by linear time-invariant (LTI) filtering of an observed noisy process, assuming known stationary signal and noise spectra.

History

The filter was proposed by Norbert Wiener during the 1940s and published in the 1949 classified-then-released report on extrapolation, interpolation and smoothing of stationary time series. Andrey Kolmogorov had published analogous results for discrete time in 1941.

Description

The goal is to minimize the mean square error between the estimate and the desired signal. In the frequency domain, the optimal filter multiplies each frequency component by the ratio of signal power to total (signal plus noise) power, attenuating frequencies where noise dominates.

Applications

Wiener filters are widely used in image deblurring, noise reduction, echo cancellation, channel equalization, and linear prediction. In image restoration they form the basis of the Wiener deconvolution method.

Limitations

The theory assumes the signal and noise are stationary random processes with known spectral characteristics, conditions that in practice must be estimated or approximated.
