The Dreyse needle gun (German: Zündnadelgewehr) was a military breech-loading bolt-action rifle, famous as the main infantry weapon of the Prussians, who adopted it for service in 1841 as the leichtes Infanteriegewehr Modell 1841. It was named after its inventor, Johann Nikolaus von Dreyse.

Mechanism

The rifle fired a paper cartridge containing the bullet, powder charge, and a percussion cap seated at the base of the bullet inside a sabot. When the bolt was closed and the trigger pulled, a long needle-like firing pin pierced the paper case and powder to strike the cap, igniting the charge. Because it loaded from the breech, a soldier could reload while prone and achieve a much higher rate of fire than with muzzle-loading rifles.

Service history

The needle gun gave Prussia a decisive firepower advantage in the Second Schleswig War (1864), the Austro-Prussian War (1866)—notably at Königgrätz—and the Franco-Prussian War (1870–71). Its weaknesses included gas leakage at the breech, breakage or fouling of the fragile needle, and shorter effective range than the French Chassepot. After 1871 it was replaced by the metallic-cartridge Mauser Model 1871.
