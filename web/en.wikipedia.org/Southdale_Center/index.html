Southdale Center

Southdale Center is a shopping mall located in Edina, Minnesota, a suburb of Minneapolis. Opened on October 8, 1956, it is widely recognised as the first fully enclosed, climate-controlled shopping mall in the United States, and it became the prototype for the modern indoor mall.

History and design

Southdale was developed by the Dayton Company and designed by the Austrian-born architect Victor Gruen. Intended as the centre of a dense new suburban community (with housing, schools, and offices that were mostly never built), the mall gathered competing department stores — Dayton's and Donaldson's — around a three-level, daylit "Garden Court" filled with plants, fountains, and a large birdcage aviary. Its enclosed, temperature-controlled interior answered Minnesota's harsh winters and created a new model that was copied across North America for the next half-century.

Later years

The centre has been remodelled and expanded repeatedly, surviving the decline of many of its imitators. It remains anchored by stores such as Macy's and hosts upscale retail in the affluent Minneapolis–Saint Paul market. In 2020 the site added the luxury Life Time Living residential development, partly realising Gruen's original mixed-use vision.
