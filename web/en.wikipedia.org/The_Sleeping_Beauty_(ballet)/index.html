The Sleeping Beauty (ballet)

The Sleeping Beauty (Russian: Spyashchaya krasavitsa) is a ballet in a prologue and three acts, with music by Pyotr Ilyich Tchaikovsky, choreography by Marius Petipa, and a libretto by Ivan Vsevolozhsky and Petipa after Charles Perrault's fairy tale. It premiered at the Mariinsky Theatre, St. Petersburg, on 15 January 1890.

Synopsis

Princess Aurora is cursed by the fairy Carabosse to prick her finger on a spindle and die; the Lilac Fairy softens the curse to a 100-year sleep. Prince Désiré awakens her with a kiss, and their wedding is celebrated with divertissements including Puss in Boots, Little Red Riding Hood, and the Bluebird pas de deux. Famous numbers include the Rose Adagio.

Status

Tchaikovsky's second ballet is considered the pinnacle of classical ballet and Petipa's masterpiece. See also The Sound of Music (film).
