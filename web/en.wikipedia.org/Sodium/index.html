Sodium is a chemical element with the symbol Na (from Latin natrium) and atomic number 11. It is a soft, silvery-white, highly reactive metal belonging to the alkali metals. Sodium is the sixth most abundant element in the Earth's crust and is essential for life, most familiar as a component of table salt (sodium chloride).

Properties

Metallic sodium is soft enough to be cut with a knife and tarnishes rapidly in air as it oxidises. It reacts vigorously — sometimes explosively — with water, releasing hydrogen and heat. Sodium has only one stable isotope, 23Na. When burned, it gives an intense yellow flame used in sodium-vapour lamps.

History

Compounds of sodium (soda) were known and traded from antiquity, but the metal itself was first isolated in 1807 by Humphry Davy through electrolysis of caustic soda. The symbol Na derives from natrium, reflecting the mineral natron.

Compounds and uses

Industrially important compounds include sodium chloride (salt), sodium hydroxide (caustic soda), sodium carbonate (soda ash), and sodium bicarbonate (baking soda). Metallic sodium is used as a coolant in some fast-neutron reactors, in chemical manufacture, and in street lighting.

Biological role

Sodium ions are a major electrolyte: together with potassium they generate the membrane potentials of cells, enabling nerve impulses and muscle contraction. Human health depends on dietary balance — excessive sodium intake is linked to hypertension — while the sodium–potassium pump consumes a large share of cellular energy.
