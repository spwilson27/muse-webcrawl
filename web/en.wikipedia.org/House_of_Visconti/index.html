House of Visconti

The House of Visconti (Italian: Casa Visconti) was an Italian noble family that ruled Milan and much of Lombardy from the late 13th century until 1447. The family's symbol was the biscione, a serpent shown devouring a man, which remains an emblem of Milan.

History

The Visconti rose to dominance in Milan under Ottone Visconti, Archbishop of Milan, whose victory over the rival Della Torre family at the Battle of Desio in 1277 established the family's rule. His great-nephew Matteo I Visconti consolidated power as lord of Milan and imperial vicar of Lombardy.

Successive lords expanded Milanese territory across northern Italy. In 1395 Gian Galeazzo Visconti obtained the hereditary title of Duke of Milan from Emperor Wenceslaus, founding the Duchy of Milan and ruling a state that included much of present-day Lombardy and beyond. Under Filippo Maria Visconti, the last of the main line, Milan fought a series of wars with Venice and Florence. On his death in 1447 without a male heir, Milan briefly became the Golden Ambrosian Republic before passing in 1450 to Francesco Sforza, husband of Filippo Maria's daughter Bianca Maria Visconti.

Legacy

The Visconti were notable patrons of the arts and architecture, associated with the construction of Milan Cathedral (begun 1386) and the Certosa di Pavia. Descendants of collateral branches survive to this day; the film director Luchino Visconti was a member of the family.
