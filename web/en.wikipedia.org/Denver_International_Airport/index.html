Denver International Airport (IATA: DEN), locally called DIA, is the primary airport serving the Denver metropolitan area in Colorado, United States. Opened in 1995, it covers about 34,000 acres — more than one and a half times the size of Manhattan and the largest airport site in North America — with six runways, including one of the longest public-use runways in the United States (16,000 ft). It is a major hub for United Airlines and a base for Frontier and Southwest, and ranks among the world's busiest airports by passenger traffic.

History

DIA replaced Stapleton International Airport, which was hemmed in by the city and plagued by noise complaints. Construction began in 1989; the project became notorious for delays and cost overruns, largely caused by a failed automated baggage system designed to move luggage throughout the airport, which was ultimately abandoned after a scaled-down version served United only. The opening, planned for 1993, slipped to 28 February 1995, and the final cost reached about $4.8 billion, roughly $2 billion over budget.

Facilities and design

The Jeppesen Terminal is roofed by a white Teflon-coated fibreglass fabric canopy of 34 peaks, designed by Fentress Architects to evoke the snow-capped Rocky Mountains (and, some say, Native American teepees). Three concourses (A, B, C) lie east of the terminal, reached by an automated train; Concourse B serves United, whose gates stretch over a mile. A 519-room Westin hotel and transit centre with commuter rail service to downtown Denver (the A Line) opened in 2015–2016. Public art includes Luis Jiménez's 32-foot blue mustang sculpture Blue Mustang — nicknamed "Blucifer" — whose falling segment killed the artist during its fabrication in 2006.

Conspiracy theories

DIA is the subject of persistent conspiracy theories alleging vast underground bunkers, secret societies, and sinister messages in its murals and dedication stone (which mentions a "New World Airport Commission"). Airport officials have repeatedly and good-humouredly denied the claims, at times marketing the airport's "conspiracy" fame in its own construction signage.
