Frankfurt Parliament

The Frankfurt Parliament (German: Frankfurter Nationalversammlung) was the first freely elected parliament for all German states, including the German-populated areas of the Austrian Empire, elected on 1 May 1848 during the German revolutions of 1848–1849. The session was held from 18 May 1848 to 31 May 1849 in the Paulskirche (St. Paul's Church) in Frankfurt am Main. Its existence was both part of and the result of the "March Revolution" within the states of the German Confederation.

The National Assembly in Frankfurt's Paulskirche.

Work and constitution

The roughly 800 deputies (585 voting members at full strength) were mostly educated middle-class men — officials, professors, lawyers — earning it the nickname "professors' parliament". The assembly proclaimed basic rights (Grundrechte) in December 1848 and adopted the Frankfurt Constitution in March 1849, envisioning a unified German federal state as a constitutional monarchy. It chose the kleindeutsche Lösung (lesser German solution, excluding Austria) and offered the imperial crown to King Frederick William IV of Prussia.

Failure

Frederick William refused the crown — famously declining to pick up a crown "from the gutter" — and Prussia and Austria withdrew their deputies. The assembly's attempt to enforce the constitution by campaign collapsed; the rump parliament moved to Stuttgart and was dispersed by Württemberg troops on 18 June 1849. Although it failed, the Frankfurt Parliament founded German parliamentary tradition, and the West German Parliamentary Council of 1948–49 consciously honoured its centenary when drafting the Basic Law.
