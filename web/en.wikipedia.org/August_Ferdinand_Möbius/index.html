August Ferdinand Möbius (1790–1868)

August Ferdinand Möbius (17 November 1790 – 26 September 1868) was a German mathematician and theoretical astronomer. He is best known as the co-discoverer, with the German mathematician Johann Benedict Listing, of the Möbius strip, and for the Möbius inversion formula in number theory.

Life

Möbius was born in Schulpforta, Saxony-Anhalt, and studied at the University of Leipzig, where he was a student of Carl Friedrich Gauss. He spent most of his career as professor of astronomy and director of the observatory at Leipzig. He died in Leipzig in 1868.

Contributions

His 1858 discovery of the Möbius strip — a two-dimensional surface with only one side — was published only after his death. In number theory, the Möbius function μ(n), introduced in 1832, is fundamental to the Möbius inversion formula and to the study of arithmetic functions. He also introduced homogeneous (barycentric) coordinates in projective geometry in his 1827 work Der barycentrische Calcul, and his name attaches to the Möbius transformations of the complex plane and the Möbius configuration.

Legacy

The asteroid 28516 Möbius and a lunar crater are named in his honour. His work on topology, though largely unrecognized in his lifetime, influenced the later development of the subject.
