Spliceosome

A spliceosome is a large ribonucleoprotein (RNP) complex found primarily within the nucleus of eukaryotic cells. The spliceosome is assembled from small nuclear RNAs (snRNAs) and numerous proteins. The spliceosome removes introns from a transcribed pre-mRNA, a process generally referred to as splicing.

Composition

The major spliceosome is built from the U1, U2, U4, U5, and U6 small nuclear ribonucleoproteins (snRNPs) and over a hundred additional proteins, assembling anew on each intron. A minor spliceosome, using U11, U12, U4atac, and U6atac snRNPs, processes a rare class of introns.

Mechanism

Splicing proceeds through two transesterification reactions: the branch-point adenosine attacks the 5′ splice site, forming a lariat, after which the freed 3′-OH attacks the 3′ splice site, ligating the exons and releasing the intron lariat. Alternative splicing allows one gene to produce multiple protein isoforms, greatly expanding proteome diversity.

Clinical significance

Mutations in spliceosomal components cause diseases including retinitis pigmentosa and certain myelodysplastic syndromes; aberrant splicing is also a hallmark of many cancers.
