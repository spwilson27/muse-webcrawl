Storytelling

Storytelling is the social and cultural activity of sharing stories, sometimes with improvisation, theatrics, or embellishment. Every culture has its own stories or narratives, which are shared as a means of entertainment, education, cultural preservation, or instilling moral values. Crucial elements of stories and storytelling include plot, characters, and narrative point of view.

History

Storytelling predates writing: early forms combined oral narrative with music, dance, and rock art, such as the cave paintings at Lascaux. Oral traditions carried epics like the Epic of Gilgamesh and the Homeric poems before they were written down. Storytellers — griots in West Africa, scops in Anglo-Saxon England, seanchaí in Ireland — held honoured roles as keepers of history and genealogy.

Forms and theory

Stories structure experience: narrative theorists analyse elements such as exposition, rising action, climax, and resolution (Freytag's pyramid), and Joseph Campbell's "hero's journey" monomyth. Storytelling remains central to literature, theatre, film, and newer media including video games and digital storytelling, and is used in education, therapy, marketing, and law.

Modern storytelling

Storytelling festivals, slam events such as The Moth, and podcasts have revived live oral narrative. See also narrative, oral tradition, and folklore.
