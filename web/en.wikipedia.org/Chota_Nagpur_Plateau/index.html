Chota Nagpur Plateau

The Chota Nagpur Plateau is a plateau in eastern India that covers much of the state of Jharkhand, as well as adjacent parts of Chhattisgarh, Odisha, and Bihar. It has an area of roughly 65,000 square kilometres and consists largely of Precambrian rocks, among the oldest on the Indian subcontinent.

Geography

The plateau is made up of several smaller plateaus, including the Ranchi plateau, the Hazaribagh plateau, and the Koderma plateau, with an average elevation around 700 metres above sea level. Its highest point is Parasnath Hill (about 1,365 m), a major Jain pilgrimage site. Rivers draining the plateau include the Damodar, Subarnarekha, North Koel, and South Koel, and the region has notable waterfalls such as Hundru Falls and Jonha Falls near Ranchi.

Resources and economy

Chota Nagpur is one of India's richest mineral regions, containing large deposits of coal, iron ore, mica, bauxite, copper, and limestone. The Damodar Valley coalfields and iron and steel centres such as Jamshedpur and Bokaro have made it a major industrial belt, and it is sometimes called the "Ruhr of India". Mining and heavy industry coexist with agriculture and forestry across much of the plateau.

People and culture

The plateau is home to many Adivasi (indigenous tribal) communities, including the Santal, Munda, Oraon, and Ho peoples, whose languages belong largely to the Munda and Dravidian families. Demands for recognition of tribal land rights and regional autonomy in this area contributed to the creation of the state of Jharkhand in 2000.
