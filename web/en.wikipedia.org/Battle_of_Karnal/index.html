Battle of Karnal

Battle of Karnal
Date24 February 1739
LocationKarnal, present-day Haryana, India
ResultDecisive Persian victory
BelligerentsAfsharid Persia; Mughal Empire

The Battle of Karnal was fought on 24 February 1739 between the armies of Nader Shah, the ruler of Afsharid Persia, and the Mughal Empire under Emperor Muhammad Shah. It was one of the most decisive battles of the eighteenth century in India and a catastrophic defeat for the Mughals.

Nader Shah had invaded India in 1738, crossing the Khyber Pass and taking Peshawar and Lahore. The Mughal court, weakened by factionalism and the rise of the Marathas, assembled a huge army at Karnal, north of Delhi. Nader feigned a retreat, drawing the Mughal vanguard under Saadat Khan out of its strong position, then ambushed it in the defiles near Kanjpura. When the main Mughal force advanced in disorder, Persian musketeers, camel-mounted swivel guns (zamburaks) and cavalry inflicted devastating losses.

Aftermath

Mughal casualties ran into the tens of thousands, while Persian losses were comparatively light. Muhammad Shah surrendered and accompanied Nader to Delhi, where in March 1739 an uprising provoked a general massacre of the population. Nader carried off an enormous treasure, including the Peacock Throne and the Koh-i-Noor and Daria-i-Noor diamonds. The battle exposed the hollowness of Mughal military power and is often treated as the effective end of the empire as a great power.
