Lake Biwa, Japan's largest freshwater lake

Lake Biwa (Japanese: 琵琶湖, Biwa-ko) is the largest freshwater lake in Japan, located entirely within Shiga Prefecture, northeast of the former capital Kyoto. It occupies about a sixth of the prefecture, covers 670 km², and has a maximum depth of 104 metres. Its name likens its shape to the biwa, a Japanese short-necked lute.

An ancient lake

At an estimated four million years old, Lake Biwa is one of the world's roughly twenty ancient lakes, and it has shifted position over its history as tectonic activity reshaped the basin. Its great age has made it a hotspot of evolution: more than 1,000 species and subspecies inhabit the lake, including about 60 endemic species such as the Biwa trout and the giant Lake Biwa catfish. The lake's deep northern basin and shallow southern basin differ markedly in character.

Human significance

The lake supplies drinking water to some 14–15 million people in the Kansai region, including Kyoto and Osaka, via its single outflow, the Seta River (continuing as the Uji and Yodo rivers). It has long been a transport artery, fishery (notably for ayu and crucian carp used for the fermented delicacy funazushi), and cultural icon — the "Eight Views of Ōmi" celebrate its landscapes. The Lake Biwa Canal linked it to Kyoto in the Meiji era. Since 1992, the lakeshore reed beds and wetlands have been protected as a Ramsar site, and water quality, degraded by eutrophication in the 1960s–70s, has substantially recovered.
