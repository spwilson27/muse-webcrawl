Rhinovirus

FamilyPicornaviridae
GenusEnterovirus
SpeciesRhinovirus A, B and C
GenomePositive-sense ssRNA, ~7.2 kb

Rhinoviruses are a group of viruses that are the most common cause of the common cold, responsible for perhaps half of all upper respiratory tract infections. They belong to the genus Enterovirus in the family Picornaviridae and comprise three species (A, B and C) with more than 160 recognized types.

Structure and transmission

Rhinoviruses are small (about 30 nm), non-enveloped, icosahedral viruses with a single-stranded positive-sense RNA genome. They spread through respiratory droplets, aerosols and fomites, and infect cells of the nasal mucosa, most types using ICAM-1 as their receptor. They replicate best at 33–35 °C, the temperature of the nasal passages.

Clinical significance

Beyond colds, rhinoviruses are important triggers of asthma exacerbations and of worsening chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. Because of the huge number of antigenically distinct types, no vaccine exists, and treatment is symptomatic.
