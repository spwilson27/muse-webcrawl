A roller coaster is a type of amusement ride employing a form of elevated railroad track that carries passengers on a train through tight turns, steep slopes, and sometimes inversions. Passengers sit either in open cars or in a linked train, and the ride is usually propelled along the track by gravity after an initial lift, although many modern coasters use launch systems to accelerate the train directly.

History

The precursors of roller coasters were the great ice slides built in Russia from the 16th century onward, in which riders slid down steep wooden constructions on sleds. Wheeled versions appeared in France in the early 19th century, including the Promenades Aériennes in Paris (1817). In the United States, the Mauch Chunk switchback railway in Pennsylvania — originally built to haul coal — began carrying thrill-seeking passengers in the 1870s. LaMarcus Thompson opened the Switchback Railway at Coney Island in 1884, generally regarded as the first true roller coaster in America, and roller coasters spread rapidly through amusement parks in the following decades.

Wooden coasters dominated until 1959, when Disneyland's Matterhorn Bobsleds introduced tubular steel track, permitting smooth inversions and complex layouts. The first modern looping coaster, Corkscrew at Knott's Berry Farm, opened in 1975. A "coaster wars" era in the 1990s and 2000s produced record-breaking heights and speeds, while launched and hybrid wood-steel designs further expanded what was possible.

Mechanics and types

A traditional coaster converts the potential energy gained on the lift hill into kinetic energy; friction and braking steadily dissipate that energy, so each successive hill is lower than the last. Trains are kept on the track by up-stop wheels beneath the rails, and riders are restrained by lap bars, over-the-shoulder restraints, or seat belts. Coasters are classified by track material (wooden, steel, or hybrid), by height category (such as megacoasters and gigacoasters), and by layout (out-and-back, twister, shuttle, or spinning, among others).

Safety

Roller coasters are statistically among the safest amusement rides, with trains protected by redundant block braking systems and daily inspections. Injuries are rare and most often result from riders ignoring posted restrictions. Nonetheless, high g-forces have been studied for their effects on the brain and neck, and several high-profile accidents have led to tightened standards, such as the ASTM F24 committee guidelines used in the United States.

Records

As of the 2020s, the tallest coaster is Kingda Ka at Six Flags Great Adventure in New Jersey (139 m), the fastest is Formula Rossa at Ferrari World Abu Dhabi (0–240 km/h in about 4.9 seconds), and the longest is Steel Dragon 2000 at Nagashima Spa Land in Japan.
