Joseph Liouville (March 24, 1809 – September 8, 1882) was a French mathematician and engineer who worked in analysis, number theory, differential geometry and mathematical physics. More than fifty concepts bear his name.

Work

Liouville founded and edited the Journal de Mathématiques Pures et Appliquées (1836), long France's leading mathematics journal, and used it to publish the revolutionary manuscripts of Évariste Galois in 1846, giving Galois theory its first audience. With Jacques Charles François Sturm he developed Sturm–Liouville theory for second-order linear differential equations, fundamental to mathematical physics. In complex analysis, Liouville's theorem (1847) states that every bounded entire function is constant. In 1844 he proved that transcendental numbers exist, constructing them explicitly using rapidly converging series — the first numbers ever shown to be transcendental.

Life

Educated at the École Polytechnique, Liouville taught there and at the Collège de France, held a chair at the Sorbonne, and was elected to the Academy of Sciences. He briefly entered politics, sitting in the Constituent Assembly of 1848. The lunar crater Liouville is named in his honour.
