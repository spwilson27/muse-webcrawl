Magnus Hirschfeld

Born: 14 May 1868, Kolberg, Kingdom of Prussia (now Kołobrzeg, Poland)
Died: 14 May 1935, Nice, France
Occupation: Physician and sexologist

Magnus Hirschfeld (14 May 1868 – 14 May 1935) was a German physician and sexologist, widely regarded as a pioneering advocate for homosexual and transgender rights. He founded the Scientific-Humanitarian Committee in 1897, the first organisation to campaign publicly for the decriminalisation of homosexuality, petitioning against Paragraph 175 of the German penal code.

Work

Hirschfeld developed a theory of "sexual intermediaries," holding that human sexuality and gender exist on a continuum — an early, if dated, challenge to strict binary categories. In 1919 he founded the Institute for Sexual Science (Institut für Sexualwissenschaft) in Berlin, which combined research, medical care, counselling, and a vast library, and which hosted some of the earliest gender-affirming surgeries. He co-wrote and appeared in the 1919 film Different from the Others, one of the first sympathetic depictions of homosexuality in cinema.

Persecution and exile

When the Nazis came to power, the Institute was ransacked on 6 May 1933 and its library famously burned in the Opernplatz book burnings days later. Hirschfeld, already abroad on a lecture tour, never returned to Germany. He died in exile in Nice on his 67th birthday. His work was largely forgotten for decades before being recovered by historians of sexuality and the modern LGBTQ movement.
