North Macedonia, officially the Republic of North Macedonia, is a landlocked country in Southeast Europe, in the southern part of the Balkan Peninsula. It is bordered by Kosovo to the northwest, Serbia to the north, Bulgaria to the east, Greece to the south, and Albania to the west. The capital and largest city is Skopje.

The country was part of Yugoslavia until 1991, when it declared independence peacefully. A long-running naming dispute with Greece over the name "Macedonia" was resolved by the Prespa agreement of 2018, under which the country adopted its current name in February 2019. North Macedonia became a member of NATO in March 2020 and is a candidate for membership of the European Union.

History

The territory of North Macedonia corresponds in part to the ancient kingdom of Paeonia and borders the historical region of Macedonia, home of the ancient kingdom of Macedon. It later came under Roman, Byzantine, and Ottoman rule; Ottoman domination lasted for roughly five centuries. After the Balkan Wars of 1912–1913 the region was partitioned, and the part that became modern North Macedonia was incorporated into Serbia and later Yugoslavia. In 1944 the Anti-fascist Assembly for the National Liberation of Macedonia proclaimed a Macedonian state within the Yugoslav federation.

Following a referendum in September 1991, the republic declared independence from Yugoslavia. In 2001 an armed conflict between ethnic Albanian insurgents and government forces ended with the Ohrid Framework Agreement, which expanded minority rights. The 2018 Prespa agreement settled the name dispute with Greece and opened the way to NATO accession.

Geography

North Macedonia is a mountainous, landlocked country defined geographically by the central valley of the Vardar River, which flows south into Greece. The Šar Mountains and Mount Korab (2,764 m, the country's highest point) lie along the western border. The country has more than 50 lakes, the largest being Lake Ohrid, Lake Prespa, and Lake Dojran; Lake Ohrid is one of Europe's oldest and deepest lakes and is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Politics

North Macedonia is a parliamentary republic. The head of state is the President, elected by popular vote, while executive power rests mainly with the government headed by the Prime Minister. The unicameral parliament, the Assembly (Sobranie), has 120 seats. The country is divided into 80 municipalities plus the City of Skopje.

Economy

The economy is open and trade-oriented, with agriculture, industry, and services all contributing. Key exports include chemicals, iron and steel products, machinery, and food. The country has pursued foreign investment through technological industrial development zones and maintains a flat income tax regime.

Demographics

According to the 2021 census, the population is about 1.8 million. Ethnic Macedonians form the majority, with significant Albanian, Turkish, Roma, Serbian, Bosniak, and Aromanian minorities. Macedonian, a South Slavic language, is the official language nationwide; Albanian is co-official at state level. The majority of the population adheres to Eastern Orthodoxy, with a substantial Muslim minority.

Culture

The country has a rich cultural heritage encompassing Byzantine churches with renowned frescoes, Ottoman-era bazaars, and a strong folk music tradition. The old town of Ohrid, sometimes called the "Jerusalem of the Balkans," is a major cultural and tourist centre. Cuisine features dishes such as tavče gravče (baked beans) and ajvar.
