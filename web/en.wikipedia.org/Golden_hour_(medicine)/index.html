Golden hour (medicine)

The golden hour is a concept in emergency medicine holding that a patient suffering major trauma or a time-critical medical emergency has the greatest chance of survival if definitive treatment — especially surgical intervention — is provided within the first hour after injury. The term is commonly attributed to R Adams Cowley, founder of the Shock Trauma Center in Baltimore.

Rationale

Many causes of early trauma death — uncontrolled haemorrhage, airway obstruction, tension pneumothorax — progress rapidly but are treatable if reached in time. The golden hour concept drove the development of organised trauma systems, rapid prehospital triage, helicopter emergency medical services, and the doctrine of transporting seriously injured patients directly to designated trauma centres rather than the nearest hospital.

Evidence and related concepts

Research has questioned whether a strict 60-minute threshold applies uniformly; outcomes correlate more with the severity of injury and time to specific interventions than with the clock alone. The concept nonetheless remains influential. Related time-critical doctrines include the "platinum ten minutes" of prehospital care and "door-to-balloon" and "door-to-needle" times for heart attack and stroke, where "time is muscle" and "time is brain."
