The 2010 Chile earthquake occurred off the coast of central Chile on 27 February 2010 at 03:34 local time, with a moment magnitude of 8.8. It was one of the strongest earthquakes ever recorded, rupturing a roughly 500-kilometre segment of the boundary where the Nazca Plate subducts beneath the South American Plate.

Damage and casualties

Strong shaking was felt in the capital, Santiago, and severe damage occurred in the regions of Maule and Biobío, especially in the city of Concepción. The earthquake generated a tsunami that devastated coastal towns such as Constitución and Robinson Crusoe Island and crossed the Pacific, causing damage as far away as Japan. The official death toll was 525, many of them victims of the tsunami. Damage was estimated in the tens of billions of US dollars.

Scientific effects

Scientists calculated that the earthquake shifted the Earth's axis slightly and shortened the length of the day by about 1.26 microseconds. The city of Concepción moved roughly three metres to the west. The quake struck in the final days of President Michelle Bachelet's government, shortly before Sebastián Piñera took office.
