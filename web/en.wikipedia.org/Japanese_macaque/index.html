Japanese macaque

Japanese macaque (Macaca fuscata)
RangeJapan: Honshu, Shikoku, Kyushu and nearby islands
HabitatSubtropical to subarctic forest
DietOmnivorous: fruit, seeds, leaves, insects, small animals
ConservationLeast Concern (IUCN)

The Japanese macaque (Macaca fuscata), also known as the snow monkey, is a terrestrial Old World monkey native to Japan. It has the northernmost range of any non-human primate and is famous for bathing in hot springs (onsen) during the snowy winters of Nagano, and for cultural traditions — such as sweet-potato washing — that primatologists pioneered in studying animal culture.

Description

Adults have brown-grey fur, a pinkish-red face and a short tail. Males weigh about 11 kg and females about 8 kg. Thick fur insulates them against temperatures as low as −15 °C in the Shimokita Peninsula, the species' northern limit.

Behaviour

Japanese macaques live in matrilineal troops of 20–100 individuals with strict hierarchies. In the 1950s, researchers at Koshima observed a young female named Imo inventing sweet-potato washing and wheat-sifting, behaviours that spread through the troop — an early landmark in the study of animal culture. Troops at Jigokudani Monkey Park famously soak in man-made hot springs in winter.

Relationship with humans

The macaque is a long-standing figure in Japanese folklore and art — including the "three wise monkeys" of Nikkō — but crop raiding brings troops into conflict with farmers, and the species is culled in some regions.
