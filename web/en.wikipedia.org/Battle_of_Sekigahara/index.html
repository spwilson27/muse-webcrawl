Battle of Sekigahara

Date21 October 1600 (Keichō 5, 15th day of the 9th month)
LocationSekigahara, present-day Gifu Prefecture, Japan
ResultDecisive Eastern Army (Tokugawa) victory
BelligerentsEastern Army of Tokugawa Ieyasu vs. Western Army loyal to the Toyotomi clan
CommandersTokugawa Ieyasu vs. Ishida Mitsunari; Mōri Terumoto (nominal)

The Battle of Sekigahara (Japanese: Sekigahara no tatakai) was a decisive battle fought on 21 October 1600 in what is now Gifu Prefecture, Japan, at the end of the Sengoku period. The forces of Tokugawa Ieyasu, leading the "Eastern Army", defeated the "Western Army" of daimyō loyal to the Toyotomi clan, commanded in the field by Ishida Mitsunari. Often described as the largest battle in Japanese history and the unofficial beginning of the Tokugawa shogunate, Sekigahara ended a century of civil war and inaugurated more than 250 years of Tokugawa rule.

Background

When the unifier Toyotomi Hideyoshi died in 1598, he left a five-year-old heir, Hideyori, and a council of five regents (the Five Elders), among whom Tokugawa Ieyasu was the most powerful. As Ieyasu manoeuvred toward supremacy, the bureaucrat Ishida Mitsunari rallied western daimyō against him. In the summer of 1600, after Ieyasu marched east against the Uesugi, Mitsunari seized Osaka and declared war. Both sides raced for the strategic crossroads of Sekigahara, where the Nakasendō and Hokkokukaidō roads met in a narrow valley.

Battle

Some 160,000 men in total took the field in rain and morning fog. Ieyasu's Eastern Army of roughly 75,000 faced a slightly larger Western Army holding strong positions on the western heights. The battle hung in the balance until the afternoon, when the young daimyō Kobayakawa Hideaki — secretly won over by Ieyasu, who legend says ordered his men to fire warning shots at Kobayakawa's position to force his hand — charged down Mount Matsuo against the Western Army's flank. His defection, and those of several other contingents, collapsed Mitsunari's army. The Western Army disintegrated; tens of thousands were killed in the fighting and pursuit.

Aftermath

Ishida Mitsunari was captured and executed in Kyoto; the lands of 87 defeated daimyō were confiscated and redistributed to Tokugawa allies. In 1603 Ieyasu received the title of shōgun from Emperor Go-Yōzei, founding the Tokugawa shogunate (Edo bakufu). The surviving Toyotomi were destroyed in the Siege of Osaka in 1615. Sekigahara remains a byword in Japan for a decisive, all-or-nothing contest.
