Poisson's equation

Poisson's equation is an elliptic partial differential equation of broad utility in theoretical physics. It states that the Laplacian of a potential field φ equals (up to a constant factor) a source distribution f: ∇²φ = f. It is named after the French mathematician and physicist Siméon Denis Poisson, who published it in 1823 in his work on electrostatics.

Electrostatics

In electrostatics, Poisson's equation reads ∇²φ = −ρ/ε0, relating the electric potential to the charge density ρ. In charge-free regions it reduces to Laplace's equation, ∇²φ = 0. It is equivalent to the combination of Gauss's law with the relation between the electric field and the potential.

Gravitation

In Newtonian gravity, Poisson's equation ∇²Φ = 4πGρ relates the gravitational potential Φ to the mass density ρ. It is the field equation of classical gravitation and the weak-field limit of Einstein's general relativity.

Solution methods

Solutions can be obtained using Green's functions, separation of variables, Fourier methods, and numerical schemes such as finite differences, finite elements, and fast multipole methods. Poisson's equation also arises in fluid mechanics, heat conduction, and semiconductor device modelling.
