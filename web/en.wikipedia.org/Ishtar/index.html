Ishtar

Ishtar (Akkadian: 𒀭𒌋𒁯 Ištar) is the ancient Mesopotamian goddess of love, war, and fertility. She is also associated with sexuality, beauty, political power, and divine law. She is the Akkadian counterpart of the Sumerian goddess Inanna, and was widely worshipped across Mesopotamia for over two thousand years.

Attributes

Ishtar's symbols include the eight-pointed star, the lion, and the rosette; her planet is Venus, the morning and evening star. She was patron goddess of Uruk and Nineveh, and her cult centre at Uruk housed the Eanna temple. Assyrian kings invoked her as a battle goddess who marched before their armies.

Myths

Her most famous myth, preserved in Akkadian and Sumerian versions, is her descent to the underworld (Kur), where she is stripped of her garments at seven gates, killed by the queen of the dead, Ereshkigal, and revived, returning only by leaving her consort Dumuzi in her place for part of the year. In the Epic of Gilgamesh, the hero's rejection of her advances leads her to unleash the Bull of Heaven on Uruk. The Ishtar Gate of Babylon, built under Nebuchadnezzar II, bears her name.
