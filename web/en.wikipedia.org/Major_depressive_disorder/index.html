Major depressive disorder

Symptoms: Persistent low mood, anhedonia, fatigue, sleep and appetite disturbance, impaired concentration
Causes: Genetic, biological, psychological and social factors
Treatment: Psychotherapy, antidepressant medication, lifestyle measures, ECT in severe cases

Major depressive disorder (MDD), also known as clinical depression, is a mental disorder characterised by at least two weeks of pervasive low mood, low self-esteem and loss of interest or pleasure in normally enjoyable activities.

Diagnosis

DSM-5 criteria require five or more symptoms during the same two-week period, including depressed mood or anhedonia, causing significant distress or impairment and not attributable to substances or another medical condition.

Causes and epidemiology

Risk reflects genetics (heritability ~40%), childhood adversity, stressful life events, personality and medical illness. It affects roughly 5% of adults worldwide at any time and is a leading cause of disability.

Treatment

Evidence-based treatments include cognitive behavioural therapy and other psychotherapies, antidepressants such as SSRIs, exercise and sleep measures; severe or refractory cases may use electroconvulsive therapy, ketamine/esketamine or transcranial magnetic stimulation.
