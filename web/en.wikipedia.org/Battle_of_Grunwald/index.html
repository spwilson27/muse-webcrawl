The Battle of Grunwald, also known as the Battle of Tannenberg (German: Schlacht bei Tannenberg) or the Battle of Žalgiris (Lithuanian), was fought on 15 July 1410 during the Polish–Lithuanian–Teutonic War. The alliance of the Kingdom of Poland and the Grand Duchy of Lithuania, led respectively by King Władysław II Jagiełło (Jogaila) and Grand Duke Vytautas, decisively defeated the German Teutonic Order, led by Grand Master Ulrich von Jungingen. Most of the Order's leadership, including von Jungingen, was killed, and the battle is regarded as one of the largest of medieval Europe and a turning point in the history of the region.

Background

The Teutonic Order's crusades against pagan Lithuania had long masked territorial expansion at the expense of both Lithuania and Poland. Lithuania's conversion to Christianity (1387) and Jogaila's marriage to Jadwiga of Poland undermined the Order's crusading rationale, but disputes over Samogitia — ceded to the Order and risen in revolt in 1409 with Polish support — drove the parties to war.

The battle

Estimates of the armies vary widely; modern historians suggest perhaps 16,500–39,000 allied troops (Poles, Lithuanians, Ruthenians, Tatars, Bohemians, and others) against 11,000–27,000 of the Order and its guests. The battle opened with the Lithuanian light cavalry's charge and subsequent retreat — whether a genuine rout or a planned feigned flight remains debated — while Polish heavy cavalry ground down the Order's banners. When the Lithuanians returned to the field and Vytautas's forces struck the Order's flank, the Teutonic army collapsed. The Grand Master and most high officers died on the field.

Aftermath

The allied army besieged the Order's capital at Marienburg (Malbork) but failed to take it, and the First Peace of Thorn (1411) imposed only modest territorial changes — Samogitia returned to Lithuania — but a heavy indemnity that crippled the Order's finances. Politically and psychologically, Grunwald broke the Order's aura of invincibility and began its long decline; it also secured Lithuania's western frontier and is remembered as a founding triumph in Polish and Lithuanian national memory, commemorated famously in Jan Matejko's monumental 1878 painting.
