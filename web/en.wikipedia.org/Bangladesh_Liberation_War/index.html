Bangladesh Liberation War

The Bangladesh Liberation War (1971) was a revolution and armed conflict sparked by the rise of Bengali nationalism in East Pakistan. It led to the independence of the People's Republic of Bangladesh from Pakistan.

Background

After the 1970 elections, in which the Awami League under Sheikh Mujibur Rahman won an outright majority, the Pakistani military junta refused to transfer power. On 25 March 1971 the Pakistan Army launched Operation Searchlight, a violent crackdown in Dhaka.

Course of the war

Bengali forces formed the Mukti Bahini guerrilla resistance. Some ten million refugees fled to India. In December 1971 India intervened directly; the joint Indo-Bangladeshi command forced the surrender of Pakistani forces in Dhaka on 16 December 1971, now Victory Day in Bangladesh.

Aftermath

The war involved mass atrocities against Bengali civilians, with death toll estimates ranging widely from hundreds of thousands to three million. Bangladesh emerged as a sovereign state, and Sheikh Mujibur Rahman became its first leader.
