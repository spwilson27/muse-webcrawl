Hartlepool United Football Club is a professional association football club based in Hartlepool, County Durham, England. The club was founded in 1908 as Hartlepools United, reflecting the union of the towns of Hartlepool and West Hartlepool. Hartlepool traditionally compete in a blue and white kit. Their home ground is Victoria Park, where they have played since their foundation.

History

The club joined the Football League in 1921 as a founder member of the Third Division North. For most of its history Hartlepool played in the third and fourth tiers of English football. In the earlier decades, the club developed close links with manager Brian Clough, who began his managerial career there in 1965 before moving on to Derby County and Nottingham Forest. The club changed its name to Hartlepool United in 1977.

Under manager Danny Wilson, Hartlepool won promotion to League One in the 2006–07 season after finishing second in League Two, having previously won promotion in 2003 under Mike Newell via the play-offs era's automatic places and again in 2007. The club suffered repeated applications for re-election in the early decades and held the record for the most such applications.

In 2017, after 96 years in the Football League, Hartlepool were relegated to the National League for the first time in their history. They returned to the Football League in 2021 by defeating Torquay United in a dramatic National League play-off final on penalties, but were relegated again in 2023.

Nickname and mascot

Hartlepool supporters are nicknamed the "Monkey Hangers", after the local legend that during the Napoleonic Wars the townspeople hanged a shipwrecked monkey — the sole survivor of a French ship — believing it to be a French spy. The club's mascot, H'Angus the Monkey, was played by Stuart Drummond, who was subsequently elected mayor of Hartlepool three times (2002–2013), at first campaigning partly on a platform of free bananas for schoolchildren.
