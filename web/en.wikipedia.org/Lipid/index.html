Lipid

Lipids are a broad group of organic compounds including fats, oils, waxes, sterols and fat-soluble vitamins. They are generally hydrophobic or amphiphilic and serve as energy stores, structural components of cell membranes and signalling molecules.

CategoriesFatty acyls, glycerolipids, phospholipids, sterols, etc.

Categories

The LIPID MAPS classification divides lipids into eight categories, including fatty acyls, glycerolipids, glycerophospholipids, sphingolipids, saccharolipids, polyketides, sterol lipids and prenol lipids.

Biological functions

Phospholipids form bilayers in membranes; triglycerides store energy in adipose tissue; cholesterol modulates membrane fluidity and is a precursor to steroid hormones. See lipid panel, cholesterol and cell membrane.

Metabolism

Lipids are digested, transported in lipoproteins, synthesised and broken down through pathways such as beta-oxidation and lipogenesis.
