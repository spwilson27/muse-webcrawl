Wilfred Grenfell

Born28 February 1865, Parkgate, Cheshire, England
Died9 October 1940, Charlotte, Vermont, United States
OccupationPhysician, missionary, humanitarian
Known forMedical mission to Labrador and Newfoundland

Sir Wilfred Thomason Grenfell (1865–1940) was a British physician and medical missionary who devoted his career to the fishing communities of Labrador and northern Newfoundland. Arriving in 1892 with the Royal National Mission to Deep Sea Fishermen, he established the first hospitals, nursing stations, schools, and cooperative stores in the region, transforming medical care for isolated coastal settlements.

The Grenfell Mission

Grenfell founded hospitals at Battle Harbour and St. Anthony, hospital ships, and a network of nursing stations staffed largely by volunteers recruited from Britain, Canada, and the United States — among them students known as "WOPS" ("Workers Without Pay"). The mission also set up industrial departments to market local crafts, a cooperative movement, orphanages, and schools. The International Grenfell Association, formed in 1912, coordinated fundraising and operations.

Recognition and legacy

Grenfell was knighted in 1927 for his services. His dramatic lectures and writings, including his autobiography A Labrador Doctor (1919), made Labrador known worldwide. A near-death experience in 1908, when he was adrift on an ice pan with his dog team, became the subject of his famous account Adrift on an Ice-Pan. The Grenfell Mission's health services were eventually absorbed into the provincial health system, and his name survives in Grenfell Campus of Memorial University in Corner Brook.
