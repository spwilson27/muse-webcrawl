Google DeepMind

Google DeepMind is a British-American artificial intelligence research laboratory. It was founded in the United Kingdom in 2010 as DeepMind Technologies by Demis Hassabis, Shane Legg, and Mustafa Suleyman, and was acquired by Google in 2014. In April 2023, DeepMind merged with Google's Brain team to form Google DeepMind, a single AI unit within Alphabet.

History

DeepMind first gained wide attention for reinforcement learning systems that learned to play Atari video games from raw pixels. Its program AlphaGo defeated world champion Lee Sedol at the board game Go in 2016, a landmark in AI. Successors included AlphaZero and, for real-time strategy, AlphaStar. In 2020 its AlphaFold 2 system largely solved the protein structure prediction problem, a breakthrough recognized when Hassabis and John Jumper shared part of the 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

Products and research

Google DeepMind develops the Gemini family of large language and multimodal models, which power Google's AI products, along with image and video generation models such as Imagen and Veo. Its research spans reinforcement learning, robotics, neuroscience-inspired AI, and applications of machine learning to science, including materials discovery, weather forecasting (GraphCast), and mathematics (AlphaGeometry, AlphaProof).

AI safety and ethics

DeepMind has published research on AI safety, interpretability, and evaluation, and maintains teams studying the societal impacts of advanced AI. Its responsible development practices were a stated condition of its acquisition and continue to be a public focus of the organisation.
