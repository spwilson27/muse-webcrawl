Mary of Teck (Victoria Mary Augusta Louise Olga Pauline Claudine Agnes; 26 May 1867 – 24 March 1953) was Queen of the United Kingdom and the British Dominions, and Empress of India, from 6 May 1910 until 20 January 1936 as the wife of King-Emperor George V.

Early life and marriages

Born and raised in England, Mary was the daughter of Prince Francis, Duke of Teck, and Princess Mary Adelaide of Cambridge, a granddaughter of George III. Known within the family as "May", she was engaged in 1891 to Prince Albert Victor, Duke of Clarence, the elder son of the Prince of Wales, but he died of influenza six weeks later. The following year she became engaged to his younger brother, Prince George, Duke of York, whom she married in July 1893.

Queen consort

On George V's accession in 1910 Mary became queen-empress. She supported her husband through the First World War, instituting strict palace economies and visiting wounded servicemen, and accompanied him to the Delhi Durbar of 1911 — the only British queen-empress to attend such a ceremony in India. Renowned for her formidable sense of duty and her connoisseurship — she greatly enlarged the Royal Collection of art and jewels — she was also associated with Queen Mary's Dolls' House, the celebrated miniature palace built in the 1920s.

Later life

Widowed in 1936, Mary lived through the abdication of her eldest son Edward VIII and the accession of George VI, and remained a working member of the royal family into the reign of her granddaughter Elizabeth II, dying weeks before Elizabeth's coronation in 1953. She is buried beside George V at St George's Chapel, Windsor.
