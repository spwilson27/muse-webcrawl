Utica, New York

Utica, New York
County: Oneida
Population: ~65,000
Region: Mohawk Valley

Utica is a city in the Mohawk Valley of central New York State and the county seat of Oneida County. It grew as an industrial and textile center on the Erie Canal and rail lines and today is home to a notable refugee-resettlement population.

History

The site was a crossing of the Mohawk River used by the Iroquois; the modern city took its name from ancient Utica in Tunisia, chosen—by lot, according to tradition—from a hat of names. Textile mills made it prosperous in the 19th century; later, electronics and defense firms followed.

Culture

Utica is known for local dishes including Utica greens, chicken riggies, and tomato pie, and for the Boilermaker 15K road race. The Munson-Williams-Proctor Arts Institute is its principal museum.
