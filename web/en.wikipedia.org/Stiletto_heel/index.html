Stiletto heel

A stiletto heel, or simply stiletto, is a shoe with a long, thin, high heel, found on some boots and shoes, usually for women. It is named after the stiletto dagger. Stiletto heels may vary in length from 2.5 centimetres (1 inch) to 25 cm (10 inches) or more, as long as the heel's diameter at the ground is no more than about 1 cm. A heel of 5 cm or less is sometimes called a kitten heel.

History

High heels themselves are centuries old, but the true stiletto became possible only in the early 1950s, when new materials — in particular a slender steel shank set into plastic or wood — gave a very thin heel enough strength to bear a wearer's weight. Designers associated with its popularisation include Roger Vivier, who created stilettos for Christian Dior in 1954, and Salvatore Ferragamo and André Perugia in Italy. The shoe became an emblem of 1950s glamour and, later, of designers such as Manolo Blahnik and Christian Louboutin, whose red-soled stilettos are a luxury status symbol.

Effects and criticism

Stilettos concentrate the wearer's weight onto a tiny area, producing very high pressure — famously calculated to exceed that under an elephant's foot — and they can damage soft flooring and get caught in gratings. Habitual wear is associated with foot pain, bunions, shortened calf muscles and altered posture. Some venues and employers' dress codes have banned or required stilettos, and such requirements have prompted legal and public debate in several countries, including campaigns against mandatory high heels in the workplace.

In popular culture

The stiletto is a recurring symbol of femininity, glamour, sexuality and power in fashion photography, film and popular music, and the word is also used for a slender switchblade knife and for a type of artificial nail.
