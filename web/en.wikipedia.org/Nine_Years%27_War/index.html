The Nine Years' War (1688–1697), also known as the War of the Grand Alliance or the War of the League of Augsburg, was a major European conflict between France under Louis XIV and a coalition known as the Grand Alliance, comprising England, the Dutch Republic, the Holy Roman Empire, Spain and Savoy. It was fought in Europe, Ireland, North America (where it was called King William's War) and at sea.

The war began when Louis XIV invaded the Rhineland, seeking to consolidate French gains from earlier reunion policies and to support the Catholic James II of England against William III, who had taken the English throne in the Glorious Revolution of 1688.

Course and outcome

Major campaigns took place in the Spanish Netherlands, where the battles of Fleurus, Steenkerque and Landen were fought, in Ireland (the Battle of the Boyne, 1690, and Aughrim, 1691), and in Piedmont and Catalonia. At sea France won at Beachy Head but lost control after Barfleur and La Hogue in 1692. Exhaustion on both sides led to the Treaty of Ryswick in 1697: Louis recognized William III as king of England and returned most of his recent conquests, though France retained Strasbourg and gained Saint-Domingue (Haiti).
