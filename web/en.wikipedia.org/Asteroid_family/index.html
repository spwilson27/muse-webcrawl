Asteroid family

An asteroid family is a population of asteroids that share similar proper orbital elements, such as semimajor axis, eccentricity, and orbital inclination. The members of the families are thought to be fragments of past asteroid collisions. An asteroid family is a more specific term than asteroid group, whose members, while sharing some broad orbital characteristics, may be otherwise unrelated to each other.

General properties

Large prominent families contain several hundred recognized asteroids, and many more smaller members that may not yet have been analyzed or are lost in the background "noise" of other asteroids. Collisional families are thought to generally have ages of between a few tens of millions to a few billions of years, depending upon the family in question, although families older than about 2 billion years tend to disperse and become harder to recognize.

Origin

Families are believed to form as the result of collisions between parent asteroids. In many or most cases, the parent body was shattered completely, but there are also several families that resulted from cratering events on a parent body that survived, such as the Vesta, Pallas, Hygiea, and Massalia families.

Identification

The principal method used to determine family membership is the search for clustering in proper orbital element space, historically through the hierarchical clustering method introduced by Kiyotsugu Hirayama in 1918; the first families recognized are accordingly called Hirayama families. Notable families include the Koronis, Eos, Themis, Flora, Eunomia, and Karin families.
