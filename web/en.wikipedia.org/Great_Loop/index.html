The Great Loop is a system of interconnected waterways that allows recreational boaters to circumnavigate the eastern portion of North America. The roughly 6,000-mile route is most often cruised counterclockwise, typically over the course of about a year, following the seasons.

Route

Starting on the U.S. East Coast, Loopers travel north via the Atlantic Intracoastal Waterway, through New York Harbor and the Hudson River, then either through the Erie Canal and Great Lakes or north via Lake Champlain and Canadian canals. The route continues down the Illinois River to the Mississippi River (or via the Tennessee–Tombigbee Waterway), then across the Gulf of Mexico coast of Florida and around the Florida peninsula, closing the loop.

Constraints and culture

Boats must be able to pass under the lowest fixed bridge on the route (about 19.6 feet near Chicago) and through locks of various sizes. Cruisers who complete the loop are known as "Loopers" and fly a burgee issued by the America's Great Loop Cruisers' Association; those who finish are said to have "crossed their wake."
