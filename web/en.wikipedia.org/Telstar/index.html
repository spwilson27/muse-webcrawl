Telstar

Telstar was a communications satellite launched by NASA on July 10, 1962. It was the first privately sponsored satellite (built by Bell Telephone Laboratories for AT&T) and enabled the first live transatlantic television broadcasts, as well as telephone calls and telegraph images relayed through space.

History

Telstar 1 was launched atop a Thor-Delta rocket from Cape Canaveral. On July 23, 1962, it relayed the first live transatlantic television signals between the United States and Europe. The satellite's orbit, with a period of about 2 hours 37 minutes, allowed transatlantic communication sessions of roughly 20 minutes per pass.

Legacy

Telstar demonstrated the feasibility of satellite communications and paved the way for modern communications satellites. The satellite inspired the hit instrumental song "Telstar" by the Tornados. Telstar 1 ceased operating in February 1963, partly because of radiation damage from high-altitude nuclear tests. A second satellite, Telstar 2, was launched in May 1963.
