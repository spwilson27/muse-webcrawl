CSS Alabama

Builder: John Laird Sons and Company, Birkenhead, England

Launched: July 29, 1862

Commissioned: August 24, 1862

Fate: Sunk June 19, 1864, off Cherbourg, France

Class: Screw sloop-of-war

CSS Alabama was a screw sloop-of-war built in 1862 for the Confederate States Navy at Birkenhead on the River Mersey, England. Commanded by Captain Raphael Semmes, she operated as a commerce raider, attacking Union merchant and naval vessels in the Atlantic, Caribbean, South Atlantic, Indian Ocean and Pacific. In a career of just under two years she captured and burned dozens of Union merchantmen and sank the warship USS Hatteras, becoming the most famous of the Confederate raiders.

Career

Built in secrecy as hull "290" and named Enrica at launch, the ship slipped out of Liverpool in July 1862, evading British authorities, and was commissioned off the Azores. Semmes took the Alabama on a cruise that ranged from Newfoundland to Brazil, around the Cape of Good Hope to the East Indies and back. She took some 65 prizes, most of which were burned at sea, and never entered a Confederate port, coaling and refitting in neutral harbours instead.

End and legacy

On June 19, 1864, Alabama fought the Union sloop USS Kearsarge outside Cherbourg harbour and was sunk after an hour-long duel. Semmes and many of his crew were rescued by the British yacht Deerhound. The damage done by Alabama and her sister raiders led to the postwar Alabama Claims, in which Britain paid the United States $15.5 million following arbitration in 1872. The wreck was located in 1984 and is protected as a historic site.
