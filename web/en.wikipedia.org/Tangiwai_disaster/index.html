The Tangiwai disaster was a deadly railway accident in New Zealand that occurred at 10:21 p.m. on 24 December 1953, when a bridge over the Whangaehu River at Tangiwai collapsed beneath a Wellington-to-Auckland express train. Of the 285 passengers and crew aboard, 151 were killed — New Zealand's worst railway disaster.

Cause

A lahar — a volcanic mudflow from the crater lake of Mount Ruapehu — had surged down the river minutes earlier, damaging the bridge's piers. A motorist, Cyril Ellis, attempted to warn the train by torchlight but could not stop it in time; he and driver Charles Parker were credited with reducing the toll by braking.

Aftermath

The disaster occurred as Queen Elizabeth II was visiting New Zealand; she referred to it in her Christmas broadcast. A board of inquiry faulted the design and inspection of the bridge, and lahar warning systems were later installed on Ruapehu.
