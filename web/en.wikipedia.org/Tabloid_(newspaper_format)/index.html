Tabloid (newspaper format)

A tabloid is a newspaper format with a smaller page size than a broadsheet. The term also refers to a style of journalism associated with sensationalism, celebrity coverage and bold headlines, though many serious newspapers now print in the tabloid size for convenience.

Format

Tabloid pages typically measure about 430 mm by 280 mm, roughly half the size of a broadsheet. The compact format suits commuters and led many broadsheets, including The Times and The Independent in the United Kingdom, to switch sizes in the 2000s.

Style connotations

In Britain, "red top" tabloids such as The Sun and the Daily Mirror are known for pun-driven headlines and celebrity news, while "middle-market" tabloids like the Daily Mail take a different editorial tone. In the United States, tabloids include the New York Daily News and supermarket tabloids devoted to celebrity stories.

History

The word derives from "tablet" and "alkaloid", a trademarked compressed medicine tablet, later applied to compressed, condensed news. Tabloid journalism flourished in the early 20th century with papers emphasising photographs, crime and sensation.
