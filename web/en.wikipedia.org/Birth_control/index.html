Birth control

Birth control, also known as contraception and fertility control, is the use of methods or devices to prevent pregnancy. Birth control has been used since ancient times, but effective and safe methods only became readily available in the 20th century. Planning, making available, and using birth control is called family planning.

Methods

Methods include long-acting reversible contraception such as intrauterine devices (IUDs) and implants, hormonal methods such as the combined oral contraceptive pill, patch and injection, barrier methods such as condoms and diaphragms, fertility-awareness methods, emergency contraception, and permanent sterilisation. The most effective methods are long-acting and do not depend on user action. Condoms additionally protect against sexually transmitted infections.

History and impact

The modern birth control movement, associated with campaigners such as Margaret Sanger, achieved a landmark with the approval of the first oral contraceptive in the United States in 1960. Access to contraception reduces unplanned pregnancy and maternal mortality, and has significant demographic, educational and economic effects; availability and legality vary widely across cultures and religions.
