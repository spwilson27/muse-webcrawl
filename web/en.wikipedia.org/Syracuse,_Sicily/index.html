Syracuse, Sicily

Syracuse (Italian: Siracusa) is a historic city on the Ionian coast of Sicily, Italy, and the capital of the province of the same name. Founded by Corinthians and Teneans in about 734 BC, it grew into one of the most powerful cities of the Greek world, and Cicero described it as “the greatest Greek city and the most beautiful of them all”. Its population today is about 116,000.

Ancient history

Syracuse was the birthplace of the mathematician and engineer Archimedes, who died in its capture during the Second Punic War (212 BC), when Rome took the city after a long siege despite his war machines. Earlier, its tyrants Gelon and Hieron I defeated Carthage at Himera (480 BC) and Etruscans at Cumae (474 BC); under Dionysius I it became the dominant power in Greek Sicily and famously repelled the Athenian Sicilian Expedition of 415–413 BC, a turning point in the Peloponnesian War. The city later passed through Roman, Byzantine, Arab, Norman, and Spanish rule.

Sights

The old town occupies the island of Ortigia, where the cathedral incorporates the columns of a 5th-century BC temple of Athena. The Neapolis archaeological park holds one of the largest Greek theatres of antiquity and the Ear of Dionysius, a great limestone cavern. Together with the Necropolis of Pantalica, Syracuse is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (inscribed 2005). The American city of Syracuse, New York, was named after it in 1820.
