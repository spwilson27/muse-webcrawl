Loan

In finance, a loan is the tender of money by one party to another with an agreement to pay it back. The recipient, or borrower, incurs a debt and is usually required to pay interest on that debt until it is repaid, as well as to repay the principal amount borrowed.

Elements

A loan is documented by a promissory note or loan agreement specifying the principal, the interest rate, the repayment schedule and the consequences of default. Loans may be secured, where the borrower pledges collateral such as property, or unsecured, where the lender relies on the borrower's creditworthiness. Interest is commonly quoted as an annual percentage rate and may be fixed or variable.

Types

  • Mortgage loans — long-term loans secured on real estate, used to buy homes.
  • Term loans and credit lines — for businesses, for equipment, working capital or expansion.
  • Personal and consumer loans — including auto loans, student loans and credit cards.
  • Payday and microloans — small, short-term loans, often at high cost.
  • Sovereign and syndicated loans — lending to governments and large corporations by groups of banks.

Economics

Lending is a core function of banks, creating deposits and allocating capital in the economy. Regulators oversee lending standards, usury limits and consumer protection, since excessive or reckless lending has been central to financial crises from ancient debt cancellations to the 2008 global financial crisis.
