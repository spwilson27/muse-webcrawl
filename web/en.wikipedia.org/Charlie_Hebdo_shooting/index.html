The Charlie Hebdo shooting was a terrorist attack on 7 January 2015, when two brothers, Saïd and Chérif Kouachi, forced their way into the offices of the French satirical weekly newspaper Charlie Hebdo in Paris and murdered 12 people, including the magazine's editor Stéphane "Charb" Charbonnier and the cartoonists Cabu, Wolinski, Tignous and Honoré. Eleven others were wounded.

Background

Charlie Hebdo had long courted controversy with caricatures, including depictions of the Islamic prophet Muhammad, which had led to threats, a firebombing of its offices in 2011, and the magazine being named on al-Qaeda's most-wanted list. The attackers, French-born brothers of Algerian descent, had been radicalised; Chérif Kouachi had previously been jailed for involvement in a jihadist network.

Attack and manhunt

Armed with assault rifles, the brothers attacked during the magazine's Wednesday editorial meeting. They also killed a police officer, Ahmed Merabet, on the street outside. After a two-day manhunt, they were cornered at a printing plant in Dammartin-en-Goële and killed in a shootout on 9 January. A linked attacker, Amedy Coulibaly, murdered a policewoman in Montrouge and four hostages at a kosher supermarket in Paris before being killed by police the same day.

Aftermath

The attacks prompted an outpouring of solidarity under the slogan "Je suis Charlie". On 11 January 2015, an estimated 3.7 million people marched across France in defence of free expression, joined by world leaders in Paris. The magazine's surviving staff published a "survivors' issue" with a print run of millions. A trial of alleged accomplices concluded in December 2020.
