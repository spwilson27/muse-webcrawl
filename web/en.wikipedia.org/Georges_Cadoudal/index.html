Georges Cadoudal (1 January 1771 – 25 June 1804) was a Breton counter-revolutionary and leader of the Chouannerie during the French Revolution. A committed royalist, he fought to restore the Bourbon monarchy and conspired against Napoleon Bonaparte.

Chouan leader

Born near Auray in Brittany, Cadoudal joined the royalist uprising in the west of France and became its most formidable guerrilla commander in Morbihan. He refused repeated offers of pardon and rank from the Republic, accepting only temporary peace settlements, and was supported with money and arms by Britain and the exiled Bourbon princes.

Conspiracy against Napoleon

After the peace of 1800 Cadoudal continued plotting. In 1803 he landed secretly in France to organise the assassination of the First Consul, Napoleon, in league with generals Pichegru and Moreau. The plot was uncovered, and Cadoudal was arrested in Paris in March 1804 after a celebrated manhunt.

Trial and death

Convicted of conspiracy, Cadoudal refused to plead for mercy and was guillotined in Paris on 25 June 1804, reportedly crying "Vive le roi!" Under the Bourbon Restoration his remains were honoured and a mausoleum raised at Kerléano.
