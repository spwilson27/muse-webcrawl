Peter Parker

Peter Benjamin Parker, also known by his alias Spider-Man, is a superhero appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer-editor Stan Lee and artist Steve Ditko, he first appeared in Amazing Fantasy #15 (August 1962). Peter Parker was groundbreaking as a teenage hero with everyday problems — school, money and family — rather than an adult sidekick or flawless paragon.

Fictional biography

Orphaned as a boy, Peter is raised in Queens, New York, by his Aunt May and Uncle Ben. A shy science student, he gains spider-like abilities — superhuman strength, agility, wall-clinging and a precognitive "spider-sense" — after being bitten by a radioactive spider. Initially using his powers for personal gain, he fails to stop a burglar who later murders Uncle Ben, teaching him the lesson summarised as "with great power comes great responsibility". As Spider-Man he devises web-shooters and balances crime-fighting with work, study and relationships with figures including Gwen Stacy, Mary Jane Watson and J. Jonah Jameson, who campaigns against him in the Daily Bugle.

Powers and themes

Peter is a gifted scientist in many portrayals, and his stories centre on guilt, responsibility and the struggle to reconcile heroism with an ordinary life. He has headlined numerous series, animated shows and films, including portrayals by Tobey Maguire, Andrew Garfield and Tom Holland, and remains one of the world's most recognisable fictional characters.
