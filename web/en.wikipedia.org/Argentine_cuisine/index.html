Argentine cuisine

Argentine cuisine reflects the country's blend of Indigenous, Spanish, and especially Italian immigrant influences, and is famous worldwide for its beef. Argentina has one of the highest per-capita beef consumption rates in the world.

Asado

The centrepiece of Argentine food culture is the asado, a barbecue of beef cuts, sausages such as chorizo and morcilla (blood sausage), and offal cooked over wood or charcoal on a parrilla (grill), typically seasoned only with salt and served with chimichurri sauce. The ritual is a major social occasion.

Other staples

Empanadas — baked or fried pastries filled with beef, chicken, or cheese — vary by province. Italian influence brought pasta, pizza, and milanesas (breaded cutlets). Dulce de leche, a caramelized milk spread, is the signature sweet, used in alfajores and cakes. Yerba mate is the national infusion, drunk communally from a gourd through a metal straw.

Wine

Argentina is a major wine producer, particularly of Malbec from Mendoza.
