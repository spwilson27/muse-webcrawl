Grand Riemann hypothesis

Zeros of L-functions are conjectured to lie on the critical line

The extended Riemann hypothesis and grand Riemann hypothesis are extensions of the famous Riemann hypothesis to wider classes of L-functions. The Riemann hypothesis asserts that all nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function have real part 1/2. The extended hypothesis makes the same claim for Dirichlet L-functions, while the grand Riemann hypothesis asserts it for all Dedekind zeta functions and, in its most general form, for the global L-functions of number theory.

Statement and consequences

All of these conjectures remain unproven. Their truth would have sweeping consequences: the extended hypothesis implies the best possible error terms in the distribution of primes in arithmetic progressions and underlies many conditional results in analytic number theory and computational number theory — for example, polynomial-time primality tests and bounds on the least prime in an arithmetic progression (Linnik's theorem) and on class numbers.

Evidence

The analogues of these hypotheses have been proved for L-functions over function fields: André Weil proved the Riemann hypothesis for zeta functions of curves over finite fields in the 1940s, and Pierre Deligne's proof of the Weil conjectures (1974) extended this to higher dimensions. These results, together with enormous numerical verification in the number-field case, give mathematicians strong confidence in the conjectures, but a proof in characteristic zero remains one of the deepest open problems in mathematics.
