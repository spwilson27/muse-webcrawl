Mahabalipuram

LocationTamil Nadu, India
FoundedPallava period, 7th–8th c. CE
UNESCOWorld Heritage Site (1984)

Mahabalipuram, also known as Mamallapuram, is a town in Chengalpattu district of Tamil Nadu, India, on the Coromandel Coast south of Chennai. It is famous for its group of 7th- and 8th-century rock-cut and structural monuments created under the Pallava dynasty, designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1984.

Monuments

The site includes the Shore Temple, one of the earliest structural stone temples in South India; the Panamalai and Five Rathas (monolithic chariot-shaped shrines); cave temples such as the Varaha and Mahishasuramardini mandapas; and the vast open-air relief known as the Descent of the Ganges (or Arjuna's Penance). A huge rounded boulder known as Krishna's Butter Ball is a popular attraction.

History

Mahabalipuram was a busy seaport of the Pallava kingdom under kings such as Narasimhavarman I (Mamalla). A buried ancient port and submerged structures offshore have attracted archaeological interest, and the town was affected by the 2004 Indian Ocean tsunami.
