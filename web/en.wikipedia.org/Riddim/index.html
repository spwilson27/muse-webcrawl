Meaning: Jamaican Patois for "rhythm"
Uses: Reggae / dancehall backing track; dubstep subgenre

Riddim

Riddim is the Jamaican Patois pronunciation of "rhythm", and in Jamaican music it denotes the instrumental backing track of a song. In reggae and dancehall, a single riddim is routinely shared among many artists, each recording their own vocal version over the same accompaniment — a practice central to Jamaican sound-system culture.

Reggae and dancehall riddims

Famous riddims such as "Stalag", "Real Rock", and "Sleng Teng" (1985, the first fully digital riddim) have underpinned hundreds of songs. From the mid-1980s, digitally produced riddims by producers like King Jammy and Steely & Clevie dominated dancehall, and compilations ("juggling" albums) collect many artists' cuts of one riddim.

Riddim dubstep

The word also names a subgenre of dubstep that emerged in the 2010s, characterised by repetitive, wonky, triplet-flavoured basslines, sparse drums, and heavy use of delay and reverb, associated with artists from the UK and US bass-music scenes. The term was borrowed from the reggae concept via dubstep's own Jamaican-influenced roots.
