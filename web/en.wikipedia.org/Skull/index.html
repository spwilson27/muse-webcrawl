Skull

Skull
Part ofSkeleton, head
Bones22 (8 cranial, 14 facial), excluding ear ossicles and hyoid
FunctionsProtects the brain; supports the face; anchors teeth
ArticulationsSutures; temporomandibular joints

The skull is the bony framework of the head in vertebrates. It supports the structures of the face and forms a protective cavity for the brain. In adult humans the skull consists of 22 bones — 8 cranial bones forming the cranium and 14 facial bones — joined mostly by immovable fibrous joints called sutures; the mandible (jaw) is the only movable bone, articulating at the temporomandibular joints.

Structure

Cranium

The cranial bones are the frontal, two parietal, two temporal, occipital, sphenoid and ethmoid bones. They enclose the cranial cavity (capacity about 1,300–1,500 cm³ in adults) and are pierced by foramina transmitting nerves and blood vessels — most importantly the foramen magnum, through which the spinal cord passes. In infants the cranial bones are separated by soft fontanelles that allow moulding during birth and rapid brain growth, fusing over the first two years of life.

Facial skeleton

The facial bones include the maxillae, zygomatic (cheek) bones, nasal bones, lacrimal bones, palatine bones, inferior nasal conchae, vomer and the mandible. They form the orbits, nasal cavity and upper jaw, and anchor the teeth in their sockets. The paranasal sinuses — air-filled cavities within several skull bones — lighten the skull and resonate the voice.

Development and variation

Skull bones form by two mechanisms: intramembranous ossification for the flat bones of the vault, and endochondral ossification for the skull base. Sexual dimorphism is modest — male skulls tend to be larger with more prominent brow ridges and mastoid processes — a feature used, with population variation in mind, in forensic anthropology.

Clinical significance

Skull fractures may be linear, depressed, or involve the base, risking injury to the brain, meninges and cranial nerves. Premature fusion of a suture (craniosynostosis) distorts head growth and may require surgery. The skull is also the site of conditions such as Paget's disease of bone.
