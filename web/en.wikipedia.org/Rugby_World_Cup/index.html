Rugby World Cup

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Rugby World Cup is a men's rugby union tournament contested every four years between the top international teams, organised by World Rugby. The tournament was first held in 1987, co-hosted by Australia and New Zealand, and has grown into one of the largest international sporting events in the world. The winners are awarded the Webb Ellis Cup, named after William Webb Ellis, the Rugby School pupil credited in legend with inventing the game by picking up the ball during a football match.

History

Proposals for a world championship circulated for decades before the International Rugby Football Board approved one in 1985. Sixteen teams contested the inaugural 1987 event, won by New Zealand. South Africa's victory on home soil in 1995, its first tournament after the end of apartheid, became one of sport's most famous moments when Nelson Mandela presented the trophy to captain Francois Pienaar. Japan's pool-stage defeat of South Africa in 2015 is regarded as the tournament's greatest upset.

Format

Twenty teams contest the modern tournament. Twelve qualify automatically by finishing in the top three of their pools at the previous event; the rest come through regional qualifying. The finals consist of a pool stage of four groups of five, followed by quarter-finals, semi-finals, a bronze final, and the final.
