Qinghai (Chinese: 青海; pinyin: Qīnghǎi) is a landlocked province of western China, named after Qinghai Lake (Lake Kokonor), the largest lake in the country. It is one of China's largest provinces by area but among the least densely populated, with under six million inhabitants. Its capital and largest city is Xining.

Geography

Qinghai occupies the northeastern part of the Tibetan Plateau, with an average elevation above 3,000 metres. The Kunlun and Qilian mountain ranges cross the province, and the arid Qaidam Basin in the northwest contains vast salt lakes and potash deposits. Qinghai is the source of China's three great rivers — the Yellow, Yangtze, and Mekong (Lancang) — whose headwaters region is protected as the Sanjiangyuan National Park, one of China's first national parks. The climate is cold, dry, and continental.

History

The region was contested over the centuries among Tibetan kingdoms, Mongol empires, and Chinese dynasties; much of it formed part of the Tibetan cultural region of Amdo, while Kokonor Mongols settled around the lake in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries. The Qing dynasty exercised loose control, and the Republic of China formally created Qinghai province in 1928, when it was dominated by the Hui warlord Ma Bufang. It came under People's Republic control in 1949.

Demographics and economy

Qinghai is ethnically diverse: Han Chinese form a narrow majority, with large Tibetan (about a quarter of the population) and Hui minorities, plus Tu, Salar, and Mongol communities. Ta'er Monastery (Kumbum), near Xining, is one of the great monasteries of Tibetan Buddhism and the birthplace of Tsongkhapa. The economy relies on salt-lake chemicals, hydropower, oil and gas from the Qaidam Basin, animal husbandry, and growing solar power generation; the Longyangxia complex combines one of the world's largest photovoltaic plants with hydroelectric generation.
