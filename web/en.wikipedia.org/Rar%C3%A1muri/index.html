Rarámuri

The Rarámuri, also spelled Rarámuri and often called the Tarahumara in Spanish, are an Indigenous people of the Sierra Madre Occidental in the Mexican state of Chihuahua, known for their remote settlements in the Copper Canyon (Barrancas del Cobre) region and their remarkable long-distance running tradition.

Culture

The name Rarámuri is often translated as "those who run fast" or "foot runners". Traditional foot races, in which men kick a wooden ball (raráhipa) and women throw hoops over distances of tens or hundreds of kilometres, are central to social life, along with tesgüino (maize beer) gatherings. Most Rarámuri practise a syncretic form of Catholicism mixed with Indigenous beliefs and live by subsistence farming and herding.

Recognition

Their endurance running drew international attention through Christopher McDougall's 2009 book Born to Run, and runners such as Lorena Ramírez have competed in ultramarathons in traditional dress and sandals.
