Six Nations of the Grand River

Six Nations of the Grand River (or simply Six Nations) is the most populous First Nations reserve in Canada, located near Brantford in southwestern Ontario. It is the only reserve in North America where all six nations of the Haudenosaunee (Iroquois) Confederacy — the Mohawk, Oneida, Onondaga, Cayuga, Seneca, and Tuscarora — live together.

History

After the American Revolutionary War, in which the Six Nations had largely sided with the British, Joseph Brant (Thayendanegea) led many of his people to Upper Canada. The Haldimand Proclamation of 1784 granted them a tract of land along the Grand River, six miles deep on each side from its mouth to its source, in compensation for lands lost in New York. Over the following two centuries the tract was reduced through sales, leases, and disputed surrenders to the present reserve of roughly 46,000 acres (about 184 km²).

Governance and population

Six Nations is governed by both an elected council established under the Indian Act in 1924 and the traditional Haudenosaunee Confederacy Council of hereditary chiefs. Of roughly 28,000 band members, about 13,000 live on the reserve. The community includes the villages of Ohsweken and is home to institutions such as the Woodland Cultural Centre.

Land claims

Six Nations has long pursued land claims against the Crown over the Haldimand Tract, filing a major lawsuit in 1995. In 2006, a reclamation of a housing development site at Caledonia brought national attention to the dispute. The community continues to assert that much of the original tract, including lands underlying several southwestern Ontario cities, was never lawfully surrendered.
