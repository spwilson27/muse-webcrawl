Antoine Houdar de la Motte
Born17 January 1672, Paris, France
Died26 December 1731, Paris, France
OccupationWriter, playwright, librettist

Antoine Houdar de la Motte (17 January 1672 – 26 December 1731) was a French writer, playwright, and critic. He was a leading figure of the "Modern" party in the Quarrel of the Ancients and the Moderns, and a member of the Académie française from 1710.

Works

La Motte wrote opera libretti and ballets for composers including Marin Marais and André Destouches, dramas such as Les Macchabées and Inès de Castro (1723), a celebrated prose translation-abridgement of the Iliad (1714) that provoked a famous dispute with Anne Dacier, and a successful rhymed edition of La Fontaine's Fables. He also wrote criticism, notably the Discours sur Homère, arguing that classical texts should be adapted to modern taste.

Later life

La Motte lost his sight in his later years but continued to attend and influence the literary salons of Paris, where his wit and urbanity were admired. His collected Œuvres appeared in 1754.
