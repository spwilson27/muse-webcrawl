Labradorite ((Ca,Na)(Al,Si)₄O₈) is a calcium-enriched feldspar mineral first identified in Labrador, Canada. It is an intermediate to calcic member of the plagioclase series. It is a defining constituent of the rock anorthosite.

Properties

Like all plagioclase members, the crystal system of labradorite is triclinic. Labradorite can display an iridescent optical effect (or schiller) known as labradorescence. The term labradorescence was coined by Ove Balthasar Bøggild, who defined it as the unusual colour reflection exhibited when light enters the crystal in a direction other than that of observation.

Occurrence

The geological type area for labradorite is Paul's Island near the town of Nain in Labrador, Canada. It has also been reported in Norway, Finland and various other locations worldwide, with notable distribution in Madagascar, China, Australia, Slovakia and the United States.

Use as a gemstone

Labradorite is used as a gemstone, usually cut as cabochons to show its play of colour. A rare variety from Finland, spectrolite, displays a particularly rich range of colours.
