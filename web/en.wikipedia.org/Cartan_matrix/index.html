Cartan matrix

In mathematics, the term Cartan matrix has three closely related meanings, all named after the French mathematician Élie Cartan. Most commonly, a Cartan matrix is a square matrix associated with an indecomposable root system or, equivalently, with a finite-dimensional semisimple Lie algebra, encoding the angles and relative lengths of its simple roots.

Definition

Given simple roots α1, …, αn and their coroots, the Cartan matrix has entries Aij = 2(αi, αj) / (αj, αj). Its diagonal entries are all 2; its off-diagonal entries are non-positive integers (0, −1, −2, or −3); and Aij = 0 if and only if Aji = 0.

Classification

An indecomposable Cartan matrix is of finite type — corresponding to a semisimple Lie algebra — precisely when it is positive definite. The finite-type Cartan matrices fall into the famous families An, Bn, Cn, Dn and the exceptional types E6, E7, E8, F4, G2, matching the classification of Dynkin diagrams, which are graphical encodings of the same data.

Generalised Cartan matrices

Relaxing positive-definiteness to positive-semidefiniteness gives affine Cartan matrices, associated with affine Lie algebras; arbitrary generalised Cartan matrices define Kac–Moody algebras, in which Cartan matrices remain a central tool.
