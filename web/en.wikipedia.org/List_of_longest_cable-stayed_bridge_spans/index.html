List of longest cable-stayed bridge spans

A cable-stayed bridge supports its deck with cables running directly from towers to the deck, unlike suspension bridges, whose decks hang from vertical suspenders attached to main cables. Cable-stayed designs are efficient for spans roughly between 200 and 1,200 metres. This list ranks completed cable-stayed bridges by the length of their main span.

History

The modern cable-stayed bridge dates to the 1950s in Germany and Sweden; the Strömsund Bridge in Sweden (1955) and the Theodor Heuss Bridge in Düsseldorf (1957) were early exemplars. From the 1990s onward, China became the leading builder of long cable-stayed spans, and cable-stayed designs displaced suspension bridges for many crossings in the 600–1,100 m range. The Changtai Yangtze River Bridge, with a 1,176 m main span, was under construction to surpass the Russky Bridge.
