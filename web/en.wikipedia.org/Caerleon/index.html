Caerleon

Caerleon (Welsh: Caerllion) is a town and community in Newport, Wales. Situated on the River Usk, it lies about 5 miles northeast of Newport city centre. Caerleon is of great archaeological importance, as it was the site of the Roman legionary fortress of Isca Augusta and an Iron Age hillfort.

Roman Isca

Founded around AD 75 as the base of Legio II Augusta during the conquest of the Silures, Isca was one of only three permanent legionary fortresses in Roman Britain, alongside Chester and York. Surviving remains include Britain's best-preserved Roman amphitheatre, sections of the fortress baths, barrack blocks—the only visible legionary barracks in Europe—and stretches of the defensive walls. Finds are displayed in the National Roman Legion Museum in the town.

Arthurian associations

In medieval Welsh tradition Caerleon became closely associated with King Arthur: Geoffrey of Monmouth placed Arthur's court there in the Historia Regum Britanniae, and the association continued through Malory to Tennyson, who wrote part of the Idylls of the King while staying in the town. The amphitheatre's grassy oval was locally identified with the Round Table. Caerleon also has a surviving medieval castle mound and was the birthplace of novelist Arthur Machen.
