China–Pakistan Economic Corridor

The China–Pakistan Economic Corridor (CPEC) is a collection of infrastructure projects under construction throughout Pakistan, originally valued at about US$46 billion and later expanded to more than US$60 billion. Announced in April 2015, CPEC is a flagship project of China's Belt and Road Initiative and is intended to modernize Pakistan's transport networks and energy infrastructure while linking China's Xinjiang region to the Arabian Sea port of Gwadar.

Projects

CPEC comprises highways, railways, pipelines, and energy projects. Key components include the upgrading of the Karakoram Highway, the ML-1 railway between Karachi and Peshawar, and the development of Gwadar Port and its associated free trade zone. Energy projects, including coal, hydro, wind, and solar plants, were prioritized in the corridor's first phase to address Pakistan's chronic electricity shortages.

Strategic significance

For China, CPEC provides an overland route to the Indian Ocean that shortens the path for energy imports from the Middle East, bypassing the Strait of Malacca. For Pakistan, the corridor promises economic growth, employment, and improved connectivity between its provinces. The corridor's security is guarded by a dedicated Pakistani force, and the project has proceeded in phases, with a second phase emphasizing industrial cooperation, agriculture, and special economic zones.

Criticism

The project has drawn criticism over debt sustainability, transparency, local opposition in Balochistan, and attacks on Chinese workers by militant groups. India objects to CPEC because routes pass through Gilgit-Baltistan, territory claimed by India.
