On 6 January 2021, the United States Capitol in Washington, D.C., was attacked by a mob of supporters of President Donald Trump, who sought to overturn his defeat in the 2020 presidential election by disrupting the joint session of Congress assembled to count electoral votes and formalise Joe Biden's victory. The attack followed weeks of false claims of election fraud and a rally that morning at which Trump urged supporters to march to the Capitol.

Events

After Trump's speech at the "Save America" rally on the Ellipse, thousands marched to the Capitol. Beginning around 2 p.m., rioters breached police barriers and the building itself, forcing the evacuation of the Senate and House chambers and suspending the certification for several hours. Rioters occupied the Senate dais, entered Speaker Nancy Pelosi's office, and clashed with United States Capitol Police and other officers; more than 140 police officers were injured. Trump supporter Ashli Babbitt was shot by a Capitol Police officer while attempting to climb through a barricaded doorway. Four other people died during or in the aftermath of the events, including a police officer who died the following day, and several officers later died by suicide.

Reinforcements, including the D.C. National Guard and federal agents, cleared the building by the evening. Congress reconvened that night and completed the certification of Biden's victory in the early hours of 7 January 2021.

Aftermath

The attack prompted the second impeachment of Donald Trump, on a charge of incitement of insurrection; he was acquitted by the Senate after leaving office. A House select committee investigated the attack through 2021–22 and recommended criminal referrals. The ensuing federal investigation was the largest in United States history: more than 1,500 people were charged with federal crimes, including members of the Proud Boys and Oath Keepers convicted of seditious conspiracy. In January 2025, at the start of his second term, President Trump issued mass pardons and commutations to nearly all of those convicted or awaiting trial in connection with the attack.
