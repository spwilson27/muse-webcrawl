S4C is the Welsh-language public service broadcaster.

S4C (Welsh pronunciation roughly "ess-pedwar-ec", from Sianel Pedwar Cymru, meaning "Channel Four Wales") is a Welsh-language free-to-air public service television channel. It was the first television channel to be aimed specifically at a Welsh-speaking audience and, after BBC One, ITV, and Channel 4, the fourth-oldest national television service in the United Kingdom.

History

S4C launched on 1 November 1982, one day before Channel 4 in the rest of the UK, following a long campaign for a Welsh-language channel. The campaign included Gwynfor Evans, president of Plaid Cymru, threatening a hunger strike in 1980, after which the Conservative government reversed its decision not to establish the channel. In its early years the analogue service shared airtime with Channel 4 programmes in English during hours when Welsh programmes were not broadcast.

Digital era

Following the digital switchover in Wales, completed in 2010, S4C became an entirely Welsh-language service, while Channel 4 became separately available. S4C is funded by the licence fee via the BBC and by direct government grant, and since 2013 it has been headquartered in Cardiff, later moving its main base to the Yr Egin creative centre in Carmarthen. Its programmes include drama, sport, children's programming (the Cyw strand for younger children and Stwnsh for older children), news produced by the BBC, and the long-running soap opera Pobol y Cwm, which transferred from BBC Cymru.

Programming

Well-known S4C productions include the animated series SuperTed and Fireman Sam (in Welsh, Sam Tân), which originated in Welsh before English versions were made, and acclaimed dramas such as Hinterland (Y Gwyll), produced in Welsh and English versions.
