National Autonomist Party

The National Autonomist Party (Spanish: Partido Autonomista Nacional, PAN) was a conservative political party in Argentina that dominated the country's politics from 1874 to 1916, a period known as the Conservative Republic. It was founded in 1874 by the alliance of Adolfo Alsina's Autonomist Party and Nicolás Avellaneda's National Party.

Dominance

PAN presidents — Avellaneda, Julio Argentino Roca (twice), Miguel Juárez Celman, Carlos Pellegrini, Luis Sáenz Peña, José Evaristo Uriburu, Manuel Quintana, José Figueroa Alcorta and Roque Sáenz Peña — held power for over four decades. The party represented the landowning oligarchy of the export boom era, and maintained control through electoral fraud and the voto cantado (public voice vote), while its leaders modernised the state, expanded railways and immigration, and consolidated national institutions. Roca, the party's towering figure, had led the military Conquest of the Desert in 1879.

Decline

Opposition grew from the Radical Civic Union (founded 1891), which staged armed revolutions and abstained from elections in protest. In 1912 President Roque Sáenz Peña enacted the Sáenz Peña Law establishing secret, compulsory and universal male suffrage; in the first free elections of 1916 the Radical Hipólito Yrigoyen won the presidency, ending the PAN era. The party dissolved soon afterwards, its remnants feeding into later conservative parties.
