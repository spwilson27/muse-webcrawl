180-degree rule

The 180-degree rule is a basic guideline of film grammar in continuity editing. It states that two characters in a scene should maintain the same left-right relationship to one another across cuts: the camera should stay on one side of an imaginary line, the axis of action (or "the line"), drawn between the characters or along a subject's direction of movement.

Principle

If all shots in a conversation are taken from one side of the line between two actors, a character who appears on the left of the frame in one shot remains on the left in reverse-angle shots, preserving the audience's spatial orientation and the direction of each character's eyeline. Crossing the line — placing the camera on the other side without a transitional shot — reverses the characters' screen positions and can disorient viewers, making it appear, for example, that a moving car has changed direction.

Exceptions and related techniques

Directors sometimes cross the line deliberately for disorientation or stylistic effect, or "reset" the axis using a shot taken directly on the line, a moving shot that crosses during the take, or an establishing shot that re-defines the geography. The rule is closely related to the 30-degree rule, which concerns the minimum change in camera angle between cuts, and to eyeline match and shot/reverse-shot conventions.
