Labour rights

Labour rights or workers' rights are the legal rights and human rights relating to labour relations between workers and employers, generally codified in labour and employment law. They include the right to organise and bargain collectively, freedom from forced labour and child labour, equality and non-discrimination, safe working conditions, reasonable working hours, and a minimum or living wage.

History

The modern concept of labour rights emerged from the labour movement of the Industrial Revolution, as factory workers campaigned against child labour, excessive hours and dangerous conditions. The International Labour Organization (ILO), founded in 1919 under the Treaty of Versailles, became the principal global standard-setter; its 1998 Declaration on Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work identifies core conventions on freedom of association, collective bargaining, forced labour, child labour and discrimination, joined in 2022 by occupational safety and health.

Key rights and debates

Universal instruments such as the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (Article 23) and the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights recognise the right to work, to just and favourable conditions, and to form trade unions. Contemporary debates concern gig-economy classification, platform work, supply-chain due diligence, and the right to disconnect.
