Thematic relation

Thematic relations, also called semantic roles or theta roles, are categories in linguistics that describe the relationship between a verb and its arguments in a sentence. They capture how participants in an event are involved: who acts, who is acted upon, who experiences something, and so on, independently of grammatical structure such as subject or object.

Common roles

Frequently proposed roles include the agent (the initiator of an action), the patient or theme (the entity affected or moved), the experiencer (one who perceives or feels), the instrument (the means used), the goal and source (endpoints of motion or transfer), and the beneficiary.

Theory

The concept was developed in generative grammar, notably by Charles Fillmore in his case grammar of 1968 and in work by Ray Jackendoff and others. Thematic roles help explain how the same participant can appear in different syntactic positions, as in active–passive alternations. See also Theta role and Argument (linguistics).
