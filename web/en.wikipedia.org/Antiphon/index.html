Antiphon

An antiphon (Greek ἀντίφωνον, ἀντί "opposite" and φωνή "voice") is a short chant in Christian ritual, sung as a refrain. The texts of antiphons are usually taken from the Psalms or other parts of Scripture. "Antiphonal music" is that performed by two choirs in interaction, often singing alternate phrases, according to the manner of antiphonal psalmody.

Use

In the Latin liturgy of the Roman Catholic Church, antiphons frame the psalms sung in the Liturgy of the Hours, the Mass, and other services. The Marian antiphons — such as the Salve Regina, Alma Redemptoris Mater, Ave Regina Caelorum, and Regina Caeli — are sung at the conclusion of Compline and other devotions. In Advent, the "O Antiphons" accompany the Magnificat at Vespers in the days before Christmas.

Byzantine chant

In the Byzantine Rite, antiphons are hymns sung at the beginning of the Divine Liturgy and in other services, continuing the ancient practice of alternating choirs.
