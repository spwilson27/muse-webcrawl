Irish folklore

Irish folklore (Irish: béaloideas) refers to the body of traditional tales, legends, customs, and beliefs transmitted orally in Ireland over many centuries. Rooted in Celtic mythology and medieval Irish literature, it encompasses mythological cycles of gods and heroes, fairy lore, ghost stories, folk cures, and the seasonal customs of the rural year.

Mythological cycles

Medieval manuscripts preserve four great cycles: the Mythological Cycle, concerning the Tuatha Dé Danann; the Ulster Cycle, centred on Cú Chulainn and the heroes of Ulaid; the Fenian Cycle, telling of Fionn mac Cumhaill and the Fianna; and the Cycles of the Kings. These stories, recorded by Christian monks from the early medieval period onward, link narrative to landscape through the tradition of dinnseanchas, the lore of place-names.

Fairy lore

The aos sí ("people of the mounds") inhabit the hollow hills and ring-forts of the countryside. Fairy beings include the leprechaun, the solitary cobbler who guards a crock of gold; the banshee (bean sídhe), whose keening foretells a death in the family; the pooka (púca), a shape-shifting trickster; and changelings, fairy infants supposedly left in place of stolen children. Belief in the fairies shaped etiquette: fairy thorn trees were left uncut, and paths between fairy forts were kept clear.

Storytelling and collection

The traditional storyteller, the seanchaí, was a central figure of rural entertainment. From the nineteenth century, collectors such as Lady Gregory and W. B. Yeats drew folklore into the Irish literary revival. In 1935 the Irish Folklore Commission was founded to record the tradition systematically; its Schools' Collection of 1937–39 gathered material from schoolchildren across the country, and the archives are now held at University College Dublin.

Customs

The year was marked by the quarter days — Imbolc, Beltane, Lughnasa and Samhain — with bonfires, divination games and protective charms. Samhain, the year's end, when the veil between worlds thinned, is a root of modern Halloween customs.
