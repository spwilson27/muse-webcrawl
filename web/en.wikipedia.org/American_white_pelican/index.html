FamilyPelecanidae

The American white pelican (Pelecanus erythrorhynchos) is a large aquatic soaring bird from the order Pelecaniformes. It breeds in interior North America, moving south and to the coasts, as far as Costa Rica, in winter.

Description

The American white pelican measures around 130–180 cm (51–71 in) in length, with a wingspan of 240–300 cm (7.9–9.8 ft), making it one of the largest flying birds in North America. Its plumage is almost entirely bright white, except for the black primary and secondary remiges visible in flight. The bill is huge and flat on the top, with a large throat sac below it; in the breeding season it turns bright orange, and adults grow a laterally flattened "horn" on the upper bill, which is shed after the eggs are laid.

Distribution and behavior

Unlike the brown pelican, the American white pelican does not dive for its food; instead it catches fish while swimming, often working cooperatively in groups to herd fish into shallow water. Breeding colonies nest on isolated islands in freshwater lakes and reservoirs of the northern Great Plains and western North America. Colonies are highly sensitive to disturbance, and abandonment was a major cause of the species' decline in the early 20th century; populations have since recovered substantially.
