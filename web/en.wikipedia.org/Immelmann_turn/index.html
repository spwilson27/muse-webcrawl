Immelmann turn

The Immelmann turn, named after the German World War I flying ace Max Immelmann, is an aerial manoeuvre used in air combat and aerobatics. In its modern form it consists of a half-loop followed by a half-roll, which reverses the aircraft's direction of flight while gaining altitude.

Description

The pilot pulls the aircraft up into the first half of a loop; at the top, inverted, the aircraft is half-rolled back to upright flight, emerging on a reciprocal heading at a higher altitude, with speed traded for height. In the First World War, Immelmann's original manoeuvre was likely a steep climbing turn with a rudder turn at the top — more akin to a modern chandelle or wingover — used to dive back onto an opponent.

Uses

In combat the manoeuvre allowed a pilot to reposition quickly for another attack. Today it is a staple of aerobatic displays and is also mirrored, in name and shape, in a roller-coaster element: the "Immelmann" inversion found on many dive and floorless coasters flips riders through a half-loop and half-twist.
