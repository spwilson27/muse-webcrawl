Jötunheimr

Old NorseJǫtunheimr, "home of the jötnar"
InhabitantsJötnar (giants)
Notable placesÚtgarðr, Gastropnir, Þrymheimr
SourcesPoetic Edda, Prose Edda

Jötunheimr (Old Norse Jǫtunheimr, "home/world of the jötnar") is, in Norse mythology, the homeland of the jötnar, the giants who stand in perennial tension and conflict with the gods. It is conceived as a wild, mountainous region lying across rivers and dark forests from Asgard and Midgard, separated from the world of gods and men by the river Ífingr.

Description in the sources

The Poetic Edda and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda describe Jötunheimr as a realm of mountains, wilderness, and strongholds. Among its named places is Útgarðr, the fortress of the giant illusionist Útgarða-Loki, whom Thor visits in the famous tale of contests against personifications of fire, thought, and old age. Other locations include Gastropnir, the stronghold of Menglöð, and Þrymheimr, home of the giant Thjazi and later his daughter Skadi. The well of Mimir, beneath one root of the world tree Yggdrasil, is also associated with the land of the giants and with primeval wisdom.

Mythological role

The jötnar of Jötunheimr are both enemies and kin of the gods: the Æsir repeatedly seek brides, treasures, and knowledge from them, and Odin himself is descended from giants on his mother's side. At Ragnarök, the giants are prophesied to sail against the gods in the ship Naglfar and storm the plain of Vígríðr for the final battle.

Modern namesake

The name survives in Jotunheimen, the great mountain range of southern Norway — home to the country's highest peaks, including Galdhøpiggen — which was given its mythological name in the nineteenth century and is now largely a national park.
