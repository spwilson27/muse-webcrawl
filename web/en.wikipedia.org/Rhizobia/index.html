Rhizobia

Rhizobia are soil bacteria that fix atmospheric nitrogen in symbiosis with leguminous plants. They infect the roots of legumes such as peas, beans, clover, alfalfa and soybeans, inducing the formation of root nodules within which the bacteria convert inert N2 gas into ammonia usable by the plant, receiving sugars in return.

Taxonomy

The rhizobia are a paraphyletic group of mostly alpha-proteobacteria, including the genera Rhizobium, Bradyrhizobium, Sinorhizobium (Ensifer), Mesorhizobium and Azorhizobium, plus some beta-proteobacteria. The ability to nodulate is carried on symbiotic plasmids or islands and can transfer between strains.

The symbiosis

The plant secretes flavonoids that activate bacterial nod genes; the bacteria produce Nod factor signals that trigger root hair curling and division of cortical cells to form the nodule. Inside nodule cells the bacteria differentiate into bacteroids and fix nitrogen using the oxygen-sensitive nitrogenase enzyme, protected by the oxygen carrier leghemoglobin, which gives active nodules their pink colour.

Agriculture

Biological nitrogen fixation by legume–rhizobium symbioses contributes tens of millions of tonnes of nitrogen annually to agriculture, and seed inoculants of selected strains are widely used to improve legume yields.
