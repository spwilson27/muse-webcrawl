The National Highways Development Project (NHDP) is a program for upgrading, rehabilitating and widening major highways in India to international standards, managed by the National Highways Authority of India (NHAI) under the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways.

Phases

The project was launched in 1998 under Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee. Its first phase comprised the Golden Quadrilateral, a network of about 5,846 km of four- and six-lane highways connecting India's four largest metros — Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata. The second phase built the North–South and East–West Corridors, linking Srinagar to Kanyakumari and Silchar to Porbandar. Later phases covered four-laning of additional stretches, six-laning, expressways, ring roads, bypasses and other works across successive phases up to Phase VII.

Funding and succession

The NHDP was financed through a combination of fuel cess, market borrowings, multilateral loans and public-private partnership concessions on build-operate-transfer terms. In 2017 the government folded the remaining NHDP works into the broader Bharatmala Pariyojana programme.
