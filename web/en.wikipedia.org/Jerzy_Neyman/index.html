Jerzy Neyman (April 16, 1894 – August 5, 1981), born Jerzy Spława-Neyman, was a Polish mathematician and statistician who spent most of his professional career at the University of California, Berkeley. With Egon Pearson he developed the Neyman–Pearson theory of hypothesis testing, the foundation of modern statistical inference.

Contributions

Neyman's 1933 paper with Pearson introduced the likelihood-ratio approach, the concepts of Type I and Type II errors, and the Neyman–Pearson lemma identifying the most powerful tests. In 1934 he presented the theory of representative sampling—including what is now called Neyman allocation for stratified samples—and the concept of confidence intervals, which he published in 1937. His work transformed statistics from a collection of ad hoc procedures into a rigorous decision-theoretic discipline.

Life

Born in Bendery in Bessarabia, Neyman studied in Kharkov and Warsaw, worked in London with Karl and Egon Pearson in the 1920s and 1930s, and emigrated to the United States in 1938 to join Berkeley, where he built one of the world's leading statistics departments and founded the Berkeley Symposia on Mathematical Statistics and Probability. He received the National Medal of Science in 1968.
