Potential energy

Symbol: U, V, or Ep

SI unit: Joule (J)

Relation: U = −∫ F · dr

Types: Gravitational, elastic, electric, chemical

In physics, potential energy is the energy held by an object because of its position relative to other objects, stresses within itself, its electric charge, or other factors. It is "potential" in the sense that it can be converted into other forms of energy, especially kinetic energy, when the object is released.

The term was introduced by the Scottish engineer and physicist William Rankine in the nineteenth century. Potential energy is defined for conservative forces: the work done by such a force depends only on the endpoints of the motion, and the potential energy is the negative of that work.

Gravitational potential energy

Near the Earth's surface, the gravitational potential energy of a mass m raised to height h is U = mgh. More generally, for two masses attracting each other, U = −GmM/r, with zero conventionally taken at infinite separation — which is why gravitational potential energy is often negative.

Other forms

A stretched or compressed spring stores elastic potential energy, ½kx². Electric potential energy is stored in systems of charges and in capacitors; chemical potential energy is stored in molecular bonds and is released in reactions such as combustion; nuclear potential energy is released in fission and fusion. In every case, only differences in potential energy are physically meaningful, because the zero point is a matter of convention.
