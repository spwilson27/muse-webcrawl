Amazon Kindle

Developer: Amazon

Type: E-reader

Release date: 19 November 2007

Display: Electronic paper (E Ink)

Online service: Kindle Store

Amazon Kindle is a series of e-readers designed and marketed by Amazon. Kindle devices use E Ink electronic paper displays that mimic printed paper, consume very little power, and remain readable in bright sunlight. Users can browse, buy, download and read e-books, newspapers and magazines from the Kindle Store, delivered wirelessly over Wi-Fi or cellular networks.

History

The original Kindle launched in November 2007 at $399 and sold out in under six hours. The Kindle 2 (2009) slimmer design was followed by the Kindle DX with a larger screen. The fourth generation introduced the touch-screen Kindle Touch, and the Kindle Paperwhite (2012) added a built-in front light. The premium Kindle Voyage and the waterproof, aluminium Kindle Oasis followed, along with the large-format Kindle Scribe (2022), which supports handwriting with a stylus, and colour models (Colorsoft) in 2024.

Software and formats

Kindle devices natively support Amazon's proprietary AZW/KFX formats and, following updates, EPUB via Send to Kindle, PDF and plain text. Features include the X-Ray reference tool, Word Wise vocabulary hints, adjustable typography, Whispersync bookmark synchronisation, and integration with the Goodreads social reading platform. Kindle software applications are also available for phones, tablets and computers.

Reception and impact

The Kindle is widely credited with popularising e-books and making digital editions a mass market. Amazon's ecosystem, including Kindle Direct Publishing for self-published authors and the Kindle Unlimited subscription, reshaped trade publishing, though the company's market power and DRM practices have drawn criticism and regulatory scrutiny.
