Paraboloid

In geometry, a paraboloid is a quadric surface that has exactly one axis of symmetry and no centre of symmetry. The term is derived from parabola, which refers to a conic section that has a similar property of symmetry. There are two kinds of paraboloid: elliptic and hyperbolic.

The elliptic paraboloid is shaped like an oval cup and can have a maximum or minimum point; with suitable axes it has the equation z = x²/a² + y²/b². When a = b it is a circular paraboloid, the surface obtained by revolving a parabola about its axis. The hyperbolic paraboloid, with equation z = x²/a² − y²/b², is a doubly ruled saddle surface shaped like a saddle — familiar from the shape of Pringles potato crisps and from hyperbolic paraboloid roofs in modern architecture.

Properties and applications

Every section of a paraboloid by a plane parallel to its axis is a parabola. The circular paraboloid has the important optical property that rays parallel to its axis are reflected to a single point, the focus; conversely, a source at the focus produces a parallel beam. This makes paraboloids useful for satellite dishes, radio telescopes, headlight and searchlight reflectors, and solar furnaces. The hyperbolic paraboloid is a doubly ruled surface, meaning it can be constructed from straight lines, a property exploited by architects such as Félix Candela in thin-shell concrete roofs.
