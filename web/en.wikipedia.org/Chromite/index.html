Chromite

Chromite is a crystalline mineral composed primarily of iron(II) oxide and chromium(III) oxide compounds. It can be represented by the chemical formula of FeCr2O4. It is an oxide mineral belonging to the spinel group. The element magnesium can substitute for iron in variable amounts as it forms a solid solution with magnesiochromite (MgCr2O4). Chromite is the only economically significant ore of chromium, an essential metal for producing stainless steel, nichrome, chrome plating and pigments.

Occurrence

Chromite is found in peridotite and other layered ultramafic intrusive rocks, and also occurs in metamorphic rocks such as serpentinites. Economically important stratiform deposits occur in large layered intrusions — most famously the Bushveld Igneous Complex of South Africa, which holds the world's largest reserves — while podiform deposits in ophiolite complexes are mined in Kazakhstan, Turkey, Albania, the Philippines and Oman. Because it is hard, dense and chemically resistant, chromite also concentrates in placer deposits.

Uses

More than 90% of mined chromite is converted to ferrochrome, the alloy fed into stainless steel production. The remainder supplies the chemical industry (chromium salts, tanning agents, pigments such as chrome yellow) and refractory materials, where chromite bricks withstand high temperatures in furnaces. Major producing countries include South Africa, Kazakhstan, India and Turkey.
