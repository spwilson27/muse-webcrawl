The Canada Border Services Agency (CBSA; French: Agence des services frontaliers du Canada, ASFC) is a federal law enforcement agency responsible for border control, customs, immigration enforcement and the protection of Canada's borders. It operates at some 1,200 service points across Canada and dozens abroad.

History

The agency was created on 12 December 2003 by amalgamating the customs functions of the former Canada Customs and Revenue Agency, the border and enforcement functions of Citizenship and Immigration Canada, and the port-of-entry inspection functions of the Canadian Food Inspection Agency. It reports to the Minister of Public Safety.

Responsibilities

CBSA officers staff ports of entry, examine travellers and goods, collect duties and taxes, enforce immigration and refugee law, interdict contraband including drugs and firearms, and administer trade programmes. The agency also operates the NEXUS trusted traveller programme jointly with U.S. Customs and Border Protection, and its officers have been armed since 2006.
