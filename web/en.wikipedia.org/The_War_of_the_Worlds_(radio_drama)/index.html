"The War of the Worlds" is an episode of the American radio drama anthology series The Mercury Theatre on the Air, directed and narrated by Orson Welles as an adaptation of H. G. Wells's 1898 novel The War of the Worlds. It was performed and broadcast live as a Halloween episode at 8 p.m. Eastern Time on October 30, 1938, over the CBS Radio Network.

Format

Written by Howard E. Koch with producer John Houseman's input, the first two-thirds of the hour-long broadcast were presented as a series of simulated news bulletins interrupting a program of dance music. The bulletins reported explosions on Mars, the landing of a meteor-like cylinder at Grover's Mill, New Jersey, and the emergence of Martians whose heat-rays devastate the countryside and march on New York City. The format and the delivery of actor Frank Readick, imitating live reporting of the Hindenburg disaster, gave the show an air of authenticity.

The "panic"

Contemporary newspapers reported that some listeners, tuning in late and missing the opening disclaimer, believed an actual invasion was underway; there were calls to police stations and newspaper offices, and isolated reports of people fleeing. Later scholarship, notably by Jefferson Pooley and Michael Socolow, has argued that the scale of the panic was greatly exaggerated by newspapers, which had an interest in discrediting their new radio competitor. Press coverage nonetheless made Welles famous and secured him his Hollywood contract with RKO Pictures.

Legacy

The broadcast became the most famous radio drama in history, a foundational text in media studies of audience gullibility and mass communication, and was studied in Hadley Cantril's 1940 book The Invasion from Mars. It was selected for preservation in the National Recording Registry in 2003.
