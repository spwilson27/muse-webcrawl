Vistula Spit canal

The Vistula Spit canal (Polish: Kanał żeglugowy przez Mierzeję Wiślaną), officially a shipping channel through the Vistula Spit, is a canal across the Polish section of the Vistula Spit creating a second connection between the Vistula Lagoon and Gdańsk Bay. It allows ships to enter the Vistula Lagoon and the port of Elbląg without having to rely on the Russian-controlled Strait of Baltiysk, the sole previous entrance.

Construction

The canal is about 1,305 metres (4,281 ft) long, including a lock about 200 metres long with two swing bridges, allowing passage of vessels up to 100 metres long, 20 metres in beam, and 5 metres draft. It was built near the village of Nowy Świat. Construction began in 2019 at a cost of roughly 2 billion złoty; the canal was officially opened on 17 September 2022, a symbolic date — the 83rd anniversary of the Soviet invasion of Poland in 1939. The first cargo ship transited in 2023 after dredging of the approach fairway and the port of Elbląg.

Background and controversy

After Russia restricted navigation through the Strait of Baltiysk, notably blocking Polish vessels around the 2006–2009 period, Polish governments pursued the canal as a strategic project assuring free access to the lagoon. Russia objected on environmental and military grounds, and environmental groups criticised the impact on the spit's protected habitats. The project became a flagship of the Law and Justice government and a subject of partisan debate in Poland.
