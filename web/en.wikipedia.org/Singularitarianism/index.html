Singularitarianism

Singularitarianism is a movement defined by the belief that a technological singularity — the creation of superintelligence — will bring about enormous, rapid changes to human civilisation, and that action should be taken to ensure the singularity benefits humanity. The term was coined by the extropian thinker Mark Plus and was popularised through the work of Eliezer Yudkowsky and the inventor and futurist Ray Kurzweil, whose 2005 book The Singularity Is Near predicted a singularity around 2045.

Beliefs

Singularitarians hold that progress in artificial intelligence, nanotechnology, biotechnology and related fields is accelerating exponentially, following patterns such as Moore's law. Many believe that a future superintelligent AI could recursively improve itself, producing an "intelligence explosion" (a concept traced to I. J. Good in 1965). Because the outcome could be extraordinarily good or catastrophic, adherents emphasise AI alignment and safety research, seeking a "friendly" superintelligence. The movement overlaps with transhumanism and life-extension advocacy, and its adherents often support cryonics and radical longevity research.

Organisations and community

The Machine Intelligence Research Institute (founded in 2000 as the Singularity Institute), the former Singularity University (founded 2008 by Kurzweil and Peter Diamandis), and various conferences and online communities have been central institutions. Within the broader rationalist community, singularitarian ideas about existential risk from AI have influenced effective altruism and longtermism.

Criticism

Critics dispute the extrapolation of exponential trends, the feasibility of recursive self-improvement, and the timelines proposed; some describe the movement's expectations in religious terms, a comparison satirised as "the rapture of the nerds". Others accept the importance of AI safety while rejecting predicted near-term dates for superintelligence.
