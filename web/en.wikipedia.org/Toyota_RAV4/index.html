Toyota RAV4

The Toyota RAV4 is a compact crossover SUV produced by Toyota since 1994. Its name derives from "Recreational Active Vehicle with 4-wheel drive". The RAV4 is widely credited with creating the modern compact crossover segment: it combined the raised driving position and all-weather capability of an SUV with the unibody construction, economy and road manners of a passenger car. By the late 2010s it had become one of the best-selling vehicles of any kind in the world, and Toyota's best-selling SUV.

Generations

  • First generation (XA10, 1994–2000) — launched in Japan and Europe in 1994 and North America in 1996; offered in two- and four-door forms, plus a short-lived RAV4 EV built with Tesla-era battery technology in California later in its life cycle.
  • Second generation (XA20, 2000–2005) — larger, four-door only, with more powerful engines.
  • Third generation (XA30, 2005–2012) — grew substantially; optional V6 and a third-row seat in some markets; a second-generation RAV4 EV (2012–2014) was developed with Tesla powertrains.
  • Fourth generation (XA40, 2012–2018) — dropped the V6 and side-hinged rear door; hybrid version introduced in 2015.
  • Fifth generation (XA50, 2018–present) — built on the TNGA-K platform with bolder styling; hybrid and plug-in hybrid (RAV4 Prime) versions became the volume sellers in many markets, with the Prime among the quickest Toyotas sold.

Sales and significance

The RAV4 repeatedly topped global SUV sales charts and passed ten million cumulative sales in 2020. Its success prompted virtually every rival manufacturer to field a compact crossover, making the segment it invented the largest in the world car market.
