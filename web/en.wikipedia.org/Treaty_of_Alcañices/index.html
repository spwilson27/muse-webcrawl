Treaty of Alcañices

The Treaty of Alcañices (Portuguese: Tratado de Alcanizes) was made on 12 September 1297 in the town of Alcañices, in the Kingdom of León, between King Denis of Portugal and King Ferdinand IV of Castile. It established the frontier between Portugal and Castile, fixing borders that, in large part, remain in place today — making them among the oldest international borders still in force in Europe.

Terms

By the treaty, Portugal renounced claims to several places in León and Galicia, while Castile ceded to Portugal a series of frontier towns and territories, including the fortified villages of the Ribacoa region (such as Almeida, Castelo Rodrigo, and Sabugal) and Olivenza. The agreement was sealed by a double marriage arrangement between the two royal houses. The border drawn in 1297 endured for centuries with only minor modifications, notably the later dispute over Olivenza, ceded by Portugal to Spain in 1801 under the Treaty of Badajoz but still claimed by Portugal.
