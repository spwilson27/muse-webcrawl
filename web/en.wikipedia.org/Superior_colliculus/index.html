Superior colliculus

The superior colliculus (Latin for "upper hill") is a paired structure on the roof of the mammalian midbrain (tectum). In non-mammalian vertebrates its homologue is the optic tectum. Together with the smaller inferior colliculi it forms the corpora quadrigemina.

Structure and function

The superior colliculus is a layered structure. Its superficial layers receive direct input from the retina and visual cortex and contain a retinotopic map of visual space. Deeper layers integrate auditory and somatosensory information into aligned spatial maps and generate motor commands. The colliculus is a key center for orienting movements — especially saccadic eye movements, but also head and body turns — toward salient stimuli.

It receives input from the basal ganglia (notably the substantia nigra pars reticulata, which tonically inhibits it) and the frontal eye fields, and projects to brainstem gaze centers. The superior colliculus also contributes to covert shifts of attention and to reflexive responses to looming or novel stimuli.
