Amber Room

The Amber Room (Russian: Янтарная комната) was a chamber decorated in amber panels backed with gold leaf and mirrors, once located in the Catherine Palace at Tsarskoye Selo near Saint Petersburg. Constructed in the 18th century, looted by Nazi Germany during World War II and taken to Königsberg, it disappeared in 1945 and became one of the most famous lost treasures in the world. A painstaking reconstruction, completed in 2003, now stands in its place.

Creation

The room's panels were begun in 1701 for Charlottenburg Palace in Berlin, designed by Andreas Schlüter and executed by Danish amber craftsmen. In 1716 the Prussian king Frederick William I gave the panels to Tsar Peter the Great of Russia as a diplomatic gift cementing an alliance. In Russia the room was expanded — eventually covering more than 55 square metres with about six tonnes of amber — and installed in the Catherine Palace under Empress Elizabeth, with additional work by Bartolomeo Rastrelli.

Disappearance

During the German invasion of the Soviet Union in 1941, curators failed to remove the panels (the amber had become too brittle), and German troops dismantled the room in 36 hours, shipping it to Königsberg Castle, where it was displayed until the castle was destroyed by bombing and fire in 1944–45. Despite decades of searching and numerous theories — sunken ships, buried bunkers, hidden mines — the original panels have never been found; isolated fragments, including a mosaic and a commode, resurfaced in Germany in 1997.

Reconstruction

Using surviving photographs and drawings, Russian craftsmen began recreating the room in 1979; the reconstruction was inaugurated in 2003 for Saint Petersburg's tercentenary, partly financed by German sponsors.
