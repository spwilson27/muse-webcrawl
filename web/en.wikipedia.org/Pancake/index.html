Pancake

A pancake is a flat cake, often thin and round, prepared from a starch-based batter and cooked on a hot surface such as a griddle or frying pan. Archaeological evidence suggests pancakes are among the earliest cereal foods eaten in prehistoric societies; Ötzi the Iceman's stomach contents included partially digested pancake-like material.

Ingredients and preparation

Most pancakes contain flour, eggs, milk and butter, often with a leavening agent such as baking powder that produces a thick, fluffy griddle cake. The batter is poured onto a hot, oiled surface and cooked until bubbles appear, then flipped and browned on the other side. They are commonly served at breakfast with maple syrup, butter, fruit or jam.

Varieties

Regional varieties include the thin French crêpe, Russian blini made with buckwheat, Scottish drop scones, Welsh crempog, Ethiopian injera, Indian dosa and uttapam, Korean jeon, Japanese okonomiyaki and dorayaki, and the Dutch baby pancake. In England, pancakes are traditionally eaten on Shrove Tuesday ("Pancake Day"), when pancake races are held.
