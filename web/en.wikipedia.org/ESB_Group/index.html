The ESB Group (Electricity Supply Board; Irish: Bord Soláthair an Leictreachais) is a state-owned electricity company operating in Ireland. It was established in 1927 to manage the Shannon hydroelectric scheme at Ardnacrusha and to build a national electricity network, and it remains Ireland's largest electricity utility.

ESB is Ireland's state-owned electricity utility, founded in 1927.

Operations

ESB Group comprises several businesses. ESB Networks builds and maintains the electricity distribution system in the Republic of Ireland; Electric Ireland is its retail supply brand; ESB Generation and Trading operates a portfolio of power stations including Moneypoint, once Ireland's only coal-fired station; and NIE Networks serves Northern Ireland. The transmission system operator, EirGrid, was separated from ESB in 2009.

History

The young Irish Free State created the ESB in 1927 as one of its first major state enterprises, centred on the ambitious Shannon Scheme, then the largest hydroelectric project of its kind. Over the following decades ESB electrified rural Ireland, completing its rural electrification programme in the 1970s. Following EU energy market liberalisation, the Irish electricity market was progressively opened to competition, and ESB rebranded its supply business as Electric Ireland in 2011.
