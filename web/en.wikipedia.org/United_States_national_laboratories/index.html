United States national laboratories

The United States national laboratories are a system of federally funded research and development centers (FFRDCs), most of them sponsored by the Department of Energy (DOE). The DOE system comprises 17 national laboratories conducting research in nuclear security, energy, environmental science and fundamental discovery science.

Origins

The system originated in the Manhattan Project, whose wartime sites at Los Alamos, Oak Ridge and Hanford became permanent laboratories under the Atomic Energy Commission in 1947 and later the Department of Energy in 1977.

Missions

The three NNSA laboratories (Los Alamos, Livermore, Sandia) steward the nuclear weapons stockpile without explosive testing. Office of Science laboratories operate major user facilities — particle accelerators, light sources, neutron sources and supercomputers — used by thousands of visiting researchers, while others focus on energy technology, fossil and renewable energy, and environmental cleanup science.

Other federal laboratories

Outside DOE, agencies such as the Department of Defense, NASA, NIST, NIH and the intelligence community also maintain national-level laboratories and FFRDCs, though "the national laboratories" most often denotes the DOE system.
