Catheter

In medicine, a catheter is a thin flexible tube inserted into a body cavity, duct or blood vessel to allow drainage, delivery of fluids or gases, or access by surgical instruments. Catheters are made from materials including silicone rubber, latex, polyurethane and various plastics, and are usually single-use sterile devices.

Common types

The urinary (Foley) catheter, retained in the bladder by an inflatable balloon, drains urine. Intravenous catheters and central venous catheters deliver fluids, blood and medication into veins. Cardiac catheterisation threads a catheter through blood vessels to the heart to measure pressures, image coronary arteries and perform interventions such as angioplasty. Sizes are commonly measured on the French scale.

Risks

The chief risks of catheterisation are infection — catheter-associated urinary tract infections and bloodstream infections are major hospital-acquired complications — plus trauma, blockage and thrombosis, so devices are removed as soon as they are no longer needed.
