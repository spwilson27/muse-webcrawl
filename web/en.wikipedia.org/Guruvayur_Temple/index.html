Guruvayur Temple is a Hindu temple dedicated to Guruvayurappan, a four-armed form of the god Vishnu in his aspect as Krishna, located in the town of Guruvayur in Kerala, India. Often called the "Dwaraka of the South", it is one of the most important places of worship for Hindus in Kerala and one of the most visited temples in India. The presiding deity is worshipped mainly in the form of the infant Krishna.

Legend

According to legend, the idol was worshipped by Krishna himself at Dwaraka and was rescued from the deluge that submerged the city by Brihaspati (Guru), the preceptor of the gods, and Vayu, the wind god — whose names combine to form "Guruvayur". The temple's existence is recorded in literature by the 16th century, and the saint-poet Melpathur Narayana Bhattathiri composed the celebrated Sanskrit work Narayaneeyam at the temple in 1586, traditionally after being cured of paralysis there.

Architecture and worship

The temple follows Kerala architectural conventions, with a sreekovil (sanctum) housing the idol, a gold-plated dwajasthamba (flagstaff), and a deepasthambam of carved stone lamps. Strict dress codes apply to devotees, and entry is restricted to Hindus. Daily rituals begin in the early morning with the Nirmalya Darshanam. The temple is also famous for conducting large numbers of marriages and for its elephant sanctuary at nearby Punnathur Kotta, which houses temple elephants including the celebrated Guruvayur Kesavan.

History

The temple survived attacks during the Mysorean invasions of the 18th century, when the idol was temporarily moved to Ambalappuzha. A major fire in 1970 damaged parts of the complex, which was subsequently rebuilt under the Guruvayur Devaswom.
