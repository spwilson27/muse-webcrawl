Cuisine of Kerala

The cuisine of Kerala, a state in southwestern India, is linked to its history, culture and geography along the Malabar Coast. Rice, coconut and spices — notably black pepper, cardamom, chilli, turmeric and curry leaves — define the cooking, along with abundant fish and seafood. Kerala has been a centre of the spice trade for millennia, and its food reflects Hindu, Muslim (Mappila), Christian (Syrian Christian) and Jewish influences.

Sadya

The sadya is a traditional vegetarian feast served on a banana leaf, especially during the Onam festival and at weddings. It consists of rice with a procession of dishes such as sambar, avial (vegetables in coconut and yoghurt), thoran (stir-fried vegetables with coconut), olan, pachadi, pickles, pappadam and desserts such as payasam.

Breakfast dishes

Popular breakfast items include appam (fermented rice pancakes with lacy edges), puttu (steamed cylinders of ground rice layered with coconut), idiyappam (rice noodle cakes), dosa and idli, commonly accompanied by coconut chutney, sambar, egg curry or kadala (chickpea) curry.

Regional traditions

Malabar (Mappila) cuisine in the north is known for biryanis, pathiri rice breads and seafood. Syrian Christian cooking features dishes such as meen pollichathu (fish wrapped in banana leaf), duck roast and beef preparations. Tapioca (kappa) with fish curry is a beloved staple combination across the state.
