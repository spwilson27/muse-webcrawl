Nevis is a small island in the Caribbean Sea, part of the inner arc of the Leeward Islands chain of the West Indies. Together with the neighbouring island of Saint Kitts, about 3 km to the north-west across a channel called The Narrows, it forms the federation of Saint Kitts and Nevis, the smallest sovereign state in the Americas by both area and population.

Geography

Nevis is roughly conical in shape, dominated by Nevis Peak, a dormant volcano rising to 985 metres, usually capped by clouds. The island has an area of about 93 square kilometres. Its capital and largest settlement is Charlestown, on the western coast. The name Nevis derives from the Spanish Nuestra Señora de las Nieves ("Our Lady of the Snows"), a reference to the white clouds around the peak resembling snow.

History

The island was inhabited by Amerindian peoples long before Christopher Columbus sighted it in 1493. English settlers arrived in 1628, and Nevis became one of Britain's most profitable sugar colonies, worked by enslaved Africans. It was the birthplace and childhood home of Alexander Hamilton, and Horatio Nelson married Frances Nisbet on Nevis in 1787. Nevis joined the federation with Saint Kitts on independence in 1983; the constitution uniquely grants Nevis the right to secede, and a 1998 referendum on independence fell short of the required two-thirds majority.

Economy and government

Nevis has its own Island Assembly and premier under the federal system. Tourism, offshore financial services, and the citizenship-by-investment programme are economic mainstays. The island is known for its historic plantation inns and quiet beaches.
