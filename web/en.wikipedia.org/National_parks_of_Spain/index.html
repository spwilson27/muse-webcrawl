National parks of Spain

There are sixteen national parks in Spain (Spanish: parques nacionales), administered under the framework of the National Parks Autonomous Agency (OAPN) together with the autonomous communities. They protect representative examples of Spain's natural heritage, from high mountain ranges to wetlands, volcanic islands, and marine environments.

History

Spain was among the pioneers of national parks in Europe: the first, the Mountain of Covadonga (now Picos de Europa), was created in 1918, followed by Ordesa y Monte Perdido the same year. The most recent addition, Sierra de las Nieves in Andalusia, was declared in 2021.

Notable parks

Notable parks include Teide on Tenerife, centred on Spain's highest peak and one of the world's most visited national parks; Doñana, a vast wetland at the Guadalquivir delta critical for migratory birds and the Iberian lynx; Garajonay on La Gomera, preserving laurel forest; Timanfaya's volcanic landscapes on Lanzarote; and Cabrera Archipelago, Spain's largest marine-terrestrial park.
