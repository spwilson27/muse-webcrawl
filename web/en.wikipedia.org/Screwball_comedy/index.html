Screwball comedy

Screwball comedy is a subgenre of romantic comedy that was popular in American cinema during the Great Depression, beginning in the early 1930s and thriving until the early 1940s. It is characterised by fast-paced repartee, farcical situations, battles of the sexes, and plots involving courtship and class conflict, often with an eccentric heroine who turns the hero's world upside down.

Characteristics

Screwball comedies typically feature rapid-fire dialogue, mistaken identities, improbable coincidences and a reversal of traditional gender roles, with a strong, unconventional female lead. The genre partly developed as a way to handle romantic and sexual themes under the restrictions of the Hays Code, substituting verbal sparring for physical intimacy.

Notable films

Classic examples include It Happened One Night (1934), Twentieth Century (1934), My Man Godfrey (1936), The Awful Truth (1937), Bringing Up Baby (1938) and His Girl Friday (1940), directed by filmmakers such as Frank Capra, Howard Hawks, Gregory La Cava and Leo McCarey, and starring actors like Cary Grant, Katharine Hepburn, Carole Lombard and William Powell.

Influence

The genre's dialogue-driven style influenced later romantic comedies, and the term "screwball" — borrowed from a baseball pitch — came to describe zany, eccentric behaviour more generally.
