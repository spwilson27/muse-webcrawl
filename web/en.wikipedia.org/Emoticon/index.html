Emoticon

An emoticon (short for "emotion icon") is a pictorial representation of a facial expression created from punctuation marks, numbers, and letters, usually written sideways, to convey a writer's feelings or intended tone in text-based communication. Common examples include :-) for a smile and :-( for sadness. Emoticons were widely used in email, chat rooms, and text messages before being largely superseded by graphical emoji.

Classic sideways emoticons: smile, wink, and frown

History

Typographic faces appeared sporadically in print as early as the nineteenth century. The modern emoticon is generally credited to Scott Fahlman of Carnegie Mellon University, who on 19 September 1982 proposed using :-) and :-( on an electronic bulletin board to distinguish jokes from serious posts. The convention spread rapidly through ARPANET and Usenet culture.

Styles

Western emoticons are read sideways, with the eyes on the left. Variations add a nose (:-) versus :)), winks (;-)), surprise (:-O), and many others. Japanese emoticons, called kaomoji, are read upright and emphasise the eyes, as in (^_^) for happiness or (T_T) for crying; they became popular on Japanese mobile phones and forums and spread globally through internet culture.

Use and interpretation

Emoticons add emotional nuance to plain text, softening statements, marking humour, and reducing ambiguity. Studies suggest readers interpret them quickly and that they can function much like real facial expressions in digital communication. In formal writing they remain uncommon, but in informal messaging they were a standard part of online etiquette from the 1980s through the 2000s.

Relation to emoji

Emoji, standardised in Unicode from 2010 onward, rendered emoticon-like faces as colourful pictographs and largely displaced text emoticons on smartphones. Many platforms automatically convert typed emoticons into emoji images, preserving the older tradition within the new medium.
