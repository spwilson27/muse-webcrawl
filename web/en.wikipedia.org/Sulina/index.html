Sulina

Sulina is a town and free port in Tulcea County, Romania, at the mouth of the Danube River where the Sulina branch flows into the Black Sea. It is one of the oldest settlements in Romania and historically one of its most important ports, serving as the gateway between the Danube and the sea. Accessible only by water, it is the easternmost point of Romania.

History

Known in Byzantine times, Sulina gained great importance in the 19th century after the Treaty of Adrianople (1829) and the Crimean War settlement of 1856, which created the European Commission of the Danube. The Commission, headquartered in Sulina, regulated navigation, dredged the Sulina channel and built lighthouses, making the town a prosperous, cosmopolitan free port with Greek, Romanian, Turkish, Jewish and other communities.

The town declined in the 20th century as river traffic shifted and the Commission was dissolved, and its population fell from over 20,000 in its heyday to a few thousand today.

Geography

Sulina sits within the Danube Delta, a vast wetland and UNESCO World Heritage Site noted for its birdlife. The town has no road connection to the rest of Romania; it is reached by boat from Tulcea. Its tall old lighthouse, built under the European Commission, remains a landmark visible from the sea.

Economy

The local economy rests on port activity, fishing, shipping services and growing nature tourism linked to the delta. Sulina's beaches on the Black Sea also attract summer visitors.

Notable sites

Landmarks include the old lighthouse (now a museum), the Commission palace, the multi-confessional cemetery — where sailors, workers and even a reputed pirate are buried under markers of several faiths — and the Greek church of St Nicholas.
