Aniene

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Aniene (Italian: [aˈnjɛːne]; Latin: Anio) is a river in central Italy, a left tributary of the Tiber. It is about 99 kilometres (62 miles) long, rising in the Simbruini mountains near Filettino in the region of Lazio and flowing westward through the Aniene valley to join the Tiber just north of Rome.

Course

The river passes the towns of Subiaco, where Saint Benedict founded his first monastery, and Tivoli, where it plunges over the famous waterfalls (cascate) beside the Villa Gregoriana. Below Tivoli it crosses the Roman Campagna, fed partly by the sulphurous springs of the Acque Albule near Bagni di Tivoli.

History

In antiquity the Anio supplied two of Rome's aqueducts, the Anio Vetus (begun 272 BC) and the Anio Novus, which drew water from the river above Tivoli. The valley was a favoured site for Roman villas, including Hadrian's Villa. The river's name may derive from Anius, a legendary Etruscan king said to have drowned in it.
