Rijkswaterstaat, founded in 1798 as the Bureau voor den Waterstaat, is the executive agency of the Dutch Ministry of Infrastructure and Water Management. It is responsible for the design, construction, management and maintenance of the main infrastructure facilities in the Netherlands, including the motorway network, the main waterway network and the main water systems.

History

The organisation was founded during the Batavian Republic to centralise water management, a vital concern in a country where roughly a third of the land lies below sea level. Over the nineteenth and twentieth centuries it directed major works including the Zuiderzee Works and, after the North Sea flood of 1953, the Delta Works.

Tasks

Rijkswaterstaat manages approximately 3,000 kilometres of motorway and thousands of kilometres of waterways, locks, bridges and storm surge barriers such as the Maeslantkering. Its mission is summarised as keeping the Netherlands safe, liveable and accessible, working with provinces, municipalities and the regional water boards (waterschappen).
