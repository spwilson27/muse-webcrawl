Infusion pump

An infusion pump is a medical device used to deliver fluids into a patient's body in controlled amounts. They are used in hospitals, nursing homes, and in the home, to administer medications, nutrients, insulin, hormones, antibiotics, chemotherapy drugs, and pain relievers, typically intravenously but also by other routes. Because infusion pumps deliver fluids with much greater precision than manual administration, they are essential to modern clinical care.

Types

  • Volumetric pumps deliver larger volumes at set rates, commonly for IV fluids and nutrition.
  • Syringe pumps drive a syringe plunger at a controlled rate and are used for small, precise doses, for example in neonatal care and anaesthesia.
  • Ambulatory pumps are portable or wearable devices enabling patients to receive infusions while mobile; insulin pumps are a specialised form.
  • Patient-controlled analgesia (PCA) pumps allow patients to self-administer preset doses of pain medication within programmed limits.
  • Elastomeric pumps use the pressure of an inflated elastomeric balloon to drive fluid, requiring no power source.

Smart pumps and safety

Modern "smart pumps" incorporate drug libraries with dose-error reduction software that warns clinicians when a programmed dose exceeds preset limits for a given drug. Despite these safeguards, infusion pumps have been associated with numerous adverse events and recalls, often related to software or user-interface problems, prompting regulators such as the U.S. Food and Drug Administration to launch safety initiatives aimed at improving pump design and reporting.
