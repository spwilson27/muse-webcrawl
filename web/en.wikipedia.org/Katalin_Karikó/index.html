Katalin Karikó

Katalin Karikó (born 17 January 1955) is a Hungarian-American biochemist who specializes in ribonucleic acid (RNA)-mediated mechanisms, particularly in vitro-transcribed messenger RNA (mRNA) for protein replacement therapy. She received the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 2023, together with Drew Weissman, for discoveries concerning nucleoside base modifications that enabled the development of effective mRNA vaccines against COVID-19.

Career

Karikó was born in Szolnok, Hungary, and grew up in Kisújszállás. She earned her PhD at the University of Szeged in 1982. After her postdoctoral position was terminated, she emigrated to the United States in 1985, working at Temple University and later the University of Pennsylvania. At Penn she met immunologist Drew Weissman; in 2005 they showed that replacing uridine with pseudouridine in synthetic mRNA prevented the inflammatory response that had blocked therapeutic use of mRNA and greatly increased protein production.

Their patents were licensed to BioNTech and Moderna, whose COVID-19 vaccines reached billions of people. Karikó joined BioNTech in 2013, becoming a senior vice president. After the prizes of 2021–2023, she became a professor at the University of Szeged and an adjunct professor at the University of Pennsylvania. Her daughter, Susan Francia, is a two-time Olympic gold medalist rower for the United States.

Awards

Besides the 2023 Nobel Prize, Karikó has received the Lasker–DeBakey Clinical Medical Research Award (2021), the Breakthrough Prize in Life Sciences (2022), and numerous other honours.
