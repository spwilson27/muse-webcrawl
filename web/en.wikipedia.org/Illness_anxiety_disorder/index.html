Illness anxiety disorder

Illness anxiety disorder (IAD), sometimes called health anxiety and formerly grouped under hypochondriasis, is a mental disorder characterised by a persistent preoccupation with having or acquiring a serious illness, despite absent or only mild somatic symptoms and repeated negative medical evaluations. The diagnosis was introduced in the DSM-5 (2013), which split the former diagnosis of hypochondriasis between illness anxiety disorder and somatic symptom disorder.

Symptoms and diagnosis

People with the disorder are excessively anxious about their health, are easily alarmed by news of illness, and engage in excessive health-related behaviours — such as repeatedly checking the body for signs of disease or frequently seeking medical reassurance — or, conversely, maladaptively avoid doctors and hospitals. The preoccupation must persist for at least six months and cause significant distress or impairment, and is not better explained by another disorder such as generalised anxiety or obsessive–compulsive disorder. Two subtypes are specified: care-seeking and care-avoidant.

Treatment

Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT), including exposure-based approaches targeting reassurance-seeking and body-checking, has the strongest evidence base. Antidepressants such as selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs) may also help, particularly where anxiety or depression co-occurs. Prevalence estimates suggest the condition affects a few tenths of a percent to around one percent of the general population, with higher rates in medical settings.
