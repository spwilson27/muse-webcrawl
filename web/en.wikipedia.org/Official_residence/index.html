Official residence

An official residence is a residence designated by an authority and assigned to an official (such as a head of state, head of government, governor, or senior minister) as their primary workplace and/or home during their term of office. Official residences often serve ceremonial as well as residential functions and are maintained at public expense.

Examples

Well-known official residences include:

  • The White House — residence and workplace of the President of the United States, in Washington, D.C.
  • 10 Downing Street — official residence of the Prime Minister of the United Kingdom, in London; Buckingham Palace is the official London residence of the British monarch.
  • Élysée Palace — residence of the President of France.
  • Rashtrapati Bhavan — residence of the President of India, in New Delhi.
  • The Kremlin — official residence of the President of Russia, in Moscow.
  • Zhongnanhai — residence and office compound of the leadership of China, in Beijing.
  • 24 Sussex Drive — official residence of the Prime Minister of Canada, in Ottawa.
  • Kirribilli House and The Lodge — residences of the Prime Minister of Australia.

Characteristics

Official residences are typically publicly owned, provided with staff, security, and offices, and used for state ceremonies, receptions, and meetings. Some officeholders choose to remain in their private homes instead, and some residences, such as Chequers in England or Camp David in the United States, serve as official country retreats.
