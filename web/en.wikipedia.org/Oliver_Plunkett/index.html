Oliver Plunkett (1 November 1625 – 1 July 1681) was the Catholic Archbishop of Armagh and Primate of All Ireland, and the last victim of the fictitious Popish Plot. He was executed at Tyburn and canonised by the Catholic Church in 1975.

Life

Educated in Rome, Plunkett was ordained in 1654 and appointed Archbishop of Armagh in 1669. Returning to an Ireland of penal restrictions on Catholics, he rebuilt churches and schools and worked to discipline the clergy, often travelling in disguise during periods of persecution.

Arrest, trial, and death

Arrested in 1679 amid the hysteria of the Popish Plot fabricated by Titus Oates, Plunkett was accused of plotting a French invasion. After a failed trial in Ireland, he was tried in London in 1681 on perjured evidence, convicted of treason, and hanged, drawn and quartered at Tyburn on 1 July 1681 — the last Catholic martyr to die in England.

Veneration

His preserved head is venerated at St Peter's Church, Drogheda. Plunkett was beatified in 1920 and canonised in 1975.
