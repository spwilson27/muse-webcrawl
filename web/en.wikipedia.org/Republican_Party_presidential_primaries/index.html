Republican Party presidential primaries

The Republican Party presidential primaries are the process by which voters of the United States Republican Party choose its nominee for President of the United States in a general election. Delegates to the Republican National Convention are allocated through state primaries and caucuses.

Process

Since the 1970s reforms, most delegates have been selected through primary elections rather than by party leaders. The Iowa caucuses and the New Hampshire primary traditionally open the season, followed by contests in Nevada and South Carolina and then "Super Tuesday". States vary between proportional allocation and winner-take-all rules, though national party rules require proportional allocation for contests held before mid-March.

History

The first Republican primaries were held in 1912, but binding primaries became decisive only after the upheavals of 1968. Notable contests include the Ford–Reagan battle of 1976 and the crowded fields of 2016 and 2024.
