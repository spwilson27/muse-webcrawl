Ludmila of Bohemia

Saint Ludmila (c. 860 – 15 September 921) was a Czech saint and martyr, the wife of Bořivoj I, the first Christian Duke of Bohemia, and the grandmother and educator of Saint Wenceslaus, whom she raised in the Christian faith. She is venerated as the patroness of Bohemia.

Life

Ludmila was the daughter of the Slavic prince Slavibor of the Sorbs or of Pšov. She married Bořivoj I around 874, and the couple converted to Christianity, according to tradition baptised by Saint Methodius himself. Their Christianising efforts met resistance from pagan nobles, and they were briefly driven into exile in Moravia.

Martyrdom

After the death of her son Vratislaus I, the nobles entrusted the young Wenceslaus to Ludmila's Christian upbringing rather than to his mother, Drahomíra, who favoured the pagan party. The conflict culminated on 15 September 921, when assassins sent by Drahomíra strangled Ludmila at Tetín castle — legend says with her own veil. She was about 61 years old.

Cult

Wenceslaus translated his grandmother's remains to St George's Basilica in Prague Castle in 925. She was soon venerated as a saint, and her feast is celebrated on 16 September. Antonín Dvořák composed the oratorio Saint Ludmila (1885–86) in her honour, and she remains a central figure of Czech national and religious identity.
