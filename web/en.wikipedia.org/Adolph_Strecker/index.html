Adolph Strecker

Adolph Strecker (1822-1871) was a German chemist best known for the Strecker synthesis of amino acids, reported in 1850.

Career

Strecker studied at Giessen under Justus von Liebig, then worked in Copenhagen and Christiania (Oslo) before holding chairs at Tubingen and Wurzburg.

Strecker synthesis

The reaction treats an aldehyde with ammonium chloride and potassium cyanide to form an alpha-aminonitrile, whose hydrolysis yields an alpha-amino acid — an early laboratory route to compounds such as alanine.

Other work

He contributed to organometallic chemistry and to the determination of atomic weights. See also Strecker amino acid synthesis and Amino acid.
