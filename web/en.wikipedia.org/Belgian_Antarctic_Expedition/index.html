Belgian Antarctic Expedition

The Belgian Antarctic Expedition of 1897–1899 was the first expedition to winter in the Antarctic region. Led by Adrien de Gerlache de Gomery aboard the RV Belgica, it was a multinational venture whose crew included Roald Amundsen of Norway, Frederick Cook of the United States, Henryk Arctowski of Poland, and Emil Racoviță of Romania. The expedition is regarded as a precursor of the Heroic Age of Antarctic Exploration, and the unplanned wintering of the Belgica in the pack ice of the Bellingshausen Sea provided the first full year of meteorological and magnetic observations from Antarctic waters.

Voyage

The Belgica left Antwerp in August 1897 and reached the Antarctic Peninsula in January 1898, making twenty landings and charting the strait later named Gerlache Strait. Members of the expedition collected extensive zoological, botanical, geological, and oceanographic specimens; Racoviță discovered the flightless midge Belgica antarctica, the largest purely terrestrial animal native to Antarctica. Sailing west into the Bellingshausen Sea, de Gerlache deliberately steered into the pack ice, and by early March 1898 the ship was frozen in.

The winter

For thirteen months the Belgica drifted with the ice, enduring the polar night from May to July 1898. The crew suffered from scurvy, depression, and rat infestations; the ship's cat, Nansen, died, and two men lost their sanity. Cook and Amundsen fed the crew fresh seal and penguin meat, which relieved the scurvy, and organised hunting and skiing excursions. In January 1899 the crew cut a canal through a kilometre of ice with saws and explosives, and the ship finally escaped in March 1899, reaching Punta Arenas in May.

Legacy

The expedition returned with a wealth of scientific data published in dozens of volumes. Amundsen's experience made him the first mate to winter in Antarctica and prepared him for his later polar achievements. The expedition's hardships also taught future explorers the importance of diet, clothing, and morale in surviving the polar night.
