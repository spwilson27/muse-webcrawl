Wayback Machine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Wayback Machine is a digital archive of the World Wide Web founded by the Internet Archive, an American nonprofit organisation based in San Francisco, California. Created in 1996 by Brewster Kahle and Bruce Gilliat, and launched to the public in 2001, it allows users to go "back in time" to see how websites looked in the past, preserving snapshots of pages that may since have changed or disappeared.

How it works

The archive is built largely by web crawlers, principally Alexa Internet in its early years and later the Internet Archive's own crawling infrastructure. Users enter a URL and are shown a calendar of capture dates; selecting a date displays the page as archived at that time. Users can also request that a page be saved immediately through the "Save Page Now" service. The name is a homage to the WABAC Machine, a time machine from the Rocky and Bullwinkle cartoons.

Uses and significance

Journalists, researchers, lawyers and historians use the Wayback Machine to cite earlier versions of pages, recover deleted content, and document how information online has changed. Archived pages have been used as evidence in legal proceedings and to hold public statements to account. The archive respects the robots exclusion standard in various ways over its history and allows site owners to request removal of their content.

Legal issues

The Wayback Machine has figured in disputes over copyright, trademark evidence and the admissibility of archived web pages as evidence. Courts in several jurisdictions have admitted Wayback captures, sometimes subject to authentication by affidavit from the Internet Archive. The service has also been blocked or restricted in some countries.
