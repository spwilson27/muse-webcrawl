Cancel culture

Cancel culture is a cultural phenomenon in which an individual thought to have acted or spoken in an unacceptable manner is ostracized, boycotted, shunned or otherwise sanctioned, often through social media pressure. The phrase emerged in Black American slang and popular culture in the 2010s and became a prominent subject of political debate in the United States and other countries.

Description

"Cancelling" typically involves withdrawing support from public figures, companies, or works after they are perceived to have done or said something objectionable. It is often compared to earlier practices such as boycotts and public shaming, though amplified by the speed and reach of social media. Supporters frame call-outs as a form of accountability — a way for people without institutional power to challenge powerful figures — while critics argue the practice amounts to mob justice, disproportionate punishment, or a threat to free expression.

Public debate

The term entered mainstream political discourse in the late 2010s and early 2020s. In July 2020, the open letter published in Harper's Magazine and signed by 153 public figures warned against what it described as an intolerant climate, prompting counter-letters defending the practice as legitimate criticism. Polling in the United States has found sharply divided and partisan views of the concept, with many respondents unfamiliar with the term or defining it in conflicting ways.
