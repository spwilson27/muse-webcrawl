Electronic news-gathering

Electronic news-gathering (ENG) is a broadcasting industry term for the use of portable electronic cameras and related technology by television news crews to record stories in the field, as distinct from studio production. The term emerged in the 1970s as lightweight video equipment displaced 16 mm film for news coverage.

Technology

ENG crews typically combine a camcorder with wireless microphones, portable lighting and a vehicle equipped for transmission — historically microwave links and satellite news gathering (SNG) trucks, and increasingly bonded cellular connections that allow live pictures to be sent over mobile data networks. Editing may be done in the field on laptops or back at the station.

Practice

ENG enabled same-day and live reporting from breaking news scenes and transformed television journalism from the late 1970s. Related styles include electronic field production (EFP), used for higher-quality feature and documentary material. The rise of mobile journalism ('mojo'), in which a single reporter shoots on a smartphone, is a further development of the ENG concept.
