Anime

Anime (Japanese: アニメ) is hand-drawn and computer-generated animation originating from Japan. Outside Japan, the term refers specifically to Japanese animation, characterised by a distinctive art style with colourful characters, fantastical themes, and often vibrant graphics. Anime spans television series, films, shorts, and web releases, and encompasses genres for every demographic.

History

Japanese animation dates to the early 20th century; the earliest surviving work is Katsudō Shashin (c. 1907). After the Second World War, Osamu Tezuka revolutionised the medium with limited-animation techniques adapted for television, launching Astro Boy in 1963. The 1970s and 1980s saw the rise of mecha series such as Mobile Suit Gundam (1979) and international successes including Akira (1988). Hayao Miyazaki and Studio Ghibli, founded in 1985, brought critical acclaim, with Spirited Away (2001) winning the Academy Award for Best Animated Feature.

Production

Anime is produced by studios using storyboards, key animation, and in-betweening, increasingly combined with CGI. Most television anime is adapted from manga, light novels, or video games, financed by production committees of publishers, broadcasters, and merchandisers. Voice actors (seiyū) are prominent public figures in Japan.

Industry and global reach

The anime industry comprises hundreds of studios and a market worth over ¥2 trillion annually when merchandising is included. Since the 1990s anime has become a global cultural export, distributed worldwide via streaming platforms; conventions, cosplay, and fan communities thrive internationally.

Genres and demographics

Works are marketed by demographic — shōnen (boys), shōjo (girls), seinen and josei (adults) — and by genre, from mecha, isekai, and slice of life to horror and sports.
