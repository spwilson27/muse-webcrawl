A police box is a public telephone kiosk or callbox for the use of members of the police, or for members of the public to contact the police. It was a feature of British streets in the 20th century.

History

Police call boxes appeared in the United States in the late 19th century. In Britain, the Metropolitan Police introduced wooden and later concrete boxes from the 1920s; the familiar blue design by surveyor Gilbert Mackenzie Trench dates from 1929. A light on top could be flashed to summon the constable on the beat. Inside were a telephone linked directly to the station, an incident book, a first aid kit, and a small rest space. The public could use an outside telephone to contact the police.

Decline and legacy

Personal radios and the 999 system made the boxes obsolete, and most were removed from the 1970s onward. The design achieved worldwide fame as the outward form of the TARDIS, the time machine in the BBC television series Doctor Who (from 1963). A handful of original and replica boxes survive, some repurposed as coffee kiosks.
