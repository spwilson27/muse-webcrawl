Gyros


Gyros served in pita

Gyros (Greek: γύρος, romanized: gýros/yíros, lit. "turn") are a Greek dish consisting of meat cooked on a vertical rotisserie, usually pork or chicken, and seasoned with Mediterranean herbs and spices. The meat is typically served in a pita, usually with tomato, onion, lettuce and tzatziki sauce. It is a popular fast food in Greece, Cyprus, and the Greek diaspora worldwide.

History

Grilling a vertical spit of stacked meat slices and cutting off pieces as it cooks was developed in Bursa in the 19th-century Ottoman Empire and became the Turkish döner kebab. Following the Greco-Turkish war and the population exchanges of the 1920s, refugees brought the dish to Greece, where it evolved into gyros, typically made with pork and served with tzatziki. Greek immigrants introduced gyros to the United States in the 1960s and 1970s, where it became popular in Chicago and New York; notably, some American versions use a processed meat cone of beef and lamb.

Preparation

In Greece, gyros meat is usually pork or chicken, marinated and stacked in thin slices onto a tall vertical spit, forming an inverted cone that rotates slowly in front of a heat source. As the outer layer crisps, it is shaved off with a long knife or an electric slicer. The meat is served in an oiled, lightly grilled pita, rolled with tomato, onion, tzatziki and sometimes fried potatoes. In Greece, pork gyros is the default; chicken is a common alternative, and in some regions kebab-style minced meat is used.

Name

The name comes from the Greek γύρος ("circle" or "turn"), a calque of the Turkish döner, from dönmek, "to turn". In Greece it is pronounced "yee-ros"; English speakers commonly say "gy-ro" or "jeer-o".
