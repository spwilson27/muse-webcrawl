Overview

Chelsea is a neighborhood on the West Side of the borough of Manhattan in New York City, roughly bounded by 14th Street to the south, the Hudson River to the west, and Sixth Avenue and 34th Street to the east and north. The district is home to the Chelsea Historic District, designated in 1970, and is known for its art galleries, the High Line elevated park and a large LGBTQ population.

History

The name derives from the estate "Chelsea" of Captain Thomas Clarke, an 18th-century landowner; his grandson Clement Clarke Moore — author of "'Twas the Night Before Christmas" and a biblical scholar — donated land for the General Theological Seminary, still located on 20th Street. In the 19th century the area developed around London Terrace and the Hudson River piers; the famed Hotel Chelsea (1884) later housed generations of writers, musicians and artists. From the 1990s, hundreds of art galleries relocated to Chelsea's former warehouse blocks, making it a centre of the contemporary art market.

Landmarks

Attractions include the High Line, Chelsea Market (in the former Nabisco factory where the Oreo cookie was invented), Chelsea Piers sports complex, the Rubin Museum of Art and the Joyce Theater, a noted dance venue. The Hudson Yards development rises at the neighborhood's northern edge.
