AN/TPQ-36 Firefinder radar

AN/TPQ-36 Firefinder
Type: Counter-battery radar
Place of origin: United States
Manufacturer: Hughes Aircraft / Raytheon
In service: 1980s–present

The AN/TPQ-36 Firefinder radar is a mobile, phased-array counter-battery radar system developed for the United States Army. It is designed to detect and track incoming artillery, mortar, and rocket fire and to compute the point of origin of hostile fire so that friendly artillery can return counter-battery fire quickly and accurately.

Description

The TPQ-36 uses an electronically steered phased-array antenna mounted on a trailer towed by a Humvee. Its computer-controlled beam scans a sector of the horizon; when it detects a projectile in flight, it tracks it briefly and extrapolates the trajectory backward to locate the firing weapon. The system can locate artillery at ranges of roughly 15 km and rockets at longer ranges, and it can handle multiple targets in rapid succession. The crew is small, and the system is air-transportable.

Development and service

The Firefinder family grew out of lessons from the Vietnam War, where earlier mortar-locating radars proved too slow and short-ranged. Hughes Aircraft developed the TPQ-36 alongside the longer-range AN/TPQ-37; production later passed to Raytheon. The system entered service in the early 1980s and has been widely exported to allied armies. It has seen extensive use in Iraq and Afghanistan for protecting forward operating bases, and systems were supplied to Ukraine following Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022, where they have been used to locate Russian artillery.

Successors

The TPQ-36 is being supplemented and replaced in U.S. service by the AN/TPQ-53 Quick Reaction Capability Radar, a truck-mounted active electronically scanned array system with greater range and 360-degree coverage.
