Laurel wreath

A laurel wreath is a round wreath made of connected branches and leaves of the bay laurel (Laurus nobilis), an aromatic broadleaf evergreen. It is a symbol of triumph, victory and honour originating in Greek and Roman antiquity and still widely used in heraldry, awards, academic dress and architecture.

Origins

In Greek mythology the laurel was sacred to Apollo; the nymph Daphne was transformed into a laurel tree to escape him, and Apollo thereafter wore its leaves. At the Pythian Games victors were crowned with laurel, and in Rome generals celebrating a triumph and emperors wore the corona laurea. The word "laureate" — as in poet laureate and Nobel laureate — derives from this practice.

Modern use

Laurel wreaths appear on medals, diplomas, university crests, military insignia and the emblem of the United Nations (in olive, a related peace symbol), and as the logo motif of film festivals marking official selection.
