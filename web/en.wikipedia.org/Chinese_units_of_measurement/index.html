Chinese units of measurement

Chinese units of measurement
Chinese: 市制 (market system)
Examples: li, jin, mu
Status: Metric (SI) official; traditional units still used

Chinese units of measurement, known in Chinese as the shìzhì ('market system'), are the traditional units used in China. Although China officially adopted the metric system, traditional units were redefined in 1959 to align conveniently with SI values and remain common in daily life.

Length and area

The li equals 500 metres; the chi is one third of a metre and the cun one third of a decimetre. Land is often measured in mu, equal to about 666.7 square metres (1/15 hectare).

Mass

The jin (catty) equals 500 grams, divided into 10 liang of 50 grams. Jin and liang are still widely used in markets. In Taiwan and Hong Kong, variant traditional values persist.
