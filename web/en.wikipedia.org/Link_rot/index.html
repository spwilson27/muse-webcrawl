Link rot

Link rot (also called link decay, dead links, or link death) is the phenomenon of hyperlinks tending over time to cease to point to the originally targeted file, web page, or server because that resource has been relocated or become permanently unavailable. A link that no longer points to its target is called a broken or dead link; the rate of link decay is a subject of study in bibliometrics and in the preservation of scholarly and legal records.

Causes and studies

Causes include website restructuring, content removal, domain expiry, server shutdown, and changes in access policies such as paywalls. Studies consistently find rapid decay: a 2024 Pew Research Center study found that 38% of web pages that existed in 2013 were no longer accessible, and analyses of legal scholarship have found that large proportions of URLs cited in court opinions and law reviews no longer lead to the cited material.

Mitigation

Mitigations include web archiving services such as the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine, permanent identifier systems (DOIs, permalinks, PURLs), link-checking software, and citation practices that couple a URL with archival copies. On Wikipedia, bots and editors repair dead external links by substituting archived versions.
