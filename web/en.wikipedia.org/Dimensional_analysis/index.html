Dimensional analysis

Dimensional analysis is the analysis of the relationships between physical quantities by identifying their base dimensions (such as length, mass, time, and electric current) and units of measure. It is widely used in physics and engineering to check the plausibility of equations, convert units, and derive scaling laws.

Principles

A fundamental requirement is dimensional homogeneity: quantities may be added or equated only if they share the same dimensions, so every physically meaningful equation must have identical dimensions on both sides. Quantities such as angles, ratios, and counts are dimensionless.

Buckingham π theorem

The Buckingham π theorem states that a physical law involving n variables with k independent fundamental dimensions can be rewritten in terms of n − k independent dimensionless parameters (π groups). This underlies similarity theory and the use of scale models in wind tunnels and towing tanks, where matching dimensionless numbers such as the Reynolds number ensures dynamically similar behaviour.

Applications

Dimensional analysis is used to guess the form of physical relationships (for example, deriving that a pendulum's period depends on √(L/g)), to convert between unit systems via the factor-label method, and to detect errors in calculations.
