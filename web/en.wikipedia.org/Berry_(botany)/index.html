Berry (botany)

In botany, a berry is a fleshy fruit produced from the ovary of a single flower, in which the entire outer layer of the ovary wall (pericarp) ripens into an edible fleshy mass, and the seeds are embedded within it. Grapes, tomatoes, bananas, blueberries, and kiwifruit are true berries in this strict sense. The everyday culinary meaning of "berry" differs: strawberries, raspberries, and blackberries are not botanical berries, while several fruits not commonly called berries — including bananas and avocados — are.

Definition

A true berry develops from a single flower with a superior ovary, and typically contains several small seeds. The pericarp is usually divided into a thin outer skin (exocarp), a fleshy middle (mesocarp), and an inner layer (endocarp) surrounding the seeds. Variants include the pepo of the cucumber family (melons, pumpkins, cucumbers), which has a hard rind, and the hesperidium of citrus fruits, which has a leathery rind and segmented juice-filled flesh.

Everyday usage

In common language, a berry is any small, pulpy, edible fruit. Under this definition the strawberry (an aggregate-accessory fruit), raspberry and blackberry (aggregate drupes), and mulberry (a multiple fruit) all qualify, whereas the botanical definition excludes them. Conversely, bananas, eggplants, and chilli peppers are botanical berries rarely described as such.
