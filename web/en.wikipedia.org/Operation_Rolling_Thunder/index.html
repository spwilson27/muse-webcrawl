Operation Rolling Thunder

Operation Rolling Thunder was a gradual and sustained aerial bombardment conducted by the United States against North Vietnam from 2 March 1965 until 2 November 1968, during the Vietnam War. Authorised by President Lyndon B. Johnson, it was the first sustained American bombing of North Vietnam.

Operation Rolling Thunder
Date2 March 1965 – 2 November 1968
LocationNorth Vietnam
Conducted byUS Air Force, Navy; South Vietnamese support

Objectives

The campaign aimed to interdict supplies to the Viet Cong, boost South Vietnamese morale, and pressure Hanoi to negotiate, using graduated escalation. Target restrictions, intended to avoid war with China and the USSR, limited effectiveness.

Course and outcome

Over 300,000 sorties dropped some 864,000 tons of bombs; hundreds of US aircraft were lost to increasingly sophisticated Soviet- and Chinese-supplied air defences. The operation failed to break North Vietnam's will or stop infiltration, and ended as Johnson halted bombing ahead of peace talks in Paris.
