False flag

A false flag operation is an act committed with the intent of disguising the actual source of responsibility and pinning blame on another party. The term originates in naval warfare, where a ship would fly the flag of another nation — a practice permitted as a ruse of war provided the true colors were shown before firing.

History

Documented false flag operations include the 1931 Mukden Incident, used by Japan to justify invading Manchuria; the 1939 Gleiwitz incident, in which Nazi Germany staged a Polish attack on a German radio station as a pretext for invading Poland; and Operation Northwoods, a 1962 U.S. proposal (rejected by President Kennedy) to stage attacks blamed on Cuba. Sweden's 1788 fabrication of a Russian attack at Puumala, used to rally support for war, is another classic example.

Modern usage

The term has broadened to cover covert operations, provocations and disinformation designed to justify retaliation or intervention. Accusations of false flag attacks are also a common feature of conspiracy theories about mass-casualty events, where the label is applied to genuine attacks attributed to governments without credible evidence.
