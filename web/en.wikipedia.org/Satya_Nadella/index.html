Satya Nadella

Satya Narayana Nadella (born 19 August 1967) is an Indian-American business executive. He is the chairman and chief executive officer (CEO) of Microsoft, succeeding Steve Ballmer in 2014 as CEO and John W. Thompson in 2021 as chairman. Before becoming CEO, he was the executive vice president of Microsoft's cloud and enterprise group, responsible for building and running the company's computing platforms.

Early life and education

Nadella was born in Hyderabad, India, into a Telugu-speaking Hindu family. His father was an Indian Administrative Service officer and his mother a Sanskrit lecturer. He attended the Hyderabad Public School, Begumpet, and earned a bachelor's degree in electrical engineering from the Manipal Institute of Technology in Karnataka in 1988. He then moved to the United States, earning a master's degree in computer science from the University of Wisconsin–Milwaukee in 1990 and an MBA from the University of Chicago Booth School of Business in 1997.

Career

Nadella worked at Sun Microsystems before joining Microsoft in 1992. At Microsoft he led projects including the move of the company's database and developer tools to cloud computing, and he ran the Server and Tools business before taking charge of the Cloud and Enterprise group. Under his leadership, Microsoft's cloud services grew into one of the world's largest cloud infrastructures.

On 4 February 2014, Nadella was announced as Microsoft's third CEO, after Bill Gates and Ballmer. His tenure has emphasized a "cloud-first, mobile-first" strategy, cross-platform services, and a cultural shift toward what he calls a "growth mindset," a concept he has credited to psychologist Carol Dweck. Major acquisitions during his tenure include LinkedIn (2016), GitHub (2018), and Activision Blizzard (2023). Microsoft's market capitalization rose from roughly $300 billion at his appointment to more than $3 trillion a decade later, driven by the growth of Azure and the company's partnership with OpenAI.

Personal life

Nadella is an avid reader of American and Indian poetry and a passionate fan of cricket, which he played competitively in school. He and his wife Anupama have supported Seattle Children's Hospital and disability advocacy; their son Zain, who had cerebral palsy, died in 2022. Nadella's memoir, Hit Refresh, was published in 2017. In 2019, Financial Times named him Person of the Year, and in 2025 he received the Padma Bhushan, India's third-highest civilian award.
