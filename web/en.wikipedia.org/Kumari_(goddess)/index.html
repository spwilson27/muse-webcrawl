Kumari (goddess)

A Kumari is a prepubescent girl worshipped as a living goddess in Nepal and in parts of India. The tradition is most famous in the Kathmandu Valley, where the Royal Kumari of Kathmandu resides at the Kumari Ghar palace at Durbar Square.

Tradition

A Kumari is chosen from the Shakya or Bajracharya caste of Newar Buddhists through a rigorous selection testing traditional marks of divinity and composure. She is believed to be an incarnation of the Hindu goddess Taleju (a form of Durga) until menstruation or serious illness, when a successor is chosen. Retired Kumaris receive a state pension.

Role

The Kumari blesses devotees, including once the King of Nepal, and appears at festivals such as Indra Jatra, carried in her chariot. Similar living-goddess traditions exist in several towns of the valley and in India, including Kumari worship during Navaratri and Durga Puja in Bengal and Assam. The practice has been discussed in relation to children's rights, and reforms allow Kumaris private tutoring and limited outings.
