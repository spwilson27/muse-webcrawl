Ice climbing

Ice climbing is a climbing discipline that involves ascending routes consisting of frozen water — frozen waterfalls, icefalls, cliffs and rock slabs covered with ice refrozen from flows of water. For protection, the climber uses ice screws placed in the ice; for progression, crampons on the boots and two ice tools swung in the hands.

Technique and grading

Ice routes are graded on the WI (water ice) scale, from WI1 (low-angle) to about WI7 (thin, technical and sustained), with mixed (M) grades for routes combining rock and ice, and dry-tooling on bare rock. Climbers kick crampon front points into the ice and swing curved ice axes overhead, moving up in a tripod rhythm.

History and venues

Developed from alpine mountaineering, modern ice climbing became a distinct sport in the 1970s with specialised equipment. Famous venues include Ouray in Colorado (site of an artificial ice park), Banff in the Canadian Rockies, Rjukan in Norway and venues in the Alps. Ice climbing featured as a demonstration cultural event at the 2014 Sochi Winter Olympics, and competitive ice climbing is governed by the UIAA.
