Dubbing

Dubbing, also called re-recording and mixing, is a post-production process used in filmmaking and video production, often in concert with sound design, in which additional or supplementary recordings (doubles) are lip-synced and "mixed" with the original production audio to create the finished soundtrack.

The term is also used for the replacement of the dialogue of a film or television programme with a translation performed by voice actors, so that the work can be distributed in another language. In this sense, dubbing is one of the two main methods — alongside subtitling — of translating audiovisual content.

Process

In production sound work, automatic dialogue replacement (ADR), also called looping, re-records actors' lines in a studio to fix clarity or change a performance. In foreign-language dubbing, translators adapt the script to match lip movements and timing; voice actors then record the new dialogue under a dubbing director, following techniques such as the French bande rythmo scrolling text or digital cueing systems.

Distribution by country

Large markets such as Germany, France, Italy, Spain, Brazil, Mexico, India and Japan maintain strong dubbing industries, and audiences there generally expect full dubbing of foreign films and series. Smaller-language markets, such as the Netherlands, Scandinavia and Portugal, traditionally favour subtitles for adult content while dubbing children's media. In Poland, a single narrator (lektor) reading over the original audio remains common for television.
