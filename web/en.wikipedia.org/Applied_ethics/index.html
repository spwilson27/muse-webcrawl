Applied ethics
FieldEthics, philosophy
Major topicsBioethics, business ethics, environmental ethics, professional ethics

Applied ethics is the branch of ethics concerned with the practical application of moral principles and theories to real-life situations and particular fields of human activity. It examines controversial issues such as abortion, animal rights, capital punishment, euthanasia, and the distribution of resources, and encompasses subfields including bioethics, business ethics, environmental ethics, and the ethics of technology.

Method

Applied ethicists typically work by clarifying the facts of a case, identifying the moral values and principles at stake, and reasoning about how general ethical theories—such as consequentialism, deontology, and virtue ethics—bear on the issue. Casuistry, the method of reasoning from paradigm cases, is also widely used, particularly in medical ethics.

Subfields

Bioethics deals with questions arising in medicine and the life sciences. Business ethics examines corporate conduct and the responsibilities of firms and professionals. Environmental ethics considers moral obligations toward the natural world, future generations, and non-human animals. Professional ethics codifies standards for occupations such as law, engineering, and journalism. More recent areas include the ethics of artificial intelligence, data ethics, and neuroethics.
