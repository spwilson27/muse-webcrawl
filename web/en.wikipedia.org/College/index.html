College

A college is an educational institution or a constituent part of one. The term has different meanings around the world: in the United States it usually denotes an institution granting bachelor's degrees, or the undergraduate division of a university; in the United Kingdom and Commonwealth it may refer to a secondary school, a further-education institution, or a constituent college of a collegiate university such as Oxford or Cambridge.

History

The word derives from the Latin collegium, an association of people living under common rules. Medieval universities organized students and masters into colleges — self-governing residential communities — a model established at Paris, Oxford, and Cambridge and later exported worldwide.

Types

Colleges include liberal arts colleges, community colleges offering two-year degrees, technical and further-education colleges, and professional or graduate colleges within universities. In some systems, colleges prepare students for university entrance examinations.

Collegiate universities

At collegiate universities, colleges provide accommodation, pastoral care, and social life, while the central university handles teaching and degrees. Famous examples include the colleges of Oxford and Cambridge and the residential colleges of Yale and Harvard.
