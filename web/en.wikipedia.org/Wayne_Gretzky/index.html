Wayne Douglas Gretzky (born 26 January 1961) is a Canadian former professional ice hockey player and former head coach. He played 20 seasons in the National Hockey League (NHL) for four teams from 1979 to 1999, and is widely regarded as the greatest ice hockey player of all time. Nicknamed "The Great One," he is the leading goal scorer, assist producer and point scorer in NHL history, and holds or shares dozens of league records.

Career

A prodigy who was playing against older boys from childhood in Brantford, Ontario, Gretzky turned professional at 17 with the Indianapolis Racers of the World Hockey Association before being sold to the Edmonton Oilers. In the NHL he led the Oilers to four Stanley Cup championships (1984, 1985, 1987, 1988) and won the Hart Trophy as the league's most valuable player nine times. In 1985–86 he set the single-season records of 92 goals (set in 1981–82) and 215 points.

His 1988 trade to the Los Angeles Kings shocked Canada — a member of Parliament even proposed government intervention — but is credited with popularising hockey in the U.S. Sun Belt. Gretzky finished his career with the St. Louis Blues and New York Rangers, retiring in 1999 with 894 goals and 2,857 points; no other player has reached 2,000 career points. His number 99 was retired league-wide, a unique honour in the NHL.

International play and legacy

Gretzky represented Canada in numerous tournaments, winning three Canada Cups and a bronze medal at the 1998 Winter Olympics in Nagano, and later served as executive director of the gold-medal-winning Canadian team at the 2002 Salt Lake City Olympics. He was inducted into the Hockey Hall of Fame in 1999, the waiting period being waived. After retirement he coached the Phoenix Coyotes (2005–2009) and has worked as a broadcaster and ambassador for the sport.
