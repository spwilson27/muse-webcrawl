Berlin State Opera

The Berlin State Opera (German: Staatsoper Berlin), also known as the Staatsoper Unter den Linden after the boulevard on which it stands, is one of the world's leading opera houses and the oldest of Berlin's three opera companies. Founded in 1742 as the court opera of Frederick the Great, it occupies a neoclassical building designed by Georg Wenzeslaus von Knobelsdorff — the first free-standing opera house in Germany. Its orchestra is the Staatskapelle Berlin, one of the oldest orchestras in the world, and its general music director from 1992 to 2023 was Daniel Barenboim.

History

The house opened in 1742 with Graun's Cleopatra e Cesare. Gaspare Spontini and later Giacomo Meyerbeer directed it in the 19th century; Wagner's The Flying Dutchman and Tannhäuser received early Berlin performances here. Under the Empire and Weimar Republic, conductors such as Richard Strauss, Bruno Walter, Otto Klemperer, and Erich Kleiber made it a centre of modern opera — the Krolloper, its experimental annex, premiered works by Hindemith and others before 1931. Burned out in the Second World War (1941, rebuilt; destroyed again 1945), it was rebuilt in East Berlin in historicist style and reopened in 1955 with Die Meistersinger. After reunification the Staatsoper retained Barenboim, who founded the annual Festtage festival. Following extensive renovation (2010–2017) — raising the ceiling for improved acoustics — the house reopened in October 2017; Christian Thielemann succeeded Barenboim as general music director in 2024.
