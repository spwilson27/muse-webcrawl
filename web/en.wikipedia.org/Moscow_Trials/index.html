Moscow trials

Date1936–1938
LocationMoscow, Soviet Union
TypeShow trials
ProsecutorAndrey Vyshinsky

The Moscow trials were a series of show trials held by the Soviet Union between 1936 and 1938 at the instigation of Joseph Stalin. They were nominally directed against "Trotskyists" and members of the "Right Opposition" of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union. The three public trials, conducted before the Military Collegium of the Supreme Court, were the most prominent part of the Great Purge.

The three trials

The first trial (August 1936), of the "Trotskyite-Zinovievite Terrorist Centre", featured Grigory Zinoviev and Lev Kamenev among sixteen defendants; all were executed. The second trial (January 1937), of the "Anti-Soviet Trotskyist Centre", included Karl Radek and Georgy Pyatakov; thirteen were executed and the rest sent to labour camps. The third trial (March 1938), of the "Bloc of Rights and Trotskyites", included Nikolai Bukharin, Alexei Rykov, and former NKVD chief Genrikh Yagoda; all the principal defendants were executed.

Conduct and verdict of history

The defendants confessed to fantastic crimes — sabotage, espionage for Germany and Japan, and plots to assassinate Stalin and restore capitalism — confessions obtained through torture, threats against families, and false promises. The American Dewey Commission concluded in 1937 that the trials were frame-ups. The Soviet Union itself later acknowledged the trials were fabricated; most victims were posthumously rehabilitated during Khrushchev's thaw and under Gorbachev.
