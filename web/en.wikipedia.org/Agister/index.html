Agister

An agister is an official of the New Forest in Hampshire, England, responsible for the welfare and management of the livestock – chiefly ponies, cattle, donkeys, pigs and sheep – turned out to graze on the open forest by local commoners. The word derives from agistment, the practice of taking in livestock to pasture for payment.

Role

Agisters are employed by the Verderers of the New Forest, the body that administers the forest's commoning system. Each agister covers a defined district of the forest and carries out day-to-day duties including:

  • Checking the health and condition of the free-roaming animals
  • Attending road traffic accidents involving livestock and humanely dispatching injured animals
  • Organising the annual pony drifts (round-ups) for health checks, worming, branding and sales
  • Collecting marking fees for animals depastured on the forest
  • Ensuring commoners comply with the Verderers' byelaws

The agister must be a practising commoner and is expected to know the forest intimately. The head agister coordinates the team and liaises with the Forestry Commission (now Forestry England), the police and the public.

History

The office dates back centuries in the administration of royal forests, where agisters accounted for agistment payments to the Crown. In the New Forest the modern system was formalised under the New Forest Acts, notably the Act of 1877 which reconstituted the Court of Verderers.
