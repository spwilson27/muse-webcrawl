Music festival

A music festival is a community event with performances of singing and instrument playing, often presented with a theme such as a musical genre, nationality, locality of musicians, or holiday. Festivals are usually held outdoors over one or more days, with tents or stages for performers and facilities for audiences who may camp on site.

History

Music festivals date back to the Pythian Games of ancient Greece. In the modern era, classical festivals such as the Salzburg Festival and Bayreuth Festival were joined by jazz, folk and rock festivals; the 1969 Woodstock festival became a defining moment of popular music culture.

Types

Major contemporary festivals include Glastonbury (England), Coachella (California), Tomorrowland (Belgium, electronic dance music) and Fuji Rock (Japan). Festivals contribute significantly to local tourism and economies, while raising issues of safety, noise, waste and environmental impact.
