Hildebrand solubility parameter

The Hildebrand solubility parameter (δ) is a numerical estimate of the degree of interaction between materials, providing a useful guideline for predicting solubility, particularly for nonpolar and slightly polar systems without hydrogen bonding. It is named after Joel Henry Hildebrand, who developed the concept in the early twentieth century.

Definition

The parameter is defined as the square root of the cohesive energy density: δ = √(ΔE/V), where ΔE is the energy of vaporisation and V the molar volume. Materials with similar δ values tend to be mutually soluble, embodying the rule of thumb that "like dissolves like". Values are commonly expressed in (cal/cm³)1/2 or in SI units of MPa1/2.

Uses and limitations

Hildebrand parameters are used in polymer science, coatings and solvent selection, and in conservation science for choosing cleaning solvents. Because the single parameter does not account for polar and hydrogen-bonding interactions separately, it was extended by the Hansen solubility parameters, which divide cohesive energy into dispersion, polar and hydrogen-bonding components.
