The War of the Sixth Coalition (1813–1814) was the conflict in which a coalition of Austria, Prussia, Russia, the United Kingdom, Sweden, Spain and several German states defeated Napoleon's French Empire, forcing his abdication and exile to Elba. It followed the catastrophic French invasion of Russia in 1812, which destroyed the Grande Armée.

The German campaign of 1813

Prussia declared war in March 1813. Napoleon won early victories at Lützen and Bautzen but, short of cavalry, could not destroy the allied armies. After an armistice, Austria joined the coalition. At the Battle of Leipzig (16–19 October 1813) — the "Battle of the Nations", the largest battle in Europe before the First World War — Napoleon was decisively beaten and forced back across the Rhine.

Invasion of France

In 1814 the allies invaded France. Despite a brilliant series of defensive victories in the Six Days' Campaign, Napoleon could not stem the advance; Paris fell on 31 March 1814. Napoleon abdicated in April and was exiled to Elba under the Treaty of Fontainebleau, and the Bourbon monarchy was restored under Louis XVIII.

Aftermath

The Treaty of Paris (1814) set France's borders back to those of 1792. The settlement was reshaped after Napoleon's return in the Hundred Days and his final defeat in the War of the Seventh Coalition, concluded at the Congress of Vienna.
