Breechloader

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A breech-loading rifle action

A breechloader is a firearm or artillery piece in which the user loads the ammunition from the breech (the rear end of the barrel) rather than from the muzzle. Loading from the breech allows a much faster rate of fire, lets the loader keep under cover or lie prone, and makes it possible to use fixed ammunition and rifled barrels effectively. Nearly all modern firearms and artillery are breechloaders.

History

Experimental breech-loading weapons date to the medieval period — early cannon used removable chambers — but sealing the breech against escaping propellant gases was a persistent technical problem. Practical designs appeared in the 19th century: the Dreyse needle gun adopted by Prussia in the 1840s, the Sharps rifle, and the Snider-Enfield and Martini-Henry conversions and designs in Britain. For artillery, the interrupted screw breech and the de Bange obturator of the 1870s solved the sealing problem, after which muzzle-loading guns were rapidly replaced. The French Canon de 75 modèle 1897, with its sliding breech and hydro-pneumatic recoil system, set the pattern for modern quick-firing artillery.

Mechanisms

Common breech mechanisms include bolt actions, falling and rolling blocks, break actions (as on shotguns and double rifles), lever and pump actions, and, in artillery, interrupted screw and sliding wedge breeches. Self-loading designs use the energy of the shot — recoil or propellant gas — to open the breech, eject the spent case and chamber a new round automatically.
