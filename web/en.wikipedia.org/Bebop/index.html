Bebop

Stylistic originsSwing, Kansas City jazz
EraEarly–mid 1940s onward
Key figuresCharlie Parker, Dizzy Gillespie, Thelonious Monk, Bud Powell

Bebop (or bop) is a style of jazz that emerged in the United States in the early 1940s. Characterised by fast tempos, complex chord progressions, intricate melodies, and virtuosic improvisation, it shifted jazz from dance music toward music for listening.

Development

Young musicians including saxophonist Charlie Parker, trumpeter Dizzy Gillespie, and pianist Thelonious Monk developed the style in after-hours sessions at Harlem clubs such as Minton's Playhouse. Bebop combos were small — typically a quintet — in contrast to the big bands of the swing era.

Influence

Bebop became the foundation of modern jazz, leading to cool jazz, hard bop, and later styles, and recordings such as "Ko-Ko" and "Salt Peanuts" remain canonical.
