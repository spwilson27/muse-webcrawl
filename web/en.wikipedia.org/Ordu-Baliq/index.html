Ordu-Baliq (meaning "city of the court", "city of the host") — also known as Mubaliq and Qara Baliq — was the capital of the Uyghur Khaganate. It stood on the left bank of the Orkhon River in present-day Mongolia, some 27 km north of the later Mongol capital Karakorum. The site forms part of the Orkhon Valley Cultural Landscape, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

The Uyghur Khaganate succeeded the Second Turkic Khaganate in 744. Its khagans made Ordu-Baliq their imperial capital, and the city became a significant centre on the Silk Road trade networks of the steppe, housing Sogdian merchants, Chinese craftsmen, and Manichaean clergy — Manichaeism being adopted as the khaganate's official religion under Bögü Khagan in 763. Diplomatic missions and trade connected the city with Tang China.

Destruction and rediscovery

In 840, after years of famine and internal strife, the Yenisei Kyrgyz invaded and destroyed Ordu-Baliq, killing the last khagan; the Uyghurs fled south and west, ending their steppe empire. The ruins were identified by Russian explorers in the late 19th century, and the site has been investigated by Mongolian and international archaeological expeditions, which have documented its palace precinct, walls and a famous dragon-headed stele bearing inscriptions.
