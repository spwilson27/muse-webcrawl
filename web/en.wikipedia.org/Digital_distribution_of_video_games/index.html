Digital distribution of video games

Digital distribution of video games is the process of delivering video game content as digital information, without the exchange or purchase of new physical media such as ROM cartridges, magnetic storage, optical discs and flash memory cards. This process has existed since the early 1980s, but it was only with network advancements in bandwidth in the 2000s that digital distribution became more prominent as a method of selling games. Currently, the process is dominated by online distribution over broadband Internet.

To facilitate the sale of games, various video game publishers and console manufacturers have created their own platforms for digital distribution. These platforms, such as Steam, Origin, PlayStation Store, Nintendo eShop and the Microsoft Store, provide centralised services to purchase and download digital content for either specific video game consoles or personal computers. Some platforms may also serve as digital rights management (DRM) systems, limiting the use of purchased games to one account.

History

Early examples of digital distribution included GameLine for the Atari 2600 in the early 1980s, which allowed subscribers to download games over telephone lines, and Sega's Meganet and Satellaview services for its consoles. The widespread adoption of broadband Internet in the early 2000s made full-size downloads practical. Valve's Steam service, launched in 2003, became the dominant platform for PC games; its success with titles such as Half-Life 2 demonstrated that large games could be sold entirely online. Microsoft's Xbox Live Arcade (2004) brought curated downloadable games to consoles, followed by the PlayStation Network and Nintendo's Wii Shop Channel and eShop.

Mobile app stores, beginning with Apple's App Store in 2008, made digital distribution the default method of selling games on smartphones and tablets. Subscription services such as Xbox Game Pass extended the model in the late 2010s, offering libraries of downloadable games for a monthly fee.

Advantages and disadvantages

Digital distribution eliminates manufacturing, packaging and shipping costs, allows instantaneous delivery, enables independent developers to reach global audiences without a retail publisher, and permits frequent patching and post-release content. It also enables dynamic pricing, sales and regional pricing.

Disadvantages include the consumer's inability to resell or lend purchased games, dependence on platform holders' servers (games can be delisted or become unplayable when services shut down), large download sizes, and the licensing rather than ownership nature of purchases. Game preservationists have raised concerns that digitally-only titles may be lost when storefronts close, as occurred with the Wii Shop Channel and the Nintendo 3DS and Wii U eShops.

Major platforms

  • Steam — Valve's platform for Windows, macOS and Linux, the largest PC storefront.
  • PlayStation Store — Sony's storefront for PlayStation consoles.
  • Microsoft Store / Xbox — Microsoft's storefront for Xbox and Windows.
  • Nintendo eShop — Nintendo's storefront for Switch and earlier systems.
  • GOG.com — CD Projekt's DRM-free storefront.
  • Epic Games Store — launched 2018 with a higher revenue share for developers.
  • App Store and Google Play — dominant mobile distribution channels.
  • itch.io — open marketplace popular with independent developers.
