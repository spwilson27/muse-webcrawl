Fibronectin is a high-molecular-weight glycoprotein of the extracellular matrix that binds to membrane-spanning receptor proteins called integrins. It also binds extracellular matrix components such as collagen, fibrin, and heparan sulfate proteoglycans. Fibronectin plays a major role in cell adhesion, growth, migration, and differentiation, and it is important for processes such as wound healing and embryonic development.

Structure

Fibronectin exists as a dimer composed of two nearly identical polypeptide chains linked by a pair of C-terminal disulfide bonds. Each monomer is folded into a series of repeating modules — type I, type II, and type III domains — that confer binding sites for different partners. The type III10 module contains the Arg-Gly-Asp (RGD) sequence recognised by integrin receptors. Alternative splicing of the single FN1 pre-mRNA generates multiple isoforms, including variants containing the extra domains EDA and EDB associated with tissue repair and development.

Function

Two broad forms are distinguished. Plasma fibronectin, produced by hepatocytes, circulates in blood and is incorporated into fibrin clots, aiding haemostasis and wound healing. Cellular fibronectin, produced by fibroblasts and other cells, is assembled into insoluble fibrillar networks in tissues, where cells use traction forces transmitted through integrins to stretch and unfold fibronectin molecules and drive fibrillogenesis.

Through its simultaneous binding to cells and to matrix components such as collagen, fibronectin acts as a molecular bridge organising the matrix, guiding cell migration during embryogenesis, and modulating the availability of growth factors.

Clinical significance

Altered fibronectin expression and assembly are associated with fibrosis, cancer progression, and impaired wound healing. Loss-of-function mutations in FN1 cause forms of glomerulopathy with fibronectin deposits, while fibronectin glomerulopathy is characterised by abnormal deposition of the protein in the kidney. Fibronectin is also exploited as a biomarker and as a coating that promotes cell attachment in tissue culture and biomaterials research.
