Malnutrition

Malnutrition occurs when an organism gets too few or too many nutrients, resulting in health problems. Specifically, it is "a deficiency, excess, or imbalance of energy, protein and other nutrients" which adversely affects the body's tissues and form. Malnutrition is a category of diseases that includes undernutrition and overnutrition. Undernutrition is sometimes used as a synonym of protein–energy malnutrition with its two classic forms, kwashiorkor and marasmus.

Causes

Undernutrition results from insufficient food (in quantity or quality), from diseases that reduce appetite or impair absorption, or from increased nutrient needs during infection, pregnancy, or growth. Micronutrient deficiencies — notably of vitamin A, iron, iodine, and zinc — affect hundreds of millions of people. Vitamin C deficiency causes scurvy, in which impaired collagen synthesis weakens connective tissue. Overnutrition, driven by excess calories and ultra-processed foods, contributes to obesity, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease; thus malnutrition in its broad sense can coexist with obesity in the same populations and even the same households (the "double burden").

Effects and burden

In children, undernutrition causes stunting (low height for age), wasting (low weight for height), and underweight, and is implicated in a large share of under-five deaths because it weakens immunity to common infections. Global estimates from UNICEF, WHO, and the World Bank put stunting at roughly 150 million children under five in recent years. Acute severe malnutrition is treated with therapeutic foods such as ready-to-use peanut-based pastes.
