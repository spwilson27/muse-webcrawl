Glycogen

Glycogen is a multibranched polysaccharide of glucose that serves as a form of energy storage in animals, fungi and bacteria. It is the animal analogue of starch, sometimes called "animal starch".

Structure and function

Glycogen is built of α(1→4) linked glucose chains with α(1→6) branch points every 8–12 residues, forming granules in the cytosol, chiefly in liver and skeletal muscle. The liver releases glucose from glycogen to maintain blood sugar; muscle glycogen fuels contraction. Synthesis is catalysed by glycogen synthase and breakdown by glycogen phosphorylase, regulated by insulin, glucagon and adrenaline.

Disorders

Glycogen storage diseases, such as Pompe disease and McArdle disease, result from enzyme defects in glycogen metabolism.
