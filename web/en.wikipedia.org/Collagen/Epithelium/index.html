Epithelium

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Epithelium (plural: epithelia) is one of the four basic types of animal tissue, along with connective tissue, muscle tissue and nervous tissue. Epithelial tissues form continuous sheets of tightly packed cells that line the outer surfaces of organs and blood vessels throughout the body, as well as the inner surfaces of cavities in many internal organs. Examples include the epidermis (the outermost skin layer), the lining of the gastrointestinal tract, the airways, and glandular tissue.

Structure

Epithelial cells are held together by cell junctions (tight junctions, adherens junctions, desmosomes and gap junctions), rest on a basement membrane of extracellular matrix rich in laminin and type IV collagen, and are avascular — nourished by diffusion from underlying connective tissue. Epithelia show polarity: the apical surface faces the lumen or exterior (often bearing microvilli or cilia), while the basal surface attaches to the basement membrane.

Classification

Epithelia are classified by the number of cell layers and the shape of the surface cells:

LayersMeaningShapesMeaning
SimpleOne layerSquamousFlat, scale-like
StratifiedTwo or more layersCuboidalCube-shaped
PseudostratifiedAppears layered but all cells touch the basement membraneColumnarTall, column-shaped
TransitionalStretchable; shape changes with distension (urinary tract)

Thus the skin surface is stratified squamous epithelium (keratinised), the alveoli are simple squamous, the intestine is simple columnar, and the trachea is pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium.

Functions

Epithelia serve as protective barriers; they absorb (intestine), secrete (glands — exocrine and endocrine glands are epithelial derivatives), filter (kidney glomeruli), transport substances across the body surfaces, house sensory receptors, and enable gas exchange in the lungs. Their rapid renewal from stem cells suits exposed surfaces to constant wear.

Medical relevance

Cancers arising from epithelium — carcinomas — are by far the most common human cancers. Epithelial dysfunction underlies diseases such as cystic fibrosis (defective ion transport) and blistering disorders where adhesion fails.
