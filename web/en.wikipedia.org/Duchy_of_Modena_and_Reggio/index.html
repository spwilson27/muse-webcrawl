Duchy of Modena and Reggio

Status: Duchy of the Holy Roman Empire / Italian state
Capital: Modena
Period: 1452–1796; 1815–1859
Ruling house: House of Este; Austria-Este

The Duchy of Modena and Reggio (Italian: Ducato di Modena e Reggio) was a small northwestern Italian state that existed from 1452 to 1859, except for a period during the Italian Wars and another from 1796 to 1814. It was ruled first by the House of Este, which had controlled Modena and Reggio as lords since the 13th century, and later by the Austria-Este branch of the Habsburgs.

History

Borso d'Este received the ducal title for Modena and Reggio from Emperor Frederick III in 1452. The Este court at Ferrara and Modena was a major centre of Renaissance culture. When the main Este line died out in 1597, Ferrara was annexed by the Papal States, but the family retained Modena and Reggio. After the Napoleonic period, the duchy was restored in 1815 under Francis IV of Austria-Este.

Annexation

In 1859, during the Second Italian War of Independence, Duke Francis V was deposed by revolution and the duchy was annexed to the United Provinces of Central Italy, then to the Kingdom of Sardinia in 1860, becoming part of the unified Kingdom of Italy.
