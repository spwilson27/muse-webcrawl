Proofreading

Proofreading is the reading of a galley proof or electronic copy of a publication to find and correct production errors in text and art. Traditionally, a proofreader compares a proof against the original manuscript (holding "copy") to ensure fidelity and correct typographical errors, while copy editing — done earlier — addresses style, grammar, and usage.

Practice

Proofreaders mark corrections using standardised proofreading marks (per standards such as BS 5261 in the UK), covering errors in spelling, punctuation, spacing, capitalisation, and formatting. Classic technique distinguishes scrupulous "reading for sense and literals". In modern digital workflows proofreading is often done on screen or in PDF, and the role frequently merges with copy editing, though the distinction is maintained in traditional publishing.

History

The term derives from printer's proofs, preliminary impressions of typeset pages. Print shops historically employed pairs of workers — a copyholder reading aloud while the proofreader marked the proof — a practice that persisted into the 20th century.
