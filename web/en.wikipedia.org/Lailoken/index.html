Lailoken

Lailoken is a semi-legendary wild man and prophet of Strathclyde in the Scottish Borders, said to have lived in the 6th century. Driven mad by grief and guilt after a great battle, he fled to the Caledonian Forest, where he lived among the wild beasts and uttered prophecies. He appears in medieval sources including the Vita Merlini Silvestris and in a fragmentary account attached to the life of Saint Kentigern (Saint Mungo).

Legend

According to the Kentigern tradition, Lailoken confessed that he had caused the deaths at the Battle of Arfderydd (573 AD) by inciting the combatants, and that a voice from heaven condemned him to wander in torment until death. Saint Kentigern gave him the sacrament shortly before he died by a prophesied "threefold death" — falling, impalement, and drowning — when he fell from a wagon onto a stake in the River Tweed.

Connection with Merlin

Lailoken is widely considered a northern British precursor or counterpart of Merlin: Geoffrey of Monmouth's Merlin Silvestris in the Vita Merlini (c. 1150) tells a closely similar story of a prophet maddened by battle who retreats to the Caledonian Forest, and scholars link Lailoken to the Welsh figure Myrddin Wyllt ("Myrddin the Wild").
