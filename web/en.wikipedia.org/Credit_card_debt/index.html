Credit card debt

Credit card debt is the outstanding unpaid balance owed by consumers on credit card accounts. Because credit cards are revolving, unsecured loans with comparatively high interest rates, balances carried from month to month can grow rapidly through interest charges.

Mechanics

Cardholders who pay only the minimum payment each month extend repayment over many years and pay substantially more in interest than the original purchase price. Interest is typically expressed as an annual percentage rate (APR) and compounded daily or monthly. Late fees and penalty rates can further increase the cost.

Economic significance

Aggregate credit card debt is tracked as a component of household debt. In the United States it surpassed one trillion dollars in the 2020s. High levels of revolving debt are associated with financial stress, delinquency, and bankruptcy, and are monitored as indicators of consumer financial health.

Repayment strategies

Common approaches include the "avalanche" method (paying the highest-interest balance first) and the "snowball" method (paying the smallest balance first for motivational effect), as well as balance transfers and debt consolidation.
