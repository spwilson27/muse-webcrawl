Wolin National Park

Wolin National Park (Polish: Woliński Park Narodowy) is a national park in north-west Poland, established in 1960. It protects the central and northern part of the island of Wolin at the mouth of the Oder estuary, together with a strip of Baltic coastal waters and part of the Szczecin Lagoon. The park covers roughly 109 square kilometres.

Landscape

The park's scenery was formed by glacial deposits left at the end of the last Ice Age. A ridge of moraine hills runs along the northern coast, ending in dramatic cliffs up to about 95 metres high that are continually eroded by the Baltic Sea. Inland there are several post-glacial lakes, including Lake Turkusowe, named for its turquoise colour.

Flora and fauna

Old beech forests cover much of the park, along with pine woods, dune vegetation and rare cliff and saltmarsh plants; over a thousand vascular plant species have been recorded. The park's emblem is the white-tailed eagle, which nests here, and other notable animals include red deer, wild boar, otters and the European bison, which are kept in an enclosure near Międzyzdroje. The coastal waters are an important area for migratory and wintering water birds.

Visiting

The park administration and a natural history museum are located in Międzyzdroje. Marked hiking and cycling trails cross the park, with viewpoints on the cliffs at Gosań and Kawcza Góra.
