Aoraki / Mount Cook

Aoraki / Mount Cook is the highest mountain in New Zealand. Its height, as of 2014, is listed as 3,724 metres (12,218 feet). It lies in the Southern Alps / Kā Tiritiri o te Moana, the mountain range which runs the length of the South Island. A popular tourist destination, it is also a favourite challenge for mountain climbers.

Name

Aoraki means "cloud piercer" in the Kāi Tahu dialect of the Māori language. The mountain's English name honours Captain James Cook. Under the Ngāi Tahu Claims Settlement Act 1998, the official name became the dual name Aoraki / Mount Cook, one of many New Zealand places given combined Māori and English names.

Geography and climbing

The mountain lies within Aoraki / Mount Cook National Park, established in 1953 and part of the Te Wahipounamu World Heritage site. The summit ridge consists of the High, Middle, and Low Peaks. The first ascent was made on Christmas Day 1894 by Tom Fyfe, Jack Clarke, and George Graham. A major rock avalanche in 1991 reduced the summit's height by about 10 metres, and a 2014 survey set the revised elevation.

Māori significance

In Māori tradition, Aoraki is the most sacred ancestor of the Ngāi Tahu iwi: the mountain is the personification of Aoraki, a sky father's son whose waka (canoe) became the South Island.
