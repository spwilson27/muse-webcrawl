Tasman Glacier

The Tasman Glacier (Haupapa in Māori) is the largest glacier in New Zealand, flowing from the Southern Alps southeastward beside Aoraki / Mount Cook. It is roughly 23 kilometres (14 mi) long, up to 4 kilometres (2.5 mi) wide and as much as 600 metres (2,000 ft) thick in its upper reaches, and lies largely within Aoraki / Mount Cook National Park.

Description

The glacier begins below Hochstetter Dome and Mount Elie de Beaumont and flows south past the eastern flanks of Aoraki / Mount Cook and Mount Tasman. Its lower portion is covered in rocky debris (moraine) left by its retreat. Historically the glacier merged with the Murchison, Hooker and Mueller glaciers; those tributaries have since separated as ice has thinned.

Retreat

Like most New Zealand glaciers, the Tasman has retreated markedly since the 1970s–1990s — by several hundred metres per year in peak periods. Its proglacial Tasman Lake began forming in the late 20th century and grew rapidly as the terminus calved icebergs into it; by the 2010s the lake was several kilometres long. Boat tours among the icebergs have become a noted attraction. The retreat is regarded as a visible regional indicator of climate warming.
