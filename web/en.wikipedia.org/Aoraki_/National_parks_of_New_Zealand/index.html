National parks of New Zealand

Aoraki / Mount Cook National Park, one of New Zealand's thirteen national parks

The national parks of New Zealand are protected natural areas administered by the Department of Conservation (DOC) under the National Parks Act 1980, preserved in perpetuity for their scenery, ecology and public benefit. New Zealand has thirteen national parks (a fourteenth, Te Urewera, was disestablished as a national park in 2014), covering more than 30,000 square kilometres in total, mostly mountainous terrain.

History

The system began with Tongariro National Park — the world's fourth national park — created after the paramount chief Te Heuheu Tukino IV gifted the sacred volcanoes of the central North Island to the Crown in 1887; it was formally established in 1894. Fiordland, the largest park, and others followed through the 20th century.

List of parks

  • North Island: Tongariro, Whanganui, Egmont (Taranaki)
  • South Island: Abel Tasman, Kahurangi, Nelson Lakes, Paparoa, Arthur's Pass, Aoraki / Mount Cook, Westland Tai Poutini, Mount Aspiring, Fiordland
  • Stewart Island: Rakiura

Three parks (Tongariro, Te Wahipounamu — comprising Westland, Aoraki/Mount Cook, Mount Aspiring and Fiordland — and the Sub-Antarctic Islands areas) are inscribed as UNESCO World Heritage Sites. The parks contain New Zealand's nine Great Walks, including the Milford, Routeburn and Kepler Tracks, and are managed to protect endemic species threatened by introduced predators such as stoats, possums and rats.
