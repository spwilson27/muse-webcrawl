Aoraki / Mount Cook is the highest mountain in New Zealand, rising 3,724 metres (12,218 ft) in the Southern Alps of the South Island. It lies in Aoraki / Mount Cook National Park, within the Canterbury Region, and is a triple-peaked massif sheltering the Tasman Glacier to its east and the Hooker Glacier to its southwest.

Name

In Māori tradition Aoraki is a figure of Ngāi Tahu cosmology, a son of Rakinui (the sky father) whose waka became the South Island. The mountain was named Mount Cook by Europeans in honour of Captain James Cook. In 1998, under the Ngāi Tahu settlement, the official name became the dual name Aoraki / Mount Cook, one of many New Zealand place names combining Māori and European names.

Climbing history

Early attempts in the 1880s by the Rev. William Spotswood Green came within about 60 metres of the summit. The first successful ascent was made on Christmas Day 1894 by New Zealanders Tom Fyfe, Jack Clarke and George Graham. In 1910 Freda du Faur became the first woman to climb it. On 3 December 1910 the first grand traverse of all three peaks was completed. The summit measured 3,764 m until a major rock avalanche in December 1991 reduced its height; a GPS survey in 2014 confirmed the present height of 3,724 m.

The young Edmund Hillary climbed in this area early in his career, and the park remains the training ground of New Zealand mountaineering. The nearby village (Mount Cook Village) serves the Hermitage Hotel and the Sir Edmund Hillary Alpine Centre.
