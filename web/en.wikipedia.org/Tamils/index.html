The Tamils (Tamil: தமிழர், Tamiḻar) are a Dravidian ethnolinguistic group native to the Indian state of Tamil Nadu and the north-eastern regions of Sri Lanka, united by the Tamil language—one of the world's longest-surviving classical languages. Tamil-speaking communities also live across a global diaspora shaped by trade, indentured labour under colonial rule, and modern migration.

History

Tamil civilisation is attested from the Sangam literature of the early centuries CE, which describes the three crowned dynasties of the Chera, Chola and Pandya kingdoms. The medieval Chola Empire (9th–13th centuries) became a maritime power projecting influence into Southeast Asia. In Sri Lanka, Tamil kingdoms centred on Jaffna flourished from the 13th century. Relations between Sri Lanka's Tamil minority and the Sinhalese-majority state led to a civil war (1983–2009) between the government and the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam.

Culture

Tamil culture is expressed in a classical literary tradition over two thousand years old, the Bharatanatyam dance form, Carnatic music, monumental temple architecture, and a cuisine built on rice, lentils and tamarind. The harvest festival of Pongal and the temple festival traditions remain central to communal life. Tamil cinema, based in Chennai, is one of India's largest film industries.
