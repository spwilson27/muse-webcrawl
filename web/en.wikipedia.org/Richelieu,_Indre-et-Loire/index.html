Richelieu, Indre-et-Loire


The Grande Rue, Richelieu — an early planned town

Richelieu is a commune in the Indre-et-Loire department in central France, in the Centre-Val de Loire region. It is famous as a pioneering example of a planned town: it was founded in 1631 by Cardinal Richelieu, chief minister of Louis XIII, beside the great château he built near his ancestral estate, and laid out on a rigorous rectangular grid.

History

Armand-Jean du Plessis, Cardinal Richelieu, acquired the modest village of his ancestors and, with royal permission, transformed it into a showcase of his power. Between 1631 and 1642 the architect Jacques Lemercier — designer of the château itself — laid out a walled town on a strict grid plan: a main street (the Grande Rue) flanked by uniform terraced houses for courtiers and officials, a central square, a church, market halls and moated ramparts with monumental gates. The château, one of the grandest of its age, was largely demolished in the 19th century; only its park, entrance pavilions and a few structures survive.

The town today

Remarkably, the town's 17th-century street plan and many of its original townhouses survive almost intact, making Richelieu a classic case study in Baroque town planning, sometimes compared with the ideal cities of the Italian Renaissance. The town has a population of roughly 1,700. Its park remains open to visitors, and the Espace Richelieu presents the vanished château through models and a virtual reconstruction.
