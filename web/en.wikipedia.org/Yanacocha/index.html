Yanacocha

Yanacocha is a gold mine in the Cajamarca region of northern Peru, at an altitude of roughly 3,500 to 4,100 metres in the Andes. Operated by Minera Yanacocha, a company long majority-owned by Newmont Corporation with partners including Buenaventura, it began production in 1993 and became one of the largest gold mines in the world and the largest in South America.

Operations

The mine is a series of open pits using heap-leach extraction with cyanide. At its peak in the mid-2000s it produced over 3 million ounces of gold per year; output has since declined as oxide ores were depleted, and the company has pursued the Yanacocha Sulfuros project to extend mine life by processing underground sulfide ores.

Controversies

Yanacocha has faced sustained local protests over water use and contamination, and a 2000 mercury spill near Choropampa caused major health complaints. The planned Conga project, an expansion onto high-altitude lakes, was suspended in 2011 amid mass protests in Cajamarca.
