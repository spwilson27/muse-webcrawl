Uriah Heep

Uriah Heep performing on stage.

Uriah Heep is a fictional character created by Charles Dickens in his 1850 novel David Copperfield; the name is also borne by an English rock band formed in London in 1969, which took its name from the Dickens character.

The Dickens character

In the novel, Uriah Heep is a clerk — later partner — in the legal practice of Mr Wickfield in Canterbury. Heep is notable for his cloying humility, obsequiousness, and insistence that he is "'umble" despite scheming to seize control of the firm and marry Wickfield's daughter Agnes. His fraud is eventually exposed by Wilkins Micawber, and Heep is imprisoned. The character's name has become a byword for an insincere, self-abasing schemer.

The band

The rock band Uriah Heep, formed by guitarist Mick Box and vocalist David Byron, became a prominent act in early-1970s hard rock and progressive rock, with albums including Demons and Wizards and The Magician's Birthday (both 1972) and songs such as "Easy Livin'" and "July Morning." The band is noted for its five-part vocal harmonies and prominent Hammond organ. Despite many line-up changes, with Box the sole constant member, the group has continued to record and tour for over five decades.
