The Federal President (German: Bundespräsident) is the head of state of the Federal Republic of Germany. Under the 1949 Basic Law, the office is largely ceremonial and non-executive: executive power is exercised by the Federal Government, headed by the Chancellor. The president is nevertheless the highest-ranking official of the state, represents the Federal Republic in matters of international law, accredits diplomats, and signs federal laws into force.

Election

The president is elected by the Federal Convention (Bundesversammlung), a body convened solely for this purpose, consisting of the members of the Bundestag and an equal number of delegates chosen by the parliaments of the sixteen federal states (Länder). Any German citizen aged forty or over and entitled to vote for the Bundestag is eligible. The term is five years, with re-election permitted only once consecutively. The president-elect takes the oath prescribed in Article 56 of the Basic Law before the assembled members of the Bundestag and Bundesrat.

Powers and duties

The president formally appoints and dismisses federal ministers on the Chancellor's proposal, signs laws after a constitutional review, accredits envoys, exercises the right of pardon in individual cases, and confers orders and honours. Most presidential acts require the countersignature of the Chancellor or a competent federal minister. On rare occasions presidents have refused to sign legislation — eight times since 1949 — citing constitutional doubts. The president also holds a number of reserve roles: proposing a candidate for Chancellor, and, in cases of parliamentary deadlock under Articles 63 and 68, deciding whether to appoint a minority chancellor or dissolve the Bundestag.

History

The office descends from the Weimar Republic's Reichspräsident (first held by Friedrich Ebert, then Paul von Hindenburg), who wielded far-reaching emergency powers — a design the framers of the Basic Law deliberately weakened in 1949. Theodor Heuss, the first Federal President (1949–1959), shaped the office's above-party, integrative character. Later notable holders include Gustav Heinemann, Richard von Weizsäcker — whose 1985 speech marking 8 May as a day of liberation was widely praised — Roman Herzog, Johannes Rau, Horst Köhler, Christian Wulff, and Joachim Gauck. Köhler (2010) and Wulff (2012) both resigned mid-term. Frank-Walter Steinmeier has held the office since March 2017 and was re-elected in 2022.

Official residences

Bellevue Palace in Berlin has been the president's primary official residence since 1994; Villa Hammerschmidt in Bonn serves as the secondary residence. The Office of the Federal President (Bundespräsidialamt) supports the office with a staff of around 180.
