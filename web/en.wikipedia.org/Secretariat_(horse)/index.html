Secretariat (horse)

Secretariat (March 30, 1970 – October 4, 1989), also known as Big Red, was an American Thoroughbred racehorse who, in 1973, became the first United States Triple Crown winner in 25 years. His record-breaking victory in the Belmont Stakes, which he won by 31 lengths, is widely regarded as one of the greatest races in history, and he is routinely ranked among the greatest racehorses of all time.

Triple Crown campaign

As a three-year-old, Secretariat won the Kentucky Derby in a record 1:59 2/5 — still the fastest Derby ever run — finishing each quarter faster than the one before. He then won the Preakness Stakes, and on June 9, 1973, in the Belmont Stakes, he pulled away from the field to win by 31 lengths in a world-record time of 2:24 for a mile and a half, a record that still stands.

Later career and stud

Secretariat lost a few races later in 1973, including an upset by Onion at Saratoga, but retired as Horse of the Year for the second time. He stood at stud at Claiborne Farm in Kentucky, siring numerous stakes winners, and his daughters became influential broodmares. He was euthanised on October 4, 1989, after developing laminitis. A necropsy famously revealed a heart estimated at about 22 pounds, far larger than average.

Legacy

Secretariat was inducted into the National Museum of Racing and Hall of Fame in 1974, appears on a US postage stamp, and was the subject of a 2010 Disney film. He is ranked among the top American racehorses of the twentieth century, behind or beside Man o' War in most assessments.
