Deposition (phase transition)

Deposition is the phase transition in which a gas transforms directly into a solid without passing through the liquid phase. It is the thermodynamic reverse of sublimation, in which a solid turns directly into a gas.

Examples

The formation of frost is the most familiar natural example: water vapour in humid air deposits as ice crystals on surfaces colder than the dew point. Snow formation in clouds involves deposition of vapour onto ice nuclei. In industrial contexts, physical vapour deposition and chemical vapour deposition are used to grow thin films and coatings on substrates in electronics and optics.

Thermodynamics

Deposition is exothermic, releasing the latent heat of sublimation. It is favoured at low temperatures and occurs when gas molecules lose enough kinetic energy upon contact with a surface to settle into a solid state.
