GR20

The GR20 crosses Corsica's mountainous interior

The GR20 is a long-distance footpath (a GR — sentier de Grande Randonnée) that crosses the Mediterranean island of Corsica diagonally from north to south. It is widely considered one of the most beautiful, and one of the toughest, long-distance trails in Europe. The route is about 180 km (112 miles) long with roughly 12,000 metres of total ascent, and typically takes about 15 days to walk.

Route

The trail runs from Calenzana, near Calvi in the north, to Conca, near Porto-Vecchio in the south, following the main watershed ridge of the island. The northern section is the more rugged and alpine, passing below peaks such as Monte Cinto (2,706 m, Corsica's highest summit) and through the famous Cirque de la Solitude (crossed until a fatal 2015 accident led to a rerouting). The southern section, beyond the Col de Bavella area, is somewhat less severe but still demanding. The route is marked with red-and-white paint blazes.

Walking the trail

Most walkers stay in the staffed mountain refuges (refuges or gîtes d'étape) spaced a day's walk apart, which offer dormitory beds, meals, and bivouac pitches; wild camping is otherwise restricted within the Parc naturel régional de Corse. The walking season runs from about June to October, with snow possible on high passes early and late in the season, and fierce heat in midsummer. The fastest known times for the full route, set by elite trail runners such as François D'Haene and Lambert Santelli, are around 30 hours.
