Homeopathy

Homeopathy
Claims: Like cures like; extreme dilution increases potency
Founder: Samuel Hahnemann (1796)
Assessment: Pseudoscience; no evidence beyond placebo

Homeopathy is a system of alternative medicine created in 1796 by Samuel Hahnemann. It is based on the claim that a substance that causes symptoms in healthy people can cure similar symptoms in the sick (like cures like), and that remedies become more potent through repeated dilution and shaking (succession), often to the point where no molecules of the original substance remain.

Scientific evaluation

Homeopathy is considered a pseudoscience. Systematic reviews by medical and scientific bodies have found no reliable evidence that homeopathic remedies are more effective than placebos, and their proposed mechanisms contradict established chemistry and physics, since dilutions frequently exceed Avogadro's limit.

Practice and regulation

Despite the lack of evidence, homeopathic products are sold widely and are regulated differently across countries; some health systems have withdrawn public funding for homeopathy.
