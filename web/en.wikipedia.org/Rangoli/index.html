Rangoli

OriginIndian subcontinent
MaterialsColoured rice, rice flour, sand, flower petals, lentils
Regional namesKolam (Tamil Nadu), Alpana (Bengal), Mandana (Rajasthan), Chowkpurana (Chhattisgarh)
OccasionsDiwali, Onam, Pongal, weddings and daily worship

Rangoli is an art form originating in the Indian subcontinent, in which patterns are created on the floor or a tabletop using materials such as powdered limestone, red ochre, dry rice flour, coloured sand, quartz powder, flower petals and coloured rocks. It is an everyday practice in many Hindu households and is elaborated for festivals such as Diwali, Onam and Pongal.

Purpose and practice

Rangoli designs are traditionally drawn at the entrance of a house to welcome guests and the goddess Lakshmi, and are believed to bring good luck and ward off evil. In its everyday form — known as kolam in Tamil Nadu — a pattern of dots and loops is drawn at dawn with rice flour, which also feeds ants, birds and other small creatures. Designs range from simple geometric figures to elaborate depictions of deities, peacocks and lotus motifs, passed down through generations and improvised by their makers.

Regional variations

The practice has many regional forms and names: alpana in Bengal, mandana on the walls and floors of Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh, chowkpurana in Chhattisgarh, and pookkalam — made entirely of flower petals — during Onam in Kerala. Competitions in schools and public spaces, and giant community rangolis, are common during festival seasons.
