Jamaica station is a major transportation hub of the Long Island Rail Road (LIRR) in the Jamaica neighbourhood of Queens, New York City. It is the LIRR's central transfer point: nearly all of the railroad's branches pass through or converge at Jamaica, and most passengers travelling between branches change trains here. More than 200,000 travellers pass through on a typical weekday, making it one of the busiest rail stations in North America.

History

The first Jamaica station opened in 1836 on the Long Island Rail Road's original main line. The present station dates from the "Jamaica Improvement" project of 1912–1913, a monumental grade-separation and rebuilding scheme that produced the current complex of six island platforms and ten tracks, together with the nearby JAY interlocking. The station building stands over the tracks on Sutphin Boulevard.

Services and connections

All LIRR branches except the Port Washington Branch stop at or pass through Jamaica; eastbound trains fan out across Long Island while westbound trains serve Penn Station, Grand Central Madison, Atlantic Terminal, and Long Island City. Since 2003 the station has been the Manhattan-side terminus of AirTrain JFK, the driverless people-mover linking the railroad and subway to John F. Kennedy International Airport. The Sutphin Boulevard–Archer Avenue–JFK Airport subway station (E, J, and Z trains) adjoins the complex, and numerous bus routes serve the surrounding streets. The opening of Grand Central Madison in 2023 reshaped service patterns, adding timed shuttle connections between Jamaica and Brooklyn.
