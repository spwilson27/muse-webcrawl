Tallahassee is the capital city of the U.S. state of Florida and the county seat of Leon County. Located in the state's Panhandle region amid rolling red hills unusual for Florida, it is a center of government, higher education, and research, and the principal city of the Tallahassee metropolitan area.

History

The name Tallahassee derives from a Muskogee word often interpreted as "old fields" or "abandoned fields," referring to the Apalachee settlements encountered by Spanish explorers; the nearby mission-era site of Anhaica was an Apalachee capital. In 1824 Tallahassee was chosen as the territorial capital because it lay roughly midway between the old Spanish capitals of St. Augustine and Pensacola. It was the only Confederate state capital east of the Mississippi not captured by Union forces during the Civil War, defended at the Battle of Natural Bridge in 1865.

Education and government

Tallahassee is home to Florida State University and Florida A&M University, a historically Black university, as well as Tallahassee Community College. State government is the largest employer; the 22-story Florida Capitol tower, opened in 1977 beside the preserved Historic Capitol, dominates the skyline.

Geography and climate

Unlike most of Florida, Tallahassee has a humid subtropical climate with four distinct seasons, including occasional winter freezes and rare snow. Its canopy roads, lined with live oaks draped in Spanish moss, are a celebrated local feature.
