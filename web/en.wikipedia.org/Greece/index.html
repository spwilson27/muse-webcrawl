Greece

Greece, officially the Hellenic Republic, is a country in Southeast Europe, situated on the southern tip of the Balkan peninsula, at the crossroads of Europe, Asia and Africa. It shares land borders with Albania, North Macedonia and Bulgaria to the north and Turkey to the east. The Aegean Sea lies to the east of the mainland, the Ionian Sea to the west, and the Sea of Crete and the Mediterranean Sea to the south. Greece has the longest coastline on the Mediterranean Basin, featuring thousands of islands. The country consists of nine traditional geographic regions and has a population of about 10.4 million. Athens is the nation's capital and largest city, followed by Thessaloniki.

History

Greece is widely considered the cradle of Western civilisation, being the birthplace of democracy, Western philosophy, Western literature, historiography, political science, major scientific and mathematical principles, theatre and the Olympic Games. The Minoan and Mycenaean civilisations flourished in the Bronze Age; after a dark age, Greek city-states (poleis) such as Athens and Sparta emerged, colonising the Mediterranean and Black Sea coasts. Alexander the Great's conquests spread Greek culture across the Hellenistic world. Greece was annexed by Rome in 146 BC, then became the heartland of the Greek-speaking Byzantine Empire. Four centuries of Ottoman rule followed Constantinople's fall in 1453. The modern Greek state emerged from the Greek War of Independence (1821–1829).

The twentieth century brought the Balkan Wars, two world wars, a civil war (1946–1949), and a military dictatorship (1967–1974). Greece joined the European Community in 1981 and adopted the euro in 2001. From 2009 the Greek government-debt crisis led to austerity programmes and bailouts that reshaped the economy and politics of the country.

Geography

About 80% of Greece consists of mountains or hills, making it one of the most mountainous countries in Europe. Mount Olympus, at 2,918 metres, is the highest peak and mythological home of the Olympian gods. The country includes some 6,000 islands and islets, of which 227 are inhabited; Crete is the largest, followed by Euboea, Lesbos and Rhodes. The climate is predominantly Mediterranean, with mild, wet winters and hot, dry summers.

Economy

Greece has a high-income economy based chiefly on services. Tourism is a major industry — Greece is consistently among the world's most visited countries — and Greece operates the world's largest merchant shipping fleet by tonnage. Major exports include petroleum products, pharmaceuticals, aluminium, olive oil and other agricultural produce.

Culture

Greek culture has evolved over millennia, from Mycenaean Greece through classical antiquity, Byzantium and the modern era. Greek is one of the oldest continuously documented languages, with a written record spanning over 3,400 years. The Greek Orthodox Church plays a central role in national life. Greek cuisine, music, dance and festivals (such as Easter celebrations and name days) remain prominent features of daily life.
