Hyperventilation

Hyperventilation is irregular breathing that occurs when the rate or tidal volume of breathing eliminates more carbon dioxide than the body can produce. This leads to hypocapnia, a reduced concentration of carbon dioxide dissolved in the blood, and respiratory alkalosis. Symptoms include light-headedness, tingling in the lips, hands or feet, headache, weakness, fainting and seizures. The rapid breathing may develop as a reaction to emotional stress, pain, or medical conditions.

Causes

Psychological causes include panic attacks, anxiety and stress. Medical causes include asthma attacks, pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, heart failure, diabetic ketoacidosis, head injury and certain drugs. Hyperventilation differs from hyperpnea, which is an appropriately increased breathing rate matching increased metabolic demand, such as during exercise.

Management

Management consists primarily of treating the underlying cause and reassurance. Breathing retraining and slowing the breath help in panic-related cases. The historical practice of rebreathing into a paper bag is no longer generally recommended, because it can be dangerous if the hyperventilation is due to a serious medical condition such as a heart or lung problem.
