Restaurant

A restaurant is a business that prepares and serves food and drinks to customers. Meals are generally served and eaten on the premises, but many restaurants also offer take-out and food delivery services. Restaurants vary greatly in appearance and offerings, including in the variety of cuisines and service models, from inexpensive fast-food restaurants to high-priced fine-dining establishments.

History

Although inns, taverns, and cookshops had served food to travellers for centuries, the modern restaurant emerged in 18th-century Paris. The word derives from the French restaurer, "to restore", and originally referred to restorative broths sold by establishments that, unlike the guild-controlled traiteurs, could serve individual portions at individual tables at flexible times. After the French Revolution, chefs formerly employed by aristocratic households opened restaurants in greater numbers, and the institution spread through Europe and the Americas during the 19th century.

Types

Common formats include fine dining, casual dining, cafés, bistros, fast-food restaurants, fast-casual outlets, buffets, food trucks, and pop-up restaurants. Service styles range from full table service by waiters, through counter service, to self-service. A restaurant's kitchen is led by a chef, with a hierarchy of roles classically organised in the brigade system associated with Auguste Escoffier.

Guides and ratings

Restaurants are reviewed by critics and rated by guides such as the Michelin Guide, whose stars are among the most coveted distinctions in fine dining, and by crowd-sourced review platforms. Restaurants are also subject to government regulation and health inspection.
