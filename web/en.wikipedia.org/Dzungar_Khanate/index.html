The Dzungar Khanate at its greatest extent

The Dzungar Khanate was an Inner Asian khanate of the Oirat Mongols, centred on the region of Dzungaria (northern Xinjiang and the Ili valley). Founded in the 1630s by Erdeni Batur of the Choros clan, it was the last great nomadic empire of the Eurasian steppe and a major power between Qing China, Tibet, the Kazakh Khanate and Tsarist Russia for more than a century.

History

Under Galdan Boshugtu Khan (r. 1671–1697) the Dzungars conquered the Yarkent Khanate, intervened in Tibet and invaded Outer Mongolia, bringing them into collision with the Qing dynasty; the Kangxi Emperor personally led campaigns that culminated in Galdan's defeat and death in 1697. His successors Tsewang Rabtan and Galdan Tseren raided Tibet (1717), fought the Kazakhs in the "Great Disaster" years, and checked Qing expansion, while developing agriculture, mining and cannon-founding with the help of captured European expertise such as the Swedish officer Johan Gustaf Renat.

Conquest and genocide

A succession crisis after 1745 gave the Qianlong Emperor his opening: Qing armies intervened in 1755, and when Amursana's revolt failed, Qianlong ordered the extermination of the Dzungars in 1757–1758. Campaigns and smallpox destroyed the Dzungar people — estimates suggest some 70–80 percent of a population of around 600,000 perished — and Dzungaria was repopulated with settlers and garrisons, becoming part of Qing Xinjiang.
