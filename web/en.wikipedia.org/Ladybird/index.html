Ladybird

Ladybirds (also called ladybugs in North America, or ladybird beetles) are beetles of the family Coccinellidae, comprising some 6,000 described species worldwide. They are small, typically 0.8 to 18 mm long, with round or oval bodies and brightly coloured wing cases — often red, orange or yellow with black spots — warning colours that advertise their distastefulness to predators.

Most ladybirds are predators of aphids, scale insects and other plant pests, making them valued agents of biological pest control in gardens and agriculture; both larvae and adults feed voraciously. Some species, such as the harlequin ladybird (Harmonia axyridis), introduced for pest control, have become invasive. In winter many species overwinter in large aggregations. Ladybirds are widely regarded as lucky in folklore across Europe and Asia.
