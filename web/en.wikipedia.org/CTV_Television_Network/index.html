CTV Television Network

The CTV Television Network, commonly known as CTV, is a Canadian English-language terrestrial television network. Launched in 1961, it is Canada's largest privately owned television network and is owned by Bell Media, a division of BCE Inc.

History

CTV was formed by a group of privately owned stations seeking to create a second English-language television service in Canada alongside the public CBC. It began broadcasting on 1 October 1961. For much of its early history the network was owned cooperatively by its affiliated stations; it was restructured as a corporation in the 1990s and acquired by Baton Broadcasting, which rebranded itself CTV Inc.

In 2000, CTV was purchased by Bell Globemedia, a consortium involving BCE. Following a period under the name CTVglobemedia, the network became part of Bell Media in 2011 when BCE regained full control.

Programming

CTV's schedule combines Canadian productions with popular American series acquired from U.S. networks. Its flagship national newscast is CTV National News, and local stations produce regional news under the CTV News banner. Notable Canadian programs aired on the network have included news magazine W5 and various drama and entertainment series produced to meet Canadian content requirements.
