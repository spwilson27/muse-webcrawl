Dancehall

Dancehall is a genre of Jamaican popular music that originated in the late 1970s. Initially, dancehall was a sparser version of reggae than the roots style that had dominated the 1970s. Named after Jamaican dance halls, where local sound systems played the music to crowds of dancers, the genre is characterized by deejays "toasting" or rapping over backing tracks known as riddims.

History

In the mid-1980s, digital instrumentation became more prevalent, dramatically changing the sound. The 1985 hit "Under Mi Sleng Teng" by Wayne Smith, built on a Casio keyboard preset by producer King Jammy, is widely credited with launching the "ragga" or digital dancehall era. Successful dancehall artists have included Yellowman, Shabba Ranks, Buju Banton, Beenie Man, Bounty Killer, Sean Paul, Elephant Man, Vybz Kartel, and Popcaan. Dancehall achieved major international chart success in the early 2000s and has influenced hip hop, reggaeton, and Afrobeats.

Culture

Dancehall culture encompasses distinctive dance moves, fashion, and the sound system session as a social institution. The genre's lyrics have sometimes drawn controversy over violent or homophobic content, prompting international campaigns and debate within Jamaica and abroad.
