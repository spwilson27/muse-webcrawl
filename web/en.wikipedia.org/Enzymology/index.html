Enzymology

Enzymology is the branch of biochemistry that studies enzymes: their structure, kinetics, mechanisms, regulation, and roles in cells and disease. Enzymes are biological catalysts, usually proteins and sometimes RNA molecules, that accelerate chemical reactions with high specificity.

Core questions

Enzymologists measure how fast reactions proceed under different substrate concentrations, temperatures, and pH values. Concepts such as the Michaelis–Menten equation, turnover number, inhibition, allosteric regulation, and transition-state stabilization explain how enzymes achieve rate enhancements.

Methods

The field combines purification and assays with crystallography, cryo-electron microscopy, spectroscopy, mutagenesis, computational modeling, and single-molecule techniques. Industrial enzymology engineers enzymes for detergents, food processing, biofuels, pharmaceuticals, and diagnostics.

History

Early work on fermentation and digestion led to Eduard Buchner's demonstration of cell-free fermentation and James Sumner's crystallization of urease. Later mechanistic studies transformed enzymes from mysterious ferments into molecules whose chemistry could be analyzed precisely. See also enzyme, enzyme kinetics, and biochemistry.
