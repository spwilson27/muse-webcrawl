British Agricultural Revolution

The British Agricultural Revolution was the unprecedented increase in agricultural production in Britain between the mid-17th and late 19th centuries, driven by new farming methods, land enclosure and rising productivity. It is often seen as a precondition for the Industrial Revolution, as it fed a growing population and released labour for industry.

Key developments

Important changes included the four-field Norfolk crop rotation promoted by Viscount Townshend, which replaced fallow land with turnips and clover; Jethro Tull's seed drill; selective breeding of livestock by pioneers such as Robert Bakewell; and parliamentary enclosure of common land. Mechanisation, drainage and improved transport further raised yields.

Consequences

Food output rose dramatically while the agricultural share of the workforce fell, though enclosure displaced many rural labourers and remains socially controversial.
