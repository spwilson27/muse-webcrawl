Acolyte

An acolyte is an assistant or follower who helps a celebrant in a religious service or procession. The word comes from the Greek akolouthos, meaning one who follows or accompanies. In many Christian denominations, an acolyte is a person who performs ceremonial duties such as carrying processional crosses, candles, and incense, and assisting the priest or minister during worship.

In the Roman Catholic Church

In the Latin Church, the ministry of acolyte is an instituted ministry. Instituted acolytes assist at Mass by preparing the altar and sacred vessels and, when needed, distributing Holy Communion as extraordinary ministers. Historically, acolyte was one of the minor orders leading toward ordination. In practice, in many parishes the duties are performed by altar servers, often young people, who are not instituted acolytes.

In other traditions

In Anglican and Lutheran churches, acolytes commonly light and extinguish candles, carry the cross in procession, and assist with the elements of communion. The term is also used more generally for a devoted follower or attendant of a person or cause.
