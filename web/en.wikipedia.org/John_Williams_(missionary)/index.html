John Williams (missionary)

Born: 29 June 1796, Tottenham, England

Died: 20 November 1839, Erromango, New Hebrides

John Williams was an English Congregational missionary active in the South Pacific, known as the "Apostle of Polynesia". Sent by the London Missionary Society, he arrived in Tahiti in 1817.

Pacific missions

Based on Raiatea and later Rarotonga, Williams used ships — including the Endeavour, which he built himself, and the Messenger of Peace — to carry Polynesian teachers to Samoa, the Cook Islands and beyond, spreading Christianity with remarkable speed. He translated scripture into Rarotongan and wrote the popular Narrative of Missionary Enterprises in the South Sea Islands (1837).

Death

He was killed by islanders at Erromango in the New Hebrides in 1839, an event commemorated across the Pacific; the London Missionary Society named a long series of mission ships John Williams after him.
