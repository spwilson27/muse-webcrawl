Marc Bloch

Marc Bloch, c. 1914–1918
Born:6 July 1886, Lyon, France
Died:16 June 1944, Saint-Didier-de-Formans, France
Field:Medieval history, historiography
Known for:Co-founding the Annales school

Marc Léopold Benjamin Bloch (6 July 1886 – 16 June 1944) was a French historian of medieval France and a co-founder, with Lucien Febvre, of the Annales school of historical writing. A veteran of the First World War, he was executed by the Gestapo in 1944 for his work in the French Resistance.

Career

The son of the historian Gustave Bloch, Marc Bloch studied at the École Normale Supérieure and taught at the University of Strasbourg from 1919, where he met Febvre. Together they founded the journal Annales d'histoire économique et sociale in 1929, the platform of a new history that emphasized geography, material life, mental structures, and the longue durée over battles and kings. Bloch's major works include The Royal Touch (1924), a study of the belief that kings could cure scrofula; French Rural History (1931); and the two-volume Feudal Society (1939–40), a comparative synthesis that remains a landmark of medieval studies.

Second World War and death

Though in his fifties and in poor health, Bloch served as a reserve captain in 1939–40; his memoir of the collapse, Strange Defeat, was published posthumously and became a classic account of France's fall. Excluded from his Paris chair by Vichy's anti-Jewish statutes, he taught at Montpellier, joined the Resistance in Lyon, and was arrested in March 1944. On 16 June 1944 he was shot with a group of fellow prisoners at Saint-Didier-de-Formans. His unfinished methodological testament, The Historian's Craft (Apologie pour l'histoire), was published in 1949 and is among the most widely read reflections on the discipline of history.

Legacy

The former University of Strasbourg II was named Marc Bloch University in his honour (until the Strasbourg universities remerged in 2009). Fernand Braudel and the second generation of the Annales school acknowledged him as a founding influence.
