Thomas Cochrane, 10th Earl of Dundonald (14 December 1775 – 31 October 1860), styled Lord Cochrane between 1778 and 1831, was a British naval officer, politician, and mercenary admiral. One of the most daring and successful captains of the Napoleonic Wars, he was nicknamed le loup des mers ("the sea wolf") by Napoleon. His exploits provided inspiration for the fictional naval heroes Horatio Hornblower and Jack Aubrey.

Royal Navy career

Commanding the tiny brig HMS Speedy, Cochrane captured the much larger Spanish frigate El Gamo in 1801 by boarding. His aggressive cruising in the Mediterranean and his defence of the castle of Trinidad at Rosas made his name, but quarrels with superiors, a conviction in the Great Stock Exchange Fraud of 1814 (which he always maintained was unjust), and expulsion from Parliament and the navy ended his British career for nearly two decades.

South America and Greece

Cochrane then took command of the navies of the independence movements of Chile (1818–1822) — where he seized Valdivia and effectively destroyed Spanish sea power in the Pacific — Brazil (1823–1825), and Greece (1827–1828). He was eventually pardoned and reinstated in the Royal Navy in 1832, promoted to flag rank, and in old age campaigned for steam propulsion and other naval reforms. He is buried in Westminster Abbey.
