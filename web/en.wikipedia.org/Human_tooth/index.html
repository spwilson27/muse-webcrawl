Human tooth

The human tooth is a hard, calcified structure found in the jaws (upper jaw — maxilla; lower jaw — mandible) of humans, used for breaking down food, aiding speech, and supporting facial structure. Humans are diphyodont: they develop two sets of teeth, 20 deciduous ("baby") teeth in childhood and up to 32 permanent teeth in adulthood.

Anatomy

A tooth consists of a crown, visible above the gumline, and one or more roots embedded in the jawbone. The crown is covered by enamel, the hardest substance in the human body; beneath it lies dentine, which surrounds the pulp chamber containing nerves and blood vessels. The root is covered by cementum, and the tooth is held in its socket by the periodontal ligament.

Types

Adult humans normally have four types of teeth: incisors (8) for cutting, canines (4) for tearing, premolars (8) and molars (12, including the third molars or "wisdom teeth") for grinding. Teeth are identified in dentistry by systems such as the FDI World Dental Federation notation and the Universal Numbering System.

Development and health

Tooth development (odontogenesis) begins before birth; deciduous teeth usually erupt from about six months of age, and permanent teeth replace them between roughly ages six and thirteen. Common diseases include dental caries (tooth decay), periodontal disease, and malocclusion; preventive care centres on oral hygiene and fluoride.
