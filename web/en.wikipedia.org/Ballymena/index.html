Ballymena

Ballymena (from Irish Baile Meánach, meaning "the middle townland") is a town in County Antrim, Northern Ireland. It is the administrative centre of the Mid and East Antrim Borough Council and one of the largest towns in the region, with a population of around 30,000 at recent censuses.

History

The area shows evidence of settlement from the early Christian period, including the church site at Connor. The modern town developed after the land was granted to the Adair family in the early 17th century, and it grew as a market town for the surrounding agricultural district. In the 19th and 20th centuries Ballymena became a centre for linen manufacture and later for manufacturing industry.

Economy and transport

The town's economy has included tobacco, tyres and food processing, alongside retail serving a wide rural catchment. Ballymena is connected by rail to Belfast and Derry, and by road via the A26. It has sometimes been described, along with nearby towns, as part of Northern Ireland's "Bible belt" because of its strong Presbyterian and evangelical traditions.
