Lon Nol (Khmer: លន់ នល់; 13 November 1913 – 17 November 1985) was a Cambodian politician and general who led the military coup of March 1970 against Prince Norodom Sihanouk, abolished the monarchy, and headed the pro-American Khmer Republic until its fall to the Khmer Rouge in April 1975.

Career

Lon Nol rose through the colonial administration and the royal army, serving as defence minister and twice as prime minister under Sihanouk, and was known for his anti-communism, anti-Vietnamese nationalism, and deep interest in mysticism and astrology. While Sihanouk was abroad in March 1970, Lon Nol and Sirik Matak staged a coup; the National Assembly deposed Sihanouk, and in October 1970 the Khmer Republic was proclaimed.

Khmer Republic

Lon Nol demanded that North Vietnamese and Viet Cong forces leave Cambodia and, with US support, expanded the army to fight both them and the growing Khmer Rouge insurgency. The war devastated the country: US bombing of the Cambodian countryside, a collapsing economy, and corruption undermined his government, and a stroke in 1971 impaired his health. Although made president in 1972, his power waned as the Khmer Rouge encircled Phnom Penh. On 1 April 1975 he fled into exile; the republic fell sixteen days later.

Later life

Lon Nol settled first in Hawaii and then in California, where he died of heart failure in 1985, maintaining until his death that he would return to liberate Cambodia.
