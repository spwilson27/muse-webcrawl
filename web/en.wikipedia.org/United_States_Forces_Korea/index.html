United States Forces Korea (USFK) is a sub-unified command of United States Indo-Pacific Command. It comprises the U.S. military personnel stationed in South Korea — about 28,500 service members — and supports the defence of the Republic of Korea under the 1953 Mutual Defense Treaty between the two countries.

History

U.S. forces first arrived on the Korean Peninsula in September 1945 to accept the Japanese surrender south of the 38th parallel. After the Korean War (1950–1953), the Mutual Defense Treaty of 1953 committed the United States to the defence of South Korea, and USFK was formally activated on 1 July 1957, replacing the Far East Command's Korea arrangements.

In November 1978, the Combined Forces Command (CFC) was established as the binational warfighting headquarters for the alliance; the USFK commander is triple-hatted as commander of the United Nations Command, the Combined Forces Command, and USFK. In 2018, USFK headquarters relocated from Yongsan Garrison in central Seoul to Camp Humphreys in Pyeongtaek, the largest overseas U.S. military installation, ending a decades-long consolidation plan.

Mission and role

USFK's mission is to deter aggression against South Korea and, if deterrence fails, to defend the peninsula alongside South Korean forces. Its presence is widely regarded as a "tripwire" guaranteeing rapid U.S. involvement in any renewed conflict with North Korea. The force includes:

  • Eighth Army — the ground component, including the 2nd Infantry Division
  • Seventh Air Force — headquartered at Osan Air Base, with F-16 and A-10 aircraft
  • Naval Forces Korea and Marine Forces Korea
  • Special Operations Command Korea (SOCKOR)

Exercises and issues

The alliance conducts large annual exercises such as Ulchi Freedom Shield and Foal Eagle. Burden-sharing of stationing costs is negotiated periodically through the Special Measures Agreement. Long-standing local issues have included land use around bases, environmental remediation of returned sites, and the planned transfer of wartime operational control (OPCON) of South Korean forces to a Korean-led combined command, which remains conditions-based.
