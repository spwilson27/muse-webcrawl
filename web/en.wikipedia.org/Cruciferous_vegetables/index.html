Cruciferous vegetables

Cruciferous vegetables are vegetables of the family Brassicaceae (also called Cruciferae) with many genera, species, and cultivars being raised for food production, such as cauliflower, cabbage, kale, garden cress, bok choy, broccoli, Brussels sprouts, mustard plant and similar green leaf vegetables. The family takes its alternative name, Cruciferae ("cross-bearing"), from the shape of their flowers, whose four petals resemble a cross.

Nutrition and health

Cruciferous vegetables are low in calories and rich in folate, vitamins C, E and K, and fibre. They are notable for glucosinolates, sulfur-containing compounds that break down during digestion into biologically active substances such as indoles and isothiocyanates, including sulforaphane. Laboratory and epidemiological research has investigated these compounds for possible roles in cancer prevention; the World Cancer Research Fund considers the evidence suggestive but not conclusive. Very high intakes of raw crucifers can interfere with iodine uptake because of goitrin, a consideration mainly for people with thyroid disorders and iodine deficiency.

Cultivation and use

Many crucifers derive from a single species, Brassica oleracea, selectively bred into wildly different forms — cabbage (leaves), kale (leaves), broccoli and cauliflower (flower buds), kohlrabi (stem) and Brussels sprouts (axillary buds). They feature in cuisines worldwide, from German sauerkraut to Korean kimchi (made with napa cabbage), and are central to dishes such as Niçoise salads and rustic Italian soups (see Cuisine of Nice).
