The V-1 flying bomb (German: Vergeltungswaffe 1, "Vengeance Weapon 1") was an early cruise missile developed by Nazi Germany during World War II. Known to Londoners as the "buzz bomb" or "doodlebug" for its distinctive pulsejet engine noise, it was the first of the so-called "wonder weapons" used against Britain.

Development and operation

Developed at Peenemünde by the Luftwaffe, the V-1 was a simple, cheap weapon: a steel fuselage with plywood wings, an 850 kg warhead, and an Argus pulsejet engine that burned 75-octane gasoline. Launched from ramps in occupied France and the Netherlands, the missile flew on autopilot for a preset distance; when a small propeller-driven counter indicated the range was reached, the controls locked and the V-1 dived to the ground.

Use and countermeasures

The first V-1s fell on London on 13 June 1944, a week after D-Day. Over 9,500 were launched at England; more than 2,400 reached London. British defences — anti-aircraft guns, barrage balloons, and fast fighters such as the Hawker Tempest that could tip the missile's wing with their own — destroyed a growing proportion, and bombing of launch sites (Operation Crossbow) further reduced the threat. Some 30,000 V-1s were produced in total, including air-launched versions from Heinkel He 111s and later attacks on Antwerp and Liège.

Legacy

The V-1 demonstrated the cruise missile concept and influenced post-war weapons; a piloted version, the Reichenberg, was developed but never used operationally.
