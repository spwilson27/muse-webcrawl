Harpers Ferry is a historic town in Jefferson County, West Virginia, situated at the confluence of the Potomac and Shenandoah rivers, where the US states of West Virginia, Maryland and Virginia meet. It was the northernmost point of Confederate-controlled territory during the American Civil War and is best known for John Brown's raid on the federal armoury in 1859. The lower part of the town lies within Harpers Ferry National Historical Park.

History

Robert Harper established a ferry across the Potomac in 1761, and the town grew around it. In 1799 the United States established one of its two national armouries there, and the town became an industrial centre using water power; the US Marine Corps detachment and rifle works were developed under John H. Hall, a pioneer of interchangeable parts. Thomas Jefferson, visiting in 1783, called the view from Jefferson Rock "worth a voyage across the Atlantic".

John Brown's raid and the Civil War

On 16–18 October 1859, abolitionist John Brown and 21 followers seized the armoury intending to arm enslaved people and start a liberation movement; US Marines under Colonel Robert E. Lee stormed the engine house, and Brown was hanged for treason in December 1859 — an event that deepened the national crisis over slavery. During the Civil War the town changed hands repeatedly, and in September 1862 Stonewall Jackson captured over 12,000 Union troops there, the largest surrender of US forces until Bataan.

Today

Much of the town is preserved by the National Park Service. It is a popular tourist destination on the Appalachian Trail, whose headquarters are located there, and for whitewater rafting on the two rivers. Repeated flooding — most destructively in 1936 — ended its industrial era.
