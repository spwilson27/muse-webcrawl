Itraconazole, sold under the brand name Sporanox among others, is an antifungal medication used to treat a number of fungal infections. These include aspergillosis, blastomycosis, coccidioidomycosis, histoplasmosis, and paracoccidioidomycosis, as well as fungal nail infections (onychomycosis).

Mechanism of action

Itraconazole is a triazole antifungal that inhibits the fungal enzyme lanosterol 14α-demethylase, blocking the synthesis of ergosterol, an essential component of the fungal cell membrane. It has a broader spectrum of activity than earlier azoles such as ketoconazole.

Side effects and interactions

Common side effects include nausea, abdominal pain, and headache. More serious effects can include liver problems and heart failure, and the drug carries a boxed warning in the United States regarding congestive heart failure. Itraconazole is a potent inhibitor of the liver enzyme CYP3A4, so it interacts with many other medications. It was developed by Janssen Pharmaceutica and approved for medical use in the early 1990s; it is on the World Health Organization's List of Essential Medicines.
