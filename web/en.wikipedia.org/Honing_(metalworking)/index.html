Honing (metalworking)

Honing is an abrasive machining process that produces a precision surface on a metal workpiece by scrubbing an abrasive grinding stone against it along a controlled path. Honing is primarily used to improve the geometric form of a surface, but it can also improve the surface finish. It is most commonly applied to the internal cylindrical surfaces of engine cylinders, hydraulic cylinders, and bearing bores.

Process

In a typical honing operation, a honing tool — a mandrel fitted with abrasive stones — is rotated and reciprocated inside the bore while the stones are expanded against the workpiece surface. The simultaneous rotation and stroking produce a characteristic cross-hatch pattern on the bore surface. This cross-hatch pattern is valued in engine cylinders because it retains oil and aids piston ring seating and lubrication.

Honing stones

Honing stones are made of abrasive grains such as silicon carbide, aluminium oxide, or diamond, bonded together. The choice of abrasive, grit size, and bond depends on the workpiece material and the finish required. Diamond and cubic boron nitride ("superabrasives") are used for high-production work and hard materials.

Applications

Honing is used to finish the bores of internal combustion engine cylinders, compressors, pumps, gun barrels, and hydraulic components. It can correct errors of roundness, taper, and size left by boring or grinding, and can achieve very tight tolerances and fine surface finishes. Related processes include lapping and superfinishing.
