The Volkswagen Karmann Ghia is a sports car marketed by Volkswagen from 1955 to 1974 in coupé and convertible body styles. It combined the chassis and mechanicals of the Volkswagen Beetle with styling by Italy's Carrozzeria Ghia and hand-built bodywork by the German coachbuilder Karmann.

Development

As postwar West Germany's economy recovered, Volkswagen sought a halo model. Ghia's Luigi Segre adapted a design originally conceived for Chrysler, and Karmann produced the bodies in Osnabrück. The Type 14 debuted at the Paris Motor Show in 1953 and went on sale in 1955; a cabriolet followed in 1957.

Design and performance

The Karmann Ghia shared the Beetle's air-cooled flat-four engine, growing from 1.2 to 1.6 litres over its life; its appeal lay in its styling rather than speed, with top speeds around 120–140 km/h depending on version. Body panels were butt-welded and hand-finished, giving the car a seamless appearance unusual for its price class.

Production and legacy

More than 445,000 Karmann Ghias were built in Germany, with additional production of the distinct Type 34 and Brazilian variants. The model was succeeded indirectly by the Volkswagen Scirocco in 1974 and is now a prized collector car.
