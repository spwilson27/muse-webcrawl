Carol Reed

Sir Carol Reed
Born: 30 December 1906, London, England
Died: 25 April 1976, London, England
Occupation: Film director
Notable works: The Third Man, Odd Man Out, Oliver!

Sir Carol Reed (30 December 1906 – 25 April 1976) was an English film director and producer, best known for Odd Man Out (1947), The Fallen Idol (1948), and The Third Man (1949), for which he was named best director by the New York Film Critics Circle. He won the Academy Award for Best Director for Oliver! (1968).

Career

The illegitimate son of actor-manager Herbert Beerbohm Tree, Reed began in theatre before moving into film as a dialogue director and assistant to Basil Dean at Ealing. Wartime service produced the acclaimed training film The Way Ahead (1944). His post-war run with writer Graham Greene — The Fallen Idol and The Third Man, both shot with expressive tilted compositions and Robert Krasker's cinematography — established him as Britain's leading director.

Later films

Later successes included Trapeze (1956), The Key (1958), and the musical Oliver!, which won the Academy Award for Best Picture. Reed was knighted in 1952, the first British film director to receive the honour for his work in cinema.
