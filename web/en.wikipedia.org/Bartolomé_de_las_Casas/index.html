Bartolomé de las Casas

Born: 1484 (or 1474), Seville, Spain
Died: 18 July 1566, Madrid, Spain
Known for: Advocacy for indigenous peoples of the Americas

Bartolomé de las Casas (c. 1484 – 18 July 1566) was a Spanish clergyman, theologian, and writer who was the first resident Bishop of Chiapas. Initially a participant in the colonization of the Americas as a soldier and encomendero, he became famous for his fierce advocacy for the rights of the indigenous peoples of the Americas, earning him the title "Defender of the Indians" and later designation as the "Apostle of the Indies".

Life and advocacy

Las Casas arrived in Hispaniola in 1502 and participated in the conquest of Cuba. After hearing a sermon by the Dominican Antonio de Montesinos in 1511 denouncing the abuse of the natives, and undergoing a conversion in 1514, he renounced his encomienda and devoted his life to reform. He repeatedly crossed the Atlantic to lobby the Spanish court, influencing the New Laws of 1542, which restricted the encomienda system. In 1550–1551 he defended the full humanity and rationality of indigenous peoples in the famous Valladolid debate against Juan Ginés de Sepúlveda, who had argued for their natural servitude.

Writings

His A Short Account of the Destruction of the Indies (1552) is a searing catalogue of Spanish atrocities in the Americas; translated widely, it became a foundation of the "Black Legend" of Spanish cruelty. His monumental Historia de las Indias chronicled the early decades of colonization. Controversially, Las Casas early in his advocacy suggested substituting African slave labour for indigenous labour — a position he later deeply repented and denounced.
