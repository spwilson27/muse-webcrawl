Agrippa the Sceptic was a Pyrrhonist philosopher of the Roman era, known to us through Diogenes Laertius and Sextus Empiricus. He is credited with formulating the Five Modes (or Tropes) of scepticism, arguments designed to show that every claim to knowledge can be challenged, leading to suspension of judgment (epoché).

The Five Modes

The modes are: (1) disagreement — undecided disputes among philosophers and ordinary people; (2) infinite regress — every proof requires a further proof; (3) relativity — things appear differently depending on the observer and circumstances; (4) hypothesis — dogmatists ultimately rest on unproven assumptions; and (5) circularity — the proof presupposes what it is meant to establish.

Agrippa's trilemma

From the modes of regress, hypothesis, and circularity derives the modern formulation known as Agrippa's trilemma (also called the Münchhausen trilemma): any attempt to justify a belief must end in an infinite regress, a circle, or an unjustified stopping point.
