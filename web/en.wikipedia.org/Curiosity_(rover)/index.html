Curiosity is a car-sized Mars rover exploring Gale Crater on Mars as part of NASA's Mars Science Laboratory (MSL) mission. Curiosity was launched from Cape Canaveral on 26 November 2011 aboard an Atlas V rocket and landed on Aeolis Palus inside Gale Crater on 6 August 2012 (UTC). The Bradbury Landing site, named for author Ray Bradbury, lies less than 2.4 km from the intended touchdown point after a journey of about 560 million kilometres.

The rover's goals include investigating the Martian climate and geology, assessing whether Gale Crater ever offered environmental conditions favourable for microbial life, and preparing for human exploration. Its landing used a novel sky-crane system, in which a rocket-powered descent stage lowered the rover to the surface on cables — a method required because Curiosity was too heavy for airbag landing.

Instruments

Curiosity carries a 2.1-metre robotic arm with a drill and scoop, the MastCam imaging system, the ChemCam laser-induced breakdown spectrometer (the first laser instrument used on another planet), the Sample Analysis at Mars (SAM) instrument suite for organic chemistry and atmospheric analysis, the CheMin X-ray diffraction instrument, the RAD radiation detector, and the DAN neutron detector for subsurface hydrogen. Total science payload mass is about 75 kg, roughly ten times that of the earlier Spirit and Opportunity rovers.

Findings

Early in the mission, Curiosity found rounded pebbles in a conglomerate rock, evidence of an ancient streambed. At Yellowknife Bay it analysed mudstone that showed a past lake environment with the chemical ingredients and neutral pH suitable for life, and SAM detected organic molecules preserved in the rocks. In 2018, scientists reported both the detection of complex organic matter in 3-billion-year-old sediments and seasonal variation in atmospheric methane. The rover has since climbed the foothills of Aeolis Mons (Mount Sharp), traversing clay-bearing and sulfate-bearing units that record Mars's transition to a drier climate, despite progressive wheel damage from sharp rocks.
