Cholula massacre

Date18 October 1519
LocationCholula, present-day Puebla, Mexico
AttackersSpanish forces of Hernán Cortés and Tlaxcalan allies
DeathsEstimated several thousand Cholultecs

The Cholula massacre was an attack carried out on 18 October 1519 by the forces of the Spanish conquistador Hernán Cortés and his Tlaxcalan allies against the city of Cholula, one of the largest and most sacred cities of Mesoamerica, during the Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire.

Background

Marching toward the Aztec capital Tenochtitlan, Cortés entered Cholula, a city famed for its great pyramid and temple of Quetzalcoatl and nominally allied to the Aztecs. According to Spanish accounts, Cortés was warned — by his interpreter Malinche and by Tlaxcalan scouts — of a planned ambush by Cholultec and Aztec forces.

The massacre

Cortés invited the city's nobles to assemble unarmed in a courtyard, where Spanish soldiers and Tlaxcalan warriors attacked them. The killing spread through the city over several days; contemporary estimates claimed thousands of Cholultecs were killed, and parts of the city were burned and looted.

Interpretations

Historians debate whether an ambush plot actually existed or whether the massacre was a pre-emptive terror tactic designed to intimidate the Aztecs and secure the Spanish rear. The event remains a notorious episode of the conquest and a fixture of Mexican historical memory.
