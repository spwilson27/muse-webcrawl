Cinema of the United States

The cinema of the United States, consisting mainly of major film studios (also known metonymously as Hollywood) along with independent films, has had a large effect on the global film industry since the early 20th century. The dominant style of American cinema is classical Hollywood cinema, which developed from 1910 to the 1960s and is still typical of most films made there today.

History

American film production began on the East Coast in the 1890s, centred on Thomas Edison's studios in New Jersey and the Biograph company in New York. Seeking distance from Edison's patent trust and seeking year-round sunshine, filmmakers migrated to Southern California; by the 1910s and 1920s the district of Hollywood in Los Angeles had become the industry's home. The studio system of the 1930s and 1940s — the "Golden Age" — was dominated by the major studios: MGM, Paramount, Warner Bros., 20th Century Fox, and RKO, with the "Little Three" of Columbia, United Artists, and Universal.

The 1948 Supreme Court decision in United States v. Paramount Pictures, Inc. ended the studios' control of exhibition and weakened the system. The 1970s brought "New Hollywood" directors such as Martin Scorsese, Francis Ford Coppola, and Steven Spielberg, and the success of Jaws (1975) and Star Wars (1977) inaugurated the modern blockbuster era of high-concept, franchise-driven production.

Influence

While Frenchmen Auguste and Louis Lumière are generally credited with the birth of modern cinema, American cinema soon came to be a dominant force in the emerging industry. It produced the first film to use a narrative format, The Great Train Robbery (1903), and has since shaped global popular culture through its genres — the western, the musical, film noir, the superhero film — and its star system.

Industry today

The United States remains one of the world's largest film markets by box-office revenue. Major studios are now divisions of media conglomerates, and production is concentrated in California, New York, Georgia, and other states offering incentives. Independent cinema persists alongside the majors, showcased at festivals such as Sundance, while streaming services have become major producers and distributors since the 2010s.
