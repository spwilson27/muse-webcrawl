Verkhoyansk (Russian: Верхоянск) is a small town in the Sakha Republic, Russia, located on the Yana River just north of the Arctic Circle. It is famous as one of the coldest permanently inhabited places on Earth — one of the two rival "Poles of Cold" of the Northern Hemisphere, along with Oymyakon.

Verkhoyansk during the polar winter.

Climate

Verkhoyansk has an extreme subarctic climate with enormous annual temperature range. It recorded −67.8 °C in 1892 (long tied as the Northern Hemisphere record low for an inhabited place), yet summer days can exceed 30 °C — in June 2020 it reached 38.0 °C (100.4 °F), the highest temperature reliably recorded north of the Arctic Circle and a landmark Arctic heatwave event. Winters are long, dark, and dry, with temperature inversions trapping frigid air in the valley.

History

Founded as a Cossack winter settlement in 1638, Verkhoyansk served as a centre of the fur trade and a place of political exile under both tsars and Soviets. Its weather station, operating since the 19th century, makes it a key site for long-term climate records in the Siberian Arctic.
