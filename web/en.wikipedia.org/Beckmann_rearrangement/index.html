Beckmann rearrangement

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Beckmann rearrangement is an organic reaction in which an oxime is converted into an amide under acidic conditions. It was discovered in 1886 by the German chemist Ernst Otto Beckmann.

Mechanism

Protonation (or other activation) of the oxime hydroxyl group turns it into a good leaving group. The group anti-periplanar to the leaving group migrates from carbon to nitrogen as water departs, forming a nitrilium ion, which is hydrolysed to the amide. The migration is stereospecific: the group trans to the hydroxyl migrates preferentially, so the geometry of the oxime determines the amide product.

Applications

The rearrangement of cyclohexanone oxime to ε-caprolactam, the monomer for nylon-6, is performed on a scale of millions of tonnes per year and is the reaction's most important industrial application. Beckmann fragmentation, a competing pathway for certain oximes, yields nitriles instead of amides.
