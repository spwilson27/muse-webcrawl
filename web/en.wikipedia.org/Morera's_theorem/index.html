Morera's theorem

In complex analysis, Morera's theorem, named after the Italian mathematician Giacinto Morera, gives a powerful test for holomorphicity: if a continuous complex-valued function f defined on an open set D in the complex plane satisfies ∮∂T f(z) dz = 0 for every closed triangular region T contained in D, then f is holomorphic on D — that is, complex differentiable at every point.

Vanishing integrals over all triangles imply holomorphicity.

Significance

The theorem is a partial converse of the Cauchy–Goursat theorem, which says that holomorphic functions have vanishing integrals over closed contours. Together they show that holomorphicity can be characterised purely in terms of contour integrals. Morera's theorem is frequently used to prove that functions defined by integrals or by uniform limits are holomorphic.

Proof idea and applications

The proof constructs a local primitive: fixing a point, define F as the integral of f along a path; the vanishing triangle condition guarantees this is well-defined, and F is shown to be holomorphic with derivative f. Since derivatives of holomorphic functions are themselves holomorphic, f is holomorphic. Standard applications include showing that uniform limits of holomorphic functions are holomorphic, justifying differentiation under the integral sign (for example for the Riemann zeta function and the Laplace transform), and proving holomorphicity of the Gamma function.
