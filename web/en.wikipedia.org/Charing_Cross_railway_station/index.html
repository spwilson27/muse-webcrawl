Charing Cross railway station (also known as London Charing Cross) is a central London railway terminus between the Strand and Hungerford Bridge, in the City of Westminster. It is one of 20 stations managed by Network Rail and is the southern terminus of services over the South Eastern Main Line to Kent and East Sussex, operated by Southeastern.

History

The station opened in 1864 for the South Eastern Railway, on the site of the former Hungerford Market, with a single-span arched trainshed crossing to Hungerford Bridge over the Thames. In 1905 part of the roof collapsed, killing six people; the station was rebuilt with a new roof. The facade on the Strand, in French Renaissance style, fronts the former Charing Cross Hotel. In the 1980s the office complex known as Embankment Place was built above the platforms on a raft of concrete.

Charing Cross is the notional centre of London: distances to the capital are measured from the replica Eleanor cross in the station forecourt, near the site of the original cross erected by Edward I.

Underground connections

The station is served by Charing Cross tube station on the Northern and Bakerloo lines, and Embankment station is a short walk away.
