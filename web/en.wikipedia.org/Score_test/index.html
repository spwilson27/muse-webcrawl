Score test

In statistics, the score test is a hypothesis test based on the gradient (score) of the likelihood function. It is one of the three classical approaches to hypothesis testing, alongside the likelihood-ratio test and the Wald test. It is also known as the Lagrange multiplier test in econometrics.

Definition

The score test evaluates the slope of the log-likelihood function at the parameter value specified by the null hypothesis. If the null hypothesis is true, the score should be close to zero because the constrained estimate is near the maximum of the likelihood. The test statistic is formed from the score vector and the Fisher information matrix, and under the null it follows a chi-squared distribution.

Advantages

A key advantage is that the score test requires estimation only under the null hypothesis, which can make it computationally simpler than the Wald or likelihood-ratio tests when the unrestricted model is hard to fit. It is widely used in generalised linear models and in tests for omitted variables and model specification.

History

The test was developed by C. R. Rao in 1948 and introduced into econometrics as the Lagrange multiplier test by Aitchison and Silvey, and later Breusch and Pagan.
