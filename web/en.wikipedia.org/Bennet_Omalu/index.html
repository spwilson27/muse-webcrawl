Bennet Omalu

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Bennet Ifeakandu Omalu (born September 30, 1968) is a Nigerian-American physician, forensic pathologist and neuropathologist who was the first to discover and publish findings on chronic traumatic encephalopathy (CTE) in American football players while working at the Allegheny County coroner's office in Pittsburgh.

Early life and education

Omalu was born in Nnokwa, Idemili South, Anambra State, Nigeria, during the Nigerian Civil War. He began medical school at the age of 16 at the University of Nigeria, Nsukka, and received his medical degree in 1990. After clinical training, he came to the United States in 1994, completing a residency in anatomical and clinical pathology at Harlem Hospital Center and a fellowship in neuropathology at the University of Pittsburgh.

CTE research

In 2002, Omalu performed the autopsy on former Pittsburgh Steelers center Mike Webster, who had died suddenly after years of cognitive and behavioral decline. Examining Webster's brain, Omalu found abnormal accumulations of tau protein resembling those seen in Alzheimer's disease, and in 2005 he published his findings in the journal Neurosurgery, naming the condition chronic traumatic encephalopathy. His work, initially resisted by the National Football League, was later vindicated by further studies and became the subject of the 2015 film Concussion, in which he was portrayed by Will Smith.

Omalu later served as chief medical examiner of San Joaquin County, California, and founded the Bennet Omalu Foundation to advance CTE research and awareness.
