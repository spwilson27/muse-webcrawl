Legalization (document)

Legalization (or legalisation) is the process of certifying a document so that it will be officially recognised by the legal system of a foreign country. Typically it certifies the authenticity of the signature of the official who signed the document, the capacity in which that person acted, and the identity of any seal or stamp on the document. Legalization does not certify the content of the document itself.

Chain legalization

Traditionally, legalization was performed through a chain of certifications: a local notary or authority, then a regional or national authority, and finally the embassy or consulate of the destination country. Each step attests to the authenticity of the previous official's signature. The process applies to documents such as birth and marriage certificates, powers of attorney, court orders, academic diplomas, and commercial documents used abroad.

Apostille

For countries that are party to the Hague Convention of 5 October 1961 Abolishing the Requirement of Legalisation for Foreign Public Documents, the lengthy chain is replaced by a single certificate called an apostille, issued by a designated competent authority in the country where the document originates. Apostilles are recognised by all other convention parties. Where the destination country is not a party, consular legalization through the embassy chain remains necessary.
