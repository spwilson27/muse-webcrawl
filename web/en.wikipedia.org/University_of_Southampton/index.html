The University of Southampton is a public research university in Southampton, England. Founded in 1862 as the Hartley Institution, it received its royal charter as a university in 1952. It is a founding member of the Russell Group of research-intensive British universities and is consistently ranked among the top universities in the United Kingdom.

Academics and research

The university enrolls roughly 23,000 students across five faculties. It is internationally prominent in electronics and computer science — its School of Electronics and Computer Science is one of the largest in Europe, and Tim Berners-Lee, inventor of the World Wide Web, held a chair there. Southampton is a world centre for oceanography, hosting the National Oceanography Centre on its Waterfront Campus, and for optoelectronics, where its Optoelectronics Research Centre pioneered the erbium-doped fibre amplifier that underpins global internet traffic. Engineering, medicine, and maritime studies are other strengths.

Campuses

The main Highfield Campus is a green, suburban site north of the city centre. Other sites include the Avenue Campus (humanities), Boldrewood Innovation Campus (engineering and maritime studies, shared with Lloyd's Register), the Waterfront Campus at the National Oceanography Centre, and a campus in Winchester for the Winchester School of Art.

Notable alumni

Alumni include broadcaster and naturalist Chris Packham, astronaut Tim Peake — who graduated in flight dynamics — and former Bank of England governor figures in economics and finance.
