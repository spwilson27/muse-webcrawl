AIM-54 Phoenix

TypeLong-range air-to-air missile
Service1974–2004 (US Navy)
PlatformGrumman F-14 Tomcat
RangeOver 190 km (100 nmi)
SpeedMach 5
GuidanceSemi-active radar midcourse, active radar terminal

The AIM-54 Phoenix was an American long-range air-to-air missile carried exclusively by the F-14 Tomcat. Developed by Hughes Aircraft from the earlier AIM-47 Falcon programme, it entered service in 1974 as the US Navy's primary fleet air-defence weapon, intended to destroy Soviet bombers and their anti-ship cruise missiles at extreme range.

System

Working with the Tomcat's powerful AWG-9 radar, the Phoenix could be launched against up to six targets simultaneously in track-while-scan mode. Weighing about 460 kg (1,000 lb), the missile flew a lofted trajectory to altitudes above 24 km (80,000 ft) and reached speeds of Mach 5, using semi-active radar guidance for most of its flight and its own active seeker for the terminal phase. Its warhead weighed 61 kg (135 lb).

Combat use

The US Navy fired Phoenix missiles in combat only rarely, in 1999 engagements over Iraq with no confirmed kills. Iran, the only export customer, claimed numerous successes with the missile during the Iran–Iraq War with its own F-14 fleet. The Phoenix was retired from US service in 2004, two years before the Tomcat itself.
