Vedanga

The Vedangas (Sanskrit: वेदाङ्ग vedāṅga, "limbs of the Veda") are six auxiliary disciplines of Hinduism that developed to support the study, correct recitation and ritual use of the Vedas. Composed mainly in the terse sutra style in the late Vedic and post-Vedic periods, they are traditionally enumerated as limbs of the Veda personified.

The six disciplines

Shiksha (phonetics and pronunciation), Chandas (poetic metre, associated with Pingala's work on prosody and combinatorics), Vyakarana (grammar, crowned by Panini's Ashtadhyayi), Nirukta (etymology, Yaska's glossary of obscure Vedic words), Kalpa (ritual instructions, including the Shrauta, Grihya, Dharma and Shulba sutras) and Jyotisha (astronomy and astrology for fixing the calendar and ritual times).

Significance

The Vedangas mark the transition from Vedic revelation (shruti) to remembered tradition (smriti) and seeded later Indian scholarship: Panini's grammar is a landmark of linguistics, the Shulba sutras contain early geometry, and Pingala's prosody anticipated binary enumeration.
