Downtown Los Angeles

The downtown Los Angeles skyline.

Downtown Los Angeles (DTLA) is the central business district of Los Angeles, California, and a diverse residential neighbourhood of roughly 85,000 people. It contains the city's government centre, historic theatre district, financial skyscrapers and the Fashion, Arts, Jewelry and Flower districts.

History

The area grew from the Spanish pueblo founded in 1781 near present-day Olvera Street. By the early 20th century, Broadway and Spring Street formed a thriving commercial and entertainment core. After decades of decline as business moved west, an adaptive-reuse ordinance in 1999 spurred conversion of historic office buildings into lofts, bringing residents back and driving a prolonged revival.

Landmarks

Notable sites include the Bradbury Building, the Walt Disney Concert Hall, Crypto.com Arena, the historic core's movie palaces, and Grand Central Market. The skyline's tallest building west of the Mississippi, the Wilshire Grand Center, opened in 2017.
