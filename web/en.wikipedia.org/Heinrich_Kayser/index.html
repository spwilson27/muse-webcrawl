Heinrich Gustav Johannes Kayser (16 March 1853 – 14 October 1940) was a German physicist known for his pioneering work in spectroscopy.

Career and research

Kayser studied in Strasbourg and Berlin, taking his doctorate in 1879 with work on the properties of sound waves. After a period in Hannover he became the first head of the physics section of the newly founded Physikalisch-Technische Reichsanstalt in Berlin in 1885, and in 1894 he moved to the University of Bonn, where he built a major spectroscopy institute and remained until his retirement in 1920.

Spectroscopy

With Carl Runge, Kayser undertook systematic photographic measurements of the spectra of the chemical elements, work that fed into their great Handbuch der Spectroscopie and detailed spectral atlases. Their series analyses helped reveal regularities in atomic spectra — including for helium and the alkaline earth metals — that were part of the empirical background to the Bohr model of the atom. Kayser also studied the absorption of ultraviolet light by the atmosphere and the spectra of the sun and stars. The CGS unit of wavenumber, the kayser (cm−1), is named after him.
