Ra

Ra (also spelled Re) is the ancient Egyptian deity of the sun and one of the most important gods in the Egyptian pantheon. He was believed to travel across the sky each day in his solar barque and through the underworld (Duat) at night, to be reborn each dawn.

Iconography and cult

Ra is usually depicted as a falcon-headed man crowned with a sun disk encircled by the uraeus serpent. His principal cult center was Heliopolis. By the Fifth Dynasty (c. 25th century BCE) he had become the pre-eminent state god, and pharaohs styled themselves "Son of Ra." He was frequently combined with other gods, as Amun-Ra in the New Kingdom and Ra-Horakhty ("Ra-Horus of the Two Horizons").

Mythology

In myth, Ra created the world and its gods; humans were said to have sprung from his tears. Nightly, his barque battled the serpent Apep (Apophis), embodiment of chaos, assisted by other gods including Set. The aging Ra was tricked by Isis, who learned his secret name and gained magical power.
