Condor Legion

The Condor Legion (German: Legion Condor; Spanish: Legión Cóndor) was a unit of the German Luftwaffe and Wehrmacht sent to support General Francisco Franco's Nationalist forces during the Spanish Civil War. Operating from 1936 to 1939, it gave Germany an opportunity to test aircraft, tactics and personnel in combat, experience that proved significant in the early campaigns of the Second World War.

Formation and operations

German involvement began in July 1936, when transport aircraft airlifted Franco's Army of Africa from Spanish Morocco to mainland Spain. By November 1936 the expanded force was formally organised as the Condor Legion under Hugo Sperrle, with Wolfram von Richthofen as chief of staff. It included fighter, bomber and reconnaissance squadrons, anti-aircraft artillery, signals and a tank training detachment (Gruppe Drohne) with Panzer I tanks. Aircraft such as the Bf 109 fighter, He 111 bomber and Ju 87 Stuka made their combat debuts in Spain.

Guernica

The Legion is most notorious for the bombing of Guernica on 26 April 1937, when German (and Italian) aircraft attacked the Basque town on market day, causing heavy civilian casualties and destroying much of the town. The raid became an international symbol of the horror of aerial bombardment, immortalised in Picasso's painting Guernica.

Legacy

Roughly 19,000 German personnel rotated through Spain; about 300 were killed. The Legion returned home in 1939 and was honoured with a parade in Berlin. Its veterans and lessons — close air support, fighter tactics and the operational use of air power — fed directly into the Luftwaffe of 1939–41, and the unit's role remained politically contentious in Germany and Spain for decades.
