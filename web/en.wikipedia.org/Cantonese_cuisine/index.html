Cantonese cuisine

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Cantonese or Yue cuisine is the cuisine of Guangdong province of China, particularly the provincial capital Guangzhou, and of the surrounding regions in the Pearl River Delta including Hong Kong and Macau. It is one of the Eight Culinary Traditions of Chinese cuisine and the style most familiar abroad, owing to the large number of Cantonese emigrants.

Characteristics

Cantonese cooking emphasises fresh ingredients, mild and natural flavours, and light seasoning that enhances rather than masks the taste of the primary ingredient. Steaming and stir-frying are favoured techniques, and sauces tend to be delicate.

Notable dishes

Well-known Cantonese dishes include dim sum (small dishes served with tea, such as har gow shrimp dumplings and siu mai), char siu (barbecued pork), wonton noodles, white cut chicken, roast goose, congee, and a wide variety of slow-cooked soups. Cantonese restaurants traditionally serve dim sum at lunchtime, accompanied by tea in the custom known as yum cha.

Influence

Through emigration to Southeast Asia, the Americas, and Europe, Cantonese cuisine became the dominant form of Chinese food in many Chinatowns worldwide and strongly influenced dishes such as chop suey and sweet and sour pork in Western Chinese restaurants.
