Ratio

In mathematics, a ratio shows how many times one number contains another. For example, if there are eight oranges and six lemons in a bowl of fruit, then the ratio of oranges to lemons is 8:6 (which is equivalent to 4:3). Ratios are written as "a to b" or a:b, or expressed as a fraction a/b. When two quantities have the same units, their ratio is a dimensionless number; ratios of quantities with different units give a rate.

Notation and terminology

The ratio of A to B is written A:B, A/B, or "A to B". The numbers in a ratio are called its terms; the first is the antecedent and the second the consequent. Ratios can be simplified by dividing both terms by their greatest common divisor, just as with fractions. A ratio of three or more quantities, such as a:b:c, is called a continued ratio.

Equivalent ratios and proportions

Two ratios are equivalent if they simplify to the same value. An equation stating that two ratios are equal, a/b = c/d, is a proportion. Proportions are central to similar triangles, scaling in maps and models, unit conversion, and the rule of three. A special case is the golden ratio, φ ≈ 1.618, defined by (a+b)/a = a/b.

Applications

Ratios appear throughout science and daily life: financial ratios such as price-to-earnings, gear ratios in machinery, aspect ratios of screens and images, concentration and dilution ratios in chemistry, odds in probability, and demographic measures such as sex ratios. In music, simple whole-number ratios of frequencies define consonant intervals—2:1 the octave, 3:2 the perfect fifth, 4:3 the perfect fourth.
