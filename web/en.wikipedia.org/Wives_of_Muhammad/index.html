Wives of Muhammad

The Islamic prophet Muhammad was married to a number of women during his lifetime; Muslim tradition counts eleven wives, who are honoured in Islam with the title Mothers of the Believers (Arabic: Ummahāt al-Muʾminīn), a phrase drawn from the Quran. Two of them, Khadija and Aisha, are especially prominent in Islamic history and theology.

Khadija

Muhammad's first wife was Khadija bint Khuwaylid, a successful Meccan merchant who employed him and proposed marriage around 595 CE, when he was about 25. Their marriage was monogamous and lasted about 25 years, until her death in 619, the "Year of Sorrow". Khadija was the first person to believe in Muhammad's prophethood, and all of his surviving children were born to her, including Fatima.

Later marriages

After Khadija's death, Muhammad married several women, among them Sawda bint Zam'a; Aisha bint Abi Bakr, daughter of his close companion Abu Bakr, who became a major transmitter of hadith and a figure of authority in early Islam; Hafsa bint Umar; Zaynab bint Khuzayma; Umm Salama; Zaynab bint Jahsh; Juwayriya bint al-Harith; Umm Habiba; Safiyya bint Huyayy; and Maymunah bint al-Harith. Scholars discuss these marriages in the context of 7th-century Arabian norms, alliances between tribes and clans, and care for widows of fallen followers.

Significance

Muhammad's wives are important sources of hadith and of information about his private life, and their conduct is treated in Islamic law and devotion as exemplary. After Muhammad's death in 632 they were forbidden to remarry, per Quranic injunction, and most lived in Medina, where Aisha in particular remained an influential teacher for decades.
