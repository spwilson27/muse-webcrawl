Mariana and Palau Islands campaign

Mariana and Palau Islands campaign
Date: June – November 1944
Location: Mariana and Palau Islands, Pacific
Result: Allied victory
Principal operations: Saipan, Guam, Tinian, Peleliu, Angaur

The Mariana and Palau Islands campaign, codenamed Operation Forager, was an offensive launched by United States forces against Imperial Japanese positions in the Mariana Islands and Palau between June and November 1944, during the Pacific War. It formed a key stage of the Allied island-hopping advance toward Japan.

Operations

The campaign opened with landings on Saipan (15 June 1944), accompanied by the great carrier battle of the Philippine Sea, the "Great Marianas Turkey Shoot," which destroyed Japanese naval air power. Guam was invaded on 21 July and Tinian on 24 July; Tinian's airfields were rapidly developed into bases for B-29 Superfortress bombers within range of the Japanese home islands. In September, forces landed on Peleliu and Angaur in the Palaus to protect the flank of the advance on the Philippines — Peleliu proved unexpectedly costly, with the Japanese fighting from fortified caves in a battle lasting over two months.

Significance

Loss of the Marianas breached Japan's inner defence zone, precipitated the fall of the Tōjō government in Tokyo, and gave the United States the bases from which the strategic bombing campaign against Japan, culminating in the atomic bomb missions flown from Tinian, was conducted.
