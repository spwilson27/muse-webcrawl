Three-letter acronym

A three-letter acronym (TLA) is an abbreviation consisting of three letters. Many TLAs are initialisms, formed from the first letters of each word in a phrase. TLAs are particularly common in computing, business, government, and other technical fields where long names are used frequently.

Usage

Three-letter acronyms are widespread in modern English and other languages. Examples include:

  • CPU – Central Processing Unit
  • USA – United States of America
  • BBC – British Broadcasting Corporation
  • ATM – Automated Teller Machine
  • DNA – Deoxyribonucleic Acid
  • CEO – Chief Executive Officer
  • SMS – Short Message Service

Self-reference and humor

The term "TLA" is itself a three-letter acronym, a property that has attracted humorous attention in hacker culture. The abbreviation TLA is sometimes jokingly described as an example of recursive naming, in the spirit of the GNU acronym. Extended three-letter acronyms (ETLAs) add a fourth letter, and the pattern is occasionally extended further for comic effect.

Airport codes

A particularly systematic use of three-letter codes is the IATA airport code system, which assigns three-letter identifiers to airports worldwide, such as LAX for Los Angeles International Airport and JFK for John F. Kennedy International Airport. Similar three-letter codes are used in railway station codes, currency codes (in extended form), and stock exchange ticker symbols.

Criticism

Heavy reliance on TLAs can make documents difficult for outsiders to understand, since the same three letters can stand for many different phrases in different contexts. Style guides commonly recommend spelling out an acronym at first use. The ambiguity of TLAs is a frequent subject of complaints in technical writing and organizational communication.
