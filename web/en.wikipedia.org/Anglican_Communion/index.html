Anglican Communion

The Anglican Communion is the third largest Christian communion in the world, after the Roman Catholic Church and the Eastern Orthodox Church. Founded in 1867 in London, it is an international association of national and regional churches in full communion with the Church of England and specifically with its principal primate, the Archbishop of Canterbury. The communion counts tens of millions of members in more than 165 countries.

History

The communion traces its roots to the Church of England's break with Rome under Henry VIII in the 16th century and the subsequent spread of Anglican churches through British colonisation and missionary work. The first Lambeth Conference of bishops, convened in 1867 by Archbishop Charles Longley, marked the emergence of a self-conscious worldwide fellowship. In the 19th and 20th centuries, provinces became autonomous, governed by their own synods and primates.

Structure

The communion has no central legislative authority. Its unity is expressed through four "instruments of communion": the Archbishop of Canterbury, the Lambeth Conference (held roughly every ten years), the Anglican Consultative Council, and the Primates' Meeting. Each member church, or province, is autonomous, typically led by a primate (archbishop, presiding bishop, or moderator) and governed synodically by bishops, clergy, and laity.

Theology and worship

Anglican identity is rooted in the Bible, the historic creeds, the sacraments of Baptism and the Eucharist, and the historic episcopate. The Book of Common Prayer (1662 and its local successors) has shaped Anglican liturgy worldwide. Anglicanism is often described as a via media between Catholicism and Protestantism, encompassing a broad spectrum from Anglo-Catholic to evangelical traditions.

Contemporary issues

Since the late 20th century the communion has debated the ordination of women (now permitted in most provinces), the consecration of openly gay bishops, and same-sex blessings, producing tensions and realignments such as the GAFCON movement and the Anglican Church in North America.
