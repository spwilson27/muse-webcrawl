Structural stability
Field: Dynamical systems
Key figures: Andronov, Pontryagin, Smale

In mathematics, structural stability is a property of a dynamical system whose qualitative behaviour is unchanged under small perturbations: a slightly perturbed system is topologically equivalent to the original, with orbits mapped to orbits by a homeomorphism.

History

The concept was introduced by Aleksandr Andronov and Lev Pontryagin in 1937. Stephen Smale and others developed the theory for diffeomorphisms, showing that hyperbolic systems are structurally stable, while many systems — especially in higher dimensions — are not.

Significance

Structural stability matters because mathematical models are approximations: conclusions drawn from a model are trustworthy only if small modelling errors do not change its predicted behaviour qualitatively. The concept underlies parts of bifurcation and chaos theory.
