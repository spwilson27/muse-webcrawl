The Port of Veracruz (Spanish: Puerto de Veracruz) is a port in the city of Veracruz on the Gulf of Mexico coast of Mexico. It is the country's oldest and historically most important port, and one of its largest, handling a major share of Mexico's foreign trade, particularly automobiles, containers, grains, and general cargo.

History

Veracruz was founded in 1519 by Hernán Cortés as Villa Rica de la Vera Cruz, the first Spanish municipality on the mainland of the Americas. During the colonial era the port was New Spain's principal Atlantic gateway, receiving the fleets carrying silver and goods between Spain and its colonies. The fortress of San Juan de Ulúa, built on an island offshore, guarded the harbour. The port was attacked or occupied several times, including during the Pastry War (1838), the Mexican–American War (1847), and the United States occupation of Veracruz in 1914.

The modern port works — breakwaters, docks, and warehouses — were constructed under President Porfirio Díaz and inaugurated in 1902. A major expansion in the Bahía de Vergara, north of the historic harbour, began in the 2010s to add deep-water container capacity.

Operations

The port is administered by the Administración del Sistema Portuario Nacional Veracruz. It is Mexico's leading port for vehicle imports and exports and a principal entry point for agricultural bulk cargo destined for central Mexico, connected by rail and highway to Mexico City and the industrial regions of Puebla and the Bajío.
