Fastnet Rock and its lighthouse, off the south-west coast of Ireland.

Fastnet Rock (Irish: Carraig Aonair, meaning "lonely rock") is a small islet in the Atlantic Ocean, about 11 kilometres off the coast of County Cork, Ireland, and the most southerly point of Ireland. It rises about 30 metres above low water and is split into two parts by a narrow channel. The rock is famous for the Fastnet Lighthouse built upon it and as the turning mark of the Fastnet Race, one of the world's best-known offshore yacht races.

The lighthouse

The first lighthouse on Fastnet Rock, a cast-iron tower, entered service in 1854 but proved too weak for the Atlantic storms; a section of the tower was found to have been shaken loose, and in 1891 a decision was made to replace it. The present granite lighthouse, designed by William Douglass and built from interlocking Cornish granite blocks, was completed in 1904 and rises 54 metres above the rock. It was the last lighthouse in Ireland to be automated, with the keepers withdrawn in 1989. For generations of emigrants leaving Ireland by ship, the lighthouse was their last sight of home, earning it the nickname "Ireland's Teardrop".

The Fastnet Race

The Fastnet Race, organised by the Royal Ocean Racing Club since 1925, traditionally ran from Cowes on the Isle of Wight around Fastnet Rock and back to Plymouth, a course of about 605 nautical miles; in recent editions the finish has moved to Cherbourg, France. The 1979 race was struck by a severe storm in which fifteen sailors died, one of the worst disasters in ocean racing history, prompting major reforms in safety equipment and race organisation.

Geography

The rock consists of steeply dipping slate and is divided into Fastnet Rock proper and the smaller Little Fastnet. It lies about 6.5 kilometres south-west of Cape Clear Island and is a landmark for shipping approaching the English Channel from the Atlantic.
