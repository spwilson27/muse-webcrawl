Euphemism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A euphemism is an innocuous word or expression used in place of one that is deemed offensive or suggests something unpleasant. Some euphemisms are intended to amuse, while others use bland, inoffensive terms for concepts the user wishes to downplay, or use technical jargon as a way to mask meaning.

The word euphemism comes from the Greek euphēmismos, from eu ("good, well") and phēmē ("speech, report"). The opposite of a euphemism is a dysphemism.

Types and examples

Common areas where euphemisms are used include death ("passed away" for died), bodily functions ("powder one's nose"), sex, excretory functions, employment ("let go" for fired), and war ("collateral damage" for civilian casualties). Euphemisms may be used to soften the impact of a topic, to be polite in mixed company, or to comply with social or religious taboos.

Over time, euphemisms themselves can acquire the negative connotations of the words they replaced, leading to replacement by new euphemisms — a process linguists call the euphemism treadmill, a term coined by Steven Pinker.

Euphemisms in politics and institutions

Governments and organizations frequently employ euphemisms to describe controversial actions: "enhanced interrogation" for torture, "ethnic cleansing" for forced displacement and massacre, and "downsizing" or "rightsizing" for mass layoffs. Critics, notably George Orwell in his essay "Politics and the English Language", argued that such language is designed to make the unpleasant seem acceptable and to obscure responsibility.
