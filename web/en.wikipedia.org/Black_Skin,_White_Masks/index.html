Black Skin, White Masks

AuthorFrantz Fanon
Original titlePeau noire, masques blancs
Published1952
SubjectColonialism, race, psychoanalysis

Black Skin, White Masks (French: Peau noire, masques blancs) is a book by the Martinican psychiatrist and philosopher Frantz Fanon, first published in 1952. Written when Fanon was 27, it applies psychoanalysis and existentialism to the psychological effects of colonialism and racism on both the colonized and the colonizer.

Contents

Fanon argues that black people under colonial rule internalize an inferiority complex, seeking to adopt the language and culture of the white colonizer — the "white mask" — in a futile bid for recognition. Chapters address language, interracial relationships, the "fact of blackness" as lived experience, and criticisms of Octave Mannoni's psychology of colonization.

Influence

Initially little noticed, the book became a foundational text of postcolonial studies and critical race theory after Fanon's later The Wretched of the Earth (1961) made him famous. Its account of racialized looking and alienation influenced generations of writers, artists, and activists.
