Planar graph

In graph theory, a planar graph is a graph that can be embedded in the plane, that is, drawn on a flat surface so that no edges cross each other. Such a drawing is called a planar embedding or planar map.

Characterisation

Kuratowski's theorem states that a finite graph is planar if and only if it contains no subgraph that is a subdivision of K5 (the complete graph on five vertices) or of K3,3 (the complete bipartite graph on six vertices, three of which connect to each of the other three). Wagner's theorem gives an equivalent characterisation in terms of graph minors.

Formulas and properties

For a simple connected planar graph with v ≥ 3 vertices, the number of edges satisfies e ≤ 3v − 6, and Euler's formula gives v − e + f = 2, where f is the number of faces. Every planar graph is four-colourable, as proved by the four-colour theorem. Maximum planar graphs, in which every face is a triangle, are called triangulations.
