State defense force

State defense forces support state National Guards during emergencies.

A state defense force (SDF) is a military unit in the United States that operates under the sole authority of a state government, authorized by federal law (32 U.S.C. § 109) and state law. Also known as state guards, state militias, or state military reserves, they are distinct from a state's National Guard, which can be federalized; state defense forces cannot be called into federal service.

Role

State defense forces typically support the National Guard during state emergencies such as natural disasters, providing personnel for search and rescue, medical support, communications, logistics, and ceremonial duties. Members are usually volunteers, often including veterans and professionals such as doctors, lawyers, and engineers. Some states also maintain naval militias.

History and examples

State guards were raised in both World Wars to replace National Guards deployed overseas. In the modern era, roughly twenty states and Puerto Rico maintain active defense forces, including the Texas State Guard, the California State Guard, the New York Guard, the Georgia State Defense Force, and the Puerto Rico State Guard. Their size, training, and equipment vary widely by state, and they are not funded by the federal government.
