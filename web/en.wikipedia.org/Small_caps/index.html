Small caps

Text in small capitals compared with regular type

Small caps (short for small capitals) are a typographic feature in which lowercase letters are replaced by capital letterforms of a reduced height, similar to the size of lowercase letters. For example, the word "Typographica" set in small caps appears as Typographica.

Uses

Small caps are used in running text where full capitals would appear too emphatic — for acronyms and initialisms, for the names of authors in bibliographies, for legal citations, in dictionaries for cross-references, and in some traditions for the first words of a chapter or the divine name in Bible translations. Well-designed typefaces include true small caps drawn specifically at the smaller size, with proportions and stroke weights adjusted; cheaper "faked" small caps are produced by software simply scaling down full capitals, which looks thin and cramped.

Implementation

In CSS, small caps are applied with font-variant: small-caps, and OpenType fonts may provide them through the smcp feature. Unicode also encodes a limited set of small capital letters in the IPA extensions and phonetic blocks, intended for phonetic transcription rather than typographic styling.
