Operation Bodyguard

Operation Bodyguard was the code name for a World War II deception strategy employed by the Allied states before the 1944 invasion of north-west Europe. Bodyguard set out an overall stratagem to mislead the German high command as to the time and place of the invasion. The plan proposed a number of subordinate operations, the most famous of which was Operation Fortitude.

The strategy was accepted at the Tehran Conference in late 1943 and coordinated by the London Controlling Section under Lieutenant-Colonel John Bevan. Its name was taken from a remark by Winston Churchill to Joseph Stalin at Tehran: "In wartime, truth is so precious that she should always be attended by a bodyguard of lies."

Subordinate operations

  • Operation Fortitude — itself divided into Fortitude North (a fictitious British Fourth Army in Scotland threatening Norway) and Fortitude South (the fictitious First United States Army Group, FUSAG, under George S. Patton, threatening the Pas-de-Calais).
  • Operation Zeppelin — suggested threats against the Balkans and Crete.
  • Operations Ironside, Vendetta and Ferdinand — threatened Bordeaux, the French Mediterranean coast and Italy respectively.
  • Operation Copperhead — a lookalike of General Montgomery visited Gibraltar and Algiers shortly before D-Day.

Methods and outcome

Bodyguard relied on the Double-Cross System of turned German agents (including Juan Pujol García, "Garbo"), false radio traffic, dummy aircraft, tanks and landing craft, and diplomatic deception. The plan succeeded: German forces — including the 15th Army at the Pas-de-Calais — were held away from Normandy for weeks after the landings on 6 June 1944, substantially aiding the Allied lodgement.
