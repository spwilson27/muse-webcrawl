Betty Boop

First appearanceDizzy Dishes (1930)
Created byMax Fleischer; Grim Natwick
StudioFleischer Studios
Voiced byMae Questel (1931–1939, 1988)

Betty Boop is an animated cartoon character created by Max Fleischer with help from animators including Grim Natwick. She originally appeared in the Talkartoon and Betty Boop film series produced by Fleischer Studios and released by Paramount Pictures. With her overt sexual appeal — a figure of the Jazz Age flapper — and signature "boop-boop-a-doop" catchphrase, she became one of the best-known cartoon characters of the 1930s.

Origins

Betty made her debut on 9 August 1930 in Dizzy Dishes as an anthropomorphic French poodle — a supporting character with long floppy ears — and was progressively humanized over subsequent cartoons, the ears becoming hoop earrings. Her design drew on the "it girl" image of Clara Bow and the singing style of Helen Kane, whose "boop" scatting she imitated; Kane sued Fleischer and Paramount in 1932 claiming the character was a caricature of her, but lost after evidence showed her own act derived from Black singer Baby Esther.

Heyday and censorship

Betty starred in dozens of cartoons, including Minnie the Moocher (1932) with Cab Calloway, and Snow-White (1933), often in surreal, jazz-infused stories. Enforcement of the Hays Code after 1934 forced a redesign: her short dress was lengthened, her character made more domestic, and the series' popularity waned; the last Betty Boop cartoons were released in 1939.

Legacy

Betty Boop experienced repeated revivals, including a cameo in Who Framed Roger Rabbit (1988), again voiced by Mae Questel. She remains a ubiquitous merchandising icon and an emblem of early American animation and pre-Code Hollywood.
