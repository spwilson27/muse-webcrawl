Robert Triffin
Born: 5 October 1911, Flobecq, Belgium
Died: 23 February 1993, Ostend, Belgium
Known for: Triffin dilemma

Robert Triffin (1911–1993) was a Belgian-American economist best known for his critique of the Bretton Woods system of fixed exchange rates, formulated in the "Triffin dilemma".

Triffin dilemma

Testifying before the US Congress in 1959 and in his book Gold and the Dollar Crisis (1960), Triffin argued that a system in which the US dollar was convertible to gold while serving as the world's reserve currency contained a contradiction: the world needed ever more dollars for trade, requiring persistent US deficits, but those deficits steadily undermined confidence that dollars could be redeemed for gold. The dilemma was realised in 1971 when the United States suspended gold convertibility, ending Bretton Woods.

Career

Triffin taught at Harvard and Yale, advised the European institutions, and advocated regional monetary cooperation in Europe, influencing thinking that later led toward the euro.
