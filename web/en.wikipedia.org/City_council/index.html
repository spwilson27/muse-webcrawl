City council

A city council is the legislative body that governs a city, town, municipality, or local government area. Councils typically consist of elected councillors who serve fixed terms and meet regularly to enact ordinances, approve budgets, and oversee local services such as roads, policing, water supply, and land use.

Structure

The precise role and size of a city council varies by jurisdiction. In council–manager government, the council appoints a professional city manager to administer daily operations, while the mayor serves largely as a ceremonial figurehead or council leader. In mayor–council government, an elected mayor exercises executive authority alongside the legislative council.

Elections

Councillors may be elected at-large across the whole city, by ward or district, or by a combination of the two. Terms commonly run from two to four years, sometimes staggered so that only part of the council is elected at each election.

Functions

Typical council functions include levying local taxes and fees, adopting zoning and planning rules, approving the municipal budget, and confirming appointments. Meetings are usually open to the public, and residents may address the council during public comment periods.
