Advanced Mobile Phone System

Advanced Mobile Phone System (AMPS) was an analog mobile phone system standard originally developed by Bell Labs and later modified in cooperation with Motorola. It was officially introduced in the Americas on October 13, 1983, and was deployed in many other countries, including Israel in 1986, Australia in 1987, Singapore in 1988, and Pakistan in 1990.

AMPS
Generation: 1G
Developer: Bell Labs
Launched: 1983
Frequency band: 800 MHz
Multiple access: FDMA

Technology

AMPS operated in the 800 MHz band using frequency-division multiple access (FDMA), allocating 30 kHz channels for voice calls modulated with FM. It was the first cellular standard to use hexagonal cells with frequency reuse and automatic handoff between cell sites.

Deployment and decline

AMPS formed the basis of the Nordic Mobile Telephone (NMT) and Total Access Communication System (TACS) variants used in Europe. In North America it was gradually replaced by digital standards such as IS-54/IS-136 (D-AMPS), IS-95 (cdmaOne), and GSM. The FCC allowed carriers to shut down AMPS service in 2008.

Security

As an analog system, AMPS calls could be intercepted with ordinary radio scanners, and cloning of phone identifiers became a significant fraud problem in the 1990s.
