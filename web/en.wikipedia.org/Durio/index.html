Durio

Durio is a genus of flowering plants in the mallow family, Malvaceae, comprising around 30 recognized species of trees native to the rainforests of Southeast Asia, particularly Borneo, Sumatra and the Malay Peninsula.

Description

Durio species are large evergreen trees that can reach 25–50 metres in height. Their fruits are typically large, spiny capsules containing seeds surrounded by a fleshy, edible aril. Several species are cultivated for their fruit, most famously Durio zibethinus, the source of the durian.

The durian

The fruit of Durio zibethinus is known as the "king of fruits" in Southeast Asia. It is noted for its strong odour, thorn-covered rind and rich, custard-like flesh. Other edible species include D. graveolens, D. oxleyanus and D. kutejensis.

Ecology

Many Durio species are pollinated by bats, and their flowers open at night. The genus is an important component of lowland dipterocarp forests, though habitat loss threatens several species.
