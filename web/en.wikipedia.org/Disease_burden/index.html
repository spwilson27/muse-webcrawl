Disease burden

Disease burden (also called burden of disease) is the impact of a health problem as measured by financial cost, mortality, morbidity, or other indicators. It is commonly quantified with the disability-adjusted life year (DALY), which combines years of life lost to premature death with years lived with disability, allowing the effects of different diseases and injuries to be compared on a single scale.

Measurement

The Global Burden of Disease Study, coordinated by the Institute for Health Metrics and Evaluation, produces comprehensive estimates for hundreds of diseases, injuries, and risk factors across countries and time. Complementary measures include the quality-adjusted life year (QALY), years of potential life lost (YPLL), and direct and indirect economic costs such as healthcare spending and lost productivity.

Global patterns

Historically dominated by communicable, maternal, and nutritional conditions, the global burden has shifted toward non-communicable diseases — cardiovascular disease, cancers, diabetes, and mental disorders — as populations age and lifestyles change, a transition that varies widely by region and income level. Environmental and behavioural risk factors such as air pollution, smoking, high blood pressure, and unsafe water account for large shares of the total burden.
