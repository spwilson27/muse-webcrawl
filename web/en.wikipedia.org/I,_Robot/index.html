I, Robot

First edition cover of I, Robot (Gnome Press, 1950).

I, Robot is a fixup collection of science fiction short stories by American writer Isaac Asimov, first published by Gnome Press in 1950. The stories originally appeared mainly in Astounding Science Fiction and Super Science Stories between 1940 and 1950. They are framed as an interview by a reporter with Dr. Susan Calvin, chief robopsychologist of U.S. Robots and Mechanical Men, Inc., who recounts episodes from her career spanning 1982 to 2064.

Contents

The nine stories—"Robbie", "Runaround", "Reason", "Catch That Rabbit", "Liar!", "Little Lost Robot", "Escape!", "Evidence", and "The Evitable Conflict"—explore the interactions between humans, robots, and morality. Several feature engineers Gregory Powell and Mike Donovan field-testing robots on space stations and other planets. It was in "Runaround" that Asimov first stated his famous Three Laws of Robotics: a robot may not injure a human being or, through inaction, allow a human being to come to harm; a robot must obey orders given it by human beings except where such orders conflict with the First Law; and a robot must protect its own existence as long as such protection does not conflict with the First or Second Laws. Many stories turn on ingenious paradoxes generated by conflicts among the Laws, which Calvin resolves by reasoning about positronic brains.

Influence and adaptations

The collection helped define the rational, engineered robot of modern science fiction, replacing earlier "Frankenstein complex" stories (a term Asimov coined) of robots turning on their creators. The 2004 film I, Robot, starring Will Smith, borrowed the title, several characters, and the Three Laws but used a largely original plot drawn in part from other works.
