Agronomy

Agronomy is the science and technology of producing and using plants for food, fuel, fiber, chemicals, recreation, and land conservation. It combines plant genetics, plant physiology, meteorology, and soil science, and applies them to crop production. An agronomist works to improve the yield, quality, and sustainability of field crops such as cereals, oilseeds, and forage.

Branches

Agronomy includes plant breeding, crop physiology, soil management and fertility, weed science, and agroecology. Modern agronomy also embraces precision agriculture, using satellite imagery, GPS-guided machinery, and sensors to vary seed and fertilizer rates within a field.

History

Systematic agronomy emerged in the nineteenth century with agricultural chemistry (notably Justus von Liebig's work on mineral nutrients) and field experiment stations such as Rothamsted in England, founded in 1843. In the twentieth century, agronomic research underpinned the Green Revolution's high-yielding varieties.

Practice

Agronomists advise farmers on crop rotation, tillage, planting dates, pest management, and nutrient plans, and conduct variety trials. Professional bodies such as the American Society of Agronomy certify practitioners and publish research journals.
