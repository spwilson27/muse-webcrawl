Exponentiation by squaring

Binary exponentiation reduces computing xⁿ to O(log n) multiplications.

Exponentiation by squaring, or binary exponentiation, is a method for computing large integer powers of a number quickly. Instead of n − 1 multiplications, it uses the binary expansion of the exponent: x is repeatedly squared, and the squares corresponding to set bits are accumulated into a result, giving O(log n) multiplications.

The method

Recursively, xⁿ equals (x²)^(n/2) when n is even, and x·(x²)^((n−1)/2) when n is odd. The same idea works in any monoid — not just for numbers but for matrices, polynomials and modular arithmetic — making it fundamental to cryptographic algorithms such as RSA and Diffie–Hellman key exchange, where exponents may have thousands of bits. Variants include the left-to-right and right-to-left binary methods, Montgomery's ladder (used to resist side-channel timing attacks), and addition-chain exponentiation, which can shave off a few further multiplications. Ancient versions of the technique appear in Indian mathematics in the work of Pingala.
