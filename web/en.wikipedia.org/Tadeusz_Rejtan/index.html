Tadeusz Rejtan

Tadeusz Rejtan
Born: 20 August 1742, Hruszówka, Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth
Died: 8 August 1780, Hruszówka
Known for: Protest at the Partition Sejm of 1773

Tadeusz Reytan (or Rejtan; 20 August 1742 – 8 August 1780) was a Polish nobleman and deputy to the Sejm of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth, remembered as a symbol of patriotic resistance for his dramatic attempt to prevent the legalisation of the First Partition of Poland.

The protest of 1773

At the opening of the Partition Sejm in Warsaw on 21 April 1773, convened under Russian pressure to ratify the cession of Commonwealth territory to Russia, Prussia and Austria, Rejtan — together with a handful of other deputies — tried to block the proceedings. According to tradition, he bared his chest and threw himself across the doorway of the chamber, begging the deputies not to leave and so allow the Sejm's confederation under the marshal Adam Poniński. The gesture failed: the Sejm approved the partition treaties.

Legacy

Rejtan returned to his estate and died in obscurity in 1780, reportedly in declining mental health. Posthumously he became an emblem of conscience in Polish political culture, immortalised in Jan Matejko's monumental 1866 painting Rejtan — the Fall of Poland, which hangs in the Royal Castle in Warsaw.
