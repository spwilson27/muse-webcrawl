Italian (italiano) is a Romance language of the Indo-European family, descended from the Vulgar Latin spoken in Tuscany. It is the official language of Italy, San Marino, and Vatican City, and one of the official languages of Switzerland. Italian is spoken by about 65 million native speakers, and is also widely studied as a foreign language for its cultural heritage in music, art, cuisine, and literature. Of all the major Romance languages, Italian is generally considered the closest to Latin in vocabulary and conservatism.

History

Italian derives from the Tuscan dialect as used by the great Florentine writers of the 14th century — Dante Alighieri, Petrarch, and Boccaccio — whose works, above all Dante's Divine Comedy, established a literary standard. For centuries Italy remained a mosaic of regional dialects and separate states, and the "language question" (questione della lingua) was debated by scholars: in the 16th century Pietro Bembo codified the Florentine of the Trecento as the model. After Italian unification in 1861, standard Italian spread through schooling, military service, and later radio and television; at unification, only a small minority of the population spoke it fluently. Alessandro Manzoni's novel The Betrothed (revised edition 1840), deliberately rewritten in contemporary Florentine, was a landmark in promoting a unified national language.

Phonology and grammar

Italian has seven vowel phonemes in stressed syllables (five in unstressed ones) and a system of consonants notable for phonemic gemination (double consonants), e.g. pala "shovel" versus palla "ball". Stress usually falls on the penultimate syllable. Grammar is inflective: nouns have two genders (masculine and feminine) and two numbers; verbs conjugate richly for person, tense, aspect, and mood, including a well-developed subjunctive. Word order is typically subject–verb–object but flexible for emphasis. The alphabet has 21 letters; the letters j, k, w, x, and y appear only in loanwords.

Dialects and regional varieties

Italy is famous for its linguistic diversity. In addition to regional varieties of standard Italian, many Italians speak a local dialetto — in linguistic terms, distinct Romance languages such as Neapolitan, Sicilian, Venetian, Piedmontese, and Sardinian (the latter often classed as a separate branch of Romance). Standard Italian has largely supplanted dialects in formal domains, but many remain vital in everyday and family life.

Italian worldwide

Large Italian emigrant communities in the Americas, Australia, and northern Europe maintain the language to varying degrees. Italian is an official language of the European Union and the working language of the Sovereign Military Order of Malta. It is also the most studied language for classical music terminology; Italian words such as piano, opera, and tempo are used internationally.
