America's Cup

The America's Cup is a sailing competition and one of the oldest international sporting trophies. It is contested between the defender, representing the yacht club that currently holds the cup, and a challenger. The trophy was first awarded in 1851, when the schooner America, representing the New York Yacht Club, won a race around the Isle of Wight in England, defeating a fleet of British yachts.

The event is a match race series between two sailing yachts. Because of its winner-takes-all format and the enormous sums spent on design and technology, the America's Cup is often described as being as much an engineering and design contest as a sporting one.

History

The original trophy, an ornate silver ewer, was awarded to the yacht America after its victory on 22 August 1851. The owners later donated the cup to the New York Yacht Club under a Deed of Gift, making it a perpetual challenge trophy. The United States successfully defended the cup for 132 years, the longest winning streak in international sport, until Australia II broke the run in 1983.

Subsequent competitions have seen the cup held by teams from Australia, New Zealand, Switzerland and the United States. The format, boat classes and rules have changed repeatedly, moving from monohull yachts to giant multihulls and, more recently, foiling monohulls that sail largely above the water.

The Deed of Gift

The America's Cup is governed by a document known as the Deed of Gift, drawn up by George L. Schuyler and accepted by the New York Yacht Club in 1857. It sets out the basic conditions of challenge, including that the match be between yacht clubs of different countries and that the challenger give notice of its challenge. Where defender and challenger cannot agree on terms, default provisions in the deed apply, which has occasionally led to court disputes.

Modern era

Recent editions have used foiling AC75 monohulls capable of speeds approaching and exceeding 50 knots. Emirates Team New Zealand became one of the most successful modern teams, winning the cup in Bermuda in 2017 and defending it in Auckland in 2021 and Barcelona in 2024.
