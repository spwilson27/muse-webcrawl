Silken Thomas

Thomas FitzGerald, 10th Earl of Kildare (1513 – 3 February 1537), known as Silken Thomas, was a leading figure in the Geraldine dynasty in Ireland who led a rebellion against Henry VIII in 1534–35. His nickname is said to derive from the silk fringes worn on the helmets of his horsemen, or from his fine clothing and handsome appearance.

Rebellion

Thomas was the son of Gerald FitzGerald, 9th Earl of Kildare, Lord Deputy of Ireland. When his father was summoned to London and imprisoned in the Tower in 1534, a false rumour reached Ireland that the earl had been executed. On 11 June 1534 Thomas appeared before the council at St Mary's Abbey, Dublin, dramatically renounced his allegiance to the King, returned the sword of state, and raised the standard of revolt. His followers promptly murdered Archbishop John Alen of Dublin, who had tried to arrange a mediation.

The rebellion — joined by the Kildare Geraldines, some Gaelic Irish lords and the O'Connors — besieged Dublin Castle and ravaged the Pale, but Thomas failed to take Dublin and his cause weakened as a royal army under William Skeffington arrived with artillery, storming Maynooth Castle in March 1535 and executing its garrison. In August 1535, ill and deserted, Thomas surrendered on a promise that his life would be spared.

Death and aftermath

The promise was disregarded. Thomas and his five uncles were attainted and executed at Tyburn on 3 February 1537. The rebellion's failure effectively ended the semi-independent Geraldine lordship of Kildare and marked a turning point in Henry VIII's assertion of direct royal control over Ireland.
