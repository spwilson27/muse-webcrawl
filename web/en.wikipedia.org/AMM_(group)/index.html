AMM is a British free improvisation group that was founded in London in 1965 by saxophonist Lou Gare, percussionist Eddie Prévost and guitarist Keith Rowe. One of the pioneering ensembles of free improvisation and electroacoustic improvisation, the group has operated for decades as an open-ended collective devoted to spontaneous, non-idiomatic music-making, typically performing long, quiet, carefully textured improvisations without predetermined structure.

History

The founding members had backgrounds in jazz — Gare and Prévost had played in a hard bop quintet led by Mike Westbrook — but became dissatisfied with idiomatic forms. Cellist Lawrence Sheaff joined the original line-up, and composer Cornelius Cardew, who had worked with John Cage and Karlheinz Stockhausen, became an important early member after encountering the group in 1966. AMM's music drew on musique concrète, experimental composition and the radical politics of the period; Rowe famously laid his guitar flat on a table and treated it as a sound source, also incorporating transistor radios into performances.

AMM's name was never officially expanded; the members reportedly agreed to keep its meaning secret. By the 1970s the group had contracted to the trio of Gare, Prévost and Rowe, and in later years pianist John Tilbury, a noted interpreter of Morton Feldman, became a central member. Gare left in the late 1970s, and after Rowe's departure in the 2000s AMM continued principally as the duo of Prévost and Tilbury, augmented by guests.

Style and influence

AMM's performances emphasise sustained listening, silence and micro-detail; a well-known maxim associated with the group is that every noise has a note and every note a noise. Their approach influenced generations of improvisers and helped define the field later termed "electroacoustic improvisation" or "EAI". Prévost's Matchless Recordings label has documented much of the group's work, and his book No Sound Is Innocent (1995) sets out the aesthetic and social philosophy behind AMM's music.
