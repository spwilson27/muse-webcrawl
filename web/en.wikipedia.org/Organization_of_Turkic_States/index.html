Organization of Turkic States

The Organization of Turkic States (OTS), formerly called the Turkic Council or the Cooperation Council of Turkic Speaking States, is an intergovernmental organisation comprising independent Turkic states: Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Turkey and Uzbekistan as full members, with Hungary, Turkmenistan and Northern Cyprus as observers. Founded in 2009, it aims to promote comprehensive cooperation among Turkic peoples and states in politics, trade, transport, education, culture and security.

History

Summits of Turkic-speaking heads of state were held from 1992 after the collapse of the Soviet Union opened Central Asia to cooperation. The Nakhchivan Agreement of 3 October 2009, signed by Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan and Turkey, formally established the Turkic Council. Uzbekistan joined as a full member in 2019. At the 2021 Istanbul summit the organisation was renamed the Organization of Turkic States, and leaders adopted the "Turkic World Vision 2040" document. Samarkand (2022) and Astana (2023) summits followed, with regular summitry since.

Structure and activities

The Council of Heads of State is the supreme body; foreign ministers coordinate, and a Secretariat in Istanbul administers work through committees on economy, transport, customs, education, media and diaspora affairs. Related bodies include TÜRKSOY (cultural cooperation), the Turkic Academy, the Turkic Culture and Heritage Foundation, and the parliamentary assembly TURKPA in Baku. Practical cooperation has centred on the Trans-Caspian "Middle Corridor" trade route, simplified customs, a Turkic Investment Fund (agreed 2023), and adoption of a common 34-letter Latin-based alphabet framework proposed by the Turkic Academy in 2024.

Significance

The OTS gives the Turkic states an institutional framework spanning some 170 million people across Eurasia (including observers). Its growing coordination in transport and energy has attracted analytical attention in the context of shifting Eurasian trade routes after 2022.
