Abe Lenstra

Born: 27 November 1920, Heerenveen, Netherlands

Died: 2 September 1985, Heerenveen, Netherlands

Position: Inside forward

Main club: SC Heerenveen

Abe Minderts Lenstra (27 November 1920 – 2 September 1985) was a Dutch footballer who spent most of his career with SC Heerenveen, the club of his home town in Friesland. Regarded as one of the greatest Dutch players of his era, he became a Frisian folk hero, and the club's stadium was later named after him.

Career

Lenstra made his debut for Heerenveen as a teenager and played for the club for nearly two decades, scoring several hundred goals in official matches. A technically gifted inside forward, he twice finished as top scorer in the Dutch league and led Heerenveen to a famous 6–5 comeback victory over Ajax in 1950, in which his team recovered from a 5–1 deficit. Loyal to his home region, he repeatedly declined transfers to bigger clubs at home and abroad, including Ajax and the Italian side Fiorentina, before finishing his playing days with SC Enschede and Enschedese Boys.

International career

Lenstra earned 47 caps for the Netherlands national team between 1940 and 1959, scoring 33 goals. His international career was interrupted by the Second World War and limited by the Netherlands' absence from major tournaments during the amateur era.

Legacy

The Abe Lenstra Stadion, opened in 1994, is named in his honour, and a statue of Lenstra stands outside the ground. He was also an accomplished speed skater and athlete in his youth, reflecting the multi-sport culture of Friesland.
