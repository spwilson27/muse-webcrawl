Young Ireland was a political and cultural movement of Irish nationalists in the 1840s. Centred on the newspaper The Nation, it sought to promote Irish culture, language, and national identity, and eventually broke with Daniel O'Connell's Repeal movement over questions of violence and denominational education.

History

The movement was founded by Thomas Davis, Charles Gavan Duffy, and John Blake Dillon. After Davis's early death and a split with O'Connell in 1846–47, the Young Irelanders formed the Irish Confederation. During the Great Famine, under William Smith O'Brien, they launched the Young Irelander Rebellion of 1848, a small and quickly suppressed rising culminating at Ballingarry, County Tipperary.

Legacy

Although the rebellion failed and its leaders were transported to Van Diemen's Land, Young Ireland profoundly influenced later Irish nationalism, the literary revival, and figures of the independence era.
