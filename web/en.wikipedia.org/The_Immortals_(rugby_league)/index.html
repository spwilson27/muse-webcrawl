The Immortals (rugby league)

SportRugby league
CountryAustralia
Established1981
Original conceptRugby League Week magazine

The Immortals is a concept recognising the greatest players in the history of Australian rugby league. The term was created in 1981 by the magazine Rugby League Week, which originally named four players: Clive Churchill, Bob Fulton, Reg Gasnier, and Johnny Raper.

History

Further players were added over the following decades, including Graeme Langlands and Wally Lewis (1999) and Arthur Beetson (2003). After Rugby League Week ceased publication, the National Rugby League (NRL) took over the concept in 2018, inducting five more players — among them Norm Provan and Mal Meninga — bringing the total to thirteen.

Criteria

Immortal status is considered the highest individual honour in the Australian game, judged on a player's overall impact, achievements, and standing in the sport's history. Inductees must be retired, and the honour has at times been restricted to players already in the Rugby League Hall of Fame.
