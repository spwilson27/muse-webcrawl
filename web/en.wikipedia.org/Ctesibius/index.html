Ctesibius


Ctesibius of Alexandria
Bornc. 285 BC, Alexandria, Egypt
Diedc. 222 BC, Alexandria, Egypt
Known forWater clock, hydraulis (water organ), pneumatics
FieldsEngineering, invention

Ctesibius or Ktesibios (Greek: Κτησίβιος; fl. 285–222 BC) was a Greek inventor and mathematician in Alexandria, Ptolemaic Egypt. He wrote the first treatises on the science of compressed air and its uses in pumps, and was the founder of the Alexandrian school of engineering. He is regarded as the "father of pneumatics" and is credited with inventing the hydraulis, an early pipe organ powered by water pressure.

Life

Very little is known of Ctesibius' life. He is said to have been the son of a barber and to have worked in his father's shop in Alexandria, where he devised a counterweight-adjustable mirror. He came to the attention of the Ptolemaic court and was supported as an inventor and researcher, possibly in association with the Museum of Alexandria.

Inventions

Ctesibius improved the clepsydra, or water clock, producing a version that kept nearly constant time by regulating the flow of water with a float regulator — a mechanism often cited as an early example of a feedback control device. He invented the hydraulis, in which air was supplied to the pipes by a water-stabilised reservoir, and the force pump, later used in fire engines and described by Hero of Alexandria. He is also associated with compressed-air catapults, singing bird automata, and theatrical figures powered by water and air pressure.

Legacy

His writings are lost, but his work was cited by later engineers including Philo of Byzantium, Vitruvius and Hero. The lunar crater Ctesibius is named in his honour.
