Jerk (cooking)

Jerk is a style of cooking native to Jamaica, in which meat is dry-rubbed or wet-marinated with a hot spice mixture called Jamaican jerk spice. The technique originated with the indigenous Taíno people and was developed by African Maroons in the island's interior, who adapted it to hunt and cook wild boar.


Jerk cooking over pimento wood

Ingredients

The two defining ingredients of jerk seasoning are allspice (called "pimento" in Jamaica) and Scotch bonnet peppers, among the hottest chili peppers in the world. Other ingredients typically include thyme, scallions, garlic, ginger, cinnamon, nutmeg, brown sugar, soy sauce and salt.

Technique

Meat — most commonly chicken or pork — is marinated for hours, then slow-cooked over a fire of pimento wood, which imparts a distinctive smoky flavour. Traditionally the meat was cooked in a pit or over a grill of green pimento sticks. Modern jerk shacks often use halved steel oil drums as cookers.

History

The Taíno method of cooking meat over a wooden frame — related to the word "barbacoa," the origin of "barbecue" — was adopted by escaped enslaved Africans, the Maroons, who seasoned the meat with native peppers and spices and cooked it in earth pits to avoid detection. Jerk has since spread throughout the Caribbean diaspora worldwide.
