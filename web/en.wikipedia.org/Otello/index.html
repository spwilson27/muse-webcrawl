Otello

Otello is an opera in four acts by Giuseppe Verdi to an Italian libretto by Arrigo Boito, based on Shakespeare's play Othello. It premiered at La Scala, Milan, on 5 February 1887 and is regarded as one of the greatest Italian operas.

Background

Verdi had written nothing for the stage for over a decade after Aida (1871). His publisher Giulio Ricordi and the conductor Franco Faccio coaxed him out of retirement, pairing him with Boito, whose libretto compressed Shakespeare into a swift, concentrated drama. After Otello's triumph, the same team produced Verdi's final opera, Falstaff (1893).

Plot

The Moor Otello, a Venetian general victorious over the Turks in Cyprus, is manipulated by his ensign Iago, passed over for promotion, into believing his wife Desdemona unfaithful with Cassio. Iago exploits a stolen handkerchief as false proof. In a jealous rage Otello smothers Desdemona, then, discovering the deception — and Emilia's exposure of Iago — kills himself.

Music

Famous numbers include the storm chorus that opens the opera, Iago's nihilistic "Credo", the love duet "Già nella notte densa", Desdemona's "Willow Song" and "Ave Maria", and Otello's final "Niun mi tema". The score is through-composed and orchestral, largely abandoning separate arias.
