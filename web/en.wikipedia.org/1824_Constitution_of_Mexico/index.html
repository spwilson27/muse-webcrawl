1824 Constitution of Mexico

The Federal Constitution of the United Mexican States of 1824 (Spanish: Constitución Federal de los Estados Unidos Mexicanos de 1824) was the first constitution of Mexico as an independent nation, enacted on October 4, 1824, following the overthrow of the short-lived First Mexican Empire of Agustín de Iturbide. It established Mexico as a federal republic with a representative government, deeply influenced by the United States Constitution and by the Spanish Constitution of 1812.

Contents

The constitution declared the nation the "Estados Unidos Mexicanos", a federation of states with powers divided among a bicameral Congress (a Chamber of Deputies and a Senate), an executive headed by a President and Vice President, and a federal judiciary. Roman Catholicism was established as the sole official religion, and the fueros (special legal privileges) of the clergy and military were retained. Guadalupe Victoria became the first president under the constitution, and, unusually for the era in Mexico, he completed a full term.

Federal structure

The federation originally comprised nineteen states and several territories, including Alta California, Baja California and Santa Fe de Nuevo México. A constituent congress dominated by federalists defeated centralist preferences, though conflicts between the two camps defined Mexican politics for decades.

Abolition and restoration

In 1835–36, under President Santa Anna, the constitution was replaced by the centralist Siete Leyes. Federalism was restored in 1846 amid the Mexican–American War, and the 1824 framework survived until the liberal Constitution of 1857 superseded it.
