Art Institute of Chicago

Established: 1879

Location: Chicago, Illinois

Type: Art museum

The Art Institute of Chicago is one of the oldest and largest art museums in the United States, founded in 1879 and located in Grant Park, Chicago. Its collection spans 5,000 years of art from around the world.

Collection

The museum is especially renowned for its Impressionist and Post-Impressionist holdings, including Georges Seurat's A Sunday Afternoon on the Island of La Grande Jatte, Edward Hopper's Nighthawks and Grant Wood's American Gothic. It also holds major collections of American art, Asian art, arms and armour, and modern and contemporary art.

Building

The Beaux-Arts main building, guarded by two bronze lion statues on Michigan Avenue, opened in 1893. The Modern Wing, designed by Renzo Piano, opened in 2009. The museum is affiliated with the School of the Art Institute of Chicago.
