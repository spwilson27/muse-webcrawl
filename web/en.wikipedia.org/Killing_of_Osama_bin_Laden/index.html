Killing of Osama bin Laden

Osama bin Laden, founder of al-Qaeda and mastermind of the September 11 attacks, was killed on 2 May 2011 (local time) in Abbottabad, Pakistan, during a raid by United States Navy SEALs of the Naval Special Warfare Development Group, in an operation codenamed Operation Neptune Spear, authorised by President Barack Obama and conducted with the CIA.

The hunt and the raid

CIA analysis traced a courier, Abu Ahmed al-Kuwaiti, to a large, unusually secretive compound in Abbottabad, near Pakistan's military academy. After months of surveillance, helicopters carrying SEALs flew from Afghanistan at night; one crashed on landing but the team breached the house and shot bin Laden on the third floor. The raid lasted about 40 minutes; several others in the compound were also killed and no Americans were injured.

Aftermath

Bin Laden's identity was confirmed by DNA comparison and his body was buried at sea from USS Carl Vinson within 24 hours, in accordance, the US said, with Islamic practice. The killing prompted celebrations in the United States, condemnation from al-Qaeda (which vowed revenge), and debate over legality, Pakistan's possible knowledge of his whereabouts, and the intelligence value of material seized in the compound.
