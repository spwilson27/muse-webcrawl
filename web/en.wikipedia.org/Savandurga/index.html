Savandurga


Image placeholder: Savandurga

Savandurga is a hill 60 km west of Bengaluru, off the Magadi road, in the Indian state of Karnataka. Formed by two granite monolith hills — the white granite Karigudda ("black hill") and the black granite Biligudda ("white hill"), named somewhat confusingly — Savandurga rises about 1,226 metres above sea level and is considered among the largest monolith hills in Asia.

History

Savandurga was a stronghold of the Magadi rulers; it came under Kempe Gowda I of Bengaluru's family, and its name is said to derive from Savina Durga, the "fort of death," reflecting the steepness of its defences. The hill fort was later held by Tipu Sultan, who considered it nearly impregnable, before being stormed by British forces under Lord Cornwallis in 1791 during the Third Anglo-Mysore War, when troops scaled it with little loss of life. Robert Home's paintings of the assault are well known.

Today

The hill is a popular destination for trekking, rock climbing and birdwatching, set amid the Savandurga reserve forest, which supports elephants, leopards and sloth bears. Pilgrims visit the temples of Savandi Veerabhadraswamy and Lakshmi Narasimha Swamy at the foot of the hill. The nearby Manchanabele reservoir on the Arkavathi River is a noted birding site.
