Aeolus

Aeolus is a name given to several figures in Greek mythology. The best known is Aeolus, keeper of the winds, son of Hippotes, who lived on the floating island of Aeolia. Other figures include Aeolus, son of Hellen and ancestor of the Aeolians, and Aeolus, son of Poseidon.

Aeolus
Role: Keeper of the winds
Abode: Aeolia
Appears in: Odyssey

Keeper of the winds

In Homer's Odyssey, Aeolus receives Odysseus hospitably and gives him a bag containing all the winds except the favorable west wind. Odysseus's crew, suspecting treasure, opened the bag within sight of Ithaca, and the released winds drove the ship back to Aeolia, where Aeolus refused further help.

Son of Hellen

Another Aeolus, son of Hellen and the nymph Orseis, ruled Aeolis in Thessaly and was the mythical ancestor of the Aeolian Greeks. His descendants feature in many genealogies of Greek myth.
