Latex

Latex is a stable dispersion (emulsion) of polymer microparticles in water, found naturally in many flowering plants as a milky fluid that coagulates on exposure to air, and also produced synthetically. Natural latex is obtained commercially chiefly from the rubber tree Hevea brasiliensis and is the raw material for natural rubber.

Natural latex

Latex occurs in laticifers — specialised cells or vessels — in about 10 per cent of flowering plants. It contains proteins, alkaloids, starches, sugars, oils, tannins, resins and gums. Its biological function is thought to be defence against herbivores: it is bitter, sticky, and in some plants toxic, sealing wounds as it coagulates.

Uses

Collected latex is processed into natural rubber for tyres, gloves, condoms, balloons, elastic, adhesives and medical devices. Some people develop latex allergy, an immune reaction to its proteins, which has driven wider use of synthetic alternatives such as nitrile.

Synthetic latex

Synthetic latices are made by emulsion polymerisation of monomers such as styrene and butadiene, and are used in paints, coatings, carpet backing and paper coating.
