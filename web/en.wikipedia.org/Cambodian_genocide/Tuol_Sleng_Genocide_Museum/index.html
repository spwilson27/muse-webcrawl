Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum is a museum in Phnom Penh, Cambodia, preserving the site of the Khmer Rouge regime's Security Prison 21 (S-21). Between 1976 and 1979, an estimated 18,000 to 20,000 people were imprisoned, tortured, and killed there; only a handful of prisoners survived. The former high school buildings were converted into a museum in 1980, shortly after the regime's fall, and the site's archives are inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World Register.

S-21 prison

The Chao Ponhea Yat High School was taken over in 1976 by the Khmer Rouge security apparatus under commandant Kaing Guek Eav, known as "Duch". Classrooms were subdivided into tiny cells and torture rooms; prisoners — including former regime cadres accused of treason, intellectuals, monks, ethnic minorities, and entire families — were photographed on arrival, interrogated under torture until they produced elaborate forced confessions, and then taken to the Choeung Ek killing field outside the city to be murdered. The prison bureaucracy documented its victims obsessively, leaving thousands of mug shots and confessions.

The museum

Vietnamese forces and Cambodian survivors entering Phnom Penh in January 1979 found the prison hastily abandoned, with bodies of the last victims still chained to beds. The site was preserved largely as found and opened as a museum in 1980, with its displays of prisoner photographs, torture instruments, and cells; a stupa at Choeung Ek holds thousands of exhumed skulls. In 2009 the Tuol Sleng archives were added to UNESCO's Memory of the World Register, and in 2025 the site was inscribed on the World Heritage List as part of the Cambodian Memorial Sites.

Trial evidence

The museum's archives formed central evidence in Case 001 of the Khmer Rouge Tribunal, which convicted Duch of crimes against humanity and sentenced him to life imprisonment in 2012. Today Tuol Sleng is Cambodia's most visited memorial site and a focal point for education about the genocide.
