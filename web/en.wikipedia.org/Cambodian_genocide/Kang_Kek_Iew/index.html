Kang Kek Iew


Placeholder image: Kang Kek Iew (Duch) at the Khmer Rouge Tribunal

Kang Kek Iew (Khmer: កាំង ហ្គេកអៀវ; 17 November 1942 – 2 September 2020), also romanised as Kaing Kek Iev and better known by his revolutionary alias Duch (or Deuch), was a Cambodian war criminal and member of the Khmer Rouge movement who oversaw the Tuol Sleng (S-21) prison camp, where more than 14,000 people were sent to their deaths.

Role in the Khmer Rouge

A former schoolteacher, Duch joined the Communist Party of Kampuchea in the 1960s. He ran the M-13 prison in the jungle during the civil war and, after the Khmer Rouge victory in 1975, directed the security office S-21 in Phnom Penh. Under his supervision, prisoners were interrogated, tortured into giving confessions, and then sent for execution at Choeung Ek.

Trial and conviction

Duch was identified by journalist Nic Dunlop in 1999 and arrested. He was the first Khmer Rouge leader tried by the Extraordinary Chambers in the Courts of Cambodia (ECCC). In 2010 he was convicted of crimes against humanity and grave breaches of the Geneva Conventions; on appeal in 2012 his sentence was increased to life imprisonment. He died in 2020 while serving his sentence.
