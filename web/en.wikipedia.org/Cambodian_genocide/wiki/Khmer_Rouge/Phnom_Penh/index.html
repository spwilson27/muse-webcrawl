Phnom Penh (Khmer: ភ្នំពេញ) is the capital and largest city of Cambodia, situated at the confluence of the Tonlé Sap, Mekong, and Bassac rivers. Once known as the "Pearl of Asia", it remains the nation's political, economic, and cultural centre.

History

Phnom Penh takes its name from Wat Phnom, the temple on the city's single hill, linked by legend to the widow Penh in 1372. It became Cambodia's capital in the mid-15th century and again in 1866, flourishing under the French protectorate and under Norodom Sihanouk in the 1950s and 1960s, when its New Khmer Architecture drew international attention. In April 1975 the victorious Khmer Rouge emptied the city at gunpoint, driving its roughly two million inhabitants into the countryside; many died in the Cambodian genocide. The city was repopulated after the fall of Democratic Kampuchea in 1979 and has grown rapidly since the 1990s.

Geography

The city lies in the south-central floodplain of Cambodia. The Tonlé Sap river reverses direction seasonally, flowing into the great lake of the same name during the monsoon. Phnom Penh's riverfront (Sisowath Quay) along the Tonlé Sap is its main promenade.

Sights

The Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda, the National Museum, Wat Phnom, the Central Market, and the Russian Market are principal attractions. The Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum and Choeung Ek memorial preserve the memory of the Khmer Rouge era.
