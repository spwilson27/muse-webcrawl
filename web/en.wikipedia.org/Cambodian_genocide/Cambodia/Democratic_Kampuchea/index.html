The flag of Democratic Kampuchea depicted a stylized three-towered Angkor Wat in yellow on red.

Democratic Kampuchea was the official name of Cambodia from 1975 to 1979, under the one-party rule of the Communist Party of Kampuchea — the Khmer Rouge — led in practice by Pol Pot. In the party's secretive terminology, power was wielded by Angkar ("the Organization"), whose membership and structure were concealed from the population it ruled.

Establishment

The Khmer Rouge defeated the United States-backed Khmer Republic of Lon Nol in April 1975 after a five-year civil war. On taking Phnom Penh on April 17, the regime immediately emptied the cities, marching millions into rural cooperatives in the belief that urban life was parasitic and corrupt. A 1976 constitution formally named the state Democratic Kampuchea: Khieu Samphan became chairman of the State Presidium (head of state), while Pol Pot served as prime minister and Nuon Chea as president of the legislature. Only in September 1977 did Pol Pot publicly acknowledge that a communist party governed the country.

Policies

The regime abolished money, banking, markets, private land, schools, and organized religion, and dissolved families' control over labour and meals in favour of communal cooperatives and collective dining. It proclaimed autarkic goals — including a fantastical target of three tonnes of rice per hectare — while exporting rice even as the population starved. Society was terrorized by continuous purges: of "new people" from the cities, of ethnic Vietnamese, Cham, and Chinese minorities, of Buddhist monks, and repeatedly of the party's own cadres, thousands of whom perished in the S-21 security prison. An estimated 1.5 to 2.2 million people — about a quarter of the population — died from execution, overwork, disease, and starvation in what became known as the Cambodian genocide.

Fall

Border clashes and massacres of Vietnamese civilians, including the Ba Chúc massacre of 1978, provoked a full-scale Vietnamese invasion in December 1978. Phnom Penh fell on January 7, 1979, and Vietnam installed the People's Republic of Kampuchea under Heng Samrin. The Khmer Rouge withdrew to the Thai frontier, where, remarkably, Democratic Kampuchea retained Cambodia's seat at the United Nations through the 1980s with Chinese, American, and ASEAN support, as part of a coalition government-in-exile from 1982. The movement finally disintegrated in the late 1990s.
