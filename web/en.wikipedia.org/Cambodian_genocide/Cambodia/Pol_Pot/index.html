Pol Pot (born Saloth Sâr; 1925–1998) was a Cambodian revolutionary and dictator who led the Khmer Rouge and ruled Cambodia as Democratic Kampuchea from 1975 to 1979. Under his regime, an attempt to create an agrarian socialist society through forced collectivisation, mass executions and slave labour caused the deaths of roughly a quarter of the country's population in the Cambodian genocide.

Early life

Saloth Sâr was born to a prosperous farming family with connections to the royal court. Educated in Phnom Penh and from 1949 in Paris as a radio electronics student, he joined the French Communist Party and the clandestine circle of Cambodian Marxists. Returning home in 1953, he rose through the Cambodian communist movement, becoming its leader in 1963 and fleeing to the maquis in 1963–64 as Prince Sihanouk's police cracked down on the left.

Path to power

After the 1970 coup against Norodom Sihanouk, the Khmer Rouge fought a civil war against Lon Nol's Khmer Republic, growing from a few thousand guerrillas into the dominant force in the country. They captured Phnom Penh on 17 April 1975.

Rule

Speaking through the faceless organisation "Angkar", Pol Pot's regime emptied the cities, abolished money and markets, collectivised agriculture, persecuted religion and ethnic minorities, and purged its own ranks in waves of paranoia. In January 1979 Vietnam invaded and drove the Khmer Rouge from power. Pol Pot continued to lead the guerrilla movement from the Thai border, stepped down formally in 1985, and was placed under house arrest by his own movement in 1997 after ordering the murder of his former comrade Son Sen. He died in April 1998, reportedly of heart failure, just as efforts were underway to bring him before an international tribunal.
