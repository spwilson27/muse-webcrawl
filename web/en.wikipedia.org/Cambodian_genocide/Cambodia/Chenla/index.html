Chenla

Chenla (Chinese: 真臘, Zhēnlà; Khmer: ចេនឡា) was the name given by Chinese sources to a Cambodian polity, or group of polities, that succeeded the kingdom of Funan and flourished from roughly the late 6th to the early 9th century AD in mainland Southeast Asia. Chenla is regarded as the predecessor of the Khmer Empire centred on Angkor.

History

According to Chinese histories, Chenla was originally a vassal of Funan but under rulers such as Bhavavarman I and Mahendravarman (Citrasena) gained independence and eventually conquered Funan itself by the early 7th century. King Ishanavarman I (r. c. 616–637) founded a capital at Ishanapura — modern Sambor Prei Kuk — whose brick temple towers are among the earliest surviving monumental Khmer architecture. Trade and diplomatic missions to China are recorded, along with the reception of Indian religions, Sanskrit inscriptions and Indianised court culture.

Division and unification

Chinese accounts of the 8th century describe Chenla splitting into "Land Chenla" in the interior and "Water Chenla" toward the Mekong Delta and coast, a period of fragmentation among competing principalities (though the accuracy and meaning of this division is debated). Early in the 9th century, Jayavarman II unified the region and, in 802, proclaimed himself universal monarch on Phnom Kulen — the conventional founding act of the Khmer Empire, ending the Chenla period.

Legacy

Chenla-period (pre-Angkorian) art and architecture — compact brick towers with carved sandstone lintels, and sculpture such as statues of Harihara — laid the foundations for classical Khmer civilisation.
