Cambodia, officially the Kingdom of Cambodia, is a country in mainland Southeast Asia bordered by Thailand, Laos and Vietnam and by the Gulf of Thailand. Its capital and largest city is Phnom Penh. The country is dominated by the floodplain of the Mekong River and the Tonlé Sap, Southeast Asia's largest freshwater lake.

History

Cambodia's early history includes the Funan and Chenla kingdoms and, from the 9th to the 15th century, the Khmer Empire, whose capital at Angkor built the temple of Angkor Wat — now the country's national symbol. After centuries of decline and pressure from Siam and Vietnam, Cambodia became a French protectorate in 1863 and part of French Indochina. King Norodom Sihanouk won independence in 1953, but the country was drawn into the Vietnam War, suffered massive American bombing, and fell in 1975 to the Khmer Rouge, whose rule under Pol Pot caused the Cambodian genocide. Vietnam invaded in 1978–79, installing the People's Republic of Kampuchea; after the 1991 Paris Peace Accords, a UN transitional authority supervised elections and the monarchy was restored in 1993.

Geography and economy

The Cambodian landscape centres on the low-lying Mekong basin, ringed by the Cardamom Mountains in the southwest and the Dangrek range in the north. The monsoon-driven flood pulse of the Tonlé Sap reverses the flow of its river each year and supports one of the world's most productive inland fisheries. The economy rests on garments and footwear exports, tourism (especially to Angkor), agriculture — rice above all — and construction. Cambodia remains one of Southeast Asia's poorer countries, and landmines left from decades of conflict still contaminate rural land.

Culture

More than 95 percent of Cambodians practise Theravada Buddhism, and Khmer is spoken by the overwhelming majority. Classical Khmer dance-drama, shadow theatre and the apsara dance, inscribed on UNESCO's heritage lists, descend from the Angkorian court.
