Suryavarman II


Placeholder image: Bas-relief of Suryavarman II at Angkor Wat

Suryavarman II (Khmer: សូរ្យវរ្ម័នទី២), posthumously named Paramavishnuloka, was a Khmer king of the Khmer Empire who reigned from 1113 to c. 1150. He is best known as the builder of Angkor Wat, the largest religious monument in the world, which he dedicated to the Hindu god Vishnu.

Reign

Suryavarman II came to power after overthrowing his great-uncle Dharanindravarman I. He reunified the empire, which had been divided, and campaigned extensively against the neighbouring kingdoms of Champa and Dai Viet, as well as against smaller polities in the Malay Peninsula. His military campaigns are depicted in the bas-reliefs of Angkor Wat.

He revived Khmer diplomatic relations with China and held elaborate court ceremonies. A Chinese source describes a procession led by the king, who stood on an elephant wearing a gold diadem.

Angkor Wat

Angkor Wat was constructed in the early 12th century as Suryavarman's state temple and eventual mausoleum. Unlike most Khmer kings, who were Shaivite, Suryavarman identified himself with Vishnu, and the temple is oriented to the west, the direction associated with death. After his death he received the posthumous name Paramavishnuloka, "he who has entered the heavenly world of Vishnu".
