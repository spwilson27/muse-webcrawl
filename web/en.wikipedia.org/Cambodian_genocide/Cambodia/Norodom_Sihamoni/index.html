Norodom Sihamoni

Norodom Sihamoni (born 14 May 1953) is the King of Cambodia. The eldest son of King Norodom Sihanouk and Queen Norodom Monineath, he was elected king by the Royal Council of the Throne on 14 October 2004, a week after his father's abdication, and crowned on 29 October 2004. Before his accession he spent much of his life in Europe, trained as a classical dancer, and served as Cambodia's ambassador to UNESCO.

Early life and education

Sihamoni was educated in Phnom Penh and then in Prague, Czechoslovakia, where he studied classical dance, music, and theatre, graduating from the Academy of Performing Arts. He later studied filmmaking in North Korea and, after the Khmer Rouge period, settled in Paris, where he taught classical dance and directed dance companies. His name combines elements of his parents' names: Siha (from Sihanouk) and Moni (from Monineath).

Before the throne

During the Khmer Rouge regime he was held under house arrest in the Royal Palace with his parents. After returning to France, he declined political roles. In 1993 he became Cambodia's ambassador to UNESCO in Paris, a post he held until his election as king, earning a reputation for modesty and cultural refinement. Fluent in Khmer, French, and Czech, he is one of few monarchs trained professionally in the arts.

Reign

As a constitutional monarch, Sihamoni reigns but does not rule; executive power rests with the prime minister (Hun Sen until 2023, then Hun Manet). The king has largely kept a ceremonial, unifying profile, signing laws, granting pardons on request, and representing Cambodia abroad, while generally avoiding public commentary on the country's contested politics.
