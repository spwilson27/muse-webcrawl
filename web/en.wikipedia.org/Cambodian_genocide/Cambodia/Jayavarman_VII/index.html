Jayavarman VII

Jayavarman VII
Reign: c. 1181 – c. 1218
Empire: Khmer Empire
Capital: Angkor Thom (Yasodharapura)
Religion: Mahayana Buddhism
Dynasty: Mahidharapura

Jayavarman VII (Khmer: ជ័យវរ្ម័នទី៧; c. 1123–c. 1218), posthumously Mahaparamasaugata, was king of the Khmer Empire from about 1181 to 1218. He is widely regarded as the most powerful and greatest of the Khmer kings, remembered as a prodigious builder and a devout Mahayana Buddhist who reshaped the empire's capital at Angkor.

Accession and wars

The son of King Dharanindravarman II, Jayavarman spent years abroad, possibly in Champa, before invading forces from Champa sailed up the Mekong and sacked Angkor in 1177, killing King Tribhuvanaditya. Jayavarman rallied the Khmer, defeated the Cham in naval and land battles — scenes carved on the walls of his temples — and was crowned in 1181. He went on to conquer Champa in the 1190s and extended Khmer power over much of the Mekong basin and the Malay Peninsula.

Building programme

Jayavarman undertook the most intensive building programme of any Angkorian king. He founded the fortified royal city of Angkor Thom, centred on the Bayon with its serene giant face towers; built the great temples of Ta Prohm (dedicated to his mother, identified with the bodhisattva of compassion's mother, Prajnaparamita) and Preah Khan (dedicated to his father); and created the vast Neak Pean and the Jayatataka reservoir. Inscriptions also credit him with 102 hospitals (arogayasala), 121 resthouses along the empire's roads, and an extensive road network — works presented as acts of Buddhist merit for the welfare of his subjects.

Legacy

His reign marked the high point of Khmer power, but the scale of construction strained the empire's resources; after his death, his successor Indravarman II presided over contraction, and iconoclastic reaction under Jayavarman VIII later defaced many Buddhist images. Jayavarman VII remains a revered national figure in Cambodia today.
