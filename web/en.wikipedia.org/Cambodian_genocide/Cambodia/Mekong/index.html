The Mekong is a transboundary river in East and Southeast Asia and one of the world's great rivers, running from the Tibetan Plateau through China, Myanmar, Laos, Thailand, Cambodia and Vietnam to the South China Sea. It is the world's twelfth-longest river and drains an area of about 795,000 km², discharging some 475 km³ of water annually.

Course

The river rises in Qinghai province, China, where its upper course is called the Lancang Jiang. It descends through deep gorges in Yunnan before forming much of the border between Myanmar, Laos and Thailand — the Golden Triangle region. Below the Khone Falls in southern Laos it crosses the Cambodian plain, passing Phnom Penh, where the Tonlé Sap river joins it, and enters Vietnam, where it splits into distributaries across the broad Mekong Delta before reaching the sea.

Ecology and fisheries

The Mekong basin supports extraordinary biodiversity, second only to the Amazon among river systems, including the giant Mekong catfish and the Irrawaddy dolphin. Its seasonal flood pulse drives the Tonlé Sap fishery in Cambodia and makes the basin's capture fishery — some two million tonnes a year — a staple protein source for tens of millions of people.

Development and disputes

China has built a cascade of large hydropower dams on the upper river, and Laos is developing mainstream and tributary dams to become the "battery of Southeast Asia". Downstream states fear the dams trap sediment and fish and worsen the droughts and saltwater intrusion afflicting the Mekong Delta. The Mekong River Commission (1995) coordinates the four lower-basin countries, though China and Myanmar participate only as dialogue partners.
