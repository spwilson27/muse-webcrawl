Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum

Building A of the former Tuol Svay Pray High School, converted by the Khmer Rouge into Security Prison 21 (S-21).

The Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum (Khmer: សារមន្ទីរឧក្រិដ្ឋកម្មប្រល័យពូជសាសន៍ទួលស្លែង) is a museum in Phnom Penh, the capital of Cambodia, chronicling the Cambodian genocide. Located on the site of a former secondary school converted into the Security Prison 21 (S-21) by the Khmer Rouge regime, it was used from 1976 to 1979 as a secret centre for detention, interrogation, torture and extermination during the Democratic Kampuchea period led by Pol Pot.

S-21 prison

The Khmer Rouge converted the Chao Ponhea Yat (Tuol Svay Pray) high school into S-21 in 1976, under the command of Kaing Guek Eav, known as "Duch". An estimated 18,000–20,000 prisoners passed through the prison — officials, soldiers, intellectuals, foreigners, and their families — of whom only a handful (commonly counted as twelve adults, including the painter Vann Nath) are known to have survived. Prisoners were photographed, forced to write detailed confessions, tortured, and then transported to the Choeung Ek extermination centre ("Killing Fields") outside the city to be killed.

The museum

Vietnamese forces discovered the prison in January 1979; it was opened as a museum in 1980. The buildings preserve cells, shackles, interrogation rooms and the mugshot photographs of victims that the regime meticulously archived — documentation later central to the Khmer Rouge Tribunal (ECCC), which in 2010 convicted Duch of crimes against humanity; he was sentenced to life imprisonment on appeal in 2012. The museum's archives were inscribed in UNESCO's Memory of the World register. Together with Choeung Ek, Tuol Sleng is the principal memorial site of the Cambodian genocide, visited by survivors, students and researchers studying mass atrocity — a modern counterpart in commemorative weight to sites of earlier conflicts such as the Congo Crisis era.
