Phnom Penh

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Phnom Penh is the capital and most populous city of Cambodia, with a population of over two million. Located at the confluence of the Tonlé Sap, Mekong, and Bassac rivers, it has been the national capital since the 15th century (with interruptions) and the centre of Cambodia's government, economy, and culture. Once known as the "Pearl of Asia", it is noted for its French colonial architecture, Buddhist temples, and the Royal Palace.

History

Legend attributes the city's founding to Lady Penh, who in 1372 found Buddha statues washed up on the Tonlé Sap and enshrined them on a hill — Wat Phnom. Phnom Penh became the royal capital in the 15th century after the abandonment of Angkor. Under the French protectorate it grew into a planned colonial city of boulevards and villas.

The Khmer Rouge era

On April 17, 1975, the Khmer Rouge captured Phnom Penh and forcibly evacuated its entire population — then swollen by war refugees to perhaps two million — into the countryside, where many died of starvation, disease, and execution. The city stood nearly empty until Vietnamese forces liberated it in January 1979. The Tuol Sleng prison (S-21) and the Choeung Ek killing fields on its outskirts are preserved as memorials to the genocide.

Modern city

Since the 1990s Phnom Penh has rebuilt and grown rapidly, with garment manufacturing, tourism, and construction driving its economy. Landmarks include the Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda, the National Museum, Wat Phnom, the art deco Central Market (Phsar Thmei), and the Independence Monument. Traffic congestion, flooding, and inequality accompany its skyline of new high-rises.
