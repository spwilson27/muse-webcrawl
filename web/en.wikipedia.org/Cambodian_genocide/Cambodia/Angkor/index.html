Angkor

Angkor (Khmer: អង្គរ, "city") was the capital city of the Khmer Empire, flourishing from approximately the 9th to the 15th century AD in what is now northern Cambodia. The vast archaeological site, extending over some 400 square kilometres north of the Tonlé Sap lake and including the remains of successive capitals, preserves the monumental temples of the empire, most famously Angkor Wat. Angkor is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the most important archaeological sites in Southeast Asia.

History

The city of Yasodharapura was founded around 900 by King Yasovarman I, centred on the temple-mountain of Phnom Bakheng. Successive kings built enormous reservoirs (baray) and state temples: the vast West Baray under Suryavarman I and Udayadityavarman II, Angkor Wat under Suryavarman II in the early 12th century, and the walled royal city of Angkor Thom, with its centrepiece the Bayon, under Jayavarman VII in the late 12th and early 13th centuries. At its height, Greater Angkor was a sprawling low-density urban complex of perhaps 750,000–1,000,000 inhabitants, among the largest pre-industrial cities in the world, sustained by a sophisticated hydraulic network of canals and reservoirs.

Decline

Angkor declined during the 14th and 15th centuries under pressure from the rising Thai kingdom of Ayutthaya, shifting trade patterns, religious change from Hinduism and Mahayana Buddhism to Theravada Buddhism, and stresses on its water infrastructure from climate variability. After sacks in 1431, the court moved south toward Phnom Penh. Angkor Wat itself was never entirely abandoned, remaining occupied by Buddhist monks.

Rediscovery and conservation

Although known to Cambodians and visited by some earlier Europeans, Angkor was brought to wide Western attention by French naturalist Henri Mouhot's accounts in the 1860s. Restoration by the École française d'Extrême-Orient, interrupted by the Cambodian civil war and Khmer Rouge period, resumed under international coordination after 1993. Tourism centred on the nearby town of Siem Reap now draws millions of visitors annually.
