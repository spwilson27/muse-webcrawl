Phnom Penh (Khmer: ភ្នំពេញ) is the capital and most populous city of Cambodia. It has been the national capital since the French protectorate period and grew into the country's centre of government, economy, and culture.

History

Legend credits the city's founding in 1372 to a wealthy widow, Penh, who placed Buddha statues in a temple on the hill (phnom) that still bears her name — Wat Phnom. Phnom Penh became the capital in the 1430s after the abandonment of Angkor, and again permanently in 1866 under King Norodom. Under French rule and especially in the 1950s–60s under Norodom Sihanouk, it was celebrated as the "Pearl of Asia", with the distinctive New Khmer Architecture of Vann Molyvann and others. On 17 April 1975 the Khmer Rouge captured the city and forcibly evacuated its entire population to the countryside; Phnom Penh remained largely empty until the Vietnamese-backed regime restored it after 1979.

Geography

The city stands at the junction of the Mekong, Bassac, and Tonlé Sap rivers in south-central Cambodia. Its low elevation exposes it to seasonal flooding; the Tonlé Sap uniquely reverses its flow during the monsoon.

Sights

Landmarks include the Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda, Wat Phnom, the National Museum, the Art Deco Central Market (Psar Thmei), and the Independence Monument. The Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum (the former S-21 prison) and the Choeung Ek killing fields memorialise the victims of the Khmer Rouge period.
