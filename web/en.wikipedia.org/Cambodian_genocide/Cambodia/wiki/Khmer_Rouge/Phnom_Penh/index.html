Phnom Penh is the capital and most populous city of Cambodia. It lies at the confluence of the Tonlé Sap, Mekong and Bassac rivers and has been the national capital since the French protectorate era, interrupted only by the Khmer Rouge years.

History

Legend dates the city's founding to 1372, when a wealthy widow named Penh found Buddha statues in a floating koki tree and built a shrine on the hill (phnom) that gave the city its name — Wat Phnom. Phnom Penh became the royal capital in the 1430s and again, permanently, under King Norodom in 1865. Under French rule it gained broad boulevards, the Royal Palace, the Silver Pagoda and the art deco Central Market, earning a reputation as the "Pearl of Asia".

The city swelled with refugees during the civil war, then on 17 April 1975 the victorious Khmer Rouge ordered its complete evacuation; for nearly four years Phnom Penh stood almost empty except for institutions such as the S-21 prison, now the Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum. People returned after the Vietnamese liberation of January 1979, and since the 1990s the city has grown rapidly, with a skyline of new towers rising over the colonial core.

Geography and sights

Phnom Penh occupies the southwestern part of the Cambodian floodplain. Its best-known sights include the Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda, the National Museum, Wat Phnom, the Independence Monument, the riverside promenade of Sisowath Quay, and the Choeung Ek memorial at the city's edge. The city is Cambodia's political, commercial and educational centre, home to the Royal University of Phnom Penh and Phnom Penh International Airport.
