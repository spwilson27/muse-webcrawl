Sihanouk, who dominated Cambodian politics for six decades.

Norodom Sihanouk (October 31, 1922 – October 15, 2012) was a Cambodian statesman and composer who led Cambodia in various capacities throughout the second half of the 20th century — as king, prime minister, head of state, exile leader, and again as king — earning the epithet "King Father" in his final years. Few political figures of the modern era held power in so many different forms across so long a span.

First reign and independence

Chosen by the French colonial authorities in 1941, at eighteen, in the expectation that he would be pliable, Sihanouk surprised them by campaigning for independence, which France granted on November 9, 1953, after his "royal crusade for independence." In 1955 he abdicated in favour of his father, Norodom Suramarit, to enter politics directly: his Sangkum movement won every seat in parliament and he dominated the government as prime minister and, after his father's death in 1960, as head of state. His era — the "Sangkum" period — brought modernization and neutrality abroad, but also one-party rule and repression of the left.

Overthrow, war, and the Khmer Rouge

Deposed in a 1970 coup by his prime minister Lon Nol while abroad, Sihanouk allied himself in Beijing exile with the Khmer Rouge insurgents against the new Khmer Republic — a fateful endorsement that lent the communists royal legitimacy in the countryside. After the Khmer Rouge victory in 1975 he returned as nominal head of state of Democratic Kampuchea, but was placed under house arrest in his palace, lost many relatives to the regime, and resigned in 1976. Following Vietnam's overthrow of the Khmer Rouge in 1979, he again led a coalition government-in-exile against the Vietnamese-backed state during the 1980s.

Restoration and abdication

The 1991 Paris Peace Agreements and UN-administered elections restored the monarchy in 1993, and Sihanouk reigned again as king over a fragile multiparty kingdom increasingly dominated by Prime Minister Hun Sen. Citing age and illness, he abdicated in October 2004 in favour of his son Norodom Sihamoni, retaining the title of King Father. A prolific filmmaker, composer, and author, Sihanouk died in Beijing in 2012; his cremation in Phnom Penh in February 2013 drew vast crowds, and his ashes were interred in the Silver Pagoda beside those of his favourite daughter, Kantha Bopha.
