Southeast Asia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Southeast Asia is the geographical region of Asia situated south of China and east of India, comprising eleven countries: Brunei, Cambodia, East Timor, Indonesia, Laos, Malaysia, Myanmar, the Philippines, Singapore, Thailand, and Vietnam. It is divided between mainland Southeast Asia (the Indochinese Peninsula) and maritime Southeast Asia (the Malay Archipelago). Home to roughly 680 million people, the region is one of the world's most culturally and biologically diverse.

Geography

The region straddles the equator between the Indian and Pacific Oceans, threaded by great rivers — the Irrawaddy, Mekong, Chao Phraya, and Red River — whose deltas support dense rice-growing populations. It sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire, with frequent earthquakes and volcanoes (including Krakatoa and Mount Pinatubo), and has a tropical monsoon climate. Its forests and seas, including the Coral Triangle, hold exceptional biodiversity.

History

Southeast Asia's early states — Funan, Srivijaya, the Khmer Empire, Pagan, and Đại Việt — blended indigenous traditions with Indian religious and political influence, later joined by Islam arriving through trade. From the 16th century European powers colonised nearly the whole region (the Philippines by Spain, Indonesia by the Netherlands, Indochina by France, Burma and Malaya by Britain), with only Thailand remaining formally independent. The Second World War and Japanese occupation accelerated independence movements; the Cold War then made the region a battleground in the Vietnam War and related conflicts.

Regional cooperation

The Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN), founded in 1967, promotes economic integration and political dialogue among ten of the eleven states and has anchored the region's rapid export-led growth, from Singapore's entrepôt economy to Vietnam's manufacturing boom.
