Cambodia, officially the Kingdom of Cambodia, is a country in mainland Southeast Asia. It borders Thailand to the northwest, Laos to the north, Vietnam to the east and the Gulf of Thailand to the southwest. Its capital and largest city is Phnom Penh. The country is dominated geographically by the low-lying Cambodian plain, the Mekong River and the Tonlé Sap, Southeast Asia's largest freshwater lake.

History

Cambodia's history is anchored in the Khmer Empire, which flourished from the 9th to the 15th century and built the vast temple city of Angkor, crowned by Angkor Wat, the largest religious monument in the world. After Angkor's decline, Cambodia passed through centuries of pressure from Siam and Vietnam before becoming a French protectorate in 1863 within French Indochina. Independence came in 1953 under King Norodom Sihanouk.

The country was drawn into the Vietnam War and suffered devastating bombing and civil war. In 1975 the Khmer Rouge under Pol Pot seized power; their regime's radical agrarian revolution caused the Cambodian genocide, in which roughly a quarter of the population perished. Vietnam invaded in 1978, ending the Khmer Rouge government, and after the 1991 Paris Peace Accords a UN transitional authority organised elections in 1993, restoring the monarchy.

Geography and economy

The Tonlé Sap's flood pulse reverses the flow of its river seasonally, supporting one of the world's most productive inland fisheries. Cambodia's economy rests on garments and footwear exports, tourism (centred on Angkor), agriculture — especially rice — and construction. The country has recorded rapid growth since the 1990s, though poverty, deforestation and governance remain significant challenges.

Culture

The overwhelming majority of Cambodians are ethnic Khmer and practise Theravada Buddhism. Khmer classical dance, shadow puppetry and the cuisine of amok and prahok reflect a culture rooted in the Angkorian heritage, whose silhouette — Angkor Wat — appears on the national flag.
