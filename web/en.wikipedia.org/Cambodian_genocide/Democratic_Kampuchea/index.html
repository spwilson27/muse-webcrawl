Democratic Kampuchea was the official name of Cambodia under the Khmer Rouge from 1975 (formally from January 1976) until the Vietnamese invasion of January 1979. Ruled in secret by the Communist Party of Kampuchea — known to Cambodians only as Angkar, "the Organisation" — it was one of the most radical and murderous experiments in social engineering of the twentieth century.

The regime

After capturing Phnom Penh on 17 April 1975, the regime emptied all cities and towns, driving millions into the countryside in a matter of days. Money, markets, private property, schools, hospitals in the Western sense, and all religions were abolished; families were broken up and the population organised into cooperatives and mobile work brigades, labouring on rice production and vast irrigation schemes under threat of death. Society was divided between "base people" and suspect urban "new people", while the Santebal security apparatus and prisons such as S-21 conducted continuous purges. Between 1.5 and 2 million people died of execution, hunger, disease and overwork in the Cambodian genocide.

Foreign relations and fall

Democratic Kampuchea sealed itself off from nearly all countries, maintaining close ties mainly with China, which supplied military and economic aid, and North Korea. Its increasingly violent border war with Vietnam — including massacres of Vietnamese villagers such as at Ba Chúc — provoked Vietnam's full-scale invasion on 25 December 1978. Phnom Penh fell on 7 January 1979, and the Khmer Rouge fled west to continue the war as an insurgency, retaining international recognition of "Democratic Kampuchea" — and Cambodia's UN seat — for years afterwards.
