Katanga Province


Placeholder image: Map of the former Katanga Province

Katanga was one of the eleven provinces of the Democratic Republic of the Congo between 1966 and 2015, when it was split into four new provinces: Tanganyika, Haut-Lomami, Lualaba and Haut-Katanga. Between 1971 and 1997, under the Zaire regime, it was known as Shaba Province. Its capital was Lubumbashi (formerly Élisabethville).

History

Katanga is rich in cobalt, copper, tin, radium, uranium and diamonds. Days after Congolese independence in June 1960, the province seceded as the State of Katanga under Moise Tshombe, with covert support from Belgium, whose mining company Union Minière du Haut-Katanga dominated the provincial economy. The secession precipitated the Congo Crisis and was ended in 1963 by United Nations forces.

In 1977 and 1978, Katangese exiles based in Angola invaded Shaba in attempts to overthrow Mobutu Sese Seko; both invasions were repelled with Western and Moroccan assistance.

Economy

The Copperbelt running through the province and neighbouring Zambia is one of the world's richest mining regions, historically producing a large share of the world's cobalt and substantial copper and uranium — Katangan uranium was used in the Manhattan Project.
