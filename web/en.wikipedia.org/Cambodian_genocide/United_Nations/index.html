United Nations


Placeholder image: Flags of member states outside UN headquarters

The United Nations (UN) is an international organization founded in 1945. Currently made up of 193 member states, the UN and its specialized agencies, funds and programmes conduct missions around the world, guided by the purposes and principles contained in its founding Charter.

History

The UN was established after the Second World War to succeed the League of Nations, with the aim of preventing future world wars, protecting human rights, delivering humanitarian aid, promoting sustainable development and upholding international law. The Charter was signed on 26 June 1945 in San Francisco and came into force on 24 October 1945; Poland's signature brought the original membership to 51 states.

Structure

The principal organs are the General Assembly, the Security Council, the Economic and Social Council, the International Court of Justice, the Secretariat and the (now largely inactive) Trusteeship Council. The system also includes specialized agencies such as UNESCO, WHO, UNICEF and the World Bank Group. The organization's headquarters are in New York City, with major offices in Geneva, Vienna and Nairobi.
