Choeung Ek


Placeholder image: The memorial stupa at Choeung Ek

Choeung Ek (Khmer: ជើងឯក) is a former orchard and Chinese cemetery about 17 kilometres south of Phnom Penh, Cambodia, that became the best-known of the sites known as the Killing Fields, where the Khmer Rouge regime executed about 17,000 people between 1975 and 1979.

History

Prisoners detained and tortured at the Tuol Sleng security prison (S-21) in Phnom Penh were transported to Choeung Ek, usually at night, and executed. Mass graves containing 8,895 bodies were discovered at the site after the fall of the Khmer Rouge regime; many of the dead were former political prisoners who had been kept at S-21.

Today Choeung Ek is a memorial site marked by a Buddhist stupa erected in 1988. The stupa has acrylic glass sides and is filled with more than 5,000 human skulls. Many of the mass graves remain undisturbed, and bones and clothing fragments still surface after heavy rain.
