Mekong

Length~4,350 km (2,700 mi)
SourceTibetan Plateau, Qinghai, China
MouthMekong Delta, South China Sea
CountriesChina, Myanmar, Laos, Thailand, Cambodia, Vietnam
Discharge (avg.)~16,000 m³/s

The Mekong is a transboundary river in East and Southeast Asia. It is the world's twelfth-longest river and the sixth-longest in Asia, with an estimated length of 4,350 km (2,703 mi), draining an area of 795,000 km² and supporting the livelihoods of tens of millions of people.

Course

The river rises on the Tibetan Plateau in Qinghai, China, where it is known as the Lancang Jiang, and flows south through Yunnan province before forming borders between Myanmar and Laos and between Laos and Thailand. It then crosses Cambodia, passing Phnom Penh, and enters Vietnam, where it divides into a network of distributaries — the Mekong Delta, the "Nine Dragons" — before emptying into the South China Sea.

Ecology

The Mekong Basin is one of the most biodiverse river systems on Earth, home to over a thousand fish species including the Mekong giant catfish and the Irrawaddy dolphin. Its flood pulse drives the extraordinary productivity of Cambodia's Tonlé Sap lake, whose flow reverses with the monsoon. Only the Amazon discharges more water and supports comparable aquatic diversity.

Development and disputes

The river is governed cooperatively by the Mekong River Commission (Cambodia, Laos, Thailand, Vietnam), while China and Myanmar are dialogue partners. Dozens of hydropower dams on the mainstream and tributaries — including China's Lancang cascade and Laos's Xayaburi and Don Sahong dams — along with sand mining, climate change, and drought, have raised persistent concerns over fisheries, sediment delivery, and the delta's survival.
