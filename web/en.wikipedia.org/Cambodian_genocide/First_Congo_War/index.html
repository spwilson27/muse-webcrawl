First Congo War


Placeholder image: Map of the First Congo War

The First Congo War (1996–1997) was a foreign invasion of Zaire led by Rwanda and supported by Uganda and Angola that replaced President Mobutu Sese Seko with the rebel leader Laurent-Désiré Kabila, renaming the country the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

Background

Following the 1994 Rwandan genocide, some two million Hutu refugees — including génocidaires who had participated in the killings — fled into eastern Zaire. There they reorganised in refugee camps and launched cross-border attacks into Rwanda. The Rwandan Patriotic Army sought to dismantle the camps and repatriate the refugees, while Mobutu's decaying state was unable to control its eastern provinces.

Course of the war

In October 1996 Rwandan and allied forces attacked the refugee camps around Goma and Bukavu, driving hundreds of thousands of refugees back into Rwanda and others westward into the forest. The Alliance of Democratic Forces for the Liberation of Congo (AFDL), a Rwandan- and Ugandan-backed coalition under Laurent-Désiré Kabila, then advanced across the country. Kinshasa fell in May 1997; Mobutu fled into exile and died in Morocco later that year.

Aftermath

Kabila's victory ended Mobutu's 32-year rule but did not stabilise the country. Relations between Kabila and his Rwandan and Ugandan backers broke down in 1998, triggering the far deadlier Second Congo War.
