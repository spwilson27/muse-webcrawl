Zaire


Placeholder image: Flag of Zaire (1971–1997)

Zaire, officially the Republic of Zaire (French: République du Zaïre), was the name of the Democratic Republic of the Congo from 1971 to 1997. Located in Central Africa, it was, by area, the largest country in sub-Saharan Africa and the third-largest in all of Africa (after Sudan and Algeria).

History

The country had gained independence from Belgium in 1960 as the Republic of the Congo. After seizing power in a 1965 coup, Mobutu Sese Seko renamed the country Zaire in 1971 as part of his authenticité campaign, which also renamed cities (Léopoldville became Kinshasa, Stanleyville became Kisangani) and the river and currency. The name Zaire derives from a Portuguese rendering of the Kikongo word nzere ("river").

Mobutu's one-party state under the Popular Movement of the Revolution was marked by a personality cult, corruption and kleptocracy, though Zaire remained a Western ally during the Cold War. Following the Rwandan genocide and the First Congo War, Laurent-Désiré Kabila's forces overthrew Mobutu in May 1997 and restored the name Democratic Republic of the Congo.
