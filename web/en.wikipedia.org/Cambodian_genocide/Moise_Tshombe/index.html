Moise Tshombe


Placeholder image: Moise Tshombe in 1962

Moise Kapenda Tshombe (sometimes written Tshombé; 10 November 1919 – 29 June 1969) was a Congolese businessman and politician. He served as the president of the secessionist State of Katanga from 1960 to 1963 and as prime minister of the Democratic Republic of the Congo from 1964 to 1965.

Katanga secession

Tshombe led CONAKAT, a party representing the Lunda people and the interests of the Katanga mining region. Eleven days after Congolese independence on 30 June 1960, he declared Katanga's secession, backed by Belgian troops and the Union Minière mining company. The secession triggered the Congo Crisis, UN intervention, and the overthrow and murder of Congo's first prime minister, Patrice Lumumba, in Katanga in January 1961. UN forces ended the secession in January 1963 and Tshombe went into exile in Spain.

Prime minister and later life

Recalled in 1964 to lead a national government against the Simba rebellion, Tshombe relied on white mercenaries and Western support. He was dismissed by President Joseph Kasa-Vubu in 1965, went into exile again, and in 1967 was kidnapped and imprisoned in Algeria, where he died in 1969, officially of a heart attack.
