Dag Hjalmar Agne Carl Hammarskjöld (29 July 1905 – 18 September 1961) was a Swedish economist and diplomat who served as the second Secretary-General of the United Nations from April 1953 until his death in 1961. He is remembered for developing the concepts of preventive diplomacy and UN peacekeeping, and he remains the only Secretary-General to have died in office.

Early life and career

Hammarskjöld was born in Jönköping into a prominent family; his father, Hjalmar Hammarskjöld, had been Prime Minister of Sweden. He studied economics and law at Uppsala University and Stockholm University, earned a doctorate in economics, and served in the Swedish civil service, including as chairman of the board of the Riksbank (Sweden's central bank) and as a minister in the Swedish government.

Secretary-General

Appointed after the resignation of Trygve Lie, Hammarskjöld was re-elected unanimously in 1957. During his tenure he helped secure the release of American airmen captured during the Korean War, organized the first United Nations Emergency Force during the Suez Crisis of 1956, and oversaw the UN response to the Congo Crisis that followed the independence of the Congo in 1960.

Death

Hammarskjöld died when his aircraft crashed near Ndola in Northern Rhodesia (now Zambia) while he was travelling to negotiate a ceasefire in the Congo. The circumstances of the crash have been the subject of repeated investigations. He was posthumously awarded the Nobel Peace Prize in 1961. His diary and spiritual reflections were published after his death as Markings (Vägmärken).
