Nuon Chea


Placeholder image: Nuon Chea at the Khmer Rouge Tribunal

Nuon Chea (Khmer: នួន ជា; born Lau Kim Korn; 7 July 1926 – 4 August 2019), also known as Long Bunruot and by his revolutionary alias Brother Number Two, was a Cambodian communist politician and revolutionary who was the chief ideologist and deputy leader of the Khmer Rouge under Pol Pot.

Role in Democratic Kampuchea

Nuon Chea studied law in Bangkok, where he joined the Thai Communist Party, and became a leading figure in the Cambodian communist movement. As deputy secretary of the Communist Party of Kampuchea he was second only to Pol Pot ("Brother Number One") and bore senior responsibility for the regime's security apparatus and purges, including the operations of the S-21 prison.

Trial and death

After the Khmer Rouge's fall, Nuon Chea lived near the Thai border and defected to the Cambodian government in 1998. He was arrested in 2007 and tried before the Extraordinary Chambers in the Courts of Cambodia. In 2014 he was convicted of crimes against humanity and sentenced to life imprisonment; in 2018 he was also convicted of genocide against the Vietnamese people. He died in 2019 while serving his sentence.
