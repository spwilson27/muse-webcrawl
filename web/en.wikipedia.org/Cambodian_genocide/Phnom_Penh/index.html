Phnom Penh


Placeholder image: Phnom Penh skyline and the Tonle Sap riverfront

Phnom Penh (Khmer: ភ្នំពេញ) is the capital and most populous city of Cambodia. It has been the national capital since the French protectorate era and grew into the country's political, economic, industrial and cultural centre. The city sits at the confluence of the Tonlé Sap, Mekong and Bassac rivers and is home to more than two million people.

History

Legend attributes the city's founding in 1372 to a wealthy widow, Penh, who found a tree containing Buddhist statues on the riverbank and built a temple on an artificial hill — phnom means "hill" — to house them. Phnom Penh became the royal capital in the 15th century, and again permanently in the mid-19th century under King Norodom.

Under French rule it was known as the "Pearl of Asia" for its wide boulevards and colonial architecture. During the Cambodian Civil War the city swelled with refugees; on 17 April 1975 the Khmer Rouge captured Phnom Penh and forcibly evacuated its entire population to the countryside, where many died. The city was repopulated after the Vietnamese-backed forces took it in January 1979.

Landmarks

Major sites include the Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda, Wat Phnom, the National Museum, the Independence Monument, and the Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum, which commemorates the victims of the S-21 prison.
