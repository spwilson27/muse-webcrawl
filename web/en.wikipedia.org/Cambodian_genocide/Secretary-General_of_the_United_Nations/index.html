Secretary-General of the United Nations


Placeholder image: The United Nations headquarters in New York

The secretary-general of the United Nations is the chief administrative officer of the United Nations and head of the United Nations Secretariat, one of the six principal organs of the organisation. The secretary-general also acts as the de facto spokesperson and leader of the UN.

Role and selection

Under the UN Charter, the secretary-general is appointed by the General Assembly on the recommendation of the Security Council, which in practice means the five permanent members can veto a candidate. The term is five years, renewable once by convention. The secretary-general may bring to the Security Council's attention any matter that threatens international peace and security (Article 99) and performs functions entrusted by the principal organs (Article 98).

Holders of the office

Gladwyn Jebb served as acting secretary-general before Trygve Lie of Norway became the first elected holder (1946–1952). He was followed by Dag Hammarskjöld of Sweden (1953–1961), who died in a plane crash in Northern Rhodesia while on a peace mission during the Congo Crisis. Later holders included U Thant, Kurt Waldheim, Javier Pérez de Cuéllar, Boutros Boutros-Ghali, Kofi Annan, Ban Ki-moon and, from 2017, António Guterres of Portugal.
