Bouillon (soup)

Type: Soup / broth
Origin: Haiti and the Caribbean
Main ingredients: Meat, root vegetables, plantains, dumplings
Served: Hot, often on Saturdays

Bouillon is a hearty traditional soup of Haiti and other parts of the Caribbean, made with meat (commonly beef, goat or chicken), root vegetables, plantains, leafy greens and dumplings, simmered together in a seasoned broth. It is a staple comfort food, traditionally prepared on Saturdays in many Haitian households.

Ingredients and preparation

A typical bouillon combines meat browned and simmered with epis (a blended seasoning base), then adds malanga (taro), yams, potatoes, carrots, plantains, spinach or watercress, and rolled flour dumplings called boy or domboy. The result is a thick, richly flavoured one-pot meal.

Cultural significance

In Haitian culture, Saturday bouillon is a long-standing weekly ritual, associated with family gatherings and with the idea of a restorative, nourishing end-of-week meal. Variations exist across the Caribbean under similar names, reflecting the region's shared African, French and Creole culinary heritage.
