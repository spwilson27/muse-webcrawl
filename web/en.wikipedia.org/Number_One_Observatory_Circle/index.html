Number One Observatory Circle

Number One Observatory Circle is the official residence of the vice president of the United States. Located on the northeast grounds of the U.S. Naval Observatory in Washington, D.C., the house was built in 1893 for the observatory superintendent. The vice president took up residence there in 1974, and it became the official residence by law in 1975. The first vice president to live there was Nelson Rockefeller, although he used it mainly for entertaining; Walter Mondale was the first to move in as a full-time resident, and every vice president since has lived there.

History

The Queen Anne-style house was designed by the architect Leon E. Dessez and originally served as the superintendent's residence, then as the home of the chief of naval operations. In 1974, Congress authorized its use as the temporary official residence of the vice president; the designation was made permanent in 1975. Before this, vice presidents lived in their own homes or hotels, and security concerns — heightened after the Kennedy assassination — prompted the creation of an official, securable residence.

Description

The three-story house, painted white since 1981, contains a living room, sitting room, sunroom, dining room, kitchen, and several bedrooms, and sits on a 72-acre (29 ha) wooded campus guarded by the Secret Service. Vice presidents have personalized the residence: the Bushes installed a horseshoe pit and a quarter-mile track, the Quayles a swimming pool, and later occupants added a gym and other amenities. In 2021, a fire on the grounds damaged an adjacent building but not the residence itself.

Residents

Full-time residents have included Walter Mondale, George H. W. Bush, Dan Quayle, Al Gore, Dick Cheney, Joe Biden, Mike Pence, and Kamala Harris. The residence also hosts official events, such as meetings with foreign leaders and the vice president's traditional hosting of foreign delegations.
