Statistical learning theory

Statistical learning theory is a framework for machine learning drawing from the fields of statistics and functional analysis. It deals with the problem of finding a predictive function based on data, and it characterizes the conditions under which a learning algorithm trained on a finite sample will generalize to unseen data.

Empirical risk minimization

The central principle is empirical risk minimization: choose the function in a hypothesis class that minimizes the average loss (risk) on the training sample, as an approximation to minimizing the expected risk under the unknown data distribution. Whether empirical risk minimization is consistent depends on the capacity of the hypothesis class, quantified classically by the VC dimension, developed by Vladimir Vapnik and Alexey Chervonenkis.

Results and applications

The theory yields generalization bounds: with high probability, the true risk of a learned function is bounded by its empirical risk plus a term that grows with hypothesis-class complexity and shrinks with sample size. Structural risk minimization trades off these terms. Support vector machines are a direct outgrowth of the theory. Alternative frameworks include PAC learning and Bayesian inference, and the theory underpins regularization methods used throughout modern machine learning.
