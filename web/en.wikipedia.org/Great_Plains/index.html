Great Plains

The Great Plains is a broad expanse of flatland in North America, located west of the Mississippi River and east of the Rocky Mountains, largely covered in prairie, steppe, and grassland. It stretches from the Canadian provinces of Alberta, Saskatchewan and Manitoba south through the United States — Montana, North Dakota, South Dakota, Wyoming, Nebraska, Colorado, Kansas, Oklahoma, New Mexico and Texas — and into northern Mexico. The region covers roughly 500,000 square miles (1.3 million km²), about one-third of the United States.

Geography and climate

The plains rise gradually from about 1,500 feet (460 m) in the east to 5,000–6,000 feet near the Rockies, forming part of the Interior Plains. The western portion, the High Plains, is a semi-arid tableland underlain by the Ogallala Aquifer. Rainfall decreases from east to west (from ~30 inches to under 15), and the climate is continental with hot summers, cold winters, violent thunderstorms, tornadoes (the southern plains form "Tornado Alley"), blizzards and recurring droughts. Major subregions include the Llano Estacado, the Sandhills of Nebraska, the Black Hills, and the Missouri Plateau.

Ecology

Tallgrass prairie in the humid east graded into mixed- and shortgrass steppe adapted to fire and grazing. Before settlement, immense herds of American bison — tens of millions — roamed with pronghorn, elk, prairie dogs, wolves and grizzly bears. Most tallgrass prairie has been converted to farmland; less than a few percent remains intact.

History

Nomadic and semi-nomadic peoples — among them the Lakota, Cheyenne, Arapaho, Crow, Comanche, Kiowa and Pawnee — developed cultures centered, after the Spanish reintroduction of the horse, on mounted bison hunting. U.S. expansion, the Homestead Act (1862), railroads, and the near-extermination of the bison by the 1880s broke Indigenous resistance and opened the plains to farming. Overplowing marginal lands set up the Dust Bowl of the 1930s, which drove hundreds of thousands from the region.

Economy and the Ogallala Aquifer

The plains are a global breadbasket of wheat, corn, sorghum, soybeans and cattle ranching, plus major oil and gas production. Irrigation depends heavily on the Ogallala Aquifer, which is being depleted far faster than it recharges, threatening long-term agriculture. Rural counties across the region have lost population for decades — the "rural flight" — prompting proposals such as the "Buffalo Commons" to restore large areas to grassland and bison.
