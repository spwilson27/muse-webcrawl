Aenesidemus

Aenesidemus (fl. 1st century BC) was a Greek Pyrrhonist philosopher from Knossos, Crete. He is credited with reviving Pyrrhonism and formulating the Ten Modes (or Tropes) of Aenesidemus — arguments designed to induce suspension of judgment (epoché) by showing the variability and relativity of perception.

Aenesidemus
Era: Hellenistic philosophy
School: Pyrrhonism
Known for: Ten Modes

Ten Modes

The modes argue that judgments differ depending on the animal, the person, the sense organ, circumstances, position, mixture, quantity, relation, frequency, and customs or laws. Because conflicting appearances cannot be decisively adjudicated, the skeptic suspends judgment and attains tranquility (ataraxia).

Works and influence

His Pyrrhonian Discourses are lost but are summarized by Photius, and the Ten Modes are preserved in the writings of Sextus Empiricus and Diogenes Laërtius. His thought influenced later skepticism and, much later, modern philosophers who rediscovered the tropes in the Renaissance.
