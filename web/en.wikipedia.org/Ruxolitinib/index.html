Ruxolitinib

Trade namesJakafi, Jakavi, Opzelura
ClassJanus kinase (JAK1/JAK2) inhibitor
RoutesOral, topical
US FDA approval2011 (myelofibrosis)
ManufacturerIncyte / Novartis

Ruxolitinib, sold under the brand names Jakafi and Jakavi (and as a cream under the name Opzelura), is a medication used to treat certain bone-marrow cancers and autoimmune conditions. It is a selective inhibitor of the Janus kinases JAK1 and JAK2, enzymes that mediate signalling by many cytokines and growth factors involved in blood-cell production and immune function.

Ruxolitinib was the first JAK inhibitor approved for clinical use.

Medical uses

Ruxolitinib was approved by the U.S. Food and Drug Administration in November 2011 for intermediate- or high-risk myelofibrosis, making it the first approved JAK inhibitor. It reduces spleen enlargement and constitutional symptoms in myelofibrosis, though it is not curative. Approvals followed for polycythemia vera in patients with an inadequate response to hydroxyurea (2014) and for steroid-refractory acute and chronic graft-versus-host disease (2019 and 2021). A topical cream formulation was approved for atopic dermatitis (2021) and for nonsegmental vitiligo (2022), the first approved treatment to restore skin pigmentation in vitiligo.

Adverse effects and mechanism

Common side effects of oral ruxolitinib include anaemia, thrombocytopenia, bruising, dizziness, and headache; stopping the drug abruptly can cause a withdrawal syndrome with rebound symptoms. By blocking JAK–STAT signalling, ruxolitinib dampens the overactive cytokine signalling driven in many patients by the JAK2 V617F mutation common in myeloproliferative neoplasms. During the COVID-19 pandemic it was studied as a treatment for hyperinflammation, though another JAK inhibitor, baricitinib, became the established therapy.
