Meatloaf

CourseMain course
Serving temperatureHot or cold
Main ingredientsGround meat, breadcrumbs or oatmeal, egg, onion, seasonings
VariationsGlazed with ketchup or barbecue sauce; stuffed; wrapped in bacon

Meatloaf is a dish of ground meat mixed with other ingredients, formed into the shape of a loaf, then baked or smoked. It is usually made with ground beef, though lamb, pork, veal, venison, poultry or combinations are also used. A quintessential comfort food in the United States, meatloaf became especially popular during the Great Depression as a way to stretch inexpensive meat with fillers such as breadcrumbs, oatmeal or crushed crackers.

History

Dishes of minced meat shaped and cooked have ancient antecedents; the Roman cookbook Apicius includes recipes for patties of minced meat with wine and pine nuts. The modern American meatloaf emerged in the late 19th century — one early recipe (1877) used chopped beef mixed with bread, onion and egg — and was standardized by industrial meatpacking and the home economics movement. German-American scrapple-influenced meat mixtures and similar European dishes (German Hackbraten, Swedish meatballs' loaf cousin, Danish forloren hare) are close relatives.

Preparation

Ground meat is combined with a binder (breadcrumbs soaked in milk, oatmeal, or cracker crumbs), beaten egg, and aromatics such as onion, garlic, Worcestershire sauce and herbs; the mixture is packed into a loaf pan or free-formed on a baking sheet and baked. A sweet-savoury topping of ketchup, tomato sauce, or a brown-sugar glaze is characteristic in the United States. Meatloaf is typically served with mashed potatoes and gravy, and leftovers are often eaten cold in sandwiches. Food-safety guidance recommends cooking ground-meat loaves to an internal temperature of 160 °F (71 °C).
