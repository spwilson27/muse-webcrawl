The Zoological Society of London (ZSL) is a charity devoted to the worldwide conservation of animals and their habitats. It was founded in London in April 1826 by Sir Stamford Raffles, the Marquess of Lansdowne, Lord Auckland, Sir Humphry Davy, Robert Peel, Joseph Sabine, Nicholas Aylward Vigors and other eminent naturalists. The society was granted a royal charter by George IV in 1829.

ZSL is based at Regent's Park in London, where it runs London Zoo.

History

The society's original purpose was to provide a collection of animals for study "at an advanced society", along the lines of the Jardin des Plantes in Paris. Raffles became the first president and chairman in 1826, but died shortly afterwards; he was succeeded by the Marquess of Lansdowne. Land in Regent's Park was obtained from the Crown, and the society's gardens — the origin of London Zoo — opened in 1828, initially to fellows of the society only. Members of the public were admitted in 1847 to help provide funding.

Institutes and zoos

ZSL runs ZSL London Zoo and ZSL Whipsnade Zoo, and carries out scientific research at the Institute of Zoology, based at Regent's Park. Its conservation work spans more than 50 countries, focusing on threatened species and habitats, and it publishes the scientific journal Journal of Zoology as well as the conservation science journal Animal Conservation.
