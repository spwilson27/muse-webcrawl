Enactivism

Enactivism
FieldCognitive science, philosophy of mind
Key workThe Embodied Mind (1991)
ProponentsFrancisco Varela, Evan Thompson, Eleanor Rosch, Alva Noë
RelatedEmbodied cognition, autopoiesis, phenomenology

Enactivism is a position in cognitive science that argues cognition arises through a dynamic interaction between an acting organism and its environment. Rather than treating the mind as a computer manipulating internal representations of a pre-given external world, enactivism holds that an organism enacts — brings forth — its world of significance through its embodied activity, a relationship described as structural coupling.

Origins

The approach was launched by biologist Francisco Varela, philosopher Evan Thompson, and psychologist Eleanor Rosch in their 1991 book The Embodied Mind: Cognitive Science and Human Experience. Drawing on Varela's work with Humberto Maturana on autopoiesis — the self-producing organization of living systems — and on the phenomenology of Maurice Merleau-Ponty and Buddhist philosophy, they argued that perception is not the recovery of a ready-made world but a history of sensorimotor engagement.

Main claims

Enactivists typically defend several related theses: cognition is embodied, depending crucially on the kind of body an organism has; perception is a form of action governed by mastery of sensorimotor contingencies (developed by J. Kevin O'Regan and Alva Noë); cognition is situated and extends into the environment; and experience is not separable from the living body's self-maintenance. Later "radical" enactivists such as Daniel Hutto and Erik Myin argue that basic cognition involves no representational content at all, while other versions retain a role for representations in linguistically mediated "higher" cognition. Shaun Gallagher, Di Paolo and others extended the framework to social cognition, treating mutual interaction ("participatory sense-making") as constitutive of understanding others.

Reception

Enactivism is one of the "4E" approaches to cognition — embodied, embedded, enactive, extended — that oppose the classical computationalist picture. Criticisms include the charge that its claims are hard to test empirically, that it underestimates the brain's internal modelling, and that appeals to autopoiesis do not by themselves explain conscious experience. It has nevertheless influenced robotics (behaviour-based and developmental robotics), cognitive archaeology, educational theory, and philosophy of perception.
