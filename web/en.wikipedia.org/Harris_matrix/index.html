Harris matrix

The Harris matrix is a tool used to depict the temporal succession of archaeological contexts on an excavated site, showing their sequence as a diagram. It was developed by the British archaeologist Edward C. Harris, who introduced it in the 1970s and set out its principles in Principles of Archaeological Stratigraphy (1979).

Harris matrix
Developed by: Edward C. Harris
Introduced: 1970s
Purpose: Diagramming archaeological stratigraphy

Principles

The matrix rests on the law of superposition and on Harris's laws of archaeological stratigraphy, which treat each unit of stratification — deposits, cuts, and interfaces — as a discrete entity in a sequence. Contexts are drawn as boxes linked vertically by lines showing which context lies directly above or below another; the resulting diagram gives the relative chronology of the site without reference to absolute dates.

Use

Harris matrices are now standard practice in single-context recording, particularly in British commercial archaeology, and software tools exist to construct and check large matrices automatically.
