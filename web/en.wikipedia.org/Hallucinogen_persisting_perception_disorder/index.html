Hallucinogen persisting perception disorder

SpecialtyPsychiatry
SymptomsPersistent visual disturbances after hallucinogen use
DurationMonths to years
ClassificationDSM-5 code 292.89

Hallucinogen persisting perception disorder (HPPD) is a disorder in which a person experiences visual disturbances that resemble those produced by hallucinogenic drugs, but persist long after the drug has left the body and occur while the person is otherwise sober. It is recognised in the DSM-5 under the diagnosis "other hallucinogen use disorder" with the specifier of persisting perception disorder.

Symptoms

Commonly reported symptoms include visual snow, halos around objects, trailing images (palinopsia), afterimages, geometric hallucinations, intensified colours, and flashes of colour. The disturbances are typically most noticeable against plain backgrounds or in dim light. In the more severe form sometimes called type 2 HPPD, the symptoms are continuous and can cause significant anxiety and impairment, whereas in type 1 the episodes are brief and intermittent.

Causes

HPPD is most often reported following use of LSD, but it has also been associated with psilocybin, mescaline, MDMA, and synthetic psychedelics. Only a small minority of hallucinogen users develop the condition; estimates of prevalence vary widely, and why some individuals are vulnerable remains unclear. Proposed mechanisms involve lasting changes in visual processing pathways and inhibitory interneurons in the visual cortex.

Treatment

There is no established cure. Abstinence from hallucinogens is generally recommended. Case reports describe some benefit from benzodiazepines such as clonazepam, certain anticonvulsants such as lamotrigine, and other agents, but evidence is anecdotal. Psychological support and treatment of comorbid anxiety or depression are also commonly advised.
