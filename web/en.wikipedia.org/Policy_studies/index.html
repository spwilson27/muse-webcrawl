Policy studies

Policy studies is an interdisciplinary academic field concerned with the analysis of the process of policymaking (the policy process) and the contents of policy, which together form the discipline's focus on public policy. Policy studies developed mainly in the United States after the Second World War, growing out of political science, public administration, economics and sociology, and from the policy sciences movement associated with Harold Lasswell, who advocated a problem-oriented, multidisciplinary approach to understanding government decisions.

Subject matter

The field studies how problems reach the government agenda, how policies are formulated, adopted, implemented and evaluated, and why governments choose some courses of action rather than others. Frameworks include the policy cycle (stages heuristic), multiple streams theory, punctuated equilibrium theory, and the advocacy coalition framework. Subfields address specific policy areas such as health policy, environmental policy, education policy and social policy.

Institutions and teaching

Policy studies is taught in schools of public policy and public affairs at many universities, which award degrees such as the Master of Public Policy and Master of Public Administration. Research appears in journals such as Policy Sciences, the Journal of Public Policy and Policy Studies Journal, and is also produced by think tanks and governmental research bodies.

Relation to policy analysis

Whereas policy analysis is typically applied and decision-focused, policy studies places greater emphasis on explaining policymaking and its effects, though the two overlap considerably in practice.
