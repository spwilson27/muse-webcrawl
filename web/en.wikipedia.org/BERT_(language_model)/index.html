BERT (language model)

BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers) is a language model introduced by researchers at Google in 2018. It was a landmark in natural language processing, demonstrating that a single pre-trained model could be fine-tuned to achieve state-of-the-art results on a wide range of tasks.

Architecture and training

BERT is based on the encoder stack of the transformer architecture and is designed to consider context from both the left and the right of each word in a sentence. It is pre-trained on a large text corpus — the BooksCorpus and English Wikipedia — using two self-supervised tasks: masked language modelling, in which random tokens are hidden and must be predicted, and next-sentence prediction. The base model has 110 million parameters and the large model 340 million.

Applications

After pre-training, BERT can be fine-tuned with a single additional output layer for tasks such as question answering, sentiment analysis, named-entity recognition, and sentence classification. On its release it improved results on benchmarks including GLUE, SQuAD, and MultiNLI. In 2019 Google incorporated BERT into its search engine to better understand queries.

Legacy

BERT spawned a large family of successors and variants, including RoBERTa, DistilBERT, ALBERT, and ELECTRA, and helped establish the "pre-train, then fine-tune" paradigm that preceded today's large language models.
