The Po is the longest river in Italy, flowing 652 kilometres (405 miles) eastward across northern Italy from its source on Monte Viso in the Cottian Alps, near the French border, to its delta on the Adriatic Sea near Adria in the Veneto. Its drainage basin of about 74,000 km² covers much of the Po Valley (Pianura Padana), the broad, fertile plain between the Alps and the Apennines that is Italy's principal agricultural and industrial region.

Course

The Po rises at Pian del Re at about 2,020 metres altitude. It flows through Piedmont — passing Turin — and then forms much of the boundary between Lombardy and Emilia-Romagna, passing Pavia (where the Ticino joins), Piacenza, Cremona, and Ferrara. Major tributaries include the Dora Riparia, Tanaro, Ticino, Adda, Oglio, and Mincio from the Alps, and the Trebbia, Taro, and Reno from the Apennines. In its lower course the river has built a large, shifting delta, a protected wetland of international importance for birdlife.

History and economy

Known to the Romans as the Padus (and in myth as the Eridanos, into which Phaethon fell), the Po has been a strategic corridor since antiquity; the Etruscans and later Venice traded along it. The valley's fertile alluvial soils support intensive agriculture — rice, maize, wheat, and dairy farming for Parmesan and related cheeses — as well as much of Italy's manufacturing industry. The river is navigable by barges from the sea to Cremona and beyond, and its waters are heavily used for irrigation and hydroelectric regulation through the Alpine tributaries.

Floods and drought

The Po has a history of catastrophic flooding, most notably in November 1951, when breaches in the Polesine killed about 100 people and displaced some 180,000. Conversely, severe droughts in 2022 and 2023 reduced flow to record lows, allowing seawater to intrude far up the delta and threatening agriculture — events linked to reduced Alpine snowpack and climate change.
