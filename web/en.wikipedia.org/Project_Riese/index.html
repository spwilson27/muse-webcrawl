Project Riese

Project Riese (German for "giant") was the codename for an enormous secret construction project undertaken by Nazi Germany between 1943 and 1945 in the Owl Mountains and at Książ Castle in Lower Silesia (then Germany, now Poland). It consisted of seven large underground complexes that were never completed; their intended purpose remains uncertain.

Construction

Work began in late 1943, supervised by the Organisation Todt, using forced labour from prisoners of the Gross-Rosen concentration camp system, thousands of whom died. The complexes — at Rzeczka, Włodarz, Jugowice, Soboń, Osówka, Sokolec, and Książ — comprised extensive tunnel systems hewn from rock, in some places with large halls, and connected to above-ground structures. Only a fraction (about 9 km of tunnels) had been excavated when the war ended.

Purpose

Historians debate whether Riese was intended as a Führer headquarters, underground armaments factories, or a research facility. Speculation in popular culture has linked it to the legendary Nazi "gold train", reportedly sought in the Wałbrzych area in 2015.
