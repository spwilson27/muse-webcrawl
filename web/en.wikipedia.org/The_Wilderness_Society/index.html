The Wilderness Society (Australia)

The Wilderness Society is an Australian environmental advocacy organisation founded in 1976 during the campaign to save the Franklin River in Tasmania. Originally known as the Tasmanian Wilderness Society, it grew into a national organisation campaigning for the protection of Australia's wilderness areas, forests and marine environments.

History

The society emerged from the campaign against the damming of Lake Pedder and was central to the Franklin Dam controversy of 1978–1983, which culminated in the High Court decision in Commonwealth v Tasmania (1983) preserving the Franklin River. Bob Brown was an early director of the society.

Campaigns

The Wilderness Society has campaigned to protect old-growth forests in Tasmania, Victoria, New South Wales and Western Australia, the Great Barrier Reef, the Kimberley region, and areas threatened by coal and gas expansion.
