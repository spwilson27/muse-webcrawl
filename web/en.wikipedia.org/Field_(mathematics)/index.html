In mathematics, a field is a set on which addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division are defined and behave as the corresponding operations on rational and real numbers do. A field is thus a fundamental algebraic structure widely used in algebra, number theory, and many other areas of mathematics.

The best known fields are the field of rational numbers, the field of real numbers and the field of complex numbers. Many other fields, such as fields of rational functions, algebraic function fields, algebraic number fields, and p-adic fields are commonly studied and used in mathematics, particularly in number theory and algebraic geometry. Finite fields are used in most cryptographic protocols.

Definition

Formally, a field is a set F with two binary operations, addition (+) and multiplication (·), such that (F, +) is an abelian group with identity 0, the non-zero elements of F form an abelian group under multiplication with identity 1, and multiplication distributes over addition. Equivalently, a field is a commutative ring in which every non-zero element has a multiplicative inverse.

a × a−1 = 1 for every a ≠ 0

Examples

  • ℚ — the rational numbers, the smallest field of characteristic zero
  • ℝ — the real numbers, a complete ordered field
  • ℂ — the complex numbers, algebraically closed
  • Fp — integers modulo a prime p, the basic finite fields
  • Rational function fields and algebraic number fields such as ℚ(√2)

Elementary properties

The characteristic of a field is either zero or a prime number p. In a field there are no zero divisors: if ab = 0 then a = 0 or b = 0. Every finite integral domain is a field.

Field extensions

A field E is an extension of F if F is a subfield of E. Extensions are vector spaces over F; their degree governs solvability questions studied by Galois theory, which famously proves that general polynomial equations of degree five or higher cannot be solved by radicals.

Applications

Fields underpin linear algebra (vector spaces are defined over fields), algebraic geometry, number theory (class field theory), and cryptography (finite-field arithmetic in AES, elliptic-curve cryptography).
