European Portuguese (português europeu) is the variety of the Portuguese language spoken in Portugal, which also serves as the reference norm for Portuguese in most of Africa and Asia. It differs from Brazilian Portuguese in pronunciation, vocabulary, grammar usage, and — until recent spelling reforms — orthography.

Phonology

European Portuguese is characterised by strong reduction of unstressed vowels: unstressed /e/ often becomes a near-deleted [ɨ], and unstressed syllables may be whispered or lost, giving the language a distinctive rhythm sometimes described as stress-timed. The vowel inventory includes nasal vowels; consonants include the alveolar trill and a dark l. The Lisbon variety famously pronounces the diphthong /ej/ as [ɐj].

Grammar and usage

Distinguishing features include preferring the gerund construction "estar a + infinitive" over the Brazilian gerund, placing clitic pronouns after the verb by default (enclisis), retaining the tu/você distinction in many regions, and using the personal infinitive. Vocabulary differs in everyday words: telemóvel (mobile phone), autocarro (bus), casa de banho (bathroom).

Orthography

The 1990 Orthographic Agreement aimed to unify spelling across the Portuguese-speaking world; Portugal completed its transition in 2015, removing silent consonants in words such as "óptimo" → "ótimo."
