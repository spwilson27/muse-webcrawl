Mary, Queen of Scots

Mary, Queen of Scots (December 8, 1542 – February 8, 1587), also known as Mary Stuart, was Queen of Scotland from December 1542 until her forced abdication in 1567, and queen consort of France from 1559 to 1560. A Catholic claimant to the English throne, she spent nearly nineteen years imprisoned in England before being tried and executed for her alleged part in a plot to assassinate Elizabeth I.

Mary Stuart, Queen of Scotland and briefly Queen of France.

Early life and France

Mary was the only surviving legitimate child of James V of Scotland, who died six days after her birth; her mother, Mary of Guise, acted as regent. Betrothed to the French dauphin, she was sent to France at the age of five and raised at the French court. She married Francis in 1558; he became Francis II in 1559 but died in 1560, leaving Mary a widow at eighteen.

Return to Scotland and marriages

Mary returned to Scotland in 1561 and ruled a kingdom divided by the Protestant Reformation, attempting a policy of religious moderation. In 1565 she married her cousin Henry Stuart, Lord Darnley, by whom she had a son, the future James VI. The marriage soured; Darnley was murdered at Kirk o' Field in 1567, and Mary's rapid marriage to the chief suspect, the Earl of Bothwell, provoked rebellion. Defeated at Carberry Hill, she was imprisoned in Loch Leven Castle and forced to abdicate in favour of her infant son.

Imprisonment in England and execution

After escaping and losing the Battle of Langside in 1568, Mary fled to England seeking Elizabeth I's protection. Instead, as the focus of Catholic plotting against the Protestant Elizabeth, she was held in custody for the rest of her life. In 1586, Elizabeth's spymaster Francis Walsingham obtained intercepted letters (the Babington Plot correspondence) implicating Mary in a plan to murder Elizabeth. She was tried by a commission of peers, convicted, and beheaded at Fotheringhay Castle on February 8, 1587.

Legacy

Mary's life has been a perennial subject of drama and biography, from Schiller's play Maria Stuart to modern films. Her son James succeeded Elizabeth as James I of England in 1603, uniting the crowns. She was originally buried at Peterborough Cathedral and reburied by James in Westminster Abbey in 1612.
