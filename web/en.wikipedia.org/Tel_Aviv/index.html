Tel Aviv

Tel Aviv-Yafo, usually referred to as Tel Aviv, is the most populous city in the Gush Dan metropolitan area of Israel. Located on the Israeli Mediterranean coastline, it serves as the economic and technological centre of the country. If East Jerusalem is considered part of Israel, Tel Aviv is the second-most populous city after Jerusalem; if not, Tel Aviv is the most populous before West Jerusalem.

History

Tel Aviv was founded in 1909 by the Yishuv (Jewish residents) as a modern housing estate on the outskirts of the ancient port city of Jaffa, then part of the Ottoman Empire. Its name was adopted in 1910, from the Hebrew translation of Theodor Herzl's novel Altneuland ("Old New Land"). The city grew rapidly through successive waves of Jewish immigration, and Israel's Declaration of Independence was proclaimed there on 14 May 1948. Tel Aviv and Jaffa were unified into a single municipality in 1950.

Economy and culture

Tel Aviv is a major centre of finance, commerce and high technology, with a strong start-up ecosystem that has earned the surrounding region the nickname "Silicon Wadi". The city is known for its Bauhaus architecture — the "White City" of Tel Aviv, with over 4,000 International Style buildings, was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2003 — as well as its beaches, nightlife and cultural institutions, including the Tel Aviv Museum of Art and the Habima Theatre.
