Boötes void

The Boötes void is an enormous, approximately spherical region of space in the constellation Boötes containing very few galaxies. With a diameter of roughly 330 million light-years, it is one of the largest known voids in the observable universe.

Discovery

The void was reported in 1981 by Robert Kirshner and colleagues as part of a survey of galactic redshifts. Its centre lies roughly 700 million light-years from Earth. Despite its size, only about 60 galaxies have been discovered within it, far fewer than would be expected in an average region of comparable volume.

Formation

Like other voids, it is understood to have formed by the gravitational collapse of matter into filaments and clusters, leaving expanding underdense regions; the Boötes void may have grown by the merger of smaller voids.
