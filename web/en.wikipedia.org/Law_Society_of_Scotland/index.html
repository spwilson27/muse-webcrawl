Law Society of Scotland

The Law Society of Scotland is the professional governing body for Scottish solicitors. It was established in 1949 under the Solicitors (Scotland) Act 1949 and is headquartered at Atria One in Edinburgh. The Society promotes the interests of the solicitors' profession and of the public in relation to the law, regulates practising solicitors, and sets standards for entry into the profession.

Functions

The Society maintains the roll of solicitors in Scotland, issues practising certificates, and inspects solicitors' firms for compliance with rules on client money and anti-money-laundering. It sets the education and training requirements for qualification, including the Diploma in Professional Legal Practice. In its regulatory role it investigates complaints about solicitors' conduct — complaints about inadequate professional service being handled by the Scottish Legal Complaints Commission — and may prosecute serious cases before the Scottish Solicitors' Discipline Tribunal. It also represents the profession to the Scottish Parliament, the UK Parliament, and international bodies, and responds to consultations on law reform.

Structure and membership

All solicitors holding a practising certificate in Scotland are members; the membership numbers around 12,000. The Society is governed by a Council elected from constituencies across Scotland, led by a President who serves a one-year term. Since the Legal Services (Scotland) Act 2010, the Society has also been an approved regulator of licensed legal services providers, permitting alternative business structures in which non-solicitors may own legal firms. Its membership should not be confused with the Faculty of Advocates, the separate body for Scottish advocates (barristers).
