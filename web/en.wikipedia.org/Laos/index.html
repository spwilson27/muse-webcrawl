Laos

Laos, officially the Lao People's Democratic Republic, is a landlocked country in Southeast Asia bordered by Myanmar and China to the northwest, Vietnam to the east, Cambodia to the southeast, and Thailand to the west. Its capital and largest city is Vientiane, on the Mekong River. With a population of about 7.5 million, Laos is the only landlocked country in Southeast Asia and one of the region's least densely populated states. It has been ruled by the communist Lao People's Revolutionary Party since 1975.

History

The first unified Lao kingdom, Lan Xang ("Kingdom of a Million Elephants"), was founded by Fa Ngum in 1353 with its capital at Luang Prabang. After its breakup in the early 18th century, the successor kingdoms fell under Siamese and later French control; Laos became part of French Indochina in 1893. Granted independence in 1953, the kingdom was soon dragged into the wider Indochina conflict: the communist Pathet Lao, backed by North Vietnam, fought the royal government in a civil war that ran parallel to the Vietnam War. The United States dropped more than two million tons of bombs on Laos — making it, per capita, the most heavily bombed country in history — largely to interdict the Ho Chi Minh trail, much of which ran through Lao territory.

In December 1975 the Pathet Lao abolished the monarchy; King Savang Vatthana abdicated and later died in a re-education camp. Prince Souphanouvong, the "Red Prince", became the republic's first president. From 1986 the government introduced market-oriented reforms under the New Economic Mechanism while retaining one-party rule.

Geography

Laos is mountainous, with the Annamite Range in the east and the Luang Prabang Range in the west; the Mekong River forms much of the western border and is the country's lifeline for transport, fishing, and hydropower. The Plain of Jars in Xiangkhouang province is a famous megalithic archaeological landscape. The climate is tropical monsoon.

Politics and economy

The Lao People's Revolutionary Party is the sole legal party; its general secretary serves as head of state. The economy has grown rapidly in recent decades on hydropower exports — Laos aspires to be the "battery of Southeast Asia" — mining, agriculture, and tourism. The China–Laos railway, opened in 2021 as part of the Belt and Road Initiative, connected Vientiane to Kunming and gave the landlocked country its first modern rail link. Unexploded ordnance from wartime bombing remains a serious development obstacle.

Demographics and culture

The ethnic Lao make up just over half the population; dozens of minority groups, including the Khmu and Hmong, make Laos one of Asia's most ethnically diverse countries. Theravada Buddhism is practised by about two-thirds of the population and shapes daily life, from the alms-giving ceremony in Luang Prabang to festivals such as Pi Mai (Lao New Year). Sticky rice is the staple food, and national dishes include larb (a minced-meat salad) and tam mak hoong (green papaya salad).
