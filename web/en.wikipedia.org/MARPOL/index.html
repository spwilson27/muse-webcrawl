MARPOL, the International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships, is the principal international convention covering prevention of pollution of the marine environment by ships from operational or accidental causes. It was adopted under the auspices of the International Maritime Organization in 1973 and modified by the Protocol of 1978 adopted after a spate of tanker accidents; the combined instrument is known as MARPOL 73/78.

Annexes

The convention contains six technical annexes: Annex I (oil), Annex II (noxious liquid substances in bulk), Annex III (harmful substances in packaged form), Annex IV (sewage), Annex V (garbage) and Annex VI (air pollution, added in 1997). States party must accept Annexes I and II; the others are optional. Together, parties represent the overwhelming majority of world shipping tonnage.

Implementation

MARPOL sets discharge standards — for instance, strict limits on oil discharges enforced through oil filtering equipment and record books — design requirements such as double hulls for tankers, and special-area rules for sensitive seas. "IMO 2020", the global 0.50% sulphur cap under Annex VI, was a landmark tightening of air-pollution rules.
