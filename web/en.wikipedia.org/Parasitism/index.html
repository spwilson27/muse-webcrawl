Parasitism

TypeSymbiotic relationship
Effect on hostHarmful (reduced fitness)
Major groupsProtozoa, helminths, arthropods, plants, fungi, viruses
Related conceptsParasitoid, brood parasite, hyperparasite

Parasitism is a close relationship between species, where one organism, the parasite, lives on or inside another organism, the host, causing it some harm, and is adapted structurally to this way of life. Parasitism is one of the most successful modes of life on Earth: a large fraction of animal species are parasites at some stage of their life cycle, and nearly every free-living organism hosts parasites of its own.

Types

Ectoparasites, such as ticks, lice, and fleas, live on the surface of the host; endoparasites, such as tapeworms, flukes, and the protozoa that cause malaria, live inside the body. Mesoparasites enter through an opening and embed themselves partially. Parasites may be obligate, requiring a host to complete their life cycle, or facultative. Parasitoids — notably many wasps — eventually kill their hosts, blurring the boundary with predation. Brood parasites, such as cuckoos, exploit the parental care of other species instead of feeding on them directly.

Life cycles and transmission

Many parasites have complex life cycles involving multiple hosts, including intermediate hosts and vectors such as mosquitoes and snails. Parasites often have greatly reduced structures (tapeworms lack a gut) but produce enormous numbers of eggs to overcome the low probability of reaching a new host. Some manipulate host behaviour to increase transmission — a phenomenon illustrated by hairworms driving crickets to water and by parasites that alter the appearance or behaviour of intermediate hosts.

Effects and coevolution

Parasites impose strong selection pressures, driving the evolution of host immune systems, and hosts in turn select for parasite evasion — an arms race sometimes described by the Red Queen hypothesis. Parasitic diseases such as malaria, schistosomiasis, and soil-transmitted helminth infections affect billions of people, especially in tropical regions, and parasites also strongly influence agriculture, livestock, and ecosystems.
