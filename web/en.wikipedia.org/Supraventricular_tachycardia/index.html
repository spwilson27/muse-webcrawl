Supraventricular tachycardia

Supraventricular tachycardia (SVT) is an umbrella term for fast heart rhythms — usually a regular rhythm at 150 to 220 beats per minute — arising from improper electrical activity in the upper part of the heart (above the ventricles). It is distinct from the potentially life-threatening ventricular tachycardia.

Types and causes

The main types are atrioventricular nodal reentrant tachycardia (AVNRT), the most common in adults; atrioventricular reentrant tachycardia (AVRT), involving an accessory pathway as in Wolff–Parkinson–White syndrome; and focal atrial tachycardia. Episodes can be triggered by caffeine, alcohol, stress, or occur without an obvious trigger.

Signs, diagnosis, and treatment

Symptoms include palpitations, light-headedness, shortness of breath, and chest discomfort. Diagnosis is by electrocardiogram. Acute episodes are treated first with vagal manoeuvres, then adenosine if needed; long-term options include beta blockers or calcium channel blockers, and catheter ablation, which is curative in most cases of AVNRT and AVRT.
