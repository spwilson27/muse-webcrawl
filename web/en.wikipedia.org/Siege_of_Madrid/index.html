Siege of Madrid

Date: November 1936 – 28 March 1939
Location: Madrid, Spain
Result: Nationalist victory

The siege of Madrid was a protracted engagement of the Spanish Civil War in which Nationalist forces under Francisco Franco attempted to capture the Republican capital from late 1936 until the city's fall in March 1939.

The 1936 assault

After advancing across western Spain, Franco's Army of Africa reached Madrid in November 1936. Fierce fighting in the Casa de Campo and the University City, and the arrival of the International Brigades and Soviet aid, halted the direct assault. The slogan ¡No pasarán! ('They shall not pass'), associated with Dolores Ibárruri, became emblematic of the defence.

Envelopment attempts

Unable to take Madrid frontally, the Nationalists tried to encircle it in the battles of Jarama and Guadalajara (1937), both of which failed to close the ring. The capital endured artillery and air bombardment for over two years.

Fall

In March 1939, internal Republican fighting between supporters of Colonel Casado's coup and Communists weakened the defence; Nationalist troops entered Madrid on 28 March 1939, days before the war ended.
