Chinese Civil War

The Chinese Civil War was a civil war in China fought between the Kuomintang-led government of the Republic of China and the forces of the Chinese Communist Party (CCP). The war is conventionally divided into two phases separated by the Second Sino-Japanese War: a first phase from 1927 to 1936, and a second, decisive phase from 1946 to 1949–1950. It ended with Communist victory on the mainland, the proclamation of the People's Republic of China in 1949, and the retreat of the Republic of China government to Taiwan.

Background

The Kuomintang and the Communists had cooperated in the First United Front (1924–1927) to reunify China against the warlords. The alliance collapsed in April 1927 when Chiang Kai-shek purged Communists in Shanghai, beginning the first phase of the war. The Communists retreated to rural base areas, most famously the Jiangxi Soviet, and survived the Nationalists' encirclement campaigns through the Long March of 1934–1935 to Yan'an in the northwest.

The war against Japan

Following the Xi'an Incident of December 1936, a Second United Front suspended the civil war in the face of Japanese invasion. During the Second Sino-Japanese War (1937–1945), Communist forces expanded greatly behind Japanese lines in northern China, while the Nationalist government bore the brunt of conventional fighting and suffered enormous losses.

Resumption and Communist victory

Negotiations after Japan's surrender in 1945 failed, and full-scale fighting resumed in 1946. Despite American support and initial numerical superiority, the Nationalists were undermined by corruption, hyperinflation, and collapsing morale. In Manchuria and north China, Lin Biao's forces won decisive victories in the Liaoshen campaign (1948); the Huaihai and Pingjin campaigns followed, and Nanjing fell in April 1949. On 1 October 1949 Mao Zedong proclaimed the People's Republic of China in Beijing. Chiang Kai-shek and the remnants of his government withdrew to Taiwan, where the Republic of China continued.

Aftermath

No formal peace treaty has ever been signed, and the political status of Taiwan remains contested. Both sides initially claimed to be the sole legitimate government of all China. The war's outcome shaped the Cold War in Asia and continues to influence cross-strait relations.
