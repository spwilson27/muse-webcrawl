Volta River Authority

The Volta River Authority (VRA) is a Ghanaian state-owned electricity generation utility established in 1961 to develop the hydroelectric potential of the Volta River. Its principal assets are the Akosombo Dam and the Kpong Dam on the Volta River, whose combined output supplies the greater part of Ghana's electricity and is exported to neighbouring Togo, Benin, and Côte d'Ivoire.

The Akosombo Dam created Lake Volta, one of the largest artificial lakes in the world.

History

The idea of damming the Volta River to power an aluminium smelter and supply electricity dated to the colonial era, but it was realised under President Kwame Nkrumah after Ghanaian independence. The Volta River Authority was created by the Volta River Development Act of 1961, and construction of the Akosombo Dam was completed in 1965, flooding a large area to form Lake Volta. The Kpong power station, downstream of Akosombo, was commissioned in 1982.

Operations

The Authority generates electricity primarily from hydroelectric power, supplemented in later decades by thermal plants such as the Takoradi thermal power station and solar installations. Ghana's power sector was restructured in the 2000s, with transmission assigned to the Ghana Grid Company (GRIDCo) and distribution to the Electricity Company of Ghana, leaving the VRA focused mainly on generation. Droughts that lower the level of Lake Volta have periodically forced power rationing, known locally as "dumsor", most severely in the mid-2010s.
