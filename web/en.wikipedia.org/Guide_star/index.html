Guide star

A guide star is a reference star used to accurately maintain the pointing of a telescope during an astronomical observation or exposure. Because the Earth rotates, a driven telescope mount still drifts slightly; a guide star lets the observer or an automated system detect and correct that drift.

Use in amateur astronomy

In astrophotography, an autoguider camera images a guide star through the main telescope or a separate guide scope and sends corrections to the mount whenever the star moves from its reference position. This allows exposures of many minutes without star trails. The guide star should be reasonably bright and near the target object so that the same corrections apply to the field being photographed.

Adaptive optics and space telescopes

Large observatories use guide stars for adaptive optics: a wavefront sensor measures how the star's light is distorted by the atmosphere, and a deformable mirror corrects the distortion in real time. Where no suitable natural star exists, observatories project a laser into the upper atmosphere to create an artificial laser guide star. Space telescopes such as the Hubble Space Telescope lock onto guide stars with fine guidance sensors to hold pointing steady to within thousandths of an arcsecond.
