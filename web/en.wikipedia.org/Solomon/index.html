Solomon

Solomon (Hebrew: שְׁלֹמֹה, Šəlōmō), also called Jedidiah, was, according to the Hebrew Bible and the Quran, a fabulously wealthy and wise king of the United Kingdom of Israel who succeeded his father David. The conventional dates of Solomon's reign are about 970 to 931 BCE, normally given in alignment with the dates of David's reign. He is described as king of the United Monarchy, which broke apart into the northern Kingdom of Israel and the southern Kingdom of Judah shortly after his death.

According to the Bible, Solomon built the First Temple in Jerusalem, which his father had sought to build, and accumulated legendary wealth through trade and tribute. His wisdom is exemplified by the Judgment of Solomon, in which he resolved a dispute between two women claiming to be the mother of a child. The Queen of Sheba is said to have visited him to test his wisdom.

Biblical narrative

Solomon is portrayed in the Books of Kings and Chronicles as the son of David and Bathsheba. God offered Solomon anything he wished, and Solomon asked only for wisdom; God granted him wisdom, wealth, and honor. Traditional authorship of the books of Proverbs, Ecclesiastes, and the Song of Songs is attributed to him. The biblical account also describes his later apostasy — influenced by his many foreign wives, he built altars to other gods — for which God decreed the division of the kingdom after his death.

Historicity and legacy

Scholars debate the historicity of a great Solomonic kingdom; archaeological evidence for a 10th-century BCE empire centered on Jerusalem remains contested. Solomon is a revered figure in Judaism, Christianity, and Islam, where he is regarded as a prophet and king renowned for his command over animals and jinn.
