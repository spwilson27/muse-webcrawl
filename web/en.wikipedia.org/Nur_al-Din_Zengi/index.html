Nur al-Din Zengi

Nur al-Din Mahmud ibn Zengi (1118–1174), commonly known as Nur al-Din ("Light of the Faith"), was a member of the Turkic Zengid dynasty who ruled the Syrian province of the Seljuk Empire and became the leading Muslim figure of the counter-crusade in the mid-12th century. He united Syria and laid the groundwork for Saladin's later successes.

Rise to power

Nur al-Din was the second son of Imad al-Din Zengi, atabeg of Mosul and Aleppo. After his father's assassination in 1146 he took Aleppo while his brother Saif al-Din Ghazi took Mosul. In 1148 he helped repel the Second Crusade at Damascus, and in 1154 he took Damascus itself, uniting Muslim Syria under his rule. He intervened repeatedly in Fatimid Egypt, sending his general Shirkuh — Saladin's uncle — whose campaigns ended with Saladin as vizier, preparing Egypt's return to Sunni rule.

Rule and piety

Renowned for personal piety, justice, and austerity, Nur al-Din built mosques, madrasas, hospitals (including the famous Bimaristan Nur al-Din in Damascus), caravanserais, and fortifications. He promoted Sunni revival against both Crusaders and Ismaili Shi'ism and revived jihad propaganda; the celebrated minbar he commissioned for the al-Aqsa Mosque was installed by Saladin after Jerusalem's capture in 1187.

Death

He died of illness in Damascus in May 1174, just as he was preparing to move against Saladin's growing independence in Egypt. His young son as-Salih Ismail succeeded in Syria, but within a decade Saladin had absorbed most of his inheritance.
