Frisch–Peierls memorandum

Frisch–Peierls memorandum
AuthorsOtto Frisch, Rudolf Peierls
DateMarch 1940
SubjectFeasibility of an atomic bomb

The Frisch–Peierls memorandum was the first technical exposition of the design of a practical atomic weapon. Written in March 1940 by the émigré physicists Otto Robert Frisch and Rudolf Peierls, then working at the University of Birmingham, it overturned the prevailing assumption that a uranium bomb would require tons of material and be impractical.

Content

Frisch and Peierls calculated that the critical mass of the fissile isotope uranium-235 needed for an explosive chain reaction was on the order of a kilogram — small enough to be delivered by an aircraft of the day. They estimated the explosive yield, discussed how isotope separation might be achieved, and warned of the radioactive fallout such a weapon would produce. They also stressed the danger that Germany might be pursuing the same idea.

Impact

The memorandum reached the British government through the MAUD Committee, whose subsequent report persuaded both Britain and the United States that an atomic bomb was feasible, contributing directly to the British Tube Alloys project and the American Manhattan Project. Frisch and Peierls, initially excluded from official work as enemy aliens, later joined both efforts.
