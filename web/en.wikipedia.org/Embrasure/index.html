Embrasure

An embrasure (or crenel) is the opening in a battlement or crenellated parapet between two raised solid portions, or merlons. Embrasures allowed defenders on castle walls and fortifications to shoot arrows or firearms at attackers while remaining sheltered behind the merlons.

Design

In medieval fortification, parapets alternated merlons and embrasures along wall walks; defenders could step into the embrasure to shoot and step back under cover to reload. In later artillery fortifications, embrasure came to denote the splayed opening through a parapet or casemate wall through which a cannon fired, typically wider on the inside than the outside so the gun could traverse while presenting a small target. The term is also used in dentistry for the space between adjacent teeth and in architecture for a splayed enlargement of a window or door opening in a thick wall.
