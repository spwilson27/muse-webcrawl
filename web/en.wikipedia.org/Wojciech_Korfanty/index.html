Wojciech Korfanty (1873–1939) was a Polish national activist, journalist and politician, best known for championing the Polish cause in Upper Silesia after the First World War.

Career

Born in Siemianowice (then in the German Empire), Korfanty edited Polish newspapers and sat in the German Reichstag (1903–1912, 1918) and the Prussian Landtag, defending Polish minority rights. In October 1918 he demanded in the Reichstag the incorporation of the Polish-inhabited eastern provinces into a reborn Poland.

Upper Silesia

In 1920 Korfanty became the Polish plebiscite commissioner in Upper Silesia. Dissatisfied with the Allied interpretation of the 1921 plebiscite result, he led the Third Silesian Uprising (May–July 1921), after which the League of Nations awarded Poland the industrial eastern part of the region, including Katowice. He later served briefly as a deputy prime minister of Poland (1923), opposed Piłsudski's Sanation regime, co-founded the opposition Front Morges, and was imprisoned in the Brest Fortress in 1930. He died in August 1939, days after returning from exile.
