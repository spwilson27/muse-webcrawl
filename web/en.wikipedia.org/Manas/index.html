Epic of Manas

The Epic of Manas is a traditional Turkic epic centred on the hero Manas and his descendants and followers, who are said to have fought in the 9th century to preserve Kyrgyz independence from the Uyghurs and other peoples. It is the centrepiece of Kyrgyz literature and culture and is among the longest epic poems in world literature: the trilogy — Manas, Semetey and Seytek, following the hero, his son and his grandson — contains over 500,000 lines in the version of the bard Sayakbay Karalaev, dwarfing the Sanskrit Mahabharata.

Content

The first part recounts how Manas, born to the childless Jakyp, unites the divided Kyrgyz tribes, leads them back to their ancestral homeland in the Ala-Too mountains, and wages wars against neighbouring peoples, including the celebrated "Great Campaign" to Beijing. The second part follows his son Semetey, raised in exile, who returns to avenge his father; the third continues with his grandson Seytek, who restores the family's fortunes. Themes of unity, loyalty, heroism and the defence of the homeland run through the trilogy.

Performance and tradition

The epic is sung by specialist bards called manaschi, who recite from memory in a trance-like performance accompanied by dramatic gestures but traditionally without instruments. The most renowned manaschi include Sagymbay Orozbakov, whose version was the first extensively recorded, and Sayakbay Karalaev (1894–1971), whose trilogy recording is the most complete. Written records of episodes date to the 19th century, when the Kazakh scholar Chokan Valikhanov transcribed the episode of the memorial feast for Kökötöy.

Cultural significance

In independent Kyrgyzstan, Manas became a national symbol: the flag's sun has forty rays for the forty Kyrgyz tribes Manas united, Manas International Airport and the Manas Order bear his name, and a monumental complex stands in Bishkek. In 2013 the Kyrgyz epic trilogy was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.
