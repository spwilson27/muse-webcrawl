Connection (principal bundle)

In mathematics, a connection on a principal bundle is a geometric structure that defines a notion of parallel transport and covariant differentiation for the bundle. Introduced in this generality by Charles Ehresmann in 1950, connections on principal bundles provide the mathematical framework underlying gauge theory in physics.

Definition

Let P be a principal G-bundle over a manifold M, where G is a Lie group. A connection may be defined in several equivalent ways: as a G-invariant distribution of horizontal subspaces complementary to the vertical subspaces tangent to the fibres; as a Lie-algebra-valued one-form ω on P (the connection form) that reproduces the generators of the group action on vertical vectors and transforms in the adjoint representation under the group action; or as a covariant derivative on associated bundles.

Curvature and local description

On a local trivialization, the connection is described by a gauge potential A, a Lie-algebra-valued one-form on the base. Its curvature is the two-form F = dA + A ∧ A, the field strength of gauge theory. The curvature measures the failure of horizontal lifts of loops to close; its integral and characteristic classes (Chern classes, the Pontryagin classes) capture the topology of the bundle. Parallel transport around a loop defines the holonomy group of the connection.

Applications

Connections on principal bundles model the fundamental interactions: electromagnetism is a connection on a U(1) bundle, and the Yang–Mills theories of the Standard Model use SU(2) and SU(3) bundles. Levi-Civita connections of general relativity can likewise be framed as connections on the frame bundle.
