Sinterklaas

Sinterklaas arrives in the Netherlands by steamboat each November.

Sinterklaas (Dutch pronunciation: [ˌsɪntərˈklaːs]) or Sint-Nicolaas is a legendary figure based on Saint Nicholas, patron saint of children. The feast of Sinterklaas celebrates the name day of Saint Nicholas on 6 December, with the main festivities held on 5 December in the Netherlands (Sinterklaasavond, "St Nicholas Eve") and on 6 December in Belgium and other formerly Dutch territories.

The tradition

Sinterklaas is depicted as an elderly, stately man with white hair and a long beard, wearing a bishop's red cope and mitre and carrying a golden crosier and a large book recording whether children have been good or naughty. He traditionally arrives from Spain by steamboat in mid-November — the intocht, broadcast nationally in the Netherlands — accompanied by helpers known as Zwarte Pieten (Black Petes), whose portrayal, traditionally in blackface, has been the subject of sustained public debate and change in recent decades. Children leave a shoe by the fireplace with a carrot for his horse, and find small gifts, chocolate letters and pepernoten (spiced biscuits) in return.

History and influence

The modern celebration was popularised by Jan Schenkman's 1850 picture book Sint Nicolaas en zijn Knecht, which established the steamboat from Spain and the servant figure. Dutch emigrants carried the tradition to colonial New Amsterdam, where "Sinterklaas" evolved linguistically and culturally into Santa Claus in the United States.
