SAT

The SAT is a standardized test widely used for college admissions in the United States. Since its debut in 1926, its name and scoring have changed several times; originally an abbreviation of Scholastic Aptitude Test, the name now no longer stands for anything. The SAT is owned and published by the College Board, a nonprofit organization, and is developed and administered on its behalf by Educational Testing Service.

Structure

The digital SAT, administered internationally from 2023 and in the United States from 2024, consists of two sections: Reading and Writing, and Math. Each section is scored on a scale of 200 to 800 points, for a total of 400 to 1600. The test is adaptive: performance on the first module of a section determines the difficulty of the second module. The optional essay was discontinued in 2021.

History

The SAT was adapted from the Army Alpha IQ test and championed by Harvard president James Bryant Conant as a means of identifying talented students regardless of background. Over the twentieth century it became a central — and contested — element of American college admissions, alongside its rival, the ACT. The score scale was recentered in 1995, the test was substantially redesigned in 2005 and again in 2016, and the paper format was replaced by a shorter digital test in 2023–2024.

Use and criticism

Most colleges and universities in the United States consider SAT scores as part of a holistic admissions process, though a growing number adopted test-optional or test-blind policies, especially after 2020. Debates over the SAT center on its predictive value for college performance, correlations between scores and family income, and the role of commercial test preparation.
