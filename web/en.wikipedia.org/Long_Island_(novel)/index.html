Long Island is a 2024 novel by the Irish writer Colm Tóibín. It is the sequel to his award-winning 2009 novel Brooklyn, continuing the story of Eilis Lacey some twenty years after the earlier book's events.

Plot

In the mid-1970s, Eilis Fiorello (née Lacey) lives on Long Island, New York, with her husband Tony Fiorello, a plumber, and their two teenage children, in a close-knit Italian-American family enclave. Her settled life is thrown into crisis when an Irishman arrives at her door to tell her that his wife is pregnant by Tony — and that he intends to leave the baby on their doorstep. Unable to accept the situation, Eilis returns to Enniscorthy, County Wexford, in Ireland, ostensibly to visit her ageing mother for her eightieth birthday. There she finds her widowed brother-in-law's household changed, old acquaintances — including Jim Farrell, the man she nearly married, and her friend Nancy Sheridan — drawn into new configurations, and she must decide, once again, between two lives on opposite sides of the Atlantic.

Reception

The novel was widely reviewed as a masterful return to Tóibín's most beloved character, praised for its quiet prose, moral ambiguity and refusal of easy resolution. It was an Oprah's Book Club selection and a bestseller in several countries. Critics noted how the sequel deepens the first novel's themes of exile, duty, secrecy and the costs of emigration, with Eilis — now middle-aged and a mother — exercising an agency constrained by the social worlds of both Ireland and Irish-America.
