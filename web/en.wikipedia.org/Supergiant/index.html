Supergiant

Supergiants are among the most massive and most luminous stars. They occupy the top region of the Hertzsprung–Russell diagram with absolute visual magnitudes between about −3 and −8. In the Yerkes spectral classification they form luminosity class I.

Properties

Supergiant radii range from about 30 to over 1,000 solar radii; the largest known stars, such as UY Scuti and Stephenson 2-18, approach 2,000 solar radii. Temperatures range from about 3,400 K for red supergiants like Betelgeuse to over 40,000 K for the hottest blue supergiants. Blue and yellow supergiants are evolved massive stars burning helium in their cores, while red supergiants are in a later phase with inert carbon–oxygen cores.

Evolution and fate

Supergiants begin life as massive main-sequence stars (roughly 8–300 solar masses). They evolve off the main sequence within a few million years and end their lives in core-collapse supernovae, leaving neutron stars or black holes. Their strong stellar winds and radiation shape the surrounding interstellar medium.

Examples

Famous supergiants include Rigel, Deneb, Antares, Betelgeuse, Mu Cephei, and Polaris.
