Diversity (politics)

Diversity, in political and social usage, refers to the inclusion and representation of people of different backgrounds — including race, ethnicity, gender, age, religion, disability, sexual orientation and social class — within a group, organization or society. It is commonly discussed alongside the related concepts of equity and inclusion, in the combination "diversity, equity, and inclusion" (DEI).

Policies

Diversity policies include affirmative action in education and employment, anti-discrimination legislation, quotas or targets for representation in legislatures and corporate boards, multiculturalism policies, and supplier-diversity and accessibility programs. Universities and employers have justified diversity efforts both as remedies for historical discrimination and as producing educational and organizational benefits; the U.S. Supreme Court's 2023 decisions in cases concerning Harvard and the University of North Carolina ended race-conscious admissions at U.S. colleges.

Debate

Supporters argue that diversity improves decision-making, innovation and fairness. Critics argue that some programs amount to reverse discrimination, tokenism or ideological conformity, and the subject has become a significant axis of political controversy in the United States and elsewhere, with legislation both promoting and restricting DEI programs.
