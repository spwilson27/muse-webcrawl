Shikharji

Shikharji (शिखरजी), also known as Sammed or Sammet Shikhar, is the most important pilgrimage site (tirtha) in Jainism. It is located on Parasnath Hill, the highest mountain in the Indian state of Jharkhand, rising about 1,365 metres.

Significance

Jain tradition holds that twenty of the twenty-four tirthankaras attained moksha (liberation) at Shikharji, including Parshvanatha, the 23rd tirthankara, after whom the hill is named. Small shrines called tonks mark the spots associated with each tirthankara along the ridge.

Pilgrimage

Pilgrims typically begin at the town of Madhuban at the foot of the hill and walk a route of about 27 kilometres visiting the tonks and temples, often barefoot and beginning before dawn. The site is sacred to both Digambara and Shvetambara Jains, though the sects have sometimes disputed administration of its temples.
