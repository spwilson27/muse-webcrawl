Stirling's approximation

Comparison of Stirling's approximation with the exact factorial

In mathematics, Stirling's approximation (or Stirling's formula) is an asymptotic approximation for factorials. It is a good approximation, leading to accurate results even for small values of n. It is named after the Scottish mathematician James Stirling, though a related result was first stated by Abraham de Moivre.

n! ~ √(2πn) (n/e)n

Equivalently, the logarithm of the factorial satisfies ln(n!) ≈ n ln n − n + ½ ln(2πn). The approximation is widely used in combinatorics, probability, and statistical mechanics, where factorials of large numbers arise naturally.

Derivation

A quick derivation uses the integral comparison ln(n!) = Σ ln k and approximates the sum by an integral, an approach connected to the Euler–Maclaurin formula. A more rigorous treatment relates the factorial to the gamma function and applies Laplace's method.

Accuracy

The relative error of the approximation decreases as n grows; even at n = 1 the approximation is within about 8 percent. Refined versions with additional terms in 1/n give the asymptotic Stirling series.

Applications

Stirling's approximation underlies estimates of entropy in statistical mechanics (through Boltzmann's formula S = k log W), the analysis of binomial coefficients, and approximations in information theory.
