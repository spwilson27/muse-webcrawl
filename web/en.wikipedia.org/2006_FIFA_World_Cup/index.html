The 2006 FIFA World Cup was the 18th edition of the FIFA World Cup, held in Germany from 9 June to 9 July 2006. Thirty-two teams competed in twelve stadiums. Italy won the tournament, defeating France 5–3 on penalties in the final in Berlin after a 1–1 draw, to claim its fourth World Cup title and its first since 1982.

The final and the Zidane headbutt

The final is remembered for two moments involving France captain Zinedine Zidane: his chipped "Panenka" penalty that opened the scoring, and, in extra time of the last match of his career, his headbutt to the chest of Italy's Marco Materazzi, who had equalised in the first half. Zidane was sent off; Italy won the shootout, with Fabio Grosso converting the decisive penalty. Zidane nonetheless received the Golden Ball as the tournament's best player.

Tournament summary

Hosts Germany, coached by Jürgen Klinsmann, exceeded expectations to finish third, with Miroslav Klose winning the Golden Boot with five goals. Debutants included Ghana, which reached the round of 16, while defending champions Brazil fell to France in the quarter-finals. The tournament set attendance records, with over 3.3 million spectators, and was praised for its organisation and atmosphere.

Italy's triumph came against the backdrop of the Calciopoli scandal then engulfing Italian club football, making the victory a moment of national redemption.
