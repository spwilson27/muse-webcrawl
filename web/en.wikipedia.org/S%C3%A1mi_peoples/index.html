Sámi peoples

The term Sámi peoples refers collectively to the distinct but related Indigenous groups of Sápmi — the Northern Sámi, Lule Sámi, Southern Sámi, Inari Sámi, Skolt Sámi, Kildin Sámi, and others — each with its own language, dialect continuum, dress traditions, and historical patterns of subsistence.

Diversity

Nine living Sámi languages are recognised, of which Northern Sámi is by far the most widely spoken. Coastal Sámi communities traditionally relied on fishing and small-scale farming, while inland groups are most associated with reindeer pastoralism. Regional variations in the gákti costume identify a wearer's home district and family.

Shared institutions

Despite their diversity, the Sámi peoples share the Saami Council (founded 1956), a common flag and national day (6 February), and coordinated political representation through the Sámi parliaments of Norway, Sweden, and Finland and the Sámi Parliamentary Council.
