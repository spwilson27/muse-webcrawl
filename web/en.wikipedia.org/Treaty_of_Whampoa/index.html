Treaty of Whampoa

Signed24 October 1844
LocationWhampoa, Qing China
PartiesFrance; Qing Empire

The Treaty of Whampoa (French: Traité de Whampoa; Chinese: 黃埔條約) was a commercial and diplomatic treaty signed between France and the Qing dynasty of China on 24 October 1844, aboard a French warship anchored at Whampoa, near Canton. It followed the British Treaty of Nanking of 1842 and the American Treaty of Wanghia of 1844, and it is counted among China's "unequal treaties".

Terms

The treaty, negotiated by Théodose de Lagrené for France and Qiying for the Qing, granted France the same privileges Britain had obtained: trade at the five treaty ports, fixed tariffs, extraterritoriality for French citizens, and most-favoured-nation status. Lagrené also secured an imperial edict tolerating Christianity and lifting the ban on its practice by Chinese converts, issued in 1846.

Significance

The treaty established France's position in China and laid groundwork for later French involvement, including the protection of Catholic missions, which became a recurring factor in Sino-French relations through the 19th century.
