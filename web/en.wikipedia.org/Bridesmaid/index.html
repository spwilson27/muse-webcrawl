A bridesmaid is a member of the bride's party in a wedding, usually a close friend or relative — often a sister — chosen to support and attend the bride before and during the ceremony. Bridesmaids typically wear matching or coordinated dresses, walk in the procession, and help with wedding preparations. The leading bridesmaid is the maid of honour (or matron of honour, if married).


Bridesmaids traditionally wear coordinated dresses.

History

The custom is often traced to ancient Rome, where bridesmaids dressed identically to the bride to confuse jealous spirits or suitors who might curse or kidnap her. Identical dress also served as legal witness protection: ten witnesses were required for a marriage, and the bridal party's finery helped satisfy the requirement in style.

Duties

Modern duties include helping plan the bridal shower and hen party (bachelorette party), assisting the bride with dressing, holding her bouquet during the ceremony, and signing the marriage register as witnesses in some jurisdictions. Costs — dresses, travel, gifts — are a perennial topic of etiquette debate; the English idiom "always a bridesmaid, never a bride" reflects the role's cultural salience and was famously used in a 1920s advertisement.

Variations

Junior bridesmaids are young attendants too old to be flower girls, and some couples now include male attendants ("bridesmen") or mixed parties. In other wedding traditions, comparable roles include the Greek koumbaros sponsor and the Chinese "sisters" who accompany the bride and play gatecrashing games with the groom.
