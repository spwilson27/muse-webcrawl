Colombian coffee growing axis

Coffee growing axis
Spanish: Eje Cafetero
Departments: Caldas, Quindío, Risaralda
Main cities: Manizales, Armenia, Pereira
UNESCO: Coffee Cultural Landscape (2011)

The Colombian coffee growing axis (Spanish: Eje Cafetero), also called the Coffee Triangle (Triángulo del Café), is a region in the central Colombian Andes comprising mainly the departments of Caldas, Quindío, and Risaralda. It is the heartland of Colombian coffee production and of the distinctive "paisa" settler culture that developed around it.

Geography and production

The region's steep volcanic slopes, altitudes between roughly 1,000 and 2,000 meters, and mild temperatures provide ideal conditions for cultivating high-quality arabica coffee. Hundreds of thousands of small family farms (fincas) dominate production, marketed internationally under the Café de Colombia designation and promoted by the fictional character Juan Valdez.

History

Coffee cultivation spread into the region in the late 19th and early 20th centuries as settlers from Antioquia colonized the central cordillera. The departments' capitals — Manizales (Caldas), Pereira (Risaralda), and Armenia (Quindío) — grew as commercial centers. A major earthquake struck the region in January 1999, devastating parts of Armenia and surrounding towns.

Recognition and tourism

In 2011 UNESCO inscribed the Coffee Cultural Landscape of Colombia as a World Heritage Site, recognizing its century-old coffee traditions and distinctive architecture of bahareque (wattle and daub) farmhouses. The region is a popular destination for coffee tourism, including theme parks and farm stays.
