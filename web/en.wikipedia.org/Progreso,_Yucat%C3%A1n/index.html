Progreso, Yucatán

The pier at Progreso extends several kilometres into the Gulf of Mexico.

Progreso is a port city in the Mexican state of Yucatán, located on the Gulf of Mexico about 30 kilometres north of the state capital, Mérida. Founded in 1871 to serve as the port for Mérida, it became the main outlet for Yucatán's henequen (sisal) exports in the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries.

Geography and the pier

Because the Yucatán shelf is very shallow, Progreso's pier was extended far into the gulf to reach deep water; the modern terminal pier, completed in the 1980s, is among the longest in the world at roughly 6.5 kilometres, with cargo and cruise facilities at its end connected to shore by a causeway.

Economy

The port handles container, bulk, and fishing traffic and is a growing cruise ship destination. Progreso is also a popular beach resort for residents of Mérida, especially during the summer season, and is known for its malecón (seafront promenade) and seafood restaurants. Nearby are the Chicxulub crater, the buried impact structure associated with the extinction of the dinosaurs, and the pre-Columbian Maya site of Dzibilchaltún.
