Octopus card

The Octopus card (Chinese: 八達通, literally "all-reaching pass") is a reusable contactless stored-value smart card for making electronic payments in Hong Kong. Launched in September 1997 for fare payment on the MTR railway and other public transport, it became one of the world's earliest and most successful electronic payment systems, and usage soon expanded to convenience stores, supermarkets, fast-food outlets, vending machines, parking and many other everyday transactions.

History and technology

The Octopus system was developed by Hong Kong's major transport operators, led by the MTR Corporation, using Sony's FeliCa contactless technology. Transactions take a fraction of a second as the card is held within a few centimetres of a reader, and value can be added at station machines, in shops, or by automatic bank transfer. Penetration is near-universal among Hong Kong's population, with tens of millions of cards and devices in circulation.

Forms and uses

Besides standard anonymous cards, there are personalised cards, concessionary versions for students, the elderly (via the JoyYou Card) and people with disabilities, and Octopus functions embedded in watches, phone cases, SIM-based and app-based mobile versions (including via Apple Pay, Samsung Pay and the Octopus App). The card is accepted on virtually all public transport in Hong Kong — MTR, buses, ferries, trams, minibuses and taxis — and by thousands of retailers.

Influence

Octopus served as a model for later transport payment systems worldwide, and its operator has provided consultancy for systems such as Dubai's Nol card and the Netherlands' OV-chipkaart.
