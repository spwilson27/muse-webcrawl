Louis, Grand Condé

Louis II de Bourbon, Prince of Condé (8 September 1621 – 11 December 1686), known as le Grand Condé ("the Great Condé"), was a French military commander and prince of the blood. One of the most celebrated generals of the seventeenth century, he achieved fame at the age of twenty-one with his victory at the Battle of Rocroi (1643), which shattered the reputation of Spanish infantry, and later became a central figure in the Fronde.

Early victories

Born at the Hôtel de Condé in Paris, Louis was educated rigorously and entered the army young. In command of the French army in the Spanish Netherlands during the Thirty Years' War, he destroyed a Spanish army at Rocroi in May 1643, an achievement that made him a national hero. He won further victories at Freiburg (1644, with Turenne) and Lens (1648), the last great battle of the Thirty Years' War.

The Fronde

During the civil wars of the Fronde (1648–1653), Condé first defended the regency of Anne of Austria and Cardinal Mazarin, then, alienated and arrested in 1650, went into open rebellion upon his release and eventually entered Spanish service against France. He was defeated by Turenne, notably at the Battle of the Dunes (1658), and was pardoned and restored by the Treaty of the Pyrenees (1659).

Later life

Restored to favour, Condé conquered Franche-Comté in 1668 and fought in the Dutch War, winning the hard-fought Battle of Seneffe in 1674. He spent his later years at his estate of Chantilly, a centre of arts and letters, patronising writers such as Molière, Racine, and Boileau, before dying at Fontainebleau in 1686.
