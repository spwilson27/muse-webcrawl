The Council of Europe (French: Conseil de l'Europe) is an international organisation founded in the wake of World War II to uphold human rights, democracy and the rule of law in Europe. Founded in 1949, it brings together 46 member states with some 675 million citizens and is an entirely separate body from the European Union (EU).

Its best-known achievement is the European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR), signed in 1950, and the European Court of Human Rights in Strasbourg, which enforces the Convention. Any person who claims their rights under the Convention have been violated by a member state may bring a case before the Court once domestic remedies are exhausted.

History

The Council was established by the Treaty of London on 5 May 1949, signed by ten states including the United Kingdom, France, Italy and the Benelux and Scandinavian countries. It grew to cover nearly all European states after the fall of the Berlin Wall. Russia was expelled in March 2022 following its invasion of Ukraine, reducing membership from 47 to 46.

Institutions

  • The Committee of Ministers, the decision-making body of foreign ministers or their deputies.
  • The Parliamentary Assembly (PACE), composed of parliamentarians from the member states.
  • The European Court of Human Rights, which delivers binding judgments.
  • The Congress of Local and Regional Authorities and the Commissioner for Human Rights.
