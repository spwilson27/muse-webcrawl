Rafael Viñoly, Uruguayan architect of landmark civic and cultural buildings.

Rafael Viñoly Beceiro (1 June 1944 – 2 March 2023) was a Uruguayan architect based in New York City. Founder of Rafael Viñoly Architects (founded 1983), he designed cultural, civic, educational and commercial buildings on six continents, noted for structural clarity and dramatic public spaces.

Career

Viñoly studied architecture at the University of Buenos Aires and co-founded the successful Argentine practice Estudio de Arquitectura before emigrating to the United States in 1978. His international breakthrough came with winning the competition for the Tokyo International Forum (completed 1996), a vast glass-roofed civic hall. Subsequent works include the Kimmel Center in Philadelphia, Jazz at Lincoln Center in New York, the Curve theatre in Leicester, Carrasco International Airport in Montevideo, and the high-rise 432 Park Avenue in Manhattan.

Controversies

His London tower 20 Fenchurch Street (the "Walkie-Talkie") drew criticism when its concave glass façade focused sunlight intensely enough to damage parked cars, prompting remedial screening; the Vdara hotel in Las Vegas had a similar solar convergence issue. Viñoly acknowledged the problems publicly. He died in New York in 2023, aged 78.
