Nika riots

The Nika riots (Greek: Στάσις τοῦ Νίκα) took place in Constantinople in January 532 AD during the reign of Emperor Justinian I and were the most violent riots in the city's history, leaving perhaps 30,000 people dead and much of the city burned.

Course of events

The riots grew out of unrest between the rival chariot-racing factions, the Blues and Greens, whose anger at arrests of their members and at Justinian's officials united them under the cry "Nika!" ("Conquer!"). The mob set fires across the city, destroyed the church of Hagia Sophia, and proclaimed the senator Hypatius emperor. Justinian prepared to flee, but according to Procopius his wife Theodora refused, declaring that the imperial purple would make a fine burial shroud.

Suppression and aftermath

The generals Belisarius and Mundus led troops into the Hippodrome, where the rioters had gathered, and massacred them. Hypatius was executed. Justinian then rebuilt the city on a grand scale, including the present Hagia Sophia, completed in 537.
