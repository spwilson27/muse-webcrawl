Regular expression

A regular expression (shortened as regex or regexp) is a sequence of characters that specifies a match pattern in text. Usually such patterns are used by string-searching algorithms for "find" or "find and replace" operations, or for input validation. Regular expression techniques are developed in theoretical computer science and formal language theory.

History

The concept arose in the 1940s from work by Warren McCulloch and Walter Pitts on neural nets, and was formalised in the 1950s by mathematician Stephen Cole Kleene, whose notation of "regular events" described the languages recognised by finite automata. Ken Thompson built regex search into the QED editor in the 1960s and then into ed and grep on Unix, spreading the notation widely.

Syntax and flavours

Patterns combine literal characters with metacharacters: . matches any character, * and + repeat the preceding element, brackets define character classes, anchors ^ and $ match string or line boundaries, and parentheses group subexpressions and capture their matches. Implementations fall into families: the POSIX basic and extended regular expressions, and the richer Perl-derived syntax of PCRE, adopted by most modern programming languages. Many features of practical regex engines, such as backreferences, go beyond the power of formal regular languages.
