Historical novel

A historical novel is a work of fiction set in the past, typically in a period notably earlier than the time of writing, which seeks to evoke the atmosphere, customs, and events of its chosen era. Historical figures may appear as characters, but the genre usually centres on fictional protagonists caught up in real events.

Development

The genre is generally held to begin with Sir Walter Scott, whose Waverley (1814) and Ivanhoe (1820) combined historical detail with romantic adventure and were imitated across Europe. Nineteenth-century masters include Alexandre Dumas (The Three Musketeers), Victor Hugo, Charles Dickens (A Tale of Two Cities), and Leo Tolstoy, whose War and Peace (1869) remains the genre's most ambitious exemplar. Writers such as Alessandro Manzoni in Italy and James Fenimore Cooper in America adapted Scott's model to their own national pasts.

Modern historical fiction

The genre remains one of the most popular forms of fiction, encompassing literary works such as Hilary Mantel's Wolf Hall trilogy alongside mystery, romance, and adventure subgenres set in periods from ancient Rome to the Second World War.
