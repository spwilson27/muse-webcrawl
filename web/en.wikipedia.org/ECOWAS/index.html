ECOWAS

The Economic Community of West African States (ECOWAS; French: Communauté économique des États de l'Afrique de l'Ouest, CEDEAO) is a regional political and economic union of fifteen countries located in West Africa. Collectively, these countries comprise an area of 5,114,162 km² and have a population of over 400 million. Considered one of the pillar regional blocs of the continent-wide African Economic Community, the organisation was founded on 28 May 1975, with the signing of the Treaty of Lagos.

Aims and institutions

ECOWAS aims to promote economic integration across the region, including a common market, freedom of movement of citizens (ECOWAS passport holders can travel visa-free among member states), and long-planned monetary union. Its institutions include the Commission (headquartered in Abuja, Nigeria), the Community Parliament, the Community Court of Justice and the ECOWAS Bank for Investment and Development.

Peacekeeping and recent developments

ECOWAS has repeatedly intervened politically and militarily in member states, including through its ECOMOG force in Liberia and Sierra Leone in the 1990s, and in The Gambia in 2017 to uphold the result of a presidential election. Following coups in the early 2020s, Mali, Burkina Faso and Niger announced their withdrawal from the bloc in 2024 and formed the Alliance of Sahel States, prompting a transition period of negotiation.
