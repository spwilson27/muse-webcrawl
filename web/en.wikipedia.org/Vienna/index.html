Vienna

Vienna (German: Wien) is the capital, most populous city, and one of the nine federal states of Austria. With roughly two million inhabitants — about a third of the country's population within its metropolitan area — it is Austria's primate city and its cultural, economic, and political centre. Vienna lies in the east of Austria, close to the borders with the Czech Republic, Slovakia, and Hungary, on the banks of the Danube.

History

Vienna began as the Roman frontier camp of Vindobona and grew into a residence of the Babenberg and then Habsburg dynasties. It withstood Ottoman sieges in 1529 and 1683 — the second lifted by a relief army under Poland's John III Sobieski. As capital of the Austrian Empire and then Austria-Hungary, Vienna became one of Europe's largest cities and a crucible of music, philosophy, and science: Haydn, Mozart, Beethoven, Schubert, Brahms, and Mahler all worked here, as did Freud and the Vienna Circle.

Culture and institutions

The city is famed for its coffee-house culture, the State Opera, the Musikverein, Schönbrunn Palace, and the Ringstrasse boulevard. Vienna hosts major international organisations, including the United Nations (Vienna International Centre), OPEC, the IAEA, and the OSCE. It regularly ranks at or near the top of global quality-of-life surveys.
