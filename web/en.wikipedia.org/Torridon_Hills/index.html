The Torridon Hills are a group of mountains in Wester Ross in the Northwest Highlands of Scotland, lying north of Glen Torridon and around the sea lochs of Loch Torridon and Loch Maree. They are among the most dramatic and geologically ancient mountains in Britain.

Geology

The hills are formed largely of Torridonian sandstone, a Precambrian sedimentary rock around a billion years old, resting on a basement of Lewisian gneiss nearly three billion years old. Glaciers carved the sandstone into towering, terraced peaks with steep flanks and shattered summits.

Principal peaks

The best-known hills are Liathach and Beinn Eighe — Britain's first National Nature Reserve, established in 1951 — together with Beinn Alligin, Beinn Dearg and the isolated peaks of Beinn Damh and Slioch further east. Liathach's traverse along the Am Fasarinen pinnacles is regarded as one of the classic ridge walks in Scotland.

Access

The village of Torridon and the hamlet of Kinlochewe serve as bases for walkers. Much of the area is cared for by the National Trust for Scotland.
