Pomona College

Pomona College is a private liberal arts college in Claremont, California, United States. Founded in 1887 by a group of Congregationalists, it is the founding member of the Claremont Colleges consortium and is consistently ranked among the leading liberal arts colleges in the United States. It enrolls roughly 1,700 students, all undergraduates, with a student-to-faculty ratio of about 8 to 1.

History

The college was incorporated on October 14, 1887, and named for Pomona, the Roman goddess of fruit — reflecting its original location in the citrus-growing Pomona Valley. Classes first met in a rented cottage in Pomona before moving to Claremont. In 1925, under president James A. Blaisdell, Pomona founded the Claremont Colleges consortium, modeled on the Oxford collegiate system, enabling small colleges to share a central library and cross-registration while retaining independent identities.

Academics

Pomona offers some 48 majors in the arts, humanities, social sciences, and natural sciences. Students may cross-register at the other Claremont Colleges — Scripps, Claremont McKenna, Harvey Mudd, and Pitzer — and the graduate institutions of the consortium. The college is known for generous financial aid, meeting the full demonstrated need of admitted students, and for high rates of alumni earning doctorates. Its motto is Cedant arma togae ("Let arms yield to the gown").

Campus and student life

The 140-acre campus, laid out with gardens by Ralph Cornell, includes Bridges Hall of Music (designed by Myron Hunt), the Smith Campus Center, and newer science and arts facilities. Traditions include Ski-Beach Day, in which students ski at a local resort in the morning and visit the beach in the afternoon, and the college's old fight song heritage of "Torchbearers." Varsity teams, the Sagehens, compete jointly with Pitzer College.
