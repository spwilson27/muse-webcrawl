Alexander II of Scotland

Alexander II (Medieval Gaelic: Alaxandair mac Uilliam; 24 August 1198 – 6 July 1249) was King of Scots from 1214 until his death. He spent his reign consolidating royal authority within Scotland and managing relations with England.

Reign

Alexander succeeded his father William the Lion in 1214. Taking advantage of King John's difficulties in England, he invaded northern England in support of the barons rebelling against John, and laid claim to the northern English counties. Under the Treaty of York (1237) with Henry III of England, Alexander abandoned these claims in exchange for estates in England, establishing a border between the two kingdoms close to the modern line.

Western campaign and death

Alexander sought to bring the Hebrides, then under Norwegian suzerainty, under the Scottish crown. In 1249 he sailed to the west on an expedition but fell ill and died on the island of Kerrera in the Inner Hebrides. He was buried at Melrose Abbey. He was succeeded by his son Alexander III.
