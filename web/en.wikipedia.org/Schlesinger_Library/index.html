Schlesinger Library in Radcliffe Yard

The Arthur and Elizabeth Schlesinger Library on the History of Women in America is a research library at the Radcliffe Institute for Advanced Study, Harvard University. It is one of the world's foremost collections documenting the lives and activities of American women.

History

The library began in 1943 when Radcliffe College alumna Maud Wood Park donated her collection of books, papers and memorabilia on women suffrage campaigners, founding the Women's Archives. In 1967 the collection was renamed for historian Arthur M. Schlesinger Sr. — an early supporter of the study of women's history — and his wife Elizabeth Bancroft Schlesinger, a noted feminist.

Collections

Holdings include books and periodicals, around 3,000 manuscript collections, photographs, audiovisual materials and an extensive culinary collection of books, manuscripts and ephemera. Notable papers include those of Betty Friedan, Julia Child, the Boston Women's Health Book Collective and the National Organization for Women. The library also maintains a large collection of women's magazines and an archive of blogs and websites documenting contemporary women's lives.

Access

The library is open to the public, and its reading room serves scholars regardless of institutional affiliation. Major renovation of its building in Radcliffe Yard was completed in 2019.
