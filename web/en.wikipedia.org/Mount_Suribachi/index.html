Mount Suribachi

Elevation169 m (554 ft)
LocationIwo Jima (Iōtō), Volcano Islands, Japan
TypeDormant volcano (cinder cone)

Mount Suribachi is a 169-metre (554 ft)-high dormant volcano that forms the southwestern tip of the Japanese island of Iwo Jima (officially Iōtō) in the Pacific Ocean. It is famous as the site where United States Marines raised the American flag on 23 February 1945 during the Battle of Iwo Jima in the Second World War.

The name Suribachi refers to a suribachi, a Japanese grinding bowl whose inverted shape the cone resembles. The mountain dominates the island's flat Motoyama plateau and was a key Japanese strongpoint: its slopes honeycombed with tunnels and gun positions connected to the island's extensive defensive network commanded the landing beaches below.

Battle of Iwo Jima

American forces landed on Iwo Jima on 19 February 1945. On the fourth day of the battle, Marines of the 28th Regiment, 5th Division, fought their way to the summit and raised a small flag; later that day a larger flag was raised by six men, an event captured in Joe Rosenthal's Pulitzer Prize-winning photograph Raising the Flag on Iwo Jima, one of the most reproduced images of the war. Fighting elsewhere on the island continued for another month, until late March 1945.

Today

Iwo Jima remains a restricted military island administered by Tokyo and used by the Japan Self-Defense Forces and the US military. A memorial stands near the summit of Suribachi, and annual commemorative ceremonies, jointly attended by Japanese and American veterans and officials, are held on the island. The mountain's summit offers a view over the invasion beaches and the Motoyama plateau.
