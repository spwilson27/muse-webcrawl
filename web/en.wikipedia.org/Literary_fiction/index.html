Literary fiction

Literary fiction is a term used in publishing to distinguish novels regarded as having literary merit — emphasis on style, character, theme and psychological or social insight — from genre or commercial fiction driven primarily by plot and category conventions such as romance, mystery or science fiction.

Characteristics

Literary fiction typically explores the human condition, favours complex characters and layered prose, and may experiment with structure and point of view. Its stories often end ambiguously rather than resolving neatly. The distinction is commercial as much as aesthetic: books are marketed, reviewed and shelved differently, and literary works are the usual candidates for prizes such as the Booker Prize and the Pulitzer Prize for Fiction.

Debate

Critics note that the boundary is porous — many genre works display literary qualities and many literary novels use genre devices — and that the label can function as a prestige category rather than a description of kind.
