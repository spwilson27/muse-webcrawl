Cruiser (motorcycle)

A cruiser is a type of motorcycle characterised by a relaxed, feet-forward riding position, low seat height, raked-out front forks, swept-back handlebars and emphasis on low-end torque and classic styling. The design descends from American motorcycles of the 1930s to 1960s.

Characteristics

Cruisers typically use large-displacement V-twin engines (though other configurations exist), have limited lean angles and suspension travel, and prioritise comfort at moderate speeds and visual presence over sport performance. Subtypes include the bobber, chopper and power cruiser.

Manufacturers

Harley-Davidson and Indian dominated the classic American cruiser market; Japanese and European makers such as Honda, Yamaha, Kawasaki, Triumph and Ducati have long offered competing models, often called metric cruisers.
