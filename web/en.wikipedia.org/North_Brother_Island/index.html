North Brother Island

North Brother Island is an island in the East River in New York City, situated between the Bronx and Rikers Island. It measures approximately 20 acres (8 hectares). Together with the smaller South Brother Island, it was once part of Queens but is now administered as part of the Bronx. The island is uninhabited and designated as a bird sanctuary; it is closed to the general public.

History

The island was uninhabited until 1885, when the city moved a hospital for patients with contagious diseases — including smallpox, typhoid, and later tuberculosis — there from Blackwell's Island. The Riverside Hospital facility included pavilions, a chapel, and staff housing. In 1904, the steamship General Slocum caught fire and sank near the island; many of the more than 1,000 victims were brought ashore there.

Typhoid Mary

The island is famous as the home of Mary Mallon, known as "Typhoid Mary", who was quarantined there from 1907 to 1910 and again from 1915 until her death in 1938, after health authorities determined she was an asymptomatic carrier of typhoid fever who had infected dozens of people while working as a cook.

Later use and abandonment

After the Second World War, the island housed student veterans studying at local universities. From the 1950s until 1963 it hosted a treatment centre for juvenile drug addicts. The facility was then abandoned, and the buildings fell into ruin as the island reverted to woodland. In 2001 it was transferred to the New York City Parks Department and designated a Forever Wild preserve, providing nesting habitat for herons and other wading birds. In 2016 a motion-activated camera study documented the recovering forest and wildlife.
