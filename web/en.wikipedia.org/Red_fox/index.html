Red fox

The red fox (Vulpes vulpes) is the largest of the true foxes and one of the most widely distributed members of the order Carnivora, present across the entire Northern Hemisphere including most of North America, Europe and Asia, plus parts of North Africa. It was introduced into Australia, where it is considered invasive.

Adults weigh 3.6 to 7.6 kg and have rusty-red fur, white underparts, black ear tips and legs, and a bushy tail with a white tip. Red foxes are omnivores, hunting rodents, rabbits and birds by sound — they can hear low-frequency sounds and use Earth's magnetic field to triangulate prey under snow — and also eat fruit and carrion.

They live in family groups in burrows called earths, communicate with about 28 different vocalisations, and feature prominently in folklore worldwide. See also Vulpes and Canidae.
