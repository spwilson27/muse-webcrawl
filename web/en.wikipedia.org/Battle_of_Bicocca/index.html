Battle of Bicocca

The Battle of Bicocca was fought on 27 April 1522 during the Italian War of 1521–1526, near Bicocca, north of Milan. An Imperial-Spanish and Papal army under Prospero Colonna decisively defeated a French army commanded by the Vicomte de Lautrec, whose Swiss mercenary pikemen attacked a fortified position and were shattered by arquebus and artillery fire. The battle is a landmark in the rise of firearms in European warfare.

Background

France had lost Milan to Imperial forces in 1521. Lautrec, short of money, was pressed by his unpaid Swiss mercenaries to give battle or let them go home. He attacked Colonna's entrenched camp in the park of Bicocca manor, where Spanish arquebusiers were dug in behind a sunken road and earthworks, supported by artillery and landsknecht pikemen under Georg von Frundsberg.

The battle

On 27 April two massive Swiss pike columns advanced without waiting for the French artillery. Crossing open ground under fire, they reached the earthworks only to be halted at the sunken road and shot down in rows by massed arquebus volleys. Some 3,000 Swiss were killed, including 22 captains. An Imperial cavalry counterstroke completed the rout; the French withdrew from the field.

Consequences

The defeat ended French power in Lombardy for the moment and broke the aura of invincibility of the Swiss pike square. The Swiss thereafter became more cautious about attacking prepared positions, and Bicocca entered the Spanish language as a word — bicoca — for something obtained cheaply or a bargain.
