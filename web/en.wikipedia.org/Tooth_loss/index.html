Tooth loss

Tooth loss is the process in which one or more teeth become loose and fall out or are removed. Loss of some teeth is normal in childhood, when deciduous (baby) teeth are shed to make way for permanent teeth; in adults, tooth loss is pathological and a major marker of oral disease burden worldwide.

Causes

The leading causes in adults are periodontal disease, in which inflammation destroys the supporting bone and ligaments, and dental caries, which can destroy the tooth beyond repair. Trauma, failed endodontic treatment, and medical conditions or treatments affecting bone also contribute. Smoking, diabetes and poor oral hygiene increase risk.

Consequences and treatment

Missing teeth impair chewing, speech and appearance and are associated with poorer nutrition and quality of life. Replacement options include removable dentures, fixed bridges and implant-supported crowns; complete loss of all teeth is called edentulism. Prevention centres on fluoride exposure, hygiene, and regular dental care, and rates of tooth retention in old age have improved markedly in many countries.
