Monogenetic volcanic field

A monogenetic volcanic field is a cluster of small volcanoes, each of which erupts only once (or during a single eruptive episode) before becoming extinct. New eruptions in such fields occur at new vents rather than reactivating old ones. See also the related article on the cinder cone.

Characteristics

These fields typically consist of dozens to hundreds of cinder cones, maars, tuff rings, and small lava flows spread over areas of hundreds or thousands of square kilometres. Activity in a field may persist for millions of years, with long quiet intervals between individual eruptions.

Michoacán–Guanajuato field

The Michoacán–Guanajuato volcanic field in Mexico contains over a thousand vents, including the historically observed volcanoes Jorullo (erupted 1759) and Parícutin (erupted 1943–1952). Because people live within such fields, forecasting where the next vent may open is an important hazard problem.

Examples

Other examples include the Auckland volcanic field in New Zealand, the San Francisco volcanic field in Arizona, and the Chaîne des Puys in France.
