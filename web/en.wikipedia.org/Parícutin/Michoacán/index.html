Michoacán

Michoacán
Capital: Morelia
Country: Mexico

Michoacán, officially Michoacán de Ocampo, is a state in west-central Mexico with a coastline on the Pacific Ocean. Its capital and largest city is Morelia. The state is known for its Purepecha (Tarascan) heritage, colonial cities, monarch butterfly reserves, and volcanic landscapes.

Geography

Michoacán lies within the Trans-Mexican Volcanic Belt, a province of active volcanism that includes the famous volcano Parícutin, born in a cornfield in 1943 near the village of Parícutin and the town of San Juan Parangaricutiro, both largely buried by lava and ash. The state also includes Lake Pátzcuaro and part of the Monarch Butterfly Biosphere Reserve in its eastern highlands.

History

Before the Spanish conquest, the region was the heartland of the Purépecha Empire, one of the major powers of Mesoamerica. Colonial Michoacán was shaped by figures such as Vasco de Quiroga.

Economy

Agriculture—especially avocados, limes, and berries—along with mining, fishing at the port of Lázaro Cárdenas, and tourism drive the state economy.
