Parícutin

Parícutin is a cinder cone volcano in the Mexican state of Michoacán, close to the village of the same name. It is famous in volcanology as the first volcano whose full life cycle — from birth to extinction — was observed and documented by scientists.

Eruption

The volcano began erupting on 20 February 1943 in a cornfield owned by farmer Dionisio Pulido, who witnessed the ground crack and swell. Within a day the cone was several metres high; within a year it had grown to over 300 metres. Lava flows buried the villages of Parícutin and San Juan Parangaricutiro, leaving only the church tower and spire of the latter visible above the lava field. Remarkably, no direct deaths were caused by the eruption. Eruptions continued until 1952, by which time the cone had reached about 424 metres above the surrounding terrain.

Geology

Parícutin is a monogenetic volcano within the Michoacán–Guanajuato volcanic field, an area containing over a thousand volcanic vents. Because such cones erupt only once, scientists do not expect Parícutin to erupt again.
