Principality of Achaea

The Principality of Achaea or Principality of Morea was one of the vassal states of the Latin Empire, which replaced the Byzantine Empire after the capture of Constantinople in 1204 during the Fourth Crusade. It controlled the Peloponnese peninsula in southern Greece.

History

The principality was founded by William of Champlitte and Geoffrey I of Villehardouin, who conquered the Morea between 1205 and 1209. Under the Villehardouin princes it prospered, and the Chronicle of the Morea records its feudal society. Mystras and other territories were ceded back to the Byzantines in 1262 after the Battle of Pelagonia.

Decline

Weakened by succession disputes and Catalan and Navarrese incursions, the principality passed to the Centurione Zaccaria family and was finally extinguished by the Byzantine Despotate of the Morea in 1432.
