Angers

Angers is a city in western France, prefecture of the Maine-et-Loire department in the Pays de la Loire region, with a population of roughly 155,000 in the city and about 430,000 in the metropolitan area. It lies on the Maine River, a short distance from its confluence with the Loire.

History

Angers was the Roman city of Juliomagus, capital of the Andecavi tribe. In the Middle Ages it became the capital of the county, then duchy, of Anjou, a great feudal principality whose counts included Geoffrey Plantagenet, father of Henry II of England. The massive Château d'Angers, built in the 13th century by Saint Louis, is ringed by 17 towers of dark schist and white tuffeau and houses the Apocalypse Tapestry (c. 1375–1382), the largest surviving set of medieval tapestries.

The city today

Sights include the Cathédrale Saint-Maurice with its twin spires, the Maison d'Adam, and the modern Terra Botanica plant theme park. Angers is known for horticulture, the Cointreau liqueur (created there in 1849), universities, and a large student population.
