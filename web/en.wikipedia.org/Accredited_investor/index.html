Accredited investor

An accredited investor is a person or entity that is allowed to invest in securities that are not registered with financial authorities, such as private placements, hedge funds, venture capital and private equity. In the United States, the term is defined by the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) under Regulation D.

United States definition

For natural persons, the main financial criteria are: (1) income exceeding $200,000 (or $300,000 together with a spouse or spousal equivalent) in each of the two most recent years, with a reasonable expectation of the same in the current year; or (2) net worth over $1 million, individually or jointly with a spouse, excluding the value of the primary residence. Amendments adopted in 2020 added knowledge-based categories, including holders of certain securities licences (Series 7, 65, 82) and "knowledgeable employees" of private funds. Entities generally qualify with assets exceeding $5 million, and all-equity-owner-accredited organisations also qualify.

Rationale and criticism

The rationale is that wealthy or sophisticated investors are better able to bear the risk of loss and to evaluate unregistered offerings without the protections of public disclosure. Critics argue the wealth thresholds are poor proxies for sophistication and exclude most households from high-growth private investments; supporters counter that the rules protect retail investors from opaque, illiquid securities.

Other jurisdictions

Analogous categories exist in other countries, such as "sophisticated investors" in Australia, "professional clients" under the EU's MiFID rules, and "accredited investors" in Canada and Singapore, each with their own asset and income thresholds.
