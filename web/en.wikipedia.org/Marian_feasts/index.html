Marian feast days

Marian feast days are specific days of the year in the liturgical calendars of the Roman Catholic Church and other churches dedicated to the honour of Mary, the mother of Jesus. They range from universal solemnities to local commemorations of apparitions and patronages.

Principal feasts

  • 1 January — Solemnity of Mary, Mother of God
  • 2 February — Presentation of the Lord (Candlemas), associated with Mary's purification
  • 25 March — Annunciation
  • 31 May — Visitation
  • 15 August — Assumption of Mary (Dormition in the East)
  • 8 September — Nativity of Mary
  • 12 September — Holy Name of Mary
  • 21 November — Presentation of Mary
  • 8 December — Immaculate Conception
  • 12 December — Our Lady of Guadalupe

History and observance

Many Marian feasts originated in the Christian East — the earliest, such as the Dormition and the Nativity of Mary, were celebrated in Jerusalem and Constantinople before spreading west. The Middle Ages added feasts of the Conception and Presentation, while the early modern and modern periods introduced commemorations linked to apparitions at Lourdes (11 February), Fatima (13 May) and elsewhere. Eastern Orthodox, Oriental Orthodox and some Anglican and Lutheran calendars retain overlapping but distinct sets of feasts.
