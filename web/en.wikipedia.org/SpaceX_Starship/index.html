SpaceX Starship


Starship stacked on Super Heavy

Starship is a fully reusable, super heavy-lift launch vehicle under development by the American aerospace company SpaceX. Consisting of the Super Heavy booster first stage and the Starship upper stage — which serves both as second stage and as a long-duration spacecraft — the system is the largest and most powerful launch vehicle ever flown, standing about 121 metres tall. It is intended for missions to Earth orbit, the Moon, Mars, and beyond, and to eventually replace SpaceX's Falcon 9 and Falcon Heavy vehicles.

Design

Both stages are made of stainless steel and are powered by Raptor rocket engines burning liquid methane and liquid oxygen ("methalox"). Super Heavy is equipped with 33 Raptors; Starship carries six, three optimized for sea level and three for vacuum. The system is designed for rapid and complete reusability: the booster returns to the launch site to be caught by the launch tower's "chopstick" arms, while Starship re-enters belly-first, protected by a heat shield of ceramic tiles, before flipping vertical for a powered landing. In orbit, Starship can be refuelled by tanker variants, enabling high-energy missions.

Development and testing

SpaceX founder Elon Musk unveiled the design concept, then called the Interplanetary Transport System, in 2016; development and testing have centred on the company's Starbase facility in Boca Chica, Texas. Early prototypes flew a series of high-altitude tests in 2020–21, culminating in the successful SN15 landing. The first integrated flight test took place on 20 April 2023 and ended when the vehicle was destroyed by its flight termination system after several engine failures and a failure to separate. Subsequent flights in November 2023, March, June, and October 2024 achieved orbit-like trajectories, successful stage separation, soft ocean splashdowns of both stages, and — on the fifth flight — the first successful catch of the Super Heavy booster by the launch tower.

Human Landing System

A variant of Starship, the Starship Human Landing System, was selected by NASA in 2021 to land astronauts on the Moon as part of the Artemis programme.
