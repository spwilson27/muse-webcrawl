Sell-by date

A sell-by date (or sell by date) is a date printed on food packaging indicating the last day a retailer should display a product for sale. It is intended as a stock-rotation guide for shops rather than a strict safety deadline for consumers; food frequently remains safe and wholesome for some time afterwards, though quality may decline.

Related date marks

Sell-by dates are one of several date labels. A use-by date is a safety-related deadline, applied mainly to highly perishable foods such as ready meals and fresh meat. A best-before date indicates the period during which a food retains its intended quality. Confusion among these labels is a recognised contributor to household food waste.

Regulation and reform

Rules vary by country. In the United States there is no uniform federal dating system for most foods (infant formula is an exception), though legislation such as the proposed Food Date Labeling Act has sought to standardise "best if used by" for quality and "use by" for safety. The United Kingdom and the European Union require use-by or best-before marks, and several retailers have removed best-before dates from fresh produce to reduce waste.
