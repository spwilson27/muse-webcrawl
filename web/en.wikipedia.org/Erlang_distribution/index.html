Erlang distribution

The Erlang distribution is a two-parameter family of continuous probability distributions with support on the positive real numbers. It was developed by A. K. Erlang to examine the number of telephone calls that might be made at the same time to operators of switching stations.

Definition

The Erlang distribution is the distribution of a sum of k independent exponential random variables each with mean 1/λ. Its probability density function is f(x; k, λ) = λ^k x^{k-1} e^{-λx} / (k-1)! for x ≥ 0, where k is the shape parameter (a positive integer) and λ is the rate parameter.

Properties

The mean is k/λ and the variance is k/λ². The Erlang distribution is a special case of the gamma distribution in which the shape parameter is an integer. When k = 1 it reduces to the exponential distribution.

Applications

It is used in queueing theory, telecommunications traffic engineering, and reliability theory to model waiting times and lifetimes.
