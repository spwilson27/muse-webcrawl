Money laundering

Money laundering is the process of illegally concealing the origins of money obtained from illicit activities such as drug trafficking, corruption, embezzlement, or gambling, by converting it into a legitimate source. It is a crime in most jurisdictions, and the International Monetary Fund has estimated laundering at 2–5% of global GDP.

Stages

Money laundering is conventionally described in three stages: placement (introducing illicit funds into the financial system), layering (moving the money through complex transactions to obscure its origin, e.g. through shell companies and offshore accounts), and integration (spending or investing the now apparently legitimate funds).

Countermeasures

Anti-money laundering (AML) regimes require banks to perform customer due diligence ("know your customer"), monitor transactions, and file suspicious activity reports. International coordination is led by the Financial Action Task Force (FATF), founded in 1989, and national financial intelligence units such as FinCEN in the United States.
