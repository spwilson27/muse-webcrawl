LogMAR chart

A LogMAR chart is an eye chart designed to provide a more accurate measurement of visual acuity than traditional charts. LogMAR stands for the logarithm of the minimum angle of resolution.

Design

The chart was designed by Ian Bailey and Jan Lovie at the National Vision Research Institute of Australia in 1976. Each line contains five letter optotypes (usually Sloan letters), the size of the letters progresses in a geometric series by a factor of about 1.26 (0.1 log units) per line, and spacing between letters and lines is proportional to letter size. A widely used version is the ETDRS chart, developed for the Early Treatment Diabetic Retinopathy Study.

Scoring

Because each letter read correctly contributes 0.02 log units, scores can be computed letter by letter, reducing rounding problems of line-based scoring. A LogMAR score of 0 corresponds to 6/6 (20/20) vision; negative values are better than normal, and each 0.1 increase corresponds to one line worse on a Snellen chart.

Use

LogMAR charts are preferred in research and clinical trials and are recommended in many low-vision and optometric settings, although Snellen charts remain common in general practice.
