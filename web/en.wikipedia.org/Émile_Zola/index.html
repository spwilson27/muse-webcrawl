Émile Édouard Charles Antoine Zola (2 April 1840 – 29 September 1902) was a French novelist, journalist and playwright, the most prominent representative of the literary school of naturalism and a major figure in the political liberalisation of France. He is best known for the twenty-volume cycle Les Rougon-Macquart and for his open letter J'Accuse…!, published during the Dreyfus affair.

Early life

Zola was born in Paris to an Italian engineer father, Francesco Zola, and a French mother. The family moved to Aix-en-Provence, where his father died when Émile was seven, leaving the family in poverty. At school in Aix he befriended the future painter Paul Cézanne. After moving to Paris in 1858, Zola failed the baccalauréat and worked as a clerk before joining the publicity department of the publisher Hachette, where he began writing fiction and criticism.

Les Rougon-Macquart

Between 1871 and 1893 Zola published the twenty novels of Les Rougon-Macquart, subtitled "the natural and social history of a family under the Second Empire". Applying his theory of naturalism — treating characters as products of heredity and environment — the cycle includes L'Assommoir (1877), about alcoholism in the Parisian working class; Nana (1880); Au Bonheur des Dames (1883), set in a department store; Germinal (1885), his masterpiece about a miners' strike; and La Bête humaine (1890). His theoretical manifesto Le Roman expérimental appeared in 1880.

The Dreyfus affair

In January 1898 Zola published J'Accuse…! on the front page of the newspaper L'Aurore, an open letter to President Félix Faure accusing the French government and army of anti-Semitism and the wrongful conviction of the Jewish artillery officer Alfred Dreyfus. Convicted of libel, Zola fled to England in July 1898 and returned the following year; his intervention is widely credited with turning public opinion and hastening Dreyfus's eventual exoneration in 1906.

Death

Zola died on 29 September 1902 of carbon monoxide poisoning caused by a blocked chimney in his Paris home; rumours that an enemy had deliberately obstructed the flue were never proven. In 1908 his remains were transferred to the Panthéon.
