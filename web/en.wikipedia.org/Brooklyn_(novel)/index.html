Brooklyn is a 2009 novel by Irish author Colm Tóibín. Set in the early 1950s, it tells the story of Eilis Lacey, a young woman from Enniscorthy, County Wexford, who emigrates to Brooklyn, New York, and is torn between the life she builds there and the home she left behind. The novel won the 2009 Costa Novel Award and was longlisted for the Booker Prize.

Plot

Eilis, unable to find suitable work in Ireland, accepts an offer arranged by a visiting priest, Father Flood, of a job in a Brooklyn department store and lodgings in a boarding house. Homesick at first, she studies bookkeeping at Brooklyn College and gradually settles. She falls in love with Tony Fiorello, an Italian-American plumber, and they marry in secret. When her sister Rose dies suddenly, Eilis returns to Ireland, where her mother and old friends — and a former suitor, Jim Farrell — draw her back into the life she might have had. Word of her marriage reaches her through an acquaintance, and she must choose; she returns to Brooklyn and to Tony.

Themes

The novel explores emigration, duty, silence and the quiet sacrifices of ordinary lives. Tóibín's restrained prose emphasises what Eilis cannot say — to her mother, to Tony, or to herself.

Adaptations and sequel

A film adaptation, Brooklyn (2015), directed by John Crowley from a screenplay by Nick Hornby and starring Saoirse Ronan, was nominated for the Academy Award for Best Picture. Tóibín published a sequel, Long Island, in 2024, continuing Eilis's story twenty years later.
