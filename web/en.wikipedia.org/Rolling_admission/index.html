Rolling admission

Rolling admission is a policy used by many colleges and universities to admit freshmen. It differs from other admission policies in that the applications are evaluated as they are received, and decisions are released on an ongoing basis rather than on a single fixed date.

Under rolling admission, candidates are usually invited to submit their applications over a large span of time, sometimes with no fixed deadline, or with a deadline late in the admission cycle. The university will then review the application and notify the applicant of its decision within a few weeks of submission, continuing until all spots in the incoming class are filled.

Advantages and disadvantages

The main attraction of rolling admission for students is the possibility of receiving an admission decision early, which reduces uncertainty. Because seats and scholarship funds are allocated as applications arrive, however, applicants who apply earlier in the cycle may have an advantage, and those who apply late may find fewer places available. Unlike early action or early decision, rolling admission decisions are non-binding.

Comparison with other plans

Other admission plans include regular decision, in which all applications are reviewed after a fixed deadline; early action, in which students apply early and receive an early non-binding decision; and early decision, in which students commit to enroll if admitted.
