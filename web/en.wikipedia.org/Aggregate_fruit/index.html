Aggregate fruit

An aggregate fruit or etaerio is a fruit that develops from the merger of several separate ovaries that were together in a single flower. In contrast, a simple fruit develops from one ovary, and a multiple fruit develops from the ovaries of several flowers fused together.

Structure

Each individual ovary of the flower produces a small fruitlet, and the fruitlets remain clustered together on a common receptacle to form the mature aggregate. The type of aggregate fruit is named after the type of fruitlet it contains:

  • Aggregate of drupelets – as in the raspberry, blackberry and dewberry, where each fruitlet is a tiny drupe containing a single seed.
  • Aggregate of achenes – as in the strawberry, where the true fruits are the achenes on the surface of a fleshy receptacle.
  • Aggregate of follicles – as in magnolia and peony.
  • Aggregate of samaras – as in the tulip tree.

Examples

Familiar aggregate fruits include the raspberry, blackberry, strawberry, custard apple, and the fruits of magnolias and buttercups. The rose hip is an aggregate of achenes enclosed within a fleshy hypanthium.

Not all compound-looking fruits are aggregates: the pineapple and mulberry are multiple fruits, formed from an entire inflorescence rather than a single flower.
