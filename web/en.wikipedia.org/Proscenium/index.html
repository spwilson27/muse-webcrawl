Proscenium

A proscenium is the vertical plane of space in a theatre, usually surrounded on the top and sides by a physical proscenium arch, where the audience sits on one side and the stage, which can change from show to show, is on the other. The arch acts like a picture frame through which the audience views the performance.

History

The term derives from the ancient Greek proskenion, the building in front of the orchestra. The modern proscenium arch developed in Renaissance Italy — the Teatro Farnese in Parma (1618) is often cited as the first permanent example — and became the dominant stage form in Western theatres through the 19th and early 20th centuries.

Features

The proscenium separates the auditorium from the backstage area, concealing rigging, curtains, and scenery changes. The area in front of the curtain, the apron, and elements such as the grand drape and teaser curtains are associated features. Alternative staging forms, such as thrust and arena stages, deliberately break the proscenium's "fourth wall" arrangement.
