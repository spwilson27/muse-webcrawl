John Churchill, 1st Duke of Marlborough (26 May 1650 – 16 June 1722) was an English soldier and statesman whose career spanned the reigns of five monarchs. Rising from humble origins at the court of the House of Stuart, he rose to become one of the most celebrated military commanders in British history, leading British and Allied forces to a series of victories over France during the War of the Spanish Succession.

Early life and career

Churchill was born at Musbury in Devon, the son of Winston Churchill, a royalist landowner whose estates had been ruined in the Civil War. In 1667 he became a page to James, Duke of York, and soon obtained a commission in the Foot Guards. He distinguished himself at the Siege of Maastricht in 1673, where he saved the life of the Duke of Monmouth and was noticed by King Louis XIV of France.

War of the Spanish Succession

In 1701 Churchill was appointed commander-in-chief of the English forces and ambassador to the Dutch Republic. His strategic masterpiece came in 1704, when he marched the Allied army from the Low Countries to the Danube and, together with Prince Eugene of Savoy, destroyed a Franco-Bavarian army at the Battle of Blenheim — the first major defeat of Louis XIV's armies in decades, which saved Vienna and knocked Bavaria out of the war. Further victories followed at Ramillies (1706), Oudenarde (1708) and the bloody Battle of Malplaquet (1709).

Later life and legacy

Churchill's influence waned as his wife Sarah's friendship with Queen Anne cooled. Accused of embezzlement, he was dismissed from his posts in 1711 and briefly went into exile, returning after Anne's death. A stroke in 1716 effectively ended his public life. He died in 1722. Parliament voted him Blenheim Palace at Woodstock, a vast baroque residence that remains his monument. Winston Churchill, his descendant and biographer, regarded him as perhaps the greatest British commander of his age.
