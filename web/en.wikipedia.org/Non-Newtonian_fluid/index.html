Non-Newtonian fluid

A non-Newtonian fluid is a fluid whose viscosity changes with the applied shear rate or shear stress, so it does not obey Newton's law of viscosity. Many common substances, including blood, paint, ketchup, and cornstarch suspensions, behave non-Newtonially.

Types

Shear-thinning (pseudoplastic) fluids such as ketchup and many polymer solutions become less viscous when sheared. Shear-thickening (dilatant) fluids such as concentrated cornstarch in water ("oobleck") become more viscous under sudden stress and can momentarily support a person's weight. Thixotropic fluids thin over time under constant shear, while Bingham plastics such as toothpaste flow only after a yield stress is exceeded.

Applications

Non-Newtonian behaviour is central to food processing, cosmetics, drilling muds, and body armour research using shear-thickening fluids. The mathematical study of such fluids falls within rheology.
