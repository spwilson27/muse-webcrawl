Premenstrual dysphoric disorder

Premenstrual dysphoric disorder (PMDD) is a severe form of premenstrual syndrome characterised by marked mood and behavioural symptoms — including depressed mood, anxiety, irritability and affective lability — that occur in the luteal phase of the menstrual cycle and remit shortly after menstruation begins. Symptoms must cause clinically significant distress or interference with work, school or relationships.

Diagnosis

PMDD was added to the DSM-5 in 2013 as a depressive disorder, after appearing in earlier editions as a condition for further study. Diagnosis requires prospective daily symptom ratings over at least two cycles confirming the cyclical pattern, distinguishing PMDD from a premenstrual worsening of another disorder. Prevalence is estimated at roughly 1.8–5.8% of menstruating women.

Treatment

First-line treatments include selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs), which are effective in PMDD even when taken only during the luteal phase, and combined oral contraceptives containing drospirenone. Cognitive behavioural therapy, calcium supplementation and lifestyle measures may also help; severe refractory cases are occasionally treated with GnRH analogues. The disorder is thought to involve an abnormal response to normal hormonal fluctuations, particularly to the progesterone metabolite allopregnanolone acting on GABA-A receptors.
