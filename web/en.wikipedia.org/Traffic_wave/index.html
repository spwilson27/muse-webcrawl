Traffic wave

A traffic wave, or phantom traffic jam, is a disturbance in the flow of traffic on a road that propagates backwards through a stream of vehicles, often without any obvious obstruction. Traffic waves arise when a small perturbation — a driver braking briefly, a lane change, or a merge — is amplified by the following distances and reaction times of drivers behind.

Dynamics

In car-following models, if drivers follow closely at near-capacity flow, a single braking event causes each following vehicle to brake slightly harder, producing a stop-and-go wave travelling upstream at roughly 15–20 km/h. Field experiments on circular tracks have demonstrated waves forming spontaneously in uniform flow once vehicle density passes a critical threshold.

Mitigation

Mitigation strategies include maintaining larger following gaps, variable speed limits that smooth flow, ramp metering, and adaptive cruise control systems tuned to damp rather than amplify perturbations.
