Pink Floyd are an English rock band formed in London in 1965. They gained a following as one of the first British psychedelic groups, distinguished by extended compositions, sonic experimentation, philosophical lyrics and elaborate live shows. They became one of the most commercially successful and influential acts in popular music, selling more than 250 million records worldwide.

History

The band was founded by Syd Barrett (guitar, lead vocals), Nick Mason (drums), Roger Waters (bass, vocals) and Richard Wright (keyboards, vocals), taking their name from two American blues musicians, Pink Anderson and Floyd Council. Under Barrett they released the hit singles "Arnold Layne" and "See Emily Play" and the acclaimed debut album The Piper at the Gates of Dawn (1967). As Barrett's mental health deteriorated, guitarist David Gilmour joined in 1967 and Barrett left the following year.

Classic era

After a period of experimentation, Pink Floyd achieved international success with The Dark Side of the Moon (1973), a concept album about conflict, greed and time that became one of the best-selling albums of all time, followed by Wish You Were Here (1975), Animals (1977) and the rock opera The Wall (1979), which produced the hit single "Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2". Waters became the band's dominant creative force, and tensions led to Wright's departure in 1979 and Waters's own acrimonious exit in 1985.

Later years and legacy

Gilmour, Mason and the returning Wright continued as Pink Floyd, releasing A Momentary Lapse of Reason (1987) and The Division Bell (1994). The classic line-up reunited briefly for Live 8 in 2005; Barrett and Wright died in 2006 and 2008 respectively. In 2014 Gilmour and Mason released The Endless River, largely built from unreleased 1993 sessions, as a tribute to Wright. Pink Floyd were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1996, and their prism artwork, inflatable pigs and spectacular stagecraft remain enduring icons of rock music.
