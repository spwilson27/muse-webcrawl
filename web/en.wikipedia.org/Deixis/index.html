Deixis

In linguistics, deixis (from Greek deixis, "pointing, reference") is the use of words and phrases whose meaning depends on the context of the utterance — on who is speaking, where, and when. Deictic expressions, or indexicals, include personal pronouns (I, you), demonstratives (this, that), spatial adverbs (here, there), temporal expressions (now, yesterday, tomorrow), and verbs of motion such as come and go. Deixis is a central topic in pragmatics and the philosophy of language.

The deictic centre

Following Karl Bühler, deictic expressions are interpreted relative to an origo or deictic centre, conventionally the speaker at the time and place of speaking: I refers to whoever speaks, here to the place of speaking, and now to the moment of speaking. When roles change — as when a speaker quotes another's words, or a narrative shifts viewpoint — the centre shifts with them. Recorded messages ("I am not here now") exploit and expose the anchoring of deictics to the utterance situation.

Categories

  • Person deixis — encodes participants: speaker, addressee, and others (I, you, they), including honorific systems that mark social relations.
  • Spatial (place) deixis — encodes location relative to the participants: here/there, this/that, often with distinctions of distance and visibility.
  • Temporal deixis — encodes time relative to the moment of speaking: now, then, yesterday, and tense itself.
  • Discourse deixis — refers to portions of the discourse itself ("in the next chapter", "that last remark").
  • Social deixis — encodes social status and relationships, as in pronoun systems distinguishing formal and familiar address (the T–V distinction).

Deixis and grammar

Grammatical categories such as tense, demonstratives, and personal pronouns are the principal codings of deixis, and every language grammaticalises some deictic distinctions — though languages differ, for example in how many degrees of distance their demonstratives mark. Philosophers including John Perry have used indexicals to argue that some beliefs ("I am making a mess") cannot be replaced by any non-indexical description, making deixis a problem for theories of meaning and reference.
