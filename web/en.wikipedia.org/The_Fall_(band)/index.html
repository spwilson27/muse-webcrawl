The Fall (band)

The Fall were an English post-punk group formed in Prestwich, Greater Manchester, in 1976. Their founder and only constant member was singer Mark E. Smith, whose abrasive, cryptic lyrical style and a constantly rotating line-up — more than sixty musicians passed through the band — made them notorious. The group took its name from Albert Camus's novel La Chute (The Fall). Over a 42-year career they released some 31 studio albums and recorded 24 sessions for BBC DJ John Peel, their most famous champion.

History

Rising from the Manchester punk scene, The Fall developed a spartan, repetitive, guitar-driven sound underpinned by Smith's declaimed, often semi-improvised lyrics about Northern English working-class life, literature and the supernatural. Landmark albums include Grotesque (After the Gramme) (1980), Hex Enduction Hour (1982) and This Nation's Saving Grace (1985). With Smith's then-wife Brix Smith on guitar, the mid-1980s brought chart singles, including covers of "Victoria" and "Ghost in My House". Later decades brought continual personnel churn, critical rediscoveries (such as The Real New Fall LP, 2003), ballet collaborations with Michael Clark, and a memoir, Smith's Renegade (2008).

Legacy

The Fall ended with Smith's death on 24 January 2018. Though they never achieved mass commercial success, the group is among the most influential of the post-punk era, cited by bands from Pavement to Sonic Youth; Peel's dictum that they were "always different, always the same" became their epitaph.
