Endocrine system

ComponentsPituitary, thyroid, parathyroids, adrenals, pancreas, gonads, pineal gland
Messenger typeHormones
FieldEndocrinology

The endocrine system is a messenger system in an organism comprising feedback loops of hormones that are released by internal glands directly into the circulatory system and that target and regulate distant organs. In vertebrates, the hypothalamus is the neural control center for all endocrine systems.

The major glands of the human endocrine system include the pituitary gland, thyroid gland, parathyroid glands, adrenal glands, the endocrine pancreas, the ovaries and testes, and the pineal gland. Unlike exocrine glands, endocrine glands are ductless and release their products into the bloodstream.

Major glands and hormones

The pituitary, often called the "master gland", secretes growth hormone, prolactin, and tropic hormones that control other glands. The thyroid gland secretes thyroxine (T4) and triiodothyronine (T3), regulating metabolism, while its parafollicular cells produce calcitonin. The adrenal glands produce cortisol, aldosterone and adrenaline. The islets of Langerhans in the pancreas secrete insulin and glucagon, which together regulate blood glucose.

Signalling and feedback

Hormones act by binding receptors on or in target cells; steroid hormones typically cross the membrane and alter gene expression, while peptide hormones activate cell-surface receptors and second-messenger cascades. Most axes are regulated by negative feedback — for example, rising thyroid hormone levels suppress further release of thyroid-stimulating hormone.

Disorders

Endocrine disorders include diabetes mellitus (insulin deficiency or resistance), hypothyroidism and hyperthyroidism, Addison's disease, Cushing's syndrome, and pituitary tumours. See also: Hormone, Pituitary gland, Thyroid, Endocrinology.
