Robert Yerkes

Robert Mearns Yerkes (May 26, 1876 – February 3, 1956) was an American psychologist and eugenicist, a pioneer in comparative psychology and primatology and in intelligence testing.

Robert Yerkes
Born: 26 May 1876, Pennsylvania
Died: 3 Feb 1956
Fields: Comparative psychology, primatology
Known for: Army Alpha/Beta tests, Yerkes–Dodson law

Work

At Harvard and Yale, Yerkes studied animal learning — the Yerkes–Dodson law relates arousal to performance. In WWI he directed US Army mental testing (Alpha and Beta tests) of ~1.75 million recruits. He founded a primate research station in Florida; its successor is the Yerkes National Primate Research Center at Emory (renamed Emory National Primate Research Center in 2022).

Criticism

His involvement in eugenics and use of test results to support restrictive immigration are now widely criticised, a history shared with early physical anthropology and anthropology.
