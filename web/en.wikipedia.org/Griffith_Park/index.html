Griffith Park

Griffith Observatory sits on the southern slope of Mount Hollywood in Griffith Park.

Griffith Park is a large municipal park in Los Angeles, California, situated in the eastern Santa Monica Mountains above the Los Feliz neighbourhood. Covering about 4,310 acres (1,740 ha) of mostly rugged chaparral and canyon terrain, it is one of the largest urban parks in North America and is often called the "Central Park of Los Angeles", though it is far larger and wilder than its New York counterpart.

History

The land formed part of the Rancho Los Feliz land grant. Colonel Griffith J. Griffith acquired much of the rancho in 1882 and in 1896 donated 3,015 acres to the city of Los Angeles as a Christmas gift, stipulating that it remain a public park. Griffith later funded the park's two most famous structures: the Griffith Observatory (opened 1935) and the Greek Theatre (opened 1930). During the Second World War the park held a prisoner-of-war and internment facility. Fire has repeatedly swept the park, notably in 1933 (when 29 workers died), 1961 and 2007.

Attractions

Besides the observatory and Greek Theatre, the park contains the Los Angeles Zoo, the Autry Museum of the American West, the Los Angeles Live Steamers Railroad Museum, Travel Town Museum, two golf courses, Bronson Canyon (the "Batcave" of the 1960s Batman series), and over 50 miles of hiking and equestrian trails. Mount Lee, at the park's western end, carries the famous Hollywood Sign, erected in 1923 just outside the park boundary in what is now often treated as part of it.

Wildlife

Despite being surrounded by freeways and city, the park supports coyotes, deer, bobcats and, famously, the mountain lion known as P-22, who lived in the park from 2012 until his death in 2022 and became a symbol of urban wildlife conservation.
