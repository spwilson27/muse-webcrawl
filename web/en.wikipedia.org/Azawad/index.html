Azawad, or Azawagh (Tuareg Berber: ⴰⵣⴰⵓⴷ Azawd; Arabic: أزواد Āzawād; French: Azaouad or Azaouâd), was a short-lived unrecognised state in northern Mali. On 6 April 2012, in the aftermath of the Tuareg rebellion of 2012, the National Movement for the Liberation of Azawad (MNLA) unilaterally proclaimed the independence of Azawad from the Republic of Mali; the declaration was not recognised by any country or international body.

History

The name Azawad originally referred to the arid basin northwest of Timbuktu. Tuareg and Arab populations of the north had rebelled against Bamako several times since Malian independence in 1960 — notably in 1963–64, 1990–95, and 2006 — demanding autonomy and development. The 2012 rebellion, swelled by heavily armed Tuareg fighters returning from the collapse of Gaddafi's Libya, rapidly seized Kidal, Gao, and Timbuktu.

Collapse

The MNLA's secular nationalists soon fell out with their Islamist allies — Ansar Dine, MUJAO, and al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb — who drove the MNLA from the northern cities by mid-2012 and imposed a harsh form of sharia. When the Islamists advanced south in January 2013, France launched Operation Serval at Mali's request, retaking the north. The MNLA renounced its independence claim in favour of autonomy, and a peace agreement was signed in Algiers in 2015, though insecurity in the region has persisted.
