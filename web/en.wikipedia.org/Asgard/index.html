Asgard (Old Norse: Ásgarðr, "Enclosure of the Æsir") is a realm in Norse mythology, the fortified home of the Æsir tribe of gods. Ruled by Odin and his wife Frigg, Asgard is described in the medieval Icelandic sources — chiefly the Poetic Edda and Snorri Sturluson's Prose Edda — as a walled stronghold in the heavens, connected to Midgard, the world of humans, by the burning rainbow bridge Bifröst.

Description

Within Asgard stand the great halls of the gods: Odin's hall Valhalla, where half of those slain in battle are gathered by the valkyries to await Ragnarök; Thor's hall Bilskirnir, said to be the largest building ever made; and Freyja's field Fólkvangr, which receives the other half of the battle-dead. Odin's high seat Hliðskjálf stands in Asgard, from which he can survey all the worlds. The plain of Iðavöllr, where the gods meet, and the well of Urd beneath the world-tree Yggdrasil are also associated with Asgard.

Building of the wall

According to the Prose Edda, Asgard's mighty wall was raised by a builder — in fact a giant (hrymthurse) in disguise — who demanded the goddess Freyja, the sun and the moon as payment. Aided by his stallion Svaðilfari, he nearly finished on time; the gods, on Loki's advice, sabotaged the work when Loki, transformed into a mare, lured the stallion away. The builder, enraged, revealed his true nature and was killed by Thor, leaving Asgard's wall incomplete in one section.

Ragnarök and renewal

At Ragnarök, the giants and the forces of chaos assault Asgard across Bifröst, which breaks beneath them; the gods fall in battle and the world is consumed by fire and flood. Afterwards, a renewed world rises from the sea, and the surviving gods return to Iðavöllr in a restored Asgard.

In popular culture

Asgard features prominently in modern adaptations of Norse myth, including Marvel Comics and the Marvel Cinematic Universe, where it is depicted as a technologically advanced alien realm and home of Thor.
