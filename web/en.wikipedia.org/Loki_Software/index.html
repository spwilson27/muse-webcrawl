Loki Software

Loki Software, Inc., also known as Loki Games or Loki Entertainment Software, was an American video game company based in Tustin, California, that specialised in porting popular video games to the Linux operating system. Founded in 1998 by Scott Draeker, it operated until its closure in early 2002, and remains the best-known attempt to establish commercial gaming on Linux during that era.

History

Loki's first title was a Linux port of Civilization: Call to Power (1999). It went on to release Linux versions of major games including Quake III Arena, Unreal Tournament, SimCity 3000, Heroes of Might and Magic III, Rune, Soldier of Fortune and Tribes 2, often in distinctive large retail boxes. Loki developed open-source tools alongside its ports, including the SDL-based work of Sam Lantinga and the Loki installer, and contributed to libraries widely used in subsequent cross-platform development.

Despite critical goodwill, sales were too small to sustain the business, and disputes with publishers over unpaid royalties contributed to its difficulties. Loki filed for bankruptcy in 2001 and ceased operations in January 2002. Its alumni and tools influenced later efforts such as icculus.org ports and, ultimately, modern Linux gaming through Valve's Steam and Proton.
