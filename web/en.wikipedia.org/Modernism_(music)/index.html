Modernism (music)

Modernist composers broke with tonal tradition in the early 20th century

Modernism in music refers, as in the other arts, to a body of work from roughly 1890 to 1975 characterised by a conscious break with past conventions — a sense that music must find fresh methods of organisation, of harmony, rhythm, and form, to reflect the transformed experience of modern life. Modernist composers questioned or abandoned tonality, traditional forms, and the assumption that music should be immediately pleasurable or beautiful.

Precursors and early modernism

The chromatic saturation of Wagner's Tristan und Isolde (1865) and the late works of Liszt and Mahler strained the tonal system to its limit. Richard Strauss's operas Salome (1905) and Elektra (1909) pushed dissonance further, while Debussy developed non-functional harmony drawn from whole-tone and pentatonic scales. In 1913 Igor Stravinsky's ballet The Rite of Spring, with its irregular rhythms and percussive orchestration, provoked a famous uproar at its Paris premiere.

Atonality and serialism

Arnold Schoenberg moved from free atonality (c. 1908) to the twelve-tone method (1921–23), in which all twelve pitch classes are ordered in a row that governs the work. His students Alban Berg and Anton Webern formed the Second Viennese School. After 1945, Pierre Boulez, Karlheinz Stockhausen, and Milton Babbitt extended serial control to rhythm, dynamics, and timbre ("total serialism"), while others — John Cage with chance music, Steve Reich and Philip Glass with minimalism — reacted against it.

Other currents

Modernism was never a single style: Charles Ives experimented independently in America with polytonality and collage; Bartók and Kodály fused folk material with modern harmony; neoclassicists such as Stravinsky (in his middle period), Hindemith, and Prokofiev revived older forms with modern harmony. By the 1970s many critics declared modernism succeeded by postmodern eclecticism, though its techniques remain foundational to contemporary composition.
