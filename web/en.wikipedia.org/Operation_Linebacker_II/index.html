Operation Linebacker II was an aerial bombing campaign conducted by US air forces from 18 to 29 December 1972 during the Vietnam War. Popularly known as the "Christmas bombings", it was the largest heavy bomber campaign mounted by the United States Air Force since the Second World War. President Richard Nixon ordered the campaign after the Paris peace talks broke down, intending to coerce North Vietnam back to negotiations and to destroy its war-making infrastructure around Hanoi and Haiphong. Strategic Air Command B-52 Stratofortresses flew 729 sorties over eleven days (with a Christmas Day pause), dropping roughly 15,000–20,000 tons of bombs, supported by tactical aircraft, EB-66 and Wild Weasel suppression missions, and naval aviation.

The campaign

The first three nights repeated rigid B-52 tactics — single-file streams at fixed altitude and routing, with post-target turns into the wind — that North Vietnamese SA-2 surface-to-air missile crews exploited, downing nine B-52s. After a one-day stand-down, SAC revised tactics: varied altitudes and headings, compressed streams, improved electronic countermeasures and chaff support. Losses fell sharply. Targets included rail yards, storage complexes, power plants, airfields, and SAM sites. North Vietnam fired over 1,200 SAMs and, by the final nights, was close to exhausting its missile inventory. North Vietnamese sources and later reporting recorded substantial civilian destruction in Hanoi — including the Kham Thien street bombings and the hit on Bach Mai Hospital — with North Vietnam reporting around 1,600 civilian deaths in Hanoi and Haiphong; the campaign drew international condemnation.

Outcome

Hanoi signalled willingness to resume talks, and bombing north of the 20th parallel ended on 29 December. The Paris Peace Accords were signed on 27 January 1973, essentially on terms available the previous October. Historians debate whether Linebacker II decisively coerced Hanoi, reassured Saigon, or simply accompanied a settlement both sides already needed; the campaign remains a central case study in coercive airpower and in the risks of predictable bomber tactics against integrated air defences. Fifteen B-52s were officially acknowledged lost to enemy action; dozens of US airmen were killed or captured.
