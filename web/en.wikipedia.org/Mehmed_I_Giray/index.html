Mehmed I Giray

Mehmed I Giray (1465–1523) was khan of the Crimean Khanate from 1515 to 1523. A son of Meñli I Giray, he is noted for uniting the Crimean Tatars and for his great raid on Moscow in 1521.

Reign

As kalga (heir) under his father, Mehmed campaigned against the Grand Duchy of Lithuania and Poland. On becoming khan in 1515 he sought to extend Crimean influence over the successor states of the Golden Horde, placing his brothers on the thrones of Kazan and Astrakhan. In 1521, allied with Kazan, he led a huge Tatar army against Muscovy, defeated the Russian forces near Kolomna, and besieged Moscow; Vasily III fled the capital and the Tatars withdrew with enormous plunder and captives after the city paid tribute.

Death

In 1523 Mehmed occupied Astrakhan, but his success alarmed the Nogais, who attacked his camp at night and killed both Mehmed and his brother, the kalga Ahmed. He was succeeded by his son Ğazı I Giray.
