Scandinavian Mountains

CountriesNorway, Sweden, Finland
Lengthabout 1,700 km
Highest pointGaldhøpiggen, 2,469 m

The Scandinavian Mountains, or the Scandes, are a mountain range running through the Scandinavian Peninsula, along the border between Norway and Sweden and extending into northern Finland. The western slopes plunge steeply to the Norwegian Sea, forming Norway's famous fjords, while the eastern side descends more gently toward Sweden.

The Scandes above a Norwegian fjord

Geology

The range originated in the Caledonian orogeny roughly 400 million years ago, when the continents Baltica and Laurentia collided. Though once far higher, the mountains were eroded and then uplifted again in the Cenozoic; repeated glaciations during the ice ages carved their valleys and fjords and left widespread high plateaus known as fjell or fell.

Peaks and climate

The highest summit is Galdhøpiggen (2,469 m) in Norway's Jotunheimen; Sweden's highest, Kebnekaise, is about 2,097 m and shrinking as its summit glacier melts. The range influences the climate of the peninsula, wringing precipitation from Atlantic air on the Norwegian side and casting a rain shadow over Sweden. Above the tree line lie alpine tundra, glaciers such as Jostedalsbreen, and habitat for reindeer, wolverine and Arctic fox.
