Harrisburg, Pennsylvania

Harrisburg is the capital city of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania, United States, and the seat of Dauphin County. With a population of about 50,000 in the city proper at the 2020 census, it is the ninth-most populous city in the state; the Harrisburg metropolitan area has a population of roughly 580,000. The city lies on the east bank of the Susquehanna River, about 105 miles (169 km) west of Philadelphia.

The site was first settled by Native American peoples, including the Susquehannock, and became a trading post under John Harris Sr. in the early 18th century; his son John Harris Jr. laid out the town in 1785 and named it Harrisburg. It became the state capital in 1812, largely because of its central location on the Susquehanna. Harrisburg grew as a transportation hub, with canals, then railroads, and later interstate highways converging there.

During the American Civil War, Harrisburg was a major Union Army training center (Camp Curtin) and a target of Confederate forces during the Gettysburg campaign of 1863, when fighting reached its suburbs at Lemoyne and Mechanicsburg. In 1979, the city came to national attention during the Three Mile Island accident at a nuclear power plant about ten miles southeast of the city, the most significant accident in American commercial nuclear power history.

Geography and economy

Harrisburg has a humid continental climate with hot summers and moderately cold winters. The city's economy is dominated by government — federal, state and county — which provides a stable employment base; health care, logistics and technology are other major sectors. Landmarks include the Pennsylvania State Capitol, completed in 1906 and renowned for its dome and artwork, the State Museum of Pennsylvania, and City Island in the Susquehanna River, home to recreational facilities and the Harrisburg Senators minor league baseball team.

Transportation

The city is served by Harrisburg International Airport in nearby Middletown, Amtrak's Keystone and Pennsylvanian services at Harrisburg Transportation Center, and several interstate highways including I-81, I-83 and the Pennsylvania Turnpike (I-76).
