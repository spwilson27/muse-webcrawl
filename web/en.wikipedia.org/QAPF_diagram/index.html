A QAPF diagram is a double ternary diagram used to classify igneous rocks according to their mineralogical composition, following the system devised by Albert Streckeisen and adopted by the International Union of Geological Sciences (IUGS). The acronym derives from the four mineral groups plotted: Quartz, Alkali feldspar, Plagioclase and Feldspathoid (foid).

Structure

Because quartz and feldspathoids cannot coexist in equilibrium, they form the apexes of two separate triangles joined along the alkali feldspar–plagioclase base. The upper triangle (QAP) classifies silica-oversaturated and saturated rocks such as granite, granodiorite, tonalite, diorite and gabbro; the lower triangle (AFP) classifies silica-undersaturated rocks such as foid syenites and foid gabbros. Modal proportions of the minerals are normalised to 100 per cent, ignoring mafic minerals unless they dominate, in which case ultramafic rocks are classified on a separate diagram.

Usage

The QAPF classification applies strictly to plutonic (intrusive) rocks whose mineral modes can be measured; a parallel version classifies volcanic rocks when modes are determinable, though volcanic rocks are more often classified chemically using the TAS diagram. Field names such as granite sensu stricto occupy precisely bounded fields defined by percentage ranges of quartz and the plagioclase proportion of total feldspar.
