United Nations Environment Programme

AbbreviationUNEP
Formation5 June 1972
HeadquartersNairobi, Kenya
Parent organisationUnited Nations
Known forGlobal environmental coordination, IPCC co-founding

The United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) is responsible for coordinating responses to environmental issues within the United Nations system. It was established by Maurice Strong, its first director, after the United Nations Conference on the Human Environment in Stockholm in June 1972. Its mandate is to provide leadership, deliver science, and develop solutions on a wide range of issues including climate change, the management of marine and terrestrial ecosystems, and green economic development.

Work

UNEP's activities include developing international environmental conventions, promoting environmental science, funding and implementing environment-related development projects, and formulating environmental policy in cooperation with national governments and NGOs. Its publications include the Global Environment Outlook and the annual Emissions Gap Report, which tracks how far countries' climate pledges fall short of the goals of the Paris Agreement. In 1988 UNEP and the World Meteorological Organization jointly founded the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC).

Governance

UNEP is governed by the United Nations Environment Assembly, which since 2012 has had universal membership of all UN member states and meets biennially in Nairobi — the first UN headquarters in the developing world. UNEP also hosts the secretariats of several multilateral environmental agreements, including the Convention on Biological Diversity, the Minamata Convention on Mercury, and the secretariat of the Montreal Protocol's Ozone unit, whose work on phasing out chlorofluorocarbons is regarded as one of the great successes of environmental diplomacy.
