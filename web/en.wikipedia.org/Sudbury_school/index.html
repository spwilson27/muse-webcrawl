Sudbury school

A Sudbury school is a type of school, usually for the K–12 age range, where students have complete responsibility for their own education, and the school is run by a direct democracy in which students and staff are almost equal citizens. Students use their time however they wish, and learn as a by-product of ordinary experience rather than through classes or a standard curriculum. The schools are named after the Sudbury Valley School, founded in Framingham, Massachusetts, in 1968 by Daniel Greenberg and colleagues.

Model

The Sudbury model has two central tenets: first, that young people are naturally curious and learn best what they need to know through their own initiative; second, that democratic participation is the best preparation for life in a democratic society. There are no grades, tests, homework, or fixed classes. The School Meeting, in which every student and staff member has one vote, makes the rules, budgets, and staffing decisions. A judicial committee handles rule infractions.

Schools

Dozens of schools worldwide have adopted the model, including schools in the United States, Europe, Israel, and Japan. Sudbury Valley School itself serves students roughly from age 4 through 19.

Criticism and research

Critics question whether children will acquire core academic skills without instruction; follow-up studies of graduates, conducted by the school and independent researchers, report that alumni enter higher education and employment at rates comparable to conventionally educated peers, though peer-reviewed research remains limited.
