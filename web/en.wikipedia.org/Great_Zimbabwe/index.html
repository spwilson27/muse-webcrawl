Great Zimbabwe

Great Zimbabwe
Location: Masvingo Province, Zimbabwe
Built: c. 11th–15th centuries AD
Culture: Ancestral Shona / Kingdom of Zimbabwe
UNESCO: World Heritage Site (1986)

Great Zimbabwe is a medieval city in the south-eastern hills of the modern country of Zimbabwe. It was the capital of the Kingdom of Zimbabwe and a centre for trade in gold and ivory. Construction began in the 11th century and continued until it was abandoned in the 15th century.

Architecture

The ruins, covering about 800 hectares, are the largest ancient stone structures south of the Sahara. Dry-stone walls — built without mortar — form the Hill Complex, the Great Enclosure with its solid conical tower, and the Valley Complex. Soapstone Zimbabwe Birds were carved on monoliths.

Trade and society

Finds of Chinese porcelain, Persian ceramics and Arabian coins show trade links to the Swahili coast at Sofala and beyond. Population may have reached 10,000–20,000.

Interpretation

Colonial-era claims attributing the site to foreign builders have been rejected by archaeologists; it was built by ancestors of the Shona people. The name Zimbabwe means 'houses of stone'.
