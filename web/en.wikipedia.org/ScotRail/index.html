ScotRail

ScotRail is the brand name for the passenger train services operated within Scotland. Since 1 April 2022 the services have been run by ScotRail Trains Ltd, an operator of last resort owned by the Scottish Government through Scottish Rail Holdings. The network covers most of Scotland's railway, including the dense suburban network around Glasgow, intercity routes between the seven cities, and rural lines such as the West Highland Line and the Far North Line.

History

The ScotRail name was first used by British Rail in 1983 for its Scottish regional services. After privatisation in the 1990s, the ScotRail franchise was held successively by National Express (1997–2004), FirstGroup as First ScotRail (2004–2015) and Abellio ScotRail (2015–2022). The Scottish Government announced in 2021 that it would bring the franchise into public ownership when the Abellio contract reached its break point, and ScotRail Trains took over on 1 April 2022.

Services and network

ScotRail operates around 2,300 services a day serving more than 350 stations. Flagship intercity services link Edinburgh, Glasgow, Aberdeen, Inverness, Dundee, Perth and Stirling, while the Glasgow suburban network is the largest commuter rail system in the United Kingdom outside London. Long-distance routes include the scenic West Highland Line to Oban, Fort William and Mallaig, the Kyle of Lochalsh line, and the Borders Railway between Edinburgh and Tweedbank, reopened in 2015. Cross-border services into England are mostly run by other operators, though ScotRail runs services to Carlisle and Newcastle.

Rolling stock

The fleet includes Class 380, 385 and 318/320 electric multiple units, Class 156, 158 and 170 diesel multiple units, and Inter7City High Speed Trains (Class 43 power cars with Mark 3 coaches) on intercity routes. Trains carry a dark blue livery with a white Saltire-inspired dot pattern. A programme of electrification and the introduction of new trains has been a recurring theme of Scottish rail policy, overseen by Transport Scotland.
