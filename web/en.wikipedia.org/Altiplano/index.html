Altiplano

Altiplano
Region: South America (Bolivia, Peru, Chile, Argentina)
Elevation: ~3,750 m (12,300 ft)

The Altiplano (Spanish for "high plain"), also known as the Andean Plateau or the Bolivian Plateau, is the area in west-central South America where the Andes are at their widest. It is the most extensive area of high plateau on Earth outside Tibet. The Altiplano is located in Bolivia, Peru and extends into northern Chile and Argentina, at an average elevation of about 3,750 metres (12,300 ft) above sea level.

Geography

The plateau stretches roughly 1,000 km from north to south. It is enclosed by the Western Cordillera and the Eastern Cordillera of the Andes. Important features of the region include Lake Titicaca, the largest lake in South America and the world's highest navigable lake, and the Salar de Uyuni, the world's largest salt flat. The puna grassland covers much of the plateau. Because of its enclosed drainage, much of the Altiplano is an endorheic basin: rivers flow into lakes and salt flats rather than to the sea.

Climate and life

The climate is cool and semi-arid to arid, with strong daily temperature fluctuations and intense solar radiation. The inhabitants, largely Aymara and Quechua peoples, have adapted over millennia to life at altitude. Traditional agriculture centres on potatoes, quinoa and other hardy crops, and herding of llamas and alpacas. Cities on the plateau include La Paz and El Alto in Bolivia, and Puno and Juliaca in Peru.

Geology

The Altiplano formed as the Nazca Plate subducted beneath the South American Plate, uplifting the crust and trapping sediments between the rising cordilleras. Its lakes, including the ancient Lake Minchin and Lake Tauca, have expanded and contracted dramatically with past climate changes.
