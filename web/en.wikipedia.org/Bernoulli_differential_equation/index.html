Bernoulli differential equation

In mathematics, a Bernoulli differential equation is a first-order ordinary differential equation of the form

y′ + P(x)y = Q(x)yn,

where n is any real number other than 0 or 1. The equation is named after Jacob Bernoulli, who discussed it in 1695; it was solved by Leibniz and by Jacob's brother Johann Bernoulli. Despite its nonlinear appearance, it can always be reduced to a linear equation by a substitution.

Solution

Dividing both sides by yn and substituting u = y1−n converts the equation into the linear equation u′ + (1−n)P(x)u = (1−n)Q(x), which is then solvable by the integrating factor method. For n = 0 the equation is already linear, and for n = 1 it is separable. Bernoulli-type equations arise in applications such as fluid flow and population models with nonlinear growth.
