Peak bagging


Peak baggers work through recognised lists of summits.

Peak bagging or hill bagging is an activity in which hikers, climbers, and mountaineers attempt to reach the summits of a collection of peaks, usually those above a certain height or prominence, or those on a recognised list. Unlike a single expedition, peak bagging is typically a long-term project pursued over years.

Lists

Famous lists include the Munros of Scotland (282 peaks over 3,000 feet, first listed by Sir Hugh Munro in 1891), the 46 High Peaks of the Adirondacks, the 48 four-thousand footers of New Hampshire's White Mountains, the Colorado fourteeners, and the Seven Summits. In England and Wales, the Wainwrights, Birketts, and Hewitts attract completers known as "baggers".

Organisations and records

Clubs such as the Adirondack Forty-Sixers and the Scottish Mountaineering Club maintain registers of completers. Some participants pursue speed records, while others object that list-driven climbing encourages crowding and erosion on popular summits.
