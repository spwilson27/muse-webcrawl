Electronic cigarette

An electronic cigarette, or e-cigarette, is a device that simulates tobacco smoking by heating a liquid — usually containing nicotine, propylene glycol, glycerine and flavourings — to produce an aerosol that the user inhales, an act known as vaping.

Construction

Most devices consist of a battery, a heating element (atomiser or coil) and a reservoir of e-liquid, in forms ranging from cigarette-like disposables to refillable tank systems and pod devices.

History

The modern e-cigarette was developed by Chinese pharmacist Hon Lik in 2003. Products spread rapidly in Europe and North America in the late 2000s and 2010s, with pod systems such as Juul becoming especially popular among young people.

Health effects and regulation

Health authorities consider e-cigarettes likely less harmful than combustible cigarettes because they avoid combustion products, but their long-term effects remain under study, and nicotine is addictive. Regulation varies widely: many countries restrict sales, advertising and flavours, and some ban the products outright.
