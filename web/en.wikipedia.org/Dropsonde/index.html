Dropsonde

A dropsonde is an expendable weather reconnaissance instrument that is dropped from an aircraft, usually at altitude, and measures atmospheric conditions as it descends by parachute toward the surface. It transmits pressure, temperature, humidity, and—by tracking its GPS position—wind speed and direction data back to the aircraft in real time, producing a vertical profile of the atmosphere.

Use in tropical cyclones

Dropsondes are a primary tool of hurricane hunting: NOAA and U.S. Air Force Reserve aircraft such as the WP-3D Orion and WC-130J fly into tropical cyclones and release dropsondes, including into the eye, where the sounding reveals the central pressure and the structure of the storm. The data are fed directly into numerical weather prediction models and measurably improve track and intensity forecasts.

Design

The standard instrument, developed at the National Center for Atmospheric Research, is a small cylinder weighing about 165 grams that descends at roughly 10–15 metres per second from typical release altitudes. Modern GPS dropsondes report data about twice per second during a fall lasting several minutes.
