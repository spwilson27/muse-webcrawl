Zanzibar

Zanzibar is a semi-autonomous archipelago in the Indian Ocean off the coast of East Africa, forming part of the United Republic of Tanzania. The main islands are Unguja (often called Zanzibar Island) and Pemba.

History

Zanzibar was a major trading centre for centuries, linking Africa with Arabia, Persia, and India. From 1698 it was ruled by the Sultanate of Oman, and in the nineteenth century it became a hub of the clove trade and, notoriously, of the East African slave trade. It became a British protectorate in 1890.

Revolution and union

In January 1964 a revolution overthrew the Sultan, and three months later Zanzibar united with Tanganyika to form Tanzania, while retaining considerable autonomy with its own president and House of Representatives.

Economy

The economy rests on spice production — especially cloves — seaweed farming, and a large and growing tourism industry centred on the historic Stone Town, a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
