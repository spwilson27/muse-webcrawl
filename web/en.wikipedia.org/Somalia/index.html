Somalia, officially the Federal Republic of Somalia, is a country in the Horn of Africa, bordered by Ethiopia to the west, Djibouti to the northwest, Kenya to the southwest, and the Gulf of Aden and the Indian Ocean to the north and east. It has the longest coastline on mainland Africa. Its capital and largest city is Mogadishu.

History

The region was an ancient centre of trade — the classical "Land of Punt" — and from the Middle Ages Somali sultanates such as Ajuran and Adal dominated the Horn's commerce with Arabia and beyond. In the late 19th century Britain and Italy colonized the north and south respectively. The two territories united on independence in 1960 under President Aden Abdullah Osman Daar.

A 1969 coup brought Mohamed Siad Barre to power; his regime collapsed in 1991 amid civil war, and Somalia fragmented among clan-based factions and regional administrations, including the self-declared (but internationally unrecognized) Republic of Somaliland. Foreign interventions, famine, and the rise of the al-Shabaab insurgency prolonged the crisis. A federal government was established in Mogadishu in 2012 and has gradually extended its authority with African Union support.

Geography and climate

Somalia's terrain is mainly arid plateau and plains, rising to the Cal Madow mountains in the north. The climate is hot and dry with irregular rainfall and two monsoon seasons; droughts are recurrent and severe. The Juba and Shabelle rivers are the only perennial rivers.

People and economy

Somalis are ethnically homogeneous but divided into clan families that structure politics and society; Islam is nearly universal. The economy rests on livestock exports, remittances from a large diaspora, agriculture, and a notably dynamic telecommunications sector, since formal banking and industry remain limited.
