Noradrenaline (Norepinephrine)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Noradrenaline, also called norepinephrine, is an organic chemical in the catecholamine family that functions in the brain and body as both a hormone and a neurotransmitter. The name "noradrenaline" is used in the United Kingdom and in the international nonproprietary name, while "norepinephrine" — from Greek roots meaning "upon the kidney" — is standard in the United States.

Function

Noradrenaline is released by the adrenal medulla into the bloodstream as part of the sympathetic "fight or flight" response, increasing heart rate, blood pressure, and blood flow to muscles. In the brain, most noradrenergic neurons originate in the locus coeruleus, and the system regulates arousal, alertness, attention, and mood. It acts on alpha and beta adrenergic receptors.

Medical use

Intravenous norepinephrine is a first-line vasopressor for raising blood pressure in septic shock and other forms of distributive shock. Drugs that increase noradrenaline signalling, such as serotonin–norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors and atomoxetine, are used in depression and ADHD. Noradrenaline is synthesised from dopamine by the enzyme dopamine beta-hydroxylase and is in turn the precursor of adrenaline (epinephrine).
