Coupling reaction

Coupling reaction
FieldOrganic chemistry
TypeCarbon–carbon / carbon–heteroatom bond formation
Common catalystsPalladium, nickel, copper
Notable examplesSuzuki, Heck, Negishi, Sonogashira, Buchwald–Hartwig

A coupling reaction in organic chemistry is a general term for a variety of reactions where two fragments are joined together with the aid of a metal catalyst, forming a new chemical bond. In one important type, a main group organometallic compound reacts with an organic halide to form a new carbon–carbon bond in the product.

Cross-coupling

Cross-coupling reactions join two different partners and are among the most useful methods in synthesis. The palladium-catalysed Suzuki–Miyaura coupling of boronic acids, the Heck reaction of alkenes, the Negishi coupling of organozinc reagents, and the Sonogashira coupling of alkynes are standard tools in pharmaceutical and materials chemistry. The 2010 Nobel Prize in Chemistry was awarded to Richard Heck, Ei-ichi Negishi, and Akira Suzuki for palladium-catalysed cross couplings.

Mechanism

Typical catalytic cycles involve oxidative addition of the organic halide to a low-valent metal, transmetalation or alkene insertion, and reductive elimination to release the coupled product and regenerate the catalyst. Homocoupling joins two identical fragments, as in the Glaser coupling of terminal alkynes.
