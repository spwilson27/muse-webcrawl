Siege of Tyre (332 BC)

The siege of Tyre was a siege of the city of Tyre, an island city and naval base in modern Lebanon, by Alexander the Great in 332 BC during his campaigns against the Achaemenid Empire. After a seven-month siege, the Macedonians stormed the city.

Background

After the Battle of Issus, Alexander moved down the Phoenician coast to neutralise the Persian navy. Tyre, protected by high walls on an island about a kilometre offshore, refused him entry to sacrifice in its temple of Melqart.

The mole

Alexander demolished the mainland city of Old Tyre and used the rubble to build a causeway, or mole, across the strait — a work of engineering that permanently joined Tyre to the mainland. When the mole neared the walls, and Phoenician and Cypriot fleets defected to him, his ships mounted siege engines and breached the southern walls in July or August 332 BC.

Aftermath

Tyre was sacked; thousands of inhabitants were killed or sold into slavery. The capture removed the last major Persian naval base in the eastern Mediterranean and secured Alexander's rear before he advanced on Egypt and Gaza.
