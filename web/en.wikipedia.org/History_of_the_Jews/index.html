History of the Jews

The history of the Jews and of Judaism spans more than three thousand years, from the ancient Israelites of the Levant through diaspora communities across Europe, North Africa, the Middle East, and the Americas, to the modern State of Israel.

Ancient period

According to the Hebrew Bible, the Israelites emerged in Canaan in the late second millennium BCE. The kingdoms of Israel and Judah were conquered by Assyria (722 BCE) and Babylon (586 BCE) respectively; the destruction of the Second Temple by Rome in 70 CE and the suppression of the Bar Kokhba revolt (135 CE) dispersed much of the Jewish population.

Diaspora

Under rabbinic leadership, Judaism was reshaped around synagogue, study, and law. Jewish communities flourished at times in Babylon, Islamic Spain, and medieval Europe, but also suffered persecution, expulsions (from England in 1290, Spain in 1492), and pogroms. The Haskalah (Jewish Enlightenment) and emancipation in the nineteenth century brought new integration and new movements.

Twentieth century

The Holocaust (Shoah), in which Nazi Germany and its collaborators murdered approximately six million Jews, devastated European Jewry. The Zionist movement, begun in the late nineteenth century, culminated in the establishment of the State of Israel in 1948. Today the largest Jewish communities are in Israel and the United States.
