Murtala Ramat Muhammed (8 November 1938 – 13 February 1976) was a Nigerian general who led the 1966 Nigerian counter-coup and later became head of state of Nigeria following the overthrow of General Yakubu Gowon in July 1975. He was assassinated in February 1976 during an unsuccessful coup attempt, after just over six months in power.

Early life and military career

Muhammed was born in Kano and educated at Barewa College in Zaria before joining the Nigerian Army in 1958. He trained at Sandhurst in the United Kingdom and saw service in the Congo and during the Nigerian Civil War (1967–1970), where he commanded the Second Division and became a controversial but prominent figure.

Head of state

Coming to power on 29 July 1975, Muhammed launched an energetic programme of reform: he purged the civil service, announced a timetable for a return to civilian rule, began the process that moved the federal capital from Lagos to Abuja, and created several new states. His government's assertive foreign policy included recognition of the MPLA government in Angola, contrary to United States preferences.

Assassination and legacy

On 13 February 1976 Muhammed was shot dead in Lagos by Lt. Col. Bukar Suka Dimka's faction during an abortive coup. His deputy, Olusegun Obasanjo, succeeded him and carried through the transition to civilian rule in 1979. Muhammed remains a popular figure in Nigeria; his portrait appears on the 20-naira note, and Lagos's international airport is named after him.
