Characidae

Characidae, the characids or characins, is a family of freshwater tropical and subtropical fish belonging to the order Characiformes. The name "characins" is the common name for many of the members of this family, though many common names are used interchangeably, mainly including tetras. Historically, most fish described as characins belonged to this family, but many have since been moved into different families. The word Characidae comes from the Greek charax, meaning "pointed stake".

Distribution and habitat

Characins are most abundant in the Amazon and Orinoco basins in South America, though they also occur throughout much of Central and South America. Examples range from the smallest fish in the world to some of the largest characiform fish. The Salminus species can reach up to 32 inches in length, while adult females of the smallest species, the Bolivian pygmy blue characin (Xenurobrycon polyancistrus), reach roughly 17 mm in length.

Relationship with humans

A number of characins become quite large and are important food or game fish. However, most stay small at less than 3 cm (1.2 in). Many of these, known generally as tetras, are popular aquarium fish due to their bright colors, general hardiness, and tolerance towards other fish in community tanks.

Systematics

Characidae is the largest family in the order Characiformes, with over 1,100 species. Its systematics have been subject to extensive revision; many genera formerly placed in Characidae have been reassigned to families such as Acestrorhynchidae, Bryconidae, Iguanodectidae, Serrasalmidae, and Triportheidae. Genera remaining in Characidae include Hyphessobrycon, Hemigrammus, Moenkhausia, Paracheirodon (the neon tetras), Gymnocorymbus, Astyanax, and Jupiaba, among many others.
