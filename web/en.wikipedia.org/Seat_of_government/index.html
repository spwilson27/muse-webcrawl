Seat of government

The Palace of Westminster in London, seat of the Parliament of the United Kingdom.

The seat of government is (as defined by Brewer's Politics) "the building, complex of buildings or the city from which a government exercises its authority". In most countries the nation's capital is also its seat of government, but the two are not always the same place.

Distinction from capital

In some states the constitutional capital differs from the actual seat of government. In the Netherlands, Amsterdam is the capital while The Hague hosts the government, parliament, supreme court and royal court. Bolivia's constitutional capital is Sucre, while La Paz is the seat of government. South Africa divides functions among three capitals: Pretoria (executive), Cape Town (legislative) and Bloemfontein (judicial).

Local government

At the subnational level, the seat of government of a county or municipality is often called the county seat in the United States, or the administrative centre elsewhere. Such towns host the courthouse, council offices and other organs of local administration.

Relocation

Governments have sometimes moved their seats, temporarily or permanently, for strategic, political or symbolic reasons. Purpose-built capitals such as Canberra, Brasília, Islamabad and Naypyidaw were constructed to house a seat of government away from an older commercial capital. During wars, governments-in-exile have exercised authority from foreign cities.
