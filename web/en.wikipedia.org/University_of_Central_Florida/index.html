The University of Central Florida (UCF) is a public research university in Orlando, Florida, United States. Founded in 1963 by the Florida Legislature as Florida Technological University to supply engineers and scientists to the growing space program at nearby Kennedy Space Center, it adopted its present name in 1978. With roughly 68,000 students, it is one of the largest universities in the United States by enrolment.

Academics

UCF offers more than 230 degree programs through 13 colleges, including the College of Engineering and Computer Science, the Rosen College of Hospitality Management — located near Orlando's tourism corridor — and the College of Medicine at Lake Nona Medical City. It is classified among "R1: Doctoral Universities — Very high research activity" and is known for research in optics and lasers (through CREOL, the Center for Research and Education in Optics and Lasers), simulation, and computer science; its competitive programming and cyber defense teams are nationally decorated.

Campus

The main campus, east of Orlando, is notable for its concentric-circle layout centered on the Reflecting Pond. Spirit Splash, in which thousands of students rush the pond during homecoming week, is a celebrated tradition.

Athletics

The UCF Knights compete in NCAA Division I and joined the Big 12 Conference in 2023. The football team completed an undefeated season in 2017, winning the Peach Bowl and claiming a share of the national championship.
