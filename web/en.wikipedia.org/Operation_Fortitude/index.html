Operation Fortitude was the code name for a World War II military deception employed by the Allied nations as part of Operation Bodyguard, the overall deception strategy surrounding the 1944 landings in Normandy (Operation Overlord). Fortitude had two parts: Fortitude North, which threatened an invasion of Norway from Scotland, and Fortitude South, the far larger effort designed to convince Germany that the main invasion would come at the Pas-de-Calais, the closest point to Britain, rather than in Normandy.

The deception

The centrepiece of Fortitude South was the fictitious First United States Army Group (FUSAG), supposedly commanded by General George S. Patton and based in south-east England opposite Calais. The phantom army was supported by inflatable tanks and landing craft, dummy buildings, fake radio traffic, and a web of double agents run under the British Double-Cross System — most famously the agent Juan Pujol García, codenamed GARBO — who fed Berlin a consistent false picture. Captured and turned agents, together with decrypted German assessments via Ultra, let the Allies verify the deception was believed.

Effect

Even after the Normandy landings on 6 June 1944, German commanders treated them as a diversion and held the powerful Fifteenth Army at the Pas-de-Calais for some seven weeks awaiting the "real" invasion. Fortitude is regarded as one of the most successful deception operations in military history and contributed substantially to the survival of the Normandy beachhead.
