Freesat is a British free-to-air satellite television service launched on 6 May 2008. It was originally a joint venture between the BBC and ITV plc and is now managed by Everyone TV. The service provides subscription-free access to television and radio channels via satellite, mainly for households outside the coverage of the terrestrial Freeview platform.

Service

Freesat broadcasts from the Astra 2 satellite group at 28.2° east, the same position used by Sky. Viewers require a satellite dish and a Freesat receiver or a television with a built-in Freesat tuner. The platform offers an electronic programme guide and, on newer devices, connected services such as catch-up players including BBC iPlayer and ITVX.

Channels

The service carries more than 160 channels, including the main BBC, ITV, Channel 4, and Channel 5 services, alongside news, film, music, and radio channels, many in high definition, with 4K content available on compatible boxes.

Reception

By the mid-2010s Freesat was in use in around two million UK households. It is particularly popular in rural areas with poor terrestrial reception and as a free alternative for former Sky subscribers using existing dishes.
