Magarey Medal

The Magarey Medal is an Australian rules football award presented annually to the fairest and most brilliant player in the South Australian National Football League (SANFL) during the home-and-away season, as judged by the field umpires. It is one of the oldest individual awards in Australian football, first presented in 1898.

History

The medal is named after William Ashley Magarey, a lawyer and football administrator who served as chairman of the South Australian Football Association and donated the award. Votes are cast by umpires on a 3–2–1 basis after each match, and the count is traditionally held in the week before the SANFL Grand Final.

Notable winners

Multiple-time winners include Michael Hyland, Walter Scott, Bob Quinn, Lindsay Head, Barrie Barbary, Barrie Robran, Russell Ebert (four medals), and Michael Tuck's contemporary Nathan Buckley, who won in 1992 before his AFL career.
