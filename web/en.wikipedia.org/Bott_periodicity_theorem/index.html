Bott periodicity theorem

The Bott periodicity theorem, in algebraic topology, describes a periodicity in the homotopy groups of the classical matrix groups discovered by Raoul Bott in 1957–1959. It is a foundational result connecting topology, geometry and analysis, and it underlies much of modern K-theory.

Statement

Bott showed that the stable homotopy groups of the unitary, orthogonal and symplectic groups are periodic. For the unitary groups, the period is 2: the homotopy groups repeat every two steps, so that πk+2(U) ≅ πk(U), with π0(U) = 0 and π1(U) = ℤ. For the orthogonal and symplectic groups the period is 8, giving the famous eightfold pattern that cycles through ℤ, ℤ/2, 0 and other groups.

Proof and ideas

Bott's original proof used Morse theory on the loop spaces of the classical groups, studying geodesics on symmetric spaces. The result can also be approached through Clifford algebras, following the work of Atiyah, Bott and Shapiro, which explains the period-8 pattern in terms of the representation theory of Clifford algebras over the real numbers.

Consequences

Bott periodicity is the engine behind topological K-theory: the K-theory groups of a space inherit the 2-fold (complex) or 8-fold (real) periodicity. It also feeds into the theory of elliptic operators and the Atiyah–Singer index theorem, and into modern condensed-matter physics, where the "tenfold way" classifies topological insulators and superconductors.
