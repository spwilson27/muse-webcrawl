Mythology of Lost


Labelled image: the Island, setting of Lost's mythology

The mythology of Lost refers to the fictional backstory and supernatural framework of the American television series Lost (2004–2010), created by Jeffrey Lieber, J. J. Abrams and Damon Lindelof. Across six seasons, the series built an elaborate mythology involving a mysterious Island with healing and electromagnetic properties, time travel, and an ancient conflict between two beings, Jacob and the Man in Black.

The Island and Jacob

The Island is portrayed as a place of unusual electromagnetic energy emanating from a source of light at its heart — described as connected to life, death and rebirth. Across millennia it was guarded by a series of protectors, most recently Jacob, who brought people to the Island to prove that humanity is not inherently corruptible, against the position of his brother, the Man in Black, who sought to leave the Island and, if freed, was implied to threaten the wider world.

The DHARMA Initiative and the Others

In the 1970s the DHARMA Initiative, a scientific research project founded by Gerald and Karen DeGroot and funded by Alvar Hanso, built stations across the Island to study its properties. Its experiments involved the numbers 4, 8, 15, 16, 23 and 42 — the core values of the fictional Valenzetti Equation. The Initiative was wiped out in "the Purge" by the Island's indigenous inhabitants, the Others, led by Benjamin Linus under Jacob's indirect direction. The series' narrative follows the survivors of Oceanic Flight 815, who become entangled in this conflict and in the Island's displacements through time.

Resolution

In the final season, the candidates chosen to succeed Jacob, flash-sideways sequences revealed to be a kind of afterlife, and the ultimate defeat of the Man in Black conclude the mythology, with Hurley becoming the Island's new protector. Many minor mysteries were deliberately left unresolved, generating ongoing debate among viewers.
