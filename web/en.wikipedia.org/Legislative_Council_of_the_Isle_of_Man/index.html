Legislative Council of the Isle of Man

The Legislative Council (Manx: Yn Choonseil Slattyssagh) is the upper chamber of the Tynwald, the parliament of the Isle of Man. Together with the directly elected House of Keys it legislates for the island, one of the oldest continuous parliaments in the world.

Composition

The Council has eleven members: the President of Tynwald, the Bishop of Sodor and Man, the Attorney General, and eight members elected by the House of Keys. Historically it included judicial officers such as the Deemsters, removed by reform.

Role

Most bills begin in the Keys; the Council acts mainly as a revising chamber, able to amend and delay but not ultimately to block legislation or money bills. Its members do not represent constituencies.

Reform

Debate over an elected or abolished upper house recurs in Manx politics, with gradual reforms shifting the Council away from ex officio membership toward members chosen by the Keys.
