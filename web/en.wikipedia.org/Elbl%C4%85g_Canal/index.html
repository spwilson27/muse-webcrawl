The Elbląg Canal (Polish: Kanał Elbląski; German: Oberländischer Kanal) is a canal in northern Poland running 80.5 kilometres from Lake Drużno, near Elbląg, south to the Masurian lake district around Ostróda and Iława. It is famous — and unique in the world at this scale — for a system of inclined planes on which boats are carried over dry land on rail-mounted cradles between stretches of water at different levels.

Construction

The canal was built between 1844 and 1860 under the direction of the Prussian engineer Georg Steenke to float timber and agricultural produce from the Oberland (Upper Prussia) down to the port of Elbing (Elbląg). Rather than building locks to overcome a rise of about 100 metres, Steenke adopted the inclined-plane system he had seen on the Morris Canal in the United States; the five planes are powered by water-driven machinery, with a water wheel system using the canal's own flow.

The inclined planes

At each slipway — Buczyniec, Kąty, Oleśnica, Jelenie and Całuny — vessels are floated onto a wheeled cradle and hauled up or down a grassy slope on rails, crossing the summit and descending into the next pound. The machinery, largely original 19th-century equipment, still operates, and passenger boats make the trip a popular tourist attraction.

Status

The canal is protected as a historic monument in Poland and was named one of the Seven Wonders of Poland in a 2011 plebiscite. A full revitalisation of the canal's structures was completed in 2015.
