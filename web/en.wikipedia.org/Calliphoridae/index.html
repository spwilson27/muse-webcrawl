Calliphoridae

Calliphoridae
Kingdom: Animalia · Phylum: Arthropoda
Class: Insecta · Order: Diptera
Common names: Blow flies, blow-flies, bottle flies

The Calliphoridae (commonly known as blow flies, blow-flies, carrion flies, bluebottles, or greenbottles) are a family of insects in the order Diptera, with almost 1,900 known species. Many species are metallic blue, green or black in colour. The maggot larvae, often used as fishing bait, are known as gentles.

Biology

Adult calliphorids feed on nectar and other sugary substances and are important pollinators of some plants. Females lay eggs on carrion, dung, wounds or decaying matter, and the larvae (maggots) feed on the substrate. Many species are among the first insects to colonise a corpse, so the development stages of their larvae are used in forensic entomology to estimate the post-mortem interval. Some species are parasites or cause myiasis in livestock and humans; sterile maggots of certain species are used medically to clean wounds.

Genera

Well-known genera include Calliphora (bluebottles), Lucilia (greenbottles), Chrysomya, Cochliomyia (including the New World screwworm) and Phormia. The screwworm Cochliomyia hominivorax, whose larvae feed on living flesh, has been eradicated from North and Central America using the sterile insect technique.
