New York Mets

The New York Mets are an American professional baseball team based in the New York City borough of Queens. The Mets compete in Major League Baseball (MLB) as a member of the National League East division. Founded in 1962 to replace the departed Brooklyn Dodgers and New York Giants, the Mets play their home games at Citi Field. Their colours — blue and orange — combine those of the Dodgers and Giants.

History

The early Mets under Casey Stengel were famously inept, losing 120 games in their first season. In 1969, the "Miracle Mets" of Gil Hodges, led by Tom Seaver, stunned baseball by winning the World Series over the Baltimore Orioles. The club lost the 1973 World Series, then won its second championship in 1986 in a dramatic seven-game series against the Boston Red Sox, famous for the Game 6 comeback involving Bill Buckner's error. The Mets lost the 2000 "Subway Series" to the Yankees and the 2015 World Series to the Kansas City Royals.

Ballparks

The Mets played at the Polo Grounds in 1962–63, then at Shea Stadium in Flushing from 1964 through 2008, before opening Citi Field next door in 2009. Beloved symbols include Mr. Met, one of baseball's most famous mascots.

Ownership and recent years

Long owned by the Wilpon family, the team was purchased in 2020 by hedge-fund manager Steve Cohen, whose spending made the Mets among MLB's highest-payroll teams. Notable players in club history include Tom Seaver, Keith Hernandez, Gary Carter, Darryl Strawberry, Dwight Gooden, Mike Piazza, David Wright and Jacob deGrom.
