Rwenzori Mountains

Highest peakMount Stanley (Margherita Peak)
Elevation5,109 m (16,762 ft)
LocationUganda–DR Congo border
LengthAbout 120 km

The Rwenzori Mountains (also spelled Ruwenzori) are a mountain range in east-central Africa on the border between Uganda and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Often identified with the legendary "Mountains of the Moon" described by Ptolemy as the source of the Nile, the range is the highest non-volcanic, non-orogenic mountain system in Africa — a massive fault-block of ancient rock uplifted by rifting along the western arm of the East African Rift.

The equatorial Rwenzoris support glaciers and giant Afro-alpine vegetation.

Geography and ecology

The range runs about 120 km long and 65 km wide and includes Mount Stanley (5,109 m), Mount Speke, and Mount Baker — the third, fourth, and fifth highest peaks in Africa. Despite lying on the equator, its summits bear permanent snowfields and glaciers, which have retreated dramatically since the early 20th century. Exceptionally wet, with rain on most days of the year, the mountains support luxuriant vegetation zones rising from rainforest through bamboo and giant heather to an Afro-alpine zone of giant groundsels and lobelias. The Ugandan side is protected as Rwenzori Mountains National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site (1994), home to elephants, chimpanzees, and the endemic Rwenzori turaco.

Exploration

Henry Morton Stanley confirmed the range's existence for Europeans in 1888, and the first ascent of the highest summit was made in 1906 by the Duke of the Abruzzi's expedition, which named Margherita Peak for Queen Margherita of Italy.
