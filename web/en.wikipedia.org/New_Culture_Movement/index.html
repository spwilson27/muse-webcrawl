New Culture Movement

The New Culture Movement was an intellectual and literary movement in China in the 1910s and 1920s that attacked traditional Confucian values and promoted a new culture based on Western ideals such as science and democracy.

History

Centred on Peking University and the journal New Youth (founded by Chen Duxiu in 1915), the movement's leaders included Cai Yuanpei, Hu Shih, Lu Xun and Li Dazhao. They advocated replacing classical Chinese with the vernacular in writing, criticised the traditional family system, and championed individualism, women's emancipation and the re-evaluation of China's heritage.

Legacy

The movement merged into the May Fourth Movement of 1919 after the Treaty of Versailles awarded German concessions in Shandong to Japan, and it decisively shaped modern Chinese literature, education and political thought.
