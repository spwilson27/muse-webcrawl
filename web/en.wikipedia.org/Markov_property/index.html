Markov property

The Markov property is a property of a stochastic process by which the future evolution of the process depends only on its present state, not on its past history. A process satisfying it is called a Markov process; informally, the process is "memoryless".

Definition

Formally, P(Xn+1 = x | X1, …, Xn) = P(Xn+1 = x | Xn) for all n and states. For continuous-time processes the condition applies at all times. The property is named for Russian mathematician Andrey Markov, who introduced Markov chains in 1906.

Types

Discrete-time, discrete-state processes are Markov chains; continuous-time versions include the Poisson process. Hidden Markov models, where observations depend on a hidden Markov state, underpin speech recognition and bioinformatics. Markov random fields generalize the idea to undirected graphs.

Applications

Markov models are used in queueing, finance, statistical mechanics, reinforcement learning (Markov decision processes), and Google's original PageRank algorithm.
