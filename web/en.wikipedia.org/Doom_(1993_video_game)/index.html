Doom (1993 video game)

Gameplay in the first level, E1M1: Hangar.

Doom is a 1993 first-person shooter game developed and published by id Software. The player assumes the role of a space marine, popularly known as Doomguy, fighting through hordes of undead humans and demons on Mars and in Hell. Released as shareware on December 10, 1993, Doom is regarded as one of the most significant and influential games in the medium, popularising the first-person shooter genre.

Development

Development began in late 1992 at id Software, with John Carmack building a new game engine featuring textured 3D environments, variable-height floors, and networked multiplayer, while John Romero and Tom Hall designed levels and story. Hall's extensive "Doom Bible" design document was largely discarded as the design evolved toward fast action, and Hall left the company before release.

Gameplay

Across three episodes — Knee-Deep in the Dead, The Shores of Hell, and Inferno — the player navigates maze-like levels, collecting weapons (including the shotgun, chaingun, rocket launcher, plasma gun, and BFG 9000), keycards, and power-ups. Doom pioneered cooperative and deathmatch multiplayer over local networks, and popularised "modding" through its WAD file format.

Impact

Doom was an enormous commercial and cultural phenomenon, estimated to have been played by millions within its first years; it drew criticism for its graphic violence and demonic imagery. Its engine was licensed widely, and an overwhelming number of "Doom clones" followed. The franchise continued with Doom II (1994) and later reboots in 2016 and 2020.
