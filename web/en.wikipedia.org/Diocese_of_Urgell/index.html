Diocese of Urgell

The Diocese of Urgell (Catalan: Bisbat d'Urgell) is a Roman Catholic diocese in Catalonia, Spain, with its seat at the Cathedral of Santa Maria in La Seu d'Urgell. It is one of the oldest dioceses in the Iberian Peninsula, with origins in late antiquity. Uniquely, the Bishop of Urgell is ex officio one of the two co-princes of the Principality of Andorra, alongside the President of France.

The Cathedral of Santa Maria de la Seu d'Urgell.

History

The diocese is traditionally traced to the fifth or sixth century; the first historically documented bishop, Justus of Urgell, attended councils in the sixth century. The diocese was caught up in the Adoptionist controversy under Bishop Felix of Urgell in the eighth century. In the Middle Ages the bishops were powerful feudal lords, and the 1278 paréage agreements established the co-principality over Andorra shared between the bishop and the Count of Foix, whose rights passed to the French crown and eventually the French head of state.

Territory

The diocese covers much of the Catalan Pyrenees, including the comarques of Alt Urgell, Cerdanya, and parts of surrounding areas, and includes the whole of Andorra. Its patron is Saint Ermengol, an eleventh-century bishop.
