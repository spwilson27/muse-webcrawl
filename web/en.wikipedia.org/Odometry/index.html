Odometry

Odometry is the use of data from motion sensors to estimate change in position over time. It is used in robotics and vehicle navigation to determine a moving system's pose relative to a starting position. Odometry is a form of dead reckoning.

Wheel odometry

The simplest method uses encoders on the wheels: counting wheel rotations and combining them with the wheel circumference gives distance travelled, and differences between wheels on opposite sides of a differential-drive robot give heading changes. For a car-like (Ackermann) vehicle, steering angle must also be incorporated. Wheel odometry is simple and cheap but suffers from systematic errors (wheel diameter mismatch, misalignment) and non-systematic errors such as wheel slip on loose or slippery surfaces, and errors accumulate without bound over time.

Visual odometry

Visual odometry estimates motion from changes in camera images, using feature tracking and geometry. It was famously used by NASA's Mars Exploration Rovers. Related techniques fuse camera data with inertial measurement units (visual-inertial odometry).

Applications

Odometry is combined with other sensors — GPS, lidar, inertial units — in sensor-fusion frameworks such as SLAM (simultaneous localization and mapping) for autonomous vehicles, warehouse robots, and planetary rovers, where odometry provides high-frequency relative motion estimates that absolute sensors correct.
