Saving Mr. Banks


Image placeholder: Saving Mr. Banks

Saving Mr. Banks is a 2013 biographical drama film directed by John Lee Hancock and written by Kelly Marcel and Sue Smith. The film centres on the life of author P. L. Travers (Emma Thompson) and her negotiations with Walt Disney (Tom Hanks) over the film adaptation of her novel Mary Poppins (1964), intercut with flashbacks to Travers's childhood in Australia with her alcoholic father (Colin Farrell), who inspired the character of George Banks.

Plot

In 1961, the financially struggling Travers reluctantly travels to Los Angeles, where Disney has pursued the film rights to Mary Poppins for twenty years. Travers proves exacting in the script sessions with screenwriter Don DaGradi and composers Richard and Robert Sherman, resisting animation, songs and any softening of her characters. Flashbacks reveal her upbringing in Queensland as Helen "Ginty" Goff, her devoted but self-destructive father Travers Goff, and her aunt Ellie, the model for Mary Poppins herself. Although Travers ultimately refuses to sign over the rights during the trip, Disney comes to understand the personal grief behind her protectiveness — the film is her attempt to redeem her father, the fictional Mr. Banks. She signs, attends the premiere, and is moved to tears by the film's ending in which Mr. Banks is redeemed.

Reception

The film premiered at the BFI London Film Festival in October 2013 and grossed about $118 million worldwide. It was received warmly by critics, with particular praise for Thompson's performance, and was named one of the top films of 2013 by the National Board of Review and the American Film Institute. Thompson was nominated for the Golden Globe, BAFTA and Screen Actors Guild awards for Best Actress.
