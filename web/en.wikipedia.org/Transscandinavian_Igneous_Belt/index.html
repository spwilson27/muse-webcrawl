Transscandinavian Igneous Belt

The Transscandinavian Igneous Belt (Swedish: Transskandinaviska magmatiska bältet), abbreviated TIB, is a major belt of mostly granitic and related igneous rocks in the Baltic Shield of northern Europe. The belt runs roughly 1,400 km from southern Sweden (Småland) northwestward through Värmland and Dalarna to the Lofoten Islands of northern Norway. It consists of several generations of intrusions emplaced during the Paleoproterozoic, about 1,850 to 1,650 million years ago, west of the older Svecofennian domain.

Geology

The belt is divided into generations (TIB 0, 1, 2, and 3) based on age and composition. Its granitoids, syenitoids, and subordinate mafic rocks originated in an Andean-type continental margin setting, where oceanic crust was subducted beneath the western edge of the growing Baltic craton. The rapakivi granites of southern Finland and Åland, though slightly younger, are often discussed alongside the belt. Much of the TIB was later reworked by the Sveconorwegian orogeny to the west.
