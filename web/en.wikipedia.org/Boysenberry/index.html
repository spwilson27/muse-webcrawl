Boysenberry

GenusRubus
Hybrid parentageRaspberry × blackberry × loganberry × dewberry
OriginCalifornia, United States

The boysenberry is a large, dark-maroon aggregate fruit, a hybrid within the genus Rubus believed to combine European raspberry, common blackberry, American dewberry, and loganberry ancestry. It is prized for its tart, complex flavour and is used in pies, preserves, and syrups.

History

The berry was developed in California in the 1920s by horticulturist Rudolph Boysen, who abandoned the trailing vines after an accident. Walter Knott, of Knott's Berry Farm, later recovered the neglected plants, revived the crop, and named it after Boysen; boysenberry products helped make his farm famous.

Cultivation

Boysenberries grow on low, trailing brambles and ripen in early summer. Commercial production later became centred in New Zealand, which became the world's largest producer and exporter, while cultivation in the United States declined due to the fruit's fragility and short shelf life.
