Teatro di San Carlo

The auditorium of the Teatro di San Carlo, Naples, the oldest continuously active opera house in the world.

The Teatro di San Carlo is an opera house in Naples, Italy, connected to the Royal Palace and adjacent to the Piazza del Plebiscito. It is the oldest continuously active venue for opera in the world, having been inaugurated on 4 November 1737 — decades before Milan's La Scala or Venice's La Fenice — by the Bourbon King Charles VII of Naples, after whom it is named.

History

Designed by the architect Giovanni Antonio Medrano and the carriage designer Angelo Carasale, the theatre was initially one of the largest in Europe, with a horseshoe-shaped auditorium of nearly 1,400 seats. After a fire destroyed the original building in February 1816, it was rebuilt within ten months to plans by Antonio Niccolini, in a neoclassical design that has been widely imitated. Gioachino Rossini served as the theatre's artistic and house composer from 1815 to 1822, and Gaetano Donizetti held a similar post for many years; operas premiered at San Carlo include Rossini's Mosè in Egitto and Donizetti's Lucia di Lammermoor (1835).

The building today

The auditorium, decorated in red and gold, seats about 1,400. The theatre underwent major restorations in the 19th and 20th centuries, and in 2010 it was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site as part of the historic centre of Naples. San Carlo maintains an opera and ballet season, an orchestra and chorus, and a museum of its history (MeMus).
