Government-owned, contractor-operated facility

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Government-owned, contractor-operated (GOCO) facilities are sites owned by a government but operated day-to-day by a private company or other non-government organisation under contract. The arrangement is common in defence, energy research, and other sectors where the state wants to retain ownership of strategic assets while using private-sector management.

United States

In the United States, GOCO arrangements are used extensively by the Department of Energy and the Department of Defense. Most of the DOE's national laboratories — such as Los Alamos, Lawrence Livermore, and Oak Ridge — are federal facilities managed by contractors, including universities and purpose-built limited liability companies. Historically, many US Army ammunition plants were also run as GOCOs.

United Kingdom

The UK's Atomic Weapons Establishment was converted to a GOCO in the 1990s before being brought back under direct Ministry of Defence control in 2021. The model has also been debated for defence support organisations.

Advantages and criticism

Supporters argue GOCOs combine public ownership with commercial flexibility in hiring and procurement. Critics question accountability, contract costs, and the revolving door between agencies and contractors.
