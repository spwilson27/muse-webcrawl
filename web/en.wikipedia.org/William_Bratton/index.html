William Joseph Bratton (born October 6, 1947) is an American law enforcement officer and police administrator who led several major U.S. police departments. He is one of only a few people to have headed both the New York City Police Department and the Los Angeles Police Department.

Career

Bratton began his career with the Boston Police Department in 1970. He later headed the New York City Transit Police, where he applied the "broken windows" theory of policing. As NYPD commissioner under Mayor Rudolph Giuliani from 1994 to 1996, he introduced the CompStat crime-statistics system, coinciding with a sharp decline in New York crime. From 2002 to 2009 he served as chief of the LAPD, overseeing reforms under a federal consent decree. He returned as NYPD commissioner under Mayor Bill de Blasio from 2014 to 2016.

Later work

After leaving public office, Bratton worked in private security consulting and co-authored books on policing, including Turnaround and The Profession.
