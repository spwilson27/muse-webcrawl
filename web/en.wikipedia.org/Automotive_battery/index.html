Automotive battery

An automotive battery is a rechargeable battery that supplies electric energy to a motor vehicle. In conventional cars it is an SLI battery — for starting, lighting and ignition — that cranks the starter motor and powers accessories when the engine is off. In electric and hybrid vehicles, a much larger traction battery pack propels the vehicle.

Lead-acid SLI batteries

The standard car battery is a 12-volt lead-acid battery of six series cells, each producing about 2.1 volts, with lead and lead dioxide plates in sulphuric acid electrolyte. Capacity is expressed in ampere-hours and cranking ability in cold cranking amps (CCA). The alternator recharges the battery while driving. Variants include flooded, absorbed glass mat (AGM) and enhanced flooded batteries for start-stop vehicles. Lead-acid batteries are among the most recycled consumer products, with the lead, plastic and acid largely recoverable.

Traction batteries

Electric vehicles use high-voltage lithium-ion packs of hundreds of volts, managed by a battery management system controlling charge, temperature and cell balance. Their capacity, measured in kilowatt-hours, determines vehicle range, and degradation over time is a key concern alongside the traditional 12-volt auxiliary battery retained in most EVs.
