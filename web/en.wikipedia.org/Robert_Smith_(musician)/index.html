Robert Smith (musician)

Robert James Smith (born 21 April 1959) is an English musician, singer, and songwriter, best known as the frontman, guitarist, and only constant member of the rock band The Cure, which he co-founded in 1976.

Career

With The Cure, Smith created a body of work spanning post-punk and gothic rock classics such as Seventeen Seconds, Faith, and Pornography, and the acclaimed Disintegration (1989), as well as pop hits including "Just Like Heaven", "Lovesong", and "Friday I'm in Love". He is recognised by his distinctive guitar playing, plaintive voice, and iconic image of back-combed hair and smudged red lipstick. Smith also played with Siouxsie and the Banshees and released an album as The Glove.

Recognition

The Cure were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2019, with Smith the band's principal songwriter across more than four decades.
