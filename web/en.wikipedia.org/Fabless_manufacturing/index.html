Fabless manufacturing

Fabless manufacturing is the design and sale of semiconductor chips and devices while outsourcing their fabrication to a specialized manufacturer called a semiconductor foundry. Fabless companies concentrate on research, design and marketing, avoiding the enormous capital costs of building and maintaining fabrication plants ("fabs"), which can cost billions of dollars.

History

The model emerged in the 1980s. Gordon Campbell and Dado Banatao founded Chips and Technologies, often credited as the first fabless company, and Bernie Vonderschmitt's Xilinx pioneered the approach for programmable logic. The founding of TSMC in Taiwan in 1987 as a pure-play foundry made the model widely practical by giving fabless firms access to leading-edge manufacturing.

Notable fabless companies

Major fabless firms include Qualcomm, Nvidia, Broadcom, AMD (fabless since its 2009 GlobalFoundries spin-off), MediaTek, Apple (for its in-house silicon designs), ARM (which licenses designs) and Marvell. The fabless model, together with its complement the pure-play foundry, restructured the semiconductor industry and underlies the modern division between design houses concentrated in the United States and manufacturing concentrated in East Asia.
