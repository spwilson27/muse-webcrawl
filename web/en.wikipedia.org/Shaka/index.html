Shaka

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Shaka kaSenzangakhona (c. 1787 – 22 September 1828), also known as Shaka Zulu, was the founder of the Zulu Empire in southern Africa. Rising from exile to chief of the small Zulu clan around 1816, within a decade he had built, through military and political revolution, a kingdom that dominated much of what is now KwaZulu-Natal, reshaping the region in the upheavals known as the Mfecane.

Rise to power

Shaka was the son of the Zulu chief Senzangakhona and Nandi. Exiled after an illegitimate birth, he served as a warrior under Dingiswayo of the Mthethwa, rising to command a regiment. On Dingiswayo's death and Senzangakhona's, Shaka seized the Zulu chieftainship and then absorbed the Mthethwa confederacy, defeating rivals such as Zwide of the Ndwandwe in a series of campaigns around 1818–1820.

Military reforms

Shaka is credited with revolutionising Nguni warfare: replacing long throwing spears with the short stabbing assegai (iklwa) used with a large war shield in close combat; organising warriors into age-grade regiments (amabutho) quartered in royal kraals; training troops to fight barefoot over long distances; and employing the "horns of the buffalo" encircling formation. Historians debate how many reforms originated with Shaka himself versus predecessors such as Dingiswayo.

Death and legacy

In the later 1820s Shaka's behaviour became increasingly erratic, especially after the death of his mother Nandi in 1827, when he imposed severe mourning decrees. On 22 September 1828 he was assassinated by his half-brothers Dingane and Mhlangana, with the attendant Mbopha. Dingane succeeded him. Shaka remains a towering and contested figure — celebrated as a nation-builder and military genius, and criticised for the violence of the Mfecane, whose scale and causes historians continue to dispute.
