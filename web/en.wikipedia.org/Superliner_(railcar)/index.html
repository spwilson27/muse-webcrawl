Superliner (railcar)

The Superliner is a type of bilevel intercity railroad passenger car used by Amtrak in the United States. Developed from the Santa Fe Railway's Hi-Level cars, Superliners entered service in the late 1970s and form the backbone of Amtrak's long-distance fleet in the western and central United States.

Superliner
Operator: Amtrak
Builders: Pullman-Standard; Bombardier Transportation
Entered service: 1979 (Superliner I)
Height: 16 ft 2 in (4.93 m)

Design

Superliner cars are roughly 16 feet tall with seating, roomettes, and lounges arranged over two levels joined by stairways. Types include coaches, sleeping cars, dining cars, Sightseer lounge cars, and transition dormitory cars that step down to single-level equipment. Their height restricts them largely to routes with generous clearances, so they are not used in the Northeast Corridor tunnels.

History

Pullman-Standard built the Superliner I fleet in the late 1970s; Bombardier delivered the Superliner II cars in the 1990s. The cars serve trains such as the California Zephyr, Empire Builder, Coast Starlight, and Southwest Chief.
