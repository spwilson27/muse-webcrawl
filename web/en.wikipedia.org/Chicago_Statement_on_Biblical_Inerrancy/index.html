Chicago Statement on Biblical Inerrancy

The Chicago Statement on Biblical Inerrancy is a statement of faith written in 1978 by more than 200 evangelical leaders at a conference convened by the International Council on Biblical Inerrancy in Chicago, Illinois. The statement was designed to defend the position of biblical inerrancy against liberal conceptions of Scripture.

Content

The statement consists of a summary, a set of affirmations and denials in nineteen articles, and an exposition. It affirms that the Bible is the authoritative written Word of God, wholly inspired and without error in the original manuscripts, and denies that inerrancy is negated by biblical phenomena such as imprecise reporting, lack of modern technical precision, or the use of hyperbole and round numbers.

Signatories and influence

Signatories included J. I. Packer, Francis Schaeffer, R. C. Sproul, John MacArthur, and Carl F. H. Henry. The statement became an influential reference point for evangelical seminaries, denominations, and publishers, and was followed by statements on biblical hermeneutics (1982) and biblical application (1986).
