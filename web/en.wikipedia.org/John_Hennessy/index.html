John Leroy Hennessy (born September 22, 1952) is an American computer scientist, academician and businessman. He was the tenth president of Stanford University (2000–2016), a founder of MIPS Technologies, and co-recipient of the 2017 Turing Award for pioneering the RISC approach to computer architecture.

Career

Hennessy earned his doctorate in computer science from Stony Brook University and joined Stanford's faculty in 1977. In 1981 he led the MIPS project, one of the first RISC microprocessor designs, and co-founded MIPS Computer Systems (later MIPS Technologies) in 1984 to commercialise it.

Stanford presidency

After serving as dean of engineering and provost, Hennessy became Stanford's president in 2000. His tenure saw major growth in interdisciplinary research, financial aid and the university's endowment. He stepped down in 2016 and later became chairman of Alphabet Inc.

Computer Architecture

With David Patterson he co-authored the standard textbook Computer Architecture: A Quantitative Approach. The two shared the 2017 ACM Turing Award for developing a systematic, quantitative approach to designing faster, lower-power microprocessors.
