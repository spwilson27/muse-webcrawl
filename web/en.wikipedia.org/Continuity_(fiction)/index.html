Continuity (fiction)

Continuity (fiction)
MediumFilm, television, comics, literature
MeaningConsistency of characters, events and details across a fictional work or series
Related termsCanon; retcon; reboot; shared universe

In fiction, continuity is the consistency of the characteristics of people, places, events and details within a story and across related works. A work "follows continuity" when nothing in it contradicts what has previously been established; an error or contradiction is a continuity error.

Within a single work

In film and television production, continuity also refers to the matching of details between shots — props, costumes, positions, lighting — overseen on set by the script supervisor (historically the "continuity person"). Famous slips, such as objects moving between takes, are staples of film trivia.

Across series and franchises

Long-running serial fiction — comic books, soap operas, science-fiction franchises such as Star Trek and Doctor Who, and cinematic universes — accumulates vast continuities that fans and creators track closely. Material officially recognised as part of the continuity is called canon. When later creators change established facts, they may use a retcon (retroactive continuity) to explain the discrepancy, split timelines, or "reboot" the series and start a new continuity altogether, as superhero publishers have repeatedly done.

Fan engagement

Continuity is a focus of fan culture: wikis cataloguing fictional universes, debates over canon, and fan works all revolve around it. Studios increasingly employ continuity editors and franchise "story groups" to keep sprawling shared universes coherent.
