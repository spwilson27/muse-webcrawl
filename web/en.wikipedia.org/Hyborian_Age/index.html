Hyborian Age

The world of the Hyborian Age as depicted in the Conan stories

The Hyborian Age is a fictional period of Earth's history within the artificial mythology created by Robert E. Howard, serving as the setting for his tales of Conan the Barbarian. Howard placed the age after the sinking of Atlantis and before the beginning of recorded ancient history, roughly 12,000 to 15,000 years ago (in his essay "The Hyborian Age", written in the 1930s, he sketched a pseudo-history spanning thousands of years). Rather than inventing a wholly fantastical world, Howard deliberately drew on real-world cultures and history, renaming and rearranging them to create a setting that readers could grasp intuitively.

Setting

The principal continent of the Hyborian Age corresponds loosely to Europe, Asia, and Africa. Its kingdoms — Aquilonia, Nemedia, Brythunia, Hyperborea, Zamora, Zingara, Argos, Corinthia, and Koth among them — are analogues of medieval and ancient peoples. The Hyborians themselves, a people descended (in Howard's scheme) from tribes that migrated after the Cataclysm that destroyed Atlantis and Lemuria, founded the dominant kingdoms of the West.

To the south lie the black kingdoms and Stygia, a sinister analogue of ancient Egypt and seat of the cult of the serpent god Set. The east holds Hyrkania, Turan, Khitai, and Vendhya, while the far west, across the ocean, contains the Pictish Wilderness, inhabited by the savage Picts. Conan himself comes from Cimmeria, a grim northern land roughly corresponding to the homeland attributed to the historical Cimmerians.

"The Hyborian Age" essay

Howard's essay "The Hyborian Age", written in 1932 and published in 1938 after his death, lays out the rise and fall of the age's peoples: the pre-Cataclysm world of Atlantis, Valusia, and Commoria; the Cataclysm itself; the rise of the Hyborian kingdoms; and their eventual overthrow by barbarian migrations and a new ice age, leading into the world of recorded history.

Legacy

The Hyborian Age remains one of the most influential settings in the sword and sorcery genre, developed further by later authors such as L. Sprague de Camp and Lin Carter and adapted into comics, films, games, and the MMORPG Age of Conan (2008).
