Human factors and ergonomics

Human factors and ergonomics (commonly referred to as human factors engineering or HFE) is the application of psychological and physiological principles to the engineering and design of products, processes, and systems. Primary goals of human factors engineering are to reduce human error, enhance productivity and system availability, increase safety, and increase comfort with a specific focus on the interaction between the human and equipment.

Overview
Also known asErgonomics (UK), human factors (US)
SubdisciplinesPhysical, cognitive, and organisational ergonomics
ApplicationsAviation, healthcare, workplace design, human–computer interaction

Domains

Physical ergonomics concerns human anatomy, anthropometry and biomechanics as they relate to physical activity — working postures, materials handling, repetitive strain and workstation layout. Cognitive ergonomics concerns mental processes such as perception, memory, reasoning and motor response as they affect interactions — mental workload, decision-making, human reliability and human–computer interaction. Organisational ergonomics concerns the optimisation of sociotechnical systems, including communication, teamwork and shift design.

History

The field crystallised during the Second World War, when psychologists and engineers studied why well-trained pilots crashed functioning aircraft, concluding that cockpit designs should fit human capabilities rather than the reverse. Professional societies such as the Ergonomics Society (1949) and the Human Factors and Ergonomics Society (1957) followed. Today human factors methods are central to aviation safety, medical device and patient safety, and software usability.

Methods

Practitioners use task analysis, anthropometric data, usability testing, cognitive walkthroughs, workload and situation-awareness measurement, and accident investigation techniques to design systems that are effective, efficient and safe.
