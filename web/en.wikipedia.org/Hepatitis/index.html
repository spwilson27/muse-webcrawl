Hepatitis

Hepatitis is inflammation of the liver tissue. Some people or animals with hepatitis have no symptoms, whereas others develop yellow discoloration of the skin and whites of the eyes (jaundice), poor appetite, vomiting, tiredness, abdominal pain, and diarrhea. Hepatitis is acute if it resolves within six months, and chronic if it lasts longer than six months. Acute hepatitis can resolve on its own, progress to chronic hepatitis, or (rarely) result in acute liver failure. Chronic hepatitis may progress to scarring of the liver (cirrhosis), liver failure, and liver cancer.

Causes

Hepatitis is most commonly caused by the viruses hepatitis A, B, C, D, and E. Other viruses can also cause liver inflammation, including cytomegalovirus, Epstein–Barr virus, and yellow fever virus. Heavy alcohol use, toxins, some medications, and autoimmune diseases can also cause hepatitis.

Viral hepatitis

Hepatitis A and E are spread mainly through contaminated food and water (the fecal–oral route) and usually cause acute, self-limited illness. Hepatitis B and C spread through blood and body fluids; both frequently become chronic and are major causes of cirrhosis and hepatocellular carcinoma. Hepatitis D occurs only in people already infected with hepatitis B.

Prevention and treatment

Effective vaccines exist for hepatitis A and B. Chronic hepatitis B can be managed with antiviral drugs such as tenofovir and entecavir, while direct-acting antivirals cure most cases of hepatitis C. World Hepatitis Day is observed on 28 July.
