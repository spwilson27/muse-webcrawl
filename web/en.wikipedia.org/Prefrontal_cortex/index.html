Prefrontal cortex

Prefrontal cortex
Part ofFrontal lobe, cerebral cortex
ComponentsDorsolateral, ventrolateral, orbitofrontal, medial prefrontal cortex
FunctionExecutive functions, planning, decision-making

The prefrontal cortex (PFC) is the cerebral cortex covering the front part of the frontal lobe. This brain region is implicated in planning complex cognitive behaviour, personality expression, decision making, and moderating social behaviour. The basic activity of this brain region is considered to be the orchestration of thoughts and actions in accordance with internal goals, known collectively as executive functions.

Structure

The prefrontal cortex is usually divided into the dorsolateral, ventrolateral (inferior frontal), orbitofrontal, and medial prefrontal regions. It is defined in part by its projection from the mediodorsal nucleus of the thalamus and is especially large and well developed in humans, making up roughly a third of the cerebral cortex.

Function and clinical significance

The PFC supports working memory, cognitive flexibility, inhibitory control, and the integration of information over time. Damage to the region, as in the famous 1848 case of Phineas Gage, can cause marked personality change and impaired social judgement. Dysfunction of prefrontal circuits is implicated in schizophrenia, ADHD, depression, and addiction. The prefrontal cortex is among the last brain regions to mature, with development continuing into the mid-twenties.
