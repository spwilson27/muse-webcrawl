Sunflowers (Van Gogh series)

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Sunflowers (original title, in French: Tournesols) is the title of two series of still life paintings by the Dutch painter Vincent van Gogh. The first series, executed in Paris in 1887, depicts the flowers lying on the ground, while the second set, executed a year later in Arles, shows a bouquet of sunflowers in a vase.

The Arles series

In August 1888, living at the Yellow House in Arles, van Gogh began painting sunflowers to decorate the house in anticipation of Paul Gauguin's visit. He wrote to his brother Theo that the sunflower was a symbol of gratitude. The Arles versions — vases with three, five, twelve, and fifteen sunflowers — use a restricted palette dominated by chrome yellow, and are among the most famous flower still lifes in Western art.

Versions and locations

Principal versions are held by the National Gallery in London, the Van Gogh Museum in Amsterdam, the Philadelphia Museum of Art, the Sompo Museum of Art in Tokyo, and the Neue Pinakothek in Munich (whose version, on loan, is displayed there); another version is in a private collection. One version, sold at Christie's in 1987 for a then-record price for a painting, was later the subject of authenticity debate. A version once in Japan was destroyed by fire during the Second World War.

Legacy

The Sunflowers have become a popular emblem of van Gogh's art, reproduced worldwide, and the sunflower itself a symbol associated with the artist. The paintings are also studied technically: conservators have documented the fading of his chrome yellow pigments and his use of unusual pigment mixtures.
