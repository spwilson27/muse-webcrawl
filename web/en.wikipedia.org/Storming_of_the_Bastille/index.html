Storming of the Bastille

The Storming of the Bastille (French: Prise de la Bastille) occurred in Paris on 14 July 1789 during the French Revolution. The Bastille, a medieval fortress and royal prison, was seen as a symbol of the abuses of the monarchy; its fall became the iconic event of the Revolution and is commemorated annually as France's national day, Bastille Day.

Background

Amid economic crisis, the dismissal of the reforming minister Jacques Necker, and fears that royal troops would dissolve the National Assembly, Parisian crowds armed themselves, seizing muskets from the Hôtel des Invalides. Needing gunpowder, they marched on the Bastille, which was defended by governor Bernard-René de Launay with a small garrison of invalides and Swiss guards.

The assault

After hours of fighting in which about 98 attackers were killed, de Launay surrendered when mutinous French Guards arrived with cannon. De Launay and several others were killed by the crowd, their heads paraded on pikes. The fortress held only seven prisoners, but its capture electrified France: the king recalled his troops and reinstated Necker, and the event marked the irreversible transfer of power to the revolutionaries. The Bastille was demolished within months.
