Mezuzah

A mezuzah (Hebrew: מְזוּזָה, "doorpost"; plural mezuzot) is a piece of parchment (klaf) inscribed with specific Hebrew verses from the Torah, enclosed in a decorative case and affixed to the doorposts of Jewish houses and buildings. It fulfils the biblical commandment: "And you shall write them upon the doorposts of your house and upon your gates" (Deuteronomy 6:9).

The parchment

The verses are the first two sections of the Shema (Deuteronomy 6:4–9 and 11:13–21), hand-written in 22 lines by a trained scribe (sofer) with a quill on kosher parchment, in the traditional Ashkenazi, Sephardi or other scripts. On the reverse is written the divine name Shaddai (Almighty), often understood as an acronym for "Guardian of the doors of Israel". A defective letter can invalidate the scroll, so mezuzot are traditionally checked by a scribe twice every seven years, and the parchment — not the case — is the essential ritual object.

Affixing and customs

The mezuzah is attached to the right-hand side of the doorframe (as one enters), in the upper third of the doorpost. Ashkenazi custom tilts it inward at an angle, Sephardi custom places it vertically. A blessing is recited when affixing it. Mezuzot are placed on most rooms of a home (bathrooms and small closets are usually exempt), and many Jews touch the mezuzah and kiss their fingers when passing, as a reminder of God's presence and commandments.

Symbolism and history

Beyond its legal function, the mezuzah symbolises Jewish identity, divine protection of the household, and the sanctification of everyday life. Archaeological and historical examples range from plain clay and metal tubes to elaborate silver and ceramic cases. Karaite Jews traditionally interpret the commandment figuratively and do not affix mezuzot, and in some times and places public mezuzot have been removed under persecution — making their quiet persistence a marker of continuity of Jewish life.
