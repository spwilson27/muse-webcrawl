Table Mountain

Table Mountain (Khoekhoe: Huriǂoaxa; Afrikaans: Tafelberg) is a flat-topped mountain forming a prominent landmark overlooking the city of Cape Town in South Africa. It is a significant tourist attraction, reached by the Table Mountain Aerial Cableway or by numerous hiking routes, and is the flagship feature of Table Mountain National Park. In 2011 it was named one of the New7Wonders of Nature.

Geology

The flat summit plateau, about 3 km from side to side, is composed of hard Ordovician quartzitic sandstone of the Table Mountain Group, which resisted erosion while the softer Malmesbury shale and Cape granite beneath weathered away. The plateau is flanked by Devil's Peak to the east and Lion's Head to the west.

Flora and fauna

The mountain lies within the Cape Floristic Region and supports roughly 1,500 plant species — more, it is often noted, than occur in the whole of the United Kingdom — most of them endemic fynbos. Wildlife includes dassies (rock hyraxes), caracals, and the endemic Table Mountain ghost frog. A cloud cap, the "tablecloth", forms when the moist south-easter wind is forced up the slopes.

History and access

The Khoekhoe called the mountain Huriǂoaxa, "sea-emerging". António de Saldanha made the first recorded European ascent in 1503. The cableway opened in 1929 and its rotating cars were introduced in 1997. The highest point, Maclear's Beacon, commemorates a 19th-century survey beacon.
