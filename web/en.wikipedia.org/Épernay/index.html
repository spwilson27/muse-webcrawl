Épernay

The Avenue de Champagne, lined with the headquarters of famous champagne houses.

Épernay (French pronunciation: [epɛʁnɛ]) is a commune in the Marne department in north-eastern France. It is the principal centre of the Champagne wine trade, situated on the River Marne south of Reims, and styles itself the capitale du Champagne.

Champagne

Épernay's Avenue de Champagne is lined with the mansions and cellars of celebrated houses including Moët & Chandon — whose founder Claude Moët established his business here in 1743 — Perrier-Jouët and Mercier. Beneath the town lie some 110 kilometres of chalk cellars holding hundreds of millions of bottles of ageing champagne. The surrounding vineyards of the Côte des Blancs and the Vallée de la Marne supply Chardonnay, Pinot Noir and Pinot Meunier grapes.

History

The town, known in Latin as Sparnacum, was repeatedly damaged in wars, including the Hundred Years' War and both World Wars; it was occupied and fought over during the Battles of the Marne. In 2015 the champagne hillsides, houses and cellars around Épernay were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List.
