The Cuba libre (Spanish: [ˈkuβa ˈliβɾe], "Free Cuba") is a highball cocktail consisting of cola, rum and lime juice, served over ice in a tall glass. It is one of the world's most popular cocktails and appears on the International Bartenders Association's list of Contemporary Classics.

History

The drink is traditionally dated to Havana around 1900, shortly after the Spanish–American War, when American Coca-Cola first reached Cuba. According to the oft-told account popularised by the Bacardi rum company, a group of U.S. soldiers mixed Coca-Cola and rum with a squeeze of lime and toasted "¡Por Cuba Libre!" — to a free Cuba — then a rallying cry of Cuban independence. Because the precise ingredients and even the name were in use in various forms, the story is partly legend, but the name unquestionably commemorates Cuban independence from Spain.

Preparation

The IBA recipe combines 50 ml of white rum and 120 ml of cola with 10 ml of fresh lime juice, built over ice in a highball glass and garnished with a lime wedge. Variations substitute dark or aged rum, and in parts of the Spanish-speaking world the drink is casually ordered as a "cubata".
