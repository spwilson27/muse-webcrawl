Robert Anthony Eden, 1st Earl of Avon (12 June 1897 – 14 January 1977), was a British politician who served as Prime Minister of the United Kingdom from 1955 to 1957. A long-serving Foreign Secretary — three times, including during the Second World War — Eden was Winston Churchill's designated successor for many years.

Early career

Educated at Eton and Christ Church, Oxford, Eden served with distinction in the First World War, becoming the youngest brigade-major in the British Army. He entered Parliament in 1923 as MP for Warwick and Leamington and rose rapidly, becoming Foreign Secretary in 1935. He resigned in February 1938 in protest at Neville Chamberlain's policy of appeasement toward Mussolini's Italy.

Foreign Secretary and premiership

Eden returned as Foreign Secretary under Churchill from 1940 to 1945 and again from 1951 to 1955, negotiating the 1954 Geneva settlement on Indochina and the agreement on the Suez Canal base. He succeeded Churchill as Prime Minister in April 1955 and won the general election of that year.

Suez Crisis and resignation

Eden's premiership was destroyed by the Suez Crisis of 1956. After Egypt's President Nasser nationalised the Suez Canal, Eden secretly colluded with France and Israel in a plan (the Protocol of Sèvres) for Israel to invade Egypt, giving Britain and France a pretext to intervene. American and Soviet pressure forced a humiliating withdrawal, and Eden's denial of collusion damaged his credibility. Suffering from ill health, he resigned in January 1957 and was succeeded by Harold Macmillan. He was created Earl of Avon in 1961. Historians frequently rank Eden among the least successful British prime ministers of the 20th century because of Suez, though his earlier diplomatic career is more favourably regarded.
