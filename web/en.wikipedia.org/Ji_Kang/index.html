Ji Kang

Ji Kang (Chinese: 嵇康; 223–262 CE), courtesy name Shuye, was a Chinese philosopher, poet and musician of the late Han and Three Kingdoms period. He was one of the Seven Sages of the Bamboo Grove, a group of scholars and artists who withdrew from court politics to pursue wine, music and philosophical conversation. A leading voice of the Neo-Daoist xuanxue ("dark learning") movement, he was executed by the ruling Sima clan.

Life

Ji Kang was born in present-day Anhui and married a princess of the Cao imperial house, holding a nominal court post. Tall and strikingly handsome, he preferred the company of the blacksmith's forge — he enjoyed working iron — and of friends such as Ruan Ji and Shan Tao. With them he formed the circle remembered as the Seven Sages of the Bamboo Grove, whose gatherings celebrated spontaneity and disdain for Confucian ritual propriety.

Thought and works

Ji Kang wrote essays on longevity (Yangsheng lun), arguing that the lifespan could be extended by nourishing life through diet, breath and equanimity; on music, contending in the "Essay on the Absence of Emotions in Sound" (Sheng wu aile lun) that music in itself carries no joy or sorrow; and on the naturalness of human desire. He was a master of the seven-stringed qin.

Death and legend

His contempt for the powerful — famously expressed in a contemptuous letter breaking off relations with Shan Tao after the latter recommended him for office — and his defence of a friend accused of unfilial conduct brought him into conflict with Zhong Hui and the Sima regime. He was executed in Luoyang in 262; tradition holds that three thousand students petitioned in vain for his life, and that on the scaffold he calmly played the qin melody "Guangling San", lamenting that with his death it would be lost. The piece survives today in later notation.
