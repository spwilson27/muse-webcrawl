Wawel Castle

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Wawel Royal Castle (Polish: Zamek Królewski na Wawelu) is a castle residency located in central Kraków, Poland, on Wawel Hill above the Vistula River. Built at the behest of King Casimir III the Great, it consists of a number of structures from different periods surrounding the Italian-styled main courtyard. The castle, being one of the largest in Poland, represents nearly all European architectural styles of the medieval, Renaissance and Baroque periods.

History

Wawel Hill was fortified from early medieval times and became the seat of Polish kings. The first stone structures date to the 11th century. After a fire in 1499, King Sigismund I the Old rebuilt the residence in Renaissance style, employing Italian architects including Francesco Fiorentino and Bartolomeo Berrecci. The castle was the political and administrative centre of the Kingdom of Poland and later the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth until the capital moved to Warsaw in the late 16th and early 17th centuries.

During the partitions of Poland the castle was used by the Austrian army as barracks and a military hospital. In the interwar period it was restored as a state residence and museum. During the Second World War it served as the seat of the Nazi Governor General Hans Frank. The adjacent Wawel Cathedral is the burial place of Polish monarchs and national heroes.

Collections

The Wawel Royal Castle National Art Collection includes the Crown Treasury and Armoury, state rooms, royal private apartments and one of the world's finest collections of Ottoman tents. Its most famous treasure is Szczerbiec, the coronation sword of Polish kings. The castle also holds a notable collection of tapestries commissioned by Sigismund II Augustus. At the foot of the hill, the Wawel Dragon statue commemorates the legendary dragon of Kraków folklore.
