Wilhelm I (Wilhelm Friedrich Ludwig; 22 March 1797 – 9 March 1888) was King of Prussia from 1861 and German Emperor from 1871 until his death in 1888. A member of the House of Hohenzollern, he was the first head of state of a united Germany. During his reign Prussia, guided by Chancellor Otto von Bismarck, fought successful wars against Denmark (1864), Austria (1866) and France (1870–71), culminating in the proclamation of the German Empire in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles on 18 January 1871.

Early life

Wilhelm was the second son of King Frederick William III and Queen Louise. He served as an officer in the Wars of Liberation against Napoleon, earning the Iron Cross at Bar-sur-Aube and entering Paris in 1814 and again in 1815. In the revolutions of 1848 he directed the suppression of the Berlin uprising, an unpopular role that earned him the nickname "Kartätschenprinz" ("Prince of Grapeshot"), and he briefly withdrew to England. He became regent for his ill brother Frederick William IV in 1858 and king on the latter's death in 1861.

Reign and unification

Wilhelm's appointment of Bismarck as minister president in 1862, amid a constitutional conflict over army reform, proved decisive. Though the two frequently clashed — Wilhelm was a cautious conservative soldier, reluctant about the imperial title and some of Bismarck's policies — he sustained the chancellor through the wars of unification and the founding of the empire. Two assassination attempts in 1878, by Karl Nobiling and Max Hödel, were used to justify Bismarck's Anti-Socialist Laws.

Death and succession

Wilhelm died in Berlin on 9 March 1888, aged 90, beginning the "Year of the Three Emperors": his son Frederick III reigned for only 99 days before dying of cancer, and was succeeded by Wilhelm's grandson, Wilhelm II.
