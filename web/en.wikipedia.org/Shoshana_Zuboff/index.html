Shoshana Zuboff (born 18 November 1951) is an American author, professor emerita at Harvard Business School, and social psychologist. She is best known for her research on the effects of information technology on work and society, and for her 2019 book The Age of Surveillance Capitalism, which coined and popularized the term surveillance capitalism.

Career

Zuboff earned her bachelor's degree in philosophy from the University of Chicago and her PhD in social psychology from Harvard University. She joined the Harvard Business School faculty, where she became the Charles Edward Wilson Professor of Business Administration, one of the first tenured women on the faculty.

Her first book, In the Age of the Smart Machine: The Future of Work and Power (1988), examined how computerization changes work, introducing the concept of "informating" — the capacity of digital technology to generate information about the processes it automates. The Support Economy (2002), co-authored with James Maxmin, analyzed the mismatch between consumers and contemporary capitalism.

Surveillance capitalism

In The Age of Surveillance Capitalism: The Fight for a Human Future at the New Frontier of Power (2019), Zuboff argued that large technology companies built a new economic logic: human experience is claimed as free raw material, translated into behavioral data, and used to produce prediction products sold in behavioral futures markets. She contends that the resulting concentration of knowledge and power threatens autonomy and democracy, and calls for legal and democratic countermeasures.
