Anders' Army

Anders' Army was the informal name of the Polish Armed Forces in the East in 1941–42, created in the Soviet Union after the Sikorski–Mayski agreement of July 1941 and commanded by General Władysław Anders. It was formed from Polish prisoners of war and deportees released from Soviet camps and prisons following Nazi Germany's invasion of the USSR.

Formation

Following the amnesty for Polish citizens in the Soviet Union, tens of thousands of Poles made their way to recruitment centres at Buzuluk, Tatishchevo, and Totskoye. Conditions were grim: the men arrived starved and in rags, and Soviet authorities, citing supply shortages, restricted the size of the army and its food allocations. Relations were further strained by the disappearance of thousands of Polish officers — later revealed to have been murdered in the Katyń massacre.

Evacuation to the Middle East

In 1942, by agreement between the Polish government-in-exile, the British, and the Soviets, Anders' Army was evacuated across the Caspian Sea to Iran. Roughly 115,000 people — soldiers and civilian dependents, including many children — left the USSR in two evacuations. In the Middle East the force was reorganized as the Polish II Corps under British command.

Italian campaign

As II Corps, the former Anders' Army fought with distinction in Italy, most famously capturing Monte Cassino in May 1944, and later fighting at Ancona and in the Gothic Line battles and the final spring offensive of 1945. After the war, most of its soldiers, unwilling to return to communist-ruled Poland, settled in exile, many in the United Kingdom under the Polish Resettlement Act 1947.
