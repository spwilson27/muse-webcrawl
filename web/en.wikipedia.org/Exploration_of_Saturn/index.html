Exploration of Saturn

The exploration of Saturn has been performed solely by crewless probes. Three missions performed flybys, while a fourth, Cassini–Huygens, entered orbit in 2004 and orbited Saturn for 13 years before its mission ended in 2017. Saturn is known mostly through telescopic observation until the space age.

Flyby missions

Pioneer 11 was the first spacecraft to reach Saturn, flying by in September 1979 and discovering the F ring and a new moon. Voyager 1 (November 1980) and Voyager 2 (August 1981) imaged the rings in detail, discovered several moons, and revealed the thick nitrogen atmosphere of Titan, which motivated an orbiter mission.

Cassini–Huygens

The Cassini–Huygens mission, a joint NASA/ESA/ASI project launched in 1997, arrived on 1 July 2004. The ESA Huygens probe landed on Titan in January 2005, the most distant landing ever made. Cassini discovered geysers erupting from Enceladus — implying a subsurface ocean — new moons and ring structures, and observed seasons change over seven Earth years. The mission ended with a "Grand Finale" descent into Saturn's atmosphere on 15 September 2017.

Proposed missions

Proposed future concepts include the NASA Dragonfly rotorcraft to Titan (planned to launch in the late 2020s) and various Enceladus orbiter/lander concepts that have not yet been selected.
