The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction

AuthorWalter Benjamin
Original titleDas Kunstwerk im Zeitalter seiner technischen Reproduzierbarkeit
LanguageGerman
Published1935 (first version); 1936 (French translation)
SubjectAesthetics, art theory, media theory

The Work of Art in the Age of Mechanical Reproduction (1935) is an essay by the German philosopher and cultural critic Walter Benjamin that examines how technologies of reproduction — photography and film above all — transform the nature of art and its social and political functions. It is one of the most influential works of twentieth-century aesthetics and media theory.

The aura

Benjamin argues that an original work of art possesses an "aura": its unique presence in time and space, its authenticity and embeddedness in tradition and ritual. Mechanical reproduction, he contends, detaches the reproduced object from the domain of tradition, substitutes a plurality of copies for a unique existence, and thereby causes the aura to wither. As reproduction emancipates art from its basis in ritual, the work's "cult value" gives way to its "exhibition value."

Politics of art

Benjamin contrasts Fascism, which responds to mass society by aestheticising politics, with Communism, which responds by politicising art. Film, in his account, has a potentially emancipatory and progressive character: it trains mass audiences in new modes of perception and critical attention, even as its products are absorbed by the capitalist culture industry.

Legacy

Written during Benjamin's exile from Nazi Germany, the essay was first published in a French translation in the Zeitschrift für Sozialforschung in 1936. It has since become a canonical text in art history, film studies, and media studies, and its concept of the aura remains widely debated.
