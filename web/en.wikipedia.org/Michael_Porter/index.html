Michael Porter

Born: 23 May 1947, Ann Arbor, Michigan, USA
Institution: Harvard Business School
Known for: Five forces analysis; generic strategies; value chain

Michael Eugene Porter (born 1947) is an American economist and professor at Harvard Business School, widely regarded as the founder of modern business strategy. His frameworks are standard in management education and consulting.

Frameworks

In Competitive Strategy (1980), Porter introduced the five forces that shape industry competition — rivalry among existing competitors, threat of new entrants, threat of substitutes, and the bargaining power of buyers and suppliers — and the three generic strategies: cost leadership, differentiation, and focus. Competitive Advantage (1985) added the value chain, analyzing a firm's activities as sources of advantage. The Competitive Advantage of Nations (1990) extended the analysis to national and regional clusters with the "diamond model."

Later work

Porter co-founded the Monitor Group consultancy (1983). Later work includes applying strategy to healthcare (Redefining Health Care, 2006, with Elizabeth Teisberg) and, with Mark Kramer, the concept of "creating shared value" (2011). He also developed the Social Progress Index with the Social Progress Imperative. Porter earned his BSE from Princeton and MBA and PhD from Harvard, where he joined the faculty at 26.
