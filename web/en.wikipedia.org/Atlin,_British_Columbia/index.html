Atlin, British Columbia

Atlin
Province: British Columbia
Region: Stikine Region, northwest BC
Population: approx. 300–500
Known for: Atlin Gold Rush (1898), Atlin Lake

Atlin is a community in northwestern British Columbia, Canada, located on the eastern shore of Atlin Lake. It is sometimes called the "Switzerland of the North" for its mountain scenery. The name derives from a Tlingit word meaning "big water," referring to the lake.

History

Atlin was founded during a gold rush in 1898, part of the wider Klondike-era rushes. Miners Fritz Miller and Kenny McLaren are credited with the discovery of gold on Pine Creek in July 1898, and within months thousands of prospectors arrived. The town grew rapidly with hotels, stores, and a newspaper, the Atlin Claim. Placer mining continued for decades, and the area remains known for gold production.

Geography

Atlin Lake is the largest natural lake in British Columbia and is regarded as the source of the Yukon River. The community is accessible by road only through the Yukon, via the Atlin Road from Jake's Corner on the Alaska Highway. The surrounding area is the traditional territory of the Taku River Tlingit First Nation, whose main community is Atlin.
