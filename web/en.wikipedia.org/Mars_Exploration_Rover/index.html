Mars Exploration Rover

Mars Exploration Rover (MER) is a NASA robotic space mission involving two Mars rovers, Spirit (MER-A) and Opportunity (MER-B), that explored the planet Mars. It began in 2003 with the launch of the two rovers to explore the Martian surface and geology; both landed successfully in January 2004 on opposite sides of the planet. Each rover was designed for a 90-sol (Martian day) primary mission but operated for years: Spirit until 2010 and Opportunity until 2018.

Mission objectives

The scientific objective was to search for and characterize rocks and soils holding clues to past water activity on Mars. The rovers carried panoramic cameras, spectrometers (Mössbauer, APXS and Mini-TES), magnets, a microscopic imager and a rock abrasion tool on a robotic arm. They were solar-powered, weighed about 185 kg, and travelled on a rocker-bogie suspension at a top speed of a few centimetres per second.

Spirit

Spirit landed in Gusev crater on January 4, 2004, investigated the Columbia Hills, and found goethite and other evidence of past water. In 2009 it became trapped in soft soil at a site called Troy; after months of failed extraction attempts, NASA ended communication attempts in 2011, by which time the rover had driven about 7.7 km.

Opportunity

Opportunity landed in Meridiani Planum on January 25, 2004, and immediately found hematite "blueberries" and jarosite, strong evidence that liquid water once soaked the region. It explored Eagle, Endurance, Erebus, Victoria and Endeavour craters, surviving repeated dust storms and setting the off-world driving record of over 45 km. A planet-encircling dust storm in June 2018 cut off solar power; after more than a thousand recovery commands, NASA declared the mission complete on February 13, 2019.

Legacy

The mission far exceeded its planned lifetime and transformed understanding of Mars as a once warmer, wetter world, paving the way for the Curiosity and Perseverance rovers.
