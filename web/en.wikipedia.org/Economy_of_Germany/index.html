The economy of Germany is a highly developed social market economy. It has the largest national economy in Europe and is the world's third-largest economy by nominal GDP. Germany is a founding member of the European Union and the Eurozone.

Overview

Germany is one of the world's leading exporters, known for high-quality manufactured goods, especially automobiles (Volkswagen, BMW, Mercedes-Benz), machinery, chemical products, and electronics. Its economic model relies on a large base of small and medium-sized enterprises, the Mittelstand, many of them world leaders in niche markets ("hidden champions").

History

After the Second World War, West Germany's Wirtschaftswunder ("economic miracle") under Ludwig Erhard transformed the country. Reunification in 1990 imposed heavy costs as the East German economy was integrated. Agenda 2010 reforms under Chancellor Gerhard Schröder liberalized the labour market in the early 2000s. Germany weathered the eurozone crisis as Europe's largest economy, though it faced an energy shock after Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine exposed dependence on Russian gas.

Sectors

Services account for roughly 70% of GDP, industry about 27%, and agriculture under 1%. Germany is also a leading producer of renewable-energy technology and hosts major financial and trade-fair centres, notably Frankfurt.
