The Royal Marechaussee (Dutch: Koninklijke Marechaussee, abbreviated to KMar) is the national gendarmerie force of the Netherlands, performing military police and civil police duties. It is one of the four services of the Dutch Armed Forces, alongside the Royal Netherlands Army, Navy and Air Force.

History

The corps was created by King William I on 26 October 1814, modelled on the French gendarmerie. After the Second World War it was re-established and took on an expanding range of tasks, including border security and the protection of the royal household.

Tasks

The Marechaussee is responsible for security at Schiphol and other Dutch airports, border control at the external borders of the Schengen Area in the Netherlands, guarding royal palaces, military policing for the armed forces, and assisting the civilian police in maintaining public order. It also provides close protection and escorts for dignitaries.

Organisation

The force is commanded by the Commander of the Royal Marechaussee, reporting to the Minister of Defence for military tasks. Its special intervention unit, the Brigade Speciale Beveiligingsopdrachten (BSB), carries out high-risk protection and observation tasks.
