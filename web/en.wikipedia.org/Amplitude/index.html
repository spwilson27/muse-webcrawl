Amplitude

The amplitude of a periodic variable is a measure of its change in a single period (such as time or spatial period). For a periodic function, amplitude is commonly defined as the maximum absolute value of the function from its mean position — the peak deviation. For a sine wave or other simple waveform, the amplitude is the maximum positive (or negative) value reached during each cycle.

Definitions

Peak amplitude is the maximum value of a waveform measured from the mean. Peak-to-peak amplitude is the difference between the maximum and minimum values, twice the peak amplitude for a symmetric waveform. Root mean square (RMS) amplitude is used in contexts such as electrical power, where the RMS value of a sine wave is its peak divided by the square root of two. Semi-amplitude is used in astronomy for the radial-velocity variation of a star caused by an orbiting companion.

Formal representation

A sinusoidal waveform can be written as x(t) = A sin(ωt + φ), where A is the amplitude, ω is the angular frequency, and φ is the phase. In wave phenomena, energy and intensity are typically proportional to the square of the amplitude. In communication engineering, varying the amplitude of a carrier wave is the basis of amplitude modulation.
