Typhoon

Typhoon
Region: Northwest Pacific Ocean
Equivalent terms: hurricane (Atlantic/NE Pacific), cyclone (Indian Ocean)
Warning centres: JMA, JTWC

A typhoon is a mature tropical cyclone that develops in the Northwest Pacific Ocean between 180° and 100°E. The term is regional: the same phenomenon is called a hurricane in the Atlantic and Northeast Pacific and a tropical cyclone in the Indian Ocean.

Classification

The Japan Meteorological Agency classifies Northwest Pacific systems by wind speed; storms reaching sustained winds of at least 119 km/h are typhoons, and the most intense may be called violent typhoons. The Joint Typhoon Warning Center additionally uses the informal label "super typhoon" for the strongest systems.

Impacts

Typhoons regularly strike the Philippines, Taiwan, Japan, China and Vietnam, bringing destructive winds, torrential rain, flooding and storm surge. The 1970 Bhola cyclone (in the Bay of Bengal) remains the deadliest tropical cyclone on record, while Pacific typhoons such as Haiyan (2013) caused catastrophic damage in the Philippines.
