Nanna

Nanna, known in Akkadian as Sin, was the Mesopotamian god of the Moon, worshipped by the Sumerians, Akkadians, Assyrians and Babylonians. He was the son of Enlil and Ninlil, and the father of the sun god Utu (Shamash) and of Inanna (Ishtar), goddess of love and war.

Worship

Nanna's chief sanctuary was the Ékišnugal temple at Ur, surmounted by the great ziggurat built by Ur-Nammu around 2100 BCE. The high priestess of Nanna at Ur — a role famously held by Enheduanna, daughter of Sargon of Akkad, the earliest known named author — was one of the most important religious offices in Sumer. In later periods the city of Harran in upper Mesopotamia became another major centre of the moon god's cult, which survived there into early Islamic times.

Mythology

In Sumerian myth, Nanna travelled each night across the sky in his crescent "boat", measuring time and decreeing the fates of the month. The hymn known as The Journey of Nanna to Nippur describes his voyage to receive blessings from his father Enlil. The crescent moon remained his symbol on cylinder seals and boundary stones for two millennia.
