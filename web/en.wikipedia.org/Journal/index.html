Journal

A journal, in its most general sense, is a record of events, transactions, observations, or thoughts kept on a regular — originally daily — basis. The word comes from the Old French journal ("daily"), from the Latin diurnalis. The term covers several distinct kinds of records, from personal diaries to scholarly periodicals and accounting books.

Personal journal

A personal journal or diary is a private record of experiences, ideas, and reflections kept over time. Diarists such as Samuel Pepys, whose shorthand diary of 1660s London is a major historical source, and Anne Frank, whose wartime diary became a world classic, show the genre's documentary and literary power. Journaling is also used therapeutically and as a tool for self-reflection and creative practice.

Academic and trade journals

In publishing, a journal is a periodical devoted to a particular discipline. Academic journals publish peer-reviewed research articles; the first, the Journal des sçavans and the Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society, both appeared in 1665. Trade journals serve particular industries or professions, while literary journals publish fiction, poetry, and criticism. The prestige of academic journals is commonly gauged by citation metrics such as the impact factor.

Journal in accounting

In bookkeeping, a journal is the book of original entry in which business transactions are first recorded in chronological order before being posted to accounts in the ledger. Specialized journals include the sales journal, purchases journal, and cash receipts journal; the general journal records transactions that fit none of these. Each entry records the date, the accounts debited and credited, and an explanation, forming the basis of the double-entry system.

Other uses

In engineering, a journal is the part of a rotating shaft that contacts a bearing. In law and government, a journal is the official record of proceedings of a legislature or court. Newspapers, most famously The Wall Street Journal, also bear the name in the older sense of a daily record of news.
