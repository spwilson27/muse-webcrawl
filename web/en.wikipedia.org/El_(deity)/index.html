El (Ugaritic: 𐎛𐎍, ʾīlu; Phoenician: 𐤀𐤋; Hebrew: אֵל, ʾĒl) is a Northwest Semitic word meaning "god" or "deity", and the name of the supreme god of the ancient Canaanite religion. In the Ugaritic texts of the Late Bronze Age, El is the aged, wise patriarch of the gods — "creator of creatures" and father of gods and men — presiding over the divine assembly from his tent-shrine at the source of the rivers.

In the Ugaritic texts

The clay tablets from Ugarit (modern Ras Shamra, Syria), dating to roughly the 13th century BCE, depict El as a bearded, bull-horned elder — the Bull El — renowned for wisdom and benevolence but also shown feasting and drinking to the point of collapse in a famous hymn. His wife is the goddess Athirat (Asherah), and his seventy sons, the Elohim, include the storm-god Baal, the sea-god Yam and Mot, god of death, whose rivalries drive the Baal Cycle.

El and Israelite religion

In the Hebrew Bible, El appears both as a generic word for a god and as a name or epithet of Israel's God — in names such as El Elyon ("God Most High"), El Shaddai, El Olam and El Bethel, and in patriarchal traditions of the pre-Mosaic period. Scholars of Israelite religion generally hold that the God of Israel was originally identified with El and only later merged with the worship of Yahweh as Israelite monotheism developed, absorbing El's titles, consort traditions and council imagery.

Legacy

The element El survives in countless biblical and modern names (Israel, Michael, Gabriel, Daniel, Elijah), in the Arabic ilah and Allah, and in later Phoenician and Aramaic religion, where El continued to be invoked for centuries.
