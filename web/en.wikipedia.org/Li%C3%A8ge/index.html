Liège (French: Liège; Walloon: Lidje; Dutch: Luik; German: Lüttich) is a city and municipality in eastern Belgium, the capital of the province of Liège and the largest city of Wallonia by metropolitan area. It lies in the valley of the river Meuse, near Belgium's borders with the Netherlands and Germany, at the confluence of the Meuse and the Ourthe. The city proper has roughly 200,000 inhabitants, and its metropolitan area about 600,000.

History

Liège grew around the martyrdom site of Saint Lambert, bishop of Maastricht, who was murdered there around 705. In the late 10th century, under Bishop Notker, it became the capital of a prince-bishopric that survived as an ecclesiastical state of the Holy Roman Empire for some eight centuries. The medieval city was a noted centre of learning and art, and its privileges were repeatedly contested between the prince-bishops and the guilds, culminating in episodes such as the destruction of the city by Charles the Bold of Burgundy in 1468.

After the Liège Revolution of 1789 and French annexation, the prince-bishopric was abolished. In the 19th century Liège became one of the first industrial centres of continental Europe, built on coal mining, steelmaking, glass, and armaments — the Fabrique Nationale at Herstal is nearby. In August 1914 the city's forts were the first major obstacle faced by the German invasion of Belgium at the Battle of Liège, delaying the German timetable and earning the city the French Légion d'honneur.

Economy and transport

Liège's traditional heavy industries have declined, but the city remains a major logistics hub: its river port is one of Europe's largest inland ports, Liège Airport is a major cargo airport, and the Liège-Guillemins railway station, designed by Santiago Calatrava and opened in 2009, is a landmark of high-speed rail architecture on the Brussels–Cologne line.

Culture

The city is known for its folklore, including the puppet theatre character Tchantchès, its August 15 festival in the Outremeuse district, the Sunday market La Batte along the Meuse, and local specialities such as Liège waffles, café liégeois, and boulets à la liégeoise (meatballs in a sweet-sour sauce with sirop de Liège). The University of Liège, founded in 1817, is a major French-speaking university. Liège is also famous in cycling as the start and finish of Liège–Bastogne–Liège, the oldest of the "Monument" classic races, first held in 1892.

Landmarks

  • The Palace of the Prince-Bishops and Place Saint-Lambert
  • The Montagne de Bueren staircase (374 steps)
  • St Bartholomew's Church and its 12th-century baptismal font
  • The Grand Curtius museum
  • Liège-Guillemins station
