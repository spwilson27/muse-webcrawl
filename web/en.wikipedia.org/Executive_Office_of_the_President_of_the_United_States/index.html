Executive Office of the President of the United States

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Executive Office of the President of the United States (EOP) is the group of federal agencies and staff units at the centre of the executive branch of the United States federal government that directly support the work of the President. It was created in 1939 by President Franklin D. Roosevelt under the Reorganization Act of 1939, following the recommendations of the Brownlow Committee, which famously concluded that "the President needs help."

Component units

The EOP includes the White House Office, which houses the President's closest personal and political staff under the White House Chief of Staff; the Office of Management and Budget (OMB), the largest EOP unit, which prepares the federal budget and reviews regulations; the National Security Council; the Council of Economic Advisers; the Office of the United States Trade Representative; the Office of Science and Technology Policy; and the Office of National Drug Control Policy, among others. Most senior EOP staff are appointed by the President without Senate confirmation, though the heads of some units, such as OMB, require it.

Role

Because its staff serve the President directly and can be hired and dismissed at will, the EOP gives a president a flexible instrument for developing policy, coordinating the executive branch, and communicating with Congress and the public, independent of the cabinet departments. Its growth over the twentieth century reflected the expansion of the federal government and of presidential responsibilities in economic management, national security, and administration.
