Intensive animal farming

Intensive animal farming
Also calledFactory farming, industrial livestock production
Main speciesPoultry, pigs, cattle, farmed fish
Key featuresHigh stocking density, confinement, mechanised feeding

Intensive animal farming, also known as factory farming or industrial livestock production, is a type of intensive agriculture used in raising livestock and poultry, designed to maximise production while minimising costs. To do so, agribusinesses keep livestock such as cattle, poultry, and fish at high stocking densities, at large scale, and use modern machinery, biotechnology, and global trade.

History

Intensive methods developed in the early 20th century with the discovery of vitamins and their role in animal nutrition, the introduction of antibiotics, and advances in housing and breeding. The system expanded rapidly after the Second World War, particularly in the United States and Europe, and now accounts for the majority of global meat, egg and dairy production. In the United States, large operations are regulated as concentrated animal feeding operations (CAFOs).

Practices

Animals are typically housed indoors in controlled environments: broiler chickens in large sheds holding tens of thousands of birds, laying hens historically in battery cages, pigs in gestation crates and fattening pens, and dairy cattle in free-stall barns. Feeding, watering, ventilation and waste removal are mechanised. Selective breeding has produced fast-growing, high-yielding animals, and antibiotics have been used for disease prevention and growth promotion, though growth-promoting uses are now banned in the European Union and restricted elsewhere.

Impacts and debate

Supporters argue intensive farming produces affordable food efficiently with less land per unit of output. Critics raise concerns about animal welfare, the spread of zoonotic disease and antimicrobial resistance, air and water pollution from manure lagoons, greenhouse gas emissions, and the concentration of the food industry. Reforms such as cage-free egg commitments, enriched cages, and bans on certain confinement systems have been adopted in several jurisdictions.
