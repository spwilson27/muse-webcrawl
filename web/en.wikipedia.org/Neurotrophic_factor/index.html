Neurotrophic factor

Neurotrophic factors are a family of biomolecules — nearly all of them peptides or small proteins — that support the growth, survival, and differentiation of both developing and mature neurons. Most are secreted by target tissue and act to prevent neurons from initiating programmed cell death (apoptosis), thus allowing them to survive.

Families

The neurotrophin family includes nerve growth factor (NGF), the first growth factor discovered, brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF), neurotrophin-3, and neurotrophin-4, which act through Trk receptor tyrosine kinases. Other families include the GDNF family of ligands, important for dopaminergic and motor neurons, and the neuropoietic cytokines.

Significance

Neurotrophic factors regulate synaptic plasticity, learning, and memory, and their dysregulation is implicated in neurodegenerative and psychiatric disorders. Rita Levi-Montalcini and Stanley Cohen received the 1986 Nobel Prize for the discovery of growth factors. Therapeutic delivery of these factors has been explored for diseases such as Parkinson's and Alzheimer's, with limited clinical success so far.
