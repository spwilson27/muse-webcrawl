Parliament of Malaysia

The Parliament of Malaysia (Malay: Parlimen Malaysia) is the national legislature of Malaysia, based on the Westminster system. It consists of the Yang di-Pertuan Agong (the King) and two houses: the Dewan Rakyat (House of Representatives) and the Dewan Negara (Senate). The Parliament meets in the Malaysian Houses of Parliament, located in the Lake Gardens area of Kuala Lumpur near the Perdana Botanical Gardens.

Composition

The Dewan Rakyat has 222 members, elected by first-past-the-post from single-member constituencies for terms of up to five years. It is the dominant house: the prime minister must command its confidence, and money bills originate there. The Dewan Negara has 70 members serving three-year terms: 26 are elected by the state legislative assemblies (two per state) and 44 are appointed by the King on the advice of the prime minister, chosen from among people who have distinguished themselves in public life or represent minority and indigenous interests.

Functions and procedure

Parliament makes federal law, approves taxation and expenditure, and scrutinises the executive through question time, debates, and select committees. A bill must pass both houses and receive royal assent; the Senate can delay but not ultimately block most legislation, and its power over money bills is limited to one month. The leader of the majority in the Dewan Rakyat becomes prime minister, and the government of the day is formed from members of both houses.

History

The legislature traces its origins to the Federal Legislative Council of the Federation of Malaya, which first sat in 1948 and continued after independence in 1957. The present Parliament was established with the formation of Malaysia in 1963. Its most turbulent episode came after the riots of 13 May 1969, when Parliament was suspended for nearly two years and the country was governed by the National Operations Council; it reconvened in 1971 with constitutional amendments restricting debate on certain "sensitive matters".
