PepsiCo

PepsiCo, Inc. is an American multinational food, snack, and beverage corporation headquartered in Harrison, New York, in the hamlet of Purchase. It is one of the largest food and beverage companies in the world, with products sold in more than 200 countries and territories and annual net revenue of roughly US$90 billion.

History

The company was formed in 1965 by the merger of Pepsi-Cola — whose flagship drink had been created by pharmacist Caleb Bradham in 1893 and trademarked as Pepsi-Cola in 1903 — and Frito-Lay, the snack company built by Herman Lay. Donald Kendall, CEO of Pepsi-Cola, and Herman Lay became the leaders of the new company.

Products and divisions

PepsiCo's portfolio includes Pepsi, Mountain Dew, Gatorade, Tropicana, 7 Up (outside the United States), Aquafina and Bubly in beverages, and Lay's, Doritos, Cheetos, Tostitos, Ruffles and Quaker in foods. Frito-Lay North America and PepsiCo Beverages North America are its largest divisions. The company has pursued diversification into healthier products, bottled water, and brands such as SodaStream (acquired in 2018) and Rockstar Energy.

Competition

PepsiCo's principal rival is The Coca-Cola Company; their decades-long rivalry in soft drinks is known as the "cola wars". In snacks, PepsiCo is the world's largest producer of savoury snacks.
