Ponta Delgada

Ponta Delgada is the largest city and the administrative capital of the Azores, an autonomous region of Portugal. Located on São Miguel Island, the largest and most populous island of the archipelago, the municipality has a population of roughly 67,000, making it the largest municipality in the Azores.

History

The settlement began in the mid-15th century as a small fishing village near a thin point of land — ponta delgada in Portuguese — of volcanic origin. It grew rapidly in importance due to its sheltered bay, becoming the principal port of São Miguel and receiving city status in 1546. The Forte de São Brás was built in the 16th century to defend the harbour against pirate attacks. In the 19th century the city prospered on the export of oranges to Britain, and later on pineapple, tea, and tobacco production. Since 1976 it has been the seat of the Regional Government of the Azores and home to the University of the Azores, founded the same year.

Geography and climate

Ponta Delgada occupies the southern coast of São Miguel. It has a Mediterranean-influenced humid subtropical climate with mild winters, warm summers, and high humidity year-round, moderated by the surrounding Atlantic Ocean and the Gulf Stream. The municipality includes the parishes surrounding the volcanic complexes of Sete Cidades to the west and Lagoa do Fogo to the east.

Economy and transport

The economy is based on services, tourism, dairy farming, and fishing. João Paulo II Airport, just outside the city, is the busiest airport in the Azores, with flights to mainland Portugal, Europe, and North America. The Portas da Cidade ("City Gates"), a triple-arched landmark built in 1783, stands beside the harbour and is the city's best-known symbol.
