Sandžak

Sandžak (Serbian Cyrillic: Санџак; pronounced [sǎndʒak]) is a historical and geo-political region in the Balkans straddling the border between southwestern Serbia and northern Montenegro. The name derives from the Ottoman Turkish word sanjak, an administrative district, referring to the Sanjak of Novi Pazar, the last Ottoman sanjak to remain under Ottoman rule in the area until the First Balkan War in 1912.

Geography

The region covers roughly 8,700 square kilometres of mountainous terrain between the Zlatibor and Prokletije ranges. Its largest city is Novi Pazar in Serbia; other towns include Sjenica, Tutin, Prijepolje, Priboj and Nova Varoš in Serbia, and Pljevlja, Bijelo Polje, Berane, Rožaje and Plav in Montenegro.

History

In the Middle Ages much of the region formed part of the Serbian medieval kingdom and contained the old capital region of Raška around Stari Ras. It came under Ottoman control in the fifteenth century and remained so for over four centuries. The Congress of Berlin (1878) left the Sanjak of Novi Pazar under Ottoman administration while Austria-Hungary gained the right to garrison it. Serbia and Montenegro divided the region between them after the First Balkan War (1912–1913).

Demographics

Sandžak is notable for its large Bosniak (Slavic Muslim) population, concentrated especially around Novi Pazar, Tutin, Sjenica and Rožaje, alongside Serb and Montenegrin majorities in other municipalities. During the Yugoslav wars of the 1990s the region experienced tension and incidents such as the Sjeverin massacre, though it avoided full-scale conflict.
