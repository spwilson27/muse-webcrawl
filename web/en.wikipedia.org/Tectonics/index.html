Tectonics

Tectonics (from the Greek tekton, "builder") is the branch of geology concerned with the structure of the Earth's crust and the large-scale processes that deform it, including the movement of lithospheric plates, mountain building, faulting and folding. Its central framework is the theory of plate tectonics.

Plate tectonics

The Earth's outer shell, the lithosphere, is divided into rigid plates that move over the ductile asthenosphere. At divergent boundaries plates pull apart and new crust forms, as at mid-ocean ridges; at convergent boundaries one plate may subduct beneath another, producing volcanic arcs and deep ocean trenches; at transform boundaries plates slide past each other along faults such as the San Andreas.

Deformation

Tectonic stresses fold sedimentary strata into anticlines and synclines and break rock along faults — normal faults under extension, reverse faults and thrusts under compression, and strike-slip faults under lateral shear. Over geological time these processes build mountain ranges such as the Himalayas and the Andes.

Methods

Tectonics draws on structural geology, geodesy (including GPS measurement of present-day plate motion), seismology and palaeomagnetism, which records the past positions of continents and confirmed continental drift in the 1960s.
