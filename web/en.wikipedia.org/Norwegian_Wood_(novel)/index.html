Norwegian Wood (Japanese: ノルウェイの森, Noruwei no Mori) is a 1987 novel by Japanese author Haruki Murakami. A nostalgic story of loss and coming of age, it is narrated by Toru Watanabe, who looks back on his days as a university student in Tokyo in the late 1960s. The title is taken from the Beatles song "Norwegian Wood (This Bird Has Flown)", which is mentioned repeatedly in the story.

Plot

Toru Watanabe, a serious and quiet student, devotes himself to his friend Kizuki's former girlfriend Naoko, a fragile young woman struggling with depression after Kizuki's suicide at seventeen. As Naoko retreats to a sanatorium in the mountains near Kyoto, Toru is drawn to Midori, an outgoing and free-spirited classmate. Torn between the two women — one bound to the past and death, the other to life — Watanabe navigates student protests, loneliness and grief, accompanied by his friend Nagasawa and the sanatorium resident Reiko.

Reception and influence

Unlike Murakami's earlier, more surreal fiction, Norwegian Wood is a work of straight realism. It was an enormous bestseller in Japan, selling millions of copies and making Murakami a literary celebrity among young readers — a fame that famously discomforted him. Often described as Murakami's breakthrough work internationally, it remains one of his most popular novels worldwide.

Adaptations

A film adaptation directed by Tran Anh Hung, with a score by Jonny Greenwood, was released in 2010, starring Kenichi Matsuyama as Toru, Rinko Kikuchi as Naoko and Kiko Mizuhara as Midori.
