The Communist Party of Czechoslovakia (Czech and Slovak: Komunistická strana Československa, KSČ) was a communist and Marxist–Leninist political party in Czechoslovakia that existed between 1921 and 1992. It was a member of the Comintern and, uniquely among Central European communist parties, won a plurality in free elections in the Czech lands in 1946.

Rule of Czechoslovakia

In February 1948 the party seized full power in a coup d'état backed by the threat of Soviet intervention, establishing a one-party state in the Czechoslovak Socialist Republic. Under Klement Gottwald the regime conducted Stalinist show trials, including that of Rudolf Slánský in 1952. In 1968 the reformist leadership of Alexander Dubček attempted to create "socialism with a human face" during the Prague Spring, provoking the Warsaw Pact invasion of August 1968, justified under the Brezhnev Doctrine. The subsequent "normalization" era under Gustáv Husák lasted until the Velvet Revolution of November 1989 ended forty-one years of party rule. After the dissolution of Czechoslovakia, the party split into the Communist Party of Bohemia and Moravia and the Party of the Democratic Left in Slovakia.
