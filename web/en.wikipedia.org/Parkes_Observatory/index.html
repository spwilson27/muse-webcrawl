Parkes Observatory

Parkes Observatory
Location: Near Parkes, New South Wales, Australia
Opened: 31 October 1961
Telescope: 64-metre radio dish
Operator: CSIRO

The Parkes Observatory is a radio astronomy observatory near the town of Parkes in New South Wales, Australia. Its 64-metre (210 ft) dish — nicknamed "The Dish" — is one of the largest single-dish radio telescopes in the Southern Hemisphere and is operated by CSIRO.

History

The telescope was completed in 1961 and designed under E. G. "Taffy" Bowen. Its design influenced later large dishes, including NASA's Deep Space Network antennas. In July 1969, Parkes received and relayed television images of the Apollo 11 Moon landing to a worldwide audience, after the primary station at Goldstone and the dish at Honeysuckle Creek.

Science

Parkes has discovered many pulsars — more than half of those known in its early decades — mapped the hydrogen in the Milky Way, and conducted searches for fast radio bursts and extraterrestrial signals.
