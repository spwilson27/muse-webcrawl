Suraj Mal

Maharaja Suraj Mal (1707–1763) was a ruler of the Jat state of Bharatpur in northwestern India. Succeeding his father Badan Singh, he consolidated and greatly expanded Jat power in the region between Delhi and Agra during the decline of the Mughal Empire.

Suraj Mal
Reign: c. 1755–1763
Predecessor: Badan Singh
Died: 25 December 1763
State: Bharatpur

Reign

Suraj Mal is remembered as a capable administrator and military leader — sometimes called the "Plato of the Jat people" — who built strong fortresses, including the Lohagarh Fort, and made Bharatpur a prosperous state. He intervened in the politics of Delhi, supported the Marathas at times, and sheltered their survivors after the Third Battle of Panipat (1761).

Death

He was killed in 1763 in a skirmish on the Hindon River near Delhi while fighting the Rohilla chief Najib-ud-Daula's forces.
