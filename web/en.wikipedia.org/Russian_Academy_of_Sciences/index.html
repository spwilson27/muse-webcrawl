The Russian Academy of Sciences (RAS; Russian: Российская академия наук) consists of the national academy of sciences of Russia and a network of research institutes across the country. Founded in Saint Petersburg by Peter the Great in 1724, on the advice of Gottfried Leibniz, it is one of the oldest scientific institutions in continuous existence.

History

The Academy's first scholars were recruited from abroad — among them the mathematicians Leonhard Euler and Daniel Bernoulli arrived in the 1720s — and it quickly became a centre for the exploration and description of the Russian Empire. Renamed several times, it became the Academy of Sciences of the USSR in 1925, with its headquarters moved to Moscow in 1934, and resumed the name Russian Academy of Sciences in 1991. In 2013 a controversial reform placed its institutes under a federal agency (later the Ministry of Science and Higher Education) and merged the separate academies of medical and agricultural sciences into the RAS.

Structure

The Academy is organised into disciplinary divisions covering mathematics, physics, chemistry, earth sciences, biology, medicine, agriculture, social sciences and humanities, with regional branches in Siberia, the Urals and the Far East. Its institute network historically employed tens of thousands of researchers, and it elects full members (academicians), corresponding members and foreign members. Its presidents have included prominent figures such as the mathematician Mstislav Keldysh.

Role

The RAS advises the Russian government on science policy, awards prizes including the Lomonosov Gold Medal, and maintains international links with other national academies. Critics of the post-2013 arrangements argue that the Academy lost effective control of its institutes and funding; government officials maintained the reforms improved management of state property and research.
