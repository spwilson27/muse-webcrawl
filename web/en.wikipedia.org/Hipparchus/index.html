Hipparchus of Nicaea (Greek: Ἵππαρχος; c. 190 – c. 120 BC) was a Greek astronomer, geographer and mathematician. He is considered the founder of trigonometry and is widely regarded as the greatest astronomer of antiquity. Working mainly on the island of Rhodes, he compiled the first known comprehensive star catalogue, discovered the precession of the equinoxes, and developed a system for predicting eclipses and the motions of the Sun and Moon that was used for centuries.

Astronomical work

By comparing his own observations with records made 150 years earlier by Timocharis and Aristyllus, Hipparchus discovered that the equinoxes precess — the stars' longitudes shift slowly relative to the equinoctial points — and estimated the rate at not less than 1° per century (the modern value is about 1.4°). He compiled a catalogue of some 850 stars with positions and brightnesses on a six-point magnitude scale, the ancestor of the magnitude system still in use.

Hipparchus computed the length of the tropical year to within about 6.5 minutes, determined the Moon's distance and approximate size, and devised a geometric model of the Moon's motion using epicycles and eccentrics that could predict eclipses. His solar theory, refined by Ptolemy, remained standard for over 1,500 years. He is also credited with inventing or improving the astrolabe and the armillary sphere.

Mathematics and geography

To support his computations Hipparchus constructed a table of chords — effectively the first trigonometric table — and developed spherical trigonometry. In geography he criticised Eratosthenes, advocated systematic use of latitude and longitude, and divided the Earth's circle into 360 degrees. Only one of his works survives complete, the Commentary on the Phaenomena of Aratus and Eudoxus; the rest of his output is known mainly through Ptolemy's Almagest, which drew heavily on it.

Legacy

The lunar crater Hipparchus and the asteroid 4000 Hipparchus are named after him, as was ESA's Hipparcos astrometry satellite (1989–1993), whose catalogue of over 100,000 stars consciously echoed his achievement.
