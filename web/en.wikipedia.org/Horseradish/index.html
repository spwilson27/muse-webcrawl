Horseradish

Scientific nameArmoracia rusticana
FamilyBrassicaceae
OriginSoutheastern Europe and western Asia

Horseradish (Armoracia rusticana) is a perennial plant of the family Brassicaceae, which also includes mustard, wasabi, broccoli, and cabbage. It is a root vegetable, cultivated and used worldwide as a spice and as a condiment. The species is probably native to southeastern Europe and western Asia.

Characteristics

The plant grows up to 1.5 metres tall, with hairless bright green leaves up to 1 metre long. Its white, tapered root produces little smell until cut or grated, when enzymes from the damaged cells convert sinigrin into allyl isothiocyanate, the pungent compound that irritates the sinuses and eyes — the same type of compound responsible for the heat of mustard and wasabi.

Culinary use

Grated horseradish root, often mixed with vinegar, is served as a condiment with roast beef in Britain (as horseradish sauce), with meats and sausages in Central and Eastern Europe, and as an ingredient in cocktail sauce and Bloody Mary cocktails. In Jewish tradition, horseradish (chrain) is eaten at Passover as a bitter herb. "Prepared horseradish" is white; the red variety is coloured with beetroot juice.

Cultivation

Horseradish is perennial in hardiness zones 2–9 and can be invasive if left unchecked. Commercial production is concentrated in regions such as Collinsville, Illinois, which calls itself the "Horseradish Capital of the World".
