Hankel function

Hankel functions, named after Hermann Hankel, are special functions of mathematical physics: the Hankel functions of the first and second kind, Hα(1)(x) and Hα(2)(x), are linear combinations of the Bessel functions of the first and second kind.

Definition

They are defined as Hα(1)(x) = Jα(x) + iYα(x) and Hα(2)(x) = Jα(x) − iYα(x), where Jα and Yα are Bessel functions. They satisfy Bessel's differential equation and standard recurrence relations, and are a pair of linearly independent solutions in the complex plane.

Asymptotics and applications

For large argument, Hankel functions behave like outgoing and incoming cylindrical waves, proportional to e±ix/√x. This makes them fundamental in problems with cylindrical symmetry involving radiation and scattering: acoustics, electromagnetism, seismology and quantum mechanics, where they represent travelling-wave solutions rather than the standing waves given by J and Y alone.

Related functions

The modified (spherical and cylindrical) Hankel functions arise for imaginary arguments and in spherical geometries.
