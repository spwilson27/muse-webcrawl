Conscription, sometimes called the draft in the United States, is the practice of compelling people to enlist into a national service, most often a military service. Conscription dates back to antiquity and it continues in some countries to the present day under various names. The modern system of near-universal national conscription of young men dates to the French Revolution in the 1790s, where it became the basis of a very large and powerful military.

Most European nations later copied the system in peacetime, so that men at a certain age would serve one to two years on active duty and then transfer to the reserve force.

History

Compulsory military service has ancient roots: the Egyptian levy under the pharaohs, the Roman legions' later recruitment, medieval feudal levies, and the Ottoman devshirme system all compelled service. The levée en masse of 1793, decreed during the French Revolutionary Wars, mobilized the entire nation for war and is regarded as the birth of modern conscription. Prussia adopted a similar system after its defeat by Napoleon, and the model spread across Europe and beyond during the nineteenth century.

Conscription by country

Many nations retain conscription in law, though the number actively drafting citizens in peacetime has shrunk since the end of the Cold War. Countries such as Israel, South Korea, Singapore, Finland, Norway, Sweden, and Switzerland maintain active conscription. Sweden has made its system gender-neutral, as has Norway. Russia and Ukraine have relied heavily on conscription and mobilization during the Russo-Ukrainian War.

Conscription of women

Historically most conscription applied only to men. A small but growing number of states conscript women on equal terms, including Norway, Sweden, Denmark, and Israel. Debates over gender equality in draft registration continue in several countries, including the United States.

Opposition

Conscription has long faced opposition on political, religious, and moral grounds. Conscientious objectors refuse service on grounds of conscience; many systems offer alternative civilian service. Draft evasion, desertion, and organized resistance movements — notably during the Vietnam War — have shaped public debate wherever the draft is used.
