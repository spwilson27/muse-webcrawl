Religious law

Religious law comprises ethical and moral codes taught by religious traditions, which adherents understand as divinely ordained or otherwise binding rules governing conduct. Different faiths maintain different legal systems, and their relationship to secular state law varies widely.

Major systems

Examples include Jewish halakha, Islamic sharia, Christian canon law, and Hindu law. Canon law, developed by the Roman Catholic Church, regulates church governance, sacraments, and discipline, and is administered through church courts. Sharia, derived from the Qur'an and the Sunnah, covers ritual, family, commercial, and criminal matters and is applied to differing degrees in various Muslim-majority states.

Religious and secular law

In some countries religious courts have official jurisdiction over matters such as marriage and inheritance for members of a faith; in others religious law operates only as a matter of private conscience or voluntary arbitration.
