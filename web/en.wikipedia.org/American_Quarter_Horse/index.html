The American Quarter Horse, also known as the Quarter Horse, is an American breed of horse that excels at sprinting short distances. Its name came from its ability to outrun other horse breeds in races of a quarter mile or less; some have been clocked at speeds up to 44 mph (70.8 km/h). The American Quarter Horse is the most popular breed in the United States, and the American Quarter Horse Association (AQHA), founded in 1940, is the largest breed registry in the world, with almost three million living registered Quarter Horses.

History

The breed descends from English Thoroughbreds imported to the American colonies in the 17th and 18th centuries and crossed with local horses of Spanish ancestry, including the Chickasaw horse. Colonists raced these sprinters over short straight courses that became quarter-mile races. On the western frontier the type developed into the classic cow horse, valued for its quickness, calm temperament and innate "cow sense" when working cattle.

In the 20th century, Thoroughbred blood was reintroduced through influential stallions. The AQHA recognises horses with one registered Quarter Horse parent and one Thoroughbred parent as "Appendix" registered. Foundation sires such as Wimpy, King, Leo and Peter McCue shaped the modern breed.

Characteristics

Quarter Horses are compact and muscular, with a short, refined head, strong hindquarters, and a height generally between 14.3 and 16 hands. Recognised coat colours include sorrel, bay, black, brown, chestnut, dun, palomino, buckskin, cremello, perlino, grey, and roan variants. Two broad types exist: the stock type (working and pleasure horses) and the taller, leaner racing and hunter type.

Uses

The breed is used for western riding disciplines such as reining, cutting, barrel racing, and roping, as well as ranch work, trail riding, driving, and English disciplines. Quarter Horse racing is popular at tracks across the United States; the richest race, the All American Futurity at Ruidoso Downs, New Mexico, offers a purse of about $3 million.

Health issues

Certain lines carry hereditary conditions including hyperkalemic periodic paralysis (HYPP), traced to the stallion Impressive; hereditary equine regional dermal asthenia (HERDA); glycogen branching enzyme deficiency (GBED); and polysaccharide storage myopathy (PSSM). The AQHA requires genetic testing for registration of horses descended from known carriers and publishes results on registration papers.
