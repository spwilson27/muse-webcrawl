Mesozoic

The Mesozoic is known as the Age of Reptiles.

The Mesozoic Era is the second-to-last era of Earth's geological history, lasting from about 252 to 66 million years ago. It comprises the Triassic, Jurassic, and Cretaceous periods, and is known as the Age of Reptiles because of the dominance of dinosaurs and other reptile groups.

Geology

The Mesozoic began with the supercontinent Pangaea intact and ended with the continents approaching their modern configuration, as the Atlantic Ocean opened and India, Africa, and South America separated. Sea levels were high for much of the era, and the climate was generally warm, with no polar ice caps.

Life

Following the end-Permian mass extinction, archosaurs — including dinosaurs, pterosaurs, and marine reptiles such as ichthyosaurs and plesiosaurs — came to dominate terrestrial and marine ecosystems. Mammals and birds originated during the era, and flowering plants (angiosperms) rose to dominance in the Cretaceous.

End of the era

The Mesozoic ended with the Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event 66 million years ago, caused largely by the Chicxulub asteroid impact, which wiped out the non-avian dinosaurs and opened the way for the mammalian radiation of the Cenozoic.
