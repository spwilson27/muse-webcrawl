The Inland Empire (locally called the I.E.) is a metropolitan region inland of and adjacent to coastal Southern California, centring on Riverside and San Bernardino counties. It is one of the most populous metropolitan areas in the United States, with more than 4.7 million residents, and encompasses cities including Riverside, San Bernardino, Ontario, Fontana, Rancho Cucamonga, Corona, and Moreno Valley, extending east toward Palm Springs and the Coachella Valley.

Geography and economy

The region occupies valleys bounded by the San Bernardino, San Gabriel, and San Jacinto mountains, with desert communities further east. Historically a centre of citrus agriculture — the navel orange industry began in Riverside in the 1870s — the Inland Empire grew rapidly in the late 20th century as comparatively affordable housing drew commuters to jobs in Los Angeles and Orange counties. Its location at the junction of major interstate highways and rail lines, and the cheap availability of large land parcels, made it one of the largest logistics and warehousing hubs in North America, served by Ontario International Airport and several intermodal facilities.

Demographics and institutions

The Inland Empire is ethnically diverse, with a large and growing Latino population. Major institutions include the University of California, Riverside; California State University, San Bernardino; and Loma Linda University and its medical centre. The region is also known for the San Manuel and Morongo tribal casinos, Route 66 heritage in its foothill cities, and the San Bernardino National Forest.

Issues

Rapid growth has brought challenges: long commutes, air pollution trapped by the mountains, warehouse-related truck traffic and land-use debates, and historically lower incomes and educational attainment than California's coastal metros — concerns that have driven regional policy debate in the 21st century.
