Livestreaming, also written live streaming, is the real-time transmission of audio and video over the internet, allowing viewers to watch events as they happen and, on interactive platforms, to communicate with the broadcaster through live chat. It is used for entertainment, video game broadcasting, news and sports coverage, education, religious services, e-commerce ("live shopping"), and personal broadcasting by streamers.

History

Early streaming media emerged in the mid-1990s with products such as RealAudio and RealVideo, and the first live webcast is often credited to the band Severe Tire Damage in 1993. Justin.tv, launched in 2007 as a "lifecasting" site, popularized user-generated live video; its gaming section spun off in 2011 as Twitch, which was acquired by Amazon in 2014 for about $970 million. YouTube added live streaming in 2011, Facebook launched Facebook Live in 2016, and mobile apps such as Periscope and later TikTok Live brought broadcasting to smartphones. The COVID-19 pandemic sharply accelerated adoption for concerts, classes, fitness, and workplace events.

Technology

A stream typically begins with capture software (such as OBS Studio) encoding video and sending it to a platform via RTMP or newer protocols. Platforms transcode the feed into multiple quality levels and distribute it through content delivery networks using adaptive streaming formats such as HLS and MPEG-DASH, while WebRTC enables sub-second latency for interactive formats. Key trade-offs involve latency, image quality, bandwidth cost, and scalability to audiences that can number in the millions for major events.

Culture and economy

Livestreaming supports a creator economy in which broadcasters earn income from subscriptions, advertising, sponsorships, and viewer donations or "tips." Video game and esports streaming form its largest entertainment segment, supplemented by music, talk, creative work, and IRL (in real life) content. Live commerce, in which hosts demonstrate and sell products in real time, became a retail sector worth hundreds of billions of dollars annually in China. Concerns around the format include content moderation of unscripted broadcasts, copyrighted music, harassment, and streamer burnout.
