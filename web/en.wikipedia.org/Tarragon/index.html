Tarragon

Tarragon (Artemisia dracunculus), also known as estragon, is a species of perennial herb in the family Asteraceae. It is widespread across much of temperate Eurasia and North America and is cultivated for its aromatic leaves, used as a culinary herb.

Tarragon
Scientific name: Artemisia dracunculus L.
Family: Asteraceae
Use: Culinary herb

Culinary use

French tarragon, propagated by division because its flowers are sterile, is one of the four fines herbes of French cooking, alongside chives, chervil and parsley. It is a key flavouring in Béarnaise sauce and is used with chicken, fish and eggs, and to flavour vinegar. Russian tarragon, grown from seed, is hardier but much less aromatic. Tarragon is also the main flavouring of the popular Georgian soft drink Tarkhuna.
