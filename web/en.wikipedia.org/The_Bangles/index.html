The Bangles

The Bangles are an American all-female rock band formed in Los Angeles in 1981. Blending 1960s-inspired jangle pop with new wave, they achieved major commercial success in the 1980s with hits including "Manic Monday", "Walk Like an Egyptian", and "Eternal Flame".

OriginLos Angeles, California, U.S.
GenresPop rock, jangle pop, Paisley Underground
Years active1981–1989; 1998–present
MembersSusanna Hoffs, Debbi Peterson, Vicki Peterson, Annette Zilinskas

History

Emerged from Los Angeles's Paisley Underground scene, the band — Susanna Hoffs and sisters Vicki and Debbi Peterson at its core — broke through with the album All Over the Place (1984). "Manic Monday", written by Prince, and the chart-topping "Walk Like an Egyptian" made them stars; "Eternal Flame" (1989), from the album Everything, became one of the best-selling singles by an all-female group.

Later years

Internal tensions and exhaustion led to a breakup in 1989 and solo careers, notably Hoffs's. The band reunited in the late 1990s, releasing Doll Revolution (2003) and Sweetheart of the Sun (2011) and continuing to tour.
