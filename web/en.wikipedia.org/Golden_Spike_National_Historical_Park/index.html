Golden Spike National Historical Park

Golden Spike National Historical Park is a United States National Historical Park located at Promontory Summit, north of the Great Salt Lake in Utah. It commemorates the completion of the first transcontinental railroad on 10 May 1869, where the Union Pacific and Central Pacific railroads met and a ceremonial golden spike was driven to join the rails.

History

The Pacific Railroad Act of 1862 authorised the two companies to build toward each other: the Central Pacific eastward from Sacramento, California — largely with Chinese immigrant labour — and the Union Pacific westward from Omaha, Nebraska, employing many Irish immigrants and Civil War veterans. On 10 May 1869 the lines met at Promontory Summit. Central Pacific president Leland Stanford drove the final, ceremonial spike of 17.6-karat gold, and the message "DONE" was telegraphed across the nation.

The main line was later rerouted across the Great Salt Lake via the Lucin Cutoff (1904), and the Promontory rails were removed for scrap in 1942. Public interest preserved the site: it was authorised as Golden Spike National Historic Site in 1965 and redesignated a National Historical Park in the John D. Dingell Jr. Conservation, Management, and Recreation Act of 2019.

The park today

The park features a visitor centre, 1.5 miles of restored track, and working replicas of the two locomotives present in 1869 — Central Pacific's Jupiter and Union Pacific's No. 119 — which are re-enacted nose-to-nose on the site of the ceremony. Annual re-enactments are held each 10 May and during a summer festival. The surrounding landscape preserves grades, cuts and fills built by both railroads, including the Central Pacific's "Ten Miles of Track in One Day" stretch.
