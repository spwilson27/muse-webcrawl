Muqaddimah

The Muqaddimah (Arabic: المقدمة, "Introduction"), also known as the Prolegomena, is a book written by the North African historian Ibn Khaldun in 1377. It is the introduction to his larger work of history, the Kitab al-Ibar, but is regarded as a major work in its own right — an early treatise on historiography, sociology, economics and the philosophy of history.

Content

Ibn Khaldun sets out a theory of the rise and decline of civilizations, centred on asabiyyah, the social cohesion or group solidarity that he argued is strong among nomadic peoples and decays as dynasties become sedentary and luxurious, typically within about four generations. The work also discusses the classification of the sciences, geography, economics, taxation and urban life.

Influence

Long read in the Muslim world, the Muqaddimah was introduced to European scholars in the 19th century and has since been praised as a pioneering work of social science, anticipating ideas later developed by thinkers such as Machiavelli, Montesquieu and modern sociologists.
