The Insolvency Act 1986 is the principal UK statute on corporate and personal insolvency.

The Insolvency Act 1986 (c. 45) is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom that provides the legal framework for corporate and personal insolvency in England and Wales and, with variations, Scotland. It consolidated earlier legislation and implemented many recommendations of the Cork Report (1982), chaired by Kenneth Cork, which sought to modernise insolvency law and promote a "rescue culture" for viable businesses.

Contents

The Act governs the main insolvency procedures. For companies it covers company voluntary arrangements, administration, administrative receivership (largely replaced by administration for later charges), liquidation (winding up), and the duties and liabilities of directors, including wrongful trading and fraudulent trading, transactions at an undervalue and preferences. For individuals it governs bankruptcy, individual voluntary arrangements and deeds of arrangement. It also regulates insolvency practitioners, who must be licensed to act as liquidators, administrators, trustees in bankruptcy and supervisors of voluntary arrangements.

Later amendments

The Act has been substantially amended, notably by the Insolvency Act 2000, the Enterprise Act 2002 — which streamlined administration and restricted administrative receivership — and the Corporate Insolvency and Governance Act 2020, which added a free-standing moratorium and restructuring plan. Its procedures are invoked through officials such as the official receiver, and disputes may be investigated at an inquest-like inquiry into a company's affairs by the Insolvency Service.
