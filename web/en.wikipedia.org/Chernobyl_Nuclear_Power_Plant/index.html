Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant

The Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant is a decommissioned nuclear power station near the abandoned city of Pripyat in northern Ukraine, about 110 km north of Kyiv. It consisted of four RBMK-1000 reactors, each capable of producing 1,000 megawatts of electrical power. The plant is notorious as the site of the world's worst nuclear accident, the explosion of Reactor No. 4 on 26 April 1986.

Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant

Country: Ukraine (formerly Soviet Union)

Reactors: 4 × RBMK-1000

Commissioned: 1977

Decommissioned: 2000

History

Construction began in the 1970s, with the first reactor commissioned in 1977. Reactor No. 4 entered service in 1983. On 26 April 1986, a flawed reactor design combined with errors during a safety test caused an uncontrolled power surge, steam explosions and a graphite fire that released large quantities of radioactive material across Europe. The disaster led to the evacuation of Pripyat and the creation of the Chernobyl Exclusion Zone.

Decommissioning

The undamaged reactors continued to operate after the accident; the last, Reactor No. 3, was shut down in 2000. The destroyed reactor was first enclosed in a concrete "sarcophagus" in 1986, and in 2016 the much larger New Safe Confinement arch was slid into place over it to allow the dismantling of the old structure and the removal of radioactive material.
