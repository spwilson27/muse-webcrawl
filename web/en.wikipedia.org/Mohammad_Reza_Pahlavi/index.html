Mohammad Reza Pahlavi


Reign: 16 September 1941 – 11 February 1979
Born: 26 October 1919, Tehran
Died: 27 July 1980, Cairo, Egypt
Dynasty: Pahlavi
Spouses: Fawzia of Egypt; Soraya Esfandiary-Bakhtiary; Farah Diba

Mohammad Reza Pahlavi (26 October 1919 – 27 July 1980) was the last Shah of Iran, ruling from 1941 until his overthrow in the Iranian Revolution of 1979. The son of Reza Shah, he came to the throne after his father's forced abdication during the Anglo-Soviet invasion of Iran.

Reign

His reign was marked by rapid modernization and close ties with the United States and the West. After Prime Minister Mohammad Mosaddegh nationalized the oil industry, a 1953 coup organized by the United States and the United Kingdom restored the Shah's authority. From 1963 he pursued the "White Revolution", a programme of land reform, women's suffrage, literacy campaigns, and industrialization funded by rising oil revenues. In 1971 he staged lavish celebrations of 2,500 years of the Persian monarchy at Persepolis, and in 1976 he replaced the Islamic calendar's epoch with an imperial one.

Overthrow and exile

Opposition grew from clerics, led by Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini, as well as from liberals and leftists resentful of authoritarian rule, the SAVAK security police, and corruption. Mass protests in 1978 forced the Shah to leave Iran in January 1979; the monarchy collapsed the following month. His admission to the United States for cancer treatment later that year helped trigger the seizure of the US embassy in Tehran and the hostage crisis. He died in Cairo in 1980 and is buried in the Al-Rifa'i Mosque.

Legacy

Assessments of his rule remain sharply divided: supporters credit him with modernization, infrastructure, and women's rights; critics cite political repression, inequality, and dependence on foreign powers.
