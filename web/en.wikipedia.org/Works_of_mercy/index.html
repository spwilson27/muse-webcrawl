Works of mercy

The works of mercy are practices valued in Christian tradition, based chiefly on the parable of the sheep and the goats in the Gospel of Matthew (chapter 25). They are traditionally divided into corporal works of mercy, concerning bodily needs, and spiritual works of mercy, concerning the needs of the soul.

Corporal works of mercy

The seven corporal works are: feed the hungry, give drink to the thirsty, clothe the naked, shelter the homeless (or harbour the harbourless), visit the sick, ransom the captive (or visit the imprisoned), and bury the dead.

Spiritual works of mercy

The seven spiritual works are: instruct the ignorant, counsel the doubtful, admonish sinners, bear wrongs patiently, forgive offences willingly, comfort the afflicted, and pray for the living and the dead.

In art and practice

The works of mercy inspired confraternities, hospitals, and religious orders dedicated to charitable service, and they are a frequent subject in Christian art, notably Caravaggio's Seven Works of Mercy (1607).
