Artemisia II of Caria

Reign: c. 353–351 BC

Predecessor: Mausolus

Spouse: Mausolus (also her brother)

Kingdom: Caria

Artemisia II of Caria (died c. 351 BC) was a naval strategist, commander and the sister and widow of Mausolus, ruler of Caria in southwestern Asia Minor. She succeeded him as satrap of Caria under the Achaemenid Empire and reigned for about two years.

Life and reign

Artemisia was the daughter of Hecatomnus of Mylasa, founder of the Hecatomnid dynasty. Following the dynastic custom of her family, she married her brother Mausolus. During his lifetime she reportedly shared in his rule and commanded naval forces. On his death in 353 BC she succeeded him and suppressed a revolt by Rhodes, capturing the island and executing its leaders.

The Mausoleum

Artemisia is best known for completing the Mausoleum at Halicarnassus, the monumental tomb of Mausolus begun during his lifetime. One of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, the tomb gave its name to all subsequent monumental tombs ("mausolea"). Ancient tradition, amplified by later writers, held that Artemisia's grief for Mausolus was so great that she mixed his ashes in her daily drink.

Legacy

Ancient authors praised Artemisia as a capable commander and ruler. The plant genus Artemisia is traditionally said to have been named in her honour. She is sometimes confused with the earlier Artemisia I of Caria, who fought at the Battle of Salamis in 480 BC.
