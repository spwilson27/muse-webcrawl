Mount Katahdin is the highest mountain in the U.S. state of Maine, at 5,270 feet (1,606 m) above sea level. Located in Baxter State Park in Piscataquis County, it is the centrepiece of the park and the northern terminus of the Appalachian Trail. The name Katahdin comes from the Penobscot word Ktaadn, meaning "the greatest mountain," and the peak is sacred to the Penobscot Nation.

Features

The Katahdin massif consists of a cluster of peaks, the highest being Baxter Peak, named for Governor Percival Baxter, who donated the surrounding land to the state. The summit is connected to Pamola Peak by the famous Knife Edge, an arête just a few feet wide in places with sheer drops on both sides. A great glacial cirque, the Great Basin, holds Chimney Pond on the mountain's east side. Katahdin is composed of granite formed from a magma intrusion during the Devonian period.

Recreation

Each year thousands of Appalachian Trail thru-hikers finish their 2,200-mile journey from Georgia by summiting Katahdin, having typically started at Springer Mountain in spring. The mountain's trails — including the Hunt Trail (the AT), the Abol Trail, and the Knife Edge — are rugged and steep, and Baxter State Park strictly limits visitor numbers to protect the fragile alpine environment. In 1846 Henry David Thoreau climbed the mountain and described the experience in The Maine Woods.
