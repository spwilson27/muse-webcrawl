Togo (1913 – December 5, 1929) was the lead sled dog of Leonhard Seppala and his dog sled team in the 1925 serum run to Nome across central and northern Alaska. Although the final leg of the relay, run by Gunnar Kaasen with the dog Balto, received most of the publicity, Togo led Seppala's team over the longest and most dangerous portion of the journey — 260 miles (420 km) through the Norton Sound wilderness — and is regarded by many mushers as the true hero of the serum run.

Early life

Togo was a small Siberian Husky pup, named after the Japanese admiral Tōgō Heihachirō. Sickly and undersized as a puppy, he was at first given away as a pet, but escaped and returned to Seppala's kennel; after repeatedly breaking loose to chase the teams and proving himself by running at the head of a team at eight months old, the reluctant Seppala recognised his ability. Togo became Seppala's prized lead dog, famous for his stamina, intelligence, problem-solving on the trail, and for competitive successes including the All Alaska Sweepstakes.

The 1925 serum run

In January 1925, diphtheria threatened the isolated town of Nome, Alaska, and the only antitoxin serum had to be relayed by dog sled from Nenana across some 674 miles in brutal winter conditions. Seppala's team covered the longest single stretch: 91 miles out from Nome to meet the relay, then 170 miles back with the serum, including a hazardous shortcut across the breaking ice of Norton Sound in a gale, with temperatures around −85 °F (−65 °C) wind chill. By contrast, Kaasen's celebrated final leg was about 55 miles. Togo's leadership across the ice was credited with saving the team and the serum.

Later life and legacy

After the run, Seppala toured the United States with Togo and his team, and Togo was eventually retired to stud at Elizabeth Ricker's kennel at Poland Spring, Maine, where he sired lines influential in the development of the Siberian Husky breed; he died there in 1929. His mounted body is displayed at the Iditarod Trail Sled Dog Race museum in Wasilla, Alaska. Popular recognition of Togo grew over time, aided by the 2019 Disney film Togo, starring Willem Dafoe as Seppala. In 2011 Time magazine named Togo the most heroic animal of all time.
