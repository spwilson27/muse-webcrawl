The chicken (Gallus gallus domesticus) is a domesticated subspecies of the red junglefowl, originally native to Southeast Asia. It is the most common and widespread domestic animal, with a total population of more than 26 billion, and the most numerous bird species in the world. Chickens are primarily kept by humans as a source of food (consuming both their meat and eggs) and, less commonly, as pets. Males are called roosters or cocks, females hens, and immature birds pullets and cockerels; castrated males are capons.

Origins and domestication

Genetic studies show the red junglefowl as the primary ancestor, with contributions from the grey junglefowl and possibly other junglefowl species. Evidence suggests domestication in Southeast Asia — likely in the region of modern Thailand and Vietnam — several thousand years ago, initially perhaps for cockfighting and ceremonial purposes rather than food. Chickens spread westward through trade, reaching the Mediterranean by the early first millennium BC and becoming a global farm animal after the expansion of European agriculture.

Biology and behaviour

Chickens are omnivorous, ground-scratching birds with a strong social hierarchy — the "pecking order". Hens lay clutches of eggs and incubate them for about 21 days. Chickens communicate with a wide range of vocalizations; roosters crow, especially at dawn, and hens cluck to chicks. They possess colour vision, a beak full of sensory nerves, and can recognize individual flock members.

As food

Modern broiler chickens have been selectively bred to reach slaughter weight in about six weeks, while laying hens may produce around 300 eggs per year. Chicken is the second most widely eaten meat in the world after pork, and poultry farming ranges from backyard flocks to highly industrialized operations raising tens of thousands of birds, practices that have drawn welfare scrutiny.

Diseases

Chickens are susceptible to diseases including avian influenza, Marek's disease, Newcastle disease, and coccidiosis. Highly pathogenic avian influenza strains such as H5N1 can spill over into humans, making poultry surveillance a public-health priority.

In culture

The chicken appears in fables ("The Sky Is Falling"), idioms ("chicken feed", "pecking order"), religious practice, and as the subject of the classic riddle "Why did the chicken cross the road?" Cockfighting, once global, is now banned in many countries.
