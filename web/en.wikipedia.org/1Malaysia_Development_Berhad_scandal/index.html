1Malaysia Development Berhad scandal

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The 1Malaysia Development Berhad scandal, often referred to as the 1MDB scandal, is an ongoing corruption and money laundering conspiracy involving the Malaysian state investment fund 1Malaysia Development Berhad (1MDB). The United States Department of Justice alleged that at least US$4.5 billion was misappropriated from the fund between 2009 and 2014.

Background

1MDB was established in 2009 by Prime Minister Najib Razak as a strategic development company wholly owned by the Malaysian Ministry of Finance. Beginning in 2015, reports by the Wall Street Journal and the Sarawak Report alleged that money from the fund had been channelled into Najib's personal bank accounts and to associates including businessman Jho Low.

Investigations and prosecutions

The scandal triggered investigations in at least six countries. In 2016 the US Department of Justice filed civil forfeiture complaints describing the scheme as the largest kleptocracy case in its history; assets allegedly bought with diverted funds included real estate, artwork, a yacht, and the financing of the film The Wolf of Wall Street through Red Granite Pictures.

Najib lost the 2018 Malaysian general election and was subsequently charged in Malaysia. In July 2020 he was convicted in the SRC International case and sentenced to 12 years in prison; he began serving the sentence in August 2022 after exhausting appeals (the sentence was later halved by the Pardons Board in 2024). Former Goldman Sachs banker Roger Ng was convicted in the United States, while Jho Low remains a fugitive. Goldman Sachs agreed in 2020 to pay nearly US$3 billion to settle investigations.
