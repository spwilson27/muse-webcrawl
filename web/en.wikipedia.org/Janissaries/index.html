The Janissaries (Ottoman Turkish: یڭیچری, yeŋiçeri, "new soldier") were an elite military unit of the Ottoman Empire. The Janissary corps was founded under Sultan Murad I in the 14th century (traditionally dated to about 1363). It was originally staffed by Christian youths, levied through the devşirme system from Balkan villages, who were converted to Islam and trained as professional soldiers personally loyal to the sultan.

Recruitment and training

Under the devşirme ("gathering"), Ottoman officials periodically collected Christian boys (typically aged about 8–18) from the Balkans. The most promising entered palace schools (Enderun) and could rise to the highest offices of state, while others became Janissaries after years of rigorous training as acemi oğlan (novices). Janissaries were legally slaves of the sultan (kapıkulu), which paradoxically made the corps a path to power and privilege; they were paid regular salaries and forbidden (originally) to marry or engage in trade.

Role and development

Janissaries were among the first standing armies in Europe and early adopters of firearms — muskets, and support from artillery and grenadiers. They formed the core of Ottoman armies in the conquests of Constantinople (1453), the Mamluks (1517), and Hungary. Their strict discipline and distinctive symbols — the börk hat with its spoon emblem, the great soup cauldrons (kazan) carried on campaign, and the music of the mehter bands — made them famed and feared.

From the late 16th century the corps deteriorated: admission was opened to Muslims and Janissaries' sons, membership became hereditary and commercial, and rolls swelled far beyond effective strength while the soldiers engaged in trades and interfered in politics, deposing and murdering sultans (Osman II in 1622; Ibrahim in 1648; Selim III in 1807).

Abolition

Determined to modernise, Sultan Mahmud II provoked a Janissary revolt in June 1826 and then used loyal artillery to bombard their barracks in Constantinople, killing thousands and abolishing the corps in what official history calls the Auspicious Incident (Vaka-i Hayriye). The Bektashi order associated with them was also suppressed. The corps was replaced by a modern army, the Asakir-i Mansure-i Muhammediye.
