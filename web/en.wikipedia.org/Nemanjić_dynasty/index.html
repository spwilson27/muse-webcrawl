Nemanjić dynasty

The Nemanjić dynasty (Serbian: Немањићи, Nemanjići) was the most important dynasty of medieval Serbia. Founded by Stefan Nemanja in the 12th century, it ruled the Serbian lands from 1166 to 1371, transforming Serbia from a Byzantine frontier principality into a kingdom (1217) and briefly an empire (1346–1371), and establishing the autocephalous Serbian Orthodox Church.

History

Stefan Nemanja, Grand Prince (veliki župan) of Raška, unified the Serbian tribes and principalities and founded a state independent in practice from Byzantium. His youngest son, Rastko — Saint Sava — obtained autocephaly for the Serbian Church in 1219, while his brother Stefan the First-Crowned was crowned king in 1217. Successors consolidated the state: Stefan Radoslav, Vladislav and Uroš I, then Dragutin and Milutin, under whom Serbia expanded into Macedonia and experienced a cultural flowering marked by monasteries such as Gračanica and Studenica.

The dynasty reached its zenith under Stefan Dušan, who crowned himself "Emperor of the Serbs and Greeks" in 1346 and promulgated Dušan's Code (1349/1354), one of the great medieval European law codes. Dušan's empire stretched from the Danube to the Gulf of Corinth. After his death in 1355, his son Stefan Uroš V proved unable to hold the magnates in check; the empire fragmented, and with Uroš's death in 1371 the main line of the dynasty became extinct, two years after Serbian forces were defeated by the Ottomans at the Battle of Maritsa.

Legacy

Many Nemanjić rulers were canonised by the Serbian Orthodox Church, and the dynasty's endowments — Hilandar on Mount Athos, Žiča, Studenica, Sopoćani, Dečani — remain central monuments of Serbian culture and several are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.
