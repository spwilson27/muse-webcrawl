Shaoxing

A canal in Shaoxing, a historic water town in Zhejiang.

Shaoxing (Chinese: 绍兴; pinyin: Shàoxīng) is a prefecture-level city in northeastern Zhejiang province, China, lying south of Hangzhou Bay between Hangzhou to the west and Ningbo to the east. It is one of China's oldest cities, with a history of some 2,500 years, and was a capital of the ancient state of Yue. The urban area is threaded by canals and lakes, and the city is famous throughout China for Shaoxing wine, a variety of huangjiu (fermented rice wine), and for Yue opera.

Shaoxing has an outsized cultural reputation: it was the birthplace or hometown of figures including the calligrapher Wang Xizhi, the writer Lu Xun (born in Shaoxing in 1881), the educator Cai Yuanpei, and premier Zhou Enlai's ancestral home. Under the Song dynasty the city briefly served as a temporary capital (1131–1132) before the court settled in Hangzhou.

Economy

Modern Shaoxing is a major textile centre—its China Textile City in Keqiao District is among the world's largest textile trading hubs—with additional strengths in wine production, chemicals, and equipment manufacturing. The prefecture-level city administers Yuecheng and Keqiao districts, Shangyu district, and the county-level cities of Zhuji, Shengzhou, and Xinchang county, with a population of over five million.
