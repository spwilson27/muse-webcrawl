The Four Horsemen of the Apocalypse are figures described in the sixth chapter of the Book of Revelation, the last book of the New Testament. They ride out as the first four of the Seven Seals are opened by the Lamb, and are traditionally interpreted as representing Conquest (or Pestilence), War, Famine, and Death.

The horsemen

The White Horse — its rider holds a bow, wears a crown, and goes forth "conquering, and to conquer". Interpretations identify him with Conquest, Pestilence, or in some readings Christ or the Antichrist.

The Red Horse — its rider carries a great sword and is given power to take peace from the earth, so that people slay one another: War.

The Black Horse — its rider holds a pair of balances (scales), and a voice announces inflated prices for wheat and barley while sparing oil and wine: Famine and economic scarcity.

The Pale Horse — its rider is named Death, and Hades follows after him; power is given them to kill a fourth of the earth "with sword, and with hunger, and with death, and with the beasts of the earth".

In art and culture

The Horsemen have inspired countless artworks, most famously Albrecht Dürer's 1498 woodcut from his Apocalypse series. They appear in literature, film, music and games, and the phrase "Four Horsemen" is now used metaphorically for any quartet of calamities.
