Peace of Nicias

The Peace of Nicias was a peace treaty signed between Athens and Sparta in 421 BCE, ending the first phase of the Peloponnesian War. It is named after the Athenian statesman and general Nicias, its chief negotiator.

Terms

The treaty, sworn for fifty years, largely restored the status quo before the war: each side was to return conquered territory and prisoners. Athens was to give back Pylos and Cythera; Sparta was to return Amphipolis and other Thracian cities. Corinth, Boeotia, Megara, and Elis — Sparta's allies — refused to sign, undermining the settlement from the start.

Breakdown

Amphipolis was never returned, and fighting soon resumed indirectly through proxies. The peace effectively lasted about six years of uneasy quiet before open war resumed, culminating in Athens's disastrous Sicilian Expedition (415–413 BCE). Thucydides treats the period as a single continuous war despite the treaty.
