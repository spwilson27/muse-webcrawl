Post-nominal letters

Post-nominals follow the name, indicating honours, degrees, and memberships.

Post-nominal letters, also called post-nominal initials, post-nominal titles, designatory letters or simply post-nominals, are letters placed after a person's name to indicate that the individual holds a position, an academic degree, an accreditation, an office, a military decoration, or an honour, or is a member of a religious institute or fraternity. They are widely used in the United Kingdom, Commonwealth countries, and the English-speaking world generally.

Usage and ordering

Where several post-nominals are held, they are listed in a prescribed order of precedence. In the United Kingdom, the conventional order runs from decorations and honours (for example VC, KCB, OBE), through appointments (such as PC, QC/KC, JP), university degrees (BA, MA, PhD), memberships of learned societies and professional bodies (FRS, FRCP, CEng), to parliamentary and military designations (MP, RN). Styles vary by country: in the United States, academic and professional credentials predominate (for example "Jane Smith, PhD, PE"), while state honours are uncommon.

Examples

  • FRS – Fellow of the Royal Society
  • OBE – Officer of the Order of the British Empire
  • MP – Member of Parliament
  • Esq – Esquire (in some traditions placed after the name)
  • SJ – member of the Society of Jesus
  • VC – recipient of the Victoria Cross

Post-nominals contrast with pre-nominal letters and titles, such as "Dr", "Sir", or "Prof", which precede the name.
