Tripoli

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tripoli (Arabic: طرابلس, Ṭarābulus) is the capital and largest city of Libya, with a population of about 1.3 million people in the city proper. It is located in northwestern Libya on the edge of the desert, on a point of rocky land projecting into the Mediterranean Sea. It is the country's political and economic centre and its principal seaport.

History

The city was founded in the 7th century BC by the Phoenicians, who named it Oea. It later passed to the rulers of Cyrenaica, the Romans, the Vandals, the Byzantines, and the Arabs. Tripoli was ruled by Spain, the Knights of St. John, and then the Ottoman Empire from 1551; Admiral Turgut Reis made it a major corsair base. It was the capital of Italian Libya from 1911 to 1943, an Allied occupation followed, and after independence in 1951 it shared capital status with Benghazi until 1963. Under Muammar Gaddafi it was the seat of government; it became a central battleground in the 2011 Libyan civil war, falling to anti-Gaddafi forces in August 2011.

Geography and economy

Tripoli overlooks the Mediterranean near the Tunisian border. Landmarks include the Red Castle (Assaraya al-Hamra), the old medina, and the Arch of Marcus Aurelius, the only Roman arch surviving intact in the city. Its economy rests on government, oil, manufacturing, and trade, and it is served by Mitiga International Airport.
