Louis Leakey

Louis Seymour Bazett Leakey (7 August 1903 – 1 October 1972) was a Kenyan-British paleoanthropologist and archaeologist whose work in East Africa transformed understanding of human evolution. Working principally with his wife, Mary Leakey, he demonstrated that humans originated in Africa, where early hominin fossils and stone tools at Olduvai Gorge in Tanzania pushed back the antiquity of humankind.

Work

Born in Kenya to English missionary parents, Leakey was educated at Cambridge. From the 1930s he led expeditions to Olduvai Gorge and sites around Lake Victoria, finding early stone tools and, in 1948 with Mary, the skull of Proconsul on Rusinga Island. In 1959 Mary discovered the robust australopithecine Zinjanthropus (now Paranthropus boisei), and in the early 1960s their team found Homo habilis, the first member of the genus Homo recognized from such early dates.

Legacy

Leakey mentored and funded the primatologists Jane Goodall, Dian Fossey, and Biruté Galdikas — his "Trimates" — whose long-term field studies of chimpanzees, gorillas, and orangutans reshaped primatology. His son Richard Leakey continued the family's fossil research in the Turkana Basin.
