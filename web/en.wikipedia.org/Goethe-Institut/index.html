Goethe-Institut

The Goethe-Institut (German: Goethe-Institut e. V.) is Germany's cultural institute, operating worldwide to promote the study of the German language abroad, encourage international cultural exchange, and convey a contemporary image of Germany. It is named after the German poet and statesman Johann Wolfgang von Goethe.

History and structure

Founded in 1951 as the successor to the Deutsche Akademie, the institute is a non-profit association headquartered in Munich, with a capital office in Berlin. It is principally financed by the German Federal Foreign Office and the Federal Government Commissioner for Culture and the Media. The Goethe-Institut maintains around 150 institutes and liaison offices in roughly 100 countries.

Activities

The institute offers German language courses and examinations aligned with the Common European Framework of Reference for Languages, trains teachers of German, and runs libraries and information services. Its cultural programmes include film festivals, exhibitions, concerts, translation funding (notably the translation of German books into other languages), and residency exchanges for artists and intellectuals.
