IndyCar, LLC (stylised as INDYCAR) is the American sanctioning body for open-wheel, single-seater auto racing, best known for organising the IndyCar Series and its crown-jewel event, the Indianapolis 500. Founded by Indianapolis Motor Speedway owner Tony George in 1994 as the Indy Racing League, it began competition in 1996 and later reunified with the rival Champ Car series in 2008.

The cars

All teams use a common Dallara chassis (currently the DW12, introduced in 2012 and later fitted with the aeroscreen cockpit-protection device) powered by 2.2-litre twin-turbocharged V6 engines supplied by Honda and Chevrolet, producing roughly 550–750 horsepower depending on the circuit. Racing takes place on a mixture of superspeedways, short ovals, permanent road courses, and temporary street circuits, a versatility unique among major series.

Indianapolis 500

The Indianapolis 500, held each Memorial Day weekend at the Indianapolis Motor Speedway since 1911, is one of the largest single-day sporting events in the world and forms part of the informal Triple Crown of Motorsport alongside the Monaco Grand Prix and the 24 Hours of Le Mans. Four-time winners include A. J. Foyt, Al Unser, Rick Mears, and Hélio Castroneves.

History

The sport's roots lie in AAA and USAC championship racing. A damaging split in 1996 between the IRL and CART/Champ Car divided American open-wheel racing for over a decade before reunification in 2008. In 2020, Roger Penske's Penske Entertainment acquired IndyCar and the Indianapolis Motor Speedway.
