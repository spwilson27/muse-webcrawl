Bolan Pass

The Bolan Pass (Urdu: درۂ بولان) is a valley and mountain pass through the Toba Kakar range in Balochistan, Pakistan, some 120 kilometres (75 mi) south of the city of Quetta and near the border with Afghanistan. With the more famous Khyber Pass to the north, it is one of the two principal routes from Central Asia into the Indian subcontinent.

Geography

The pass follows the Bolan River valley from the plains near Sibi up through a winding gorge to the high plateau at Quetta, rising from a few hundred metres above sea level to about 1,790 metres (5,870 ft) at its summit. The route is about 89 kilometres long.

History

The Bolan Pass has been used by traders, migrants and armies for millennia; nearby Mehrgarh, one of the earliest farming settlements in South Asia (occupied from around 7000 BC), lies near its southern approach. Traders from Central Asia brought goods — and, over centuries, elements of language and culture — into the Indus valley through the pass.

During the First Anglo-Afghan War, the British Army of the Indus marched through the Bolan Pass in 1839 on its way to Kandahar and Kabul. In the later 19th century the pass became central to British frontier strategy against a feared Russian advance, and the "Sind–Pishin" railway was driven through the gorge, opening in stages in the 1880s. The line, with its numerous tunnels and bridges, remains an engineering landmark and is still in use by Pakistan Railways.

Today

The pass carries the N-65 national highway and the Quetta–Sibi railway (part of the Rohri–Chaman line). It remains the main road and rail link between Quetta and the Indus plains, though the route is vulnerable to flash floods that occasionally sweep away bridges in the gorge.
