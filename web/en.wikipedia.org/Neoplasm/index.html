Neoplasm

Neoplasm
Synonym: Tumor (tumour)
Field: Oncology, pathology
Types: Benign, premalignant, malignant (cancer)

A neoplasm is an abnormal growth of tissue that results when cells divide more than they should or do not die when they should. Neoplasms may be benign (noncancerous), premalignant, or malignant (cancerous); a neoplasm is commonly called a tumor. The defining feature of a neoplasm is uncoordinated growth that persists after the stimulus that provoked it has ceased, arising because the constituent cells have acquired heritable genetic or epigenetic alterations affecting growth control.

Classification

Neoplasms are classified by their tissue of origin and behavior. Benign tumors remain localized and usually grow slowly (e.g., lipomas, uterine fibroids/leiomyomas, common moles/nevi). Malignant neoplasms invade surrounding tissue and may metastasize to distant sites; major categories include carcinomas (epithelial), sarcomas (connective tissue), leukemias and lymphomas (blood-forming tissues), germ cell tumors, and blastomas. Premalignant lesions, such as carcinoma in situ or some adenomatous polyps, show neoplastic features with the potential to progress.

Causes and biology

Neoplasia results from accumulated mutations in oncogenes, tumor suppressor genes (such as TP53 and RB1), and genes maintaining genome stability, driven by carcinogens (tobacco smoke, ultraviolet and ionizing radiation, asbestos), certain infections (HPV, hepatitis B and C, Helicobacter pylori), chronic inflammation, and inherited syndromes. Clonal evolution within the tumor generates heterogeneity.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on imaging and, definitively, histopathological examination of biopsy or excision specimens, increasingly supplemented by molecular testing. Treatment depends on type and stage and includes surgical excision, radiation therapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, hormonal therapy, and immunotherapy. Benign neoplasms may need removal only if they cause symptoms by compression or cosmetic concerns.
