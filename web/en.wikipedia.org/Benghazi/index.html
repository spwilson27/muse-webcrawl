Benghazi

Benghazi is the second-most-populous city in Libya and the largest city in the country's eastern Cyrenaica region, with a major port on the Mediterranean Sea.

History

Founded by Greeks around 525 BC as Euesperides and later refounded as Berenice, the city passed through Roman, Arab, Ottoman and Italian rule. It was heavily fought over in the Second World War, changing hands several times between Axis and Allied forces.

Modern era

Benghazi was the cradle of the 2011 Libyan uprising against Muammar Gaddafi. In September 2012 an attack on the US diplomatic compound killed Ambassador J. Christopher Stevens and three other Americans. The city was later contested during Libya's second civil war.

Geography and economy

The city lies on the Gulf of Sidra, benefits from trade and oil-related commerce, and is home to the University of Benghazi, founded in 1955 as Libya's first university.
