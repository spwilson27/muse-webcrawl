Dussehra

Dussehra, also called Vijayadashami, is a major Hindu festival celebrated at the end of Navaratri each year, on the tenth day of the Hindu month of Ashvin (usually September or October). It marks the victory of good over evil.

Significance

In northern and western India, Dussehra commemorates the god Rama's victory over the demon king Ravana, as narrated in the Ramayana. In eastern India, the festival — as Vijayadashami — marks the goddess Durga's defeat of the buffalo demon Mahishasura and the conclusion of Durga Puja.

Celebrations

Festivities commonly include Ramlila performances re-enacting Rama's life, processions, and the burning of large effigies of Ravana, his brother Kumbhakarna and son Meghnada, often filled with fireworks. In Mysore, Karnataka, the festival is famous for a grand royal procession led by decorated elephants.

Regional forms

The festival is observed across India, Nepal and Bangladesh under various names and customs, and is a public holiday in India.
