Mifflin-St Jeor equation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation is a formula used to estimate a person's basal metabolic rate (BMR), the amount of energy expended while at rest in a neutrally temperate environment. Introduced by M. D. Mifflin and colleagues in 1990, it is widely regarded as one of the most accurate formulas for estimating resting metabolic rate in healthy adults, and it has largely superseded the older Harris-Benedict equation in clinical and dietary practice.

Formula

The equation takes different forms for men and women:

For men: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age in years) + 5

For women: BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age in years) − 161

Once BMR is estimated, total daily energy expenditure is commonly obtained by multiplying by an activity factor ranging from about 1.2 for sedentary individuals to 1.9 for very active ones.

Development and validation

The equation was derived from a study of 498 healthy subjects (247 men and 251 women) aged 19 to 78 years, including normal-weight, overweight, and obese individuals. In a 2005 review published by the Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics, the Mifflin-St Jeor equation was found to predict resting metabolic rate within 10% of measured values in a larger proportion of non-obese and obese individuals than other commonly used equations, leading to its recommendation for clinical use.
