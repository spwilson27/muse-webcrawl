Frame bundle

A frame at a point of a manifold is an ordered basis of its tangent space.

In mathematics, a frame bundle is a principal fiber bundle F(E) associated to a vector bundle E. The fiber over a point x is the set of all ordered bases, or frames, of the vector space Ex. The general linear group acts on F(E) by change of basis, making it a principal GL-bundle; when E is the tangent bundle of a smooth manifold, F(E) is the bundle of linear frames of that manifold.

Definition

Given a vector bundle E → X of rank n, a frame at x is a linear isomorphism from Rn to the fiber Ex, equivalently an ordered basis (e1, …, en) of Ex. The frame bundle is the space of all frames at all points, topologised so that it is itself a smooth bundle over X whenever E is smooth. Its great utility is that objects which depend on a choice of basis — such as components of tensors — can be regarded as basis-independent functions or forms on the frame bundle.

Reductions and the orthonormal frame bundle

If the bundle carries extra structure, the frame bundle can be correspondingly reduced. For a Riemannian manifold, one considers only orthonormal frames, obtaining the orthonormal frame bundle, a principal bundle for the orthogonal group O(n); on an oriented manifold the structure reduces further to SO(n). Connections, torsion and curvature of the manifold can all be expressed naturally as forms on the frame bundle, and the theory of G-structures is built on such reductions.

Solder form

The frame bundle possesses a canonical Rn-valued 1-form, the solder form (or tautological form), which identifies each tangent space of the manifold with Rn using the frame itself. Together with a connection form it yields the structure equations of Cartan, in which torsion and curvature appear as the basic invariants of the geometry.
