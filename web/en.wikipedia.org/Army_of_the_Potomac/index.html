The Army of the Potomac was the principal Union Army in the Eastern Theater of the American Civil War. It was created in July 1861 shortly after the First Battle of Bull Run and was disbanded in June 1865 following the surrender of the Confederate Army of Northern Virginia in April.

History

The army was organized by General George B. McClellan, who built it into a large, well-equipped force but was criticized for reluctance to engage. After the Peninsula Campaign and the Battle of Antietam, McClellan was replaced by Ambrose Burnside, whose tenure ended in disaster at Fredericksburg. Joseph Hooker succeeded him and was defeated at Chancellorsville.

Meade and Grant

George G. Meade took command days before the Battle of Gettysburg in July 1863, where the army won its most celebrated victory. From 1864, Ulysses S. Grant, as general-in-chief, accompanied the army and directed its strategy through the Overland Campaign and the Siege of Petersburg, culminating in Lee's surrender at Appomattox Court House on April 9, 1865.

Organization

At its peak the army numbered over 120,000 men organized into infantry corps, a cavalry corps, and artillery reserve. Its corps included the I, II, III, V, VI, IX, and XI Corps at various times.
