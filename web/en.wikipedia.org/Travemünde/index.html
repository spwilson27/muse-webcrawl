Travemünde

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Travemünde is a borough of the city of Lübeck, Germany, located at the mouth of the river Trave on Lübeck Bay, an arm of the Baltic Sea. Founded in the 12th century — the town was established by Henry the Lion in 1187 — it has been Lübeck's seaport since the Middle Ages. The borough is a traditional seaside resort as well as one of Germany's largest Baltic ferry ports.

History

The fortress and settlement guarding the Trave estuary were contested between Lübeck and Denmark; Lübeck secured full control in the 14th century. From 1806 to 1813 the town was occupied by France. Its development as a bathing resort began in 1802, and in the 19th century it became a fashionable spa with a casino. Thomas Mann, a native of Lübeck, set scenes of Buddenbrooks in Travemünde.

Transport and sights

Ferries link Travemünde with ports in Sweden, Finland and Latvia. Landmarks include the old lighthouse (built 1539, among Germany's oldest), the four-masted barque Passat (1911), now a museum ship, and the Travemünder Woche sailing regatta held each July. The neighbouring Priwall peninsula, reached by ferry, was divided by the inner German border until 1990.
