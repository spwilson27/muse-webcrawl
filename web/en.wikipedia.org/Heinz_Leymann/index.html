Heinz Leymann (17 July 1932 – 1999) was a Swedish psychologist of German origin, known as the pioneering researcher of mobbing — systematic psychological harassment and bullying in the workplace. Leymann worked at the Swedish National Institute of Occupational Health and at Umeå University, where he studied the causes, dynamics and health consequences of long-term hostile behaviour at work.

Research on mobbing

Borrowing the term "mobbing" from animal behaviour research, Leymann defined it as hostile and unethical communication directed systematically by one or more individuals toward another person, who is pushed into a helpless position and subjected to hostile acts at least once a week over a period of at least six months. He identified 45 characteristic mobbing behaviours and described the typical phases of escalation, from initial conflict through stigmatisation to expulsion from working life.

Leymann's clinical work with victims documented severe consequences including post-traumatic stress, depression and suicide; he estimated that a significant share of Swedish suicides had workplace conflicts in their background. He founded a clinic for mobbing victims and his research spurred public debate and legislation on victimisation at work in Sweden and influenced anti-bullying research internationally. He also developed the Leymann Inventory of Psychological Terror (LIPT), a questionnaire used to measure exposure to mobbing.
