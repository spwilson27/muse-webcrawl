Microinsurance

SectorInsurance, financial inclusion
ClientsLow-income households
Risks coveredHealth, life, crop, livestock, property

Microinsurance is the protection of low-income people against specific perils in exchange for regular premium payments proportionate to the likelihood and cost of the risk involved. It is part of the wider microfinance movement and targets people who are ignored by mainstream commercial and social insurance schemes.

Design and delivery

Products must be simple to understand, affordable and easy to enrol in, since clients often have low literacy and irregular incomes. Common products include credit-life insurance bundled with microloans, health cover, funeral insurance and index-based weather insurance for farmers, in which payouts are triggered by measured rainfall or satellite data rather than individual loss assessment. Distribution channels include microfinance institutions, cooperatives, retailers and mobile-money platforms, which allow premiums to be collected in very small amounts.

Challenges

Providers face high transaction costs relative to tiny premiums, scarce actuarial data and difficulties building trust among people unfamiliar with insurance. Regulation tailored to low-value products, consumer education and technology-based claims handling are widely seen as keys to scaling coverage.
