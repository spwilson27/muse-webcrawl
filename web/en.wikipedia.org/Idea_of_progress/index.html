Idea of progress

The idea of progress is the notion that advances in science, technology, economic development, and social organisation are vital to improving the human condition, and that civilisation has advanced, is advancing, and will continue to advance in desirable ways.

History

Although hints of the idea appear in antiquity, it took its modern form during the Enlightenment, in thinkers such as Condorcet, who sketched a historical picture of the progress of the human mind, and Turgot. In the 19th century the idea was developed by figures including Hegel, Marx, Comte, and Spencer, and became bound up with industrialisation and political reform movements.

Twentieth century and after

The world wars, totalitarianism, and environmental concerns led many to question the idea, and postmodern thinkers treated it with suspicion. Others continue to defend measured versions of it, pointing to gains in health, longevity, and material welfare.
