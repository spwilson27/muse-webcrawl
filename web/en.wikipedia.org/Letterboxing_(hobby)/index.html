Letterboxing (hobby)

Letterboxing is an outdoor hobby combining orienteering, art and puzzle solving. Participants hide small, weatherproof boxes in publicly accessible places (called letterboxes) and distribute clues to their locations; finders record the visit by stamping the box's logbook with their personal stamp and stamping their own logbook with the box's hand-carved stamp.

History

The pastime began on Dartmoor in Devon, England, in 1854, when a guide named James Perrott left a bottle for visitors' cards at Cranmere Pool. Leaving self-addressed postcards in the boxes for the next finder to post became customary, hence the name. By the late 20th century there were thousands of letterboxes on Dartmoor, catalogued in a regularly published clue book.

Modern practice

Letterboxing spread to North America in the 1990s, aided by a 1998 Smithsonian article and websites such as Atlas Quest, where clues are published online. American letterboxers often carve elaborate rubber stamps. The hobby is a precursor and cousin of geocaching, which substitutes GPS coordinates for written clues.
