Kazakh (also spelled Qazaq) is a Turkic language of the Kipchak branch spoken in Central Asia by the Kazakhs. It is the official language of Kazakhstan and a significant minority language in the Ili Kazakh Autonomous Prefecture in Xinjiang, China, and in parts of Mongolia and Russia.

Writing system

Kazakh has historically been written in several scripts. An Arabic-derived script was used until the early 20th century and is still used by Kazakhs in China. A Latin alphabet was introduced in the Soviet Union in 1929, replaced by a Cyrillic alphabet in 1940, which remains in use in Kazakhstan. The Kazakh government has announced a transition to a Latin-based alphabet, to be completed during the 2020s–early 2030s.

Features

Like other Turkic languages, Kazakh is agglutinative, with vowel harmony, no grammatical gender, and subject–object–verb word order. It has nine vowel phonemes and a rich system of suffixes expressing tense, mood, person and case (seven cases including nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, locative, ablative and instrumental).
