Kuching (Jawi: کوچيڠ‎), officially the City of Kuching, is the capital and most populous city of the Malaysian state of Sarawak on the island of Borneo. It stands on the banks of the Sarawak River near the coast of the South China Sea. Kuching is the largest city in Malaysian Borneo and the fourth-largest in Malaysia; its name is popularly associated with the Malay word kucing ("cat"), and the city embraces the link with cat statues, a Cat Museum and the nickname "Cat City".

History

Sarawak was part of the Sultanate of Brunei before the English adventurer James Brooke helped suppress a rebellion and was made Rajah of Sarawak in 1841. Kuching became the capital of the Brooke family's "White Rajah" kingdom, which ruled Sarawak for a century. The town's landmarks from that era include the Astana (the Rajah's palace, 1870), Fort Margherita (1879) and the Sarawak Museum (1891).

Kuching was occupied by Japan from December 1941 to 1945 — the notorious Batu Lintang POW camp lay just outside the town — after which Sarawak was ceded to Britain as a Crown Colony in 1946. Sarawak joined the Federation of Malaysia in 1963, with Kuching as its capital. The city was elevated to city status in 1988, uniquely administered by two local authorities: Kuching North (DBKU) and Kuching South (MBKS).

Geography and culture

Kuching has an equatorial rainforest climate with heavy rainfall year-round. Its population is a mix of Malay, Chinese, Iban, Bidayuh, Orang Ulu and other communities. Attractions include the Kuching Waterfront promenade, the Sarawak State Museum and the Borneo Cultures Museum (opened 2022, one of Southeast Asia's largest museums), the Main Bazaar shophouses, and the golden-domed New Sarawak State Legislative Assembly Building across the river, reached by the Darul Hana pedestrian bridge. Nearby are Bako and Kubah national parks, famous for proboscis monkeys and pitcher plants, and the Sarawak Cultural Village, which hosts the annual Rainforest World Music Festival at nearby Damai.

Cuisine

Local specialities include Sarawak laksa (a spiced coconut-broth noodle soup), kolo mee (springy noodles tossed in seasoned oil), and layer cakes (kek lapis Sarawak). Anthony Bourdain famously praised Sarawak laksa as a "breakfast of the gods".
