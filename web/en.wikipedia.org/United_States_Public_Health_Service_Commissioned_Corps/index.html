United States Public Health Service Commissioned Corps

The United States Public Health Service Commissioned Corps (USPHSCC) is the federal uniformed service of the U.S. Public Health Service and one of the nation's eight uniformed services. Its roughly 6,000 officers — physicians, nurses, engineers, scientists, pharmacists and other health professionals — serve throughout the federal government in public health roles.

Founded1889 (organized corps)
HeadSurgeon General of the United States
SizeAbout 6,000 officers
ParentU.S. Public Health Service, Department of Health and Human Services

Role

Commissioned Corps officers are assigned to agencies such as the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, the Food and Drug Administration, the National Institutes of Health, the Indian Health Service and the Bureau of Prisons, and they deploy to public health emergencies including epidemics, hurricanes and other disasters.

Status

Though a non-military uniformed service, the Corps uses naval-derived ranks and can be militarized by the President in time of war or emergency. Its officers responded prominently to crises including the Ebola outbreak in West Africa and the COVID-19 pandemic.

History

The Corps grew out of the Marine Hospital Service founded in 1798; a commissioned officer corps was organized in 1889 under Surgeon General John Maynard Woodworth, modeling the service on military lines.
