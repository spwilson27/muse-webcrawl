Hygge

Hygge (pronounced [ˈhykə] in Danish) is a word in Danish and Norwegian that describes a cosy, contented mood evoked by comfort and conviviality. As a cultural category with its sets of associated practices, hygge has more or less the same meaning in both places and in both languages; however, the emphasis on hygge as a core part of Danish culture is a recent phenomenon, dating to the late 20th century. In the 21st century, the concept expanded internationally as a lifestyle trend.

Etymology

The word hygge comes from a Danish word meaning "to give courage, comfort, joy". It derives from the Old Norse word hyggja, meaning "to think", and is related to the English word "hug".

Cultural practices

Hygge is associated with candlelight (Danes burn more candles per capita than any other Europeans), comfortable interiors, warm drinks, simple home-cooked food, and relaxed gatherings with friends and family. It is often invoked in winter, when Denmark's long dark evenings make indoor warmth a necessity. Activities described as hyggelig include board games, shared meals, reading by firelight and time spent in nature wearing warm clothing.

International popularity

From around 2016, a wave of books and articles introduced hygge to English-speaking audiences, and "hygge" was shortlisted as a word of the year by major dictionaries. Commentators linked the concept to Denmark's consistently high rankings in world happiness surveys, while critics noted its commercialization and its partly manufactured character as a national brand.
