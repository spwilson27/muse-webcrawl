Moika Palace

The Moika Palace, also known as the Yusupov Palace, is a former residence of the House of Yusupov in Saint Petersburg, Russia, located at 94 Moika River Embankment. The palace is notable as the site of the murder of Grigori Rasputin in December 1916. It now serves as a museum and cultural centre.

History

The palace was first built around 1770 to a design by the French architect Jean-Baptiste Vallin de la Mothe for the Shuvalov family. It passed through several owners before being purchased in 1830 by Prince Nikolai Borisovich Yusupov, who enlarged and richly redecorated it. Over the following decades the palace acquired lavish interiors including a private theatre, a Moorish drawing room, and extensive art collections assembled by one of Imperial Russia's wealthiest families.

Murder of Rasputin

In the early hours of 30 December 1916 (17 December Old Style), Prince Felix Yusupov and his co-conspirators, including Grand Duke Dmitri Pavlovich and the politician Vladimir Purishkevich, lured the mystic Grigori Rasputin to the palace. According to Yusupov's memoirs, Rasputin survived poisoned food and drink and was shot several times before his body was thrown from a bridge into the Malaya Nevka River. A waxwork exhibit in the palace basement now recreates the scene, and the episode remains one of the most famous events of the final months of the Russian Empire.

The palace today

After the Russian Revolution the palace was nationalised. It later served as a teachers' cultural centre, which helped preserve its interiors largely intact. Today the Yusupov Palace operates as a museum, open to visitors, and its Rococo Revival theatre hosts concerts and performances.
