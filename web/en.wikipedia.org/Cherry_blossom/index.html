Cherry blossom

A cherry blossom, also known as Japanese cherry, or sakura, is a flower of many trees of genus Prunus or Prunus subg. Cerasus. Wild species of the cherry tree are widely distributed, mainly in the Northern Hemisphere. They are common in Japan, Korea, and China.

Cherry blossom (sakura)
Genus:Prunus (subg. Cerasus)
Typical cultivar:'Somei-yoshino' (Prunus × yedoensis)
Blooming season:Spring (March–May in Japan)

In Japan

In Japan, cherry blossoms symbolize both the beauty and transience of life, and the custom of hanami — picnicking and gathering under flowering trees — dates back more than a thousand years. The Japan Meteorological Agency issues annual blossom forecasts, and the "sakura front" moving north across the archipelago is a major seasonal news event. The Somei Yoshino cultivar accounts for the great majority of planted trees.

Around the world

Japan donated cherry trees to Washington, D.C., in 1912; the trees around the Tidal Basin are the focus of the National Cherry Blossom Festival each spring. Cherry blossom festivals are also held in Vancouver, Paris, Stockholm, and many other cities. In South Korea, blossom viewing centres on places such as Jinhae, and in China on Wuhan University and Yuyuantan Park in Beijing.

Culinary uses

Salted cherry blossoms and leaves are used in Japanese cuisine: blossoms flavour sakuramochi, sakura tea, and seasonal sweets, while pickled leaves wrap rice cakes. Not all species produce edible fruit, and ornamental trees are grown purely for their flowers.
