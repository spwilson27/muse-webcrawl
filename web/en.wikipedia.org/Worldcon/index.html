Worldcon

The World Science Fiction Convention, commonly called Worldcon, is an annual convention of the World Science Fiction Society (WSFS). Held in a different city each year since 1939 (except during 1942–1945), it is the longest-running science fiction convention and is best known as the venue at which the Hugo Awards are presented.

History

The first Worldcon was held in New York City in 1939 in conjunction with the World's Fair. The convention has since been hosted in cities across North America and, increasingly, around the world, including London, Melbourne, Yokohama, Helsinki, Dublin, and Chengdu. Site selection is decided by a vote of WSFS members two years in advance.

Activities

Programming includes panel discussions, author readings and signings, an art show, a dealers' room, the Masquerade costume competition, and the WSFS business meeting, at which the society's constitution and the rules of the Hugo Awards are debated and amended.

Hugo Awards

The Hugo Awards, presented annually since 1953 (with the first awarded in 1953 and annually from 1955), honour the best science fiction and fantasy works and achievements of the previous year, as voted by members of the convention.
