Gawhar Shad

Gawhar Shad (Persian: گوهرشاد, "joyful jewel"; died 19 July 1457) was the chief consort of Shah Rukh, ruler of the Timurid Empire, and one of the most powerful and influential women of the medieval Islamic world. A generous patron of architecture and culture, she played a central political role at the Timurid court in Herat for half a century.

Life

Gawhar Shad was the daughter of Ghiyasuddin Tarkhan, a noble of Turkic origin. She married Shah Rukh, the youngest son of Timur, and became the dominant figure among his wives. After Shah Rukh made Herat his capital in 1409, she shaped the court's cultural and political life and was instrumental in the careers of her sons Ulugh Beg, governor of Samarkand, and Baysunghur, a noted patron of Persian art.

Patronage

Her commissions rank among the masterpieces of Timurid architecture. They include the Gawhar Shad Mosque (1418) within the Imam Reza shrine complex in Mashhad, and the great Musalla complex in Herat (begun 1417), comprising a mosque, madrasa and her own mausoleum, whose ribbed dome and minarets still stand in part today. She also founded madrasas and mosques in other cities of Khorasan.

Death

After Shah Rukh's death in 1447, Gawhar Shad continued to intervene in the succession struggles of her grandsons. In 1457, at an advanced age, she was executed on the orders of the Timurid prince Abu Sa'id Mirza during his campaign to take Herat. She was buried in her mausoleum in Herat beside her son Baysunghur.
