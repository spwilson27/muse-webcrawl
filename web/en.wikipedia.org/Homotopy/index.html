Homotopy

In topology, a branch of mathematics, two continuous functions from one topological space to another are called homotopic if one can be "continuously deformed" into the other; such a deformation is called a homotopy between the two functions. Formally, a homotopy between functions f and g from a space X to a space Y is a continuous map H : X × [0, 1] → Y such that H(x, 0) = f(x) and H(x, 1) = g(x) for all x.

Properties

Homotopy is an equivalence relation on the set of continuous maps between two spaces. Spaces that can be continuously deformed into each other are called homotopy equivalent; they share many invariants, including their homotopy groups and (co)homology groups. A space that can be deformed to a point is called contractible. Homotopy theory studies spaces and maps up to homotopy equivalence and underlies the fundamental group and higher homotopy groups, which classify the obstructions to deforming maps and spaces.

Examples

On a punctured plane, a loop around the hole is not homotopic to a constant loop, while in the unpunctured plane every loop is. Two embeddings of a cup and a doughnut are the classic "topologist's" example of a homotopy equivalence between surfaces with one hole.
