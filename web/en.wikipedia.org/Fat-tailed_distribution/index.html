Fat-tailed distribution

A fat-tailed distribution (or heavy-tailed distribution) is a probability distribution whose tail decays more slowly than an exponential, so extreme values occur more often than under a normal distribution. Many follow a power law.

Examples include the Pareto distribution, the Cauchy distribution, and Student's t-distribution. Fat tails matter in finance, insurance, and risk management, where they imply that extreme events — market crashes, catastrophic claims — are far more likely than Gaussian models predict. The concept is popularised in Nassim Nicholas Taleb's work on black swan events.
