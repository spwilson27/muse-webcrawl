Executive producer

IndustryFilm, television, music, video games, theatre
Typical roleFinancing, senior oversight, rights holder
Reports toStudio / investors (varies)

An executive producer (EP) is a senior producer role in the entertainment industry whose exact meaning varies by medium and by production. In film, the executive producer is typically the person or company that supplied or arranged the financing, owns the underlying rights, or initiated the project, and who oversees the production at a strategic rather than day-to-day level. In television, by contrast, the executive producer is usually the showrunner — the senior writer-producer who runs the series creatively and managerially.

Film

On a feature film, executive producers are often financiers, studio executives, or the holders of the source material's rights; a star or director who helped attach financing or package the project may also receive the credit. The producer (without the qualifier) is normally responsible for the production's daily management, hiring key personnel, and delivering the film on schedule and budget. Because the EP credit is not tightly defined by guild rules in film, it is sometimes granted for contributions ranging from supplying the entire budget to largely honorary involvement.

Television

In television the credit carries a more operational meaning. The executive producer is typically the highest-ranking producer on the show, often its creator and showrunner, in charge of the writers' room, casting, budgets, and final creative decisions; series may have several executive producers, including senior writers, the producing director, and representatives of the studio and network. Writers ascend to producer titles — co-producer, producer, supervising producer, co-executive producer — as a seniority ladder, with executive producer at the top.

Other media

In music, an executive producer oversees the making of an album at a high level, sometimes financing it or shaping its overall direction, while the record producer runs the recording sessions. In video games, executive producers manage development teams and publisher relationships. In all cases, the title signals seniority and ultimate responsibility for the business side of a production, though the creative involvement of the holder varies widely.
