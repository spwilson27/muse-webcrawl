Spa, Belgium

Spa (Walloon: Spå) is a city and municipality in the province of Liège, Belgium, whose name has become the generic word for a health resort with mineral springs. Located in a valley in the Ardennes, the town has been famed since the Roman era for its healing iron-bearing waters, called pouhons, and reached its height of fashion in the eighteenth century, when it was nicknamed the "Café of Europe".

History

The springs were known to the Romans and were used by the Tonagri people; in the Middle Ages the town grew around its ironworks and springs. From the sixteenth century onwards visitors came from across Europe to "take the waters". Spa became a fashionable meeting place for royalty and aristocracy — Peter the Great visited in 1717 — and in 1763 it opened what is often described as the world's first modern casino, La Redoute. In 2021 Spa was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as part of the Great Spa Towns of Europe.

Attractions

Visitors can taste the waters at springs such as the Pouhon Pierre-le-Grand in the town centre. The town is closely associated with motor racing: the nearby Circuit de Spa-Francorchamps, one of the world's most famous racing circuits, hosts the Belgian Grand Prix of Formula One. The surrounding Ardennes forests and the Haute Fagnes plateau offer extensive walking.

The word "spa"

Because of the town's fame, the word spa entered English and other languages as a generic term for a mineral spring, a resort with such springs, and later any place offering health and beauty treatments. Folk etymologies deriving the word from Latin phrases are not supported; it comes directly from the town's name, itself from a Walloon word for a spring or fountain.
