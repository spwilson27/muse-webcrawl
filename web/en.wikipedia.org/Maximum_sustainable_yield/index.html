Maximum sustainable yield

In population ecology and economics, maximum sustainable yield (MSY) is theoretically the largest yield (or catch) that can be taken from a species' stock over an indefinite period. It is a cornerstone concept in fisheries science and management.

Model

Under logistic growth, population growth is fastest at half the carrying capacity K. Harvesting the stock at that level in principle removes exactly the surplus growth, giving the maximum yield sustainable indefinitely: MSY = rK/4, attained at stock size K/2.

Criticism

MSY has been criticised because real populations fluctuate, age structure and multi-species interactions matter, and estimates are uncertain; managing exactly at MSY risks collapse. Modern practice often uses precautionary reference points below the theoretical maximum.
