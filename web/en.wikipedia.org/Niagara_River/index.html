The Niagara River is a river that flows north from Lake Erie to Lake Ontario, forming part of the border between the Canadian province of Ontario and the U.S. state of New York. It is about 58 kilometres (36 mi) long and includes the famous Niagara Falls. The river drains the upper Great Lakes into Lake Ontario and is a key part of the Great Lakes–St. Lawrence waterway.

The name is generally derived from an Iroquoian word, rendered as "Onguiaahra" or similar, recorded by early French explorers, and is often interpreted as "the strait" or "thundering waters".

Course

The river leaves Lake Erie near Buffalo, New York, and Fort Erie, Ontario, flows around Grand Island, and splits around Goat Island at the brink of the Falls, where roughly half its flow is diverted (under treaty) for hydroelectric generation by the American and Canadian power stations. Below the falls the river rushes through the Niagara Gorge, including the Whirlpool Rapids and the Niagara Whirlpool, before broadening past Lewiston and Queenston and emptying into Lake Ontario at Niagara-on-the-Lake.

History and use

The river was a strategic corridor in the French and Indian War and the War of 1812, with battles at Fort Erie, Chippawa, and Lundy's Lane. The Welland Canal allows shipping to bypass the falls. Bridges crossing the river include the Peace Bridge, the Rainbow Bridge, and the Lewiston–Queenston Bridge. Because the river concentrates the outflow of four Great Lakes, it has also been an important site for pollution monitoring and remediation under the Niagara River Toxics Management Plan.
