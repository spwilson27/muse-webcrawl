The Border Collie is a breed of herding dog developed in the Anglo-Scottish border region for working livestock, especially sheep. Renowned for its intelligence, energy, and obedience, it is widely regarded as the most intelligent dog breed and the world's premier sheepdog, dominating competitive sheepdog trials and excelling in dog sports such as agility and flyball.

History

The breed descends from the landrace collies of the British Isles, refined by shepherds in Northumberland and the Scottish borders. Most modern Border Collies trace to a single dog, Old Hemp (born 1893), whose quiet, intense working style became the breed standard. The International Sheep Dog Society was founded in 1906 and maintains the working stud book; the breed was recognised by the American Kennel Club only in 1995, over the objections of working-dog enthusiasts who feared breeding for appearance over ability.

Characteristics

Border Collies work with a distinctive crouching posture and an intense fixed stare known as "the eye", controlling sheep by presence rather than barking or nipping. They are medium-sized, athletic dogs with a strong drive to work; without sufficient mental and physical stimulation they may develop destructive or obsessive behaviours, including herding children and vehicles. Coat colours include black and white, red and white, tricolour, and blue merle.

Intelligence and research

The breed ranks first in Stanley Coren's well-known study of canine intelligence. Individual Border Collies have demonstrated remarkable vocabulary learning: the dog Chaser learned the names of over 1,000 objects and could retrieve them by name, and Rico showed fast mapping of new words. The breed has some inherited health concerns, including collie eye anomaly, hip dysplasia, and epilepsy.
