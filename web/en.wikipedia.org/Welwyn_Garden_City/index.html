Welwyn Garden City is a town in Hertfordshire, England, about 20 miles (32 km) north of London. It was the second garden city in England (after Letchworth) and one of the first new towns. It is unique in being both a garden city and a designated new town, and it is notable for its neo-Georgian town centre and its planned layout of tree-lined boulevards and neighbourhood units.

History

The town was founded in 1920 by Sir Ebenezer Howard, the originator of the garden city movement, following the success of Letchworth Garden City. The French planner Louis de Soissons designed the town in a neo-Georgian style around a central vista, Parkway. Industry was deliberately attracted — the Shredded Wheat factory (1926) became a landmark — and the town grew steadily. In 1948 Welwyn Garden City was designated a new town under the New Towns Act 1946.

Geography and character

The town lies on either side of the Mimram valley; the west side contains most housing, while the town centre and much employment lie to the east. Generous green space, including the Campus and Stanborough Park, preserves the garden city ethos, and the town is a popular commuter settlement with rail links to London King's Cross and Moorgate.
