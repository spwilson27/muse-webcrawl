A75 autoroute

The A75 autoroute, also called La Méridienne, is a motorway in France running approximately 340 kilometres from Clermont-Ferrand in the north to Pézenas and Béziers on the Mediterranean coast in the south. It crosses the Massif Central, one of the most mountainous routes of any European motorway, and is largely toll-free.

Route

From Clermont-Ferrand the A75 heads south through Issoire, Saint-Flour, and the Aubrac plateau, climbing to over 1,100 metres at the Col de la Fageolle. It then descends through Millau, Lodève, and Clermont-l'Hérault to the coastal plain near Béziers.

Millau Viaduct

The motorway's most famous structure is the Millau Viaduct, opened in December 2004, which carries the road across the Tarn gorge. At 343 metres, its tallest mast exceeds the height of the Eiffel Tower, making it the tallest bridge in the world. The viaduct — the only tolled section of the A75 — eliminated the notorious summer traffic jams through the town of Millau.

Engineering

Other notable structures include the Garabit-influenced viaducts and tunnels built to carry the two-lane-each-way carriageway through rugged volcanic terrain, with long gradients and numerous runaway-vehicle arrestor beds.
