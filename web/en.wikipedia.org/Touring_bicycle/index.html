Touring bicycle

A touring bicycle fitted with panniers and racks

A touring bicycle is a bicycle designed or modified to handle bicycle touring: long-distance, self-supported travel. Touring bicycles are built for comfort, durability and load-carrying rather than speed, and differ from racing bicycles in geometry, gearing and fittings.

Design features

Typical features include a longer wheelbase and relaxed frame geometry for stability under load; a strong frame, often steel for its durability and ease of repair; wide-range, low gearing for climbing with luggage; sturdy wheels with many spokes; clearance for wide tyres and mudguards; and multiple mounting points (braze-ons) for front and rear racks, panniers and several water bottles. Drop handlebars or trekking bars offer multiple hand positions for long days in the saddle.

Types

Variations include expedition bikes for remote, unpaved routes; road touring bikes for paved journeys; and lighter "credit-card" touring setups. Related designs include the randonneur bicycle used in long-distance audax events and the tandem. Classic examples include the Dawes Galaxy and the Surly Long Haul Trucker.
