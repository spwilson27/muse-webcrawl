Cartesian product

In mathematics, the Cartesian product is a mathematical operation that returns a set (or product set) from multiple sets. For sets A and B, the Cartesian product A × B is the set of all ordered pairs (a, b) where a is in A and b is in B. It is named after René Descartes, whose formulation of analytic geometry gave rise to the concept.

Definition and cardinality

If |A| = m and |B| = n, then A × B has m × n elements. The product of two copies of the real line, ℝ × ℝ, is the Cartesian plane on which points are given by coordinate pairs (x, y). Products of n sets give ordered n-tuples, generalising to Cartesian powers such as ℝ³ for three-dimensional space.

Properties

The Cartesian product is not commutative (A × B ≠ B × A in general, though the two are in natural bijection) and not associative, though it distributes over unions and intersections. In set theory, ordered pairs are typically defined by the Kuratowski definition, and products are foundational for defining relations, functions, and graphs of functions.

Generalised products

The Cartesian product of an arbitrary indexed family of sets underlies the axiom of choice: the statement that a product of non-empty sets is non-empty is equivalent to it. In relational databases, the Cartesian product (CROSS JOIN) combines every row of one table with every row of another.
