Agnelli family

The Agnelli family is an Italian multi-industry business dynasty. Founded by Giovanni Agnelli, who established the Fiat automobile company in Turin in 1899, the family has controlled Fiat and much of Italian industry for over a century through the holding company Exor, one of Europe's largest investment firms.

History

Giovanni Agnelli (1866–1945) co-founded Fabbrica Italiana Automobili Torino (FIAT) in 1899 and built it into Italy's dominant industrial group, becoming a senator under Mussolini. His grandson Gianni Agnelli (1921–2003), known as L'Avvocato ("the Lawyer"), led Fiat from 1966 and became Italy's most influential businessman and a global style icon, rescuing the company through the crises of the 1970s. His brother Umberto also chaired Fiat and served as prime minister briefly in the family tradition of political involvement.

Holdings

Through Exor, chaired by Gianni's grandson John Elkann, the family controls or holds major stakes in Stellantis (formed from Fiat Chrysler and PSA), Ferrari, CNH Industrial, Juventus football club — owned by the family since 1923 — and The Economist Group. Exor has diversified into luxury goods, insurance (PartnerRe), and technology investments.

Generations and inheritance disputes

The dynasty passed through the deaths of Gianni's son Edoardo (2000) and Umberto's son Giovanni Alberto (1997), which placed John Elkann, son of Margherita Agnelli, in line as heir. A long-running inheritance dispute between Margherita Agnelli and her children over Gianni Agnelli's estate and the family holding structure became one of Italy's most prominent family legal battles in the 2020s.
