A jack is a flag flown from a short staff at the bow (front) of a vessel, known as a jack staff. In most countries the jack is a national or naval flag flown by warships while in harbour, and it is conventionally the same size as the ensign flown at the stern. The best-known example is the Union Jack of the United Kingdom, flown by Royal Navy ships as their jack.

Usage

Traditionally, jacks are hoisted when a warship is at anchor or alongside, and hauled down when the ship gets under way, although practice varies: the United States Navy flies its jack (the Union Jack — the blue canton of the national flag — from 2002 to 2019 and again from 2019, with the First Navy Jack used in between and during the War on Terror) in port and, following a 2019 directive, again the blue union jack. Some navies fly jacks at sea as well.

Designs

Jack designs differ from country to country. Some are identical to the national flag (e.g., France, Germany), some are the canton alone (the historical US jack), some are distinct naval emblems (such as Russia's jack, a red flag bearing a blue saltire and white cross derived from the era of Peter the Great), and some are civil jacks used by merchant vessels, such as pilot jacks. The British Union Jack itself combines the crosses of St George (England), St Andrew (Scotland), and St Patrick (Ireland).

Etymology

The word "jack" for a small flag is old in English; small flags were flown at the bow as early as the 17th century. Folk explanations link the Union Jack's name to King James I (Latin Jacobus), but the term likely simply denotes a small flag.
