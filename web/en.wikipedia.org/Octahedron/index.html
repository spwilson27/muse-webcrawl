Octahedron

In geometry, an octahedron (plural: octahedra or octahedrons) is a polyhedron with eight faces. A regular octahedron is a Platonic solid composed of eight equilateral triangles, four of which meet at each vertex. It is the dual polyhedron of the cube.

A regular octahedron.

Geometry

The regular octahedron has 8 faces, 12 edges, and 6 vertices. If the edge length is a, the surface area is 2√3 a2 and the volume is (√2/3) a3. Its coordinates can be given by the permutations of (0, ±1, ±φ) in truncated forms, and it can be seen as two square pyramids joined at their bases.

Occurrence

Octahedral geometry appears in nature and chemistry: many transition-metal complexes adopt octahedral coordination geometry, and crystals of diamond and fluorite may exhibit octahedral habits. Spatial subdivision schemes related to the octahedron recur in computer graphics alongside structures such as the octree.
