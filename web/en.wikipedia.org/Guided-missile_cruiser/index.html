Guided-missile cruiser

A guided-missile cruiser is a warship, classed as a cruiser, whose principal armament consists of guided missiles. Such ships are typically large, multi-mission surface combatants able to engage aircraft, ships, submarines and land targets, and often serve as the air-defence centrepiece of a carrier battle group or fleet.

Development

The type emerged in the 1950s when the United States Navy converted wartime gun cruisers to carry the Terrier, Talos and Tartar surface-to-air missiles; USS Boston and USS Canberra were the first operational guided-missile cruisers. Purpose-built designs followed, including nuclear-powered cruisers such as USS Long Beach. The Soviet Union built missile cruisers around very large anti-ship missiles, culminating in the Kirov class, the largest surface combatants built after the Second World War.

Modern cruisers

The best-known modern class is the US Navy's Ticonderoga class, equipped with the Aegis combat system and vertical launching systems carrying Standard, Tomahawk and other missiles; some have been adapted for ballistic missile defence. Russia operates the Slava class and the surviving Kirov-class battlecruisers. As destroyers have grown in size and capability, the practical distinction between large guided-missile destroyers and cruisers has narrowed.
