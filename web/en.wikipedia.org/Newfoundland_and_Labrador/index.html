Newfoundland and Labrador is the easternmost province of Canada, in the country's Atlantic region. The province consists of the island of Newfoundland and the continental region of Labrador, on the mainland of the Labrador Peninsula. It is the most linguistically homogeneous province in Canada, with the great majority of residents speaking English as a first language, though it also has its own distinctive dialects and cultures.

Newfoundland was a British colony and then a dominion before it joined the Canadian Confederation in 1949, the last province to do so. The province's official name was simply "Newfoundland" until 2001, when the Canadian constitution was amended to add "and Labrador".

Geography

The province is the largest of the four Atlantic provinces by area. Labrador is bordered by Quebec and, across the Strait of Belle Isle, faces the island of Newfoundland. The island's coasts are famous for fjords, sea cliffs, and spring icebergs carried south by the Labrador Current — the stretch of ocean off the east coast known as "Iceberg Alley". The province observes its own time zone, Newfoundland Time, a half-hour ahead of Atlantic Time.

Economy

Historically the economy centred on the cod fishery, which collapsed in the early 1990s. Since the late 1990s, offshore oil production (including the Hibernia and Hebron fields), iron ore mining in Labrador, nickel mining at Voisey's Bay, and hydroelectric generation — notably the Churchill Falls station, whose power is sold largely to Quebec under a long-term contract — have been major contributors. Disputes with Quebec over Labrador's boundary (settled in 1927) and the Churchill Falls contract remain defining features of the province's politics.
