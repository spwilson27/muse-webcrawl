Public defender

A public defender consulting with a client.

A public defender is a lawyer employed by the state to represent criminal defendants who cannot afford to hire private counsel. The institution is most fully developed in the United States, where the Sixth Amendment right to counsel, as interpreted by the Supreme Court, obliges federal and state governments to provide defence lawyers to indigent defendants in criminal prosecutions. Public defender offices are government agencies staffed by salaried attorneys, supplemented in many jurisdictions by court-appointed private lawyers and contract counsel.

Legal foundations in the United States

In Gideon v. Wainwright (1963), the Supreme Court unanimously held that states must provide counsel to defendants in felony cases who cannot afford their own. Argersinger v. Hamlin (1972) extended the right to misdemeanour cases involving possible imprisonment, Rothgery v. Gillespie County (2008) fixed the right's attachment at the initial appearance before a magistrate, and Strickland v. Washington (1984) set the standard for claims of ineffective assistance of counsel. The first American public defender office was established in Los Angeles County in 1913, championed by Clara Shortridge Foltz, who had proposed the idea at the 1893 Chicago World's Fair.

Organisation and criticism

Public defender systems vary: some states run statewide systems (e.g. Florida's elected public defenders in each judicial circuit), while others rely on counties. Federal cases are handled by federal public defender organisations and community defender offices under the Criminal Justice Act. Chronic underfunding and excessive caseloads are longstanding criticisms — studies and litigation, such as the Hurrell-Harring settlement in New York (2014), have found caseloads so high that defenders can spend only minutes per case, fuelling disputes about whether the promise of Gideon is being met. Defenders handle the majority of criminal cases in the United States, since most defendants qualify as indigent.

Other jurisdictions

Analogous functions exist elsewhere under different names: duty counsel and legal-aid lawyers in Canada, duty solicitors in England and Wales (where publicly funded defence is provided largely by private firms under legal aid contracts), and public defenders' offices in several Latin American and European countries, such as Brazil's Defensoria Pública.
