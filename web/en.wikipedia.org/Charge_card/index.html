Charge card

TypePayment card
Balance ruleMust be paid in full each billing cycle
Credit limitOften no preset spending limit
Major issuersAmerican Express; historically Diners Club

A charge card is a type of payment card that requires the cardholder to pay the balance in full upon receipt of the statement, usually monthly. Unlike a credit card, a charge card does not permit the user to carry a revolving balance subject to interest payments; failure to pay in full typically incurs substantial late fees and may result in suspension of charging privileges.

Distinction from credit cards

The defining feature of the charge card is the absence of a revolving credit line: the full amount charged must be repaid each cycle, so issuers do not advertise an interest rate for purchases. Many traditional charge cards, such as the American Express Green, Gold, and Platinum cards, have no preset spending limit, though this does not mean unlimited purchasing power — approval is based on spending patterns, payment history, and known resources. Issuers earn revenue principally from annual fees and merchant discount fees rather than interest.

History

Charge cards descend from the merchant-issued charge coins and plates of the late 19th and early 20th centuries. The modern general-purpose charge card began in 1950 with Diners Club, followed in 1958 by American Express and Carte Blanche. Bank-issued revolving credit cards, beginning with BankAmericard in 1958, eventually overtook charge cards in popularity, leaving charge cards as a premium niche product.
