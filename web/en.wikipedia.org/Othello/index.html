Othello (full title: The Tragedy of Othello, the Moor of Venice) is a tragedy written by William Shakespeare, probably in 1603. It follows the Moorish general Othello, serving the Venetian state, whose marriage to Desdemona is destroyed by his ensign Iago, who manipulates him into believing she has been unfaithful with his lieutenant, Michael Cassio.

Plot

Resentful at being passed over for promotion and suspicious that Othello has slept with his wife Emilia, Iago plots revenge. He engineers Cassio's disgrace in Cyprus, plants Desdemona's handkerchief with Cassio as "ocular proof" of adultery, and stokes Othello's jealousy until the general smothers Desdemona in her bed. Emilia exposes the plot; Iago kills her and is arrested, while Othello, realizing his error, stabs himself and dies beside his wife.

Source and themes

The story is adapted from the tale "Un Capitano Moro" in Cinthio's Gli Hecatommithi (1565). Major themes include jealousy, love, betrayal, race and racism, reputation, and the conflict between appearance and reality. The play has been continuously popular on stage and inspired Verdi's opera Otello (1887) and numerous films. In modern criticism and performance, the portrayal of Othello as a Black Moor in white Venetian society has made the play a key text in discussions of race in Shakespeare.
