Río de la Plata

The Río de la Plata (Spanish for "River of Silver"), called the River Plate in British English, is the estuary formed by the confluence of the Paraná and Uruguay rivers between Argentina and Uruguay. It flows southeast into the Atlantic Ocean and, at up to about 220 kilometres wide near its mouth, is among the widest river estuaries in the world.

Geography

The estuary begins at the Paraná Delta near the island of Martín García and widens progressively past the port cities of Buenos Aires (Argentina) and Montevideo (Uruguay). Its brownish waters carry heavy sediment loads from the Paraná. A submerged shoal, the Barra del Indio, roughly divides the fresher inner river from the more saline outer estuary.

History

Juan Díaz de Solís reached the estuary in 1516, followed by Magellan's expedition and Sebastian Cabot, whose tales of silver helped give the river its name. It later became the namesake of the colonial Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata (1776). In 1939 the estuary was the site of the Battle of the River Plate, the first major naval battle of the Second World War, ending with the scuttling of the German cruiser Admiral Graf Spee off Montevideo.

Ecology and navigation

The estuary supports important fisheries and the endangered La Plata dolphin. Shipping channels to Buenos Aires and up the Paraná require constant dredging because of silting.
