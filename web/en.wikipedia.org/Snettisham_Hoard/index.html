Snettisham Hoard

The Snettisham Hoard is a series of deposits of Iron Age metalwork found near the village of Snettisham in Norfolk, England, between 1948 and 1990. The hoards consist mainly of gold, silver, and bronze torcs — neck rings — dating to about 70 BC, and constitute the largest deposit of precious-metal torcs found anywhere in Iron Age Europe. Most of the material is held by the British Museum, with some pieces in the Norwich Castle Museum.

Gold torcs from the Snettisham Hoard, British Museum

Discoveries

The first finds were made in 1948 when a field was deep-ploughed; over subsequent years five separate hoards (labelled A–E) were recovered. In 1990 metal detectorist Ken Hill found Hoard F, a single pit containing dozens of torcs. In total more than 150 torcs and fragments, over 170 ingots and pieces of scrap, and coins have been recovered from the site, amounting to some 20 kg of gold and 40 kg of silver.

The Great Torc

The most famous object is the Snettisham Great Torc, found in Hoard C in 1950. Made around 100–70 BC from eight twisted strands of electrum (a gold–silver alloy), each itself twisted from eight wires, it weighs a little over 1 kg and displays elaborate La Tène decoration on its terminals. A complete gold torc of similar scale was among the 1990 finds.

Interpretation

The deliberate burial of so much wealth in separate pits within a small enclosure has been interpreted as a votive offering, possibly at a shrine of the local Iceni tribe, rather than a hidden treasury. The finds illuminate metalworking skill and the ritual practices of late Iron Age Britain.
