Triglyceride

A triglyceride (triacylglycerol, TAG) is an ester derived from glycerol and three fatty acids. Triglycerides are the main constituents of body fat in humans and other vertebrates and of vegetable fats, and are a major component of blood lipids.

Triglyceride
Type: Lipid ester
Components: Glycerol + 3 fatty acids
Function: Energy storage
Related: Uric acid (metabolic marker)

Structure and metabolism

The three fatty acids may be saturated or unsaturated. Dietary triglycerides are hydrolysed by pancreatic lipase, absorbed, reassembled and transported in chylomicrons; in the liver they are packaged in VLDL. Hormone-sensitive lipase releases fatty acids during fasting for oxidation, producing glycerol and energy. Excess energy is stored efficiently because triglycerides are hydrophobic and energy-dense (~9 kcal/g).

Health

Elevated blood triglycerides (hypertriglyceridemia) are associated with metabolic syndrome, type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular risk and, at very high levels, acute pancreatitis. Levels are measured in a lipid panel after fasting.
