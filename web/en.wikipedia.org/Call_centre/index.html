Call centre

A call centre (British and Commonwealth spelling) or call center (American spelling) is a managed capability, often a centralised office, used for receiving or transmitting a large volume of enquiries by telephone. Inbound call centres handle product or service enquiries from consumers; outbound centres are used for telemarketing, market research, debt collection, and customer follow-up. Open-plan offices with rows of agent workstations equipped with computers, headsets, and telephones are characteristic.

Technology

Key technologies include automatic call distribution (ACD), interactive voice response (IVR) systems, computer telephony integration (CTI), call recording, and customer relationship management (CRM) software. Modern centres are often "contact centres", handling email, web chat, social media, and messaging alongside calls, increasingly assisted by artificial intelligence and chatbots.

Industry

From the 1990s, many companies outsourced call centre operations to countries with lower labour costs, notably India and the Philippines, leading to a large business process outsourcing (BPO) industry. The work is frequently criticised for low pay, close monitoring of performance metrics such as average handling time, and high staff turnover; at the same time it provides significant employment, including work-from-home and distributed "virtual call centre" models.
