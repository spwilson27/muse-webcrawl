Multiple listing service

TypeReal estate database / cooperative
AbbreviationMLS

A multiple listing service (MLS) is a database and software system used by real estate brokers to share information about properties for sale and to establish contractual offers of compensation between participating brokers. Originating in the United States in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, MLSs are generally organised locally or regionally and are closely associated with local associations of Realtors.

Function

When a seller signs a listing agreement, the property is entered into the MLS, exposing it to all member brokers and, through syndication and public portals, to buyers. Access rules, commission arrangements and data standards (such as the RESO Data Dictionary) govern the system.

Debates

MLSs have been the subject of antitrust scrutiny over rules on commissions and access for discount and internet-based brokers; major litigation in the 2020s led to changes in how buyer-agent compensation is advertised in the United States.
