Euler's identity

In mathematics, Euler's identity is the equality eiπ + 1 = 0, where e is Euler's number, i is the imaginary unit, and π is the ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter.

Named afterLeonhard Euler
FieldMathematical analysis, complex analysis

Explanation

The identity is a special case of Euler's formula, eix = cos x + i sin x, evaluated at x = π, which gives eiπ = cos π + i sin π = −1. Adding 1 to both sides yields the identity.

Significance

Euler's identity is celebrated for linking five fundamental mathematical constants—0, 1, e, i, and π—with the basic operations of addition, multiplication, and exponentiation. The physicist Richard Feynman called it "the most remarkable formula in mathematics"; it is frequently cited as an example of deep mathematical beauty.
