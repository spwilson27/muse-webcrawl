Late Roman army

The Late Roman army is the military establishment of the Roman Empire from the reforms of Diocletian (from c. 284 AD) to the end of the West in 476 and beyond in the East. It was reorganised into static frontier troops (limitanei) under duces and mobile field armies (comitatenses) under magistri militum.

Structure

Legions shrank to units of roughly 1,000–1,200 men, supplemented by auxilia palatina, cavalry vexillationes and scholae guards. Recruitment drew increasingly on barbarian foederati, and equipment changed: oval shields, spatha long swords, ridge helmets and mail or scale armour.

Debate

Historians debate its size (perhaps 400,000–600,000 men in total), effectiveness, and the degree of 'barbarisation'; the eastern army evolved into the Byzantine army.
