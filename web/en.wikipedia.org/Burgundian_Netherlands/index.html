The Burgundian Netherlands were the group of fiefdoms in the Low Countries ruled by the Dukes of Burgundy of the House of Valois-Burgundy between 1384 and 1482. Beginning when Philip the Bold acquired the County of Flanders through his marriage to Margaret III, the dukes steadily assembled — by inheritance, purchase, and conquest — a personal union of territories including Flanders, Brabant, Holland, Zeeland, Hainaut, Namur, Limburg, and Luxembourg, together with their lands farther south around Burgundy itself.

Government and culture

Under Philip the Good (r. 1419–1467), the Burgundian court became one of the most splendid in Europe, patronizing Early Netherlandish painters such as Jan van Eyck and Rogier van der Weyden, the composer Guillaume Du Fay, and lavish chivalric orders like the Order of the Golden Fleece, founded in 1430. The dukes pursued centralizing policies: the States General of the Netherlands first met in 1464, and Charles the Bold created central institutions including the Parliament of Mechelen (1473), provoking resistance from the wealthy cities, notably Ghent and Bruges.

End

Charles the Bold died at the Battle of Nancy in 1477, leaving his daughter Mary of Burgundy as heiress. Pressed by France and by the cities, Mary conceded the Great Privilege (1477) and married Maximilian of Habsburg. When Mary died in 1482, the Netherlands passed to the Habsburgs, becoming the Habsburg Netherlands, while France seized the Duchy of Burgundy proper.
