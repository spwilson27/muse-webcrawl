Second Anglo-Maratha War

The Second Anglo-Maratha War (1803–1805) was the second conflict between the British East India Company and the Maratha Confederacy in India. Sparked by internal Maratha rivalries and the Treaty of Bassein, it ended with decisive British victories that greatly expanded Company territory and reduced the Marathas to subordinate powers.

Background

After the Peshwa Baji Rao II was driven from Pune by Yashwant Rao Holkar in 1802, he signed the Treaty of Bassein with the British, accepting a subsidiary alliance. The Scindia and Bhonsle chiefs regarded this as an affront and went to war; Holkar joined later.

Campaigns

In the Deccan, Major-General Arthur Wellesley (later Duke of Wellington) defeated the combined Scindia–Bhonsle armies at Assaye (1803) — a battle he later considered his finest — and at Argaon and Gawilghur. In the north, General Gerard Lake captured Aligarh and defeated Scindia's regulars at Laswari, entering Delhi and Agra. Holkar fought on into 1805 before making peace.

Aftermath

By the treaties of Deogaon and Surji-Anjangaon, the Marathas ceded extensive territories including Orissa and lands between the Yamuna and Ganges. British supremacy in India was substantially established, though the Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817–1819) would complete it.
