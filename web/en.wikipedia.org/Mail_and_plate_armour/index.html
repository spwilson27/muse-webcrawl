Mail and plate armour

Mail and plate armour is a type of personal armour combining elements of mail (chainmail) and small metal plates. It was widely used in parts of Asia, the Middle East, and Eastern Europe from the medieval period into the early modern era.

Description

In this form of armour, metal plates of various shapes are linked together by rings of mail, or plates are attached over a mail or fabric backing. The construction combined the flexibility of mail with the greater resistance to blows and points offered by solid plates.

Regional forms

Examples include the Ottoman and Persian krug-type armours, Mughal and other Indian mail-and-plate coats, and armours of the steppe peoples. Japanese armour also included combinations of mail (kusari) and small lacquered plates (karuta) sewn to fabric.

History

Mail-and-plate constructions appeared as armourers sought better defence against arrows, lances and later edged weapons while keeping weight manageable. They remained in use in some regions into the 17th and 18th centuries.
