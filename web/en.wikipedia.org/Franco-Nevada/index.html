Franco-Nevada

Franco-Nevada Corporation is a Canadian company that owns royalties and streams in gold mining and other commodity and energy investments. Headquartered in Toronto, it is one of the world's leading gold royalty and streaming companies, listed on the Toronto Stock Exchange and the New York Stock Exchange under the symbol FNV.

History

The original Franco-Nevada Mining Corporation was founded in the 1980s by Seymour Schulich and Pierre Lassonde, pioneering the gold royalty business model; it acquired the royalty on Nevada's Goldstrike mine, which became highly profitable. The company merged with Newmont Mining in 2002. A new Franco-Nevada was launched in 2007 when Newmont spun off its royalty portfolio in an initial public offering led again by Schulich and Lassonde.

Business model

Unlike operating miners, Franco-Nevada does not operate mines or bear operating and capital costs; instead it finances mining projects in exchange for royalties — a percentage of production or revenue — or streams, the right to purchase metal at a fixed, discounted price. Its diversified portfolio includes major assets such as the Cobre Panama copper-gold mine and the Antamina mine in Peru, plus oil and gas royalties.
