Iran–Contra affair

Date1985–1987 (revealed November 1986)
AdministrationRonald Reagan (United States)
Key figuresOliver North, John Poindexter, William Casey, Robert McFarlane
InvestigationsTower Commission; congressional hearings; Independent Counsel Lawrence Walsh

The Iran–Contra affair was a political scandal in the United States during the Reagan administration in which senior officials secretly facilitated the sale of arms to Iran — then under an arms embargo — hoping to secure the release of American hostages held in Lebanon, and used proceeds of the sales to fund the Contra rebels fighting Nicaragua's Sandinista government, in violation of the Boland Amendment's ban on such aid.

Exposure

The scheme unravelled in October–November 1986, when a supply plane was shot down over Nicaragua and a Lebanese magazine reported the arms sales. National Security Council aide Oliver North and his secretary destroyed documents; televised congressional hearings followed in 1987, along with the Tower Commission's review of the National Security Council's role.

Consequences

Fourteen officials were charged and eleven convicted, though several convictions were overturned on appeal and President George H. W. Bush pardoned six figures, including former Defense Secretary Caspar Weinberger, in 1992. President Reagan's popularity fell sharply but recovered; no evidence established that he authorised the diversion of funds, though investigators criticised his lax management style. The affair remains a landmark case study in covert action, executive accountability, and congressional oversight.
