Nu metal

Nu metal
Stylistic origins: Heavy metal, hip hop, alternative metal, funk metal, industrial
Cultural origins: Mid-1990s, United States
Peak popularity: Late 1990s–early 2000s

Nu metal (sometimes stylized as nü-metal) is a subgenre of alternative metal that combines elements of heavy metal music with elements of other music genres such as hip hop, alternative rock, funk, industrial, and grunge. Nu metal bands have drawn on a variety of influences, and the genre typically features syncopated, down-tuned guitar riffs, hip-hop-influenced rhythms and DJ techniques, and vocals alternating between singing, rapping, and screaming.

History

The genre emerged in the mid-1990s with bands such as Korn, whose self-titled 1994 debut is widely regarded as its starting point, and Deftones. It achieved mainstream commercial success in the late 1990s and early 2000s with acts including Limp Bizkit, Linkin Park, Slipknot, Papa Roach, Staind, and P.O.D. Linkin Park's Hybrid Theory (2000) became one of the best-selling albums of the 21st century. The genre declined in popularity in the mid-2000s but experienced renewed interest and revival tours in the 2010s and 2020s.

Characteristics

Nu metal songs are often built on rhythmic, groove-oriented riffs played on seven-string or down-tuned guitars, with prominent bass and drums influenced by hip hop and funk. Turntables, samples, and keyboards are common, and lyrics frequently address pain, personal struggle, and alienation.
