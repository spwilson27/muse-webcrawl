Thomas Earl Starzl (March 11, 1926 – March 4, 2017) was an American physician, researcher and expert on organ transplantation, often called the "father of modern transplantation". He performed the first successful human liver transplant and developed much of the clinical and immunological groundwork that made transplantation routine.

Career

After training in Chicago and working at the University of Colorado, Starzl attempted the first human liver transplant in 1963; in 1967 his team achieved the first success, with the patient surviving more than a year. In 1981 he moved to the University of Pittsburgh, where his programme became the world's busiest transplant centre. He championed the immunosuppressants cyclosporine and later tacrolimus, greatly improving graft survival.

Later work and honours

Starzl's later research explored microchimerism — the persistence of donor immune cells in recipients — as a mechanism of graft tolerance. He received the Lasker–DeBakey Clinical Medical Research Award (2012) and the National Medal of Science, and wrote the memoir The Puzzle People (1992).
