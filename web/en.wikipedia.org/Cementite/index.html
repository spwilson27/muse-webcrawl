Cementite is a compound of iron and carbon, specifically iron carbide with the formula Fe₃C. It is a key microstructural constituent of steels and cast irons, where its hardness and brittleness strongly influence mechanical properties. Cementite is metastable: given enough time and temperature it can decompose into ferrite and graphite.

Occurrence in steel

In the iron–carbon phase diagram, cementite appears as the lamellar partner of ferrite in pearlite, as a grain-boundary network in hypereutectoid steels, and as primary plates in white cast iron. Its proportion rises with carbon content, making high-carbon steels harder but less ductile.

Properties

Cementite has an orthorhombic crystal structure, is very hard (around 800 HV) and brittle, and is ferromagnetic below its Curie temperature of about 215 °C. It is named for its early association with the cementation process of steelmaking.
