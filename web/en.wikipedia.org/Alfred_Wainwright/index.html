Alfred Wainwright

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Born: 17 January 1907, Blackburn, Lancashire, England

Died: 20 January 1991 (aged 84), Kendal, Cumbria, England

Occupation: Author, illustrator, hillwalker

Known for: Pictorial Guide to the Lakeland Fells; the Wainwrights

Alfred Wainwright MBE (17 January 1907 – 20 January 1991), known to many simply as A. Wainwright, was a British fellwalker, guidebook author and illustrator. His seven-volume Pictorial Guide to the Lakeland Fells, published between 1955 and 1966 and written and drawn entirely by hand, is regarded as one of the finest works on the English Lake District ever produced.

Life

Wainwright was born into poverty in Blackburn, the son of a stonemason. He left school at thirteen but studied accountancy at night school and eventually became borough treasurer of Kendal in Cumbria, a post he held until his retirement. A holiday walk in the Lake District in 1930 — when he first climbed Orrest Head above Windermere — began a lifelong devotion to the fells.

The Pictorial Guides and the Wainwrights

Beginning in 1952, Wainwright spent thirteen years systematically walking, mapping and drawing the Lakeland fells, producing the guides in a distinctive manuscript style, each page handwritten and illustrated with his own pen-and-ink drawings. The 214 summits described in the guides are known as the Wainwrights, and "bagging" them all is a popular goal for British hillwalkers. He also devised the Coast to Coast Walk, a long-distance route from St Bees in Cumbria to Robin Hood's Bay in Yorkshire, described in a 1973 book and now one of Britain's most popular trails. Despite growing fame, Wainwright remained shy and reclusive. His ashes were scattered on Haystacks, his favourite fell.
