Caponata

Caponata (Sicilian: capunata) is a Sicilian dish consisting of chopped fried eggplant (aubergine) and other vegetables, seasoned with olive oil, tomato sauce, celery, olives, and capers, in an agrodolce (sweet and sour) sauce. It is commonly served as an antipasto, side dish, or room-temperature salad.

Ingredients and preparation

Eggplant is the defining ingredient, traditionally diced and fried in olive oil, then combined with a sauce of tomatoes, onions, celery, green olives, and capers, balanced with vinegar and a little sugar. Variations add pine nuts, raisins, almonds, basil, or even cocoa in the Palermitan style; some coastal versions include swordfish or octopus. Like other eggplant dishes, caponata is considered to improve after resting and is often eaten the day after it is made.

History

The dish reflects Sicily's layered history of Arab, Spanish, and Italian influences: the sweet-and-sour profile and use of eggplant, pine nuts, and raisins point to Arab and Catalan culinary traditions. The name's origin is disputed; theories connect it to capone (a gurnard fish once served with the sauce) or to the cauponae, taverns of ancient Rome. Caponata became a symbol of Sicilian home cooking and is eaten year-round, with special prominence at summer meals and festivals.

Related dishes

Caponata is often compared to French ratatouille and Spanish pisto, which share the Mediterranean combination of eggplant, peppers, and tomato, though caponata's agrodolce sauce and capers set it apart.
