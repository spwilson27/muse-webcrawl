Smedley Butler

Smedley Darlington Butler (July 30, 1881 – June 21, 1940), nicknamed "Old Gimlet Eye", was a United States Marine Corps officer and writer. During his 34-year career, he participated in military actions in the Philippines, China, Central America, Mexico, and France in World War I. At the time of his death, Butler was the most decorated Marine in U.S. history, having received 16 medals, five for heroism, including two Medals of Honor — one of only two Marines to receive the medal twice for separate actions.

Early life and career

Butler was born in West Chester, Pennsylvania, the son of Congressman Thomas Stalker Butler. He left school shortly before his seventeenth birthday to enlist in the Marine Corps during the Spanish–American War, lying about his age. He earned the brevet rank of captain for his actions during the Boxer Rebellion in China in 1900, where he was wounded at Tientsin.

Military campaigns

Butler served in the so-called Banana Wars in Nicaragua, Honduras, Mexico, and Haiti. He received his first Medal of Honor for the capture of Veracruz in 1914 and his second for actions during the occupation of Haiti in 1915. In World War I he commanded Camp Pontanezen in Brest, France, and was promoted to brigadier general. He later served as director of public safety in Philadelphia (1924–1925), where he enforced Prohibition, and commanded Marine forces in China from 1927 to 1929.

Later life and activism

After being passed over for Commandant of the Marine Corps, Butler retired in 1931 and became a prominent anti-war speaker. His 1935 book War Is a Racket argued that American military interventions served the interests of big business, famously declaring that he had been "a racketeer, a gangster for capitalism." In 1934 he testified before Congress regarding the alleged "Business Plot", a purported conspiracy by wealthy businessmen to overthrow President Franklin D. Roosevelt. Butler died of cancer in 1940 at the Naval Hospital in Philadelphia.
