Popish Plot

The Popish Plot was a fictitious conspiracy invented by Titus Oates that gripped England between 1678 and 1681. Oates claimed that Catholic conspirators, acting on behalf of the Jesuits and with the approval of the Pope, planned to assassinate King Charles II, massacre Protestants, and install the king's Catholic brother James, Duke of York, on the throne. The allegations triggered a wave of anti-Catholic hysteria, parliamentary investigations, and trials that sent at least 22 men to their deaths before the plot was exposed as a fabrication.

Origins

Oates, a defrocked Anglican clergyman who had briefly converted to Catholicism and spent time in Jesuit seminaries in France and Spain, presented his claims to a London magistrate, Sir Edmund Berry Godfrey, in September 1678. When Godfrey was found murdered the following month — a crime that remains unsolved — public suspicion of a Catholic conspiracy became panic. Oates testified before Parliament and the Privy Council, naming dozens of alleged conspirators.

Trials and aftermath

The ensuing trials were marked by perjured testimony and public fervour. Among those executed were William Howard, Viscount Stafford, and Archbishop Oliver Plunkett of Armagh, the last Catholic martyr executed in England. The crisis fed into the Exclusion Crisis, in which the Whig opposition attempted to bar James from the succession. By 1681 the plot's credibility collapsed as Oates's inconsistencies mounted; after James became king in 1685, Oates was convicted of perjury, pilloried, and imprisoned. The Popish Plot remains a classic example of mass hysteria and the political exploitation of religious fear.
