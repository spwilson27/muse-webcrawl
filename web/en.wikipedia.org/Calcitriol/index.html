Calcitriol

IUPAC name(1R,3S)-5-[2-[(1R,3aS,7aR)-1-[(2R)-6-hydroxy-6-methylheptan-2-yl]-7a-methyl-2,3,3a,5,6,7-hexahydro-1H-inden-4-yl]ethylidene]-4-methylidenecyclohexane-1,3-diol
FormulaC27H44O3
Other names1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol; 1,25-(OH)2D3

Calcitriol is the hormonally active metabolite of vitamin D, also known as 1,25-dihydroxycholecalciferol. It plays a central role in calcium and phosphate homeostasis, increasing the absorption of dietary calcium from the intestine, promoting bone mineralization, and regulating parathyroid hormone secretion.

Synthesis

Vitamin D3 (cholecalciferol), formed in the skin or ingested, is first hydroxylated in the liver to calcidiol (25-hydroxycholecalciferol), the main circulating form. A second hydroxylation in the kidney by the enzyme 25-hydroxyvitamin D 1α-hydroxylase (CYP27B1) produces calcitriol. This step is tightly stimulated by parathyroid hormone and low calcium or phosphate levels.

Mechanism of action

Calcitriol binds the intracellular vitamin D receptor (VDR), which forms a heterodimer with the retinoid X receptor and regulates gene expression in the intestine, bone, parathyroid glands, and immune cells, among other tissues.

Medical use

As a medication, calcitriol is used to treat hypocalcemia, secondary hyperparathyroidism in chronic kidney disease, hypoparathyroidism, and certain forms of rickets and osteoporosis. Topical calcitriol is also used in psoriasis. Overdose causes hypercalcemia, so blood calcium is monitored during treatment.
