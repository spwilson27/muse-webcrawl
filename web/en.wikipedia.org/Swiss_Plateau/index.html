Swiss Plateau

The Swiss Plateau or Central Plateau (German: Schweizer Mittelland) is one of the three major landscapes in Switzerland, lying between the Jura Mountains and the Swiss Alps. It covers about 30 percent of Switzerland's surface area but is home to roughly two thirds of the population, including the cities of Zurich, Bern, Geneva, and Lausanne.

Geography

The plateau stretches from Lake Geneva in the southwest to Lake Constance in the northeast. It consists largely of molasse — sedimentary rock formed from debris eroded from the rising Alps — sculpted by glaciers into rolling hills, broad valleys, and numerous lakes such as Lake Zurich, Lake Neuchâtel, and Lake Lucerne. The Aare is its principal river.

Economy and settlement

With its relatively mild climate and fertile soils, the plateau is Switzerland's main agricultural and industrial region. Nearly all major Swiss cities, transport corridors, and government institutions are located there, making it the economic and political heartland of the country, with population densities well above the Swiss average.
