Deutsche Luft Hansa

Deutsche Luft Hansa A.G. (from 1933 styled Lufthansa) was the German flag carrier airline from its founding in 1926 until the end of the Second World War in 1945. It should not be confused with the present-day Lufthansa, founded in 1953 as a new company.

Founding and growth

Luft Hansa was created in January 1926 in Berlin through the merger of Deutscher Aero Lloyd and Junkers Luftverkehr. Backed by the government and manufacturers, it rapidly built a network across Europe and pioneered long-distance routes, including services to South America in the 1930s using catapult ships and aircraft such as the Dornier Wal flying boats and the Junkers Ju 52.

Nazi era and war

Close to the state from the beginning, the airline came under Nazi control after 1933; several of its executives had ties to the regime, and during the war its aircraft and staff were largely absorbed into the Luftwaffe, while workshops maintained military aircraft using forced labor. Scheduled services shrank as the war turned against Germany.

Dissolution

Luft Hansa ceased operations in April 1945 and was formally liquidated by the Allies in 1951. Its assets, name, and crane trademark were later acquired by the newly founded Deutsche Lufthansa of 1953, which presents itself as a successor in branding but a legally separate company.
