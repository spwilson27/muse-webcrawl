James Clark Ross

Born15 April 1800, London, England
Died3 April 1862, Aylesbury, England
OccupationRoyal Navy officer, polar explorer

Sir James Clark Ross (15 April 1800 – 3 April 1862) was a British Royal Navy officer and polar explorer known for his explorations of the Arctic and Antarctic. He located the North Magnetic Pole in 1831 and later led the first expedition to the Antarctic sea and ice shelf that bear his name.

Arctic service

Ross entered the navy at age 11 under his uncle John Ross and sailed on several Arctic expeditions with William Edward Parry in search of the Northwest Passage. During his uncle's 1829–1833 expedition, James Clark Ross led the sledge party that reached the North Magnetic Pole on 1 June 1831.

Antarctic expedition

From 1839 to 1843 Ross commanded HMS Erebus and HMS Terror on an expedition to the Antarctic. He discovered the Ross Sea, Victoria Land, Mount Erebus and Mount Terror, and the Ross Ice Shelf, and charted much of the Antarctic coastline.

Later life

Ross's final expedition in 1848 searched for the lost Franklin expedition. He was knighted in 1843 and remains one of Britain's most celebrated polar explorers; numerous Antarctic and Arctic features are named for him.
