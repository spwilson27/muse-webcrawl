Kola Peninsula

The Kola Peninsula is a peninsula of about 100,000 square kilometres in the far northwest of Russia, lying mostly within the Arctic Circle. It is bordered by the Barents Sea to the north and the White Sea to the east and southeast, and forms most of Murmansk Oblast.

Geography and resources

The terrain is tundra and taiga with many lakes and rivers. The peninsula is rich in minerals including apatite, nickel, copper and rare earths, mined around Monchegorsk and the Khibiny Mountains. Murmansk, the largest city in the Arctic, is a major ice-free port.

Strategic importance

Kola hosts bases of Russia's Northern Fleet, including nuclear submarine bases around Severomorsk and the Kola Bay, making it one of the most heavily militarised Arctic regions. The indigenous Sámi people have traditionally inhabited the interior, herding reindeer.
