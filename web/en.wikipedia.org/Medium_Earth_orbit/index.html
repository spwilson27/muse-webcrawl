Medium Earth orbit

A medium Earth orbit (MEO) is an Earth-centered orbit with an altitude above a low Earth orbit (LEO) and below a high Earth orbit (HEO) — between 2,000 km (1,243 mi) and 35,786 km (22,236 mi) above sea level.

Uses

MEO is most commonly used for navigation satellite systems. The Global Positioning System (GPS) satellites orbit at about 20,200 km altitude, as do Russia's GLONASS, Europe's Galileo, and China's BeiDou systems. MEO is also used by some communications satellites, including the O3b constellation, which provides broadband services to equatorial regions.

Characteristics

Satellites in MEO have orbital periods ranging from about 2 to nearly 24 hours. They experience significant radiation from the Van Allen radiation belts, requiring radiation-hardened electronics. Compared with geostationary orbit, MEO satellites have lower latency and require less powerful transmitters, but a constellation of many satellites is needed for continuous coverage.
