Pedagogy


Pedagogy concerns the methods and practices of teaching.

Pedagogy is the method and practice of teaching, especially as an academic subject or theoretical concept. The word derives from the Greek paidagōgos, the slave who escorted children to school, and came to denote the art or science of education.

Approaches

Modern pedagogy draws on psychology and learning theory. Behaviourist approaches emphasise reinforcement and drill; constructivist approaches, following Piaget and Vygotsky, hold that learners build knowledge actively and benefit from scaffolding within the zone of proximal development. Critical pedagogy, associated with Paulo Freire's Pedagogy of the Oppressed (1968), treats education as a political act aimed at liberation from oppression. Other influential strands include Montessori and Waldorf education, inquiry-based learning, and evidence-based methods such as spaced practice and retrieval practice.

Related terms

Andragogy refers specifically to adult education, and heutagogy to self-determined learning. Academic study of pedagogy overlaps with curriculum theory, educational psychology, and the sociology of education.
