Richard Stallman

Richard Matthew Stallman (born 16 March 1953), also known by his initials rms, is an American free software movement activist and programmer. He campaigns for software to be distributed in such a manner that its users have the freedom to use, study, distribute, and modify that software — software that ensures these freedoms is termed free software.

GNU Project and Free Software Foundation

In September 1983 Stallman announced the GNU Project to create a completely free Unix-like operating system, and in 1985 he founded the Free Software Foundation (FSF). He wrote much of GNU's core software, including the Emacs text editor, the GCC compiler and the GDB debugger, and authored the GNU General Public License (GPL), the most widely used free software licence, whose "copyleft" mechanism requires derivatives to remain free.

Views and activism

Stallman argues that proprietary software is an ethical wrong because it denies users control over their computing. He campaigns against digital restrictions management (which he calls "digital rights management" rebranded), software patents, and mass surveillance, and insists on the term "GNU/Linux" for systems combining GNU software with the Linux kernel. His views, expressed in essays such as the GNU Manifesto and the book Free Software, Free Society, helped inspire the wider open-source movement, though Stallman distinguishes his position from it.

Later years

Stallman resigned as president of the FSF and from his visiting scientist role at MIT in September 2019 following controversy over remarks he made, and returned to the FSF board in 2021, a decision that drew renewed criticism and calls for his removal.
