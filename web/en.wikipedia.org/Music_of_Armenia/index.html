Music of Armenia

The music of Armenia draws on indigenous folk traditions of great antiquity and a distinguished sacred music tradition, shaped by Armenia's position between Europe, the Middle East and Central Asia. Key instruments include the duduk (a double-reed woodwind made of apricot wood), the dhol drum, the zurna, the kanun and the tar.

History

Armenian liturgical chant developed after the adoption of Christianity in 301 AD; the sharakan hymns form a large repertoire, later notated with the khaz system. In the 19th century, Komitas Vardapet collected and transcribed thousands of folk songs, laying the foundation of Armenian ethnomusicology and modern Armenian art music. Aram Khachaturian became Armenia's most internationally famous composer, known for the ballet Gayane (with its "Sabre Dance") and Spartacus.

Folk and popular music

Folk song and dance music are sustained by ashugh (minstrel) traditions and village festivities; dances such as the kochari are emblematic. In the modern era, Armenian musicians have been prominent in jazz, rock and pop both in Armenia and the large diaspora; duduk playing by masters such as Djivan Gasparyan brought the instrument to world audiences, and Armenian duduk music has been recognised by UNESCO as intangible cultural heritage.
