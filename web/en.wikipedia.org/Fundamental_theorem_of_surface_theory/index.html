Fundamental theorem of surface theory

In differential geometry, the fundamental theorem of surface theory (also known as Bonnet's theorem, after Pierre Ossian Bonnet) states that every set of data of certain types, namely smooth functions E, F, G, L, M, N defined on a simply connected open subset of the two-dimensional space which satisfy the Gauss–Codazzi equations (in the forms established in the 19th century), determines the existence of an isometric immersion of that open subset into Euclidean 3-space E3, such that the first fundamental form of the induced metric tensor is given by E, F, G and the second fundamental form by L, M, N. Moreover, the isometric immersion is unique up to rigid motions of E3.

Statement

The first and second fundamental forms are the coefficients of the respective quadratic forms on the surface. The Gauss–Codazzi equations are the compatibility conditions ensuring that given data can be realised as a surface. The theorem is the surface-theoretic analogue of the fundamental theorem of curves: just as a curve in space is determined up to congruence by its curvature and torsion, a surface is determined up to congruence by its two fundamental forms.

Applications

The theorem underlies the classical approach to surface theory via moving frames and Cartan's method of equivalence. It is used to construct surfaces with prescribed properties, such as surfaces of constant mean curvature or constant Gaussian curvature, and to study deformations and rigidity of surfaces.
