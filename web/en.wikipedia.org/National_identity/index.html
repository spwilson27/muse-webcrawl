National identity

National identity is a person's identity or sense of belonging to one or more states or nations. It is the sense of a nation as a cohesive whole, as represented by distinctive traditions, culture, language, and politics. National identity may be ascribed by birth or acquired through naturalisation, and it can coexist with ethnic, religious, regional, and supranational identities.

Formation

Scholars such as Benedict Anderson have described nations as "imagined communities," created and sustained through shared language, print media, education, and collective memory. Ernest Gellner linked national identity to industrialisation and mass schooling, while Anthony D. Smith emphasised the role of pre-modern ethnic myths and symbols. States cultivate national identity through anthems, flags, holidays, monuments, and national curricula.

Components

Common components of national identity include a shared history and founding myths, a common language or languages, attachment to a homeland or territory, shared customs and cuisine, and civic values or legal traditions. Debates often distinguish ethnic nationalism, based on descent and culture, from civic nationalism, based on shared political institutions and citizenship.

Challenges

Globalisation, migration, and supranational bodies such as the European Union have complicated exclusive national identities, leading to hybrid and multiple identities. Conversely, perceived threats to national identity can fuel nationalist movements, secession campaigns, and debates over immigration and multiculturalism.
