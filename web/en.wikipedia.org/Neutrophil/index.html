Neutrophil

Neutrophils are a type of granulocyte and phagocyte, forming an essential part of the innate immune system. They are the most abundant white blood cells in most mammals, typically 40–70% of leukocytes, and first responders to bacterial infection; pus consists largely of dead neutrophils.

Neutrophil
SystemInnate immune system
LifespanHours to ~5 days
Share of WBCs40–70%

Function

Neutrophils kill microbes by phagocytosis, degranulation of antimicrobial enzymes, and release of neutrophil extracellular traps (NETs) — webs of DNA and proteins that trap pathogens.

Clinical significance

Neutropenia (low counts) raises infection risk and can result from chemotherapy or bone-marrow disorders; neutrophilia accompanies acute bacterial infection and inflammation. Absolute neutrophil count guides treatment decisions.
