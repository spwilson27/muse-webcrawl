Walia ibex

The walia ibex (Capra walie) is a rare wild goat found only in the Semien Mountains of northern Ethiopia. A stocky mountain ungulate with large, scimitar-shaped horns in both sexes, it lives on steep cliff faces at high altitude and is classified as Vulnerable, having recovered from near-extinction through protection in and around Simien Mountains National Park.

Description and behaviour

Walia ibex have a brown upper coat, greyish-brown chest, white belly, and black markings on the legs; males are considerably larger than females, weighing up to about 125 kg, and have a black beard and larger horns. They are sure-footed grazers and browsers that live in herds of five to twenty, retreating to precipitous cliffs to escape predators such as leopards. The rut takes place mainly from March to May, unusual among ibexes.

Conservation

Hunting and habitat pressure reduced the species to perhaps 150–200 animals in the late 1960s. Establishment of the Simien Mountains National Park (a UNESCO World Heritage Site) and enforcement against poaching enabled a slow recovery to roughly a thousand or more individuals, and the IUCN status was improved from Endangered to Vulnerable. The walia ibex is a national symbol of Ethiopia, appearing on currency and as the nickname of the national football team, the Walias.
