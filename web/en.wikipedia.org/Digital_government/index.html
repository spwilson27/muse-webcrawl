Digital government

Digital government refers to the use of digital technologies by governments to deliver public services, engage citizens, and run internal operations. It extends the earlier concept of e-government, emphasising redesigned, user-centred services and data-driven administration rather than simply putting existing forms online.

A citizen accessing public services through a government web portal.

Development

Governments began publishing information on websites in the 1990s, then added transactional services such as tax filing and licence renewal. In the 2010s, dedicated digital service teams—such as the UK Government Digital Service (founded 2011) and the United States Digital Service (2014)—promoted agile methods, shared platforms, and design standards across departments. Estonia is frequently cited as a leader, with its X-Road data exchange layer and near-universal digital public services.

Components

  • Digital identity and authentication systems
  • Online service portals ("once-only" data submission)
  • Open government data and APIs
  • Digital payments and notifications
  • Interoperability frameworks between agencies

Challenges

Barriers include legacy IT systems, cybersecurity risks, privacy protection, and digital exclusion of citizens without internet access or digital skills. International benchmarking includes the UN E-Government Development Index.
