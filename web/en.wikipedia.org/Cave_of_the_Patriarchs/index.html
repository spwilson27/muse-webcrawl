The Cave of the Patriarchs, also called the Cave of Machpelah and known to Muslims as the Ibrahimi Mosque, is a series of caves located in the heart of the Old City of Hebron in the West Bank. According to the Hebrew Bible, Abraham purchased the cave and the field of Machpelah as a burial plot, and tradition holds that the patriarchs and matriarchs — Abraham and Sarah, Isaac and Rebecca, Jacob and Leah — are buried there, making it one of the holiest sites in Judaism and a revered site in Islam and Christianity.


The massive Herodian enclosure over the cave

Structure and history

The caves are surmounted by a monumental rectangular enclosure of massive drafted stone blocks built under Herod the Great in the first century BC, one of the finest surviving Herodian structures. In the Byzantine period a church was built within the enclosure; after the Muslim conquest it became a mosque; Crusaders reconverted it to a church, and Saladin restored it as a mosque, adding minarets later completed under the Mamluks. The interior contains cenotaphs of the patriarchs and matriarchs, a Mamluk-era minbar, and restricted entrances to the caves themselves below.

Access and division

For centuries Jews were barred from the building and permitted only to ascend to the seventh step of the exterior staircase. After 1967 the site came under Israeli control and was opened to prayer by both faiths; following the 1994 massacre, in which a Jewish settler killed 29 Muslim worshippers, the complex was physically divided into Jewish and Muslim sections with separate entrances, an arrangement formalised under the Hebron Protocol of 1997, with limited days each year when each side may use the whole hall.

Significance

For Jews the site is second in holiness only to the Temple Mount; for Muslims, Abraham (Ibrahim) is a prophet and patriarch, and the mosque is among Islam's important shrines in Palestine. The site's contested status keeps it at the centre of the politics of Hebron.
