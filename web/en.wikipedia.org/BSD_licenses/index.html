BSD licenses

The BSD licenses are a family of permissive free software licences.

BSD licenses are a family of permissive free software licenses, imposing minimal restrictions on the use and distribution of covered software. This is in contrast to copyleft licenses, which have share-alike requirements. The original BSD license was used for its namesake operating system, the Berkeley Software Distribution (BSD), a Unix-like operating system.

Variants

The original, four-clause license included an "advertising clause" requiring acknowledgement of the copyright holder in advertising materials — a provision widely criticised as burdensome and GPL-incompatible. The clause was rescinded by the University of California in 1999, producing the widely used three-clause ("Modified BSD") license. A further simplified two-clause ("Simplified BSD" or FreeBSD) license omits the non-endorsement clause, and a zero-clause variant (0BSD) places almost no conditions beyond preserving the license text.

Terms

All variants permit redistribution of source and binary forms, with or without modification, provided the copyright notice and disclaimer are retained. BSD-licensed code may be incorporated into proprietary products, which distinguishes it from copyleft licences such as the GNU General Public License.
