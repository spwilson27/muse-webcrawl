Miep Gies

Miep Gies
Born: 15 February 1909, Vienna, Austria-Hungary
Died: 11 January 2010 (aged 100), Hoorn, Netherlands
Known for: Hiding Anne Frank and her family; preserving Anne's diary

Hermine "Miep" Gies (née Santruschitz; 15 February 1909 – 11 January 2010) was one of the Dutch citizens who hid Anne Frank, her family and four other Jewish people from the Nazis in an annex above Otto Frank's business premises in Amsterdam during the Second World War. After the group's arrest in August 1944, Gies recovered Anne Frank's diaries from the ransacked annex and kept them unread, returning them to Otto Frank after the war; they were published as The Diary of a Young Girl in 1947.

Early life

Born in Vienna, she was sent to the Netherlands in 1920 as an undernourished child under a relief programme for Austrian children and was fostered in Leiden, where she was nicknamed Miep. In 1933 she began working as a secretary for Otto Frank's company Opekta in Amsterdam, and became a close friend of the Frank family. She married Jan Gies in 1941, refusing to join a Nazi women's association — a stance that nearly cost her her passport and freedom.

The hiding place

From July 1942 to August 1944, Miep and Jan Gies, together with Johannes Kleiman, Victor Kugler and Bep Voskuijl, supplied the eight people in the Secret Annex with food, books, news and companionship, at mortal risk. After the Gestapo raid of 4 August 1944, Miep unsuccessfully tried to bribe an officer for their release. She found Anne's diary papers scattered on the annex floor and stored them in a drawer, intending to return them to Anne.

Later life and recognition

Otto Frank, the sole survivor of the annex group, lived with the Gieses for seven years after the war. Miep co-wrote the memoir Anne Frank Remembered (1987). She was recognised as Righteous Among the Nations by Yad Vashem, received honours from Germany and the Netherlands, and in 1994 received the Wallenberg Medal. She died in 2010, a month short of her 101st birthday.
