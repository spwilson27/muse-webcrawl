Dance-punk

Dance-punk (also known as disco-punk, punk-funk, or funk-punk) is a post-punk subgenre that emerged in the late 1970s, combining the energy and attitude of punk rock with the rhythms of disco and funk.

Dance-punk
Stylistic originsPost-punk, disco, funk, punk rock, new wave
Cultural originsLate 1970s, United States and United Kingdom
Typical instrumentsElectric guitar, bass guitar, drums, drum machine, synthesizer

Characteristics

Dance-punk is characterized by propulsive, danceable basslines, four-on-the-floor or funk-derived drumming, scratchy rhythm guitar, and vocals that may be shouted, deadpan, or sung. Bands typically strip rock instrumentation down to rhythmic essentials, drawing on James Brown, disco, and krautrock as much as on punk.

History

Pioneering acts of the late 1970s and early 1980s included Talking Heads, Public Image Ltd, Gang of Four, ESG, Liquid Liquid, the Contortions, and A Certain Ratio. Manchester's Factory Records scene and New York's no wave and downtown scenes were key centres. The genre influenced later developments including acid house and rave culture.

Revival

Dance-punk was revived in the early 2000s as part of the post-punk revival, led by New York's DFA Records and bands such as LCD Soundsystem, The Rapture, and Liars, alongside British acts like Franz Ferdinand and Bloc Party, and Canadian acts such as Death from Above 1979. The revival fed into indie dance and electroclash scenes and had a lasting influence on 2000s indie rock.
