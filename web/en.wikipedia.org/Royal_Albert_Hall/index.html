Royal Albert Hall

The Royal Albert Hall is a concert hall on the northern edge of South Kensington, London, England. One of the United Kingdom's most treasured and distinctive buildings, it has a capacity of up to 5,272 seats and since its opening by Queen Victoria in 1871 has hosted leading performers in classical music, rock, pop, ballet, opera and sport.

History

The Hall was conceived as the centrepiece of "Albertopolis", the cultural quarter inspired by Prince Albert, consort of Queen Victoria, and was funded partly from the profits of the Great Exhibition of 1851. Designed by Captain Francis Fowke and Major-General Henry Scott of the Royal Engineers, the elliptical building is constructed of Fareham red brick with terracotta decoration and a glazed iron dome. Queen Victoria opened it in 1871 and named it in memory of Albert, who had died in 1861.

The Proms and other events

Since 1941 the Hall has been the home of the Proms, the BBC's annual summer season of classical concerts founded by Henry Wood in 1895, culminating in the Last Night of the Proms. Its famous acoustic "echo" was famously tamed in 1969 by the installation of large fibreglass "mushrooms" (acoustic diffusing discs) above the auditorium. Beyond music, the Hall has hosted the Miss World pageant, sumo wrestling, tennis, boxing, the Eurovision Song Contest (1968), film premieres with live orchestra, and graduation ceremonies for Imperial College and other universities.
