Donald Davies

Donald Watts Davies (1924–2000) was a Welsh computer scientist who was one of the inventors of packet switching, a fundamental technology behind modern computer networking, and the coiner of the term "packet".

Career

Davies worked at the National Physical Laboratory (NPL) in London, where he proposed in 1965 that messages be split into short units sent independently over a network. With Roger Scantlebury, he built the NPL Data Network, which first operated in 1969 and demonstrated packet switching in practice. He also developed the first public demonstration of the concept, and influenced the design of the ARPANET, whose designers independently developed the same idea. Davies was knighted with the CBE in 1983, and the British Computer Society established the Donald Davies Medal in his honor.
