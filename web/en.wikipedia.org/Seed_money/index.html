Seed money

Seed money is the earliest funding a new business receives, used to prove its concept before larger rounds.

Seed money, also known as seed funding or seed capital, is the earliest stage of investment in a new business or project, provided to support it until it can generate cash of its own or attract further investment. The name derives from the analogy of planting a seed. Seed funding typically finances activities such as market research, product development and building an initial team, and is used to demonstrate a proof of concept.

Sources

Seed capital may come from the founders themselves ("bootstrapping"), from friends and family, from angel investors, from early-stage venture capital funds that specialize in seed rounds, from business accelerators and incubators such as Y Combinator, or from crowdfunding campaigns. Government grants and competitions can also provide seed money, sometimes without taking equity.

Instruments

Seed investment is often made through convertible instruments — convertible notes or simple agreements for future equity (SAFEs) — which postpone setting a valuation until a later priced round. Amounts vary widely, from a few tens of thousands of dollars in a pre-seed round to several million dollars for a full seed round in technology sectors. Because most new businesses fail, seed investors face the highest risk of any financing stage, balanced by the lowest entry valuations.

Stages of funding

Seed money precedes the traditional Series A round. A company that uses seed funding successfully to reach milestones — a working product, early revenue or user growth — is better positioned to raise growth capital; one that cannot may exhaust its "runway" and close. In academia and the non-profit sector, "seed funding" also describes small internal grants intended to generate preliminary results that support applications for larger external funding.
