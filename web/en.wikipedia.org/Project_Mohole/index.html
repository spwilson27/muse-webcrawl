Project Mohole

The drilling vessel CUSS I tested ocean drilling for Project Mohole.

Project Mohole was an American scientific drilling project of the late 1950s and 1960s that attempted to drill through the Earth's crust into the mantle by reaching the Mohorovičić discontinuity (the "Moho") beneath the ocean floor, where the crust is thinnest. It was conceived by the American Miscellaneous Society and funded by the National Science Foundation.

Phase one

In 1961 the drilling vessel CUSS I, using early dynamic positioning, drilled experimental holes off Guadalupe Island, Mexico, in about 3,600 metres of water, penetrating 183 metres into the seabed and recovering sediment and basalt cores — a landmark demonstration that deep-ocean drilling was feasible.

Cancellation and legacy

Rising costs, management disputes, and political controversy led Congress to cancel the project in 1966 before a purpose-built drilling ship was completed. The techniques pioneered by Mohole led directly to the Deep Sea Drilling Project (1968), which used the Glomar Challenger and produced core evidence central to the confirmation of plate tectonics. Reaching the Moho by drilling remains an unachieved goal of the International Ocean Discovery Program.
