Menin Gate

CommemoratesMissing Commonwealth soldiers of the Ypres Salient

The Menin Gate Memorial to the Missing is a war memorial in Ypres, Belgium, dedicated to the British and Commonwealth soldiers who were killed in the Ypres Salient of the First World War and whose graves are unknown. Designed by Sir Reginald Blomfield and built by the Imperial War Graves Commission, it was unveiled on 24 July 1927.

The memorial

The gate is a monumental arched structure spanning the road east out of Ypres — the route along which hundreds of thousands of soldiers marched to the front line. Its internal halls and staircases carry the names of more than 54,000 officers and men who died in the Salient before 16 August 1917 and have no known grave; those who died later are commemorated at Tyne Cot Cemetery. A lion sculpted by William Reid Dick surmounts the eastern side.

The Last Post ceremony

Every evening at 8 p.m., buglers of the local Last Post Association sound the Last Post under the gate, a ceremony held almost continuously since 1928 (interrupted only during the German occupation in the Second World War, when it was performed in England). It is one of the best-known acts of remembrance in the world.
