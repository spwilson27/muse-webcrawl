Splitting field

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


A splitting field as a minimal extension containing all roots

In abstract algebra, a splitting field of a polynomial over a field K is the smallest field extension of K in which the polynomial splits, i.e. decomposes into linear factors. More precisely, an extension L/K is a splitting field for a non-constant polynomial p in K[x] if p factors into linear terms over L and L is generated over K by the roots of p.

Existence and uniqueness

Every polynomial has a splitting field: one can adjoin roots successively using quotient rings by irreducible factors (Kronecker's construction). Splitting fields are unique up to isomorphism over K. Every splitting field is a normal extension, and over a perfect field (for example in characteristic zero) it is also separable, hence a Galois extension; its Galois group acts on the roots of the polynomial, which is the starting point of Galois theory.

Examples

The splitting field of x2 + 1 over the real numbers is the complex numbers. The splitting field of x3 − 2 over the rationals is ℚ(∛2, ω), where ω is a primitive cube root of unity, an extension of degree 6 with Galois group isomorphic to S3.
