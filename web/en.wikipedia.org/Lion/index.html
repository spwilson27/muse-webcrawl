The lion (Panthera leo) is a large cat of the genus Panthera, native to Africa and India. It has a muscular, broad-chested body, a short rounded head, round ears, and a dark tuft of hair at the tip of its tail. Adult males are larger than females and have a prominent mane. Uniquely among cats, lions are social, living in groups called prides.

Distribution and habitat

Lions once ranged across much of Africa, southern Europe and Asia as far east as India. Today they survive mainly in sub-Saharan Africa, with a small population of Asiatic lions in and around Gir Forest National Park in Gujarat, India. They inhabit grasslands, savannas and shrublands, generally avoiding dense forest.

Behaviour

A pride typically consists of related females, their offspring and a small number of adult males. Females do most of the cooperative hunting, preying mainly on medium-sized to large ungulates such as wildebeest, zebra and buffalo. Males defend the pride's territory, marked by roaring and scent marking; a lion's roar can be heard up to 8 kilometres (5 mi) away.

Conservation

The species is listed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List. African populations have declined sharply since the late twentieth century owing to habitat loss, depletion of prey and conflict with humans over livestock. Conservation measures include protected areas, anti-poaching work and community-based programmes.

Cultural significance

The lion has been an icon of strength and royalty since antiquity, appearing in heraldry, national emblems and literature, and is often styled the "king of beasts".
