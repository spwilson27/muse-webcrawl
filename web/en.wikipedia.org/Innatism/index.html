Innatism

Innatism is a philosophical and psychological doctrine that the mind is born with ideas, knowledge or structures, rather than being a blank slate. It contrasts with empiricism, which holds that knowledge derives from experience.

History

Plato argued for recollection of Forms; Descartes held that ideas such as God and mathematics were innate; Leibniz defended innate principles against Locke's Essay Concerning Human Understanding, in which Locke attacked innate ideas. Kant proposed a priori forms of intuition and categories.

Modern views

In linguistics, Noam Chomsky argued for an innate language faculty and universal grammar, citing the poverty of the stimulus. Developmental psychology studies core knowledge in infants, while nativist accounts compete with empiricist and constructivist theories.
