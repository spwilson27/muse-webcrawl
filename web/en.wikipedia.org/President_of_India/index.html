The President of India (Hindi: Bhārat ke Rāṣṭrapati) is the head of state of the Republic of India and the supreme commander of the Indian Armed Forces. The president is indirectly elected by an electoral college comprising the elected members of both houses of Parliament and of the legislative assemblies of the states and union territories, using proportional representation by single transferable vote. The office was created when India became a republic on 26 January 1950, replacing the Governor-General.

Powers and duties

Under the Constitution of India, the executive power of the Union is vested in the president, but is exercised on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister, making the president primarily a constitutional and ceremonial head. Formal powers include appointing the Prime Minister, chief justice, governors and ambassadors; summoning and proroguing Parliament; giving assent to bills (including a suspensive veto and, in practice, a "pocket veto"); promulgating ordinances when Parliament is not in session; granting pardons; and proclaiming emergencies — national, state (President's Rule) and financial.

Election and qualifications

A candidate must be a citizen of India, at least 35 years old, and qualified for election to the Lok Sabha, and must not hold an office of profit. The term is five years; the president may be re-elected, removed by impeachment for violation of the Constitution, and is immune from criminal proceedings while in office.

History

The first president, Rajendra Prasad, is the only person to have served two full terms. Others include Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan, Zakir Husain, V. V. Giri, Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed (who signed the 1975 Emergency proclamation), Neelam Sanjiva Reddy, Zail Singh, R. Venkataraman, Shankar Dayal Sharma, K. R. Narayanan, A. P. J. Abdul Kalam, Pratibha Patil (the first woman president), Pranab Mukherjee, Ram Nath Kovind, and Droupadi Murmu, the first president born in independent India and the first from a tribal community.
