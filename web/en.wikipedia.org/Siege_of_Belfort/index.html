The Siege of Belfort was a major episode of the Franco-Prussian War. The fortress town of Belfort, guarding the Belfort Gap between the Vosges and the Jura, was besieged by Prussian forces under General August von Werder from November 1870 until February 1871.

The defence

The French garrison, commanded by Colonel Pierre Philippe Denfert-Rochereau, numbered some 17,000 men. The defenders resisted for 103 days, repelling assaults and withstanding heavy bombardment even after most other French fortresses had fallen. A French relief attempt by General Bourbaki's Army of the East in January 1871 was defeated at the Battle of the Lisaine (Héricourt).

End of the siege

Following the armistice of 28 January 1871, the garrison was ordered to surrender; on 18 February the defenders marched out with the honours of war, retaining their arms and baggage. In recognition of the town's resistance, Belfort was excluded from the territory ceded to Germany by the Treaty of Frankfurt: although the rest of Alsace was annexed, the Territoire de Belfort remained French.

Legacy

Denfert-Rochereau became known as the "Lion of Belfort," a name also given to Frédéric Auguste Bartholdi's colossal sandstone lion carved into the rock beneath the citadel in 1880.
