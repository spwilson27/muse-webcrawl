The Royal College of Music (RCM) is a conservatoire established by royal charter in 1882, located in South Kensington, London, UK. It is one of the four Associated Board of the Royal Schools of Music conservatoires and a member of Conservatoires UK.

History

The college was founded following a campaign led by the Prince of Wales, later Edward VII, who became its first president. It opened in 1883 with George Grove as its first director. The present building on Prince Consort Road, designed by Sir Arthur Blomfield, opened in 1894, directly opposite the Royal Albert Hall.

Teaching and performance

The RCM trains gifted musicians from all over the world for international careers as performers, conductors and composers. Its facilities include the Amaryllis Fleming Concert Hall, the Britten Theatre, and the Parry Rooms. In 2021 the college opened a major redevelopment, the More Music project, adding new performance spaces, a museum and improved facilities.

Museum and collections

The Royal College of Music Museum holds over 15,000 instruments, manuscripts, portraits and other items relating to the history of music, including the earliest surviving stringed keyboard instrument, a clavicytherium dating from around 1480.

Notable alumni

Alumni include Gustav Holst, Ralph Vaughan Williams, Benjamin Britten, Joan Sutherland, Andrew Lloyd Webber and Alfie Boe.
