Científicos

Científicos (Spanish for "scientists") were a group of scholars and administrators who advised the Mexican president Porfirio Díaz during the Porfiriato (1876–1911). Influenced by positivism, the philosophy of Auguste Comte, they promoted policies of modernization, foreign investment, and technocratic government under the slogan of "order and progress."

History

The Científicos coalesced around figures such as José Yves Limantour, Secretary of Finance from 1893, and the writer and educator Justo Sierra. They favoured scientific management of the economy, encouraged railway construction and foreign capital, and sought to place Mexico among the modern industrial nations. Their influence grew as Díaz aged, and they became a focus of resentment for groups excluded from power, including followers of Francisco I. Madero, whose revolution in 1910 ended the Porfirian regime.

Ideas

Drawing on positivist thought, the Científicos believed society should be governed by experts applying scientific principles rather than by traditional political elites. Critics accused them of subordinating Mexican interests to foreign investors and of justifying authoritarian rule.

Legacy

The term científicos remains in use in Mexican historiography for the technocratic advisers of the Díaz era, and by extension for later groups of expert policymakers.
