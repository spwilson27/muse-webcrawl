Voseo

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Voseo is the use, in Spanish, of the pronoun vos instead of tú for the second person singular familiar, together with its corresponding verb forms. It is the norm in the River Plate region (Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay) and is widespread in parts of Central America, Colombia, Venezuela, Ecuador, Bolivia, and Chile, while tú (tuteo) predominates in Spain, Mexico, and the Caribbean.

Forms

Rioplatense voseo uses distinctive present-tense endings: vos hablás ("you speak"), vos comés, vos vivís, and an imperative formed from the infinitive with final stress (hablá, mirá). The object and possessive forms remain those of tuteo (te, tu). Chilean voseo uses different endings (e.g. hablái, estái), and mixed systems combining vos with tú verb forms occur in several regions.

History

In medieval and Golden Age Spanish, vos was a respectful form of address. As vuestra merced (the ancestor of usted) took over polite usage in Spain, vos fell from fashion there, but it survived and generalised in peripheral colonies where courtly norms arrived late. By the 19th and 20th centuries, voseo had become a marker of national identity in Argentina and Uruguay, used across all social classes and in advertising and media.

Sociolinguistics

Attitudes vary: in some countries voseo is standard and prestigious, in others it is stigmatised or restricted to intimate or rural speech. Language academies, including the Real Academia Española, now recognise voseo as a legitimate feature of the Spanish of the regions where it is used.
