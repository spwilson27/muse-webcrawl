Sclerotherapy

Sclerotherapy

Field: Phlebology, dermatology

Uses: Treatment of varicose veins and spider veins

Sclerotherapy is a medical procedure used to treat blood vessel malformations, varicose veins and spider veins. A solution called a sclerosant is injected into the affected vessel, irritating its inner lining so that the vein swells, sticks together and eventually scars and is reabsorbed by the body.

Procedure

Using a fine needle, the clinician injects the sclerosant — agents such as hypertonic saline, sodium tetradecyl sulfate or polidocanol — directly into the vein. Foam sclerotherapy, in which the agent is mixed with air or gas to form a foam, is often used for larger veins, and ultrasound guidance may be used to target deeper vessels. Compression stockings are typically worn after treatment.

History

Attempts to treat varicose veins by injection date to the 19th century, but modern sclerotherapy developed in the 20th century with safer sclerosing agents and, later, ultrasound guidance.

Risks

Side effects can include bruising, staining of the skin, small skin sores and, rarely, deep vein thrombosis or allergic reactions. Sclerotherapy is generally less invasive than surgical vein stripping and is performed on an outpatient basis.
