Suffolk County, New York

Suffolk County is the easternmost county in the U.S. state of New York, occupying the eastern two-thirds of Long Island, including its North and South Forks. It is bounded by Nassau County to the west and surrounded by the Atlantic Ocean, Long Island Sound, and several bays. With roughly 1.5 million residents it is the fourth-most populous county in New York State. The county seat is Riverhead, and the largest community is Brookhaven.

Geography and communities

Suffolk comprises ten towns: Babylon, Brookhaven, East Hampton, Huntington, Islip, Riverhead, Shelter Island, Smithtown, Southampton, and Southold. The South Fork — the Hamptons — is a well-known summer resort area, while the North Fork is noted for vineyards and farmland. Fire Island, a barrier island with car-free communities, lies off the South Shore. Montauk Point, at the island's eastern tip, is home to the oldest lighthouse in the state, commissioned by George Washington in 1792.

History

One of New York's original twelve counties, Suffolk was established in 1683 and named for the English county of Suffolk. English settlers from Connecticut founded its earliest towns in the 1640s. The county contains the Shinnecock and Poospatuck reservations of Native American nations whose presence long predates European settlement.
