3D Robotics, Inc. (3DR) is an American technology company known for its work on unmanned aerial vehicles and drone software. It was founded in 2009 by Chris Anderson, then editor-in-chief of Wired, and Jordi Muñoz, whom Anderson met through the online DIY Drones community. The company grew out of that community's open-source autopilot projects, including ArduPilot and the Pixhawk flight controller.

Solo and the consumer market

In 2015, 3DR launched Solo, marketed as "the world's first smart drone," with autonomous camera features built around GoPro cameras. Despite positive reviews, Solo could not compete with the lower prices and integrated cameras of DJI's Phantom line. 3DR exited consumer hardware manufacturing in 2016, laying off much of its workforce.

Enterprise pivot

The company refocused on commercial applications with Site Scan, a drone data platform for construction and surveying, and partnered with Autodesk and Sony. It later concentrated on providing drone software and government-contracted systems, becoming a supplier for U.S. defense and public-safety programs that sought non-Chinese drone technology.
