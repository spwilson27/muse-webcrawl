The Battle of Jemappes (6 November 1792) was fought during the War of the First Coalition, near the town of Jemappes in present-day Belgium, between the revolutionary French army of General Charles François Dumouriez and the Austrian army commanded by Albert Casimir, Duke of Teschen. It was the first major offensive victory of the French Revolutionary Wars and opened the Austrian Netherlands to French occupation.

Background

After the French victory at Valmy in September 1792 halted the allied invasion of France, Dumouriez turned north to invade the Austrian Netherlands. His army of some 40,000 men, many of them enthusiastic volunteers of the levée, advanced on Mons, defended by roughly 13,200 Austrians entrenched on the heights around Jemappes and the villages west of the city.

The battle

Dumouriez launched frontal attacks along the Austrian line on the morning of 6 November. French columns were repeatedly repulsed by artillery fire, but superior numbers and the energy of the volunteer battalions told. General Louis-Philippe d'Orléans (the future king of France), fighting under the name "General Égalité," rallied troops on the French right, while Dumouriez personally led charges in the centre. By mid-afternoon the Austrian redoubts had been stormed and the defenders withdrew toward Mons, abandoning the position.

Aftermath

Mons surrendered the following day, and within weeks Brussels and most of the Austrian Netherlands were in French hands. Jemappes was celebrated in revolutionary France as proof that citizen armies could defeat the professional forces of the old regime, though the gains were temporarily reversed after the French defeat at Neerwinden in March 1793.
