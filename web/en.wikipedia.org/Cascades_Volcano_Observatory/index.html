Cascades Volcano Observatory

Mount St. Helens, the most active volcano monitored by the CVO

The Cascades Volcano Observatory (CVO) is a volcano observatory in Vancouver, Washington, United States, operated by the United States Geological Survey (USGS). It monitors the volcanoes of the Cascade Volcanic Arc in Oregon, Washington, and Idaho, including Mount St. Helens, Mount Rainier, Mount Hood, Mount Baker, Glacier Peak, Mount Adams, Mount Shasta, Mount Bachelor, Three Sisters, Mount Jefferson, Lassen Peak, Medicine Lake, and Newberry Volcano.

History

The observatory was established in the aftermath of the catastrophic eruption of Mount St. Helens on May 18, 1980, which killed 57 people, including USGS volcanologist David A. Johnston, who had been manning an observation post near the volcano. The observatory was formally founded in 1982 and later named in Johnston's honour. It grew out of the temporary USGS monitoring effort during the 1980 eruption crisis.

Work

The CVO tracks volcanic unrest using seismic monitoring, ground deformation measurements (such as GPS and tiltmeters), gas sampling, and remote sensing, and it issues forecasts, alerts, and hazard assessments for the Cascade Range volcanoes, several of which lie near major population centres of the Pacific Northwest. During the 2004–2008 eruption of Mount St. Helens it provided continuous monitoring and public information as a new lava dome grew in the crater.
