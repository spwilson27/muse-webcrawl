Suriname

Suriname, officially the Republic of Suriname, is a country on the northeastern Atlantic coast of South America, bordered by French Guiana to the east, Guyana to the west, Brazil to the south, and the Atlantic Ocean to the north. It is the smallest sovereign state in South America by area and population, with about 600,000 inhabitants; its capital and largest city is Paramaribo.

History

Indigenous peoples inhabited the region for millennia before European colonization. The English established the first colony in 1650, but in 1667 the Dutch acquired Suriname under the Treaty of Breda in exchange for New Amsterdam (New York). Dutch Guiana became a plantation colony based on enslaved African labor producing sugar, coffee, and cacao; after abolition in 1863, indentured laborers were brought from India, Java, and China, creating today's exceptionally diverse society. Suriname gained independence from the Netherlands on 25 November 1975. A military coup in 1980 led by Dési Bouterse ushered in a period of military rule, the December Murders of 1982, and a civil war (1986–1992) before democracy was restored.

Geography and culture

Suriname is the most forested country on Earth, with about 93% of its land covered by tropical rainforest, much of it protected in the Central Suriname Nature Reserve, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Dutch is the official language, while Sranan Tongo is a widely spoken lingua franca; the population includes people of Hindustani, Creole, Javanese, Maroon, Indigenous, Chinese, and European descent, and Hindus, Christians, and Muslims form the largest religious groups. The economy depends on gold, oil, and bauxite/alumina exports, and significant offshore oil discoveries have been made since 2020.
