Fondaparinux (trade name Arixtra) is an anticoagulant medication chemically related to low molecular weight heparins. It is a synthetic pentasaccharide — a chain of five sugar units — whose structure reproduces the antithrombin-binding sequence found naturally in heparin. Fondaparinux was developed by Sanofi-Synthélabo and was approved for medical use in the United States in 2001.

Unlike heparin and low molecular weight heparins, which are extracted from animal tissue, fondaparinux is produced entirely by chemical synthesis, giving it a precisely defined molecular structure and a molecular weight of about 1,728 daltons.

Medical uses

Fondaparinux is used to prevent venous thromboembolism (deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism) in patients undergoing major orthopedic surgery of the lower limbs, such as hip fracture surgery, hip replacement, and major knee surgery, and in abdominal surgery patients at risk of thromboembolic complications. It is also used in the treatment of deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism, and in the management of acute coronary syndromes, including unstable angina and myocardial infarction.

In the OASIS-5 trial, fondaparinux was shown to be as effective as enoxaparin in treating acute coronary syndrome while causing significantly less major bleeding. Fondaparinux is also used off-label in the treatment of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia (HIT), because it rarely cross-reacts with the antibodies responsible for that condition.

Mechanism of action

Fondaparinux binds selectively and reversibly to antithrombin III, greatly potentiating antithrombin's inhibition of activated factor X (factor Xa). By neutralizing factor Xa, it interrupts the blood coagulation cascade and inhibits thrombin formation and thrombus development. Fondaparinux does not inhibit thrombin (factor IIa) directly, because the molecule is too short to bridge antithrombin and thrombin — a property that distinguishes it from unfractionated heparin.

Pharmacology

Fondaparinux is administered by subcutaneous injection once daily. It has complete bioavailability after subcutaneous administration, reaches peak plasma concentrations in about two to three hours, and has an elimination half-life of roughly 17 to 21 hours in patients with normal renal function. The drug is not metabolized to a significant extent and is excreted largely unchanged in the urine, so it is contraindicated in severe renal impairment. Unlike heparin, fondaparinux has no specific reversal agent in routine clinical use.

Adverse effects

The principal adverse effect is bleeding, as with other anticoagulants. Other reported effects include anemia, injection-site reactions, and, rarely, thrombocytopenia. Because fondaparinux does not bind platelet factor 4 appreciably, it carries a much lower risk of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia than heparin products.
