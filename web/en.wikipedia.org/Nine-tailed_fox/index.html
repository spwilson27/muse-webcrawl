The nine-tailed fox is a mythical fox spirit appearing in the folklore of China, Japan, Korea and Vietnam. Originating in ancient Chinese texts such as the Classic of Mountains and Seas, the nine-tailed fox (Chinese: jiǔwěihú) was originally an auspicious symbol of prosperity and royal power before gradually acquiring more ambivalent and seductive associations.

In Chinese tradition, the fox spirit huli jing can cultivate magical powers over long lifetimes, gaining an additional tail with each century of cultivation, and can transform into a beautiful woman; the most famous example is Daji, the consort blamed for the fall of the Shang dynasty in the novel Investiture of the Gods.

In other cultures

In Japan, the kyūbi no kitsune is among the most powerful of fox spirits, associated with the goddess Inari; the courtesan Tamamo-no-Mae is a legendary nine-tailed fox of the imperial court. In Korean folklore the gumiho (kumiho) is typically depicted as a malevolent being that seduces men and eats their hearts or livers, seeking to become human. The nine-tailed fox remains a popular figure in modern anime, games and television dramas.
