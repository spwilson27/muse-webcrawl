Band-e Amir National Park

Band-e Amir National Park is Afghanistan's first national park, established in 2009. It protects a chain of six deep-blue lakes in the Hindu Kush of Bamyan Province, at an elevation of about 3,000 metres, separated by natural dams of travertine.

Geography

The lakes — Band-e Haibat, Band-e Zulfiqar, Band-e Qambar, Band-e Panir, Band-e Gholaman and Band-e Pudina — are fed by springs and snowmelt, and their intense blue colour comes from the mineral content of the water and the travertine deposits laid down over millennia. Local legend attributes the dams to Hazrat Ali, the cousin and son-in-law of the Prophet Muhammad.

Tourism and wildlife

Before decades of conflict, Band-e Amir was one of Afghanistan's most celebrated tourist destinations, and it has again drawn Afghan visitors in calmer periods. The park supports ibex, urial, wolves and migratory birds, and its protection is managed with support from international conservation organisations.
