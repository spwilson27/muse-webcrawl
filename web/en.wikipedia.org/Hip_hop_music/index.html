Hip hop music

Hip hop
OriginThe Bronx, New York City, 1970s
Key date11 August 1973 (Kool Herc's party)
ElementsMCing, DJing, breakdancing, graffiti

Hip hop music is a genre of popular music developed in the Bronx, New York City, in the 1970s by African American and Latino youth. It consists principally of rhythmic vocal delivery — rapping or MCing — over beats constructed by DJs from loops, samples and drum machines, and forms one element of a wider hip hop culture that also includes DJing, breakdancing and graffiti art.

Origins

The genre is conventionally dated to a party on 11 August 1973 at 1520 Sedgwick Avenue, where DJ Kool Herc extended the percussive "breaks" of funk records using two turntables, while Coke La Rock hyped the crowd — the first MCing. Grandmaster Flash refined turntable technique and Afrika Bambaataa organised the culture and coined its unifying ethos.

Growth and golden age

"Rapper's Delight" (1979) by the Sugarhill Gang carried rap onto the charts. In the 1980s Run-DMC, LL Cool J, the Beastie Boys and Public Enemy made hip hop a commercial and artistic force; a late-1980s "golden age" produced De La Soul, A Tribe Called Quest and Eric B. & Rakim. Regional styles followed: West Coast gangsta rap (N.W.A, Tupac Shakur), East Coast lyricism (The Notorious B.I.G., Jay-Z, Nas), and Southern, Midwestern and many international variants.

Modern era

By the late 2010s hip hop had become the most-consumed genre in the United States, absorbing R&B, pop, trap and electronic influences through artists such as Kendrick Lamar, Nicki Minaj, Drake and Cardi B. Its production methods — sampling, beat-making, the mixtape — reshaped popular music generally, and its language and fashion influence global youth culture. See also rapping and breakdance.
