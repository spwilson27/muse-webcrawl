Cheyenne is the capital and most populous city of the US state of Wyoming, and the county seat of Laramie County. Located in southeastern Wyoming near the borders with Colorado and Nebraska, it had a population of about 65,000 at the 2020 census.

History

Cheyenne was founded in July 1867 as a staging point for the Union Pacific Railroad's transcontinental construction, at the spot where the line crossed Crow Creek. Named for the Cheyenne Native American people, it boomed almost overnight as railroad workers and speculators arrived, earning the nickname "Magic City of the Plains". It became the capital of Wyoming Territory in 1869 and state capital on statehood in 1890.

Geography and economy

The city sits on the high plains east of the Laramie Mountains at nearly 1,850 metres elevation, with a semi-arid climate and frequent strong winds. Government, nearby F. E. Warren Air Force Base, railroads (Union Pacific and BNSF), and energy and data-centre development anchor the economy.

Culture

Cheyenne hosts Cheyenne Frontier Days each July, one of the world's largest outdoor rodeos and Western celebrations, dating to 1897. Landmarks include the Wyoming State Capitol, the historic Union Pacific depot, and the Wyoming State Museum.
