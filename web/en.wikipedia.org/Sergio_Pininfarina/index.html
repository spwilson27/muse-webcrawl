Sergio Pininfarina

Sergio Pininfarina (born Sergio Farina; 8 September 1926 – 3 July 2012) was an Italian automobile designer and, later, a senator for life in the Italian Parliament. He led the Turin coachbuilding and design firm Pininfarina, founded by his father Battista "Pinin" Farina, and presided over the design of some of the most admired production cars of the 20th century, including many Ferraris.

Career

Pininfarina graduated in mechanical engineering from the Polytechnic University of Turin in 1950 and joined the family firm, Carrozzeria Pinin Farina. After the death of his father in 1966 he became chairman, and in 1961 the family name had been legally changed to Pininfarina by decree of President Giovanni Gronchi. Under Sergio the company expanded from a craft coachbuilder into an industrial design and engineering group, building cars under contract for major manufacturers and opening its own wind tunnel in 1972 — the first in Italy for full-scale cars.

Designs

Sergio personally oversaw or directed designs such as the Ferrari 275 GTB, Ferrari Dino 206/246, Ferrari Testarossa, Ferrari F40 (engineering), Alfa Romeo Spider "Duetto", Fiat 124 Sport Spider, Lancia Gamma Coupé, and the Peugeot 406 Coupé. The firm's restrained, elegant style became the archetype of Italian sports-car design, and its partnership with Ferrari — begun in 1952 — lasted six decades.

Public life

Beyond the firm, Pininfarina served as president of Confindustria, Italy's employers' federation (1988–1992), and as a Member of the European Parliament (1979–1988). In 2005 President Carlo Azeglio Ciampi appointed him a senator for life. He died in Turin in 2012, aged 85.
