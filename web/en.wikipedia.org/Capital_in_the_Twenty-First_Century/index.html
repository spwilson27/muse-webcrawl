Capital in the Twenty-First Century

Capital in the Twenty-First Century (French: Le Capital au XXIe siècle) is a 2013 book by French economist Thomas Piketty. Based on decades of historical data on income and wealth compiled with Emmanuel Saez and others, it argues that inequality tends to rise when the rate of return on capital (r) exceeds the growth rate of the economy (g). The English translation, published in 2014, became an international bestseller.

Argument

Piketty traces the distribution of income and wealth in Europe and the United States since the eighteenth century. Inequality fell in the mid-twentieth century, he argues, not because of the normal workings of capitalism but because of the shocks of two world wars, inflation, and deliberate policies such as progressive taxation. Since about 1980 inequality has risen again. When r > g, inherited wealth grows faster than earned income, concentrating capital and threatening democratic meritocratic values.

Proposals

To counter this trend Piketty proposes a progressive global tax on net wealth, combined with a top income tax rate of up to 80 percent, greater transparency of financial data, and investment in education.

Reception

The book was praised by economists including Paul Krugman and Branko Milanović as a landmark work that returned distribution to the centre of economics. Critics, including writers for the Financial Times and economists such as Daron Acemoglu and James Robinson, challenged some data choices, the generality of r > g, and the book's treatment of politics. It has sold several million copies and inspired the growth of inequality research, the World Inequality Database, and a 2019 documentary film.
