Fictitious force

A fictitious force, also called a pseudo force, d'Alembert force, or inertial force, is a force that appears to act on objects when their motion is described using a non-inertial reference frame, such as an accelerating or rotating frame.

ExamplesCentrifugal, Coriolis, Euler
OriginFrame acceleration

Passengers on a rotating ride feel an outward "centrifugal" pseudo force.

Types

Examples include the straight-line inertial force in an accelerating lift, the centrifugal force and Coriolis force in a rotating frame, and the Euler force when the rotation rate changes.

Examples

Unlike "real" forces, fictitious forces lack a third-law reaction force and vanish in an inertial frame. On Earth, the Coriolis force deflects winds and ocean currents and drives weather-system rotation.

General relativity

In general relativity, gravity itself is viewed as a fictitious force arising from spacetime curvature, following Einstein's equivalence principle.
