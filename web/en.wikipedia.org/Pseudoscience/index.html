Pseudoscience

Pseudoscience consists of statements, beliefs, or practices that claim to be both scientific and factual but are incompatible with the scientific method. It is often characterized by contradictory, exaggerated or unfalsifiable claims; reliance on confirmation bias rather than rigorous attempts at refutation; lack of openness to evaluation by other experts; absence of systematic practices when developing hypotheses; and continued adherence long after the pseudoscientific hypotheses have been experimentally discredited.

The demarcation problem

Distinguishing science from pseudoscience — the demarcation problem — has been a central concern of the philosophy of science. Karl Popper proposed falsifiability as the criterion of scientific theories, while Imre Lakatos and others argued that research programmes should be judged by whether they make progressive, novel predictions. Paul Feyerabend and Larry Laudan questioned whether any single criterion could serve the purpose, yet the term remains widely used by scientists and educators.

Examples

Claims commonly described as pseudoscientific include astrology, homeopathy, creation science, dowsing, phrenology, and many forms of alternative medicine that reject controlled testing. The classification of particular fields can be contentious, and practitioners typically dispute the label.

Societal impact

Pseudoscientific beliefs can have harmful consequences when they displace evidence-based medicine or public policy, as in vaccine hesitancy and climate change denial. Scientific organizations and skeptics' movements devote considerable effort to public education and critical thinking.
