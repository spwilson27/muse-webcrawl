Dream Stele

MaterialRed granite
Height3.6 m
CreatedReign of Thutmose IV, c. 1400 BC
LocationGiza, Egypt (in situ)

The Dream Stele, also known as the Sphinx Stele, is an epigraphic stele erected between the front paws of the Great Sphinx of Giza by the ancient Egyptian pharaoh Thutmose IV in the first year of his reign, around 1400 BC.

Text

The stele recounts that the young prince Thutmose, while hunting, fell asleep in the shadow of the Sphinx, which was then buried up to its neck in sand. In a dream, the Sphinx — identified with the god Horemakhet (Horus of the Horizon) — spoke to him, promising him the kingship of Egypt if he would clear away the sand that covered its body.

Thutmose fulfilled the condition, restored the Sphinx, and became pharaoh. The inscription is damaged, and the ending that would have described the clearing of the sand is lost.

Significance

The Dream Stele is one of the earliest records of the Sphinx being worshipped as a god and is often used by historians as evidence of Thutmose IV's piety — and, by some, of uncertainty about his claim to the throne, suggesting the dream story served to legitimise his accession.
