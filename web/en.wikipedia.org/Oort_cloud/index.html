Oort cloud

The Oort cloud is a vast theoretical cloud of predominantly icy planetesimals proposed to surround the Sun at distances ranging from 2,000 to 200,000 astronomical units (AU). It is named after Dutch astronomer Jan Oort, who proposed it in 1950 to explain the origin of long-period comets.

The cloud is divided into a disc-like inner Hills cloud and a spherical outer cloud, thought to contain billions to trillions of comets with a total mass of several Earth masses. It is believed to have formed from planetesimals ejected by the giant planets early in the Solar System's history. Occasional gravitational perturbations by passing stars or galactic tides send objects inward as long-period comets.

Evidence

No direct observation of the Oort cloud exists; its existence is inferred from the isotropic distribution and aphelia of long-period comets. Sedna and other detached objects may belong to the inner cloud.
