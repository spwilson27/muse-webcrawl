Binding affinity

Binding affinity is the strength of the binding interaction between a single biomolecule, such as a protein or DNA, and its ligand or binding partner, such as a drug or inhibitor. It is a measure of how tightly a ligand binds to its target at equilibrium.

Measurement

Binding affinity is most commonly quantified by the equilibrium dissociation constant, Kd: the lower the Kd, the higher the affinity. The related association constant Ka is its reciprocal. Values are determined experimentally by techniques such as surface plasmon resonance, isothermal titration calorimetry, and radioligand binding assays.

Significance

In pharmacology, high binding affinity is a key property sought in drug discovery, although affinity alone does not guarantee efficacy. Binding affinity is governed by non-covalent intermolecular forces including hydrogen bonding, van der Waals forces, electrostatic interactions, and hydrophobic effects.

Related concepts

Affinity differs from avidity, which describes the accumulated strength of multiple simultaneous binding interactions, such as those between an antibody and a multivalent antigen.
