Nigerian cuisine

Nigerian cuisine consists of dishes and food traditions from the many ethnic groups of Nigeria, including the Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo, Ibibio, Efik, Tiv, and Fulani. It draws on indigenous ingredients such as yams, cassava, plantains, rice, beans, cocoyams, peanuts, and a wide variety of vegetables and peppers, combined with palm oil and richly seasoned stocks.

Staples

Swallow foods — starchy doughs eaten by hand with soups and stews — are central to Nigerian meals. They include pounded yam, eba (made from garri, a cassava product), fufu, amala, and semolina or wheat dough. Popular soups eaten with them include egusi (made from ground melon seeds), ogbono, okra soup, and vegetable soups such as edikang ikong and afang.

Rice dishes

Jollof rice, cooked in a blended tomato and pepper base, is Nigeria's most famous dish and the subject of a friendly rivalry with Ghanaian jollof. Other rice dishes include fried rice, coconut rice, and ofada rice served with a spicy pepper sauce.

Snacks and street food

Common snacks and street foods include suya (spiced grilled meat skewers), akara (fried bean cakes), moi moi (steamed bean pudding), puff-puff (sweet fried dough), chin chin, roasted plantain (boli), and meat pies. Drinks include zobo (a hibiscus infusion), kunu, palm wine, and ginger beer.
