Quantum state

In quantum physics, a quantum state is a mathematical entity that embodies the knowledge of a quantum system. Quantum mechanics specifies the construction of, and the dynamical evolution of, a quantum state. The statistics of measurements performed on a system prepared in a given state can be calculated from that state using the Born rule.


The Bloch sphere for a single qubit

A pure state of a system with a finite-dimensional state space is represented by a unit vector in a Hilbert space, written in bra–ket notation as |ψ⟩. Statistical mixtures of pure states, and states of subsystems of entangled systems, are represented instead by density matrices ρ, which are positive operators of unit trace. A state is pure exactly when it can be written as a single vector, equivalently when Tr(ρ²) = 1.

Evolution

Between measurements, an isolated system's state evolves deterministically and reversibly according to the Schrödinger equation, generated by the system's Hamiltonian. In the Heisenberg picture the state is fixed and the observables evolve instead. When a system interacts with its environment, decoherence can transform a pure state into an effective mixture, described by open-system dynamics such as the Lindblad equation.

Examples

The state of a two-level system, or qubit, corresponds to a point on the Bloch sphere. The ground and excited states of an atom, the polarisation states of a photon, and spin states of an electron are further examples. Multi-particle states may be entangled, meaning they cannot be factored into independent states of the parts.
