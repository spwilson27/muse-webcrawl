Milton Keynes is a city in Buckinghamshire, England, about 50 miles (80 km) northwest of London. It was designated a new town on 23 January 1967, planned to relieve housing congestion in London, and built on a bold grid pattern of distributor roads with roundabouts, separated redways for cycling and walking, and extensive parkland. It took its name from the existing village of Milton Keynes within the designated area.

The town incorporated older settlements including Bletchley — home of Bletchley Park, the wartime codebreaking center — Wolverton, and Stony Stratford. Milton Keynes is known for its modernist architecture, its grid squares lined with trees, the Concrete Cows sculpture, the National Bowl music venue, and one of Britain's largest covered shopping centers. It was granted city status in 2022 during the Platinum Jubilee civic honors.

Economy and transport

The city is a major employment center, home to organizations such as the Open University and the Red Bull Racing Formula One team, and sits on the West Coast Main Line and the M1 motorway between London and Birmingham. Its population continues to grow toward a planned capacity well above its present size.
