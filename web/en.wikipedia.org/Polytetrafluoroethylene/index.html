Polytetrafluoroethylene (PTFE) is a synthetic fluoropolymer of tetrafluoroethylene. It is best known by the DuPont brand name Teflon. PTFE is a thermoplastic polymer that is white at room temperature with a density of about 2.2 g/cm3.

Discovery

PTFE was discovered accidentally in 1938 by Roy J. Plunkett while he was working for DuPont in New Jersey. Plunkett found that a sample of tetrafluoroethylene gas had polymerized spontaneously into a white, waxy solid with unusual slippery properties.

Properties

PTFE has one of the lowest coefficients of friction of any solid, is highly non-reactive, and withstands temperatures up to about 260 °C in continuous use. The strength of the carbon–fluorine bond makes it resistant to nearly all chemicals and solvents.

Applications

PTFE is widely used as a non-stick coating for cookware, as insulation for wiring in aerospace and electronics, in seals and gaskets, and in medical implants. Because it belongs to the class of per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS), its production has drawn environmental scrutiny.
