Senate

A senate is a deliberative assembly, often the upper house or chamber of a bicameral legislature. The name derives from the Roman Senate — senatus, from senex, "old man" — which was composed of elders and was one of the most enduring institutions of ancient Rome.

Historical origin

The Roman Senate advised magistrates and controlled state finances and foreign policy, surviving from the monarchy through the Republic and into the Empire. Modern senates consciously borrow the name and the association with deliberation and experience.

Senates around the world

The United States Senate has 100 members, two from each state, serving staggered six-year terms; it confirms presidential appointments, ratifies treaties and tries impeachments. Other senates include those of Canada (appointed), France, Spain, Italy, Australia (elected, representing states), the Philippines, and many sub-national legislatures. Some senates are coequal with the lower house, while others have mainly revising or delaying powers.
