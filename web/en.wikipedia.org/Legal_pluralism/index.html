Legal pluralism

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: Customary and state courts coexisting in one jurisdiction]

Legal pluralism is the existence of multiple legal systems within one society or territory, applying to the same population or subject matter. It is a central concept in the anthropology and sociology of law, challenging the assumption — sometimes called legal centralism — that law is exclusively the creation of the state and uniform within it.

Forms

Classic examples arise from colonialism, in which colonial powers retained indigenous customary law and religious law for personal matters while imposing their own law in other domains. In many post-colonial states, state law coexists with customary courts and, in some, Islamic law; India applies different religious personal laws to Hindus, Muslims, and others in matters such as marriage and inheritance. Legal pluralism also describes the medieval European coexistence of canon law, royal law, manorial custom, and the law merchant (lex mercatoria), and, in modern scholarship, the overlapping orders of international law, transnational regulation, and private governance — sometimes termed global legal pluralism.

Scholarly approaches

Anthropologists such as Sally Falk Moore described "semi-autonomous social fields" that generate their own binding rules; John Griffiths's influential 1986 article distinguished "weak" pluralism, officially recognized by the state, from "strong" pluralism, in which normative orders operate independently of state recognition. Brian Tamanaha and others later debated whether the concept risks stretching the idea of "law" to cover all social control.

Significance

Legal pluralism informs debates on human rights, indigenous peoples' rights, and legal reform: state law may conflict with customary norms on issues such as land tenure, gender equality, and marriage age, and development programmes increasingly work with, rather than against, non-state justice systems through which most disputes in much of the world are actually resolved.
