Bajío

El Bajío is a region of central Mexico, a relatively low-lying plateau area (the name means "lowland") lying mainly within the states of Guanajuato, Querétaro, Aguascalientes and parts of Jalisco and Michoacán. It is drained in part by the Lerma River and sits at elevations of roughly 1,700 to 2,000 metres.

History

During the colonial period the Bajío became one of New Spain's wealthiest zones, combining rich silver mining — especially at Guanajuato — with fertile agricultural plains that supplied the mining towns. The region was a cradle of the Mexican War of Independence: the movement began in 1810 with Miguel Hidalgo's call to arms at Dolores, and key insurgent leaders came from Bajío towns. Its colonial cities, including Guanajuato and San Miguel de Allende, are noted for their preserved architecture and several are UNESCO World Heritage Sites.

Modern economy

In recent decades the Bajío has become one of Mexico's principal manufacturing corridors, with major automotive and aerospace plants around León, Celaya, Irapuato, Querétaro and Aguascalientes, alongside leather goods and footwear production centred on León. It remains an important agricultural area, sometimes called the breadbasket of Mexico, producing grains, vegetables and strawberries for domestic and export markets.
