Density gradient centrifugation

Density gradient centrifugation is a laboratory technique for separating particles, cells or molecules by spinning them in a medium whose density increases with depth, commonly sucrose or caesium chloride.

Types

In rate-zonal separation, particles separate mainly by size and shape. In isopycnic separation, particles migrate until they reach the point where the gradient density equals their own buoyant density.

Uses

The technique famously featured in the Meselson–Stahl experiment (1958), which demonstrated semi-conservative DNA replication, and remains standard for purifying organelles, viruses and nucleic acids.
