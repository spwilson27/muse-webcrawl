Dashain

Dashain (Nepali: दशैँ), also known as Vijaya Dashami, is a major Hindu religious festival in Nepal and in parts of India (where it is related to Durga Puja and Navaratri and Dussehra traditions). It is the longest and most important festival in the Nepali calendar, celebrated for 15 days in the month of Ashvin (usually September–October), honouring the goddess Durga's victory over the demon Mahishasura and, in Ramayana tradition, Rama's victory over Ravana.

Rituals

The festival begins with Ghatasthapana, when a sacred vessel (kalasha) is installed and barley seeds are sown in sand to sprout jamara, yellow-green shoots used in blessings. On Fulpati (day seven), sacred flowers and leaves are carried in procession; historically a royal procession brought fulpati from Gorkha to Kathmandu. Days eight and nine, Maha Ashtami and Maha Navami, involve animal sacrifices to Durga and the worship of tools, vehicles, and machinery (Vishwakarma puja). On Vijaya Dashami (day ten) and the following days, elders place tika — a mixture of rice, yogurt, and vermilion — on the foreheads of younger relatives along with jamara, giving blessings and money (dakshina).

Customs

Dashain is a major homecoming festival: people return to their ancestral villages, schools and government offices close, and families feast — traditionally on goat meat (mutton). New clothes, bamboo swings (ping) erected in villages, kite flying, and card games are hallmarks of the season. The festival concludes on day fifteen, Kojagrat Purnima, when the goddess of wealth, Laxmi, is worshipped.

Controversy

Animal sacrifice during Dashain has drawn criticism from animal-welfare groups, and some communities have substituted fruit or vegetable offerings.
