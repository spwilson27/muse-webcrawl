Martial arts

Martial arts are codified systems and traditions of combat — practiced for self-defense, military and law-enforcement applications, competition, physical and mental health, entertainment, and the preservation of cultural heritage. The term derives from Latin, "arts of Mars," the Roman god of war, and entered English usage in the 1960s, initially for East Asian fighting arts.

Varieties

Martial arts are commonly grouped by technique: striking arts such as karate, taekwondo, boxing, and Muay Thai; grappling arts such as judo, Brazilian jiu-jitsu, wrestling, and aikido; and weapon-based arts such as kendo, fencing, and eskrima. Many modern systems are hybrids, and mixed martial arts (MMA) combines techniques across these categories under a competitive rule set.

History

Organized fighting systems are ancient and worldwide: Egyptian and Greek wrestling and pankration, Chinese kung fu traditions associated in legend with the Shaolin Monastery, Indian kalaripayattu, and Japanese bujutsu developed by the samurai class. In the 20th century, arts such as judo (founded by Kanō Jigorō in 1882) and karate spread globally, followed after World War II by taekwondo, and in the 1990s by the rapid rise of MMA.

Training and philosophy

Training typically combines forms or kata, partner drills, sparring, conditioning, and etiquette. Many arts embed philosophical or ethical teachings — discipline, humility, and self-control — and rank progress with belt or grading systems.
