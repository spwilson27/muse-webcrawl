Grassroots democracy

Related concepts: Direct democracy, participatory democracy, subsidiarity, decentralisation

Grassroots democracy is a political principle and organisational approach that emphasises decision-making power at the lowest possible level — by the people most directly affected — rather than by distant or hierarchical authorities. It favours breadth of participation and consensus-oriented processes over centralised control, and is a core tenet of many green parties, anarchist and libertarian-socialist traditions, and various social movements.

Principles

Grassroots democracy is usually associated with ideas such as decentralisation, federalism and subsidiarity: decisions should be taken at the smallest unit capable of handling them, with higher levels coordinating only what cannot be done locally. In political parties practising grassroots democracy, local chapters retain substantial autonomy, leadership positions may be rotated or shared, and delegates to higher bodies may be bound by mandates from the base. The German Green Party's early "Basisdemokratie" is a frequently cited example of these principles applied to party organisation.

Practice

Mechanisms associated with grassroots democracy include town meetings, neighbourhood assemblies, participatory budgeting, workers' councils, initiative and referendum procedures, and consensus decision-making. In India, the panchayati raj system of village councils is sometimes described as an institutional form of grassroots democracy, as are the gram sabhas (village assemblies) recognised in the Indian constitution.

Criticism

Critics argue that grassroots processes can be slow, vulnerable to capture by the most vocal participants, and ill-suited to large-scale or urgent decisions. Supporters respond that wider participation produces more legitimate and durable outcomes and guards against oligarchy — the tendency, described by Robert Michels as the "iron law of oligarchy", for organisations to concentrate power in a small leadership.
