Katanga is a region in the south-east of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, corresponding broadly to the former Katanga Province (called Shaba between 1972 and 1997). One of Africa's richest mineral regions, it forms part of the Central African Copperbelt and is a leading world source of copper and cobalt, historically also producing the uranium used in the first atomic bombs.

History

Under Belgian colonial rule the mining giant Union Minière du Haut-Katanga developed the region's copper industry from 1906. Eleven days after Congolese independence in 1960, Katanga seceded under Moise Tshombe, with Belgian and mercenary support — the State of Katanga — precipitating the Congo Crisis and a major United Nations intervention. The secession was ended by UN forces in January 1963. Separatist grievances resurfaced in the Shaba invasions of 1977 and 1978, when Katangese rebels based in Angola twice invaded and were repelled with Western assistance.

Geography and economy

The region is a high plateau of savanna around the copperbelt cities of Lubumbashi (the former provincial capital), Kolwezi, and Likasi. Mining dominates its economy and politics; since 2015 the former province has been divided into Tanganyika, Haut-Lomami, Lualaba, and Haut-Katanga provinces.
