Caracalla

Marcus Aurelius Antoninus (born Lucius Septimius Bassianus, 4 April 188 – 8 April 217), better known by the nickname Caracalla, was a Roman emperor who reigned from 198 to 217. A member of the Severan dynasty, he was the elder son of Emperor Septimius Severus and Empress Julia Domna. He ruled jointly with his father from 198 and briefly with his younger brother Geta, whom he murdered in 211.

Early life and accession

Caracalla was born in Lugdunum (modern Lyon) in Gaul, where his father was governor. His nickname came from a hooded Gallic cloak he habitually wore and distributed to the people. He was named Caesar in 195 and Augustus, as co-ruler, in 198. On Septimius Severus's death at Eboracum (York) in February 211, Caracalla and Geta succeeded jointly, but relations between the brothers collapsed; in December 211 Caracalla had Geta murdered in their mother's presence and ordered a damnatio memoriae against him.

Reign

Caracalla's most famous act was the Antonine Constitution (Constitutio Antoniniana) of 212, which granted Roman citizenship to all free men throughout the empire — probably in part to broaden the tax base. He debased the coinage, introducing the antoninianus, and raised military pay substantially to secure the army's loyalty. In Rome he built the enormous Baths of Caracalla, among the largest thermae in the city. From 213 he campaigned on the northern frontiers and then in the East, massacring inhabitants of Alexandria in 215 after they mocked him, before invading Parthia in 216.

Death and legacy

On 8 April 217, while travelling near Carrhae, Caracalla was assassinated by a disaffected soldier, Julius Martialis, in a plot associated with the praetorian prefect Macrinus, who succeeded him. Ancient historians portray Caracalla as a cruel tyrant, though modern assessments note his administrative continuity with his father's policies and the long-term significance of his universal citizenship grant.
