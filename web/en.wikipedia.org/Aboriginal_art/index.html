Aboriginal art

Aboriginal art (Indigenous Australian art) is art made by Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples of Australia. It encompasses one of the world's oldest continuing art traditions, from ancient rock engravings and paintings to the contemporary art movement that has become internationally celebrated since the 1970s.

Traditional forms

Traditional forms include rock art (some sites in northern Australia, including in the Kimberley and Arnhem Land, contain paintings tens of thousands of years old), bark painting, carved and painted objects, body painting, ceremonial ground designs, and sand drawing. Motifs and stories relate to the Dreaming (Tjukurrpa and related concepts): the ancestral beings whose journeys created the land and its law. Because many designs encode restricted sacred knowledge, their reproduction is governed by cultural protocols.

Contemporary movement

In 1971–72, at the settlement of Papunya in the Northern Territory, schoolteacher Geoffrey Bardon encouraged men including Clifford Possum Tjapaltjarri and Kaapa Tjampitjinpa to paint traditional designs in acrylic on board and canvas, giving rise to the Western Desert "dot painting" movement and the Papunya Tula artists' company. Artists such as Emily Kame Kngwarreye later achieved international fame, and Aboriginal art became a major presence in Australian galleries and the art market, alongside urban Indigenous artists working in many other media.
