Neutron optics

Neutron optics is the study and manipulation of neutron beams using optical principles, exploiting the wave nature of the neutron. Because neutrons have wavelengths comparable to those of X-rays and interact with matter through a refractive index close to unity, neutron optical components include mirrors, guides, lenses and interferometers adapted to these properties.

Reflection and guides

Neutrons undergo total external reflection from polished surfaces at grazing incidence below a critical angle. This principle underlies neutron guides, which transport neutrons from reactors or spallation sources to instruments with low loss, and supermirrors—multilayer coatings that extend the critical angle several times beyond that of natural nickel.

Refraction and lenses

Compound refractive lenses, often made of magnesium fluoride or other low-absorption materials, focus neutron beams by stacking many individual lens elements to compensate for the extremely weak refraction.

Interferometry

Perfect-crystal neutron interferometers split and recombine coherent neutron beams and have been used in precision tests of quantum mechanics and gravitation, including the demonstration of the gravitational phase shift of neutrons.
