Science fiction film

The science fiction film (or sci-fi film) is a film genre that uses speculative, fictional science-based depictions of phenomena that are not fully accepted by mainstream science — such as extraterrestrial life, spacecraft, robots, time travel, and interstellar travel — often to explore social and philosophical questions.

History

The genre began with Georges Méliès's A Trip to the Moon (1902) and developed through landmarks such as Metropolis (1927). In the 1950s, films like Destination Moon, The Day the Earth Stood Still, and Invasion of the Body Snatchers reflected Cold War anxieties. 2001: A Space Odyssey (1968) raised the genre's artistic ambitions, and the enormous success of Star Wars (1977) and Close Encounters of the Third Kind transformed science fiction into a dominant commercial blockbuster genre, a position consolidated by Blade Runner, The Terminator, The Matrix, and the superhero and franchise films of the 21st century.

Characteristics and themes

Science fiction films commonly explore the impact of technology, first contact with alien life, dystopian futures, artificial intelligence, and the nature of humanity. The genre has frequently served as a vehicle for social commentary, disguising contemporary concerns in futuristic settings. Its visual effects tradition, from model work to CGI, has driven much of cinema's technical innovation.
