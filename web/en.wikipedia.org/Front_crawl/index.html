Front Crawl

The front crawl or forward crawl, also known as the Australian crawl or American crawl, is a swimming stroke usually regarded as the fastest of the four primary strokes. As such, the front crawl stroke is almost universally used during a freestyle swimming competition, and "freestyle" is often used as a synonym for front crawl.

Technique

The swimmer lies face down in the water and propels the body with alternating overarm strokes: one arm moves through the air and enters the water ahead while the other pulls beneath the body. The legs perform a flutter kick, alternating up and down movements driven from the hips. The body rolls slightly from side to side with each stroke, and breathing is coordinated by turning the head to the side, usually every two or three strokes, in the trough of the bow wave.

History

The stroke developed from the overarm styles of indigenous swimmers observed by Europeans in the Americas and the Pacific. In Australia, the Solomon Islands swimmer Alick Wickham and the Richmond Cavill family popularised and refined the stroke around 1900, adding the flutter kick. Competitive freestyle events at the Olympic Games are contested over distances from 50 metres to 1500 metres, and front crawl is also used in open-water and marathon swimming.
