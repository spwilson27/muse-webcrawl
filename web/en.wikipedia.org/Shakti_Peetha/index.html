Shakti Peetha

Kamakhya Temple, Assam

The Shakti Peethas (Sanskrit: शक्ति पीठ, "seats of Shakti") are significant shrines and pilgrimage destinations in Shaktism, the goddess-focused tradition of Hinduism. According to tradition, they mark the places where parts of the body of the goddess Sati fell to earth as Shiva, grief-stricken, carried her corpse across the subcontinent.

Legend

In the myth, Sati, wife of Shiva and daughter of Daksha, immolated herself at her father's sacrifice after Daksha insulted Shiva. The enraged Shiva performed the Tandava dance of destruction while carrying her body; to restore cosmic order, Vishnu dismembered the corpse with his Sudarshana Chakra, and the pieces fell at sites across South Asia, sanctifying each as a Peetha. Each site is associated with a form of the Goddess (Shakti) and a corresponding form of Shiva as Bhairava.

The lists

Texts differ on the number of Peethas. Common lists name 51 (or 52) Peethas, corresponding to the letters of the Sanskrit alphabet; extended lists give 108. Four sites are especially exalted as the Adi Shakti Peethas — associated with major body parts — and eighteen are singled out as the Maha Shakti Peethas in the Ashtadasha Shakti Peetha stotra attributed to Adi Shankara. Famous Peethas include Kamakhya in Assam (the yoni), Kalighat in Kolkata (the toes), Jwalamukhi in Himachal Pradesh (the tongue), Hinglaj in Balochistan, Pakistan (the crown/head), and Sharada Peeth in Azad Kashmir (the right hand).

Significance

The Peethas map a sacred geography uniting the Indian subcontinent, with sites in present-day India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, and Sri Lanka. They remain major centres of pilgrimage and festival observance, particularly during Navaratri.
