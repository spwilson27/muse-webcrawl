Trench
A long narrow excavation in the ground

A trench is a type of excavation or depression in the ground that is generally deeper than it is wide (as opposed to a wider gully, or ditch), and narrow compared with its length (as opposed to a simple hole or pit).

Trenches have been used for military purposes since prehistory. In geology, trenches are formed along tectonic plate boundaries, most notably the deep oceanic trench where one plate subducts beneath another. In civil engineering, trenches are created to install underground infrastructure or utilities (such as gas mains, water mains or telephone lines), or later to access these installations. Trenches have also often been dug for defensive purposes.

Military use

In the First World War, trench warfare became the defining feature of the Western Front. Elaborate systems of front-line, support and reserve trenches, connected by communication trenches, were protected by barbed wire and machine guns, producing a static battlefield across France and Belgium from 1914 to 1918. See also trench knife.

Geology

Oceanic trenches are topographic depressions of the sea floor, relatively narrow in width, but very long. The Mariana Trench is the deepest, reaching nearly 11 kilometres below sea level.
