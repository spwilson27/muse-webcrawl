Georgy Zhukov

Georgy Konstantinovich Zhukov
Born: 1 December 1896, Strelkovka, Russian Empire
Died: 18 June 1974, Moscow, Soviet Union
Rank: Marshal of the Soviet Union
Battles: Khalkhin Gol, Leningrad, Moscow, Stalingrad, Kursk, Berlin

Georgy Konstantinovich Zhukov (1 December 1896 – 18 June 1974) was a Soviet military commander and Marshal of the Soviet Union who oversaw some of the Red Army's most decisive victories in the Second World War. He is one of the most decorated commanders in Russian and Soviet history.

Career

Born into a peasant family, Zhukov was conscripted into the Imperial Russian Army in the First World War and joined the Red Army in the Civil War. In 1939 he won a decisive victory over Japanese forces at Khalkhin Gol in Mongolia, pioneering the massed use of armour. During the German invasion he helped organise the defences of Leningrad and Moscow, planned the counteroffensives at Stalingrad and Kursk, and coordinated the great offensives of 1944. In April–May 1945 he commanded the 1st Belorussian Front in the Battle of Berlin and accepted the German surrender in Karlshorst.

Postwar

Zhukov served as Minister of Defence from 1955 to 1957, when Nikita Khrushchev removed him amid fears of his popularity. He spent his retirement writing memoirs and was partially rehabilitated before his death in 1974.
