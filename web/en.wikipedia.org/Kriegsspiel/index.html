Kriegsspiel

Kriegsspiel (German for "war game") is a genre of wargame developed by the Prussian Army in the 19th century for training officers. It is regarded as the first modern wargame and an ancestor of both military simulation and hobby wargaming.

History

Georg von Reisswitz presented an early version to the Prussian court in 1812; his son Georg Heinrich Rudolf Johann von Reisswitz published the system in 1824, using realistic maps, dice for combat outcomes and an umpire. The Prussian General Staff adopted the game, and versions spread to other armies. Later variants ranged from the rigid, rule-heavy Kriegsspiel to Helmuth von Moltke's freer form judged by experienced umpires.

Play

Players command forces represented by blocks or markers on a topographic map, issuing written orders; an umpire resolves movement and combat using rules, tables and dice, with opposing sides often in separate rooms with limited information — an early model of the fog of war.
