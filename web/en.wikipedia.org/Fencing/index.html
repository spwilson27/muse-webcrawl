Fencing

Fencing is a combat sport in which two competitors fight using bladed weapons — the foil, épée, or sabre — scoring points by making contact with an opponent in accordance with the rules of the specific weapon. Modern fencing is governed internationally by the Fédération Internationale d'Escrime (FIE) and has been contested at every modern Olympic Games since 1896.

History

Fencing developed from the practice of military swordsmanship and duelling. Sword schools flourished in Germany and Italy in the 14th and 15th centuries, and treatises such as those of Fiore dei Liberi formalised technique. In the 18th century, the lighter small sword gave rise to the foil as a training weapon, and Domenico Angelo's school in London codified much of modern technique. The Italian and French schools dominated the 19th century, and fencing was included in the first modern Olympics in Athens in 1896.

Weapons

Three weapons are used in modern sport fencing:

  • The foil, a light thrusting weapon targeting the torso, governed by right-of-way rules.
  • The épée, a heavier thrusting weapon targeting the whole body, with no right-of-way; double touches are possible.
  • The sabre, a cutting and thrusting weapon targeting everything above the waist, also governed by right-of-way.

Technique

Fencers move on a metallic strip called a piste, 14 metres long and 1.5 metres wide. Fundamental skills include the advance, retreat, lunge, parry, and riposte. Referees use electrical scoring apparatus: wires run through the fencer's clothing to register valid hits with lights and sounds.

Competitions

Major events include the World Fencing Championships, the World Cup circuit, and the Olympic Games. Historically dominant nations include Italy, France, Hungary, Russia, and the former Soviet Union; China and the United States have become major powers in the 21st century. Famous fencers include Edoardo Mangiarotti of Italy, the most decorated fencer in Olympic and World Championship history, and Hungarian sabreur Aladar Gerevich, winner of seven consecutive Olympic team gold medals.
