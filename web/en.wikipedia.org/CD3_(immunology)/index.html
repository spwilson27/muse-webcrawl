CD3 (immunology)

In immunology, the CD3 (cluster of differentiation 3) complex is a protein complex and T cell co-receptor that is involved in activating both cytotoxic T cells and T helper cells. It is composed of four distinct chains: CD3γ, CD3δ and two CD3ε chains, which associate with the T cell receptor (TCR) and the ζ-chain.

Function

The CD3 complex transduces the signal generated when the TCR recognises antigen presented by major histocompatibility complex molecules. Its cytoplasmic tails contain immunoreceptor tyrosine-based activation motifs (ITAMs), which are phosphorylated by the kinase Lck, initiating the signalling cascade that leads to T cell activation.

Clinical significance

CD3 is a defining marker of T lymphocytes and is used in immunohistochemistry to identify T cells in tissue. Antibodies against CD3, such as muromonab-CD3 (OKT3), were among the first monoclonal antibodies used clinically to suppress T cell function in organ transplantation. CD3 is also targeted by bispecific antibodies that redirect T cells against tumour cells, and it forms part of the target biology of CAR T cell signalling domains.
