The Circuit des 24 Heures du Mans, also known as the Circuit de la Sarthe, is a semi-permanent motorsport race course near Le Mans, France, best known as the venue of the 24 Hours of Le Mans endurance race, first held in 1923. At 13.626 km (8.467 mi) it is one of the longest circuits in use, combining purpose-built track with closed public roads.

The circuit runs from the permanent pit straight past the Dunlop Curve and the Esses to Tertre Rouge, then down the famous Mulsanne Straight (Ligne droite des Hunaudières) — over 5 km of public road where prototypes once reached more than 400 km/h before two chicanes were added in 1990. The lap continues through Mulsanne Corner, Indianapolis and the Arnage corner, the slowest point on the track, then the Porsche Curves and the Ford Chicanes back to the start.

History

The layout has been modified many times for safety, notably after the 1955 Le Mans disaster, the worst accident in motor racing history. The shorter Bugatti Circuit, built in 1965 within the main track, hosts the French motorcycle Grand Prix and other events. The circuit is operated by the Automobile Club de l'Ouest (ACO).
