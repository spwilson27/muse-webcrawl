Capitulations of Granada

The Capitulations of Granada (also known as the Treaty of Granada) were signed on 25 November 1491 between Boabdil, the last Nasrid sultan of Granada, and King Ferdinand II of Aragon and Queen Isabella I of Castile. The treaty ended the Granada War and provided for the surrender of the Emirate of Granada, the last Muslim polity in the Iberian Peninsula, which took place on 2 January 1492.

Terms

The capitulations contained 67 articles guaranteeing the Muslims of Granada — the Mudéjares — the right to keep their religion, laws, customs, property, and weapons, and exempting them from certain taxes. Boabdil was granted lordship over the Alpujarras region. These protections were progressively eroded in the following decade: forced conversions ordered under Cardinal Cisneros provoked the Rebellion of the Alpujarras (1499–1501), after which the capitulations were declared void and Muslims in Castile were ordered to convert or emigrate in 1502.

Significance

The fall of Granada completed the centuries-long process known as the Reconquista and, in the same year as Columbus's first voyage, marked 1492 as a turning point in Spanish and European history.
