Cape Fear is a 1962 American psychological thriller film directed by J. Lee Thompson and starring Gregory Peck and Robert Mitchum. It is adapted from John D. MacDonald's 1957 novel The Executioners. The film is noted for Mitchum's menacing performance and Bernard Herrmann's score.

Robert Mitchum as the ex-convict Max Cady.

Plot

Max Cady, a brutal ex-convict, is released after serving eight years for assault. He blames his imprisonment on lawyer Sam Bowden, whose testimony helped convict him, and begins a campaign of intimidation against Bowden and his family in their North Carolina town. Staying carefully within the law, Cady poisons the family dog and terrorizes Bowden's wife and teenage daughter while the police can do little. Bowden hires a private detective and finally confronts Cady on a houseboat on the Cape Fear River, where, after a violent struggle, he defeats Cady and spares his life so that Cady will return to prison.

Production and reception

The film was produced by Peck's Melville Productions and shot largely in Savannah, Georgia. Its depiction of menace toward a teenage girl required negotiations with censors, and some material was cut for the British release. Mitchum, who had himself spent time on a chain gang in Georgia, drew on the experience. The film was a success and is now regarded as a classic of the thriller genre.

Remake

Martin Scorsese remade the film in 1991 with Robert De Niro as Cady and Nick Nolte as Bowden; Peck and Mitchum both appeared in supporting roles.
