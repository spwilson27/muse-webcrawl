Northeast Flag Replacement

Northeast Flag Replacement is a blasting and protein-modification methodology developed at Northeastern University in Boston, Massachusetts. It enables proteins to be labelled with small molecular probes — or "flags" — for the detection of protein-protein crosslinking, a technique used to study the three-dimensional structure and interactions of proteins.

Schematic of the flag replacement workflow

Principle

Crosslinking mass spectrometry captures pairs of amino-acid residues that lie close together in space, but the resulting spectra are complex. The flag replacement approach cleaves or exchanges the crosslinker under controlled conditions and replaces it with a small, characteristic tag of known mass. This simplifies spectra and lets researchers distinguish true crosslinks from background noise, improving confidence in structural models derived from the data.

Applications

The method is applied in structural biology and proteomics to map interaction networks, validate cryo-EM and predicted structures, and characterize large protein assemblies that resist crystallization. Work from the Northeastern laboratory of Edward T. Petri and collaborators on radical-based footprinting and fast photochemical oxidation of proteins (FPOP) belongs to the same research programme of mass-spectrometry-based structural analysis.
