The 800 metres is a common track running event, the shortest of the middle-distance races. It is run over two laps of a standard 400-metre track. The event combines the speed of a sprinter with the endurance and tactics of a distance runner, and is often described as one of the most demanding races in athletics.

Race format

Runners start in lanes and, in most championship races, run the first 100 metres in lanes before breaking for the inside. Positioning through the first lap is critical; races frequently become tactical, with a slow first lap followed by a sprint finish, or fast front-running as in David Rudisha's world-record Olympic victory at London 2012.

History

The event has been in the men's Olympic program since the first modern Games in 1896. Women first ran it at the 1928 Amsterdam Games, but it was removed after claims it was too strenuous and not restored until 1960. Jarmila Kratochvílová's women's world record of 1:53.28, set in 1983, is the longest-standing individual world record in track and field.
