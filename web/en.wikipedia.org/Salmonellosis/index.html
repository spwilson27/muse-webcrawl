Salmonellosis

SpecialtyInfectious disease, gastroenterology
SymptomsDiarrhoea, fever, abdominal cramps, vomiting
Onset6 hours to 6 days after exposure

Salmonellosis is a symptomatic infection caused by bacteria of the type Salmonella. It is one of the most common foodborne illnesses worldwide. In humans, the most common symptoms are diarrhoea, fever, abdominal cramps, and vomiting. Symptoms typically occur between 12 hours and 36 hours after exposure, and last from two to seven days.

Cause and transmission

The infection is usually contracted from contaminated food of animal origin — especially eggs, poultry, meat, and unpasteurised milk — or produce contaminated with animal faeces, and by contact with infected animals including pet reptiles and poultry. Most cases are caused by the non-typhoidal serovars of Salmonella enterica, principally Typhimurium and Enteritidis. Typhoid fever, caused by serovar Typhi, is a distinct, more serious systemic illness. The infectious dose is usually large, but the bacteria's acid resistance means even modest contamination can cause illness, particularly in the elderly, infants, and the immunocompromised.

Diagnosis, treatment, and prevention

Diagnosis is by stool culture or molecular testing. Most cases resolve without specific treatment; the mainstay is oral rehydration to replace lost fluids. Antibiotics are reserved for severe or invasive disease because they can prolong carriage and resistance is increasing. Complications include dehydration, bacteraemia, and reactive arthritis. Globally, non-typhoidal salmonellosis is estimated to cause tens of millions of illnesses annually. Prevention centres on thorough cooking, avoiding cross-contamination, handwashing, and control of Salmonella in egg-laying flocks. See also Foodborne illness and Typhoid fever.
