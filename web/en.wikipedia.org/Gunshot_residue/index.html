Gunshot residue

Gunshot residue (GSR), also called cartridge discharge residue or firearm discharge residue, is the material expelled from a firearm when it is discharged. It consists principally of burnt and unburnt particles from the propellant, primer compounds containing lead, barium and antimony, and traces of metals from the bullet and cartridge case.

Formation

When a cartridge fires, hot gases escape from the muzzle and from openings in the action, carrying microscopic particles that condense and settle on nearby surfaces, including the shooter's hands, face and clothing, as well as on victims or objects close to the muzzle.

Detection

Forensic analysis most commonly uses scanning electron microscopy with energy-dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (SEM-EDX) to identify characteristic spheroidal particles containing lead, barium and antimony. Older colorimetric tests, such as the dermal nitrate (paraffin) test, are considered unreliable. Residue is fragile: it can be lost through hand washing or ordinary activity within hours, and its presence alone does not prove a person fired a weapon, since transfer and environmental sources are possible.
