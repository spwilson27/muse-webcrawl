Troposphere


The troposphere is the lowest layer of the atmosphere

The troposphere is the lowest layer of Earth's atmosphere and is where nearly all weather phenomena take place. It contains about 75% of the atmosphere's mass and almost all of its water vapor and aerosols.

Structure

The troposphere extends from Earth's surface to an average height of about 12 km—higher at the equator (up to 18 km) and lower at the poles (around 6–8 km). Temperature decreases with altitude at an average rate of about 6.5 °C per kilometre, until the tropopause, the boundary with the stratosphere above. The layer is heated mainly from below, by the Sun-warmed surface, which drives convection and the circulation that produces clouds and storms.

Composition and effects

Human activities influence the troposphere through emissions of pollutants and greenhouse gases; tropospheric ozone, formed by photochemical reactions, is a harmful air pollutant near the ground, unlike the protective ozone layer in the stratosphere.
