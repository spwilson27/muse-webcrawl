The Tajikistani Civil War (1992–1997) was fought between the government of Tajikistan, backed by Russia and Uzbekistan, and the United Tajik Opposition, an alliance of democratic, liberal and Islamist forces. It followed the collapse of the Soviet Union and Tajik independence.

Course

Violence erupted in 1992 between supporters of the old-guard government and opposition demonstrators in Dushanbe. Fighting was also regional and clan-based, devastating the south and causing tens of thousands of deaths and around a million displaced people. Emomali Rahmon emerged as head of state in late 1992.

Peace settlement

UN-mediated talks produced the General Agreement on the Establishment of Peace and National Accord, signed in Moscow on 27 June 1997. The opposition received a 30% share of government posts and its fighters were integrated into the national army.
