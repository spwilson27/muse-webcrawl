Steam tractor

A steam tractor is a vehicle powered by a steam engine which is used for pulling. In North America, the term steam tractor usually refers to a type of farm tractor powered by a steam engine, but elsewhere — most notably in Britain — it usually means a traction engine, a self-propelled steam engine designed either for drawing wheeled vehicles or for powering stationary machinery by belt.

History

Steam tractors were developed from portable steam engines in the later 19th century and were widely used in farming from the 1890s until the 1920s, when internal combustion tractors displaced them. Heavy direct-ploughing engines could pull gang ploughs across large fields, while smaller "steam tractors" in North America powered threshing machines via long drive belts. Manufacturers included J. I. Case, Advance-Rumely, Hart-Parr, and the Avery Company.

Characteristics and legacy

Steam tractors were extremely heavy — many weighed 10 to 25 tons — requiring careful operation on soft ground and bridges, but they produced enormous belt horsepower and smooth torque. Today preserved machines are demonstrated at steam fairs and threshing shows, and their design directly influenced the layout of early gasoline tractors, which adopted the word "tractor" from the traction engine.
