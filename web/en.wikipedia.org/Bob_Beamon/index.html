Bob Beamon

Robert “Bob” Beamon (born 29 August 1946) is an American former track and field athlete, best known for his world-record long jump at the 1968 Mexico City Olympics. His leap of 8.90 metres (29 feet 2½ inches) shattered the existing world record by 55 centimetres and gave the language the word “Beamonesque” for an extraordinary athletic feat.

Early life

Beamon was born in South Jamaica, Queens, New York. His mother died when he was an infant, and he was raised by his grandmother. He took up the long jump at school and later attended the University of Texas at El Paso, losing his scholarship after refusing to compete against Brigham Young University in protest at its racial policies.

The 1968 Olympic jump

At Mexico City on 18 October 1968, aided by the thin altitude air and a maximum allowable tailwind of 2.0 m/s, Beamon jumped so far that the optical measuring device could not reach the mark, and officials had to use a steel tape. The record stood for almost 23 years, until Mike Powell jumped 8.95 m at the 1991 World Championships in Tokyo. Beamon's mark remains the Olympic record and the second-longest wind-legal jump in history.

Later life

Beamon never again approached his record distance and left athletics in the early 1970s. He later worked in youth sports and community development in New York, graduated from Adelphi University in 1972, and has been an ambassador for the Olympic movement.
