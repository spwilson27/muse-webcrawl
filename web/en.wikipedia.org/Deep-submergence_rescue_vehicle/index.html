Deep-submergence rescue vehicle

TypeSubmarine rescue submersible
First built1970s (US Navy DSRVs)
CapacityUp to ~24 survivors per trip

A deep-submergence rescue vehicle (DSRV) is a type of deep-submergence vehicle used to rescue personnel from disabled submarines lying on the seabed. DSRVs are transported to the scene by aircraft, truck, or a host "mother" submarine or rescue ship, then dive to the stricken vessel.

Operation

The DSRV mates with the escape hatch of the disabled submarine using a skirt that seals against the hull around the hatch. Once sealed, the space between the vehicles is drained and the hatches opened, allowing survivors to transfer. Because the rescue vehicle can carry only a limited number of people — typically up to 24 — repeated trips may be needed, and rescued crews may be transferred under pressure to deck decompression chambers to avoid decompression sickness.

History

The loss of USS Thresher in 1963 spurred the US Navy to develop the Mystic-class DSRVs in the 1970s. Similar systems have been operated by the United Kingdom (LR5, later the NATO Submarine Rescue System), Russia, China, Japan, South Korea, and others. The Kursk disaster of 2000, in which rescue efforts failed, prompted increased international cooperation in submarine rescue.
