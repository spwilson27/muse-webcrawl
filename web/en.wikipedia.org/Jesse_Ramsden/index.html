Jesse Ramsden FRS FRSE (October 6, 1735 – November 5, 1800) was a British mathematician, astronomical and scientific instrument maker, regarded as the outstanding instrument maker of the 18th century.

Career

Apprenticed first to a clothworker and then to a mathematical instrument maker in London, Ramsden set up his own business around 1762 and became famous for the precision of his instruments. He invented an influential dividing engine that mechanised the graduation of circular instruments, published its description at Parliament's direction, and received a premium from the Board of Longitude for it. His great theodolites—built for the Ordnance Survey's Principal Triangulation of Great Britain, begun under General William Roy—were engineering landmarks, as were his large equatorial telescopes and the Ramsden eyepiece still named after him. He also built the giant Shuckburgh equatorial and supplied instruments to observatories across Europe.

Honours

Ramsden was elected a Fellow of the Royal Society in 1786 and received its Copley Medal in 1795. The lunar crater Ramsden is named in his honour.
