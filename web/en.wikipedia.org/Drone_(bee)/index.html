Drone (bee)

A drone is a male honey bee. Unlike the female worker bees, drones do not forage, feed larvae, or build comb, and they lack stingers. Their sole function is to mate with an unfertilized queen during her nuptial flight; a drone dies shortly after successful mating.

Development and anatomy

Drones develop from unfertilized eggs—the haplodiploid sex-determination system of bees means drones are haploid, carrying only their mother's genes. A drone takes about 24 days to develop from egg to adult. Drones are larger and stockier than workers, with notably big compound eyes that meet at the top of the head, an adaptation for spotting queens in flight.

Behaviour

On warm afternoons, drones gather in drone congregation areas high in the air, where virgin queens arrive to mate with multiple drones. Drones are fed by workers and make up a small fraction of the colony in spring and summer. In autumn, when mating season ends and resources dwindle, workers expel the drones from the hive, and they perish.
