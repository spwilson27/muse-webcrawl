Art film

An art film, art movie, or arthouse film is typically an independent film aimed at a niche market rather than a mass market audience. It is "intended to be a serious, artistic work, often experimental and not designed for mass appeal"; "made primarily for aesthetic reasons rather than commercial profit"; and containing "unconventional or highly symbolic content".

Film critics and film studies scholars typically define an art film using a "...canon of films and those formal qualities that mark them as different from mainstream Hollywood films". The term "arthouse" is also used to describe the cinemas that show such films.

Characteristics

Art films often feature a psychological or character-driven narrative instead of a strongly goal-driven plot, ambiguous or open endings, realistic performances, and expressive cinematography. Director David Bordwell identifies traits such as realism, authorship (the director as author), and ambiguity as hallmarks of the art cinema mode of narration. Art film is often associated with auteur directors whose personal vision dominates the work.

History

Precursors of art films include Intolerance (1916) by D. W. Griffith and the works of Soviet directors such as Sergei Eisenstein. The European art cinema flourished after the Second World War with Italian neorealism (Vittorio De Sica's Bicycle Thieves, 1948) and the French New Wave (Jean-Luc Godard, François Truffaut) of the late 1950s and 1960s. Directors such as Ingmar Bergman, Federico Fellini, Michelangelo Antonioni, Akira Kurosawa, Satyajit Ray, and later Wong Kar-wai, Abbas Kiarostami and others expanded the form's international reach. With the rise of film festivals — Cannes, Venice, Berlin — art films gained institutional exhibition channels distinct from commercial theatres.

Art film vs. mainstream film

Whereas mainstream films pursue narrative clarity and emotional engagement for the broadest audience, art films may deliberately frustrate narrative expectations, dwell on atmosphere and symbolism, and invite interpretive viewing. Nonetheless, the boundary is porous, and many directors work in both modes.
