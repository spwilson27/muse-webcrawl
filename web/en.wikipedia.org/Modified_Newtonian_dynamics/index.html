Modified Newtonian dynamics

Proposed1983
ProposerMordehai Milgrom
Key parametera₀ ≈ 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s²
PurposeExplain galaxy rotation curves without dark matter

Modified Newtonian dynamics (MOND) is a theory of gravity proposed in 1983 by Israeli physicist Mordehai Milgrom as an alternative to dark matter. It modifies Newton's laws at very low accelerations — below a characteristic scale a₀ of about 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁰ m/s² — so that the gravitational attraction declines more slowly than the inverse-square law, producing the flat rotation curves observed in spiral galaxies.

Motivation

Observations show that stars in the outer regions of galaxies orbit far faster than the visible matter alone would predict. The standard explanation postulates halos of dark matter; MOND instead changes the dynamics. The theory successfully predicts the baryonic Tully–Fisher relation, linking a galaxy's total baryonic mass to the fourth power of its rotation velocity.

Difficulties

MOND struggles to explain the cosmic microwave background, galaxy cluster dynamics, and gravitational lensing without additional assumptions, and relativistic extensions such as TeVeS were developed to address these. The majority of astrophysicists favour the ΛCDM dark-matter model, though MOND remains an active minority research programme.
