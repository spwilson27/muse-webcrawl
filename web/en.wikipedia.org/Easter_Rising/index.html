Easter Rising

The Easter Rising (Irish: Éirí Amach na Cásca), also known as the Easter Rebellion, was an armed insurrection in Ireland during Easter Week in April 1916. Launched by Irish republicans against British rule, it aimed to establish an independent Irish Republic while the United Kingdom was fighting in the First World War.

Background

The Irish Republican Brotherhood planned the rising with support from Germany and Irish America. Patrick Pearse and James Connolly led the rebels; a planned German arms shipment was intercepted and the Volunteer chief of staff, Eoin MacNeill, attempted to call the rising off, reducing turnout.

The rising

On Easter Monday, about 1,600 rebels seized key buildings in Dublin, including the General Post Office, where Pearse read the Proclamation of the Irish Republic. British reinforcements, artillery and the gunboat Helga shelled rebel positions; after six days Pearse ordered a surrender to prevent further civilian deaths.

Aftermath

Fifteen leaders, including Pearse and Connolly, were executed by firing squad in May 1916, and Roger Casement was hanged in August. The executions transformed public opinion, propelling Sinn Féin to victory in Ireland in the 1918 general election and setting the stage for the Irish War of Independence.
