Hand cannon

The hand cannon is the earliest type of portable firearm and the direct ancestor of the handgun and the cannon. It consisted of a simple metal barrel, loaded with gunpowder and a projectile, ignited through a touch hole, and often mounted on a wooden pole or stock.

History

Hand cannons appeared in China by the 13th century; the Heilongjiang hand cannon, dated to about 1288, is among the oldest surviving firearms. The technology spread westward through the Islamic world to Europe, where hand cannons appear in manuscripts and artefacts from the early 14th century, such as the Loshult gun from Sweden.

Design and use

Early examples were cast in bronze or iron, muzzle-loaded, and fired by applying a hot wire, coal or slow match to the touch hole. The pole could be braced against the chest, ground or under the arm to manage recoil. Accuracy and rate of fire were poor, but the weapons were simple, intimidating and effective at short range in sieges and battles.

Development

By the 15th century the addition of stocks, serpentinelocks and triggers produced the arquebus and matchlock, beginning the evolution toward modern firearms.
