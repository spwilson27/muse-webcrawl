Evolution is the change in the heritable characteristics of biological populations over successive generations. It is the unifying theory of biology: all known life descends, with modification, from a common ancestor that lived roughly 3.5–3.8 billion years ago. Evolutionary change arises from mutation, recombination and other sources of genetic variation, and is shaped by natural selection, sexual selection, genetic drift and gene flow.

History

Charles Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace independently proposed natural selection as the mechanism of evolution in 1858; Darwin presented the full argument, with extensive evidence, in On the Origin of Species (1859). Darwin lacked a correct theory of inheritance; the rediscovery of Mendel's work in 1900 and the development of population genetics by Fisher, Haldane and Wright in the 1920s–30s produced the modern synthesis, uniting selection with Mendelian genetics. Molecular biology later confirmed the picture at the level of DNA sequences.

Mechanisms and outcomes

Natural selection increases the frequency of traits that improve survival and reproduction in a given environment; adaptation is its product. Genetic drift changes allele frequencies by chance, especially in small populations. When populations become reproductively isolated, speciation can occur. Over geological time, evolution has produced the entire diversity of life, punctuated by mass extinctions such as the one that ended the non-avian dinosaurs 66 million years ago.

Evidence

Evidence includes the fossil record and transitional forms, comparative anatomy and embryology, biogeography, observed evolution in the laboratory and field (from Darwin's finches to antibiotic resistance), and genomic comparisons showing shared ancestry written into DNA, including endogenous retroviruses and pseudogenes shared between species.

Applications

Evolutionary biology underpins medicine (vaccine and drug design, understanding resistance), agriculture (breeding, pesticide resistance management), conservation, and computer science (evolutionary algorithms). Within science there is no significant controversy about the fact of evolution, though its mechanisms and tempo remain active research topics.
