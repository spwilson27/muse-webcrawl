Florida Panhandle

The Florida Panhandle is the northwestern part of the U.S. state of Florida. It is a strip of land roughly 200 miles (320 km) long and 50 to 100 miles (80 to 160 km) wide, lying between Alabama on the north and west, Georgia on the north, and the Gulf of Mexico to the south. Its eastern boundary is arbitrarily defined. Major cities include Pensacola, Panama City, and Tallahassee, the state capital. The Panhandle lies in the Central Time Zone, unlike the rest of Florida, and is culturally closer to the Deep South than to peninsular Florida.

History

The region was part of Spanish Florida and then of the British colony of West Florida, whose capital was Pensacola. There were recurring proposals in the 19th century to cede the area west of the Apalachicola River to Alabama, which sought a Gulf coastline, but they were never enacted.

Geography and tourism

The Gulf coast of the Panhandle is famous for its fine white quartz sand beaches; the stretch marketed as the Emerald Coast includes Destin, Fort Walton Beach, and Panama City Beach, long a major spring-break and family vacation destination. Inland are rolling red-clay hills and pine forests, unlike the flat limestone terrain of the peninsula. The region hosts major military installations including Eglin Air Force Base, the largest air force base in the world by area, Naval Air Station Pensacola, and Tyndall Air Force Base.
