Michel Aflaq

Michel Aflaq (Arabic: ميشيل عفلق; 9 January 1910 – 23 June 1989) was a Syrian philosopher, sociologist, and Arab nationalist. He is credited with founding the Ba'athist movement together with Salah al-Din al-Bitar and Zaki al-Arsuzi, and his writings supplied the ideological core of Ba'athism: Arab unity, freedom from foreign domination, and Arab socialism.

Early life and education

Aflaq was born in Damascus to a Greek Orthodox Christian family. He studied history at the Sorbonne in Paris from 1928 to 1932, where he met al-Bitar and was exposed to nationalist, socialist, and communist ideas. Returning to Syria, he worked as a teacher and journalist while organizing against the French Mandate.

Founding the Ba'ath

In the 1940s Aflaq and al-Bitar built the Arab Ba'ath ("Resurrection") movement, holding its founding congress in Damascus in 1947. His essays, later collected in Fi Sabil al-Ba'ath ("On the Road to Resurrection"), argued that the Arab nation was a single eternal entity whose decline could be reversed only by a revolutionary vanguard party and by treating Islam as the supreme cultural achievement of the Arab spirit—an inclusive framing designed to unite Arabs across religions.

Later life

Aflaq briefly served as Minister of Education in 1949. After the Ba'ath took power in Syria and Iraq in 1963, he became secretary-general of the party's National Command, but the 1966 Syrian coup ousted him, and he fled. He later settled in Iraq, where the rival Iraqi Ba'ath honored him as the movement's founder while he exercised little real power. He died in Paris in 1989 and was buried in Baghdad.
