Saddam Hussein

Saddam Hussein (28 April 1937 – 30 December 2006) was an Iraqi politician who served as President of Iraq from 1979 until 2003. A leading member of the Iraqi Ba'ath Party, he built a totalitarian dictatorship marked by a pervasive cult of personality, wars against Iran and Kuwait, and widespread human-rights abuses. He was deposed by the US-led invasion of 2003, tried by an Iraqi tribunal, and executed by hanging.

Rise to power

Saddam joined the Ba'ath Party in 1957 and took part in the failed 1959 assassination attempt against Prime Minister Abd al-Karim Qasim. After the Ba'ath seized power in 1968, he became deputy to President Ahmed Hassan al-Bakr and built a formidable security apparatus. In 1979 he forced al-Bakr's resignation, became president, and purged the party leadership.

Presidency

Saddam nationalized Iraq's oil wealth and funded extensive development, but his rule relied on terror. In 1980 he invaded Iran, beginning the eight-year Iran–Iraq War; during the conflict his regime used chemical weapons, including against Kurdish civilians at Halabja in 1988, and conducted the genocidal Anfal campaign against Iraqi Kurds. His 1990 invasion of Kuwait triggered the Gulf War, in which a US-led coalition expelled Iraqi forces in 1991. Iraq then endured a decade of sanctions and no-fly zones.

Downfall and death

In March 2003 the United States and allies invaded Iraq, alleging possession of weapons of mass destruction that were never found. Saddam was captured in December 2003 near Tikrit, tried for crimes against humanity over the 1982 Dujail massacre, convicted, and hanged on 30 December 2006.
