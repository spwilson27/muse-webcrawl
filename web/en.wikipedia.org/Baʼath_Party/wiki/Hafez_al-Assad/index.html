Hafez al-Assad

Hafez al-Assad (6 October 1930 – 10 June 2000) was a Syrian statesman and military officer who served as President of Syria from 1971 until his death in 2000. A member of the Ba'ath Party and an Alawite, he seized power in the 1970 "Corrective Movement" and built a highly centralized authoritarian state that his family would rule for over five decades.

Early life and rise

Assad was born in Qardaha in the Alawite coastal region of Syria. He joined the Ba'ath Party as a teenager and entered the military academy at Homs, training as an air force pilot. He was part of the Military Committee that brought the Ba'ath to power in 1963, became commander of the air force, and then Minister of Defence in 1966.

Presidency

After defeating his rival Salah Jadid in 1970, Assad consolidated power through the Ba'ath Party, the security services, and a cultivated cult of personality. His forces intervened in Lebanon's civil war from 1976, establishing long Syrian dominance there. In 1982 his government crushed an Islamist uprising in Hama, killing thousands. Abroad, Assad positioned Syria as a frontline state against Israel, fought in the 1973 October War, and later joined the US-led coalition against Iraq in the 1991 Gulf War.

Death and legacy

Assad died of a heart attack on 10 June 2000 and was succeeded by his son Bashar al-Assad. His rule brought stability to a Syria long plagued by coups, but at the cost of pervasive repression, and the dynasty he founded endured until its collapse in December 2024.
