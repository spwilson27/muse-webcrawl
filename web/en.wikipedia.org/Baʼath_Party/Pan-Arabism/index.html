Pan-Arabism and the Ba'ath Party

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Pan-Arabism is a nationalist ideology that advocates the political unification of the Arab world, from the Atlantic Ocean to the Arabian Sea. It was a central pillar of the Ba'ath Party, founded in Damascus in 1947 by Michel Aflaq, Salah al-Din al-Bitar and Zaki al-Arsuzi.

Ideology

The Ba'athist slogan "Unity, Freedom, Socialism" placed Arab unity first: the party held that the Arab nation was a single eternal entity artificially divided by colonial borders, and that a vanguard party organised across those borders would lead a renaissance (ba'ath means "resurrection" or "renaissance").

History

Ba'athist movements took power in Syria (1963) and Iraq (1968), but the two branches became bitter rivals, each claiming to be the legitimate heir of the founding doctrine. The rivalry between the Syrian and Iraqi Ba'ath regimes demonstrated the practical limits of pan-Arab unity, and the ideology declined after the defeat of 1967 and the death of Egypt's Gamal Abdel Nasser, pan-Arabism's most prominent champion outside the party.
