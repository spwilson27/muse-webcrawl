Zaki al-Arsuzi

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Zaki al-Arsuzi (Arabic: زكي الأرسوزي‎; c. 1900 – 1968) was a Syrian philosopher, philologist and Arab nationalist thinker. He is regarded as one of the principal founders of Arab nationalism and a co-founder of the Ba'ath Party, although his role was later eclipsed by that of Michel Aflaq and Salah al-Din al-Bitar.

Life

Born in Latakia into an Alawite family, al-Arsuzi studied at the Sorbonne in Paris. Returning to Syria, he taught philosophy and became active in nationalist politics in Alexandretta, organising resistance to the province's transfer to Turkey. In 1940 he founded the Arab Ba'ath ("Resurrection") movement and its associated party, one of several groups that merged in 1947 to form the Arab Ba'ath Party.

Thought

Al-Arsuzi emphasised language and culture as the foundations of the Arab nation, arguing in works such as The Genius of Arabic in Its Tongue that the Arabic language embodied the nation's spirit. After the 1963 Ba'athist coup in Syria he was honoured as the party's intellectual father, partly to counter the Iraqi branch's loyalty to Aflaq.
