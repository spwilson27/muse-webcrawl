Crypsis

Crypsis
Field: Evolutionary ecology
Types: Visual, olfactory, auditory camouflage
Related: Mimicry, countershading

Crypsis is the ability of an animal to avoid detection by other animals. It is a form of camouflage and deceptive strategy used both by prey avoiding predators and by predators ambushing prey.

Methods

Visual crypsis includes background matching, disruptive coloration that breaks up outlines, countershading, and counter-illumination in marine animals. Some animals actively change appearance — chameleons, octopuses, and winter-coated hares and ptarmigans. Other senses matter too: prey may suppress odors or sounds, and some moths are acoustically cryptic to bat echolocation.

Distinctions

Crypsis differs from mimicry, in which an animal resembles a specific other object or organism, and from simple concealment by hiding. The term is also used in ecology for cryptic species that are hard to distinguish.
