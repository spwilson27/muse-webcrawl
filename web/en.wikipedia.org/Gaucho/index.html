Gaucho

A gaucho is a skilled horseman of the South American pampas, the grasslands of Argentina, Uruguay, Paraguay, and southern Brazil. Gauchos were traditionally nomadic cattle herders, renowned for their horsemanship, independence, and distinctive dress, and they became a national symbol in Argentina and Uruguay.

Life and culture

Gauchos emerged in the eighteenth century among mixed European and Indigenous populations who hunted the vast herds of feral cattle and horses on the plains. Their traditional clothing includes baggy trousers (bombachas), a poncho, a wide leather belt with coins, and a knife (facón); they traditionally drink mate through a metal straw and use boleadoras — weighted cords — to catch cattle.

Historical role

Gaucho fighters played a major part in the South American wars of independence and later in the civil wars of the nineteenth century, often following caudillo leaders. As ranching was fenced and commercialised, the free gaucho way of life declined, but the figure was romanticised in literature such as José Hernández's epic poem Martín Fierro (1872).
