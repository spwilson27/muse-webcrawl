Apraxia of speech

Apraxia of speech
SpecialtySpeech-language pathology, neurology
SymptomsEffortful, inconsistent speech sound errors; groping articulatory movements
CausesStroke, trauma, neurodegeneration

Apraxia of speech (AOS) is a motor speech disorder in which a person has difficulty planning and programming the movements needed for speech, in the absence of muscle weakness or paralysis. Speech is typically slow and effortful, with inconsistent sound errors, distorted substitutions, and visible groping for articulatory positions. Acquired apraxia of speech most often results from stroke affecting the left frontal lobe, including the insula and Broca's area, while a progressive form occurs in some neurodegenerative conditions.

Characteristics

Hallmark features include inconsistent errors across repeated attempts at the same word, difficulty increasing with word length and complexity, disturbed prosody, and relatively preserved automatic speech such as greetings. It is distinguished from dysarthria, in which errors reflect impaired muscle execution and are more consistent, and from aphasia, which is a language disorder.

Treatment

Speech-language therapy uses motor learning approaches, including intensive drill on sounds and words, integral stimulation ("watch me, listen to me, say it with me"), and articulatory-kinematic cues. In severe cases, augmentative and alternative communication may supplement spoken output.
