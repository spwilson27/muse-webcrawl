The Global Methane Pledge is an international initiative launched by the United States and the European Union at the COP26 climate summit in Glasgow in November 2021. Participating countries commit to a collective goal of reducing global methane emissions by at least 30% from 2020 levels by 2030.

Rationale

Methane is a potent greenhouse gas, more than 80 times as powerful as carbon dioxide over a 20-year period, though it persists in the atmosphere for only about a decade. Because cutting methane yields fast climate benefits, scientists and the UN Environment Programme have described it as the strongest lever available to slow near-term warming; the pledge's target is estimated to shave at least 0.2 °C off projected warming by 2050. Major sources include oil and gas operations, coal mines, landfills, livestock and rice cultivation.

Implementation

The pledge is voluntary and not legally binding. Participants agree to develop national action plans, improve emissions inventories and pursue sectoral measures such as leak detection and repair in the energy sector and reductions in waste and agriculture. At COP27 and COP28 the initiative expanded its membership and financing, and launched sectoral "pathways"; some major emitters, including China, India and Russia, have not joined, though China issued its own methane action plan in 2023.
