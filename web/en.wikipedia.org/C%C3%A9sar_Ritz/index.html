César Ritz

César Ritz
Born: 23 February 1850, Niederwald, Switzerland
Died: 24 October 1918, Küssnacht, Switzerland
Occupation: Hotelier

César Ritz, born Cäsar Ritz (23 February 1850 – 24 October 1918), was a Swiss hotelier and founder of several hotels, most famously the Hôtel Ritz in Paris and the Ritz and Carlton Hotels in London (the forerunners of the modern Ritz-Carlton Hotel Company). He was an early celebrity hotelier and developer of luxury hotels. His nickname was "king of hoteliers, and hotelier to kings", and it is from his name and that of his hotels that the term ritzy derives.

Early life and career

Ritz was born in Niederwald, Switzerland, the youngest of thirteen children in a peasant family. He began his career as a waiter and worked in Parisian hotels and restaurants before managing hotels in Monte Carlo, Lucerne and elsewhere. In 1888 he opened a restaurant in Baden-Baden with the chef Auguste Escoffier, beginning a famous partnership.

The Ritz hotels

With Escoffier as his chef, Ritz opened the Hôtel Ritz, Paris, in 1898, setting new standards of luxury, hygiene and service — including private bathrooms and electric lighting. The Ritz Hotel, London, followed in 1906. Ritz's innovations transformed the grand hotel industry, and his name became synonymous with luxury hospitality.
