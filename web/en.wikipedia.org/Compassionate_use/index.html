Compassionate use

Compassionate use, or expanded access, is a pathway for patients with an immediately life-threatening or serious disease to gain access to an investigational medical product — a drug, biologic, vaccine, or medical device — outside of clinical trials, when no comparable or satisfactory alternative therapy exists. Because the product has not been approved, its safety and effectiveness are not yet established.

United States

In the United States, expanded access is regulated by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) under mechanisms such as the emergency use IND, individual patient IND, and treatment IND for wider use. The manufacturer must agree to supply the product, a physician must sponsor the request, and an institutional review board must approve. The FDA's Project Facilitate, launched in 2019, assists oncologists with single-patient requests. The federal Right to Try Act of 2018 created an additional pathway allowing eligible patients to seek certain investigational drugs without FDA authorisation, though it has been little used in practice.

Elsewhere and ethics

The European Union provides for compassionate use programmes coordinated through the European Medicines Agency, with individual member states running named-patient schemes. Ethical debate centres on balancing hope for desperate patients against the risk of unproven treatments, the fairness of access (media-savvy campaigns can skew who receives scarce supplies), possible discouragement of clinical-trial enrolment, and whether and how data from compassionate use should inform approval decisions.
