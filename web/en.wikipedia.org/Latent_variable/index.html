Latent variable

In statistics, latent variables (from Latin latent, present participle of lateo, "lie hidden") are variables that can only be inferred indirectly through a mathematical model of other observable variables that can be directly observed or measured. Mathematical models that aim to explain observed variables in terms of latent variables are called latent variable models.

Examples

Classic examples include psychological constructs such as intelligence, attitudes, and personality traits, which cannot be measured directly but are inferred from test scores and questionnaire responses; economic concepts such as quality of life and business confidence; and factors extracted in factor analysis. Observed variables linked to a latent variable are called indicators or manifest variables.

Models

Important latent variable models include factor analysis, item response theory (Rasch models), latent class analysis, latent profile analysis, hidden Markov models, and structural equation modeling, which combines measurement and structural components. In Bayesian machine learning, latent variables underlie topic models such as latent Dirichlet allocation and variational autoencoders. Identification—the ability to estimate latent effects uniquely—requires sufficient indicators and model constraints and is a central technical concern.
