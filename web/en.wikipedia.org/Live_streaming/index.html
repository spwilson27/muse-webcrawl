Live streaming

Live streaming is the real-time delivery of digital media over the internet for immediate consumption. It encompasses live video broadcasts by professional outlets and individual creators, live audio, and real-time data feeds. Unlike downloads or on-demand streaming, live streaming is transmitted and watched (or listened to) as events unfold, typically with interactive features such as live chat.

History

Experiments in internet audio streaming began in the early 1990s — the band Severe Tire Damage performed what is often cited as the first live internet concert in 1993 — and RealNetworks' RealAudio (1995) and RealVideo made streaming practical for early web users. Webcam culture grew through sites like JenniCam (1996) and Stickam. Justin.tv (2007) popularized personal live video; its gaming offshoot, Twitch (2011), became the dominant game-streaming platform and was acquired by Amazon in 2014. YouTube Live, Facebook Live, Instagram Live, Periscope (later absorbed into Twitter/X), and TikTok LIVE brought broadcasting to mainstream social media, while e-commerce live shopping became a major industry in China through platforms such as Taobao Live.

Technology

Encoders compress camera and microphone input (commonly H.264 or newer codecs) and send it via protocols such as RTMP or SRT to a platform's ingest servers, which transcode it into multiple bitrates and deliver it to viewers through content delivery networks using adaptive streaming protocols such as HLS and MPEG-DASH. Modern low-latency technologies (WebRTC, LL-HLS) have reduced delays from tens of seconds to near real time, enabling auctions, gaming, and interactive shows. Popular broadcasting software includes OBS Studio.

Uses and culture

Live streaming covers video game play and esports, live news and sports, concerts, religious services, education, product launches, and "IRL" lifestyle broadcasting. Creator economies have grown around subscriptions, donations, and virtual gifts. The medium also raises moderation challenges, since harmful content may be broadcast before it can be reviewed.
