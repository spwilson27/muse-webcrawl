Retraction

Retraction refers to the act of taking back or withdrawing something previously said, published, or done. The term is used in several distinct senses, including in academic publishing, journalism and law, anatomy and topology.

A journal retraction notice

Academic publishing

In academic publishing, a retraction is the act by which a published paper in a journal is withdrawn because its findings are no longer considered trustworthy. Retractions may result from scientific misconduct such as fabrication, falsification or plagiarism, from honest error, or from other serious problems such as unethical research or duplicate publication. The retracted paper usually remains available, marked as retracted, together with a retraction notice explaining the reason.

Full retraction is distinguished from a correction (or erratum), in which only part of a paper is amended, and from an expression of concern, in which editors alert readers to unresolved concerns. The Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE) publishes guidelines on when retraction is appropriate. The website Retraction Watch, founded in 2010, tracks retractions and related issues in scientific publishing.

Notable retractions include the 1998 Lancet paper by Andrew Wakefield and colleagues that falsely suggested a link between the MMR vaccine and autism; it was fully retracted in 2010. Studies suggest the number of retractions has grown over time, reflecting both an increase in the volume of published research and greater scrutiny.

Journalism

In journalism, a retraction is a public statement by a news organisation withdrawing a previously reported story, or part of it, because it was factually incorrect. Retractions differ from corrections, which fix minor errors without withdrawing the substance of a report. Media law in some jurisdictions treats a prompt retraction as mitigating damages in defamation cases.

Mathematics

In topology, a retraction is a continuous mapping from a topological space into a subspace that fixes every point of the subspace; the subspace is then called a retract of the larger space. In category theory, a retraction is a morphism that is a right inverse of another morphism, also called a section's counterpart.

Anatomy and physiology

In anatomy, retraction is the movement of a body part backward or toward the body, the opposite of protraction; for example, retraction of the scapula draws the shoulder blades together. In surgery and dentistry, retraction refers to holding tissue aside to expose an operative field, often with an instrument called a retractor. In wound care, retraction can also describe the drawing back of wound edges.
