Education in Germany

Education in Germany is primarily the responsibility of the individual federal states (Länder), with the federal government playing a minor role. Schooling is compulsory, generally from age six to eighteen, and public schools are free, as is most university education. The German system is distinguished by early tracking into different secondary school types and by its dual system of vocational training, which combines apprenticeships in companies with part-time vocational schooling.

Primary and secondary education

Children attend the Grundschule (primary school) for four years (six in Berlin and Brandenburg). After primary school, pupils are sorted — based on grades, teacher recommendation, and parental choice, varying by state — into the Hauptschule, Realschule, or Gymnasium, though many states have merged the first two into comprehensive or unified secondary schools. The Gymnasium, lasting eight or nine years, concludes with the Abitur, the qualification for university entrance. The Realschule leads to a mid-level leaving certificate after grade 10, and the Hauptschule prepares pupils mainly for vocational training.

Vocational training

The dual system (duale Ausbildung) trains roughly half of each age cohort in recognized occupations: trainees work in a company several days a week and attend a vocational school (Berufsschule) for the remainder, over two to three and a half years, earning a chamber-certified qualification. The system is widely credited with Germany's comparatively low youth unemployment.

Higher education

Germany's universities include historic institutions such as Heidelberg (1386) and Munich's LMU, alongside Fachhochschulen (universities of applied sciences). Public universities charge no or nominal tuition for most students, including internationals in many states. Degrees were converted to the bachelor's/master's structure under the Bologna Process, though traditional qualifications persist in medicine, law, and teaching. Germany consistently ranks among the most popular destinations for international students.

History

Prussian reforms under Wilhelm von Humboldt in the early nineteenth century established the humanistic Gymnasium and the research university model that influenced higher education worldwide. Compulsory schooling dates to eighteenth-century Prussian edicts. The system was centralized under Nazi rule, divided between East and West after 1949, and harmonized after reunification in 1990.
