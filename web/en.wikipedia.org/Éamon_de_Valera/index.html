Éamon de Valera

Éamon de Valera (14 October 1882 – 29 August 1975) was an Irish statesman and political leader who dominated 20th-century Ireland. He served several terms as head of government and was President of Ireland from 1959 to 1973.

Revolutionary period

Born in New York to an Irish mother and Spanish father, de Valera was raised in County Limerick. A mathematics teacher, he joined the Irish Volunteers and commanded a garrison in the 1916 Easter Rising; his death sentence was commuted, partly because of his American birth. He became president of Sinn Féin and of the underground Dáil government, escaped from Lincoln Prison in 1919, and toured America raising funds for the Irish Republic. He opposed the Anglo-Irish Treaty of 1921, and his side lost the ensuing civil war.

In government

De Valera founded Fianna Fáil in 1926 and won power in 1932. As Taoiseach he dismantled the Treaty's constraints, introduced the 1937 Constitution of Ireland, kept Ireland neutral during the Second World War ("the Emergency"), and pursued economic protectionism. After periods in opposition he became President in 1959, serving two terms before retiring at 90. His austere, Gaelic, Catholic vision of Ireland shaped the state for generations and remains a subject of historical debate.
