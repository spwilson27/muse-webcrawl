Last Glacial Period

The Last Glacial Period (LGP), also known colloquially as the last ice age, occurred from the end of the Last Interglacial to the end of the Younger Dryas, encompassing the period c. 115,000 – c. 11,700 years ago. It was the most recent glacial period within the Quaternary Ice Age, during which enormous ice sheets covered much of North America, northern Europe, and Asia.

Climatic development

Following the warm Eemian interglacial, temperatures declined in steps punctuated by abrupt Dansgaard–Oeschger warming events and Heinrich ice-rafting episodes. The period reached its coldest phase, the Last Glacial Maximum (LGM, about 26,500–19,000 years ago), when global sea level stood roughly 120–130 meters below present, exposing continental shelves such as Beringia—allowing human migration into the Americas—and lowering global mean temperature by roughly 6 °C. Deglaciation was interrupted by the Younger Dryas cold reversal (c. 12,900–11,700 years ago) before the abrupt onset of the warm Holocene.

Regional names

The same glacial period carries different regional names: the Wisconsin glaciation in North America, the Weichselian in northern Europe, the Würm in the Alps, the Devensian in Britain, and the Midlandian in Ireland.

Life

The period witnessed the extinction of much of the Pleistocene megafauna (woolly mammoths, woolly rhinoceroses, and giant ground sloths), the expansion of anatomically modern humans across all continents, and celebrated cave art such as that of Lascaux and Altamira.
