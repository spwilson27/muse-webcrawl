In mathematics and computational geometry, a Delaunay triangulation for a given set P of discrete points in a plane is a triangulation DT(P) such that no point in P is inside the circumcircle of any triangle in DT(P). It is named after Boris Delaunay, who described it in 1934.

Properties

Delaunay triangulations maximize the minimum angle of all the angles of the triangles, tending to avoid sliver triangles. The triangulation is unique when no four points lie on a common circle. Its dual graph is the Voronoi diagram of the same point set: the circumcentres of Delaunay triangles are the vertices of the Voronoi cells.

Algorithms

Many algorithms exist for computing Delaunay triangulations, including the Bowyer–Watson incremental algorithm, edge-flipping methods, and divide-and-conquer approaches. In two dimensions they run in O(n log n) time. The problem generalises to higher dimensions using circumspheres, though slivers become harder to avoid.

Applications

Delaunay triangulations are widely used in finite element mesh generation, terrain modelling, computer graphics, and interpolation of scattered data, because their well-shaped triangles produce numerically stable results.
