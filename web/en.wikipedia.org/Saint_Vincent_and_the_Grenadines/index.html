Saint Vincent and the Grenadines is an island country in the eastern Caribbean, comprising the main island of Saint Vincent and a chain of smaller islands, the northern Grenadines, stretching south toward Grenada. Its capital, Kingstown, sits on the southwestern coast of Saint Vincent.

Geography

Saint Vincent is a volcanic island dominated by La Soufrière, an active volcano rising to 1,234 m. The Grenadines include Bequia, Mustique, Canouan, Mayreau, Union Island and Palm Island, prized for their beaches and reefs. The Tobago Cays form a protected marine park of exceptional natural beauty.

History

The Kalinago fiercely resisted European settlement; Saint Vincent also became a refuge for shipwrecked and escaped Africans, forming the Garifuna ("Black Carib") community. After the Second Carib War (1795–1797), led by chief Joseph Chatoyer, thousands of Garifuna were exiled to Roatán, Honduras. Britain consolidated control through the 19th century; slavery was abolished in the 1830s. The country became independent in 1979 as a Commonwealth realm.

Economy

Bananas have historically been the main export, alongside arrowroot (Saint Vincent is a leading producer). Tourism, especially yacht tourism in the Grenadines, is a growing pillar. The 2021 eruption of La Soufrière displaced some 20,000 people and heavily damaged northern farms, from which the country has been recovering.
