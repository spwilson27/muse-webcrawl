Narrator

A narrator is the person or voice who tells a story to the audience within a work of fiction, film, theatre or other narrative medium. The narrator is distinct from the author: even in autobiography, the narrating self is a constructed persona. Every narrative has at least an implied narrator.

Types of narrator

Narrators may participate in the story (homodiegetic) — as its protagonist or as an observer — or stand outside it (heterodiegetic). A narrator may be omniscient, with access to all characters' thoughts, or limited. The unreliable narrator, a term coined by Wayne C. Booth, misleads the reader through bias, ignorance or deception, as in many works of Nabokov and Ishiguro. Narrators also vary in person — first, second or third — which defines the narrative mode.

In other media

Film uses voice-over narration and camera "narration"; video games may employ dynamic narrators; radio drama and audiobooks rely entirely on the narrating voice. In non-fiction, documentary narration serves a similar framing role.
