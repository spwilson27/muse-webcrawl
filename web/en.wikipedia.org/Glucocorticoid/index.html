Glucocorticoid

Glucocorticoids are a class of corticosteroid hormones produced by the zona fasciculata of the adrenal cortex. The principal human glucocorticoid is cortisol. They regulate metabolism, the stress response and the immune system.

Glucocorticoid
Class: Corticosteroid
Main natural hormone: Cortisol
Synthetic examples: Prednisone, dexamethasone, hydrocortisone
Receptor: Glucocorticoid receptor

Effects

Glucocorticoids raise blood glucose by promoting gluconeogenesis, mobilise amino acids and fats, and powerfully suppress inflammation and immunity. Secretion follows a circadian rhythm controlled by the hypothalamic–pituitary–adrenal axis.

Medical use

Synthetic glucocorticoids treat asthma, allergies, autoimmune and inflammatory diseases, adrenal insufficiency and some cancers, and are adjuncts in anaphylaxis. Long-term use can cause Cushing-like effects: weight gain, diabetes, osteoporosis, hypertension and infection risk, contrasted with mineralocorticoids such as aldosterone.
