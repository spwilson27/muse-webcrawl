Erdős–Stone theorem

In extremal graph theory, the Erdős–Stone theorem is an asymptotic result generalising Turán's theorem to arbitrary forbidden subgraphs. It was proved by Paul Erdős and Arthur Stone in 1946 and is sometimes described as the fundamental theorem of extremal graph theory.

Statement

The theorem states that the extremal number ex(n, H) — the greatest number of edges in an n-vertex graph containing no subgraph isomorphic to H — satisfies ex(n, H) = (1 − 1/(χ(H) − 1) + o(1)) · n²/2, where χ(H) is the chromatic number of the forbidden graph H. Thus, asymptotically, only the chromatic number of H matters, and the extremal graphs resemble Turán graphs on χ(H) − 1 parts.

Relation to Turán's theorem

For complete graphs H = Kr+1, the chromatic number is r + 1 and the formula recovers the edge count of the Turán graph given by Turán's theorem. The Erdős–Stone theorem extends this to all H with χ(H) ≥ 3, while graphs with chromatic number 2 (bipartite graphs) have extremal numbers of smaller order.
