The Battle of the Seelow Heights (16–19 April 1945) was part of the Battle of Berlin, the final major offensive of the Second World War in Europe. Marshal Georgy Zhukov's 1st Belorussian Front assaulted the Seelow Heights, the strongest German defensive position east of Berlin, held by Army Group Vistula under General Gotthard Heinrici.

Background

The heights rise above the Oderbruch floodplain about 90 kilometres east of Berlin. Heinrici, anticipating the attack, withdrew most of his troops from the first line just before the Soviet artillery preparation, which consequently fell largely on empty positions. The flooded, marshy ground further hampered the attackers.

The battle

Zhukov opened with a massive bombardment and a night attack lit by searchlights, but the assault bogged down against the second German line. Frustrated by slow progress, Zhukov committed his tank armies early. After three days of heavy fighting and very high casualties on both sides, the heights were taken on 19 April, opening the road to Berlin.

Aftermath

The German 9th Army fell back and was largely destroyed in the Halbe pocket. Soviet forces encircled Berlin within days; the capital surrendered on 2 May 1945.
