Piazza Venezia

Piazza Venezia is a central hub of Rome, Italy, located at the foot of the Capitoline Hill, where several major avenues converge, including the Via dei Fori Imperiali and the Via del Corso. It takes its name from the Palazzo Venezia, the former embassy of the Republic of Venice.

Description

The square is dominated by the Victor Emmanuel II Monument (the Vittoriano or "Altare della Patria"), the enormous white marble monument built between 1885 and 1935 to honour the first king of united Italy; since 1921 it has housed the Tomb of the Italian Unknown Soldier, guarded by an eternal flame. On the west side stands the Palazzo Venezia, a Renaissance palace that was Mussolini's headquarters — he addressed crowds from its balcony — and which now contains a museum. On the north side, the Palazzo Bonaparte was home to Letizia Bonaparte, Napoleon's mother.

History

The present square took shape during the demolition campaigns of the late 19th and early 20th centuries, which cleared medieval and Renaissance buildings to create a monumental centre for the new Italian capital. Today it is one of Rome's busiest traffic intersections and a focal point of national ceremonies.
