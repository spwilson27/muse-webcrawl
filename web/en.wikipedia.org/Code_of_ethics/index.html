Code of ethics

A code of ethics is a guide of principles designed to help professionals conduct business honestly and with integrity. A code of ethics document may outline the mission and values of a business or organisation, how professionals are supposed to approach problems, the ethical principles based on the organisation's core values, and the standards to which the professional is held.

Corporate and professional codes

Many companies and professional bodies adopt codes of ethics to govern the conduct of employees and members. Examples include the medical profession's Hippocratic Oath tradition, codes of professional responsibility for lawyers, and engineering codes that hold paramount the safety, health, and welfare of the public.

Code of practice

A related concept is the code of practice, adopted by a profession or by a governmental or non-governmental organisation to regulate that profession. A code of practice may be styled as a code of professional responsibility, which will discuss difficult issues and the decisions practitioners must often make.

Religious and philosophical codes

Many religions have implicit codes of behaviour concerning personal conduct, morality, and ethics, such as the Five Precepts in Buddhism or the Ten Commandments in the Abrahamic traditions. Philosophical ethical codes, by contrast, rest on reasoned principles rather than divine authority.

Enforcement

Violations of a code of ethics may result in disciplinary action, including reprimand, suspension, or expulsion from a professional body, and in some regulated fields the loss of a licence to practise.
