Wabash River

The Wabash River is a 503-mile-long (810 km) river in the eastern United States. It rises in northwestern Ohio, flows west through Indiana — where it is the state river — and then south, forming the Illinois–Indiana border, before joining the Ohio River. It is the largest northern tributary of the Ohio River.

Course

The river begins near the headwaters of the St. Marys River in Darke and Mercer counties, Ohio, and flows past Huntington, Peru, Logansport, Lafayette, and Terre Haute in Indiana. Below Terre Haute it turns south and separates Indiana from Illinois, passing Vincennes, before its confluence with the Ohio River near the Wabash's junction with the state of Kentucky.

History

The Wabash valley was a major corridor for Native American peoples including the Miami and Wea, and later for French traders. It was the scene of significant conflicts in the Northwest Indian War, including Harmar's defeat (1790), St. Clair's defeat (1791), and the Battle of Tippecanoe (1811), fought near its tributary Tippecanoe River. The Wabash and Erie Canal, once the longest canal in North America, followed much of its course in the nineteenth century.

In culture

Indiana's state song, "On the Banks of the Wabash, Far Away" (1897), by Paul Dresser, celebrates the river and was one of the best-selling songs of its era.
