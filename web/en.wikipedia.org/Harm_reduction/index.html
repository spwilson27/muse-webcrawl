Harm reduction

Harm reduction, or harm minimization, refers to a range of public health policies and practices intended primarily to reduce the negative social and physical consequences associated with human behaviours — most prominently the use of recreational drugs — without necessarily requiring people to stop the behaviour entirely.

Drug policy

Well-known harm reduction measures include needle and syringe exchange programmes, which reduce transmission of HIV and hepatitis C; supervised injection sites; opioid substitution therapy with methadone or buprenorphine; wide availability of the overdose antidote naloxone; heroin-assisted treatment in some European countries; and drug-checking services. The approach emerged in the 1980s, notably in the Netherlands and in Merseyside, England, in response to the HIV epidemic among people who inject drugs.

Other applications

Harm reduction principles also inform policies on tobacco (nicotine replacement and e-cigarettes), alcohol (managed alcohol programmes), and sexual health ( condom distribution and safer-sex education). Critics argue such measures may enable risky behaviour, while supporters cite evidence that they reduce deaths, disease, and public costs without increasing drug use.

Organisations

Harm Reduction International and national bodies advocate for the approach, which is endorsed in varying degrees by the World Health Organization, UNAIDS, and many national governments.
