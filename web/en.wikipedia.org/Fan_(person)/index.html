Fan (person)

A fan or fanatic, sometimes also termed an aficionado or enthusiast, is a person who exhibits strong interest or admiration for something or somebody, such as a celebrity, a sport, a sports team, a genre, a politician, a book, a movie, a video game or an entertainer. Collectively, the fans of a particular object or person are referred to as its fanbase or fandom. They may show their enthusiasm in a variety of ways, such as by promoting the object of their interest, being members of a related fan club, holding or participating in fan conventions, or writing fan mail. They may also engage in creative activities ("fan labor") such as creating fanzines, writing fan fiction, making memes or fan art.

Etymology

The word "fan" is commonly traced to an abbreviation of "fanatic", itself from the Latin fanaticus, meaning "divinely inspired" or "frenzied", originally describing devotees of a temple (fanum). In late 19th-century America, "fan" became attached to followers of baseball and other sports, and then spread to popular entertainment generally.

Characteristics

Fans often organize into communities — fandoms — with their own conventions, vocabulary and creative output. Media scholar Henry Jenkins described fans as "textual poachers" who actively appropriate and remake media content. Fan activity ranges from casual spectatorship through collecting and cosplay to highly committed "stan" culture on social media. Excessive or obsessive fandom, such as stalking of celebrities, is viewed negatively and is sometimes distinguished from ordinary enthusiasm.

Types of fans

  • Sports fans — supporters of teams or athletes, often with strong regional identity.
  • Music fans — followings of bands and singers, e.g. Beatlemania and later named fandoms.
  • Media fandoms — organized science fiction and fantasy fandom, dating to the 1930s, is the model for many later communities.
  • Celebrity fans — focused on individual public figures.
