Westphalia (German: Westfalen) is a region of northwestern Germany, forming the eastern part of the state of North Rhine-Westphalia. It encompasses roughly the area between the Rhine and the Weser, including the Ruhr industrial district in the southwest, the Münsterland in the north, Ostwestfalen-Lippe in the east, and the Sauerland uplands in the south. Major cities include Dortmund, Bielefeld, Münster, and Paderborn.

History

The name derives from the Westphali, a branch of the early Saxons recorded by Charlemagne's chroniclers. In the Middle Ages much of the region was ruled by prince-bishops and by the Duchy of Westphalia under the Electors of Cologne. The Peace of Westphalia (1648), negotiated in Münster and Osnabrück at the end of the Thirty Years' War, gave its name to the modern Westphalian system of sovereign states. After 1815 Westphalia was a Prussian province; in 1946 it was merged with northern Rhineland (and later Lippe) into North Rhine-Westphalia.
