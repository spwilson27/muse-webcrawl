John Brown (abolitionist)

John Brown (May 9, 1800 – December 2, 1859) was an American abolitionist who believed armed insurrection was the only way to overthrow slavery in the United States.

A strict Calvinist who saw himself as God's instrument, Brown fought in Bleeding Kansas, where his men killed five pro-slavery settlers at Pottawatomie Creek in 1856. In October 1859 he led a raid on the federal arsenal at Harpers Ferry, Virginia, hoping to ignite a slave revolt. Captured by US Marines under Robert E. Lee, he was tried and hanged; his raid deepened sectional tensions before the Civil War, and Union soldiers marched to "John Brown's Body".
