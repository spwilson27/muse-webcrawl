The Way of the World

Written byWilliam Congreve
CharactersMirabell, Millamant, Lady Wishfort, Mrs Fainall, Fainall, Witwoud, Foible, Waitwell
Date premieredMarch 1700
Place premieredLincoln's Inn Fields Theatre, London
GenreRestoration comedy, comedy of manners

The Way of the World is a play written by the English playwright William Congreve. It premiered in early March 1700 in the theatre in Lincoln's Inn Fields in London. It is widely regarded as one of the best Restoration comedies and is still performed occasionally.

Plot

The play concerns the lovers Mirabell and Millamant, who wish to marry but must first overcome the objections of Millamant's aunt, Lady Wishfort, whose consent is required for Millamant to receive her full dowry. Mirabell plots to trick Lady Wishfort by having his servant Waitwell impersonate a fictitious uncle, Sir Rowland, and court her; meanwhile the villainous Fainall schemes to seize his wife's fortune and blackmail Lady Wishfort over her past affair with Mirabell. The famous "proviso scene" in Act IV, in which Mirabell and Millamant negotiate the terms of their marriage, is one of the most celebrated scenes in English comedy.

Reception

The play received a mixed reception on its first performance — Congreve complained that it had not been as successful as he had hoped — but its reputation grew steadily, and it is now considered the masterpiece of Restoration comedy of manners, praised for the wit and complexity of its dialogue and the subtlety of its characterisation.
