Transport in Greater Tokyo

The transport network of Greater Tokyo is among the most extensive and heavily used urban transport systems in the world, serving a metropolitan population of roughly 37–38 million. It is dominated by an exceptionally dense railway network, complemented by subways, buses, expressways, two major airports and extensive port facilities.

Railways

The backbone of the system consists of JR East commuter lines (such as the Yamanote loop line), the two subway networks (Tokyo Metro and Toei), and numerous private railway companies (including Tokyu, Odakyu, Keio, Seibu, Tobu and Keisei) whose lines radiate into the suburbs of Kanagawa, Saitama and Chiba prefectures. Through-running between subway and suburban lines is widespread. Tokyo Station is the principal intercity terminal, where Shinkansen services connect to northern and western Japan; other major hubs include Shinjuku — by some measures the world's busiest station — Ikebukuro, Shibuya, Ueno and Shinagawa.

Fares are distance-based and largely integrated through rechargeable smart cards (Suica and Pasmo), accepted across operators. Rail carries the majority of motorised trips in the region, with average load factors historically so high that station staff ("pushers") were employed to assist boarding on the most crowded lines.

Roads and air

Greater Tokyo is served by a network of tolled urban expressways (Shuto Expressway) and radial and ring national expressways, though road congestion and high parking costs keep car mode share comparatively low for a city of its size. Haneda Airport (Tokyo International) handles mostly domestic and growing international traffic close to the city centre, while Narita International Airport in Chiba serves most long-haul international flights. The Port of Tokyo and neighbouring Yokohama are major cargo gateways.
