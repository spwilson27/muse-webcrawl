Disc jockey

A disc jockey, commonly abbreviated as DJ, is a person who plays recorded music for an audience. Types of DJs include radio DJs, who present programmes on radio stations; club DJs, who mix music in nightclubs and at festivals; and mobile DJs, who perform at weddings and parties.

History

The term was coined in the 1930s for radio announcers who played gramophone records. In the 1970s, disco and hip hop culture in New York transformed the role: pioneers such as DJ Kool Herc and Grandmaster Flash developed techniques including breakbeat mixing, scratching and beat juggling using two turntables and a mixer.

Techniques

Modern DJs use turntables, CDJs or DJ software to beatmatch tracks, blend them with crossfaders and equalisers, and shape the energy of a dancefloor over a set. Turntablism treats the turntable itself as a musical instrument. Digital controllers and streaming have made DJing widely accessible, while top club and festival DJs have become headline performers in electronic dance music.
