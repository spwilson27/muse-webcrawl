Sébastien Le Prestre de Vauban

Sébastien Le Prestre, marquis de Vauban (1633 – 30 March 1707), commonly known simply as Vauban, was a French military engineer and Marshal of France who revolutionised the art of fortification and siege warfare under Louis XIV. His designs for fortresses, and his systematic methods for attacking them, dominated European warfare for more than a century.

Career

Born to an impoverished noble family in Burgundy, Vauban joined the army during the Fronde and, after being captured, entered royal service. Over a career spanning five decades he directed some 53 sieges — all successful — and designed or rebuilt roughly 160 fortresses along France's frontiers, the ring of strongholds known as the pré carré. His siegecraft, based on parallels, ricochet fire, and methodical approach trenches, made the taking of fortresses a matter of time and logistics rather than chance, and he was almost as notable for minimising casualties among his own troops.

Works and legacy

Vauban's fortifications evolved through what later writers systematised as his "three systems", characterised by angled bastions, detached ravelins, and multi-layered outworks adapted to terrain. He also wrote on engineering, agriculture, and taxation; his Projet d'une dîme royale (1707), proposing a proportional income tax, displeased the king and was suppressed. In 2008, twelve of his fortified sites were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as the Fortifications of Vauban.
