RangeMediterranean Basin

The kermes oak (Quercus coccifera) is an evergreen shrub or small tree in the beech family Fagaceae, native to the Mediterranean region. Growing typically 1–6 metres tall in maquis and garrigue scrubland, it has small, glossy, spiny-toothed holly-like leaves and acorns that mature in their second year.

Name and dye

The species takes its name from the kermes scale insects (Kermes species) that live on it. The dried bodies of the female insects were harvested in antiquity and the Middle Ages to produce a rich crimson-red dye — the original "crimson" and one source of the word "scarlet" cloth — before being displaced by cheaper cochineal from the Americas after the 16th century.

Ecology

Kermes oak is drought-tolerant, resprouts vigorously after fire and grazing, and is a characteristic plant of western Mediterranean scrub. Its acorns are an important food for wildlife, and its dense thorny growth shelters nesting birds.
