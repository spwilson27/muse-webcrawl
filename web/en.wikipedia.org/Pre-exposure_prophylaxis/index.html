Pre-exposure prophylaxis

Pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) is the use of medications to prevent the spread of disease in people who have not yet been exposed to the disease-causing agent, usually a virus. The term typically refers to the use of specific antiretroviral drugs to prevent HIV infection. When used as prescribed, PrEP is highly effective, reducing the risk of acquiring HIV from sex by about 99%.

HIV PrEP

The first regimen approved for HIV PrEP, in 2012 by the US FDA, was a daily oral combination of tenofovir disoproxil fumarate and emtricitabine (Truvada). A second oral combination, tenofovir alafenamide/emtricitabine (Descovy), was approved in 2019, and long-acting injectable cabotegravir (Apretude), given every two months, was approved in 2021. The World Health Organization recommends PrEP for populations at substantial risk of HIV infection.

Use and monitoring

PrEP is recommended for people at elevated risk, including some men who have sex with men, serodiscordant couples, sex workers and people who inject drugs. Users require baseline and periodic HIV testing, kidney function monitoring and screening for other sexually transmitted infections. PrEP does not protect against other STIs.
