Associate degree

LevelUndergraduate (post-secondary)
Typical duration2 years full-time
ProvidersCommunity colleges, junior colleges, technical colleges
AbbreviationsAA, AS, AAS

An associate degree is an undergraduate academic degree awarded by colleges and universities upon completion of a course of study typically lasting two years. It is the principal credential offered by community colleges in the United States and also exists in countries including Canada, Australia, and the Netherlands.

Types

Common forms include the Associate of Arts (AA) and Associate of Science (AS), which are often designed as transfer degrees whose credits apply toward a bachelor's degree, and the Associate of Applied Science (AAS), which is oriented toward direct entry into technical or vocational employment. Associate degrees generally require around 60 semester credit hours.

Role and outcomes

Because community colleges charge relatively low tuition and have open admissions policies, associate degrees are an important route into higher education, including for adult and first-generation students. Holders typically earn more than those with only a high school diploma, though less on average than bachelor's degree holders; many graduates stack the credential into a four-year degree.
