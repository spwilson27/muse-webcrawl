A Study in Scarlet

A Study in Scarlet
Author: Arthur Conan Doyle
Published: 1887 (Beeton's Christmas Annual)
Book edition: 1888 (Ward, Lock & Co.)
Series: Sherlock Holmes

A Study in Scarlet is an 1887 detective novel by British writer Arthur Conan Doyle. It is the first work featuring Sherlock Holmes and Dr. John Watson, and it established many of the conventions of the detective genre. The title derives from Holmes's remark about his work: "There's the scarlet thread of murder running through the colourless skein of life."

Plot

Dr. Watson, invalided home from the Second Anglo-Afghan War, is introduced to the eccentric consulting detective Sherlock Holmes, and the two take rooms at 221B Baker Street. Holmes is consulted by Scotland Yard detectives Gregson and Lestrade over a body found in an empty house in Brixton, with the word "RACHE" written in blood on the wall. Through observation and deduction Holmes identifies the murderer as Jefferson Hope, an American cab driver. The novel's second half flashes back to Utah Territory, where Hope's motive lies in the fate of his fiancée Lucy Ferrier among the Mormon settlers of Salt Lake City — a portrayal of early Mormonism that Doyle later conceded was unfair and mistaken.

Publication history

Doyle, then a young doctor, wrote the book in a few weeks in 1886. After rejections, Ward, Lock & Co. bought the copyright outright for £25 and published it in Beeton's Christmas Annual for 1887, with illustrations by the author's father, Charles Altamont Doyle. Its modest initial success grew with the later Holmes stories; first editions of the Annual are now among the most valuable items in detective fiction.
