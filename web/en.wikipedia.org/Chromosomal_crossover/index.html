Chromosomal crossover

Chromosomal crossover, or crossing over, is the exchange of genetic material between homologous chromosomes during meiosis, producing recombinant chromosomes.

Mechanism

During prophase I of meiosis, homologous chromosomes pair and align gene by gene. Enzymes such as Spo11 introduce double-strand breaks, and repair by homologous recombination can resolve as crossovers, visible as chiasmata. Typically one to several crossovers occur per chromosome pair.

Significance

Crossover shuffles alleles into new combinations, increasing genetic diversity, and helps ensure proper chromosome segregation. The frequency of recombination between genes underlies genetic linkage mapping.
