Jotunheimen National Park

Jotunheimen National Park (Norwegian: Jotunheimen nasjonalpark) is a national park in Norway, established in 1980, that encompasses the mountainous Jotunheimen range. Covering about 1,151 square kilometres (444 sq mi), it forms part of the larger Jotunheimen area of more than 3,500 square kilometres. The park contains the highest mountains in Norway and in Northern Europe, including Galdhøpiggen (2,469 m) and Glittertind (2,465 m), as well as glaciers, alpine lakes, and valleys such as Gjende and Bygdin.

Name

The name Jotunheimen means "home of the giants" (jotun being a giant or troll in Norse mythology). It was popularised by the poet Aasmund Olavsson Vinje in the 1860s, having earlier been used in the form "Jotunfjeldene" by Christian Frederik Holm and others inspired by the region's exploration by mountaineers such as William Cecil Slingsby.

Geography and nature

The park's landscape was shaped by glaciation and includes more than 250 peaks over 2,000 metres, numerous glaciers (covering roughly a tenth of the park), and clear mountain lakes. The famous Besseggen ridge walk, celebrated in Henrik Ibsen's play Peer Gynt, runs between the emerald-green Lake Gjende and the deep blue Lake Bessvatnet. Wildlife includes reindeer, wolverine, lynx, golden eagle, and gyrfalcon; alpine flora features glacier buttercup, which grows at higher elevations here than anywhere else in Europe.

Recreation

Jotunheimen is one of Norway's most popular areas for hiking, mountaineering, glacier walking, and skiing. The Norwegian Trekking Association (DNT) maintains an extensive network of marked trails and staffed lodges, including Gjendesheim, Glitterheim, and Spiterstulen. The boat service across Lake Gjende connects trailheads at Gjendesheim and Memurubu.
