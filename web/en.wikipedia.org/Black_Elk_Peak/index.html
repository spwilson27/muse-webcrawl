Black Elk Peak

Black Elk Peak is the highest natural point in South Dakota and the highest summit in the United States east of the Rocky Mountains, at 7,244 feet (2,208 m) above sea level. It lies in the Black Elk Wilderness within the Black Hills National Forest, in Pennington County.

Name

The peak was long known as Harney Peak, after General William S. Harney. In 2016 the U.S. Board on Geographic Names renamed it for Black Elk (1863–1950), the Lakota holy man for whom the summit — Hinhan Kaga, "Making of Owls" — was a sacred place where, as a boy of nine, he received the great vision recounted in Black Elk Speaks.

The summit tower

A stone fire lookout tower, built by the Civilian Conservation Corps in 1938–39, stands on the summit, which is reached by hiking trails from Sylvan Lake and other trailheads in Custer State Park and the wilderness. On clear days the view reaches into four states.
