Professional engineer

A professional engineer (PE in the United States; P.Eng. in Canada) is a licensed engineer whose credentials certify they are legally authorised to practise engineering, sign and seal engineering drawings and documents, and offer engineering services to the public in their jurisdiction. Regulation of engineers exists to protect public health, safety, and welfare by ensuring that work affecting them is performed by qualified practitioners.

Licensing in the United States

Licensure is by state. The typical path (the "4–4–8" model in many states) is: earn an ABET-accredited engineering degree, pass the Fundamentals of Engineering (FE) exam to become an Engineer in Training/Intern, gain about four years of qualifying experience under licensed engineers, then pass the Principles and Practice of Engineering (PE) exam. Licensed engineers must follow a code of ethics holding public welfare paramount, and maintain their competence through continuing education where required.

Other jurisdictions

In Canada, provincial and territorial regulators license the P.Eng. In the United Kingdom, engineering is not title-restricted by law, but Chartered Engineer (CEng) status is awarded by the Engineering Council through professional institutions. The title EUR ING is registered by FEANI in Europe. Mutual-recognition agreements (such as the Washington Accord for degrees) ease international mobility, but practice rights remain jurisdictional.

Scope

Only licensed engineers may seal drawings for public works or, in many industries, approve designs for construction. Many engineers working in industry under the "industrial exemption" in US law practise without a licence when their work is internal to a manufacturing company, though debate about the exemption's breadth continues.
