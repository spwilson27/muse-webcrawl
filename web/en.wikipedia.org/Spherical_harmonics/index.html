Spherical harmonics

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia


Visualisations of spherical harmonics

In mathematics and physical science, spherical harmonics are special functions defined on the surface of a sphere. They are often employed in solving partial differential equations in spherical geometry, and any function defined on the sphere can be written as a sum of spherical harmonics, much as periodic functions can be expanded in Fourier series.

Definition

Spherical harmonics Ylm(θ, φ) are eigenfunctions of the angular part of the Laplacian in spherical coordinates, characterised by degree l ≥ 0 and order m with |m| ≤ l. They are formed from associated Legendre polynomials multiplied by a complex exponential in the azimuthal angle, and they are orthonormal over the sphere.

Applications

Spherical harmonics describe electron orbitals in atoms (s, p, d, f corresponding to l = 0, 1, 2, 3), the Earth's gravitational and magnetic fields, the cosmic microwave background anisotropy, antenna patterns, and lighting in computer graphics.
