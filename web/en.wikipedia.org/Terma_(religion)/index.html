Terma (religion)

Terma (Tibetan: གཏེར་མ་, gter ma, "treasure") are key Tibetan Buddhist and Bön teachings said to have been concealed by Padmasambhava and other masters in the 8th century, to be rediscovered at the appropriate time by treasure revealers called tertöns.

Types of terma

Terma may be physical objects or texts hidden in rocks, lakes, temples, or statues ("earth treasures"), or they may be received directly in the mind of the tertön ("mind treasures"). Famous cycles include the Bardo Thodol (Tibetan Book of the Dead), revealed by Karma Lingpa, and the treasures of Nyangrel Nyima Özer and Guru Chöwang.

Significance

The terma tradition, central to the Nyingma school, allowed Tibetan Buddhism to renew its teachings by revealing texts considered perfectly suited to later ages, and validated new practices as authentic transmissions from the imperial period.
