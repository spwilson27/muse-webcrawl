Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe

The Organization for Security and Co-operation in Europe (OSCE) is the world's largest regional security-oriented intergovernmental organisation, with 57 participating states in Europe, Central Asia and North America. It emerged from the Conference on Security and Co-operation in Europe (CSCE), created in the early 1970s as a forum for dialogue between East and West during the Cold War, and was renamed OSCE in 1995. Its approach to security covers politico-military, economic and environmental, and human dimensions.

The OSCE secretariat is based in Vienna, Austria.

History

The Helsinki Final Act, signed in 1975 by 35 states including the United States, Canada and nearly all of Europe, committed signatories to respect borders, human rights and fundamental freedoms, and became a touchstone for dissident movements in the Eastern Bloc. After the Cold War the CSCE was institutionalised, acquiring a secretariat, the Office for Democratic Institutions and Human Rights (ODIHR), a High Commissioner on National Minorities and field missions.

Activities

The OSCE works in conflict prevention, crisis management and post-conflict rehabilitation; it deploys field operations (chiefly in South-Eastern Europe, Eastern Europe, the South Caucasus and Central Asia), observes elections through ODIHR, promotes media freedom, and addresses transnational threats including terrorism and human trafficking. From 2014 to 2022 its Special Monitoring Mission observed the conflict in eastern Ukraine. Decisions are taken by consensus, and its commitments are politically rather than legally binding.

Structure

Summit meetings of heads of state and a Ministerial Council set the agenda; the Chairpersonship rotates annually among participating states. The Secretariat in Vienna supports the Secretary General, and the Parliamentary Assembly brings together parliamentarians from member states.
