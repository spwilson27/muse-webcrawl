Marilyn Monroe

Born: Norma Jeane Mortenson, 1 June 1926, Los Angeles, California
Died: 4 August 1962 (aged 36), Los Angeles
Occupation: Actress, model, singer
Spouses: James Dougherty, Joe DiMaggio, Arthur Miller

Marilyn Monroe (born Norma Jeane Mortenson) was an American actress and model, among the most popular sex symbols of the 1950s and early 1960s and an enduring cultural icon.

Career

After a difficult childhood in foster care, she modelled during wartime and signed with 20th Century Fox. Breakthrough roles in Niagara, Gentlemen Prefer Blondes and How to Marry a Millionaire (all 1953) made her a star. She fought typecasting, studied at the Actors Studio, formed Marilyn Monroe Productions, and won acclaim for Bus Stop (1956) and Some Like It Hot (1959), winning a Golden Globe for the latter. The Seven Year Itch (1955) produced the famous subway-grate image.

Death and legacy

Monroe died of a barbiturate overdose in 1962 during production difficulties on her final films. The American Film Institute named her the sixth-greatest female screen legend; her image remains ubiquitous in popular culture.
