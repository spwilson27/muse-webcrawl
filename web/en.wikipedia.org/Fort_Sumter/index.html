Fort Sumter

LocationCharleston Harbor, South Carolina
Built1829–1861 (unfinished at outbreak of war)
Named forGeneral Thomas Sumter

Fort Sumter is a sea fort built on an artificial island protecting Charleston, South Carolina, from naval invasion. Its origin dates to the War of 1812, when the British invasion of other coastal cities demonstrated the need for stronger harbour defences. Construction began in 1829 as part of the Third System of American coastal fortifications. The fort is best known as the site of the battle that ignited the American Civil War.

The Battle of Fort Sumter

After South Carolina seceded in December 1860, Major Robert Anderson moved his small Union garrison from Fort Moultrie to the more defensible Sumter. Confederate forces under Brigadier General P. G. T. Beauregard demanded its surrender. At 4:30 a.m. on April 12, 1861, Confederate batteries opened fire — the first shots of the Civil War. After a 34-hour bombardment, Anderson surrendered on April 13; remarkably, no one on either side was killed during the battle itself (two Union soldiers died in an accidental explosion during the evacuation salute).

Later war service

Union forces tried repeatedly to retake the fort. Bombardments in 1863–1865 reduced it to rubble, yet Confederate troops held it until evacuating in February 1865 as Sherman's army approached. On April 14, 1865, Anderson returned to raise the original flag over the fort — the same day President Lincoln was shot.

Preservation

The fort saw later service as a lighthouse station and, with new concrete batteries (Battery Huger), during the Spanish–American War and both World Wars. It became Fort Sumter National Monument in 1948, administered by the National Park Service and accessible by ferry from Charleston.
