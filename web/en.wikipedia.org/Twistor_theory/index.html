Twistor theory

Twistor theory is a mathematical framework in physics, introduced by Roger Penrose in 1967, that maps the geometric structures of Minkowski spacetime into complex projective space known as twistor space. In this correspondence, points of spacetime correspond to complex two-dimensional planes (alpha planes) in twistor space, while individual twistors correspond to light rays. Penrose proposed twistors as a route toward a theory of quantum gravity in which spacetime itself would emerge from more primitive twistor structures.

Development and applications

The theory proved a powerful tool in classical field theory: the Penrose transform relates massless fields on spacetime to cohomology classes on twistor space, and instanton solutions of Yang–Mills theory were elegantly constructed using Ward's twistor correspondence. Although twistor theory has not yet yielded a complete quantum theory of gravity, its ideas experienced a major revival in the 2000s, when Edward Witten's twistor string theory and subsequent work by others revolutionised the calculation of scattering amplitudes in gauge theories, leading to tools such as the amplituhedron programme.
