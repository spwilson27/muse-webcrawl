Eastern Question

In diplomatic history, the Eastern Question was the issue of the political and economic instability in the Ottoman Empire from the late 18th to early 20th centuries, and the strategic competition among the European great powers over the empire's declining territories, especially in the Balkans and around the Turkish Straits.

Eastern Question
PeriodLate 18th century – 1923
Central stateOttoman Empire ("the sick man of Europe")
Powers involvedBritain, Russia, France, Austria-Hungary, Germany, Italy
ResolutionDissolution of the Ottoman Empire; Treaty of Lausanne (1923)

Origins

As Ottoman power waned, Russia pressed southward for warm-water ports, culminating in wars such as the Russo-Turkish War of 1768–74 and the Treaty of Küçük Kaynarca. Britain and France sought to preserve the empire as a buffer against Russian expansion toward the Mediterranean and the route to India.

Key episodes

The Greek War of Independence (1821–29), the Crimean War (1853–56), the Congress of Berlin (1878) after the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–78, the Bosnian Crisis (1908) and the Balkan Wars (1912–13) were all chapters of the Eastern Question. Each redistributed Ottoman territory while the powers strove to keep the Straits question from triggering a general European war.

Resolution

The Ottoman defeat in the First World War ended the question: the empire was partitioned, the sultanate abolished, and the Republic of Turkey recognised by the Treaty of Lausanne in 1923.
