Hamid Karzai (born 24 December 1957) is an Afghan politician who served as President of Afghanistan from 2001, initially as head of the interim administration after the fall of the Taliban, and from 2004 to 2014 as the country's elected president. A Popalzai Pashtun from Kandahar, he studied political science in India and was involved in supporting the mujahideen during the Soviet–Afghan War.

Presidency

Chosen at the Bonn Conference in December 2001 to lead the Afghan Interim Administration, Karzai won the 2004 presidential election and was re-elected in 2009 in a vote marred by fraud allegations. His tenure saw the rebuilding of Afghan state institutions alongside a prolonged international military presence, and was marked by tensions with the United States, widespread corruption, and a resurgent Taliban insurgency.

Later life

Karzai was succeeded by Ashraf Ghani in 2014. He remained in Kabul after the Taliban returned to power in 2021, appearing in public and meeting with Taliban figures, while advocating for the reopening of girls' schools and an inclusive government.
