Trapezoidal rule

In calculus, the trapezoidal rule (also known as the trapezoid rule or trapezium rule) is a technique for numerical integration, approximating the definite integral of a function. It works by approximating the region under the graph of the function as a trapezoid and calculating its area.

Formula

For a function f integrated over [a, b] divided into n subintervals of equal width h = (b − a)/n, the composite trapezoidal rule gives: the integral is approximately h times [ (f(a) + f(b))/2 + f(x1) + ... + f(xn−1) ]. The single-interval rule approximates the integral by (b − a)·(f(a) + f(b))/2.

Error

The error of the composite rule is proportional to h2 and to the second derivative of the function; it is exact for linear functions. The trapezoidal rule is one of the Newton–Cotes formulas and is related to Simpson's rule, which combines it with the midpoint rule for higher accuracy.
