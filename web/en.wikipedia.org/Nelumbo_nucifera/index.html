Nelumbo nucifera

Scientific classification
Kingdom:Plantae
Order:Proteales
Family:Nelumbonaceae
Genus:Nelumbo
Species:N. nucifera

Nelumbo nucifera, also known as the sacred lotus, Indian lotus, or simply lotus, is one of two extant species of aquatic plant in the family Nelumbonaceae. It is sometimes colloquially called a water lily, although this more often refers to members of the family Nymphaeaceae, to which the true water lilies belong.

Under favourable circumstances, the seeds of this aquatic perennial may remain viable for many years; the oldest recorded lotus germination was from seeds 1,300 years old recovered from a dry lakebed in northeastern China. The plant is native to Asia, from Iran eastwards through India and China to Japan and northern Australia, and has been cultivated for millennia as an ornamental plant, as a food crop, and for its profound religious and cultural symbolism.

Description

The lotus roots itself in the mud of ponds and slow-flowing rivers and sends up long stalks bearing large, circular, peltate leaves that float on the surface or stand above it. The leaves are strongly water-repellent — the celebrated "lotus effect", in which water droplets roll off carrying dirt with them, a property arising from microscopic wax structures on the leaf surface. The flowers, borne on stalks above the leaves, are large and showy, usually pink or white, and open in the morning and close at night. The plant is also remarkable for regulating the temperature of its flowers, maintaining them at roughly 30–35 °C during flowering.

Cultivation and uses

Nearly all parts of the plant are used in Asian cuisines: the rhizomes (lotus root) are eaten as a vegetable, the seeds are eaten fresh or dried and used in pastes and desserts, and the leaves serve as a flavourful wrapping for food. Lotus seeds, rhizome starch, and other parts are also used in traditional Chinese medicine.

Cultural significance

The lotus is a sacred symbol in Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainism, and other traditions, symbolising purity, enlightenment, rebirth, and beauty unsullied by muddy origins. It is the national flower of India and of Vietnam, and appears throughout Asian art and architecture.
