The Big Sleep (1939) is a hardboiled crime novel by American-British writer Raymond Chandler, the first to feature the detective Philip Marlowe. The work has been adapted twice into film, first in 1946 with Humphrey Bogart as Marlowe, and again in 1978 with Robert Mitchum.

Plot

Los Angeles private detective Philip Marlowe is hired by the dying General Sternwood to resolve a blackmail attempt against his younger daughter, Carmen. The case draws Marlowe into a web of pornography, gambling, and murder involving the general's missing son-in-law, Rusty Regan, the gambler Eddie Mars, and Carmen's elder sister Vivian. The famously convoluted plot — Chandler himself reportedly did not know who killed the chauffeur Owen Taylor — is driven less by its resolution than by its atmosphere and dialogue.

Composition

Chandler constructed the novel by "cannibalising" his earlier short stories published in pulp magazines such as Black Mask, principally "Killer in the Rain" (1935) and "The Curtain" (1936), weaving their plots together and expanding them.

Reception and legacy

The novel is widely regarded as a landmark of detective fiction and of American literature, frequently appearing on lists of the greatest novels of the 20th century. Its first-person narration, wisecracking voice, and depiction of Los Angeles set the template for the hardboiled genre.

Film adaptations

  • The Big Sleep (1946), directed by Howard Hawks, starring Humphrey Bogart and Lauren Bacall.
  • The Big Sleep (1978), directed by Michael Winner, starring Robert Mitchum, set in England.
