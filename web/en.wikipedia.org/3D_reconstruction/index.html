3D reconstruction

3D reconstruction is the process of capturing the shape and appearance of real objects and representing them as three-dimensional models in a computer. It is a central problem in computer vision, with applications in cultural heritage preservation, robotics, surveying, medicine, film production, and virtual and augmented reality.

Passive methods

Passive methods infer geometry from ordinary images without emitting light. Structure from motion (SfM) recovers camera positions and a sparse point cloud from overlapping photographs; multi-view stereo then densifies the cloud. Photogrammetry, developed from survey practice, applies the same principles to measurement. Other passive cues include shape from shading, texture, silhouettes, and defocus.

Active methods

Active sensors project energy and measure its return. Laser scanners and LiDAR time the return of pulses to build dense point clouds; structured-light systems project known patterns and triangulate their deformation; time-of-flight cameras measure depth at every pixel. Medical scanners such as CT and MRI reconstruct volumetric models from slice data.

Pipeline

A typical workflow acquires images or scans, registers them into a common coordinate frame, fuses the data into a surface (for example by Poisson reconstruction), and textures the mesh from the source imagery. Neural methods such as neural radiance fields (NeRF) and 3D Gaussian splatting represent scenes as continuous functions learned from photographs, enabling novel-view synthesis alongside geometry recovery.
