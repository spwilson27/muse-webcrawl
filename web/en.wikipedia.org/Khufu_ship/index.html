Khufu ship

Length: 43.4 m
Beam: 5.9 m
Material: Lebanon cedar
Date: c. 2500 BC

The Khufu ship is an intact full-size vessel from ancient Egypt, buried in a pit at the foot of the Great Pyramid of Giza around 2500 BC, during the reign of the pharaoh Khufu. It is one of the oldest, largest and best-preserved vessels from antiquity, often described as a "solar barque" intended to carry the resurrected king with the sun god Ra across the heavens.

Discovery and reconstruction

The sealed pit was discovered in 1954 by Egyptian archaeologist Kamal el-Mallakh. The ship was found dismantled into 1,224 pieces, carefully layered in the pit. Hag Ahmed Youssef Moustafa spent years reassembling it; the reconstructed vessel is 43.4 metres long and built chiefly of Lebanon cedar planks sewn together with rope, without nails.

Function

Scholars debate whether the ship was a ritual solar barque, a funerary vessel that carried Khufu's body to Giza, or both. Signs of wear suggest it may actually have been used on water before burial. A second, similar pit was found beside it.

Display

From 1982 the ship was housed in the Giza Solar Boat Museum beside the pyramid; in 2021 it was transported in a specially built vehicle to the Grand Egyptian Museum.
