The Fédération Internationale de l'Automobile (FIA; English: International Automobile Federation) is an international organisation with two primary functions surrounding use of the automobile: promoting road safety and mobility, and sanctioning and governing motor sport. Founded in 1904 as the Association Internationale des Automobile Clubs Reconnus (AIACR), and reconstituted under its current name in 1946, the FIA is headquartered at 8 Place de la Concorde, Paris, with offices in Geneva and Valleiry.

The FIA is best known to the public as the governing body for many of the world's top motor racing series, most prominently the Formula One World Championship, the World Rally Championship, the World Endurance Championship, and Formula E. Its mobility division represents motoring organisations and motor car users worldwide and campaigns on road safety, consumer protection, and sustainable transport.

History

The AIACR was founded in Paris on 20 June 1904 by motoring associations from 13 countries to regulate international racing and represent motorists. After the Second World War it was renamed the FIA in 1946. The Fédération Internationale du Sport Automobile (FISA) was created in 1978 as an autonomous sporting sub-body, but a power struggle in the early 1990s led to FISA's dissolution in 1993 and the FIA resuming direct control of sport. Long-serving presidents include Jean-Marie Balestre (1985–1993) and Max Mosley (1993–2009), followed by Jean Todt (2009–2021).

Structure

The FIA's supreme bodies are the General Assembly and two World Councils: the World Motor Sport Council (WMSC), which regulates all FIA-sanctioned racing, and the World Council for Automobile Mobility and Tourism (WCAMT). National sporting authorities (ASNs) and mobility clubs constitute its membership and run events under FIA regulations in their territories.
