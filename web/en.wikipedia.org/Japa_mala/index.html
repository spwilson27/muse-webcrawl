Japa mala

A japa mala (Sanskrit: माला, mālā, "garland") is a loop of prayer beads commonly used in Indian religions such as Hinduism, Jainism, Sikhism, and Buddhism for the spiritual practice known as japa — the meditative repetition of a mantra or a divine name. The mala is used to keep count while a mantra is recited, chanted, or mentally repeated.

Structure

A traditional mala consists of 108 counting beads plus one additional bead, the sumeru (or guru bead), which marks the beginning and end of the circuit. The number 108 is considered sacred in several Indian traditions. Half malas of 54 beads and wrist malas of 27 beads also exist. When the practitioner reaches the sumeru after a full round, the mala is turned around rather than crossed over, and counting continues in the opposite direction.

Materials

Beads are made from a variety of materials, each associated with particular deities and purposes:

  • Tulsi (holy basil) wood — associated with Vishnu and Krishna
  • Rudraksha seeds — associated with Shiva
  • Sandalwood — calming and used across traditions
  • Lotus seeds, bodhi seeds, crystal (sphatika), pearl, and gemstones
  • Bone or other materials in some Tantric and Tibetan Buddhist practices

Usage

The mala is typically held in the right hand, with the beads draped over the middle finger and moved one by one with the thumb as each repetition of the mantra is completed. In some traditions the index finger is avoided, and the mala is kept in a cloth bag (gomukhi) to keep it from view and from touching the ground. Japa may be performed aloud, in a whisper, or silently in the mind.
