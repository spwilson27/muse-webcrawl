Özkonak Underground City

Özkonak Underground City is an ancient city built into the volcanic rock of Cappadocia in Nevşehir Province, Turkey, near the town of Özkonak. Like the better-known underground cities of Derinkuyu and Kaymaklı, it was carved out of soft tuff to provide refuge — probably during the Byzantine era, when the region's inhabitants sheltered from Arab raids.

Description

The city descends several levels below the surface and includes stables, storage rooms, wineries, living quarters, ventilation shafts and a water well. A distinctive feature of Özkonak is a communication system of small holes between floors, and narrow tunnels defended by large rolling stone doors and traps through which hot oil could be poured on intruders. Only a few of its levels are open to visitors today.

Discovery

The city was discovered in 1972 by a local farmer, Latif Acar, who investigated where the surplus water from his fields was disappearing; he found an underground room, from which the whole city was eventually excavated and opened to tourism.
