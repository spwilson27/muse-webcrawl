Triple Crown of Acting

The Academy, Emmy and Tony Awards

The Triple Crown of Acting is a term used in the American entertainment industry to describe actors who have won an Academy Award, a Primetime Emmy Award, and a Tony Award in the acting categories — the three highest honours for film, television, and theatre respectively. It is distinct from the EGOT, which also requires a Grammy Award.

Criteria

Only competitive (non-honorary) wins in acting categories count toward the Triple Crown. The awards may be won at any point in a performer's career, in lead or supporting roles, across any combination of film, television, and stage work.

Achievers

As of the mid-2020s, only 24 people — 15 women and 9 men — have achieved the Triple Crown of Acting. The first to complete it was Helen Hayes, whose Tony win in 1947 followed her Academy Award (1932) and preceded her Emmy (1953); she is sometimes called the "First Lady of American Theatre". Other achievers include Ingrid Bergman, Sir Thomas Mitchell, Audrey Hepburn, Melvyn Douglas, Jessica Tandy, Jeremy Irons, Vanessa Redgrave, Maggie Smith, Al Pacino, Geoffrey Rush, Ellen Burstyn, Helen Mirren, Frances McDormand, Jessica Lange, and Viola Davis, who completed her crown with a Tony and Emmy before winning the Academy Award for Fences (2016).

The youngest person to achieve the Triple Crown was Anne Bancroft at age 39; the longest it took any performer to complete the set was 41 years, in the case of Helen Hayes between her first and final qualifying wins.

Related distinctions

Performers who hold three of the four EGOT awards but lack one are sometimes described as having a "missing piece". Three performers — Helen Hayes, Viola Davis, and Jennifer Hudson among them — hold both the Triple Crown of Acting and the EGOT.
