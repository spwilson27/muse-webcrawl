Military law

Military law
Scope: Legal system governing members of the armed forces
US system: Uniform Code of Military Justice (1950)
Trial forum: Court-martial

Military law is the body of law that governs the members of a state's armed forces. It provides a separate disciplinary and criminal system for service personnel, covering offences such as desertion, insubordination, absence without leave and conduct prejudicial to good order that have no direct civilian equivalent, in addition to ordinary crimes.

Systems

In the United States, military justice is governed by the Uniform Code of Military Justice, enacted in 1950, and administered through courts-martial, non-judicial punishment ("captain's mast"/Article 15) and military courts of appeal, with the Judge Advocate General's Corps providing legal officers. The United Kingdom consolidated its service law in the Armed Forces Act 2006. Many countries maintain similar codes descending from historical articles of war.

Scope and debate

Military law generally follows service members worldwide and may also apply to some civilians accompanying forces. It is distinct from martial law — the imposition of military rule over a civilian population — and from international humanitarian law, which regulates conduct in armed conflict. Reform debates commonly address command influence over prosecutions, the handling of sexual offences, and the rights of the accused compared with civilian courts.
