Member states of the United Nations

The member states of the United Nations comprise 193 sovereign states. The United Nations (UN) is the world's largest intergovernmental organization, founded in 1945 after the Second World War. In addition to the 193 member states, there are two observer states: the Holy See and Palestine.

Membership

Membership is open to all "peace-loving states" that accept the obligations of the UN Charter. Admission requires a decision of the General Assembly upon the recommendation of the Security Council. The original members were the 51 states that signed the Charter or the Declaration by United Nations; membership grew with decolonization in the 1950s and 1960s, the end of the Cold War, and the admission of newly independent states. South Sudan, admitted in 2011, is the most recent member.

Representation issues

Notable questions have included the representation of China — the seat was held by the Republic of China until 1971, when General Assembly Resolution 2758 transferred it to the People's Republic of China — and the division and unification of states such as Germany and Yemen, and the dissolution of the Soviet Union, Yugoslavia, and Czechoslovakia.

Observers and non-members

The Holy See and Palestine hold non-member observer state status, allowing participation without a vote in the General Assembly. Some other entities, such as Taiwan and Kosovo, are not UN members and are recognized by only some states.
