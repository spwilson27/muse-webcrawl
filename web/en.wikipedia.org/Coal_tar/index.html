Coal tar

Coal tar is a thick dark liquid which is a by-product of the production of coke and coal gas from coal. It is a type of creosote. It has both medical and industrial uses. Medicinally it is a topical medication for the skin conditions psoriasis and seborrheic dermatitis (dandruff). Together with coal tar pitch, it may be used as the paved surface of roads. Industrially it is a preservative for railroad ties and used in the treatment of roads.

Composition

Coal tar is a complex mixture of phenols, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), and heterocyclic compounds. Among its constituents are benzene, toluene, xylenes, naphthalene, anthracene, phenanthrene, and many other compounds. Around 200 substances can be identified in typical coal tar, but the composition varies with the coal used and the temperature of carbonization.

Production

Coal tar is obtained by cooling the gas formed during the destructive distillation (carbonization) of coal to coke. High-temperature carbonization, around 900–1100 °C, produces a tar richer in aromatic compounds than low-temperature processes. The tar condenses from the hot gases along with ammonia liquor and is separated in tar separators and precipitators.

Applications

Refined coal tar was historically a vital feedstock for the chemical industry, yielding dyes, drugs, explosives, and flavourings. Coal tar pitch is used as a binder in aluminium smelting electrodes and in roofing. Medicinal coal tar preparations, available since the 19th century, remain in use for psoriasis and eczema. Wood preservative creosote derived from coal tar protects railway sleepers and utility poles.

Health effects

Coal tar contains many carcinogenic PAHs; occupational exposure during paving, roofing, and aluminium production is associated with increased cancer risk. Coal tar itself is listed as a known human carcinogen, though medicinal skin preparations used as directed carry a much lower risk profile.
