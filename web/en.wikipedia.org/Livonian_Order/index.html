The Livonian Order (German: Deutscher Orden in Livland) was an autonomous branch of the Teutonic Order, formed in 1237 from the remnants of the Livonian Brothers of the Sword, and a ruling power in the region of modern Latvia and Estonia until its dissolution in 1561. Together with the bishops of Riga and other ecclesiastical states it formed the loose Livonian Confederation of Terra Mariana.

Origins

The Livonian Brothers of the Sword, founded in 1202 during the Northern Crusades, conquered much of present-day Latvia and southern Estonia in alliance—and rivalry—with the bishops of Riga. After the Sword Brothers were crushed by the Samogitians and Semigallians at the Battle of Saule in 1236, their survivors were incorporated into the Teutonic Order in 1237 as its Livonian branch, governed by a Landmeister (territorial master) nominally subject to the Grand Master in Prussia.

Wars and rule

The Order campaigned eastward against Novgorod and Pskov until checked by Alexander Nevsky at the Battle on the Ice on Lake Peipus in 1242. It completed the conquest of Courland and Semigallia by the end of the 13th century, suppressed the great Estonian St. George's Night Uprising in 1343, and purchased Danish Estonia in 1346. Internally the Order contested control of Riga and trade with the archbishops for centuries. Under Wolter von Plettenberg (master 1494–1535), the Order defeated Muscovy at the Battle of Lake Smolino (1502) and secured a half-century of stability; Plettenberg cautiously permitted Lutheran preaching while keeping the Order formally Catholic.

Dissolution

Weakened by internal decay, religious division, and powerful neighbours, the Order collapsed during the Livonian War (1558–1583), when Ivan IV of Russia invaded. The last master, Gotthard Kettler, secularized the Order's remaining lands: by the Treaty of Vilnius (1561) the Livonian Order was dissolved, Kettler became hereditary Duke of Courland and Semigallia as a Polish-Lithuanian vassal, and the remaining territories were partitioned among Poland-Lithuania, Sweden, and Denmark.
