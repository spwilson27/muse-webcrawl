1938 Yellow River flood

The 1938 Yellow River flood (花園口決堤事件) was a deliberate flood created by the Nationalist government of China during the Second Sino-Japanese War. In June 1938, Chinese troops breached the dikes of the Yellow River at Huayuankou, near Zhengzhou in Henan, to slow the advancing Imperial Japanese Army. The resulting flood was one of the deadliest in history, killing an estimated 400,000–900,000 Chinese civilians and displacing millions.

The decision

After the fall of Xuzhou in May 1938, Japanese forces advanced west toward the strategic rail junction of Zhengzhou. On 5–7 June 1938, Nationalist engineers blasted open the southern dike at Huayuankou. The river, elevated above the plain on a bed of its own silt, poured south-east across Henan, Anhui and Jiangsu, eventually flowing into the Huai River system and the Yellow Sea.

Effects

The water inundated some 54,000 square kilometres, drowned hundreds of thousands, destroyed crops and villages, and contributed to the Henan famine of 1942–43 by wrecking irrigation and soils. Around 3.9 million people became refugees. Militarily, the flood delayed the Japanese advance on Wuhan by several months, but did not prevent the city's fall in October 1938, nor did it protect China for long.

The Nationalist government initially blamed Japanese bombing for the breach, and the decision remained secret and controversial for decades. The Yellow River did not return to its northern course until the dikes were repaired in 1947 with UNRRA assistance.
