Dag Krister Volle (26 April 1963 – 30 August 1998), better known as Denniz PoP, was a Swedish DJ, music producer, and songwriter. He co-founded the recording studio Cheiron in Stockholm in 1992 and is widely credited with launching the modern Swedish pop music phenomenon, shaping the sound of international pop in the 1990s.

Career

Volle began as a DJ in the Stockholm club scene before moving into remixing and production. With Tom Talomaa he founded Cheiron Studios, whose first major success came with Ace of Base's global hits including "All That She Wants". Cheiron went on to work with artists such as Dr. Alban, Backstreet Boys, Britney Spears, NSYNC, Robyn, and 5ive, producing hits such as "...Baby One More Time".

Mentorship and legacy

Denniz PoP recruited and mentored a generation of Swedish producers and songwriters, most famously Max Martin, along with Rami Yacoub, Kristian Lundin, and Andreas Carlsson. His approach — melody-first songwriting, meticulous production, and an assembly of specialist collaborators — became the template for Cheiron's extraordinary run of late-1990s hits and for Swedish pop's enduring global influence.

Death

Denniz PoP died of stomach cancer on 30 August 1998, at the age of 35. The annual Swedish music export award for songwriting and production, the Denniz PoP Awards, was established in his honor in 2013.
