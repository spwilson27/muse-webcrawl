Napoleon II (Napoléon François Joseph Charles Bonaparte; 20 March 1811 – 22 July 1832) was the son of Emperor Napoleon I and Marie Louise of Austria. Styled King of Rome from birth, he was briefly recognised by Bonapartists as Emperor of the French following his father's second abdication in 1815, though he never ruled.

Life

After Napoleon's defeat, the young prince was taken to Vienna and raised at the court of his grandfather, Emperor Francis I of Austria. In 1818 he was granted the title Duke of Reichstadt. Known in France by the affectionate nickname l'Aiglon ("the Eaglet"), he grew up under the watchful control of the Austrian chancellor Metternich, was educated as an Austrian officer, and was kept from any political role.

Death and legacy

Frail in health, Napoleon II died of tuberculosis at Schönbrunn Palace in 1832, aged 21. In 1940 Adolf Hitler had his remains transferred to Les Invalides in Paris, where they rest near his father's tomb. His life inspired Edmond Rostand's play L'Aiglon (1900), and Bonapartist succession passed to his cousin, the future Napoleon III.
