Laser diode

A laser diode (LD) is a semiconductor device that produces coherent light by stimulated emission at a p–n junction, analogous to a light-emitting diode but with an optical cavity formed by the cleaved crystal facets. Current injection creates a population inversion in the active region.

Applications

Laser diodes are the most common lasers sold, used in fibre-optic communications, barcode readers, laser pointers, CD/DVD/Blu-ray drives, laser printers, pumping of solid-state lasers, and increasingly in lighting and materials processing. Wavelengths range from ultraviolet (GaN) through visible to infrared (GaAs, InP).

History

First demonstrated in 1962 by groups at GE, IBM and MIT Lincoln Laboratory; practical continuous-wave room-temperature operation came with double-heterostructure devices around 1970, work recognised by the 2000 Nobel Prize to Alferov and Kroemer.
