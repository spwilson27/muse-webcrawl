The Battle of Lyndanisse was a battle during the Livonian Crusade, fought by the Danes under King Valdemar II of Denmark against Estonian tribes on 15 June 1219. It took place at Lyndanisse, the site of the future city of Tallinn (known in Danish as Reval), and secured Danish control of northern Estonia.

Background

Valdemar II landed in Estonia in 1219 as part of the Northern Crusades against the pagan Estonians, building a fortress on Toompea hill at the site of the Estonian stronghold of Lyndanisse.

The battle and the Dannebrog legend

The Estonians attacked the Danish camp from several directions and initially drove the Danes back. According to Danish legend, at the height of the battle a red banner with a white cross — the Dannebrog — fell from the sky, rallying the Danish troops, who counter-attacked and won. The Dannebrog is thus traditionally dated to 15 June 1219, making it among the oldest national flags in continuous use.

Aftermath

The victory established Danish Estonia, with Tallinn growing up around the fortress. Denmark held northern Estonia until 1346, when it was sold to the Teutonic Order after the St. George's Night Uprising.
