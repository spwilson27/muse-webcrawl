Grand Est (literally "Great East") is an administrative region in northeastern France. It was created on 1 January 2016 by the territorial reform of 2014–2015, through the merger of the former regions of Alsace, Champagne-Ardenne, and Lorraine. Its capital and largest city is Strasbourg, seat of the European Parliament. The region borders Belgium, Luxembourg, Germany, and Switzerland, and is the only French region to border three other countries.

History of the region

The provisional name "Alsace-Champagne-Ardenne-Lorraine" was replaced by "Grand Est" following a public consultation and a vote of the regional council in 2016. The merger was unpopular in Alsace, where many felt the historic region's identity was submerged; in 2021 the two Alsatian departments were combined into the European Collectivity of Alsace, which remains within Grand Est but with additional powers.

Geography

Grand Est encompasses the Rhine plain and the Vosges mountains in the east, the chalk Champagne plains in the west, and the Lorraine plateau in between. Major rivers include the Rhine, Moselle, Meuse, and Marne. Other significant cities are Reims, Metz, Nancy, Mulhouse, and Troyes.

Economy and culture

The region is famous for Champagne wine, produced around Reims and Épernay, as well as Alsatian wines, beer, and a strong industrial and agricultural base. Strasbourg hosts several European institutions, and the region's history — Alsace and Moselle were annexed by Germany between 1871 and 1918 and again in 1940 — gives it a distinctive bilingual heritage reflected in local law and culture.
