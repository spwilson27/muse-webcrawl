Hot chocolate

Hot chocolate, also known as drinking chocolate, is a heated drink consisting of shaved or melted chocolate or cocoa powder, heated milk or water, and usually a sweetener. It may be topped with whipped cream or marshmallows. Hot chocolate made with melted chocolate is sometimes called drinking chocolate and is characterised by lower sweetness and a thicker consistency.

History

The first chocolate beverage is believed to have been created by the Maya around 2,500–3,000 years ago; these early drinks were bitter, spiced, and served cold or at room temperature, frothed by pouring between vessels. Chocolate arrived in Europe after the Spanish conquest of the Aztec Empire, where sweetened, heated versions served in chocolate houses became fashionable in the 17th and 18th centuries.

Variations

Regional variations include the thick Spanish chocolate con churros, spiced Mexican hot chocolate made with cinnamon (and traditionally chilli), and Italian cioccolata calda, which is notably dense. Powdered mixes were developed in the 19th and 20th centuries for convenience. In the Netherlands, the addition of a press to separate cocoa butter led to modern cocoa powder, making the drink easier to prepare.
