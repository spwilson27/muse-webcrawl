Albert Szent-Györgyi de Nagyrápolt (September 16, 1893 – October 22, 1986) was a Hungarian biochemist who won the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 1937 for discoveries concerning biological combustion processes, with special reference to vitamin C and the catalysis of fumaric acid.

Vitamin C

Working at Szeged in the early 1930s, Szent-Györgyi isolated a substance he first called "hexuronic acid" from adrenal glands and, in large quantities, from Hungarian paprika. He showed it was identical to the antiscorbutic factor, vitamin C — thereafter named ascorbic acid.

Later work

His studies of fumaric acid and other dicarboxylic acids fed into the elucidation of the citric acid cycle. After the Second World War he emigrated to the United States, working at Woods Hole on muscle contraction and, later, on the electronic basis of cancer, advocating free-radical and quantum-biological ideas.
