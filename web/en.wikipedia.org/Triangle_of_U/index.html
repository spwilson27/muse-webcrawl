Triangle of U

[Image: Diagram of the triangle of U, showing the relationships of six Brassica species]

The triangle of U is a theory about the evolution and relationships between six members of the plant genus Brassica. Published in 1935 by the Korean–Japanese botanist Woo Jang-choon (whose name is rendered in Japanese as Nagaharu U), the theory states that the genomes of three ancestral diploid species of Brassica combined to create three common tetraploid vegetable and oilseed crop species.

The model

The three diploid species at the corners of the triangle are Brassica rapa (genome AA, 2n = 20; turnip, Chinese cabbage), Brassica nigra (BB, 2n = 16; black mustard), and Brassica oleracea (CC, 2n = 18; cabbage, kale, broccoli, cauliflower). Pairwise hybridisation followed by chromosome doubling (allopolyploidy) produced the three amphidiploid species at the edges: Brassica juncea (AABB, 2n = 36), Brassica napus (AACC, 2n = 38; rapeseed/canola, rutabaga), and Brassica carinata (BBCC, 2n = 34; Ethiopian mustard).

Significance

The triangle of U has been confirmed by DNA and protein studies and remains a classic framework in plant genetics and breeding. The relationships it describes guide crop improvement, including the resynthesis of B. napus from its parent species, and underpin modern molecular work on the Brassica genomes, which have also revealed an ancient whole-genome triplication shared by the diploid species.
