Black metal

Stylistic originsThrash metal, death metal, hardcore punk
Cultural originsEarly 1980s, England; mid-1980s, Scandinavia
Typical instrumentsElectric guitar, bass guitar, drums, vocals
SubgenresAtmospheric black metal, symphonic black metal, war metal, blackened death metal

Black metal is an extreme subgenre of heavy metal music. Common traits include fast tempos, a shrieking vocal style, heavily distorted guitars played with tremolo picking, raw (lo-fi) recording, unconventional song structures, and an emphasis on atmosphere. Artists often appear in corpse paint and adopt pseudonyms.

During the 1980s, several thrash metal and death metal bands formed a prototype for black metal. This so-called first wave included bands such as Venom, Bathory, Mercyful Fate, Hellhammer and Celtic Frost. A second wave arose in the early 1990s, spearheaded by Norwegian bands such as Mayhem, Darkthrone, Burzum, Immortal, Emperor, Satyricon and Gorgoroth. The early Norwegian black metal scene developed the style of its predecessors into a distinct genre.

History

The term "black metal" was coined by the English band Venom with their second album Black Metal (1982). Although generally considered speed metal or thrash metal rather than black metal by today's standards, the album's lyrics and imagery focused more on anti-Christian and Satanic themes than any before it.

The second wave emerged in the early 1990s in Norway. Bands stripped down the production quality, emphasized minor-key tremolo riffs and blast beats, and cultivated an ideology of anti-Christianity, misanthropy and paganism. The scene became notorious after a series of church burnings and murders committed by members of the scene.

Characteristics

Guitar playing is characterized by fast, tremolo-picked minor riffs, often with little or no variation. Drummers frequently use blast beats and double bass drumming. Vocals are typically high-pitched shrieks or rasps. Production is frequently intentionally raw and thin, a reaction against the polished death metal sound of the early 1990s.

Subgenres and styles

Later styles include symphonic black metal, which adds keyboards and orchestral elements; atmospheric black metal, which emphasizes long, textured compositions; depressive suicidal black metal; and blackgaze, which fuses black metal with shoegaze.
