Qal'at al-Bahrain

Qal'at al-Bahrain (Arabic: قلعة البحرين, "Fort of Bahrain"), also known as the Bahrain Fort or Portuguese Fort, is an archaeological site and fort on the northern coast of Bahrain Island. A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 2005, its artificial mound (tell) was occupied from around 2300 BC to the 18th century AD.

History

The site was the capital and harbour of the ancient Dilmun civilization, a trading centre linking Mesopotamia and the Indus Valley. Successive layers include Dilmun temples and warehouses, Kassite, Hellenistic-influenced, and Islamic remains. The sea fort on top was built and expanded in the early 16th century, first under local Hormuzi control and then by the Portuguese, who held Bahrain from 1521 until 1602.

Excavations

Danish expeditions led by P. V. Glob and Geoffrey Bibby excavated the site in the 1950s, and a French mission continued from the late 1970s; only a portion of the tell has been excavated. A site museum opened in 2008.
