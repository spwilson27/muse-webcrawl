The Russell 1000 Index is a stock market index maintained by FTSE Russell that measures the performance of the 1,000 largest publicly traded companies in the United States by market capitalisation. It represents approximately 92–93% of the total market value of US equities and is widely used as a benchmark for large-cap investment, rivalling the S&P 500.

Composition

The index comprises the top 1,000 constituents of the broader Russell 3000 Index. Together with the small-cap Russell 2000 Index — the next 2,000 stocks — the Russell 1000 makes up the Russell 3000. Membership is reviewed each year in a process known as reconstitution, carried out in June, when stocks are added or removed based on market capitalisation rankings as of a rank day in late spring.

Variants and investing

FTSE Russell publishes growth and value style sub-indices of the Russell 1000, and numerous index funds and exchange-traded funds track it; the best known is the iShares Russell 1000 ETF (ticker IWB). A related total-return version includes reinvested dividends. The index's committee-free, rules-based reconstitution is credited with transparency, though the annual June reconstitution is a heavily traded event that can move the prices of added and deleted stocks.
