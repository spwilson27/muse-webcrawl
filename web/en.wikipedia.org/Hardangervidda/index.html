Hardangervidda is a mountain plateau in southern Norway and the largest high-mountain plateau in Europe, covering roughly 6,500 square kilometres at an average elevation of about 1,100 metres. Much of it is protected as Hardangervidda National Park, Norway's largest national park.

Geography

The plateau stretches across the counties of Vestland, Viken, and Vestfold og Telemark. Its landscape of rolling moorland, lakes, and rivers was sculpted by ice-age glaciers. The Hardangerjøkulen glacier lies at its northern edge.

Wildlife

Hardangervidda is home to one of the world's largest herds of wild reindeer, numbering in the thousands, along with arctic fox, Norwegian lemming, and rich birdlife around its wetlands. The plateau's rivers and lakes support trout and Arctic char.

Use and history

The area has been used since the Stone Age for hunting and fishing; old drovers' routes cross it, and it remains popular for hiking, cross-country skiing, and cabin tourism, served by the Norwegian Trekking Association's network of huts. The Bergen Railway and National Road 7 cross its northern part.
