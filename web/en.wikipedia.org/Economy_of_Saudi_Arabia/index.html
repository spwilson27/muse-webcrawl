Economy of Saudi Arabia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Oil and gas dominate Saudi exports and government revenue.

The economy of Saudi Arabia is one of the top twenty economies in the world and the largest in the Arab world. It is dominated by petroleum: Saudi Arabia possesses around 17% of the world's proven petroleum reserves, is a leading producer and exporter of crude oil through the state company Saudi Aramco, and is a founding and dominant member of OPEC. Oil accounts for roughly 40% or more of GDP and the great majority of export earnings and government revenue.

Oil sector

Oil was discovered in commercial quantities in 1938 at Dammam. The industry was gradually nationalised, and Saudi Aramco became wholly state-owned by 1980. Its partial flotation on the Tadawul exchange in 2019 was one of the largest IPOs in history. Saudi Arabia's low production costs and spare capacity give it a central role in global oil markets.

Diversification

Under Saudi Vision 2030, launched in 2016, the kingdom aims to diversify away from oil through the Public Investment Fund, development of mining, tourism, entertainment and technology, and giga-projects such as Neom. Non-oil growth and private-sector employment for Saudi nationals are key targets of the programme.

Labour and trade

The workforce includes a large expatriate population, particularly in the private sector. Major exports besides crude oil include petrochemicals, plastics and refined products; China, Japan, India and the United States are among the largest trading partners.
