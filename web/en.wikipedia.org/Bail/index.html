Bail

Bail is a set of conditions, typically including a sum of money or property, imposed by a court to secure the temporary release of an accused person awaiting trial, in exchange for a guarantee that they will return for their court appearances. If the defendant fails to appear, the bail may be forfeited and a warrant issued for their arrest.

Bail
Field: Criminal procedure
Purpose: Release of accused pending trial; ensure appearance
Forms: Cash bail, surety bond, recognizance
Key risk: Failure to appear

Practice

In many jurisdictions a judge sets bail taking into account the seriousness of the charge, the defendant's ties to the community, prior record, and flight risk. Defendants who cannot pay the full amount themselves may use a commercial bail bondsman, who posts a surety bond for a non-refundable fee, commonly around ten percent. Some jurisdictions instead release defendants on their own recognizance — a written promise to appear — or attach non-monetary conditions such as travel restrictions, curfews, or electronic monitoring.

Reform and criticism

Cash bail has been criticised for detaining poor defendants who pose little risk while allowing wealthy defendants to buy release, leading some jurisdictions — including several U.S. states and countries that never adopted commercial bonds — to restrict or abolish money bail in favour of risk-based release decisions. Bail in some form dates back to medieval English law, where sheriffs could release prisoners on sureties.
