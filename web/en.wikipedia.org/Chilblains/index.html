Chilblains
Also calledPernio, perniosis
SpecialtyDermatology, rheumatology
CauseAbnormal vascular response to cold, damp conditions
Affected sitesToes, fingers, ears, nose

Chilblains, also known as pernio, are a medical condition in which damage occurs to skin and other tissues in response to cold, damp weather. They are itchy, red or purple patches and swelling that appear on exposed extremities, most often the toes and fingers, typically in winter.

Signs and causes

Chilblains occur when cold causes abnormal constriction of small blood vessels, followed by inflammation as the tissue rewarms too quickly. Lesions burn and itch, and in severe cases blister or ulcerate. They are common in temperate maritime climates with poorly heated housing, and are more frequent in women, people with low body mass, and those with Raynaud's phenomenon or lupus; "chilblain lupus" is a recognised form. Pernio-like "COVID toes" were widely reported during the COVID-19 pandemic, though the association remains debated.

Management

Most cases resolve in one to three weeks. Management centres on keeping the affected parts warm and dry, avoiding rapid rewarming and scratching, and gentle exercise of the extremities. Calcium channel blockers such as nifedipine may help recurrent or severe cases, and secondary infection of broken skin may require treatment. Prevention — warm footwear, gloves and adequately heated rooms — is the mainstay for susceptible people.
