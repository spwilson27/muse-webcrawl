Autódromo José Carlos Pace

Location: Interlagos, São Paulo, Brazil

Opened: 1940

Length: 4.309 km (2.677 mi)

Turns: 15

Major events: Formula One Brazilian / São Paulo Grand Prix

The Autódromo José Carlos Pace, better known as Interlagos, is a motorsport circuit in the Interlagos district of São Paulo, Brazil. Named after Brazilian racing driver José Carlos Pace, who died in a plane crash in 1977, it is famous as the home of the Formula One Brazilian Grand Prix (renamed the São Paulo Grand Prix in 2021) and is regarded as one of the most atmospheric and challenging tracks on the calendar.

History

The circuit opened in 1940 on land between two artificial lakes, Guarapiranga and Billings — hence the name Interlagos ("between lakes"). The original layout was nearly 8 km long. Interlagos first hosted the Brazilian Grand Prix in 1972 and became a regular Formula One venue from 1973; local hero Emerson Fittipaldi won the first two World Championship races there. The event moved to Jacarepaguá in Rio de Janeiro for most of the 1980s before returning in 1990 to a shortened, redesigned circuit.

Characteristics

The modern 4.309 km circuit runs anti-clockwise and features dramatic elevation changes, a long uphill main straight, the Senna S curves, and the tight infield section. Situated at roughly 800 metres altitude, it is known for unpredictable weather and has produced many dramatic title deciders, including the 2007, 2008 and 2012 championship finales. Ayrton Senna's emotional home victories in 1991 and 1993 are among its most celebrated moments.
