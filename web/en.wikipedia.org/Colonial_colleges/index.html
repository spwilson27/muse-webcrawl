Colonial colleges

The colonial colleges are nine institutions of higher education founded in the American colonies before the United States declared independence in 1776. Seven of the nine later became members of the Ivy League, and all remain among the most prestigious universities in the United States.

The nine colleges

In order of founding they are: Harvard (1636), the College of William & Mary (1693), Yale (1701), the University of Pennsylvania (1740), Princeton (1746), Columbia (1754, as King's College), Brown (1764), Rutgers (1766, as Queen's College), and Dartmouth (1769). Most were founded by Protestant denominations to train clergy, though Benjamin Franklin's Pennsylvania pioneered a more secular, practical curriculum.

Characteristics

Modelled on Oxford and Cambridge, the colleges offered a classical curriculum of Latin, Greek, mathematics, and moral philosophy to small classes of young men. Their royal charters, denominational ties, and roles in the American Revolution — many alumni signed the Declaration of Independence — give them an outsized place in early American history.
