In Greek mythology, Ariadne was a Cretan princess, the daughter of King Minos and Queen Pasiphaë. She is best known for helping the Athenian hero Theseus escape the Labyrinth after he slew the Minotaur, by giving him a ball of thread ("Ariadne's thread") to retrace his path.

Myth

Minos had the craftsman Daedalus build the Labyrinth to house the Minotaur, the half-bull offspring of Pasiphaë, and required Athens to send youths and maidens as tribute to be devoured. When Theseus volunteered, Ariadne fell in love with him and supplied the thread — her "clue" (the origin of the English word). Theseus killed the Minotaur, escaped by the thread, and sailed away with Ariadne, but abandoned her on the island of Naxos (versions differ: he may have been ordered by the gods, or she hanged herself in grief). There the god Dionysus found her and married her; Zeus granted her immortality, and her wedding crown was set among the stars as the constellation Corona Borealis.

Worship and later reception

Ariadne was worshipped on Naxos, Cyprus, and elsewhere, possibly preserving a pre-Greek Minoan goddess. Her story inspired abundant art and music, from Titian's Bacchus and Ariadne to Richard Strauss's opera Ariadne auf Naxos (1912). The "thread of Ariadne" survives as a term in logic and computing for exhaustive problem-solving by following all threads of a problem.
