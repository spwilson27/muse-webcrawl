Cyan

Cyan is the colour between blue and green on the visible spectrum of light, with a dominant wavelength of roughly 490–520 nanometres. It is an additive primary colour in the RGB model (made by combining blue and green light) and one of the subtractive primary colours in the CMYK model used in colour printing, alongside magenta, yellow and black.

Terminology

The name comes from the Ancient Greek kyanos (κύανος), meaning a dark blue enamel or pigment. "Cyan" was first recorded as a colour name in English in the 19th century. In everyday language, cyan shades are often grouped under names such as turquoise, aqua and teal, and the colour is commonly associated with water, tropical seas and swimming pools.

Uses

Cyan is one of the four CMYK process inks; the standard printing cyan corresponds to the hex colour #00AEEF ("process cyan"). In the RGB colour model, pure cyan is #00FFFF, the complement of red. Cyan dyes and pigments have been used since antiquity in forms such as Egyptian blue, while modern phthalocyanine pigments provide vivid cyan colours.
