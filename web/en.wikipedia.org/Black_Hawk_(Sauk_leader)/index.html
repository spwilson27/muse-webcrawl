Black Hawk (c. 1767 – October 3, 1838), born Ma-ka-tai-me-she-kia-kiak, was a Sauk leader and warrior who lived in what is now the Midwestern United States. He is best known for leading a band of Sauk, Meskwaki, and Kickapoo warriors — known as the "British Band" — in the Black Hawk War of 1832 against the United States.

Early life

Black Hawk was born around 1767 in Saukenuk, a large Sauk village at the confluence of the Rock and Mississippi Rivers. He became a respected war leader through campaigns against the Osage and other rivals, and he fought alongside the British during the War of 1812.

Black Hawk War

In 1832, disputing an 1804 treaty that had ceded Sauk lands east of the Mississippi, Black Hawk led his band back across the river into Illinois to reclaim their homeland and replant corn at Saukenuk. The movement triggered war with the Illinois militia and U.S. Army. After defeats at Stillman's Run and elsewhere, the band retreated north and was destroyed at the Battle of Bad Axe in August 1832. Black Hawk surrendered and was briefly imprisoned.

Later life and legacy

Black Hawk dictated an autobiography, published in 1833, one of the first Native American autobiographies published in the United States. He died in 1838 in Iowa Territory. His name and image have been widely commemorated, including in the Chicago Blackhawks hockey team and the UH-60 Black Hawk helicopter.
