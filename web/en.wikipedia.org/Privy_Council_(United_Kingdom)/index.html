Privy Council (United Kingdom)

The Privy Council (formally His Majesty's Most Honourable Privy Council) is a formal body of advisers to the sovereign of the United Kingdom. Its members, known as privy counsellors, are styled "The Right Honourable" and are appointed for life, mostly senior politicians, judges, and members of the royal family.

Functions

The Council principally gives effect to government decisions through Orders in Council — legislative instruments used for matters ranging from the constitution of British Overseas Territories to royal charters and the approval of statutes of universities. The sovereign attends meetings but acts on ministerial advice; day-to-day work is conducted by committees, most importantly the Judicial Committee.

Judicial Committee

The Judicial Committee of the Privy Council (JCPC) is the highest court of appeal for the Crown Dependencies, British Overseas Territories, and a number of independent Commonwealth countries that have retained it, including Jamaica and Mauritius. Its decisions are technically advice to the monarch, delivered as a report.

History

Descending from the Norman curia regis, the Privy Council governed England alongside the monarch until the rise of the Cabinet in the late 17th and 18th centuries. It played a central role in colonial administration and in the government of Ireland. The Accession Council, a special meeting of the Council, proclaims a new sovereign on the death of the old.
