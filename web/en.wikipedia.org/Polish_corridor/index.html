Polish Corridor

Created1919, Treaty of Versailles
RegionPomerelia, lower Vistula valley
EffectSeparated East Prussia from the rest of Germany
Ended1939 (German invasion of Poland)

The Polish Corridor was a territory located in the region of Pomerelia (Pomeranian Voivodeship of interwar Poland), which provided the Second Polish Republic with access to the Baltic Sea, thus dividing the bulk of Weimar Germany from the German province of East Prussia.

Background

The corridor was created by the Treaty of Versailles in 1919, realising one of US President Woodrow Wilson's Fourteen Points: an independent Poland with free and secure access to the sea. The port of Danzig (Gdańsk) was made a Free City under League of Nations protection, and Poland built the new port of Gdynia within the corridor.

Interwar tensions

The arrangement was intensely resented in Germany across the political spectrum, and disputes over transit rights and the status of Danzig were constant sources of friction. In March 1939 Germany demanded the return of Danzig and an extraterritorial road and rail link across the corridor; Poland refused. The dispute was a central element of the crisis that led to the German invasion of Poland on 1 September 1939 and the start of the Second World War.
