Arrest

An arrest is the act of apprehending and taking a person into custody, usually because the person has been suspected of or observed committing a crime. After being taken into custody, the person can be questioned further or charged.

Procedure

In many jurisdictions an arrest requires probable cause — a reasonable basis to believe the person committed an offence. Police may arrest with or without a warrant depending on the circumstances. Arrested persons commonly have rights such as the right to remain silent and the right to legal counsel; in the United States these are summarised in the Miranda warning.

Alternatives and related powers

Not every police encounter is an arrest: stops, citations and summonses may require a person to appear in court without detention. A citizen's arrest is a limited power, recognised in some jurisdictions, for private persons to detain someone caught committing a serious offence.

Consequences

An arrest is not a conviction; the arrested person is presumed innocent unless and until proved guilty in court. Arrest records may nevertheless have consequences for employment and travel.
