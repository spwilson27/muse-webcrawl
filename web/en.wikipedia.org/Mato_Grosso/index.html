Mato Grosso

Mato Grosso is a state in west-central Brazil, the country's third largest by area at about 903,000 square kilometres, bordered by Rondônia, Amazonas, Pará, Tocantins, Goiás, Mato Grosso do Sul, and Bolivia. Its capital and largest city is Cuiabá. The name means "thick bush" in Portuguese.

Geography

The state spans three great biomes: Amazon rainforest in the north, the Cerrado savanna of the central plateaus, and the Pantanal, the world's largest tropical wetland, in the south-west, shared with Mato Grosso do Sul and Bolivia. The Xingu Indigenous Park and parts of Amazonia and Chapada dos Guimarães national parks lie within the state. Major rivers include the Paraguay, Cuiabá, Xingu headwaters and the Guaporé.

Economy

Mato Grosso is Brazil's agricultural powerhouse and its leading producer of soybeans, maize and cotton, and the largest cattle herd in the country. Agricultural expansion has driven deforestation in its Amazon and Cerrado portions, a focus of environmental debate. Agribusiness is centred on towns such as Rondonópolis, Sinop and Lucas do Rio Verde; the state is landlocked, exporting via ports reached by highway, river and the Ferronorte railway.

History

Gold strikes in the early 18th century drew bandeirantes to Cuiabá (founded 1719), and the captaincy of Mato Grosso was created in 1748 to hold the western frontier against Spain. The state lost territory to create Rondônia and, in 1977, Mato Grosso do Sul.
