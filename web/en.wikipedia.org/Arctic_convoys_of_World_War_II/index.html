Arctic convoys of World War II
DatesAugust 1941 – May 1945
RouteUnited Kingdom and Iceland to Murmansk and Arkhangelsk, USSR
Convoys78 (outbound PQ/JW, homeward QP/RA)

The Arctic convoys of the Second World War carried Allied supplies from Britain and Iceland to the Soviet ports of Murmansk and Arkhangelsk between August 1941 and May 1945. Sailing through the Norwegian and Barents seas within range of German aircraft, U-boats and surface warships based in occupied Norway, and through some of the worst weather of any convoy route, they were among the most dangerous Allied operations of the war.

Operations

Some 78 convoys ran in both directions. Outbound convoys were designated PQ (later JW) and homeward convoys QP (later RA). They delivered roughly four million tons of cargo — thousands of tanks and aircraft, vehicles, fuel, food and raw materials — under the Lend-Lease programme and British aid to the Soviet Union.

The PQ 17 disaster

The most famous convoy, PQ 17, sailed in June 1942. Fearing an attack by the German battleship Tirpitz, the Admiralty ordered the convoy to scatter on 4 July. Deprived of mutual protection, the merchant ships were hunted individually: 24 of the 35 merchantmen were sunk, with the loss of hundreds of vehicles, aircraft and tanks. The catastrophe led to a temporary suspension of the convoys and a lasting controversy over the scatter order.

Later convoys and legacy

Convoys resumed in the winter darkness of 1942–43, and in December 1943 Royal Navy forces sank the German battlecruiser Scharnhorst at the Battle of the North Cape while covering convoy JW 55B. Some 3,000 Allied sailors died on the Arctic run. British veterans received the Arctic Star medal only in 2012, after a long campaign, and Russian commemorations of the convoys continue in Murmansk and Arkhangelsk.
