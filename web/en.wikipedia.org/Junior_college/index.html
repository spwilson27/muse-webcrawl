A junior college is a post-secondary educational institution offering two-year programmes that typically lead to an associate degree, a diploma or a certificate, or prepare students to transfer to a four-year college or university. The term is used in several countries with somewhat different meanings.

By country

In the United States, junior colleges arose in the early 20th century — Joliet Junior College in Illinois (1901) is generally regarded as the first public one — to let students complete the first two years of a bachelor's degree close to home. Many American junior colleges later became comprehensive "community colleges" with open admissions, vocational training and adult education; some private junior colleges remain, and a few have expanded into four-year institutions. In Japan, junior colleges (tanki daigaku) are predominantly two-year institutions, historically attended mostly by women, offering fields such as early-childhood education, nutrition and nursing. In India, "junior college" usually refers to higher-secondary schooling (classes 11–12) attached to a college, while in Singapore junior colleges are pre-university institutions where students prepare for the A-level examinations. The Netherlands' former junior colleges and similar two-year schools elsewhere, such as Philippine and Malaysian programmes, typically feed into universities.

Characteristics

Junior colleges generally emphasize teaching over research, smaller classes and lower tuition than universities, and articulation agreements that guarantee transfer credit to partner four-year institutions. Graduates may enter the workforce with applied credentials or continue to a bachelor's degree.
