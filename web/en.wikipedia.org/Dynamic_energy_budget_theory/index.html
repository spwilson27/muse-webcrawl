Dynamic energy budget theory

Dynamic energy budget (DEB) theory is a formal metabolic theory that describes how organisms acquire and use energy and matter over their whole life cycle, as a function of their state and their environment.

Overview

The theory was developed by Bas Kooijman beginning in the late 1970s. In the standard DEB model, an organism is described by structural biomass and an energy reserve. Food is assimilated into reserve, which is then mobilized and allocated: a fixed fraction (the kappa rule) goes to growth and somatic maintenance, and the remainder to development, reproduction and maturity maintenance.

Applications

DEB theory applies across species from bacteria to whales, enabling predictions of growth, reproduction and survival under varying food and temperature conditions. It is used in ecotoxicology, aquaculture, ecology and climate-change research, supported by the large Add-my-Pet database of species parameters.
