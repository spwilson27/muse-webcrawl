Pluperfect

The pluperfect (shortening of plusquamperfect), usually called past perfect in English, is a type of verb form, generally treated as one of the tenses in certain languages, used to refer to an action already completed at a point in the past. Examples in English are: "we had arrived"; "they had written".

The word pluperfect derives from the Latin plus quam perfectum, "more than perfect". The word "perfect" in this sense means "completed"; it contrasts with the "imperfect" (uncompleted or ongoing). In English grammar, "pluperfect" (e.g. "had written") is now usually called the past perfect, since it combines past tense with perfect aspect. (The same term is sometimes used in relation to the grammar of other languages.) English also has a past perfect progressive (or past perfect continuous) form: "had been writing".

Meaning

The pluperfect is traditionally described as a tense whose primary function is to place an event before some other past event. For example, in the sentence "The train had left when we arrived at the station", the departure of the train (pluperfect) occurred before the arrival (simple past). The pluperfect can also be used to express a counterfactual condition in some contexts, as in "If I had known, I would have acted differently."

Formation in various languages

In English, the pluperfect is formed with the auxiliary verb had followed by the past participle of the main verb. In Romance languages such as French, Spanish, and Italian, it is formed with the imperfect tense of an auxiliary verb (usually "to have" or "to be") plus the past participle. In Latin, the pluperfect active was formed with a dedicated set of endings attached to the perfect stem, while the pluperfect passive used the perfect passive participle with the imperfect of esse ("to be").

German forms the Plusquamperfekt with the preterite of haben or sein plus the past participle. Ancient Greek had a pluperfect tense formed from the perfect stem with a prefixed augment and secondary endings.
