Wolof language

Wolof (Wolof: ولوف) is a language of Senegal, Mauritania and the Gambia that belongs to the Senegambian branch of the Niger–Congo language family. It is the native language of the Wolof people, and is spoken as a lingua franca by the large majority of the population of Senegal, although French is the country's official language.

Speakers and status

Several million people speak Wolof as a first language, and many more as a second language. In Senegal it serves as the principal language of interethnic communication and of the capital, Dakar. Although French holds official status, Wolof dominates everyday commerce, media and popular culture.

Writing

Wolof has historically been written in an adapted Arabic script known as Wolofal, still used for religious and traditional purposes. The official Latin-based orthography, standardized by the Senegalese government in the 1970s, uses letters such as ë, ñ, ŋ and x to represent sounds not present in French.

Features

Wolof is notable for its noun class system, expressed through agreement on determiners and other modifiers, and for its rich system of verbal extensions and focus constructions. It has borrowed vocabulary from Arabic, French, Portuguese and English.
