Faster-than-light

Also calledFTL, superluminal
Speed of light299,792,458 m/s (c)
Status in physicsForbidden for matter/information by special relativity

Faster-than-light (also FTL or superluminal) travel and communication are the conjectural propagation of matter or information at speeds greater than the speed of light (c). The special theory of relativity implies that only particles with zero rest mass (such as photons) may travel at the speed of light, and that nothing may travel faster, because an object would require infinite energy to accelerate to c.

Physics

Certain phenomena appear superluminal without carrying information faster than light — for example phase velocities, Cherenkov radiation contexts, and the recession of distant galaxies due to the expansion of space, which general relativity permits to exceed c. Hypothetical FTL mechanisms in general relativity, such as the Alcubierre warp drive and traversable wormholes, require exotic matter with negative energy density and raise problems of causality, since true FTL travel would allow effects to precede causes in some reference frames. Tachyons, hypothetical particles that always move faster than light, have never been observed.

In fiction

Faster-than-light travel is a staple of science fiction, enabling interstellar settings; fictional devices include warp drives, hyperspace, jump drives, and wormholes. The 2011 OPERA experiment's apparent superluminal neutrino result was later traced to experimental error, reaffirming the light-speed limit.
