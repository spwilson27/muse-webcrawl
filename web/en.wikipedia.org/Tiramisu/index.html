Tiramisu

Tiramisu is an Italian dessert made of ladyfinger biscuits (savoiardi) dipped in coffee, layered with a whipped mixture of eggs, sugar and mascarpone, and dusted with cocoa. The name comes from the Italian tirami su, "pick me up" or "cheer me up", referring to its coffee and cocoa.

CourseDessert
Place of originItaly
Main ingredientsSavoiardi, mascarpone, eggs, sugar, coffee, cocoa

History

Tiramisu's origins are disputed between the Veneto and Friuli-Venezia Giulia regions. It is commonly credited to the restaurant Le Beccherie in Treviso, where it is said to have been created around 1969–1972, associated with restaurateur Ado Campeol, sometimes called the "father of tiramisu". Earlier antecedents in layered, coffee-soaked desserts have been proposed, but the modern recipe rose to worldwide popularity in the 1980s and 1990s.

Preparation and variations

The classic recipe uses raw egg yolks beaten with sugar and mascarpone, sometimes lightened with whipped cream; many modern versions omit raw eggs or use pasteurised ones. Variations flavour the cream with Marsala wine or amaretto, and reinterpret the dessert with fruit, matcha or other flavourings, in cakes, glasses and frozen forms.
