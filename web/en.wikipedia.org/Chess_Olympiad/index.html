Chess Olympiad

The Chess Olympiad is a biennial chess tournament in which teams representing nations of the world compete. FIDE organises the tournament and selects the host nation. Amidst the COVID-19 pandemic, FIDE held an Online Chess Olympiad in 2020 and 2021, which featured mixed teams. The use of the name "Chess Olympiad" dates to the first official event, held in London in 1927.

History

Unofficial olympiads were held in Paris (1924) and Budapest (1926) before FIDE organised the first official Olympiad in London in 1927, won by Hungary. The event became biennial after the Second World War. The Soviet Union dominated from its debut in 1952 until the end of the Cold War; after 1991, Russia, Ukraine, Armenia, China, and the United States have been among the leading teams. A separate Women's Chess Olympiad began in 1957 and has been held concurrently since 1976.

Format

Teams of four players (plus a reserve) compete over eleven rounds under the Swiss system. Since 2008, standings are decided by match points. Individual medals are awarded for board prizes based on performance rating. The open section draws around 180–190 national teams, making the Olympiad one of the largest team sporting events in the world.

Recent editions

The 44th Olympiad was held in Chennai, India, in 2022, where Uzbekistan won the open section and Ukraine the women's. The 45th Olympiad took place in Budapest in 2024, with India winning both the open and women's events.
