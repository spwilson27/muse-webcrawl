Supermoto

Supermoto, originally known in the United States as Superbikers, is a form of motorcycle racing held on tracks that alternate between three kinds of surfaces: the hard-packed dirt of flat track, the irregular jumps of motocross, and the paved tarmac of road racing. The motorcycles themselves are customised dirt bikes fitted with road-racing wheels and tyres, typically 17-inch rims.

History

The discipline traces to the ABC television show Superbikers in 1979, which sought to find America's best all-round rider. The sport flourished in France in the 1980s (as Supermotard), and a world championship, the FIM Supermoto World Championship (S1GP), was established in 2002, alongside the Supermoto of Nations team event.

Machines and technique

Supermoto bikes are usually single-cylinder four-stroke machines of 250–700 cc with long-travel suspension lowered for pavement, oversized brakes, and slick or cut-slick tyres. Riders famously back the rear wheel into corners ("backing it in"), sliding the bike sideways under braking — a technique shared with flat track and speedway.
