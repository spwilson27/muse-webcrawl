Thurible

A thurible (via Old French from Medieval Latin turibulum) is a metal censer suspended from chains, in which incense is burned during worship services. It is used in Christian churches by Roman Catholics, Eastern Orthodox, and some Lutherans and Anglicans, and in other religious traditions. The acolyte or altar server who carries the thurible is called the thurifer.

Description

A thurible is a metal bowl or vessel, usually with a pierced cover to allow smoke to escape, suspended from three or four chains. A fourth chain, where present, is used to raise the lid. Charcoal embers are placed inside and incense grains are sprinkled on them by the celebrant, producing fragrant smoke. The thurible is swung gently to disperse the smoke and keep the coals glowing.

Use

Incense is used to bless and honor people and objects: the altar, the Book of the Gospels, the gifts of bread and wine, the congregation, and the body of a deceased person at funerals may all be incensed. In the Roman Rite it is used at solemn Masses, processions, Benediction of the Blessed Sacrament, and the Liturgy of the Hours. Bodies of tradition associate the rising smoke with prayers ascending to heaven (cf. Psalm 141:2; Revelation 8:3–4).

The Botafumeiro

The most famous thurible is the Botafumeiro of Santiago de Compostela Cathedral in Spain, one of the largest censers in the world. Weighing about 80 kg, it is swung across the transept by a team of eight men (tiraboleiros), reaching speeds of up to 68 km/h.
