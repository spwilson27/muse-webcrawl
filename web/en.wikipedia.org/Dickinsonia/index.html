Dickinsonia

Dickinsonia
Temporal rangeEdiacaran, ~567–550 million years ago
GenusDickinsonia Sprigg, 1947
Type speciesDickinsonia costata

Dickinsonia is a genus of extinct organism of the Ediacaran biota, living roughly 567–550 million years ago, before the Cambrian explosion. Its fossils are flat, oval, quilted discs composed of many rib-like segments radiating from a central groove, ranging from a few millimetres to over a metre in length. It was among the first Ediacaran fossils discovered, described by Reg Sprigg from the Ediacara Hills of South Australia in 1947.

A fossil of Dickinsonia costata, showing the characteristic quilted body.

Classification debate

The affinities of Dickinsonia were disputed for decades: it was variously interpreted as a jellyfish, a worm, a lichen, a fungus, or a member of an extinct kingdom ("Vendozoa"). In 2018, analysis of fossil biomarkers preserved in specimens from the White Sea region of Russia recovered cholesteroids — sterols characteristic of animals — providing strong evidence that Dickinsonia was an early animal, one of the oldest known.

Palaeobiology

Dickinsonia apparently grew by adding segments at one end, moved slowly across microbial mats, and fed by absorbing nutrients through its underside (external digestion of the mat beneath it). Trace fossils recording its movement and feeding are among the earliest evidence of animal locomotion.
