Heritability of IQ

Research on the heritability of IQ examines how much of the variation in intelligence test scores within a population is associated with genetic variation. Studies of twins and adoptees generally estimate the heritability of IQ in adulthood to be roughly between 0.5 and 0.8 in the populations studied, with estimates increasing from childhood to adulthood.

Interpretation

Heritability is a population statistic: it describes variation within a particular group at a particular time, not the degree to which an individual's intelligence is "caused" by genes, and high heritability does not imply that environmental interventions cannot change scores. Secular gains in test scores (the Flynn effect) show that average IQ can change substantially across generations.

The topic has generated continuing controversy, including disputes about group differences, the validity of extrapolating heritability estimates across populations, and interactions between genes and environment such as the finding that heritability may be lower in disadvantaged environments.
