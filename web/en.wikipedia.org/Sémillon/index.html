Sémillon

Sémillon is a golden-skinned grape used predominantly to make dry and sweet white wines, most notably in France and Australia. In France it is the principal variety of the sweet wines of Sauternes and Barsac.

History

The grape has been cultivated in Bordeaux since at least the 18th century. Susceptible to botrytis (noble rot) under the right conditions, it produces the honeyed, long-lived dessert wines for which the region is famous.

Wine characteristics

Dry Sémillon is typically full-bodied with flavours of lemon, green apple, and lanolin, developing honeyed and nutty notes with age. In the Hunter Valley of Australia it produces notably age-worthy dry whites.

Regions

Beyond Bordeaux, significant plantings exist in Australia, South Africa, Chile, and California, often blended with Sauvignon blanc.
