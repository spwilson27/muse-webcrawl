Harran

Harran is a district and town in Şanlıurfa Province, southeastern Turkey, near the Syrian border. Identified with the ancient city of Harran (Carrhae), it is one of the oldest continuously inhabited places in the region, mentioned in the Hebrew Bible as a home of Abraham's family and famous in Roman history as the site of the Battle of Carrhae in 53 BC.

History

Harran was a major centre on trade routes between Anatolia, Mesopotamia, and the Levant from the third millennium BC. In 53 BC the Roman army of Marcus Licinius Crassus was destroyed there by the Parthians under Surena, one of Rome's worst defeats. Under Islamic rule the city became a centre of learning: the Sabians of Harran were noted astronomers and translators, and scholars such as Thabit ibn Qurra came from the city. Harran was largely destroyed during the Mongol invasions of the thirteenth century.

Modern Harran

Today the village is known for its distinctive conical beehive houses built of mud brick and adobe, and for the ruins of its great mosque and university, among the oldest in the Islamic world.
