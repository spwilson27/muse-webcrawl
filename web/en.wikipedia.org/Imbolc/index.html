Imbolc or Imbolg, also called Saint Brigid's Day, is a Gaelic traditional festival marking the beginning of spring. It is held on 1 February, roughly halfway between the winter solstice and the spring equinox, and is one of the four Gaelic seasonal festivals along with Samhain, Beltane, and Lughnasadh.

Traditions

Historically observed in Ireland, Scotland, and the Isle of Man, the festival is associated with the goddess Brigid and her Christian successor, Saint Brigid of Kildare. Customs include weaving Brigid's crosses from rushes, making a Brigid doll (brídeóg), visiting holy wells, and divination for the coming season's weather.

Modern observance

Imbolc is celebrated by modern pagans, including Wiccans, for whom it is one of the eight sabbats of the Wheel of the Year. In Ireland, St Brigid's Day became a public holiday from 2023.
