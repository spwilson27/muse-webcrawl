Degenerate matter
A white dwarf is supported against gravity by electron degeneracy pressure.
TypesElectron-degenerate, neutron-degenerate, quark matter
Found inWhite dwarfs, neutron stars, metallic hydrogen
Governing principlePauli exclusion principle

Degenerate matter occurs when the Pauli exclusion principle significantly alters a state of matter at low temperature. The term is used in astrophysics to refer to dense stellar configurations. Degenerate matter is supported by degeneracy pressure, which arises from the quantum mechanical requirement that no two identical fermions may occupy the same quantum state. Unlike thermal pressure, degeneracy pressure does not depend strongly on temperature.

Electron-degenerate matter

Electron degeneracy pressure supports white dwarf stars against gravitational collapse. As a star's core contracts, electrons are forced into higher momentum states, producing an outward pressure. Above the Chandrasekhar limit of about 1.44 solar masses, electron degeneracy pressure can no longer support the star.

Neutron-degenerate matter

In neutron stars, gravity is resisted by neutron degeneracy pressure and repulsive strong-force interactions. Neutron-degenerate matter is extraordinarily dense — a teaspoonful would weigh billions of tonnes.

Other forms

Metals at room temperature contain electron gases that are partially degenerate. Metallic hydrogen in the interiors of gas giants such as Jupiter is also believed to be degenerate. Still more exotic hypothetical forms include quark matter and preon matter at extreme densities.
