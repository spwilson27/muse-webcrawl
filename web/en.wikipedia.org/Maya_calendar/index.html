Maya calendar

The Maya calendar is a system of calendars used in pre-Columbian Mesoamerica and in many modern communities in the Guatemalan highlands, Veracruz, Oaxaca and Chiapas, Mexico. The essentials of the Maya calendar are based upon a system which had been in common use throughout the region, dating back to at least the 5th century BCE. It shares many characteristics with calendars employed by other earlier Mesoamerican civilizations, such as the Zapotec and Olmec, and contemporary or later ones such as the Mixtec and Aztec calendars.

The Tzolk'in and Haab'

The most important calendar was the 260-day Tzolk'in, combining 20 named days with 13 numbers, used for ritual and divination. The 365-day Haab' was a solar calendar of 18 months of 20 days plus a short month of 5 days (Wayeb'). The two cycles synchronised every 52 years in the Calendar Round, after which a date combination repeated.

The Long Count

For longer periods the Maya used the Long Count, a vigesimal (base-20) count of days from a mythical creation date, conventionally correlated to 11 August 3114 BCE in the Gregorian calendar. Long Count inscriptions tracked dynastic history across centuries. The completion of a 13-baktun cycle on 21 December 2012 attracted widespread popular attention, although Maya scholars stressed that the Maya themselves did not predict an apocalypse.
