Bell's theorem is a result in the foundations of quantum mechanics proved by physicist John Stewart Bell in his 1964 paper "On the Einstein Podolsky Rosen Paradox". It shows that no physical theory based on local hidden variables can reproduce all the predictions of quantum mechanics: correlations between measurements on entangled particles violate inequalities (Bell inequalities) that any local realistic theory must obey.

Background

Einstein, Podolsky and Rosen (1935) argued that quantum mechanics was incomplete and might be supplemented by hidden variables. Bell formalised the EPR assumptions of locality and realism and derived testable constraints, later generalised in the CHSH inequality.

Experiments

Experiments beginning with Freedman and Clauser (1972) and Alain Aspect (1982) found violations of Bell inequalities in agreement with quantum theory. "Loophole-free" tests in 2015 closed the major experimental loopholes simultaneously. The results underpin quantum information technologies such as device-independent quantum cryptography and certified randomness.
