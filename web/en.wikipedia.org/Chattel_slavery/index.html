Chattel slavery

Chattel slavery is a system in which enslaved people are treated legally as the personal property — chattels — of their owner, who may buy, sell, trade or bequeath them. It was the form of slavery practised in the Americas, where enslaved Africans and their descendants were held as property for life and their status was inherited.

Unlike some earlier forms of servitude, chattel slavery was typically racialised, lifelong and hereditary. Abolition movements in the 19th century ended legal chattel slavery in most of the Atlantic world, though its economic and social legacies persisted.
