Pisa is a city in Tuscany, central Italy, straddling the River Arno just before it empties into the Ligurian Sea. It is the capital of the province of Pisa and is known worldwide for its Leaning Tower, the campanile of the cathedral on the Piazza dei Miracoli (a UNESCO World Heritage Site). Although most visitors come for the tower, the city also preserves extensive medieval walls, Romanesque churches, and Gothic and Renaissance palaces.

History

An Etruscan and then Roman port, Pisa rose in the early Middle Ages into one of Italy's four great maritime republics. Pisan fleets fought Saracens across the Mediterranean, took part in the First Crusade (1099), and built trading colonies from Sardinia to the Levant. The city's golden age in the 11th–13th centuries produced its distinctive Pisan Romanesque architecture — the cathedral (begun 1063), baptistery, and tower (begun 1173, already leaning within a decade because of soft alluvial soil). Defeat by Genoa at the Battle of Meloria (1284) and the silting of its harbour ended Pisa's sea power; Florence conquered it in 1406. The leaning tower, stabilised by engineering work completed in 2001, leans at about 3.97 degrees.

Education and science

The University of Pisa, founded in 1343, counts Galileo Galilei — born in Pisa in 1564 — among its students and lecturers, as well as Nobel laureates Enrico Fermi and Carlo Rubbia. Together with the Scuola Normale Superiore and Scuola Superiore Sant'Anna, it gives the small city an exceptionally large student population.

Transport

Pisa International Airport (Galileo Galilei) is Tuscany's principal airport. The city lies on the main Tyrrhenian railway between Genoa and Rome and is connected by motorway to Florence and Livorno.
