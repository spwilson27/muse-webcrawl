Danza de los Voladores

The Danza de los Voladores ("Dance of the Flyers"), also known as Palo Volador, is an ancient Mesoamerican ceremony and ritual still performed today, most famously at Papantla in the Mexican state of Veracruz, by the Totonac people and other groups. In 2009 UNESCO inscribed the ritual ceremony of the Voladores on its Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

The ritual

Five men climb a pole 20 to 30 metres high. Four of them — the voladores — tie ropes around their waists, while the fifth, the caporal, stands on a small platform at the top playing a flute and drum. The four flyers launch themselves backwards into the air, descending slowly as the ropes unwind from the pole, each making 13 turns before reaching the ground; four flyers times 13 turns gives 52, the number of years in the Mesoamerican calendar cycle.

Origins

According to tradition, the ceremony began more than 500 years ago as a plea to the gods to end a severe drought and bring rain: the flyers, dressed as birds, descend like messengers between the heavens and the earth. The rite was suppressed in the colonial era but survived, particularly in the Totonacapan region.
