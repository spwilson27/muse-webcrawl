Cahal Pech is a Maya site located near the town of San Ignacio in the Cayo District of Belize. The name means "Place of the Ticks" in the Yucatec Maya language (from cahal, "place", and pech, "tick"), a name given in the 1950s because of ticks found during early excavations. The site occupies a hilltop overlooking the confluence of the Mopan and Macal rivers and the modern town below.

History

Cahal Pech is one of the earliest Maya sites in the Belize River valley, settled by around 1200 BCE in the Middle Preclassic period. It developed into a medium-sized ceremonial centre with temple pyramids, palaces, plaza groups and two ball courts; its acropolis complex (Structure A-1 and associated buildings) is among the most substantial at the site, with the tallest temple reaching about 23 metres (77 ft). The site remained occupied into the Late Classic and was abandoned around 900 CE, in common with many lowland Maya centres, though some Postclassic offerings suggest later visitation.

Archaeology

Excavations began in the 1950s and were renewed from 1988 under the Belize Department of Archaeology. Finds include carved monuments, burials with jade and ceramic offerings, and evidence of long-distance trade. The site is now an archaeological park with a small visitor centre and museum, easily accessible from San Ignacio.
