Mahanadi River

CountryIndia
StatesChhattisgarh, Odisha
Length858 km (533 mi)
SourceSihawa, Dhamtari district, Chhattisgarh

The Mahanadi is a major river in east-central India. It rises in the hills of southeastern Chhattisgarh near Sihawa, flows eastward through Chhattisgarh and Odisha for about 858 kilometres, and drains into the Bay of Bengal near Paradip, forming a large delta. Its name means "great river" in Sanskrit.

Course and dams

Major tributaries include the Seonath, Jonk, Hasdeo, Mand, Ib, Ong and Tel. The Hirakud Dam, completed in 1957 near Sambalpur, is one of the longest earthen dams in the world and was among the first major river-valley projects of independent India, built for flood control, irrigation and hydroelectric power. The Mahanadi delta is one of India's most fertile rice-growing regions, and the river is notorious for devastating monsoon floods, mitigated in part by Hirakud.

Water disputes

Sharing of the Mahanadi's waters between Chhattisgarh and Odisha has been the subject of a long-running inter-state dispute, referred to a tribunal in 2018.
