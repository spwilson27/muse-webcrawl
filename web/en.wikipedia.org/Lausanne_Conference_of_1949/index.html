Lausanne Conference of 1949

The Lausanne Conference of 1949 was convened by the United Nations Conciliation Commission for Palestine (UNCCP) in Lausanne, Switzerland, from 27 April to 12 September 1949. Delegations from Israel and the Arab states (Egypt, Jordan, Lebanon, and Syria — participating as a combined Arab delegation at first) met under UN auspices following the 1948 Arab–Israeli War to attempt a comprehensive settlement of the Palestine question, including borders, the status of Jerusalem, and the Palestinian refugee problem.

Background

The conference followed the armistice agreements signed at Rhodes earlier in 1949 between Israel and its neighbors. The UNCCP, established by UN General Assembly Resolution 194 (December 1948) — which also affirmed that refugees wishing to return to their homes should be permitted to do so — was charged with facilitating a final peace. The Conciliation Commission consisted of representatives of the United States, France, and Turkey.

Proceedings

On 12 May 1949 the parties signed the Lausanne Protocol, agreeing to work toward the territorial objectives embodied in the 1947 UN Partition Plan map, which each side interpreted differently: Israel regarded it as a basis for negotiation, the Arab states as implying territorial terms and, above all, refugee return as preconditions.

The central disputes proved intractable. The Arab delegations insisted on implementation of the right of return for the roughly 700,000 Palestinian refugees before or alongside territorial negotiations; Israel offered to repatriate a limited number — most famously a conditional offer of 100,000 refugees as part of an overall settlement — and argued that the bulk of the refugees should be resettled in Arab states with compensation. Territory was equally divisive: Israel sought recognition of the armistice lines (which left it with considerably more territory than the partition plan allotted), while Arab states sought concessions, particularly in the Negev and Galilee, and an international regime for Jerusalem as envisaged by the UN.

Outcome and legacy

The conference adjourned in September 1949 without agreement and is generally judged a failure, though it produced some of the most detailed early negotiations on the refugee question and compensation. The UNCCP continued to exist in a largely caretaker role for years afterward. The issues framed at Lausanne — borders, Jerusalem, and the refugees — have remained at the core of the Israeli–Palestinian conflict ever since.
