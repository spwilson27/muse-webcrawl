Categorical theory

In mathematical logic, a categorical theory is a first-order theory all of whose models (of a given cardinality, in the usual usage) are isomorphic to one another. A theory that is categorical in some infinite cardinal is called categorical in that power; by the Łoś–Vaught test, a theory categorical in some infinite cardinality and with no finite models is complete.

Examples

The theory of dense linear orders without endpoints is ℵ0-categorical (Cantor's theorem: the rationals are the unique countable example), but not categorical in uncountable cardinalities. The theory of algebraically closed fields of a fixed characteristic is categorical in every uncountable cardinality but not in ℵ0.

Morley's theorem

The subject was transformed by Morley's categoricity theorem (1965), which states that a countable first-order theory categorical in one uncountable cardinality is categorical in all uncountable cardinalities. This result launched modern stability theory and the classification programme in model theory.
