Duchy of Pomerania

The Duchy of Pomerania was a duchy in Pomerania on the southern shore of the Baltic Sea, ruled by the House of Griffin (the Greifen dynasty) from the 12th century until 1637. At various times it was a fief of Poland, Denmark, the Holy Roman Empire, and Brandenburg.

History

The duchy emerged under Wartislaw I in the 12th century and was Christianised through the missions of Otto of Bamberg. It was repeatedly partitioned among heirs into smaller duchies such as Pomerania-Stettin and Pomerania-Wolgast, and reunified under Bogislaw X in 1478. The duchy adopted Lutheranism during the Reformation. Devastated in the Thirty Years' War, it ceased to exist when the last Griffin duke, Bogislaw XIV, died in 1637; its territory was subsequently divided between Sweden and Brandenburg-Prussia by the Peace of Westphalia (1648).
