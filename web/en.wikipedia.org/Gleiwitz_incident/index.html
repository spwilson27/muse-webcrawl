Gleiwitz incident
Date31 August 1939
LocationGleiwitz, Germany (now Gliwice, Poland)
TypeFalse flag attack
PerpetratorsSS, SD (Operation Himmler)

The Gleiwitz incident was a false flag attack on the German radio station Sender Gleiwitz on the night of 31 August 1939, staged by Nazi forces as part of Operation Himmler. SS operatives dressed in Polish uniforms seized the station and broadcast a short anti-German message in Polish, while the body of Franciszek Honiok, a Silesian farmer arrested the day before, was left at the scene as "evidence" of Polish aggression.

Purpose

The incident was one of about twenty staged border provocations intended to give Adolf Hitler a propaganda pretext for invading Poland. In his Reichstag speech of 1 September 1939, Hitler cited the Gleiwitz attack among the alleged Polish border violations justifying war. Germany invaded Poland that morning, beginning the Second World War in Europe.

Exposure

The operation was organised by Reinhard Heydrich and Heinrich Müller and led on the ground by Alfred Naujocks, whose affidavit at the Nuremberg trials described the plot in detail. Concentration camp prisoners used as additional corpses were referred to by the SS code name "canned goods" (Konserve).
