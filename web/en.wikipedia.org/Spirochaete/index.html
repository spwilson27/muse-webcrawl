Spirochaete

A spirochaete is a member of the phylum Spirochaetota, a group of Gram-negative bacteria distinguished by their long, helically coiled (corkscrew-shaped) cells and by endoflagella that run between the inner and outer membranes, enabling a characteristic twisting motility through viscous media such as mucus and connective tissue.

Notable members

Medically important spirochaetes include Treponema pallidum, the cause of syphilis; Borrelia burgdorferi, which causes Lyme disease; and Leptospira species, which cause leptospirosis. Many other spirochaetes are free-living in aquatic environments or live commensally in animal guts.
