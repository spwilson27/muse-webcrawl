Public housing authority

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A public housing authority (also known as a housing authority or housing agency) is a governmental body that governs some or all publicly owned housing in a jurisdiction, most commonly a city, county or state in the United States. Housing authorities administer public housing developments and the Housing Choice Voucher programme (Section 8), and receive funding primarily from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD).

Organisation

There are roughly 3,300 public housing authorities in the United States. Although created under state enabling legislation and governed by boards appointed by local officials, they operate with substantial federal funding and regulation. The largest is the New York City Housing Authority (NYCHA), which houses hundreds of thousands of residents.

Functions

Housing authorities typically:

  • own and manage public housing developments;
  • administer rental assistance vouchers that subsidise tenants in private housing;
  • maintain waiting lists and determine eligibility based on income;
  • inspect units for compliance with housing quality standards.

History

The American public housing system originated with the United States Housing Act of 1937, which established a federal-local framework under which local authorities would build and operate housing for low-income families, funded by federal loans and subsidies. Later legislation, notably the Housing and Community Development Act of 1974, created the Section 8 programme and shifted policy emphasis toward vouchers.
