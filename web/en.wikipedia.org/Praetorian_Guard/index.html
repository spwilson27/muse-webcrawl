Praetorian Guard

The Praetorian Guard (Latin: cohortes praetoriae) was an elite unit of the Imperial Roman army that served as personal bodyguards and intelligence agents for the Roman emperors. During the era of the Roman Republic, the Praetorians served as a small escort force for high-ranking officials, while during the Roman Empire they became a privileged and powerful political force in Rome, capable of making and unmaking emperors.

History

Augustus established the Guard as a permanent corps in 27 BC, initially nine cohorts of about 500 men. Under Sejanus, prefect under Tiberius, the Guard was concentrated in the Castra Praetoria barracks in Rome, greatly increasing its political weight. The Praetorians proclaimed Claudius emperor in AD 41 after the murder of Caligula, and in AD 193 they notoriously auctioned the imperial throne to Didius Julianus.

Organisation and privileges

Praetorians enjoyed higher pay, shorter service terms (16 years versus 20–25 for legionaries), and donatives from emperors seeking their loyalty. The corps was commanded by one or two Praetorian prefects (praefecti praetorio), among the most powerful officials in the empire.

Abolition

After backing the usurper Maxentius, the Guard was defeated at the Battle of the Milvian Bridge in AD 312 and permanently disbanded by Constantine I, who demolished their barracks.
