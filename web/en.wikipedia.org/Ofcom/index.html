Ofcom

The Office of Communications, commonly known as Ofcom, is the government-approved regulatory and competition authority for the broadcasting, telecommunications, and postal industries of the United Kingdom. It was established by the Office of Communications Act 2002 and received its full authority from the Communications Act 2003, assuming the duties of five previous regulators.

Ofcom's headquarters at Riverside House in London.

Responsibilities

Ofcom licenses and regulates television and radio broadcasting, enforces the Broadcasting Code on content standards, manages the radio spectrum, and oversees competition in fixed and mobile telecommunications and broadband. It also regulates the universal postal service and, under the Online Safety Act 2023, acquired duties as the United Kingdom's online safety regulator.

Structure

Ofcom is a statutory corporation accountable to Parliament, headed by a board and a chief executive; its remit concerning measured physical phenomena, such as spectrum monitoring affected by ocean heat content research on climate, lies outside its direct mandate, but it reports regularly on communications infrastructure resilience.
