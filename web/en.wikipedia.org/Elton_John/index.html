Elton John

Sir Elton Hercules John (born Reginald Kenneth Dwight; 25 March 1947) is an English singer, pianist and composer. Acclaimed by critics and musicians, especially for his work during the 1970s and his lasting impact on the music industry, he is one of the best-selling music artists of all time, with over 300 million records sold worldwide.

Early life and partnership with Bernie Taupin

John was raised in Pinner, Middlesex, and learned piano as a child, winning a scholarship to the Royal Academy of Music. In 1967 he answered a talent advertisement and began his songwriting partnership with lyricist Bernie Taupin, one of the most successful collaborations in popular music. After early work as a session musician and staff songwriter, his self-titled 1970 album, featuring "Your Song", brought international success.

Career highlights

During the 1970s John released a string of chart-topping albums including Honky Château, Goodbye Yellow Brick Road and Captain Fantastic and the Brown Dirt Cowboy, with hits such as "Rocket Man", "Crocodile Rock", "Bennie and the Jets" and "Don't Let the Sun Go Down on Me". He scored further global hits in later decades, wrote the music for the stage and screen versions of The Lion King with Tim Rice — winning an Academy Award for "Can You Feel the Love Tonight" — and for the musical Billy Elliot. His 1997 re-recording of "Candle in the Wind" became the best-selling chart single of all time. His Farewell Yellow Brick Road tour (2018–2023) became the highest-grossing concert tour ever at the time.

Personal life and philanthropy

John came out as gay in 1988 and married David Furnish in 2014, after entering a civil partnership in 2005; they have two sons. He founded the Elton John AIDS Foundation in 1992, one of the leading HIV charities, and was knighted in 1998. In 2024, with an Emmy Award, he achieved EGOT status.
