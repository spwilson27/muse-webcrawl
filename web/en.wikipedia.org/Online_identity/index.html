Online identity

An online identity, also called an internet persona, is a social identity that an internet user establishes in online communities and websites. It may be tightly linked to a person's real-world identity — as with a profile under one's real name — or deliberately pseudonymous or anonymous, detached from offline identifiers.

Forms

Online identities range from authenticated accounts on social networks, email addresses and digital identity credentials, to pseudonyms on forums, avatars in games and virtual worlds, and fully anonymous posting. Platforms differ in their requirements: some enforce real-name policies, while others are built around pseudonymity. A single person commonly maintains several identities for different contexts — professional, social, recreational.

Issues

Research on online identity examines self-presentation and the "online disinhibition effect", in which anonymity loosens social restraints. Practical and legal questions include identity theft and impersonation, verification and authentication (passwords, two-factor authentication, single sign-on), privacy and data protection, the persistence and aggregation of personal data, and the use of sockpuppet accounts to deceive. Systems such as OpenID and federated login aim to manage identity across services, while decentralised identity proposals seek to give users control independent of large platforms.
