Dietary supplement

A dietary supplement is a manufactured product intended to supplement a person's diet in the form of a pill, capsule, tablet, powder, or liquid. Supplements provide nutrients such as vitamins, minerals, amino acids, fatty acids, fibre, or botanicals, either extracted from food sources or synthesised.

Regulation

In the United States, dietary supplements are regulated under the Dietary Supplement Health and Education Act of 1994 (DSHEA). Unlike drugs, they do not require pre-market approval for safety or efficacy from the Food and Drug Administration, though manufacturers are responsible for ensuring safety and the FDA can act against adulterated products. In the European Union, food supplements are governed by Directive 2002/46/EC, which harmonises rules for vitamins and minerals.

Use and evidence

Supplement use is widespread; surveys suggest roughly half of American adults take them. Evidence supports supplementation for specific deficiencies and situations—such as folic acid in pregnancy to prevent neural tube defects and vitamin D where deficiency is common—while large trials of multivitamins in well-nourished populations have generally found little benefit for preventing cardiovascular disease or cancer. Some supplements can interact with medicines or cause harm in excess.

Industry

The global supplement market is worth tens of billions of dollars annually. Common categories include multivitamins, vitamin D, fish oil, probiotics, protein powders, and herbal products such as echinacea and ginkgo.
