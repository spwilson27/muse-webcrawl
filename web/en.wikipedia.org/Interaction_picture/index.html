Interaction picture

In quantum mechanics, the interaction picture (also known as the Dirac picture, after Paul Dirac) is an intermediate representation between the Schrödinger picture and the Heisenberg picture. Whereas in the other two pictures either the state vector or the operators carry all the time dependence, in the interaction picture both state vectors and operators evolve in time: the operators evolve as if under a chosen "free" part of the Hamiltonian, and the states evolve as if under only the remaining "interaction" part.

Definition

The Hamiltonian is split as H = H0 + Hint, where H0 describes a solvable system and Hint the interaction. Interaction-picture operators are obtained from Schrödinger-picture operators by evolving them with H0 alone: AI(t) = eiH0t/ℏ AS e−iH0t/ℏ. The state vector correspondingly evolves under an effective Hamiltonian built from the interaction-picture interaction term, according to iℏ d/dt |ψI(t)⟩ = Hint,I(t) |ψI(t)⟩.

Use

The interaction picture is the natural setting for time-dependent perturbation theory: treating Hint as small, the solution can be expanded in a series of time-ordered integrals of Hint,I, and iteration produces the Dyson series for the time-evolution operator U(t, t0) = T exp[−(i/ℏ) ∫ Hint,I(t') dt']. Its expectation values agree with those computed in the other pictures, since the pictures are related by unitary transformations.

Quantum field theory

In quantum field theory the interaction picture underlies the standard perturbative derivation of scattering amplitudes: fields obey the free-field equations and mode expansions (so creation and annihilation operators carry their simple free time-dependence), while states evolve under the interaction, generating the Feynman diagram expansion of the S-matrix. The picture has a subtlety in relativistic theories — Haag's theorem shows that under certain assumptions the interaction picture cannot strictly exist in quantum field theory — but it is used formally throughout perturbative practice.
