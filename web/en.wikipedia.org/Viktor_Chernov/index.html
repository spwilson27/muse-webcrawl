Viktor Mikhailovich Chernov (Russian: Ви́ктор Миха́йлович Черно́в; 7 December 1873 – 15 April 1952) was a Russian revolutionary and one of the founders of the Socialist Revolutionary Party (SRs). He served as Minister of Agriculture in the Russian Provisional Government of 1917 and as chairman of the short-lived Russian Constituent Assembly in January 1918.

Early life and revolutionary activity

Chernov was born in Novouzensk in Samara Governorate. He studied law at Moscow University, where he became involved in populist (narodnik) circles, and was arrested and exiled in the 1890s. He later emigrated to Western Europe, where he became the principal theorist of the Socialist Revolutionary Party, founded in 1901–1902, editing its newspaper Revolutionary Russia.

Revolution of 1917

Chernov returned to Russia after the February Revolution of 1917 and joined the Provisional Government as Minister of Agriculture in May 1917. His attempts at land reform were blocked by the government and overtaken by peasant seizures of land. In the elections to the Constituent Assembly in November 1917, the SRs won the largest share of the vote, and Chernov was elected chairman of the Assembly when it convened on 18 January 1918. The Bolsheviks dispersed the Assembly after a single day.

Emigration and death

After the dispersal of the Assembly, Chernov participated in anti-Bolshevik governments during the Russian Civil War before emigrating permanently in 1920. He lived in Prague, Paris and, after 1941, New York, where he continued to write on Russian politics and the history of the revolutionary movement. He died in New York in 1952.
