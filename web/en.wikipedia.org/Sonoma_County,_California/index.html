Sonoma County, California

Sonoma County is a county located in the U.S. state of California. As of the 2020 census, its population was 488,863. Its county seat and largest city is Santa Rosa. Sonoma County comprises the Santa Rosa–Petaluma Metropolitan Statistical Area and is part of California's North Coast region, north of the San Francisco Bay Area.

The county is internationally known for its wine production. Sonoma County wine regions include the Sonoma Valley, Russian River Valley, Alexander Valley, and Dry Creek Valley American Viticultural Areas, and the county hosts hundreds of wineries. Agriculture, tourism, and food production are major parts of the economy.

Geography

Sonoma County covers about 1,575 square miles (4,080 km²) of land, stretching from the Pacific coast in the west to the Mayacamas Mountains in the east. The Russian River flows through the county, and redwood forests survive in parks such as Armstrong Redwoods State Natural Reserve near Guerneville.

History

The area was home to Pomo, Coast Miwok, and Wappo peoples before Spanish and Mexican colonization; Mission San Francisco Solano, the northernmost California mission, was founded in Sonoma in 1823. The Bear Flag Revolt of 1846 began in the town of Sonoma. The county was one of California's original 27 counties created in 1850. In October 2017 the Tubbs Fire devastated parts of Santa Rosa, becoming one of the most destructive wildfires in California history at the time.
