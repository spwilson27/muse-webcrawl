Break of gauge

Break of gauge
TypeRailway infrastructure interface
CauseDifferent track gauges meeting
SolutionsTransshipment, bogie exchange, variable-gauge axles, dual gauge

A break of gauge occurs when a railway line of one track gauge meets a line of a different gauge, so that trains cannot run through from one line to the other. Breaks of gauge are a longstanding obstacle to seamless rail transport, historically requiring passengers to change trains and freight to be unloaded and reloaded.


A gauge-changing facility on the Spain–France corridor.

Solutions

Several techniques mitigate a break of gauge: transshipment of goods and passengers; bogie exchange, in which wagon bodies are lifted and their bogies swapped; variable-gauge axles that adjust their wheel spacing while passing through a changer at low speed; dual-gauge track with three or four rails; and containerisation, which makes transshipment faster. Piggyback operation, carrying rail vehicles on transporter wagons, is another option.

Examples

Famous breaks of gauge occur at the borders of Spain and France (Iberian vs standard gauge), between standard-gauge western Europe and the 1,520 mm networks of the former Soviet states, and at China's borders with Mongolia, Russia and Kazakhstan. In Australia, breaks between the states' differing gauges plagued interstate travel for much of the twentieth century.

History

In nineteenth-century Britain the "gauge war" between Brunel's broad gauge and the standard gauge led Parliament to legislate for standardisation in the Gauge Act 1846. Many countries later converted networks to a single gauge at great expense.
