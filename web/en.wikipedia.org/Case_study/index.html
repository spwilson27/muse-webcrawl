Case study

A case study examines a particular instance in depth and in context

A case study is an in-depth, detailed examination of a particular case (or cases) within a real-world context. Case studies are a widely used methodology in the social sciences, business, law, medicine, and policy studies. Typically they draw on multiple sources of evidence — interviews, observation, documents, and quantitative data — to explore a bounded system such as a person, organisation, event, programme, or community.

Approaches

Scholars such as Robert K. Yin and Robert E. Stake have codified case study methodology. Yin distinguishes exploratory, descriptive, and explanatory case studies, emphasising their suitability for "how" and "why" questions about contemporary phenomena that the researcher cannot control. Stake distinguishes intrinsic case studies (undertaken to understand a particular case), instrumental case studies (examining a case to illuminate a broader issue), and collective case studies of multiple cases.

Applications

Case studies are used in clinical medicine and psychology (case reports of individual patients, such as Freud's case histories), in business education (the Harvard Business School case method, in which students analyse real business decisions), in law (the study of leading cases), and in evaluation research. A frequently debated question is the extent to which findings from a single case can be generalised: critics emphasise the absence of statistical generalisability, while defenders point to analytic generalisation and the value of rich, contextual understanding, as in "critical cases" that can falsify general claims.
