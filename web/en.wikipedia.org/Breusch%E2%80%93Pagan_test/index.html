The Breusch–Pagan test, developed by Trevor Breusch and Adrian Pagan in 1979, is a statistical test for heteroscedasticity in a linear regression model. It tests the null hypothesis that the error variances are all equal (homoscedasticity) against the alternative that the variance depends on the values of the explanatory variables.


Residuals whose spread grows with fitted values suggest heteroscedasticity.

Procedure

After estimating the regression by ordinary least squares, the squared residuals are regressed on the independent variables. The test statistic, half (in the original form) or the sample size times the coefficient of determination of this auxiliary regression, is asymptotically chi-squared distributed under the null.

Variants

Koenker's studentised version relaxes the original assumption of normally distributed errors and is widely used. The test is a Lagrange multiplier (score) test and can be adapted to other forms of the variance function; the related White test uses a more general auxiliary regression including squares and cross-products of regressors.

Use

Detecting heteroscedasticity matters because ordinary least squares standard errors are then biased, invalidating inference; analysts respond with robust standard errors or generalised least squares. The Breusch–Pagan test is included in most major statistical software packages.
