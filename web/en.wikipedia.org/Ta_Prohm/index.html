Ta Prohm

Ta Prohm (Khmer: ប្រាសាទតាព្រហ្ម, "Ancestor Brahma") is a temple at Angkor, Cambodia, built in the Bayon style largely in the late 12th and early 13th centuries. It was founded by the Khmer king Jayavarman VII as a Mahayana Buddhist monastery and university, and is famous for being left largely unrestored, overgrown by enormous silk-cotton and strangler fig trees.

History

Jayavarman VII dedicated the monastery in 1186 to his mother, identified with the bodhisattva Prajnaparamita. A famous stele records that the temple housed more than 12,500 people, including 18 high priests and over 600 dancers, and administered 314 villages. After the fall of the Khmer Empire the temple was abandoned and swallowed by jungle.

The trees

Unlike most Angkor temples, Ta Prohm was deliberately conserved in a condition close to that in which early European explorers found it: massive trees grow from the ruined towers and crush the galleries, creating the site's iconic, romantic atmosphere. Conservation today, led by the APSARA Authority with the Archaeological Survey of India, balances stabilising the stonework with preserving the trees.

In popular culture

The temple's appearance in the 2001 film Lara Croft: Tomb Raider made it one of the most visited sites at Angkor, often nicknamed the "Tomb Raider temple".
