Outbreak

In epidemiology, an outbreak is a sudden increase in occurrences of a disease above what is normally expected in a defined community, geographical area or season. An outbreak may be restricted to one location or extend more widely; a widespread outbreak affecting a whole country or continent is an epidemic, and a global one a pandemic.

Investigation

Outbreak investigation typically involves verifying the diagnosis, establishing a case definition, finding cases systematically, describing them by time, place and person, developing and testing hypotheses about the source, and implementing control measures such as isolation, vaccination, or recall of contaminated food.

Types

Outbreaks are classified by their pattern of spread: common-source (point or continuing), propagated (person-to-person), or mixed. Foodborne, waterborne and healthcare-associated outbreaks are common categories investigated by public health agencies.
