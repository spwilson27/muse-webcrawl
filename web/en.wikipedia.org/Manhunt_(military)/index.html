Manhunt (military)

In a military context, a manhunt is an operation conducted by armed forces, intelligence services, or special operations units to locate, capture, or kill a specific high-value individual (HVI) — such as an enemy commander, terrorist leader, or fugitive head of state. Military manhunting combines intelligence collection, surveillance, and direct action, and has become an increasingly prominent feature of twenty-first-century counterterrorism and counterinsurgency warfare.

Concept and methods

Unlike conventional operations directed against places or formations, a manhunt is organised around a person. Intelligence analysts build a picture of the target's "pattern of life" — associates, communications, finances, movements, and safe houses — often organised through target decks or lists such as the "high-value target" lists used in Iraq and Afghanistan. Techniques include signals intelligence, human intelligence networks, aerial surveillance by drones and aircraft, raids by special operations forces, and, controversially, lethal strikes intended to kill the target outright.

Advocates argue that removing key leaders can degrade an insurgent or terrorist network's ability to plan and coordinate; critics reply that "decapitation" can be counterproductive, that targets are quickly replaced, and that the intelligence-driven targeting cycle (sometimes described as "find, fix, finish, exploit, analyse") can consume disproportionate resources and cause civilian harm.

Notable examples

  • The search for Pancho Villa after his 1916 raid on Columbus, New Mexico, which led to the Punitive Expedition into Mexico under General John J. Pershing.
  • The pursuit of Iraqi president Saddam Hussein in 2003 by Task Force 121, ending with his capture near Tikrit in December 2003 (Operation Red Dawn).
  • The hunt for al-Qaeda leader Osama bin Laden, which lasted nearly a decade and ended in May 2011 when U.S. Navy SEALs killed him in Abbottabad, Pakistan (Operation Neptune Spear).
  • The campaign against Abu Musab al-Zarqawi in Iraq, ending in his death in a 2006 airstrike.
  • Israel's long-running operations against Palestinian militant leaders, and the post-1972 effort to track down those responsible for the Munich Olympics massacre.
  • The 1993 hunt for Somali warlord Mohamed Farrah Aidid, culminating in the Battle of Mogadishu.

Legal and ethical issues

Military manhunts raise questions under international humanitarian law concerning distinction, proportionality, and the status of targets — particularly where intelligence agencies rather than uniformed forces carry out killings, and where operations occur in countries with which the hunting state is not formally at war. The use of armed drones for targeted killing, and programmes such as "signature strikes", have been especially contentious.
