Israeli Air Defense Command

CountryIsrael
BranchIsraeli Air Force
RoleGround-based air and missile defence
SystemsIron Dome, David's Sling, Arrow 2/3, Patriot

The Israeli Air Defense Command is the Israel Defense Forces body, subordinate to the Israeli Air Force, responsible for the ground-based air defence of Israel. It operates a layered network of radar, command systems and interceptors against aircraft, rockets, artillery, mortars, cruise missiles and ballistic missiles.

History

Israeli air defence began with anti-aircraft artillery after the state's founding and adopted American Hawk surface-to-air missiles in the 1960s. During the 1973 Yom Kippur War, air-defence units engaged large numbers of Arab aircraft. The Patriot system was first used in combat over Israel during the 1991 Gulf War against Iraqi Scud missiles, an experience that spurred development of the Arrow anti-ballistic-missile programme with the United States.

Layered defence

The command's modern architecture is layered. Iron Dome, operational from 2011, intercepts short-range rockets and shells; David's Sling covers the medium-to-long-range band against larger rockets, cruise missiles and aircraft; Arrow 2 and Arrow 3 intercept ballistic missiles in the upper atmosphere and in space. These are supplemented by Patriot batteries and by early-warning radars. In April and October 2024 the network, together with allied forces, conducted the largest missile-defence operations in history against mass Iranian missile and drone attacks.

Organisation

The command is organised into regional air-defence battalions and regiments operating the interceptor batteries, supported by the air force's surveillance and control network. A dedicated school trains operators, technicians and officers for the force.
