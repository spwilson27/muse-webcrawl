Franciscan Missions in the Sierra Gorda of Querétaro

The mission church of Santiago de Jalpan, the first of the five Sierra Gorda missions.

The Franciscan Missions in the Sierra Gorda of Querétaro are five mission churches built in the mid-18th century in the Sierra Gorda mountains of the Mexican state of Querétaro. Founded under the direction of the Franciscan friar Junípero Serra, they were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 2003 for their richly decorated "mestizo Baroque" facades, which blend European Christian iconography with the hands and symbols of the indigenous Pame builders who constructed them.

The five missions

  • Santiago de Jalpan (built 1751–1758), in Jalpan de Serra — dedicated to Saint James, its facade features an eagle and imagery mixing Franciscan and indigenous motifs.
  • San Miguel Concá (1754–1758), the smallest of the five, notable for a large indigenous sun and a trinity depiction at the top of the facade.
  • San Francisco de Asís del Valle de Tilaco (1754–1762), the best preserved, with a slanted tower and mermaids on its facade.
  • Santa María de la Purísima Concepción del Agua de Landa (1760–1768), in Landa de Matamoros, the last built, with a facade crowded with saints and pre-Hispanic-influenced carving.
  • San Salvador de Tancoyol (1760s), dedicated to Our Lady of Light, noted for its elaborate crosses and empty niches.

History

The missions were established from 1751, when Junípero Serra arrived in the Sierra Gorda to evangelise the Pame people, organising mission communities where indigenous converts lived, worked and learned trades and agriculture. Their success made the Sierra Gorda the model for Serra's later mission work in Alta California. After secularisation in the 19th century the churches were neglected, and their facades were rediscovered under layers of plaster in the 1960s, leading to restoration campaigns.
