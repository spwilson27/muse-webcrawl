Ambisonics

Ambisonics is a full-sphere surround sound format: in addition to the horizontal plane, it covers sound sources above and below the listener. Unlike most channel-based surround formats, its transmission channels do not carry speaker signals; instead they carry a speaker-independent representation of the sound field called B-format, which can then be decoded to any speaker layout.

Ambisonics
Developed: 1970s, UK
Key figures: Michael Gerzon, Peter Craven
Format: B-format (WXYZ and higher orders)
Applications: recording, games, VR/AR audio

History

Ambisonics was developed in the 1970s largely by Michael Gerzon and colleagues, under the auspices of the British National Research Development Corporation. Despite technical advantages, it achieved limited commercial success in its original first-order form in the following decades.

Higher-order Ambisonics and modern use

Higher-order Ambisonics (HOA) extends the spherical-harmonic representation beyond first order for greater spatial resolution. Ambisonics has seen a revival as the standard audio format for 360-degree video and virtual reality, being supported by platforms such as YouTube and by game engines including Unity and Unreal.
