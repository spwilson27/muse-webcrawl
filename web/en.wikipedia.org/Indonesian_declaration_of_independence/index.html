Indonesian declaration of independence

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Indonesian declaration of independence (Indonesian: Proklamasi Kemerdekaan Indonesia) was read at 10:00 on Friday, 17 August 1945, in Jakarta, by Sukarno, accompanied by Mohammad Hatta. It marked the start of the Indonesian National Revolution and the diplomatic and armed resistance of the newly established Republic of Indonesia against the forces of the Netherlands, which sought to re-establish colonial rule over the Dutch East Indies.

Background

Indonesia had been a Dutch colony for over three centuries before Japanese forces occupied the archipelago in 1942 during the Second World War. As Japan's defeat became imminent, nationalist leaders were pressed by radical youth groups (pemuda) to declare independence immediately. In the so-called Rengasdengklok Incident of 16 August, young activists abducted Sukarno and Hatta to compel a prompt proclamation; the leaders agreed, and the text was drafted that night at the home of Japanese Rear Admiral Maeda.

The text

We, the people of Indonesia, hereby declare the independence of Indonesia. Matters concerning the transfer of power and other matters will be carried out in a conscientious manner and in the shortest possible time.

Jakarta, 17 August 1945
In the name of the people of Indonesia,
Sukarno — Hatta

Aftermath

The Netherlands did not recognise the declaration and fought two major "police actions" to restore control. After international pressure, particularly from the United States and the United Nations, sovereignty was formally transferred on 27 December 1949. Indonesia today commemorates 17 August as its Independence Day, a public holiday celebrated with flag ceremonies, games, and parades.
