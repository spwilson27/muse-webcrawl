Enforcement Acts

The Enforcement Acts were three bills passed by the United States Congress between 1870 and 1871 during Reconstruction. They were criminal codes that protected African Americans' right to vote, hold office, serve on juries, and receive equal protection of laws, and authorized federal action against the Ku Klux Klan.

The Acts

The First Enforcement Act (May 1870) prohibited state officials from discriminating in voter registration on the basis of race. The Second (February 1871) provided federal oversight of elections. The Third, known as the Ku Klux Klan Act (April 1871), made state officials liable under federal law for depriving citizens of constitutional rights and empowered the president to suspend habeas corpus to suppress Klan violence.

Effects

President Ulysses S. Grant used the Acts to prosecute Klansmen and break the Klan's power in South Carolina and elsewhere. Federal enforcement waned after Reconstruction ended in 1877, though parts of the statutes remain in force today as civil rights law.
