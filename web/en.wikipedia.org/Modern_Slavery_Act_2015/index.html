Modern Slavery Act 2015


Citation: 2015 c. 30
Royal assent: 26 March 2015
Commenced: 31 July 2015 (in part)
Introduced by: Theresa May, Home Secretary

The Modern Slavery Act 2015 is an Act of the Parliament of the United Kingdom. It is designed to combat modern slavery in the UK and consolidates previous offences relating to trafficking and slavery. The Act, which extends to England and Wales, received royal assent on 26 March 2015 after being introduced by the then Home Secretary, Theresa May.

Provisions

The Act creates offences of slavery, servitude and forced or compulsory labour, and of human trafficking, with maximum sentences up to life imprisonment. It provides for slavery and trafficking prevention orders and risk orders, establishes the office of the Independent Anti-Slavery Commissioner, and includes protections for victims, including a statutory defence for victims who commit offences as a direct consequence of being trafficked or enslaved.

Transparency in supply chains

Section 54 of the Act requires commercial organisations with a total annual turnover of at least £36 million that supply goods or services in the UK to publish an annual statement setting out the steps they have taken to ensure that slavery and human trafficking are not taking place in their supply chains or their own business — or to state that they have taken no such steps.

Reception

The Act was described by the Home Office as the first of its kind in Europe. It has since influenced similar legislation elsewhere, including Australia's Modern Slavery Act 2018. Critics have pointed to limited enforcement mechanisms for the supply-chain provisions and low prosecution rates relative to estimated victim numbers.
