Andrew Goodman

Andrew Goodman (November 23, 1943 – June 21, 1964) was an American civil rights activist. He was one of three civil rights workers murdered by members of the Ku Klux Klan during the Freedom Summer campaign in Mississippi in 1964, together with James Chaney and Michael Schwerner.

Early life and activism

Goodman was born and raised on the Upper West Side of New York City, the middle son of Robert and Carolyn Goodman, both politically engaged. He studied anthropology at Queens College and became involved in civil rights causes as a teenager, travelling to Washington, D.C., to picket and volunteer.

Murder

In June 1964 Goodman travelled to Ohio for training before going to Mississippi to register African American voters. On June 21, his first full day in Mississippi, he, Chaney, and Schwerner were arrested on a pretext near Philadelphia, Neshoba County, released at night, and then ambushed and shot by Klansmen, including local law enforcement officers. Their bodies were found buried in an earthen dam 44 days later, after an intensive FBI investigation (code-named MIBURN) that drew national attention and helped build support for the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965.

Legacy

Edgar Ray Killen was convicted of manslaughter in 2005 for orchestrating the murders. Goodman's family established the Andrew Goodman Foundation to continue his work on voting rights and civic engagement.
