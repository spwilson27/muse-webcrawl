Takeda Shingen (1521–1573) was a Japanese daimyō of Kai Province during the Sengoku period, renowned as one of the era's most capable military strategists and as the long-time rival of Uesugi Kenshin.

Career

Shingen exiled his father Nobutora in 1541 and set about conquering Shinano Province, fighting the famous series of Battles of Kawanakajima against Kenshin between 1553 and 1564. His army was famed for its cavalry charges and disciplined infantry; his battle standard bore the Sun Tzu maxim "swift as the wind, silent as the forest, fierce as fire, immovable as a mountain."

Death and legacy

In 1572 he marched toward Kyoto, defeating Tokugawa Ieyasu at Mikatagahara, but died in 1573, possibly of illness or a wound. His son Katsuyori led the clan to destruction at Nagashino in 1575. Shingen is remembered for capable civil administration, flood-control works and legal codes in Kai.
