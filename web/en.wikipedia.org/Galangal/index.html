Galangal

Galangal is a common name for several tropical rhizomatous spices in the ginger family (Zingiberaceae), most commonly Alpinia galanga (greater galangal), Alpinia officinarum (lesser galangal), and Kaempferia galanga. The rhizomes are used widely in Southeast Asian cooking and in traditional medicine.

Culinary use

Greater galangal is a staple of Indonesian, Malaysian, and Thai cuisine: it is one of the defining aromatics of Thai tom kha soup, Indonesian rendang, and many curry pastes and sambals. The fresh rhizome has a sharp, citrusy, almost pine-like flavour quite distinct from ginger, to which it is related. Lesser galangal, native to China, was much used in medieval European cooking — it appears in recipes of the period alongside ginger and pepper — and remains in use in liqueurs, bitters, and some Eastern European and Russian preparations.

Names and history

The word "galangal" derives, via Arabic khalanjan, from Chinese gāoliángjiāng, "good ginger from Gaozhou". The spice reached Europe through Arab traders by the early Middle Ages; Hildegard of Bingen praised it as a medicine, and it was traded at high prices before falling out of general European use in the early modern period.
