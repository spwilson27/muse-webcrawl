Emperor Yao (Chinese: 堯), traditionally c. 2356 – 2255 BC, is a legendary Chinese ruler, one of the Three Sovereigns and Five Emperors. Venerated in Confucian tradition as a model of wisdom and selflessness, Yao is said to have invented the game of weiqi (Go) and to have passed over his own son in choosing a successor.

Legend

According to the traditional histories such as the Records of the Grand Historian, Yao ascended the throne at twenty and ruled for about a century. He ordered court astronomers to fix the calendar by observing the stars, and commanded officials to control the great flood waters, a task eventually accomplished under his successor Shun by Yu the Great. His capital is placed at Pingyang, in modern Shanxi.

Abdication

Yao's most celebrated act was passing the throne not to his son Danzhu, whom he judged unworthy, but to Shun, a commoner famed for filial piety, to whom he also gave his two daughters in marriage. This abdication to a worthy minister rather than a hereditary heir became a central ideal in Confucian political thought.

Historical assessment

Modern historians regard Yao as legendary rather than historical, though some have proposed that the stories may preserve memories of tribal confederations in Neolithic China. Oracle bone inscriptions do not mention him.
