The 1930 FIFA World Cup was the inaugural FIFA World Cup, the world championship for men's national association football teams. It took place in Uruguay from 13 to 30 July 1930. FIFA selected Uruguay as host because the country was celebrating the centenary of its first constitution and its national team had won the football tournaments at the 1924 and 1928 Summer Olympics.

Tournament

Thirteen teams participated — seven from South America, four from Europe, and two from North America — all by invitation, as no qualification matches were held. All matches were played in Montevideo, with the final held at the newly built Estadio Centenario. In the first World Cup final, Uruguay defeated Argentina 4–2 before a crowd of about 68,000, rematching the 1928 Olympic final.

Legacy

The tournament established the World Cup as football's premier event. Guillermo Stábile of Argentina was the top scorer with eight goals, and the United States finished third, its best-ever result.
