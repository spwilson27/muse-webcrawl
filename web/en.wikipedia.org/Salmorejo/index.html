Salmorejo

Salmorejo, also sometimes called salmorejo cordobés, is a traditional cold soup originating from Córdoba in Andalusia, southern Spain. It is made from tomatoes, bread, olive oil and garlic, blended into a thick, smooth purée and typically served chilled, garnished with diced jamón serrano and chopped hard-boiled egg.

Preparation

Stale white bread, traditionally the local pan de telera, is soaked and blended with ripe tomatoes, garlic, salt and extra virgin olive oil until the mixture emulsifies into a dense, creamy soup considerably thicker than gazpacho, to which it is related. Unlike gazpacho, salmorejo contains no peppers, cucumber or onion. It is chilled for several hours before serving and finished with a drizzle of olive oil and the classic garnish.

History

The soup evolved from earlier Andalusian bread-and-oil emulsions dating back to the period of al-Andalus; tomatoes, a New World ingredient, were incorporated only after their introduction to Spain. Salmorejo became emblematic of Córdoba's cuisine and is served throughout Spain in summer. A related, thicker dip-like preparation, porra antequerana, comes from Antequera in Málaga province.
