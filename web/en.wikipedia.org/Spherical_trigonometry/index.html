Spherical trigonometry

Spherical trigonometry is the branch of spherical geometry that deals with the metrical relationships between the sides and angles of spherical triangles, traditionally expressed using trigonometric functions. On the sphere, geodesics are great circles. Spherical trigonometry is of great importance for calculations in astronomy, geodesy, and navigation.

Spherical triangles

A spherical triangle is formed by three great-circle arcs intersecting pairwise. Its angles sum to more than 180°; the excess over 180° is proportional to the triangle's area (Girard's theorem). The spherical law of cosines states that for sides a, b, c and opposite angles A, B, C: cos a = cos b cos c + sin b sin c cos A.

History

The subject was developed in Hellenistic astronomy and greatly advanced by medieval Islamic mathematicians, including al-Battānī and Naṣīr al-Dīn al-Ṭūsī, who first treated it as a discipline independent of astronomy. Napier's rules simplified computations for right-angled spherical triangles.

Applications

Spherical trigonometry underlies celestial navigation, great-circle distance calculations, and the geometry of the celestial sphere.
