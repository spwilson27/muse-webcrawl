Atomic spectroscopy

Atomic spectroscopy
Field: Analytical chemistry, physics
Techniques: AAS, AES/OES, AFS, ICP-MS
Analytes: Elemental composition

Atomic spectroscopy is the study of the electromagnetic radiation absorbed and emitted by atoms, and a family of analytical techniques that use those characteristic transitions to identify elements and measure their concentrations. Because each element has a unique set of energy levels, its spectrum of lines acts as a fingerprint.

Techniques

In atomic absorption spectroscopy (AAS), a sample is atomised in a flame or graphite furnace and the absorption of light from an element-specific lamp is measured. Atomic emission spectroscopy (AES) measures light emitted by excited atoms; in inductively coupled plasma optical emission spectroscopy (ICP-OES), an argon plasma at several thousand kelvin excites many elements simultaneously. ICP mass spectrometry (ICP-MS) goes further, ionising the sample and separating ions by mass, achieving detection limits in the parts-per-trillion range.

History

The foundations were laid in the 19th century by Kirchhoff and Bunsen, who showed that heated elements emit characteristic lines and used the method to discover caesium and rubidium; Fraunhofer's earlier observation of dark lines in the solar spectrum had recorded the same phenomenon in absorption.
