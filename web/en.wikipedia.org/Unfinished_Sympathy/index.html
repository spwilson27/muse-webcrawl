"Unfinished Sympathy" is a song by the English trip hop group Massive Attack, released in February 1991 as the second single from their debut album Blue Lines. It features vocals by Shara Nelson and a string arrangement by Wil Malone, built around a sample of John Lee Hooker material and layered orchestration.

Background and composition

The song combines hip hop beats, soul-influenced vocals, and sweeping orchestral strings. Nelson's vocal performance is frequently cited as one of the finest in British dance music. During its release, the group temporarily shortened its name to "Massive" to avoid controversy during the Gulf War.

Music video

The video, directed by Baillie Walsh, is a single unbroken tracking shot following Nelson as she walks along West Pico Boulevard in Los Angeles, passing various characters while seemingly oblivious to her surroundings. It is regularly ranked among the greatest music videos of all time.

Reception and legacy

"Unfinished Sympathy" reached number 13 on the UK Singles Chart. It has been repeatedly voted one of the greatest songs of all time in critics' polls and is considered a defining track of the trip hop genre.
