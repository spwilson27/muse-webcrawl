Daniel Gray Quillen (1940–2011) was an American mathematician who was one of the founders of algebraic K-theory and a central figure in homotopical algebra. He received the Fields Medal in 1978.

Work

Quillen earned his PhD at Harvard in 1964 under Raoul Bott, with a thesis on partial differential equations. In the late 1960s he introduced model categories — an axiomatic framework for homotopy theory — and in 1972–73, during a year at the Institut des Hautes Études Scientifiques, he defined the higher algebraic K-groups, transforming the subject. He also proved the Quillen–Suslin theorem (1976, independently with Andrei Suslin), resolving Serre's conjecture that projective modules over polynomial rings are free.

Career

Quillen taught at MIT before becoming Waynflete Professor of Pure Mathematics at Magdalen College, Oxford, in 1984, a post he held until retiring in 2006. He died in 2011 after several years with Alzheimer's disease.
