Robert II of Scotland

Born2 March 1316, Paisley Abbey
Died19 April 1390, Dundonald Castle

Robert II (2 March 1316 – 19 April 1390) was King of Scots from 1371 until his death. He was the first monarch of the House of Stewart (Stuart), which would rule Scotland — and later England and Ireland — for centuries. The son of Walter Stewart, 6th High Steward of Scotland, and Marjorie Bruce, daughter of Robert the Bruce, he was heir presumptive to his uncle David II for much of his life.

Early life and guardianship

Robert served as Guardian of Scotland during David II's minority and during the king's captivity in England after the Battle of Neville's Cross (1346), leading resistance to Edward Balliol and the English. His relationship with David II was often strained, and he was briefly imprisoned after rebelling in 1363.

Reign

Succeeding David II in 1371 at the age of 54, Robert II ruled as an often passive king, delegating power to his sons, notably John, Earl of Carrick (later Robert III), and Robert Stewart, Earl of Fife. Scotland renewed war with England, recovering territory and winning the Battle of Otterburn in 1388, though Robert himself took little personal part. His many children — by his wife Elizabeth Mure and his second wife Euphemia de Ross, and others — founded the web of Stewart lordships that dominated later medieval Scotland.

Death and succession

He died at Dundonald Castle in 1390 and was succeeded by his son as Robert III.
