Eugene V. Debs

Eugene Victor Debs (November 5, 1855 – October 20, 1926) was an American socialist, political activist and trade union leader, one of the founding members of the Industrial Workers of the World (IWW), and the Socialist Party of America's candidate for President of the United States five times between 1900 and 1920 — the last time from a federal prison cell.

Union career

Born in Terre Haute, Indiana, Debs worked on the railroads and rose in the Brotherhood of Locomotive Firemen. Concluding craft unions were too weak, he founded the industrial American Railway Union in 1893, which won a strike against the Great Northern Railway but was crushed in the Pullman Strike of 1894; Debs served six months in prison, where he read socialist literature and emerged a committed socialist.

Socialist campaigns

Debs helped found the Social Democratic Party and then the Socialist Party of America (1901). His presidential vote peaked at about 6% in 1912. He opposed American entry into the First World War; his anti-war Canton Speech of 1918 led to conviction under the Espionage Act and a ten-year sentence. Running in 1920 as Convict No. 9653, he won over 900,000 votes. President Harding commuted his sentence in December 1921.

Legacy

Debs's health never recovered from prison; he died in 1926. His home in Terre Haute is a National Historic Landmark, and his vision of democratic socialism and industrial unionism influenced later American labour and civil-liberties movements.
