Noah's Ark

Noah's Ark (Hebrew: תיבת נח, Tevat Noaḥ) is the ship in the Genesis flood narrative in the Bible, through which God spares Noah, his family, and examples of all the world's animals from a global deluge. According to Genesis, God gave Noah instructions for building the ark and bringing pairs of animals aboard; after the floodwaters receded, the vessel came to rest "on the mountains of Ararat."

The Genesis account

Genesis chapters 6–9 tell how God, seeing human wickedness, resolved to destroy creation by flood, but instructed the righteous Noah to build an ark of gopher wood, three hundred cubits long, fifty wide and thirty high, with three decks. Noah, his sons, and their wives boarded with the animals, and rain fell for forty days and nights; the waters prevailed for 150 days before receding.

Parallels

Similar flood-and-boat stories occur in earlier Mesopotamian literature, including the Epic of Gilgamesh (where the hero is Utnapishtim), the Atrahasis epic, and the Sumerian flood story of Ziusudra. The ark also appears in the Quran, where it is called Safinat Nūḥ, "Noah's ship."

Searches for the ark

Since antiquity there have been reports and expeditions seeking physical remains of the ark, most often on Mount Ararat in present-day Turkey. No scientific evidence for the vessel has been found, and searches continue to attract popular interest.
