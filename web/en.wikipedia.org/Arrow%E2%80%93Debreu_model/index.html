Arrow–Debreu model

The Arrow–Debreu model, developed by Kenneth Arrow and Gérard Debreu in 1954, is a foundational model of general equilibrium in economics. It proves, under specified assumptions, that a competitive equilibrium exists for an economy with complete markets.

Assumptions

The model assumes a finite number of commodities distinguished by physical characteristics, location, date and state of the world; consumers with convex preferences; firms with convex production sets; and a complete set of markets in which every commodity can be traded. Prices adjust so that aggregate excess demand is zero.

Significance

The existence proof used fixed-point theorems and set a standard of rigour in economic theory. The model's framework underlies the two fundamental theorems of welfare economics and much of modern macroeconomics and finance theory, while its strong assumptions — complete markets and perfect competition — define the gap that later research has sought to relax.
