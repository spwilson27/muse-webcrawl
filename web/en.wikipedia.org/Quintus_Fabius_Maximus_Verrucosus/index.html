Quintus Fabius Maximus Verrucosus

Quintus Fabius Maximus Verrucosus
Born: c. 280 BC
Died: 203 BC
Offices: Consul (five times), Dictator (217 BC)
Agnomen: Cunctator ("the Delayer")

Quintus Fabius Maximus Verrucosus (c. 280–203 BC), known as Cunctator, "the Delayer", was a Roman statesman and general of the Second Punic War. Appointed dictator after the disastrous Roman defeat at Lake Trasimene in 217 BC, he adopted the strategy of avoiding pitched battle with Hannibal, instead shadowing the Carthaginian army, harassing its foragers and cutting off its supplies.

Fabian strategy

Fabius's tactics — later called the Fabian strategy — were deeply unpopular with a Roman public eager for revenge, and his master of horse, Minucius, openly defied them. Yet after aggressive Roman commanders led the army to catastrophe at Cannae in 216 BC, the Senate vindicated Fabius's approach, and Rome largely avoided decisive battle in Italy while rebuilding its strength. The poet Ennius celebrated him with the line unus homo nobis cunctando restituit rem — "one man, by delaying, restored the state to us".

Later career

Fabius held the consulship five times and served as censor and princeps senatus. In his old age he opposed the young Scipio Africanus's plan to carry the war to Africa, arguing that Italy must first be freed of Hannibal. He died in 203 BC, before the war's conclusion at Zama.

Legacy

His name survives in the term "Fabian" for gradualist, delaying strategies, later adopted by the British socialist Fabian Society, and in military usage for strategies of attrition that refuse decisive battle.
