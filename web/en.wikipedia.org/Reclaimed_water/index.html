Reclaimed water

Reclaimed water, also known as recycled water, is wastewater that has been treated to remove solids and impurities and then reused for beneficial purposes such as irrigation, industrial processes, groundwater recharge, and environmental restoration. Water reuse is a key strategy for conserving freshwater supplies, particularly in arid regions.

Uses

The most common use is non-potable irrigation of parks, golf courses, and agricultural land. Treated wastewater is also used for industrial cooling, toilet flushing in dual-plumbed buildings, dust control, and recharging aquifers. With advanced treatment, reclaimed water can supplement drinking water supplies, a practice known as potable reuse.

Treatment

Reuse generally requires at least secondary treatment, often followed by filtration and disinfection. Advanced processes such as microfiltration, reverse osmosis, and ultraviolet disinfection with advanced oxidation are used for potable reuse schemes, as in Orange County, California's Groundwater Replenishment System.

Distribution

Reclaimed water is commonly distributed through a separate network of pipes identified by purple coloring, leading to the nickname "purple pipe" water.
