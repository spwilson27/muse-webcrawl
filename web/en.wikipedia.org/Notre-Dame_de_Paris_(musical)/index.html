Notre-Dame de Paris (musical)

Notre-Dame de Paris is a French sung-through musical with music by Riccardo Cocciante and words by Luc Plamondon, adapted from Victor Hugo's 1831 novel The Hunchback of Notre-Dame. It premiered in Paris in 1998 and became one of the most successful French-language musicals ever staged, launching a wave of large-scale French musical theatre productions.

Synopsis

Set in Paris in 1482, the story is narrated by the poet Gringoire. The hunchbacked bell-ringer Quasimodo, the archdeacon Frollo and the dashing captain Phoebus all fall in love with the young Romani dancer Esmeralda. Jealous and tormented, Frollo stabs Phoebus and lets Esmeralda take the blame; she is condemned for witchcraft and murder, briefly rescued by Quasimodo to sanctuary in the cathedral, but is finally captured and hanged. Quasimodo kills Frollo and dies beside Esmeralda's body.

Production and songs

The original French production, directed by Gilles Maheu, presented the story with a rock-pop score and spectacular modern choreography and staging, including performers scaling a great movable wall representing the cathedral. The song "Belle", performed by the characters Quasimodo, Frollo and Phoebus, was a major hit in France and was voted "Song of the Century" in a French poll. Other well-known numbers include "Le temps des cathédrales" and "Vivre", the latter recorded in English as "Live (for the One I Love)" by Celine Dion.

International versions

An English-language version with lyrics by Will Jennings opened in London in 2000. Productions and tours have since played around the world — including in Canada, Italy, Spain, Russia, South Korea, China and the Middle East — and the original French version has been repeatedly revived and toured internationally, with performances seen by millions of spectators.
