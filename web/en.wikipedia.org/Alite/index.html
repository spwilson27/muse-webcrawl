Alite

Alite is the mineralogical name given to tricalcium silicate, Ca3SiO5, the most important constituent of Portland cement clinker. In cement chemistry notation it is written C3S. Alite typically makes up 50–70% of ordinary Portland cement and is chiefly responsible for the cement's early strength development.

Polished section of cement clinker showing angular alite crystals in a matrix of belite.

Formation and structure

Alite forms in the cement kiln at temperatures above about 1,250 °C, when belite (dicalcium silicate) reacts with free lime. Pure tricalcium silicate is stable only at high temperature; in industrial clinker it survives to room temperature as impure, substituted forms — incorporating small amounts of MgO, Al2O3, and Fe2O3 — crystallising mainly in monoclinic (M1 and M3) polymorphs.

Hydration

When cement is mixed with water, alite hydrates rapidly to produce calcium silicate hydrate (C–S–H gel), the main binding phase of hardened concrete, together with calcium hydroxide (portlandite). The reaction is strongly exothermic and drives the setting and early strength gain of concrete in the first days and weeks after placing.
