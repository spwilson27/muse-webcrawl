Badakhshan Province

Badakhshan Province is one of the 34 provinces of Afghanistan, located in the far northeastern part of the country between the Hindu Kush and the Amu Darya (Oxus) river. Its capital is Fayzabad. The province borders Tajikistan, Pakistan and a short frontier with China through the Wakhan Corridor.

Geography

Badakhshan is overwhelmingly mountainous, containing some of Afghanistan's highest peaks, including Noshaq (7,492 m) in the Hindu Kush. The Wakhan Corridor extends eastward between Tajikistan and Pakistan to the Chinese border. The province is prone to earthquakes, landslides and avalanches.

History

The region was known in antiquity for lapis lazuli mined at Sar-e Sang, traded as far as ancient Egypt and Mesopotamia. Marco Polo passed through and praised its rubies and lapis. A former kingdom straddling the Amu Darya, Badakhshan was divided between the Afghan and Russian (later Tajik) sides in the nineteenth century.

People and economy

The population is predominantly Tajik, with Pamiri peoples in the east and small Kyrgyz communities in the Wakhan. Livelihoods rest on subsistence agriculture, livestock, opium poppy in some districts, and artisanal mining of lapis lazuli. See also Wakhan Corridor.
