Akron, Ohio

Akron is a city in and the county seat of Summit County, Ohio, United States. Located about 40 miles south of Cleveland on the Cuyahoga River at the summit of the Ohio and Erie Canal, it has a population of roughly 190,000, making it the fifth-largest city in Ohio. Akron became known worldwide as the "Rubber Capital of the World" for its role as the historic centre of the American tire industry.

History

Founded in 1825 by Simon Perkins and Paul Williams, Akron grew with the canal era and was laid out at the highest point ("akron" derives from the Greek for summit) of the Ohio and Erie Canal. In 1870 B. F. Goodrich established the first rubber factory; Goodyear (1898), Firestone (1900), and General Tire followed, and the city boomed with the automobile industry, building institutions such as the Goodyear Airdock for airships. Tire production largely left by the 1980s, and the city has reinvented itself around polymers research — the University of Akron is noted for polymer science — and healthcare.

Culture

Akron is the birthplace of Alcoholics Anonymous, founded in 1935 after a meeting between Bill Wilson and Dr. Bob Smith, and of basketball star LeBron James. The All-American Soap Box Derby has been held there since 1934. The city's arts scene includes the Akron Art Museum and the rock band Devo, formed at nearby Kent State.
