Horse latitudes

Sailing ships could be becalmed for weeks in the horse latitudes

The horse latitudes are the subtropical latitudes between 30 and 35 degrees both north and south of the Equator. These regions are characterized by the relatively calm winds of the subtropical high-pressure belts and are notorious in sailing lore for long periods in which ships lay becalmed.

Origin of the name

Several explanations are offered for the term. The most popular holds that Spanish ships transporting horses to the Americas were sometimes delayed so long in these calm zones that the animals died of thirst and were thrown overboard. Another links the name to the "dead horse" ceremony held by sailors who had worked off their advance pay. The etymology remains uncertain.

Climate

Descending air in the subtropical highs suppresses cloud formation, so the horse latitudes coincide with most of the world's great arid belts, including the Sahara, Arabian, Kalahari and Australian deserts. The calm conditions flank the trade winds to the equatorward side and the westerlies poleward; the zone between the trades near the equator is the doldrums.
