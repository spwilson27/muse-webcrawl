137 (number)

137 (one hundred thirty-seven) is the natural number following 136 and preceding 138. It is the 33rd prime number; together with 139 it forms a pair of twin primes.

In mathematics

137 is a prime number and a Chen prime, since 137 + 2 = 139 is also prime. As the sum of the squares 11² + 4², it is also expressible via 137 = 121 + 16. It is an Eisenstein prime with no imaginary part and is the fourth Stern prime. The number 137 is the numerator in several classic approximations in number theory.

In physics

The number is famous because the fine-structure constant, a dimensionless quantity characterizing the strength of the electromagnetic interaction, is approximately α ≈ 1/137.036. Its near-integer reciprocal fascinated physicists including Wolfgang Pauli and Richard Feynman; Feynman remarked that the number was one of physics' "magic numbers" whose origin was not understood. The physicist Arthur Eddington controversially attempted to prove the value was exactly 1/137. Modern measurements give α−1 ≈ 137.035999.

In other fields

In chemistry, the element caesium (Cs) has no stable isotope with mass number 137, but caesium-137 is a significant radioactive fission product associated with nuclear fallout and is used in industrial gauges and medical devices. In religion and mysticism, the number 137 has been noted in connection with Kabbalah, whose Hebrew spelling sums to 137.
