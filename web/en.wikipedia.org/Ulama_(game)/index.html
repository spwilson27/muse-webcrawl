Ulama (game)

Ulama
OriginMesoamerica
PlayersTeams of 1–8 (varies by version)
EquipmentSolid rubber ball, hip guards
RelatedMesoamerican ballgame, pok-ta-pok

Ulama is a ball game played in Mexico, principally in the state of Sinaloa, and is a survival of the pre-Hispanic Mesoamerican ballgame. The word derives from the Nahuatl ollamaliztli, from ōlli, meaning rubber. Players strike a heavy solid rubber ball using only their hips, keeping it in play on a long narrow court.

History

The Mesoamerican ballgame was played for more than 3,000 years across the region, with ballcourts found at sites from Arizona to Nicaragua. After the Spanish conquest the game declined under colonial pressure, but the hip-ball version survived in isolated communities in Sinaloa, where it has been played continuously to the present day. It is regarded as one of the oldest continuously played team sports in the world.

Versions and rules

Three main versions survive: ulama de cadera (hip ulama), the most widespread, in which a ball of about 4 kilograms is struck with the hips; ulama de antebrazo (forearm ulama), played with a lighter ball; and ulama de palo, played with a wooden bat. In hip ulama, teams score points when the opposing side fails to return the ball past the centre line or sends it out of the court; games are played to eight points.

Cultural significance

Ulama has been promoted as a symbol of Mexican pre-Hispanic heritage, with demonstration matches held at cultural festivals and efforts made to teach the game to younger generations.
