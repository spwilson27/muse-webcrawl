Frame rate

Frame rate
UnitFrames per second (fps), hertz (Hz)
Common values24, 25, 30, 50, 60, 120 fps

Frame rate is the frequency at which consecutive images, called frames, appear on a display or are captured by a camera. It is usually expressed in frames per second (fps) or in hertz (Hz).

Standards

Motion picture film has traditionally been shot and projected at 24 frames per second. Television adopted rates tied to mains electricity frequency: 25 fps in PAL regions and approximately 29.97 fps in NTSC regions. Modern video games and computer displays commonly target 60 fps, and high-refresh monitors operate at 120 Hz or higher for smoother motion.

Perception

The human visual system perceives continuous motion from images presented at rates as low as about 10–12 fps, though flicker and motion smoothness continue to improve at higher rates. Higher frame rates reduce motion blur and judder but require more bandwidth and processing power; some filmmakers have experimented with high frame rate cinema at 48 or 120 fps.

Frame rate versus refresh rate

Frame rate describes how many new images a source produces per second, while refresh rate describes how often a display updates. When the two are mismatched, artefacts such as screen tearing may occur, mitigated by technologies such as variable refresh rate.
