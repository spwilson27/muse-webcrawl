Digg

TypeSocial news aggregation website
Founded2004
FoundersKevin Rose, Owen Byrne, Ron Gorodetzky, Jay Adelson
HeadquartersSan Francisco / New York, U.S.

Digg is a social news aggregation website where users submit links and vote ("digg") them up or down, with the most popular stories promoted to the front page. Launched in 2004, it was a pioneer of user-curated news and a major driver of web traffic in the mid-2000s.

History

Founded by Kevin Rose and colleagues, Digg grew rapidly and became emblematic of "Web 2.0" social media. A controversial 2010 redesign ("Digg v4") alienated its community and accelerated a decline, with many users migrating to Reddit. In 2012 the company was sold and broken up: the brand and site went to Betaworks, and the platform was rebuilt as a curated news aggregator.

Features and influence

Digg's voting model influenced many successors. Its community episodes — such as the 2007 AACS encryption-key controversy, when users flooded the site with a DRM key Digg tried to remove — became landmarks in debates over user control of platforms.
