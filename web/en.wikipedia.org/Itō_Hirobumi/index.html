Itō Hirobumi

Prince Itō Hirobumi (伊藤 博文; 16 October 1841 – 26 October 1909) was a Japanese statesman and politician during the Meiji era. A samurai of Chōshū Domain and a leading member of the Meiji oligarchy, he was the chief architect of the Meiji Constitution (1889) and served four times as Prime Minister of Japan — the first person to hold the office.

Early life

Born Hayashi Risuke of humble origins and adopted into the Itō family, he studied under Yoshida Shōin, joined the sonnō jōi movement, and in 1863 was one of the "Chōshū Five" who secretly travelled to study in Britain. Returning during the Shimonoseki crisis, he worked for the Meiji government and visited the United States and Europe to study Western institutions.

Statesman

Itō led the 1882 mission to Europe to study constitutions, drawing especially on Prussian and Austrian models, and drafted the Meiji Constitution promulgated in 1889. As prime minister he presided over the First Sino-Japanese War (1894–95), negotiated the Treaty of Shimonoseki, and founded the Seiyūkai political party in 1900. In 1905 he became the first Resident-General of Korea, forcing through the protectorate treaties that subordinated Korea's foreign affairs and government to Japan.

Assassination

Itō resigned as Resident-General in June 1909. On 26 October 1909 he was shot at Harbin railway station by An Jung-geun, a Korean independence activist, and died of his wounds; his killing accelerated Japan's annexation of Korea in 1910.
