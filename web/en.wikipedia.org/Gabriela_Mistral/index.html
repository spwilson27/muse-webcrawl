Gabriela Mistral (born Lucila Godoy Alcayaga; 7 April 1889 – 10 January 1957) was a Chilean poet, educator and diplomat. In 1945 she became the first Latin American author to receive the Nobel Prize in Literature, awarded "for her lyric poetry which, inspired by powerful emotions, has made her name a symbol of the idealistic aspirations of the entire Latin American world". Her pen name combines the names of two of her favourite poets, Gabriele D'Annunzio and Frédéric Mistral.

Life

Born in Vicuña in the Elqui Valley of northern Chile, she was raised largely by her mother and half-sister after her father left the family. She began work as a teacher's aide as a teenager and, despite limited formal training, rose through Chile's provincial school system, teaching in La Serena, Barrancas, Temuco and Santiago. The suicide in 1909 of her fiancé, the railway worker Romelio Ureta, shadowed her early poetry, notably the Sonetos de la muerte (Sonnets of Death, 1914), which won a national literary competition and brought her fame.

Work and career

Her first major collection, Desolación (Desolation, 1922), was published in New York. Ternura (Tenderness, 1924) turned to poems and songs for children, a theme central to her educational thought. In 1922 she was invited to Mexico by education minister José Vasconcelos to help design that country's rural school and library system, and she later travelled widely in Europe and the Americas as a lecturer and Chilean consul, serving in Madrid, Lisbon, Nice and elsewhere, and representing Chile at the League of Nations and later the United Nations. Later collections included Tala (1938), whose proceeds she donated to children orphaned by the Spanish Civil War, and Lagar (1954).

Death and legacy

Mistral died of pancreatic cancer in Hempstead, New York, in 1957; her remains were returned to Chile and buried at Montegrande in the Elqui Valley. She appears on the Chilean 5,000-peso banknote, and her image as a schoolteacher-poet made her a national icon. Her later fame also prompted discussion of her private life, including her long partnership with Doris Dana and the custody of her nephew Juan Miguel ("Yin Yin"), whose death in 1943 deeply affected her.
