Personal jurisdiction

Personal jurisdiction (also in personam jurisdiction) is a court's power over the parties to a lawsuit. In United States law, a court may exercise it only consistently with state long-arm statutes and the Due Process Clause of the Fourteenth Amendment.

Types

General jurisdiction exists where a defendant is "at home" — domicile for individuals, incorporation or principal place of business for corporations — allowing suit on any claim. Specific jurisdiction covers claims arising from the defendant's contacts with the forum state.

Doctrine

The modern test comes from International Shoe Co. v. Washington (1945): the defendant must have "minimum contacts" with the forum such that the suit does not offend "traditional notions of fair play and substantial justice". Later cases address purposeful availment, stream of commerce, and internet contacts. Consent, presence when served ("tag jurisdiction") and waiver are other bases.
