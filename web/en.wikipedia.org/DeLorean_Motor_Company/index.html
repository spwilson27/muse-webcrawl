The DeLorean Motor Company (DMC) was an American automobile manufacturer formed by engineer and executive John DeLorean in 1975. It is best known for its single production model, the stainless-steel, gull-wing-door DeLorean sports car, built in Northern Ireland from 1981 to 1983.

Founding and the DMC-12

DeLorean, formerly a rising star at General Motors, secured British government backing to build a factory in Dunmurry, a suburb of Belfast, intended to create jobs in a region of high unemployment. The car featured a brushed stainless steel body, rear-mounted V6 engine developed by Peugeot, Renault, and Volvo, and styling by Giorgetto Giugiaro.

Collapse

Production delays, quality problems, cost overruns, and a weak U.S. market pushed the company into receivership in February 1982. Roughly 9,000 cars were built. John DeLorean was arrested in October 1982 on drug trafficking charges in an FBI sting; he was acquitted in 1984, but the company was defunct.

Successor company

A Texas-based company acquired the DeLorean name and parts inventory in the 1990s, supplying owners and later announcing revival projects. The original car achieved lasting fame as the time machine in the Back to the Future film trilogy (1985–1990).
