International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea

The International Tribunal for the Law of the Sea (ITLOS) is an independent judicial body established by the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS) to adjudicate disputes arising from the interpretation and application of the Convention. It was formally established when UNCLOS entered into force on 16 November 1994, held its first session in 1996, and has its seat in Hamburg, Germany.

Composition and jurisdiction

The Tribunal is composed of 21 judges, elected by the states parties to UNCLOS for nine-year terms, with representation reflecting the principal legal systems of the world and equitable geographical distribution. It is open to states parties, and in certain cases to entities other than states, including international organisations and natural or juridical persons, notably before its Seabed Disputes Chamber, which handles disputes concerning mining in the international deep seabed area (the "Area").

ITLOS has jurisdiction over disputes concerning, among other matters, maritime delimitation, navigation, fisheries, marine environmental protection, and the prompt release of detained vessels and crews. It can also prescribe provisional measures, and its chambers or the full Tribunal may give advisory opinions where an international agreement so provides; the Seabed Disputes Chamber gave a notable advisory opinion in 2011 on the responsibilities of states sponsoring deep seabed mining.

Cases

The Tribunal's first case was the M/V "Saiga" (No. 1) case (Saint Vincent and the Grenadines v. Guinea) in 1997 concerning the prompt release of an oil tanker. Other significant judgments include the maritime delimitation between Bangladesh and Myanmar in the Bay of Bengal (2012), the M/V "Virginia G" case (2014), and the advisory opinion requested by the Sub-Regional Fisheries Commission (2015) on illegal, unreported and unregulated fishing. Its docket has grown steadily, and in 2024 it delivered a landmark advisory opinion clarifying states' obligations regarding greenhouse gas emissions and climate change under UNCLOS.
