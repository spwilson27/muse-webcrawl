Les Six

Les Six ("The Six") is a name given to a group of six composers, five of them French and one Swiss, who worked in Montparnasse in Paris in the years after the First World War: Georges Auric, Louis Durey, Arthur Honegger, Darius Milhaud, Francis Poulenc and Germaine Tailleferre.

Members: Auric, Durey, Honegger, Milhaud, Poulenc, Tailleferre
Active: c. 1917–early 1920s
Associated with: Jean Cocteau, Erik Satie

Origins

The critic Henri Collet coined the name in a 1920 article comparing the group to the Russian "Five". With the writer Jean Cocteau as their spokesman and Erik Satie as a spiritual godfather, the composers reacted against Wagnerian heaviness and impressionist vagueness in favour of clarity, wit and the spirit of the music hall and jazz.

Work together

Their collective projects included the album L'Album des Six (1920) and the ballet Les mariés de la tour Eiffel (1921), with a libretto by Cocteau. The group soon loosened as members pursued divergent paths — Honegger toward large-scale oratorio, Milhaud toward polytonality and Brazilian rhythms, Poulenc toward a blend of levity and deepening Catholic faith.
