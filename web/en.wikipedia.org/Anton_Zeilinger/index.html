Anton Zeilinger (born 20 May 1945) is an Austrian quantum physicist and Nobel laureate. He shared the 2022 Nobel Prize in Physics with Alain Aspect and John Clauser "for experiments with entangled photons, establishing the violation of Bell inequalities and pioneering quantum information science".

Research

Zeilinger's group in Vienna performed landmark experiments on quantum entanglement and quantum teleportation, including the first teleportation of an independent qubit in 1997, entanglement swapping, and long-distance quantum communication across the Danube and between the Canary Islands over distances exceeding 100 km. His work also addressed multi-particle (GHZ) entanglement, quantum interference of large molecules such as fullerenes, and tests of quantum nonlocality.

Career

Zeilinger studied at the University of Vienna, worked at MIT and other institutions, and became professor at the University of Innsbruck and then the University of Vienna. He founded the Institute for Quantum Optics and Quantum Information (IQOQI) of the Austrian Academy of Sciences, of which he served as president from 2013 to 2022.
