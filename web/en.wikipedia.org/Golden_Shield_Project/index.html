The Golden Shield Project (Chinese: 金盾工程), also named the National Public Security Work Informatization Project, is the Chinese nationwide network-security fundamental constructional project led by the Ministry of Public Security of the People's Republic of China. The project commenced in 1998, began work in 2003, and the first phase was completed in 2006. Its best-known component is the system of internet censorship and surveillance popularly called the Great Firewall of China.

Scope

The Golden Shield was designed as a large-scale public security information system, incorporating databases on the population, surveillance technologies, and control over access to foreign information networks. Fang Binxing, former president of Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications, is often described as a key architect of the filtering system. The project is distinct from, though related to, broader surveillance programmes and the Great Cannon, an attack tool observed in 2015.

Methods

Techniques attributed to the Great Firewall include IP address blocking, DNS filtering and redirection, URL and keyword filtering, deep packet inspection, and connection resets, as well as requirements that platforms and internet companies police content themselves. Access from China to many foreign websites and services — including Google, Facebook, YouTube, X (Twitter) and Wikipedia — is blocked. Users inside China commonly rely on domestic platforms such as Baidu, WeChat and Weibo; some circumvent blocks with VPNs, whose unauthorised use is itself restricted.

Criticism and analysis

Critics describe the system as the world's most extensive apparatus of online censorship, restricting freedom of expression and access to information. Chinese officials defend it as necessary for cybersecurity, social stability and the protection of national sovereignty in cyberspace.
