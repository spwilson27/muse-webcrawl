Inna Shevchenko (born 23 June 1990) is a Ukrainian feminist activist and a leading member of the international feminist movement FEMEN, known for its topless protests against sexism, religion, and authoritarian politics.

Early life

Shevchenko was born in Kherson, Ukraine. She studied journalism at Taras Shevchenko National University of Kyiv and became involved in student and civic activism before joining FEMEN.

FEMEN activism

Shevchenko became one of the group's most visible figures, organising and participating in protests in Ukraine and abroad. In 2012 she gained international attention after sawing down a wooden cross in central Kyiv, an action taken in support of the Russian punk group Pussy Riot. Facing possible prosecution and threats, she left Ukraine and sought asylum in France.

In Paris she helped establish FEMEN's international headquarters and training centre. In 2013 she was granted political asylum in France. Her image was reported to have inspired the depiction of Marianne on a French postage stamp issued that year, a connection publicly acknowledged by the stamp's designer.

Writing and speaking

Shevchenko has written and spoken widely on feminism, secularism, and free expression, and co-authored works associated with the movement. She remains a prominent, and often controversial, voice in debates over protest tactics and women's rights.
