Tissue (biology)

In biology, tissue is a level of organisation in multicellular organisms consisting of a group of similar cells, and their extracellular matrix, that work together to perform a specific function. Tissues sit between the cellular level and the organ level: several types of tissue combine to form organs, and organs combine into organ systems. The study of tissues is called histology, and their microscopic examination is central to pathology and medicine.

Animal tissues

Animal tissues are traditionally grouped into four basic types:

  • Epithelial tissue — sheets of tightly packed cells covering body surfaces, lining cavities and forming glands; it protects, absorbs and secretes.
  • Connective tissue — cells embedded in an abundant extracellular matrix; it includes loose and dense connective tissue, cartilage, bone, adipose tissue and blood, and serves support, binding, storage and transport.
  • Muscle tissue — contractile tissue of three kinds: skeletal (voluntary movement), cardiac (heart wall) and smooth (walls of organs and vessels).
  • Nervous tissue — neurons and supporting glial cells that generate and conduct electrical signals and coordinate the body.

Plant tissues

Plant tissues derive from meristems, regions of dividing cells at shoot and root tips. They are classified as dermal tissue (the protective epidermis, often with a waxy cuticle), vascular tissue (xylem, which conducts water and minerals, and phloem, which conducts sugars), and ground tissue (parenchyma, collenchyma and sclerenchyma for photosynthesis, storage and support). Unlike most animal tissues, many plant tissues retain the capacity to generate new organs throughout life.

Development

In animals, all tissues ultimately derive from the germ layers of the early embryo — ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm — through the process of differentiation, in which cells activate distinct sets of genes. Adult stem cells maintain and repair tissues such as skin, blood and the gut lining, while other tissues, including most neurons and cardiac muscle, regenerate poorly — a limitation that tissue engineering seeks to overcome.
