Western Turkic Khaganate

The Western Turkic Khaganate was a Turkic khaganate formed from the western half of the Göktürk (First Turkic) Khaganate after its division around 583. Ruled by the Ashina clan, it controlled the steppes and oasis states of Central Asia from the Aral Sea and the borders of Persia to the Altai Mountains, dominating the northern routes of the Silk Road in partnership with Sogdian merchant networks.

History

The Göktürk empire, founded in 552, was effectively split between an eastern khaganate in Mongolia and a western one under Istämi and his descendants. The western khagans allied successively with the Byzantine Empire against Sasanian Persia and with Persia against the Hephthalites, whom they destroyed in the 560s. Under Tong Yabghu Qaghan (618–628) the khaganate reached its peak: Turkic forces campaigned with the Byzantines against Persia in the Caucasus and received embassies from Tang China. The embassy of the Byzantine envoy Zemarchus (568) and the visit of the Chinese pilgrim Xuanzang to Tong Yabghu's court around 630 are famous snapshots of its diplomacy.

Decline and fall

After Tong Yabghu's assassination in 628, succession struggles between the two great tribal confederations, the Duolu and the Nushibi, paralysed the state. Tang China exploited the division: in 657 a Tang expedition under Su Dingfang defeated the khagan Ashina Helu at the Battle of Irtysh River, and by 659 the khaganate's territory had been organised into Tang protectorates. Turkic rule in the west was later reconstituted by successor states, including the Türgesh and, further west, the Khazar Khaganate.

Society

The khaganate was a confederation of nomadic tribes ruling sedentary Sogdian city-states along the trade routes; Sogdian served as a language of commerce and administration, and the khagans profited from taxing and protecting Silk Road caravans. Religious life was plural — Tengriism, Buddhism, Manichaeism, Zoroastrianism and Nestorian Christianity all circulated within its borders.
