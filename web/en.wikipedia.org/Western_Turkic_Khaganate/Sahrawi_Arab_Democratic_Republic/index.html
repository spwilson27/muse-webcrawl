Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic

Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic (SADR)
Proclaimed: 27 February 1976, Bir Lehlou
Claimed capital: Laayoune
Government seat: Tindouf camps, Algeria; Free Zone administered from Tifariti
President: Brahim Ghali
Recognition: partial; African Union member

The Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic (SADR) is a partially recognised state that claims sovereignty over the whole of Western Sahara, a territory in northwest Africa administered in large part by Morocco. It was proclaimed by the Polisario Front on 27 February 1976, as Spanish colonial rule ended.

Territory

The SADR in practice administers the roughly 20–25 per cent of Western Sahara lying east of the Moroccan Wall (the Berm), known as the Free Zone or Liberated Territories, while its government-in-exile operates from the Sahrawi refugee camps in Algeria's Tindouf Province. Morocco controls the remainder, including the claimed capital Laayoune and the entire coastline.

Recognition

Dozens of states have at various times recognised the SADR, though a number have withdrawn or frozen recognition under diplomatic pressure. The SADR has been a full member of the African Union since 1982. The United Nations does not recognise Sahrawi sovereignty and continues to list Western Sahara as a non-self-governing territory pending a self-determination referendum, the subject of the long-running Western Sahara conflict.
