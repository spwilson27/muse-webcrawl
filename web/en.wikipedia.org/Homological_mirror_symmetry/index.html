Homological mirror symmetry

Homological mirror symmetry is a mathematical conjecture proposed by Maxim Kontsevich in his 1994 address at the International Congress of Mathematicians. It proposes that mirror symmetry between Calabi–Yau manifolds — a duality originating in string theory — can be understood as an equivalence of categories: the derived category of coherent sheaves on a Calabi–Yau manifold should be equivalent to the derived Fukaya category of its mirror.

Background

Classical mirror symmetry exchanged the complex geometry of one Calabi–Yau manifold with the symplectic geometry of another, famously allowing physicists to compute curve counts (Gromov–Witten invariants) on the quintic threefold. Kontsevich's formulation placed this duality in the language of triangulated categories: on the "B-side" (complex geometry) the relevant structure is the derived category of coherent sheaves; on the "A-side" (symplectic geometry) it is the Fukaya category, whose objects are Lagrangian submanifolds equipped with additional data.

Progress

The conjecture has been proved in several cases, including elliptic curves and certain higher-dimensional examples such as abelian varieties and some toric and quartic surfaces (K3 surfaces), by work of Kontsevich, Yuri Soibelman, Paul Seidel, Kenji Fukaya and others. It remains a driving problem in symplectic geometry, algebraic geometry, and mathematical physics, with connections to the SYZ conjecture of Strominger, Yau, and Zaslow, which explains mirror symmetry geometrically through special Lagrangian torus fibrations.
