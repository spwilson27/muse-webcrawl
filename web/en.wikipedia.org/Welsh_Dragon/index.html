Welsh Dragon

The Welsh Dragon (Welsh: Y Ddraig Goch, "the red dragon") is a heraldic symbol representing Wales. A red dragon passant on a green and white field appears on the national flag of Wales, and the dragon has been associated with Wales for many centuries in mythology, literature and royal heraldry.

Origins

The dragon standard was used by Romano-British and early Welsh rulers; the Historia Brittonum tells of a red dragon representing the Britons fighting a white dragon representing the Saxons beneath Dinas Emrys, a prophecy retold by Geoffrey of Monmouth in connection with Merlin. Owain Glyndŵr raised a golden dragon standard, and Henry Tudor carried a red dragon banner at the Battle of Bosworth in 1485, after which the dragon became a royal badge of the Tudor dynasty.

The flag

The present flag — the red dragon on a green and white field, the Tudor livery colours — was officially adopted in 1959, though versions had been used since the early 20th century. A Royal Badge of Wales featuring the dragon within a wreath was also used on official documents.

Cultural use

The dragon is a ubiquitous Welsh emblem, appearing on buildings, sports jerseys (notably the national rugby team) and public art, and in the Welsh-language motto "Y Ddraig Goch Ddyry Cychwyn" ("the red dragon gives a lead").
