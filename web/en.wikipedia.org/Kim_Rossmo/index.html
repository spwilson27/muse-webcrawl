Kim Rossmo

D. Kim Rossmo is a Canadian criminologist and former police detective, best known as the originator of geographic profiling, a criminal investigative methodology that analyses the locations of a connected series of crimes to determine the most probable area in which the offender lives.

Career

Rossmo joined the Vancouver Police Department in 1978 and rose to the rank of detective inspector. While completing a doctorate in criminology at Simon Fraser University — becoming the first officer in Canada to earn a PhD while serving — he developed the geographic profiling model that formed the basis of his 1995 dissertation and the Rigel analysis software.

Geographic profiling

The technique rests on research into criminal spatial behaviour: offenders tend to operate within an awareness space anchored on their home, but avoid offending in an immediate buffer zone around it. Rossmo's formula generates a probability surface over the hunting area of a serial offender, helping investigators prioritise suspects, patrols and information canvasses. The method has been adopted by police agencies internationally for serial murder, rape, robbery and arson cases.

Missing women investigation

In 1998 Rossmo's geographic analysis of women disappearing from Vancouver's Downtown Eastside suggested a single serial predator was responsible — a conclusion later borne out by the arrest of Robert Pickton — but his warnings were not fully acted upon at the time, an episode later examined by the Missing Women Commission of Inquiry. Rossmo left the Vancouver police in 2001 after a dispute over the non-renewal of his contract. He subsequently held academic posts, including a research chair in criminology at Texas State University.
