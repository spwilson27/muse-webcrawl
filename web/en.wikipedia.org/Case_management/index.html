Case management

Case managers coordinate services for individual clients

Case management is a collaborative process of assessment, planning, facilitation, care coordination, evaluation and advocacy for options and services to meet an individual's and family's comprehensive health needs through communication and available resources to promote patient safety, quality of care, and cost-effective outcomes. The term is used across several fields, notably health care, social work, mental health, disability services, and legal practice.

Health care and social work

In health care and social services, a case manager coordinates services for people with complex needs — for example patients with chronic illness, people with mental health conditions, older adults, or children in the care system. The process typically includes:

  1. Screening and intake — identifying individuals who could benefit from coordinated support.
  2. Assessment — evaluating the client's needs, strengths, and resources.
  3. Care planning — developing an individualised plan with goals and services.
  4. Linking and coordination — arranging services from multiple providers and agencies.
  5. Monitoring and evaluation — reviewing progress and adjusting the plan.
  6. Advocacy — acting on the client's behalf to obtain needed services and benefits.

Models

Models of case management range from brokerage models, in which the case manager chiefly links clients to services, to intensive and assertive community treatment models, in which a team provides most services directly to people with severe mental illness. Managed care organisations employ case (or "care") managers to coordinate treatment and control costs, particularly for high-risk patients.

Legal case management

In law, case management refers to the scheduling and management of litigation by courts (judicial case management) and the internal organisation of case files, deadlines, and documents by law firms, often supported by dedicated practice-management software.
