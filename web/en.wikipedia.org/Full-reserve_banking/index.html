Full-reserve banking

Full-reserve banking (also known as 100% reserve banking, or sovereign money system) is a system of banking where banks must keep the full amount of each depositor's funds in cash or central bank reserves, ready for immediate withdrawal on demand. Banks would make their profit by lending out funds from term deposits or shareholders' capital rather than from demand deposits.

Proposals

The idea was advanced during the Great Depression in the Chicago Plan of 1933, supported by economists such as Irving Fisher. Modern proposals have been debated in Iceland after its 2008 banking collapse and in Switzerland, where a 2018 referendum on the Sovereign Money Initiative was rejected by voters.

Arguments

Supporters argue full reserves would eliminate bank runs and reduce boom-and-bust credit cycles by removing private money creation. Critics respond that credit would migrate to unregulated shadow banks and that lending capacity and economic flexibility would suffer.
