National saving

In economics, national saving is the total amount saved by a country's residents and government in a given period, equal to national income minus consumption and government spending. It consists of private saving by households and businesses plus public saving (the government budget balance). In the national accounts identity, national saving equals domestic investment plus the current account balance.

Definition

Formally, if Y is gross domestic product, C is consumption, and G is government purchases, national saving S = Y − C − G. It can be decomposed into private saving (disposable income minus consumption) and public saving (tax revenue minus government spending). A government budget surplus raises national saving, while a deficit (dissaving) reduces it.

Role in growth

National saving finances investment in plant, equipment, infrastructure, and housing, which raises the capital stock and future output. In an open economy, saving in excess of domestic investment flows abroad as net foreign investment, reflected in a current account surplus; a shortfall of saving, as in the United States in many recent decades, is financed by capital inflows from abroad.

Determinants

Saving rates vary widely across countries and over time, influenced by demographics, income growth, interest rates, pension systems, fiscal policy, and cultural factors. China's national saving rate has at times exceeded 40% of GDP, among the highest in the world. Economists debate the extent to which government deficits crowd out private investment by reducing national saving.
