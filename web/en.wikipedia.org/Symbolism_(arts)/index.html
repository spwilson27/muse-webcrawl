Symbolism (arts)

Symbolist art favoured myth, dreams, and the inner life over naturalism.

Symbolism was a late 19th-century art movement of French and Belgian origin in poetry and other arts, seeking to represent absolute truths symbolically through language and metaphorical images, mainly as a reaction against naturalism and materialism. In literature, the style originates with the 1857 publication of Charles Baudelaire's Les Fleurs du mal, and Jean Moréas published the Symbolist Manifesto in 1886.

Poetry and literature

Symbolist poets such as Stéphane Mallarmé, Paul Verlaine, and Arthur Rimbaud prized suggestion over statement, using free verse, musicality, and private symbols. The movement influenced writers across Europe and America, including W. B. Yeats and T. S. Eliot, and prepared the ground for literary modernism.

Visual art

In painting, Symbolists such as Gustave Moreau, Odilon Redon, Puvis de Chavannes, and Gustav Klimt depicted mythological, religious, and dream subjects with emotional intensity rather than realistic detail. Symbolist themes — love, death, decadence, the femme fatale — also shaped theatre, music, and the early cinema.
