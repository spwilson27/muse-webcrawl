Crystal

A crystal or crystalline solid is a solid material whose constituents (such as atoms, molecules, or ions) are arranged in a highly ordered microscopic structure, forming a crystal lattice that extends in all directions. In addition, macroscopic single crystals are usually identifiable by their geometrical shape, consisting of flat faces with specific, characteristic orientations.

The scientific study of crystals and crystal formation is known as crystallography. The process of crystal formation via mechanisms of crystal growth is called crystallization or solidification.

Structure

The arrangement of atoms in a crystal is described by its unit cell, the smallest repeating unit of the lattice. Crystal structures are classified into seven crystal systems — cubic, tetragonal, orthorhombic, hexagonal, trigonal, monoclinic, and triclinic — according to their symmetry. Most metals, many minerals, ice, and common salt are crystalline.

Properties

Because of their ordered structure, crystals may exhibit anisotropic physical properties, piezoelectricity, and characteristic melting points. Defects in the lattice, such as dislocations and vacancies, strongly influence mechanical and electrical behaviour.

Occurrence

Crystals form in nature through cooling of molten rock, precipitation from solution, and deposition from vapour. Snowflakes, diamonds, and quartz are familiar examples. Large, well-formed specimens are prized as gemstones and collector's minerals.
