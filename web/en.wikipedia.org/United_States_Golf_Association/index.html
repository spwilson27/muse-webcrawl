United States Golf Association

The United States Golf Association (USGA) is the governing body for golf in the United States and Mexico. Founded in 1894, it conducts national championships including the U.S. Open, maintains the Rules of Golf jointly with The R&A, and administers the handicap system.

History

Five clubs founded the association in New York in 1894 to settle disputes over the national amateur championship. The first U.S. Amateur and U.S. Open were staged in 1895, and the organization grew into the central authority for American golf.

Functions

The USGA writes and interprets the Rules of Golf with The R&A, tests and regulates equipment, runs the World Handicap System in its territory, funds turfgrass research, and conducts more than a dozen national championships for professionals and amateurs of all ages.

Museum

The USGA Golf Museum and Library, at its New Jersey headquarters campus (Golf House), holds one of the world's largest collections of golf artifacts and books.
