The Organization of American States (OAS; Spanish: Organización de los Estados Americanos) is an international organisation of states in the Americas, founded in 1948 to promote regional solidarity, democracy, human rights, security, and development. It is the world's oldest regional organisation, with roots in the International Union of American Republics established in 1890.

History

The OAS Charter was signed in Bogotá, Colombia, on 30 April 1948 and entered into force in 1951. Key instruments include the American Treaty on Pacific Settlement and, after the end of the Cold War, the Inter-American Democratic Charter of 2001, which allows the organisation to respond to unconstitutional interruptions of democratic order. In 1962 Cuba's government was excluded from participation; the suspension was lifted in 2009, though Cuba declined to return. In the 2020s several states, including Nicaragua and Venezuela, moved to withdraw from the organisation.

Structure and work

The General Assembly is the supreme organ; the Permanent Council handles day-to-day affairs in Washington. The Inter-American Commission on Human Rights and the Inter-American Court of Human Rights form the region's human rights system. The OAS runs election observation missions, development programmes, and security cooperation against drug trafficking and organised crime. Its headquarters occupy the Pan American Union Building on Constitution Avenue in Washington, D.C.
