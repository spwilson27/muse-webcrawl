Hurricane Sandy

Hurricane Sandy (unofficially referred to as "Superstorm Sandy") was an extremely destructive and deadly post-tropical cyclone, as well as the strongest hurricane of the 2012 Atlantic hurricane season. The storm made landfall in Brigantine, New Jersey, on October 29, 2012, and caused severe flooding in New York City, particularly along its waterfront areas. Sandy formed in the Caribbean Sea and passed over Jamaica, Cuba and the Bahamas before heading north parallel to the U.S. East Coast.

Damage was estimated at nearly $70 billion, making Sandy one of the costliest hurricanes on record in the United States. At least 233 people died in connection with the storm across eight countries. The storm's unusual leftward turn into New Jersey was influenced by its interaction with a blocking ridge over the Atlantic and an approaching trough; the resulting merger with a cold air mass gave it hybrid characteristics that led meteorologists to call it a "superstorm".

Meteorological history

Sandy developed from a tropical wave in the western Caribbean Sea on October 22, 2012. It strengthened into a hurricane before making landfall in Jamaica, then crossed Cuba as a Category 3 hurricane. Moving through the Bahamas, Sandy weakened and re-strengthened as it tracked northeastward. On October 29 it transitioned into an extratropical cyclone shortly before making landfall in New Jersey.

Impact

New York City suffered severe flooding, with the storm surge overtopping the Battery and inundating subway tunnels in Lower Manhattan. Millions of residents across the Northeast lost electrical power. Coastal communities in New Jersey, including the Jersey Shore, were devastated, with homes destroyed and boardwalks washed away. Flooding and wind damage also occurred in the Caribbean, where Haiti experienced food shortages following the destruction of crops.

Political and cultural effects

Governor Chris Christie of New Jersey and President Barack Obama toured the damage together in the days before the 2012 presidential election, drawing wide media attention. The storm prompted extensive discussion of climate change, infrastructure resilience and flood insurance reform in the United States.
