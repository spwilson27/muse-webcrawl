Portcullis

A portcullis (from the Old French porte coulissante, "sliding door") is a heavy, vertically sliding lattice grille of wood, metal, or a combination of the two, used to fortify the entrances of castles, forts, and other strongholds. It slides down grooves in the jambs of a gateway and locks into sockets in the floor or sill, providing a rapidly closable barrier against attackers.

Design and operation

The portcullis was typically made of oak timbers reinforced or entirely faced with iron, the vertical and horizontal bars forming a grid whose lower ends were often shod with iron spikes. It was raised and lowered by chains or ropes attached to a winch or windlass housed in a chamber above the gate passage, frequently assisted by counterweights. Because it could be dropped quickly, it could trap attackers beneath the gateway — sometimes between two portcullises — where they could be attacked through murder-holes in the vault above, a configuration sometimes called a "portcullis trap."

History

Sliding gates of this kind were known in the ancient world, but the portcullis became a standard feature of European fortification during the medieval period, especially from the 12th century onward as stone castles proliferated. Gatehouses were often equipped with two portcullises in succession, together with heavy wooden doors, so that an enemy who penetrated the outer grille still faced a second barrier. Portcullises appear in the defences of notable castles such as the Tower of London and Dover Castle, and the device continued in use in some fortifications into the early modern era.

Heraldry

The portcullis is a well-known heraldic badge. It was used by the Beaufort family and became associated with the Tudor dynasty: Henry VII adopted the crowned portcullis as a royal badge, and it appears on the Palace of Westminster. Today the crowned portcullis is the emblem of the Parliament of the United Kingdom, appearing on parliamentary publications and letterheads, and it formerly appeared on the British one penny coin. Portcullis House, an office building opposite the Palace of Westminster, takes its name from the badge.
