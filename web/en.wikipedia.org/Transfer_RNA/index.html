Transfer RNA (abbreviated tRNA) is an adaptor molecule composed of RNA, typically 76 to 90 nucleotides in length, that serves as the physical link between the nucleotide sequence of nucleic acids (DNA and RNA) and the amino acid sequence of proteins. It does this by carrying an amino acid to the protein synthesis machinery of a cell (a ribosome) in response to a complementary codon in a messenger RNA (mRNA) sequence.

Structure

Each tRNA folds into a characteristic cloverleaf secondary structure with four arms: the acceptor stem, ending in the invariant 3′-terminal CCA sequence to which the amino acid attaches; the D arm; the TΨC arm; and the anticodon arm, whose three-nucleotide anticodon (positions 34–36) pairs with the mRNA codon. In three dimensions the molecule folds further into an L-shape. tRNAs contain many post-transcriptionally modified bases — pseudouridine (Ψ), inosine, dihydrouridine, and others — more than any other RNA class, which fine-tune structure and decoding.

Function in translation

Before translation, each tRNA is "charged" with its specific amino acid by an aminoacyl-tRNA synthetase, an enzyme that ensures high fidelity by proofreading. During protein synthesis the ribosome's A site accepts the aminoacyl-tRNA whose anticodon matches the current mRNA codon; its amino acid is added to the growing polypeptide, and the spent tRNA exits via the E site. Base pairing at the third codon position is flexible — Crick's wobble hypothesis (1966) — so fewer tRNA species are needed than the 61 sense codons would suggest. Francis Crick had postulated the adaptor molecule's existence in 1955, before tRNA was isolated by Robert Holley and colleagues, who determined the first tRNA sequence (yeast alanine tRNA) in 1965.

Genes and related RNAs

Cells carry many tRNA genes (about 500 in the human genome, for 49 anticodon families), reflecting demand for protein synthesis. Certain mitochondrial diseases result from mutations in mitochondrial tRNA genes, such as MELAS. Transfer-messenger RNA (tmRNA) rescues stalled ribosomes in bacteria, combining tRNA and mRNA functions.
