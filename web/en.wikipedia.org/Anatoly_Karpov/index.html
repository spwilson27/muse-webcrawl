Anatoly Karpov

Anatoly Yevgenyevich Karpov (born 23 May 1951) is a Russian chess grandmaster, former World Chess Champion (1975–1985), and a FIDE World Champion under the FIDE system (1993–1999). He is widely considered one of the greatest chess players of all time, with an exceptional record of tournament victories; his total professional tournament wins have been estimated at more than 160.

Early life

Karpov was born in Zlatoust, in the Ural Mountains of the Russian SFSR, Soviet Union. He learned chess at the age of four and progressed rapidly, becoming a Soviet master by his teens. He won the World Junior Chess Championship in 1969 and earned the grandmaster title in 1970.

World champion

After victories in the 1974 Candidates matches, including a celebrated win over former champion Boris Spassky, Karpov was set to challenge Bobby Fischer for the world title in 1975. When Fischer refused to defend the title under FIDE's conditions, Karpov was declared world champion by default on 3 April 1975. He defended the title twice against Viktor Korchnoi, in 1978 at Baguio City and in 1981 at Merano. Karpov lost the title to Garry Kasparov in 1985, and their five championship matches (1984–1990) became one of the most famous rivalries in chess history. After the 1993 split in world chess, Karpov became FIDE World Champion and held that title until 1999.

Style of play

Karpov's positional style, often described as boa-constrictor-like, emphasized prophylaxis, gradual accumulation of small advantages, and precise endgame technique. He is frequently cited as the archetype of the positional player in contrast to Kasparov's dynamic, tactical approach.

Later life

Karpov remained active in chess into the twenty-first century, winning the strong Linares tournament in 1994 with one of the highest-rated tournament performances in history. He later entered Russian politics, serving as a deputy in the State Duma, and has been involved in charitable and chess-promotion activities.
