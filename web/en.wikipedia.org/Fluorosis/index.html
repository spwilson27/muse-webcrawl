Fluorosis

Fluorosis is a condition caused by excessive intake of fluoride over a prolonged period. It takes two principal forms: dental fluorosis, which affects the developing teeth of children, and skeletal fluorosis, a crippling bone disease seen in regions where drinking water contains naturally high fluoride concentrations.

Dental fluorosis

Dental fluorosis occurs when children consume excess fluoride while their permanent teeth are forming, up to about eight years of age. Fluoride interferes with the ameloblasts that produce tooth enamel, producing changes that range from faint white flecks and streaks, through brown staining, to pitting and loss of enamel in severe cases. Mild fluorosis is cosmetic; severe fluorosis can compromise the teeth.

Skeletal fluorosis

Skeletal fluorosis results from years of high fluoride intake, typically from groundwater in parts of India, China, and the East African Rift. Fluoride is incorporated into bone mineral, initially causing joint pain and stiffness; advanced disease produces calcification of ligaments, osteosclerosis, and deformity, sometimes with neurological complications from spinal cord compression.

Causes and sources

Sources of excess fluoride include naturally fluoridated groundwater, fluoride-rich foods and tea, industrial emissions, and — in developed countries — swallowing fluoridated toothpaste by young children or overuse of fluoride supplements. Water fluoridation programmes target about 0.7 mg/L, a level intended to prevent dental caries while minimising fluorosis.

History

The mottled enamel of fluorosis was described in the early 20th century by dentists such as Frederick McKay, who investigated "Colorado stain." H. Trendley Dean's epidemiological work in the 1930s–40s established the relationship between fluoride levels, caries protection, and fluorosis risk.

Prevention

Prevention consists of keeping total fluoride intake within recommended limits: monitoring natural water fluoride, defluoridation of high-fluoride supplies, supervising children's toothpaste use, and adjusting supplements to local water conditions.
