Satoshi Ōmura (Japanese: 大村 智; born 12 July 1935) is a Japanese biochemist known for the discovery and development of hundreds of pharmaceuticals derived from microorganisms. Together with William C. Campbell, he was awarded the 2015 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine for discoveries concerning a novel therapy against infections caused by roundworm parasites, centred on the drug ivermectin.

Ōmura, whose screening of soil microbes led to ivermectin.

Work

At the Kitasato Institute, Ōmura developed innovative methods for isolating and culturing soil microorganisms and screening their chemical products. In the 1970s he isolated from a soil sample collected near a golf course in Itō, Shizuoka, a bacterium, Streptomyces avermitilis, that produced a potent antiparasitic compound, avermectin. In partnership with Merck, the compound was chemically modified into ivermectin, introduced in 1981 for animal health and adapted for human use in 1987. Donated free by Merck for river blindness (onchocerciasis) and lymphatic filariasis, ivermectin has reached hundreds of millions of people and brought river blindness to the verge of elimination in many regions. Over his career Ōmura's group discovered more than 500 new compounds, dozens of which became medicines, veterinary drugs, or research reagents.
