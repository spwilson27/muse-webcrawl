Abenomics

Abenomics (アベノミクス, Abenomikusu) refers to the economic policies implemented by the Japanese government led by Prime Minister Shinzo Abe after he took office for a second time in December 2012. The programme was intended to end two decades of deflation and stagnation following the collapse of the Japanese asset price bubble, and combined aggressive monetary easing, flexible fiscal policy and structural reforms to promote private investment — the so-called "three arrows".

The three arrows

  • Monetary policy: the Bank of Japan, under Governor Haruhiko Kuroda, launched quantitative and qualitative monetary easing in April 2013, aiming to double the monetary base and achieve a 2 percent inflation target; negative interest rates (2016) and yield-curve control followed.
  • Fiscal policy: large supplementary budgets and public-works spending, alongside staged increases of the consumption tax (to 8 percent in 2014 and 10 percent in 2019).
  • Growth strategy: structural reforms including corporate-governance changes, labour-market measures to raise female participation ("womenomics"), agricultural reform and participation in Trans-Pacific Partnership negotiations.

Effects and criticism

Abenomics weakened the yen, lifted corporate profits and equity prices (the Nikkei 225 roughly tripled during Abe's tenure), and tightened labour markets, with unemployment falling below 2.5 percent. However, the 2 percent inflation target proved elusive for most of the period, real wage growth was weak, and public debt rose to roughly 260 percent of GDP — the highest in the developed world. Critics argued the third arrow of structural reform was never fully delivered.
