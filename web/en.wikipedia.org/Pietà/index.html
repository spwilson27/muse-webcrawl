Pietà

The Pietà (Italian for "pity" or "compassion") is a subject in Christian art depicting the Virgin Mary cradling the dead body of Jesus after the Crucifixion. It arose in Germany around 1300 (as the Vesperbild) and became one of the most moving themes of Renaissance and Baroque devotion.

Michelangelo's Pietà

The most famous example is Michelangelo's marble Pietà (1498–1499) in St. Peter's Basilica, Vatican City, carved when the artist was 24; it is the only work he signed. Mary is shown youthful and serene, holding Christ's body across her lap in a pyramidal composition. The sculpture was badly damaged by an attack in 1972 and is now displayed behind protective glass. Michelangelo returned to the subject in the later Florentine and Rondanini Pietàs.

Other versions

Artists from Giovanni Bellini and Titian to Käthe Kollwitz have treated the theme; northern wooden Vesperbilder emphasise grief and wounds, while Italian versions stress ideal beauty.
