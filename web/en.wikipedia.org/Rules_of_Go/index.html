Rules of Go

The rules of Go govern a two-player strategy board game in which Black and White alternately place stones on the intersections of a grid (standard sizes 19×19, 13×13 and 9×9). The object is to control more territory than the opponent. Although the game originated in China more than 2,500 years ago, its rules have been transmitted mainly through Japanese, Chinese and Korean rule sets, which differ in detail but agree in almost all practical situations.

Basic rules

Black plays first. A stone or a connected group of stones must have at least one liberty — an adjacent empty point — or it is captured and removed. A player may not make a move that leaves their own group without liberties ("suicide"), except where the move captures opponent stones. The ko rule forbids a move that would return the board to a previous position, preventing infinite loops; most rule sets use the simple ko rule (the immediately preceding position may not be repeated) supplemented by rules for longer cycles. Players may pass; two consecutive passes end the game.

Scoring

Two scoring systems are in use. Under area scoring (Chinese rules), a player's score is their stones on the board plus surrounded empty territory. Under territory scoring (Japanese and Korean rules), the score is empty territory plus captured stones. The two systems almost always give the same winner; White receives compensation, called komi (usually 6.5 or 7.5 points), for Black's advantage of the first move. Dead stones are removed by agreement at the end; disputes are handled differently across rule sets, notably in the treatment of bent-four-in-the-corner and other unusual positions.

Handicaps and time

To equalise players of different strengths, the weaker player takes Black and places a number of handicap stones on the star points before play begins. Competitive games use clocks, commonly with byō-yomi (Japanese) or Canadian overtime systems to guarantee a minimum thinking pace after the main time expires.
