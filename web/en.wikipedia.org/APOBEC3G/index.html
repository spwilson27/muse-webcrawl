APOBEC3G

APOBEC3G
Gene: APOBEC3G (chromosome 22q13)
Protein family: APOBEC cytidine deaminases
Function: Antiviral restriction factor

APOBEC3G (apolipoprotein B mRNA editing enzyme, catalytic subunit 3G) is a human enzyme belonging to the APOBEC family of cytidine deaminases. It is best known as a host restriction factor that inhibits replication of HIV and other retroviruses.

Antiviral mechanism

APOBEC3G is packaged into budding HIV virions in infected cells. During reverse transcription in the next target cell, it deaminates cytidines to uridines in the virus's single-stranded minus-strand DNA. The resulting G-to-A hypermutation of the viral genome introduces stop codons and missense mutations that render most progeny viruses noninfectious. It also inhibits reverse transcription through deaminase-independent mechanisms.

Viral countermeasure

HIV evades APOBEC3G with its accessory protein Vif (virion infectivity factor), which binds APOBEC3G and recruits an E3 ubiquitin ligase complex that targets it for proteasomal degradation, preventing its incorporation into virions. The APOBEC3G–Vif arms race is a notable example of host–virus coevolution, and the interaction has been studied as a potential drug target: blocking Vif would restore the enzyme's antiviral activity.

Family and broader roles

Humans have seven APOBEC3 genes (A3A–A3H) clustered on chromosome 22, expanded relative to other primates. Family members act against retroviruses, retrotransposons, and DNA viruses; APOBEC3-mediated mutational signatures are also frequently found in cancer genomes.
