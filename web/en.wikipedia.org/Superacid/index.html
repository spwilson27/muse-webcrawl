Superacid

A superacid is an acid with an acidity greater than that of 100% pure sulfuric acid, which has a Hammett acidity function (H0) of −12. Commercially important superacids include trifluoromethanesulfonic acid (triflic acid) and fluorosulfuric acid, which are about a thousand times stronger than sulfuric acid.

History

The term was coined by James Bryant Conant in 1927. George A. Olah's work with superacids — for which he won the 1994 Nobel Prize in Chemistry — enabled the observation of stable carbocations. "Magic acid", a mixture of fluorosulfuric acid and antimony pentafluoride, famously dissolved a candle by protonating its hydrocarbons.

Examples

Fluoroantimonic acid, a mixture of hydrogen fluoride and antimony pentafluoride, is the strongest known superacid system, with an H0 as low as −28. Superacids are used industrially in petrochemical alkylation and in the synthesis and study of reactive intermediates.
