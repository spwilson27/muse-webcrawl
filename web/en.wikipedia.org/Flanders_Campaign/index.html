Flanders Campaign

The Flanders Campaign (1793–1795) was a major theatre of the French Revolutionary Wars, fought in the Low Countries between the armies of the French Republic and a coalition of Austria, Great Britain, Hanover and the Dutch Republic. The campaign formed part of the War of the First Coalition (1792–1797), the first major effort by European monarchies to contain revolutionary France.

Course of the campaign

After the French victory at Jemappes (November 1792) and defeat at Neerwinden (March 1793), coalition forces under the Prince of Coburg advanced into northern France, winning at Famars and besieging Valenciennes, while a British expeditionary force under the Duke of York captured Dunkirk's approaches but was checked at Hondschoote (September 1793). In 1794 the tide turned: French armies, reorganised under the levée en masse and directed by the Committee of Public Safety, defeated the Allies at Tourcoing (May 1794) and decisively at Fleurus (26 June 1794), where Jourdan's Army of the Sambre-et-Meuse broke the Austrian position.

The French then overran the Austrian Netherlands. During the exceptionally hard winter of 1794–95, General Pichegru's forces crossed the frozen rivers of the Dutch Republic; French cavalry famously captured the Dutch fleet icebound at Den Helder. Amsterdam fell in January 1795, the Stadtholder William V fled to England, and the pro-French Batavian Republic was proclaimed.

Consequences

By the Peace of Basel (1795) Prussia and Spain left the war, and the Dutch Republic became a French client state. The campaign secured France's "natural frontiers" on the Rhine and demonstrated the military power of the mass-conscript armies of the Revolution, reshaping European warfare. The British expeditionary force withdrew through Germany in early 1795, ending Britain's first land campaign of the wars in failure.
