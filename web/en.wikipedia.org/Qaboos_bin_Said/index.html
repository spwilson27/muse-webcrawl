Qaboos bin Said


Sultan Qaboos bin Said

Qaboos bin Said Al Said (Arabic: قابوس بن سعيد آل سعيد; 18 November 1940 – 10 January 2020) was the Sultan of Oman from 23 July 1970 until his death in 2020. A fourteenth-generation descendant of the founder of the House of Al Said, he deposed his father, Sultan Said bin Taimur, in a palace coup in 1970 and ruled Oman for nearly fifty years, making him the longest-serving ruler in the Arab world at the time of his death.

Early life and education

Qaboos was born in Salalah, Dhofar, the only son of Sultan Said bin Taimur. He was educated in Salalah and Pune, India, and then at the Royal Military Academy Sandhurst in England, where he graduated in 1962. He subsequently served with the British Army's Cameronians regiment in West Germany before returning to Oman in 1964, where his father kept him largely confined to the palace in Salalah.

Accession and rule

On 23 July 1970, with British support, Qaboos overthrew his father and proclaimed a programme of modernisation, ending Oman's isolation. He renamed the country the Sultanate of Oman, suppressed the Dhofar Rebellion with Iranian and British assistance, abolished slavery, and used oil revenues to build roads, schools, hospitals and ports across the country.

Qaboos pursued a notably independent foreign policy, maintaining relations with both the United States and Iran and acting as a discreet intermediary in regional disputes, including hostage negotiations and the early contacts that led to the 2015 Iran nuclear deal.

Death and succession

Qaboos died on 10 January 2020 after a long illness. Having no children, he was succeeded by his cousin Haitham bin Tariq, whose name was revealed in a sealed letter opened after the Sultan's death, in accordance with Qaboos's wishes.
