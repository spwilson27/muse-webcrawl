Pál Turán

Pál Turán (Hungarian: Turán Pál; 18 August 1910 – 26 September 1976) was a Hungarian mathematician who worked primarily in extremal combinatorics. He had a long collaboration with fellow Hungarian mathematician Paul Erdős, lasting 46 years and resulting in 28 joint papers.

He is best known for Turán's theorem in graph theory, which gives the maximum number of edges in a graph containing no complete subgraph on r vertices, and for the Turán graph, the extremal construction attaining that bound. His work effectively founded the field of extremal graph theory.

Biography

Turán was born in Budapest into a Hungarian Jewish family. He entered the University of Budapest in 1930 and received his Ph.D. in 1935 under the supervision of Lipót Fejér. Because of anti-Jewish numerus clausus restrictions, he could not obtain a university post for several years and supported himself as a tutor. During the Second World War he was sent to a labour camp. After the war he became a professor at the University of Budapest (later Eötvös Loránd University) and a member of the Hungarian Academy of Sciences. He founded and long directed the Alfréd Rényi Institute of Mathematics' predecessor research groups in Budapest.

Work

Turán's theorem (1941) states that the Turán graph T(n, r) — the complete balanced r-partite graph on n vertices — has the most edges of any n-vertex graph that does not contain an (r+1)-clique. The theorem launched extremal graph theory. He also proved results associated with the Turán–Kubilius inequality in probabilistic number theory, the Turán sieve, and contributed to approximation theory and group theory. With Erdős he founded the field of extremal graph theory and worked on the Erdős–Turán conjecture on additive bases.

Death and legacy

Turán died of leukemia in Budapest in 1976. The journal Combinatorica and several prizes in Hungarian mathematics honour his memory.
