The United Automobile, Aerospace and Agricultural Implement Workers of America, better known as the United Auto Workers (UAW), is an American labor union that represents workers in the United States (including Puerto Rico) and Canada. Founded in 1935 as part of the Congress of Industrial Organizations (CIO), it grew rapidly after the Flint sit-down strike of 1936–37 forced General Motors to recognize it.

History

Under leaders such as Walter Reuther, president from 1946 to 1970, the UAW won landmark contracts including the 1950 "Treaty of Detroit" with GM, which linked wages to productivity and provided pensions and health benefits. Membership peaked at about 1.5 million in 1979. The union also played a notable role in the civil rights movement and in wider progressive politics.

Recent decades

Membership declined with the contraction and automation of the U.S. auto industry and the growth of non-union transplants in the South. The UAW diversified into higher education, gaming, health care, and public-sector workers. In 2023 the union struck all three Detroit automakers simultaneously (the "Stand Up Strike") and won record wage increases and other concessions.
