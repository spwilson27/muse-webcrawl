Philippines campaign (1944–1945)
Date: 20 October 1944 – 15 August 1945
Location: Philippine Islands
Result: Allied victory; liberation of the Philippines
Belligerents: United States, Philippine Commonwealth vs Empire of Japan

The Philippines campaign of 1944–1945, also known as the liberation of the Philippines, was the American, Mexican, Australian, and Filipino campaign to defeat and expel the Imperial Japanese forces occupying the Philippines during the Second World War. The Japanese had overrun all of the islands in the 1941–42 campaign, and the liberation fulfilled General Douglas MacArthur's famous pledge, made after his escape to Australia in 1942, that "I shall return."

Leyte and the naval battles

The campaign opened on 20 October 1944 with landings on Leyte by the US Sixth Army, supported by the largest naval armada of the war. The Imperial Japanese Navy's attempt to destroy the invasion fleet produced the Battle of Leyte Gulf (23–26 October 1944), often called the largest naval battle in history, in which Japan lost most of its remaining capital ships and first employed organised kamikaze attacks. MacArthur waded ashore at Palo, Leyte, announcing, "I have returned."

Luzon and Manila

On 9 January 1945, Allied forces landed in Lingayen Gulf and advanced on Manila. The Battle of Manila (3 February – 3 March 1945), fought against Japanese naval troops under Rear Admiral Iwabuchi Sanji who refused orders to withdraw, destroyed the city — including the historic Intramuros — and killed an estimated 100,000 Filipino civilians, many in deliberate massacres. It was among the most destructive urban battles of the Pacific war.

End of the campaign

Fighting continued across Luzon, the Visayas, and Mindanao until Japan's surrender in August 1945; Filipino guerrillas, who had resisted throughout the occupation, played a major role in intelligence and combat operations. General Tomoyuki Yamashita's forces in northern Luzon held out in the mountains until 2 September 1945. Total Japanese deaths in the campaign exceeded 300,000; American deaths numbered roughly 14,000. The Commonwealth government under President Sergio Osmeña was restored, and the Republic of the Philippines became independent on 4 July 1946.
