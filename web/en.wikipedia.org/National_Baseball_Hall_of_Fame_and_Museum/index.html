National Baseball Hall of Fame and Museum

The National Baseball Hall of Fame and Museum is a history museum and hall of fame in Cooperstown, New York, operated by private interests. It serves as the central point of the history of baseball in the United States and displays baseball-related artefacts and exhibits.

History

The museum was established in 1936 and dedicated in 1939, built around the (now discredited) legend that Abner Doubleday invented baseball in Cooperstown. The first class of inductees included Ty Cobb, Babe Ruth, Honus Wagner, Christy Mathewson, and Walter Johnson.

Election

Players are elected by the Baseball Writers' Association of America (BBWAA) after retirement, requiring 75% of votes cast; a separate era committees process considers managers, executives, umpires, and older players. Induction ceremonies are held each summer.

Museum

The museum houses tens of thousands of artefacts, photographs, and documents. Its plaque gallery displays bronze plaques of the more than 340 inductees.
