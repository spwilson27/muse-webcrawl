Sediment gravity flow

A sediment gravity flow is one of several types of sediment transport mechanisms in which the interstitial fluid is driven downslope mainly by the force of gravity acting on the sediment particles themselves. The excess density of the sediment–fluid mixture relative to the surrounding water causes it to flow along the sea floor or lake bed.

Types

Sediment gravity flows are classified by their sediment-support mechanism into four main types: turbidity currents (supported by fluid turbulence), liquefied flows (supported by escaping pore fluid), grain flows (supported by grain-to-grain collisions), and debris flows (supported by matrix strength and buoyancy).

Deposits

Deposits of turbidity currents, called turbidites, are major components of deep-marine sedimentary sequences and form important hydrocarbon reservoirs. The classic Bouma sequence describes the idealized vertical succession of structures in a turbidite bed.

Triggers

Flows may be triggered by earthquakes, storm waves, rapid sediment loading on delta fronts, or the collapse of slope material, and can travel hundreds of kilometres across very gentle slopes.
