The Moulin Rouge (French for "Red Mill") is a cabaret in Paris, on the Boulevard de Clichy, at Place Blanche, in the district of Pigalle. It was founded in 1889 by Charles Zidler and Joseph Oller, who also owned the Paris Olympia.

History

The Moulin Rouge opened on 6 October 1889, at the height of the Belle Époque. Its distinctive red windmill on the roof and its large dance floor made it an icon of Parisian nightlife. It became famous for the can-can dance, a provocative quadrille popularized by dancers such as La Goulue and Jane Avril, immortalized in posters by Henri de Toulouse-Lautrec.

The original building burned down in 1915 and was rebuilt, reopening in 1921. During the interwar years it hosted stars such as Mistinguett, and after the Second World War performers including Édith Piaf, Yves Montand and Charles Aznavour appeared there.

Today

The Moulin Rouge remains a major tourist attraction, presenting the revue Féerie with a troupe of Doriss Girls performing in elaborate costumes of feathers, rhinestones and sequins. The venue seats about 850 guests for dinner shows.

In popular culture

The cabaret has inspired numerous works, including the 1952 film Moulin Rouge directed by John Huston and the 2001 jukebox musical film Moulin Rouge! directed by Baz Luhrmann.
