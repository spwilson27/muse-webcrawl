Columbus is the capital and most populous city of the U.S. state of Ohio. Located at the confluence of the Scioto and Olentangy rivers in the central part of the state, it had a population of 905,748 at the 2020 census, making it the 14th-most populous city in the United States and the second-most populous city in the Midwest after Chicago. The Columbus metropolitan area has more than two million residents.

History

The site was chosen in 1812 as a compromise location for the state capital, replacing Chillicothe and Zanesville, and was laid out on the east bank of the Scioto River opposite the existing settlement of Franklinton. Named for Christopher Columbus, the city was incorporated in 1816 and grew steadily with the arrival of the National Road, canals, and later railroads. Columbus became a major manufacturing and distribution centre during the nineteenth and twentieth centuries.

Economy and culture

The city's economy is diverse, encompassing education, government, insurance, banking, fashion, defence, aviation, food, logistics, steel, energy, and medical research. Major employers include Ohio State University, the state government, Nationwide Insurance, JPMorgan Chase, and Cardinal Health. Ohio State University, founded in 1870, is one of the largest universities in the United States.

Notable attractions include the Columbus Museum of Art, the Center of Science and Industry (COSI), Franklin Park Conservatory, the Columbus Zoo and Aquarium, and the Ohio Statehouse. The city hosts the Ohio State Buckeyes, whose football games at Ohio Stadium draw crowds of over 100,000.

Geography and climate

Columbus has a humid continental climate with hot, humid summers and cold, dry winters. The terrain is relatively flat, drained by the Scioto and Olentangy rivers and their tributaries.
