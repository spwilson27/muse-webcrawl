Franco Basaglia (1924–1980) was an Italian psychiatrist who led the movement to reform mental health care in Italy, culminating in the closure of psychiatric hospitals under the 1978 "Basaglia Law" (Law 180). He was a central figure of the international anti-institutional and democratic psychiatry movements.

Work at Gorizia and Trieste

As director of the asylum at Gorizia from 1961, Basaglia was appalled by the coercive conditions of institutional psychiatry and began dismantling internal barriers, inspired by therapeutic communities and phenomenology. His collective book L'istituzione negata ("The Institution Denied", 1968) became a manifesto of the movement. From 1971 in Trieste he went further, opening the hospital to the city and building community-based services, symbolised by "Marco Cavallo," a large blue papier-mâché horse paraded out of the asylum by patients in 1973.

Law 180 and legacy

Basaglia's work and the Psichiatria Democratica movement he founded led to Law 180 of 1978, which prohibited new admissions to mental hospitals and mandated community care; Italy became the first country to abolish the asylum system by law. Basaglia died of cancer in 1980. His legacy remains influential — and debated — in global mental health, and the World Health Organization has cited Trieste as a model of community psychiatry.
