Portland is the most populous city in the U.S. state of Oregon and the seat of Multnomah County. It lies at the confluence of the Willamette and Columbia rivers in the Pacific Northwest, about 60 miles (97 km) east of the Pacific Ocean. With a population of roughly 635,000 at the 2020 census and some 2.5 million in its metropolitan area, Portland is the second-most populous city in the Pacific Northwest after Seattle.

History

The site was a seasonal Native American settlement and trading location for thousands of years before the arrival of Euro-American settlers, who began clearing land in 1843 at a spot known as "The Clearing." The city was founded by William Overton and Asa Lovejoy; its name was decided by a coin flip between Lovejoy, from Boston, Massachusetts, and Francis W. Pettygrove, from Portland, Maine — Pettygrove won, and the famous "Portland Penny" is held by the Oregon Historical Society. Portland was incorporated in 1851 and grew as a port city, shipping timber, wheat, and other goods down the Willamette and Columbia.

Geography and climate

Portland sits in the Willamette Valley, with the Tualatin Mountains (the West Hills) rising to the west and the Cascade Range, including Mount Hood, visible to the east. The Willamette River bisects the city, crossed by numerous bridges that give Portland the nickname "Bridgetown." Its climate is temperate oceanic with warm, dry summers and cool, rainy winters; the mild, wet conditions support the roses for which the city is famous, celebrated since 1907 by the Portland Rose Festival.

Economy and culture

Portland's economy historically rested on timber and shipping; today it includes a large technology sector (the "Silicon Forest"), athletic and outdoor apparel companies (Nike is headquartered in nearby Beaverton, and Adidas has its North American headquarters in Portland), manufacturing, and a well-known food and craft beer scene. The city is regarded as a center of counterculture and progressive politics, popularized by the unofficial slogan "Keep Portland Weird." It is home to Powell's City of Books, among the world's largest independent bookstores, and to extensive park land including Forest Park, one of the largest urban forests in the United States.
