Joseph Paxton

Sir Joseph Paxton
Born: 3 August 1803, Milton Bryan, Bedfordshire
Died: 8 June 1865, Sydenham, London
Known for: The Crystal Palace, Chatsworth gardens

Sir Joseph Paxton (3 August 1803 – 8 June 1865) was an English gardener, architect, engineer and Member of Parliament, best known for designing the Crystal Palace in Hyde Park for the Great Exhibition of 1851.

Chatsworth

As head gardener at Chatsworth House for the Duke of Devonshire, Paxton built great glasshouses, including the Great Conservatory, and cultivated the giant water lily Victoria amazonica, whose leaf structure inspired his glasshouse designs.

The Crystal Palace

Paxton's prefabricated iron-and-glass design was erected in about six months for the 1851 exhibition and later re-erected in Sydenham, where it stood until destroyed by fire in 1936. It pioneered modular, mass-produced building.

Later life

Knighted in 1851, Paxton served as MP for Coventry from 1854 until his death, and designed country houses, public parks and railway schemes.
