Kalibo
Country: Philippines

Region: Western Visayas
Known for: Ati-Atihan festival

Kalibo, officially the Municipality of Kalibo, is the capital of the province of Aklan in the Western Visayas region of the Philippines. It is a principal gateway for travellers to the resort island of Boracay and is famous throughout the Philippines as the home of the Ati-Atihan festival.

Ati-Atihan festival

Held every January in honour of the Santo Niño (the Child Jesus), the Ati-Atihan is often called the "Mother of All Philippine Festivals". Participants blacken their faces with soot, wear colourful costumes, and parade through the streets dancing to drums while chanting "Hala Bira! Viva Santo Niño!" The festival is said to commemorate a 13th-century pact between Malay settlers and the indigenous Ati people, later blended with Catholic devotion.

Economy and transport

Kalibo is known for weaving of piña cloth from pineapple fibres, an Aklan tradition recognized by UNESCO. Kalibo International Airport handles domestic and international flights, and the town's port and bus terminals connect visitors to Caticlan, the jump-off point for Boracay. The Bakhawan Eco-Park, a community-managed mangrove forest near the coast, is a noted local ecotourism site.
