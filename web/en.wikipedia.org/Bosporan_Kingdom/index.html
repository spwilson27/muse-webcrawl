Bosporan Kingdom

The Bosporan Kingdom, also known as the Kingdom of the Cimmerian Bosporus, was an ancient Greco-Scythian state located in eastern Crimea and the Taman Peninsula on the shores of the Cimmerian Bosporus (the modern Strait of Kerch). It was the first truly Hellenistic state in the northern Black Sea region and one of the longest-lived kingdoms of the ancient world, enduring for roughly nine centuries from the 5th century BC into late antiquity.

History

Greek colonies such as Panticapaeum, Phanagoria, and Hermonassa were founded by Miletus and other Ionian cities from the 7th–6th centuries BC. Around 480 BC they united under the Archaeanactid dynasty, succeeded in 438 BC by the Spartocids, under whom the kingdom grew wealthy exporting grain, fish, and slaves to Athens and the Aegean. In the late 2nd century BC the last Spartocid ceded the kingdom to Mithridates VI of Pontus. After the Mithridatic Wars, Rome made it a client kingdom under the Asander and later the Tiberian-Julian dynasty, which ruled into the 4th century AD. The kingdom finally disintegrated amid Gothic and Hunnic invasions around AD 370s.

Culture and economy

The Bosporan Kingdom blended Greek civic culture with Scythian and Sarmatian influences, visible in its rich kurgan burials, goldwork, and terracotta. Its prosperity rested on controlling the straits and the trade between the steppe, the Sea of Azov fisheries, and the Mediterranean world.
