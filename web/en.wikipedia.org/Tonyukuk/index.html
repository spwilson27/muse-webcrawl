Tonyukuk (Old Turkic: 𐱃𐰆𐰪𐰸𐰸; c. 646 – c. 726), also known as Bilge Tonyukuk ("Tonyukuk the Wise"), was a statesman and military commander of the Second Turkic Khaganate and the chief adviser to four successive khagans. He is the author of the Tonyukuk inscriptions, among the oldest known texts in a Turkic language, which he composed in his own lifetime.

Life

Tonyukuk was born into the Ashide clan and grew up during the period of Tang Chinese dominion over the Turks, receiving a Chinese-influenced education. When Ilterish Qaghan founded the Second Turkic Khaganate in 682, Tonyukuk joined him and became the architect of the restored state's strategy, counselling against settling in Chinese-style walled cities and against adopting Buddhism, on the grounds that the steppe way of life was the source of Turkic strength. He directed campaigns against the Türgesh, Kirghiz, Tang China and the Toquz Oghuz, and managed the empire's diplomacy for four decades under Ilterish, his brother Kapaghan, and after a succession struggle under Bilge Khagan.

The inscriptions

Tonyukuk erected two stone stelae at Bain Tsokto, southeast of modern Ulaanbaatar in Mongolia, probably around 720–725, recounting his career in the first person in runic Old Turkic script. The inscriptions describe the restoration of the khaganate and the wars of its first decades, and are among the three great Orkhon inscription monuments alongside those of Kul Tigin and Bilge Khagan. Their candid, autobiographical voice makes them a unique source for early Turkic history and political thought.
