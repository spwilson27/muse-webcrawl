Employment Division v. Smith

Employment Division, Department of Human Resources of Oregon v. Smith, 494 U.S. 872 (1990), is a landmark United States Supreme Court case on the Free Exercise Clause of the First Amendment.

Facts

Alfred Leo Smith and Galen Black, counselors at a drug rehabilitation clinic and members of the Native American Church, were fired for sacramental use of peyote, illegal in Oregon, and denied unemployment benefits for work-related misconduct.

Decision

Justice Antonin Scalia's majority held that neutral, generally applicable laws do not violate free exercise even if they burden religious practice, so the Sherbert compelling-interest test does not apply to such laws. The decision was widely criticized by religious groups.

Aftermath

Congress responded with the Religious Freedom Restoration Act of 1993 to restore the prior test for federal law; the Court limited its application to the states in City of Boerne v. Flores (1997). Oregon later exempted sacramental peyote use.
