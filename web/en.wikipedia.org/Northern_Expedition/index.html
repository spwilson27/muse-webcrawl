Northern Expedition

The Northern Expedition was a military campaign launched by the Kuomintang (KMT) and its National Revolutionary Army between 1926 and 1928 to defeat the warlord factions controlling northern China and reunify the country under the Republic of China. Commanded by Chiang Kai-shek, it ended the Warlord Era and brought the KMT government to Nanjing, though its unity was incomplete and marred by the violent split between the Nationalists and the Chinese Communist Party.

Background

After the death of Yuan Shikai in 1916, China fragmented among regional warlords. Sun Yat-sen's Kuomintang, reorganised in the 1920s with Soviet assistance under the First United Front with the Communists, built a party army trained at the Whampoa Military Academy. Sun's death in 1925 left Chiang Kai-shek as the movement's dominant military figure.

The campaign

The expedition set out from Guangdong in July 1926. Nationalist forces, aided by Communist-led labour and peasant movements, rapidly took Hunan, Wuhan, and the middle Yangtze, then turned east to Nanjing and Shanghai in early 1927. In April 1927 Chiang purged the Communists in the Shanghai Massacre, splitting the United Front and establishing a rival Nationalist government at Nanjing. After a pause to consolidate, the advance resumed in 1928; Beijing was taken in June 1928, and in December the Manchurian warlord Zhang Xueliang pledged allegiance to Nanjing, nominally completing reunification.

Consequences

The campaign inaugurated the Nanjing decade of Nationalist rule, but warlord power persisted regionally, the Communists survived to build rural soviets, and the Central Plains War of 1930 showed how fragile the settlement was.
