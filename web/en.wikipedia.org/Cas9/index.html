Cas9

Cas9
TypeRNA-guided DNA endonuclease
SystemCRISPR, type II
Common sourceStreptococcus pyogenes (SpCas9)

Cas9 (CRISPR associated protein 9) is a protein that plays a vital role in the immunological defence of certain bacteria against DNA viruses and plasmids and is heavily used in genetic engineering. Its main function is to cut DNA, thereby altering a cell's genome.

Mechanism

Cas9 is guided to its target by a guide RNA complementary to a DNA sequence adjacent to a short motif called a protospacer adjacent motif (PAM). Its two nuclease domains, HNH and RuvC, cleave the two strands of DNA, producing a double-strand break that the cell repairs by non-homologous end joining (often causing knockouts) or by homology-directed repair (allowing precise edits when a template is supplied).

Applications

The CRISPR–Cas9 system was adapted for genome editing in 2012 in work led by Jennifer Doudna and Emmanuelle Charpentier, who received the 2020 Nobel Prize in Chemistry. Cas9 editing is now routine in research and underlies clinical therapies, including the first approved CRISPR treatment, for sickle cell disease and beta thalassemia. Variants such as catalytically dead Cas9 (dCas9), base editors, and prime editors extend its uses to gene regulation and single-letter edits without double-strand breaks.
