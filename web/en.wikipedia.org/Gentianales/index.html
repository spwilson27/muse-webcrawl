Gentianales

Gentianales is an order of flowering plants, included within the asterid clade of eudicots. Under the APG IV system it comprises five families: Apocynaceae, Gelsemiaceae, Gentianaceae, Loganiaceae, and Rubiaceae — together more than 20,000 species, making it one of the larger orders of flowering plants. The order includes many economically important plants, notably coffee (Rubiaceae), as well as garden ornamentals such as gentians, frangipani, and oleander.

Characteristics

Members of the order typically have opposite leaves, often with an interpetiolar stipule or ridge, and flowers with a sympetalous corolla whose lobes are frequently contorted in bud. Many species produce secondary compounds such as iridoids and alkaloids; the Apocynaceae and Loganiaceae include potent sources of alkaloids such as strychnine and the cardiac glycosides of oleander.

Classification

In the Cronquist system the order contained fewer families and placed the Rubiaceae in its own order, Rubiales. Molecular phylogenetic studies consolidated the modern circumscription, and APG IV (2016) retains the five-family arrangement, with Gentianales placed in the lamiid (euasterid I) clade alongside Lamiales, Solanales, Boraginales, and others.
