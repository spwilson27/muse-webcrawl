Lake Tana (Amharic: ጣና ሐይቅ‎) is the largest lake in Ethiopia and the source of the Blue Nile. It lies in the Amhara Region in the northwestern highlands of the country, at an elevation of about 1,788 metres, and is roughly 84 kilometres long and 66 kilometres wide, with a surface area of around 3,000 square kilometres. The lake was formed by volcanic activity that dammed the headwaters of the Blue Nile in the early Pleistocene.

Geography and ecology

Lake Tana is shallow — its maximum depth is only about 15 metres — and is fed by numerous rivers, the largest being the Gilgel Abay, Reb, and Gumara. At its southern shore lies the city of Bahir Dar. The lake contains some 37 islands, about 20 of which are occupied by ancient monasteries of the Ethiopian Orthodox Church, some founded as early as the 14th century and housing medieval manuscripts, crowns, and religious treasures. The lake's fish fauna is distinctive, including a unique species flock of large Labeobarbus cyprinid fishes found nowhere else; the lake region was designated a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve in 2015.

The Blue Nile

The Blue Nile flows out of the lake at its southern end near Bahir Dar and drops over the Tis Issat Falls ("smoking water") about 30 kilometres downstream before carving a deep gorge across the Ethiopian highlands toward Sudan, where it joins the White Nile at Khartoum.
