Mahr

In Islam, a mahr (Arabic: مهر; also transliterated mehr, meher, or mehrieh) is the obligation, in the form of money or possessions, paid by the groom to the bride at the time of marriage. It is a mandatory part of the Islamic marriage contract (nikah) and is the bride's exclusive property, intended in part to provide her with financial security.

Qur'anic basis and practice

The Qur'an (4:4) instructs husbands to give women their dowries willingly. The amount of mahr is agreed between the parties before the marriage and recorded in the contract. It may be divided into a prompt portion (mu'ajjal), paid at the wedding, and a deferred portion (mu'akhkhar), payable later — often upon divorce or the husband's death — which can act as a deterrent to arbitrary divorce and as protection for the wife.

Distinctions

Mahr is frequently mistranslated as "dowry", but it differs from a dowry in that it is paid by the groom (or his family) to the bride herself rather than by the bride's family, and it is also distinct from a bride price paid to the bride's family. Customs and typical amounts vary widely across Muslim communities and legal schools.
