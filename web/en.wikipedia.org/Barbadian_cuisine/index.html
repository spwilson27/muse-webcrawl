Barbadian cuisine
National dish: Cou-cou and flying fish
Region: Barbados, Caribbean
Influences: African, British, Creole, Indian

Barbadian cuisine, also called Bajan cuisine, is the cooking of Barbados, blending African, Portuguese, Indian, Irish, Creole and British influences. It is built around the island's seafood, tropical produce, and a distinctive seasoning based on herbs and Scotch bonnet peppers.

National dish

The national dish is cou-cou and flying fish: cou-cou is a cornmeal and okra pudding, similar in texture to polenta or fufu, served with steamed or fried flying fish in a Creole tomato and onion sauce. Flying fish is a symbol of Barbados, appearing on coins and in the island's nickname, "the land of the flying fish".

Typical dishes

Other staples include pudding and souse (pickled pork with steamed sweet potato), fish cakes, cutters (salt bread sandwiches), macaroni pie — a beloved baked macaroni and cheese — rice and peas, and pepperpot. The Friday-night fish fry at Oistins is a famous social institution.

Drinks

Barbados claims to be the birthplace of rum, with Mount Gay distilling since 1703. Rum punch, falernum, mauby (a bitter bark drink) and banks beer accompany meals, and sorrel is drunk at Christmas.
