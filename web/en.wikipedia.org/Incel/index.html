Incel

Incel (a portmanteau of "involuntary celibate") is a term associated with an online subculture of people, predominantly men, who define themselves as unable to obtain romantic or sexual partners despite desiring them. Discussions in incel forums are often characterised by resentment, misogyny, and self-pity, and some attacks by self-identified incels have drawn public attention to the community.

Incel communities developed primarily in online forums and imageboards.

History

The term was coined in 1997 by a Canadian woman known as Alana, who created a website, "Alana's Involuntary Celibacy Project", as a supportive space for people of all genders struggling with loneliness. The term later migrated to forums such as 4chan, Reddit communities (notably r/incels, banned in 2017), and dedicated sites, where it took on a more rigid ideology centred on the belief that attractiveness and social hierarchies determine romantic success.

Ideology and criticism

Researchers describe a shared worldview involving concepts such as the "black pill" — the belief that one's romantic prospects are fixed by genetics and appearance. Critics, and monitoring organisations such as the Southern Poverty Law Center, have characterised parts of the subculture as extremist and misogynist. Several mass attacks, including the 2014 Isla Vista killings and the 2018 Toronto van attack, were committed by men who identified with or praised incel ideology, prompting debate over radicalisation and intervention.
