Onondaga Lake is a lake in Central New York, northwest of and bordering the city of Syracuse. The lake is 4.6 square miles (12 km²) in area and is fed principally by Ninemile Creek and Onondaga Creek, draining via the Seneca River to Lake Ontario. Sacred to the Onondaga Nation — near its shores the Peacemaker is said to have united the Haudenosaunee Confederacy — the lake became one of the most polluted lakes in the United States in the 20th century, and the focus of one of the largest lake-restoration programs in the country.

History

The lake's salt springs made Syracuse an early center of American salt production in the 19th century. Industrial development followed: the Solvay Process Company's soda ash works (later Allied Chemical/Honeywell) discharged vast quantities of waste onto the western shore from the 1880s to 1986, and municipal sewage and industrial effluents contaminated the lake bottom with mercury, PCBs, and other pollutants. Swimming was banned in 1940 and fishing in 1970; the lake was listed as a federal Superfund site in 1994.

Cleanup and recovery

Under legal settlements, Honeywell dredged and capped contaminated lake sediments (2012–2014), removing more than 2 million cubic yards of material, and built wastewater improvements alongside Onondaga County's upgraded treatment system. Mercury levels in fish have declined, bald eagles returned to winter on the shore, and over 100 bird species have been documented; Onondaga Lake Park and the Lakeview Amphitheater anchor the redeveloped shoreline, though fish-consumption advisories remain in place.
