Mortal Kombat (1995 film)

[Image: Theatrical poster for Mortal Kombat (1995)]

Mortal Kombat is a 1995 American martial arts fantasy film directed by Paul W. S. Anderson and written by Kevin Droney. Based on the video game series of the same name, it follows three Earthrealm warriors - the Shaolin monk Liu Kang (Robin Shou), the actor Johnny Cage (Linden Ashby) and the soldier Sonya Blade (Bridgette Wilson) - who are summoned by the thunder god Raiden (Christopher Lambert) to compete in a tournament whose outcome will decide whether the evil realm of Outworld, ruled by emperor Shao Kahn and served by the sorcerer Shang Tsung (Cary-Hiroyuki Tagawa), may invade Earth.

Production and release

Produced by Larry Kasanoff and released by New Line Cinema, the film was shot in California and Thailand and adapted elements of the first two games, toning down their graphic violence to secure a PG-13 rating. It was released in the United States on August 18, 1995, and topped the box office on its opening weekend.

Reception

The film grossed about $122 million worldwide against a reported $20 million budget, making it a commercial success and, for many years, one of the most successful video game adaptations. Reviews were mixed: critics praised the fight choreography, production design and Lambert's and Tagawa's performances, while criticizing the script and visual effects. Its techno soundtrack went platinum, popularizing the "Techno Syndrome" theme. A sequel, Mortal Kombat Annihilation, followed in 1997 to a far poorer reception.
