American Gods (2001) is a fantasy novel by British author Neil Gaiman. The novel is a blend of Americana, fantasy, and various strands of ancient and modern mythology, all centring on the mysterious and taciturn Shadow Moon. It is Gaiman's fourth solo novel.

The central premise is that gods and mythological creatures exist because people believe in them. Immigrants to the United States brought versions of their gods with them; as belief waned, these "Old Gods" weakened, while new gods — of media, technology, and globalisation — arose. The released prisoner Shadow Moon becomes the bodyguard and errand boy of the enigmatic con man Mr. Wednesday, who is in fact Odin, gathering the old gods for a coming war against the new.

Reception and awards

The novel won the 2002 Hugo Award for Best Novel, the Nebula Award for Best Novel, the Locus Award for Best Fantasy Novel, and the Bram Stoker Award for Best Novel. A tenth-anniversary edition, incorporating Gaiman's preferred text, was published in 2011. A sequel, and the related novella The Monarch of the Glen and the novel Anansi Boys (2005, centred on the sons of Mr. Nancy), followed in the same fictional universe.

Adaptations

A television adaptation, American Gods, developed by Bryan Fuller and Michael Green, aired on Starz from 2017 to 2021, starring Ricky Whittle as Shadow and Ian McShane as Mr. Wednesday. Dark Horse Comics published a comic book adaptation beginning in 2017.
