Art festival

An art festival is a festival centred on the visual arts, typically presenting exhibitions, installations, performances and sales of work by many artists over a period of days or weeks. Art festivals range from juried outdoor shows and craft fairs, where artists sell directly to the public, to major international exhibitions such as biennales that anchor a city's cultural calendar.

Forms

The most famous periodic exhibitions include the Venice Biennale (founded 1895), Documenta in Kassel and numerous biennials and triennials worldwide, which showcase contemporary art in national pavilions and curated shows. At the other end of the scale, community art festivals and juried art fairs — such as the Ann Arbor Art Fair or the many summer shows held in North American and European towns — combine exhibitions with street performance, music and food, and serve as marketplaces in which artists are selected by jury and visitors can buy original work.

Significance

Art festivals contribute to cultural tourism and local economies, provide exposure and income for artists, and create forums for new artistic movements. Critics sometimes note commercial pressures and the "festivalisation" of culture, but festivals remain a principal way in which visual art reaches mass audiences outside museums and galleries.
