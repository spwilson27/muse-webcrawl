Dom Pérignon

Dom Pérignon is a brand of vintage Champagne produced by the Champagne house Moët & Chandon. It is named after Dom Pierre Pérignon (1638–1715), a Benedictine monk who served as cellar master at the abbey in Hautvillers and made important contributions to the production and quality of Champagne wine in an era when the region's wines were mostly still red wines.

Dom Pérignon is always a vintage wine, meaning that it is not made in weak years, and all grapes used to make the wine were harvested in the same year. Its first vintage was 1921, which was first released for sale in 1936. Two million bottles are typically produced in each vintage.

The wine is composed of approximately equal amounts of Chardonnay and Pinot Noir. It is aged for a minimum of eight years before release, longer than the minimum required for Champagne. The brand is owned by LVMH Moët Hennessy Louis Vuitton.

History

Dom Pierre Pérignon became the procurator and cellar master of the Benedictine abbey of Saint-Pierre d'Hautvillers in 1668. The abbey owned extensive vineyards, and Pérignon devoted himself to improving the quality of the wines made from them. The legend that he invented sparkling wine — supposedly exclaiming "Come quickly, I am tasting the stars!" — is largely myth, as the addition of sugar to achieve a secondary fermentation was documented in England before his death.

The first "prestige cuvée" was introduced by Eugène Mercier in the 19th century, and Dom Pérignon was the first of these to be widely marketed. The 1921 vintage was first bottled around 1928 and showed in 1936; it was an immediate success, setting a pattern for luxury Champagne marketing.

Vintages

Recent vintages include 1990, 1995, 1996, 1998, 2000, 2002, 2004, 2005, 2006, 2008, 2009, 2010, 2012 and 2013. The 2008 vintage is widely regarded as exceptional, often compared by critics to the legendary 1996. A rosé version, Dom Pérignon Rosé, is also produced in limited quantities.
