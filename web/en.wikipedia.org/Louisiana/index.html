Louisiana (French: La Louisiane) is a state in the Deep South and South Central regions of the United States. It borders Texas to the west, Arkansas to the north, Mississippi to the east (along the Mississippi River and the Pearl River), and the Gulf of Mexico to the south. Its capital is Baton Rouge and its largest city is New Orleans, famed for its distinctive music, Creole cuisine, Mardi Gras festival and French colonial heritage.

History

Louisiana was inhabited by Native American peoples for thousands of years before European exploration. The French explorer René-Robert Cavelier, Sieur de La Salle, claimed the Mississippi basin for France in 1682 and named it for King Louis XIV. France ceded the territory to Spain in 1762, regained it in 1800, and in 1803 sold it to the United States in the Louisiana Purchase, which nearly doubled the young nation's size. Louisiana became the 18th state in 1812.

The state seceded during the Civil War and was the site of the last land engagement of the conflict's Trans-Mississippi theatre. In the 20th century the populist politics of Governor Huey Long dominated the state; Long was assassinated in the State Capitol in 1935. In 2005 Hurricane Katrina devastated New Orleans when the federal levee system failed, causing more than 1,000 deaths in the state and mass displacement.

Geography and climate

Louisiana is shaped by the Mississippi River, whose delta forms vast marshes and bayous along the Gulf Coast. The state is losing coastal wetlands at one of the fastest rates in the world owing to subsidence, sea-level rise and the channelization of the river. Its climate is humid subtropical, with hot summers and frequent severe weather, including hurricanes.

Government and law

Unlike the other 49 states, which base private law on English common law, Louisiana's civil law derives from French and Spanish codes, ultimately from the Napoleonic Code — a legacy of its colonial past. Uniquely, its local governments are called parishes rather than counties. Louisiana also holds its statewide elections apart from the federal cycle and uses a "jungle primary" system.

Culture

Louisiana's culture blends French, Spanish, African, Native American and Anglo influences. It is the birthplace of jazz and home to Cajun and zydeco music, gumbo and jambalaya, and significant French-speaking communities (Cajuns, descended from Acadian exiles). The state's motto is "Union, Justice, Confidence."
