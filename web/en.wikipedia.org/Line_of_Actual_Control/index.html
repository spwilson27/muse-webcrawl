Line of Actual Control

The Line of Actual Control (LAC) is a notional demarcation line that separates Indian-controlled territory from Chinese-controlled territory in the Himalayan region. The term was first used by Chinese premier Zhou Enlai in 1959 and later adopted after the Sino-Indian War of 1962.

The line runs through three sectors — western (Ladakh), middle (Uttarakhand and Himachal Pradesh) and eastern (Arunachal Pradesh) — but India and China differ in their perceptions of its alignment in many places, a source of recurring standoffs, including the 2020–2021 border clashes around Galwan Valley and Pangong Tso.

Status

The LAC is distinct from the Line of Control with Pakistan. Efforts to clarify it through confidence-building agreements in 1993 and 1996 have not produced a mutually agreed map.
