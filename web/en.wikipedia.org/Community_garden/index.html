Community garden

A community garden is a piece of land gardened or cultivated by a group of people individually or collectively. Most community gardens are divided into individual or family plots, though some are gardened as a whole. They are found in urban neighbourhoods—often on vacant lots—as well as in suburbs and rural areas, and are used to grow vegetables, fruits, herbs, and flowers.

History

Community gardening has roots in the allotment gardens of nineteenth-century Europe and in wartime "victory gardens" in the United States, Britain, Canada, and Australia during the First and Second World Wars, when citizens were encouraged to grow food to supplement rations. The modern community gardening movement grew in the 1970s, when urban activists in cities such as New York began converting derelict lots into gardens; organisations such as Green Guerillas (founded 1973) promoted the practice.

Benefits and challenges

Studies associate community gardens with improved access to fresh food, increased physical activity, stronger neighbourhood ties, reduced crime in surrounding areas, and higher nearby property values. They can also provide environmental benefits, including storm-water absorption and urban cooling. Common challenges include insecure land tenure—many gardens occupy borrowed land vulnerable to development—as well as soil contamination, water access, and sustaining volunteer participation.
