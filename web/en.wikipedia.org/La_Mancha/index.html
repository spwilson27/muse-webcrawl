La Mancha

La Mancha is a natural and historical region in south-central Spain, an arid, elevated plateau largely within the autonomous community of Castilla–La Mancha, spanning the provinces of Ciudad Real, Albacete, Cuenca and Toledo.

Geography

The plain, averaging about 610 metres above sea level, is known for vineyards — among the largest wine-growing areas in the world — saffron, Manchego cheese and the white windmills that dot its ridges.

Literature

La Mancha is immortalised as the setting of Miguel de Cervantes's Don Quixote (1605, 1615), whose hero tilts at windmills he mistakes for giants. Tourist routes follow Quixote's journeys through villages such as Consuegra and Campo de Criptana.
