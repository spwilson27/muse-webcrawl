The surface of Mars, explored by orbiters, landers, and rovers since the 1960s.

The exploration of Mars has been carried out by uncrewed spacecraft since the 1960s, driven by interest in the planet's geology, climate history, and potential for past or present life. Dozens of flyby, orbiter, lander, and rover missions have been launched by several space agencies, with a success rate of roughly half.

Early missions

The Soviet Union launched the first probes in the early 1960s, but NASA's Mariner 4 returned the first close-up images in 1965, revealing a cratered, Moon-like surface. Mariner 9 became the first spacecraft to orbit another planet in 1971, arriving during a global dust storm and later mapping Olympus Mons and Valles Marineris. The Viking landers of 1976 performed the first successful surface operations and conducted experiments searching for microbial life, with ambiguous results.

Modern era

Exploration resumed vigorously in the 1990s and 2000s with Mars Global Surveyor, Mars Pathfinder and its Sojourner rover, the twin Mars Exploration Rovers Spirit and Opportunity (2004), and orbiters from ESA, India, and later the UAE. The Curiosity rover (2012) and Perseverance rover (2021) — the latter caching samples and flying the Ingenuity helicopter — extended surface science, and China's Tianwen-1 mission delivered the Zhurong rover in 2021. Sample return and eventual human missions remain long-term goals. See also Mars, timeline of Mars Science Laboratory, and list of missions to Mars.
