Susceptance

Susceptance is the imaginary part of electrical admittance, the reciprocal of impedance in alternating current (AC) circuits. It is denoted by the symbol B and measured in siemens (S).

Definition

Where impedance is written Z = R + jX (resistance plus reactance), admittance is Y = 1/Z = G + jB, where G is conductance and B is susceptance. A capacitive element contributes positive susceptance, while an inductive element contributes negative susceptance.

Relationship to reactance

For a pure reactance, susceptance is simply the negative reciprocal of reactance, B = −X/(R² + X²) in general, reducing to B = −1/X when resistance is zero. Susceptance is widely used in power-system analysis and in the design of filters and matching networks.
