Agape

Greekἀγάπη (agápē)
MeaningSelfless, charitable, universal love
Contrasts withEros, philia, storge

Agape (Ancient Greek ἀγάπη, agápē) is the Greek term for a form of love distinguished from romantic or erotic love (eros) and from friendship (philia). In Christian theology it came to denote the selfless, sacrificial and unconditional love of God for humanity, and the love humans are called to show for God and their neighbours. It is frequently translated "charity" in older English versions of the New Testament.

In the New Testament

Though rare in secular Greek, agapē is used extensively in the Septuagint and the New Testament, where it names the highest form of love. Its most famous treatment is Paul's "hymn to love" in 1 Corinthians 13 ("Love is patient, love is kind..."), and John's Gospel and epistles state that "God is love" (ho theos agapē estin). Jesus presents love of God and of neighbour as the two great commandments.

Later usage

Early Christians also used "Agape" for the communal love-feast that accompanied worship, a usage later restricted as the Eucharist was separated from the meal. Theologians such as Anders Nygren (in Agape and Eros) contrasted agape — spontaneous, God-initiated and indifferent to the merit of its object — with eros as acquisitive desire. C. S. Lewis, in The Four Loves, treated agape as "Gift-love" and the highest of the loves.
