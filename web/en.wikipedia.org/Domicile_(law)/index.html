Domicile (law)

In law, domicile is a concept relevant to private international law (conflict of laws) and denotes the country with which a person is identified for the purposes of law. It is commonly used as the connecting factor to determine which system's law governs matters such as marriage, divorce, succession, and taxation.

Principles

Every person is attributed a domicile at birth, termed a domicile of origin, which is generally that of the parents. A person may acquire a domicile of choice by residing in a jurisdiction with the intention of remaining there permanently or indefinitely. In common law, no person can be without a domicile, and a person can have only one domicile at a time; a domicile is not lost until a new one is acquired.

Domicile and residence

Domicile is distinct from residence or habitual residence: a person may reside in several places but be domiciled in only one. Civil law systems generally rely on habitual residence instead of the common-law concept of domicile.
