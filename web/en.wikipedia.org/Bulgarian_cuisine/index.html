Bulgarian cuisine

Bulgarian cuisine is the traditional cooking of Bulgaria, part of the broader Balkan culinary tradition. It draws on Slavic, Thracian, Greek and Ottoman influences and is characterised by fresh vegetables, dairy products — especially yogurt and white brined cheese (sirene) — herbs such as savory (chubritsa), and slowly cooked meat dishes.

Typical dishes

Signature dishes include shopska salad, a mixture of chopped tomatoes, cucumbers, onions and peppers topped with grated sirene; banitsa, a baked filo pastry filled with egg and cheese; kavarma, a slow-cooked meat and vegetable stew served in a clay pot; kebapche and kyufte, grilled minced-meat rolls and meatballs; and tarator, a cold soup of yogurt, cucumber, dill, garlic and walnuts eaten in summer. Soups such as shkembe chorba (tripe soup) and bob chorba (bean soup) are staples.

Dairy and produce

Bulgarian yogurt, made with Lactobacillus bulgaricus, is famous worldwide and central to the cuisine, appearing in drinks such as ayran and in cold soups and desserts. Sirene, a white cheese similar to feta, is eaten daily. The country's climate favours peppers, tomatoes, aubergines and beans; lyutenitsa, a roasted pepper and tomato relish, is prepared in autumn across the country.

Drink

Bulgaria has an ancient winemaking tradition, with local grape varieties such as Mavrud and Melnik, and produces the grape brandy rakia, the customary aperitif accompanying salads.
