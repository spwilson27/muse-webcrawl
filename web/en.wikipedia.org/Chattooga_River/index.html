Geography

The Chattooga River (also spelt Chatuga) is a 57-mile (92 km) river in the southeastern United States. It rises near Cashiers in Jackson County, North Carolina, flows southwest through the Appalachian Mountains and forms most of the border between South Carolina and Georgia before joining the Tallulah River to form the Tugaloo River at Lake Tugalo.

Wild and Scenic status

On 10 May 1974 the Chattooga was designated a National Wild and Scenic River — one of the first rivers protected under the Wild and Scenic Rivers Act of 1968. Roughly 39 miles of the river and a corridor of surrounding national forest land (the Chattooga River Corridor) are protected from development; no roads cross the designated stretch.

Recreation

The Chattooga is renowned for whitewater rafting and kayaking, with rapids divided into sections of increasing difficulty. Section IV is considered one of the most demanding commercially rafted runs in the eastern United States, culminating in the Five Falls — a series of five Class IV–V rapids. The river achieved wider fame as a filming location for the 1972 film Deliverance, based on James Dickey's novel. The watershed also supports the Chattooga's noted trout fishery, and trails reach landmarks such as Bull Sluice and Woodall Shoals.
