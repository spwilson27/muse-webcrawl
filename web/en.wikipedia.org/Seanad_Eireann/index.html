Seanad Eireann

Seanad Eireann — usually written with Irish diacritics as Seanad Éireann — is the senate, or upper house, of the Irish parliament, the Oireachtas. (This page carries the unaccented form of the name; see the main article at Seanad Éireann for full detail.)

Overview

The Seanad has 60 members. Unlike the directly elected Dáil Éireann, most senators are chosen indirectly: 43 from vocational panels by an electorate of TDs, councillors and outgoing senators, 6 by university graduates, and 11 nominated by the Taoiseach. The chamber reviews, amends and can delay legislation, and debates matters of public policy, but the Dáil is the dominant house, controlling government formation and finance.

Reform debates

The Seanad's indirect election and perceived weakness have made it a recurring subject of reform proposals. In October 2013 the Irish electorate voted in a referendum to retain the chamber, rejecting the government's proposal to abolish it.
