Venturi effect

The Venturi effect is the reduction in fluid pressure that results when a fluid flows through a constricted section (or choke) of a pipe. It is named after the Italian physicist Giovanni Battista Venturi (1746–1822), who described the phenomenon in 1797.

Principle

By the continuity equation, fluid speed must increase as it passes through a constriction to satisfy conservation of mass; by Bernoulli's principle, this increase in velocity is accompanied by a decrease in pressure or potential energy. The effect is quantified by the pressure difference between the unrestricted and constricted sections of flow.

Applications

The Venturi effect is exploited in carburetors, where it draws fuel into an airstream; in water aspirators and ejector pumps; in Venturi meters used to measure flow rate; in atomizers and spray equipment; and in devices that use moving air over a surface to entrain additional flow.
