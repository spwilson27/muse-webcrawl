Azerbaijanis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Azerbaijanis (Azerbaijani: Azərbaycanlılar) are a Turkic ethnic group living mainly in the South Caucasus and northwestern Iran. The largest population lives in the Republic of Azerbaijan, where they form the overwhelming majority, with a still larger community in northwestern Iran (Iranian Azerbaijan). They speak Azerbaijani, an Oghuz Turkic language, and are predominantly Shia Muslims, with religious observance shaped by the secular traditions of both states.

History

The Azerbaijani ethnos formed through centuries of interaction between the indigenous peoples of the Caucasus and Iranian plateau and Turkic-speaking groups who arrived from the 11th century onward. The region was ruled successively by Persian empires, Arab caliphates, Turkic dynasties and, in the north, the Russian Empire after the treaties of Gulistan (1813) and Turkmenchay (1828) divided Azerbaijani-populated lands between Persia and Russia.

Modern period

The Azerbaijan Democratic Republic of 1918–1920 was the first secular democratic republic in the Muslim-majority world. After seven decades as a Soviet republic, Azerbaijan regained independence in 1991. In Iran, Azerbaijanis are the country's largest ethnic minority and are deeply integrated into Iranian political, commercial and religious life, though Azerbaijani-language education has been a recurring cultural demand.

Culture

Azerbaijani culture is noted for mugham music (inscribed by UNESCO), carpet weaving, the ashik minstrel tradition, and a cuisine featuring plov, dolma and kebabs. The Novruz spring festival is the most widely celebrated holiday on both sides of the border. A large diaspora also lives in Turkey, Russia, Europe and North America.
