Churrasco

Churrasco is the Portuguese and Spanish name for grilled beef prominent in South American and Iberian cuisines, and the cooking style that grew out of the traditions of the gauchos (cowboys) of the pampas of southern Brazil, Argentina, Paraguay and Uruguay. The term is also used to refer to the barbecue event itself, and in Brazil to a wide variety of meats roasted on a churrasqueira (barbecue grill).

Brazilian churrasco

In Brazil, meats — most famously picanha (top sirloin cap) — are seasoned mainly with coarse salt, skewered on large swords or spits, and roasted over charcoal or wood embers. In a churrascaria (steakhouse) running rodízio service, waiters circulate among the tables carving successive cuts — beef, pork, lamb, chicken, sausages and grilled pineapple — directly onto diners' plates until they signal they are full. Side dishes typically include farofa, vinaigrette salsa, fried banana, cheese bread (pão de queijo) and salads.

Other varieties

In Argentina and Uruguay the corresponding tradition is the asado; in Chile, churrasco also denotes a thin steak served in sandwiches such as the churrasco italiano. In Portugal, frango no churrasco is grilled chicken, often marinated and served with piri piri sauce. Chimichurri is a common accompaniment in the Río de la Plata region.
