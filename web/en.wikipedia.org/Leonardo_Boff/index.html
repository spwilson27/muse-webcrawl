Leonardo Boff

Leonardo Boff (born 14 December 1938), born Genézio Darci Boff, is a Brazilian theologian, philosopher, writer and former Catholic priest known for his active support for Latin American liberation theology and for his later work on ecology.

Born: 14 December 1938, Concórdia, Brazil
Occupation: Theologian, writer
Known for: Liberation theology

Life

Boff was born in Concórdia, Santa Catarina, of Italian immigrant descent. He entered the Franciscan order in 1959 and was ordained a priest in 1964. He studied theology in Brazil and earned a doctorate at the University of Munich in 1970. For two decades he edited and taught in Petrópolis, becoming one of the best-known voices of liberation theology.

Conflict with the Vatican

His book Church: Charism and Power (1981), which criticized the hierarchical structure of the Catholic Church, brought him before the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith under Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger. In 1985 he was silenced — forbidden to teach or publish for a year. Under renewed pressure in 1992, he left the Franciscan order and the priesthood.

Later work

Boff subsequently taught ethics and philosophy of religion at the State University of Rio de Janeiro and wrote extensively on spirituality and the ecological crisis, arguing in works such as Cry of the Earth, Cry of the Poor that care for the planet and care for the poor are inseparable.
