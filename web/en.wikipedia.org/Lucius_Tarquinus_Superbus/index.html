Lucius Tarquinus Superbus

This article uses the spelling "Tarquinus", a variant form found in some editions and translations of the Roman historians. For the standard treatment of the last king of Rome, see Lucius Tarquinius Superbus.

Lucius Tarquinus Superbus — the name rendered Lucius Tarquinius Superbus in most modern texts, and in Latin tradition simply Tarquinius Superbus, "Tarquin the Proud" — was remembered by Roman tradition as the seventh and last king of Rome (r. traditionally 535–509 BC).

The tradition

Under this spelling, the narrative is the same as that preserved by Livy and Dionysius of Halicarnassus: Tarquinus seized the throne after the murder of Servius Tullius, governed by terror, completed the Capitoline temple of Jupiter, and was expelled after his son Sextus assaulted Lucretia, precipitating the revolution of 509 BC that founded the Roman Republic. Exiled first to Caere and later Cumae, he died in 495 BC.

Name forms

Roman royal names were transmitted in variant spellings in manuscripts and early printed editions. The gentilicium appears most commonly as Tarquinius, from the Etruscan city of Tarquinii, while Tarquinus occurs as a shortened or corrupted form. English usage settled on "Tarquin" for the royal house, hence "Tarquin the Proud" and "Tarquin the Elder" (Lucius Tarquinius Priscus).
