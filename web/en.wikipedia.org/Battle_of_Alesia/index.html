Battle of Alesia

Battle of Alesia
Date: September–October 52 BC
Location: Alesia (Alise-Sainte-Reine, France)
Combatants: Roman Republic vs Gallic tribes
Commanders: Julius Caesar vs Vercingetorix
Result: Decisive Roman victory

The Battle of Alesia or Siege of Alesia (September–October 52 BC) was the climactic engagement of the Gallic Wars, fought around the hilltop oppidum of Alesia in modern France. Julius Caesar's Roman legions besieged the Gallic army of Vercingetorix, which had taken refuge inside the stronghold, and defeated both the garrison and a huge Gallic relief army. The victory effectively ended organised Gallic resistance to Roman rule.

Caesar's lines of circumvallation and contravallation at Alesia.

The siege

Caesar surrounded Alesia with some 18 kilometres of circumvallation — ditches, ramparts, towers, and traps — to starve out the defenders, and then built a second, outward-facing line of about 21 kilometres (contravallation) to defend against the approaching relief force, which Gallic sources numbered in the hundreds of thousands. After desperate fighting, including a near-breakthrough at the weakest point of the lines, the relief army was repulsed and dispersed. Starving, Vercingetorix surrendered — in Caesar's account, riding out to lay his arms at Caesar's feet. He was held prisoner for six years, displayed in Caesar's triumph in 46 BC, and then executed.

The site, identified in the 1860s under Napoleon III at Alise-Sainte-Reine with the oppidum on Mont Auxois, is now marked by the MuséoParc Alésia and a monumental statue of Vercingetorix.
