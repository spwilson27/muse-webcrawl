Free group

Cayley graph of the free group F₂

In mathematics, the free group FS over a given set S consists of all words that can be built from members of S and their inverses, where two words are considered different unless one can be transformed into the other by cancelling adjacent inverse pairs. The elements of S are called the generators of the free group.

Construction

A word in S is a finite sequence of symbols drawn from S together with formal inverses. After deleting every occurrence of a symbol immediately followed by its inverse (and vice versa), each group element has a unique reduced word. The group operation is concatenation followed by reduction; the empty word is the identity. The free group on one generator is isomorphic to the integers under addition; the free group on two generators is already non-abelian and contains free subgroups of every countable rank.

Universal property

Free groups are characterized by a universal property: any function from S into a group G extends uniquely to a group homomorphism from FS to G. In categorical language, the free group functor is left adjoint to the forgetful functor from groups to sets.

Properties and applications

Every group is a quotient of a free group, which leads to the notion of a group presentation by generators and relations. The Nielsen–Schreier theorem states that every subgroup of a free group is itself free. Free groups play central roles in algebraic topology — the fundamental group of a bouquet of circles is free — and in combinatorial group theory, and they are the setting for results such as the Banach–Tarski paradox.
