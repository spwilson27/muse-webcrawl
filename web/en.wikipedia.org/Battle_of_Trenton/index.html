Battle of Trenton

Date26 December 1776
LocationTrenton, New Jersey
ResultAmerican victory
BelligerentsUnited States (Continental Army) vs. Hessian auxiliaries of Great Britain
CommandersGeorge Washington vs. Johann Rall (mortally wounded)

The Battle of Trenton was a small but pivotal battle of the American Revolutionary War, fought on the morning of 26 December 1776 in Trenton, New Jersey. After General George Washington's crossing of the Delaware River north of Trenton the previous night, Continental Army troops surprised and defeated a garrison of Hessian soldiers commanded by Colonel Johann Rall. The victory dramatically revived American morale after a string of defeats around New York.

The crossing of the Delaware on the night of 25–26 December 1776.

Background

By December 1776 the American cause was near collapse. Driven out of New York, Washington's army had retreated across New Jersey into Pennsylvania, shrinking through casualties, desertion and expiring enlistments. Washington resolved on a bold stroke: a night crossing of the ice-filled Delaware to attack the Hessian outpost at Trenton before the year — and many enlistments — ended.

The battle

In a driving storm of snow and sleet, some 2,400 men with artillery crossed at McConkey's Ferry and marched nine miles to Trenton, arriving around 8 a.m. The Hessians, though not unprepared, were overwhelmed by the sudden attack from two directions and by American artillery dominating the town's main streets. Rall attempted to rally his regiments but was mortally wounded. After about an hour of fighting, the bulk of the garrison surrendered: some 900 prisoners were taken, while American losses were remarkably light.

Aftermath

Washington recrossed into Pennsylvania with his prisoners, then returned days later to win a second victory at Princeton on 3 January 1777. The "Ten Crucial Days" of Trenton and Princeton transformed the strategic and psychological state of the war, reviving enlistments and proving the Continental Army could defeat professional European troops.
