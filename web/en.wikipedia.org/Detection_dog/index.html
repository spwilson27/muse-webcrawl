A detection dog, or sniffer dog, is a dog trained to use its sense of smell to detect substances such as explosives, illegal drugs, wildlife scat, currency, blood, and contraband electronics. Detection dogs are widely used by law enforcement, customs and border agencies, the military, and increasingly in conservation biology and medicine.

Abilities

Dogs possess an olfactory system vastly more sensitive than that of humans, with up to roughly 300 million scent receptors. Trained dogs can detect target odors at concentrations of parts per trillion and can distinguish a target scent among many distracting odors. When a dog locates the target odor it signals its handler with a trained alert, commonly a passive response such as sitting or staring, or an active response such as scratching.

Training

Training is built on play and reward: the dog learns to associate the target odor with a favorite toy or food reward, and searches are gradually extended to larger, more complex areas with hidden samples. Handlers must learn to read subtle changes in the dog's behavior. Reliability depends on regular maintenance training and blind testing, because dogs are susceptible to unintentional cueing by their handlers.

Applications

Explosives-detection dogs screen airports, public events, and military routes; narcotics dogs search vehicles, buildings, and cargo; agricultural detector dogs, such as the U.S. "Beagle Brigade," inspect baggage for prohibited food and plant material. Conservation dogs locate endangered species and their scat for research, and medical detection dogs have been trained to identify diseases including cancers, malaria, and COVID-19 by scent.
