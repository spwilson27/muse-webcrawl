Modulation (music)

In music, modulation is the process of changing from one key (tonic or key signature) to another within a piece or section of music. It is a fundamental structural device in tonal music: modulating away from the home key creates tension and contrast, and returning confirms the tonic. The new key is usually confirmed by its own cadence.

Common targets

In major keys, pieces most often modulate to the dominant (a perfect fifth above), the submediant (relative minor), or the mediant. In minor keys, the relative major and the dominant minor are common destinations. Sonata form, for example, conventionally moves from the tonic to the dominant (or relative major) in its exposition.

Techniques

  • Pivot-chord (common-chord) modulation — a chord diatonic in both keys is reinterpreted, smoothing the transition.
  • Chromatic modulation — a note is altered chromatically, for instance turning a tonic chord into a dominant seventh of the new key.
  • Direct (phrase) modulation — the new key begins abruptly at a phrase boundary, common in popular music, where a final chorus is often raised by a semitone or whole tone ("truck driver's gear change").
  • Common-tone and enharmonic modulations — a single held note, or an enharmonically reinterpreted chord such as a diminished seventh or German augmented sixth, links distant keys.
