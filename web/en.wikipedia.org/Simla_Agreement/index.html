Simla Agreement

Signed2 July 1972
LocationShimla, Himachal Pradesh, India
SignatoriesIndira Gandhi (India); Zulfikar Ali Bhutto (Pakistan)
PartiesIndia and Pakistan
SubjectPost-war settlement after the Indo-Pakistani War of 1971

The Simla Agreement was a peace treaty signed between India and Pakistan on 2 July 1972 in Shimla, the capital of the Indian state of Himachal Pradesh. It followed the Indo-Pakistani War of 1971, which had ended with the creation of Bangladesh. The agreement, signed by Indian Prime Minister Indira Gandhi and Pakistani President Zulfikar Ali Bhutto, sought to lay the foundations for a durable peace and to normalise relations between the two countries.

Provisions

Under the agreement, both countries resolved to settle their differences by peaceful means through bilateral negotiations, without recourse to third-party mediation — a clause India has since invoked against international involvement in the Kashmir dispute. The two sides agreed to respect the Line of Control in Jammu and Kashmir resulting from the December 1971 ceasefire, renaming the earlier ceasefire line, and neither side was to alter it unilaterally. The agreement also provided for the withdrawal of troops to their own side of the international border and set out steps toward restoring diplomatic, trade and communications links.

Prisoners of war

India held more than 90,000 Pakistani prisoners of war after the 1971 conflict. Their repatriation was handled through subsequent negotiations, including the Delhi Agreement of 1973, and the last prisoners were released in 1974 after Pakistan recognised Bangladesh.

Legacy

The Simla Agreement remains a cornerstone document in India–Pakistan relations. India regards it as establishing bilateralism as the governing principle for resolving disputes, including Kashmir, while Pakistan has at times sought to involve the United Nations or third parties. Later initiatives such as the 1999 Lahore Declaration were framed as building upon the Simla framework.
