Emerson, Lake & Palmer

OriginLondon, England
GenresProgressive rock
Years active1970–1979, 1991–1998, 2010
MembersKeith Emerson, Greg Lake, Carl Palmer

Emerson, Lake & Palmer (ELP) were an English progressive rock supergroup formed in London in 1970. The band consisted of Keith Emerson (keyboards) of the Nice, Greg Lake (vocals, bass, guitar) of King Crimson, and Carl Palmer (drums) of Atomic Rooster. With nine gold albums in the US and an estimated 48 million records sold worldwide, they were one of the most commercially successful progressive rock bands of the 1970s.

History

ELP made their live debut at the Isle of Wight Festival in August 1970 and released their self-titled debut album the same year, featuring the hit single "Lucky Man". Their classical adaptations — Mussorgsky's Pictures at an Exhibition (1971), and music by Ginastera, Copland, and Bernstein — became a signature. Tarkus (1971), Trilogy (1972), and Brain Salad Surgery (1973) cemented their fame, with increasingly elaborate stage shows.

Later years and legacy

After the ambitious orchestral tours supporting Works (1977), the band split in 1979, reforming in 1991 for Black Moon before dissolving again in 1998; a 40th-anniversary concert followed in 2010. Emerson and Lake both died in 2016. ELP are remembered for Emerson's virtuoso Hammond organ and Moog synthesizer performances and for defining the spectacle and ambition of 1970s progressive rock.
