The Magga-vibhanga Sutta ("Analysis of the Path") is a Buddhist discourse (Pali: sutta) preserved in the Samyutta Nikaya of the Pali Canon (SN 45.8). In it, the Buddha gives definitions of each of the eight factors of the Noble Eightfold Path, the path of practice said to lead to the cessation of suffering (dukkha) and to awakening. The sutta is one of the most frequently cited canonical definitions of the path factors.

Content

The discourse is set at Savatthi, where the Buddha addresses the assembled monks. He announces that he will teach and analyse the Noble Eightfold Path, and then defines its factors:

  • Right view (sammā-diṭṭhi): knowledge of suffering, its origin, its cessation, and the way leading to its cessation — the Four Noble Truths.
  • Right intention (sammā-saṅkappa): intention of renunciation, intention of goodwill (non-ill will), and intention of harmlessness.
  • Right speech (sammā-vācā): abstaining from lying, divisive speech, abusive speech, and idle chatter.
  • Right action (sammā-kammanta): abstaining from killing, stealing, and sexual misconduct.
  • Right livelihood (sammā-ājīva): abandoning wrong livelihood and earning a living by right livelihood.
  • Right effort (sammā-vāyāma): the four right exertions — preventing and abandoning unwholesome states, and arousing and maintaining wholesome states.
  • Right mindfulness (sammā-sati): the four foundations of mindfulness — contemplation of body, feelings, mind, and mental objects.
  • Right concentration (sammā-samādhi): the four jhānas, the progressively deeper states of meditative absorption.

Context

The sutta opens the analysis (vibhanga) style of exposition later systematised in the Abhidhamma. Its definitions of right concentration in terms of the four jhānas and of right view in terms of the Four Noble Truths have been especially influential in Theravada commentarial tradition and in modern meditation teaching.
