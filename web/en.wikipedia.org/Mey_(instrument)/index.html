Mey (instrument)

ClassificationDouble-reed aerophone
OriginTurkey / Anatolia
Related instrumentsDuduk, balaban, guanzi

The mey is a Turkish folk double-reed woodwind instrument. It is a small cylindrical pipe, typically 20–40 cm long, traditionally carved from the wood of the plum tree (also apricot, olive, or rosewood), fitted with a large double reed made from a single piece of flattened rush or cane. The mey produces a soft, mournful, vocal timbre well suited to lyrical folk melodies.

Construction and playing

The body has seven finger holes on the front and one thumb hole behind. The large reed, called kamış, sits in the player's mouth and is controlled with the lips while a small clamp or bridle adjusts its opening. Circular breathing is commonly used to sustain the long melodic lines of the uzun hava (free-rhythm "long air") repertoire. Meys are built in several sizes and tunings — ana (main), orta (middle), and cura (small/high) — to suit different regional pitch standards.

Context

The mey is closely related to the Armenian duduk and the Azerbaijani balaban, all descendants of ancient double-reed pipes of the region. In Turkey it is prominent in folk ensembles and in the music of the Alevi tradition, often accompanying the bağlama, and has become familiar through film and television soundtracks. Its smaller size and narrower bore distinguish its brighter, more piercing sound from the warmer duduk.
