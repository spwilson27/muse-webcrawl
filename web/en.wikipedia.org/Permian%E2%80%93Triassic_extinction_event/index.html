The Permian–Triassic extinction event, colloquially known as the Great Dying, was an extinction event that occurred about 251.9 million years ago, forming the boundary between the Permian and Triassic geologic periods. It was the Earth's most severe known extinction event, with the extinction of 57% of biological families, 83% of genera, and an estimated 90% of marine species and 70% of terrestrial vertebrate species.

Causes

The leading explanation is massive volcanism of the Siberian Traps, one of the largest known volcanic events, which released enormous quantities of carbon dioxide and other gases — including by baking coal and carbonate deposits — driving rapid global warming (possibly 8–10 °C in the oceans), ocean acidification, anoxia and euxinia, and collapse of the carbon cycle. Contributions from an impact event have been proposed but not substantiated.

Effects and recovery

Trilobites, rugose and tabulate corals, blastoids, and many other groups vanished entirely; on land, dominant synapsid faunas were decimated and the disaster taxon Lystrosaurus briefly dominated. Recovery was unusually slow, taking roughly 5–10 million years for complex ecosystems, partly because of recurring environmental crises in the Early Triassic. The event cleared ecological space for the rise of archosaurs and, eventually, the dinosaurs.
