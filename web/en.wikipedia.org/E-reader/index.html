An e-reader, also called an e-book reader, is a mobile electronic device designed primarily for reading digital e-books and periodicals. Most e-readers use electronic paper (e-ink) displays, which mimic printed paper, consume very little power, and are readable in bright sunlight, at the cost of slow refresh and (historically) monochrome rendering.

History

Early dedicated devices included the Rocket eBook and SoftBook (1997–98) and Sony's Librie (2004), the first with an E Ink screen. The category took off with the Amazon Kindle (2007), which coupled a store, wireless delivery and aggressive pricing; Barnes & Noble's Nook, Kobo readers and tablets followed. Dedicated e-readers declined relative to tablets and phones in the 2010s but retained a loyal market, and colour e-ink models have since appeared.

Features and formats

Devices typically store thousands of books, offer adjustable fonts, built-in dictionaries, front-lighting and weeks of battery life. Common formats include EPUB (the open standard supported by most vendors), Amazon's Kindle formats (KF8/AZW3, earlier MOBI), and PDF. Many libraries lend e-books via services such as OverDrive/Libby. Issues of contention include digital rights management, ownership versus licensing, and vendors' ability to revoke purchased titles remotely.
