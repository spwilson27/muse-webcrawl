First Mexican Empire

The First Mexican Empire (Spanish: Primer Imperio Mexicano) was a short-lived monarchy and the first independent post-colonial Mexican state, lasting from 1821 to 1823. Its sole emperor was Agustín de Iturbide, who reigned as Agustín I from May 1822 until his abdication in March 1823.

Independence

Mexico achieved independence from Spain through the Plan of Iguala (1821), proclaimed by Iturbide and Vicente Guerrero, which promised independence, preservation of the Roman Catholic faith, and equality between Spaniards and creoles — the "Three Guarantees." The Treaty of Córdoba confirmed independence, and Iturbide entered Mexico City in September 1821 at the head of the Army of the Three Guarantees.

Reign of Agustín I

The Plan of Iguala envisioned a constitutional monarchy, ideally under a Spanish prince. When Spain refused to recognize independence or provide a monarch, a popular and military demonstration in May 1822 led Congress to proclaim Iturbide emperor. He was crowned on 21 July 1822. The Empire's territory stretched from Central America (which voluntarily annexed itself) to the present-day southwestern United States.

Fall

The Empire faced bankruptcy, conflict between the Emperor and Congress — which Iturbide dissolved in October 1822 — and growing republican opposition. In December 1822 Antonio López de Santa Anna rose in Veracruz, and the Plan of Casa Mata (February 1823) united provincial forces against the Emperor. Iturbide abdicated on 19 March 1823 and went into exile. A provisional government convened a new congress, and Mexico became a federal republic under the Constitution of 1824. Iturbide returned in 1824 and was executed.

Mexico's second experiment with empire, under Maximilian I, came four decades later during the French intervention.
