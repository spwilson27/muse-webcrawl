Avatar

Avatar (Sanskrit: avatāra, "descent") is a concept in Hinduism meaning the deliberate earthly manifestation or incarnation of a deity, most famously the ten avatars of Vishnu, including Rama and Krishna. By extension the word has come to mean any embodiment or representation of a person or idea.

In Hinduism

Avatars are said to descend to restore dharma (cosmic order) when it is threatened by evil. The Bhagavad Gita expresses this promise: whenever righteousness declines, Krishna declares, he manifests himself. Lists of Vishnu's avatars vary; the Dashavatara commonly includes Matsya the fish, Kurma the tortoise, Varaha the boar, Narasimha the man-lion, Vamana the dwarf, Parashurama, Rama, Krishna, Buddha or Balarama, and Kalki, who is yet to come.

Modern uses

In computing, an avatar is a graphical representation of a user — an icon, profile picture, or controllable character in a game or virtual world. The term was popularized in science fiction, notably Neal Stephenson's novel Snow Crash (1992), and by the 2009 film Avatar.
