Pupusa

OriginEl Salvador
TypeStuffed corn tortilla / flatbread
Main ingredientsMasa, cheese, beans, chicharrón
Served withCurtido, tomato sauce

A pupusa is a thick griddle cake or flatbread from El Salvador, traditionally made with cornmeal or rice flour and stuffed with a savoury filling. It is regarded as the national dish of El Salvador and is also eaten in Honduras and by Salvadoran diaspora communities worldwide.

Preparation

The dough (masa) is flattened by hand and wrapped around fillings such as quesillo cheese, refried beans, chicharrón (seasoned ground pork), or loroco flower buds. The filled cake is patted into a disc and cooked on a hot griddle (comal) until lightly browned and the cheese melts. Pupusas are served with curtido, a lightly fermented cabbage slaw, and a thin tomato sauce.

History and culture

The dish has pre-Columbian origins among the Pipil peoples of the region. Pupuserías — restaurants specialising in pupusas — are ubiquitous in El Salvador, and the second Sunday of November is celebrated as National Pupusa Day. In the 2000s, El Salvador and Honduras disputed the dish's origin, though it is internationally identified with El Salvador.
