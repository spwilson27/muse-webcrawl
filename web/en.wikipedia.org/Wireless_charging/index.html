Wireless charging

MethodNear-field inductive coupling (most common)
Main standardQi (Wireless Power Consortium)
ApplicationsPhones, wearables, toothbrushes, EVs

Wireless charging, or inductive charging, is the transfer of electrical energy between two objects through an electromagnetic field, without physical connectors. In the most common implementation, an alternating current in a coil in the charging pad generates a magnetic field that induces a current in a coil in the receiving device, which charges its battery.

Standards

The Qi standard, maintained by the Wireless Power Consortium, became the dominant phone standard after Apple adopted it in 2017; its Qi2 revision (2023) added a magnetic alignment profile derived from Apple's MagSafe. The rival PMA standard of the Power Matters Alliance was absorbed into the AirFuel Alliance. Typical phone charging rates range from 5 W to 15 W or more under vendor-specific fast-charging extensions, generally with lower efficiency and more heat than wired charging.

Other applications

Inductive charging is used in electric toothbrushes, medical implants, RFID tags, and industrial equipment. For electric vehicles, stationary inductive pads (standardised as SAE J2954) and experimental dynamic systems that charge vehicles while driving have been demonstrated, though plug-in charging remains dominant.
