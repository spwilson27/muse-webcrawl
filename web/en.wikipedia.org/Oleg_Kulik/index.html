Oleg Kulik

Oleg Borisovich Kulik (born 15 April 1961 in Kyiv, Ukrainian SSR) is a Ukrainian-born Russian performance artist, sculptor, photographer, and curator, best known for his performances in the persona of a dog. A central figure of Moscow Actionism in the 1990s, Kulik used the "man-dog" character to explore the boundaries between human and animal and to comment on post-Soviet society.

The dog performances

Kulik developed the dog persona in the early 1990s, together with the "Mad Dog" performances with Alexander Brener and later collaborations with curator Viktor Misiano and artist Mila Bredikhina. In projects such as The Last Taboo, Guarded by the Lonely Cerberus (1994) and appearances in Zurich, Stockholm, and New York, he performed naked on all fours, chained and barking at — and sometimes lunging at — audiences. At a 1996 exhibition in Stockholm he bit a visitor, an incident widely reported. He framed the work as "zoophrenia": an investigation of a pre-cultural, animal state.

Other work and curating

Beyond the dog persona, Kulik has produced sculpture, photography (including the Red Square and window-display series), and large-scale curatorial projects, notably I Believe! (2007) and exhibitions of contemporary Russian art. He was a curator of the Regina Gallery in Moscow in the early 1990s and helped shape the Moscow art scene of the period.
