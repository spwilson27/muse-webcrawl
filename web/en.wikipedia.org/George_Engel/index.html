George Engel (April 15, 1836 – November 11, 1887) was a German-American anarchist and labor organizer who was one of the defendants tried and executed in connection with the Haymarket affair of 1886 in Chicago.

Early life

Engel was born in Kassel, Germany, and emigrated to the United States in 1873, eventually settling in Chicago, where he worked as a toymaker and became active in the socialist and anarchist movements. He was a member of the International Working People's Association.

Haymarket affair

On May 4, 1886, a bomb was thrown at police during a labor rally in Chicago's Haymarket Square. Engel had not been present at the rally, but he was arrested and charged with conspiracy to murder. The prosecution argued that a meeting at his home had planned violence against the police.

Trial and execution

Engel was convicted along with seven other defendants in a trial widely criticized for bias and lack of evidence. He refused to appeal for clemency on principle and was hanged on November 11, 1887, alongside August Spies, Albert Parsons, and Adolph Fischer. The surviving defendants were pardoned in 1893 by Governor John Peter Altgeld, who denounced the trial's unfairness.
