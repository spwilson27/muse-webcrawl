Still Bay

The Still Bay (also written Stillbay) is a Middle Stone Age industry of southern Africa, named after the town of Stillbaai in South Africa's Western Cape, dated to roughly 75,000–70,000 years ago. It belongs to the period sometimes called the African Middle Stone Age's stone-tool "golden age," alongside the Howiesons Poort industry.

Characteristics

The Still Bay is defined by distinctive bifacially worked, foliate (leaf-shaped) stone points, often made of fine-grained silcrete that was deliberately heat-treated to improve flaking. Sites such as Blombos Cave have yielded Still Bay points together with engraved ochre and shell beads, evidence interpreted as among the earliest signs of symbolically mediated behaviour in Homo sapiens.

Significance

The brief appearance and disappearance of the Still Bay, possibly during a period of population fragmentation in glacial conditions, is debated in studies of how behavioural modernity emerged in Africa.
