Sofia

Sofia (Bulgarian: София) is the capital and largest city of Bulgaria, with a population of about 1.3 million in the city proper. It is situated in the Sofia Valley in the western part of the country, at the foot of the Vitosha massif. One of Europe's oldest capitals, its history spans some 2,400 years.

History

Founded by the Thracian tribe of the Serdi, the city was known in Roman times as Serdica and was a favourite residence of Constantine the Great, who reportedly called it "my Rome". It later passed between Byzantine and Bulgarian rule before nearly five centuries of Ottoman rule (1382–1878). After Bulgaria's liberation in the Russo-Turkish War, Sofia was chosen as capital in 1879, growing from a town of some 20,000 into a modern European capital, though it suffered heavy Allied bombing in 1943–44.

Geography and climate

At an elevation of about 500–550 metres, Sofia has a humid continental climate with warm summers and cold, snowy winters. The mineral springs that first attracted settlement still supply public fountains. Vitosha, immediately to the south, is a popular skiing and hiking area and a protected nature park.

Landmarks and culture

Notable landmarks include the gold-domed Alexander Nevsky Cathedral, the medieval Boyana Church (a UNESCO World Heritage Site), the Roman ruins of Serdica, the National Palace of Culture, and Sofia University, founded in 1888. The city is Bulgaria's economic centre, accounting for a large share of national GDP, and hosts a growing information-technology sector.
