Raurimu Spiral

The Raurimu Spiral is a single-track railway spiral and horseshoe curve on the North Island Main Trunk line in the central North Island of New Zealand. It climbs 139 metres (456 ft) in about 6.8 km of track between Raurimu and National Park, solving the problem of a 200-metre height difference in a short horizontal distance.

Design and construction

Surveyor Robert W. Holmes devised the spiral in 1898 after concluding that the direct gradient was far too steep for steam locomotives. The line loops over itself in a complete circle with two short tunnels, two full horseshoe curves, and an average gradient of about 1 in 52. Opened in 1908 as part of the completed Main Trunk, it has been called an engineering masterpiece and remains in daily use by freight and passenger trains, including the Northern Explorer.
