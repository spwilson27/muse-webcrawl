Liang dynasty

Liang dynasty 梁
502–557
Capital: Jiankang
Founder: Emperor Wu of Liang

The Liang dynasty (502–557), also known as the Southern Liang, was the third of the Southern dynasties during China's Northern and Southern dynasties period. It succeeded the Southern Qi and was followed by the Chen dynasty, ruling much of southern China from its capital at Jiankang (modern Nanjing).

Emperor Wu

Most of the dynasty's history was dominated by its founder, Emperor Wu (Xiao Yan), who reigned from 502 to 549. A patron of Buddhism and scholarship, he presided over a culturally flourishing court but spent heavily on temples and, late in his reign, neglected military affairs.

Fall

The Hou Jing rebellion (548–552) devastated the capital and fatally weakened the dynasty; Emperor Wu starved during the siege of Jiankang. After a succession struggle, the general Chen Baxian deposed the last Liang emperor in 557 and founded the Chen dynasty, though a rump Later Liang state survived under Northern Zhou and Sui protection until 587.
