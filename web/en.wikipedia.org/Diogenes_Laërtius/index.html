Diogenes Laërtius

Diogenes Laërtius (fl. 3rd century CE) was a biographer of the Greek philosophers. Nothing is known about his life; his surname may derive from the town of Laerte in Cilicia or from the Roman family name Laërtii. His principal work, Lives and Opinions of Eminent Philosophers, is one of the principal surviving sources on the history of Greek philosophy.

The Lives

The work is divided into ten books, covering philosophers from Thales and the Ionian school through Pythagoras, Plato, the Academy, the Stoics, and Epicurus, whose letters and doctrines preserved in Book X are especially valuable. Diogenes compiles anecdotes, quotations, lists of writings, and sometimes wills, often uncritically, but he preserves much material that would otherwise be lost—entirely in Greek, without reference to Roman or Christian thought.

Influence

The Lives became a standard reference in the Renaissance after Ambrogio Traversari's Latin translation (1433), shaping biographical writing about philosophers ever since. Scholars treat his testimony cautiously but regard it as indispensable for Hellenistic schools such as the Cynics, Stoics, and Epicureans.
