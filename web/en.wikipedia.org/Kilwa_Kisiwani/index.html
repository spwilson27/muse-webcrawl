Kilwa Kisiwani

Location: Lindi Region, Tanzania
Founded: c. 10th century
Status: UNESCO World Heritage Site (1981)

Kilwa Kisiwani is an island and former port city off the southern coast of present-day Tanzania. Between the 13th and 15th centuries it was one of the most important trading centres of the Swahili Coast, controlling much of the gold trade from Sofala and the interior of southeastern Africa.

History

Tradition holds that the settlement was founded in the 10th century by Ali bin al-Hasan Shirazi, a Persian prince. Kilwa rose to prominence as the southernmost port reachable in a single monsoon sailing season from Arabia, making it the entrepôt through which Zimbabwean gold reached markets in Arabia, Persia, India, and China. The Great Mosque of Kilwa, built largely of coral stone, and the palace of Husuni Kubwa testify to the city's wealth.

Decline

Portuguese forces under Francisco de Almeida seized Kilwa in 1505, and the city declined thereafter, though it later revived as a centre of the slave trade in the 18th and 19th centuries. The ruins of Kilwa Kisiwani and nearby Songo Mnara were inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1981.
