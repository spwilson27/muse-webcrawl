Avon River / Ōtākaro

LocationChristchurch, Canterbury, New Zealand
Lengthabout 14 km (8.7 mi)
SourceSprings near Avonhead
MouthAvon Heathcote Estuary / Ihutai

The Avon River / Ōtākaro flows through the centre of the city of Christchurch in the Canterbury region of New Zealand's South Island. Its Māori name, Ōtākaro, means "the place of a game", referring to the children who played on its banks. The river rises from springs in the suburb of Avonhead and winds eastwards through Hagley Park and the central city before joining the Heathcote River / Ōpāwaho to form the Avon Heathcote Estuary / Ihutai, which opens into Pegasus Bay.

Course

The river is short and low-gradient, fed mainly by spring water, which keeps it clear and relatively constant in flow. It passes the Christchurch Botanic Gardens, the Canterbury Museum, the Arts Centre and Cathedral Square. Punting on the river from the Antigua Boatsheds is a well-known tourist activity.

History

The area around the river was an important food-gathering site (mahinga kai) for Ngāi Tahu. European settlers named the river after the River Avon in Scotland; Shakespeare's connection was reinforced when the Deans brothers named it after the Avon in Lanarkshire. Following the 2010 and 2011 Canterbury earthquakes, land along the river in the eastern suburbs was badly damaged by liquefaction and was zoned red, meaning houses were demolished and the land cleared. A large tract along the river corridor has become the Ōtākaro Avon River Corridor, planned as parkland and ecological restoration.

Ecology

The river supports native fish such as longfin eels (tuna) and inanga, and its banks are planted with both native species and the English willows and oaks planted by early settlers.
