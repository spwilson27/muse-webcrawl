Elephant

Family: Elephantidae
Order: Proboscidea
Genera: Loxodonta, Elephas

Elephants are the largest living land animals. Three living species are currently recognised: the African bush elephant, the African forest elephant, and the Asian elephant. They are the only surviving members of the family Elephantidae and the order Proboscidea.

Characteristics

Distinctive features include a long trunk (a fusion of nose and upper lip) used for breathing, grasping, and drinking; large ear flaps; column-like legs; and tusks — enlarged incisor teeth of ivory. Elephants have the largest brains of any land animal and display self-awareness, empathy, and complex social behaviour.

Behaviour and society

Elephants live in matriarchal family groups led by the oldest female. They communicate through calls including low-frequency infrasound that travels long distances, and through seismic signals detected through their feet. Gestation lasts about 22 months, the longest of any land animal.

Distribution and conservation

African elephants range across sub-Saharan Africa and Asian elephants across South and Southeast Asia. All species are threatened by habitat loss and poaching for ivory; the African forest and Asian elephants are listed as critically endangered and endangered respectively.
