Malay literature

Malay literature is the literature of the Malay language and the Malay world of Southeast Asia, encompassing oral traditions, classical court texts written in Jawi script, and modern literature from Malaysia, Indonesia, Brunei and Singapore.

Classical literature

Classical works include the Sejarah Melayu (Malay Annals), a romanticised history of the Malacca Sultanate; the epic Hikayat Hang Tuah; court poetry in the syair and pantun forms; and religious and legal texts influenced by Islam. The 19th-century writer Abdullah bin Abdul Kadir (Munshi Abdullah) is often called the father of modern Malay literature for his vivid autobiographical prose.

Modern literature

In the 20th century Malay literature developed through journalism, novels and short stories, with writers addressing colonialism, nationalism and social change. In Malaysia, institutions such as Dewan Bahasa dan Pustaka promote the language and its literature, and national laureates (Sasterawan Negara) are honoured for their work.

Forms

Characteristic forms include the rhymed quatrain pantun, the narrative poem syair, prose romances called hikayat, and oral storytelling traditions.
