Northern Avenue, Yerevan

Northern Avenue (Armenian: Հյուսիսային պողոտա, Hyusisayin poghota) is a pedestrian avenue in downtown Yerevan, the capital of Armenia. Opened in 2007, it runs about 450 metres through the Kentron district, linking the Opera area at Freedom Square with Abovyan Street towards Republic Square.

Residential buildings lining Northern Avenue

History

The avenue was first envisaged in Alexander Tamanian's 1924 master plan for Yerevan but was never built under Soviet rule; the land remained occupied by older, mostly modest houses. Construction began in 2002 after the city expropriated and demolished those buildings — a process that drew protests from displaced residents over compensation — and the avenue opened on 16 November 2007, with further sections completed by 2014.

Description

The avenue is lined with upscale residential buildings in modern interpretations of Armenian neoclassical style, along with offices, luxury shops, hotels, cafés, and restaurants, above an underground parking and retail level. It has become a fashionable promenade and a centrepiece of post-Soviet Yerevan's redevelopment, as well as a focus of debate about gentrification and heritage loss.
