Moskva (Russian: Москва, "Moscow") was a guided-missile cruiser of the Slava class, launched in 1979 as Slava and serving as the flagship of the Russian Black Sea Fleet. She was the lead ship of her class, designed during the Cold War primarily to attack U.S. carrier battle groups with long-range P-500 Bazalt anti-ship missiles.

Service history

After a lengthy refit in the 1990s, Moskva returned to service in 2000. She led the Russian naval task force off Georgia in 2008, deployed to the Mediterranean during the Syrian civil war to provide air defense for Russian forces, and participated in exercises across the Black Sea and Mediterranean.

Sinking

During the Russian invasion of Ukraine, on 13 April 2022 Moskva was struck — according to Ukrainian officials, by two Neptune anti-ship missiles — and caught fire. Russia stated the fire detonated ammunition. The ship sank under tow on 14 April 2022, becoming the largest warship lost in combat since the Second World War and a major symbolic blow to the Russian Navy. The wreck was declared a Ukrainian underwater cultural heritage site.
