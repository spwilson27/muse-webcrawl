Rehabilitation (penology)

Rehabilitation in penology is the process of reforming an offender so that they can re-enter society and lead a law-abiding life. It is one of the classical aims of punishment, alongside deterrence, incapacitation, retribution, and restitution.

Approaches

Rehabilitative programmes in prisons include education and vocational training, cognitive-behavioural therapy, treatment for substance dependence, and work experience. After release, probation supervision, halfway houses, and employment support are used to reduce the risk of reoffending.

Evidence and debate

Interest in rehabilitation waned in the 1970s after influential reviews questioned whether correctional treatment worked, giving rise to the "nothing works" doctrine. Later meta-analyses showed that well-designed programmes — particularly those following the risk-need-responsivity model — can reduce recidivism by meaningful margins, and rehabilitation has returned to the centre of penal policy in many countries.
