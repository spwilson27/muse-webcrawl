Adele Eva Goldberg (born 1963) is an American linguist known for her work in cognitive linguistics and construction grammar. She is a professor of linguistics at Princeton University and has written influential books arguing that grammatical knowledge consists of learned pairings of form and function — "constructions" — at all levels, from morphemes to complex sentence patterns.

Goldberg received her PhD in linguistics from the University of California, Berkeley, in 1992, where she was associated with the group around Charles Fillmore and George Lakoff that developed construction grammar as an alternative to rule-based generative approaches. Her 1995 book Constructions: A Construction Grammar Approach to Argument Structure showed how argument-structure patterns such as the ditransitive ("She baked him a cake") carry meaning themselves, rather than being derived entirely from the verb. Her later work, including Constructions at Work (2006) and Explain Me This (2019), explores how constructions are learned by children and adults from usage, how they compete, and why languages are structured the way they are.

Research themes

Her research addresses language acquisition, the relationship between learning and generalisation, and the role of frequency and function in shaping grammar. She has served as president of the Cognitive Science Society and has been recognised with fellowships and awards in linguistics and cognitive science.
