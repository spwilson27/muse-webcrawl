Hot Wheels

Hot Wheels is an American brand of scale model cars invented by Elliot Handler and introduced by the toy company Mattel in 1968. It was the primary competitor of the Matchbox brand, and its die-cast cars — with their distinctive Spectraflame paint, red-lined wheels, and low-friction axles designed for speed on plastic orange track — became one of the best-selling toy lines in the world.

History

Handler, co-founder of Mattel, set out to improve on existing die-cast toy cars by making them faster and more customisable in style, drawing on Southern California hot-rod and custom-car culture. The original 1968 "Sweet Sixteen" models included the Custom Mustang, Python, and Beatnik Bandit, many designed by Harry Bradley. The free-spinning wheels and track sets helped Hot Wheels quickly overtake rivals in popularity.

Products and culture

Hot Wheels cars are produced in many scales, principally 1:64, and range from licensed replicas of real cars to original fantasy designs. The brand has expanded into track sets, video games, animated series, and live events. Collecting Hot Wheels is a substantial hobby, with limited editions and "Treasure Hunt" models commanding high prices; rare individual cars, such as the 1969 "Beach Bomb" prototype, have been valued in the hundreds of thousands of dollars. Mattel celebrated the brand's 50th anniversary in 2018, by which time billions of cars had been sold.
