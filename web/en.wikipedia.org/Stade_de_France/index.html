Stade de France

The Stade de France, France's national stadium.

The Stade de France is the national stadium of France, located in Saint-Denis, just north of Paris. With a capacity of about 81,338, it is among the largest stadiums in Europe. It was built for the 1998 FIFA World Cup and hosted the final, in which France defeated Brazil 3–0 to win its first World Cup.

Design

Designed by architects Michel Macary, Aymeric Zublena, Michel Regembal and Claude Costantini, the stadium is distinguished by its elliptical floating roof disc, which covers all seats while leaving the pitch open. A movable lower stand can retract to reveal an athletics track, allowing the stadium to host track and field events, as it did for the 2003 World Athletics Championships and the 2024 Summer Olympics.

Events

The Stade de France is the regular home of the France national football and rugby union teams, and hosts the finals of the Coupe de France and the Top 14 rugby championship. It staged the 2007 and 2023 Rugby World Cup finals and matches of UEFA Euro 2016. It is also France's premier concert venue, hosting acts before audiences of up to 90,000.

Incidents

On 13 November 2015, during a friendly match against Germany, three suicide bombers detonated explosives outside the stadium as part of the coordinated Paris attacks. The stadium has since been the subject of heightened security planning for major events.
