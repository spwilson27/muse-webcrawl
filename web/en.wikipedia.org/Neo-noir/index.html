Neo-noir (Greek for "new-black") is a revival of film noir, a genre that had originally flourished during and after World War II, taking its visual style from German Expressionist cinematography. Neo-noir films update the themes and visual conventions of classic noir — shadowy lighting, moral ambiguity, femme fatales, doomed protagonists — for contemporary settings, frequently in colour and with graphic content unavailable to the earlier era under the Hays Code.

Characteristics

Neo-noir shares noir's interest in crime, corruption and psychological darkness, but reflects later anxieties: corporate power, technology, urban decay, and unreliable or fragmented identity. Subgenres include tech-noir (Blade Runner, The Terminator), which blends noir with science fiction, and neo-westerns such as No Country for Old Men.

History

The movement is often dated from the 1960s and 1970s with films such as Point Blank (1967) and Chinatown (1974), the latter a period homage to classic noir. The 1980s and 1990s brought Body Heat, Blade Runner, Basic Instinct, L.A. Confidential, Se7en and the Coen brothers' Fargo. Neo-noir has also flourished on television (True Detective) and in video games and comics.
