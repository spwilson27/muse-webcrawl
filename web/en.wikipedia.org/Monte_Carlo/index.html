Monte Carlo (French: Monte-Carlo; Ligurian: Monte Carlu) is an administrative ward and the most famous district of the Principality of Monaco, a sovereign city-state on the French Riviera. Internationally synonymous with luxury and gambling, Monte Carlo is home to the Monte Carlo Casino, the Opéra de Monte-Carlo, grand hotels such as the Hôtel de Paris and Hôtel Hermitage, and a harbour filled with superyachts.

History

The district takes its name — "Mount Charles" — from Prince Charles III of Monaco, who in the 1860s sought to rescue the principality's finances by granting a casino concession on the then-barren Spélugues plateau. The casino, developed by François Blanc and opened in 1863, proved so profitable that Monaco abolished direct income tax for residents in 1869. A railway connection and palatial hotels followed, and Monte Carlo became the playground of European royalty and high society; the casino complex, including the opera house designed by Charles Garnier, was completed in its present form by 1879.

Sport and culture

Monte Carlo hosts the Monaco Grand Prix, run on the Circuit de Monaco through its streets since 1929 and considered one of motor racing's crown jewels, and the season-opening Rallye Monte-Carlo, first held in 1911. The Monte Carlo Masters tennis tournament is played at the Monte Carlo Country Club (actually in neighbouring Roquebrune-Cap-Martin, France). The district is also home to the Ballets de Monte-Carlo, the Monte-Carlo Philharmonic Orchestra and a celebrated spring arts festival.

Namesake

The district's fame from gambling gave its name to the Monte Carlo method, the class of computational algorithms that use random sampling — a reference, coined by the method's developers at Los Alamos in the 1940s, to the randomness of the casino's roulette wheels.
