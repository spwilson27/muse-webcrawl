2007–2008 financial crisis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The 2007–2008 financial crisis, also known as the global financial crisis (GFC), was a severe worldwide economic crisis that occurred in the late 2000s. It is considered by most economists to have been the most serious financial crisis since the Great Depression. Predatory lending in the form of subprime mortgages in the United States, tied into complex securitised products, triggered a collapse of the US housing bubble and a cascade of failures in the global financial system.

Course of the crisis

Stress appeared in 2007 with the failure of subprime lenders and the seizure of interbank lending, the run on Northern Rock in the United Kingdom, and the near-collapse of Bear Stearns in March 2008. The crisis peaked in September 2008 with the bankruptcy of Lehman Brothers — the largest in US history — the rescue of insurer AIG, and the near failure of money market funds. Stock markets fell worldwide and world trade contracted sharply, ushering in the Great Recession.

Responses

Governments responded with bank rescues and guarantees, large fiscal stimulus packages, and unconventional monetary policy including near-zero interest rates and quantitative easing. In the United States, the $700 billion Troubled Asset Relief Program (TARP) was enacted in October 2008. Regulatory reforms followed, including the Dodd–Frank Act of 2010 and the Basel III banking standards, alongside the European sovereign debt crisis that emerged in the crisis's aftermath.

Causes

Inquiries such as the US Financial Crisis Inquiry Commission identified a combination of excessive risk-taking by financial institutions, failures of regulation and credit rating agencies, complex mortgage-backed securities and derivatives, rising household debt, and failures of corporate governance.
