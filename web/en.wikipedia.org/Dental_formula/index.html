Dental formula

Dental formula notation for permanent and deciduous dentition

A dental formula is a summary of the number and kinds of teeth in a mammal's dentition, written as the count of incisors, canines, premolars, and molars in one half of the upper and lower jaws, multiplied by two.

Examples

The adult human dental formula is 2.1.2.3 / 2.1.2.3, giving 32 teeth; the deciduous formula is 2.1.0.2 / 2.1.0.2, giving 20. Dogs have 2(3.1.4.2 / 3.1.4.3) = 42 teeth, and cats 2(3.1.3.1 / 3.1.2.1) = 30.

Use

Dental formulas are standard descriptors in mammalogy, zoology, and archaeology for identifying species from skeletal remains.
