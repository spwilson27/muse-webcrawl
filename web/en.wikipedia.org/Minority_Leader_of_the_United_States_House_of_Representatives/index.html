Minority Leader of the United States House of Representatives

The Minority Leader of the United States House of Representatives is the floor leader of the minority party in the House and is the counterpart to the Speaker of the House. The minority leader is chosen by a vote of the party caucus (or conference) at the start of each Congress and serves as the party's principal strategist, spokesperson, and chief negotiator in the chamber.

Role and duties

The minority leader directs the party's legislative strategy on the floor, coordinates with the minority whip to count and marshal votes, negotiates with the Speaker and the Senate, and presents the party's alternative agenda to the public. By tradition, the minority leader is the party's presumptive candidate for Speaker should the party win a majority in the next election, and often does become Speaker when control of the House changes hands.

The position emerged in its modern form in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, as party organizations in the House formalized. Notable minority leaders have included James R. Mann, John Nance Garner, Sam Rayburn, Charles Halleck, Gerald Ford, Bob Michel, Dick Gephardt, Nancy Pelosi, John Boehner, and Kevin McCarthy. Hakeem Jeffries of New York became the Democratic minority leader in January 2023, the first African American to lead a party in either chamber of Congress.

Selection and succession

Members of the minority party elect their leader in a closed caucus vote following each general election. The leader, in turn, works with elected party officers—the whip, caucus chair, and campaign committee chair. If the minority party wins control of the House, the leader is normally nominated by the party as its candidate for Speaker.
