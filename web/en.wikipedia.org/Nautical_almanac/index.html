Nautical almanac

A nautical almanac is a publication describing the positions of celestial bodies and other data used by mariners for celestial navigation. It tabulates, for each hour of the year, the Greenwich Hour Angle and declination of the Sun, Moon, planets, and selected stars.

History

The first Nautical Almanac and Astronomical Ephemeris was published in Britain for 1767 under Nevil Maskelyne, providing lunar-distance tables that let seafarers determine longitude. The American Ephemeris and Nautical Almanac began in 1855. Since 1958 the British (HM Nautical Almanac Office) and U.S. (Naval Observatory) almanacs have been unified in content.

Contents and use

With a sextant measurement of a body's altitude and an accurate time, a navigator uses the almanac plus sight-reduction tables to compute a line of position. Modern editions also include sunrise/sunset and twilight times, moon phases, and correction tables. Although satellite navigation has displaced routine celestial navigation, almanacs are still published annually and celestial techniques remain a backup skill in naval training.
