The Boston Symphony Orchestra (BSO) is an American orchestra based in Boston, Massachusetts. It is the second-oldest of the five major American orchestras commonly known as the "Big Five". Founded in 1881 by Henry Lee Higginson, it performs most of its concerts at Boston's Symphony Hall and its summer season at Tanglewood in the Berkshires.

History

Higginson engaged Georg Henschel as first conductor and personally subsidised the orchestra for decades. Under Pierre Monteux, Serge Koussevitzky, Charles Munch, Erich Leinsdorf, William Steinberg, Seiji Ozawa (music director 1973–2002, the longest tenure) and James Levine, the BSO built a celebrated recording legacy. Koussevitzky founded the Tanglewood Music Center and commissioned many works, including Bartók's Concerto for Orchestra. The Boston Pops, drawing on BSO players, was made world-famous by Arthur Fiedler and later John Williams.

Today

Andris Nelsons became music director in 2014. The orchestra is noted for its sumptuous string sound and its recordings of Shostakovich symphonies under Nelsons, which won multiple Grammy Awards.
