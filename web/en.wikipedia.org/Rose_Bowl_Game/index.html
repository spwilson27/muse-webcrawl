Rose Bowl Game

The Rose Bowl Game is an annual American college football bowl game played at the Rose Bowl stadium in Pasadena, California, traditionally on January 1 (or January 2 when the 1st falls on a Sunday). Nicknamed "The Granddaddy of Them All", it is the oldest bowl game, first played in 1902 and annually since 1916.

History

The game grew out of the Tournament of Roses: a 1902 football match between Michigan and Stanford proved so one-sided that chariot races replaced football until 1916. The first game in the present stadium was played in 1923. For decades the game matched the champions of the Pac-12 and Big Ten conferences.

Modern era

The Rose Bowl joined the Bowl Championship Series in 1998 and the College Football Playoff rotation in 2014, hosting national semifinals every three years and occasionally the championship-deciding game of earlier eras, such as the celebrated 2006 USC–Texas game. The Keith Jackson Broadcast Center and the game's sunset over the San Gabriels are part of its lore. See also college football.
