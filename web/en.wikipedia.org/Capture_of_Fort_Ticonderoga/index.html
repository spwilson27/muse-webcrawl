The capture of Fort Ticonderoga occurred on 10 May 1775, early in the American Revolutionary War, when a small force of Green Mountain Boys led by Ethan Allen, joined by Benedict Arnold, surprised and seized the British-held fort on Lake Champlain at dawn. The garrison, caught asleep, surrendered without a shot.

Significance

It was the first offensive victory for American forces in the war. The fort's cannon were later hauled by Henry Knox overland to Boston in the winter of 1775–76, where their placement on Dorchester Heights forced the British evacuation of the city in March 1776.

Dispute over command

Allen and Arnold feuded over authority during the expedition, a quarrel that foreshadowed Arnold's later discontent. The small neighbouring fort at Crown Point was taken the next day.
