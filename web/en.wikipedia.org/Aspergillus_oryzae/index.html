Aspergillus oryzae

KingdomFungi
OrderEurotiales
Japanese nameKoji mould (麹菌, kōji-kin)
Domesticated fromAspergillus flavus lineage

Aspergillus oryzae is a filamentous fungus domesticated in East Asia for food fermentation. Known in Japan as koji mould, it is grown on steamed grains to produce the enzyme-rich starter used in making soy sauce, miso, sake, and other fermented foods.

Koji fermentation

When grown on rice, barley, or soybeans, A. oryzae secretes powerful amylases that convert starch into sugars and proteases that break down proteins, providing fermentable sugars for yeasts and developing the savoury flavours characteristic of koji-based products. Its use in sake brewing dates back well over a thousand years.

Domestication and safety

Genomic studies indicate A. oryzae was domesticated from an Aspergillus flavus-like ancestor under selection for fermentation performance; domesticated strains have lost the ability to produce aflatoxin. The fungus and its enzymes are widely regarded as safe and are also used industrially to manufacture digestive aids and commercial enzyme preparations.
