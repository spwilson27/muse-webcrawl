Farrier

OccupationTradesperson
Activity sectorsEquine care
TasksHoof trimming, horseshoeing, corrective shoeing

A farrier is a specialist in equine hoof care, including the trimming and balancing of horses' hooves and the placing of shoes on their hooves, if necessary. A farrier combines some blacksmith's skills (fabricating, adapting, and adjusting metal shoes) with some veterinarian's skills (knowledge of the anatomy and physiology of the lower limb) to care for horses' feet.

Work

Farriers trim hooves that grow continuously, remove and replace worn shoes typically every four to eight weeks, and assess lameness and hoof balance. Tools of the trade include rasps, nippers, hoof knives, hammers, and a forge or pre-made shoes. Corrective or therapeutic shoeing, prescribed with veterinarians, can address conditions such as laminitis and navicular syndrome.

History and training

The word derives from the Latin ferrarius, iron worker. Farriery was historically combined with blacksmithing; in the United Kingdom it is a regulated profession, with farriers required to register after completing an apprenticeship and examinations under the Farriers (Registration) Act. In many other countries certification is voluntary, offered by professional associations.
