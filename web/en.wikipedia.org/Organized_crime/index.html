Organized crime

Organized crime is a category of transnational, national, or local group of centralised enterprises run by criminals to engage in illegal activity, most commonly for profit. Organised criminal groups typically maintain continuing structures, use violence, intimidation, and corruption, and supply illegal goods and services such as drugs, gambling, loansharking, and trafficking.

Characteristics

Defining features include durability, hierarchy or network structure, restricted membership, use of violence or its threat, and infiltration of legitimate businesses and institutions. Money laundering converts illicit proceeds into apparently legitimate assets.

Notable groups

Well-known examples include the Sicilian Mafia (Cosa Nostra) and 'Ndrangheta in Italy, the American Mafia, Japanese yakuza, Chinese triads, Russian mafia groups, and Mexican and Colombian drug cartels.

Combating organised crime

Law enforcement responses include racketeering statutes such as the US RICO Act (1970), witness protection programmes, asset forfeiture, and international cooperation under the UN Convention against Transnational Organized Crime (2000).
