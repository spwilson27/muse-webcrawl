Riot grrrl is an underground feminist punk movement that began in the early 1990s in the United States, particularly in Olympia, Washington, and Washington, D.C. Combining punk's do-it-yourself ethos with third-wave feminism, it encouraged women to form bands, publish fanzines and organise their own chapters and conventions. The spelling "grrrl" — with a growling triple r — reclaimed the word "girl" as a term of anger and power.

Origins

The movement grew out of the Pacific Northwest indie scene around labels such as K Records and Kill Rock Stars. In the summer of 1991 the International Pop Underground Convention in Olympia featured a celebrated "Girl Night," and that August the first Riot Grrrl Convention took place in Washington, D.C. Fanzines such as Bikini Kill and Girl Germs circulated a widely reprinted manifesto calling for "girls" to make music and media free from sexism.

Music and themes

Bands including Bikini Kill (fronted by Kathleen Hanna), Bratmobile and Heavens to Betsy wrote short, raw songs about rape, domestic abuse, eating disorders, sexuality and female empowerment — topics largely absent from mainstream rock. Concerts often asked men to move to the back so women could occupy the front. Later Sleater-Kinney, formed from riot grrrl bands, carried the movement's sound to wider acclaim.

Media backlash and legacy

By 1992–93 intense, often sensationalised press coverage led many participants to declare a media blackout and step back from the label. The movement nonetheless reshaped punk culture, zine publishing and feminist activism, and is credited with opening space for later acts from Pussy Riot to contemporary queer and feminist punk scenes. Historians also note criticisms from the start that the movement centred white, middle-class perspectives.
