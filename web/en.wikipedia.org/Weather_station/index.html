Weather station
A facility with instruments for measuring atmospheric conditions, used for weather observation and forecasting.

A weather station is a facility, either on land or sea, with instruments and equipment for measuring atmospheric conditions to provide information for weather forecasts and to study the weather and climate. The measurements taken include temperature, atmospheric pressure, humidity, wind speed, wind direction, and precipitation amounts.

Instruments

Weather stations commonly use a thermometer for temperature, a barometer for pressure, a hygrometer for humidity, an anemometer for wind speed, a wind vane for wind direction, and a rain gauge for precipitation. Instruments are often housed in a Stevenson screen, a white louvered box that shields them from direct sunlight and precipitation while allowing air to circulate. More elaborate stations may include a pyranometer for solar radiation, a ceilometer for cloud height, and soil temperature and moisture probes.

Types

Stations range from staffed synoptic stations reporting at standardized times to automatic weather stations (AWS) that transmit data by radio or satellite. Personal weather stations, owned by hobbyists, contribute observations to citizen-science networks such as the Citizen Weather Observer Program. Marine observations come from weather buoys and volunteer observing ships. Data from the global network feed numerical weather prediction models through the World Meteorological Organization's Global Telecommunication System.
