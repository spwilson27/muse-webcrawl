Polyuria

Polyuria is the production of abnormally large volumes of dilute urine. In adult humans, it is generally defined as urine output exceeding 3 litres per day, compared with normal output of about 1 to 2 litres.

Causes

Polyuria may result from increased fluid intake (polydipsia), osmotic diuresis as in uncontrolled diabetes mellitus, or disorders of antidiuretic hormone (ADH) production or response, namely central and nephrogenic diabetes insipidus. Other causes include hypercalcaemia, hypokalaemia, certain medications such as diuretics and lithium, and recovery from urinary tract obstruction.

Diagnosis

Evaluation typically includes measurement of urine and serum osmolality, blood glucose, and electrolytes. A water deprivation test may be used to distinguish diabetes insipidus from primary polydipsia.

Treatment

Treatment is directed at the underlying cause: glycaemic control in diabetes mellitus, desmopressin for central diabetes insipidus, and correction of electrolyte disturbances.
