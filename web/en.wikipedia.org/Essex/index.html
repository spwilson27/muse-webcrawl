Essex

Essex is a ceremonial county in the East of England, bordering the North Sea to the east and Greater London to the south-west. Its county town is Chelmsford, which is also its only city. Other major settlements include Southend-on-Sea, Colchester, and Basildon.

History

The name derives from the Kingdom of the East Saxons, founded in the 6th century. Colchester claims to be Britain's oldest recorded town and was the first Roman capital. Essex was the home of the painter John Constable, whose landscapes of the Stour Valley earned the area the name "Constable Country."

Geography

The county is largely low-lying, with a long, indented coastline of estuaries and salt marshes, and includes the Dengie and Tendring peninsulas. Parts of the south-west are within London's commuter belt.
