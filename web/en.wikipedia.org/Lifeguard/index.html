Lifeguard

A lifeguard is a rescuer who supervises the safety and rescue of swimmers, surfers, and other water sports participants at swimming pools, beaches, rivers, and water parks. Lifeguards are trained in water rescue, first aid, and cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR), and are responsible for preventing accidents through surveillance and enforcing safety rules.

Duties

Primary duties include scanning assigned zones of water continuously, recognising swimmers in distress, performing rescues with equipment such as rescue tubes and boards, administering first aid and oxygen, and educating the public about hazards such as rip currents. Lifeguards also maintain facilities and test water quality at pools.

Certification

Certification typically requires strong swimming ability, timed swim and retrieval tests, and courses from organisations such as the American Red Cross, the Royal Life Saving Society, and Surf Life Saving clubs in Australia and New Zealand, where volunteer surf lifesavers patrol beaches.

History

Organised lifesaving developed in the late 19th and early 20th centuries with the growth of recreational swimming and surf bathing; surf lifesaving clubs in Australia and lifeguard services in the United States are among the best-known traditions.
