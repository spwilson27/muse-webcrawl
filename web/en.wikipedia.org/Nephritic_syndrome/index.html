Nephritic syndrome

Nephritic syndrome is a syndrome comprising signs of nephritis — inflammation of the kidneys — specifically of the glomeruli. It is characterised by haematuria (blood in the urine, often with dysmorphic red blood cells and red-cell casts), proteinuria (usually modest, below the nephrotic range), hypertension, reduced urine output (oliguria), and a fall in glomerular filtration rate that may progress to acute kidney injury. Mild oedema, especially periorbital, may also occur.

Causes

Nephritic syndrome results from glomerular inflammation damaging the filtration barrier. Causes include post-streptococcal (post-infectious) glomerulonephritis, the classic cause in children following a throat or skin infection; IgA nephropathy (Berger disease), the most common primary glomerulonephritis worldwide; rapidly progressive (crescentic) glomerulonephritis, including anti-GBM disease and ANCA-associated vasculitis; lupus nephritis; and membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis.

Diagnosis and management

Evaluation includes urinalysis and microscopy, quantification of proteinuria, renal function tests, complement levels (C3/C4), serology for autoimmune and infectious causes, and often a renal biopsy, which establishes the specific glomerular disease. Treatment targets the underlying cause — for example, immunosuppression for rapidly progressive disease — together with salt restriction, antihypertensive therapy (often ACE inhibitors), diuretics for fluid overload, and supportive care; severe cases may require dialysis.

Contrast with nephrotic syndrome

Nephritic syndrome is conventionally contrasted with nephrotic syndrome, in which heavy proteinuria (>3.5 g/day), hypoalbuminaemia, marked oedema, and hyperlipidaemia predominate, with little or no haematuria or hypertension. Many glomerular diseases can present with features of both.
