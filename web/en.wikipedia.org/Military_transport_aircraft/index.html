Military transport aircraft

Military transport aircraft are fixed-wing and rotary-wing aircraft used by armed forces to airlift troops, weapons and supplies. They range from strategic intercontinental airlifters such as the Lockheed C-5 Galaxy and Boeing C-17 Globemaster III to tactical transports like the Lockheed C-130 Hercules and Airbus A400M Atlas that can operate from short, unpaved strips, down to light transports and helicopters.

Design features

Transports typically have high wings, rear loading ramps, reinforced floors with roller systems, and (in tactical types) multi-wheel low-pressure landing gear for rough fields. Many can airdrop paratroops and palletised cargo, perform aeromedical evacuation, and refuel in flight. Some airlifters use turboprops for field performance; strategic types use turbofans for speed and range.

History

Military transport aviation matured in the Second World War with the Douglas C-47 Skytrain and glider operations. The Berlin Airlift (1948–49) demonstrated strategic airlift as a political instrument; the C-130 (1954) set the tactical template still in use; and jet airlifters from the C-141 Starlifter to the C-17 made rapid global deployment routine. The Antonov An-124 and the unique six-engine An-225 (destroyed in Ukraine in 2022) were the largest production and one-off airlifters respectively.

Roles

Beyond logistics, transport airframes serve as tankers, airborne early warning platforms, gunships, maritime patrol aircraft and VIP transports, and underpin humanitarian disaster-relief operations worldwide.
