Alberta is one of the thirteen provinces and territories of Canada. Located in Western Canada, it borders British Columbia to the west, Saskatchewan to the east, the Northwest Territories to the north, and the US state of Montana to the south. It is Canada's fourth-most populous province and the fourth largest by area. Its capital is Edmonton, while Calgary is its largest city.

Geography

Alberta's landscape ranges from the peaks of the Canadian Rockies in the southwest — protected in Banff and Jasper national parks, among the world's most visited mountain parks — through foothills and aspen parkland to vast northern boreal forest and the dry prairies of the south. The province contains most of Canada's oil reserves, centred on the Athabasca oil sands around Fort McMurray, one of the largest petroleum deposits on Earth.

History and economy

Indigenous peoples have inhabited the region for at least 10,000 years. European fur traders arrived in the 18th century, and the territory was governed by the Hudson's Bay Company before becoming part of Canada in 1870. Alberta became a province in 1905, named for Princess Louise Caroline Alberta, daughter of Queen Victoria. The discovery of oil at Leduc in 1947 transformed the provincial economy; today energy, agriculture, and technology drive a prosperous but resource-dependent economy, and Albertans enjoy among the highest incomes in Canada. The province is also famous for the Calgary Stampede, the annual rodeo billed as "The Greatest Outdoor Show on Earth".
