Rhythmic gymnastics

Rhythmic gymnastics is a sport in which gymnasts perform on a floor with an apparatus: hoop, ball, clubs, ribbon and rope. The sport combines elements of gymnastics, dance and calisthenics; gymnasts must be strong, flexible, agile, dexterous and coordinated.

History

Rhythmic gymnastics grew out of a number of fitness and expressive movement systems developed in the 19th and early 20th centuries. The Soviet Union developed it as a competitive sport after the Second World War. The first World Championships were held in Budapest in 1963, and the sport was added to the Olympic programme at Los Angeles 1984, with group competition following at Atlanta 1996.

Competition

Individual gymnasts perform four routines, each with a different apparatus, to music lasting 75 to 90 seconds. Groups of five gymnasts perform two routines, one with a single apparatus type and one with two types. Scores combine difficulty (body difficulty and apparatus difficulty), artistry and execution, under a Code of Points revised every Olympic cycle.

Apparatus

  • Rope: made of hemp or synthetic material; used for leaps, throws and swings.
  • Hoop: plastic or wood, 80–90 cm in diameter.
  • Ball: rubber or synthetic, 18–20 cm in diameter.
  • Clubs: two bottle-shaped clubs, usually 40–50 cm long.
  • Ribbon: a satin ribbon about 6 m long attached to a stick.
