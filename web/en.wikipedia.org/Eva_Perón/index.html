María Eva Duarte de Perón (7 May 1919 – 26 July 1952), better known as Eva Perón or Evita, was the First Lady of Argentina from 1946 until her death in 1952, as the wife of President Juan Perón. She became one of the most influential figures in Argentine history.

Early life and career

Born into poverty in the town of Los Toldos, Eva Duarte moved to Buenos Aires at the age of fifteen to pursue a career in acting, working in radio and film. She met Colonel Juan Perón at a charity event in 1944 and married him the following year.

First Lady

As First Lady, Perón campaigned for women's suffrage, which was enacted in Argentina in 1947, and she founded and ran the Eva Perón Foundation, which distributed aid, built hospitals, schools and housing for the poor. She was adored by the working-class descamisados ("shirtless ones") and fiercely opposed by the military and the elite. In 1951 a mass movement sought to make her vice-presidential candidate, but she declined amid pressure from the military and her failing health.

Death and legacy

She died of cervical cancer on 26 July 1952, at the age of 33. Her embalmed body became the focus of political struggles after the 1955 coup that overthrew Juan Perón. Her life inspired the musical Evita by Andrew Lloyd Webber and Tim Rice.
