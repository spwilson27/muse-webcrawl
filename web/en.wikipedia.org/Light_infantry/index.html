Light infantry
Riflemen of the 95th Rifles, archetypal light infantry

Light infantry refers to soldiers whose role is to fight in loose order as skirmishers ahead of the main line of battle, emphasising speed, mobility, marksmanship and initiative rather than the close-order volley fire of line infantry. Historically lightly equipped — often lacking heavy packs and sometimes armed with rifles rather than smoothbore muskets — light infantry screened the army, harassed the enemy, and fought in broken terrain where formed bodies could not operate.

History

Light troops existed in antiquity, such as the Greek peltasts and Roman velites. In the 18th century, irregular frontier warfare in North America and the use of Austrian and Hungarian pandours and Croatian troops encouraged European armies to raise permanent light units: French chasseurs, Prussian and Austrian Jäger, and British light companies in every battalion. The rifle-armed 95th Rifles (later the Rifle Brigade) of the Napoleonic Wars became the archetype, fighting in open order in distinctive green uniforms.

Modern usage

Today "light infantry" generally denotes infantry units without heavy armoured vehicles, designed for rapid deployment by air and for operations in mountains, jungle, urban areas or other difficult terrain. Examples include airborne, air assault, mountain and ranger formations, and the United States Army's light infantry divisions. Such units trade firepower and protection for strategic and tactical mobility.
