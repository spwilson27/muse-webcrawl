Kamakura period

Dates: 1185–1333

Government: Kamakura shogunate

Founded by: Minamoto no Yoritomo

Followed by: Kenmu Restoration / Muromachi period

The Kamakura period (鎌倉時代, 1185–1333) is a period of Japanese history that marks the governance of the Kamakura shogunate, officially established in 1192 in Kamakura by the first shogun, Minamoto no Yoritomo, after his victory in the Genpei War. Though an imperial court continued in Kyoto, real power lay with the warrior government (bakufu) — the first of three shogunates that ruled Japan until 1868.

Politics

After Yoritomo's death in 1199, power passed to the Hōjō clan, who governed as regents (shikken) through puppet shoguns. The shogunate appointed military governors (shugo) and stewards (jitō) across the provinces and issued the Jōei Code (1232), the first warrior law code. The period's greatest external crisis was the Mongol invasions of 1274 and 1281, both defeated with the help of storms remembered as kamikaze ("divine winds"); the cost of defence, with no conquered land to reward warriors, weakened the regime.

Culture and religion

Kamakura saw the flourishing of new Buddhist schools aimed at ordinary people — Pure Land (Hōnen, Shinran), Nichiren Buddhism, and Zen, transmitted from China and patronised by the warrior class. Bushidō values began to form, and war tales such as the Tale of the Heike were compiled. The period ended in 1333 when Ashikaga Takauji and Nitta Yoshisada destroyed the shogunate in support of Emperor Go-Daigo's Kenmu Restoration.
