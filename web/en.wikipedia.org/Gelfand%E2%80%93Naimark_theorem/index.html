Gelfand–Naimark theorem

The Gelfand–Naimark theorem, proved by Israel Gelfand and Mark Naimark in 1943, is a foundational result in functional analysis describing the structure of C*-algebras. It states that every commutative C*-algebra is isometrically *-isomorphic to the algebra of continuous functions vanishing at infinity on some locally compact Hausdorff space.

Statement

For a commutative unital C*-algebra A, the Gelfand representation maps A onto C(X), the continuous complex-valued functions on its spectrum X (the space of characters, or maximal ideals, with the weak-* topology), where X is compact Hausdorff. In the non-unital case, A is isomorphic to the functions vanishing at infinity on a locally compact space.

Significance

The theorem reduces the study of commutative C*-algebras to topology: geometric properties of the space X correspond to algebraic properties of the algebra. It is the basis of Gelfand duality between compact Hausdorff spaces and commutative unital C*-algebras, which inspired the philosophy of noncommutative geometry — treating general (noncommutative) C*-algebras as "noncommutative topological spaces." A related result, the Gelfand–Naimark–Segal (GNS) construction, shows every C*-algebra can be represented as operators on a Hilbert space.
