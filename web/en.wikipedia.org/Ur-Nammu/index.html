Ur-Nammu (or Ur-Namma, Ur-Engur, Ur-Gur, Sumerian: 𒌨𒀭𒇉, rendered Ur-Namma) was a Sumerian king who founded the Third Dynasty of Ur, reigning approximately 2112–2095 BC (middle chronology). Having served as governor of Ur under Utu-hengal of Uruk, who had defeated the Gutians, Ur-Nammu seized power and reunified Sumer and Akkad, beginning the period known as the Neo-Sumerian Empire or Sumerian Renaissance.

Reign

Ur-Nammu proclaimed himself "King of Sumer and Akkad" and extended his control over the city-states of southern Mesopotamia and into Elam. He undertook extensive building works across his kingdom, repairing temples and walls, digging irrigation canals, and standardising weights, measures and administration. His most famous construction is the Great Ziggurat of Ur, dedicated to the moon god Nanna, which still stands in partly reconstructed form; he also built ziggurats at Eridu, Uruk and Nippur.

Code of Ur-Nammu

Ur-Nammu is credited with the Code of Ur-Nammu, the oldest known surviving law code, predating the Code of Hammurabi by roughly three centuries. Written in Sumerian on clay tablets, its surviving provisions impose mainly monetary fines for offences such as bodily injury, in contrast to the "eye for an eye" punishments of later codes, though it prescribes death for crimes such as murder and robbery. Its prologue presents the king as the establisher of justice and equity in the land.

Death

According to the literary composition known as the Death of Ur-Nammu, the king died in battle against the Gutians, abandoned by his troops, and descended to the underworld, where he presented gifts to its deities. He was succeeded by his son Shulgi, under whom the empire reached its greatest extent and administrative sophistication.
