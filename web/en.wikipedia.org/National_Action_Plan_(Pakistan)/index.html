National Action Plan (Pakistan)

The National Action Plan (NAP) is a twenty-point counter-terrorism programme adopted by the Government of Pakistan in January 2015, in the wake of the December 2014 Army Public School massacre in Peshawar, in which Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan gunmen killed around 150 people, mostly schoolchildren.

Key provisions

The plan combined military and civilian measures: establishment of military courts to try terrorism suspects (enabled by the 21st Amendment to the Constitution), lifting of the moratorium on the death penalty for terrorism cases, a crackdown on terrorist financing and proscribed organisations, registration and regulation of madrassas, action against hate speech and extremist literature, protection of minorities, and reforms of the criminal justice system. It also endorsed ongoing military operations such as Zarb-e-Azb in North Waziristan.

Implementation and criticism

An apex committee structure oversaw implementation at federal and provincial levels. Thousands of executions followed the lifting of the moratorium, and military courts convicted hundreds of militants. Critics, including human rights organisations, raised concerns about due process in military trials, while analysts noted uneven progress on civilian provisions such as madrassa reform, choking terror finance, and prosecuting banned groups, which remained recurring points of debate in Pakistani politics.
