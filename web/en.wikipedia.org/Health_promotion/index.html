Health promotion

Health promotion is "the process of enabling people to increase control over, and to improve, their health", as defined by the World Health Organization (WHO) in the Ottawa Charter, adopted at the first International Conference on Health Promotion in Ottawa, Canada, in 1986. It moves beyond a focus on individual behaviour toward a wide range of social and environmental interventions, and is a core function of public health.

Ottawa Charter

The Ottawa Charter identified three basic strategies for health promotion: advocacy for health, enabling people to achieve equity in health, and mediating between different interests in society. It set out five action areas: building healthy public policy, creating supportive environments, strengthening community action, developing personal skills, and reorienting health services toward prevention and health promotion rather than treatment alone.

Approaches

Health promotion works through health education, social marketing, policy change, and community development. Settings-based approaches promote health in schools, workplaces, hospitals, and cities — for example through the WHO Healthy Cities movement. Common programme areas include tobacco control, healthy diet and physical activity, sexual health, mental health promotion, and injury prevention.

Relation to disease prevention

Health promotion overlaps with, but is broader than, disease prevention. Prevention targets specific diseases through measures such as immunisation and screening, whereas health promotion addresses the social determinants of health — income, education, housing, and environment — that shape health outcomes across whole populations.
