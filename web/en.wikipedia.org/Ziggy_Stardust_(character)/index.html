Ziggy Stardust (character)

David Bowie in character as Ziggy Stardust

Ziggy Stardust is a fictional character created by English musician David Bowie for his 1972 concept album The Rise and Fall of Ziggy Stardust and the Spiders from Mars. The character is an androgynous, bisexual rock star who acts as a messenger for extraterrestrial beings shortly before an impending apocalyptic disaster. Ziggy Stardust was Bowie's best-known alter ego and a defining figure of the glam rock era.

Conception

Bowie assembled the persona from several influences: the British rocker Vince Taylor, who had developed messianic delusions; the "Legendary Stardust Cowboy", an American outsider musician; Japanese kabuki theatre and fashion designer Kansai Yamamoto, who supplied the striking costumes; and the model of the alienated rock star consumed by fame and excess. The name combined "Ziggy" (from a tailor's shop sign) with "Stardust".

The story and the tour

On the album, Ziggy fronts the band the Spiders from Mars, brings a message of hope to a dying Earth, becomes a fame-corrupted "leper messiah", and is ultimately torn apart by his fans ("Rock 'n' Roll Suicide"). Bowie toured in character through 1972–73, then dramatically "retired" Ziggy on stage at London's Hammersmith Odeon on 3 July 1973, announcing it as "the last show that we'll ever do" — a retirement of the persona, not of Bowie himself.

Legacy

Ziggy Stardust became an icon of 1970s popular culture, influencing glam, punk, and later pop performers, and the character's imagery — red mullet, lightning-bolt makeup (associated with the follow-up Aladdin Sane), and flamboyant costumes — remains among the most recognisable in rock music.
