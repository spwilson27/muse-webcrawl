Sayfo

The Sayfo (Syriac for "sword"), also known as the Assyrian genocide, was the mass killing of Assyrian Christians in the Ottoman Empire and Qajar Persia during the First World War, principally in 1915. It took place alongside the Armenian genocide and the Greek genocide, with the Christian populations of the region targeted as suspected collaborators with the Entente powers.

Assyrian refugees fleeing the massacres

Events

Beginning in late 1914, Ottoman forces and irregular units attacked Assyrian villages in Hakkari and northwestern Persia. Massacres, forced marches, starvation and disease killed a large share of the population: estimates commonly range from about 150,000 to 300,000 deaths, from a pre-war population of perhaps 500,000–600,000. Survivors fled to the British-controlled Baqubah refugee camp in Iraq and later formed diaspora communities in Iraq, Syria, and eventually Europe, North America and Australia.

Recognition

Assyrian organisations campaign for international recognition of the Sayfo as genocide. Several national parliaments and US state legislatures have passed recognition resolutions, and memorials have been erected in cities including Sydney, Paris and Chicago. The events are commemorated annually on 7 August, Assyrian Martyrs Day.
