Librettist

A librettist is a person who writes the libretto — the text of an opera, operetta, oratorio, cantata, musical or ballet — including all dialogue, lyrics and stage directions. The librettist's work provides the dramatic structure and words that the composer sets to music.

Role and collaboration

Librettists have often worked in sustained partnership with particular composers: Pietro Metastasio's libretti were set by dozens of Baroque composers; Arrigo Boito wrote for Verdi; Hugo von Hofmannsthal for Richard Strauss; and W. H. Auden and Chester Kallman for Stravinsky and Henze. In musical theatre, the corresponding role is divided between the book writer and the lyricist, as in the partnership of Oscar Hammerstein II with Richard Rodgers.

History

Early libretti were often adaptations of plays or myths; by the 18th century, figures like Metastasio set conventions of opera seria. Though frequently overshadowed by composers, celebrated librettists such as Lorenzo Da Ponte, author of Mozart's Le nozze di Figaro, Don Giovanni and Così fan tutte, achieved lasting fame.
