Algebraically closed field

Algebraically closed field
The complex numbers form the prototypical algebraically closed field
DefinitionA field in which every non-constant polynomial in one variable has a root
Main exampleComplex numbers ℂ
Key theoremFundamental theorem of algebra

In mathematics, a field F is algebraically closed if every non-constant polynomial in one variable with coefficients in F has a root in F. Equivalently, every such polynomial splits into a product of linear factors over F, and F has no proper algebraic extension.

Examples

The field of complex numbers ℂ is algebraically closed: this is the content of the fundamental theorem of algebra, first rigorously proved by Gauss. By contrast, the real numbers are not algebraically closed — the polynomial x² + 1 has no real root — and neither are the rational numbers, since x² − 2 has no rational root. No finite field is algebraically closed. The field of algebraic numbers (roots of rational polynomials) is an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero smaller than ℂ, and the algebraic closure of the p-adic numbers gives examples in characteristic zero arising from finite fields' closures in positive characteristic.

Algebraic closure

Every field has an algebraic closure — an algebraically closed algebraic extension — unique up to isomorphism; its existence is proved using Zorn's lemma. The algebraic closure of the reals is ℂ, obtained by adjoining a root of x² + 1. Steinitz's theorem states that algebraically closed fields are classified by their characteristic and transcendence degree over their prime field.

Equivalent statements

For a field F, the following are equivalent: F is algebraically closed; every non-constant polynomial over F has a root in F; every non-constant polynomial splits into linear factors; every matrix over F has an eigenvalue in F; and F admits no finite extension of degree greater than 1.
