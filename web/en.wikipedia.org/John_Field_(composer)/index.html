John Field (composer)

Born26 July 1782, Dublin, Ireland
Died23 January 1837, Moscow, Russia
OccupationComposer, pianist
Known forInventing the nocturne

John Field (26 July 1782 – 23 January 1837) was an Irish composer and pianist, best known as the inventor of the nocturne, a short lyrical piano piece that strongly influenced Frédéric Chopin.

Life

Born in Dublin into a musical family, Field studied in London with Muzio Clementi, whose piano firm he represented, and performed as a boy in London concerts praised by Haydn. In 1802 he accompanied Clementi on a sales tour to Paris, Vienna and St Petersburg, where he settled in 1803. He became the most sought-after piano teacher in St Petersburg and Moscow, taught Mikhail Glinka, and toured Europe as a virtuoso. His health declined in the 1830s; after treatment in Naples he returned to Moscow, where he died in 1837.

Music

Field published his first nocturnes in 1812–1814; he wrote 18 in all, characterised by a singing, bel canto melody over arpeggiated accompaniment — a texture Chopin transformed in his own nocturnes. Field also composed seven piano concertos, sonatas, a quintet, divertissements and many shorter pieces, and was admired by contemporaries including Liszt, who edited his nocturnes and wrote an admiring preface. His melodic style influenced early Romantic piano music in Russia as well as Western Europe.
