Imperfect

The imperfect presents a past situation as ongoing or habitual rather than completed.
CategoryGrammatical tense–aspect
AspectImperfective, past
Contrasts withPreterite, aorist, perfect
Found inRomance languages, Slavic languages, Ancient Greek, Latin, Sanskrit

The imperfect (abbreviated IMPF) is a verb form that combines past tense (reference to a past time) with imperfective aspect (reference to a continuing or repeated event or state). It generally indicates a continuing, ongoing, habitual or background action in the past — "I was walking", "he used to read every night" — in contrast to a completed past event.

Meaning and use

Across languages, the imperfect typically serves several related functions:

  • Progressive past: an action in progress at a past reference point — Spanish Estaba lloviendo ("It was raining").
  • Habitual past: repeated or customary action — French Nous allions à la plage chaque été ("We went to the beach every summer").
  • Background description: setting the scene in narration, against which completed (preterite or aorist) events stand out.
  • States: past states of being, knowledge or feeling, which have no natural endpoint.

In particular languages

Romance languages

The Romance imperfect descends from the Latin imperfect indicative, formed with the infix -ba- (e.g. amābat, "he/she was loving"). Spanish (imperfecto), French (imparfait), Italian (imperfetto) and Portuguese (pretérito imperfeito) all retain a dedicated imperfect tense, used in constant interplay with the preterite: the imperfect supplies background and habit, the preterite advances the storyline with completed events.

Slavic languages

Most Slavic languages express the imperfective past through aspectual verb pairs rather than a separate tense; Old Church Slavonic and modern Bulgarian and Macedonian, however, preserve a true imperfect tense alongside the aorist.

Ancient Greek and Latin

Ancient Greek marked the imperfect with an augment and secondary endings on the present stem, contrasting with the aorist stem for punctual action. Latin's imperfect likewise formed one member of its elaborate system of past tenses, alongside the perfect and pluperfect.

Terminology

The name comes from Latin imperfectus, "unfinished, incomplete", reflecting the form's portrayal of an action as not brought to completion. It should not be confused with the perfect, which views a past event as complete with present relevance, nor with the broader category of imperfective aspect, which is not restricted to past time.
