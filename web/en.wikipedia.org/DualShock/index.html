DualShock

The DualShock is a line of gamepads developed by Sony for the PlayStation family of consoles. Introduced in 1997 for the original PlayStation, it took its name from its two ("dual") vibration motors, which provide force feedback ("shock"), and its layout — two analog sticks flanking a directional pad — became an industry template.

Manufacturer: Sony
Introduced: 1997
Successor: DualSense

History

The original DualShock refined Sony's earlier Dual Analog Controller, adding rumble in both grips and clickable sticks (L3/R3). The DualShock 2 for the PlayStation 2 made nearly all buttons pressure-sensitive. The DualShock 3 for the PlayStation 3 restored vibration after an initial Sixaxis model without it and added Bluetooth wireless and motion sensing. The DualShock 4 for the PlayStation 4 added a touchpad, a light bar, a Share button and a headphone jack, and adopted a USB-charged internal battery.

Legacy

The line was superseded by the DualSense for the PlayStation 5 in 2020. The DualShock's basic arrangement of sticks, buttons and triggers influenced controllers across the industry for more than two decades.
