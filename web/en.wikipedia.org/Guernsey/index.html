Guernsey

Guernsey is an island in the English Channel off the coast of Normandy and the largest island of the Bailiwick of Guernsey, a self-governing British Crown Dependency. Its capital is St Peter Port. Together with Jersey, it forms part of the Channel Islands.

Geography

Guernsey has an area of about 65 square kilometres and a population of roughly 64,000. The bailiwick also includes the islands of Alderney, Sark, Herm, Jethou and Lihou. The island's terrain rises from low-lying northern coasts to cliffs in the south.

History

The Channel Islands were part of the Duchy of Normandy and remained attached to the English Crown after Normandy was lost to France in 1204. Guernsey remained loyal to the English crown during the English Civil War while Jersey supported the Royalists. During the Second World War the islands were occupied by German forces from 1940 to 1945, the only part of the British Isles to be occupied; many island children were evacuated to England, and German fortifications still dot the coast.

Government and economy

The bailiwick is not part of the United Kingdom but a possession of the Crown, represented by a Lieutenant Governor. Its legislature is the States of Guernsey. The economy is dominated by financial services, which account for a large share of income, alongside tourism and horticulture, historically the growing of tomatoes and flowers. Guernsey issues its own pound sterling banknotes and coins. The island's traditional language, Guernésiais, a Norman dialect, is now spoken by only a small minority.
