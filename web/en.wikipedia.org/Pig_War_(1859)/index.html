Pig War (1859)

Date
1859 (crisis); dispute resolved 1872
Location
San Juan Island, Pacific Northwest
Parties
United States; British Columbia (United Kingdom)
Casualties
One pig

The Pig War was a bloodless confrontation in 1859 between the United States and the British Empire over the boundary between the US and British North America in the San Juan Islands of the Pacific Northwest. It is called the Pig War because it was triggered by the shooting of a pig, the conflict's only casualty.

Incident

On 15 June 1859, American settler Lyman Cutlar shot a pig belonging to Irishman Charles Griffin of the Hudson's Bay Company after it repeatedly rooted in his potato garden. When British authorities threatened arrest, American settlers appealed for military protection. Brigadier General William S. Harney sent troops under Captain George Pickett to San Juan Island; the British responded with warships, and both sides escalated to over a thousand troops and several vessels — without firing a shot.

Resolution

President James Buchanan dispatched Winfield Scott to negotiate; the two nations agreed to a joint military occupation of the island that lasted 12 years. In 1872, arbitration by German Emperor Wilhelm I awarded the islands to the United States, fixing the boundary through Haro Strait. The episode is commemorated at San Juan Island National Historical Park.
