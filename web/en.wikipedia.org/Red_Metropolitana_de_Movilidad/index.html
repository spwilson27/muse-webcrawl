Red Metropolitana de Movilidad

Red Metropolitana de Movilidad (Metropolitan Mobility Network), known simply as RED, is the public transport system of Santiago, Chile. It integrates the city's bus network with the Santiago Metro and the Metrotren Nos commuter rail service under a single fare system paid with the bip! smartcard. Created as Transantiago in 2007, it was rebranded under the RED name in 2019.

History

Transantiago launched on 10 February 2007, replacing Santiago's fragmented system of privately operated "micros amarillas" with a trunk-and-feeder network of concessioned operators, GPS-controlled services and integrated fares. The launch was widely criticised as chaotic: too few buses, confusing routes, overcrowding and long waits led to a political crisis for the government of Michelle Bachelet, and the system required years of adjustments, subsidy increases and fleet expansion.

Operation

Under RED, bus services are provided by private concessionaires under contracts supervised by the Directorio de Transporte Público Metropolitano (DTPM). The system has progressively renewed its fleet, including large numbers of electric buses — Santiago operates one of the largest electric bus fleets outside China — and low-floor, air-conditioned vehicles meeting the "RED estándar" specification, painted in the system's red-and-white livery. Dedicated bus corridors and priority lanes structure the trunk network, feeding Metro lines that form the system's backbone.
