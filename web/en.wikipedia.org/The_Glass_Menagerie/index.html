The Glass Menagerie

The Glass Menagerie is a memory play by Tennessee Williams that premiered in Chicago in 1944 and catapulted Williams from obscurity to fame. The play has strong autobiographical elements, featuring characters based on its author, his histrionic mother, and his mentally fragile sister, Rose. In 2007, it was ranked among the greatest plays of the 20th century.

Characters

  • Tom Wingfield – the play's narrator and protagonist, an aspiring poet who works in a shoe warehouse to support his family.
  • Amanda Wingfield – Tom and Laura's mother, a faded Southern belle clinging to memories of her youth and seventeen gentleman callers.
  • Laura Wingfield – Tom's older sister, painfully shy and slightly disabled, who collects small glass animals and has dropped out of business school.
  • Jim O'Connor – an acquaintance of Tom's from the warehouse, invited to dinner as a prospective "gentleman caller" for Laura.

Plot

The play is presented as Tom's memories of his mother and sister in St. Louis during the Great Depression. Amanda, abandoned by her husband, worries obsessively about Laura's future and persuades Tom to bring home a suitor. Jim, the gentleman caller, is kind to Laura and dances with her, accidentally breaking the horn of her favorite glass unicorn, but reveals he is already engaged. After the disastrous evening, Tom leaves home to join the merchant marine, yet remains haunted by his memory of Laura, whom he asks, in the closing monologue, to blow out her candles.

Themes and style

Williams called the work a "memory play" and employed unconventional staging: a narrator who steps in and out of the action, projected images and titles, and music to evoke the fragility of recollection. Major themes include memory, escape, abandonment, and the conflict between duty and desire.

Productions and adaptations

After its Chicago premiere, the play moved to Broadway in 1945, winning the New York Drama Critics' Circle Award. It has been revived many times, notably in 2013 with Cherry Jones and Zachary Quinto and in 2017 with Sally Field. Film adaptations include versions in 1950, 1973 (television), and 1987, directed by Paul Newman.
