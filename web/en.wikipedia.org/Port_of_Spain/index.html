Port of Spain, officially the City of Port of Spain, is the capital of Trinidad and Tobago and the country's third-largest municipality after San Fernando and Chaguanas. It is located on the Gulf of Paria, on the northwest coast of the island of Trinidad, and is part of a larger metropolitan urban area with a population of several hundred thousand.

History

The site was originally an Amerindian fishing village called Cumucurapo. The Spanish founded a settlement nearby in the 16th century, and the town grew after 1757, when the island's capital was moved from San José de Oruña (modern St. Joseph). Port of Spain became the capital of the British colony after Britain captured Trinidad in 1797, and grew with land reclamation that created its modern waterfront. It served as the capital of the short-lived West Indies Federation from 1958 to 1962, and became the capital of independent Trinidad and Tobago in 1962.

Geography and culture

The city rises from reclaimed land and mangrove flats into the foothills of the Northern Range. Its landmarks include the Queen's Park Savannah, a large open park bordered by the "Magnificent Seven" mansions, Woodford Square, the Red House (the seat of Parliament), the National Academy for the Performing Arts, and the twin Financial Towers of its modern skyline. Port of Spain is a centre of the Trinidad Carnival and of calypso and steelpan music, and hosts many of the country's government ministries, courts, and the headquarters of regional institutions.
