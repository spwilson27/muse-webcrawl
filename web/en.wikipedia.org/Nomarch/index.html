Nomarch

Nomarch (Greek: νομάρχης) was the title of the governor of a nome (province) in ancient Egypt, and was later reused for provincial officials in Greece in the 19th and 20th centuries.

Ancient Egypt

Egypt was divided from an early period into nomes — eventually about 22 in Upper Egypt and 20 in Lower Egypt. Their governors, the nomarchs, administered justice, taxation, irrigation, and local cults. In the Old Kingdom the office became increasingly hereditary, and powerful nomarch families contributed to the decentralisation of the First Intermediate Period. Middle Kingdom kings, especially Senusret III, curbed their power, but nomarchs remained important regional officials. Their elaborate rock tombs at sites such as Beni Hasan, Meir, and Asyut are key sources for provincial life.
