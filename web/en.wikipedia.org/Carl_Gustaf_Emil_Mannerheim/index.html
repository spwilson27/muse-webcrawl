Carl Gustaf Emil Mannerheim (4 June 1867 – 27 January 1951) was a Finnish military leader and statesman. He served as the military leader of the Whites in the Finnish Civil War of 1918, as Regent of Finland (1918–1919), as commander-in-chief of the Finnish Defence Forces during the Second World War, and as the sixth president of Finland (1944–1946). He is widely regarded as Finland's greatest national figure.

Marshal Mannerheim, commander-in-chief of Finnish forces during the Second World War.

Early life and Imperial Russian service

Mannerheim was born into a Swedish-speaking noble family at Louhisaari Manor in Askainen. After being expelled from the Hamina Cadet School, he joined the Imperial Russian Army in 1887, served in the Chevalier Guard in St Petersburg, and fought in the Russo-Japanese War. In 1906–1908 he undertook a famous intelligence-gathering expedition across Central Asia to China, travelling some 14,000 km, which also produced important ethnographic collections.

Finnish Civil War and interwar years

Returning to newly independent Finland in late 1917, Mannerheim commanded the White forces to victory in the Civil War of 1918. He served briefly as Regent before losing the 1919 presidential election. During the interwar decades he chaired the Defence Council and oversaw the fortification line across the Karelian Isthmus later named the Mannerheim Line.

Second World War and presidency

As commander-in-chief, Mannerheim led Finland through the Winter War (1939–40) and the Continuation War (1941–44). In June 1942 he was given the unique title Marshal of Finland. He became president in August 1944 to steer Finland out of the war, signing the Moscow Armistice with the Soviet Union, and resigned in 1946 on grounds of health.

Legacy

Mannerheim died in Lausanne on 27 January 1951 and was buried with full military honours in Helsinki's Hietaniemi Cemetery. In a 2004 Finnish television vote he was chosen as the greatest Finn of all time. His birthday, 4 June, is celebrated as the Flag Day of the Finnish Defence Forces.
