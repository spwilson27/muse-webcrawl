Pomacentridae

Pomacentridae is a family of ray-finned fishes, comprising the clownfishes and damselfishes. The family includes about 430 species in some 29 genera. They are primarily marine, while a few species inhabit freshwater and brackish environments. They are found mainly in tropical and warm temperate waters, especially on coral reefs of the Indo-Pacific and Atlantic.

Characteristics

Pomacentrids are small, deep-bodied, laterally compressed fishes, typically less than 15 cm long, and many are brilliantly coloured, which makes them popular in the marine aquarium trade. They generally have a single nostril on each side of the head and an interrupted lateral line. Many species are strongly territorial: damselfishes of the genus Stegastes cultivate and defend gardens of filamentous algae, and will aggressively chase much larger fishes and even divers from their patches.

Reproduction and behaviour

Males typically prepare a nest site on rock or rubble and guard the demersal eggs until they hatch. Clownfishes (subfamily Amphiprioninae) live in a famous mutualism with sea anemones, whose stings they tolerate through a protective mucus layer; in return they defend the anemone and supply it with nutrients. Clownfish are sequential hermaphrodites: the largest individual of a group is female and the second-largest male, and if the female dies the male changes sex.

Genera

Major genera include Abudefduf (sergeant majors), Amphiprion (clownfishes), Chromis (chromis), Dascyllus (humbugs), Pomacentrus and Stegastes.
