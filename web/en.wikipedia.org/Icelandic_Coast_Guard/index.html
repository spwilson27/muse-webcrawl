The Icelandic Coast Guard (Icelandic: Landhelgisgæsla Íslands) is Iceland's maritime law-enforcement and defence service. As Iceland maintains no standing army, the Coast Guard is the country's principal armed service, responsible for fisheries protection, search and rescue, maritime surveillance, hydrographic surveying, and explosive ordnance disposal.

History

The service traces its origins to the 1920s, when Iceland, under Danish sovereignty, began operating its own patrol vessels; it was formally established in 1926. Its best-known episodes are the Cod Wars with the United Kingdom (1958–1961, 1972–1973, and 1975–1976), during which Icelandic patrol vessels cut the trawl wires of British fishing boats inside Iceland's unilaterally extended fishing limits. No lives were lost in the confrontations, and Iceland ultimately secured recognition of its 200-nautical-mile exclusive economic zone.

Fleet and aircraft

The Coast Guard operates offshore patrol vessels, including ICGV Þór, commissioned in 2011, and ICGV Freyja, acquired in 2021, along with survey and inshore craft. Its air wing has operated helicopters, notably Super Puma aircraft used for search and rescue, and surveillance fixed-wing aircraft. The service also mans Iceland's NATO air-defence radar stations and conducts bomb disposal nationwide.

Duties

Beyond fisheries patrol, the Coast Guard runs Iceland's maritime rescue coordination, charts Icelandic waters, enforces pollution regulations, and supports police and civil protection operations.
