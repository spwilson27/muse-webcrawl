Crown colony

A Crown colony was a type of colonial administration in the British Empire in which the colony was ruled directly by a governor appointed by the Crown, normally advised by a nominated legislative council, in contrast to self-governing colonies with elected assemblies enjoying responsible government, and to protectorates and chartered-company territories.

Characteristics

In a Crown colony the governor exercised executive authority on behalf of the monarch and the government in London, and legislation was made by the governor with a council whose members were officials or appointees rather than elected representatives — though elected elements were introduced in many colonies over time. The system was often imposed after rebellions or when London judged a colony unready for self-rule: Jamaica was made a Crown colony after the Morant Bay rebellion of 1865, and Hong Kong, Gibraltar, Malta and many Caribbean and African territories were administered as Crown colonies for much of their history.

Later usage

From 1981 the remaining colonies were reclassified as British Dependent Territories and, from 2002, British Overseas Territories, so "Crown colony" survives mainly as a historical term.
