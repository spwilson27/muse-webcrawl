Thomas Piketty (born 7 May 1971) is a French economist whose work focuses on income and wealth inequality. A professor at the École des hautes études en sciences sociales (EHESS) and the Paris School of Economics, which he co-founded, he built, with colleagues including Emmanuel Saez, the World Inequality Database of long-run income and wealth series from tax records.

Capital in the Twenty-First Century

His 2013 book Le Capital au XXIe siècle (English translation 2014) became an international bestseller. It argues that when the rate of return on capital (r) persistently exceeds the growth rate of output (g) — the inequality r > g — wealth concentrates and inequality tends to rise, as it did in the nineteenth century and again since the late twentieth century. Piketty proposed remedies including a global progressive tax on capital. The book drew praise for its historical data and criticism over aspects of its data handling and theory. His follow-up, Capital and Ideology (2019), extended the analysis to the political and ideological systems that justify inequality.
