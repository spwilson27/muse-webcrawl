Persius

Aulus Persius Flaccus (34 – 62 AD), usually known in English as Persius, was a Roman poet and satirist of Etruscan origin. His six satires, written in a dense, moralising style influenced by Stoicism, critique the literary taste, wealth and hypocrisy of Neronian Rome.

Life

Persius was born at Volaterrae in Etruria into a wealthy equestrian family. Educated at Rome, he studied under the Stoic philosopher Lucius Annaeus Cornutus, who became his mentor, and counted the poet Lucan among his friends. He died young, in 62 AD, leaving his satires unpublished; they were edited and issued posthumously by Cornutus.

Works

The Satires comprise a short prologue and six poems, in hexameters. Unlike Juvenal's later indignant satire, Persius's work is introspective and philosophical, urging self-examination and freedom from slavery to appetite and public opinion. His compressed, figurative Latin made him a favourite of scholars, grammarians and Church writers throughout late antiquity and the Middle Ages, though modern readers often find him difficult.

Influence

Persius was widely read in schools from late antiquity onward; his Stoic moralising resonated with Christian readers, and his obscurity became proverbial. Commentaries on his work survive from antiquity (the so-called scholia), and he influenced later satirists including Dryden, who translated him into English.
