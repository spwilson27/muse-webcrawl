Mormon Trail

[Image: Mormon pioneer wagon company crossing the plains]

The Mormon Trail is the 1,300-mile (2,100 km) route travelled by members of the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints from Nauvoo, Illinois, to the Salt Lake Valley in present-day Utah between 1846 and 1869. After the expulsion of the Latter-day Saints from Illinois, Brigham Young led the first pioneer company west, wintering at Winter Quarters, Nebraska, and arriving in the Salt Lake Valley in July 1847. Over the following two decades an estimated 70,000 emigrants followed the trail, many pushing handcarts.

Route

From Nauvoo the pioneers crossed Iowa to Winter Quarters (near present-day Omaha) and Kanesville (Council Bluffs). Westward the trail followed the north bank of the Platte River through Nebraska, passing Fort Kearny, Chimney Rock and Scotts Bluff, then continued along the North Platte to Fort Laramie in Wyoming. It shared stretches with the Oregon and California Trails as far as Fort Bridger, before diverging southwest through Echo Canyon and Emigration Canyon into the Salt Lake Valley.

Handcart companies and memory

Beginning in 1856, ten handcart companies carried poorer emigrants west with two-wheeled carts. Two of these - the Willie and Martin companies - were caught by early blizzards in Wyoming in 1856 and suffered heavy losses before rescue parties from Salt Lake City reached them. The trail is commemorated by the Mormon Pioneer National Heritage Area and numerous monuments; the annual Trek re-enactments retrace portions of it, and July 24 is celebrated in Utah as Pioneer Day.
