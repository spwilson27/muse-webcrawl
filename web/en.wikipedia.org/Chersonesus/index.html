Chersonesus

Chersonesus or Chersonesos (Greek: Χερσόνησος, "peninsula") may refer to several places in the ancient world, so named because they were situated on or were peninsulas. The most famous is Chersonesus Taurica, the Greek colony in southwestern Crimea near modern Sevastopol. Other places bearing the name include the Thracian Chersonese (the Gallipoli peninsula), the Cimbric Chersonese (Jutland), and Chersonesus in Crete and in Egypt.

Chersonesus Taurica

Chersonesus in Crimea was founded by settlers from Heraclea Pontica in the 5th century BC on the southwestern coast of Crimea. It survived for nearly two thousand years — as a Greek polis, a Roman and Byzantine outpost, and a trading city — and its extensive ruins, just outside Sevastopol, are a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It is traditionally the place where Prince Vladimir of Kievan Rus' was baptised in 988.

Thracian Chersonese

The Thracian Chersonese is the ancient name of the Gallipoli peninsula in Turkey. Colonised by Athens in the 6th century BC, it guarded the entrance to the Hellespont and was ruled for a time by Miltiades the Elder and his family.

Other uses

The Cimbric Chersonese was the Roman name for Jutland, homeland of the Cimbri. Chersonesus also named a town on the northwest coast of Crete and a settlement near Alexandria in Egypt. In zoology, Chersonesus-derived names occasionally appear in taxonomy.
