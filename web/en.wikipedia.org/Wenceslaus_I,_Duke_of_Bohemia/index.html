Wenceslaus I, Duke of Bohemia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Wenceslaus I (Czech: Václav I; c. 907 – 28 September 935), also known as Saint Wenceslaus or "Good King Wenceslaus", was Duke of Bohemia from 921 until his assassination in 935. Raised a Christian by his grandmother Ludmila of Bohemia, he became renowned for his piety, charity to the poor and promotion of Christianity in Bohemia. He was murdered by retainers of his younger brother, Boleslaus the Cruel, at Stará Boleslav, and was soon venerated as a martyr and saint.

Life

Wenceslaus was the son of Duke Vratislaus I and Drahomíra, a princess of the pagan Hevelli tribe. After his father's death he succeeded as a minor, with his grandmother Ludmila as regent until Drahomíra had her murdered in 921. As duke, Wenceslaus founded the rotunda of St. Vitus at Prague Castle, on the site of the present St. Vitus Cathedral. In foreign policy he submitted to the East Frankish king Henry the Fowler in 929, accepting tributary status to secure peace — a policy that angered Bohemian nobles and his brother.

Death and veneration

On 28 September 935, travelling to Stará Boleslav for the feast of Saints Cosmas and Damian, Wenceslaus was attacked and killed by his brother's men as he sought sanctuary in the church. Boleslaus succeeded him. Wenceslaus's remains were later translated to St. Vitus in Prague, and his cult spread widely; he became the patron saint of Bohemia and of the Czech state, and 28 September is a Czech public holiday. His helmet and armour are preserved as relics.

In popular culture

The Victorian carol "Good King Wenceslas", written by John Mason Neale in 1853, celebrates the saint's charity to a poor peasant on St. Stephen's Day (26 December). The equestrian statue in Prague's Wenceslaus Square is a national symbol and a traditional gathering place in times of crisis.
