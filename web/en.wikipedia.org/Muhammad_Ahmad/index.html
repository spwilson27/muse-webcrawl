Muhammad Ahmad

Born12 August 1844, Labab Island, Dongola, Sudan
Died22 June 1885 (aged 40), Omdurman, Sudan
Known forSelf-proclaimed Mahdi; founder of the Mahdist State

Muhammad Ahmad bin Abd Allah (1844–1885) was a Sudanese religious leader of the Samaniyya Sufi order who, in 1881, proclaimed himself the Mahdi — the prophesied redeemer of Islam — and launched a revolt against Turco-Egyptian rule in Sudan. His followers, the Ansar, destroyed an Egyptian army under Hicks Pasha in 1883 and captured Khartoum in January 1885, in a siege in which General Charles George Gordon was killed.

Rise of the Mahdist movement

Born near Dongola into a family of boat-builders claiming descent from the Prophet Muhammad, Muhammad Ahmad became a respected ascetic teacher on Aba Island in the White Nile. His proclamation as Mahdi drew support from Sudanese tribes resentful of Egyptian taxation, corruption and suppression of the slave trade. Declaring a jihad, he framed his mission as restoring pure Islam and preparing for the return of Jesus and the end of the age.

The Mahdist State

After Khartoum's fall, he established his capital at Omdurman and governed the Mahdist State (1885–1899) as a theocracy, with himself as successor of the Prophet, aided by khalifas modelled on the first caliphs. He died of typhus only months after his victory. His successor, Abdallahi ibn Muhammad, ruled until Anglo-Egyptian forces under Kitchener destroyed the Mahdist army at the Battle of Omdurman in 1898.
