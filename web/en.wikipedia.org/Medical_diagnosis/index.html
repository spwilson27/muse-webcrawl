Medical diagnosis

Medical diagnosis is the process of determining which disease or condition explains a person's symptoms and signs. It typically combines a medical history, physical examination, and diagnostic tests such as laboratory studies and imaging.

Process

Clinicians form a differential diagnosis — a list of candidate conditions — and narrow it using further evidence, sometimes expressed in Bayesian terms of pre-test and post-test probability. Diagnoses may be clinical, radiological, laboratory or pathological, and are recorded using classifications such as the ICD.

Errors and advances

Diagnostic error is a significant source of patient harm, prompting interest in checklists, second opinions, and computer-aided and AI-assisted diagnosis.
