Rack railway

A rack railway in the Swiss Alps.

A rack railway (also rack-and-pinion railway, cog railway or cogwheel railway) is a steep-grade railway with a toothed rack rail, usually laid between the running rails. Trains are fitted with one or more cog wheels or pinions that mesh with this rack rail, allowing operation on gradients too steep for adhesion alone — conventional adhesion railways are generally limited to grades of about 10 percent or less.

Systems

Several rack systems have been developed, including the Riggenbach system (ladder-like rack), the Strub system, the Abt system (using multiple vertical rack blades), the Locher system (horizontal rack meshing with side pinions, used on the very steep Pilatus Railway in Switzerland, with a gradient of 48 percent), and the Marsh/Fell systems. The Pilatus Railway remains the steepest rack railway in the world.

Examples

Famous rack railways include the Jungfrau Railway and Gornergrat Railway in Switzerland, the Manitou and Pike's Peak Railway in Colorado, the Snowdon Mountain Railway in Wales, and the Nilgiri Mountain Railway in India. The first rack railway was the Middleton Railway in Leeds, England, fitted with a rack in 1812 for John Blenkinsop's locomotives.
