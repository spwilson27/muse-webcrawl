Medication

A medication (also called a medicament, medicine, pharmaceutical drug, or simply a drug) is a substance used to diagnose, cure, treat, or prevent disease. Drug therapy (pharmacotherapy) is an important part of the medical field and relies on the science of pharmacology for continual advancement and on pharmacy for appropriate management. Medications are distinct from medicine in the broad sense of the practice of healing.

Classification

Drugs are classified in many ways: by chemical structure, by mechanism of action (for example, beta blockers), by therapeutic use (for example, antibiotics, analgesics, antihypertensives), and by legal status — prescription-only medicines versus over-the-counter drugs. The Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) classification system maintained by the WHO organises drugs by the organ system they act on and their chemical properties.

Development and regulation

Modern drug development proceeds from discovery and preclinical testing through phased clinical trials (Phases I–IV) to regulatory approval and post-marketing surveillance (pharmacovigilance). Development commonly takes over a decade, and most candidate compounds fail. After patent expiry, generic equivalents may be marketed.

Administration

Medications are delivered by routes including oral, intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous, inhalational, topical, rectal and transdermal. The route affects onset and bioavailability. Dosage forms include tablets, capsules, solutions, suspensions, ointments and patches.

Adverse effects

All effective drugs can cause adverse drug reactions, ranging from mild side effects to severe harm. Interactions with other drugs, foods or diseases complicate prescribing, particularly in patients taking many medicines (polypharmacy).
