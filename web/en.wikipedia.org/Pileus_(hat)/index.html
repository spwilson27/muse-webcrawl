Pileus (hat)

Type
Brimless felt cap
Origin
Ancient Greece and Rome
Symbolism
Freedom; freed slaves

The pileus (Greek: πῖλος, pilos) was a brimless, conical or rounded felt cap worn in ancient Greece and Rome. Sailors, fishermen, and workers commonly wore it, and the Dioscuri (Castor and Pollux) are often depicted with the pileus.

Symbol of liberty

In Rome, the pileus became the emblem of freedom: at manumission, a freed slave was ceremonially presented with a pileus, and the cap appeared on coins with the goddess Libertas. After the assassination of Julius Caesar, Brutus issued a famous denarius showing a pileus between two daggers, celebrating the restoration of liberty. Through this lineage, the pileus is an ancestor of the Phrygian cap used as a liberty symbol in the American and French revolutions.
