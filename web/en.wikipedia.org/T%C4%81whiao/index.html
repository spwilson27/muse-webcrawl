Tāwhiao (c. 1822 – 31 August 1894), born Tūkāroto Matutaera Pōtatau Te Wherowhero Tāwhiao, was the second Māori King, leading the Kīngitanga (Māori King movement) from 1860 until his death.

Reign

Tāwhiao succeeded his father Pōtatau in 1860, shortly before the invasion of the Waikato during the New Zealand Wars. After defeat in 1864, he and his followers withdrew into the King Country, where he lived in exile for some twenty years.

Return and later life

In 1881 Tāwhiao laid down arms and negotiated a return; in 1884 he travelled to London to petition Queen Victoria over confiscated lands, without success. He is remembered for prophetic sayings (tongi) that remain influential in Waikato-Tainui tradition.
