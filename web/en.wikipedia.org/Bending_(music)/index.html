Bending (music)

Bending
TypeMusical technique / ornament
InstrumentsGuitar, harmonica, saxophone, synthesiser
EffectContinuous raising (or lowering) of pitch

A guitarist bending a note upward.

Bending is a musical technique in which a player smoothly raises or lowers the pitch of a note, producing an expressive, vocal-like transition between tones. It is fundamental to blues, rock and jazz phrasing.

Techniques by instrument

On guitar, the player pushes or pulls a fretted string across the fretboard, increasing its tension to raise pitch by a semitone, whole tone or more; vibrato is often added at the target pitch. Harmonica players bend notes by changing the shape of the mouth and throat to draw reeds down in pitch, essential to blues harp. Saxophonists and singers bend with embouchure and breath, and synthesisers provide pitch-bend wheels and controllers standardised in MIDI.

Notation and style

Bends are notated with curved arrows on staff or tablature, often specifying the interval ("full" = whole tone, "½" = semitone), pre-bends and releases. Masters of the technique include B. B. King, whose economical, singing bends defined electric blues guitar, and Stevie Ray Vaughan and David Gilmour in rock.
