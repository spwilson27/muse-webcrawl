The Korean People's Army (KPA; Korean: 조선인민군) is the military force of North Korea and the armed wing of the Workers' Party of Korea. Under the Songun ("military-first") policy and its successor ideology, the KPA is the central institution of North Korean society, absorbing a large share of the state's resources.

Organisation

The KPA comprises the Ground Force, the Korean People's Navy, the Korean People's Army Air and Anti-Air Force, the Strategic Force responsible for ballistic missiles, and special operations forces. It is commanded through the Ministry of Defence and the General Staff, with supreme command vested in the head of the State Affairs Commission. North Korea maintains one of the world's largest standing armies, with well over a million active personnel and millions more in reserve and paramilitary organisations, though much of its conventional equipment dates from the Soviet era.

History

The KPA traces its lineage to the Korean People's Revolutionary Army of 1932, the anti-Japanese guerrilla force associated with Kim Il Sung. It was formally established as a regular army on 8 February 1948, shortly before the proclamation of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea. On 25 June 1950 the KPA invaded South Korea, beginning the Korean War; after three years of fighting involving United Nations and Chinese forces, the war ended in the 1953 armistice, which the KPA commemorates as victory.

Nuclear and missile forces

Since the 2000s the KPA's Strategic Force has developed nuclear weapons and a range of ballistic missiles, including intercontinental systems tested from 2017, which North Korea presents as a deterrent against the United States and South Korea.
