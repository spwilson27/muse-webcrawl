Goal kick

A goal kick is a method of restarting play in a game of association football. It is awarded when the ball last touched by an attacking player passes over the goal line without a goal having been scored, and without the ball entering the goal. The kick is taken by the defending team from inside its own goal area. The procedure is described in Law 16 of the Laws of the Game.

Procedure

The ball is placed anywhere inside the goal area and kicked by a player of the defending team, usually the goalkeeper. Since a 2019 law change, the ball is in play as soon as it is kicked and clearly moves — it no longer needs to leave the penalty area — and opponents must remain outside the penalty area until the ball is in play, though they may enter as soon as it moves. A goal may be scored directly from a goal kick against the opposing team, but not an own goal; if the ball goes directly into the kicker's own goal, a corner kick is awarded instead.

Tactics

Teams may restart with a long kick toward the forwards, or — especially since the 2019 change — play short passes out from the back, inviting the opposition to press high up the pitch in an attempt to draw them out of position. Deliberately delaying a goal kick is also a common time-management tactic and may be punished as time-wasting.
