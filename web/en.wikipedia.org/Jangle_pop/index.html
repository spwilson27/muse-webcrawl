Jangle pop is a subgenre of rock and pop music characterised by bright, chiming, arpeggiated electric guitar sounds — the "jangle" — most famously produced by 12-string Rickenbacker guitars, combined with melodic, often wistful pop songwriting. The style takes its signature sound from the mid-1960s recordings of the Byrds, whose Roger McGuinn pioneered the compressed, ringing 12-string guitar tone heard on "Mr. Tambourine Man" (1965). The term itself derives from the Bob Dylan lyric "in the jingle-jangle morning" in that song.

History

Origins and 1980s heyday

Although jangly guitar pop recurred throughout the 1970s in the work of Big Star, the Searchers' revivalists, and power pop bands, jangle pop cohered as a distinct movement in the early 1980s on American college radio and in the British independent scene. R.E.M., formed in Athens, Georgia, brought the sound to prominence with their debut EP Chronic Town (1982) and album Murmur (1983), built around Peter Buck's arpeggiated Rickenbacker playing. In the United States the so-called Paisley Underground in Los Angeles (the Dream Syndicate, the Bangles, Rain Parade) and bands such as the Feelies, Let's Active, and 10,000 Maniacs developed related sounds. In Britain, the Smiths married Johnny Marr's jangling guitar work to Morrissey's lyrics, while Aztec Camera and Lloyd Cole and the Commotions brought the style into the charts. Parallel scenes flourished in New Zealand (the "Dunedin sound" of the Chills and the Clean on Flying Nun Records) and Australia (the Go-Betweens, the Church).

Legacy

By the late 1980s jangle pop was absorbed into the broader categories of alternative rock and indie pop, influencing the British C86 scene and later indie bands worldwide. Revivals and echoes can be heard in the work of Teenage Fanclub, the La's, and numerous 2000s–2010s indie acts drawing on the chiming guitar aesthetic.

Characteristics

Jangle pop songs are typically mid-tempo, melodic, and guitar-based, eschewing heavy distortion and synthesizers in favour of ringing open chords, arpeggios, and harmony vocals. Lyrics often have a nostalgic or melancholic cast. The genre is closely related to power pop and folk rock but generally more understated and less aggressive than the former.
