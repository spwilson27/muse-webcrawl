Saint-Pierre, Martinique

Saint-Pierre is a town and commune of France's Caribbean overseas department of Martinique, founded in 1635 by Pierre Belain d'Esnambuc. It lies on the island's northwestern coast at the foot of the volcano Mount Pelée.

The 1902 eruption

Before its destruction, Saint-Pierre was the island's cultural and commercial capital, nicknamed the "Paris of the Caribbean". On 8 May 1902, a pyroclastic surge from Mount Pelée destroyed the city within minutes and killed roughly 28,000–30,000 people — nearly the entire population — making it one of the deadliest volcanic disasters of the 20th century. The prisoner Ludger Sylbaris, protected by his thick-walled cell, became one of its few famous survivors.

Rebuilding

The town was rebuilt on the same site beginning in the 1920s, though the capital moved permanently to Fort-de-France. Today Saint-Pierre has about 4,000 inhabitants; ruins of the theatre, fort and cathedral are preserved, and the Frank A. Perret Memorial Museum commemorates the eruption.
