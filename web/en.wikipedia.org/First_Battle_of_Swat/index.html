Date25 October – 8 December 2007
LocationSwat District, Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, Pakistan

The First Battle of Swat, code-named Operation Rah-e-Haq, was a military operation by the Pakistan Army against militants of the Tehrik-e-Nifaz-e-Shariat-e-Mohammadi (TNSM) led by Maulana Fazlullah in the Swat District of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa (then the North-West Frontier Province) in late 2007. It was the first phase of the army's campaign to dislodge Fazlullah's movement, which had seized control of much of the valley and imposed its own interpretation of Islamic law through an FM radio campaign and armed enforcement.

Background

Fazlullah, a cleric known as "Mullah Radio" for his pirate FM broadcasts, built a large following in Swat demanding enforcement of sharia. By 2007 his armed followers had attacked police posts, girls' schools, and government installations, and had extended their control over most of the district outside the capital, Mingora.

The operation

In late October 2007 the government deployed regular army troops and Frontier Corps units into Swat. Fighting concentrated around the militant strongholds of Imam Dheri, Kuza Banda, and the hilltop positions overlooking Mingora. Helicopters and artillery supported ground advances, and the army retook a number of villages, but the militants melted into the mountains and among the population. Several hundred people, including militants, soldiers, and civilians, were reported killed, and tens of thousands of residents were displaced.

Aftermath

The operation wound down in December 2007 without eliminating Fazlullah's network. In 2008 the provincial government negotiated a peace agreement, and in early 2009 a further accord formalized the Nizam-e-Adl Regulation in exchange for a ceasefire. When the militants advanced into neighboring Buner district, the army launched the much larger Second Battle of Swat (Operation Rah-e-Rast) in May 2009, which retook the valley.
