Energy security is the association between national security and the availability of natural resources for energy consumption. The International Energy Agency defines energy security as "the uninterrupted availability of energy sources at an affordable price". Long-term energy security concerns timely investment in supply aligned with economic development and environmental needs, while short-term energy security focuses on the ability of the energy system to respond promptly to sudden changes in the supply–demand balance.

Threats and measures

Energy security is threatened by geopolitical instability in producer regions, manipulation of supplies, competition over resources, attacks on infrastructure, accidents and natural disasters. Responses include diversifying sources, fuels and suppliers, holding strategic petroleum reserves (IEA members are required to hold stocks equivalent to at least 90 days of net imports), investing in domestic production and renewable energy, improving energy efficiency, and building resilient grids and interconnectors. The 1970s oil crises prompted the founding of the IEA; the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine triggered a major reorientation of European gas policy away from Russian pipeline supplies.

Energy security vs. energy independence

Energy independence — producing all energy domestically — is one extreme strategy, but integration into diversified international markets can also enhance security. Analysts often evaluate energy security through the "four As": availability, accessibility, affordability and acceptability (environmental and social).
