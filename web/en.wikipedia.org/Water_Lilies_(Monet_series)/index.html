Water Lilies (Monet series)

Water Lilies (French: Nymphéas) is a series of approximately 250 oil paintings by French Impressionist Claude Monet (1840–1926). The paintings depict his flower garden at his home in Giverny, France, and were the main focus of his artistic production during the last thirty years of his life. Many of the works were painted while Monet suffered from cataracts.

The garden at Giverny

Monet settled in Giverny in 1883 and, beginning in 1893, developed a water garden on land across from his house, diverting a stream to create a pond spanned by a Japanese-style wooden bridge. He filled the pond with water lilies and surrounded it with weeping willows and irises. From 1899 onward the pond and its reflections became his nearly exclusive subject.

The paintings

The series ranges from comparatively small canvases of the pond's surface to enormous decorative panels. Monet increasingly dissolved solid form into shimmering fields of colour and reflection, so that many late works approach abstraction. Between 1914 and his death in 1926 he worked on the Grandes Décorations, a cycle of huge mural-sized panels intended as a gift to the French state.

Legacy and locations

The eight-panel cycle of the Grandes Décorations was installed in 1927 in two oval rooms of the Musée de l'Orangerie in Paris, where it remains. Other major groups of Water Lilies are held by the Musée Marmottan Monet, the Museum of Modern Art in New York, the Art Institute of Chicago, and many other museums worldwide. Individual canvases from the series rank among the most valuable Impressionist paintings sold at auction.
