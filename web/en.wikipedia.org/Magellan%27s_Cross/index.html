Magellan's Cross

Magellan's Cross (Spanish: Cruz de Magallanes) is a Christian cross said to have been planted by explorers of the Spanish expedition of Ferdinand Magellan on the island of Cebu in the Philippines in April 1521. It is housed in a small chapel next to the Basilica Minore del Santo Niño in Cebu City and is one of the city's best-known landmarks.

History

According to tradition, Magellan ordered the cross planted after the baptism of Rajah Humabon and other local leaders into Christianity. The original cross is now encased within a larger wooden cross made of tindalo wood, a measure said to protect it from devotees who chipped away pieces as relics. The octagonal pavilion housing it was built in the 19th century and features ceiling paintings depicting the baptism and the planting of the cross.

Significance

The cross symbolises the introduction of Christianity to the Philippines and remains a popular pilgrimage and tourist site, particularly during the Sinulog festival.
