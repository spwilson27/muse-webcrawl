The D1 Grand Prix is a professional drifting championship series in Japan, founded in 2001 by racing driver Keiichi Tsuchiya — known as the "Drift King" — together with Daijiro Inada, founder of Option magazine and the Tokyo Auto Salon. It grew out of amateur drifting competitions on mountain passes and circuits and is credited with establishing drifting as an organised professional motorsport.

Format

Events begin with solo qualifying runs in which drivers are scored on their racing line, drift angle, speed, and overall impression. The best qualifiers advance to tandem battles (tsuiso), in which two cars drift together in lead and follow roles; the follow driver scores by mimicking the lead driver's line and angle while staying in close proximity. Early events used human judges, later supplemented by the DOSS electronic scoring system using GPS and sensor data. Typical cars are modified rear-wheel-drive Japanese coupés such as the Nissan Silvia, Nissan 180SX, Toyota AE86, Toyota Supra, and Mazda RX-7.

Influence

The D1 Grand Prix inspired professional drifting series worldwide, including Formula Drift in the United States, and produced star drivers such as Yasuyuki Kazama, Nobushige Kumakubo, Ken Nomura, and Daigo Saito. Exhibitions and spinoff series have been held in the United States, Malaysia, and elsewhere. The series helped turn drifting from a street subculture into a globally recognised discipline of motorsport.
