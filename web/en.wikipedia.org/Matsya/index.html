Matsya

Matsya (Sanskrit: मत्स्य, "fish") is the fish avatar of the Hindu god Vishnu. Often described as the first of Vishnu's ten primary avatars (the Dashavatara), Matsya is said to have rescued the first man, Manu, from a great deluge. Matsya may be depicted as a giant fish, often golden in color, or anthropomorphically with a human torso connected to the rear half of a fish.

The earliest account of Matsya is found in the Shatapatha Brahmana (c. 8th–6th centuries BCE), where Matsya is not associated with any particular deity. The fish-saviour later merged with the identity of the god Brahma and then with Vishnu. The legends associated with Matsya expand, evolve, and vary in Hindu texts. These legends start the theme of the mytho-history of the world creation soon after, which later turned into efforts for preservation.

The legend

According to the legend, the sage-king Manu (also called Satyavrata) was washing his hands in a river when a small fish swam into his hands and begged him to save its life. He put the fish in a jar, but it soon outgrew the jar, then a tank, then a river, and finally had to be released into the ocean. Recognizing the fish as a divine being, Manu was warned of a coming great flood that would destroy all life.

Matsya instructed Manu to build a great boat and to take aboard the seven sages, the seeds of all plants, and one of each species of animal. When the deluge came, Manu tied the boat with the serpent Vasuki to the horn of the great fish, which towed it to safety on a mountaintop. After the flood, Manu repopulated the earth and became the progenitor of humanity.

Iconography

Matsya is depicted either in animal form as a fish, or in a combination form, anthropomorphic with the upper body of a four-armed man and the lower body of a fish. In the anthropomorphic form, he holds a conch (shankha) and a discus (chakra) in two of his hands, while the other two are in the boon-giving (varada) and protection (abhaya) gestures.

Temples

Matsya is venerated in a handful of temples, including the Koneswaram temple in Trincomalee, Sri Lanka, and temples in Andhra Pradesh such as the Nagalapuram Vedanarayana Temple, where Matsya is the central deity. Matsya Jayanti, the birthday of Matsya, is celebrated on Chaitra Shukla Tritiya.
