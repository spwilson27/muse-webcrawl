Aircraft

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

An aircraft is a vehicle that is able to fly by gaining support from the air. It counters the force of gravity by using either static lift (as in balloons and airships) or the dynamic lift of an airfoil (as in aeroplanes and helicopters), or in a few cases direct thrust from engines.

Classification

Aircraft are commonly divided into lighter-than-air and heavier-than-air types. Lighter-than-air craft — balloons and dirigibles — float using buoyant gas. Heavier-than-air craft include fixed-wing aeroplanes (powered by propellers or jets), rotorcraft such as helicopters and autogyros, gliders and sailplanes, and ornithopters (experimental). Aircraft may be crewed or uncrewed (drones/UAVs), and are used for civil transport, military purposes, recreation, research and cargo.

History

Human flight began with kites, balloons (Montgolfier brothers, 1783) and gliders (notably Otto Lilienthal's experiments in the 1890s). The Wright brothers achieved the first sustained, controlled, powered heavier-than-air flight on 17 December 1903 at Kitty Hawk, North Carolina. Aircraft developed rapidly through two world wars, from fabric biplanes to all-metal monoplanes and jet aircraft (operational from 1944). Commercial aviation expanded after 1945 with airliners such as the de Havilland Comet, Boeing 707 and wide-body jets like the Boeing 747 and Airbus A380.

How aircraft fly

A fixed-wing aircraft generates lift as air flows over its wings, and requires forward motion provided by thrust from propellers or jet engines. Control is achieved with surfaces such as ailerons, elevators and rudders. Helicopters generate lift and thrust from rotating blades and can hover and take off vertically.

Impact

Aviation has shrunk global travel times, enabled international commerce and tourism, and transformed warfare, while raising concerns about noise, safety and carbon emissions.
