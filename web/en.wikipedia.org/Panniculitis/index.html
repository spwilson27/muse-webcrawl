Panniculitis

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Panniculitis is a group of disorders characterised by inflammation of subcutaneous fat (the panniculus adiposus) beneath the skin. It typically presents as tender, red or violet nodules or plaques, most often on the shins, which may be accompanied by fever and malaise. Panniculitides are classified according to whether the inflammation is mainly septal (in the fibrous septa between fat lobules) or lobular, and whether vasculitis is present.

Types and causes

  • Erythema nodosum – the commonest form, a septal panniculitis arising as an immune reaction to infections (especially streptococcal sore throat, tuberculosis, coccidioidomycosis), sarcoidosis, inflammatory bowel disease, pregnancy or drugs such as sulfonamides and oral contraceptives.
  • Erythema induratum (nodular vasculitis) – a lobular panniculitis with vasculitis, classically associated with tuberculosis.
  • Pancreatic panniculitis – caused by circulating pancreatic enzymes in pancreatitis or pancreatic cancer, producing fat necrosis.
  • Lupus panniculitis – associated with systemic and discoid lupus erythematosus.
  • Cold panniculitis, traumatic and post-injection forms, and panniculitis associated with alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency.
  • Subcutaneous panniculitis-like T-cell lymphoma – a malignancy that can mimic benign panniculitis and is a key reason for biopsy.

Diagnosis and treatment

Because the various forms look similar clinically, a deep skin biopsy extending into the fat is usually required, along with investigations for underlying causes. Treatment targets the underlying disease; symptomatic therapy includes rest, leg elevation, non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs and, for erythema nodosum, potassium iodide. Some forms resolve spontaneously over weeks, leaving bruise-like discoloration.
