General National Congress

The General National Congress (GNC; Arabic: المؤتمر الوطني العام) was the legislative authority of Libya between 2012 and 2014, and again, in rival form, from 2014 to 2016. It was elected in the Libyan General National Congress election of 7 July 2012 — the country's first free national election in more than forty years — taking over from the National Transitional Council that had governed during and after the 2011 Libyan civil war.

Composition and work

The Congress had 200 members: 80 elected through party lists and 120 as independents from individual constituencies. Its largest bloc was the National Forces Alliance of Mahmoud Jibril, followed by the Muslim Brotherhood's Justice and Construction Party. The GNC chose Mohammed Magarief as its first president (speaker) and selected Ali Zeidan, and later Abdullah al-Thani, as prime minister. It oversaw a difficult transition marked by militia violence, including an attack on its own premises, and in May 2013 passed a controversial political isolation law barring former regime officials from public life. Its decision to extend its own mandate beyond February 2014 provoked mass protests.

Replacement and rival congress

Elections for a new Council of Deputies (House of Representatives) were held on 25 June 2014, and the GNC was due to hand over power in August 2014. Instead, after Islamist-aligned forces seized Tripoli in the Second Libyan Civil War, a group of former GNC members reconvened the Congress in the capital as a rival legislature to the internationally recognised House of Representatives, which sat in Tobruk. Under the UN-brokered Libyan Political Agreement of December 2015, the GNC's remnants were reconstituted as the advisory High Council of State, and the Tripoli congress dissolved itself in April 2016.
