Named for: Eugène Charles Catalan
Occurs in: combinatorics, number theory

Catalan's constant, denoted G, is a mathematical constant defined by the alternating series G = 1 − 1/9 + 1/25 − 1/49 + …, equivalently the Dirichlet beta function evaluated at 2, β(2). Its value is approximately 0.915965594177219.

Properties

It is not known whether Catalan's constant is irrational. The constant arises in numerous contexts: in combinatorics, in integrals and series evaluations, in the volumes of certain polyhedra, and in physics, for example in problems about random walks and in quantum field theory.

Computation

G has been computed to hundreds of billions of digits using rapidly convergent series identities. It is named after Eugène Charles Catalan, who studied the constant in the 19th century.
