Prague Castle (Czech: Pražský hrad) is a castle complex in Prague, Czech Republic, founded around 870 and continuously developed for more than a millennium. It has served as the seat of Bohemian kings, Holy Roman emperors, and presidents of Czechoslovakia and the Czech Republic. The Bohemian Crown Jewels are kept within a hidden room inside the complex.

History

The castle was founded by Prince Bořivoj I of the Přemyslid dynasty around 870. It flourished under Charles IV in the 14th century, who rebuilt it in Gothic style and founded St. Vitus Cathedral. The Second Defenestration of Prague, which triggered the Thirty Years' War, occurred here in 1618. Habsburg rulers, especially Maria Theresa in the 18th century, rebuilt the complex in a unified neoclassical style. After 1918 it became the seat of the president of Czechoslovakia, remodeled by architect Jože Plečnik under Tomáš Masaryk, and it hosted Václav Havel after the Velvet Revolution of 1989.

Buildings

The complex covers roughly 70,000 square metres and includes the Old Royal Palace with its Gothic Vladislav Hall, St. George's Basilica (founded c. 920), the Golden Lane of small colorful houses, the Daliborka Tower, the Royal Garden, and the Spanish Hall. The Changing of the Guard, with a fanfare at noon, is a popular ceremony.

St. Vitus Cathedral

The Metropolitan Cathedral of Saints Vitus, Wenceslaus and Adalbert dominates the complex. Founded in 1344 and completed only in 1929, it is the largest and most important church in the Czech Republic, containing the tombs of Bohemian kings and the Chapel of St. Wenceslaus, and is renowned for Alfons Mucha's stained-glass window.

The castle today

Prague Castle is the official office of the President of the Czech Republic and one of Prague's most visited attractions, drawing around two million visitors a year. Much of the complex is open to the public, including gardens, museums, and the cathedral.
