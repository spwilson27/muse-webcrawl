Welland Canal

Location: Ontario, Canada
Length: 43.4 km (27.0 mi)
Locks: 8
Opened: 1829 (first); 1932 (fourth, current)
Connects: Lake Erie to Lake Ontario

A vessel transiting Lock 7 of the Welland Canal, part of the St. Lawrence Seaway.

The Welland Canal is a ship canal in Ontario, Canada, and part of the St. Lawrence Seaway. It connects Lake Ontario and Lake Erie, enabling vessels to bypass Niagara Falls. The canal, which is 43.4 kilometres (27.0 mi) long, raises and lowers ships 99.5 metres (326 feet) through a series of eight locks, allowing them to cross the Niagara Escarpment.

History

The first Welland Canal, promoted by businessman William Hamilton Merritt, opened in 1829, running from Port Dalhousie on Lake Ontario to Port Robinson on the Welland River. Enlarged second (1845) and third (1887) canals followed straighter routes. The fourth and current canal, begun in 1913 and delayed by the First World War, opened in 1932; its locks can accommodate Seawaymax vessels up to about 225 metres long. The Welland By-Pass, opened in 1973, rerouted the canal east of the city of Welland.

Operation

The canal is operated by the St. Lawrence Seaway Management Corporation. Roughly 3,000–4,000 vessel transits occur in a typical navigation season, carrying cargoes such as iron ore, grain, coal, and steel, primarily aboard Great Lakes bulk carriers known as "lakers". Transit takes about 11 hours. Historic features include the twin flight locks at Thorold and the Garden City Skyway crossing.
