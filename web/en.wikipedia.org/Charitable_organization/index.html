Charitable organization

SectorNonprofit / voluntary sector
PurposesRelief of poverty, education, religion, health, public benefit
U.S. designation501(c)(3) organization
Key restrictionNo private inurement; assets dedicated to charitable purposes

A charitable organization is a type of nonprofit organization organized and operated for purposes that the law recognizes as charitable, such as the relief of poverty, the advancement of education or religion, the promotion of health, and other purposes beneficial to the public. Charitable organizations typically enjoy exemption from income tax, and donations to them are often tax-deductible for the donor.

Legal foundations

The modern legal definition of charity in the English-speaking world traces to the preamble of the English Charitable Uses Act 1601 and the categories developed in the 1891 case Commissioners for Special Purposes of Income Tax v Pemsel. In the United Kingdom, charities are regulated by the Charity Commission for England and Wales under the Charities Act 2011, which lists recognized descriptions of charitable purposes and requires that each purpose be for the public benefit.

United States

In the United States, most charities are organized as nonprofit corporations and seek recognition as 501(c)(3) organizations under the Internal Revenue Code, which covers religious, charitable, scientific, educational, and similar purposes. They are prohibited from substantial lobbying and all political campaign activity, and no part of their net earnings may inure to private individuals. Private foundations — typically funded by a single family or corporation — are subject to stricter rules than broadly supported public charities.

Forms and activities

Charitable organizations range from small local associations to vast international bodies such as the Red Cross and Médecins Sans Frontières. They deliver services directly, award grants, conduct research, and advocate for causes. Governance is vested in trustees or directors who owe fiduciary duties to the organization's purposes rather than to owners.
