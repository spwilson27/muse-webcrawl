Compromise of 1790

DateSummer 1790
PrincipalsAlexander Hamilton, Thomas Jefferson, James Madison
IssuesLocation of the US capital; federal assumption of state debts
LegislationResidence Act; Funding Act of 1790

The Compromise of 1790 was an agreement between Alexander Hamilton, Thomas Jefferson, and James Madison in which Hamilton won the decision for the federal government to assume and fund the states' debts, while Jefferson and Madison obtained the national capital (District of Columbia) for the South — located on the Potomac River in Virginia and Maryland. It was the first great legislative compromise of the new United States and a famous instance of political logrolling.

The "dinner table bargain": Hamilton, Jefferson, and Madison

Background

As Secretary of the Treasury, Hamilton proposed that the federal government assume the remaining debts the states had incurred during the Revolutionary War, consolidating them into federal obligations to establish national credit. The plan stalled in Congress: several states, especially southern ones like Virginia that had already paid down much of their debt, objected to subsidizing heavier debtors, and the assumption bill was defeated in the House. At the same time, Congress was deadlocked over the permanent seat of government, with northern and southern states pulling toward New York or Philadelphia versus a southern site on the Potomac.

The bargain

According to Jefferson's later account, he hosted a dinner in New York in June 1790 at which he and Madison agreed with Hamilton: Madison would not block assumption (and would help deliver a few southern votes), and in return Hamilton would support placing the permanent capital on the Potomac — with Philadelphia serving as temporary capital for ten years in the meantime. Hamilton's recollection differed in details, but the sequence of votes matched the deal.

Outcome

The Residence Act, signed by President Washington on 16 July 1790, fixed the capital district on the Potomac, and the Funding Act of August 1790 authorized federal assumption of state debts up to $21.5 million. The bargain placed the capital amid slaveholding territory, strengthened federal finances and credit, and became a celebrated — and criticized — model of back-room compromise, later immortalized in the song "The Room Where It Happens" in the musical Hamilton.
