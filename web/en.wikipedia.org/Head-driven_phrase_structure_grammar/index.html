Head-driven phrase structure grammar

Head-driven phrase structure grammar (HPSG) is a highly lexicalized, constraint-based grammar framework developed by Carl Pollard and Ivan Sag, and elaborated with collaborators including Robert Levine and others. It is a type of phrase structure grammar, as opposed to a dependency grammar, and is the immediate successor of generalized phrase structure grammar. HPSG draws on ideas from several disciplines: linguistics, the philosophy of language, and computer science.

Framework

In HPSG, linguistic objects are modelled as typed feature structures — attribute–value matrices in which phonology, syntax, and semantics are integrated into a single structure called a sign. A grammar consists of highly structured lexical entries together with a small set of general principles and grammar rules (schemata). Key features include HEAD, encoding properties shared between a phrase and its head daughter; valence features such as SUBJ and COMPS, which state a head's requirements for its dependents; and list-valued nonlocal features such as QUE, REL, and SLASH, used to model questions, relative clauses, and unbounded dependencies.

Because it is surface-oriented and monostratal, HPSG describes sentences with a single level of syntactic structure rather than transformational derivations. Its formalism is expressed in a typed logic, making it well suited to computational implementation.

Applications

HPSG grammars have been implemented for many languages in computational-linguistics projects such as the DELPH-IN consortium's LinGO English Resource Grammar, and are used in natural language processing for parsing and generation. The framework was laid out in Pollard and Sag's two volumes, Information-based Syntax and Semantics (1987) and Head-driven Phrase Structure Grammar (1994).
