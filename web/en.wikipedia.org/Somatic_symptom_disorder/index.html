Somatic symptom disorder

Somatic symptom disorder, also known as somatoform disorder or somatization disorder, is a mental disorder characterized by one or more chronic physical symptoms that coincide with excessive and maladaptive thoughts, emotions, and behaviors connected to those symptoms. The symptoms are not deliberately produced or feigned, and — unlike earlier diagnoses — they need not be medically unexplained; the disorder may be diagnosed alongside a known medical condition when the person's response to symptoms is disproportionate and impairing.

Diagnosis

The diagnosis was introduced in the DSM-5 (2013), replacing the DSM-IV categories of somatization disorder, undifferentiated somatoform disorder and pain disorder. Criteria require one or more distressing or disruptive somatic symptoms, together with excessive thoughts, feelings or behaviours related to them — such as persistent high anxiety about health or excessive time and energy devoted to the symptoms — typically lasting more than six months.

Treatment

No single medication treats the disorder itself; management emphasizes a steady relationship with one primary care physician, regular scheduled visits, avoidance of unnecessary tests and procedures, and treatment of co-occurring anxiety or depression. Cognitive behavioural therapy has evidence of benefit in reducing symptom distress and health anxiety. The condition is relatively common in primary care and is associated with high healthcare use and functional impairment.
