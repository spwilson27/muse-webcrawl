In traditional festive legend, Santa Claus's reindeer are magical flying reindeer who pull Santa Claus's sleigh through the night sky on Christmas Eve, delivering gifts to children around the world. The best known are the eight reindeer named in the 1823 poem "A Visit from St. Nicholas", with a ninth, Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer, added by popular culture in the 20th century.

The original eight

Clement Clarke Moore's poem lists the team called by name: "Now Dasher! now Dancer! now Prancer and Vixen! On, Comet! on, Cupid! on, Donder and Blitzen!" The final two names derive from Dutch (or German) words for thunder and lightning — donder and bliksem — and were later standardized as Donner and Blitzen. The poem fixed the image of the reindeer as tiny, matching its miniature sleigh and "little old driver".

Rudolph and later additions

Rudolph was created by copywriter Robert L. May for the Montgomery Ward department store in 1939 as a promotional storybook character whose glowing red nose guides the sleigh through fog; Johnny Marks's 1949 song version, recorded by Gene Autry, became a Christmas standard. Other works have proposed still more reindeer as jokes or minor characters, but the canonical team remains the eight plus Rudolph.

Biological notes

Commentators often note that, since male reindeer shed their antlers in winter while females retain theirs, an antlered Christmas team would most plausibly be female — or castrated males, which also keep their antlers. Reindeer (Rangifer tarandus) are real Arctic deer long domesticated across northern Eurasia, though none are known to fly.
