Earth-grazing fireball

An Earth-grazing fireball is a very bright meteor that enters the Earth's atmosphere at a shallow angle, travels a long path through the upper atmosphere, and then exits back into space rather than burning up completely or striking the ground. Such objects are also sometimes called Earth grazers.

Characteristics

Because the trajectory is nearly tangent to the atmosphere, an Earth-grazing fireball can remain visible for an unusually long time and travel hundreds or even thousands of kilometres. Friction with the air slows the object and alters its orbit around the Sun, so it leaves the atmosphere on a changed path.

Notable events

The Great Daylight Fireball of 1972, observed over the western United States and Canada on 10 August 1972, is the best-known example; a meteoroid passed within about 58 kilometres of the surface before returning to space. On 13 October 1990, a smaller object grazed the atmosphere over Czechoslovakia and Poland and was recorded by cameras of the European Fireball Network, allowing scientists to compute its orbit before and after the encounter.
