The Israeli–Palestinian conflict is an ongoing political and armed conflict over land and self-determination between Israelis and Palestinians in the region of Palestine/Israel. It is one of the world's longest-running conflicts, with roots in competing national movements that emerged in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.

Origins

Zionist immigration to Ottoman and then British-ruled Palestine, Palestinian Arab opposition to it, and Britain's conflicting wartime promises set the stage. The UN proposed partition in 1947; Israel declared independence in 1948, and the ensuing war displaced hundreds of thousands of Palestinians.

Later developments

Israel occupied the West Bank and Gaza Strip in the 1967 Six-Day War. The Oslo Accords of the 1990s created the Palestinian Authority and a framework for negotiation, but a final-status agreement has not been reached, and violence — including uprisings (intifadas), wars in Gaza, and settlement expansion — has continued into the 21st century.
