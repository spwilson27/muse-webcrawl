Vertical launching system

A vertical launching system (VLS) is an advanced system for holding and firing missiles on mobile naval platforms, such as surface ships and submarines. Each vertical launching system consists of a number of cells, which can hold one or more missiles ready for fire. Typically, each cell can carry a different type of missile, allowing the ship flexibility to load the best set for any given mission. When the missile is launched, in a "hot launch" the missile exits the cell under its own thrust; in a "cold launch" the missile is ejected by gas pressure and ignites once clear of the ship.

History and major types

The concept was developed most prominently by the United States Navy: the Mark 41 VLS, introduced in 1986 aboard USS Bunker Hill, became the standard launcher of US and allied cruisers and destroyers and can fire anti-air, anti-submarine, land-attack (Tomahawk) and ballistic-missile-defence interceptors from the same cells. Other systems include the European Sylver, the Russian 3S14, and submarine launch tubes. Compared with trainable deck launchers, VLS offers rapid all-round fire, reduced radar signature, and a larger, more flexible magazine.
