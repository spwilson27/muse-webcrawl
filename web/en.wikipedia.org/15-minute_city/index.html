15-minute city

The 15-minute city is an urban planning concept in which most daily necessities and services — work, shopping, education, healthcare and leisure — can be reached within a quarter of an hour on foot or by bicycle from any point in the city.

History

The term was coined by Franco-Colombian scientist Carlos Moreno in 2016 and popularised by Paris mayor Anne Hidalgo, who made it central to her 2020 re-election campaign and the transformation of Paris. Similar ideas appear in earlier new urbanism and in Clarence Perry's 1920s "neighbourhood unit."

Features and reception

Key principles are proximity, density, diversity and ubiquity, supported by mixed-use zoning, complete streets and local amenities. Supporters cite reduced car use, lower emissions and stronger neighbourhood life; critics question feasibility in low-density suburbs, and the concept has attracted misinformation and conspiracy theories claiming it would restrict residents' movement, which planners deny.
