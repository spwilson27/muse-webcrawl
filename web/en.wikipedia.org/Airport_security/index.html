Airport security

Airport security refers to the techniques and methods used to protect passengers, staff, aircraft, and airport property from accidental or malicious harm, crime, terrorism, and other threats. Measures include screening of passengers and baggage with metal detectors, X-ray and CT scanners, explosive-trace detection, identity checks, perimeter control, and surveillance. International standards are set by the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO) under Annex 17.

History

Screening was introduced in response to the hijacking wave of the late 1960s and early 1970s; the United States made passenger screening mandatory in 1973, and matching checked baggage to passengers followed disasters such as the 1988 bombing of Pan Am Flight 103 over Lockerbie. After the September 11 attacks, the U.S. federalized screening under the new TSA, and measures expanded to include shoe removal, liquid restrictions (after the 2006 transatlantic plot), full-body scanners, and behavioural detection. Trusted-traveller programmes such as TSA PreCheck and Global Entry expedite screening for vetted passengers.

Criticism

Critics describe some measures as "security theatre" that imposes cost and inconvenience disproportionate to the risk addressed, and body scanners have raised privacy and health debates. Defenders argue that layered, visible security deters attacks and has coincided with a sharp decline in successful aviation terrorism.
