The Mellotron is an electro-mechanical musical keyboard instrument developed in Birmingham, England, in 1963, which evolved from the earlier American Chamberlin instrument. Each key presses a length of magnetic tape strip against a playback head, reproducing a pre-recorded sound such as strings, choir, or flute.

Operation

Inside the instrument, each of the 35 or more keys is connected to a strip of tape roughly eight seconds long. When a key is pressed, the tape is drawn across a playback head by a rotating capstan; when released, a spring returns the tape to its start position. Pressing harder on later models could vary the volume, and a pitch control allowed slight tuning changes. Different tape frames could be fitted to change the available sounds.

History and use

The Mellotron became a signature sound of progressive rock. The Beatles used it on "Strawberry Fields Forever" (1966), whose opening flute introduction was played by Paul McCartney. The Moody Blues, King Crimson, Genesis, and Yes made extensive use of its string and choir tapes. Its unstable, wavering playback quality gave recordings a haunting character that later samplers and digital recreations have sought to emulate.

Legacy

Although heavy, temperamental, and expensive, the Mellotron is regarded as a landmark instrument — effectively an analog sampler decades before digital sampling. Modern versions and digital emulations remain in production.
