Continental Motors, Inc., doing business as Continental Aerospace Technologies, is an American aircraft engine manufacturer located at the Mobile International Airport in Mobile, Alabama, United States. The company produces air-cooled, horizontally opposed piston engines for general aviation aircraft, as well as diesel (Jet A fueled) engines derived from automotive technology.

History

The company traces its origins to 1905, when it was founded as an automobile and industrial engine builder in Muskegon, Michigan. Continental supplied engines to many early American automobile manufacturers. It entered the aircraft engine business in the 1920s, producing the A-40 and the radial engines used in training aircraft. During World War II Continental built engines under license, including Rolls-Royce Merlin V-12s for fighter aircraft.

After the war, Continental's opposed-piston engines — the C series, O-200, O-300, IO-360, IO-520 and IO-550 families — became standard equipment in Cessna, Beechcraft and Piper general aviation aircraft. Teledyne acquired the company in 1969, creating Teledyne Continental Motors. In 2011 the company was sold to AVIC (Aviation Industry Corporation of China) interests, and its headquarters and principal production moved to Mobile, Alabama.

Products

Current production includes the gasoline O-200, IO-360, IO-470, IO-520 and IO-550 families, the CD-135 and CD-155 diesel engines (derived from automotive diesel designs), and the Titan experimental engine line. Continental also operates a network of service centers and supplies factory rebuilt and new replacement engines.
