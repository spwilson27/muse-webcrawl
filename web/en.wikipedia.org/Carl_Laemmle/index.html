Carl Laemmle

Carl Laemmle
Born: 17 January 1867, Laupheim, German Empire
Died: 24 September 1939, Los Angeles, California, U.S.
Occupation: Film producer
Known for: Founding Universal Pictures

Carl Laemmle (17 January 1867 – 24 September 1939) was a German-American film producer and the founder of Universal Pictures. Born in Laupheim in the Kingdom of Württemberg, he emigrated to the United States in 1884.

Career

Laemmle began in the nickelodeon business in Chicago, then moved into film distribution and production. In 1909 he founded the Independent Moving Pictures Company (IMP), defying the Edison Trust's monopoly. His legal and business battles with the Trust helped end its control of the industry. He was a pioneer of the star system, famously publicising actress Florence Lawrence — previously anonymous as "the Biograph Girl" — by name.

Universal

In 1912 Laemmle merged IMP with several smaller companies to form the Universal Film Manufacturing Company, later Universal Pictures. In 1915 he opened Universal City in California, then the largest film studio complex in the world. He gave early opportunities to figures including Irving Thalberg and supported his nephew Max Laemmle and son Carl Laemmle Jr. He lost control of Universal in 1936 after the costly production of Show Boat.

Philanthropy

In the 1930s Laemmle worked to help Jewish refugees escape Nazi Germany, personally guaranteeing affidavits for hundreds of families from his home town and region.
