Maa

Native to: Kenya, Tanzania

Ethnicity: Maasai, Samburu, Chamus peoples

Native speakers: approx. 1.5 million (2020s estimates)

Family: Nilo-Saharan > Eastern Sudanic > Nilotic > Eastern Nilotic > Lotuxo-Maa > Maa

Writing system: Latin

ISO 639-3: mas

Maa is a group of closely related Eastern Nilotic languages, or in some analyses a single language with several dialects, spoken by the Maasai and closely related peoples of southern Kenya and northern Tanzania. It is one of the best-known members of the Lotuxo–Maa branch of Eastern Nilotic, a group that also includes the more distantly related Lotuko and Bari languages of South Sudan and Uganda.

Varieties

The principal varieties of Maa include Maasai proper (spoken across the Rift Valley of Kenya and into northern Tanzania), Samburu (spoken by the Samburu people of the lowlands of northern Kenya), and Chamus (also written Ilchamus or Njemps), spoken around Lake Baringo. These varieties are largely mutually intelligible, and their speakers share a strongly pastoralist culture organised around cattle. The Maasai variety is sometimes treated as the standard form, and a written standard based on it has been promoted by language-development organisations and regional radio broadcasting.

Phonology and grammar

Maa is a tonal language with two level tones, high and low, which distinguish lexical items and mark grammatical categories. Its consonant inventory includes implosive consonants, and its vowel system is built around an advanced-tongue-root harmony distinction typical of Eastern Nilotic languages. Maa is a verb–subject–object language in its basic clause order, with prepositions, and it marks nouns for case: the nominative case is marked by tone, a pattern shared with other Eastern Nilotic languages such as Turkana. Nouns distinguish masculine, feminine and neuter/neuter-like genders, and number is expressed by a rich set of singular and plural suffixes.

Status

Although Maa remains vigorous in rural pastoralist communities, it is under pressure from Swahili and English, the official languages of Kenya and Tanzania, which dominate schooling, administration and media. Literacy materials, dictionaries and a translation of the Bible exist in Maasai, and community radio stations broadcast partly in the language.
