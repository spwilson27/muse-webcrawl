El Castillo, Chichen Itza

El Castillo (Spanish for 'The Castle'), also known as the Temple of Kukulcan, is a Mesoamerican step-pyramid that dominates the centre of the Chichen Itza archaeological site in the Mexican state of Yucatán. Built by the pre-Columbian Maya civilisation sometime between the 8th and 12th centuries CE, the pyramid served as a temple to the feathered-serpent deity Kukulcan and functions as a monumental calendar in stone.

Architecture

The structure is a square-based pyramid 30 metres high with nine terraced levels and stairways on all four sides; each stairway has 91 steps, which with the top platform make 365 — the number of days in the solar year. At the equinoxes, the play of light and shadow on the northern balustrade creates the appearance of a serpent descending the staircase, ending at the carved serpent heads at its base.

Interior and restoration

An earlier temple is preserved inside the present pyramid, containing a chacmool figure and a red-painted jaguar throne, once reached by an interior stairway. The pyramid was excavated and restored in the 20th century. Chichen Itza is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, and El Castillo was named one of the New7Wonders of the World in 2007.
