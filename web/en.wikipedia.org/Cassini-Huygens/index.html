Cassini–Huygens

Cassini–Huygens
Operators: NASA / ESA / ASI
Launch: 15 October 1997
Saturn arrival: 1 July 2004
End of mission: 15 September 2017

Cassini–Huygens, commonly called Cassini, was a space-research mission by NASA, the European Space Agency (ESA), and the Italian Space Agency (ASI) to send a space probe to study the planet Saturn and its system, including its rings and natural satellites. The mission comprised the Cassini orbiter and the Huygens lander, which touched down on Titan.

Mission

Launched aboard a Titan IVB/Centaur on 15 October 1997, Cassini entered orbit around Saturn on 1 July 2004 after a 6.7-year cruise with gravity assists from Venus, Earth, and Jupiter. On 25 December 2004 the Huygens probe separated, landing on Titan on 14 January 2005 — the most distant landing ever made.

Discoveries

Cassini revealed water-ice geysers erupting from Enceladus, feeding Saturn's E ring and indicating a subsurface ocean; mapped Titan's hydrocarbon lakes and seas; observed storms, a hexagonal jet stream at Saturn's north pole, and new moons and ring structures. The mission was extended twice (Equinox and Solstice missions) before the "Grand Finale": 22 orbits through the gap between Saturn and its rings, ending with a controlled atmospheric entry on 15 September 2017.

Namesakes

The orbiter was named for Giovanni Domenico Cassini, discoverer of four Saturnian moons and the Cassini Division; the lander for Christiaan Huygens, discoverer of Titan.
