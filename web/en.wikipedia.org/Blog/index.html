Blog

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A blog (a truncation of "weblog") is an informational website consisting of discrete, often informal diary-style text entries (posts). Posts are typically displayed in reverse chronological order, so that the most recent post appears first, at the top of the web page. Until 2009, blogs were usually the work of a single individual or a small group, and often covered a single subject; in the 2010s multi-author blogs (MABs) developed, with posts written by large numbers of authors and professionally edited.

History

Early blogs evolved from online diaries in the mid-1990s. Justin Hall, who began personal blogging in 1994 while a student at Swarthmore College, is recognised as one of the earliest bloggers. The term "weblog" was coined by Jorn Barger in December 1997; the shorter "blog" was coined by Peter Merholz in 1999. Dedicated platforms — Open Diary (1998), LiveJournal and Blogger (1999) — made publishing accessible to non-technical users, and WordPress (2003) became the dominant content management system. By the mid-2000s blogs had become a significant force in journalism and politics; subsequent years saw microblogging services such as Twitter (2006) and social media absorb much of blogs' former role.

Types

Blogs vary by genre — personal, political, business, fashion, travel, food, and technology blogs — and by media type: a blog comprising videos is a vlog, one of links a linklog, and one of photographs a photoblog. Many blogs feature reader comments, creating threaded conversations, and syndicate content through RSS feeds.

Impact

Blogs lowered barriers to publishing, influenced news cycles and elections, and gave rise to professional "blogger" careers and blog advertising networks. They also raised legal questions concerning defamation, anonymity, and employment.
