Tuva

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Tuva (Russian: Тува́), officially the Republic of Tuva, is a federal subject of Russia located in southern Siberia, bordering Mongolia to the south. Its capital is Kyzyl, which lies near the geographical centre of Asia, marked by an obelisk in the city. Tuva covers about 170,500 km² and has a population of roughly 330,000, the majority of them ethnic Tuvans, a Turkic people.

History

The region was ruled successively by Xiongnu, Turkic khaganates, the Mongol Empire and Qing China before becoming a Russian protectorate in 1914. In 1921 it became the Tuvan People's Republic (Tannu Tuva), a nominally independent state recognised only by the USSR and Mongolia; it was annexed by the Soviet Union in 1944. After 1991 it remained within the Russian Federation as a republic.

Geography

Tuva is a mountain-rimmed basin drained by the upper Yenisei River, ranging from dry steppe and taiga to alpine tundra. Its isolation — with no railway connection to the rest of Russia, though a line to Kyzyl has long been under construction — has preserved its environment and culture.

Culture

Tuva is internationally famous for throat singing (khöömei), in which a singer produces two or more pitches simultaneously; groups such as Huun-Huur-Tu and singer Sainkho Namtchylak have carried it worldwide. Shamanism and Tibetan Buddhism coexist in Tuvan spiritual life. The republic is also known to stamp collectors for its colourful 1920s–30s issues, and to physicists through Richard Feynman's celebrated fascination with "Tannu Tuva", recounted in Tuva or Bust!
