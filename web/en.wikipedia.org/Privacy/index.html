Privacy

Privacy is the ability of an individual or group to seclude themselves or information about themselves, and thereby express themselves selectively. The concept is closely linked to anonymity, confidentiality and security, and its boundaries vary across cultures. Modern debates centre on information privacy (or data privacy): the right to control how personal data is collected, used and shared, particularly in the digital environment.

Concepts and history

Samuel Warren and Louis Brandeis defined privacy in their influential 1890 article "The Right to Privacy" as "the right to be let alone". Alan Westin later described privacy as the claim of individuals to determine when, how and to what extent information about them is communicated. Privacy can be categorised into bodily, territorial, communications and information privacy. Anthropologists note that the desire for privacy in some form appears across societies, though its form — seclusion of the home, control of personal information, protection of the body — differs.

Legal protection

Privacy is recognised in Article 12 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and Article 8 of the European Convention on Human Rights. In the United States, constitutional privacy has been inferred from several amendments (as in Griswold v. Connecticut, 1965), while statute law protects specific domains such as health data. The European Union's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, 2018) is the most influential comprehensive data-protection regime, granting rights of access, rectification, erasure and data portability and imposing heavy fines. Many other jurisdictions, including California (CCPA/CPRA) and Brazil (LGPD), have enacted similar laws.

Technology and surveillance

The rise of the internet, smartphones, social media and large-scale data analytics has transformed privacy debates. Business models based on behavioural advertising, state mass-surveillance programmes revealed in 2013, data breaches and facial recognition have all prompted calls for stronger protection, as well as technical responses including encryption, privacy-enhancing technologies and privacy by design. Critics warn of a "privacy paradox", in which people express concern while continuing to share data freely, and of surveillance capitalism as an economic system built on extracting behavioural data.
