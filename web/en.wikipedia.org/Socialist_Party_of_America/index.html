Socialist Party of America

The Socialist Party of America (SPA) was a socialist political party in the United States founded in 1901 through the merger of the Social Democratic Party and elements of the Socialist Labor Party. Its most famous leader, Eugene V. Debs, ran for president five times, winning 6 percent of the national vote in 1912.

Peak years

At its pre–World War I peak the party had over 100,000 members, elected more than a thousand officials — including mayors of Milwaukee and other cities — and published hundreds of newspapers, notably the Appeal to Reason. Its platform demanded public ownership of railroads and utilities, labor protections, and women's suffrage.

Repression and split

The party's opposition to American entry into the First World War brought prosecution under the Espionage Act; Debs was imprisoned and ran his 1920 campaign from a federal penitentiary. In 1919 the party split over affiliation with the Communist International, its left wing departing to form American communist parties.

Later history

Under Norman Thomas, the party remained a visible if smaller force through the 1930s and 1940s. Disputes over the Cold War, civil rights, and the Democratic Party realignment strategy fragmented it; in 1972 it renamed itself Social Democrats, USA, and successor organizations including the Socialist Party USA and Democratic Socialists of America trace lineage to it.
