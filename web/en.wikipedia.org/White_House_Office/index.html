The White House Office is an entity within the Executive Office of the President of the United States (EOP). It is headed by the White House chief of staff and includes the president's closest staff, including the national security adviser, press secretary, White House counsel, and senior advisers.

Organisation

The office comprises units responsible for communications, legislative affairs, political strategy, public engagement, scheduling and advance, speechwriting, and the Staff Secretary, who controls the paper flow to the president. Staff of the White House Office serve at the pleasure of the president and generally require no Senate confirmation.

History

President Franklin D. Roosevelt created the White House Office in 1939 under Reorganization Plan No. 1, following the Brownlow Committee's famous conclusion that "the President needs help." Before that, presidents relied on a small personal secretariat funded irregularly by Congress.

Staff size

The size of the White House staff grew through the 20th century, from a handful of clerks under early presidents to several hundred employees in modern administrations, reflecting the expanding policy and communications demands of the presidency.
