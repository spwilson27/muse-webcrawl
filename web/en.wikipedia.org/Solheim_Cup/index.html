Solheim Cup

The Solheim Cup is a biennial golf tournament for professional women golfers, contested by teams representing Europe and the United States. Named after Karsten Solheim, founder of the PING equipment company, the first edition was held in 1990 at Lake Nona, Florida.

Format

The event follows the Ryder Cup model: twelve-player teams meet over three days in foursomes, fourball, and twelve singles matches, each worth one point (28 points in total). Teams qualify through points lists (LET for Europe, LPGA Tour for the US) plus captains' picks.

History

The United States has won the majority of editions, but Europe claimed landmark victories including a record win at Gleneagles in 2019 and a first away win in Colorado in 2013. The 2023 contest in Spain finished tied 14–14, letting Europe retain the cup; the US regained it in 2024 in Virginia.
