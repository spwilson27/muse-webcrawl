Yerevan (Armenian: Երևան) is the capital and largest city of Armenia and one of the world's oldest continuously inhabited cities. Situated along the Hrazdan River, Yerevan is the administrative, cultural, and industrial center of the country, home to roughly one third of Armenia's population.

History

Yerevan traces its origins to the Urartian fortress of Erebuni, founded in 782 BC by King Argishti I on the southeastern edge of the modern city. The fortress guarded the Ararat plain and its name is generally considered the source of the name "Yerevan". The city later passed through Persian, Arab, Seljuk, Mongol, and Ottoman hands. In 1828 it was ceded to the Russian Empire under the Treaty of Turkmenchay. After the First World War, Yerevan became the capital of the First Republic of Armenia in 1918; it was subsequently the capital of the Armenian SSR within the Soviet Union from 1920, and of the independent Republic of Armenia from 1991.

Geography

The city sits on the northeastern edge of the Ararat plain at an average elevation of about 990 metres above sea level. The Hrazdan River flows through a deep gorge dividing the city, and on clear days the twin peaks of Mount Ararat, lying across the border in Turkey, dominate the southwestern horizon.

Culture and landmarks

Yerevan's centre, laid out by architect Alexander Tamanian in the 1920s, is built largely of pink volcanic tuff, earning the city the nickname "the Pink City". Landmarks include Republic Square, the Cascade complex, the Matenadaran manuscript repository, the Tsitsernakaberd Armenian Genocide Memorial, and the Ararat cognac factory. The city hosts the Yerevan Opera Theatre, numerous museums, and several universities, including Yerevan State University, founded in 1919.

Transport

Zvartnots International Airport serves the city, and the Yerevan Metro, opened in 1981, currently runs a single line. Yerevan is the hub of Armenia's railway and road networks.
