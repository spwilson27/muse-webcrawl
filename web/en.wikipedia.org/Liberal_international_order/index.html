Liberal international order

The liberal international order (LIO), also called the rules-based international order, is the set of global, rule-based, structured relationships among states, based on political liberalism, economic liberalism and liberal internationalism, that has operated since the late 1940s.

Features

The order is associated with institutions founded after the Second World War — the United Nations, the Bretton Woods institutions (the International Monetary Fund and the World Bank), the GATT and later the World Trade Organization, and alliances such as NATO — together with commitments to open markets, multilateral cooperation, collective security and the promotion of democracy and human rights, underwritten especially by the United States.

Criticism and debate

Critics argue the order has always been partial and hierarchical, applied inconsistently by its leading powers. Since the 2010s, scholars and policymakers have debated challenges from rising powers, populism within liberal democracies, and great-power rivalry, and whether the order is in crisis or adapting.
