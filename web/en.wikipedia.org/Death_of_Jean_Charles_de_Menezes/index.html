Death of Jean Charles de Menezes

Jean Charles de Menezes (7 January 1978 – 22 July 2005) was a Brazilian man killed by officers of the London Metropolitan Police Service at Stockwell station on the London Underground. He had been misidentified as one of the fugitives involved in the previous day's failed bombing attempts, themselves carried out two weeks after the 7 July 2005 London bombings.

Events

De Menezes, an electrician who had lived in London since 2002, left his flat in Tulse Hill, which was under surveillance because it shared an address block linked to one of the suspects. A surveillance officer did not positively identify him, but he was followed to Stockwell station. Plain-clothes firearms officers boarded his train and shot him seven times in the head at close range under the "Operation Kratos" shoot-to-kill policy for suspected suicide bombers. Early official accounts — that he had worn a bulky coat, vaulted the ticket barrier, and run from police — were later shown to be wrong; he had walked normally and used his travel card.

Aftermath

The Independent Police Complaints Commission's Stockwell reports criticized police command failures. No individual officer was prosecuted; the Metropolitan Police was convicted in 2007 under health and safety law and fined. An inquest in 2008 returned an open verdict after the coroner ruled out unlawful killing. In 2016 the European Court of Human Rights ruled, in a divided decision, that the decision not to prosecute did not violate de Menezes's right to life. His family received compensation and a public apology, and a permanent memorial stands at Stockwell station.
