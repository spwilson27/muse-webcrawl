Flatbread

Flatbread
TypeBread
Main ingredientsFlour, water, salt
VariationsNaan, pita, tortilla, roti, chapati, focaccia, lavash, matzah

A flatbread is a bread made with flour; water, milk, yogurt, or other liquid; and salt, and then thoroughly rolled into flattened dough. Many flatbreads are unleavened, although some are leavened, such as pizza and pita bread. Flatbreads range from below one millimetre to a few centimetres thick so that they can be easily eaten without being sliced.

History

Flatbreads are among the earliest processed foods: evidence from Jordan's Black Desert (Shubayqa 1) shows charred flatbread remains baked by Natufian hunter-gatherers about 14,400 years ago, millennia before agriculture. Flatbreads were staples in ancient Egypt and Sumer, and remain central to cuisines across the Middle East, Central and South Asia, the Mediterranean and Latin America.

Varieties

Well-known flatbreads include Indian naan, roti and chapati; Middle Eastern pita and lavash; Mexican corn and flour tortillas; Italian focaccia and piadina; Ethiopian injera (made from teff); Scandinavian crispbreads; and Jewish matzah, an unleavened flatbread eaten at Passover.
