Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center

The Boeing Aviation Hangar, with aircraft displayed on the floor and suspended from the ceiling.

The Steven F. Udvar-Hazy Center, also called the Udvar-Hazy Center, is the Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum's annex near Washington Dulles International Airport in Chantilly, Virginia. Opened in December 2003, it displays thousands of aviation and space artefacts — including aircraft far too large for the museum's building on the National Mall in Washington, D.C.

History

The centre is named for businessman Steven F. Udvar-Házy, co-founder of the International Lease Finance Corporation, whose $65 million donation to the Smithsonian in 1999 made the project possible. A second phase, completed in the early 2010s, added restoration, archives and collections-storage facilities including the Mary Baker Engen Restoration Hangar.

Collection

Two vast hangars — the Boeing Aviation Hangar and the James S. McDonnell Space Hangar — display hundreds of aircraft and spacecraft at multiple levels. Signature exhibits include the Space Shuttle Discovery, which arrived in 2012; the Enola Gay, the B-29 that dropped the first atomic bomb; a Lockheed SR-71 Blackbird; a Concorde; and the Boeing B-29 and 707 prototype (Dash 80). An observation tower overlooks Dulles Airport operations, and the centre houses an IMAX theatre.
