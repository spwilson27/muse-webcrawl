Pedology

Pedology (from Greek: πέδον, pedon, "soil"; and λόγος, logos, "study") is the study of soils in their natural environment. It is one of two main branches of soil science, the other being edaphology, which is concerned with the influence of soils on living things. Pedology deals with pedogenesis, soil morphology, and soil classification, while edaphology focuses on how soils influence plants, animals, and human use.

History

The discipline was founded by the Russian geologist Vasily Dokuchaev, who in the 1880s recognised soil as an independent natural body formed by the combined action of climate, organisms, relief, parent material, and time—the five soil-forming factors later formalised by Hans Jenny as the CLORPT model. Dokuchaev's zonal concept underpins modern soil classification systems such as Soil Taxonomy and the World Reference Base.

Concepts

Pedologists describe soils by their horizons—layers such as the organic O, topsoil A, eluvial E, illuvial B, and parent-material C horizons—and map their distribution across landscapes. Applications include land-use planning, agriculture, archaeology, and predicting soil responses to climate change.
