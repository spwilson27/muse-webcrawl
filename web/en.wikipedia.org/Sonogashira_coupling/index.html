Sonogashira coupling

The Sonogashira coupling is a cross-coupling reaction used in organic synthesis to form carbon–carbon bonds between a terminal alkyne and an aryl or vinyl halide. It employs a palladium catalyst as well as a copper co-catalyst and an amine base, and is performed under mild conditions, which has made it one of the most widely used reactions for preparing enynes and arylalkynes.

The reaction was reported in 1975 by Kenkichi Sonogashira and co-workers, who showed that the combination of palladium and copper iodide catalysis greatly improved on earlier alkyne coupling methods such as the Stephens–Castro coupling, which required harsh stoichiometric copper reagents.

Mechanism

The reaction proceeds through two interlocking catalytic cycles: a palladium cycle of oxidative addition, transmetalation, and reductive elimination, and a copper cycle in which the terminal alkyne is deprotonated and converted to a copper acetylide that transfers the alkynyl group to palladium. A common side reaction is the Glaser homocoupling of two alkyne molecules, usually suppressed by excluding oxygen.

Applications

Sonogashira couplings are used in the synthesis of pharmaceuticals, natural products, conjugated polymers, and molecular materials. Copper-free variants have been developed for substrates sensitive to copper or prone to homocoupling. The reaction belongs to the family of palladium-catalyzed cross-couplings recognized by the 2010 Nobel Prize in Chemistry awarded for the related Heck, Negishi, and Suzuki reactions.
