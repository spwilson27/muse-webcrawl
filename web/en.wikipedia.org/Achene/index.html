Achene

An achene is a type of dry fruit produced by many species of flowering plants. Achenes are monocarpellate (formed from one carpel) and indehiscent (they do not open at maturity). They contain a single seed that nearly fills the pericarp (fruit wall), but does not adhere to it — inside, the seed coat is free of the pericarp except at a single point, the funiculus.

Examples

Familiar examples include the "seeds" on the outside of a strawberry, which are true achenes embedded on a fleshy receptacle; the fruits of buttercups (Ranunculus), in which the achenes are clustered; cannabis and hop fruits; and the cypselae of the sunflower family, which are often loosely called achenes. Sunflower and "seed" heads of dandelions consist of many small achenes, the dandelion's being topped by a parachute-like pappus adapted for wind dispersal.

Related fruit types

  • Utricle: like an achene, but the pericarp is loose and bladdery, as in quinoa and amaranth.
  • Caryopsis (grain): like an achene, except the pericarp is fused to the seed coat; typical of grasses such as wheat and maize.
  • Cypsela: a dry one-seeded fruit derived from an inferior ovary, characteristic of the Asteraceae (sunflower family).
  • Samara: an achene with a wing, as in elm, ash and maple.
