Nylon 6

[Image placeholder: Nylon 6]

Nylon 6 (or polycaprolactam) is a polymer developed by Paul Schlack at IG Farben in Germany in 1938 as a repro of the nylon 66 fibre without violating the DuPont patent. It is a semicrystalline polyamide made by ring-opening polymerization of caprolactam, each monomer unit contributing six carbon atoms, hence the name.

Properties and uses

Nylon 6 fibres are tough, elastic and resistant to abrasion, and are used in textiles, carpets, clothing, tyre cords and ropes. As an engineering plastic it is used for gears, bearings, fittings and automotive parts, and it can be melt-processed by extrusion, injection moulding and spinning. Unlike nylon 66, which is made from two monomers, nylon 6 derives from a single monomer, and its properties — including a somewhat lower melting point (about 220 °C) — differ slightly.

History

Marketed in Germany under the name Perlon, nylon 6 became one of the most widely produced synthetic polymers in the world.
