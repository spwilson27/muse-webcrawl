Amerigo Vespucci

Born9 March 1454, Florence, Italy
Died22 February 1512 (aged 57), Seville, Spain
Known forDemonstrating that the New World was a separate continent

Amerigo Vespucci (9 March 1454 – 22 February 1512) was an Italian merchant, explorer, and navigator from the Republic of Florence, from whose name the term "America" is derived. He became a Spanish citizen in 1505.

Between 1497 and 1504, Vespucci participated in at least two voyages of the Age of Discovery, first on behalf of Spain (1499–1500) and then for Portugal (1501–1502). In 1503 and 1505, two booklets were published under his name — the Mundus Novus and the Letter to Soderini — containing colorful descriptions of these explorations and other alleged voyages. Both publications were extremely popular and widely read across much of Europe.

Legacy

Although historians still dispute the number and origins of Vespucci's voyages, he came to be seen by contemporaries as the man who first recognized that the lands across the Atlantic were not Asia's eastern outskirts but an entirely separate continent — a "New World". In 1507, cartographer Martin Waldseemüller produced a world map on which he named the new continent America, after the Latin feminine form of Vespucci's first name. In 1508, Vespucci was appointed chief navigator (piloto mayor) of Spain, a post he held until his death in Seville in 1512.
