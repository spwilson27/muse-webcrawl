Cocaine

Cocaine is a tropane alkaloid obtained from the leaves of the coca plants Erythroxylum coca and Erythroxylum novogranatense, native to western South America. It is a powerful stimulant of the central nervous system and a local anaesthetic. Cocaine hydrochloride, a white crystalline powder, is usually insufflated ("snorted") or injected, while the freebase form known as crack cocaine is smoked. Cocaine is among the most widely trafficked illicit drugs in the world and is classified internationally as a controlled substance, though it retains limited medical use as a topical anaesthetic in ear, nose, and throat surgery.

History

Andean peoples have chewed coca leaves for thousands of years for their mild stimulant effect and to combat altitude sickness. Cocaine was first isolated in 1860 by the German chemist Albert Niemann. In the 1880s it was promoted in Europe and America by figures including Sigmund Freud, who published Über Coca (1884), and was used in tonics, wines such as Vin Mariani, and the original formulation of Coca-Cola, from which the cocaine was removed by 1903. Karl Koller introduced it as a local anaesthetic in eye surgery in 1884. Growing recognition of its addictiveness led to prohibition, including the United States Harrison Narcotics Tax Act of 1914 and the 1961 Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs.

Pharmacology

Cocaine blocks the reuptake of the monoamine neurotransmitters dopamine, serotonin, and norepinephrine by binding their transporter proteins, prolonging and intensifying their signalling. The dopamine transporter blockade in the mesolimbic reward system underlies its euphoriant and reinforcing effects. Cocaine also blocks voltage-gated sodium channels, which produces local anaesthesia but also contributes to dangerous cardiac arrhythmias. Effects of a single dose typically last 15–60 minutes depending on route; the drug is metabolised mainly to benzoylecgonine, detectable in urine for several days.

Health effects and dependence

Short-term effects include euphoria, increased energy and alertness, dilated pupils, raised heart rate and blood pressure, and decreased appetite. Adverse effects range from anxiety, paranoia, and tremor to hyperthermia, seizures, myocardial infarction, and stroke; the combination with alcohol forms cocaethylene, which is more cardiotoxic and longer-acting. Cocaine has high dependence liability, with compulsive redosing and intense craving; chronic use can cause nasal septum damage (from insufflation), lung injury (from smoking), and persistent psychiatric symptoms. No approved pharmacotherapy exists for cocaine dependence; treatment is principally psychosocial, with contingency management showing the strongest evidence.

Production and trade

Coca cultivation for cocaine is concentrated in Colombia, Peru, and Bolivia. Leaves are processed into coca paste and cocaine base in jungle laboratories and refined into cocaine hydrochloride for export, principally to North America and Europe. Global production reached record levels in the early 2020s according to the UN Office on Drugs and Crime, and cocaine is the second most used illicit drug worldwide after cannabis.
