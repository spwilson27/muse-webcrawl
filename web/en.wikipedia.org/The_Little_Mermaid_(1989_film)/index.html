The Little Mermaid is a 1989 American animated musical fantasy film produced by Walt Disney Feature Animation, loosely based on Hans Christian Andersen's 1837 fairy tale of the same name (see The Little Mermaid).

Plot

Ariel, a sixteen-year-old mermaid princess, longs to live on land. After rescuing Prince Eric from a shipwreck, she makes a bargain with the sea witch Ursula, trading her voice for human legs for three days. With the help of Flounder and Sebastian the crab, Ariel ultimately defeats Ursula and marries Eric.

Music and reception

The film's songs, including "Under the Sea", "Part of Your World", and "Kiss the Girl", were composed by Alan Menken with lyrics by Howard Ashman. "Under the Sea" won the Academy Award for Best Original Song. The film is credited with beginning the Disney Renaissance, a period of renewed success for Disney animation.
