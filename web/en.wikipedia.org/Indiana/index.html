Indiana

Indiana is a state in the Midwestern region of the United States. It borders Lake Michigan to the northwest, Michigan to the north and northeast, Ohio to the east, the Ohio River and Kentucky to the south and southeast, and the Wabash River and Illinois to the west. Nicknamed "the Hoosier State", Indiana is the 38th-largest state by area and the 17th-most populous, with a population of about 6.8 million. Its capital and largest city is Indianapolis.

History

Indiana was inhabited by Indigenous peoples for thousands of years before European colonization. The territory became a crossroads of French, British, and American interests in the 18th century; figures such as George Rogers Clark campaigned there during the American Revolutionary War. The Indiana Territory was created in 1800, and Indiana was admitted to the Union as the 19th state on December 11, 1816. Its name means "Land of the Indians". During the Civil War, Indiana was a staunch Union state. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries, the discovery of natural gas and the growth of manufacturing transformed its economy.

Geography

Indiana's terrain ranges from the dunes and wetlands along Lake Michigan in the north, across the flat, fertile central till plain — some of the best farmland in the Corn Belt — to the unglaciated, hilly country of the south along the Ohio River. The highest point is Hoosier Hill at 383 m (1,257 ft). Major rivers include the Ohio, the Wabash, the White, and the St. Joseph.

Economy and culture

Indiana's economy combines agriculture (especially corn and soybeans), manufacturing — it is a leading producer of steel, motor vehicle parts, and pharmaceutical products — and logistics, reflecting its motto as the "Crossroads of America". The state is widely known for the Indianapolis 500 automobile race, held annually at the Indianapolis Motor Speedway, and for its passionate basketball culture, dramatized in the film Hoosiers. Residents are called Hoosiers, a nickname of uncertain origin dating to the 1830s.
