Associated Press


AP headquarters in New York City.

The Associated Press (AP) is an American not-for-profit news agency headquartered in New York City. Founded in May 1846, it operates as a cooperative, unincorporated association owned by its contributing newspapers and broadcasters in the United States.

History

The AP was formed by six New York newspapers that pooled the cost of gathering news from the Mexican–American War by telegraph. The cooperative model let member papers share the expense of newsgathering, and the AP became the dominant American wire service. In the landmark 1945 antitrust case Associated Press v. United States, the Supreme Court ruled against restrictive membership practices that excluded competing newspapers.

AP journalists have won more than fifty Pulitzer Prizes, including dozens for photography. The agency's Stylebook is the standard reference for American journalism usage.

Operations

The AP operates hundreds of bureaus around the world and produces news reports in text, photo, video and interactive formats in several languages. Its content is published by thousands of newspapers, broadcasters and websites, reaching a large share of the world's population each day. AP reporters also conduct the well-known AP poll of college football and basketball rankings in the United States.
