Anoxia

Anoxia is a condition in which there is a complete absence of oxygen supply to an organ or tissue, or to an environment. It is distinguished from hypoxia, in which oxygen supply is reduced but not eliminated. The term is used in medicine, physiology, and environmental and geological sciences.

Medicine

In medicine, anoxia most often refers to severe oxygen deprivation of the brain (cerebral anoxia), which can result from cardiac arrest, severe asthma, carbon monoxide poisoning, drowning or complications at birth (perinatal anoxia). Brain cells are exceptionally sensitive: irreversible injury can begin within minutes, and outcomes range from full recovery to permanent cognitive impairment or death. Treatment focuses on rapidly restoring oxygenation and circulation.

Environmental science

Anoxic waters are areas of seawater, freshwater or groundwater depleted of dissolved oxygen. Such conditions occur naturally in stratified basins such as the deep Black Sea — the world's largest anoxic water body — and can be worsened by nutrient pollution, which fuels algal blooms whose decomposition consumes oxygen (eutrophication). Anoxic "dead zones", such as the one in the northern Gulf of Mexico, cannot support most marine life.

Geology

Oceanic anoxic events in the geological record are intervals when large parts of the oceans became depleted in oxygen, often coinciding with mass extinctions and the deposition of organic-rich black shales that later became oil source rocks.
