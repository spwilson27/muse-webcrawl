Ambracia

Ambracia (Greek: Ἀμβρακία, Ambrakia) was a city of ancient Greece on the site of modern Arta, in Epirus, situated on the northern bank of the Ambracian Gulf near the mouth of the Arachthus River. It was founded in the late 7th century BCE as a colony of Corinth and became a prosperous port and member of the Hellenistic world.

History

Ambracia thrived on trade and sent ships to the Battle of Salamis. It was ruled in the early 3rd century BCE by Pyrrhus of Epirus, who made it his capital and adorned it with palaces and temples. The city was captured by the Aetolians, then taken by the Roman consul Marcus Fulvius Nobilior in 189 BCE after a famous siege involving early use of military mining. Under Augustus, its inhabitants were transferred to the new city of Nicopolis after 31 BCE, and Ambracia declined, though it survived into Byzantine times as Arta.

Legacy

The Ambracian Gulf (modern Gulf of Arta) preserves the city's name. Remains in Arta include stretches of massive polygonal walls, a small theatre and temple foundations. The 17th-century Bridge of Arta is the subject of a famous Greek folk ballad.
