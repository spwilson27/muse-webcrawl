Tanit (Punic: 𐤕𐤍𐤕, Tīnīt) was a chief goddess of Carthage, worshipped alongside her consort Baal Hammon. As a sky goddess and symbol of motherhood and fertility, she bore the epithet "Face of Baal" and was the pre-eminent deity of the Carthaginian pantheon from the 5th century BCE.

Cult

Tanit was venerated in tophets — sacred precincts such as the Tophet of Carthage at Salammbô — where urns containing cremated remains of infants and animals were buried. Whether these remains represent child sacrifice, as Greco-Roman sources claimed, remains a subject of scholarly debate.

Sign of Tanit

Her symbol, a geometric figure combining a triangle, a horizontal bar with raised ends, and a disc, appears on stelae, mosaics, and amulets across the Punic world, from Carthage to Sardinia, Sicily, and Ibiza.
