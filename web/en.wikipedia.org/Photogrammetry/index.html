Photogrammetry

Photogrammetry is the science and technology of obtaining reliable information about physical objects and the environment through the process of recording, measuring, and interpreting photographic images. Its best-known application is the production of topographic maps and 3D models from aerial and terrestrial photographs.

Principles

Photogrammetry relies on the geometry of perspective projection: by measuring the same point in two or more photographs taken from different positions, its three-dimensional coordinates can be computed by triangulation. Modern digital methods use structure-from-motion algorithms, which automatically identify corresponding points across many images, estimate camera positions, and produce dense point clouds and textured meshes.

Applications

The technique is used in cartography, surveying, architecture, archaeology (recording sites non-invasively), forensic science, film and video-game production, and cultural-heritage preservation. Close-range photogrammetry measures objects from millimetres to metres in size, while aerial photogrammetry — historically performed with specialized metric cameras in aircraft — now commonly uses drones.

History

The French officer Aimé Laussedat developed the first photogrammetric methods in the 1850s. Aerial photogrammetry expanded rapidly after the First World War and was a mainstay of map-making throughout the 20th century, before digital workflows and satellite imagery transformed the field.
