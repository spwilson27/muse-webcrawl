Shingle beach

A shingle beach, also known as a pebble beach, is a beach which is armoured with pebbles or small- to medium-sized cobbles (as opposed to fine sand). Typically, the stone composition may grade from characteristic sizes ranging from 2 to 200 millimetres diameter.

Shingle beaches are found in many parts of the world, including the coasts of Britain, New Zealand, and Japan. Notable examples include Chesil Beach in Dorset, England, an 18-mile-long tombolo of flint pebbles.

Such beaches typically have steeper profiles than sandy beaches and support distinctive plant communities, such as the vegetated shingle at Dungeness in Kent, one of the most important shingle habitats in Europe.
