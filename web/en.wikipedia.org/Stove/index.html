Stove

A stove or range is a device that burns fuel or uses electricity to generate heat inside or on top of the apparatus. The most common types are the kitchen stove for cooking food and the room-heating stove. Kitchen stoves rely on the application of direct heat for the cooking process and often include an oven; heating stoves warm a room by radiation and convection.

History

Enclosed stoves developed from open hearths over centuries. The cast-iron Franklin stove (1742) improved heating efficiency, and the Oberlin stove (1834) popularized the cast-iron cooking range. Gas stoves spread in the 19th century once town gas networks existed, and electric stoves followed in the early 20th century; induction cooktops arrived in the late 20th century.

Types

Cooking stoves may be fuelled by wood, coal, gas, or electricity; cooktops include coil, smooth-top ceramic, and induction types. Heating stoves include wood-burning stoves, pellet stoves, and masonry heaters. Portable camp stoves burn gas, liquid fuel, or solid fuel tablets.

Safety and efficiency

Stoves involve risks of burns, fire, and carbon monoxide poisoning from combustion appliances; modern standards require ventilation, flame-failure devices, and efficiency ratings. See also oven, induction cooking, and wood-burning stove.
