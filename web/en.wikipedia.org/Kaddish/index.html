Kaddish

Kaddish (Aramaic: קדיש, "holy" or "sanctification") is a hymn of praise to God found in Jewish prayer services. Written primarily in Aramaic, it magnifies and sanctifies God's name and expresses hope for the establishment of God's kingdom on earth. Although strongly associated in popular culture with mourning, the Kaddish itself makes no mention of death; it is the Mourner's Kaddish that is recited by mourners.

Forms

Several versions of the prayer exist: the Half Kaddish (Hatzi Kaddish), which separates sections of the service; the Full Kaddish (Kaddish Shalem), recited after the Amidah; the Mourner's Kaddish (Kaddish Yatom), said by those in mourning and on the anniversary of a death (yahrzeit); the Rabbinical Kaddish (Kaddish de-Rabbanan), following study; and the Burial Kaddish, a longer form used at funerals and at the completion of a Talmudic tractate.

Usage

Kaddish can only be recited publicly in the presence of a minyan, a quorum of ten Jewish adults. Mourners traditionally recite the Mourner's Kaddish daily for eleven months after the death of a parent, and for thirty days for other close relatives, and thereafter on each yahrzeit. The central response of the congregation, "Amen, may His great name be blessed forever and for all eternity," is regarded as an especially powerful affirmation.

Cultural references

The prayer has inspired works such as Allen Ginsberg's long poem "Kaddish" (1961) and Leonard Bernstein's Symphony No. 3, subtitled "Kaddish".
