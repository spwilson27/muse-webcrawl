Angels Flight

Angels Flight is a landmark narrow-gauge funicular railway in the Bunker Hill district of downtown Los Angeles, California. Billed as "the shortest railway in the world," it carries passengers up a steep 96-metre (315 ft) incline between Hill Street and Olive Street.

History

The railway opened on 31 December 1901, built by Colonel J. W. Eddy, with two counterbalanced cars, Olivet and Sinai, connected by a cable. It charged one cent a ride and served the fashionable residences of Bunker Hill. It closed in 1969 when the neighbourhood was demolished for redevelopment; the cars were stored for nearly three decades.

Restoration

Angels Flight reopened in 1996 half a block south of its original site. A fatal 2001 accident, in which car Sinai rolled back and killed a passenger, closed it until 2010. After further safety investigations and repairs following a 2013 derailment, it reopened again on Labor Day 2017. The railway appears in numerous films and novels set in Los Angeles, including the film La La Land.
