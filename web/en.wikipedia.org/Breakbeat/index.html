Breakbeat

Breakbeat is a broad type of electronic music that tends to use drum breaks sampled from early recordings of funk, jazz, and R&B. It has been used in genres such as hip hop, jungle, drum and bass, hardcore, UK garage, and big beat.

History

In the 1970s, Bronx DJ Kool Herc pioneered the technique of looping the percussive "break" sections of funk records using two turntables, laying the foundation of hip hop. The most sampled break, the "Amen break" from the Winstons' 1969 track "Amen, Brother", became a building block of jungle and drum and bass.

Characteristics

Unlike the steady four-on-the-floor pulse of house and techno, breakbeat uses syncopated, sampled drum patterns, often at tempos between 110 and 150 beats per minute. Producers chop, pitch, and rearrange break samples to create new rhythms.

Subgenres

Notable styles include Florida breaks, nu skool breaks, progressive breaks, and breakcore. Artists associated with breakbeat include The Prodigy, Fatboy Slim, The Chemical Brothers, and Plump DJs.
