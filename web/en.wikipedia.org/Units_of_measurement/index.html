Unit of measurement

A unit of measurement, or unit of measure, is a definite magnitude of a quantity, defined and adopted by convention or by law, that is used as a standard for measurement of the same kind of quantity. Any other value of that quantity can be expressed as a simple multiple of the unit of measurement.

Systems of units

Systems of units include the International System of Units (SI), the modern form of the metric system, which defines seven base units (metre, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole, and candela) and derived units such as the newton and joule. Other systems include imperial units and United States customary units, still widely used in the United States, and natural units in physics.

History

Early units were based on the human body and local custom — cubits, feet, and grains — leading to inconsistency. The metric system, developed during the French Revolution, aimed for a rational, universal decimal system. The 2019 redefinition of the SI fixed all base units in terms of fundamental physical constants.

Conversion

Quantities can be converted between units using conversion factors; dimensional analysis checks that equations remain consistent. Mixed-unit practice has caused notable failures, such as the loss of NASA's Mars Climate Orbiter in 1999, attributed to confusion between pound-force seconds and newton seconds.
