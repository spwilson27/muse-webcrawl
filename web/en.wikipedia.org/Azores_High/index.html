Azores High

[Image placeholder: the subtropical high over the North Atlantic]

The Azores High, also known as the North Atlantic (subtropical) High or the Bermuda–Azores High, is a large semi-permanent centre of high atmospheric pressure typically found over the North Atlantic Ocean near the Azores. It is part of the belt of subtropical anticyclones that encircles the globe, and its western extension near Bermuda is often called the Bermuda High.

Behaviour

The high is produced by descending air of the Hadley circulation. It migrates seasonally: in winter it sits further south near the Azores, while in summer it strengthens, expands and shifts northwest toward Bermuda. Air circulates clockwise around the system in the Northern Hemisphere, feeding the trade winds on its southern flank and the westerlies on its northern flank.

Weather effects

The Azores High strongly influences the climate of Western Europe and North America. When it extends northeastward in summer it brings settled, warm, dry weather to Iberia and sometimes the British Isles; its position also steers Atlantic hurricanes toward the Caribbean or the US East Coast, or recurves them into the ocean. In winter, a displaced or weakened high can allow storm systems to track into Europe. Blocking patterns involving the high are implicated in European heatwaves and droughts, and its fluctuations are linked to the North Atlantic Oscillation.

Research

Studies indicate the Azores High has expanded since the industrial era, a trend climate models attribute to anthropogenic warming, with consequences including reduced rainfall in the western Mediterranean.
