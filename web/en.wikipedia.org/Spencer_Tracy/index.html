Spencer Tracy

Spencer Bonaventure Tracy (April 5, 1900 – June 10, 1967) was an American actor. A major star during the Golden Age of Hollywood, Tracy was known for his naturalistic acting style and versatility. He starred in 75 films over a career spanning from 1930 until 1967, and he was one of the first actors to receive nine Academy Award nominations for Best Actor, sharing the record. He won the award in two consecutive years, for Captains Courageous (1937) and Boys Town (1938).

Career

Tracy was discovered by director John Ford while performing on Broadway in The Last Mile (1930) and signed with Fox Film Corporation. He moved to Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer in 1935, where he became one of the studio's biggest stars. His notable films include Fury (1936), San Francisco (1936), Captains Courageous (1937), Boys Town (1938), and later Adam's Rib (1949), Father of the Bride (1950), Bad Day at Black Rock (1955), The Old Man and the Sea (1958), Inherit the Wind (1960), Judgment at Nuremberg (1961), It's a Mad, Mad, Mad, Mad World (1963), and Guess Who's Coming to Dinner (1967), completed shortly before his death.

Partnership with Katharine Hepburn

Tracy made nine films with Katharine Hepburn, beginning with Woman of the Year (1942) and ending with Guess Who's Coming to Dinner (1967). Their off-screen relationship lasted for 26 years until his death, although Tracy remained married to Louise Tracy, from whom he lived largely apart; the couple had two children. Tracy struggled with severe alcoholism throughout his adult life.

Legacy

Tracy was named the ninth-greatest male star of classic American cinema by the American Film Institute in 1999. He is regarded as one of the finest actors of his generation, and was nominated posthumously for the Academy Award for his final role.
