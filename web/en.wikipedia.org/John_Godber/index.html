John Godber

John Harry Godber OBE (born 18 July 1956) is an English playwright, director, and former teacher, best known for his observational comedies about working-class life in Northern England. He served as artistic director of Hull Truck Theatre from 1984 to 2010, transforming it into a nationally recognised company, and later founded the John Godber Company.

Plays

Godber's breakthrough came with Bouncers (1977), in which four actors play both nightclub doormen and their many customers; the play has been widely produced internationally. Other major works include Up 'n' Under (1984), about an inept amateur rugby league team, later filmed in 1998; Teechers (1987), a play-within-a-play about school drama; April in Paris (1992); The Debt Collector; and many plays written for Hull Truck and touring companies, characterised by direct address, physical ensemble playing, and minimal staging. He has also written extensively for television, including episodes of Grange Hill and Brookside.

Honours

Godber was appointed OBE in the 2004 New Year Honours for services to drama, and several of his plays are set texts in British schools and universities.
