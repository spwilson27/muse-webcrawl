Nerve

Nerve
SystemPeripheral nervous system
ComponentsAxons bundled in fascicles; endoneurium, perineurium, epineurium
FunctionTransmits sensory, motor, and autonomic signals between CNS and body

A nerve is an enclosed, cable-like bundle of nerve fibers — the axons of neurons — in the peripheral nervous system. Nerves carry electrochemical impulses between the central nervous system (brain and spinal cord) and the muscles, skin, glands, and internal organs. Sensory (afferent) nerves carry information toward the CNS, motor (efferent) nerves carry commands away from it, and most nerves are mixed, containing both.

Structure

Each axon, often wrapped in a myelin sheath produced by Schwann cells, is surrounded by a delicate layer of connective tissue, the endoneurium. Axons are grouped into bundles called fascicles, each wrapped by the tougher perineurium, which maintains a blood–nerve barrier. Fascicles and blood vessels are in turn bound together by the epineurium, the outer sheath of the whole nerve. Human nerves range from microscopic twigs to the thumb-thick sciatic nerve, the body's largest.

Classification

Nerves are named by their origin: the 12 pairs of cranial nerves emerge from the brain and brainstem, while 31 pairs of spinal nerves emerge from the spinal cord through intervertebral foramina, often recombining in plexuses (cervical, brachial, lumbar, sacral) that supply the limbs. Nerve fibers are also classified by diameter and conduction velocity (Erlanger–Gasser classification, types A, B, C), reflecting function from fast proprioception to slow pain and autonomic fibers.

Injury and disease

Nerves may be injured by compression (as in carpal tunnel syndrome), stretch, laceration, or ischemia. Seddon's classification grades injury from neurapraxia (transient conduction block, full recovery) through axonotmesis (axon divided, wallerian degeneration, regrowth possible at roughly 1 mm/day) to neurotmesis (complete division, requiring surgical repair). Diseases of nerves (neuropathies) — diabetic, toxic, inflammatory (e.g., Guillain–Barré syndrome), hereditary — typically cause numbness, pain, and weakness in characteristic distributions. Blocking nerve conduction pharmacologically is the basis of local anesthetic nerve blocks.
