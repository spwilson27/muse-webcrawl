Taiwan

Taiwan, officially the Republic of China (ROC), is a country in East Asia. It is an island in the western Pacific Ocean, separated from mainland China by the Taiwan Strait, with the Philippines to the south and Japan to the northeast. Its capital is Taipei.

History

Indigenous Austronesian peoples inhabited Taiwan for thousands of years. Dutch and Spanish colonists arrived in the 17th century, followed by Han Chinese settlement under the Qing dynasty. Japan ruled Taiwan from 1895 to 1945. After the Chinese Civil War, the ROC government retreated to Taiwan in 1949, while the People's Republic of China was established on the mainland. Taiwan democratized in the late 1980s and 1990s, holding its first direct presidential election in 1996.

Geography

The main island is mountainous in the east, with the Central Mountain Range and peaks such as Yushan (3,952 m), and fertile plains in the west where most people live. The climate is subtropical and tropical, with typhoons in summer.

Politics and status

Taiwan's political status is disputed. The PRC claims Taiwan as part of its territory, while Taiwan maintains its own elected government, military and constitution. Most countries maintain unofficial relations with Taiwan.

Economy

Taiwan is a highly developed market economy, a global leader in semiconductors — notably TSMC — electronics, and information technology. See also China, Taipei.
