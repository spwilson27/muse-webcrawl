Castles and Town Walls of King Edward in Gwynedd
Location: Gwynedd and Conwy, Wales
Sites: Beaumaris, Caernarfon, Conwy, Harlech
UNESCO World Heritage Site (1986)

The Castles and Town Walls of King Edward in Gwynedd is a UNESCO World Heritage Site in north-west Wales, comprising the four castles of Beaumaris, Caernarfon, Conwy, and Harlech, together with the town walls of Caernarfon and Conwy. They were built during the reign of Edward I of England following his conquest of Wales in the late 13th century.

Construction

The castles were designed under the direction of James of St George, a master mason from Savoy, and formed part of Edward's "iron ring" of fortresses intended to secure English rule over the former principality of Gwynedd. Caernarfon, with its polygonal towers and banded masonry, was intended as a seat of royal power; Conwy and Harlech guarded strategic coastal and road routes, while Beaumaris, begun in 1295, was the last and most technically perfect concentric castle of the group.

Significance

UNESCO inscribed the site in 1986 as an outstanding example of late 13th- and early 14th-century military architecture in Europe, and as evidence of the colonisation and defence works carried out under Edward I.
