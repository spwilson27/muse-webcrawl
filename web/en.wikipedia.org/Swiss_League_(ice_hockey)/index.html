Swiss League (ice hockey)

The Swiss League (SL), known until 2017 as the National League B, is the second tier of the Swiss ice hockey league system, below the National League. It is administered by the Swiss Ice Hockey Federation and serves as the principal development and promotion pathway to the top division.

Format

Teams play a regular season of home and away fixtures, followed by playoffs to determine the league champion. The champion earns the right to contest promotion to the National League — historically through a series against the top league's last-placed team, with promotion subject to licensing and arena requirements, which has meant that sporting promotion is not always taken up.

History

The second division was founded in 1947 as the Nationalliga B. It has always been fully professional or semi-professional in character and has included historic clubs such as EHC Olten, HC La Chaux-de-Fonds, HC Ajoie, EHC Visp and SC Langenthal, several of which have spent seasons in the top flight. The league was rebranded as the Swiss League in 2017 as part of a naming reform of Swiss hockey leagues.

Role

The Swiss League acts as a bridge between the amateur MyHockey League and the elite National League, and hosts farm and development teams affiliated with top-league clubs alongside independent clubs with their own promotion ambitions.
