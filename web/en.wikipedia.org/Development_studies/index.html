Development studies

Development studies is an interdisciplinary branch of social science that examines the processes of social, economic, political, and environmental change in developing countries and the policies intended to improve living standards, reduce poverty, and promote sustainable growth.

History

The field emerged after the Second World War, when decolonisation created dozens of new states and Western governments and institutions such as the World Bank and the United Nations began systematic programmes of aid and modernisation. Early development theory was dominated by modernization theory, which held that poor countries would follow a path of industrialisation similar to that of Western Europe and North America. From the 1960s, dependency theory and later world-systems theory challenged this view, arguing that underdevelopment was produced by the structure of the global economy itself.

Key topics

Development studies covers poverty and inequality, economic growth, health and education, gender, governance, human rights, agriculture and food security, urbanisation, migration, and the environmental consequences of development. Approaches range from neoclassical economics to post-development critiques that question the very concept of development.

Institutions

Major research and teaching centres include the Institute of Development Studies at the University of Sussex, the London School of Economics, and Oxford's Department of International Development. International organisations such as UNDP, the World Bank, and regional development banks are major employers of development specialists.
