The United States Department of Energy (DOE) is an executive department of the US federal government that oversees US national energy policy and energy production, and manages the country's nuclear weapons programme, nuclear reactor production for the United States Navy, energy-related research, radioactive waste disposal, and domestic energy production. The DOE sponsors more basic and applied scientific research than any other US federal agency, largely through its system of national laboratories.

History

The department was created by the Department of Energy Organization Act, signed by President Jimmy Carter on August 4, 1977, and began operations on October 1, 1977, in response to the 1973 oil crisis. It consolidated the Federal Energy Administration, the Energy Research and Development Administration, the Federal Power Commission, and functions of several smaller agencies. Its lineage includes the Manhattan Project and the Atomic Energy Commission, which had developed the atomic bomb and governed nuclear energy after the Second World War.

Responsibilities

  • Nuclear security — through the semi-autonomous National Nuclear Security Administration (NNSA), maintaining the nuclear weapons stockpile and nonproliferation programmes
  • Research — the Office of Science funds the national laboratories, including Oak Ridge, Argonne, Lawrence Berkeley, Fermilab, and Brookhaven
  • Energy programmes — energy efficiency, renewable energy, fossil energy, and nuclear energy research and deployment; the Strategic Petroleum Reserve
  • Environmental management — cleanup of Cold War nuclear weapons production sites such as Hanford
