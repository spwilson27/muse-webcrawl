NGC 4038 Group


Labelled image: the Antennae Galaxies, the group's namesake pair

The NGC 4038 Group is a group of galaxies in the constellation Corvus, roughly 55–65 million light-years from Earth. The group takes its name from NGC 4038, one of the two interacting galaxies known as the Antennae Galaxies (NGC 4038 and NGC 4039), whose long tidal tails give them their insect-like appearance.

Members

Catalogues of nearby galaxy groups list roughly 13 to 27 members, depending on the membership criteria. Prominent members include the Antennae pair, the spiral galaxies NGC 4027 (a one-armed "magellanic" spiral), NGC 3981, NGC 4033, NGC 4036, and the edge-on spiral NGC 4013's group companions in some listings; dwarf and irregular members include NGC 3955, NGC 3956, NGC 3957, and NGC 4024. The group lies relatively close to the plane of the Milky Way in projection, complicating membership determination.

Context

The NGC 4038 Group is part of the wider cloud of galaxies in the direction of the Virgo Cluster and the Virgo Supercluster, in the region of the Crater and Corvus clouds. Its dominant members, the Antennae, are a classic example of a galaxy merger in progress and are heavily studied for their intense starburst activity, large populations of young massive star clusters, and extended tidal tails of stars and neutral hydrogen.
