Treaty of Nerchinsk

The Treaty of Nerchinsk of 1689 was the first treaty between the Tsardom of Russia and Qing-dynasty China, and one of the first treaties between China and a European power conducted on a basis of equality. It settled the border along the Amur River after armed clashes between Russian Cossacks and Qing forces over the fortress of Albazin.

Negotiations and terms

Negotiations were conducted in Latin with the help of Jesuit interpreters Jean-François Gerbillon and Thomas Pereira. Russia agreed to abandon Albazin and withdraw from the Amur basin; the border was set along the Argun River and the Stanovoy Range. In return, Russia gained the right to trade with China, and relations between the two empires were regularised. The treaty halted Russian expansion toward the Amur for roughly 170 years, until the Treaty of Aigun (1858) and the Convention of Peking (1860) redrew the boundary.
