Hard power

Hard power is the ability of a state or political actor to influence others through coercive means, principally military force and economic sanctions or inducements — the use of "carrots and sticks".

Concept

The term is most associated with political scientist Joseph Nye, who contrasted hard power with soft power: the ability to attract and persuade through culture, values, and policies. Nye later proposed "smart power" as an effective combination of both.

Elements

Hard power rests on military capability — armed forces, weapons, and alliances — and economic leverage such as trade access, aid, and sanctions. Its effectiveness depends on credibility and context. See also Soft power.
