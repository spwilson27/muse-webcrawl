Countersink

Countersink
TypeConical hole / cutting tool
Common angles82°, 90°, 100°, 110°, 120°
Used forFlat-head screws, rivets, deburring

A countersink is a conical hole cut into a manufactured object, or the cutter used to cut such a hole. Its most common purpose is to allow the head of a countersunk bolt, screw, or rivet to sit flush with or below the surface of the surrounding material.

Forms and angles

Countersinks are made in standard included angles matched to fastener heads: 90° is standard for metric flat-head screws, 82° for imperial (UNC/UNF) screws, and 100° is common for rivets in aircraft skin. Countersink cutters may be fluted, cross-hole ("zero-flute"), or combined with a drill as a counterbore-style tool.

Uses

Beyond flush fastening, countersinking is used to deburr the edge of a drilled hole, to provide a lead-in for taps and fasteners, and in woodworking to hide screw heads that may later be plugged. In printed circuit boards, countersinks allow component leads and connectors to sit properly.

Countersink vs. counterbore

A countersink produces a conical recess, whereas a counterbore produces a flat-bottomed cylindrical recess, typically for socket-head cap screws and washers. Choosing between them depends on the geometry of the fastener head to be accommodated.
