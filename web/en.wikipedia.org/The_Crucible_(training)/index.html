The Crucible (training)

TypeMilitary training exercise
ServiceUnited States Marine Corps
Duration54 hours
LocationsMCRD Parris Island; MCRD San Diego

The Crucible is the final test of United States Marine Corps recruit training. Introduced in 1996 by Commandant General Charles C. Krulak, it is a 54-hour field exercise that serves as the culminating event of boot camp, after which recruits earn the title of United States Marine.

Description

During the Crucible, recruits march roughly 48 miles, receive limited food and sleep — typically two to three MREs and only a few hours of sleep — and work in squads to complete a series of physically and mentally demanding stations. The events include obstacle and confidence courses, day and night assault courses, pugil-stick bouts, and problem-solving exercises named for Medal of Honor recipients and Marine Corps battles.

Eagle, Globe, and Anchor ceremony

The exercise ends with a final march and a warrior's breakfast, at which drill instructors present each recruit with the Eagle, Globe, and Anchor emblem — the symbolic moment at which a recruit becomes a Marine.
