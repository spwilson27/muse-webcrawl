Rome I harmonises choice-of-law rules for contracts across the European Union.

The Rome I Regulation (Regulation (EC) No 593/2008) is a European Union regulation that determines which national law applies to contractual obligations in civil and commercial matters involving a conflict of laws. Adopted on 17 June 2008 and applying to contracts concluded after 17 December 2009, it converted the 1980 Rome Convention into directly applicable EU law, ensuring that the same choice-of-law rules are used by courts throughout the Union.

Main rules

The general principle is freedom of choice: a contract is governed by the law chosen by the parties (Article 3), whether or not that law is the law of an EU Member State. In the absence of choice, Article 4 supplies default rules, generally pointing to the law of the habitual residence of the party who performs the characteristic obligation — for example, the seller in a contract for the sale of goods, or the service provider in a contract for services.

Rome I contains protective rules for weaker parties: consumer contracts (Article 6) are generally governed by the law of the consumer's habitual residence, and individual employment contracts (Article 8) by the law of the place where the employee habitually works, so that a choice of law cannot deprive such parties of mandatory local protection. Special rules cover contracts of carriage and insurance. Article 21 allows a court to refuse a foreign rule that is manifestly incompatible with the public policy (ordre public) of the forum, and Article 9 preserves the overriding mandatory provisions of the forum.

Scope and exclusions

The Regulation excludes, among other things, revenue, customs and administrative matters, questions of company law and trusts, arbitration agreements and choice-of-court agreements, and obligations arising out of family relationships. It is complemented by the Rome II Regulation (No 864/2007) on non-contractual obligations such as tort, and by the Brussels I Regulation on jurisdiction.
