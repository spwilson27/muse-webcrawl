Analog stick

An analog stick (or analogue stick), also known as a control stick or thumbstick, is an input device for a game controller that measures the direction and magnitude of a user's input using a small stick tilted from a central position. Unlike a directional pad, which registers only discrete directions, an analog stick provides continuous, proportional control, allowing finer movement in video games.

History

Early analog controls appeared on devices such as the Vectrex controller in the 1980s. The analog stick became a standard feature of console gaming with the Nintendo 64 controller in 1996, which used a single thumbstick to enable three-dimensional movement in games such as Super Mario 64. Sony's Dual Analog Controller and later the DualShock for the PlayStation added twin sticks and vibration feedback, a layout that became the industry standard.

Design

Most analog sticks use two potentiometers mounted at right angles to measure tilt along the X and Y axes. Newer designs may use Hall effect sensors, which measure magnetic fields and are less prone to wear and the drifting problems associated with potentiometer-based sticks. The stick typically sits in a circular gate and returns to center via a spring.

Use in games

Modern controllers usually include two analog sticks: the left stick commonly controls character movement, while the right stick controls the camera. Pressing the stick down often functions as an additional button (L3/R3). Analog sticks are also used in flight simulators, drones, and assistive input devices.
