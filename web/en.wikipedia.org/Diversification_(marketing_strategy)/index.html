Diversification (marketing strategy)

Diversification strategy

Type: Marketing / growth strategy

Framework: Ansoff matrix

Quadrant: New products in new markets

Risk level: Highest of the four Ansoff strategies

In marketing strategy, diversification is the growth strategy in which a company markets new products in new markets. It is one of the four strategies of the Ansoff matrix, developed by Igor Ansoff in 1957, alongside market penetration, market development, and product development. Because it moves a firm away from both its current products and its current customers, it is generally considered the riskiest of the four.

Forms

Ansoff distinguished between concentric diversification, where the new products have technological or marketing synergies with existing lines though aimed at new customer groups; horizontal diversification, where new products appeal to existing customers but are technologically unrelated; and conglomerate diversification, where there is no relationship at all between the new and old businesses.

Marketing considerations

A firm pursuing diversification cannot rely on its established brand recognition, distribution channels, or customer knowledge in the new market, and must build positioning, pricing, and promotion essentially from scratch — or acquire a business that already possesses them. Marketers therefore treat thorough market research and staged entry as prerequisites, and many firms enter through acquisition or joint venture rather than internal development.

Examples

Famous examples include a credit-card company launching travel services, or a tobacco firm moving into food products. Amazon's expansion from online retail into cloud computing (Amazon Web Services) is often cited as a successful modern diversification: an entirely new product offered to a largely new market of business customers.
