Electric discharge

An electric discharge is the release and transmission of electricity in an applied electric field through a medium such as a gas, liquid, or vacuum. When the field exceeds the dielectric strength of the medium, it becomes partially ionized and conductive, allowing current to flow.

Branching discharges produced by a Tesla coil. Lightning is the largest natural example of an electric discharge.

Types

  • Spark discharge — a brief, bright channel of ionization, as in lightning or a spark plug.
  • Arc discharge — a sustained, high-current discharge with a hot plasma channel; used in welding and arc lamps.
  • Glow discharge — a low-pressure plasma emitting light, used in neon signs and fluorescent lamps.
  • Corona discharge — a partial discharge around a conductor with a strongly non-uniform field.
  • Brush discharge — a discharge resembling a brush, occurring between curved electrodes.
  • Dielectric barrier discharge — a discharge between electrodes separated by a dielectric layer.

Applications

Electric discharges are used in gas-discharge lamps, plasma displays, welding, electrostatic precipitators, ozone generation, and ignition systems. Partial discharges in electrical insulation are an important diagnostic indicator of equipment condition.
