Basilica

In ancient Roman architecture, a basilica was a large public building where business or legal proceedings took place. The term later came to describe a Christian church building modelled on that form, and in Catholic usage an important church granted special privileges by the Pope.

Roman basilicas

Roman basilicas were rectangular halls with a raised apse, serving as courts and marketplaces; examples include the Basilica Julia and Basilica Aemilia in the Roman Forum.

Church architecture

Early Christians adopted the basilica plan — a central nave flanked by aisles, with an apse at one end — for congregational worship.

Canonical basilicas

Major and minor basilicas are honorific titles; the four major basilicas are all in Rome, including St Peter's Basilica.
