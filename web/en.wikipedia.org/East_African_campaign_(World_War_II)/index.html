The East African campaign was fought in East Africa during the Second World War by Allied forces, mainly from the British Empire, against Axis forces, primarily from Italy. It formed part of the Mediterranean and Middle East theatre and was decided by an Allied offensive that began with simultaneous invasions of Italian East Africa from Sudan and Kenya in early 1941.

Background

On 10 June 1940, Italy declared war on France and the United Kingdom. Italian forces in Italian East Africa (Africa Orientale Italiana) — comprising Ethiopia (annexed in 1936), Eritrea and Italian Somaliland — numbered roughly 290,000 men, mostly colonial troops (askari) commanded by Italian officers, under the Viceroy, Prince Amedeo, Duke of Aosta. British-led forces in the region were initially far weaker, with some 5,500 troops in Sudan, 8,500 in Kenya and 1,500 in British Somaliland.

Italian offensive (1940)

In July 1940 Italian forces advanced into Sudan, seizing Kassala, Gallabat and Moyale, and in August invaded and conquered British Somaliland against a small British and Commonwealth garrison. Thereafter, short of supplies and awaiting events elsewhere, the Italians remained largely on the defensive.

Allied counter-offensive (1941)

General Sir Archibald Wavell, Commander-in-Chief Middle East, planned a two-pronged offensive. In the north, Lieutenant-General William Platt advanced from Sudan into Eritrea in January 1941, defeating the Italians in the hard-fought Battle of Keren (5 February – 1 April) before taking Asmara and the port of Massawa. In the south, Lieutenant-General Alan Cunningham advanced from Kenya through Italian Somaliland, capturing Mogadishu and pushing into Ethiopia. Ethiopian patriot forces and units of the Ethiopian regular army, accompanied by Emperor Haile Selassie, who re-entered his country in January 1941, harassed Italian communications.

Fall of Addis Ababa and Gondar

Cunningham's forces entered Addis Ababa on 6 April 1941. The Duke of Aosta surrendered the remaining Italian field force at Amba Alagi on 19 May. The campaign formally ended with the surrender of the last Italian stronghold at Gondar on 27 November 1941. Ethiopia regained its independence, and Allied forces secured the Red Sea route for shipping.
