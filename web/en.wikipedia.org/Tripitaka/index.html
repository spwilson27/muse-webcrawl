Tripiṭaka

Tripiṭaka
Meaning"Three Baskets" (Sanskrit: Tripiṭaka; Pali: Tipiṭaka)
DivisionsVinaya Piṭaka; Sutta Piṭaka; Abhidhamma Piṭaka
TraditionsTheravāda (Pali Canon); Mahāyāna (Chinese and Tibetan canons)

Tripiṭaka (Sanskrit) or Tipiṭaka (Pali), literally "Three Baskets", is the traditional term for the canonical collections of scriptures of Buddhism. The three divisions are the Vinaya Piṭaka (rules of monastic discipline), the Sutta Piṭaka (discourses attributed to the Buddha and his close disciples), and the Abhidhamma Piṭaka (systematic philosophical and psychological treatises).

Canons

The Pali Canon, preserved by the Theravāda school in Sri Lanka and Southeast Asia, is the only early canon surviving complete in an Indic language; its texts were first written down in the 1st century BC. Mahāyāna Buddhism uses expanded canons in Chinese translation (the Chinese Tripiṭaka, including many Mahāyāna sutras and commentaries) and Tibetan (the Kangyur and Tengyur). East Asian xylographic editions culminated in the Tripiṭaka Koreana, carved on more than 80,000 woodblocks at Haeinsa temple in the 13th century and now a UNESCO Memory of the World treasure.

Transmission

Tradition holds the teachings were recited communally at councils beginning shortly after the Buddha's death, transmitted orally for centuries before being committed to writing. Academic scholarship dates the composition and redaction of the texts across several centuries.
