Beriberi

Cause: Thiamine (vitamin B1) deficiency
Symptoms: Neuropathy, weakness, oedema, heart failure

Beriberi is a disease caused by a deficiency of thiamine (vitamin B1). It takes two main forms: wet beriberi, which affects the cardiovascular system and causes oedema and heart failure, and dry beriberi, which damages the peripheral nervous system, causing pain, weakness, loss of sensation, and wasting of the leg muscles. In infants of thiamine-deficient mothers it can be rapidly fatal.

Causes

The disease historically devastated populations dependent on polished white rice, from which the thiamine-rich husk has been removed. Today it occurs mainly with malnutrition, alcohol use disorder, bariatric surgery, or diets based on highly milled grains. Thiamine deficiency also causes Wernicke–Korsakoff syndrome.

History

Beriberi was a leading cause of death in 19th-century Asia. Japanese naval surgeon Takaki Kanehiro reduced it dramatically in the 1880s by changing sailors' diet. Christiaan Eijkman's experiments with chickens fed polished rice in the 1890s led to the discovery of vitamins, and Casimir Funk coined the term "vitamine" for the protective substance isolated in 1912; thiamine itself was isolated in 1926.

Treatment

Treatment is thiamine supplementation, which often produces rapid improvement in wet beriberi; prevention relies on dietary diversity, food fortification, and parboiling rice.
