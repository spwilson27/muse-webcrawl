Troglobite

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A troglobite (or troglobiont) is an animal species, or population of a species, strictly bound to underground habitats, such as caves. Such animals are distinct from trogloxenes, which visit caves, and troglophiles, which can complete their life cycles in caves but also live outside them. Troglobites typically descend from surface ancestors that colonised the subterranean environment and then evolved in isolation.

Adaptations

Life in permanent darkness produces characteristic traits, collectively called troglomorphy: loss of eyes and pigmentation, elongation of appendages, enhanced non-visual senses such as chemoreception and mechanoreception, slow metabolism, long lifespan, and low reproductive output. Energy in caves is scarce, arriving mainly as detritus washed in from the surface, bat guano, and chemosynthetic microbes, and troglobites are adapted to survive long periods without food.

Examples

Well-known troglobites include the olm, a blind cave salamander of the Dinaric Alps; the Texas blind salamander; the Mexican blind cave fish (Astyanax mexicanus cave forms); numerous cave beetles, spiders, harvestmen, millipedes, and crustaceans such as the Alabama cave shrimp. Many cave species are extremely localised, sometimes known from a single cave system, which makes them highly vulnerable to groundwater pollution and disturbance.
