Unconventional (oil and gas) reservoir

An unconventional reservoir is an underground accumulation of oil or natural gas that cannot be produced at economic rates by conventional vertical wells and natural pressure alone, because the hydrocarbons are trapped in rock of very low permeability or in unusual geological settings. Production from such reservoirs generally requires specialised technology, including horizontal drilling, multi-stage hydraulic fracturing, steam injection or mining.

Contrast with conventional reservoirs

In a conventional reservoir, oil and gas generated in a source rock migrate upward into a porous, permeable reservoir rock (often sandstone or limestone) sealed by a trap, from which they flow readily to the wellbore. In unconventional reservoirs the source rock and reservoir are frequently the same unit (as in shale), or the hydrocarbon itself is too viscous to flow (as in oil sands and extra-heavy oil). Unconventional accumulations are regionally extensive and continuous rather than confined to discrete traps.

Types

Tight gas and tight oil occur in low-permeability sandstones and carbonates. Shale gas and shale oil are produced directly from organic-rich shale. Coalbed methane is adsorbed in coal seams and released by dewatering. Oil sands (tar sands), such as Alberta's Athabasca deposits, contain bitumen that is mined or melted in place with steam. Gas hydrates are ice-like solids trapping methane beneath the seabed and in permafrost; they are not yet produced commercially.

Impact

The combination of horizontal drilling and hydraulic fracturing in the 2000s–2010s made US shale resources economic and transformed global energy markets — the "shale revolution" — making the United States the world's largest oil and gas producer. Unconventional development has also raised environmental concerns over water use, wastewater disposal and induced seismicity, methane leakage, and land disturbance, as well as debate over the ultimately recoverable resource base given the steep production decline rates of shale wells.
