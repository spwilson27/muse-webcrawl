The X-Men, Marvel's mutant superhero team.

The X-Men are a team of mutant superheroes appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics. Created by writer Stan Lee and artist Jack Kirby, they debuted in The X-Men #1 (cover-dated September 1963). The X-Men are mutants — humans born with an "X-gene" granting superhuman abilities — who use their powers to protect a world that fears and hates them.

Fictional history

The team was founded by telepath Professor Charles Xavier, who trains young mutants at his school in Westchester County. The original team comprised Cyclops, Marvel Girl (Jean Grey), Beast, Angel, and Iceman, opposed by Xavier's former friend Magneto and his Brotherhood of Evil Mutants. After flagging sales led to reprints, the series was relaunched in 1975 with Giant-Size X-Men #1, introducing an international team including Wolverine, Storm, Colossus, and Nightcrawler. Under writer Chris Claremont (1975–1991), stories such as the Dark Phoenix Saga and "Days of Future Past" made the X-Men Marvel's best-selling franchise.

Themes and legacy

The X-Men have long been read as a metaphor for marginalized groups — civil rights, the LGBTQ+ movement, and other minorities — with Xavier's dream of peaceful coexistence contrasted with Magneto's separatism. The franchise has expanded into numerous spin-off titles (New Mutants, X-Force, Excalibur), animated series including the acclaimed X-Men: The Animated Series (1992) and X-Men '97 (2024), and a film series launched by X-Men (2000), which helped ignite the modern superhero film boom.
