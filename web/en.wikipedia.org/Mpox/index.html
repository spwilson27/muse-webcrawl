Mpox

CauseMonkeypox virus (MPXV)
SymptomsFever, lymphadenopathy, rash progressing to lesions
IncubationTypically 6–13 days (range 5–21)
Former nameMonkeypox (renamed by WHO in 2022)

Mpox (formerly called monkeypox) is an infectious viral disease caused by the monkeypox virus (MPXV), a member of the Orthopoxvirus genus that also includes the smallpox virus. It can occur in humans and other animals. Symptoms typically begin with fever, headache, muscle aches and swollen lymph nodes, followed by a rash that forms blisters and crusts over; illness usually lasts two to four weeks and is often self-limiting, though severe cases occur, particularly in children, pregnant people and the immunocompromised.

Transmission and clades

The virus spreads through close contact with lesions, body fluids, respiratory droplets and contaminated materials, and zoonotically from animal reservoirs (likely African rodents) in Central and West Africa, where the disease is endemic. Two major clades exist: clade I (Central Africa, historically more severe) and clade II (West Africa), the latter responsible for the global outbreak of 2022–2023.

Outbreaks and response

The first human case was identified in the Democratic Republic of the Congo in 1970. In July 2022 the World Health Organization declared the multi-country outbreak a Public Health Emergency of International Concern; the emergency was lifted in May 2023 after vaccination and behaviour change reduced cases. In August 2024 the WHO again declared an emergency over the spread of clade Ib in Central and East Africa. In November 2022 the WHO renamed the disease "mpox" to reduce stigma. Smallpox vaccines (such as JYNNEOS/MVA-BN) cross-protect, and the antiviral tecovirimat has been used for severe cases.
