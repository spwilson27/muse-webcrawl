Orthogonal frequency-division multiple access

[Image placeholder: Orthogonal frequency-division multiple access]

Orthogonal frequency-division multiple access (OFDMA) is a multi-user version of the orthogonal frequency-division multiplexing (OFDM) digital modulation scheme. OFDMA achieves multiple access by assigning subsets of subcarriers — grouped as resource blocks — to individual users, allowing several users to transmit simultaneously on one channel.

Applications

OFDMA is used in the downlink of 4G LTE and in IEEE 802.11a/g Wi-Fi variants; Wi-Fi 6 (802.11ax) adopted OFDMA in both directions, as did 5G NR. WiMAX (802.16) also employed OFDMA.

Advantages and disadvantages

Benefits include flexible allocation of bandwidth to users with different data rates, robustness against multipath fading, and efficient spectrum use. Drawbacks include sensitivity to frequency offsets and phase noise, and a high peak-to-average power ratio in the transmitter.
