Galvanic series

The galvanic series determines the electrochemical potential and nobility of metals and metal alloys. When two metals are submerged in an electrolyte and joined electrically, the less noble (anodic) metal will experience galvanic corrosion. The galvanic series ranks metals by their measured corrosion potential in a given environment, most commonly flowing seawater.

Use in engineering

Each alloy or metal has a characteristic potential; the rate of corrosion is a function of the relative difference in potential and of the relative areas of the anode and cathode. Engineers use the series to select compatible fasteners and fittings, to design cathodic protection systems, and to specify sacrificial anodes such as zinc or magnesium blocks on ship hulls. A galvanic series derived from one environment should not be transferred uncritically to another, since passivation and films can reorder the ranking.

Typical ordering

From most anodic (least noble) to most cathodic (most noble) in seawater, a representative ordering is: magnesium and its alloys, zinc, aluminium alloys, cadmium, mild steel and cast iron, lead–tin solder, lead and tin, nickel alloys (active), brasses and bronzes, copper, nickel alloys (passive), stainless steels (passive), titanium, silver, graphite, gold and platinum.
