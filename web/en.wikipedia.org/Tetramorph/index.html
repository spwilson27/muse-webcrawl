A tetramorph is a symbolic arrangement of four differing elements, or the combination of four elements into one form. The term is used above all for the four winged "living creatures" of biblical vision — with the faces of a man, a lion, an ox, and an eagle — that appear in the Book of Ezekiel (chapters 1 and 10) and in the Book of Revelation (chapter 4), where they attend the throne of God.

Biblical origins

In Ezekiel's inaugural vision the prophet sees four creatures (later identified as cherubim) emerging from a storm cloud, each with four faces — human, lion, ox, and eagle — four wings, and wheels beside them. In Revelation the imagery is redistributed: four separate creatures, one like a lion, one like an ox, one with a human face, and one like a flying eagle, each with six wings and covered in eyes, ceaselessly proclaiming "Holy, holy, holy".

The four evangelists

From the early Church Fathers onward, the four creatures were matched to the Four Evangelists, an assignment standardised by Jerome: the winged man for Matthew (whose gospel opens with Christ's human genealogy), the lion for Mark (the voice crying in the wilderness, and Christ's royal dignity), the ox — the sacrificial animal — for Luke (opening with the priest Zechariah and stressing Christ's sacrifice), and the eagle for John (whose gospel soars to the divine Word). The symbols pervade Christian art, from Insular gospel books such as the Book of Kells to Romanesque tympana and the great rose windows.

Wider use

Because the four creatures also correspond to the four fixed signs of the zodiac (Aquarius, Leo, Taurus, Scorpio), the tetramorph appears in astrology and occultism as well; related fourfold beings — lamassu and sphinxes combining human, animal, and bird features — are found in ancient Near Eastern art, the likely iconographic background to Ezekiel's vision.
