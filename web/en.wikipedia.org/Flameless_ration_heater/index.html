Flameless ration heater

A flameless ration heater (FRH) is a form of self-heating food packaging that heats food without a flame, developed for military field rations such as the United States Meal, Ready-to-Eat (MRE). It was introduced into U.S. service in the early 1990s.

Mechanism

The heater is a plastic bag containing a pad impregnated with finely ground magnesium-iron alloy powder, salts and other additives. When the user adds a small amount of water, an exothermic oxidation reaction begins — effectively rapid rusting of the magnesium-iron alloy — producing heat and small amounts of hydrogen gas. Placed against an entrée pouch and folded inside the meal's cardboard sleeve, the heater raises the food's temperature by roughly 100 °F (about 56 °C) in 10–12 minutes.

Usage and safety

Instructions famously advise resting the heating meal "on a rock or something" so liquid does not leak. The hydrogen evolved makes heaters prohibited from use in enclosed spaces such as aircraft cabins and tents in quantity, and used heaters must be disposed of as hazardous waste in some jurisdictions because of residual chemicals. Civilian versions are sold for camping, emergency preparedness and self-heating meals.
