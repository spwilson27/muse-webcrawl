Gerhard Berger (born 27 August 1959) is an Austrian former Formula One racing driver and motorsport executive. He competed in 210 Grands Prix between 1984 and 1997, winning ten races and finishing third in the World Drivers' Championship twice, in 1988 and 1994.

Career

Berger rose through Formula Three and touring cars before debuting in Formula One with ATS in 1984. His first victory came with Benetton at the 1986 Mexican Grand Prix — also the team's first win. He joined Ferrari for 1987–1989, surviving a fiery crash at Imola in 1989, then moved to McLaren as teammate to Ayrton Senna, with whom he formed a famous friendship. A second Ferrari stint (1993–1995) and a return to Benetton followed; his final win, at the 1997 German Grand Prix, came shortly after the death of his father and his own illness, and he retired at the end of that season.

After racing

Berger served as competitions director at BMW Motorsport, co-owned Scuderia Toro Rosso with Dietrich Mateschitz, and from 2017 chaired the ITR, promoter of the DTM touring-car series.
