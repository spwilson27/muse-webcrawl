A fireteam or fire team is a small military sub-subunit of infantry designed to optimise "bounding overwatch" and "fire and movement" tactical doctrine in combat. It is the smallest unit above the individual soldier and serves as the basic building block of a squad in many modern armies.

[Image placeholder: A four-soldier fireteam advancing in a wedge formation]

Depending on mission requirements, a typical fireteam consists of four or fewer members: an automatic rifleman, a grenadier, a rifleman, and a designated team leader. The role of each fireteam leader is to ensure that the fireteam operates as a cohesive unit, and two or three fireteams are organised into a squad or section in co-ordinated operations under a squad leader.

Concept

The fireteam concept is based on the need for tactical flexibility in infantry operations. A fireteam is capable of autonomous movement and of providing a base of fire while another fireteam manoeuvres, a technique known as fire and movement or bounding overwatch. Military theorists consider the fireteam the smallest effective tactical unit: small enough to be controlled by voice and hand signals, yet carrying enough firepower — especially with an automatic weapon at its core — to be effective on its own.

Organisation by nation

United States

The U.S. Army builds its infantry squads from two fireteams of four soldiers each, led by a team leader (usually a sergeant or corporal) and comprising a rifleman, a grenadier armed with an M203 or M320 grenade launcher, and an automatic rifleman with an M249 light machine gun. The U.S. Marine Corps rifle squad comprises three fireteams of four Marines, organised around the automatic rifleman and, in modern tables of organisation, a designated marksman.

Other nations

The British Army section of eight soldiers is divided into two four-man fireteams, Charlie and Delta. The Canadian Army uses a similar structure, and many NATO and Commonwealth forces adopted fireteam-based organisation during the twentieth century as automatic weapons became standard at the lowest levels.

History

The fireteam emerged from First World War stormtrooper and infiltration tactics and the French army's organisation around the Chauchat automatic rifle, and was refined in the Second World War, for example in the U.S. Marine Corps' 1944 reorganisation of the rifle squad into three four-man fire teams built around the Browning Automatic Rifle. It has remained the standard building block of dismounted infantry since.
