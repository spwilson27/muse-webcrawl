Qi (standard)

Qi (pronounced "chee"; from the Chinese 氣, qì) is an open interface standard for wireless power transfer using inductive charging, developed by the Wireless Power Consortium (WPC). It allows compatible devices — most prominently smartphones — to charge by being placed on a charging pad, typically delivering up to 15 watts in its widely deployed form, with faster profiles added in later versions.

Qi
Type: Wireless charging standard
Developer: Wireless Power Consortium
Launched: 2010 (version 1.0)
Method: Inductive coupling
Qi2 (2023): Adds magnetic alignment (MagSafe-based)

How it works

A Qi transmitter contains a coil that generates an alternating magnetic field; a receiver coil in the device converts the field back into electric current to charge the battery. The receiver communicates with the transmitter by modulating the load, allowing the pad to adjust power output and detect foreign objects. Effective range is only a few millimetres, so devices must sit in close contact with the pad.

Adoption and Qi2

Qi was adopted by most major Android phone makers, and Apple added Qi charging to the iPhone in 2017. In January 2023 the WPC announced Qi2, whose Magnetic Power Profile — based on Apple's MagSafe technology, contributed to the consortium — uses a ring of magnets to snap devices into optimal alignment, improving efficiency and enabling higher power. A later extension (Qi2.2) raised charging rates further toward 25 W.
