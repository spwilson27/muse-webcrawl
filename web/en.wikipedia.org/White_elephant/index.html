White elephant

MeaningA possession that is useless, troublesome, or expensive to maintain
OriginSacred white elephants of Southeast Asian monarchies

A white elephant is a possession that its owner cannot dispose of, and whose cost — particularly that of maintenance — is out of proportion to its usefulness. In modern usage, it is an object, building project, undertaking, or business venture considered useless or of dubious value.

Origin

The term derives from the sacred white elephants kept by monarchs in Burma, Thailand (Siam), Laos, and Cambodia. White elephants were regarded as symbols of royal power and good fortune; they could not be put to work and were extremely costly to keep. According to legend, the King of Siam would gift a white elephant to a courtier who had fallen out of favour — an honour that could financially ruin the recipient, who could neither refuse nor dispose of the animal.

Modern usage

The expression is applied to grand infrastructure projects, stadiums, and programmes whose upkeep vastly exceeds their benefit. "White elephant gift exchanges", popular at holiday parties, play on the idea of unwanted possessions, with participants swapping impractical or humorous gifts.
