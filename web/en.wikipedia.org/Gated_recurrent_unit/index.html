Gated recurrent unit

Image placeholder: the GRU cell with its reset and update gates

Gated recurrent units (GRUs) are gating mechanisms in recurrent neural networks, introduced in 2014 by Kyunghyun Cho and colleagues. Like the long short-term memory unit (LSTM), the GRU is designed to address the vanishing gradient problem that makes plain recurrent networks difficult to train on long sequences, enabling the network to retain information over long periods and to form connections in its interpretation of data such as that found in handwriting and speech recognition and machine translation.

Architecture

A GRU cell maintains a hidden state that is updated at each time step under the control of two gates. The update gate decides how much of the previous hidden state to carry forward versus how much of a new candidate state to adopt. The reset gate decides how much of the past state to forget when computing the candidate activation. Because the GRU has no separate cell state and uses two gates instead of LSTM's three, it has fewer parameters and is often faster to train, while performing comparably to LSTM on many tasks.

History and use

The GRU was proposed in Cho et al.'s 2014 paper on neural machine translation, "Learning Phrase Representations using RNN Encoder–Decoder for Statistical Machine Translation," and was evaluated in detail by Chung et al. (2014), who found its performance comparable to LSTM. GRUs were widely adopted in sequence modelling — machine translation, speech recognition, and music generation — before attention-based transformer architectures became dominant; they remain in use where small models or streaming inference are needed.
