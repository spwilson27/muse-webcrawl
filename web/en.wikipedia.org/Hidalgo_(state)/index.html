Hidalgo, officially the Free and Sovereign State of Hidalgo (Spanish: Estado Libre y Soberano de Hidalgo), is one of the 32 states that constitute the Federal Entities of Mexico. It is divided into 84 municipalities, and its capital city is Pachuca de Soto. It is located in east-central Mexico and is bordered by San Luis Potosí and Veracruz on the north, Puebla on the east, Tlaxcala and the State of Mexico on the south, and Querétaro on the west.

The state is named after Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla, the priest who issued the Cry of Dolores (Grito de Dolores) that began the Mexican War of Independence in 1810. It was created by President Benito Juárez in 1869, carved out of territory that had belonged to the State of Mexico.

Geography

Hidalgo's terrain is among the most mountainous in Mexico, dominated by the Sierra Madre Oriental. The state ranges from the high, cold plateau around Pachuca to the subtropical Huasteca region in the northeast. Its best-known natural feature is the Prismas Basálticos (Basaltic Prisms) of Santa María Regla, towering geometric columns formed by the slow cooling of volcanic lava, among the tallest such formations in the world.

History

The region was home to cultures including the Toltecs, whose capital Tula flourished from roughly 900 to 1150 CE. After the Spanish conquest, the rich silver mines of Pachuca and Real del Monte made the area one of New Spain's most important mining districts; Cornish miners who arrived in the 19th century introduced pasties (pastes) and football to Mexico.

Economy and culture

Mining, agriculture, and manufacturing are central to the economy. Hidalgo is known for its barbacoa, pastes, pulque, and the archaeological zones of Tula and Huapalcalco. The state is home to the Universidad Autónoma del Estado de Hidalgo, founded in 1869.
