Hayden Planetarium

The Hayden Planetarium is a public planetarium, part of the Rose Center for Earth and Space of the American Museum of Natural History in New York City. Founded in 1935 with a donation from banker and philanthropist Charles Hayden, it is one of the best-known planetariums in the world.

History

The original planetarium opened in 1935, featuring a Zeiss Mark II projector. In 1997 the original building was closed and demolished, and in 2000 the planetarium reopened as the centrepiece of the new Rose Center for Earth and Space, designed by James Stewart Polshek. The new theatre sits inside an 87-foot-diameter sphere, directly supported by pillars, and appears to float inside a six-storey glass cube. Astrophysicist Neil deGrasse Tyson has served as its Frederick P. Rose Director since 1996 and oversaw the redevelopment.

Facilities and shows

The Space Theater in the upper half of the Hayden Sphere uses high-resolution full-dome digital projection and customised shows — past productions have included Passport to the Universe, Dark Universe, narrated by Tyson, and Worlds Beyond Earth. The lower half of the sphere houses the Big Bang Theater, which depicts the birth of the universe. Exhibits around the sphere include the Scales of the Universe walkway and the Cullman Hall of the Universe. The planetarium operates one of the most comprehensive digital universe atlases, used by scientists and in productions worldwide.
