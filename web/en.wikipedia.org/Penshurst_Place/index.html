Penshurst Place is a historic house near Penshurst in Kent, England, and the ancestral home of the Sidney family. Its centrepiece, the Baron's Hall built for Sir John de Pulteney in 1341, is one of the most complete and evocative survivals of a 14th-century great hall in England.

History

The estate passed through several owners, including the Dukes of Buckingham, before Henry VIII seized it and used it as a hunting lodge within reach of Hever Castle. In 1552 Edward VI granted Penshurst to Sir William Sidney, a courtier and soldier. His son Henry Sidney served as Lord Deputy of Ireland, and the house became the birthplace of the poet and soldier Sir Philip Sidney (1554–1586) and of the poet Mary Sidney, Countess of Pembroke. Ben Jonson's poem "To Penshurst" (1616) celebrated the house as a model of generous, harmonious country-house life in contrast to ostentatious prodigy houses.

Architecture and gardens

Successive centuries added a state dining room, Queen Elizabeth Room, long gallery and other apartments around the medieval core, leaving an accreted, picturesque composition. The gardens — among the oldest in England in continuous record, with an Italian garden, herbaceous borders and a historic mile of double borders — surround the house within a large park. Penshurst Place is open to the public and has been used as a filming location for productions including Wolf Hall and The Other Boleyn Girl.
