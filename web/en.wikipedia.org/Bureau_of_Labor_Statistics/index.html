The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) is a unit of the United States Department of Labor. It is the principal fact-finding agency for the U.S. government in the broad field of labor economics and statistics and serves as part of the U.S. Federal Statistical System. The BLS is a governmental statistical agency that collects, processes, analyses and disseminates essential statistical data to the American public, the U.S. Congress, other federal agencies, state and local governments, and business and labour representatives.

Statistical reports

The BLS publishes many of the most closely watched U.S. economic indicators, including the Consumer Price Index (CPI), the principal measure of inflation; the Employment Situation report with the monthly unemployment rate, derived from the Current Population Survey (conducted with the Census Bureau) and the Current Employment Statistics survey; the Producer Price Index; the Employment Cost Index; and productivity statistics. It also maintains the Occupational Outlook Handbook, a widely used career guide.

History

The Bureau of Labor was established in 1884 within the Department of the Interior, became an independent department in 1888, and joined the new Department of Labor in 1913. Its first commissioner was Carroll D. Wright. The agency is headed by a commissioner appointed by the President and confirmed by the Senate for a four-year term; the commissioner is the only political appointee, a design intended to protect statistical independence.
