Rabi cycle


Placeholder image: Rabi cycle

The Rabi cycle describes the cyclic behaviour of a two-level quantum system in the presence of an oscillatory driving field, named after Isidor Isaac Rabi. A system prepared in one state will oscillate between its two states at the Rabi frequency when driven near resonance.

Description

For an atom interacting with an electromagnetic field of frequency close to the transition frequency, the populations of the ground and excited states oscillate sinusoidally. The generalised Rabi frequency grows with both the field amplitude (through the dipole matrix element) and the detuning. On exact resonance, complete population inversion is possible — the basis of the pi-pulse in quantum control.

Applications

Rabi oscillations underpin atomic clocks, nuclear magnetic resonance, quantum optics experiments, and the control of qubits in quantum computing, where calibrated Rabi pulses implement single-qubit gates.
