Alastair Pilkington

Sir Lionel Alexander Bethune Pilkington (7 January 1920 – 5 May 1995), known as Alastair Pilkington, was a British engineer and businessman who invented the float glass process, which revolutionised the manufacture of flat glass worldwide.

Early life and war service

Pilkington was born in Calcutta, India, the son of a member of the Pilkington glass-making family of St Helens, Lancashire. He was educated at Sherborne School and Trinity College, Cambridge, where he studied mechanical sciences. During the Second World War he served in the Royal Artillery, was captured during the Battle of Crete in 1941, and spent four years as a prisoner of war in Germany.

Float glass

After the war Pilkington joined the family firm, Pilkington Brothers. In 1952 he conceived the idea of floating a ribbon of molten glass on a bath of molten tin, producing perfectly flat glass without the costly grinding and polishing required by the older plate-glass process. After seven years of development and substantial investment, the float process was announced in 1959. It was licensed to manufacturers around the world and became the standard method of making high-quality flat glass.

Later career

Pilkington became chairman of Pilkington Brothers in 1973 and retired in 1985. He was knighted in 1970 and later served as a director of the Bank of England and as chancellor of Loughborough University.
