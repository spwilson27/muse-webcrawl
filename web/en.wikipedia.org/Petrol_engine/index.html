Petrol engine

A petrol engine (gasoline engine in American English) is an internal combustion engine with spark ignition, designed to run on petrol and similar volatile fuels. In most petrol engines, fuel and air are usually pre-mixed before compression, and the mixture is ignited by a spark plug.

Petrol engineType: Internal combustion engine
Ignition: Spark plug
Fuel: Petrol (gasoline)
Cycle: Otto cycle (typically)

Operation

Most petrol engines use the four-stroke Otto cycle: intake, compression, power, and exhaust. The compression ratio is typically lower than in diesel engines (which use compression ignition) because the air–fuel mixture can auto-ignite prematurely, causing engine knocking. Modern engines increasingly use direct injection, turbocharging, and variable valve timing for efficiency.

Applications

Petrol engines power most passenger cars, motorcycles, and small machinery such as lawn mowers and chainsaws. Their dominance in cars is being challenged by diesel engines in some markets and, increasingly, by electric vehicles.
