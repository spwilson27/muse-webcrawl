Yoshinori Ohsumi

Yoshinori Ohsumi
Born: 9 February 1945, Fukuoka, Japan
Field: Cell biology
Known for: Discovering mechanisms of autophagy
Honour: Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine, 2016

Yoshinori Ohsumi (大隅 良典, born 1945) is a Japanese cell biologist who won the 2016 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine "for his discoveries of mechanisms for autophagy" — the process by which cells digest and recycle their own components.

Research

Ohsumi trained at the University of Tokyo and did postdoctoral work at Rockefeller University. From the late 1980s, working at Tokyo, he used baker's yeast as a model: by blocking vacuolar degradation in starved yeast mutants, he made autophagosomes accumulate visibly, and in 1993 identified the first autophagy genes (ATG genes). He went on to characterise the Atg protein machinery and its homologues in mammals, transforming a little-studied phenomenon into a central field of biology.

Significance

Autophagy failure is now implicated in cancer, neurodegenerative diseases such as Parkinson's, type 2 diabetes, infection, and ageing; modulating it is an active drug-development strategy. Ohsumi became a professor at the Tokyo Institute of Technology's Frontier Research Center. He is known for emphasising curiosity-driven basic research — his yeast work began when the field was unfashionable — and he used his prize funding and public voice to support young scientists in Japan.
