Pimlico Race Course

LocationBaltimore, Maryland, United States
Opened1870
Course typeFlat, dirt (1 mile oval)
Notable racePreakness Stakes

Pimlico Race Course is a thoroughbred horse racetrack in Baltimore, Maryland, best known as the home of the Preakness Stakes, the second jewel of American horse racing's Triple Crown, held each May on the third Saturday of the month. Nicknamed "Old Hilltop" for a small rise in the infield that was a favourite vantage point in its early years, Pimlico opened in 1870 and is among the oldest racetracks in the United States.

History

The Maryland Jockey Club, founded in Annapolis in 1743, built the course on land in northwest Baltimore; the opening meeting in October 1870 featured the Dinner Party Stakes, won by a colt named Preakness, after whom the track's signature race—first run in 1873—was named. Pimlico hosted the celebrated 1938 match race between Seabiscuit and the Triple Crown winner War Admiral, often called the "Race of the Century", in which Seabiscuit prevailed. The infield hill that gave the track its nickname was levelled in 1938 to improve sightlines.

The Preakness and modern era

The Preakness Stakes, run over 1 3/16 miles for three-year-olds, anchors a meet that also includes the Black-Eyed Susan Stakes for fillies. In recent decades the ageing facility prompted prolonged debate over renovation or relocation; under agreements reached in the 2020s, Maryland moved to consolidate racing at a rebuilt Pimlico, with ownership transferred to the state and plans for a new year-round training and racing centre on the historic site.
