Hauntology (music)

Hauntology is a loosely defined style and aesthetic in electronic music and art, emerging in the 2000s, concerned with nostalgia, memory and "lost futures". Taking its name from Jacques Derrida's concept of hauntology (a pun on "ontology" and "haunting"), the style evokes the cultural output of post-war Britain — public information films, library music, BBC Radiophonic Workshop sounds, and cheap seaside entertainment — processed through crackle, tape hiss and decay.

Characteristics

Hauntological music typically samples or imitates analogue-era sources: warped vinyl loops, detuned synthesisers, ghostly vocal fragments and broadcast ephemera. Rather than simple retro styling, it aims at an eerie sense of a future that never arrived — the optimistic modernism of the post-war decades viewed from a disappointed present. The writer Mark Fisher was a key theorist of the tendency.

Artists and labels

The British label Ghost Box, founded in 2004, became a focal point, releasing work by The Focus Group, Belbury Poly and The Advisory Circle. Related artists include The Caretaker (whose work explored memory loss and dementia), Burial, Boards of Canada, and the Mordançage-like sound worlds of Philip Jeck. The aesthetic also fed into the later vaporwave genre's treatment of cultural memory.
