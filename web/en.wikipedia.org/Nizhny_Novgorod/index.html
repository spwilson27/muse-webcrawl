Nizhny Novgorod is a city in western Russia, located at the confluence of the Oka and Volga rivers, about 440 kilometres east of Moscow. It is the administrative centre of Nizhny Novgorod Oblast and one of Russia's largest cities, with a population of roughly 1.2 million. From 1932 to 1990 it bore the name Gorky, in honour of the writer Maxim Gorky, who was born there.

History

The city was founded in 1221 by Prince Yuri II of Vladimir as a frontier fortress against the Mordvin peoples and Volga Bulgaria. In 1611–1612 the merchant Kuzma Minin and Prince Dmitry Pozharsky raised a volunteer army in the city that liberated Moscow from Polish occupation, an event commemorated by Russia's Unity Day. In the nineteenth century the Nizhny Novgorod Fair, transferred from Makaryev in 1817, became one of the largest trade fairs in the Russian Empire. During the Soviet era the city was a major centre of military industry — home to the GAZ automobile works and aircraft and submarine production — and was closed to foreigners; it was also the place of internal exile of the physicist and dissident Andrei Sakharov from 1980 to 1986.

Landmarks and transport

The red-brick Nizhny Novgorod Kremlin, dating largely from the early sixteenth century, overlooks the meeting of the rivers; the Chkalov Staircase descends from it to the Volga embankment. The city hosted matches of the 2018 FIFA World Cup and is served by Strigino International Airport, a metro system, and rail links including high-speed trains to Moscow.
