United Nations Volunteers

United Nations Volunteers (UNV) is a programme of the United Nations that promotes volunteerism for peace and development worldwide. Created by the UN General Assembly in 1970 and administered by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP), UNV is headquartered in Bonn, Germany. Each year it mobilizes several thousand UN Volunteers — professionals who serve in UN agencies, peacekeeping and political missions, and development projects in well over 100 countries — as well as a large pool of online volunteers.

History and mandate

The General Assembly established UNV in resolution 2659 (XXV) of 1970, and the first volunteers were deployed in 1971. UNV's mandate is to support the UN system in delivering peace, development and humanitarian programmes and to promote the recognition of volunteers' contributions — including through International Volunteer Day, marked every 5 December since 1985, and the State of the World's Volunteerism Report, published every three years since 2011.

Categories and work

UN Volunteers serve as international or national volunteers, community volunteers, youth volunteers and university volunteers, typically on assignments of six to twelve months renewable up to several years. They work in fields such as health, education, governance, human rights, engineering and mission support; in many peacekeeping operations they have made up a large share of civilian staff, serving as electoral observers, human rights officers and administrators. The UNV Online Volunteering service, launched in 2000, connects organizations with volunteers who contribute remotely — translating, designing, writing and researching for hundreds of thousands of hours annually.

Funding

UNV is funded through voluntary contributions to a Special Voluntary Fund and by the UN partners that host volunteers, who reimburse the costs of assignments.
