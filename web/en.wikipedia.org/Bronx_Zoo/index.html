The Bronx Zoo (also historically the Bronx Zoological Park and the Bronx Zoological Gardens) is a zoo within Bronx Park in the Bronx, New York City. It is one of the largest zoos in the United States by area and is the largest metropolitan zoo in the country, comprising 265 acres (107 ha) of park lands and naturalistic habitats separated by the Bronx River. The zoo is operated by the Wildlife Conservation Society (WCS), which also runs the city's other zoos and the New York Aquarium.

History

The zoo opened on November 8, 1899, with 22 exhibits and 843 animals. Its first director, William Hornaday, promoted naturalistic exhibits and conservation. The zoo played a central role in saving the American bison from extinction, breeding herds that were later reintroduced to the Great Plains. In 1906 the zoo drew lasting controversy for exhibiting Ota Benga, a Congolese man, in the Monkey House.

Exhibits

Notable exhibits include the African Plains, Wild Asia (served by a monorail), the Congo Gorilla Forest, JungleWorld, Tiger Mountain, and Madagascar!. The Children's Zoo and the Butterfly Garden are popular family attractions. The zoo participates in numerous Species Survival Plans and international conservation programmes run by WCS.

Conservation

The Bronx Zoo is the flagship park of the Wildlife Conservation Society, founded as the New York Zoological Society in 1895. It supports field conservation in dozens of countries and notable breeding successes, including snow leopards and lowland gorillas.
