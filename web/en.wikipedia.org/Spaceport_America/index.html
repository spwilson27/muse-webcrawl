Spaceport America

Spaceport America is a licensed commercial spaceport in the Jornada del Muerto desert basin in Sierra County, New Mexico, United States, near the town of Truth or Consequences. Opened in 2011, it is described as the world's first purpose-built commercial spaceport. It is owned and operated by the New Mexico Spaceport Authority, a state agency.

Facilities

The site covers about 27 square miles (70 km²) and includes a 3,657-metre (12,000 ft) runway, a vertical launch area, and the distinctive hangar-terminal building known as the "Virgin Galactic Gateway to Space", designed by Foster + Partners, much of it set into the desert landscape. Its inland location, low population density, restricted airspace adjoining the White Sands Missile Range, and roughly 300 days of sunshine a year are cited as advantages for flight testing.

Tenants and operations

The anchor tenant is Virgin Galactic, which moved its flight operations there in 2019 and flew its first fully crewed spaceflight, Unity 22, from the spaceport in July 2021 with founder Richard Branson aboard. Other users have included UP Aerospace, which conducted the site's first launch in 2006, and companies conducting rocket testing and unmanned aircraft trials. The spaceport has attracted debate in New Mexico over its public cost — around $220 million, largely publicly funded — relative to the pace of commercial operations.
