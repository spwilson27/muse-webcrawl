Tucson (O'odham: Cuk-Ṣon) is a city in and the county seat of Pima County, Arizona, United States, and is the second-largest city in Arizona after Phoenix. It lies in the Sonoran Desert, about 60 miles north of the Mexican border, in a valley ringed by five mountain ranges, most prominently the Santa Catalinas. Tucson is home to the University of Arizona and is recognised by UNESCO as a City of Gastronomy.

History

The area has been inhabited for thousands of years, first by Paleo-Indians and later by the Hohokam culture, and then by the Tohono O'odham, whose name for a village at the foot of Sentinel Peak—Cuk-Ṣon, "at the base of the black mountain"—gives the city its name. Spanish missionaries arrived in the late seventeenth century; Mission San Xavier del Bac, founded in 1692, still stands south of the city. A presidio was established in 1775. Tucson passed to Mexico in 1821 and to the United States with the Gadsden Purchase of 1854. It served briefly as the western capital of the Confederate Territory of Arizona during the Civil War.

Geography and climate

Tucson has a hot desert climate, with very hot summers moderated by the North American monsoon, which brings dramatic thunderstorms in July and August, and mild winters with more than 350 sunny days a year on average. Saguaro National Park, in two districts east and west of the city, protects forests of the giant saguaro cactus.

Economy and institutions

The University of Arizona, founded in 1885, is the city's largest employer together with Davis–Monthan Air Force Base and the defence contractor Raytheon. The city is also known for its optics and astronomy industries, earning it the nickname "Optics Valley".
