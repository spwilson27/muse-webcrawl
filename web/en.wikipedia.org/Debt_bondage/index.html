Debt bondage

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Debt bondage, also known as debt slavery, bonded labour, or peonage, is the pledge of a person's services as security for the repayment of a debt, where the terms of the repayment are not clearly or reasonably defined, and the person who is holding the debt thus controls the labourer. The United Nations Supplementary Convention on the Abolition of Slavery (1956) defines debt bondage as the status arising from a pledge by a debtor of their personal services, or those of a person under their control, where the value of the services is not applied toward the debt or their length and nature are not limited and defined.

Debt bondage is considered a form of modern slavery and forced labour. The International Labour Organization and anti-slavery organisations estimate that millions of people — a large share of them in South Asia — work in bonded conditions, in industries such as brick kilns, agriculture, carpet weaving, quarrying, and domestic work.

Mechanism

Bondage typically begins when a poor family borrows a small sum for an emergency — a wedding, funeral, illness, or food — from an employer or moneylender. Wages are set so low, and charges for food, tools, housing, and interest so manipulated, that the debt never decreases and may grow; the debt is then inherited by children, binding successive generations. Workers may be prevented from leaving or taking other employment until the debt is "repaid", sometimes by violence or confiscation of identity documents. Migrant workers can fall into similar traps through recruitment fees, a practice addressed by the "employer pays" principle.

History and law

Forms of debt servitude existed in ancient Greece and Rome (where nexum was abolished in the fourth century BC), in colonial Latin America as peonage, and in the post-emancipation American South, where the Peonage Act of 1867 outlawed the practice. India formally abolished bonded labour with the Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act of 1976, and Pakistan and Nepal have similar laws, though enforcement remains difficult and rescued labourers often return to bondage without rehabilitation support.
