The 1980 eruption of Mount St. Helens was a cataclysmic volcanic eruption in Skamania County, Washington, on May 18, 1980. The largest landslide in recorded history and a directed lateral blast devastated 230 square miles (600 km²) of forest, killed 57 people, and reduced the mountain's summit by 1,314 feet (401 m). It remains the deadliest and most economically destructive volcanic event in United States history.

Prelude

A magnitude 4.2 earthquake on March 20, 1980 announced the volcano's reawakening after 123 years of dormancy. Steam explosions followed, and a bulge grew on the north flank at up to 5 feet per day as magma rose within the mountain. Authorities evacuated the immediate area, though some residents, including lodge owner Harry R. Truman, refused to leave.

The eruption

At 8:32 a.m. on May 18, a magnitude 5.1 earthquake triggered the collapse of the entire north face — some 2.5 km³ of rock. The landslide uncorked the cryptodome, unleashing a superheated lateral blast that flattened everything in its path at speeds up to 670 mph. A vertical Plinian column rose more than 80,000 feet and spread ash across eleven states. USGS volcanologist David A. Johnston, observing from a ridge six miles away, radioed "Vancouver! Vancouver! This is it!" moments before the blast reached his post.

Aftermath

Ashfall darkened Spokane and closed highways and airports across the Northwest. In 1982 the area was designated the Mount St. Helens National Volcanic Monument. The volcano erupted again, less violently, from 2004 to 2008.
