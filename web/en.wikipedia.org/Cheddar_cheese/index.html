Cheddar cheese

Cheddar cheese is a natural cheese originating in the English village of Cheddar in Somerset. It is a hard, off-white (or orange if annatto is added) cow's-milk cheese ranging in flavour from mild to extra mature.

Production

Cheddar is made by the distinctive process of "cheddaring": after heating, the curd is cut, stacked and turned so that whey drains and a firm, layered texture develops. The cheese is then milled, salted, pressed and aged from a few months to several years.

History

Cheese has been made at Cheddar since at least the 12th century. Joseph Harding, a 19th-century Somerset dairyman, systematised modern production methods. Cheddar became the most widely produced cheese style in the world, made across the United Kingdom, Ireland, the United States, Canada, Australia and New Zealand.

Protected status

Only cheese made in the four counties of south-west England may use the PDO name "West Country Farmhouse Cheddar".
