Church of the Holy Trinity, Stratford-upon-Avon

The Church of the Holy Trinity is a Grade I listed parish church of the Church of England in Stratford-upon-Avon, Warwickshire, England. It stands on the banks of the River Avon and is frequently called "Shakespeare's Church" because it is the place of William Shakespeare's baptism and burial.

History

The present building dates mainly from 1210 onward, constructed on the site of an earlier Saxon monastery. It is cruciform in plan, with a chancel, nave, aisles, and a central tower with a spire. The church retains a 15th-century porch, fine stained glass, and one of the few surviving pre-Reformation sanctuary doors with a sanctuary knocker.

Shakespeare connections

Parish registers record Shakespeare's baptism on 26 April 1564 and his burial on 25 April 1616. His grave in the chancel is marked by a ledger stone bearing the famous curse against moving his bones. A wall-mounted funerary monument near the grave, erected shortly after his death, shows a bust of the playwright holding a quill. Several members of Shakespeare's family, including his wife Anne Hathaway, are also buried in the church.

Today

Holy Trinity remains an active parish church and one of the most visited churches in England, receiving hundreds of thousands of visitors each year drawn by its Shakespeare associations and its setting beside the Avon.
