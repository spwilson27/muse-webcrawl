National parks of New Zealand

The national parks of New Zealand are protected natural areas administered by the Department of Conservation (DOC) under the National Parks Act 1980. There are 13 national parks, covering more than 20,000 square kilometres — about 8 percent of the country.

History

Tongariro, gifted to the Crown by Ngāti Tūwharetoa chief Horonuku Te Heuheu Tukino IV in 1887, became New Zealand's first national park (1894) and one of the world's earliest. Egmont (now Taranaki) and Fiordland followed; most others were created in the 20th century. Paparoa (1987) was the first established under the modern act's ecological criteria.

The parks

North Island: Tongariro, Whanganui, Taranaki. South Island: Abel Tasman (the smallest), Kahurangi, Nelson Lakes, Paparoa, Arthur's Pass, Westland Tai Poutini, Aoraki / Mount Cook, Mount Aspiring, and Fiordland (the largest, at 12,607 km²). Stewart Island's Rakiura National Park (2002) was the most recent until its status as the 13th park. Three parks — Tongariro, Te Wahipounamu (encompassing four South-Westland parks), and the Sub-Antarctic Islands — are associated with UNESCO World Heritage listings.

Recreation

The parks contain New Zealand's Great Walks, including the Milford, Routeburn, and Kepler Tracks, and are managed to preserve native ecosystems while allowing public access.
