Total station

A total station (also called a total station theodolite) is an electronic surveying instrument that combines an electronic theodolite — for measuring horizontal and vertical angles — with an electronic distance meter (EDM), which measures distances by timing or phase-comparing modulated infrared or laser signals. From these two measurements it computes the three-dimensional coordinates of points in space.

Operation

The operator sights a reflective prism held on a pole at the target point, or, in reflectorless models, measures directly off surfaces. Angle and distance data are logged internally and processed by onboard software to yield coordinates, which can be downloaded for mapping and CAD work. Distances are typically measured to accuracies of a few millimetres over kilometres. Robotic total stations track a moving prism automatically and can be operated by a single surveyor; scanning models capture dense point clouds.

Applications

Total stations are standard tools in land surveying, construction layout, civil engineering, mining, forensics and archaeology, where they are used to record the positions of excavated features. Leading manufacturers include Leica Geosystems, Trimble, Topcon and Sokkia.
