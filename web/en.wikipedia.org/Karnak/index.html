Karnak is a vast ancient Egyptian temple complex on the east bank of the Nile at Luxor, ancient Thebes. Built and enlarged over some 1,500 years by successive pharaohs from the Middle Kingdom to the Ptolemaic period, it was the principal sanctuary of the god Amun-Re and the largest religious complex of the ancient world.

The complex

The site comprises three main precincts enclosed by mudbrick walls: the great Precinct of Amun-Re, the Precinct of Mut, and the Precinct of Montu. Within the Amun precinct stand the famous Great Hypostyle Hall — 134 colossal papyrus-shaped columns erected under Seti I and Ramesses II — along with ten pylons, obelisks of Hatshepsut and Thutmose I, the sacred lake, and the Festival Hall of Thutmose III. An avenue of ram-headed sphinxes once linked Karnak to Luxor Temple, three kilometres south, along which the annual Opet Festival procession carried the image of Amun.

History and significance

As the cult of Amun rose with Thebes' political fortunes, Karnak accumulated immense wealth in land, workshops, and offerings, recorded in detail by temple inscriptions such as the Annals of Thutmose III. The complex remained in use into the Roman era, and early Christians and Muslims later reused parts of it. Excavated and conserved since the nineteenth century, Karnak, as part of "Ancient Thebes with its Necropolis," is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of Egypt's most visited monuments.
