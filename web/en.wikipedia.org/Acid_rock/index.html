Acid rock

Acid rock is a form of psychedelic rock, which evolved in the 1960s and stressed the cultural influence of hallucinogenic drugs, particularly LSD, on musical styles. The term, which derives from lysergic acid diethylamide (LSD), is sometimes used interchangeably with psychedelic rock, but can also refer more specifically to a heavier, harder, or rawer subgenre characterised by extended improvisation, distorted guitars, and surreal or drug-referential lyrics.

Characteristics and history

Acid rock emerged from the mid-1960s garage rock and folk rock scenes in the United States, especially San Francisco's Haight-Ashbury scene, alongside British psychedelia. Bands associated with the style include Jefferson Airplane, the Grateful Dead, the Jimi Hendrix Experience, Iron Butterfly, the Doors, Pink Floyd (early), Cream, and the 13th Floor Elevators — whose frontman Roky Erickson is often credited with early use of the term "psychedelic". The music frequently featured long jam sessions, feedback, wah-wah and fuzz effects, modal melodies, and lyrics of heightened or altered perception.

Legacy

Acid rock influenced the development of heavy metal, progressive rock, and stoner rock, and its improvisational approach fed into later jam-band culture. The term fell from common use by the mid-1970s as psychedelic rock diversified, but it remains used by critics to describe the heavier wing of 1960s psychedelia and revivals thereof.
