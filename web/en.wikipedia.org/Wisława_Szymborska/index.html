Wisława Szymborska

Born2 July 1923, Prowent (Kórnik), Poland
Died1 February 2012, Kraków, Poland
OccupationPoet, essayist, translator
AwardsNobel Prize in Literature (1996)

Maria Wisława Anna Szymborska (2 July 1923 – 1 February 2012) was a Polish poet, essayist, and translator. She was awarded the 1996 Nobel Prize in Literature "for poetry that with ironic precision allows the historical and biological context to come to light in fragments of human reality". In Poland she is sometimes called the "Mozart of poetry" for the lightness and wit of her verse.

Life

Szymborska was born in Prowent, near Poznań, and moved with her family to Kraków in 1931, living there for the rest of her life. During the Second World War she studied Polish literature and sociology at the Jagiellonian University, partly in clandestine classes. From 1953 to 1981 she worked on the editorial staff of the literary weekly Życie Literackie, where her column of book reviews, "Optional Reading", became famous in its own right.

Work

Her first two collections (1952, 1954) were written in the socialist-realist manner she later disowned; from Calling Out to Yeti (1957) onward she developed her characteristic voice — spare, ironic, and philosophical. Famous poems include "Cat in an Empty Apartment", "View with a Grain of Sand", "The End and the Beginning", and "Nothing Twice". Though her output was modest — around 350 poems — her work has been translated into many languages and is read worldwide.
