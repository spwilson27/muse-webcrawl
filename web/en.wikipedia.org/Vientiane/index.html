Vientiane is the capital and largest city of Laos. It lies on the banks of the Mekong River near the border with Thailand and is noted for its relaxed atmosphere, tree-lined boulevards, and Buddhist temples.

History

Vientiane became the capital of the Kingdom of Lan Xang in 1563, when King Setthathirath moved the capital from Luang Prabang amid fears of Burmese invasion. He built Pha That Luang, which remains Laos's national symbol. The city was looted and largely destroyed by Siamese forces in 1827–1828 after the rebellion of King Anouvong. Under French rule from the late 19th century it was rebuilt as the capital of French Laos. After independence and the Laotian Civil War, it became the capital of the Lao People's Democratic Republic in 1975.

Landmarks

  • Pha That Luang — great golden stupa, national monument
  • Wat Si Saket — oldest surviving temple, with thousands of Buddha images
  • Patuxai — victory gate resembling the Arc de Triomphe
  • Haw Phra Kaew — former temple of the Emerald Buddha

Geography and economy

The city sits in a bend of the Mekong on a fertile plain. It is Laos's economic centre, linked to Thailand by the First Thai–Lao Friendship Bridge (1994) and, since 2021, by the Boten–Vientiane railway to China.
