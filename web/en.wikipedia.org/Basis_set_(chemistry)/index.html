Basis set (chemistry)

A basis set in theoretical and computational chemistry is a set of functions (called basis functions) used to represent the electronic wave function in the Hartree–Fock or density functional theory methods, turning the partial differential equations of the model into algebraic equations suitable for computation. Molecular orbitals are expressed as linear combinations of the basis functions, most commonly atom-centred functions resembling atomic orbitals.

Contracted Gaussian functions approximate atomic orbitals.

Types

Basis functions are usually Gaussian-type orbitals, which decay too quickly at the nucleus and too slowly at long range but allow efficient evaluation of integrals; they are combined into contracted functions fitted to Slater-type orbitals. Important families include the minimal STO-3G set; the split-valence Pople sets such as 3-21G and 6-31G, which may be augmented with polarisation functions (indicated by asterisks, e.g. 6-31G*) and diffuse functions (+); the correlation-consistent cc-pVDZ/TZ sets of Dunning, designed for systematic convergence with post-Hartree–Fock methods; and the Karlsruhe def2 sets. Larger basis sets give more accurate results at steeply increasing computational cost, and incomplete-basis errors such as basis set superposition error must often be corrected.
