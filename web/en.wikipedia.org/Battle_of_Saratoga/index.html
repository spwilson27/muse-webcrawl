The Battles of Saratoga (19 September and 7 October 1777) conclusively decided the fate of British General John Burgoyne's army during the American Revolutionary War and are generally regarded as a turning point in the war. The battles were fought on the same ground, nine miles south of Saratoga, New York.

Background

Burgoyne advanced south from Canada intending to cut New England off from the other colonies, but his campaign stalled after losses at Bennington and the failure of supporting columns. The American Northern Army under Horatio Gates, with key contributions from Benedict Arnold and Daniel Morgan's riflemen, entrenched on Bemis Heights.

The battles

The first battle, at Freeman's Farm (19 September), was a tactical British success but cost Burgoyne men he could not replace. In the second, at Bemis Heights (7 October), American attacks — led in the field by Arnold despite his relief from command — overran the British redoubts, including the Breymann redoubt. Burgoyne retreated to Saratoga and, surrounded, surrendered his army of nearly 6,000 men on 17 October 1777.

Aftermath

The surrender convinced France to enter the war openly as an American ally, transforming the conflict into a global war and setting the path to Yorktown.
