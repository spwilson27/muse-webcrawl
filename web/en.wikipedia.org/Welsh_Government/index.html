The Welsh Government (Welsh: Llywodraeth Cymru) is the executive government of the devolved administration of Wales. It is headed by the First Minister and a cabinet of ministers, and is accountable to the Senedd. It is responsible for devolved matters including health, education, local government, transport, agriculture and much of the Welsh economy.

History

Devolution followed the 1997 referendum, and the National Assembly for Wales was established in 1999 under the Government of Wales Act 1998, with the executive initially known as the Welsh Assembly Government. The Government of Wales Act 2006 formally separated the executive from the legislature, and from 2011 the body was styled the Welsh Government. A further referendum in 2011 granted the Assembly primary law-making powers in devolved areas; the Senedd and Elections (Wales) Act 2020 renamed the legislature Senedd Cymru / Welsh Parliament.

Structure

The First Minister is nominated by the Senedd and appointed by the monarch. Ministers and deputy ministers are appointed by the First Minister, supported by a permanent civil service led by the Permanent Secretary. The Welsh Government's main offices are at Cathays Park in Cardiff, with offices in other Welsh towns.

Responsibilities

Devolved areas include the National Health Service in Wales, schools and further education, housing, environment and rural affairs, and Welsh-language policy. Reserved matters such as defence, foreign affairs and most taxation remain with the UK Government, though Wales has gained some tax-varying powers, including the Welsh rates of income tax from 2019.
