Barnett Shale

The Barnett Shale is a geological formation of Mississippian age (354–323 million years old) located in the Bend Arch–Fort Worth Basin in north-central Texas, United States. It is one of the largest onshore natural gas fields in the United States and the birthplace of the modern shale gas revolution.

Development

The formation, named for outcrops near Barnett Springs, underlies about 5,000 square miles including the Dallas–Fort Worth metroplex. Gas production was long considered uneconomic because the shale's low permeability trapped the gas. In the 1980s and 1990s, Mitchell Energy, led by George P. Mitchell, experimented persistently with hydraulic fracturing, eventually developing slickwater fracturing; combined with horizontal drilling in the early 2000s, this made the Barnett highly productive and launched the shale boom that spread to plays such as the Marcellus and Haynesville. Production peaked around 2012 at over 5 billion cubic feet per day and has since declined as drilling shifted to more liquids-rich plays.

Resources and issues

The U.S. Geological Survey has estimated tens of trillions of cubic feet of undiscovered gas in the formation. Drilling within an urban area brought disputes over air emissions, water use, noise, and induced seismicity, contributing to wider debates about fracking.
