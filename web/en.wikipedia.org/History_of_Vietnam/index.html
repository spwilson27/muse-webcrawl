History of Vietnam

The history of Vietnam stretches back to legendary kingdoms in the Red River Delta, a millennium of Chinese domination, a thousand years of independent dynasties expanding southward, French colonization, and the wars of the 20th century that reunified the country under communist government in 1976.

Early history and Chinese domination

The semi-legendary Hồng Bàng dynasty and the Đông Sơn culture, famed for its bronze drums, flourished in the Red River valley. In 111 BC Han China conquered Nam Việt, beginning a thousand years of Chinese rule punctuated by revolts, including those of the Trưng Sisters (AD 40–43) and Lady Triệu. In 938 Ngô Quyền defeated Chinese forces at the Battle of Bạch Đằng River, restoring independence.

Independent dynasties

The Lý, Trần, and Lê dynasties built a Confucian bureaucratic state and repelled Mongol invasions in the 13th century. Lê Lợi's Lam Sơn uprising expelled the Ming in 1428. Over following centuries the Vietnamese state expanded south (Nam tiến) at the expense of Champa and the Khmer Empire, reaching the Mekong Delta. The country was divided between the Trịnh and Nguyễn lords in the 17th–18th centuries, reunified briefly by the Tây Sơn rebellion, and then by Nguyễn Phúc Ánh, who founded the Nguyễn dynasty in 1802.

French colonization and the wars

France conquered Vietnam between 1858 and 1884, incorporating it into French Indochina. Nationalist and communist resistance grew; Hồ Chí Minh proclaimed the Democratic Republic of Vietnam in 1945. After the First Indochina War, the 1954 Geneva Accords divided Vietnam at the 17th parallel. The ensuing Vietnam War ended with the fall of Saigon on 30 April 1975, and the Socialist Republic of Vietnam was proclaimed in 1976.

Đổi Mới and modern Vietnam

Wars with Cambodia and China followed in the late 1970s. The Đổi Mới reforms of 1986 introduced market mechanisms while preserving one-party rule, producing rapid economic growth. Vietnam joined ASEAN in 1995, normalized relations with the United States the same year, and joined the WTO in 2007.
