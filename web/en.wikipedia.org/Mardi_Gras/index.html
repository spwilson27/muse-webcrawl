Mardi Gras

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

[Image: A Mardi Gras parade float in New Orleans]

Mardi Gras (French for "Fat Tuesday") refers to the festive season of carnival celebrations culminating on Shrove Tuesday, the day before Ash Wednesday and the start of Lent. The most famous celebrations are held in New Orleans, Louisiana, but the tradition is observed in many places with Catholic heritage, including Mobile, Alabama (where U.S. Mardi Gras began), Brazil (Carnaval), Venice and Binche.

New Orleans

In New Orleans the season begins on Twelfth Night (6 January) and is organised by social clubs called krewes, which stage parades with floats and throw beads, doubloons and trinkets to crowds. The colours purple, green and gold—justice, faith and power—were established by the Rex krewe in 1872. Parades by Rex and Zulu on Mardi Gras day are the season's climax.

History

French colonists brought the tradition to Louisiana at the turn of the 18th century; the first recorded parade in New Orleans took place in 1837, and organised krewe parades date from the Mistick Krewe of Comus in 1857. Celebrations were suspended during the World Wars and severely disrupted by Hurricane Katrina in 2005–2006, when a scaled-down carnival became a symbol of the city's recovery.

Traditions

King cake, eaten from Twelfth Night through Fat Tuesday, hides a small figurine; whoever finds it hosts the next party or buys the next cake. Masked balls, flambeaux carriers and the meeting of Rex and Comus courts close the festivities at midnight, when Lent begins.
