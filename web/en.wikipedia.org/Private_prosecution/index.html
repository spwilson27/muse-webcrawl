Private prosecution

A private prosecution is a criminal prosecution started by a private individual or organization who is not acting on behalf of a public prosecuting authority. In common law jurisdictions, prosecution was historically the responsibility of the victim; the rise of professional police and public prosecutors in the 19th century made public prosecution the norm, but private prosecution survives as a safeguard.

England and Wales

Section 6(1) of the Prosecution of Offences Act 1985 preserves the right of any person to bring a private prosecution, though the Crown Prosecution Service may take over and discontinue it. Notable private prosecutors include charities such as the RSPCA; the Post Office's prosecutions of subpostmasters in the Horizon scandal were private prosecutions brought by a corporation.

Elsewhere

Private prosecution is possible, with varying restrictions, in jurisdictions including Canada, Australia, South Africa, and Hong Kong; in the United States federal criminal prosecutions cannot be brought privately, though some states allow limited forms.
