Henry Hill

Henry Hill Jr. (June 11, 1943 – June 12, 2012) was an American mobster who became an associate of the Lucchese crime family of New York City from 1955 until 1980, when he was arrested on narcotics charges and became an FBI informant. Hill testified against his former Mafia associates, resulting in fifty convictions, including those of caporegime Paul Vario and James Burke.

Hill's life story was documented in Nicholas Pileggi's 1985 book Wiseguy, which was subsequently adapted by Martin Scorsese into the critically acclaimed 1990 crime film Goodfellas, in which Hill was portrayed by Ray Liotta.

Early life

Henry Hill Jr. was born on June 11, 1943, in Manhattan, New York City, to an Irish-American father, Henry Hill Sr., an electrician, and Carmela Costa, a Sicilian immigrant. Hill grew up in Brownsville, Brooklyn, in a working-class family of seven children. From an early age he idolised the local mobsters, who ran the neighbourhood in a style contrasting sharply with his father's hard, poorly paid work.

At age 11, Hill began running errands at a cab stand and pizzeria across the street from his home, businesses run by Lucchese caporegime Paul Vario and his brothers. His first arrest came in 1960, at age 16, for attempting to use a stolen credit card; he refused to give up his associates and earned the mob's respect, particularly that of James "Jimmy the Gent" Burke, who became a father figure to him.

Criminal career

After a stint in the United States Army, Hill returned to organised crime, engaging in hijacking, extortion, loansharking and bookmaking in partnership with Burke and Thomas DeSimone. Hill was an associate rather than a made man, since his father was of Irish descent and full Italian ancestry was then required for induction.

In December 1978, Hill participated in the Lufthansa heist at John F. Kennedy International Airport, at the time the largest cash robbery in American history, netting an estimated $5.875 million. Paranoia and greed followed: Burke allegedly had most of the other participants killed to keep their shares and avoid scrutiny. Hill, fearing he would be next and facing a narcotics trafficking charge after a 1980 arrest, agreed to enter the Witness Protection Program and testify.

Informant and later life

Hill's testimony led to fifty convictions. He and his family lived under assumed identities in several locations but were expelled from the witness protection program in the early 1990s after repeated arrests for drug-related offences. Hill later became a minor celebrity, publishing cookbooks, opening a restaurant, and marketing his own line of pasta sauce. He married Karen Friedman Hill in 1965; the couple had two children and divorced in 1989.

Hill died on June 12, 2012, in Los Angeles, California, a day after his 69th birthday, from complications of heart disease.

In popular culture

In addition to Wiseguy and Goodfellas, Hill's story informed numerous documentaries and interviews. He was a frequent guest on The Howard Stern Show and appeared in the 2004 film The Last Days of American Crime discussions and various true-crime retrospectives.
