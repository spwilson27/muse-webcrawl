Union Cycliste Internationale

The Union Cycliste Internationale (UCI) is the world governing body for sports cycling and oversees international competitive cycling events. It is headquartered in Aigle, Switzerland, at the UCI World Cycling Centre. The UCI issues racing licences to riders, enforces disciplinary rules (including on doping), manages the classification of races and the points ranking systems in the various cycling disciplines, and supervises the world championships.

History

The UCI was founded on 14 April 1900 in Paris by the national cycling organisations of Belgium, France, Italy, Switzerland and the United States, in part because of disputes with the International Cycling Association over British representation. In 1965, under pressure from the International Olympic Committee, the UCI split into an amateur body (FIAC) and a professional body (FICP), acting as a coordinating organisation; the two were reunified in 1992. The UCI moved its headquarters to Aigle in 2002, opening the World Cycling Centre with a velodrome and BMX track.

Disciplines and events

The UCI governs eight disciplines: road cycling, track cycling, mountain bike, BMX racing, BMX freestyle, cyclo-cross, trials and indoor cycling. It organises world championships in each, awarding the famous rainbow jersey to the winners. Its flagship road events include the men's and women's UCI WorldTours, and it sanctions the Grand Tours — the Tour de France, Giro d'Italia and Vuelta a España — though those races are owned by private organisers.

Anti-doping and controversies

The UCI has been a central actor in cycling's long struggle with doping, from the Festina affair of 1998 to the United States Anti-Doping Agency's 2012 report that led to Lance Armstrong being stripped of his seven Tour de France titles. Critics have accused the UCI of conflicts of interest in policing the sport it promotes; in 2015 the Cycling Independent Reform Commission reported on these governance problems. The UCI also ran the controversial "biological passport" programme and controversially restricted bicycle design through its equipment regulations.
