Signature dishes
Salade niçoise · Socca · Pissaladière · Ratatouille · Pan bagnat · Daube niçoise · Tourte de blettes
Key ingredients
Olive oil, olives (cailletier), tomatoes, anchovies, chickpea flour, Swiss chard, mesclun

The cuisine of Nice (cuisine niçoise) is the traditional cooking of the city of Nice and the surrounding County of Nice on the French Riviera. Annexed by France only in 1860, Nice belonged for centuries to the House of Savoy, and its cuisine reflects this in-between position: Mediterranean and Provençal in character, with strong Italian (especially Ligurian and Piedmontese) influences, based on olive oil rather than butter, and on vegetables, legumes and seafood.

Characteristic dishes

Salade niçoise — in its traditional form tomatoes, hard-boiled eggs, Niçoise olives, anchovies and olive oil, without cooked vegetables — is defended by purists' associations against additions such as potatoes or green beans. Socca is a large chickpea-flour crêpe baked in a wood-fired oven and eaten with black pepper, cousin to Ligurian farinata. Pissaladière is an onion tart with anchovy paste and olives; pan bagnat sandwiches salade niçoise ingredients in a round loaf; ratatouille stews summer vegetables; and tourte de blettes is a sweet-savoury Swiss chard pie with pine nuts and raisins. Mesclun, a mix of young salad leaves, originated in Nice.

Protection and identity

Because Nice's dishes are widely imitated and disputed, local bodies such as the Cercle de la Capelina d'Or and the city-backed "Cuisine Nissarde" label (established 1990s) certify restaurants serving authentic recipes. The cuisine is a core element of Niçard identity, bound up with the local Occitan dialect and the memory of the county's separate history.
