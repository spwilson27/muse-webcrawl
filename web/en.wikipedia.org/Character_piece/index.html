Character piece


A character piece is a short piano composition evoking a single mood or idea.

A character piece is a musical composition, usually for solo piano, that expresses a specific mood, scene or idea rather than following an abstract formal scheme. Character pieces flourished in the 19th century as part of the Romantic movement, when composers wrote short, evocative works with poetic titles for domestic performance and the growing market in sheet music.

History

Although descriptive keyboard pieces existed earlier — Couperin's titled harpsichord works are forerunners — the genre took shape with Beethoven's Bagatelles and, above all, Schubert and Mendelssohn. Mendelssohn's Songs Without Words (1829–1845) established the model of the lyrical piano miniature. Schumann gave many of his pieces fanciful titles, as in Carnaval and Kinderszenen, and Chopin elevated forms such as the nocturne, mazurka and ballade.

Composers

Important contributors include Fanny Hensel, Clara Schumann, Brahms (whose late intermezzi are character pieces), Grieg (Lyric Pieces), Tchaikovsky (The Seasons), MacDowell and, in the 20th century, composers as varied as Debussy and Bartók (Mikrokosmos). Character pieces were also written for orchestra and for chamber ensembles, though the piano remained the genre's natural home.

Characteristics

A character piece is typically brief — a few minutes long — in a simple binary or ternary form, and unified by a single "character": a dance, a landscape, a literary figure or an emotion, often signalled by its title. Its appeal lay in immediacy of expression rather than large-scale development.
