Gérard Genette

Born7 June 1930, Paris, France
Died11 May 2018, Ivry-sur-Seine, France
OccupationLiterary theorist
Known forNarratology; paratext; transtextuality

Gérard Genette (7 June 1930 – 11 May 2018) was a French literary theorist associated with structuralism and widely regarded as the founder of modern narratology, the systematic study of narrative discourse. His terminology for analysing stories — including focalization, diegesis, and the paratext — remains standard in literary studies.

Career

Genette studied at the École Normale Supérieure and taught at the Sorbonne before becoming director of studies at the École pratique des hautes études. He co-founded the journal Poétique and the associated Poétique collection at Éditions du Seuil, which published much of the structuralist school.

Major works

His multi-volume Figures (1966–1972) culminated in Figures III, whose section "Discours du récit" (translated as Narrative Discourse, 1980) proposed a comprehensive taxonomy of narrative based largely on Marcel Proust's À la recherche du temps perdu. In Palimpsestes (1982) he developed the concept of transtextuality and hypertextuality — the relations of one text to another. Seuils (1987; Paratexts) studied the "thresholds" of books: titles, prefaces, covers, and other framing devices.

Legacy

Genette's distinctions between story, narrative, and narrating, and his categories of order, duration, frequency, mood, and voice, shaped generations of narrative theorists, even as he later revised and softened parts of the system in Narrative Discourse Revisited (1988).
