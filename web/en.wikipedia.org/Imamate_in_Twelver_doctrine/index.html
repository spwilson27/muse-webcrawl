Imamate in Twelver doctrine

The Imamate in Twelver Shia Islam is the doctrine that, after the Prophet Muhammad, spiritual and temporal leadership of the Muslim community belongs to a divinely appointed line of twelve Imams, beginning with Ali ibn Abi Talib. Twelvers hold that the Imams are infallible (ma'sum), possess divinely granted knowledge, and are designated by explicit appointment (nass) from their predecessor.

The Twelve Imams

The twelve are: Ali ibn Abi Talib; Hasan ibn Ali; Husayn ibn Ali; Ali Zayn al-Abidin; Muhammad al-Baqir; Ja'far al-Sadiq; Musa al-Kazim; Ali al-Rida; Muhammad al-Jawad; Ali al-Hadi; Hasan al-Askari; and Muhammad al-Mahdi. The Imams' authority is held to combine religious guidance with legitimate political sovereignty, even when the Imams were excluded from actual rule.

The Mahdi and occultation

The twelfth Imam, Muhammad al-Mahdi, is believed by Twelvers to have entered occultation (ghayba) in 874 CE — a lesser occultation during which he communicated through deputies, followed by the greater occultation, which continues today. Twelvers await his return as the Mahdi, who will establish justice on earth. During the occultation, jurists (fuqaha) exercise collective guardianship in matters of law, a doctrine developed in modern form as wilayat al-faqih in Iran.
