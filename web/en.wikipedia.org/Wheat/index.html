KingdomPlantae
FamilyPoaceae (grasses)
GenusTriticum

Wheat is a grass widely cultivated for its seed, a cereal grain that is a worldwide staple food. The many species of wheat together make up the genus Triticum; the most common is common wheat (Triticum aestivum), bred for bread-making.

History

Wheat was domesticated in the Fertile Crescent around 9600 BCE, from wild emmer and einkorn, becoming a foundation of the Neolithic Revolution. Bread wheat arose from hybridisation involving goatgrass. From the Near East it spread across Europe, Asia and, after 1500, the Americas and Australia.

Cultivation

Wheat is grown on more land than any other food crop. Major producers include China, India, Russia, the United States, France and Ukraine. Winter wheat is sown in autumn; spring wheat in spring. The Green Revolution varieties developed by Norman Borlaug in the mid-20th century dramatically raised yields.

Uses and nutrition

Wheat grain is milled into flour for bread, pasta (from durum wheat), noodles, cakes and beer. It provides carbohydrates, protein (including gluten — problematic for people with coeliac disease), fibre and B vitamins. Global trade in wheat is strategically important, and price shocks have repeatedly affected food security.
