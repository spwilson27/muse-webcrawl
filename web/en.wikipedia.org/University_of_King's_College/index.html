The University of King's College is a public liberal arts university in Halifax, Nova Scotia, Canada. Founded in 1789 by loyalist bishop Charles Inglis, it is Canada's oldest chartered university. King's is known for its Foundation Year Programme for first-year students and its School of Journalism, and it is formally associated with neighbouring Dalhousie University, with which it shares the Faculty of Arts and Social Sciences.

History

King's College was founded in Windsor, Nova Scotia, in 1788–89 by United Empire Loyalists led by Charles Inglis, the first Anglican bishop of Nova Scotia, with a royal charter granted by George III in 1802. After a fire forced the Windsor campus to close in 1920, the college moved to Halifax in 1923 with Carnegie Foundation funding, on condition of its association with Dalhousie. The original Windsor site is now King's-Edgehill School.

Academics

King's offers interdisciplinary honours programmes in Contemporary Studies, Early Modern Studies, and the History of Science and Technology, as well as degrees through the shared Faculty of Arts and Social Sciences. Most first-year students take the Foundation Year Programme (FYP), a great-books survey of Western thought from ancient times to the present. The School of Journalism offers undergraduate and master's degrees and produces the King's Journalism Review.

Residential life

Modelled partly on Oxford and Cambridge, King's has a small collegiate campus around a central quad, with residence bays, a chapel, and traditions including formal meals. Its literary and theatrical societies are among the oldest in Canada.
