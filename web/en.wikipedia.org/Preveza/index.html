Preveza

Preveza (Greek: Πρέβεζα) is a city in northwestern Greece, located in the region of Epirus at the mouth of the Ambracian Gulf. It is the capital of the Preveza regional unit and has a population of roughly 20,000. The city sits on a peninsula at the southern tip of Epirus, across the narrow entrance of the gulf from the promontory of Actium in Aetolia-Acarnania.

The harbour of Preveza at the entrance to the Ambracian Gulf.

History

The area of Preveza is rich in ancient history: the Battle of Actium, in which Octavian defeated Mark Antony and Cleopatra, was fought nearby in 31 BC, and Octavian founded the city of Nicopolis a few kilometres north of modern Preveza to commemorate his victory. The modern town developed around a Byzantine and later Venetian and Ottoman fortress at the gulf's mouth. Preveza is best known historically for the Battle of Preveza in 1538, a major Ottoman naval victory over a Christian Holy League fleet, commanded by Hayreddin Barbarossa. The town passed between Venetian and Ottoman control several times before being incorporated into Greece in 1912 during the First Balkan War.

Geography and economy

Preveza is connected to the opposite shore by the Aktio–Preveza immersed tunnel, opened in 2002, which crosses under the entrance to the Ambracian Gulf. The city's economy is based on fishing, agriculture (notably citrus fruit), and tourism; the nearby beaches of the Ionian coast and the wetlands of the Ambracian Gulf, an important bird habitat, draw visitors. Aktion National Airport serves the area.
