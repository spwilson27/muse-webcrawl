Rose

A rose is a woody perennial flowering plant of the genus Rosa, in the family Rosaceae, or the flower it bears. There are over three hundred species and tens of thousands of cultivars, grown worldwide for their beauty, fragrance and symbolic meaning.

Description

Roses are erect shrubs, climbers or trailers, usually with stems armed with prickles. Flowers are typically five-petalled in wild species, often doubled in cultivars, in colours from white through pink, red and yellow; the fruit is a fleshy hip rich in vitamin C. Most species are native to Asia, with smaller numbers in Europe, North America and Africa.

Cultivation and uses

Cultivated since antiquity in China, Persia and the Mediterranean, garden roses are classed as species, old garden roses and modern groups such as hybrid teas and floribundas. Roses yield attar of roses and rose water for perfumery and cooking, hips for syrups and teas, and cut flowers for the florist trade. The rose is the national flower of England and the United States and an enduring emblem of love. See also Rosaceae and rose hip.
