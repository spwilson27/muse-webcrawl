Wright Peak

Elevation4,580 ft (1,396 m)
LocationEssex County, New York, United States
RangeMacIntyre Range, Adirondack Mountains

Wright Peak is a mountain in the MacIntyre Range of the Adirondack Mountains in Essex County, New York. At 4,580 feet (1,396 m), it is the 16th-highest peak in the Adirondacks and one of the 46 Adirondack High Peaks. It is named for Charles H. Wright, and is usually climbed together with Algonquin Peak and Iroquois Peak from the Adirondack Loj trailhead near Lake Placid.

1942 bomber crash

On 16 January 1942, a U.S. Army Air Corps B-47-era training flight — a twin-engine bomber on a night navigation exercise — crashed into the summit of Wright Peak in poor weather, killing the crew. Members of an Army recovery party reached the wreckage in deep snow. Debris from the aircraft, including engine parts, remains scattered near the summit and is treated as a memorial site.

Hiking

The standard route follows the Van Hoevenberg Trail toward Algonquin Peak, branching onto a spur trail to Wright's open summit, which offers views of Algonquin, Mount Colden, and the Flowed Lands. The exposed summit supports fragile alpine vegetation.
