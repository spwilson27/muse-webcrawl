Sautéing

Sautéing (from the French sauté, "jumped, bounced") is a cooking method that uses a relatively small amount of hot fat in a shallow pan over relatively high heat. Ingredients are usually cut into pieces or thinly sliced to facilitate fast, even cooking.

Technique

The pan and fat are heated until the fat shimmers; food is added in a single layer without crowding so that surfaces brown rather than steam. The food is tossed or flipped — the name refers to the jumping motion of the ingredients. Sautéing promotes caramelisation and the Maillard reaction while keeping interiors tender.

Equipment

A sauteuse or sauté pan has a wide flat base and straight or slightly flared sides. Olive oil, clarified butter, and other fats with suitable smoke points are commonly used.
