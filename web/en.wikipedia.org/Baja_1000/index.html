The Baja 1000 is an off-road race that takes place annually on Mexico's Baja California Peninsula in November. It is one of the most prestigious off-road races in the world, attracting competitors from around the globe as well as celebrities and amateur racers. The race was founded in 1967 by Ed Pearlman and is sanctioned by SCORE International.

Course and format

The course alternates between a loop race starting and finishing in Ensenada (roughly 600–850 miles) and a full "peninsula run" from Ensenada to La Paz of over 1,000 miles. The terrain includes desert, mountains, dry lake beds, beaches and silt beds, and much of the route follows public roads and tracks shared with local traffic. Vehicles race against the clock, starting in intervals, with Trophy Trucks — unlimited, high-horsepower pick-ups — competing for the overall win, typically finishing in under 20 hours in peninsula years; solo motorcycle riders who complete the entire distance alone earn "Ironman" distinction.

History and culture

The race grew out of timed motorcycle runs down the peninsula in the early 1960s, notably by Bud Ekins and Dave Ekins for Honda in 1962. Early editions drew figures such as Steve McQueen and helped popularise off-road racing and the Volkswagen-based Baja Bug. The event is notorious for its dangers — spectator traps, livestock, and remote desert terrain — but remains the centrepiece of the SCORE World Desert Championship, alongside the Baja 500 and San Felipe 250.
