Estate in land

An estate in land is, in the law of England and Wales and other common-law jurisdictions, an interest in real property that confers a right to possession. Estates are defined by their duration and are the basis of the doctrine of estates in land law.

Types of estate

The principal freehold estates are the fee simple absolute in possession — the largest estate, effectively permanent ownership — the fee tail, and the life estate, which lasts for the holder's lifetime. Leasehold estates, such as a term of years, grant possession for a fixed period.

Future interests

Estates may be present or future; remainders and reversions govern who takes possession when a prior estate ends.
