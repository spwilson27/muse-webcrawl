Marriage

Marriage, also called matrimony or wedlock, is a culturally and often legally recognised union between people called spouses. It establishes rights and obligations between them, as well as between them and their children (if any), and between them and their in-laws.

Forms and functions

Marriage takes varied forms across cultures: civil, religious or customary; monogamous or polygamous. Historically it has organised kinship, inheritance, property, sexuality and the upbringing of children, and formed alliances between families and groups.

History

Anthropologists debate the origins of marriage; formal institutions appear in the earliest recorded societies. In the West, the Christian churches came to treat marriage as a sacrament, while the modern era saw civil registration, rising ages at first marriage, the decline of arranged marriage in favour of romantic choice, and — from the late twentieth century — legal recognition of same-sex marriage, first in the Netherlands in 2001.

Legal aspects

States set requirements for age, consent and prohibited degrees of kinship, and regulate the consequences of marriage for taxation, inheritance, immigration, medical decisions and the dissolution of marriage by divorce or annulment.

Contemporary trends

In many developed countries marriage rates have fallen and cohabitation risen, while the institution remains near-universal in much of the world. Debates continue over its religious definition, gender roles within it, and its place beside other recognised partnerships.
