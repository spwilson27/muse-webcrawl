Vasubandhu

Vasubandhu (Sanskrit: वसुबन्धु; Tibetan: དབྱིག་གཉེན་; c. 4th to 5th century CE) was an influential Indian Buddhist monk and scholar from Gandhara. He was a philosopher who wrote treatises on the Abhidharma (the systematic philosophy of early Buddhism) and co-founded, with his half-brother Asaṅga, the Yogācāra school of Mahāyāna Buddhism, one of the most influential traditions of Indian Buddhist philosophy. Along with Dignāga, he is regarded as one of the principal architects of Indian Buddhist philosophy.

Life

Vasubandhu was born in Puruṣapura (modern Peshawar) and trained in the Sarvāstivāda-Vaibhāṣika tradition in Kashmir. He composed the Abhidharmakośa ("Treasury of Abhidharma"), a verse summary of Abhidharma doctrine with his own commentary that was critical of Vaibhāṣika orthodoxy. Later, influenced by Asaṅga, he adopted the Mahāyāna and wrote foundational Yogācāra works. Traditional accounts say he spent his later years associated with Nālandā and died at the age of 80 in Ayodhyā.

Works and philosophy

His Yogācāra writings include the Viṃśikā ("Twenty Verses"), a famous defence of "consciousness-only" (vijñapti-mātra) that argues external objects need not be posited beyond experience, using the analogy of dreams, and the Triṃśikā ("Thirty Verses"), which sets out the doctrine of the eight consciousnesses and the three natures. His Abhidharmakośa remains a standard textbook of Abhidharma studied across East and Central Asia.
