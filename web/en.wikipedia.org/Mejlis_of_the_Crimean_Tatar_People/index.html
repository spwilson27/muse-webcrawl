Mejlis of the Crimean Tatar People

The Mejlis of the Crimean Tatar People (Crimean Tatar: Qırımtatar Milliy Meclisi) is the single highest executive-representative body of the Crimean Tatars, acting in the periods between sessions of the Qurultay, the national congress. It was established in 1991, following the Crimean Tatars' return from the Central Asian exile imposed after their 1944 deportation.

History

The Second Qurultay of the Crimean Tatar People, convened in Simferopol in June 1991, elected a 33-member Mejlis as its executive organ. Under its first chairman, the former Soviet dissident Mustafa Dzhemilev, the Mejlis coordinated the mass return and resettlement of Crimean Tatars in Crimea, campaigned for restoration of their political rights, and represented the community before the Ukrainian state and international organisations. Ukraine recognised the Qurultay and the Mejlis through a presidential Council of Representatives of the Crimean Tatar People in 1999.

After 2014

Following Russia's annexation of Crimea in 2014, which the Mejlis opposed and boycotted, Russian authorities moved against the body. In April 2016 the Supreme Court of Crimea banned the Mejlis as an "extremist organisation"; the ban was upheld by Russia's Supreme Court later that year. In April 2017 the International Court of Justice ordered Russia to refrain from maintaining the ban as a provisional measure in Ukraine's case against Russia. Many Mejlis members have since lived in exile in Kyiv or elsewhere, while deputy chairman Akhtem Chiygoz and others were prosecuted in Crimea.

Structure

The Mejlis has 33 members elected by the Qurultay, a congress of delegates chosen by Crimean Tatars worldwide. It works through departments for political-legal affairs, information, culture and external relations, and historically maintained a network of regional mejlises across Crimea.
