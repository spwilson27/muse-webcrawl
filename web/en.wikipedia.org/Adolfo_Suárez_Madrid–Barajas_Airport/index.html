Adolfo Suárez Madrid–Barajas Airport (IATA: MAD, ICAO: LEMD) is the main international airport serving Madrid, Spain. Opened in 1931, it is Spain's busiest airport and one of Europe's largest aviation hubs, serving as the principal hub of Iberia and a major gateway between Europe and Latin America. In March 2014 it was renamed in honor of Adolfo Suárez, Spain's first democratically elected prime minister after the Franco era.

Terminals

The airport has five passenger terminals. Terminals 1, 2 and 3 are contiguous in the older complex; Terminal 4, designed by Antonio Lamela and Richard Rogers with a distinctive wave-form roof supported by yellow struts, opened in 2006 together with its satellite building T4S, connected by an underground automated people mover. The airport has four runways arranged in two parallel pairs.

Traffic and connections

Passenger traffic exceeded 60 million in 2023, recovering from the COVID-19 pandemic. The airport connects to central Madrid by metro (line 8), commuter rail (Cercanías C-1/C-10 to the airport station at T4), buses, and road links to the city's ring motorways.
