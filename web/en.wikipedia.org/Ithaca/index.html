Ithaca (Greek: Ιθάκη, Ithaki) is a Greek island in the Ionian Sea, off the northeastern coast of Kefalonia, and a separate regional unit of the Ionian Islands region. The island is world-famous as the home of Odysseus, the hero of Homer's Odyssey, whose ten-year voyage home from the Trojan War ends with his return to Ithaca and his faithful wife Penelope.

Geography

Ithaca has an area of about 118 square kilometres (46 sq mi) and is divided by a narrow isthmus into northern and southern parts. The terrain is mountainous and rocky, indented by deep bays; the capital and principal port is Vathy, set on a sheltered natural harbour. The population is a little over three thousand.

The Homeric question

Scholars have long debated whether modern Ithaca is the island Homer describes. Homer calls Ithaca "low-lying" and westernmost of its group, which fits neighbouring islands imperfectly; some researchers have proposed nearby Kefalonia or Lefkada instead. Archaeological finds, including a Hellenistic inscription referring to a shrine of Odysseus, support the traditional identification. The island's name has become a byword for one's homeland and the goal of a long journey, most famously in Constantine Cavafy's poem "Ithaca" (1911).
