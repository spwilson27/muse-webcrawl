Game of Thrones is an American fantasy drama television series created by David Benioff and D. B. Weiss for HBO. It is an adaptation of A Song of Ice and Fire, a series of fantasy novels by George R. R. Martin, the first of which is A Game of Thrones (1996). The show was shot in the United Kingdom, Canada, Croatia, Iceland, Malta, Morocco, and Spain. It premiered on HBO in the United States on April 17, 2011, and concluded on May 19, 2019, with 73 episodes broadcast over eight seasons.

Set on the fictional continents of Westeros and Essos, Game of Thrones has a large ensemble cast and follows several story arcs throughout the course of the show. The first major arc concerns the Iron Throne of the Seven Kingdoms of Westeros through a web of political conflicts among powerful noble families. A second arc covers the plight of the last descendant of the realm's deposed ruling dynasty, who lives in exile and plots a homecoming invasion. A third arc involves the Night's Watch, a military order defending the realm against threats from beyond Westeros's northern border.

Cast and characters

The series features one of the largest ensemble casts in television history. Notable characters include Eddard "Ned" Stark (Sean Bean), head of House Stark; his children Robb, Sansa, Arya, Bran, and Rickon; his reputed illegitimate son Jon Snow (Kit Harington); Daenerys Targaryen (Emilia Clarke), the exiled "Mother of Dragons"; Tyrion Lannister (Peter Dinklage), a witty dwarf of House Lannister; and his siblings Cersei and Jaime Lannister.

Production

Development began in 2007, when HBO acquired the screen rights to Martin's novels. The pilot episode was filmed in 2009; after a poor reception of the first pilot, HBO demanded extensive reshoots and recasting. Filming locations included Belfast's Titanic Studios and Dubrovnik, which served as King's Landing from the second season onward.

Reception

Game of Thrones attracted record viewership on HBO and has an international fan base. Critics praised its acting, complex characters, story, scope, and production values, though its frequent use of nudity and violence was criticized. The series received 59 Primetime Emmy Awards, the most by a drama series, including Outstanding Drama Series four times. The final season, and especially its concluding episodes, received a more polarized response from critics and audiences, with many criticizing its shortened length and creative decisions, though the production values and performances remained praised.
