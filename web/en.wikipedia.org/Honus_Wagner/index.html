Honus Wagner

Johannes Peter "Honus" Wagner
Born: February 24, 1874, Chartiers Borough, Pennsylvania
Died: December 6, 1955, Carnegie, Pennsylvania
Position: Shortstop
Teams: Louisville Colonels (1897–1899), Pittsburgh Pirates (1900–1917)

Johannes Peter "Honus" Wagner (February 24, 1874 – December 6, 1955), nicknamed "the Flying Dutchman" for his speed and German heritage, was an American baseball shortstop who played 21 seasons in Major League Baseball from 1897 to 1917, almost entirely for the Pittsburgh Pirates. He is widely regarded as one of the greatest players of all time and was one of the first five players elected to the Baseball Hall of Fame in 1936.

Career

Wagner won eight National League batting titles, a record equalled only by Tony Gwynn, and led the league in slugging six times and in stolen bases five times. He finished his career with 3,420 hits, 1,732 runs batted in, and a .328 batting average. In 1909 he starred for the Pirates in their World Series victory over the Detroit Tigers, outplaying Ty Cobb in a famous head-to-head matchup. A versatile player, Wagner played every position except catcher during his career, though he is remembered primarily as a shortstop.

The T206 baseball card

Wagner is also famous for the T206 baseball card issued by the American Tobacco Company in 1909–1911. Wagner objected to his image being used to promote tobacco — accounts differ as to whether his objection was moral or financial — and production of his card was halted, making it exceptionally rare. The T206 Honus Wagner has repeatedly set records as the most valuable sports card in the world, with one example selling for over $7 million in 2022.

Legacy

After retiring as a player, Wagner served for decades as a coach with the Pirates. A statue of Wagner stands outside the Pirates' ballpark in Pittsburgh. He is consistently ranked among the finest shortstops and all-around players in baseball history.
