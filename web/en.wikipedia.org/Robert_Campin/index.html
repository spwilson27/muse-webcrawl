Robert Campin

The Mérode Altarpiece, attributed to Campin and his workshop

Robert Campin (c. 1375 – 26 April 1444), now usually identified with the Master of Flémalle (earlier the Master of the Merode Triptych), was the first great master of Flemish and Early Netherlandish painting.

Life

Campin was born in Valenciennes and is documented as a master painter in Tournai from 1406, where he ran a large and successful workshop. His registered apprentices included Rogier van der Weyden and Jacques Daret. He was prosecuted for adultery in 1432 and banished from Tournai for a year, though the sentence was reduced through the intervention of Margaret of Bavaria.

Works

No signed or documented panel by Campin survives; his oeuvre has been reconstructed around the panels once assigned to the Master of Flémalle. Key attributed works include the Mérode Altarpiece (c. 1427), the Werl Triptych (1438) and a Virgin and Child before a Firescreen in the National Gallery, London.

Style and significance

Campin pioneered the use of oil paint to render domestic interiors crowded with symbolic objects, observed in minute naturalistic detail. Alongside Jan van Eyck he transformed Northern painting, placing biblical scenes in contemporary bourgeois settings.
