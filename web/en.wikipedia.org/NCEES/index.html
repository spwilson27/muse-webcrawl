NCEES

National Council of Examiners for Engineering and Surveying
Founded: 1920
Headquarters: Greenville, South Carolina
Type: Nonprofit organization
Members: 69 state and territorial licensing boards

The National Council of Examiners for Engineering and Surveying (NCEES) is an American nonprofit organization composed of the engineering and surveying licensing boards of all U.S. states and territories. It develops and administers the standardized examinations used for professional licensure of engineers and surveyors in the United States.

History

NCEES was founded in 1920, when a group of state board members met in Chicago to coordinate licensure standards as individual states began regulating engineering practice. It was originally named the National Council of State Boards of Engineering Examiners. Headquartered for many years in Clemson and later Seneca, South Carolina, it moved to Greenville in 2020.

Examinations

NCEES develops the Fundamentals of Engineering (FE) exam, typically taken by students near graduation, and the Principles and Practice of Engineering (PE) exams taken after several years of work experience on the path to becoming a Professional Engineer. For surveyors it develops the Fundamentals of Surveying (FS) and Principles and Practice of Surveying (PS) exams. Most NCEES exams are now delivered year-round by computer-based testing.

Other services

Beyond examinations, NCEES maintains records and credentials-evaluation services that let licensed engineers seek comity licensure in additional states, publishes model laws and rules for member boards, and conducts periodic surveys of the professions. See also Professional Engineer.
