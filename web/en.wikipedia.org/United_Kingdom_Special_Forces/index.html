United Kingdom Special Forces

United Kingdom Special Forces (UKSF) is a directorate of the British Armed Forces that commands the United Kingdom's special forces units, operating as part of the Ministry of Defence's Strategic Command. Its core components are the Special Air Service (SAS), the Special Boat Service (SBS), the Special Reconnaissance Regiment (SRR) and supporting elements including the Special Forces Support Group and 18 (UKSF) Signal Regiment. The directorate is headed by the Director Special Forces, and the UK government traditionally neither confirms nor denies details of its operations.

Composition

The 22 SAS Regiment, based at Hereford, provides the Army's principal special forces capability, supported by the reserve regiments 21 and 23 SAS. The SBS, drawn mainly from the Royal Marines and based at Poole, specializes in maritime operations. The Special Reconnaissance Regiment, formed in 2005, conducts covert surveillance. The Special Forces Support Group — built around a company of Royal Marines, a company of the Parachute Regiment and RAF Regiment personnel — provides dedicated conventional support such as fire support and force protection to UKSF operations.

Roles and history

UKSF tasks include counter-terrorism (the SAS's storming of the Iranian Embassy in London in 1980 made it world-famous), strategic reconnaissance, hostage rescue, direct action and military assistance to partner forces. UKSF was created as a unified directorate in 1987 in response to lessons from the Falklands War. Since 2001 its units have been heavily committed in Afghanistan and Iraq, and to counter-Islamist operations in the Middle East and Africa.

Selection

Volunteers from any service undergo the joint UKSF selection course, centred on the Brecon Beacons and culminating in jungle training and resistance-to-interrogation exercises; pass rates are famously low.
