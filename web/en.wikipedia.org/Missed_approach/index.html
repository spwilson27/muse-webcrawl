Missed approach

A missed approach is a procedure followed by a pilot when an instrument approach cannot be completed to a landing. Every published instrument approach procedure includes a missed approach segment, beginning at the missed approach point (MAPt) or upon reaching the decision altitude/height without the required visual references. The procedure specifies the route to be flown — typically a climb ahead, possibly with turns — to a holding fix where the aircraft waits for further clearance.

When it is flown

A missed approach is executed when the runway environment is not in sight at the decision point, when the aircraft is not in a position to land safely, when instructed by air traffic control, or when an abnormal situation arises, such as a runway incursion or windshear. The equivalent manoeuvre initiated from a visual approach is called a go-around; the two are closely related and the terms are often used interchangeably in practice.

Procedure design

Missed approach procedures are designed under criteria such as ICAO's PANS-OPS or the FAA's TERPS, assuming a nominal climb gradient of 2.5 per cent unless a higher gradient is published. The design protects the aircraft against obstacles while it climbs to a safe altitude and proceeds to the missed approach holding point. Pilots are expected to begin the published procedure without delay; air traffic control may instead issue alternative instructions such as radar vectors.

Crew actions

The standard sequence calls for applying go-around thrust, raising the nose to the go-around attitude, retracting flap by one stage, confirming a positive rate of climb, retracting the landing gear, and following the flight director or navigation guidance for the missed approach track, before completing checklists and informing ATC. Because missed approaches are flown infrequently in line operations and involve high workload close to the ground, they are emphasised in recurrent simulator training.
