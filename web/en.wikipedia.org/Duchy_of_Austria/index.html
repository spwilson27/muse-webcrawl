Duchy of Austria

Map of the Duchy of Austria in the late Middle Ages (placeholder image)

The Duchy of Austria (Latin: Austriae Ducatus) was a medieval and early modern principality of the Holy Roman Empire, established in 1156 by the Privilegium Minus, which raised the Margraviate of Austria to the status of a duchy. It was the nucleus of the Habsburg monarchy and of the modern state of Austria, and it endured until it was elevated into the Archduchy of Austria in 1453 in practice, though the ducal title continued to be used.

History

The March of Austria was established in 976 under the Babenberg dynasty as a frontier march of the Duchy of Bavaria against the Magyars. In 1156 Emperor Frederick Barbarossa issued the Privilegium Minus, granting Margrave Henry II ("Jasomirgott") the ducal title with extraordinary privileges, including hereditary succession in both male and female lines.

Habsburg rule

After the extinction of the Babenbergs in 1246 and a period of Bohemian rule under Ottokar II, Austria passed to Rudolf of Habsburg in 1282 following his victory at the Battle on the Marchfeld. The Habsburgs made the duchy the core of their expanding dynastic lands. Duke Rudolf IV promoted the (initially forged) Privilegium Maius claiming the title of archduke, which was recognized by Emperor Frederick III in 1453.

Legacy

The duchy's core lands, divided into Austria below the Enns and Austria above the Enns, correspond roughly to modern Lower Austria and Upper Austria. Its history is the starting point of the continuous political entity of Austria.
