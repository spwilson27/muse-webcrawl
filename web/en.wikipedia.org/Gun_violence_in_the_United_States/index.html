Gun violence in the United States

Firearm deaths in the US have exceeded 40,000 per year since 2017.

Gun violence in the United States results in tens of thousands of deaths and injuries annually. In recent years the United States has recorded roughly 45,000–49,000 firearm deaths per year (48,830 in 2021, declining to about 46,700 in 2023), of which suicides constitute a slight majority and homicides most of the remainder. The US firearm homicide rate is many times higher than that of other high-income countries, a disparity commonly attributed to far higher rates of gun ownership and availability.

Patterns

Gun violence is unevenly distributed: homicide victims are disproportionately young men, and rates vary sharply by state and city, reflecting differences in poverty, gun laws and demographics. Mass shootings attract intense public attention but account for a small fraction of total deaths. Non-fatal shootings outnumber fatal ones by roughly two to one.

Research and policy

The Dickey Amendment (1996) discouraged federal research funding on gun violence for two decades; dedicated CDC and NIH funding resumed in 2019. Policy responses debated include background checks, safe-storage laws, red-flag laws and community violence intervention programmes.
