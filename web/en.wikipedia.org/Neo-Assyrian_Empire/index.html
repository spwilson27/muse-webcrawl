Neo-Assyrian Empire

Neo-Assyrian Empire
Period: c. 911 – 609 BC
Capital: Assur, later Nineveh
Language: Akkadian, Aramaic

The Neo-Assyrian Empire was the fourth and penultimate stage of ancient Assyrian history, spanning roughly 911 to 609 BC. Beginning with the accession of Adad-nirari II, it grew into the largest empire the world had yet seen, dominating Mesopotamia, the Levant, Egypt, and parts of Anatolia and western Iran.

Notable kings

Great rulers included Ashurnasirpal II, who moved the capital to Kalhu (Nimrud); Tiglath-Pileser III, who reformed the empire's administration; Sargon II; Sennacherib, who rebuilt Nineveh; Esarhaddon, who conquered Egypt; and Ashurbanipal, whose library at Nineveh preserved much of cuneiform literature.

Fall

After Ashurbanipal's death, civil war and a coalition of Babylonians and Medes destroyed the empire; Nineveh fell in 612 BC and the last Assyrian stronghold, Harran, in 609 BC.
