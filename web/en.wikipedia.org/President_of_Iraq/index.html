President of Iraq

President of the Republic of Iraq
Incumbent: Abdul Latif Rashid (since October 2022)
Residence: Radwaniyah Palace, Baghdad
Term: Four years, renewable once
First holder: Muhammad Najib ar-Ruba'i (1958, as head of the Sovereignty Council)

The presidency is Iraq's head-of-state office, largely ceremonial since 2005.

The president of Iraq is the head of state of the Republic of Iraq. The office took its modern form under the 2005 constitution, which made Iraq a parliamentary republic: the president is elected by the Council of Representatives, ratifies laws and treaties, formally tasks the nominee of the largest parliamentary bloc with forming a government, and serves as a symbol of national unity, while executive power rests with the prime minister.

History

The republic was proclaimed in 1958 after the overthrow of the Hashemite monarchy; early presidents, and especially Saddam Hussein (1979–2003), held sweeping executive power as heads of Ba'athist governments. After the 2003 invasion of Iraq, the presidency was reconstituted: Ghazi al-Yawer served as interim president (2004–2005), followed by Jalal Talabani (2005–2014), Fuad Masum (2014–2018), Barham Salih (2018–2022) and Abdul Latif Rashid (from 2022). By informal power-sharing convention since 2005, the president is a Kurd, the prime minister a Shia Arab, and the parliamentary speaker a Sunni Arab.
