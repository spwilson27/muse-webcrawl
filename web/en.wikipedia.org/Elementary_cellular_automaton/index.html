Elementary cellular automaton

Elementary cellular automaton
DimensionsOne-dimensional, two states
NeighbourhoodSelf and two adjacent cells
Number of rules256
Popularised byStephen Wolfram

An elementary cellular automaton is a one-dimensional cellular automaton in which each cell has two possible states (0 or 1) and its next state depends on its current state and the states of its two immediate neighbours.

Rules and numbering

There are 23 = 8 possible configurations of a cell and its two neighbours, and 28 = 256 possible rules. In Wolfram's numbering scheme, the outputs for the eight neighbourhoods (111 down to 000) form the binary digits of the rule number. Famous examples include Rule 30, which generates apparent randomness and was used as a random number generator, and Rule 110, which Matthew Cook proved to be Turing complete.

Behaviour classes

Stephen Wolfram classified the rules into four classes: evolution to a homogeneous state; evolution to simple separated periodic structures; chaotic, aperiodic behaviour; and complex localised structures capable of universal computation.
