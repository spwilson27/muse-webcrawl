Rangaku

Rangaku (蘭学, "Dutch learning") was a body of knowledge developed by Japan through its contacts with the Dutch enclave of Dejima during the Tokugawa period of national isolation (sakoku). It allowed Japan to keep abreast of Western science, technology, and medicine despite the closure of the country to most foreigners from the 1640s to the 1850s.

Development

Dutch books on medicine, astronomy, physics, geography, and military science were translated by interpreter-scholars in Nagasaki and later studied in Edo and other centres. A landmark was the 1774 translation of a Dutch anatomy text as Kaitai Shinsho by Sugita Genpaku and colleagues, which demonstrated the accuracy of Western anatomical knowledge.

Legacy

Rangaku scholars such as Hiraga Gennai, Ogata Kōan, and Philipp Franz von Siebold's Japanese students prepared the groundwork for Japan's rapid modernisation after the Meiji Restoration, and many coined technical terms still used in Japanese science.
