Burlington is the most populous city in the US state of Vermont and the seat of Chittenden County. Located on the eastern shore of Lake Champlain, about 45 miles south of the Canadian border, it had a population of 44,743 at the 2020 census and anchors the largest metropolitan area in the state.

History

Burlington was chartered in 1763 by the royal governor of New Hampshire and settled from 1773, growing slowly until after the American Revolutionary War, when its lake port made it a centre for the lumber and lake trade. The University of Vermont was founded there in 1791. In the 19th century, canals and railroads tied the port to Montreal and Boston, and the city became a major lumber and manufacturing centre. Its mayor from 1981 to 1989 was Bernie Sanders.

Geography and economy

The city rises from the lake shore to the "Hill Section" around the university. Major employers include the University of Vermont, the UVM Medical Center, and technology and food companies; the pedestrian Church Street Marketplace is the retail and cultural heart of downtown. Burlington International Airport serves the region, and a ferry crosses the lake to Port Kent, New York. In 2015 Burlington became the first US city to run entirely on renewable electricity.
