Crudo

Crudo is a dish of raw fish or seafood, dressed, typically, with olive oil, citrus juice, sea salt and garnishes. The word is Italian for "raw". Crudo differs from sashimi in being seasoned and dressed, and from ceviche in that the fish is not marinated long enough to be "cooked" by acid.

Preparation

Very fresh, high-quality fish such as sea bass, tuna, salmon, scallops or amberjack is sliced or diced, then dressed immediately before serving with oil, lemon or yuzu, herbs, chilli, or aromatics. The emphasis is on the clean flavour and texture of the raw fish.

History

Raw-fish preparations exist in many coastal cuisines; the modern restaurant crudo became prominent in Italian and Italian-American fine dining in the late 20th and early 21st centuries, popularised by chefs in New York and spread widely through contemporary menus.
