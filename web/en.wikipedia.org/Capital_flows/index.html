Capital flows

Capital flows
FieldInternational finance
ComponentsForeign direct investment, portfolio investment, bank lending, official reserves
Recorded inBalance of payments (capital and financial account)

Capital flows are the movements of money across international borders for the purpose of investment, trade, or business operations. In the balance of payments, they are recorded in the capital and financial account and mirror the current account balance: a country running a current account deficit must, by definition, receive net capital inflows.

Types

Major categories include foreign direct investment (FDI), portfolio investment in equities and bonds, cross-border bank lending, and changes in official reserve assets. Flows differ in volatility: FDI is generally the most stable, while short-term bank and portfolio flows can reverse suddenly, producing "sudden stops" associated with currency and financial crises.

Global patterns

Gross capital flows expanded enormously with financial globalisation in the 1990s and 2000s, contracted sharply in the 2008 financial crisis, and have since been shaped by monetary policy in advanced economies and the rise of emerging-market finance.
