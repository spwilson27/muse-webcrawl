T. C. Steele

Theodore Clement Steele (September 11, 1847 – July 24, 1926) — American Impressionist painter known for his Indiana landscapes. Member of the Hoosier Group.

Theodore Clement Steele (September 11, 1847 – July 24, 1926) was an American Impressionist painter known for his Indiana landscapes. Steele was an innovator and leader in American Midwest painting and one of the most famous of Indiana's Hoosier Group painters. In addition to painting, Steele contributed writings, public lectures, and hours of community service on art juries that selected entries for national and international exhibitions, most notably the Universal Exposition (1900) in Paris, France, and the Louisiana Purchase Exposition (1904) in Saint Louis, Missouri.

Early life and education

Steele was born in Owen County, Indiana, the eldest child of Samuel Hamilton and Harriet (Newitt) Steele. He began painting as a teenager and exhibited early promise. In 1880 he went to Munich, Germany, to study at the Academy of Fine Arts, supported by a group of Indiana patrons who each subscribed money toward his training in exchange for paintings.

Career

After returning to Indianapolis in 1885, Steele became a leading portraitist and landscape painter. With fellow artists J. Ottis Adams, William Forsyth, Richard Gruelle and Otto Stark he formed the Hoosier Group. In 1907 he moved to Brown County, Indiana, where the rugged hills provided the subjects for his most celebrated landscapes. His home there, the "House of the Singing Winds", is now the T. C. Steele State Historic Site.

Legacy

Steele's works are held by major institutions including the Indianapolis Museum of Art. He is regarded as Indiana's most renowned painter, credited with bringing Impressionism to the American Midwest.
