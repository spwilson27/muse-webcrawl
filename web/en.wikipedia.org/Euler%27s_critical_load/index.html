Euler's critical load

Euler's critical load is the compressive load at which a slender column will suddenly bend or "buckle". Leonhard Euler derived the formula in 1757, and it remains a cornerstone of structural engineering.

Formula

The critical load is given by Pcr = π²EI / (KL)², where E is the modulus of elasticity of the material, I is the minimum area moment of inertia of the column's cross-section, L is the unsupported length of the column, and K is the column effective-length factor, which depends on how the ends are restrained (K = 1 for pinned–pinned, 0.5 for fixed–fixed, 0.7 for fixed–pinned, and 2 for fixed–free).

Limitations

The formula assumes a perfectly straight, homogeneous, concentrically loaded column. Real columns with imperfections or with slenderness ratios too low for elastic buckling — where the critical stress exceeds the yield strength — fail by yielding or inelastic buckling instead, so design codes supplement Euler's formula with empirical curves such as the Johnson parabola and safety factors.
