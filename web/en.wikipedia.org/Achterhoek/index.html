Achterhoek
Country: Netherlands
Province: Gelderland
Largest town: Doetinchem
Dialect: Achterhoeks (Low Saxon)

The Achterhoek is a cultural region in the eastern Netherlands, in the province of Gelderland, lying east of the IJssel river and bordering Germany. Its name means "rear corner", referring to its position relative to Utrecht and the rest of the country.

Geography

The region is largely rural, characterized by a patchwork of fields, woodlands, and small towns including Doetinchem, Winterswijk, Zutphen (at its edge), Lochem, and Groenlo. Rivers such as the Berkel and the Oude IJssel cross the area.

Culture

The Achterhoek is known for its Low Saxon dialect (Achterhoeks), castle estates, and events such as the Zwarte Cross festival near Lichtenvoorde, one of the largest festivals in the Netherlands. Grolsch beer originated in Groenlo in the region.
