Cornelius Cardew (7 May 1936 – 13 December 1981) was an English experimental composer, a pioneer of indeterminacy, graphic notation and free improvisation in Britain. A former assistant to Karlheinz Stockhausen, he became the central figure of the English avant-garde through works such as the 193-page graphic score Treatise and the text-based The Great Learning, before turning in the 1970s to political activism and tonal, accessible music.

A page of Treatise (1963–67), Cardew's celebrated graphic score.

Life and work

Cardew, son of the potter Michael Cardew, studied at the Royal Academy of Music and then in Cologne, working as Stockhausen's assistant on Carré. Influenced by John Cage and American experimentalism, he wrote Treatise (1963–67), a graphic score offering no instructions for interpretation. In 1966 he joined the free-improvisation group AMM. His The Great Learning (1968–70), seven "paragraphs" based on Confucian texts in Ezra Pound's translation, was written for the Scratch Orchestra, the experimental ensemble he co-founded in 1969 with Michael Parsons and Howard Skempton, open to musicians and non-musicians alike.

Politics and death

In the 1970s Cardew embraced Marxism-Leninism, repudiating his avant-garde works as elitist in the book Stockhausen Serves Imperialism (1974), and composed piano music in a tonal, folk-influenced idiom intended for political rallies. He was a founding member of the Revolutionary Communist Party of Britain (Marxist–Leninist). On 13 December 1981 he was killed by a hit-and-run driver near his home in Leyton, east London; the driver was never identified.
