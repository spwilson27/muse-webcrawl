Personally identifiable information

Personally identifiable information (PII) is any data that could identify a specific individual, either alone or when combined with other information. Examples include names, postal and email addresses, telephone numbers, government identification numbers, biometric records, financial account numbers, and dates of birth. Privacy and data-protection laws require organisations to safeguard PII, and its loss in data breaches can lead to identity theft.

Definitions

Definitions vary by jurisdiction. US guidance from the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) distinguishes information that directly identifies an individual from information "linked or linkable" to one. The European Union's General Data Protection Regulation uses the broader concept of "personal data" relating to an identified or identifiable natural person, including online identifiers such as IP addresses and cookies. Some frameworks treat a subset of especially risky items as "sensitive" PII, including health, financial and biometric data.

Protection and breaches

Organisations protect PII through access controls, encryption, data minimisation and anonymisation or pseudonymisation where possible. Research has shown that purportedly anonymous datasets can sometimes be re-identified by combining quasi-identifiers such as ZIP code, birth date and sex. Major breaches have exposed the PII of hundreds of millions of people, prompting notification laws and substantial regulatory penalties.
