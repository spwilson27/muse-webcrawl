Peacekeeping

Peacekeeping is an activity aimed at creating conditions that favour lasting peace. It is distinguished from both peacemaking and peacebuilding, and from the enforcement of peace by military force. Research generally finds that peacekeeping reduces civilian and battlefield deaths, as well as the risk of renewed war.

Principles

Within the United Nations framework, the deployment of peacekeepers is based on three bedrock principles: consent of the parties, impartiality, and non-use of force except in self-defence and defence of the mandate. These principles were articulated by Secretary-General Dag Hammarskjöld and Canadian foreign minister Lester B. Pearson during the Suez Crisis of 1956.

History

The first armed peacekeeping operation was the United Nations Emergency Force (UNEF I), deployed in 1956 to supervise the withdrawal of British, French, and Israeli forces from Egyptian territory. Earlier observer missions, such as UNTSO in Palestine (1948), were unarmed. During the Cold War, superpower vetoes limited large deployments, but missions were sent to the Congo (ONUC, 1960–1964), Cyprus (UNFICYP, 1964–present), and elsewhere.

After the Cold War ended, the number and size of operations expanded dramatically, with missions in Cambodia, El Salvador, Mozambique, Somalia, Rwanda, and the former Yugoslavia. Failures in Somalia (1993), Rwanda (1994), and Srebrenica (1995) prompted major reviews, including the 2000 Brahimi Report, which called for more robust mandates and better resourcing.

Composition

Peacekeeping forces are contributed voluntarily by UN member states. Troop-contributing countries have historically included Bangladesh, Nepal, India, Pakistan, Ethiopia, and Rwanda among the largest contributors. Missions combine military contingents, civilian police, and civilian staff working on governance, human rights, elections, and the rule of law.

Effectiveness

Statistical studies consistently find that the presence of peacekeepers substantially reduces the risk that a civil war will recur, lowers violence against civilians, and helps contain conflict. Peacekeeping is also comparatively inexpensive: the entire UN peacekeeping budget is a small fraction of global military spending.
