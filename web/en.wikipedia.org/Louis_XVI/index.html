Louis XVI (1754–1793) was the last King of France before the fall of the monarchy during the French Revolution. Well-intentioned but indecisive, he supported the American Revolution against Britain, yet his government's mounting debt and failed reforms helped precipitate the crisis of 1789.

Reign and revolution

Louis came to the throne in 1774 and appointed reforming ministers such as Turgot and Necker, but resistance from the privileged orders blocked fiscal reform. In 1789, facing bankruptcy, he summoned the Estates-General for the first time since 1614; its transformation into the National Assembly, the storming of the Bastille on 14 July 1789, and the October march on Versailles forced the court to Paris and turned Louis into a constitutional monarch under the Constitution of 1791.

Flight, trial and execution

In June 1791 the royal family's attempted escape — the Flight to Varennes — was thwarted, destroying public trust. War with Austria and Prussia radicalized politics; in August 1792 an insurrection stormed the Tuileries, and the monarchy was abolished the following month. Tried by the National Convention for treason, "Citizen Louis Capet" was convicted and guillotined on 21 January 1793. Marie Antoinette was executed that October. Their deaths deepened the Revolution's turn toward the Reign of Terror.
