George Campbell Scott (October 18, 1927 – September 22, 1999) was an American actor, director and producer. He achieved prominence for his stage work and for his portrayal of General George S. Patton in the film Patton (1970), for which he won the Academy Award for Best Actor — an award he famously refused to accept.

Early life

Scott was born in Wise, Virginia. He served in the United States Marine Corps from 1945 to 1949 and studied journalism at the University of Missouri before turning to acting.

Career

Scott earned critical acclaim in Anatomy of a Murder (1959) and The Hustler (1961), and played General "Buck" Turgidson in Stanley Kubrick's Dr. Strangelove (1964). His performance in Patton opened with one of cinema's most famous monologues. When the Academy voted him Best Actor, Scott declined the award, calling the ceremony a "meat parade"; he was the first actor to refuse an Oscar.

Later life and death

Scott continued to work in film, television and theatre, notably as Ebenezer Scrooge in A Christmas Carol (1984). He died in 1999 of a ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm.
