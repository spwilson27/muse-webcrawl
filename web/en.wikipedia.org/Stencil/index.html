Stencil

A stencil template and the design produced by applying ink through its apertures.

A stencil is a thin sheet of material — such as paper, plastic, metal or wood — with letters or a design cut out of it, used to produce the letters or design on an underlying surface by applying pigment through the cut-out areas. Stencilling is one of the oldest methods of image reproduction and remains widely used in industry, craft, signage and street art.

Technique

The cut-out shapes must remain connected to the sheet by bridges, which leave characteristic gaps in letters such as O and A. Pigment is applied with a brush, roller or spray. Because the template can be reused indefinitely, stencilling allows identical images or text to be repeated quickly.

History

Some of the earliest known images, including the hand stencils of Palaeolithic cave art, were made by blowing pigment around objects. Stencils were refined in China and Japan for textile printing (such as Japanese katazome), used in Europe for playing cards, wallpaper and book illustration, and industrialised in the 19th and 20th centuries for labelling cargo, vehicles and military equipment. The mimeograph and silk-screen printing both derive from stencil processes.

Street art

Stencils became a prominent medium of graffiti and street art because they allow complex images to be applied rapidly in public places. Artists such as Blek le Rat in Paris and Banksy in Britain popularised the form from the 1980s onward.
