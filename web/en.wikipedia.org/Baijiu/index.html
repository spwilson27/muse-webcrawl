Baijiu (Chinese: 白酒; pinyin: báijiǔ; literally "white (clear) liquor") is a colorless Chinese distilled liquor typically made from fermented sorghum, although wheat, barley, millet and rice are also used. It is the world's most consumed spirit by volume.

Production

Baijiu is produced through solid-state fermentation and distillation using qu, a grain-based starter culture containing yeasts, molds and bacteria that simultaneously saccharifies and ferments the grain. Distillation is followed in many styles by aging in ceramic vessels.

Aroma classifications

Baijiu is conventionally divided into aroma types, including strong aroma (nongxiang), exemplified by Wuliangye and Luzhou Laojiao; sauce aroma (jiangxiang), exemplified by Maotai; light aroma (qingxiang), such as Fenjiu; and rice aroma (mixiang), produced mainly in Guangxi and Guangdong.

History and culture

Distilled spirits have been produced in China for many centuries, and baijiu occupies a central place in banquets and business culture, where communal toasting with small cups is customary. Leading producers include Kweichow Moutai, Wuliangye and Yanghe.
