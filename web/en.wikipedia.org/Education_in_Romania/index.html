Education in Romania

Education in Romania is based on a free-tuition, egalitarian system administered by the Ministry of Education. Compulsory education runs for eleven years, from preparatory grade through the first two years of high school. Access to free education is guaranteed by Article 32 of the Constitution of Romania.

Structure

The system comprises kindergarten (grădiniță, ages 3–6), primary school (grades 1–4), gymnasium (grades 5–8), and high school or vocational school (grades 9–12/13). At the end of grade 8 students sit a national evaluation examination that determines high-school placement. Secondary education culminates in the Bacalaureat (Bac), a national examination required for university entry.

Higher education

Romania follows the Bologna Process structure of bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees. Major universities include the University of Bucharest, Babeș-Bolyai University in Cluj-Napoca, Alexandru Ioan Cuza University in Iași, and the Politehnica University of Bucharest. Public higher education is tuition-free for a quota of students admitted on merit, with others paying fees.

History

Modern Romanian education was shaped by the reforms of Spiru Haret in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. During the communist period education was centralized and ideologically controlled; after 1989 the system underwent repeated reforms, including the 2011 National Education Law, amid ongoing debates about funding, rural access and examination integrity.
