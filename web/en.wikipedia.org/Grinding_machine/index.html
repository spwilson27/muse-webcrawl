A grinding machine, often shortened to grinder, is a power tool or machine tool used for grinding — a type of machining in which an abrasive wheel removes material from a workpiece. Grinding achieves very fine finishes and tight dimensional tolerances, often measured in micrometres, and is used both for finishing hardened parts and for sharpening tools.

Types

Common types include the bench grinder, with two wheels mounted on a bench for hand sharpening; surface grinders, which finish flat surfaces on a reciprocating or rotary table; cylindrical grinders, for round workpieces, including centerless grinders that support the work without centres; tool and cutter grinders for sharpening milling cutters and drills; belt grinders; and angle grinders, handheld tools used in fabrication and construction.

Process

The grinding wheel consists of abrasive grains (such as aluminium oxide, silicon carbide, cubic boron nitride or diamond) held in a bonding material. As the wheel rotates at high speed, each grain acts as a tiny cutting edge. Wheels must be dressed to restore their cutting geometry and trued to run concentrically. Coolant is often applied to control heat, which can otherwise damage the workpiece.

Safety

Grinding presents hazards from wheel breakage, sparks and airborne particles; guards, eye protection and ring-testing of wheels are standard precautions.
