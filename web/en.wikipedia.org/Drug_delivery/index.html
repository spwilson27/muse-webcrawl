Drug delivery

Drug delivery is the process or formulation technology by which a pharmaceutical compound is administered or transported in the body to safely achieve its desired therapeutic effect. It encompasses the route of administration, the dosage form, the release profile of the active ingredient, and technologies intended to target delivery to a specific site.

Routes of administration

Drugs may be given enterally (oral, rectal), parenterally (intravenous, intramuscular, subcutaneous injection), by inhalation, transdermally through the skin, intranasally, or by other routes. Oral delivery is the most convenient but is limited by degradation in the gastrointestinal tract and first-pass metabolism in the liver; many biologics must instead be injected.

Controlled release and targeting

Controlled-release formulations — including osmotic tablets, matrix systems, implants and depot injections — release drug at a predetermined rate to maintain steady concentrations and reduce dosing frequency. Targeted delivery seeks to concentrate the drug at diseased tissue while sparing healthy tissue, for example by antibody–drug conjugates or nanoparticles that exploit the enhanced permeability of tumour vasculature.

Carriers and technologies

Important carrier systems include liposomes, polymeric nanoparticles and micelles, dendrimers, and lipid nanoparticles, which became widely known as the delivery vehicle for mRNA vaccines. Medical devices such as insulin pumps, inhalers and drug-eluting stents also form part of the field.
