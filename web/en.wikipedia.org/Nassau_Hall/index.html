Nassau Hall

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Nassau Hall
Location: Princeton University, Princeton, New Jersey
Completed: 1756
Architect: Robert Smith
Named for: William III, Prince of Orange of the House of Nassau

Nassau Hall is the oldest building at Princeton University in Princeton, New Jersey. Completed in 1756, it originally housed the entire College of New Jersey, as the university was then known, and was reputed at the time to be the largest stone building in the American colonies. It currently houses the office of the university president and other administrative offices, and is the symbolic centre of the university.

History

The building was designed by the architect Robert Smith and constructed in 1754–1756. It was named in honour of King William III of England, a member of the Dutch House of Orange-Nassau. At various times it has served as a dormitory, classroom building, chapel, library, and museum.

During the American Revolutionary War, Nassau Hall was occupied successively by British and American troops and was damaged at the Battle of Princeton on 3 January 1777; a cannonball scar on its south wall is traditionally attributed to artillery directed by Alexander Hamilton. In 1783, the Continental Congress met in Nassau Hall for about four months, making Princeton the temporary capital of the United States; there Congress received the news that the Treaty of Paris had been signed, ending the war.

Architecture

Built of local stone in a late Georgian style, the building is four storeys high with a distinctive central cupola. It survived fires in 1802 and 1855, after each of which it was rebuilt within its original walls to designs that included work by Benjamin Latrobe and John Notman.
