Venus (Latin: Venus, genitive Veneris) is a Roman goddess whose functions encompass love, beauty, desire, sex, fertility, and prosperity. In Roman mythology she was the mother of the hero Aeneas, ancestor of the Roman people, and thus an ancestress of Rome itself. She is the Roman counterpart of the Greek Aphrodite.

Worship

Though an Italic goddess of gardens and springtime may underlie her, Venus's cult was transformed by identification with Aphrodite; her first temple in Rome was dedicated in 293 BC. Julius Caesar claimed descent from her through Aeneas and built her a temple as Venus Genetrix ("Venus the Mother"); Augustus continued the association. Her festivals included the Veneralia (April 1) and the Vinalia.

Myth and iconography

Venus's husband was Vulcan, god of fire, but her lover was Mars, with whom she was caught in an ingenious net in a famous tale. Her son by the Trojan Anchises was Aeneas; by Mars she was mother (in some accounts) of Harmonia. She is attended by the Graces and by Cupid, and her sacred plants are the rose and myrtle; doves and swans draw her chariot.

Legacy in art

Venus became one of the most-depicted figures in Western art, from the Venus de Milo and Botticelli's Birth of Venus to Titian's Venus of Urbino, providing a sanctioned pretext for the female nude. The second planet from the Sun bears her name.
