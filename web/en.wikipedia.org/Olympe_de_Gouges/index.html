Olympe de Gouges (born Marie Gouze; 7 May 1748 – 3 November 1793) was a French playwright and political activist whose writings on women's rights and abolitionism reached a large audience in various countries.

Declaration of the Rights of Woman

Responding to the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen of 1789, she published the Declaration of the Rights of Woman and of the Female Citizen in 1791, asserting that women are born free and equal to men in rights. The document demanded full legal, political, and social equality, including the right to vote, hold office, and own property.

Abolitionism and death

Her play L'Esclavage des Noirs attacked slavery in the French colonies. Her association with the moderate Girondins and her criticism of Robespierre led to her arrest during the Reign of Terror; she was guillotined in Paris on 3 November 1793.
