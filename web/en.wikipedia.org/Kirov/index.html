Kirov is a city in Russia, the administrative centre of Kirov Oblast, situated on the Vyatka River about 900 kilometres northeast of Moscow. Founded as Khlynov in the twelfth century, it was later called Vyatka, and was renamed in 1934 in honour of the assassinated Soviet leader Sergey Kirov, despite Kirov having no particular connection to the city.

History

The city grew as a fur-trading outpost of Novgorod and later Moscow on the edge of the Siberian frontier, and it became a provincial capital under Catherine the Great, who renamed it Vyatka in 1780. In the nineteenth century it was a place of political exile — the writer Alexander Herzen and the revolutionary architect of satire Mikhail Saltykov-Shchedrin both served exile there.

Economy and culture

Kirov is an industrial and railway centre producing machinery, tyres and timber products, and is famous for Dymkovo toys, brightly painted clay figurines made in the Dymkovo suburb since the sixteenth century. Referendums on restoring the name Vyatka have repeatedly been postponed by the authorities.
