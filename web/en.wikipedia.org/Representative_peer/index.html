Representative peer

A representative peer is one of a select group of peers elected by the whole of the Peerage of Scotland or the Peerage of Ireland to sit in the House of Lords of the Parliament of the United Kingdom. The system was created by the Acts of Union: the Act of Union 1707, which united Scotland with England, and the Acts of Union 1800, which united Ireland with Great Britain.

Scottish representative peers were elected at Holyrood Palace for each Parliament.

Scotland

Unlike English peers, who (with some exceptions) all sat in the Lords by right, the much larger body of Scottish peers elected sixteen of their number to represent them in each Parliament. Elections took place at the Palace of Holyroodhouse in Edinburgh. The system lasted until the Peerage Act 1963, which granted all Scottish peers the right to sit in the House of Lords, abolishing the representative elections.

Ireland

The Irish peerage elected twenty-eight temporal peers to sit for life, together with four spiritual peers (bishops of the Church of Ireland) who sat by rotation for a session at a time. Irish representative elections ceased after the creation of the Irish Free State in 1922, though existing life peers retained their seats; the last Irish representative peer died in 1961.
