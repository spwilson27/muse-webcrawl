Chinese tea culture

Chinese tea culture (Chinese: 中国茶文化) refers to the methods of preparation of tea, the equipment used, and the occasions on which tea is consumed in China. Tea culture in China differs from that of Europe, Britain or Japan in the preparation, flavour, and the occasion when it is consumed. Tea is still consumed regularly, both at casual and formal occasions in modern China. In addition to being a popular beverage, tea is used in traditional Chinese medicine as well as in Chinese cuisine.

History

Tea drinking is held to have originated in China, with the legendary emperor Shennong said to have discovered tea as a beverage around 2737 BC. By the Tang dynasty (618–907), tea drinking had become widespread, and Lu Yu wrote The Classic of Tea (茶經, Chájīng), the first monograph on tea in the world, around 760–780 AD. During the Song dynasty (960–1279), tea houses flourished and elaborate tea competitions were held among scholars and officials. The Ming dynasty saw the shift from compressed tea cakes to loose-leaf tea, steeped in teapots in a manner closer to modern practice.

The tea ceremony

The Chinese tea ceremony, sometimes called gongfu cha (功夫茶, "tea made with skill"), emphasises small teapots, multiple short infusions, and close attention to water temperature, leaf quantity, and timing. It developed especially in the Chaoshan region of Guangdong and in Fujian. The ceremony is intended to bring out the aroma and taste of fine oolong and other teas, and to create a calm, sociable atmosphere.

Teahouses and social customs

Teahouses have long served as social centres in China, places for business negotiation, family gatherings, and leisure. Offering tea is a customary gesture of respect: younger people serve tea to elders, and guests are routinely offered tea on arrival. In Cantonese yum cha culture, tea accompanies dim sum, and diners tap the table with two fingers to silently thank the person who poured. Tea also figures in wedding rituals, where the bride and groom serve tea to their parents as a sign of gratitude and formal introduction to the family.

Types of tea

Chinese teas are commonly divided by processing into green, white, yellow, oolong, black (called "red tea", 红茶, in Chinese), and dark or fermented teas such as pu-erh. Famous varieties include Longjing (Dragon Well) green tea from Hangzhou, Tieguanyin oolong from Fujian, Da Hong Pao rock tea from the Wuyi Mountains, and jasmine-scented teas.
