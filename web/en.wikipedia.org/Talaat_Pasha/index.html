Mehmed Talaat (1874 – 15 March 1921), commonly known as Talaat Pasha, was an Ottoman Turkish politician and one of the principal leaders of the Ottoman Empire during the final years of its existence. A leading member of the Committee of Union and Progress (CUP), he served as Minister of the Interior and later as Grand Vizier (1917–1918), forming with Enver Pasha and Djemal Pasha the ruling triumvirate known as the "Three Pashas".

Role in the First World War

Talaat was a central figure in the Ottoman government's wartime policies. As Minister of the Interior, he ordered the arrest of Armenian intellectuals and community leaders in Constantinople on 24 April 1915 and signed the Tehcir (deportation) Law of May 1915. Historians widely regard him as one of the chief architects of the Armenian genocide, in which hundreds of thousands of Ottoman Armenians died through massacres, deportations, and death marches. He was also implicated in policies against Ottoman Greeks and Assyrians.

Flight, trial, and assassination

After the Ottoman defeat in 1918, Talaat fled to Berlin. In 1919 an Ottoman court-martial convicted him of war crimes and sentenced him to death in absentia; the verdict was later annulled under the nationalist government in Ankara. On 15 March 1921, Talaat was assassinated in Berlin by Soghomon Tehlirian, an Armenian survivor, as part of Operation Nemesis. Tehlirian was acquitted by a German jury after a trial that drew international attention to the genocide.

Legacy

Talaat remains a deeply controversial figure: commemorated in Turkey by some as a statesman, his remains were repatriated in 1943 and reburied in Istanbul, while internationally he is remembered chiefly for his responsibility for the Armenian genocide, which Turkey's government disputes as a matter of wartime relocation.
