The Mille Miglia (Italian for "Thousand Miles") was an open-road endurance motor race held in Italy from 1927 to 1957. Along with the Targa Florio and Le Mans, it helped establish the prestige of grand touring cars and made famous marques such as Alfa Romeo, Mercedes-Benz, Porsche and Ferrari. Cars raced on public roads — unclosed — on a figure-eight loop of roughly 1,600 kilometres from Brescia down to Rome and back.

History

The race was created in 1927 by four young Brescian enthusiasts, including Count Aymo Maggi and Count Franco Mazzotti, in response to the Italian Grand Prix moving away from Brescia. Italian drivers and cars dominated early editions; the 1930 victory of Tazio Nuvolari, said to have overtaken Achille Varzi in darkness with his headlights off, became legend. In 1955 Stirling Moss, navigated by Denis Jenkinson using a pioneering pacenote "toilet roll" device, won at an average speed of nearly 98 mph in a Mercedes-Benz 300 SLR — a record for the event. Juan Manuel Fangio, Alberto Ascari and Piero Taruffi were among the other famous winners.

End and revival

The race was banned after 1957, when a crash at Guidizzolo killed Alfonso de Portago, his co-driver and nine spectators. Since 1977 the Mille Miglia has been revived as a regularity rally ("Mille Miglia Storica") for cars built before 1957, remaining one of the most prestigious events in historic motoring.
