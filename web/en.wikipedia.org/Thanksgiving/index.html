Thanksgiving is a national holiday celebrated in the United States, Canada and several other countries as a day of giving thanks for the harvest and the blessings of the past year. In the United States it falls on the fourth Thursday of November; the Canadian holiday, with distinct origins, is held on the second Monday of October.

Origins

The American tradition is commonly traced to the harvest feast of 1621 at Plymouth Colony, shared by the Pilgrims and the Wampanoag people, including chief Massasoit. Days of thanksgiving were proclaimed sporadically thereafter; George Washington issued the first national thanksgiving proclamation in 1789. After a long campaign by writer Sarah Josepha Hale, President Abraham Lincoln proclaimed a national Thanksgiving Day in 1863, during the Civil War. In 1941 Congress fixed the date as the fourth Thursday in November.

Traditions

Families gather for a large meal centred on roast turkey — so emblematic that the President annually "pardons" a turkey. Other traditions include the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York (since 1924), professional and college American football games, charitable food drives, and the start of the Christmas shopping season with Black Friday the following day. Native American perspectives are also marked by the National Day of Mourning, observed at Plymouth since 1970.

Travel and economics

Thanksgiving is among the busiest travel periods of the year in the United States, and the Wednesday before and Sunday after are among the heaviest days for air and road travel.
