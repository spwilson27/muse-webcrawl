Federal agency

A federal agency is an organisation within a federal government charged with the oversight and administration of specific functions. In the United States, the term "government agency" or "administrative agency" usually applies to one of the United States federal executive departments, an independent federal agency, or a subordinate bureau or office of either.

Types

Agencies may be created by statute, by executive order, or by departmental directive. Executive agencies are housed within cabinet departments — for example the Food and Drug Administration within the Department of Health and Human Services — while independent agencies such as the Environmental Protection Agency and the Federal Communications Commission operate outside direct presidential control of their day-to-day decisions, with commissioners appointed for fixed terms.

Function

Modern agencies combine rulemaking, enforcement and adjudicative functions. Their regulations, published in the Federal Register and codified in the Code of Federal Regulations, have the force of law. The Administrative Procedure Act of 1946 governs agency rulemaking and judicial review of agency action.
