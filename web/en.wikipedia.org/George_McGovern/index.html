George Stanley McGovern (1922–2012) was an American historian and politician who represented South Dakota in the United States Senate and was the Democratic Party's nominee for President in 1972, running on an anti–Vietnam War platform.

Early life and war service

The son of a Wesleyan Methodist minister, McGovern flew 35 combat missions as a B-24 bomber pilot in the Second World War and received the Distinguished Flying Cross. He earned a PhD in history from Northwestern University and taught at Dakota Wesleyan University before entering politics.

Congressional career

McGovern served in the House of Representatives from 1957 to 1961 and directed President Kennedy's Food for Peace programme. Elected to the Senate in 1962, he became a leading liberal voice on agriculture, hunger, and — increasingly — opposition to the Vietnam War, co-sponsoring the McGovern–Hatfield Amendment to end the war by congressional action.

The 1972 campaign

Benefiting from new delegate-selection rules he had helped write, McGovern won the 1972 Democratic nomination. His campaign was damaged by the withdrawal of his first running mate, Thomas Eagleton, and by an image of radicalism; he lost to Richard Nixon in one of the largest landslides in American history, carrying only Massachusetts and the District of Columbia.

Later life

Defeated for the Senate in 1980, McGovern devoted himself to fighting world hunger, serving as the first UN Global Ambassador on World Hunger and co-founding an international school-feeding programme with Bob Dole. He died in 2012.
