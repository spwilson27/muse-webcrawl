Inter-state river water disputes in India

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The river systems of India, shared between multiple states, have given rise to numerous inter-state river water disputes over the sharing of waters for irrigation, drinking, and hydroelectric power. As state populations and demand for water have grown, upstream and downstream states have contested allocations from rivers such as the Cauvery (Kaveri), Krishna, Godavari, Ravi–Beas, and Mahanadi.

Constitutional and legal framework

Under the Constitution of India, water is primarily a State subject (Entry 17, State List), but Parliament may provide for adjudication of inter-state river disputes under Article 262 and Entry 56 of the Union List. The Inter-State River Water Disputes Act, 1956 empowers the Union government to constitute a tribunal when negotiations fail. Tribunal awards are binding, and the Supreme Court's jurisdiction was originally barred, though the Court has entertained appeals under Article 136.

Major disputes

The Cauvery dispute between Karnataka and Tamil Nadu (with Kerala and Puducherry) traces to agreements of 1892 and 1924 under British rule; a tribunal constituted in 1990 delivered its final award in 2007, and the Supreme Court modified allocations in 2018, leading to the creation of the Cauvery Water Management Authority. Other major tribunals have addressed the Krishna waters (in two rounds, complicated by the 2014 bifurcation creating Telangana), the Godavari, the Narmada, the Ravi–Beas waters (linked to the Sutlej–Yamuna Link canal controversy in Punjab and Haryana), and the Mahanadi between Odisha and Chhattisgarh.

Issues and reform proposals

Tribunals have often taken decades, and implementation of awards has faced political resistance and agitation, including violent protests during drought years. Reform proposals include a single permanent tribunal, time limits on proceedings, a national water data agency, and amendments enacted in later years to streamline the process.
