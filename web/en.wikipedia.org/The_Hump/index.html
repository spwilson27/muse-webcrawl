The Hump

LocationEastern Himalayas, between Assam (India) and Kunming (China)
Period1942–1945
OperatorsUS Army Air Forces (Air Transport Command), China National Aviation Corporation

The Hump was the name given by Allied airmen to the eastern end of the Himalayan Mountains, over which they flew military transport aircraft from Assam in British India to Kunming in China during the Second World War. The airlift supplied the Chinese war effort and American air forces in China after the Japanese closed the Burma Road in 1942.

Operations

Flying unpressurised aircraft such as the C-47, C-46, and later the C-54 over peaks rising to 15,000 feet and beyond, crews faced extreme weather, icing, turbulence, Japanese fighters, and a chronic shortage of airfields and navigational aids. At its peak the operation delivered some 71,000 tons of cargo in a single month (July 1945). In total the airlift carried roughly 650,000 tons of supplies.

Cost and legacy

More than 500 aircraft and over 1,300 aircrew were lost on the route, whose wreckage-strewn path through the mountains was grimly nicknamed the "aluminum trail". The Hump airlift is considered a landmark in military logistics and strategic air transport.
