Tuareg rebellion (2012)


Rebel fighters in northern Mali, 2012

The Tuareg rebellion of 2012 was an armed conflict in northern Mali led by the National Movement for the Liberation of Azawad (MNLA), a secular Tuareg separatist movement. Beginning in January 2012, the rebellion drove the Malian army from the north and on 6 April 2012 the MNLA unilaterally declared the independence of "Azawad".

Course of the conflict

The rebels were strengthened by fighters and heavy weapons returning from Libya after the fall of Muammar Gaddafi in 2011. Dissatisfaction with the government's handling of the rebellion triggered a military coup in Bamako in March 2012, which further collapsed state control. The MNLA soon lost control of the north to its Islamist allies-turned-rivals—Ansar Dine, MUJAO, and al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb—which imposed strict sharia law in cities such as Timbuktu and Gao.

Aftermath

When Islamist forces advanced south in January 2013, France intervened militarily in Operation Serval, retaking the northern cities, and a UN peacekeeping mission (MINUSMA) followed. A peace agreement signed in Algiers in 2015 granted greater autonomy but not independence, and insecurity in northern Mali persisted.
