Centrism

Centrism is a political outlook or position involving acceptance or support of a balance of social equality and a degree of social hierarchy while opposing political changes that would result in a significant shift of society strongly to the left or the right. Centrist parties and politicians generally advocate gradual reform within existing institutions, compromise, and pragmatic policy-making.

Characteristics

What counts as the centre depends on a country's political culture: positions considered centrist in one context may be mainstream-left or mainstream-right in another. Centrists often support mixed economies, moderate taxation and welfare provision, and civil liberties. Critics argue centrism can amount to unprincipled splitting of differences, while supporters present it as evidence-based moderation.

History

Centrist movements include Christian democratic and liberal parties in Europe, "Third Way" politics associated with the 1990s, and various catch-all parties elsewhere. In Marxist terminology, "centrism" also had a distinct historical meaning for currents between reformism and revolution, such as those around the "Two-and-a-half International" of the early 1920s.
