ShakeAlert is an earthquake early warning system managed by the United States Geological Survey (USGS) for the West Coast of the United States. It detects significant earthquakes via a network of seismic sensors quickly enough that alerts can reach people before strong shaking arrives — seconds to tens of seconds of warning.

Operation

Seismic stations detect the fast-moving, weaker P waves of an earthquake and estimate its location and magnitude before the slower, more damaging S waves arrive. Alerts are distributed through the Wireless Emergency Alerts system, mobile apps such as MyShake, and partner systems that can trigger automated actions like slowing trains, opening fire-station doors, and pausing surgeries.

Rollout

Public alerting began in California in 2019 and was extended to Oregon and Washington by 2021, completing coverage of the three-state system. ShakeAlert does not predict earthquakes; it provides rapid notification once an earthquake has begun.
