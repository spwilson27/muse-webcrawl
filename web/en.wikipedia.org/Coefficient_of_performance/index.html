Coefficient of performance

The coefficient of performance or COP (sometimes CP or CoP) of a heat pump, refrigerator or air conditioning system is a ratio of useful heating or cooling provided to the work (energy) required. Higher COPs equate to higher efficiency, lower energy (input) consumption and thus lower operating costs. COP is highly dependent on operating conditions, especially absolute temperature and the relative temperature difference between the source and sink.

DefinitionUseful heat moved ÷ work input
UnitsDimensionless
Typical valuesRefrigerators 2–4; air-source heat pumps 2–5
Theoretical limitCarnot COP for given reservoir temperatures

Equation

For a refrigerator, COP = Qc/W, the heat removed from the cold reservoir divided by work input. For a heat pump, COP = Qh/W, the heat delivered to the hot side divided by work input; the two are related by COPheating = COPcooling + 1. A COP may exceed 1 because a heat pump moves heat rather than converting work into heat, unlike resistive heating whose effective COP is exactly 1.

Theoretical limit

The second law of thermodynamics bounds the COP. For cooling, the Carnot limit is Tc/(Th − Tc) with temperatures in kelvin; real machines reach only a fraction of this. COP falls sharply as the temperature lift between source and sink increases, which is why air-source heat pumps perform less well in very cold weather.

Seasonal measures

Because COP varies with conditions, seasonal metrics such as SEER and HSPF average performance over a representative climate year and are used on consumer energy labels.
