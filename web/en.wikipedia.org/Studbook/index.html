Studbook

A studbook is the official registry of animals within a specific breed whose parents are known, maintained by a breed association, breed registry, or studbook society. Typically an animal must be recorded in the studbook to be considered purebred and of that breed. The studbook may be "closed", meaning that both parents must themselves be registered, or "open", allowing some animals with unregistered ancestry to be admitted if they meet breed standards.

Horses

The most famous studbook is the General Stud Book for Thoroughbred horses, first published in 1791 in England, which traces the pedigrees of all Thoroughbreds to foundation stock. The Thoroughbred studbook is closed: a horse can only be registered if both parents are registered Thoroughbreds. Similar registries exist for most horse breeds worldwide.

Dogs and other domestic animals

Kennel clubs maintain studbooks for purebred dogs; the registration of litters and pedigrees through organisations such as The Kennel Club (UK) and the American Kennel Club allows breeders and buyers to verify ancestry. Studbooks are also kept for cattle, sheep, pigs, and many other breeds of livestock and companion animals.

Captive wildlife

In zoo and conservation breeding, studbooks record the pedigrees of captive populations of endangered species. Regional and international studbooks, coordinated by organisations such as the World Association of Zoos and Aquariums, are used to plan matings that maximise genetic diversity and minimise inbreeding in programmes such as the European Endangered Species Programme (EEP) and the Species Survival Plan (SSP).
