Joan Baez

Joan Chandos Baez (born January 9, 1941) is an American singer, songwriter, musician, and activist. Her contemporary folk music often includes songs of protest and social justice. Baez has performed publicly for over 60 years, releasing more than 30 albums. Fluent in Spanish and English, she has also recorded songs in at least six other languages.

Career

Baez came to prominence at the 1959 Newport Folk Festival and became a central figure of the American folk revival, recording traditional ballads and songs by contemporaries. She helped introduce audiences to Bob Dylan, with whom she performed in the early 1960s. Her recordings of "We Shall Overcome" became an anthem of the civil rights movement, and she sang at the March on Washington in 1963.

Activism

Baez has been a lifelong advocate of nonviolence, civil rights, and human rights, opposing the Vietnam War, co-founding the Institute for the Study of Nonviolence, and supporting causes from opposition to the death penalty to LGBT rights. She was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2017 and received Kennedy Center Honors in 2021. Her farewell concert tour took place in 2019.
