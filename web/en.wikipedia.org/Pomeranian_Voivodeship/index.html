Pomeranian Voivodeship (Polish: województwo pomorskie) is a province (voivodeship) in north-western Poland on the southern shore of the Baltic Sea. Its capital is Gdańsk, the historic port city at the head of the Tricity conurbation (Gdańsk, Gdynia and Sopot). The voivodeship was created in the 1999 administrative reform from the former Gdańsk, Elbląg and Słupsk voivodeships.

Geography

The province encompasses the eastern part of historical Pomerania (Pomerelia) and stretches along the Baltic coast, including the Hel Peninsula, the Bay of Gdańsk and the Vistula Lagoon. Inland lie the lakelands of Kashubia, the Tuchola Forest — one of the largest forests in Poland — and the fertile Vistula delta (Żuławy). The longest Polish river, the Vistula, reaches the sea here.

People and culture

The region is home to the Kashubians, a West Slavic ethnic group with their own language, Kashubian, which is recognized as a regional language in Poland. Major cities besides the Tricity include Słupsk, Elbląg (administratively in Warmian-Masurian but historically linked), Tczew, Wejherowo, Starogard Gdański and Malbork, site of the largest castle in the world by land area, built by the Teutonic Order.

Economy

The economy rests on the ports of Gdańsk and Gdynia — among the busiest on the Baltic — shipbuilding and ship repair, petrochemicals (the Gdańsk refinery), amber processing, tourism along the coast, and agriculture in the Vistula delta. Gdańsk Lech Wałęsa Airport and a growing motorway and rail network connect the region with the rest of Poland and Europe.

History

Over the centuries the territory passed between Polish, Teutonic, Prussian and German rule. Gdańsk's role in the outbreak of the Second World War (the attack on Westerplatte, 1 September 1939) and as the birthplace of the Solidarity movement in 1980 gives the region an outsized place in modern Polish and European history.
