Optician

An optician, or dispensing optician, is a technical practitioner who designs, fits, and dispenses lenses, spectacles, and contact lenses to correct a person's vision, working from prescriptions written by optometrists or ophthalmologists. Opticians measure the eyes and face, help customers select frames, verify lens powers, and adjust finished eyewear for fit and comfort.

Roles and training

The scope of the profession varies by country and jurisdiction. In the United Kingdom, dispensing opticians complete an approved course and register with the General Optical Council; contact lens opticians undertake further training. In the United States, about half of the states license opticians, requiring formal education or apprenticeship plus examinations such as those of the American Board of Opticianry. In some jurisdictions opticians may also conduct refractions, but diagnosis and treatment of eye disease remain the province of optometrists and ophthalmologists.

History

The trade descends from the spectacle makers of medieval Italy and the London guild of spectacle makers chartered in 1629. The modern profession was formalized in the twentieth century as lens technology, contact lenses, and complex prescriptions such as progressive lenses demanded specialist dispensing skills distinct from those of the examining clinician.
