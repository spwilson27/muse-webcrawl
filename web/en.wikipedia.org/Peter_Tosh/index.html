Peter Tosh

Peter Tosh, born Winston Hubert McIntosh (19 October 1944 – 11 September 1987), was a Jamaican reggae musician. Along with Bob Marley and Bunny Wailer, he was one of the core members of the band the Wailers (1963–1974), after which he established himself as a successful solo artist and an outspoken advocate of Rastafari and the legalisation of cannabis.

The Wailers

Born in Grange Hill, Westmoreland Parish, and raised largely by his aunt in Kingston, Tosh taught himself guitar and in the early 1960s joined Marley, Bunny Wailer, Junior Braithwaite, Beverley Kelso and Cherry Smith in the vocal group that became the Wailers, recording for Studio One and later Lee "Scratch" Perry, and for Island Records on Catch a Fire and Burnin' (1973), including his own composition "Get Up, Stand Up".

Solo career

Frustrated with his treatment by Island, Tosh left the Wailers in 1974. His solo debut Legalize It (1976) became an anthem of the cannabis movement; Equal Rights (1977) featured "Stepping Razor". Signed to Rolling Stones Records, he recorded Bush Doctor (1978), including a hit duet with Mick Jagger on "(You Gotta Walk) Don't Look Back". He won a posthumous Grammy Award for Best Reggae Album for No Nuclear War (1987).

Death

Tosh was murdered on 11 September 1987 when a three-man gang invaded his Kingston home; the killer, Dennis "Leppo" Lobban, was convicted. Tosh is remembered as the militant, uncompromising counterpart to Marley, famous for his unicycle riding and his guitar built in the shape of an M16 rifle.
