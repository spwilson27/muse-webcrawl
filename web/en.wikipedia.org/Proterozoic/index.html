Proterozoic

The Proterozoic (IPA: /ˌproʊtərəˈzoʊɪk, ˌprɒt-, -əroʊ-, -trə-, -troʊ-/) is the third of the four geologic eons of Earth's history, spanning the time from 2,500 to 538.8 million years ago (Ma), the longest eon of the planet's geologic time scale. It is preceded by the Archean and followed by the Phanerozoic, and is the most recent part of the Precambrian "supereon". The Proterozoic is subdivided into three geologic eras: the Paleoproterozoic, Mesoproterozoic, and Neoproterozoic.

The name Proterozoic combines two words of Greek origin: protero- meaning "former, earlier", and -zoic, meaning "of life". The eon witnessed the transition in Earth's atmosphere from an oxygen-free state to one containing free oxygen, several severe glaciations possibly of global extent ("Snowball Earth"), the emergence and diversification of eukaryotic cells, and — late in the eon — the first large multicellular organisms of the Ediacaran biota.

Major events

Oxygenation

Early in the Proterozoic, photosynthetic cyanobacteria had been producing oxygen for hundreds of millions of years, but most of it was absorbed by reduced minerals in the oceans and crust. Around 2.4–2.1 billion years ago, during the Great Oxidation Event, free oxygen began accumulating in the atmosphere. This transformed surface chemistry, precipitated vast banded iron formations, likely triggered the Huronian glaciation, and ultimately made aerobic respiration and complex life possible.

Continents and supercontinents

Modern-style plate tectonics assembled and broke apart several supercontinents during the eon, including Columbia (Nuna) in the Paleoproterozoic–Mesoproterozoic and Rodinia in the Neoproterozoic, whose breakup near the end of the Proterozoic may have contributed to global glaciations.

Snowball Earth

The Cryogenian Period (720–635 Ma) saw the Sturtian and Marinoan glaciations, among the most severe in Earth history, during which ice may have reached the equator. Volcanic carbon dioxide buildup is thought to have eventually ended these episodes with rapid warming.

Life

Eukaryotic cells — arising through endosymbiosis, including the acquisition of mitochondria and, later, chloroplasts — diversified during the Proterozoic. Sexual reproduction likely evolved in this interval. In the final period, the Ediacaran (635–538.8 Ma), soft-bodied multicellular organisms such as Dickinsonia and Charnia appeared in the fossil record, foreshadowing the animal radiation of the Cambrian.
