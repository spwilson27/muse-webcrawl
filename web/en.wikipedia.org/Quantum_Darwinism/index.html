Quantum Darwinism

Quantum Darwinism is a theory proposed by Wojciech Zurek in 2003 that aims to explain the transition from the quantum to the classical world through a process analogous to natural selection. According to the theory, the states that survive interaction with the environment — the "pointer states" — are those that can be copied many times into the environment, and only information about them becomes accessible to observers.

Overview

Zurek combined decoherence with the concept of "environment as a witness": photons and other environmental particles scatter off an object and carry away redundant records of its pointer state. Because many independent observers can intercept small fragments of the environment and agree on the state, the information behaves objectively and classically.

Experimental tests

Laboratory experiments using photonic and quantum-dot systems have demonstrated the redundant encoding of information predicted by the theory, supporting its picture of emergent classical objectivity.
