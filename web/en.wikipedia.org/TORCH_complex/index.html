TORCH complex

The TORCH complex is a group of congenital infections that can be transmitted from mother to fetus during pregnancy and may cause serious developmental abnormalities. TORCH is an acronym for the classical members of the group.

Members

The letters stand for Toxoplasma gondii, Other agents (such as syphilis, varicella-zoster, and parvovirus B19), Rubella virus, Cytomegalovirus, and Herpes simplex virus.

Clinical features

Common signs across the group include growth restriction, microcephaly, intracranial calcifications, hearing loss, eye abnormalities, and hepatosplenomegaly, though each pathogen has distinctive features.

Diagnosis and prevention

Diagnosis relies on maternal and neonatal serology and PCR testing. Prevention includes rubella vaccination before pregnancy, food hygiene to avoid toxoplasmosis, and standard infection precautions.
