Costa Rica (Spanish: [ˈkosta ˈrika]; literally "Rich Coast"), officially the Republic of Costa Rica, is a country in Central America. It borders Nicaragua to the north, the Caribbean Sea to the northeast, Panama to the southeast, and the Pacific Ocean to the southwest. It has a population of around five million in a land area of nearly 51,180 km² (19,760 sq mi). An estimated 352,381 people live in the capital and largest city, San José, with around two million people in the surrounding metropolitan area.

The sovereign state is a presidential, unitary, constitutional republic. It is known for its long-standing and stable democracy and for its highly educated workforce. It has no standing army, having constitutionally abolished the military in 1949. Costa Rica consistently ranks among the highest in Latin America on the Human Development Index and is noted for its environmental policies: roughly a quarter of its land area is protected jungle or national park, and it is home to about 5–6 percent of the world's biodiversity despite its small size.

History

Indigenous peoples inhabited the region for thousands of years before Christopher Columbus arrived on the Atlantic coast in 1502. Costa Rica became part of the Spanish Captaincy General of Guatemala and remained a relatively poor, isolated colony. It gained independence from Spain in 1821 as part of the Federal Republic of Central America, becoming fully sovereign in 1848.

In 1948 a disputed election led to a brief civil war, after which José Figueres Ferrer headed a junta that drafted a new constitution guaranteeing free elections with universal suffrage and the abolition of the army. Costa Rica has since enjoyed one of the most stable democracies in the region. In 1987 President Óscar Arias won the Nobel Peace Prize for his role in ending the Central American crises.

Geography

Costa Rica lies between latitudes 8° and 12°N and is crossed by volcanic mountain ranges including the Cordillera de Guanacaste, Cordillera Central, and Cordillera de Talamanca. The highest point is Cerro Chirripó at 3,819 metres. The country has both Caribbean and Pacific coastlines and a tropical climate with distinct dry and rainy seasons.

Economy

The economy has shifted from agriculture (coffee, bananas, pineapples) toward services, technology, medical devices, and ecotourism. Tourism is one of the country's main sources of foreign exchange, drawing over two million visitors annually.
