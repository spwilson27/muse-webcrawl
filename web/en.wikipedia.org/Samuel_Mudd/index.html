Samuel Mudd

Samuel Alexander Mudd Sr. (December 20, 1833 – January 10, 1883) was an American physician who was imprisoned for aiding and conspiring with John Wilkes Booth in the assassination of President Abraham Lincoln in 1865. Mudd set Booth's broken leg in the early morning after the assassination and was later convicted by a military commission of conspiracy.

The assassination and trial

Fleeing Washington after shooting Lincoln on 14 April 1865, Booth arrived at Mudd's farm near Bryantown, Maryland, before dawn; Mudd set his fractured leg, fashioned a splint and crutch, and the two fugitives departed that afternoon. Mudd delayed reporting the visit and had previously met Booth — who had bought a horse from him and visited the area in 1864 — facts that undermined his claim not to have recognized his patient. A military commission convicted him of aiding the conspirators and sentenced him to life imprisonment at Fort Jefferson in the Dry Tortugas, escaping the death penalty by one vote.

Pardon and later life

At Fort Jefferson, Mudd worked in the prison hospital and distinguished himself during a yellow fever epidemic in 1867, treating guards and prisoners after the post doctor died. President Andrew Johnson pardoned him in February 1869. He returned to Maryland, resumed farming and medical practice, and died of pneumonia in 1883. His descendants campaigned for decades to clear his name; the Army Board for Correction of Military Records recommended setting aside the conviction in 1992, but the decision was overturned, and later court challenges failed on jurisdictional grounds. His name is popularly — though probably incorrectly — linked to the phrase "your name is mud".
