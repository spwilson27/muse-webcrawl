Federal Foreign Office

The Federal Foreign Office (German: Auswärtiges Amt, abbreviated AA) is the foreign ministry of the Federal Republic of Germany, responsible for both the country's foreign policy and its relationship with the European Union. It is headed by the Federal Minister for Foreign Affairs and maintains Germany's missions abroad — embassies, consulates and permanent representations.

Formed1870 / 1951 (in present form)
HeadquartersWerderscher Markt, Berlin
MissionsAbout 230 missions abroad
ParentFederal Government of Germany

History

The Auswärtiges Amt originated in 1870 as the foreign office of the North German Confederation and retained its name through the Empire, the Weimar Republic and the Third Reich. Re-established for the Federal Republic in 1951 in Bonn, it moved to Berlin after reunification, occupying the former Reichsbank building on Werderscher Markt.

Tasks

The office conducts Germany's external relations, coordinates European policy, staffs about 230 diplomatic missions, and provides consular services to German citizens abroad. Its minister is a member of the federal cabinet.
