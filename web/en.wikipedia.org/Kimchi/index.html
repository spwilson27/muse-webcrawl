Kimchi (Korean: 김치, gimchi) is a traditional Korean side dish consisting of salted and fermented vegetables, most commonly napa cabbage and Korean radish, made with a widely varying selection of seasonings including gochugaru (Korean chilli powder), spring onions, garlic, ginger, and jeotgal (salted seafood). It is a staple food in Korean cuisine, served at almost every meal.

History

Pickled and fermented vegetables in Korea date back over a thousand years; early kimchi was made without chilli peppers, which only arrived after their introduction from the Americas in the 16th–17th centuries. By the 18th century, hot peppers and napa cabbage became standard, producing the familiar red baechu-kimchi. Traditionally, kimchi for winter was prepared communally in late autumn — the practice of gimjang — and stored in earthenware jars (onggi) buried in the ground. Gimjang culture was inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2013 (South Korea) and 2015 (North Korea).

Varieties

There are hundreds of varieties, including baechu-kimchi (napa cabbage), kkakdugi (cubed radish), chonggak-kimchi (ponytail radish), oi sobagi (stuffed cucumber), and white water kimchis such as dongchimi and nabak-kimchi. Regional styles vary: southern kimchi tends to be saltier and spicier, with more jeotgal.

Fermentation and nutrition

Kimchi ferments through lactic acid bacteria such as Leuconostoc and Lactobacillus species, producing its sour flavour and probiotic content. It is rich in vitamins A and C, fibre, and capsaicin. Today, dedicated kimchi refrigerators maintain optimal fermentation and storage temperatures in most Korean households.
