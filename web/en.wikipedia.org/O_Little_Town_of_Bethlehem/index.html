"O Little Town of Bethlehem" is a Christmas carol. Based on an 1868 text written by Phillips Brooks, the Episcopal priest and later bishop of Massachusetts, the carol is popular on both sides of the Atlantic. In the United States it is sung to the tune "St Louis" by Brooks's organist Lewis Redner; in the United Kingdom and Ireland it is sung to "Forest Green," an English folk tune adapted by Ralph Vaughan Williams.

Origin

Brooks was inspired after a visit to Bethlehem in 1865, where he attended the Christmas Eve service at the Church of the Nativity. Three years later he wrote the poem for his church, the Church of the Holy Trinity in Philadelphia, and Redner composed the music for the Sunday school children's choir.

Text

The carol opens with the famous lines: "O little town of Bethlehem, / How still we see thee lie! / Above thy deep and dreamless sleep / The silent stars go by." Four stanzas reflect on the quiet arrival of Christ and a prayer that he enter believers' hearts. It is now in the public domain and appears in most major hymnals.
