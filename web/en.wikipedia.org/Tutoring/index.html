Tutoring

Tutoring is private academic instruction, usually given to an individual or small group, supplementing or replacing classroom teaching. A tutor provides personalised feedback, paces instruction to the learner, and targets specific difficulties, in contrast to the one-to-many model of a school class.

Effectiveness

Educational psychologist Benjamin Bloom's "2 sigma problem" (1984) reported that students tutored one-to-one performed about two standard deviations better than classroom-taught peers, a result that has motivated research into tutoring programmes and intelligent tutoring systems. Modern forms include peer tutoring, volunteer and school-based schemes, commercial after-school tutoring (widespread in East Asia in institutions such as juku and hagwon), and online tutoring platforms connecting students with remote tutors.
