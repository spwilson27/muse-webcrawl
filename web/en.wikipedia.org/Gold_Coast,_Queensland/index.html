The Gold Coast is a coastal city in the state of Queensland, Australia, approximately 66 km south-southeast of the state capital Brisbane. It is Queensland's second-largest city and Australia's sixth-largest, famous for its long sandy beaches, surfing spots, high-rise skyline and theme parks.

Geography

The city stretches along some 57 km of coastline from the New South Wales border at Coolangatta north to Beenleigh. Its best-known district, Surfers Paradise, is lined with apartment towers including Q1, once the world's tallest residential building. Behind the coast lie canals and waterways — the city has more kilometres of navigable waterway than Venice and Amsterdam combined — and the rainforested hinterland of the McPherson Range, including Lamington and Springbrook national parks, part of a World Heritage area.

Economy and tourism

Tourism is the dominant industry, drawing around 10 million visitors a year to beaches, theme parks (Dreamworld, Sea World, Warner Bros. Movie World), nightlife and events. The city hosted the 2018 Commonwealth Games. The traditional custodians of the region are the Yugambeh people.

History

Captain James Cook sailed past in 1770; European settlement began with timber-getting in the 19th century. The area was promoted as a holiday destination from the late 1800s, and the name "Gold Coast" — originally applied derisively to its high prices in the 1940s — became official in 1958.
