Blenheim Palace is a country house in Woodstock, Oxfordshire, England. It is the seat of the Dukes of Marlborough and the only non-royal, non-episcopal country house in England to hold the title of palace. One of England's largest houses, it was built between 1705 and 1722 and was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1987.

The palace was a gift from a grateful nation to John Churchill, 1st Duke of Marlborough, in recognition of his victory at the Battle of Blenheim in 1704 during the War of the Spanish Succession. It was designed in the rare English Baroque style by Sir John Vanbrugh, assisted by Nicholas Hawksmoor, though construction was plagued by disputes, cost overruns, and the political fall from favor of the Duke and his formidable wife, Sarah Churchill, who clashed repeatedly with the architect.

The palace and park

The palace's state rooms include the Great Hall, the Saloon, and a sequence of staterooms hung with tapestries commemorating Marlborough's victories, as well as the Long Library. The extensive parkland — over 2,000 acres — was landscaped in the 1760s by Lancelot "Capability" Brown, who dammed the River Glyme to create the great lake spanned by Vanbrugh's Grand Bridge. Formal gardens added later include water terraces and parterres designed in the early 20th century.

Winston Churchill

Blenheim is celebrated as the birthplace of Sir Winston Churchill, who was born there on 30 November 1874, a grandson of the 7th Duke. Churchill proposed to his future wife, Clementine Hozier, in the Temple of Diana in the palace gardens in 1908, and he is buried nearby at St Martin's Church, Bladon. The palace houses a permanent exhibition devoted to his life.

Today

The palace remains the family home of the Dukes of Marlborough while also operating as a visitor attraction, hosting exhibitions, concerts, and events, and serving as a filming location for numerous films and television productions.
