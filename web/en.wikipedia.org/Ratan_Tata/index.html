Ratan Naval Tata (28 December 1937 – 9 October 2024) was an Indian industrialist and philanthropist who served as chairman of the Tata Group, India's largest conglomerate, from 1991 to 2012, and briefly as interim chairman in 2016–17. Under his leadership the group expanded globally with landmark acquisitions including Tetley Tea, the Anglo-Dutch steelmaker Corus, and the British car brands Jaguar and Land Rover. He was widely admired for his integrity, humility, and philanthropy.

Early life and career

Born into the Tata family, Ratan Tata was raised largely by his grandmother after his parents separated. He studied architecture at Cornell University and completed the Advanced Management Program at Harvard Business School. Joining the Tata group in 1962, he worked on the shop floor of Tata Steel before rising through Tata's electronics and textile businesses. He succeeded J. R. D. Tata as chairman of Tata Sons in 1991, just as India liberalised its economy.

Leadership of Tata Group

Tata consolidated the sprawling group, raised Tata Sons' stakes in its companies, and pushed into new sectors including telecommunications and passenger cars. The Tata Indica (1998) was India's first indigenously developed passenger car, and the Tata Nano (2008) was billed as the world's cheapest car. Major global deals included Tata Tea's purchase of Tetley (2000), Tata Steel's acquisition of Corus (2007), and Tata Motors' purchase of Jaguar Land Rover from Ford (2008). Group revenues grew more than twentyfold during his tenure.

Philanthropy and honours

Tata chaired the Tata Trusts, which control the majority of Tata Sons and fund education, healthcare, and rural development across India. He received the Padma Bhushan in 2000 and the Padma Vibhushan, India's second-highest civilian honour, in 2008, as well as honorary British knighthoods. After retirement he became a notable angel investor in Indian start-ups. His death in October 2024 drew tributes from across India and the world.
