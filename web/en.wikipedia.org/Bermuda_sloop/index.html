Bermuda sloop

A Bermuda sloop is a type of fore-and-aft rigged sailing vessel developed in Bermuda in the seventeenth century and widely adopted in the eighteenth. It is defined by its rig: a single mast, raked aft, carrying a triangular mainsail behind the mast and one or more jibs before it — the rig now known as the Bermuda rig, which became the standard rig of modern yachts.

Development

Bermudian shipwrights, working with local cedar, built fast, weatherly hulls. Early Bermudian vessels used lateen-derived rigs, but by the early 1700s the triangular Bermudian mainsail — attributed in part to Dutch influences and to sailors such as Captain William Crane — had emerged. Sloops from Bermuda excelled in trade, piloting, privateering, and (regrettably) smuggling and the slave trade, owing to their speed and ability to sail close to the wind.

Legacy

The Bermuda rig — a high aspect-ratio triangular mainsail on a tall mast — proved so efficient that it displaced gaff rigs on most small sailing craft during the twentieth century and is today the default rig of racing and cruising yachts worldwide.
