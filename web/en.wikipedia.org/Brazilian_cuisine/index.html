Brazilian cuisine

Brazilian cuisine is the set of cooking practices and traditions of Brazil, shaped by Indigenous, African, Portuguese, and later European and Asian immigrant influences. It varies greatly by region, reflecting the country's size and diversity.

National dishes

Feijoada, a stew of black beans with pork and beef, is widely considered the national dish, traditionally served with rice, farofa (toasted cassava flour), collard greens, and orange slices. Rice and beans form the everyday staple across most of the country.

Regional cuisines

In Bahia, Afro-Brazilian cooking uses dendê (palm oil), coconut milk, and peppers in dishes such as acarajé and moqueca. Minas Gerais is known for pão de queijo and hearty pork dishes. In the south, churrasco (barbecue) reflects gaúcho tradition, while Amazonian cuisine features açaí, tacacá, and freshwater fish.

Drinks and sweets

Brazil is the world's largest coffee producer. The caipirinha, made with cachaça, lime, and sugar, is the national cocktail. Sweets include brigadeiros and beijinhos, staples of birthday parties.
