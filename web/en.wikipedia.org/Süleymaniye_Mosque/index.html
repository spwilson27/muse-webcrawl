The Süleymaniye Mosque (Turkish: Süleymaniye Camii) is an Ottoman imperial mosque located on the Third Hill of Istanbul, Turkey. Commissioned by Sultan Suleiman the Magnificent and designed by the imperial architect Mimar Sinan, it was built between 1550 and 1557 and is regarded as a masterpiece of classical Ottoman architecture. It was the largest mosque in Istanbul until the completion of the Çamlıca Mosque in 2019.

History

Suleiman ordered the mosque in 1550 as the centrepiece of a vast charitable complex (külliye) expressing both his piety and the power of the empire at its height. Sinan, the sultan's chief architect, modelled the design partly on the Hagia Sophia, which the mosque deliberately echoes and attempts to surpass in harmony. The mosque was damaged by fire in 1660 and restored under Mehmed IV, and it was heavily restored by the Fossati brothers in the nineteenth century.

Architecture

The mosque prayer hall is surmounted by a dome 53 metres high and 27.5 metres in diameter, supported by half-domes to the north and south and by great buttresses concealed within the walls. Four slender minarets with ten balconies (şerefe) mark the corners of the courtyard; tradition reads the ten balconies as signifying that Suleiman was the tenth Ottoman sultan, and the four minarets that he was the fourth sultan to rule from Istanbul. The interior is restrained, with light admitted through many windows and ornament concentrated in Iznik tiles, carved marble and the calligraphy of Ahmed Karahisari.

The complex

The surrounding külliye included four madrasas, a medical school, a hospital (darüşşifa), a public kitchen (imaret), a caravanserai, a bathhouse (hamam) and shops whose rents supported the foundation. In the garden behind the mosque stand the mausoleums (türbe) of Suleiman himself, of his wife Hürrem Sultan (Roxelana), and of the architect Sinan, whose tomb is at the edge of the complex.
