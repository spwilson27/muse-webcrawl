Moti Mahal

Moti Mahal is a restaurant in Daryaganj, Delhi, India, famous in the history of North Indian cuisine. It is credited — along with its founders, notably the chef Kundan Lal Gujral — with popularizing tandoori chicken as a restaurant dish and with creating butter chicken and the modern form of dal makhani.

History

The original Moti Mahal was established in Peshawar in the 1920s by Mokha Ram; after the Partition of India in 1947, Kundan Lal Gujral, who had worked at the Peshawar restaurant, re-established Moti Mahal in Delhi. According to the restaurant's well-known account, Gujral experimented with cooking marinated chicken in the clay tandoor ovens normally used for bread, producing tandoori chicken. To keep leftover tandoori chicken moist, a rich gravy of butter, tomatoes, and cream was devised — butter chicken (murgh makhani) — and a slow-cooked black lentil dish with butter and cream became dal makhani.

Influence and legacy

These dishes became staples of Punjabi restaurant cooking worldwide. Moti Mahal grew into a chain with franchises in India and abroad. The invention of butter chicken has also been the subject of litigation between rival restaurant families in Delhi claiming descent from the original founders.
