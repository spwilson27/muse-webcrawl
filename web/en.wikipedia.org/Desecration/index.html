Desecration

Desecration is the act of depriving anything of respect, or the state of disrespect toward something regarded as sacred — a place, object, symbol or grave. Desecration takes many forms, from the ritual violation of a consecrated church or the theft of sacramental vessels, to the vandalism of cemeteries, the defacement of religious images, and symbolic acts such as burning a national flag. Because sacredness is defined by communities, what counts as desecration — and how severely it is punished — varies greatly across cultures and legal systems.

Forms

Common targets of desecration include places of worship, altars, scriptures, sacred hosts in Catholic practice, burials and war memorials. Host desecration accusations in medieval Europe were a recurring pretext for anti-Jewish violence and are now regarded as blood-libel-type myths. Iconoclasm — the destruction of images for religious reasons — has been interpreted by its practitioners as purification and by its victims as desecration. Desecration of graves ranges from body snatching for anatomy schools in the 18th and 19th centuries to ideologically motivated attacks on minority cemeteries today.

Legal treatment

Many jurisdictions criminalize the desecration of graves, religious objects and war dead; the Geneva Conventions and the Hague Convention for the Protection of Cultural Property address desecration of religious and cultural sites in war. Flag desecration laws have been especially contentious: in the United States the Supreme Court held in Texas v. Johnson (1989) that flag burning is protected symbolic speech, while many other countries continue to penalize insulting national symbols. Desecration of the Quran and other scriptures has repeatedly triggered international protests and violence, keeping the subject at the intersection of blasphemy law, free expression and public order.
