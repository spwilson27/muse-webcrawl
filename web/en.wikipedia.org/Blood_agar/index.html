Blood agar

Blood agar is an enriched bacterial growth medium used to culture fastidious organisms and to differentiate bacteria by their haemolytic properties. It consists of a base medium such as tryptic soy agar with 5–10% animal blood added, most commonly sheep blood.

Composition and preparation

The blood is added aseptically to the sterile base after it has cooled to about 45–50 °C, so that the red blood cells remain intact. The medium is poured into Petri dishes and stored refrigerated. Heating the blood agar produces chocolate agar, in which the lysed red cells release additional growth factors used for organisms such as Haemophilus and Neisseria.

Haemolysis patterns

Three patterns are distinguished. Beta haemolysis is the complete lysis of red blood cells, producing a clear zone around the colony, as with Streptococcus pyogenes. Alpha haemolysis is partial lysis, giving the medium a greenish discoloration. Gamma haemolysis denotes no haemolysis at all. These patterns remain a first-line clue in clinical microbiology for identifying streptococci and other pathogens.

Uses

Blood agar is a routine primary plating medium for throat, wound, blood and other clinical specimens, and supports the growth of most common bacterial pathogens.
