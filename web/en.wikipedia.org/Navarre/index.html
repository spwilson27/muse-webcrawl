Navarre

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Navarre (Spanish: Navarra; Basque: Nafarroa), officially the Chartered Community of Navarre, is a landlocked foral autonomous community and province in northern Spain, bordering the Basque Autonomous Community, La Rioja, and Aragon in Spain, and France to the north. Its capital and largest city is Pamplona.

History

Navarre grew out of the medieval Kingdom of Navarre, founded in the early 9th century around Pamplona as a frontier polity between Christian Iberia, the Basque country, and Frankish and Muslim powers. Its kings, including Sancho III the Great, at times dominated much of Christian Spain. The southern part of the kingdom was conquered by Ferdinand II of Aragon in 1512 and incorporated into Spain, while Lower Navarre north of the Pyrenees remained linked to France through its kings, most famously Henry IV. Navarre retained its historic fueros (charters) long after unification, and today enjoys an unusually broad degree of fiscal self-government under the Spanish constitution of 1978.

Geography and culture

The region descends from the Pyrenees through rolling hills and the fertile Ribera plains along the Ebro. Basque is co-official in the north, Spanish throughout. Navarre is internationally known for the festival of San Fermín in Pamplona, with its running of the bulls, and for its wine, asparagus, and piquillo peppers. See also Pamplona and Kingdom of Navarre.
