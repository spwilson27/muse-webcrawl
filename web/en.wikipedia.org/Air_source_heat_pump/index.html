Air source heat pump

An air source heat pump (ASHP) is a heat pump that can absorb heat from air outside a building and release it inside; it can also work in reverse to cool a building. Air-source heat pumps use a small amount of electricity to run a vapour-compression refrigeration cycle, and can move several times more heat energy than the electrical energy they consume, giving coefficients of performance commonly between 2 and 4.

Operation

An outdoor unit draws in air and extracts heat from it using a refrigerant evaporator, even at low outdoor temperatures. The compressor raises the refrigerant's temperature and pressure, and the heat is released indoors through a condenser — into air, radiators or underfloor heating. A reversing valve allows the same system to provide cooling in summer.

Use and efficiency

Air source heat pumps are widely used for domestic and commercial space heating and hot water, and are promoted as a low-carbon alternative to gas boilers because they can be powered by increasingly renewable electricity. Efficiency falls as the outdoor temperature drops, so "cold climate" models are designed to keep operating usefully at temperatures well below freezing.
