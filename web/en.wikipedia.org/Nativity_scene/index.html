Nativity scene

A Nativity scene is the special exhibition, particularly during the Christmas season, of art objects representing the birth of Jesus. While the term "nativity scene" may be used of any representation of the subject of the Nativity of Jesus in art, it has a more specialized meaning referring to seasonal displays, either using model figures in a setting or live participants (a living nativity). Other characters from the nativity story, such as shepherds, sheep, angels, the Magi, an ox and a donkey, and the Virgin Mary and Joseph, are also included.

History

Saint Francis of Assisi is traditionally credited with creating the first live nativity scene in 1223 at Greccio, Italy, to communicate the Christmas story to local people. The custom spread through Europe, developing rich regional traditions: the crèche of Provence with its santon figurines, the Neapolitan presepio, and the szopka of Kraków.

Traditions

Nativity scenes range from small household displays to elaborate civic installations. In some traditions the figure of the Christ child is added only on Christmas Eve, and the Magi arrive at Epiphany. Living nativity scenes, with human actors and sometimes real animals, are performed in churches and public spaces in many countries.
