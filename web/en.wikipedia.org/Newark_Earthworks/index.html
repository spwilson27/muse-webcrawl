Newark Earthworks

The Newark Earthworks in Newark, Ohio, are the largest set of geometric earthen enclosures in the world, built by the Hopewell culture about 2,000 years ago, between roughly 100 BCE and 500 CE. The surviving Great Circle and Octagon earthworks are preserved as a state memorial and, since 2023, as part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Description

The complex originally covered more than 3,000 acres. Its best-preserved elements are the Great Circle, a circular enclosure about 1,200 feet in diameter with walls up to 14 feet high, and the Octagon Earthworks, an octagonal enclosure connected to a smaller circle, whose geometry aligns with the 18.6-year cycle of lunar standstills. A third major element, the Wright Earthworks, is a partially preserved square enclosure.

Significance

The earthworks demonstrate sophisticated Hopewell geometry, surveying and astronomy. They are among the eight Hopewell Ceremonial Earthworks inscribed on the UNESCO list in 2023. The Octagon's grounds were long leased to a golf club, an arrangement that ended after litigation, with full public access following in 2025.
