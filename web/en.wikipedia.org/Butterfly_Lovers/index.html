Butterfly Lovers

The Butterfly Lovers
Chinese folk legend
Protagonists: Liang Shanbo and Zhu Yingtai
Setting: Eastern Jin dynasty

The Butterfly Lovers (梁山伯与祝英台, Liáng Shānbó yǔ Zhù Yīngtái) is one of China's most famous folk legends, often called the Chinese equivalent of Romeo and Juliet. It tells of the tragic love between Liang Shanbo and Zhu Yingtai.

Story

Zhu Yingtai, a young woman, disguises herself as a man to study at an academy, where she shares a room for three years with her classmate Liang Shanbo, who never suspects her sex. When she is recalled home and betrothed to another man, Liang visits, learns the truth, and dies of grief. On her wedding day Zhu stops at his grave; it opens, she leaps in, and the lovers emerge transformed into a pair of butterflies.

In the arts

The legend has been retold in opera, theatre, film and television. It is inseparably associated with the Butterfly Lovers' Violin Concerto (1959) by He Zhanhao and Chen Gang, one of the best-known works of Chinese orchestral music.
