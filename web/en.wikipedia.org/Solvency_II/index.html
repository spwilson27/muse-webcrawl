Solvency II
TypeEU directive (2009/138/EC)
Applies toInsurance and reinsurance undertakings in the EU/EEA
In force1 January 2016
SupervisorEIOPA (European Insurance and Occupational Pensions Authority)

Solvency II is a European Union directive that harmonises the prudential regulation of insurance and reinsurance companies across the EU. In force since 1 January 2016, it replaced the patchwork of earlier Solvency I rules with a risk-based framework broadly modelled on banking's Basel accords, aiming to ensure insurers hold enough capital to withstand adverse events and to protect policyholders.

Three pillars

Pillar 1 sets quantitative requirements: assets and liabilities are valued on a market-consistent basis, and firms must hold capital against the Solvency Capital Requirement (SCR)—calibrated to a 99.5% value-at-risk over one year—plus a lower Minimum Capital Requirement (MCR) that triggers ultimate supervisory intervention. Firms may use the standard formula or, with approval, internal models. Pillar 2 covers governance, risk management, and the Own Risk and Solvency Assessment (ORSA), together with supervisory review. Pillar 3 requires disclosure and reporting to supervisors and the public, including the annual Solvency and Financial Condition Report.

Development and debate

The project began in the early 2000s under the Lamfalussy process, with extensive quantitative impact studies (QIS) run by CEIOPS and later EIOPA. Industry critics argued the standard formula penalised long-term and equity investments, leading to adjustments such as the matching adjustment and volatility adjustment for long-term guarantees. Several non-EU jurisdictions, including Bermuda and Switzerland, have been recognised as "equivalent" regimes.
