Sampler (musical instrument)

A sampler is an electronic musical instrument that records and plays back samples — recorded fragments of sound — typically triggered from a keyboard or pad controller, so that a recorded sound can be replayed at different pitches. Samplers became foundational instruments in hip hop, electronic dance music and pop production from the 1980s onward.

History

Early sample playback instruments such as the Mellotron (1960s) used tape strips, and the first commercial digital samplers — the Fairlight CMI (1979) and E-mu Emulator (1981) — brought digital sampling to studios at high cost. Affordable machines such as the Ensoniq Mirage (1984) and Akai's S-series, and especially the Akai MPC drum-machine samplers (from 1988), made sampling central to hip hop and electronic music production. By the late 1990s, software samplers running on personal computers had largely replaced dedicated hardware, though hardware grooveboxes and pads experienced a revival.

Features

A sampler records audio through an analogue-to-digital converter, stores it in memory, and allows editing — trimming, looping, reversing, time-stretching and pitch-shifting — and processing with filters, envelopes and effects. Multisampling assigns different recordings across a keyboard range to imitate acoustic instruments realistically. The creative reuse of samples raised influential legal questions about copyright and clearance, notably in hip hop.
