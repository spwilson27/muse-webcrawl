Charles Joseph Minard (27 March 1781 – 24 October 1870) was a French civil engineer recognised for his significant contributions in information graphics in civil engineering and statistics. His 1869 chart of Napoleon's disastrous Russian campaign of 1812 is celebrated as one of the finest statistical graphics ever produced; Edward Tufte called it possibly "the best statistical graphic ever drawn".

Career

Minard studied at the École Polytechnique and the École des Ponts et Chaussées, and spent his career in the corps of bridge and road engineers, inspecting canals, railways and ports across Europe. After retiring in 1851 he devoted himself to producing more than fifty thematic maps, or cartes figuratives, visualising flows of goods, passengers and emigrants.

The Napoleon map

Published in 1869 when Minard was 88, the map combines six variables: the size of the Grande Armée (shown by the width of a band), its location on a two-dimensional surface, the direction of movement, the temperature during the retreat from Moscow, and the dates. Of some 422,000 men who crossed the Niemen in June 1812, only about 10,000 are shown returning. Minard, a pacifist, intended the graphic as an anti-war statement: to "inspire thoughtful men with horror of war".

Legacy

Minard pioneered the flow map and the proportional-symbol transport map, and his work is foundational for data visualisation and thematic cartography.
