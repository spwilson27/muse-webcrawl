Single-8

ManufacturerFujifilm
Introduced1965
Film width8 mm
Cartridge capacity50 ft (15 m)

Single-8 is an 8 mm motion picture film format introduced by Fujifilm of Japan in 1965 as an alternative to Kodak's Super 8 format, launched the same year. Single-8 uses exactly the same film stock dimensions, perforations and frame size as Super 8, so finished films can be shown on any Super 8 projector; the difference lies entirely in the cartridge design used inside the camera.

Cartridge design

Whereas the Kodak Super 8 cartridge uses a coaxial supply and take-up arrangement, the Single-8 cartridge places the two spools side by side in a cassette resembling later audio and video cassettes. The design allows the film to be rewound, permitting techniques such as backwinding for dissolves and multiple exposures, which standard Super 8 cartridges cannot do. Single-8 film was supplied on a thin polyester base rather than acetate, allowing the standard 50-foot load in a slimmer cartridge.

History and decline

Single-8 gained a loyal following in Japan and a modest one in Europe, with cameras made by Fujifilm (Fujica) and other makers. Kodak's format, however, dominated worldwide, and as home video displaced amateur film in the 1980s both formats declined. Fujifilm continued manufacturing Single-8 film long after most rivals left the market; the last stock, Fujichrome R25N and RT200N, was discontinued in the early 2010s, and processing ended soon afterwards, leaving small third-party suppliers to serve remaining enthusiasts.
