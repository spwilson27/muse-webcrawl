Nicholas Philip Trist (1800–1874)

Nicholas Philip Trist (2 June 1800 – 11 February 1874) was an American lawyer, diplomat, planter, and businessman. He is best known for negotiating the Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo in 1848, which ended the Mexican–American War and ceded vast territories — including California and the Southwest — from Mexico to the United States.

Life and career

Born in Charlottesville, Virginia, Trist studied briefly at West Point and read law under Thomas Jefferson, whose granddaughter Virginia Jefferson Randolph he married. He served as private secretary to President Andrew Jackson, as chief clerk of the State Department, and as U.S. consul in Havana, where his conduct drew controversy over the slave trade.

The treaty of 1848

In 1847 President James K. Polk sent Trist as peace commissioner to negotiate with Mexico. After the fall of Mexico City, Polk — impatient with the stalemate — recalled Trist. Judging that the moment for peace would be lost, Trist disobeyed the recall and negotiated the treaty, signed at Guadalupe Hidalgo on 2 February 1848. Mexico ceded about half its territory in exchange for $15 million. Polk accepted the treaty but refused to pay Trist his salary and expenses for the period after the recall; Congress finally compensated him more than two decades later, in 1871.

Trist later worked as a railroad lawyer and paymaster and supported the Union during the Civil War before retiring to Alexandria, Virginia.
