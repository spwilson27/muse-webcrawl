Byzantine Senate

The Byzantine Senate or Eastern Roman Senate (Greek: Σύγκλητος) was the continuation of the Roman Senate in the Eastern Roman Empire, based in Constantinople. Established by Constantine the Great in the 4th century, it survived for centuries, though its role gradually declined from a genuine deliberative body to a largely ceremonial assembly of high dignitaries.

History

Constantine founded a senate in his new capital modelled on that of Rome. Under Constantius II its membership expanded and its privileges were clarified. Senators held ranks such as illustres and later were integrated into the Byzantine hierarchy of court titles. The Senate retained a voice in imperial successions, occasionally acclaiming or resisting emperors, and was consulted on major legislation.

Decline

From the 7th century the Senate became more a collective body of palace officials than an independent institution. Mentions of it fade after the 12th century, and it disappears from sources in the empire's final centuries.
