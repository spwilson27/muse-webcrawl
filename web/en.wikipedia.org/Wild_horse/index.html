Wild horse

A wild horse is a horse living in a wild state. Most modern “wild” horses, such as American mustangs and Australian brumbies, are feral descendants of domesticated horses. The only generally recognised truly wild horse to survive into recent times is Przewalski's horse of Central Asia.

History

Horses evolved in North America and spread to Eurasia before becoming extinct in the Americas near the end of the Pleistocene. Domesticated horses were reintroduced to the Americas by Europeans from the fifteenth century onward, and escaped animals formed free-roaming herds.

Management

Feral horse populations are managed in many countries because they can affect grazing land, water, native wildlife and their own welfare when numbers exceed forage. Methods include adoption, fertility control, fencing and, controversially, culling. Przewalski's horse has been reintroduced to parts of Mongolia and China from captive stock.
