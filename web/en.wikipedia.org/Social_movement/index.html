Social movement

A social movement is a loosely organized effort by a large group of people to achieve a particular goal, typically a social or political one. This may be to carry out a social change, or to resist or undo one. Social movements have been described as "organizational structures and strategies that may empower oppressed populations to mount effective challenges and resist the more powerful and advantaged elites". They are usually informal groupings of individuals or organizations, distinct from political parties, although the two often interact.

Types

Sociologist David Aberle categorized movements by what they seek to change and how much change they seek, yielding reformative movements (seeking limited change within a system), revolutionary movements (seeking to overthrow a system), redemptive movements (seeking radical change in individuals) and alterative movements (seeking partial change in individual behaviour). Examples include the labour movement, the women's suffrage movement, the civil rights movement, the environmental movement and LGBTQ movements.

Study

Scholars study how movements mobilize resources, frame grievances, exploit political opportunities and sustain collective identity. Theories have moved from early crowd psychology toward resource mobilization and political process models, and more recently to the roles of networks and social media. Social movements are considered a key way in which citizens participate in politics between elections and drive long-term social change.
