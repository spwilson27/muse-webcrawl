AlUla (Arabic: ٱلْعُلَا) is an ancient Arabic oasis city located in the Medina Province of north-western Saudi Arabia. Situated in the Hejaz, a region known for its association with the journey of the Islamic prophet Muhammad, it lies on the ancient incense trade route that linked southern Arabia with the Mediterranean world.

History

The area was the capital of the ancient Lihyanites (Dedanites) and contains the archaeological site of Hegra (Madā'in Ṣāliḥ), the first UNESCO World Heritage Site in Saudi Arabia, with monumental Nabataean tombs comparable to Petra. The old town of AlUla, with its labyrinth of mud-brick houses, was inhabited into the twentieth century.

Modern development

The Royal Commission for AlUla, established in 2017, oversees the area's development as a cultural tourism destination under Saudi Vision 2030, including the annual Winter at Tantora festival, the Maraya concert hall — the world's largest mirrored building — and reopening of heritage sites to visitors.
