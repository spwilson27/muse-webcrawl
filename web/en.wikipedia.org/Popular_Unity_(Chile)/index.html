Popular Unity (Chile)

Popular Unity (Spanish: Unidad Popular, UP) was a left-wing political coalition in Chile that governed the country from 1970 to 1973 under President Salvador Allende. Formed in December 1969, it united the Socialist Party, the Communist Party, the Radical Party, the Social Democratic Party, MAPU, and other smaller groups in support of Allende's fourth presidential candidacy.

Salvador Allende, president of Chile 1970–1973.

Government

Allende won a plurality in the September 1970 election and was confirmed by Congress. His "Chilean road to socialism" (vía chilena al socialismo) sought a democratic transition to socialism: the unanimous congressional nationalization of copper in 1971, accelerated land reform, nationalization of banks and large industries, wage increases, and expanded social programs. Initial economic growth gave way to inflation, shortages, and black markets; opposition grew from business groups, the Christian Democrats, the United States government (which funded opposition media and strikes), and eventually much of the middle class.

Overthrow

Amid strikes, the failed Tanquetazo of June 1973, and a congressional declaration accusing the government of constitutional violations, the armed forces under General Augusto Pinochet seized power in the coup of 11 September 1973. Allende died in the bombing of La Moneda Palace, the UP was outlawed, and Chile entered a seventeen-year military dictatorship.
