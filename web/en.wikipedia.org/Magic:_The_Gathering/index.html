Magic: The Gathering

Magic: The Gathering (Magic or MTG) is a tabletop and digital collectible card game created by Richard Garfield and released in 1993 by Wizards of the Coast. It was the first trading card game and remains one of the most popular, with tens of millions of players worldwide and more than 25,000 distinct cards printed. Players take the role of Planeswalkers — powerful mages duelling by casting spells and summoning creatures drawn from a customised deck.

Magic's five colours of mana form the game's strategic framework.

Gameplay

Each player begins with 20 life points and wins by reducing opponents to zero, by emptying their library, or by an alternative card condition. Cards are played using mana, chiefly produced by land cards, in five colours — white, blue, black, red, and green — each with a characteristic philosophy and strengths. Games proceed through structured phases (untap, upkeep, draw, main phases, combat, end step), and spells can be answered with instants, creating a stack of effects that resolve in reverse order. Constructed formats require decks of at least 60 cards, while limited formats (draft and sealed) build 40-card decks on the spot from booster packs.

History

Garfield, a doctoral student in mathematics, designed the game so that each player's deck was unique — a radical concept at the time. The first printings (Alpha and Beta, 1993) sold out rapidly; early cards such as Black Lotus became famously valuable, with mint Alpha copies selling for hundreds of thousands of dollars. Hasbro acquired Wizards of the Coast in 1999. Digital versions include Magic: The Gathering Online (2002) and Magic: The Gathering Arena (2019). The story is set across a multiverse of planes, explored in expansion sets released several times a year, and "Universes Beyond" crossovers have incorporated franchises such as The Lord of the Rings.

Organised play

Competitive Magic grew around the Pro Tour (launched 1996), Grand Prix events, and a Hall of Fame. The multiplayer format Commander (100-card singleton decks led by a legendary creature) became the game's most popular casual format. Card legality, rarity (common, uncommon, rare, mythic rare), and the Reserved List — a promise never to reprint certain early cards — continue to shape the game's large secondary market.
