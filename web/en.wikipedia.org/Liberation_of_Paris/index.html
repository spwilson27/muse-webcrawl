Liberation of Paris

The liberation of Paris (French: libération de Paris) was a military battle that took place during World War II from 19 August 1944 until the German garrison surrendered the French capital on 25 August 1944. Paris had been occupied by Nazi Germany since the signing of the Second Armistice at Compiègne on 22 June 1940.

Course of events

As Allied forces advanced after the Normandy landings, the French Resistance in Paris rose in open insurrection on 19 August. General Philippe Leclerc's French 2nd Armored Division, supported by the US 4th Infantry Division, fought into the city. The German commander, Dietrich von Choltitz, disobeyed Hitler's orders to destroy Paris's landmarks and surrendered at the Hôtel Meurice on 25 August.

Aftermath

Charles de Gaulle arrived the same day and delivered a famous speech at the Hôtel de Ville, asserting that Paris had liberated itself with the support of the armies of France, before leading a triumphal parade down the Champs-Élysées on 26 August.
