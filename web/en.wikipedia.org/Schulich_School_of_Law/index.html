Schulich School of Law

The Schulich School of Law is the law school of Dalhousie University in Halifax, Nova Scotia, Canada. Founded in 1883 as the Dalhousie Law School, it is the oldest university-based common law school in Canada. The school is housed in the Weldon Law Building on Dalhousie's Studley campus. It was renamed in 2009 following a major donation from Canadian philanthropist Seymour Schulich.

History

Dalhousie Law School was established in 1883 with the support of the Nova Scotia Barristers' Society. Its founding dean, Richard Chapman Weldon, modeled the curriculum on Harvard Law School, emphasizing university-based legal education with a national orientation rather than purely local apprenticeship. The school has educated numerous Canadian political and judicial leaders, including prime ministers, premiers, and Supreme Court justices.

Academics

The school offers the Juris Doctor (J.D.) degree as its principal program, together with graduate degrees (LL.M. and Ph.D. in law) and combined degrees. It is known for its Marine & Environmental Law Institute, health law programs, and the Schulich Academic Excellence Fund. The school publishes the Dalhousie Law Journal and is home to the Dalhousie Legal Aid Service, one of Canada's oldest community legal clinics.

Notable alumni

Graduates include former prime ministers Brian Mulroney (by honorary association through Dalhousie) and others in public life, along with many appellate judges, premiers of Atlantic provinces, and leaders of the Canadian bar.
