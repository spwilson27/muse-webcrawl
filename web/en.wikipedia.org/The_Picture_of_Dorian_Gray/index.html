The Picture of Dorian Gray

The Picture of Dorian Gray is a philosophical novel by Irish writer Oscar Wilde, first published complete in the July 1890 issue of Lippincott's Monthly Magazine and in revised book form in 1891. It is Wilde's only novel. The story centres on a portrait of the beautiful young Dorian Gray, painted by artist Basil Hallward: Dorian wishes that the portrait should age instead of him, and the wish comes true.

Plot

Influenced by the hedonistic Lord Henry Wotton, Dorian pursues a life of sensation and cruelty — driving the actress Sibyl Vane to suicide, ruining friends, and eventually murdering Basil — while remaining youthful and unmarked. His portrait, hidden in an attic, ages and disfigures with every sin. In a final attempt to destroy the evidence of his soul, Dorian stabs the painting and is found dead, withered and aged, beside the restored portrait.

Themes and reception

The novel examines beauty, youth, art, and moral corruption, framed by a famous preface of aphorisms including "There is no such thing as a moral or an immoral book." Its publication provoked scandal, and passages from it were used against Wilde at his 1895 trials. It is now regarded as a classic of Gothic literature and has been adapted many times for stage and screen.
