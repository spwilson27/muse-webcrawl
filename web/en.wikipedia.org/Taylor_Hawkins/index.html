Oliver Taylor Hawkins (February 17, 1972 – March 25, 2022) was an American musician, best known as the drummer of the rock band Foo Fighters, with whom he recorded eight studio albums. Before joining Foo Fighters in 1997, Hawkins was the touring drummer for rock singer-songwriter Alanis Morissette on her Can't Not tour.

Early life

Hawkins was born in Fort Worth, Texas, and grew up in Laguna Beach, California. He began playing drums as a teenager, inspired by drummers such as Roger Taylor of Queen and Stewart Copeland of the Police.

Career

After playing in the band Sylvia and backing Alanis Morissette, Hawkins joined Foo Fighters in 1997, replacing William Goldsmith. He became one of the band's most visible members, occasionally singing lead vocals on songs such as "Cold Day in the Sun" and the band's cover of Pink Floyd's "Have a Cigar". He also led side projects including Taylor Hawkins and the Coattail Riders and the supergroup NHC.

Death

Hawkins died on March 25, 2022, in Bogotá, Colombia, where Foo Fighters were scheduled to perform at the Estéreo Picnic Festival. Tribute concerts were held in London and Los Angeles in September 2022.

Discography

With Foo Fighters: There Is Nothing Left to Lose (1999), One by One (2002), In Your Honor (2005), Echoes, Silence, Patience & Grace (2007), Wasting Light (2011), Sonic Highways (2014), Concrete and Gold (2017), Medicine at Midnight (2021).
