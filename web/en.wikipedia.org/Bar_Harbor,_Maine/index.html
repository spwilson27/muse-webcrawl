Bar Harbor is a resort town on Mount Desert Island in Hancock County, Maine, United States. As of recent censuses the population is about 5,500. Situated on Frenchman Bay, the town is a popular tourist destination and the principal gateway to Acadia National Park, much of which lies within and around the town.

History

The area was the homeland of the Wabanaki people. European settlement began in the 1760s, and the town was incorporated in 1796 under the name Eden. In the mid-nineteenth century, artists of the Hudson River School publicised the island's scenery, and by the 1880s Bar Harbor had become a fashionable summer colony for wealthy families — the Rockefellers, Morgans, Vanderbilts, Astors, and others built grand "cottages" along the shore. A devastating fire in October 1947 destroyed many of the great estates and much of the town's forest, ending the golden age of the summer colony; the town had been renamed Bar Harbor in 1918, after the sand and gravel bar, revealed at low tide, that connects it to Bar Island.

Geography and attractions

Bar Harbor overlooks Frenchman Bay and the Porcupine Islands. Cadillac Mountain, the highest point on the United States Atlantic coast at 1,530 feet (470 m), rises within Acadia National Park nearby and is among the first places in the country touched by the rising sun for part of the year. Attractions include the Shore Path, the Abbe Museum of Wabanaki culture, the Jackson Laboratory (a major genetics research institute founded in 1929), College of the Atlantic, and ferry and cruise-ship calls. Tourism, centred on the national park, dominates the local economy.
