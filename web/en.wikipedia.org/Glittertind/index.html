Glittertind is a mountain in the Jotunheimen area of Norway, on the border between the municipalities of Lom and Vågå. For much of the twentieth century its summit glacier made it Norway's highest point, marginally above Galdhøpiggen; because the ice cap has thinned, the rock summit of Glittertind measures about 2,452 metres including the remaining glacier and about 2,447 metres without it, so Galdhøpiggen (2,469 m) is now generally reckoned the country's highest mountain.

Geography and climbing

The mountain rises east of the Visdalen valley and is covered on its upper slopes by a glacier. The normal route of ascent starts from the Norwegian Trekking Association cabins at Spiterstulen or Glitterheim and crosses the glacier to the summit; the climb is a long but technically straightforward walk for experienced hikers, typically undertaken in summer. The first recorded ascent was made on 14 July 1841 by Harald Nicolai Storm Wergeland and the hunter and outdoorsman Jo Gjende.

Name

The name combines glitter, from the river Glitra — itself from Old Norse glitra, "to glitter, sparkle" — with tind, "mountain peak".
