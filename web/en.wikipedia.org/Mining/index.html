Mining

Mining is the extraction of valuable geological materials and minerals from the surface of the Earth and other astronomical objects. Mining is required to obtain most materials that cannot be grown through agricultural processes or feasibly created artificially, including metals, coal, oil shale, gemstones, limestone, rock salt, and rare earth elements.

Methods

Surface mining — including open-pit, strip, and mountaintop-removal mining — removes soil and rock overlying the deposit. Underground mining reaches deep deposits through shafts and tunnels using room-and-pillar, longwall, and block-caving methods. Other approaches include placer mining of stream deposits, in-situ leaching, and solution mining.

History

Mining is among humanity's oldest activities: flint was mined in the Stone Age, and copper, gold, and later coal and iron mining underpinned the Bronze Age, Iron Age, and Industrial Revolution. Modern mining is highly mechanised and dominated by large multinational companies, though artisanal and small-scale mining still employs tens of millions of people.

Impacts

Mining can cause soil erosion, deforestation, water contamination from acid mine drainage and tailings, and habitat loss. It is also one of the most hazardous industries, though safety has improved markedly in developed countries. Mine reclamation and stricter regulation aim to reduce environmental damage.
