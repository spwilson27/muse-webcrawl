John Kendrew

Born: 24 March 1917, Oxford, England
Died: 23 August 1997 (aged 80), Cambridge, England
Fields: Crystallography, molecular biology
Institutions: University of Cambridge; MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology
Awards: Nobel Prize in Chemistry (1962)

Sir John Cowdery Kendrew (24 March 1917 – 23 August 1997) was an English biochemist, crystallographer and science administrator. He shared the 1962 Nobel Prize in Chemistry with Max Perutz for determining the first three-dimensional structures of proteins.

Myoglobin structure

After wartime operational research work, Kendrew joined Perutz at the Cavendish Laboratory, Cambridge, and chose the oxygen-storage protein myoglobin from sperm whale muscle for X-ray crystallographic analysis. In 1957–1960 his group produced first a 6 Å and then a 2 Å resolution structure — the first protein structure ever solved — revealing the now-classic globin fold of alpha helices.

LMB and administration

Kendrew was a founder of the MRC Laboratory of Molecular Biology in Cambridge and a driving force behind the creation of the European Molecular Biology Laboratory (EMBL) in Heidelberg and the journal Journal of Molecular Biology. He later served as president of St John's College, Oxford (1974–1979).
