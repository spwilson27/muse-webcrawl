Dimetrodon

Dimetrodon is an extinct genus of non-mammalian synapsid that lived during the Cisuralian age of the early Permian period, around 295–272 million years ago. It was a member of the family Sphenacodontidae and is characterised by a large neural spine sail on its back, formed by elongated spines extending from the vertebrae. Despite its popular image, Dimetrodon was not a dinosaur; it belonged to the lineage leading to mammals and lived tens of millions of years before the first dinosaurs.

Description

Large species of Dimetrodon were among the biggest predators of their time, reaching 3.1 metres (10 ft) or more in length and weighing perhaps 250 kg. The skull had differentiated teeth — its name means "two measures of teeth" — including stabbing caniniform teeth and smaller shearing teeth, an early step toward the varied dentition of mammals. The sail was probably covered in skin, may have supported a hump of tissue in some interpretations, and has been proposed to function in thermoregulation, display, or both.

Discovery and classification

Fossils were first collected in the Red Beds of Texas in the 1870s and named by Edward Drinker Cope in 1878 during the "Bone Wars" era. Most fossils come from Texas and Oklahoma, with material also known from New Mexico and Germany. Dimetrodon sits within Pelycosauria in the traditional grade of early synapsids, closer to mammals than to reptiles.

Paleoecology

Dimetrodon was an apex terrestrial predator of lowland floodplain ecosystems, feeding on amphibians such as Eryops and Diadectes, other synapsids, and freshwater prey. Its sphenacodontid relatives include the herbivorous sail-backed Edaphosaurus, which evolved a similar sail independently.
