Cave painting

TypeParietal art
PeriodUpper Palaeolithic to later prehistory
Famous sitesLascaux; Altamira; Chauvet; Sulawesi
Oldest knownOver 45,000 years old (Sulawesi, Indonesia)

Cave paintings are a form of parietal art — art on cave walls or ceilings — largely of prehistoric origin. The most famous examples are the Upper Palaeolithic paintings of France and Spain, but painted caves are known worldwide. Along with engravings and drawings they form the largest surviving body of prehistoric art.

Age and distribution

The oldest known figurative cave paintings are the animal paintings of Sulawesi, Indonesia, dated to more than 45,000 years ago, including a depiction of a warty pig dated to at least 45,500 years ago. In Europe, the earliest paintings include those of Chauvet Cave in France (about 36,000 years old) and El Castillo in Spain, where a red disk has been dated to over 40,000 years ago. The art flourished in the Magdalenian period, culminating in the polychrome bison of Altamira (Spain) and the halls of Lascaux (France), around 17,000 years ago. Some researchers attribute certain Spanish cave markings, dated to more than 64,000 years, to Neanderthals.

Subject matter and technique

Animals — horses, bison, aurochs, deer, mammoths, lions, and rhinoceroses — dominate; human figures are rare and schematic, while hand stencils (made by blowing pigment over a hand pressed to the wall) and abstract signs such as dots and lines are common. Pigments came from charcoal, ochres, hematite, and manganese dioxide, applied by finger, brush, or spraying through hollow bones or reeds.

Interpretation

Explanations have included hunting magic, shamanic ritual and trance imagery, initiation rites, mythological "art for art's sake", and structuralist readings of animal symbolism. No single theory accounts for all the evidence, and interpretation remains an active field of research. Major painted caves are protected as UNESCO World Heritage Sites, and fragile originals such as Lascaux have been closed to the public and replaced with full-scale replicas.
