Gath (Hebrew: גַּת, Gaṯ) was one of the five cities of the Philistine pentapolis in the south-central Levant. In the Hebrew Bible it is the home of Goliath, the giant whom David defeated, and of King Achish, to whom David fled from Saul. The city is generally identified with Tell es-Safi, a large mound in the Shephelah foothills of Israel between the coastal plain and the Judaean mountains.

Biblical accounts

Gath appears repeatedly in the books of Samuel and Kings. The Ark of the Covenant was briefly held there after its capture by the Philistines. David served Achish, king of Gath, receiving the town of Ziklag. Later, Gath was captured by King Hazael of Aram-Damascus (c. 830 BCE) and by Uzziah of Judah; the prophet Amos already speaks of its calamity, and after the Assyrian conquests it fades from the record.

Archaeology

Excavations at Tell es-Safi, directed by Aren Maeir of Bar-Ilan University since 1996, have uncovered one of the largest Philistine cities of the Iron Age, with massive fortifications, temples, and evidence of an early Aegean-style material culture that gradually assimilated local elements. A siege trench dug around the city — among the earliest known examples of siege warfare — is attributed to Hazael's conquest, which destroyed the city around 830 BCE. A pottery shard found at the site bearing two names written in Semitic script similar to "Goliath" attracted wide attention, though it does not refer to the biblical figure himself.
