Drag (clothing)

Drag is a type of entertainment and performance in which people dress in clothing and adopt a persona associated with a gender other than their own, often with exaggerated, theatrical styling. A person who performs drag is known as a drag artist; a male performer presenting as a woman is a drag queen, and a female performer presenting as a man is a drag king.

History

Drag has roots in theatre, where men played women's roles for centuries because women were barred from the stage in Elizabethan England and elsewhere. In the 20th century, drag developed as a distinct art form in gay bars and ballroom culture, notably in New York's Harlem ball scene, and later became mainstream through performers such as Divine and RuPaul.

Drag performance typically combines lip-syncing, dancing, comedy, and elaborate costumes and makeup. Since 2009, the television competition RuPaul's Drag Race has brought drag to a global audience and launched many performers' careers.

Cultural significance

Drag has long been associated with LGBTQ culture and with playful subversion of gender norms. Drag shows range from cabaret and comedy revues to pageants and story hours. In recent years drag has also been the subject of political debate in some countries, with disputes over drag performances in public libraries and schools.
