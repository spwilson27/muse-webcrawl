Candlemas

Candlemas, also known as the Feast of the Presentation of Jesus Christ, the Feast of the Purification of the Blessed Virgin Mary, and the Feast of the Holy Encounter, is a Christian feast day commemorating the presentation of Jesus at the Temple. It falls on February 2, forty days after Christmas.

Biblical basis

The feast is based on the account in the Gospel of Luke (2:22–40), in which Mary and Joseph bring the infant Jesus to the Temple in Jerusalem in accordance with Jewish law. There the child is recognized by Simeon, who calls him "a light for revelation to the Gentiles," and by the prophetess Anna.

Observance

The name Candlemas derives from the tradition of blessing candles on this day and carrying them in procession, symbolizing Christ as the light of the world. The feast is among the oldest in Christianity, attested in Jerusalem in the fourth century by the pilgrim Egeria. In the Roman Rite it concludes the Christmas–Epiphany season in older reckonings; in Eastern Orthodox churches it is one of the Twelve Great Feasts.

Folk customs

Candlemas weather lore holds that a sunny day predicts more winter — a tradition related to Groundhog Day in North America. In France the day is associated with crêpes, in Mexico with tamales, and in many regions candles blessed at Candlemas were kept in homes for protection against storms and illness.
