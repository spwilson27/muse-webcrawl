Classification of electromagnetic fields

The classification of electromagnetic fields is the algebraic classification of the electromagnetic field tensor at a point in spacetime, analogous to the Petrov classification of the Weyl tensor in general relativity. Because the field tensor is an antisymmetric 4×4 matrix (a bivector), its type is determined by its Lorentz-invariant quantities.

Invariants and types

Writing the fields as E and B, the two independent invariants are proportional to B² − E² (from FabFab) and to E·B (from the dual contraction). A field is classified as:

  • Null: both invariants vanish, so E = B, E ⊥ B—the case of pure electromagnetic radiation such as a plane wave; the field has a single repeated principal null direction structure characteristic of radiation.
  • Non-null: at least one invariant is nonzero. A frame then exists in which E and B are parallel; the field is further termed electric-like (a frame exists with B = 0), magnetic-like (a frame exists with E = 0), or general non-null.

Algebraic formulation

In terms of the complex 3×3 (self-dual) representation, the field tensor can be diagonalized; its eigenvalue structure—whether it is diagonalizable with distinct eigenvalues, degenerate, or nilpotent—mirrors the Segre and Petrov schemes. This classification underlies Rainich's "already unified" geometrodynamics and is used in studying exact solutions of the Einstein–Maxwell equations, electromagnetic radiation, and invariants of charged black holes.
