Merzbow

Merzbow
Real name: Masami Akita
Born: 19 December 1956, Tokyo, Japan
Genres: Noise, industrial, experimental
Active: 1979–present

Merzbow is the noise-music project of Japanese artist Masami Akita (born 1956), the best-known and most prolific figure in the Japanese noise ("Japanoise") scene. Since 1979 he has released hundreds of albums of harsh, densely layered electronic and concrete sound, on labels including his own ZSF Produkt, and international labels such as Relapse, Important and Cold Spring.

Name and approach

The name derives from Merzbau, the monumental assemblage by Dada artist Kurt Schwitters, reflecting Akita's background in art and his collage-like treatment of found sound. Early works used tape loops, junk metal and feedback; later recordings moved through analogue synthesisers and, from around 1999, laptop-based digital processing, sometimes rhythmically structured, as on Pulse Demon (1996) and the 50-CD Merzbox (2000). Collaborators include Boris, Keiji Haino, Balázs Pándi and Mats Gustafsson.

Themes

Akita is a committed vegan and animal-rights campaigner; many releases, such as 13 Japanese Birds (2009), address animal cruelty, and he has written books on art, music, fetish culture and animal rights. His work is treated as a central reference point in academic and critical writing on noise as music.
