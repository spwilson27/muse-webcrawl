Solar barque

A solar barque (or solar bark, solar boat) is a mythological vessel used by the sun god in many religions, most famously the Egyptian god Ra, who was believed to travel across the sky by day and through the underworld (Duat) by night in such a boat.

Egyptian mythology

Ra's barque was called the Mandjet ("Barque of Millions of Years") for the day journey and the Mesektet for the night journey. During the night voyage, Ra, in ram-headed form, passed through the twelve hours of the Duat, bringing light to the dead and battling the serpent Apophis, assisted by gods such as Set.

Khufu ship

The best-known physical example is the Khufu ship, a full-sized vessel of cedar wood buried around 2500 BC in a pit beside the Great Pyramid of Giza, often interpreted as a solar barque for the pharaoh's use in the afterlife. It was excavated and reconstructed from 1954 and is now displayed at the Grand Egyptian Museum.

Other cultures

Petroglyphs of boats associated with the sun appear in Bronze Age Scandinavia, and solar boats feature in the mythology of other cultures along the Nile and in Mesopotamia.
