William Cecil, 1st Baron Burghley

William Cecil, 1st Baron Burghley (13 September 1520 – 4 August 1598) was an English statesman, the chief adviser of Queen Elizabeth I for most of her reign. He served twice as Secretary of State (1550–53 and 1558–72) and as Lord High Treasurer from 1572 until his death, and is regarded as the architect of Elizabethan government.

Career

Educated at St John's College, Cambridge, Cecil entered the service of the Protector Somerset under Edward VI and survived imprisonment after Somerset's fall. He prudently kept a low profile during the reign of Mary I, conforming outwardly to Catholicism, and emerged in 1558 as Elizabeth's Secretary of State. Together the queen and Cecil — prudent, meticulous and devoted to paperwork — managed the religious settlement of 1559, the recoinage, and England's survival through decades of war with Spain and conspiracy around Mary, Queen of Scots, whose execution in 1587 Cecil engineered.

Government and intelligence

Burghley ran an extensive intelligence network (later headed by Francis Walsingham), supervised crown finances, and built great houses at Burghley and Theobalds. His younger son Robert Cecil succeeded him as the queen's chief minister. Burghley's combination of cautious finance, administrative reform and Protestant foreign policy defined the Elizabethan state.
