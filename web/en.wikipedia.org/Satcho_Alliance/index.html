The Satchō Alliance (Japanese: 薩長同盟, Satchō dōmei), formally known as the Satsuma–Chōshū Alliance, was a military and political pact formed in 1866 between the feudal domains of Satsuma and Chōshū in Japan. The name combines the first characters of the two domains' names. The alliance was a decisive step toward the downfall of the Tokugawa shogunate and the Meiji Restoration of 1868.

Background

Satsuma and Chōshū had long been rivals. Satsuma forces had helped expel Chōshū troops from Kyoto in the Kinmon incident of 1864, and Satsuma had participated in the shogunate's First Chōshū expedition. Both domains, however, were governed by reform-minded samurai who had come to believe—under the slogan sonnō jōi ("revere the Emperor, expel the barbarians")—that the shogunate was too weak to resist Western pressure and had to be replaced.

By 1865 Chōshū, which had modernized its military under leaders such as Takasugi Shinsaku, faced a second punitive expedition from the shogunate. It needed modern weapons, which could be obtained through foreign merchants in Nagasaki—territory influenced by Satsuma. Meanwhile, Satsuma leaders Saigō Takamori and Ōkubo Toshimichi increasingly saw an alliance as the only way to break the shogunate's power.

Formation

The alliance was brokered by Sakamoto Ryōma and Nakaoka Shintarō, samurai from Tosa Domain associated with the Kaientai trading company. On 7 March 1866, representatives of the two domains met in Kyoto and concluded a secret six-point agreement. Its terms included mutual military assistance if Chōshū were attacked, Satsuma's help in procuring arms and in mediating between Chōshū and the Imperial Court, and a commitment to work together against the shogunate.

Consequences

True to the pact, Satsuma refused to contribute troops to the shogunate's Second Chōshū expedition of 1866 and facilitated arms shipments to Chōshū; Chōshū's modernized forces defeated the shogunal army, humiliating the bakufu. In 1867 Satsuma and Chōshū issued a secret imperial edict calling for the overthrow of Shogun Tokugawa Yoshinobu and marched on Kyoto, triggering the Boshin War (1868–1869), which ended in the restoration of imperial rule. Men from the two domains dominated the new Meiji government for decades, a dominance later criticized as the hanbatsu (domain clique) system.
