Alfa Romeo, the Italian automobile manufacturer, has a distinguished history in Formula One spanning several distinct eras. The marque dominated the first two seasons of the World Championship in 1950 and 1951, returned as a works team and engine supplier in the 1970s and 1980s, and lent its name to the Sauber team from 2018 to 2023.

Dominance, 1950–1951

Alfa Romeo's supercharged 158 "Alfetta" — designed before the Second World War — won all eleven races it entered in 1950, and Giuseppe "Nino" Farina took the inaugural drivers' title ahead of teammates Juan Manuel Fangio and Luigi Fagioli. In 1951 the evolved 159, driven by Fangio, won the championship again after a season-long duel with Ferrari. Rising costs led Alfa to withdraw from Grand Prix racing at the end of 1951.

Return as engine supplier and works team

Alfa engines appeared sporadically in the 1960s, and the marque returned in earnest supplying flat-12 engines to Brabham from 1976, producing the controversial BT46B "fan car" victory in Sweden in 1978. A full works team followed from 1979 to 1985, with drivers including Bruno Giacomelli and Riccardo Patrese; despite occasional pole positions and podiums, no wins came, and Alfa withdrew as a constructor after 1985, ending its engine supply after 1988.

Sauber partnership, 2018–2023

In 2018 Alfa Romeo became title sponsor of the Swiss Sauber team, rebranded Alfa Romeo Racing from 2019 (later Alfa Romeo F1 Team). Operated from Hinwil with Ferrari engines, the team scored midfield results with drivers including Kimi Räikkönen, Valtteri Bottas and Zhou Guanyu. The partnership ended after 2023 ahead of Sauber's transformation into the Audi works team.
