Hindu festivals

Hindu festivals are celebrations observed by Hindus around the world, generally following the lunisolar Hindu calendar. Festivals typically mark seasonal changes, harvests, the birthdays of deities, mythological events, and rites of passage. Many are regional in character, while a smaller number — such as Diwali and Holi — are celebrated nearly everywhere Hinduism is practised.

Major festivals

Diwali (Deepavali), the festival of lights, is celebrated over five days in the month of Kartika and commemorates, among other traditions, Rama's return to Ayodhya after exile; oil lamps (diyas) are lit and Lakshmi, goddess of wealth, is worshipped. Holi, the spring festival of colours, marks the arrival of spring and is associated with legends of Krishna and of Prahlada. Navaratri and Durga Puja honour the goddess in her many forms over nine nights. Ganesh Chaturthi celebrates the birth of Ganesha, while Krishna Janmashtami marks Krishna's birth. Makar Sankranti and Pongal are harvest festivals tied to the sun's movement, and Raksha Bandhan celebrates the bond between siblings.

Observance

Common elements of festival observance include fasting (vrata), ritual bathing, temple worship and puja, processions, communal feasts, the giving of charity, and the exchange of sweets. Because the Hindu calendar is lunisolar, festival dates in the Gregorian calendar shift from year to year.
