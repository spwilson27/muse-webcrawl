Sudbury Valley School

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Sudbury Valley School building in Framingham, Massachusetts.

The Sudbury Valley School is a private school in Framingham, Massachusetts, United States, founded in 1968 by a group of parents and educators including Daniel Greenberg, Mimsy Sadofsky and Hanna Greenberg. It is the origin of the Sudbury model of education, in which students are entirely responsible for their own learning.

Model

The school enrols students aged roughly four through nineteen, ungraded and unseparated by age. There are no required classes, curriculum, tests or grades: students spend their time as they choose — playing, reading, pursuing projects, or requesting instruction — and learn staff are available as resources. The school is run democratically by a School Meeting in which every student and staff member has one vote, deciding budgets, rules and staff hiring; a student-led judicial committee handles rule infractions.

Influence

Sudbury Valley's founders published extensively on the model, and dozens of schools worldwide describe themselves as Sudbury schools, adopting free age-mixing, democratic governance and self-directed learning. Supporters argue the model produces self-motivated, responsible graduates; critics question how students acquire structured knowledge in traditional subjects. Alumni outcome studies published by the school report high rates of college attendance and career satisfaction.
