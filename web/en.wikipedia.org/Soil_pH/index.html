Soil pH

Soil pH is a measure of the acidity or basicity (alkalinity) of a soil. It is measured on the pH scale, typically in a slurry of soil mixed with water (or a salt solution such as calcium chloride). Soil pH is considered a master variable in soil science because it affects many chemical and biological processes, including nutrient availability, microbial activity, and the solubility of potentially toxic elements.

Ranges

Most soils fall between pH 3.5 and 10. Soils are often described as strongly acidic below about 5.0, slightly acidic between 6.0 and 6.5, neutral near 7, and alkaline above 7.3. In high-rainfall regions, leaching of basic cations tends to make soils acidic, while soils in arid regions are often alkaline or saline. Acid sulfate soils can reach values below 3.5, and some alkaline sodic soils exceed 9.

Factors affecting soil pH

Parent material, climate, vegetation, and time govern natural soil pH. Human activities also matter: nitrogen fertilizers and acid deposition can acidify soils, while liming raises pH. Plant roots and soil organisms influence the pH of the rhizosphere through respiration and exudates.

Effects on plants

pH controls the availability of nutrients. In strongly acidic soils, phosphorus becomes fixed by iron and aluminium compounds, and aluminium and manganese may reach toxic concentrations. In alkaline soils, iron, manganese, zinc, and phosphorus can become deficient. Most agricultural crops grow best between pH 5.5 and 7.5, though some, such as blueberries and rhododendrons, prefer acidic conditions, while others tolerate alkalinity. Soil pH is a standard measurement in soil classification and soil testing.

Measurement

Laboratory measurement uses a glass electrode pH meter in a soil-water suspension. Field kits use indicator dyes or test strips. Results depend on the soil-to-solution ratio and the solution used, so methods must be reported with the value.

Amending soil pH

Acidic soils are commonly amended with agricultural lime (calcium carbonate or dolomite) to raise pH. Lowering the pH of alkaline soils is more difficult; elemental sulfur, oxidized by soil bacteria to sulfuric acid, is sometimes used for acid-loving plants. Buffer pH tests estimate lime requirements.
