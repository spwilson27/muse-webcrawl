Mathematical Platonism

FieldPhilosophy of mathematics
Related toPlatonism, realism

Mathematical Platonism is the view that mathematical entities — numbers, sets, geometric figures — exist independently of human minds, language, and practices, in an abstract realm, and that mathematical truths are discovered rather than invented.

Arguments

Supporters appeal to the indispensability of mathematics in science (the Quine–Putnam argument) and to the objectivity and necessity of mathematical truths. Critics, following Paul Benacerraf, ask how humans could know about causally inert abstract objects.

Alternatives

Rival positions include nominalism, which denies the existence of abstract mathematical objects, formalism, which treats mathematics as manipulation of symbols, and intuitionism.
