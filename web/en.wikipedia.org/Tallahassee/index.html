Tallahassee is the capital city of the U.S. state of Florida and the county seat of Leon County. Located in the state's Panhandle region amid rolling red hills unusual for Florida, it was chosen as territorial capital in 1824 because it lay midway between the old Spanish colonial capitals of St. Augustine and Pensacola. Its name derives from a Muskogee word meaning "old fields".

History

The area was long inhabited by the Apalachee people, whose main town, Anhaica, stood near the present city; Spanish missions followed in the seventeenth century. After the United States acquired Florida in 1821, Tallahassee was founded as capital in 1824. During the Civil War it was the only Confederate state capital east of the Mississippi not captured by Union forces. In 2000 the city was at the centre of the Florida recount in the U.S. presidential election.

Government and education

As state capital, Tallahassee hosts the Florida Capitol complex, the state Supreme Court, and the governor's mansion; state government is the city's largest employer. It is also a major university city, home to Florida State University (founded 1851) and Florida A&M University, a historically Black university founded in 1887, as well as Tallahassee Community College.

Geography and culture

Unlike most of Florida, Tallahassee has distinct seasons, canopy roads shaded by live oaks and Spanish moss, and a landscape of clay hills. Nearby attractions include Wakulla Springs, one of the world's largest freshwater springs, and the Maclay Gardens. The city's population is about 200,000, making it one of the smaller U.S. state capitals relative to its state's major metros.
