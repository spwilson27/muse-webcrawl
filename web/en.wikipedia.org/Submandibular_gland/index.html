Submandibular gland

The submandibular glands are a pair of major salivary glands located beneath the floor of the mouth, under the mandible. Together with the parotid and sublingual glands, they produce most of the saliva, contributing roughly 65–70% of resting salivary flow.

Submandibular gland
Type: Salivary gland
Duct: Wharton's duct
Nerve: Chorda tympani
Secretion: Mixed serous and mucous

Anatomy

Each gland is about the size of a walnut and wraps around the posterior edge of the mylohyoid muscle. Its duct, Wharton's duct, runs forward to open at the sublingual caruncle beside the frenulum of the tongue.

Function

The gland produces a mixed serous and mucous secretion containing enzymes such as amylase and lysozyme, aiding digestion, lubrication, and oral hygiene.

Clinical significance

The submandibular gland is the most common site for salivary stones (sialolithiasis), which can obstruct the duct and cause painful swelling at mealtimes. It can also be affected by infections and tumours.
