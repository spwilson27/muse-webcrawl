Battle of Gergovia
Date: 52 BC
Location: Gergovia, Gaul (modern Auvergne, France)
Result: Gallic victory
Part of the Gallic Wars

The Battle of Gergovia was fought in 52 BC between the Roman legions of Julius Caesar and the Gallic forces of Vercingetorix, chief of the Arverni, at the hilltop oppidum of Gergovia. It was one of Caesar's rare defeats in the Gallic Wars.

Background

Vercingetorix had raised a general revolt of the Gallic tribes and adopted a scorched-earth strategy to starve the Romans. Caesar, returning from operations against other tribes, besieged the Arvernian stronghold of Gergovia, a heavily fortified town on a steep plateau south of modern Clermont-Ferrand, while Vercingetorix camped nearby with a large army.

The battle

Caesar planned a limited operation — a feint against the Gallic camps combined with an attack on an outlying hill — but his legionaries pressed on beyond their orders and assaulted the walls of the town itself. Caught under missile fire and by Gallic reinforcements, the Romans were driven down the slope with the loss of some 700 soldiers, including 46 centurions, according to Caesar's own account. Caesar blamed the troops' indiscipline for the reverse, held his ground for two days to restore their confidence, and then withdrew to deal with a revolt of the Aedui.

Significance

Gergovia was the high point of Vercingetorix's revolt and remains a symbol of French resistance, celebrated alongside the later siege of Alesia, where Caesar finally destroyed Vercingetorix's army later the same year.
