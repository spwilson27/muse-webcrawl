The National Action Party (Spanish: Partido Acción Nacional, PAN) is a major political party in Mexico. Founded in 1939 by Manuel Gómez Morín, it is a conservative party rooted in Christian democratic and social-conservative thought, and for most of the twentieth century it was the principal opposition to the long-dominant Institutional Revolutionary Party (PRI).

History

The PAN was founded in reaction to the statist and anticlerical policies of the post-revolutionary governments. It won its first governorship (Baja California, 1989, Ernesto Ruffo Appel) as electoral reforms opened the system. In 2000 its candidate Vicente Fox won the presidency, ending 71 years of PRI rule; Felipe Calderón retained the presidency for the PAN in 2006, launching the controversial militarised campaign against drug cartels. The party lost the presidency in 2012 and has since competed as a leading opposition force, frequently in coalition (including the 2024 Broad Front for Mexico alliance).

Ideology and support

The PAN advocates free markets, federalism, and social conservatism; its support is strongest in northern and central-western (Bajío) states and among business and middle-class voters, and among practising Catholics. It holds numerous governorships and municipal governments and is a member of the Centrist Democrat International.
