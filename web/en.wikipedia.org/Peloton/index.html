Peloton

In a road cycle race, the peloton (from French, originally meaning "platoon") is the main group or pack of riders. Riders in a group save energy by riding close (drafting or slipstreaming) behind and near other riders, greatly reducing aerodynamic drag. The reduction in drag is dramatic: riding in the middle of a well-developed group can cut drag by 50–70% and, in large pelotons, by over 90% for riders sheltered in the centre.

Formation and tactics

The peloton's shape is determined by wind direction, terrain, and pace. In crosswinds, riders form diagonal echelons across the road, and the group can split into several parts. Riders constantly adjust position: the front is where attacks are launched, while the interior is sheltered but risky if a crash occurs.

Teams cooperate within the peloton to protect their leaders, chase down breakaways, and position sprinters for the finish. A smaller group that escapes ahead is a breakaway; groups behind are sometimes called gruppetti, and riders who cooperate to catch the leaders may form a chase group.

Science

Computational fluid dynamics studies show that the peloton behaves like a self-organising system with phase transitions between relaxed and competitive states, driven by the trade-off between conserving energy and maintaining position. The phenomenon is analogous to flocking in birds and drafting in other racing sports.

Other uses

The word is used metaphorically for the main body of competitors in a race of any kind, and it gave its name to the exercise-equipment and media company Peloton Interactive.
