Gérard Debreu

Gérard Debreu (1921–2004)

Gérard Debreu (4 July 1921 – 31 December 2004) was a French-born American economist and mathematician. He is best known for his contributions to general equilibrium theory and for proving, together with Kenneth Arrow, the existence of a competitive equilibrium in an abstract model of an economy. In 1983 he was awarded the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences "for having incorporated new analytical methods into economic theory and for his rigorous reformulation of the theory of general equilibrium."

Life and career

Debreu was born in Calais, France. He studied mathematics at the École Normale Supérieure in Paris, where his studies were interrupted by the Second World War; he served in the French army and was present at the liberation of Paris. After the war he turned from pure mathematics to economics, influenced by the work of Maurice Allais and the Bourbaki group's rigorous approach to mathematics.

In 1950 Debreu received a fellowship that took him to the United States, where he joined the Cowles Commission at the University of Chicago. He later held positions at Yale University and, from 1962, at the University of California, Berkeley, where he remained for the rest of his career. He became a United States citizen in 1975.

Work

Debreu's most famous work is his 1959 monograph Theory of Value: An Axiomatic Analysis of Economic Equilibrium, which presented general equilibrium theory in a fully axiomatic form using set theory and topology. In 1954, with Kenneth Arrow, he had published the landmark paper "Existence of an Equilibrium for a Competitive Economy," which used Kakutani's fixed-point theorem to prove that a system of prices exists at which supply equals demand in all markets simultaneously.

Debreu also worked on the theory of utility, cardinal utility, and the integrability problem — the question of when a demand function can be derived from utility maximisation. His research emphasised mathematical rigour and generality over empirical application.

Honours

  • Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences, 1983
  • Legion of Honour (France)
  • Member of the United States National Academy of Sciences
