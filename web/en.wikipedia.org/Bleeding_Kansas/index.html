Bleeding Kansas, also known as Bloody Kansas or the Border War, was a series of violent confrontations in Kansas Territory between 1854 and 1861 between pro-slavery and anti-slavery settlers. The conflict foreshadowed the American Civil War.

Background

The Kansas–Nebraska Act of 1854 let settlers decide by popular sovereignty whether Kansas would allow slavery. Abolitionists and Free-Staters from New England and pro-slavery "Border Ruffians" from Missouri flooded in, creating rival governments and constitutions at Lecompton and Topeka.

Violence

Fraudulent elections, the sacking of the free-state town of Lawrence in May 1856, and John Brown's retaliatory Pottawatomie massacre days later — in which five pro-slavery men were killed — made national headlines and deepened sectional crisis. Guerrilla violence continued sporadically until statehood.

Outcome

Kansas entered the Union as a free state under the Wyandotte Constitution on January 29, 1861, just weeks before the Civil War began; the territory's struggles had helped give rise to the Republican Party and destroyed the national Democratic consensus on slavery's expansion.
