Overton window

The Overton window is the range of policies politically acceptable to the mainstream population at a given time. It is named after Joseph Overton, a former vice president of the Mackinac Center for Public Policy, who described it in the 1990s.

Concept

Policies are often arrayed from "more freedom" to "less freedom", with positions labelled popular, sensible, acceptable, radical and unthinkable. Politicians generally support ideas inside the window; the window can be shifted by advocates, think tanks, media and events that change public opinion rather than by politicians alone.

Usage

The term is widely used in political commentary to describe how once-fringe ideas become mainstream or vice versa, and strategies that deliberately propose extreme positions to move the window.
