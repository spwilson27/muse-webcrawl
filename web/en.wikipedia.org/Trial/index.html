Trial
A formal examination of evidence before a judge or jury

In law, a trial is a coming together of parties to a dispute, to present information (in the form of evidence) in a tribunal, a formal setting with the authority to adjudicate claims or disputes. One form of tribunal is a court. The tribunal, which may occur before a judge, jury, or other designated trier of fact, aims to achieve a resolution to their dispute.

Types

Trials may be criminal, in which a government prosecutes a person accused of a crime, or civil, in which private parties resolve disputes over contracts, property or injury. A trial may be a bench trial, decided by a judge alone, or a jury trial.

Process

Typical stages include selection of a jury, opening statements, presentation of evidence and witness testimony, cross-examination, closing arguments, instructions to the jury, deliberation and a verdict. The standard of proof is beyond reasonable doubt in criminal cases and the balance of probabilities in most civil cases.
