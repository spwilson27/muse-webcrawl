Decapoda

The Decapoda or decapods (literally "ten-footed") are an order of crustaceans within the class Malacostraca, and include many familiar groups, including crabs, lobsters, crayfish, shrimp, and prawns. Most decapods are scavengers. The order includes roughly 15,000 living species in around 2,700 genera, and about 3,300 fossil species.

Anatomy

As their name implies, decapods have five pairs of thoracic legs (pereiopods); the first pair is often enlarged into claws called chelae. The head and thorax are fused into a cephalothorax covered by a carapace, and the gills are enclosed in a branchial chamber. Members range from tiny pea crabs a few millimetres wide to the Japanese spider crab, with a leg span approaching 3.7 metres, and the American lobster, which can exceed 20 kilograms.

Classification and ecology

Decapods are divided into two suborders: Dendrobranchiata, the prawns, which release their eggs into the water, and Pleocyemata, in which females brood eggs on their pleopods — including the infraorders Caridea (true shrimp), Astacidea (lobsters and crayfish), and Brachyura (true crabs). Decapods occupy nearly every aquatic habitat from abyssal vents to mountain streams, and some crabs are fully terrestrial. They support major commercial fisheries worldwide and are also central to seafood cuisine.
