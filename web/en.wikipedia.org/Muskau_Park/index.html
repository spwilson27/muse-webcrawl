Muskau Park

Official nameMuskauer Park / Park Mużakowski
LocationLusatia, on the Germany–Poland border
AreaAbout 830 hectares (560 ha in Poland, 270 ha in Germany)
CreatedFrom 1815, by Prince Hermann von Pückler-Muskau
DesignationUNESCO World Heritage Site (2004)

Muskau Park (German: Muskauer Park; Polish: Park Mużakowski) is a landscape park in the Upper Lusatia region, straddling the border between Germany and Poland along the Lusatian Neisse river. At roughly 830 hectares it is the largest English-style landscape park in Central Europe. It was created in the nineteenth century by Prince Hermann von Pückler-Muskau around his seat at Bad Muskau, and in 2004 it was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List as a pioneering masterpiece of landscape design.

The New Castle (Neues Schloss) at Bad Muskau seen across the park's lake.

History

Prince Hermann, a flamboyant travel writer and gardener, inherited the Muskau estate in 1811 and from 1815 devoted decades — and much of his fortune — to remodelling the grounds in the English landscape style of Humphry Repton, whom he admired. Rather than surrounding the castle with formal gardens, Pückler created a sequence of "painted" views: sweeping lawns, serpentine lakes and rivers, clumps of trees, and carefully framed prospects of the Old and New Castles and an English cottage. His book Hints on Landscape Gardening (1834) codified his methods and influenced garden design across Europe. Financial strain forced him to sell Muskau in 1845; later owners, including Prince Frederick of the Netherlands and the von Arnim family, maintained and extended the design.

Division and restoration

After the Second World War, the new German–Polish border along the Neisse cut the park in two, with the larger eastern portion in Poland. The castles were damaged in 1945 and the halves were managed separately for decades. Following German reunification and Poland's accession to the European Union, the two countries undertook a joint restoration, rebuilding bridges across the river and reviving Pückler's vistas; the park is now managed cooperatively, and its bilingual World Heritage listing is regarded as a model of cross-border heritage conservation.

Features

Principal buildings include the moated Old Castle (Altes Schloss), the New Castle — burned in 1945 and reconstructed in the 1990s–2010s — the Orangery, and the English House. The Polish side preserves extensive parkland and meadows, while the German side adjoins the spa town of Bad Muskau.
