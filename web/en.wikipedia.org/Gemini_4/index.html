Gemini 4 (officially Gemini IV) was the second crewed spaceflight in NASA's Project Gemini, occurring in June 1965. It was the seventh crewed American spaceflight. The crew, James McDivitt and Ed White, performed the first spacewalk by an American, with White becoming the first American to make an extravehicular activity (EVA), when he floated free outside the spacecraft, tethered to it, for roughly 23 minutes.

Mission

The Titan II GLV rocket launched Gemini 4 from Cape Kennedy on 3 June 1965. The original plan for McDivitt to rendezvous with the spent Titan upper stage proved too difficult, burning excessive fuel; the attempt was abandoned and attention turned to White's spacewalk. During the third orbit, over Hawaii, White opened the hatch and propelled himself out using a hand-held maneuvering unit firing compressed oxygen. Photographs of White floating against the Earth became iconic. Recovery came on 7 June 1965 in the Atlantic Ocean, where the crew was picked up by USS Wasp.

Objectives

The flight was intended to evaluate the effects of prolonged spaceflight (four days, roughly the minimum time to fly to the Moon and back), to demonstrate EVA, and to conduct 11 scientific experiments. The mission showed that multi-day flights were feasible and that astronauts could work outside their spacecraft.
