Tzintzuntzan

LocationMichoacán, Mexico
CulturePurépecha (Tarascan)
Periodc. 1300–1522 AD

Tzintzuntzan is a town and archaeological site in the Mexican state of Michoacán, on the shore of Lake Pátzcuaro. Its name in the Purépecha language means "place of the hummingbirds". From about 1300 it was the capital of the Tarascan (Purépecha) Empire, the second-largest state in pre-Hispanic Mesoamerica.

Archaeology

The site is famous for its five circular stepped pyramids called yácatas, built on a massive artificial platform overlooking the lake. The yácatas housed temples dedicated to the Purépecha gods, chiefly Curicaueri, the fire god.

History

Tzintzuntzan grew into a city of perhaps 25,000–35,000 people and administered an empire renowned for its metalworking in copper, bronze, gold and silver. After the Spanish conquest in the 1520s–30s, the capital's importance declined and the population was relocated.
