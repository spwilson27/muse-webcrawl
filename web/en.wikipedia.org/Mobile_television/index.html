Mobile television

Mobile television is television content watched on a small handheld device, such as a mobile phone, tablet, or portable media player. It encompasses both broadcast services designed for mobile reception and television streamed or downloaded over mobile networks and Wi-Fi.

Broadcast standards

Dedicated mobile broadcast standards were developed in the 2000s, including DVB-H in Europe, MediaFLO in the United States, ISDB-T 1seg in Japan and Brazil, and DMB in South Korea. These services offered live channels optimised for small screens and low battery consumption but saw limited commercial success as smartphones and faster data networks spread; MediaFLO shut down in 2011 and DVB-H services were discontinued in most markets.

Streaming era

With the spread of 3G, 4G, and 5G networks, mobile television shifted to internet streaming via apps and services such as YouTube, Netflix, and broadcasters' catch-up platforms. Phones and tablets became major screens for sports, news, and short-form video, and usage measurement increasingly folds mobile viewing into overall television ratings.

History

Portable television sets such as Sony's Watchman (1982) provided early mobile viewing of broadcast signals. The first mobile-phone television services appeared in South Korea and Japan in the mid-2000s, and the category expanded rapidly after the introduction of the iPhone in 2007 and app-based distribution.
