György Lukács

György Lukács
Born: 13 April 1885, Budapest, Hungary
Died: 4 June 1971, Budapest
Known for: Reification; class consciousness; Western Marxism

György Lukács (13 April 1885 – 4 June 1971) was a Hungarian Marxist philosopher, literary historian and critic. He is regarded as a founder of the tradition known as Western Marxism and was influential in the development of Marxist literary theory and the sociology of literature.

History and Class Consciousness

Lukács's most famous work, History and Class Consciousness (1923), developed the concept of reification — the process by which social relations under capitalism take on the character of things — and theorised the proletariat as the subject-object of history capable of achieving class consciousness. The book was condemned by the Communist International, and Lukács later recanted parts of it, but it profoundly influenced later thinkers including the Frankfurt School.

Life and politics

Lukács served as a people's commissar for education in the short-lived Hungarian Soviet Republic of 1919, spent the interwar years in exile in Vienna, Berlin and Moscow, and returned to Hungary after the Second World War. He participated in Imre Nagy's government during the Hungarian Revolution of 1956 and was deported to Romania after its suppression, later being allowed to return to Budapest.

Literary theory

In works such as The Historical Novel and Studies in European Realism, Lukács championed the realist novel of Balzac and Thomas Mann against modernist experimentation, a position debated in the famous "expressionism debate" with Ernst Bloch and others.
