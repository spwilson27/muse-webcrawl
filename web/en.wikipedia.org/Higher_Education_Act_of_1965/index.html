The Higher Education Act of 1965 (HEA) is a landmark United States federal law governing federal financial aid and administration in postsecondary education. It was signed by President Lyndon B. Johnson on 8 November 1965 at Southwest Texas State College (now Texas State University), his alma mater, as part of his Great Society domestic agenda.

Structure

The Act is organised into titles addressing different areas: institutional aid and community service (Title I), teacher quality and libraries (Title II), aid to developing institutions (Title III), student assistance (Title IV), teacher preparation (Title V), international education (Title VI), graduate programs (Title VII), and other provisions. Title IV is the best known, authorising the federal student aid programs administered by the U.S. Department of Education.

Programs

Programs created or authorised by the Act and its amendments include the Pell Grant (originally the Basic Educational Opportunity Grant, renamed in 1980 for Senator Claiborne Pell), Federal Work-Study, federal student loans (Stafford, PLUS and Perkins loans), TRIO programs, and the GEAR UP program.

Reauthorization

The Act has been reauthorized repeatedly, notably in 1972, 1980, 1986, 1992, 1998 and 2008 (the Higher Education Opportunity Act). The 1972 amendments created the Basic Grant and underpinned Title IX's regulatory machinery in education. Periodic reauthorization debates have addressed loan limits, accreditation, and institutional accountability.
