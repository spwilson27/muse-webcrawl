Opium

Opium is a dried latex obtained from the seed capsules of the opium poppy (Papaver somniferum). It contains a mixture of alkaloids, principally morphine, codeine, thebaine, papaverine and noscapine. Opium and its derivatives, the opioids, are powerful analgesics but carry a high risk of dependence.

Production

Opium is harvested by scoring the unripe seed pods so that the milky latex exudes and dries into a brown gum, which is scraped off by hand. Afghanistan has in recent decades been the largest producer of illicit opium, while licit production for pharmaceuticals is concentrated in countries such as India, Turkey and Australia.

History

The opium poppy was cultivated in Mesopotamia and the Mediterranean from Neolithic times. Opium was traded widely in the early modern period; British sales of Indian opium to China led to the Opium Wars of 1839–42 and 1856–60. In the 19th century, morphine was isolated from opium (1804–06) and heroin synthesised (1874), transforming both medicine and illicit drug markets.

Uses and effects

Medicinally, opium preparations such as laudanum were once common painkillers and sedatives; today its alkaloids are used in purified form. Recreationally, opium is smoked, eaten or taken as a tincture, producing euphoria and sedation, with risks of tolerance, addiction and fatal respiratory depression.
