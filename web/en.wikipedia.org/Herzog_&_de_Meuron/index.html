Herzog & de Meuron Basel Ltd. is a Swiss architecture firm founded in Basel in 1978 by Jacques Herzog and Pierre de Meuron, both born in Basel in 1950 and graduates of ETH Zurich. The practice is known for its inventive use of materials and building surfaces, and in 2001 the two founders jointly received the Pritzker Architecture Prize.

Work

Early projects such as the Ricola storage building (1987) and the Dominus Winery in Napa Valley (1997), built of gabion baskets filled with local stone, established the firm's fascination with materiality. The conversion of London's Bankside power station into Tate Modern (2000) made them internationally famous. Later landmarks include the Allianz Arena in Munich (2005), the Beijing National Stadium — the "Bird's Nest" — for the 2008 Summer Olympics (with artist Ai Weiwei as artistic consultant), the Elbphilharmonie concert hall in Hamburg (2016), the Tate Modern Switch House extension (2016), and 1111 Lincoln Road in Miami Beach.

Practice

With several hundred employees and offices in cities including London, New York, Hong Kong, and Madrid, Herzog & de Meuron has remained a partnership-led studio. Herzog and de Meuron have also taught widely, including at ETH Zurich and Harvard.
