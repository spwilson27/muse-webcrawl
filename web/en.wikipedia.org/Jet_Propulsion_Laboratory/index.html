Jet Propulsion Laboratory

The Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) is a federally funded research and development centre in Pasadena, California, managed by the California Institute of Technology (Caltech) for NASA. JPL is NASA's lead centre for the robotic exploration of the solar system, having built and operated interplanetary missions including the Voyager probes and the Mars rovers.

History

JPL traces its origins to 1936, when Caltech professor Theodore von Kármán and a group of students and enthusiasts — the so-called "Suicide Squad", including Frank Malina and Jack Parsons — began rocket experiments in the Arroyo Seco. The laboratory took shape during the Second World War, developing the Corporal and Sergeant missiles, and in 1958 built Explorer 1, the first US satellite. Transferred to the newly created NASA that year, JPL turned to lunar and planetary missions.

Missions

JPL spacecraft have visited every planet of the solar system. Landmarks include the Mariner missions to Venus and Mars, the Viking Mars landers (1976), Voyager 1 and 2 (1977), Galileo to Jupiter, Cassini to Saturn, and the Deep Space Network of tracking stations that communicates with them. On Mars, JPL landed the Pathfinder, Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity and Perseverance rovers; Perseverance (2021) also flew the Ingenuity helicopter, the first aircraft flown on another world.

Present day

Employing roughly 6,000 people, JPL continues to develop missions such as Europa Clipper and Earth-observing satellites, and operates NASA's Deep Space Network. Its mission control centre is famous for the "seven minutes of terror" of Mars landings and for the lucky-peanuts tradition on landing day.
