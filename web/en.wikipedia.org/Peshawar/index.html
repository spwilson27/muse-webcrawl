Peshawar

Peshawar is the capital and largest city of the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa province of Pakistan. Located near the eastern end of the historic Khyber Pass, close to the border with Afghanistan, it is one of the oldest continuously inhabited cities in South Asia, with a recorded history dating back to at least 539 BCE. Its population is over two million.

History

Known in antiquity as Purushapura, the city was a capital of the Kushan emperor Kanishka in the 2nd century CE and a great centre of Gandharan Buddhism; Kanishka's great stupa was then among the tallest structures in the world. The city later passed through Hindu Shahi, Ghaznavid, Mughal, Durrani and Sikh hands — the Sikh general Hari Singh Nalwa governed it in the 1830s — before coming under British rule in 1849. Its bazaars were a hub of trade between Central Asia, India and Afghanistan.

Geography and economy

Peshawar lies in a broad valley west of the Indus River, ringed by hills. The historic walled city contains landmarks such as the Mahabat Khan Mosque, the Qissa Khawani Bazaar ("Bazaar of Storytellers") and the Chowk Yadgar. The city is a commercial centre for the region's agricultural produce and for trade with Afghanistan, and hosts the University of Peshawar and the Peshawar Museum, famed for its Gandharan art.

Modern city

Primarily Pashto-speaking, Peshawar has long hosted large Afghan refugee communities. It has suffered repeated militant attacks, most notoriously the 2014 massacre at the Army Public School. The city is home to the Peshawar Zalmi cricket franchise and the Arbab Niaz Stadium.
