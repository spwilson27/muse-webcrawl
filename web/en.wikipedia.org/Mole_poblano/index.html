Mole poblano

Place of originPuebla, Mexico
TypeSauce
Main ingredientsDried chillies, nuts, seeds, spices, chocolate
Typically servedOver turkey or chicken

Mole poblano is a rich, dark sauce of Mexican cuisine, originating in the state of Puebla and often described as the national dish of Mexico. It combines dried chillies (typically ancho, pasilla, and mulato), nuts, seeds, torn stale tortillas or bread, spices such as cinnamon and cloves, and a small amount of chocolate, ground into a smooth sauce served most famously over turkey or chicken and sprinkled with sesame seeds.

Legend and history

Popular legend credits the nuns of the Convent of Santa Rosa in Puebla with inventing the dish in the colonial era, improvising a sauce for a visiting viceroy or archbishop from the ingredients at hand and serving it over turkey. Whatever its precise origin, mole poblano reflects the fusion of Indigenous Mesoamerican ingredients — chillies, chocolate, tomatoes — with Old World spices, nuts, and livestock introduced under Spanish rule.

Preparation and use

Traditional preparation is labour-intensive: chillies are toasted and soaked, ingredients are fried and ground (historically on a metate), and the sauce is simmered at length. Mole poblano is central to celebrations such as Cinco de Mayo in Puebla and is sold as a paste or powder for quicker preparation.
