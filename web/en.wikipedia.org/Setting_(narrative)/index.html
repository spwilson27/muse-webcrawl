Setting (narrative)

A setting is the time and geographic location within a narrative, either nonfiction or fiction. It is a literary element and the initial background into which a story is woven. Most novels and other forms of literature have more than one setting, and settings may change over the course of a story.

Elements of setting

A setting may include the location (including the social environment and customs of the place), the time period (the era in which the story occurs, or the season), the weather, and the atmosphere or mood. Writers often use setting to establish the tone of a work and to influence the behavior and decisions of characters.

Types of setting

Settings vary in specificity. A backdrop setting serves mainly as scenery against which the action unfolds and could be exchanged for another without changing the story. An integral setting is essential to the plot: the story could not happen the same way anywhere else. Fictional settings may also be invented worlds, as in fantasy and science fiction.

Function

Setting can function like another character in a story, shaping conflict (for example, a character struggling against a harsh environment) and reinforcing themes. The study of setting is part of narratology, the theory of narrative structure.
