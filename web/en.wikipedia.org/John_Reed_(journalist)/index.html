John Silas Reed (October 22, 1887 – October 19, 1920) was an American journalist, poet and socialist activist, best known for Ten Days That Shook the World (1919), his eyewitness account of the October Revolution in Russia. A founder of the Communist Labor Party of America, he is one of only a few Americans buried at the Kremlin Wall Necropolis in Moscow.

Early career

Reed was born in Portland, Oregon, and graduated from Harvard University in 1910, where he moved in radical circles. In New York he wrote for The Masses and other magazines, reported on the Paterson silk strike of 1913, and gained fame covering the Mexican Revolution in 1913–14, collected in Insurgent Mexico (1914). He reported from the battlefronts of the First World War in Europe in 1914–16, travelling with his wife, the journalist Louise Bryant.

Russia and Ten Days That Shook the World

Reed arrived in Petrograd in September 1917 and witnessed the Bolshevik seizure of power at first hand, interviewing Lenin and Trotsky and attending the Second Congress of Soviets. His book, published in 1919 with a foreword by Lenin, remains a classic of reportage and a vivid primary source for the revolution. Returning to the United States, he helped found the Communist Labor Party in 1919 and was prosecuted for sedition.

Death

Reed returned to Soviet Russia in 1919–20, attended the Second Congress of the Communist International, and died of typhus in Moscow in October 1920, days before his 33rd birthday. His life was portrayed by Warren Beatty in the film Reds (1981).
