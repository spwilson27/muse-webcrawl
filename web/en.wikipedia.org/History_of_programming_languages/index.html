History of programming languages

The history of programming languages spans from nineteenth-century mechanical devices to the thousands of languages in use today, documenting efforts to express computation in forms humans can write and machines can execute.

Early developments

Ada Lovelace's notes on Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine (1840s) included an algorithm often described as the first computer program. In the twentieth century, Konrad Zuse designed Plankalkül in the 1940s, and assembly languages emerged alongside the first stored-program computers, followed by early compilers such as Grace Hopper's A-0 system.

The first high-level languages

FORTRAN (1957), developed by John Backus's team at IBM, demonstrated that compiled code could rival hand-written assembly. LISP (1958) introduced symbolic processing and garbage collection; COBOL (1959) targeted business use; ALGOL influenced language design for decades.

Modern eras

The 1970s brought structured programming, C, and Smalltalk's object orientation; the 1980s and 1990s produced C++, Perl, Python, Java, and JavaScript, the last three riding the growth of the World Wide Web. Twenty-first-century languages such as Go, Rust, Swift, and Kotlin reflect concerns with concurrency, safety, and mobile platforms, while functional and multi-paradigm designs regained prominence.
