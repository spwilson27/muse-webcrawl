TÜRKSOY (Turkish: Uluslararası Türk Kültürü Teşkilatı, the International Organization of Turkic Culture) is an international cultural organization of countries with Turkic populations, founded in 1993. Its purpose is to preserve, develop and transmit Turkic culture, language, history and art to future generations, and to promote cooperation among Turkic peoples.

The acronym TÜRKSOY is composed of the words Türk (Turkish/Turkic) and soy (ancestry, lineage), and is also read as a contraction of Türk Soyu — "the Turkic lineage."

History

The organization's roots go back to meetings of the culture ministers of Turkic-speaking countries held in Baku and Istanbul in 1992, shortly after the dissolution of the Soviet Union and the independence of the Turkic republics of Central Asia and the Caucasus. The founding agreement was signed on 12 July 1993 in Almaty, Kazakhstan, by the ministers of culture of Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Turkey, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan. Tajikistan, a Persian-speaking country, soon left; the six remaining states became the founding members.

TÜRKSOY's headquarters are in Ankara, Turkey. The organization is headed by a Secretary General elected for a three-year term; the first was Polad Bülbüloğlu of Azerbaijan, and the post has since been held by figures including Düsen Kaseinov of Kazakhstan (2008–2022) and Sultan Raev of Kyrgyzstan (from 2022).

Activities

TÜRKSOY organizes festivals, concerts, exhibitions, scholarly congresses and artist gatherings across the Turkic world. Since 2011 it has designated a "Cultural Capital of the Turkic World" each year — Astana was the first, followed by cities such as Eskişehir, Kazan, Merv, Sheki, Turkistan, Bursa, Shusha and Anau. It popularized the celebration of Nowruz internationally, including concerts at the United Nations and UNESCO, and it promotes occasions such as the Nevruz spring festival and the works of classical figures like Yunus Emre, Alisher Navoiy and Magtymguly Pyragy.

The organization publishes books and a periodical in Turkic languages, supports the restoration of cultural heritage, and coordinates an opera and ballet festival, a congress of literary journals, and a forum of cinematographers of the Turkic world.

Members

Full members are Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Turkey, Turkmenistan and Uzbekistan. Observer members have included autonomous republics and regions such as Tatarstan, Bashkortostan, Sakha (Yakutia), Khakassia, Tuva, Altai and Gagauzia, as well as the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus. Several Russian federal subjects suspended or withdrew participation after 2015 tensions between Russia and Turkey.

Relations

TÜRKSOY works in cooperation with UNESCO and ISESCO and maintains ties with the Organization of Turkic States (OTS), TURKPA (the parliamentary assembly) and the Turkic Academy. It is sometimes described as a "UNESCO of the Turkic world."
