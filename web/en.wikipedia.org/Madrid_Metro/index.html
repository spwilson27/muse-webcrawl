Madrid Metro

Locale: Madrid, Spain

Transit type: Rapid transit (metro), light metro

Lines: 13 (plus 3 light-metro lines)

Stations: approx. 300

System length: approx. 294 km

Opened: 17 October 1919

Annual ridership: approx. 700 million (2024)

The Madrid Metro (Spanish: Metro de Madrid) is the rapid transit system serving the city of Madrid and its metropolitan area in Spain. Opened in 1919, it is one of the largest metro systems in the world and the third-largest in Europe by route length after London and Moscow, with around 294 kilometres of line and some 300 stations including its light-metro branches.

History

King Alfonso XIII inaugurated the first line, just 3.48 km between Sol and Cuatro Caminos, on 17 October 1919; the king had personally contributed to the project's financing. The network grew steadily through the twentieth century, with a circular Line 6 completed in 1995. Between 1995 and 2007 Madrid carried out one of the fastest metro expansions ever undertaken, adding well over 100 km of line, including the MetroSur circle serving the southern suburbs and the airport extension of Line 8. This expansion programme was internationally studied for its low cost per kilometre and rapid construction using tunnel-boring machines.

Network and operation

The system comprises twelve numbered conventional lines and the Ramal branch, plus three light-metro (tram-like) lines in the western and northern suburbs. Most central lines use narrow loading-gauge trains due to the small tunnel profiles of the early network, while newer lines are built to a larger profile. Trains run on standard-gauge or Iberian-gauge track depending on line age, and drive on the left, a legacy of Madrid traffic before 1924. Madrid Metro is publicly owned through the Community of Madrid and coordinates fares with the regional transport consortium.

Design

The network's diamond-shaped logo and station architecture date from the system's early decades; disused Chamberí station survives as a museum of the 1919 metro. See also the article on Macau International Airport for another transport system built under tight land constraints.
