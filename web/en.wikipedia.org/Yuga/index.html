A yuga (Sanskrit: युग) is a unit of time in Hindu cosmology, one of a repeating cycle of four ages through which the world is said to pass. Together the four yugas form a chatur yuga or maha yuga lasting 4,320,000 human years. The doctrine appears in texts such as the Mahabharata, the Manusmriti, and the Puranas.

The four yugas

The four yugas decline in both duration and righteousness (dharma), which is said to stand on four legs in the first age and only one in the last:

Lengths are calculated in divine years, one of which equals 360 human years, with each age flanked by dawn and twilight periods (sandhya).

Larger cycles

One thousand maha yugas make a kalpa, a single day of the creator god Brahma, equal to 4.32 billion years; a kalpa is in turn divided into fourteen manvantaras, each ruled by a Manu and containing about 71 maha yugas. According to traditional chronology, the present Kali Yuga began in 3102 BCE, the date associated with the departure of Krishna and the Mahabharata war.
