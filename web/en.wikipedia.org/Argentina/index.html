Argentina

Capital: Buenos Aires
Official language: Spanish (de facto)
Population: ~46 million
Currency: Argentine peso

Argentina, officially the Argentine Republic, is a country in the southern half of South America. It covers an area of 2,780,400 km² (1,073,500 sq mi), making it the second-largest country in South America after Brazil, the fourth-largest country in the Americas, and the eighth-largest country in the world. It shares the bulk of the Southern Cone with Chile to the west, and is also bordered by Bolivia and Paraguay to the north, Brazil to the northeast, Uruguay and the South Atlantic Ocean to the east, and the Drake Passage to the south.

History

The area was inhabited by indigenous peoples for thousands of years before Spanish colonization began in the 16th century. Argentina declared independence from Spain on 9 July 1816, in the May Revolution's aftermath. The 19th century saw civil wars and the rise of the cattle and grain export economy; by the early 20th century Argentina was among the world's wealthiest countries per capita. The presidency of Juan Perón (1946–1955) and his wife Eva ("Evita") reshaped Argentine politics. A period of military dictatorships (1976–1983), including the "Dirty War" and the Falklands War of 1982, was followed by a return to democracy in 1983.

Geography

Argentina's terrain ranges from the subtropical north to the subantarctic south. The Andes form the western border; Aconcagua, at 6,961 m, is the highest mountain in the Americas. The fertile Pampas grasslands in the center are the agricultural heartland, while Patagonia in the south is a dry, windswept plateau.

Culture

Argentina is famous for the tango, developed in Buenos Aires in the late 19th century, and for writers such as Jorge Luis Borges and Julio Cortázar. Football is the most popular sport; the national team has won the FIFA World Cup three times (1978, 1986, 2022), and Diego Maradona and Lionel Messi are among the most celebrated players in the sport's history.
