Haskins Laboratories, Inc. is an independent 501(c) non-profit corporation, founded in 1935 and located in New Haven, Connecticut, since 1970. Its scientists conduct basic research on the biology and psychology of speech, language, and reading, and on the development of technology for people with language problems. The Laboratories maintain formal affiliations with Yale University and the University of Connecticut and are supported largely by grants from agencies including the National Institutes of Health and the National Science Foundation.

History

The Laboratories were founded in Schenectady, New York, by physicist and biologist Caryl Parker Haskins, doing contract research on microbiology and radiation physics. During World War II, Haskins scientists — including Franklin S. Cooper and Alvin Liberman — worked on technology for blinded veterans, including a reading machine; this led after the war to a sustained research program on how humans perceive speech sounds from the acoustic signal. In the 1950s the team built the Pattern Playback, a machine that converted simplified, hand-painted spectrogram-like patterns back into sound, allowing researchers to isolate the acoustic cues (such as formant transitions) that listeners use to identify consonants and vowels.

Research

Haskins research produced the motor theory of speech perception (advanced by Liberman and colleagues), which proposed that speech is perceived by reference to the speaker's articulatory gestures; investigations of categorical perception; and Donald Shankweiler and others' work on the phonological basis of reading disability (dyslexia). Haskins scientists, including Katherine Harris and Louis Goldstein, pioneered articulatory phonology and electromagnetic articulography (the "Haskins tongue" measurement tradition) and developed gestural-computational models of speech production. The Laboratories also run global-literacy initiatives applying reading science to improve educational outcomes for children and adults in underserved populations.
