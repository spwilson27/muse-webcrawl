Différance

Différance is a French term coined by the philosopher Jacques Derrida. It is a deliberate pun on the French word différence, exploiting the fact that the two spellings are pronounced identically while differing in writing — an illustration of Derrida's argument about the relation between speech and writing.

Meaning

The term combines two senses of the verb différer: to differ (in space, between signs) and to defer (in time, postponing presence). Derrida argued that meaning in language arises from differences between signs and is endlessly deferred along chains of signifiers, so no sign ever delivers a fully present, self-contained meaning.

Context

Derrida introduced the term in his 1968 lecture "La Différance" and developed it in works such as Of Grammatology. He insisted that différance is "neither a word nor a concept," but a play of differences that makes signification possible. The idea is central to deconstruction and influenced later literary and cultural theory.
