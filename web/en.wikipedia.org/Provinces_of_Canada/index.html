The provinces and territories of Canada are the first-level administrative divisions of the country. Canada has ten provinces — Alberta, British Columbia, Manitoba, New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Nova Scotia, Ontario, Prince Edward Island, Quebec, and Saskatchewan — and three territories: the Northwest Territories, Nunavut, and Yukon.

Provinces and territories compared

The provinces derive their powers from the Constitution, principally the Constitution Act, 1867, which divides authority between the federal Parliament and provincial legislatures; provinces are co-sovereign within their areas of jurisdiction and each has its own lieutenant governor representing the Crown. The territories, by contrast, exercise powers delegated by federal statute, and their governments have gradually been "devolved" responsibilities resembling those of provinces. Nunavut, created in 1999 from the eastern Northwest Territories, is the largest and least populous division.

History

Confederation in 1867 united Ontario, Quebec, Nova Scotia, and New Brunswick. Manitoba and the Northwest Territories joined in 1870, British Columbia in 1871, Prince Edward Island in 1873, Saskatchewan and Alberta in 1905, and Newfoundland in 1949. Quebec is by far the largest province by area, while Ontario, with more than 14 million people, holds the largest share of Canada's population.
