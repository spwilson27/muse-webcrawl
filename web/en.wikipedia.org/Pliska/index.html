Pliska (Bulgarian: Плиска) is a small town in northeastern Bulgaria, in Shumen Province, near the site of the first capital of the First Bulgarian Empire. The ancient city of Pliska was the capital of Bulgaria from 681, after the victory of Khan Asparuh over the Byzantine Empire, until 893, when Simeon I moved the capital to Preslav.

The ancient capital

The original Pliska covered an unusually large area of about 23 square kilometres, enclosed by earthen ramparts; an inner stone-walled citadel contained the khan's palace, a great basilica, and other ceremonial buildings. Under Khan Krum the city was burned by the Byzantine emperor Nikephoros I in 811, but Krum ambushed and killed Nikephoros at the Battle of Pliska in the Varbitsa Pass shortly afterwards — one of the worst defeats in Byzantine history.

Christianisation and ruins

Pliska was where Boris I received baptism in 864/865 and where the disciples of Saints Cyril and Methodius were welcomed in 886, helping establish Slavic literacy. Today the archaeological reserve preserves the ruins of the palace complex and the Great Basilica, once among the largest churches in early medieval Europe, while the modern town stands a few kilometres away.
