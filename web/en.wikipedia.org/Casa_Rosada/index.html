The Casa Rosada (Spanish for "Pink House") is the president of the Argentine Republic's official workplace, located in Buenos Aires on the eastern side of the Plaza de Mayo. The palatial mansion is known officially as Casa de Gobierno, "House of Government".

History

The building stands on the site of the old Fort of Buenos Aires, constructed from 1594. In the 19th century the fort's remains housed customs and government offices; under President Domingo Faustino Sarmiento the building was painted its distinctive pink — traditionally explained as an attempt to blend the red and white colours of the rival Unitarian and Federalist parties, though ox blood in the paint has also been proposed. The current palace took shape between 1873 and 1898 through the merging of the post office palace and the old government house, linked by a triumphal arch.

Significance

The Casa Rosada's balcony is famous as the spot from which Juan and Eva Perón addressed mass rallies; the scene is dramatised in the musical Evita. The building contains the Hall of Busts of Argentine presidents and a museum of presidential artefacts. The president's actual residence is the Quinta de Olivos in the suburbs.
