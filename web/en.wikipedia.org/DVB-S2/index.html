DVB-S2

DVB-S2 is a digital television broadcast standard developed by the DVB Project as the second-generation successor to the popular DVB-S system for satellite broadcasting. It was ratified by ETSI in 2005 (EN 302 307) and was designed to achieve a significant increase in capacity and performance over its predecessor.

Overview

DVB-S2 uses modern coding and modulation techniques, including LDPC (low-density parity-check) forward error correction concatenated with BCH codes, and supports QPSK, 8PSK, 16APSK, and 32APSK modulation constellations. It introduced adaptive coding and modulation (ACM), allowing transmission parameters to be adjusted dynamically according to reception conditions, which is particularly valuable for interactive and IP-based services. The standard can deliver roughly 30 percent more capacity than DVB-S under the same conditions.

Applications

The standard is used worldwide for satellite television distribution, including high-definition television services, professional contribution feeds, direct-to-home broadcasting, and broadband satellite internet access. An extension known as DVB-S2X, published in 2014, added finer modulation and coding granularity and further efficiency gains for professional and high-throughput satellite applications.
