Meuse–Argonne offensive

Date26 September – 11 November 1918
LocationMeuse River to Argonne Forest, France
ResultAllied victory
BelligerentsUnited States, France; German Empire

The Meuse–Argonne offensive was a major part of the final Allied offensive of World War I that stretched along the entire Western Front. Fought from 26 September 1918 until the Armistice of 11 November 1918, it was the largest battle in American military history, involving some 1.2 million American soldiers under General John J. Pershing, along with French forces, against the German Army.

Course of the battle

The American First Army attacked northward between the Meuse River and the Argonne Forest toward the vital Sedan–Mézières railway, overcoming dense terrain and the prepared German defences of the Hindenburg Line. Fighting included the exploit of the "Lost Battalion" and the actions of Sergeant Alvin York. By early November the Allies had broken through to Sedan, cutting German lateral communications days before the armistice.

Casualties

American casualties numbered about 122,000, including more than 26,000 dead, making it the deadliest battle in U.S. history; the Meuse-Argonne American Cemetery near Romagne contains over 14,000 graves.
