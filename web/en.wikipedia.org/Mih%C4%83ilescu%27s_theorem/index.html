Mihăilescu's theorem

FieldNumber theory
Conjectured byEugène Charles Catalan (1844)
Proved byPreda Mihăilescu (2002)
StatementThe only consecutive powers are 8 and 9

Mihăilescu's theorem, formerly known as Catalan's conjecture, is a theorem in number theory stating that the only solution in the integers of the equation xa − yb = 1, for x, y > 0 and exponents a, b > 1, is x = 3, a = 2, y = 2, b = 3. In other words, the only consecutive perfect powers greater than 1 are 8 and 9.

History

The conjecture was posed by Eugène Charles Catalan in 1844. Partial results accumulated over the following century and a half: Lebesgue handled a special case in 1850, and in 1976 Robert Tijdeman applied Baker's method in transcendental number theory to show the number of solutions is finite, though without identifying them. It was proved in 2002 by the Romanian mathematician Preda Mihăilescu, using cyclotomic fields and Galois modules; the result was published in 2004.

The proof

Mihăilescu's proof combines bounds from lower bounds for linear forms in logarithms with deep results about cyclotomic fields, including a key double Wieferich prime criterion: if xp − yq = 1, then p2 divides yq−1 − 1 and q2 divides xp−1 − 1. Case analysis then eliminates all possibilities except (3, 2, 2, 3).

Related problems

The theorem is related to Pillai's conjecture on the gaps between perfect powers and to the abc conjecture, which would imply strong generalizations. The equation's solution pair 8 and 9 remains the unique pair of consecutive powers.
