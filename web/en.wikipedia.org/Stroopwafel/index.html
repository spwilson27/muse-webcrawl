Stroopwafel
Origin: Gouda, Netherlands
Type: Waffle cookie with syrup filling

A stroopwafel (literally "syrup waffle") is a thin, round waffle cookie made from two layers of sweet baked dough joined by a caramel or syrup filling. It was invented in Gouda, Netherlands, in the late 18th or early 19th century, traditionally made from bakers' leftovers.

Preparation

A stiff dough of flour, butter, brown sugar, yeast, milk and eggs is pressed in a waffle iron, sliced horizontally while warm, and filled with a hot syrup of brown sugar, butter and cinnamon. Stroopwafels are often placed over a hot drink so the steam softens the caramel.

Popularity

Sold fresh at Dutch markets and in packages worldwide, the stroopwafel has become an emblematic Dutch treat and a popular airline snack.
