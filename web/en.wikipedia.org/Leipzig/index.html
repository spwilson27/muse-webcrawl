Leipzig

Leipzig is the most populous city in the German state of Saxony, with a population of over 600,000. Situated at the confluence of the White Elster, Pleiße and Parthe rivers, it has been an important trading centre since the Middle Ages and is renowned for its trade fairs, publishing industry and musical tradition. Leipzig played a pivotal role in the Peaceful Revolution of 1989 that preceded German reunification.

History

First mentioned in 1015, Leipzig received city and market privileges around 1165 and grew where the Via Regia and Via Imperii trade routes crossed. Its university, founded in 1409, is one of Germany's oldest. The trade fairs made it a hub of European commerce and printing. In October 1813 the city was the site of the Battle of Leipzig — the "Battle of the Nations" — the largest battle in Europe before the First World War, in which Napoleon's armies were decisively defeated; the colossal Monument to the Battle of the Nations (1913) commemorates it. Leipzig was heavily bombed in the Second World War and became part of East Germany in 1949.

The Peaceful Revolution

In autumn 1989, Monday prayers at the St. Nicholas Church grew into mass Monday demonstrations demanding reform. On 9 October 1989 some 70,000 people marched peacefully despite fears of a crackdown — a turning point that helped bring down the East German regime a month later.

Culture

Johann Sebastian Bach worked at the St. Thomas Church from 1723 until his death in 1750, and the Gewandhaus Orchestra, the Leipzig Opera and the Thomanerchor remain world famous. The city is also associated with Mendelssohn, Schumann and Wagner, and today with a lively contemporary art scene centred on the Spinnerei galleries.
