Oxygen therapy

Oxygen therapy, also referred to as supplemental oxygen, is the use of oxygen as a medical treatment. It is indicated for acute and chronic hypoxemia — low blood oxygen levels — and is a mainstay of treatment for conditions such as COPD exacerbations, pneumonia, heart failure, and major trauma.

Delivery methods

Oxygen can be delivered by nasal cannula at low flows, by simple or non-rebreather face masks at higher concentrations, or by high-flow nasal systems. In critical care, patients may receive oxygen through mechanical ventilation; hyperbaric oxygen therapy delivers 100 percent oxygen at elevated pressure for indications such as carbon monoxide poisoning and decompression sickness.

Long-term therapy

Long-term oxygen therapy (LTOT) for patients with chronic severe resting hypoxemia, particularly in COPD, has been shown to improve survival. Home oxygen is supplied by concentrators, compressed gas cylinders, or liquid oxygen systems.

Risks

Oxygen is a drug with potential harms: excessive oxygen can cause oxygen toxicity, worsen hypercapnia in some chronic lung disease patients, and increase fire risk. Guidelines therefore recommend targeting defined saturation ranges rather than administering oxygen indiscriminately.
