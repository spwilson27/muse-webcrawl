CETP inhibitor

CETP inhibitor
Drug classLipid-modifying agents
TargetCholesteryl ester transfer protein
ExamplesTorcetrapib, dalcetrapib, anacetrapib, evacetrapib, obicetrapib

CETP inhibitors are a class of drugs that inhibit cholesteryl ester transfer protein (CETP), which normally transfers cholesteryl esters from HDL to VLDL and LDL in exchange for triglycerides. Inhibition raises HDL cholesterol and apolipoprotein A1 and lowers LDL cholesterol.

Development history

Torcetrapib, the first in class, increased mortality in the ILLUMINATE trial (2006), attributed to off-target aldosterone and blood-pressure effects. Dalcetrapib and evacetrapib showed no cardiovascular benefit. Anacetrapib modestly reduced coronary events in REVEAL (2017), driven by LDL/ApoB lowering, but was not marketed. Obicetrapib, a newer agent, has shown large LDL reductions in phase 3 trials.

Significance

The class history reshaped understanding that raising HDL-C alone does not guarantee benefit in coronary artery disease, much as with niacin.
