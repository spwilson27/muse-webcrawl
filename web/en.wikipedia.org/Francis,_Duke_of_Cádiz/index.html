Francis of Assisi, Duke of Cádiz (Spanish: Francisco de Asís María Fernando; 1822–1902) was king consort of Spain as the husband of Queen Isabella II, whom he married in 1846. A member of the Spanish Bourbon dynasty, he was Isabella's double first cousin.

Marriage and reign

The marriage was arranged for dynastic and diplomatic reasons, partly to exclude a French candidate, and was personally unhappy; the couple lived largely separate lives at court. Francis received the title of king and the style of Majesty but exercised little real political power, though he was a noted patron of the arts and was involved in court intrigues, notably in dealings with his confidant the Marquis of Salamanca.

Exile and later life

When the revolution of 1868 deposed Isabella, Francis accompanied her into exile in Paris. The couple formally separated in 1870, and Francis settled at Épinay-sur-Seine, where he died in 1902. His son, Alfonso XII, became king of Spain in 1874, although Francis's actual paternity of Isabella's children was questioned by contemporaries and remains a subject of historical debate.
