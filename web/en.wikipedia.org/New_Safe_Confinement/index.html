New Safe Confinement

The New Safe Confinement (NSC) is a steel structure built to enclose the remains of Reactor Number 4 at the Chernobyl Nuclear Power Plant in Ukraine, the site of the 1986 Chernobyl disaster. It replaced the deteriorating original concrete "sarcophagus" erected in the months after the accident.

Design

The structure is a giant movable arch, 108 metres high, 257 metres wide and 162 metres long, with a total weight of roughly 36,000 tonnes. It is the largest movable land-based structure ever built. The arch was assembled on a construction site about 300 metres west of the reactor and then slid into place over the old shelter on rails, an operation completed on 29 November 2016.

Purpose

The confinement isolates the destroyed reactor from the environment for a planned service life of 100 years, preventing the release of radioactive dust and protecting the site from weather. It also provides remote-handling cranes and equipment so that the unstable original sarcophagus and the fuel-containing materials inside can eventually be dismantled and removed.

Construction and funding

The project was managed by the European Bank for Reconstruction and Development through the Chernobyl Shelter Fund, with contributions from more than 40 countries and the European Union. A consortium led by the French firms Bouygues Travaux Publics and Vinci Construction Grands Projets built the structure. Total costs were around 1.5 billion euros. The structure was formally handed over to Ukraine in July 2019.
