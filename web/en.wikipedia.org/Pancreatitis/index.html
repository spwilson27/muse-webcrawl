Pancreatitis is a condition characterised by inflammation of the pancreas. The pancreas is a large organ behind the stomach that produces digestive enzymes and the hormones insulin and glucagon. Pancreatitis occurs acutely — beginning suddenly and lasting a few days — or chronically, developing over many years. Damage occurs when digestive enzymes are activated prematurely inside the pancreas and begin to digest the gland itself.

Signs and symptoms

The main symptom of acute pancreatitis is severe upper abdominal pain, often radiating to the back, accompanied by nausea and vomiting. In severe disease, systemic inflammation can cause fever, low blood pressure, respiratory failure and multi-organ dysfunction. Chronic pancreatitis causes recurrent pain, weight loss, fatty stools (steatorrhoea) from enzyme insufficiency, and eventually diabetes as the islet cells are destroyed.

Causes

Worldwide, the two most common causes of acute pancreatitis are gallstones migrating into the pancreatic duct and heavy alcohol use; other causes include very high blood triglycerides, certain drugs, trauma, infections such as mumps, and genetic mutations such as in PRSS1 and CFTR. Chronic pancreatitis is most often due to prolonged alcohol use, but may also be autoimmune or hereditary.

Diagnosis and treatment

Diagnosis rests on the characteristic pain together with blood levels of amylase or lipase more than three times the upper limit of normal, or imaging findings on CT or MRI. Treatment of acute pancreatitis is supportive — aggressive intravenous fluids, pain relief and early enteral nutrition — while the underlying cause, such as gallstones, is addressed. Severe necrotising pancreatitis may require endoscopic or surgical drainage of collections. Chronic pancreatitis is managed with alcohol abstinence, enzyme replacement and pain control.
