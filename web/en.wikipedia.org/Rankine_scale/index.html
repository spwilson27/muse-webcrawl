Rankine scale

The Rankine scale is an absolute scale of thermodynamic temperature named after the Glasgow University engineer and physicist William John Macquorn Rankine, who proposed it in 1859. Its symbol is °R (or °Ra, to distinguish from the Réaumur and Rømer scales).

Definition

Like the kelvin, the Rankine scale starts at absolute zero, so 0 °R is absolute zero. However, its degree interval is the same size as a Fahrenheit degree rather than a Celsius degree. A temperature of −459.67 °F corresponds exactly to 0 °R. Conversion formulas:

  • °R = °F + 459.67
  • °R = K × 9/5
  • K = °R × 5/9

Usage

The Rankine scale was historically used in some fields of engineering in the United States, particularly in thermodynamics work expressed in imperial units. The US National Institute of Standards and Technology discourages its use. The freezing point of water is 491.67 °R and the boiling point 671.67 °R.
