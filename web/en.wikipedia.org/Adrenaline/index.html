Adrenaline

Adrenaline, also called epinephrine, is a hormone and neurotransmitter secreted mainly by the adrenal medulla. It is central to the fight-or-flight response of the sympathetic nervous system.

Effects

Adrenaline increases heart rate and contractility, dilates airways and pupils, constricts many blood vessels, and promotes glycogen breakdown and lipolysis, mobilising energy for action.

Medical use

As a drug, adrenaline is the first-line treatment for anaphylaxis, is used in cardiac arrest resuscitation, and is added to local anaesthetics to prolong their effect and reduce bleeding.

Discovery

It was among the first hormones identified, isolated around 1900-1901 and synthesised soon afterwards. See also Adrenal gland, Noradrenaline and Anaphylaxis.
