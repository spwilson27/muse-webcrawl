Surround sound

A 5.1 surround-sound speaker layout: centre, front left/right, surround left/right, and a subwoofer.

Surround sound is a technique for enriching the fidelity and depth of sound reproduction by using multiple audio channels from speakers arranged around the listener. Its first application was in movie theatres; it is now common in home cinema systems, television, video games, and music production.

History

Early experiments included Walt Disney's Fantasound system for Fantasia (1940) and the multichannel sound of Cinerama and CinemaScope in the 1950s. Dolby Stereo (1975) brought practical four-channel matrixed surround to cinemas, and Dolby Surround and Pro Logic carried it into homes in the 1980s. Digital formats — Dolby Digital (AC-3) and DTS — arrived in the 1990s, providing discrete channels on LaserDisc, DVD, and cinema prints.

Channel configurations

Configurations are denoted by the number of full-range channels followed by the number of low-frequency effects (LFE) channels: 5.1 consists of front left, centre, front right, two surrounds, and a subwoofer; 7.1 adds two rear surrounds. Object-based systems such as Dolby Atmos and DTS:X add height channels and position audio objects in three-dimensional space rather than assigning them to fixed channels.

Media and encoding

Surround mixes are delivered via matrix encoding (deriving channels from a stereo pair), discrete digital streams on Blu-ray and streaming services, and broadcast standards. Binaural rendering can simulate surround over headphones using head-related transfer functions.
