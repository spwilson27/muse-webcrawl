Non-Resident Indian and Overseas Citizen of India

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

A Non-Resident Indian (NRI) is an Indian citizen who resides outside India for employment, business, or other purposes indicating an intention to stay abroad for an uncertain period. The term is used in Indian tax and banking law, including under the Income Tax Act, 1961 and the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 (FEMA), which classify individuals according to the number of days physically present in India during a financial year.

An Overseas Citizen of India (OCI) is a foreign citizen of Indian origin who is registered under the Citizenship Act, 1955 and is entitled to a multiple-entry, lifelong visa for visiting India, along with parity with NRIs in most financial and economic fields. OCI status is not Indian citizenship, and cardholders cannot vote, hold constitutional office, or buy agricultural land in India.

History

Large-scale Indian emigration dates from the colonial era, when indentured labourers were transported to Fiji, Mauritius, the Caribbean, Malaya, and East and South Africa. Post-independence waves included professionals and students moving to the United States, United Kingdom, Canada, and Australia, and blue-collar workers moving to the Gulf states of the Middle East from the 1970s.

Legal framework

The Ministry of External Affairs, through the Protector General of Emigrants, oversees emigration clearance for workers travelling to notified countries. Remittances from NRIs and other members of the Indian diaspora are a major source of foreign exchange for India, which is among the world's largest recipients of remittances.
