Dual space

A vector space and its dual space of linear functionals

In mathematics, any vector space V has a corresponding dual vector space (or simply dual space) consisting of all linear forms on V, together with the vector space structure of pointwise addition and scalar multiplication. The dual space is denoted V* or V′ and its elements are also called covectors or one-forms.

Algebraic dual

For a finite-dimensional space, the dual has the same dimension as V, and a basis of V determines a dual basis of functionals that pick out coordinates. There is a canonical evaluation map from V into its double dual V**, which for finite-dimensional spaces is an isomorphism; this identification underlies the distinction between vectors and covectors in geometry and physics.

Continuous dual

For a normed or topological vector space, the continuous dual space consists of the continuous (equivalently, bounded) linear functionals. Examples include the identification of the dual of Lp with Lq for conjugate exponents, and the dual of a Hilbert space with itself via the Riesz representation theorem. Reflexive spaces are those for which the canonical map into the double dual is surjective.

Applications

Dual spaces appear throughout functional analysis, differential geometry (cotangent spaces), and physics, where momenta and wave vectors live naturally in dual spaces.
