São Paulo

São Paulo is the capital of the state of São Paulo in southeastern Brazil and the most populous city in Brazil and the Southern Hemisphere, with around 11.5 million inhabitants in the municipality and more than 22 million in its metropolitan area. It is Brazil's financial, commercial, and cultural centre, and one of the largest cities in the world.

History

São Paulo was founded on 25 January 1554 by Jesuit missionaries, including Manuel da Nóbrega and José de Anchieta, who established the mission of São Paulo dos Campos de Piratininga on a plateau between the Tamanduateí and Anhangabaú rivers. For centuries a poor frontier outpost, it became the launching point of the bandeirantes, expeditions that ranged across the interior seeking enslaved people and precious metals.

The coffee boom of the late 19th century transformed the city. Railroads linked the coffee-growing interior to the port of Santos, and mass immigration — especially from Italy, Portugal, Spain, Japan, Germany, and the Middle East — supplied labour and built the city's famously diverse culture. Industrialisation in the 20th century made São Paulo Brazil's economic engine, and rapid growth produced both gleaming financial districts along Avenida Paulista and vast peripheral districts.

Economy and culture

São Paulo generates roughly a tenth of Brazil's GDP and hosts the São Paulo Stock Exchange (B3), the headquarters of most major Brazilian banks and corporations, and Latin America's largest helicopter fleet. It is a leading cultural centre, home to the São Paulo Museum of Art (MASP), the São Paulo Biennial, the Ibirapuera Park complex designed by Oscar Niemeyer, and major universities including the University of São Paulo. The city hosts the Brazilian Grand Prix at Interlagos and one of the world's largest LGBT Pride parades.

Geography and climate

The city sits on a plateau at about 760 metres elevation in the Atlantic Plateau, some 70 km from the Atlantic coast. It has a humid subtropical climate with mild winters and warm, rainy summers. The Tietê and Pinheiros rivers cross the city; centuries of canalisation and pollution have been the target of long-running cleanup programmes.
