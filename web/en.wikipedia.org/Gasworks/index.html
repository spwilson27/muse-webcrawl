Gasworks

A gasworks (also called a gas plant or gas house) is an industrial plant for the manufacture of flammable gas, principally coal gas ("town gas"), from coal. Gasworks were a characteristic feature of industrial cities from the early 19th century until the mid-20th century, when manufactured gas was displaced by natural gas.

Process

In a gasworks, coal was heated in sealed retorts in the absence of air (carbonisation), producing coal gas together with coke, coal tar, and ammoniacal liquor. The raw gas was cooled, scrubbed to remove tar and ammonia, and purified of hydrogen sulfide before storage in large telescoping holders — the gasholders (gasometers) that dominated urban skylines. The gas was distributed through mains for street lighting, domestic lighting, cooking, and heating.

History and legacy

The first public gasworks were built in London in the 1810s, following the pioneering work of William Murdoch and the chartering of the Gas Light and Coke Company in 1812. Gas lighting spread rapidly through the industrialised world. The conversion of Britain and other countries to natural gas in the 1960s and 1970s made gasworks obsolete. Many former sites were contaminated by tar and chemical wastes and have required remediation; a few iconic gasholders have been preserved, some converted into housing, parks, and arts venues, as at Bromley-by-Bow and King's Cross in London and Gas Works Park in Seattle.
