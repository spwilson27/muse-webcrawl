Merry Pranksters

The Merry Pranksters were a loosely organized group associated with the American novelist Ken Kesey. In 1964, they undertook a cross-country road trip in a psychedelically painted school bus named Furthur, traveling from La Honda, California, to the New York World's Fair and the publication party for Kesey's novel Sometimes a Great Notion. The trip, and the Pranksters' acid-fuelled happenings that followed, became a founding myth of the 1960s psychedelic counterculture.

The bus trip

The driver was Neal Cassady, the Beat generation icon who had been Dean Moriarty in Jack Kerouac's On the Road and Carlo Marx in Allen Ginsberg's works. The Pranksters filmed and recorded the journey extensively, intending to make a movie — the footage was only edited and released decades later as Magic Trip (2011).

The Acid Tests

Back in California, the Pranksters staged the Acid Tests (1965–1966), multimedia parties where attendees took LSD, still legal at the time, amid light shows and music by the Warlocks, soon renamed the Grateful Dead. Owsley Stanley supplied much of the LSD. Tom Wolfe's 1968 book The Electric Kool-Aid Acid Test immortalized the group and popularized the slogan "You're either on the bus or off the bus."
