Jack W. Szostak

Jack William Szostak
Born: 9 November 1952, London, England
Fields: Genetics, biochemistry
Awards: Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine (2009)

Jack William Szostak (born November 9, 1952) is a Canadian-American biologist of Polish-British descent, Professor of Genetics at Harvard Medical School and investigator at Massachusetts General Hospital. He shared the 2009 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine with Elizabeth Blackburn and Carol W. Greider for the discovery of how chromosomes are protected by telomeres and the enzyme telomerase.

Early life and education

Szostak was born in London and grew up in Canada. He earned his BSc from McGill University at age 19 and his PhD in biochemistry from Cornell University in 1977, under Ray Wu. He established his laboratory at Harvard Medical School in 1979.

Research

Early in his career Szostak made key contributions to understanding recombination and chromosome stability in yeast, constructing the first artificial chromosome. His work with Blackburn showed that a unique DNA sequence in telomeres protects chromosomes from degradation, leading to the discovery of telomerase. He later turned to in vitro evolution, isolating functional RNAs and aptamers from random sequences, and to the origin of life: his laboratory studies protocells — model membrane vesicles capable of growth, division and non-enzymatic RNA copying — as a path toward understanding how the first cells arose.

Awards

Besides the Nobel Prize, Szostak has received the Lasker Award and the Dr A. H. Heineken Prize, and is a member of the National Academy of Sciences.
