In Greek mythology, Selene (Ancient Greek: Σελήνη, "Moon") is the goddess and personification of the Moon. She is the daughter of the Titans Hyperion and Theia, and sister of the sun god Helios and the dawn goddess Eos. Selene drives her moon chariot across the heavens each night, drawn by a pair of horses or oxen.

Mythology

Selene is best known for her love of the mortal shepherd-prince Endymion, whom Zeus granted eternal sleep so that he would remain forever young. In the myth, Selene descends nightly to visit the sleeping Endymion in his cave on Mount Latmus in Caria, and bore him fifty daughters in some accounts. The story was a favourite subject of later art and poetry.

Like other celestial deities, Selene was also associated with magic and witchcraft; Thessalian witches were said to be able to "draw down the moon." In later antiquity her worship was largely absorbed into that of Artemis and Hecate, with whom she was frequently identified.

Iconography

In art, Selene is typically shown as a beautiful woman with a crescent moon on her brow or rising from her shoulders, sometimes wearing a veil that billows in an arc above her head. Scenes of Selene and Endymion appear on Roman sarcophagi, where the myth symbolised hopes of a blessed afterlife.

Legacy

The element selenium is named after Selene. Her Roman counterpart is Luna, and her name gives rise to terms such as "selenology," the study of the Moon's geology.
