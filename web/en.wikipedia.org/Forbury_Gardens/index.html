Forbury Gardens

Forbury Gardens is a public park in the centre of Reading, Berkshire, England, occupying the site of the outer court of Reading Abbey. The gardens lie on the Forbury, a hill that formed part of the abbey precinct and was the scene of fighting during the English Civil War. The site adjoins the ruins of the abbey church and the Abbey Gateway, and is a short walk from Reading's main shopping streets and railway station.

History

The Forbury was used for fairs and gatherings for centuries and served as a defensive position in the siege of Reading in 1643. After the dissolution of the abbey, the land passed through various owners. In 1854 the Reading Corporation purchased the site, and the Forbury Gardens were laid out as public pleasure gardens, opening in 1856. A bandstand, carriage drives, and planting were added, and in 1886 the gardens gained their dominant feature: the Maiwand Lion.

Maiwand Lion

The Maiwand Lion is a large cast-iron statue of a lion, 31 feet (about 9.5 m) long, commemorating the officers and men of the Royal Berkshire Regiment who died at the Battle of Maiwand in Afghanistan in 1880, during the Second Anglo-Afghan War. Sculpted by George Blackall Simonds and cast at his foundry in Reading, it was unveiled in December 1886. The statue is one of the world's largest cast-iron statues and has become a symbol of Reading; it is often mistakenly said that its legs are anatomically incorrect, a persistent local urban legend.

Features

The gardens contain formal flowerbeds, lawns, a bandstand used for summer concerts, refreshment facilities, and a replica of the Bayeux Tapestry among the displays of the adjoining Museum of Reading in the Town Hall. The Victoria Gates and the surrounding walls and the abbey ruins are listed structures, and the gardens themselves are included on the Register of Historic Parks and Gardens.

2020 attack

On 20 June 2020, three people were stabbed to death and others injured in an attack in Forbury Gardens. The attacker, Khairi Saadallah, was convicted of three murders and sentenced to a whole-life order in January 2021. A memorial to the victims was subsequently established in the gardens.
