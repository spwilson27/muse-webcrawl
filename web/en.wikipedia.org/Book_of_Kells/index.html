The Book of Kells (Latin: Codex Cenannensis; Irish: Leabhar Cheanannais; Dublin, Trinity College Library, MS A. I. (58), sometimes known as the Book of Columba) is an illuminated manuscript Gospel book in Latin, containing the four Gospels of the New Testament together with various prefatory texts and tables. It was created in a Columban monastery in either Scotland, England, or Ireland and may have had contributions from various Columban institutions from each of these areas. It is generally believed to have been created c. 800 AD.

The manuscript takes its name from the Abbey of Kells, County Meath, which was its home for centuries. It is a masterwork of Western calligraphy and represents the pinnacle of Insular illumination. It is also widely regarded as one of Ireland's finest national treasures. The illustrations and ornamentation of the Book of Kells surpass those of other Insular Gospel books in extravagance and complexity, combining traditional Christian iconography with the swirling motifs characteristic of Insular art.

Contents and decoration

The manuscript today comprises 340 leaves (680 pages). The text is accompanied by many full-page miniatures, including depictions of the Virgin and Child, portraits of the evangelists, and the arrest and temptation of Christ. The most famous page is the Chi Rho monogram page (folio 34r), which opens the Gospel of Matthew with a riot of interlace, spirals, and hidden figures. Ten decorated pages survive, along with lavishly ornamented initials on nearly every page of text.

History

The book was probably begun on Iona, the island monastery founded by St Columba, and taken to Kells for safety after Viking raids in the early 9th century. The Annals of Ulster record it as "the most precious object of the western world" when it was stolen in 1007 and later recovered buried under a sod of turf, its jeweled cover lost. In 1661 it was donated to Trinity College Dublin, where it has remained; since the 19th century it has been on public display, and a major exhibition draws hundreds of thousands of visitors each year.
