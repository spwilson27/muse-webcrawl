Intangible cultural heritage

Intangible cultural heritage (ICH) is the practices, representations, expressions, knowledge and skills — as well as the instruments, objects, artefacts and cultural spaces associated therewith — that communities, groups and, in some cases, individuals recognize as part of their cultural heritage. It is sometimes called "living cultural heritage", and is manifested in oral traditions, performing arts, social practices and rituals, knowledge about nature and the universe, and traditional craftsmanship.

Definition

The definition is drawn from UNESCO's Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage, adopted in Paris in 2003 and in force from 2006. Unlike tangible heritage — monuments, buildings and objects — intangible heritage is transmitted from generation to generation and is constantly recreated by communities in response to their environment and history, providing them with a sense of identity and continuity.

Domains

The Convention identifies five broad domains:

  • Oral traditions and expressions, including language as a vehicle
  • Performing arts (music, dance, theatre)
  • Social practices, rituals and festive events
  • Knowledge and practices concerning nature and the universe
  • Traditional craftsmanship

UNESCO lists

UNESCO maintains the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity, the List of Intangible Cultural Heritage in Need of Urgent Safeguarding, and a Register of Good Safeguarding Practices. Before 2008, elements were proclaimed "Masterpieces of the Oral and Intangible Heritage of Humanity" (2001, 2003 and 2005 proclamations); these were incorporated into the Representative List. Well-known inscribed elements include the Mediterranean diet, tango, flamenco, Chinese calligraphy, yoga, traditional Mexican cuisine, and the art of falconry. Hundreds of elements from well over 100 countries have been inscribed.

Safeguarding

Safeguarding measures include documentation, research, preservation, promotion, transmission through formal and non-formal education, and revitalization. The Convention stresses the participation of the communities themselves in identifying and managing their heritage.
