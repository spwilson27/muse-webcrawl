The Model 500 telephone is a desk telephone manufactured by the Western Electric Company from 1951 to 1986 and still widely found in service decades later. Designed with the industrial design firm of Henry Dreyfuss, it became the standard telephone issued to customers of the Bell System in North America during the mid-20th century and an enduring icon of American industrial design.

Design and development

The 500 series replaced the 1937 Model 302. Development began in 1945 with Dreyfuss's firm working from human-factors measurements of more than 2,000 faces to shape the handset, which combined the receiver and transmitter into a single comfortable unit. The new telephone featured a modern thermoplastic housing, a number card held in the centre of the rotary dial, an adjustable-volume bell, and a coiled handset cord. The internal network circuitry improved voice quality and sidetone control over the 302.

Production and variations

Production began in 1950–51; most units were black, but from 1954 coloured sets in up to a dozen colours were offered. Variants included wall models (554), key telephones, and later Touchtone descendants (1500 with ten buttons, then the 2500 with twelve). Western Electric refurbished 500 sets for decades; after the 1984 Bell System divestiture, production of the design continued under licence and by other makers. Many millions were manufactured, and the phone remains instantly recognisable worldwide.
