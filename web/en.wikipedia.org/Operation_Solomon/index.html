Operation Solomon

Operation Solomon
Date: 24–25 May 1991
Location: Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, to Israel
Result: 14,325 Ethiopian Jews airlifted in 36 hours

Operation Solomon (Hebrew: מִבְצָע שְׁלֹמֹה, Mivtza Shlomo) was a covert Israeli military operation in 1991 to airlift 14,325 Ethiopian Jews to Israel in 36 hours. On 24 and 25 May 1991, a fleet of Israeli aircraft — including El Al airliners stripped of seats and Israeli Air Force C-130 transports — made 41 flights from Addis Ababa as the Ethiopian government of Mengistu Haile Mariam collapsed and rebel forces closed on the capital.

Background

The Beta Israel community had been immigrating to Israel since the late 1970s, notably in Operation Moses (1984–85) via Sudan. As Ethiopia's civil war reached its climax, Israel negotiated permission for a final mass departure, reportedly accompanied by a payment of about $35 million to the Ethiopian government. Evacuees were permitted to take almost no luggage, and many arrived in Israel barefoot.

Records

One El Al Boeing 747 carried 1,088 passengers — more than twice its designed capacity and, with babies born during the flights, a world record for the most passengers on a single aircraft. The airlift remains one of the largest emergency evacuations of its kind.
