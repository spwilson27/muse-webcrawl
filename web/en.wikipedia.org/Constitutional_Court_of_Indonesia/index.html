Constitutional Court of Indonesia

The Constitutional Court of the Republic of Indonesia (Mahkamah Konstitusi) is one of the apex courts in Indonesia's judicial system, established on 13 August 2003 under the amended 1945 Constitution as part of the post-Suharto reformasi reforms. It sits in Jakarta and consists of nine justices: three nominated by the President, three by the Supreme Court, and three by the People's Representative Council (DPR).

Jurisdiction

The Court has the authority to review laws against the Constitution, decide disputes over the authority of state institutions, rule on the dissolution of political parties, resolve disputes concerning general election results, and give a decision on the DPR's opinion that the President or Vice President has committed a legal violation warranting impeachment.

Notable history

Its first chief justice was Jimly Asshiddiqie. The Court has issued landmark rulings on electoral rules, blasphemy law, and natural-resource legislation, and it has also faced controversy, including the 2013 arrest of Chief Justice Akil Mochtar for bribery in election-dispute cases.
