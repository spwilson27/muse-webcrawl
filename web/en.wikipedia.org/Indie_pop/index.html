Indie pop

Indie pop favours melody, jangling guitars and a do-it-yourself ethos.

Indie pop is a music genre and subculture that combines the guitar-driven sound of indie rock with pop's emphasis on melody, hooks and lighter themes. It emerged in the United Kingdom in the early 1980s, drawing on the independent-label ethos of post-punk while embracing the melodic songwriting of 1960s pop and groups such as the Velvet Underground and the Byrds.

Characteristics

Compared with indie rock, indie pop tends to be softer, more melodic and less abrasive, with jangling guitar tones, upbeat tempos and frequently wistful or romantic lyrics. A do-it-yourself aesthetic — self-released records, fanzines, small independent labels — was central to the scene's identity.

History

Scottish bands such as Orange Juice and Aztec Camera, and labels like Postcard Records of Scotland, pioneered the sound. The 1986 NME cassette compilation C86 became a touchstone for the British indie pop scene, featuring bands such as Primal Scream, The Pastels and The Shop Assistants. Labels including Sarah Records in Bristol cultivated a distinctive, gentle "twee" strain. In the United States, indie pop developed around labels such as K Records in Olympia, Washington, home to Beat Happening, and intersected with the riot grrrl movement. Later acts such as Belle and Sebastian, Camera Obscura and The Magnetic Fields carried the genre into the 2000s, while Swedish and Japanese scenes contributed their own variants.
