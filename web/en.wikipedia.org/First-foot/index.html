First-foot

The first-foot is the first person to enter a home after midnight on New Year's Day, a tradition central to Scottish Hogmanay and also observed in northern England, the Isle of Man and parts of Ireland. The first-footer is believed to set the household's luck for the coming year.

Customs

Traditionally the ideal first-foot is a tall, dark-haired man — a preference sometimes linked to avoiding fair-haired "Viking" visitors. The visitor brings symbolic gifts: coal for warmth, shortbread or black bun for food, salt, a coin for prosperity, and whisky for cheer, and is in turn offered food and drink.

Related traditions

Similar customs exist elsewhere, such as the Greek podariko on New Year's Day. In Scotland, first-footing rounds of visiting neighbours may continue into the early hours of 1 January.
