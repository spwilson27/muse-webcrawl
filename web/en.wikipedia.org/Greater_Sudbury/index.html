Greater Sudbury (French: Grand Sudbury) is a city in Northern Ontario, Canada, and the largest city in the region by population. It was formed on 1 January 2001 by the amalgamation of the former city of Sudbury with six suburban municipalities of the Regional Municipality of Sudbury.

Geography

The city sits on the Canadian Shield within the Sudbury Basin, a 1.85-billion-year-old meteorite impact structure whose rich nickel–copper deposits made the area one of the world's great mining camps. With an area of about 3,200 square kilometres, Greater Sudbury is the largest city in Ontario by land area, and contains more than 330 lakes, including Lake Wanapitei, one of the largest lakes entirely within a single city in the world.

History

The area is the traditional territory of the Atikameksheng Anishnawbek. Sudbury was founded in 1883 during the construction of the Canadian Pacific Railway, when rich mineral deposits were exposed in railway cuttings; copper was soon followed by nickel, in demand for armour plate. Mining drew waves of immigrants — Finns, Ukrainians, Italians, Poles and French Canadians — giving the city a strongly multicultural and significantly francophone character.

Decades of smelting scarred the landscape, but a landmark regreening programme begun in 1978 has planted millions of trees and restored much of the environment. The Inco Superstack, completed in 1972 and for decades the tallest smokestack in the world, became a symbol both of industry and of pollution control.

Economy and culture

Mining and its supply and services sector remain the economic core, supplemented by health care, education (Laurentian University and Cambrian College), government services and tourism. Science North and its IMAX theatre, and the Big Nickel roadside attraction, are well-known landmarks. Health Sciences North serves as the regional hospital, and SNOLAB, a deep underground physics laboratory in the Vale Creighton Mine, hosts internationally significant neutrino and dark-matter experiments.
