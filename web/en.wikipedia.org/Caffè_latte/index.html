Caffè latte

TypeCoffee drink
OriginItaly
IngredientsEspresso, steamed milk

Caffè latte (Italian: [kafˈfɛ lˈlatte]), often shortened to just latte in English, is a coffee drink made with espresso and steamed milk, traditionally served in a glass. Unlike a cappuccino, which contains equal parts espresso, steamed milk, and milk froth, a latte contains much more steamed milk than foam.

The term comes from the Italian caffellatte, meaning "milk coffee". In Italy the drink is typically consumed at breakfast at home, brewed on a stovetop moka pot and served in a bowl; the café version became popular internationally through American coffee chains in the late 20th century.

Preparation

A latte is made by pouring steamed milk over one or two shots of espresso. It is topped with a thin layer of milk foam, which baristas may decorate with free-poured patterns known as latte art, such as hearts and rosettas. A standard latte uses a ratio of roughly one part espresso to three to five parts steamed milk.

Variations

Variations include the flat white, which uses micro-foamed milk and a higher proportion of coffee; the caffè mocha, which adds chocolate; and iced lattes served over ice. In many countries flavored syrups such as vanilla, caramel, or hazelnut are added. Outside Italy, "latte" by itself always refers to the coffee drink, whereas in Italian ordering a latte alone would bring a glass of milk.
