The United States Department of Homeland Security (DHS) is the US federal executive department responsible for public security, roughly comparable to the interior or home ministries of other countries. Its stated missions involve anti-terrorism, border security, immigration and customs, cybersecurity, and disaster prevention and management. It was created by the Homeland Security Act of 2002 in response to the September 11 attacks, and is the largest reorganisation of the federal government since the creation of the Department of Defense in 1947.

Creation

The department combined 22 disparate federal agencies and programmes, including the United States Coast Guard, the Secret Service, the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA), the Customs Service, and the Immigration and Naturalization Service (whose functions were split among new agencies). Tom Ridge, the first Secretary, led the department's stand-up beginning in March 2003.

Components

  • U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) and U.S. Border Patrol
  • U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE)
  • U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS)
  • Transportation Security Administration (TSA)
  • Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA)
  • United States Coast Guard (during peacetime)
  • United States Secret Service
  • Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA)

Criticism

The department has drawn criticism over the years regarding overlapping mandates, management challenges (it was long listed by the Government Accountability Office as a "high-risk" agency), immigration enforcement practices, and the balance between security and civil liberties. Its response to Hurricane Katrina in 2005, through FEMA, led to major reforms in disaster management law.
