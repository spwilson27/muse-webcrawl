Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter

Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO)
Operator: NASA Goddard
Launch: 18 June 2009
Status: Operational (extended mission)
Target: The Moon

The Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) is a NASA robotic spacecraft currently orbiting the Moon in an eccentric polar mapping orbit. Its data have been used to create detailed three-dimensional maps of the lunar surface and to characterise radiation, temperature and potential resources for future exploration.

Launch and mission

LRO was launched on 18 June 2009 together with the LCROSS impactor aboard an Atlas V rocket. It entered lunar orbit on 23 June 2009 and, after commissioning, began its one-year exploration mission from a low polar orbit, later extended repeatedly.

Instruments

The payload includes the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera (LROC), the Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter (LOLA), the Diviner radiometer, the Lyman-Alpha Mapping Project, a neutron detector (LEND) and the CRaTER cosmic-ray telescope.

Results

LRO has imaged Apollo landing sites at metre-scale resolution, discovered evidence of recent lunar volcanism and tectonic activity, and measured the coldest known temperatures in the Solar System in shadowed polar craters.
