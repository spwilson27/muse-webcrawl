Real estate

Real estate is property consisting of land and the buildings on it, along with its natural resources such as crops, minerals, or water; immovable property of this nature; an interest vested in this item of real property; and, more generally, the business of buying, selling, or renting land, buildings, or housing. The term contrasts with personal property, which is movable.

Categories

Real estate is commonly divided into residential (houses, apartments, and other dwellings), commercial (offices, retail premises, hotels), industrial (warehouses, factories, logistics facilities), and land (undeveloped land, farms, and ranches). Property rights in real estate may include ownership (freehold), leasehold interests, easements, and other estates in land, regulated by national and local law and recorded in land registries.

Markets and industry

The real estate industry encompasses developers, brokers and agents, property managers, appraisers, lenders, and investors. Because property is immobile and heterogeneous, real estate markets are local and relatively illiquid compared with markets for financial securities. Real estate is a major store of household wealth and a significant sector of most economies; its cycles of boom and bust — such as the United States housing bubble whose collapse in 2007–2008 triggered the Great Recession — can have economy-wide consequences. Investors may access the sector directly or through vehicles such as real estate investment trusts (REITs).
