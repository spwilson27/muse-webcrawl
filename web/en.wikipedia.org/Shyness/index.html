Shyness

Shyness (also called diffidence) is the feeling of apprehension, lack of comfort, or awkwardness especially when a person is around other people. This commonly occurs in new situations or with unfamiliar people; a shy person may simply opt to avoid these situations. Although shyness can be a characteristic of people who have low self-esteem, the primary defining characteristic of shyness is a strong tendency to overestimate negative social evaluation.

Shyness can originate from genetic traits, the environment in which a person is raised, and personal experiences. Shyness can be a personality trait or can manifest at certain stages of development in children.

Causes

The cause of shyness is often considered to stem from a combination of hereditary traits and environmental influences. Various scientific studies have shown that shyness has a strong genetic component: identical twins are far more likely to both be shy than fraternal twins. Prenatal exposure to certain substances and stressful events during early childhood also contribute.

Environments with authoritarian parenting styles, overprotective parenting, or inconsistent parental reactions can foster shyness. Schools where children are pushed to conform and are not allowed to express themselves may also be causes.

Traits

In contrast to extraversion, people who are shy may have low social skill, or be socially withdrawn. They tend to be highly self-conscious and easily embarrassed. Shyness is distinct from social anxiety, although the two can co-occur; shyness is a temperament or trait, whereas social anxiety disorder is a diagnosable condition involving intense fear of social situations.

Prevalence

Some cultures, such as those in the United States, tend to view shyness negatively, whereas in some Asian cultures shyness is viewed more positively and may be associated with modesty and good manners. Rates of self-reported shyness vary by culture, with higher reported rates in collectivist cultures such as Japan and Taiwan.
