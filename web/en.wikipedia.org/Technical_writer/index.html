Technical writer

Technical writers produce manuals, help systems, and other documentation for technical products.

A technical writer is a professional communicator whose task is to transfer information between two or more parties through any medium that best facilitates the transfer. Technical writers research and create information products — such as user manuals, online help, API documentation, tutorials, white papers, and standard operating procedures — for a specific audience, most commonly the users and administrators of technical products.

Work and skills

Technical writers work with engineers, developers, and subject-matter experts to understand a product, then design and write content that is accurate, clear, and structured for retrieval. Modern practice emphasizes task-oriented writing, structured authoring (e.g. DITA and topic-based writing), single-source publishing, and content management systems, with output delivered as printed documents, websites, embedded help, and chatbots. Many technical writers also edit, create diagrams and videos, and test documentation against the product.

Field and organizations

The field grew rapidly during and after the Second World War with the expansion of military, industrial, and later software technology. The Society for Technical Communication (founded 1953) is a major professional association, and related titles include technical author, information developer, content designer, and documentation specialist. Technical writers are employed across software, manufacturing, aerospace, pharmaceuticals, finance, and government.
