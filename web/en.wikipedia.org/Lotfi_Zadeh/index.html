Lotfi Aliasker Zadeh (4 February 1921 – 6 September 2017) was a mathematician, computer scientist and professor of electrical engineering and computer sciences at the University of California, Berkeley, best known for proposing fuzzy mathematics, including the concept of fuzzy sets, in his landmark 1965 paper "Fuzzy Sets".

Life and career

Born in Baku to an Iranian Azerbaijani father and a Russian Jewish mother, Zadeh moved to Tehran in 1931 and studied electrical engineering at the University of Tehran before emigrating to the United States in 1944. He earned a master's degree at MIT and a PhD from Columbia University, joined Berkeley in 1959, and chaired its electrical engineering department from 1963 to 1968.

Work

With Charles Desoer, Zadeh co-authored an influential textbook on linear system theory. His fuzzy set theory — allowing degrees of membership between 0 and 1 rather than binary membership — was initially controversial but found wide practical application in control systems, consumer appliances and artificial intelligence. He later developed fuzzy logic, computing with words, and the theory of perceptions. Among his honours were the IEEE Medal of Honor (1995) and membership of several national academies.
