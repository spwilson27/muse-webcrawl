Non-Resident Indian

Non-Resident Indian (NRI) is a legal and fiscal status under Indian law for an Indian citizen who resides outside India for employment, business, or other purposes indicating an intention to stay abroad for an uncertain period. The term is closely related to the broader category of the Indian diaspora, which also includes Persons of Indian Origin (PIOs) and Overseas Citizens of India (OCIs) who hold foreign citizenship.

Legal and tax status

Under the Foreign Exchange Management Act and the Income Tax Act, residential status depends chiefly on the number of days spent in India during a financial year. NRIs may hold special bank accounts in India (NRE, NRO, and FCNR accounts) and invest under rules specific to non-residents, but they cannot vote in Indian elections unless present in their constituency, and agricultural land purchases are restricted.

Diaspora

India has one of the world's largest diasporas, with major NRI populations in the Gulf states, the United States, Canada, the United Kingdom, and Southeast Asia. Remittances from NRIs and other overseas Indians exceed US$100 billion annually, the highest of any country, and are economically significant for many Indian states, notably Kerala.
