Magneto (Marvel Comics)

Alter ego: Max Eisenhardt (born Erik Lehnsherr)
Created by: Stan Lee and Jack Kirby
First appearance: The X-Men #1 (1963)
Abilities: Generation and control of magnetic fields

Magneto is a character appearing in American comic books published by Marvel Comics, commonly in association with the X-Men. Created by writer Stan Lee and artist/co-writer Jack Kirby, he debuted as the X-Men's first adversary and evolved into one of Marvel's most complex antiheroes — a powerful mutant able to generate and control magnetic fields, in the tradition of the fictional powers discussed under magic in fantasy, here explained as mutant biology.

Fictional biography

Born Max Eisenhardt, a German Jew, he survived the Holocaust, including imprisonment at Auschwitz — an experience that shaped his conviction that mutants must never again be victims. As Magneto he founded the Brotherhood of Mutants to fight for mutant supremacy, placing him in ideological conflict with his old friend Charles Xavier, who sought peaceful coexistence. Over decades of stories he has alternated between villain, reluctant ally, and outright member of the X-Men, and fathered the twins Quicksilver and the Scarlet Witch in earlier continuity.

In other media

Magneto was portrayed by Ian McKellen in the original X-Men film trilogy and later films, and by Michael Fassbender as a younger man in the prequel series beginning with X-Men: First Class (2011). The character consistently ranks among the greatest comic book villains of all time in critics' polls.
