Charles Edgar Duryea (December 15, 1861 – September 28, 1938) was an American engineer and, with his younger brother J. Frank Duryea, a pioneer of the American automobile industry. Charles designed the engine of the brothers' first gasoline-powered car, successfully tested in Springfield, Massachusetts, in September 1893, and in 1895 co-founded the Duryea Motor Wagon Company — generally regarded as the first company formed in the United States to manufacture and sell gasoline automobiles.

The first Duryea car

Working from drawings and an engine design by Charles and mechanical work by Frank, the brothers converted a used horse-drawn buggy with a 4-horsepower, single-cylinder gasoline engine. Frank drove it in its first public test on September 21, 1893 (often dated to September 20 by Springfield newspapers). In 1895 a Duryea Motor Wagon won the Chicago Times-Herald race, America's first automobile race, driven by Frank through snow over 54 miles.

Later career

The brothers feuded bitterly over credit for the invention, and Charles spent much of his life promoting his own role, sometimes at the expense of accuracy; historians generally credit Charles with the design leadership and Frank with key engineering contributions. Charles went on to build cars under his own name in Pennsylvania, promoted the Duryea Power Company, and in 1914 introduced the "Buggyaut," a high-wheeled, inexpensive car. He continued experimenting with vehicles and gas lighting into old age and died in Philadelphia in 1938.
