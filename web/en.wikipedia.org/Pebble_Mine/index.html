Pebble Mine

The Pebble Mine is the common name of a proposed copper-gold-molybdenum mining project in the Bristol Bay region of Southwest Alaska, near Lake Iliamna and Lake Clark. The Pebble deposit is one of the largest known undeveloped copper-gold resources in the world, but the project has been fiercely contested because the site drains into the headwaters of the Bristol Bay salmon fishery.

Pebble Mine
Location: Bristol Bay Borough, Alaska
Commodities: Copper, gold, molybdenum
Developer: Pebble Limited Partnership

Controversy

Opponents—including commercial and subsistence fishers, Alaska Native groups and environmental organizations—argue that mine waste could devastate the world's largest sockeye salmon run. Supporters cite jobs and mineral revenues. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency studied the watershed and moved to restrict development under the Clean Water Act.

Permitting history

In 2020 the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers denied the project's key federal permit, finding the proposed mitigation inadequate; the EPA subsequently issued a final determination in 2023 prohibiting the use of certain waters as disposal sites, effectively blocking the mine as proposed. Litigation and political contest over the project have continued.
