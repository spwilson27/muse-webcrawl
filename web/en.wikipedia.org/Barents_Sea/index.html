Barents Sea

The Barents Sea is a marginal sea of the Arctic Ocean, located off the northern coasts of Norway and Russia. It is bounded by Svalbard to the north-west, Franz Josef Land and Novaya Zemlya to the north-east and east, and the Kola Peninsula to the south.

Name

The sea is named after the Dutch navigator Willem Barentsz, who explored it in the 1590s while searching for the Northeast Passage; it was earlier known to Russians as the Murmean Sea.

Oceanography and ecology

Relatively warm Atlantic water from the North Atlantic Current keeps the southern Barents ice-free year-round. The sea is one of the world's most productive fishing grounds, especially for cod, managed jointly by Norway and Russia since 1976.

Resources

The Barents contains major oil and gas fields, including Norway's Snøhvit and Goliat and Russia's giant Shtokman gas field. A 2010 treaty settled the forty-year maritime delimitation dispute between Norway and Russia.
