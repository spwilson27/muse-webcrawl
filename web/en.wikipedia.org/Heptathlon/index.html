Heptathlon

Events7 (track and field)
Olympic debut1984 (women)
World record7,291 points — Jackie Joyner-Kersee (1988)

A heptathlon is a combined track and field event consisting of seven disciplines contested over two consecutive days. It is the premier women's multi-event competition in outdoor athletics, held at the Olympic Games since 1984, and replaced the pentathlon as the standard women's combined event. A men's indoor heptathlon is contested at the World Indoor Championships.

Events

The women's outdoor heptathlon comprises, on day one, the 100 metres hurdles, high jump, shot put, and 200 metres; and on day two, the long jump, javelin throw, and 800 metres. Performances in each event are converted to points using scoring tables, and the athlete with the highest total wins. The men's indoor version uses the 60 metres, long jump, shot put, and high jump on day one, and the 60 metres hurdles, pole vault, and 1000 metres on day two.

History

American Jackie Joyner-Kersee dominated the event in the 1980s and 1990s, winning Olympic gold in 1988 and 1992 and setting the world record of 7,291 points at the 1988 Seoul Olympics — a mark that still stands. Other notable champions include Carolina Klüft of Sweden and Jessica Ennis-Hill of Great Britain, winner at London 2012, and Nafissatou Thiam of Belgium, a three-time Olympic champion.

Scoring

Scoring tables, revised periodically by World Athletics, award points based on time, distance, or height, calibrated so that world-class performances in each discipline earn roughly comparable totals.
