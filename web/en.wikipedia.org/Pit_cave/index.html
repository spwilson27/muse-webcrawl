Pit cave

A pit cave, also called a vertical cave or pothole, is a type of natural cave that consists primarily of one or more deep vertical shafts rather than horizontal passages. Such caves typically form in limestone and other soluble rock, where water percolating down joints dissolves a vertical passage, sometimes connecting with horizontal cave systems at depth.

Exploration

Because pit caves cannot be walked into, they are explored using single rope technique (SRT): cavers rappel down the shaft and ascend the rope to exit. Deep pits demand careful rigging, and explorers must guard against hazards such as falling rocks, flooding and bad air near the bottom. Notable examples include the Sotano de las Golondrinas in Mexico, a vast open-air pit, and very deep systems such as Vrtoglavica in Slovenia, which contains a single vertical drop of about 603 metres — among the deepest known single pitches in the world.

Ecology and significance

Pit caves provide specialized habitats and can act as natural traps, preserving the bones of animals that fall in; they are therefore of interest to biologists and paleontologists. Some pits, like the cenotes of Mexico's Yucatán Peninsula, form where the roof of a water-filled cave has collapsed.
