Brass

Brass is valued for its gold-like appearance and workability

Brass is an alloy of copper and zinc, in proportions that can be varied to achieve different colours and mechanical, electrical, and chemical properties. It has been known and used since ancient times, produced historically by cementation of copper with calamine (zinc ore), and from the early modern period by direct alloying with metallic zinc.

Properties

Brass is malleable, casts well, resists corrosion (including from salt water, depending on composition), and has low friction, making it suitable for gears, bearings, locks, valves, plumbing fittings, and ammunition casings. Its acoustic properties make it the standard material for brass instruments such as trumpets and trombones. Some brasses have antimicrobial properties. Lead is sometimes added to improve machinability, though lead-free brasses are increasingly used for drinking-water applications.

Types

Common varieties include admiralty brass, naval brass, cartridge brass (70/30), muntz metal, and gilding metal. Increasing zinc content raises strength but reduces corrosion resistance; at high zinc levels the alloy becomes harder and more brittle.
