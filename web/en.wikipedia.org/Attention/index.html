Attention


The dichotic listening task, a classic attention experiment

Attention is the cognitive process of selectively concentrating on one aspect of the environment while ignoring other things. The psychologist William James famously wrote in 1890: "Everyone knows what attention is. It is the taking possession by the mind, in clear and vivid form, of one out of what seem several simultaneously possible objects or trains of thought." Attention has also been described as the allocation of limited cognitive processing resources, and it is studied in psychology, neuroscience, education, and human–computer interaction.

Types of attention

Researchers distinguish several forms: selective attention (focusing on one stimulus amid distractors, as in the cocktail-party effect, where one conversation is followed among many), divided attention (sharing resources between tasks, with performance costs), sustained attention or vigilance (maintaining focus over time), and executive attention (resolving conflict between responses). Attention may be directed overtly, by moving the eyes, or covertly, without eye movement.

Theories and findings

Classic experiments by Colin Cherry (dichotic listening) and Donald Broadbent (filter theory) in the 1950s launched modern attention research; Anne Treisman's feature-integration theory and attenuation model refined the picture, and later work mapped attention onto brain networks involving frontal and parietal cortex, including distinct alerting, orienting and executive-control networks described by Michael Posner. Phenomena such as inattentional blindness — failing to notice a clearly visible but unexpected object when attention is engaged elsewhere — demonstrate how selective the system is.

Clinical and applied aspects

Attention is impaired in conditions including ADHD, schizophrenia, and after brain injury, and it is a key target in rehabilitation. In the modern economy, attention is also treated as a scarce resource competed for by media and technology — the "attention economy".
