The Battle of Atlanta was fought on July 22, 1864, during the Atlanta Campaign of the American Civil War. Union forces under Major General William Tecumseh Sherman defeated the Confederate Army of Tennessee, commanded by General John Bell Hood, in a major battle just southeast of the city of Atlanta, Georgia.

The battle

Hood, newly appointed to command, sent Lieutenant General William J. Hardee's corps on a long night march around the Union left flank to attack the Army of the Tennessee under Major General James B. McPherson. The assault struck in the early afternoon and briefly overran parts of the Union line, but federal reserves restored the position. McPherson was killed by Confederate skirmishers while riding to the front — one of the highest-ranking Union officers lost in the war. Fighting around Bald Hill (Leggett's Hill) continued until nightfall, with Confederate attacks repulsed at heavy cost.

Aftermath

Despite the Union victory, Atlanta did not fall immediately; Sherman tightened his siege and cut the city's rail lines over the following weeks, forcing Hood to evacuate on September 1, 1864. The capture of Atlanta was a major boost to Northern morale and to President Abraham Lincoln's re-election campaign.
