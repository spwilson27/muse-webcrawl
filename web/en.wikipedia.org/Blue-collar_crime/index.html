Blue-collar crime

Blue-collar crime is a term used in criminology to refer to crimes committed by individuals from a lower social class, in contrast to white-collar crime, which is associated with higher-status occupational offenders. Blue-collar crimes typically include offences such as theft, burglary, vandalism, assault and drug-related offences.

Characteristics

Unlike white-collar offences, blue-collar crimes are often more visible, are more likely to be reported to and recorded by police, and tend to be prosecuted through the ordinary criminal courts. Criminologists note that official statistics may over-represent blue-collar crime because of differences in policing and detection.

Criticism of the term

Some sociologists argue the blue-collar/white-collar distinction oversimplifies the relationship between class and offending and reflects the priorities of the criminal justice system as much as the actual distribution of harmful behaviour.
