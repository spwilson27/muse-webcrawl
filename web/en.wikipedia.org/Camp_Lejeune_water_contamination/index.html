Camp Lejeune water contamination
LocationMarine Corps Base Camp Lejeune, North Carolina
Period1953–1987
ContaminantsTCE, PCE, benzene, vinyl chloride

From 1953 to 1987, drinking water at Marine Corps Base Camp Lejeune in North Carolina was contaminated with volatile organic compounds, including trichloroethylene (TCE), perchloroethylene (PCE), benzene and vinyl chloride, at concentrations far above safe levels. As many as one million Marines, family members and civilian workers may have been exposed.

Sources and discovery

The contamination came principally from an off-base dry-cleaning business whose waste PCE entered the Tarawa Terrace water system, and from leaking underground fuel storage tanks and industrial activities affecting the Hadnot Point system. The problem was identified in the early 1980s, and the most contaminated wells were shut down by 1985–1987.

Health effects and response

Studies by the Agency for Toxic Substances and Disease Registry linked the exposures to increased risks of several cancers — including kidney, liver and bladder cancer and non-Hodgkin lymphoma — as well as birth defects and other conditions. The Honoring America's Veterans and Caring for Camp Lejeune Families Act of 2012 provided health care for affected veterans and families, and the Camp Lejeune Justice Act, enacted as part of the PACT Act of 2022, allowed victims to file compensation claims against the federal government.
