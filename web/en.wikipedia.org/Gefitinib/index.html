Gefitinib, sold under the brand name Iressa among others, is a medication used to treat certain types of non-small-cell lung cancer (NSCLC). It is a small-molecule inhibitor of the epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) tyrosine kinase, and was among the first targeted cancer therapies of its kind.

Mechanism of action

Gefitinib binds reversibly to the ATP-binding site of the intracellular tyrosine kinase domain of EGFR, blocking downstream signalling that drives cell proliferation and survival. Tumors bearing activating mutations in the EGFR gene — most commonly exon 19 deletions and the L858R point mutation — are particularly dependent on this signalling and respond dramatically to the drug.

Clinical history

Gefitinib was approved in Japan in 2002 and received accelerated approval in the United States in 2003 for advanced NSCLC after chemotherapy. Subsequent trials in unselected patients failed to show a survival benefit, and its use was restricted, but the landmark IPASS trial (2009) demonstrated that patients whose tumors carried EGFR mutations responded far better to gefitinib than to chemotherapy, establishing the principle of mutation-guided therapy in lung cancer. It was re-approved in the US in 2015 as first-line treatment for EGFR-mutant NSCLC.

Side effects and resistance

Common adverse effects include an acne-like skin rash and diarrhoea, both related to EGFR inhibition in normal tissues; a rare but serious risk is interstitial lung disease. Most responding tumors eventually develop resistance, frequently through the secondary EGFR T790M mutation — a mechanism later targeted by the third-generation inhibitor osimertinib.
