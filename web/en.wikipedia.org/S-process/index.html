S-process

The s-process or slow neutron capture process is a series of reactions in nuclear astrophysics that occur in stars, particularly asymptotic giant branch (AGB) stars. The s-process is responsible for the creation (nucleosynthesis) of approximately half of the atomic nuclei heavier than iron.

Mechanism

In the s-process, atomic nuclei capture neutrons one at a time, slowly enough that when a radioactive isotope is produced it usually undergoes beta decay before capturing another neutron. This keeps the synthesis path close to the valley of beta stability. The process can proceed for thousands of years in the interiors of AGB stars, where thermal pulses periodically mix neutron-rich material to the surface.

The neutron sources are principally the reactions 13C(α,n)16O and 22Ne(α,n)25Mg, which operate during different phases of AGB star evolution.

Products and evidence

The s-process builds elements from iron-peak seeds up through strontium, barium, and lead, terminating near bismuth, where alpha decay recycles the heaviest nuclei. Its signatures include characteristic abundance peaks at nuclei with magic neutron numbers and the presence of technetium — an element with no stable isotopes — in the spectra of red giant stars, direct evidence that nucleosynthesis occurs there now.

The s-process contrasts with the r-process (rapid neutron capture), which occurs in explosive environments such as supernovae and neutron star mergers and produces the other half of the heavy-element abundances.
