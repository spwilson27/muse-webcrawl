NeXTSTEP is a discontinued object-oriented, multitasking operating system developed by NeXT for its workstations and later ported to other architectures. First released in 1989 alongside the NeXT Computer, NeXTSTEP combined a Mach-based kernel with BSD Unix, a PostScript-based windowing system (Display PostScript), and a powerful object-oriented application framework written in Objective-C.

Features

NeXTSTEP was widely praised for its elegant graphical interface — including the dock, shelf and three-dimensional window styling — and for its development tools, notably Interface Builder, which let programmers construct interfaces graphically and connect them to code. Its application frameworks (the "kits") and the Objective-C language made rapid development of custom business applications a major selling point. The system also shipped with advanced bundled software such as a digital library (the Digital Librarian and bundled reference works), Mathematica on early machines, and sophisticated mail and text systems.

History

Version 1.0 shipped in September 1989. Version 3.0 (1992) added features such as CD-ROM support, and in 1993, after NeXT left the hardware business, NeXTSTEP was released for Intel PCs, Sun SPARC and HP PA-RISC systems. In 1994 NeXT and Sun published the vendor-neutral OPENSTEP specification; NeXT's implementation, OPENSTEP 4.0, followed in 1996.

Legacy

After Apple acquired NeXT in 1997, NeXTSTEP's descendants — through Apple's Rhapsody project and the Carbon/Cocoa transition — became Mac OS X (2001), and through it iOS, watchOS and tvOS. Modern macOS still carries NeXTSTEP's fingerprints: the Cocoa frameworks, classes prefixed "NS" (for NeXTSTEP), and the Objective-C runtime.
