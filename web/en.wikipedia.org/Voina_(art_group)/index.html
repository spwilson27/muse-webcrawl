Voina (art group)

Voina (Russian: Война́, "War") is a Russian street-art collective known for provocative and politically charged performance art. Founded in 2005 by Oleg Vorotnikov and Natalia Sokol, the group has staged dozens of actions satirizing Russian political power, corruption and the church–state alliance, in the tradition of Moscow Actionism. Former members of the collective went on to form the protest punk band Pussy Riot.

Notable actions

Voina's actions are usually performed without permission in public spaces and documented on video. In June 2010 members painted a giant phallus on the roadway of St. Petersburg's Liteyny Bridge; as the drawbridge rose in front of the FSB headquarters it created a widely circulated image, and the work — titled Dick Captured by the FSB — unexpectedly received the state-backed Innovation Prize for contemporary art in 2011. In another action, "Palace Revolution" (2010), members overturned police cars to protest police corruption, leading to the arrest and imprisonment of Vorotnikov and Leonid Nikolayev on hooliganism charges; the British street artist Banksy raised money for their legal defence. In 2011 a member threw live cockroaches into a courtroom during a trial.

Prosecution and aftermath

Russian authorities repeatedly prosecuted members, and in 2011 an Interpol notice was issued for Vorotnikov, who went into exile in Europe. The group split as activists including Nadezhda Tolokonnikova and Yekaterina Samutsevich pursued the project that became Pussy Riot, whose 2012 "punk prayer" in Moscow's Cathedral of Christ the Saviour led to internationally condemned prison sentences. Voina's mixture of street action, media spectacle and direct confrontation with the state made it one of the most influential protest-art groups of its era.
