Alpha particle

An alpha particle, also called an alpha ray or α-particle, is a type of ionizing radiation consisting of two protons and two neutrons bound together into a particle identical to a helium-4 nucleus (⁴₂He²⁺). It is emitted in alpha decay of heavy radionuclides at speeds typically around 4–7% of the speed of light, with energies of roughly 4–9 MeV.

Alpha particle
Composition: 2 protons, 2 neutrons
Charge: +2e; Mass: 4.0015 u
Symbol: α, ⁴₂He²⁺

Properties and hazards

Alpha particles are highly ionizing but have very low penetration: they are stopped by a sheet of paper or the dead outer layer of skin. They are hazardous mainly when alpha-emitting material is inhaled, ingested, or enters wounds — as with radon progeny in lungs or polonium-210 poisoning.

History

Ernest Rutherford classified alpha and beta rays in 1899 and, with Hans Geiger, showed in 1908 that alpha particles are helium nuclei. Alpha scattering in the Geiger–Marsden experiment (1909) led Rutherford to propose the nuclear model of the atom in 1911.

Sources and uses

Sources include uranium, radium, plutonium-239, and americium-241, the latter used in smoke detectors. Cosmic rays are about 10% alpha particles. Alpha particles are also produced in ternary fission and in particle accelerators.
