Rosin

A piece of pine rosin

Rosin, also called colophony or Greek pitch, is a solid resin obtained from conifers, principally pines. It consists mainly of resin acids, particularly abietic acid. Rosin is produced by heating fresh liquid oleoresin collected from tapped trees to vapourise the volatile terpene component (turpentine), leaving the molten rosin, which solidifies into a translucent, amber, brittle mass. Chemically modified rosins are also made from wood stumps (wood rosin) and from tall oil, a by-product of paper pulping.

Uses

Rosin's best-known use is on the bows of string instruments—violins, violas, cellos and basses—where it provides the friction that allows the bow hair to grip the string and set it vibrating. Different grades are made for different instruments and climates, sometimes with additives such as beeswax or metallic particles.

Rosin is also applied by dancers to their shoes and to stage floors to prevent slipping, by gymnasts, weightlifters and baseball players to improve grip, and in powdered form by bull riders on their ropes and gloves. Industrially, rosin and its derivatives are used in adhesives, printing inks, varnishes, soaps, paper sizing, paints, chewing gum bases, and as a flux in soldering, where its mild acidity cleans metal surfaces. The name "colophony" derives from Colophon, an ancient Ionian city associated with its production.

Properties and safety

Rosin is brittle at room temperature but melts at modest heat; it is soluble in alcohol, ether and many oils but not in water. Its smoke and dust are recognised respiratory sensitisers—"colophony asthma" is a known occupational hazard in electronics soldering—and prolonged skin contact can cause allergic contact dermatitis. Grades are classified by colour, with the palest (water-white) grades commanding the highest prices.

Production

China is the world's largest producer of gum rosin, tapping species such as Masson pine, with significant production also in Indonesia, Brazil, Vietnam and Portugal. Rosin was historically a strategic naval store, used with tar and pitch for waterproofing wooden ships.
