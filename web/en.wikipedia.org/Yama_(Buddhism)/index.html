Yama (Buddhism)

TraditionBuddhism
DomainThe dead; the underworld
EquivalentsYanluo Wang (China); Enma (Japan)

Yama is a deity of death and the underworld in Buddhist cosmology, inherited from earlier Indian religion. In Buddhism he is regarded as a dharmapala and as the judge of the dead, presiding over the hell realms (Naraka) within the cycle of samsara.

Role in Buddhist cosmology

Unlike an eternal god of damnation, the Buddhist Yama is himself a being within samsara, subject to impermanence and rebirth. He is said to dwell in the Yama heaven or, in other traditions, to rule over the pretas and the hells, questioning the dead about their deeds before they are reborn according to their karma.

In East Asian Buddhism

In China, Yama became Yanluo Wang, chief of the Ten Kings of Hell, and in Japan he is Enma, often depicted as a stern judge with a register of the deeds of the dead. Stories of Yama feature prominently in sermons on karma and moral conduct.

Iconography

Yama is typically shown with a fierce expression, sometimes with the head of a bull or buffalo, holding a staff or noose, and riding a buffalo. In Tibetan Buddhism, Yamaraja appears as a wrathful protector, while Yama Dharmaraja is linked to practices associated with Yamantaka.
