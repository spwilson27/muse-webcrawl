Censer

A censer is a vessel made for burning incense or perfume in some solid form, producing a fragrant smoke. Censers have been used since antiquity in religious ceremonies and domestic settings across many cultures. They range from simple open bowls to elaborate covered containers suspended from chains, such as the thurible used in Christian liturgy.

Religious use

In Roman Catholic, Orthodox, and some Anglican and Lutheran worship, a thurible is swung by a thurifer to disperse incense smoke, symbolising prayer rising to heaven and the sanctification of people and objects. In Judaism, censers were used in the Temple in Jerusalem. Incense burners are also central to Buddhist, Hindu, Taoist and Shinto practice, where offerings of incense accompany prayer and meditation.

History

Ancient Egyptian, Greek and Roman censers survive in metal and ceramic. In China, hill censers (boshanlu) of the Han dynasty were crafted as miniature mountains. Mesoamerican cultures produced elaborate ceramic effigy censers. During the medieval period, metalwork censers became treasured works of art in both Christian Europe and the Islamic world.

Types

Censers may be stationary, hand-held, or suspended. The Botafumeiro of Santiago de Compostela Cathedral in Spain is a famous giant thurible swung across the transept by a team of men.
