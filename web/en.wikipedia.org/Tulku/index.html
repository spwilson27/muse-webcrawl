Tulku

A tulku is a recognised reincarnation of a previous Buddhist master

A tulku (Tibetan: སྤྲུལ་སྐུ་, Wylie: sprul sku) is a revered teacher in Tibetan Buddhism who has been reborn repeatedly, over the centuries, with the intention of continuing their work for the benefit of beings. The term is a translation of the Sanskrit nirmāṇakāya, the "emanation body" of a Buddha.

Recognition

When a tulku dies, a search is conducted to find the child who is his or her reincarnation. Traditional methods include interpreting dreams and omens, consulting oracles, and testing a candidate child's ability to recognise objects, places and people associated with the predecessor. Once recognised, the young tulku is enthroned and given an intensive monastic education.

History

The tulku system developed in Tibet from the 12th and 13th centuries. The first recognised lineage was that of the Karmapas, beginning with the recognition of Rangjung Dorje as the reincarnation of Karma Pakshi. The most famous tulku lineage is that of the Dalai Lamas, the spiritual leaders of the Gelug school, the fourteenth and current of whom is Tenzin Gyatso. Other prominent lineages include the Panchen Lamas and numerous lamas of all schools of Tibetan Buddhism.

In the modern world

The tulku tradition has spread beyond Tibet with the Tibetan diaspora and the growth of Tibetan Buddhism in the West, and a small number of Westerners have been recognised as tulkus. The system has also been a source of political controversy, particularly regarding the recognition of the Panchen Lama and the expected future recognition of the 15th Dalai Lama.
