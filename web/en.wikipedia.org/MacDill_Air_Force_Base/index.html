MacDill Air Force Base (IATA: MCF, ICAO: KMCF) is a United States Air Force installation located on the Interbay Peninsula in south Tampa, Florida, on the shore of Tampa Bay. It is home to the 6th Air Refueling Wing of Air Mobility Command, flying the KC-135 Stratotanker, and is notable as the headquarters of two major unified combatant commands: United States Central Command (CENTCOM) and United States Special Operations Command (SOCOM).

History

Construction began in 1939, and the base was formally activated in 1941 as MacDill Field, named for Colonel Leslie MacDill, an Army Air Corps officer killed in a 1938 aircraft accident. During the Second World War it trained bomber crews on aircraft including the B-17 Flying Fortress and B-26 Marauder; the base's wartime motto for the B-26, notorious for its landing speed, was "One a day in Tampa Bay." After the war it hosted Strategic Air Command bombers and, during the Cuban Missile Crisis, tactical fighter units on alert.

Resident commands

MacDill hosts more than two dozen tenant organizations. CENTCOM, established in 1983, directs US military operations in the Middle East, Central Asia, and parts of South Asia. SOCOM, established in 1987, oversees the special operations forces of all the services. Other tenants include the Joint Communications Support Element and a directorate of the Defense Intelligence Agency. The base population, including civilian employees and contractors, numbers in the tens of thousands.

Units and aircraft

The host 6th Air Refueling Wing operates KC-135R Stratotankers providing air-to-air refuelling in support of the resident commands and worldwide operations. In the 2010s and 2020s the wing was selected to receive the KC-46 Pegasus as the Air Force recapitalized its tanker fleet.

Geography

The base occupies roughly 5,600 acres on a peninsula roughly eight miles south of downtown Tampa, surrounded on three sides by Tampa Bay, with beaches and a marina among its recreational facilities.
