Reading

Reading a printed book

Reading is the process of taking in the sense or meaning of symbols, often letters or characters, by looking at or feeling them. For literate people, reading is a means of language acquisition, communication and sharing of information and ideas. Reading aloud to oneself or others is also possible. It is a complex cognitive process involving decoding symbols to derive meaning, and is a basic skill taught in primary education worldwide.

Process

Skilled reading combines word recognition (decoding print into sounds and known words) with language comprehension. Readers' eyes move in a series of fixations and rapid jumps called saccades, taking in groups of words at a time. Research in cognitive psychology and neuroscience has identified brain regions, including the visual word form area, specialised for rapid word recognition.

Instruction

Methods of teaching reading have been debated for decades, notably in the "reading wars" between phonics-based instruction, which teaches systematic letter–sound correspondences, and whole-language approaches emphasising meaning and context. Scientific reviews have generally found that systematic phonics benefits beginning readers, especially those at risk of difficulty; dyslexia and other reading disabilities affect a significant minority of learners.

History

Reading emerged with writing systems in the ancient world and for most of history was confined to small literate elites, often practised aloud. Silent reading became common in the medieval period, and mass literacy spread with printed books, public schooling and cheaper paper in the 19th and 20th centuries. In the digital era, much reading now takes place on screens, prompting research on how the medium affects comprehension and attention.
