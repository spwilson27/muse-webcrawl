Jacques Cartier

Jacques Cartier (1491 – 1557) was a French-Breton maritime explorer who made three voyages to North America for France, charting the Gulf of St. Lawrence and the St. Lawrence River and claiming the land he called Canada for the French crown.

Born31 December 1491, Saint-Malo, Brittany
Died1 September 1557, Saint-Malo

Voyages

Commissioned by Francis I to seek a route to Asia and lands rich in gold, Cartier first explored the Gulf of St. Lawrence in 1534, planting a cross at Gaspé. In 1535 he sailed up the St. Lawrence River to the Iroquoian settlements of Stadacona and Hochelaga (present-day Quebec City and Montreal). A third voyage in 1541–42 attempted a colony at Charlesbourg-Royal, which failed amid winter, scurvy and conflict.

Legacy

Cartier's reports introduced the name "Canada" — from the Iroquoian kanata, meaning village — and his charts guided later French exploration, though the supposed gold and diamonds he brought back proved to be quartz and iron pyrite.
