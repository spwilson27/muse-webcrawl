Lorentz factor

Lorentz factor γ
Definition: γ = 1 / √(1 − v²/c²)
Named after: Hendrik Lorentz
Field: Special relativity
[Image placeholder: Graph of the Lorentz factor approaching infinity as v approaches c]

The Lorentz factor is a quantity expressing how much measurements of time, length, and other physical properties change for an object while that object is moving. It appears in several expressions in special relativity and arises from the Lorentz transformations. It is named after the Dutch physicist Hendrik Lorentz.

Definition

γ = 1 / √(1 − β²), where β = v/c

Here v is the relative velocity of the object and c is the speed of light. At everyday speeds γ is indistinguishable from 1; as v approaches c, γ grows without limit.

Occurrence

The Lorentz factor governs time dilation (a moving clock ticks slower by the factor γ), length contraction (a moving object shortens along its direction of motion by γ), and the relativistic expressions for momentum and kinetic energy: p = γmv and Ek = (γ − 1)mc². The total energy of a particle is E = γmc².

Numerical values

At v = 0.5c, γ ≈ 1.155; at 0.9c, γ ≈ 2.294; at 0.99c, γ ≈ 7.089; at 0.999c, γ ≈ 22.37.
