Image sensor

Image sensor
[Image: A CMOS image sensor chip with its Bayer colour filter array]
FunctionConverts light into electrical signals
Main typesCCD, CMOS (active-pixel sensor)
ApplicationsDigital cameras, smartphones, scanners, telescopes

An image sensor is a sensor that detects and conveys information used to form an image. It converts the variable attenuation of light waves — as they pass through or reflect off objects — into electrical signals. Image sensors are used in electronic imaging devices of both analogue and digital types, including digital cameras, camera phones, medical imaging equipment, and night-vision devices.

Technologies

The two dominant technologies are the charge-coupled device (CCD), invented at Bell Labs in 1969 by Willard Boyle and George E. Smith, and the CMOS active-pixel sensor, which integrates readout and processing circuitry at each pixel or pixel column. CCDs long offered superior image quality and dominated professional cameras, but CMOS sensors offer lower power consumption, faster readout, and lower cost, and have displaced CCDs in nearly all consumer applications. A colour filter array, most commonly the Bayer pattern, is laid over the pixels so that each records one primary colour, and the full image is reconstructed by demosaicing.

Characteristics

Important properties include pixel size and count ("megapixels"), sensor format (from tiny phone sensors to "full frame" 36 × 24 mm and medium format), sensitivity (ISO range), dynamic range, and readout speed, which determines rolling-shutter distortion. Larger sensors generally collect more light per pixel and perform better in low light. Modern developments include backside-illuminated (BSI) designs, stacked sensors with integrated memory, and global shutters for machine vision.
