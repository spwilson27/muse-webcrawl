Volta River

The Volta River is the main river system in the West African country of Ghana. It flows south from the uplands of Burkina Faso to the Atlantic Ocean at the Gulf of Guinea, draining a basin of roughly 400,000 km² shared by six countries.

Course and tributaries

The system is formed by three principal headstreams named for the colour of their waters: the Black Volta (Mouhoun), the White Volta (Nakambé) and the Red Volta (Nazinon), all rising in Burkina Faso and converging in northern Ghana. Below the confluence the river was dammed at Akosombo in the 1960s, creating Lake Volta, one of the largest artificial lakes in the world by surface area (about 8,500 km²). The Oti River joins from Togo, and the Afram River drains the Kwahu Plateau. From the dam the Volta flows about 100 km south to its mouth near Ada.

Importance

The Akosombo Dam's hydroelectric plant long supplied most of Ghana's electricity and exports power to neighbouring countries; a second dam at Kpong lies downstream. Lake Volta supports a major inland fishery and ferry transport but also submerged about 15,000 homes at its filling. The river gave its name to the former French colony of Upper Volta (now Burkina Faso) and to Ghana's Volta Region.
