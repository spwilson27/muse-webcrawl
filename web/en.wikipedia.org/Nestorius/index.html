Nestorius

Bornc. 386, Germanicia, Roman Syria
Diedc. 450, Egypt
OfficeArchbishop of Constantinople (428–431)
Known forThe Nestorian controversy; Council of Ephesus

Nestorius (c. 386 – c. 450) was the Archbishop of Constantinople from 428 to 431. His teachings — including his objection to the title Theotokos ("God-bearer") for the Virgin Mary — ignited the Nestorian controversy, one of the defining Christological disputes of the early Church. He was condemned and deposed by the Council of Ephesus in 431.

Life

Born at Germanicia in Roman Syria and educated at Antioch, Nestorius gained a reputation as a preacher and was appointed Archbishop of Constantinople by Emperor Theodosius II in 428. His chaplain's sermons against the popular title Theotokos, and Nestorius's own preference for Christotokos ("Christ-bearer"), provoked fierce opposition led by Cyril of Alexandria.

Controversy and exile

The dispute concerned the unity of Christ's divine and human natures. Cyril accused Nestorius of dividing Christ into two persons. At the Council of Ephesus in 431 Nestorius was condemned and deposed. He was eventually exiled to Egypt, where he died around 450. His later work, the Bazaar of Heraclides, rediscovered in the 19th century, has led some scholars to question whether his views were as heterodox as his opponents claimed.

Legacy

The Church of the East, which revered Nestorius and refused to accept the Ephesus decision, was long labelled "Nestorian." Modern ecumenical scholarship and the 1994 Common Christological Declaration between the Catholic Church and the Assyrian Church of the East have softened these divisions.
