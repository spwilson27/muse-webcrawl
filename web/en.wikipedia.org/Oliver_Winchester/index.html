Oliver Winchester

Oliver Fisher Winchester (November 30, 1810 – December 11, 1880) was an American businessman and politician who founded the Winchester Repeating Arms Company. His firm produced the famous Winchester repeating rifles that became emblematic of the American frontier.

Early life and business

Winchester was born in Boston, Massachusetts, and worked as a carpenter and dry-goods merchant before manufacturing men's shirts in New Haven, Connecticut. The success of his shirt business gave him capital to invest in firearms manufacturing. In 1855 he became involved with the Volcanic Repeating Arms Company, and in 1857 he reorganised the business as the New Haven Arms Company.

Winchester Repeating Arms

Under Winchester's direction, Benjamin Tyler Henry developed the Henry rifle of 1860, the first practical repeating rifle, used in the American Civil War. In 1866 the company was reorganised as the Winchester Repeating Arms Company, and the Model 1866 "Yellow Boy" was followed by the Model 1873 — later marketed as "The Gun That Won the West" — and the Model 1876 and 1894 rifles. Winchester served as president of the company until his death in 1880.

Politics and legacy

Winchester was active in Connecticut politics and served as lieutenant governor of the state in 1866–67. His widow, Sarah Winchester, is famous in folklore for the Winchester Mystery House in San Jose, California, though the popular tale that she built it ceaselessly to appease the ghosts of rifle victims is disputed.
