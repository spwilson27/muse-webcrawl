Huldrych Zwingli

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Huldrych (or Ulrich) Zwingli (1 January 1484 – 11 October 1531) was a leader of the Reformation in Switzerland, born during a time of emerging Swiss patriotism and increasing criticism of the Swiss mercenary system. He founded the Swiss Reformed Church and is, after Martin Luther and John Calvin, the third most important figure of the Protestant Reformation.

Born: 1 January 1484, Wildhaus, Swiss Confederacy
Died: 11 October 1531 (aged 47), Kappel am Albis
Occupation: Priest, theologian, reformer
Movement: Swiss Reformation; Reformed theology

Reform in Zurich

Appointed people’s priest at the Grossmünster in Zurich in 1519, Zwingli preached continuously through Scripture and attacked practices he held to be unbiblical, including indulgences, clerical celibacy and the mercenary trade. The city council authorised his reforms after public disputations in 1523; images were removed from churches, the Mass was replaced by a simple communion service in 1525, and Zurich became the centre of a civic Reformation. His Sixty-Seven Articles (1523) set out his programme.

Controversy and death

Zwingli’s purely memorialist view of the Lord’s Supper brought him into sharp conflict with Luther at the Marburg Colloquy of 1529, permanently dividing the Lutheran and Reformed movements. He died in battle at Kappel in 1531, fighting as a chaplain with Zurich’s army against the Catholic Forest Cantons. His successor in Zurich was Heinrich Bullinger.
