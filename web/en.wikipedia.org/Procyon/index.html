Procyon

Procyon is the brightest star in the constellation of Canis Minor and usually the eighth-brightest star in the night sky, with an apparent visual magnitude of 0.34. It has the Bayer designation α Canis Minoris. The name comes from the Ancient Greek Προκύων (Prokyōn), meaning "before the dog", because it precedes Sirius, the "Dog Star", across the sky as the Earth rotates.

The system

Procyon is a binary star system approximately 11.46 light-years from the Sun, making it one of the Sun's nearer stellar neighbours. The primary, Procyon A, is a yellow-white F-type main-sequence star about twice the Sun's radius and seven times its luminosity, some 1.5 billion years old and beginning to evolve off the main sequence. Its companion, Procyon B, is a faint white dwarf discovered visually in 1896 — its existence had been predicted decades earlier from the wobble in Procyon A's proper motion, one of the first hints of a body like Sirius B.

Observation

Procyon forms one vertex of the Winter Triangle asterism, together with Sirius and Betelgeuse. It is best seen in late winter evenings in the Northern Hemisphere.
