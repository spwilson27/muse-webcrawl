American Guide Series

The American Guide Series was a group of books and pamphlets published in 1937–1949 by the Federal Writers' Project, a Depression-era program under the Works Progress Administration. The series produced guidebooks for every state, along with guides for cities, highways, and specialized topics, employing thousands of unemployed writers, editors, and researchers.

Content

Each state guide typically combined essays on history, geology, folklore, and culture with detailed descriptions of cities and towns and a set of automobile tours. Contributors included John Cheever, Ralph Ellison, Richard Wright, Studs Terkel, and Zora Neale Hurston, who collected folklore for the Florida guide.

Legacy

The guides are regarded as a landmark of American documentary writing and a rich record of American life in the 1930s; many were reprinted for decades and influenced later travel literature.
