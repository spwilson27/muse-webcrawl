Tilaka

In Hinduism, the tilaka is a mark worn usually on the forehead, at the ajna chakra, and sometimes other parts of the body such as the neck, arm or chest. It is applied with sacred materials such as sandalwood paste, sacred ash (vibhuti), kumkum or turmeric, and its shape and substance indicate the wearer's tradition or sect.

Forms

Vaishnavites typically wear a U-shaped mark made of white clay or sandalwood paste, sometimes with a central red or yellow line. Shaivites wear tripundra, three horizontal lines of ash, often with a central dot. Shaktas commonly wear a dot of red kumkum. Tilaka styles can also mark initiation, festival participation or daily worship.

Significance

The tilaka signifies devotion, serves as a blessing and protection, and is believed to cool and sanctify the wearer's forehead. It is applied at religious ceremonies, temple visits and festivals throughout the Hindu world.
