Box Hill, Surrey

Box Hill is a summit of the North Downs in Surrey, England, about 19 miles (31 km) southwest of London, overlooking the River Mole near Dorking. Rising to about 224 metres, it is named for the box trees on its steep chalk slopes and is protected by the National Trust within the Surrey Hills Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty.

Features

The hill offers panoramic views, chalk grassland rich in orchids and butterflies, and ancient box and yew woodland. The Salomons Memorial viewpoint commemorates Leopold Salomons, who bought part of the hill in 1914 and gave it to the nation. A zig-zag road climbs the hill past the Burford Bridge Hotel.

Cycling

Box Hill became famous to a new generation as a key climb of the road cycling events at the London 2012 Olympic Games, when the men's race completed nine laps of a circuit including the hill; it remains one of Britain's most ridden amateur climbs.

History

The hill appears in literature from the 18th century; Jane Austen set the picnic episode of Emma at Box Hill, and it was a favourite subject of artists including J. M. W. Turner.
