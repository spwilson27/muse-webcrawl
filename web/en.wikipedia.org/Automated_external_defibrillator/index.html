Automated external defibrillator
AbbreviationAED
PurposeTreatment of life-threatening cardiac arrhythmias
TreatsVentricular fibrillation; pulseless ventricular tachycardia

An automated external defibrillator (AED) is a portable electronic device that automatically diagnoses life-threatening cardiac arrhythmias — principally ventricular fibrillation and pulseless ventricular tachycardia — and treats them by delivering an electric shock (defibrillation) that can restore an effective heart rhythm.

Use

AEDs are designed for use by lay responders. The rescuer attaches adhesive electrode pads to the casualty's chest; the device analyses the rhythm, advises whether a shock is indicated, and gives voice and visual prompts. Semi-automatic models require the user to press a button to deliver the shock, while fully automatic models deliver it after a warning.

Public access defibrillation

AEDs are installed in airports, stations, schools, sports venues and workplaces, often marked with the ILCOR heart-and-lightning symbol. Early defibrillation combined with cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) greatly improves survival from out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, and many jurisdictions protect lay users through Good Samaritan legislation.
