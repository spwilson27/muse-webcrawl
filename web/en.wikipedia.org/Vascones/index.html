Vascones

The Vascones were a pre-Roman tribe of the Iberian Peninsula, occupying roughly the territory of the modern Spanish region of Navarre and parts of neighbouring areas in the western Pyrenees and the upper Ebro valley. They are generally regarded as the ancestors of the modern Basques, and their name is the origin of the word "Basque" (via Latin Vascones and Gascon forms).

History

The Vascones are mentioned by Roman authors including Strabo, Pliny the Elder and Ptolemy. Unlike some neighbouring peoples, they were not recorded as resisting Roman expansion and appear to have become allies of Rome, possibly against the Celtiberians. Their main settlement was Pompaelo (modern Pamplona), said by Strabo to have been founded by Pompey, and other towns included Oeaso and Calagurris (the latter sometimes attributed to them).

Language and identity

The Vascones are believed to have spoken a form of the Aquitanian language, closely related to or ancestral to the Basque language — one of the few pre-Indo-European languages to survive in Europe. Inscriptions and personal names from the region support this connection. Romanisation was relatively light in the northern, mountainous part of their territory, helping the language survive.
