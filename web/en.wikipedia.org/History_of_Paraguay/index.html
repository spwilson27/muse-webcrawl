History of Paraguay

The history of Paraguay spans from the pre-Columbian era, when the region was inhabited by Guaraní-speaking peoples, through Spanish colonization in the 16th century, independence in 1811, and the country's turbulent modern development. Landlocked between Argentina, Brazil, and Bolivia, Paraguay's history has been shaped by isolation, authoritarian rulers, and two devastating wars.

Pre-colonial and colonial era

The Guaraní were semi-nomadic peoples who practiced agriculture, particularly cassava and maize cultivation. Spanish explorer Juan de Salazar founded Asunción on 15 August 1537, which became one of the earliest colonial settlements in the Río de la Plata region. Jesuit missions (reducciones) among the Guaraní from the early 17th century created a distinctive colonial society until the expulsion of the Jesuits in 1767.

Independence and the Francia era

Paraguay declared independence from Spain on 14–15 May 1811. José Gaspar Rodríguez de Francia ruled as dictator from 1814 until his death in 1840, pursuing a policy of strict isolation and self-sufficiency. His successor Carlos Antonio López modernized the country before his son Francisco Solano López led Paraguay into the catastrophic War of the Triple Alliance (1864–1870) against Brazil, Argentina, and Uruguay, which killed a large share of Paraguay's population.

Chaco War and Stroessner

Paraguay defeated Bolivia in the Chaco War (1932–1935), securing most of the disputed Gran Chaco. After decades of instability, General Alfredo Stroessner seized power in 1954 and ruled until 1989, one of the longest dictatorships in South American history. His fall opened the way to democratic transition.

Modern era

Since 1989 Paraguay has held regular elections, though the Colorado Party has dominated politics. Fernando Lugo's election in 2008 ended 61 years of Colorado rule, but he was removed in a controversial impeachment in 2012. Agriculture, especially soybeans, and hydroelectric power from the Itaipu Dam remain central to the economy.
