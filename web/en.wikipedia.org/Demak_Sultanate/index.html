Demak Sultanate

The Demak Sultanate was a Javanese Muslim state located on Java's north coast in Indonesia, at the site of the modern city of Demak. A port fief to the Hindu-Buddhist Majapahit empire that eventually usurped the role of Majapahit as the premier state in the Indonesian archipelago, Demak was the first Muslim sultanate on Java. It was founded toward the end of the 15th century, probably by Raden Patah, a Javanese prince said to be the son of the Majapahit king Brawijaya V and a Chinese princess.

The Great Mosque of Demak, one of the oldest mosques in Java

History

Demak grew in importance as a trading port on the north coast of Central Java, benefiting from the decline of Majapahit in the late 15th century. Under Raden Patah, Demak expanded its influence over the surrounding ports of the pasisir (north coast), including Jepara, Tuban, and Gresik. The sultanate reached its zenith under Sultan Trenggana (reigned c. 1521–1546), who extended Demak's power across eastern Java, conquered the remaining Hindu regions of the interior, and mounted expeditions against the Portuguese at Malacca.

Decline

After Trenggana's death in 1546 during a campaign in East Java, a succession struggle broke out. Power shifted among rival princes, and Demak's dominance waned. By the late 16th century, the inland agrarian kingdom of Pajang and then Mataram eclipsed Demak. In 1596 Demak was attacked and largely destroyed, ending its role as a major power.

Legacy

Demak is remembered in Javanese tradition as the cradle of Islam on Java. The Great Mosque of Demak (Masjid Agung Demak), traditionally dated to 1479, is among the oldest mosques in Indonesia and is associated with the Wali Sanga, the nine saints credited with spreading Islam in Java.
