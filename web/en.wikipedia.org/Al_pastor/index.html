Al pastor

Course: Main course
Place of origin: Mexico
Region: Mexico City
Main ingredients: Pork, pineapple, dried chiles, achiote

Al pastor (from Spanish, "shepherd style"), also known as tacos al pastor, is a taco made with spit-grilled pork. The cooking method is based on the lamb shawarma brought by Lebanese immigrants to Mexico; al pastor features a flavor palate that uses traditional Mexican marinades and spices. It is a popular street food that has spread to the United States.

History

During the 19th century, variations of a vertically grilled meat dish, now known by several names, started to spread throughout the Ottoman Empire. A wave of Lebanese immigrants arrived in Mexico in the late 19th and early 20th centuries, introducing the region's lamb shawarma. In the 1920s, pork replaced lamb in the dish, and vendors in Puebla began serving tacos árabes on flour tortillas. The modern taco al pastor, served on corn tortillas with onion, cilantro, and pineapple, became established in Mexico City by the 1960s.

Preparation

Pork is marinated in a combination of dried chiles, spices, pineapple, and typically achiote paste, then slowly cooked on a vertical rotisserie called a trompo ("spinning top"). As the meat cooks, the outside crisps; the taquero slices off thin strips with a large knife, catching them in a small corn tortilla. A slice of the pineapple that often crowns the trompo may be flicked onto the taco. Tacos al pastor are commonly served with onion, chopped cilantro, salsa, and a squeeze of lime.

Variations

In northern Mexico and in the Mexican diaspora, similar dishes include tacos de trompo and tacos de adobada. In the United States, al pastor seasoning blends are sold commercially, and the style has been adapted to chicken and even cauliflower versions.
