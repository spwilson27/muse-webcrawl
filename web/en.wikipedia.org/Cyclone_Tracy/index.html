Severe Tropical Cyclone Tracy was a tropical cyclone that devastated the city of Darwin, Northern Territory, Australia, on Christmas Day 1974. Although small in size, Tracy was one of the most destructive cyclones in Australian history, destroying more than 70 percent of Darwin's buildings.

Impact

Tracy crossed the coast near Darwin in the early hours of 25 December with gusts estimated above 200 km/h. Sixty-six people were killed, including many aboard vessels at sea. Roughly 41,000 of the city's 47,000 residents were left homeless, prompting one of the largest air evacuations in Australian history, with over 35,000 people flown or driven south in the following days.

Aftermath

Darwin was rebuilt to much stricter cyclone-resistant building codes, which became a model for construction across northern Australia. Tracy remains a defining event in the city's history and in Australian disaster management, commemorated in memorials and in the song "Santa Never Made It into Darwin".
