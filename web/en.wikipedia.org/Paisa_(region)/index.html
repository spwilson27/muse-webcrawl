The Paisa region is a geographical and cultural region of northwestern Colombia, comprising the department of Antioquia and the coffee-growing departments of Caldas, Quindío and Risaralda, with extensions into northern Valle del Cauca and northwestern Tolima. Its inhabitants, called paisas, are recognised throughout Colombia for a distinctive dialect, cuisine, and a strongly regional identity associated with entrepreneurship, hospitality and hard work.

Geography and settlement

The region occupies mountainous terrain where the Western and Central cordilleras of the Andes divide. Spanish settlement began in the sixteenth century, centred on mining communities in Antioquia. In the late eighteenth and nineteenth centuries, the celebrated "Antioquean colonisation" pushed southward from Antioquia, founding towns that became the heart of Colombia's coffee economy, among them Manizales, Pereira and Armenia.

Culture

Paisa culture is closely tied to coffee cultivation and to the figure of the arriero, the mule driver who historically carried goods through the mountains. Signature dishes include the bandeja paisa — a platter of beans, rice, ground beef, fried egg, plantain, chicharrón, avocado and arepa — and the region is famous for its arepas, aguardiente and the annual Flower Festival (Feria de las Flores) in Medellín. Paisa Spanish is distinguished by the use of vos and characteristic intonation.

The region has produced many of Colombia's leading industrial groups, a legacy often attributed to the trading networks formed during the gold-mining and coffee eras. Medellín, the Antioquian capital, is the country's second city and a major industrial and innovation centre.
