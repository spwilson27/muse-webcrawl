Crisis of the Roman Republic

The crisis of the Roman Republic was the period of political instability and civil violence that began in 134 BC with the tribunate of Tiberius Gracchus and ended in 27 BC with the constitutional settlement that made Augustus the first Roman emperor, effectively ending the Roman Republic.

Causes

Underlying causes included growing economic inequality, the displacement of small farmers by slave-worked estates (latifundia), military pressures, and the erosion of political norms (mos maiorum). The Gracchi brothers' reform attempts ended in political murder — the first bloodshed in Roman domestic politics in centuries — and violence thereafter became a political tool.

Course

The period saw the Social War, Sulla's marches on Rome and dictatorship, the rise of Pompey and Caesar, Caesar's crossing of the Rubicon and dictatorship, his assassination in 44 BC, the triumviral proscriptions, and the final civil war between Octavian and Mark Antony, resolved at the Battle of Actium in 31 BC.
