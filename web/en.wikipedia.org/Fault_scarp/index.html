Fault scarp

A fault scarp is a small step or offset on the ground surface caused by movement along a fault.

A fault scarp is a small step or offset on the ground surface where one side of a fault has moved vertically with respect to the other. It is the topographic expression of faulting attributed to the displacement of the land surface by movement along faults. Fault scarps can be rainfall or erosion-modified remnants of much larger geological displacements.

Because of the dramatic terrain that results in areas with large displacements, fault scarps are often used as evidence in paleoseismology — the study of prehistoric earthquakes. Measuring the height of a scarp and dating the sediments offset by it allows geologists to estimate the magnitude and timing of past earthquakes on a fault.

Formation

Fault scarps form when a dip-slip fault ruptures the surface during an earthquake, displacing one block upward or downward relative to the other. A single earthquake may produce a scarp of a few centimetres to several metres in height. Over time, erosion degrades the steep free face of the scarp into a gentler slope, and repeated earthquakes can build cumulative scarps many metres high, such as those along the Wasatch Fault in Utah or the scarps of the Basin and Range Province in the western United States.
