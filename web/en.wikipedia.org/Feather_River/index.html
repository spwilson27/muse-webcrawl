Feather River

The Feather River is the principal tributary of the Sacramento River, in the Sacramento Valley of Northern California in the United States. The river's main stem is about 73 miles (117 km) long, but counting its farthest headwaters tributary, the total length is about 210 miles (340 km). Its drainage basin is roughly 6,200 square miles (16,000 km²).

Course

The Feather River rises in several forks in the Sierra Nevada. The Middle Fork Feather River originates near Beckwourth in the Sierra Valley, while the North Fork is impounded by Lake Almanor. The forks join at Lake Oroville, formed by Oroville Dam — the tallest earthen dam in the United States — and the main stem flows south through the Sacramento Valley to join the Sacramento River near Verona.

History and use

The river was a centre of gold mining during the California Gold Rush, and the Feather River Canyon carried one of the great transcontinental rail routes. Today the river is a major source of water for the California State Water Project and of hydroelectric power, and its Middle Fork is designated a National Wild and Scenic River.
