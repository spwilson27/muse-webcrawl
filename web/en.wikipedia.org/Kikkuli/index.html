Kikkuli was a master horse trainer (assussanni) from the land of Mitanni who served the Hittite king Suppiluliuma I in the 14th century BC. He is famous as the author of the Kikkuli Text, a set of cuneiform tablets prescribing a seven-month conditioning programme for chariot horses — the oldest surviving manual of horse training.

The Kikkuli Text

The text, preserved in four tablets found at the Hittite capital Hattusa, begins "Thus speaks Kikkuli, master horse trainer from the land of Mitanni." It details, day by day over some 214 days, the feeding, watering, blanketing, swimming, interval galloping, and overnight stabling of chariot horse teams. Feed rations of hay, grain, and chaff, controlled water intake, and progressive workloads are specified with remarkable precision, resembling modern interval training used in endurance riding and eventing.

Indo-Aryan terminology

The manual is also a key linguistic document: it preserves numerals and technical terms from an Indo-Aryan language — such as aika- "one", tera- "three", panza- "five", satta- "seven" and na- "nine", in compounds counting training laps — evidence of the Indo-Aryan element among the Mitanni elite, comparable to the names of Mitanni gods invoked in Hittite treaties.

Legacy

Modern equestrians and historians have tested Kikkuli's programme experimentally, finding it a sound, systematic conditioning method. The text remains a principal source on Bronze Age horsemanship and on the spread of chariot warfare across the Near East.
