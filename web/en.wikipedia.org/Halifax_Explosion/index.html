Halifax Explosion

The Halifax Explosion was a disaster in the Canadian port of Halifax, Nova Scotia, on the morning of 6 December 1917. The French cargo ship SS Mont-Blanc, laden with high explosives, collided with the Norwegian vessel SS Imo in the Narrows, a strait connecting Halifax Harbour to Bedford Basin. A fire on board the Mont-Blanc ignited her cargo, causing a massive explosion that devastated the Richmond district of Halifax.

The disaster

Nearly all of the older, wooden buildings north of the blast were destroyed, and a tsunami created by the explosion flooded parts of the shoreline. Approximately 2,000 people were killed by the blast, debris, fires, or collapsed buildings, and an estimated 9,000 others were injured. The blast was the largest human-made explosion at the time, releasing energy equivalent to roughly 2.9 kilotons of TNT; it remained the largest artificial explosion until the development of nuclear weapons.

Aftermath

Relief poured in from across Canada and the United States; Boston's aid train arrived the next day despite a blizzard, beginning a bond commemorated each year when Nova Scotia sends Boston its official Christmas tree. The community of Hydrostone was built during reconstruction. A judicial inquiry initially blamed the Mont-Blanc's masters, but the case against them was ultimately dropped. Every year on December 6, Halifax holds a memorial service at the Memorial Bell Tower in Fort Needham Park.
