Emancipation of minors

Emancipation of minors is a legal mechanism by which a minor before reaching the age of majority (often 18) is freed from control by their parents or guardians, and the parents or guardians are freed from responsibility for the minor. In most countries, a minor may be emancipated through certain situations, such as marriage or military service, or through a court order upon petition.

Effects

An emancipated minor generally gains many of the legal capacities of an adult, such as the ability to enter into contracts, keep their own earnings, make medical decisions and live independently. The exact scope varies by jurisdiction, and emancipation usually does not lower the legal age for activities otherwise regulated by age, such as voting or purchasing alcohol.

By country

In the United States, emancipation laws vary by state; some states allow minors to petition a court, while others recognize emancipation only in limited circumstances. Similar concepts exist in other legal systems, often tied to marriage or judicial discretion.
