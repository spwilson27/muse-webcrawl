Procrastination

Procrastination is the act of unnecessarily delaying or postponing something despite knowing that there will be negative consequences for doing so. It is a human behavior that is commonly attributed to habits of self-control failure, including poor time management, distraction, and avoidance related to anxiety. The word itself comes from the Latin procrastinatus: pro- (forward) and crastinus (of tomorrow).

Psychological perspectives

Researchers distinguish procrastination from simple laziness: procrastinators often intend to complete the task and feel distress about the delay. Explanations include the temporal discounting of rewards, task aversiveness, fear of failure, perfectionism, and difficulties with emotion regulation. Some psychologists frame procrastination primarily as a problem of mood management — the avoidance of negative feelings associated with a task — rather than a defect of character or time management.

Types

Procrastination is commonly divided into passive procrastination, in which a person is paralyzed by indecision and fails to finish on time, and active procrastination, in which a person deliberately delays because they believe they work better under pressure. Chronic procrastination affects an estimated 15–20 percent of adults and is associated with poorer academic performance, lower wages, and worse health outcomes in correlational studies.

Causes

Contributing factors include impulsiveness, low conscientiousness, depression, anxiety, and attention-deficit disorders. Genetic research suggests procrastination shares heritable components with impulsivity. Environmental factors such as the availability of immediate distractions — especially digital media — also increase delay.

Management

Strategies shown to help include breaking tasks into smaller steps, setting specific implementation intentions ("if–then" plans), reducing friction to start, self-forgiveness for past procrastination, and cognitive-behavioral techniques. Minimizing distractions and making future rewards more salient can also reduce delay.
