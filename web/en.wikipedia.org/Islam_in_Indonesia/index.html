Islam in Indonesia

Islam in Indonesia is overwhelmingly the faith of the majority of the population: Indonesia is the world's most populous Muslim-majority country, with about 87 per cent of its roughly 275 million people identifying as Muslim, the great majority of them Sunni, following the Shafi'i school of law.

History

Islam spread through the archipelago mainly from the 13th century along maritime trade routes, carried by Gujarati, Arab, and Chinese Muslim merchants and by Sufi teachers, and was propagated on Java according to tradition by the nine saints, the Wali Songo. Rather than arriving by conquest, Indonesian Islam absorbed much pre-Islamic Hindu-Buddhist and indigenous custom, producing a notably syncretic tradition, especially in Java.

Organisations and practice

The two largest Islamic organisations in the world are Indonesian: Nahdlatul Ulama (founded 1926), representing traditionalist Islam, and Muhammadiyah (founded 1912), representing modernist Islam. Indonesia is constitutionally a Pancasila state rather than an Islamic state, officially recognising six religions; the western province of Aceh, however, implements sharia law under a special autonomy arrangement.
