Organology
Field: Musicology, ethnomusicology
Subject: Musical instruments — their history, construction, classification and acoustics

Organology (from Greek organon, "instrument", and logos, "study") is the science of musical instruments and their classification. It embraces the history, makers, technical developments, acoustics, and sociological aspects of instruments, as well as the systems used to classify them.

Classification

The most widely used classification is the Hornbostel–Sachs system, published in 1914 by Erich Moritz von Hornbostel and Curt Sachs, which divides instruments by the way they produce sound: idiophones (the body itself vibrates), membranophones (a stretched membrane), chordophones (strings), aerophones (a column of air) and, added later, electrophones. Earlier systems included the ancient Indian division of instruments into string, wind, solid percussion and membrane percussion categories.

Scope

Organologists study both Western and non-Western instruments, their construction and playing techniques, and their cultural roles. The field draws on museum collections — major ones include the Metropolitan Museum of Art and the Horniman Museum — and is represented professionally by the Galpin Society and the American Musical Instrument Society.
