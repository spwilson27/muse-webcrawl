The University of Bonn, officially the Rhenish Friedrich Wilhelm University of Bonn (German: Rheinische Friedrich-Wilhelms-Universität Bonn), is a public research university in Bonn, Germany. Founded in 1818 by King Frederick William III of Prussia, it is one of Germany's largest and most prestigious universities, a member of the German Universities Excellence Initiative, and noted for mathematics, economics, physics, and law.

History

The university was founded in 1818 in the Prussian Rhine Province, taking over the electoral palace as its main building. It attracted scholars including the historian Barthold Niebuhr and the poet August Wilhelm Schlegel. Karl Marx and Friedrich Nietzsche studied at Bonn in the 19th century. The university rebuilt after the Second World War, during which its main building was heavily damaged, and grew as Bonn served as the capital of West Germany (1949–1990).

Academics

Bonn has seven faculties, including Catholic and Protestant theology, law and economics, medicine, philosophy, mathematics and natural sciences, and agriculture. It hosts the Hausdorff Center for Mathematics and multiple Collaborative Research Centres, and in 2019 was designated a University of Excellence with six Clusters of Excellence, the most of any German university at that time.

Notable people

Alumni and faculty include Nobel laureates such as Wolfgang Paul and Reinhard Selten, the mathematician Felix Hausdorff, and Pope Benedict XVI, who held a chair of theology there. The university is also associated with the Arithmeum museum of mathematics.
