Statistical power

The statistical power of a binary hypothesis test is the probability that the test correctly rejects the null hypothesis when a specific alternative hypothesis is true. It is denoted 1 − β, where β is the probability of a Type II error (failing to reject a false null hypothesis).

Factors affecting power

Power increases with larger effect sizes, larger sample sizes, higher significance levels (α), lower variability in the data, and the use of one-tailed rather than two-tailed tests. A power analysis, performed before a study, estimates the sample size required to detect an effect of a given size with a given degree of confidence.

Convention

Following Jacob Cohen, a power of 0.80 is often taken as a conventional target in study design: a study should have an 80% chance of detecting an effect if one exists. Many published studies, particularly in psychology and neuroscience, have been criticised as underpowered, contributing to failures of replication when significant results from low-power studies prove to be false positives or inflated estimates.
