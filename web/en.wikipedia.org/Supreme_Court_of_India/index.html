Supreme Court of India

The Supreme Court of India is the supreme judicial authority and the highest court of the Republic of India. It is the final court of appeal for all civil and criminal cases in the country and is located in New Delhi. Established on 28 January 1950 under the Constitution of India, it succeeded the Federal Court of India and the Judicial Committee of the Privy Council as the apex court.

Composition

The Court comprises the Chief Justice of India and up to 33 other judges, appointed by the President of India on the recommendation of the collegium of senior judges. Judges serve until the age of 65.

Jurisdiction and powers

The Court exercises original jurisdiction over disputes between the Union and the states and over enforcement of fundamental rights (Article 32), appellate jurisdiction over High Courts, and advisory jurisdiction on references from the President. It is a court of record with the power to punish for contempt, and it can review its own judgments. Landmark decisions include Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973), which established the basic structure doctrine, and Justice K. S. Puttaswamy v. Union of India (2017), recognizing privacy as a fundamental right.
