Schaumburg-Lippe was a state in northwestern Germany, one of the smallest member states of the German Empire. It originated in 1647 from the division of the County of Schaumburg and was ruled by a branch of the House of Lippe from its capital at Bückeburg.

History

When Count Otto V of Schaumburg died without heirs in 1640, his lands were partitioned; the portion around Bückeburg passed to Count Philipp of Lippe-Alverdissen, founding the Schaumburg-Lippe line. The county was raised to a principality in 1807 when it joined the Confederation of the Rhine. It later belonged to the German Confederation, the North German Confederation, and from 1871 the German Empire, where despite an area of only about 340 km² it held a vote in the Bundesrat.

Free State and aftermath

Prince Adolf II abdicated on 15 November 1918 during the German Revolution, and Schaumburg-Lippe became a free state within the Weimar Republic. After the Second World War it was merged in 1946 into the new state of Lower Saxony. Its territory corresponds closely to the modern district of Schaumburg. The princely family retains Bückeburg Palace, and the former principality's Mausoleum in Bückeburg is among the largest private tombs in Europe.
