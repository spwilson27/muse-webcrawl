Norma (opera)

Norma is an opera in two acts by Vincenzo Bellini with an Italian libretto by Felice Romani after the play Norma, ou L'infanticide (Norma, or The Infanticide) by Alexandre Soumet. It was first produced at La Scala in Milan on 26 December 1831.

The opera is regarded as a leading example of the bel canto genre, and the soprano prayer "Casta diva" in Act 1 is famous. The title role is considered one of the most difficult in the soprano repertoire, requiring both dramatic power and extraordinary vocal agility and control.

Composition history

Crushed under the weight of a dubious prior career move, Bellini recruited Felice Romani — with whom he had worked on several earlier operas including Il pirata and La sonnambula — to write the libretto. Romani adapted Soumet's tragedy, which had been performed in Paris earlier in 1831. Bellini composed the score in the autumn of 1831, and the opera premiered the day after Christmas that year at La Scala with Giuditta Pasta in the title role.

Performance history

The premiere was a fiasco, partly because the audience was hostile and partly because the singers were indisposed. Bellini wrote that the performance was a "solenne fiasco" (solemn fiasco). However, the opera was performed again the following night to much greater success, and it soon spread throughout Europe. In the 20th century, the role of Norma became closely associated with Maria Callas, who performed it nearly 90 times and made two studio recordings, in 1954 and 1960. Other celebrated Normas have included Rosa Ponselle, Joan Sutherland, and Montserrat Caballé.

Roles

  • Norma, high priestess of the Druids — soprano
  • Pollione, Roman proconsul in Gaul — tenor
  • Oroveso, chief of the Druids and Norma's father — bass
  • Adalgisa, a young priestess — soprano or mezzo-soprano
  • Clotilde, Norma's confidante — soprano
  • Flavio, Pollione's friend — tenor

Synopsis

The action takes place in Gaul under Roman occupation. Norma, the high priestess of the Druids, has secretly broken her vows and borne two children to Pollione, the Roman proconsul. Pollione has now fallen in love with the young priestess Adalgisa, who reciprocates his feelings but is tormented by guilt. When Adalgisa confesses to Norma, the high priestess realizes Pollione's betrayal. Enraged, Norma contemplates killing her children but cannot bring herself to do it. She confesses her own guilt before the Druids and is condemned to death by burning; Pollione, moved by her nobility, joins her on the pyre.

Recordings

The opera has been recorded many times. Notable recordings include the two featuring Maria Callas conducted by Tullio Serafin, Joan Sutherland's recordings under Richard Bonynge, and Montserrat Caballé's 1972 recording. The aria "Casta diva" remains one of the most recorded pieces in the soprano repertoire.
