Lockheed AC-130

The Lockheed AC-130 is a heavily armed, long-endurance, ground-attack variant of the C-130 Hercules transport, operated by the United States Air Force. Armed with side-firing cannons and precision-guided munitions, it provides close air support, air interdiction, and force protection for ground troops, orbiting targets in a pylon turn so its left-side weapons can fire continuously.

Development

The gunship concept was proven during the Vietnam War, when AC-47 and AC-119 aircraft supported troops at night; the first AC-130A "Spectre" prototypes followed in 1967. Successive variants — AC-130H Spectre, AC-130U "Spooky", and the current AC-130J Ghostrider based on the C-130J — have added sensors, armor, and precision weapons.

Armament

Armament has historically included 20 mm Vulcan cannons, a 40 mm Bofors cannon, and a 105 mm howitzer — the largest gun on any combat aircraft. The AC-130J combines a 30 mm cannon and 105 mm howitzer with wing-mounted precision munitions such as GBU-39 small diameter bombs and AGM-176 Griffin missiles.

Service

AC-130s have fought in Vietnam, Grenada, Panama, the Gulf War, Somalia, the Balkans, Afghanistan, and Iraq. The type is operated exclusively by Air Force Special Operations Command from bases including Hurlburt Field, Florida, and Cannon Air Force Base, New Mexico.
