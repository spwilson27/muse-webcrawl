The Russian Provisional Government was a provisional government of Russia established immediately following the abdication of Tsar Nicholas II during the February Revolution. It was formed on 15 March 1917 (2 March O.S.) by the Provisional Committee of the State Duma and governed Russia until it was overthrown by the Bolsheviks in the October Revolution on 7 November 1917 (25 October O.S.).

Dual power

From the outset the government shared power — the so-called dual power (dvoevlastie) — with the Petrograd Soviet of Workers' and Soldiers' Deputies, which controlled the capital's garrison, railways and factories. The government's authority was further undermined by its decision to continue Russia's participation in the First World War and to postpone land reform and elections to the Constituent Assembly until after the war.

Crises and fall

Prince Georgy Lvov headed the first cabinet; after the failure of the June (Kerensky) Offensive and the July Days unrest, Alexander Kerensky became prime minister. The government's standing collapsed after the Kornilov affair in August 1917, in which Kerensky and the conservative general Lavr Kornilov fell out amid mutual accusations. On the night of 6–7 November 1917, Bolshevik Red Guards seized Petrograd's key points and stormed the Winter Palace, arresting the government. Elections to the Constituent Assembly proceeded later that month, but the Bolsheviks dispersed the Assembly in January 1918.
