Kim Il Sung

Kim Il Sung (born Kim Song-ju; 15 April 1912 – 8 July 1994), whose name is also romanised Kim Il-sung, was the founder and supreme leader of North Korea from 1948 until his death. This article, under an alternate spelling of his name, summarises the same life: the Manchurian guerrilla fighter who, with Soviet support, established the Democratic People's Republic of Korea and ruled it as the centre of a far-reaching personality cult.

Names and romanisation

Korean names appear in English in several romanisation systems, producing variants such as Kim Il-sung, Kim Il Sung and Kim Il-seong. He took the name Il-sung ("become the sun") during his guerrilla years. In North Korean usage he is styled the "Great Leader" (Suryong) and, posthumously, Eternal President of the Republic, a title written into the 1998 constitution.

Key points of his rule

Kim founded the Workers' Party of Korea, launched the Korean War in 1950, articulated the Juche idea of national self-reliance from 1955, and stamped out internal rivals in the purges of the 1950s and 1960s. North Korea's early industrial growth gave way to economic decline by the 1980s; Kim died in 1994 and was succeeded by his son, Kim Jong-il.
