Ultimate Fighting Championship

The Ultimate Fighting Championship (UFC) is an American mixed martial arts (MMA) promotion company based in Las Vegas, Nevada. It is the largest MMA promotion in the world and produces events worldwide showcasing the top-ranked fighters in the sport. The UFC is owned by TKO Group Holdings and stages dozens of events per year, including numbered pay-per-view cards and Fight Night shows.

History

The first UFC event was held on 12 November 1993 in Denver, Colorado. Conceived by Art Davie and Rorion Gracie as a tournament to determine the most effective martial art, the early events were minimal-rules contests in which Brazilian jiu-jitsu practitioner Royce Gracie defeated larger opponents. Political pressure and bans nearly destroyed the promotion in the late 1990s, but under Zuffa ownership from 2001, with Dana White as president, the sport adopted the Unified Rules, gained sanctioning by athletic commissions, and grew through the reality series The Ultimate Fighter (2005). UFC was sold to WME-IMG (Endeavor) in 2016 for about $4 billion, and in 2023 it was combined with WWE under TKO Group Holdings.

Rules and divisions

Bouts take place in an eight-sided cage known as the Octagon. Fights are three or five rounds of five minutes, judged on striking, grappling and aggression under the Unified Rules of Mixed Martial Arts. The UFC maintains men's and women's weight divisions from strawweight to heavyweight, with championship belts and official rankings for each.

Significance

The UFC is credited with transforming mixed martial arts from a marginal spectacle into a mainstream global sport, producing stars such as Anderson Silva, Georges St-Pierre, Jon Jones, Conor McGregor and Ronda Rousey, and establishing a dominant business model in combat sports.
