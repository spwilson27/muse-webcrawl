The Bluebook


The cover of a recent edition of The Bluebook.

The Bluebook: A Uniform System of Citation is a style guide that prescribes the system of legal citation used in the United States. It is compiled by the editors of the law reviews of Harvard, Columbia, the University of Pennsylvania, and Yale. It is the dominant style guide for legal writing in American law schools, courts, and law reviews.

History

The first edition of the Bluebook was published in 1926 as a small pamphlet prepared at Harvard Law School to standardize the citation of legal authorities in law review articles. Its distinctive blue cover gave the guide its name. Over the decades it grew substantially: what began as a booklet of a few dozen pages became a manual of several hundred pages, revised roughly every five years. The 21st edition was published in 2020.

Contents

The Bluebook is organized into rules covering the citation of cases, statutes, constitutions, books, journals, and electronic sources, as well as typeface conventions for law review articles and court documents. It prescribes elaborate abbreviations for court names, reporters, and periodicals, and rules for signals such as see, see also, and cf. that indicate how a cited authority relates to the writer's proposition.

Criticism

The Bluebook has frequently been criticized for its complexity and opacity. Judge Richard Posner famously attacked it as an overgrown system whose mastery consumes law students' time without corresponding benefit, and a number of courts and jurisdictions have adopted alternative or simplified citation systems. Yale Law School's Yale Law Journal nevertheless remains one of its sponsoring editors. A public-domain alternative, the Indigo Book, was released in 2015 after a copyright dispute over the extent to which citation rules could be protected.
