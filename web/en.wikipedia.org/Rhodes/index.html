Rhodes

Rhodes (Greek: Ρόδος, romanized: Ródos) is the largest of the Dodecanese islands of Greece and is their historical capital; it is the ninth largest island in the Mediterranean Sea. Administratively, the island forms a separate municipality within the Rhodes regional unit, which is part of the South Aegean administrative region. The principal town of the island, Rhodes (city), had about 56,000 inhabitants in 2023; the island's population is around 125,000.

History

Rhodes has been inhabited since the Neolithic period and was influenced by Minoan and Mycenaean civilisations. The city of Rhodes was founded in 408 BC by the synoecism of three cities — Ialysos, Kamiros and Lindos — and prospered as a maritime and commercial power. Its harbour was watched over by the Colossus of Rhodes, a giant statue of the sun god Helios completed in 280 BC and one of the Seven Wonders of the Ancient World, toppled by an earthquake in 226 BC.

Knights and Ottomans

After Byzantine and Genoese episodes, the Knights Hospitaller seized Rhodes in 1309 and ruled until 1522, fortifying the city whose medieval Old Town — including the Palace of the Grand Master and the Street of the Knights — survives as a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Suleiman the Magnificent conquered the island after a six-month siege in 1522, driving the Knights to Malta. Rhodes remained Ottoman for nearly four centuries.

Modern era

Italy seized Rhodes in 1912 during the Italo-Turkish War and held the Dodecanese until the Second World War; the islands were ceded to Greece in 1947–48. The island's Jewish community, once several thousand strong, was deported by the Nazis in 1944. Today tourism is the mainstay of the island's economy, drawing visitors to its beaches, the acropolis of Lindos, and the medieval city.

Geography and climate

Rhodes lies in the eastern Aegean, about 18 km from the Turkish coast. The island is spear-shaped, 79.7 km long and 38 km wide, with a mountainous interior culminating in Mount Attavyros (1,215 m). It enjoys a Mediterranean climate with around 300 days of sunshine a year.
