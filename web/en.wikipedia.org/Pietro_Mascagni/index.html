Pietro Mascagni

Pietro Mascagni (7 December 1863 – 2 August 1945) was an Italian composer known mainly for his operas. His one-act masterpiece Cavalleria rusticana (1890), written for a publisher's competition, was an enormous success and is generally considered the first work of operatic verismo.

Career

Born in Livorno, Mascagni studied at the Milan Conservatory, where he roomed with Puccini. The triumph of Cavalleria rusticana made him famous at 26; its Intermezzo remains one of the best-known pieces in opera. His subsequent operas include L'amico Fritz (1891), Iris (1898), and Le maschere (1901), though none matched the first success. Mascagni also worked extensively as a conductor and administrator, including periods directing opera houses in Italy and touring abroad.
