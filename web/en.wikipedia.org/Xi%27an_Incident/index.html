Xi'an Incident

Date12–26 December 1936
LocationXi'an, Republic of China
Principal actorsZhang Xueliang, Yang Hucheng, Chiang Kai-shek
OutcomeSecond United Front against Japan

The Xi'an Incident was a political crisis that unfolded in Xi'an, China, in December 1936. On 12 December, the Nationalist general Zhang Xueliang and Yang Hucheng arrested the leader of the Republic of China, Chiang Kai-shek, who had flown to Xi'an to direct campaigns against the Chinese Communists. The kidnapping forced Chiang to negotiate an end to the civil war and a united front against Japanese aggression.

Background

Zhang's Northeastern Army had lost Manchuria to Japan in 1931 and was reluctant to fight the Communists while Japan advanced. After secret contacts with Zhou Enlai, Zhang concluded that Chiang's policy of "internal pacification before external resistance" had to be reversed by force.

The incident and negotiations

Chiang was seized at the Huaqing hot springs after a pre-dawn raid in which his bodyguards were killed. The mutineers issued demands including reorganization of the government and an end to the anti-Communist campaigns. Negotiations — involving Zhou Enlai for the Communists and T. V. Soong and Chiang's wife Soong Mei-ling for the government — produced an agreement, and Chiang was released on 25–26 December and flown back to Nanjing with Zhang accompanying him.

Aftermath

The incident led to the Second United Front in 1937, in which Nationalists and Communists suspended their civil war to resist the full-scale Japanese invasion that began that July. Zhang was court-martialled and spent more than five decades under house arrest; Yang was later executed in 1949.
