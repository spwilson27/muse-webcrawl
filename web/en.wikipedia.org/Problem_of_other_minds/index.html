Problem of other minds

The problem of other minds is a philosophical problem: how can one know that other people (or animals, or machines) have minds and conscious experiences, given that one has direct access only to one's own mental states?

Formulation

Each person observes their own sensations, thoughts, and feelings from the first-person perspective, but observes only the behaviour and bodies of others. The problem, related to solipsism, asks what justifies the belief that others are conscious rather than mindless automata — the philosophical zombie thought experiment presses the same point.

Responses

The argument from analogy, associated with John Stuart Mill, infers others' minds from similarity of behaviour and body to one's own case. Critics note it generalizes from a single case. Other responses include inference to the best explanation, Ludwig Wittgenstein's remarks on criteria and private language, behaviourism, and contemporary work on theory of mind and mirror neurons in cognitive science.
