Conscription in South Korea

Conscription in South Korea has existed since 1957. Under the Military Service Act, all able-bodied South Korean men must complete mandatory military service, a policy maintained because the country remains technically at war with North Korea following the 1953 armistice that ended the Korean War without a peace treaty.

Terms of service

Men are typically conscripted between the ages of 18 and 28. Service lengths, gradually shortened over the decades, are approximately 18 months in the Army and Marine Corps, 20 months in the Navy, and 21 months in the Air Force. Conscripts receive a physical grade at examination; those unfit for active duty may perform alternative service as social service agents or in public institutions. After discharge, servicemen remain in the reserve forces, with periodic training obligations into their thirties and beyond.

Exemptions and evasion

Deferments are available for students and certain others, and exemptions or reductions have at times been granted to elite athletes and classical musicians who win major international competitions, a provision debated in connection with Olympic medallists and, publicly, the members of BTS. Draft evasion — by falsifying medical records, renouncing citizenship, or fleeing abroad — is a serious criminal offence. Conscientious objectors were imprisoned for decades; following a landmark 2018 Constitutional Court ruling, an alternative civilian service system for objectors was introduced in 2020.

Public debate

Mandatory service is widely accepted as a necessity given the northern threat, but debates continue over its length, the treatment and pay of conscripts, the fairness of exemptions, and proposals — long resisted — to extend some form of obligation to women, especially as South Korea's low birth rate shrinks the pool of eligible men.
