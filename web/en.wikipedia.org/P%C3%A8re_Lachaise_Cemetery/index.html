Père Lachaise Cemetery

Location20th arrondissement, Paris, France
Established1804
Area44 hectares (110 acres)
IntermentsMore than 1 million

Père Lachaise Cemetery (French: Cimetière du Père-Lachaise) is the largest cemetery in Paris, covering 44 hectares (110 acres) in the 20th arrondissement. Established in 1804, it is named after Père François de la Chaise, confessor to King Louis XIV, who lived on the site.

History

Initially unpopular because of its distance from the city, the cemetery gained prestige when administrators transferred the remains of famous figures such as Molière, La Fontaine, and the medieval lovers Héloïse and Abélard. It later became the resting place of the mur des Fédérés, where 147 Communards were executed in 1871 at the end of the Paris Commune.

Notable burials and visitors

Among the famous interred are Frédéric Chopin, Oscar Wilde, Édith Piaf, Marcel Proust, Gertrude Stein, and Jim Morrison of The Doors, whose grave is a pilgrimage site. Receiving around 3.5 million visitors a year, Père Lachaise is the most visited necropolis in the world and functions as an open-air museum of funerary sculpture.
