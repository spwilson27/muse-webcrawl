Nanchang uprising

The Nanchang uprising (Chinese: 南昌起义) was an armed insurrection launched in the city of Nanchang, Jiangxi, on 1 August 1927 by forces led by the Chinese Communist Party (CCP) and left-wing Kuomintang officers. It was the first major engagement of the Chinese Civil War and is commemorated in the People's Republic of China as the founding of the People's Liberation Army; 1 August remains PLA Day.

Background and course

After Chiang Kai-shek's purge of communists in Shanghai in April 1927 and the collapse of the Wuhan left-Kuomintang government, the CCP resolved to stage its own uprisings. Communist-influenced National Revolutionary Army units under He Long, Ye Ting, Zhu De and others, directed by Zhou Enlai and a front committee, seized Nanchang in the early hours of 1 August after several hours of fighting, defeating the local garrison of about 3,000 men.

Aftermath

Unable to hold the city against advancing Nationalist forces, the insurgents withdrew south toward Guangdong within days, suffering heavy losses from battle, disease and desertion on the march. Remnants of the force eventually joined Mao Zedong's forces in the Jinggang Mountains in spring 1928, forming a core of the Chinese Red Army.
