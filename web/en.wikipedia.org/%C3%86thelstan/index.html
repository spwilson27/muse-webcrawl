Æthelstan or Athelstan (c. 894 – 27 October 939) was King of the Anglo-Saxons from 924 to 927 and King of the English from 927 to his death in 939. He was the son of King Edward the Elder and his first wife, Ecgwynn. Modern historians regard him as the first king of England and one of the "greatest Anglo-Saxon kings".

Reign

When Edward died in July 924, Æthelstan was accepted as king by the Mercians. In 927 he conquered the last remaining Viking kingdom, York, and took the title King of the English. His victory at the Battle of Brunanburh in 937 preserved the unity of his kingdom against a coalition of Scots, Vikings and Strathclyde Britons.

Government and law

Æthelstan centralised government, issued law codes, and controlled coinage across his realm. His court was a centre of learning and religious reform, and he maintained diplomatic relations with rulers across Europe, marrying his half-sisters into continental royal houses.
