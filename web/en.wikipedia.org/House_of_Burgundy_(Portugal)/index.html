The Portuguese House of Burgundy was the first dynasty of the Kingdom of Portugal, ruling from the establishment of the County of Portugal in 1093 (and the kingdom from 1139) until the succession crisis of 1383–1385. It was a cadet branch of the Capetian House of Burgundy.

History

The dynasty was founded by Henry of Burgundy, who received the County of Portugal as a fief from Alfonso VI of León. His son Afonso Henriques defeated the Moors at the Battle of Ourique in 1139 and proclaimed himself the first King of Portugal; independence was recognised by León in 1143 and by the papacy in 1179. Successive kings consolidated the kingdom, completing the conquest of the Algarve in 1249 and fixing Portugal's borders, among the oldest in Europe.

End of the dynasty

The line ended in the male line with Ferdinand I in 1383. His daughter Beatrice's claim, backed by Castile, was contested by John of Aviz, whose victory at the Battle of Aljubarrota in 1385 established the House of Aviz as Portugal's second dynasty.
