Spartacist uprising

Date5–12 January 1919
LocationBerlin, Germany
ResultUprising suppressed by government and Freikorps
LeadersRosa Luxemburg, Karl Liebknecht

The Spartacist uprising was a general strike and armed uprising in Berlin in January 1919 during the German Revolution of 1918–19. It was led by the newly founded Communist Party of Germany, whose predecessor, the Spartacus League, gave the revolt its name.

Events

Triggered by the dismissal of Berlin's left-wing police chief Emil Eichhorn, mass protests escalated into an occupation of newspaper offices and an attempt to overthrow the provisional government of Friedrich Ebert. The government, relying on Freikorps paramilitary units organised by Gustav Noske, retook the city after several days of street fighting.

Aftermath

Communist leaders Rosa Luxemburg and Karl Liebknecht were arrested and murdered by Freikorps soldiers on 15 January 1919, an event that deepened the split on the German left for a generation.
