Russian names

Many traditional Russian given names derive from the Orthodox calendar of saints.

Russian names follow the East Slavic naming pattern of three parts: a given name (imya), a patronymic (otchestvo), and a family name or surname (familiya). The patronymic is formed from the father's given name and is used in formal address together with the given name, while surnames were historically adopted from occupations, places, or ancestors' names.

Given names

Traditional Russian given names largely come from the Orthodox Christian calendar — names of Greek, Latin, and Hebrew origin such as Ivan, Nikolai, Alexander, Anna, Maria, and Olga — alongside older Slavic names such as Vladimir, Yaroslav, Svetlana, and Lyudmila. Most names have numerous diminutive forms: Alexander may be Sasha or Shura, Maria is Masha, and Ivan is Vanya. Diminutives range from affectionate family forms to familiar short forms, and choosing among them signals social distance and intimacy.

Patronymics

The patronymic is formed by adding -ovich/-evich for men and -ovna/-evna for women to the father's name; thus the son of Ivan is Ivanovich and the daughter Ivanovna. Addressing a person by given name plus patronymic — for example, "Ivan Petrovich" — is the standard respectful form used for teachers, elders, and in professional life.

Surnames

Common Russian surnames end in -ov/-ev (Ivanov, Smirnov, Kuznetsov), -in (Pushkin), or -sky (Dostoevsky). Women's surnames take feminine endings, typically adding -a: Ivanova, Pushkina. Surnames became universal only in the nineteenth century, when peasants adopted or were assigned them.
