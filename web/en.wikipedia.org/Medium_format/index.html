Medium format

Medium format has several meanings in photography, but is commonly used for a film format or digital sensor size between the smaller 35 mm format and the larger large format (4×5 inch and up). In the film era, medium-format systems used roll films with widths between 35 mm and 70 mm — most famously 120 film — and produced negatives or transparencies much larger than 35 mm frames, yielding superior image quality, finer detail and smoother tonal gradation.

Film formats

The most common medium-format frame sizes, all exposed on 120 or 220 roll film, include 6×4.5 cm, 6×6 cm (square), 6×7 cm, 6×8 cm, 6×9 cm and panoramic formats such as 6×12 cm and 6×17 cm. Cameras were made by manufacturers including Hasselblad, Mamiya, Pentax, Bronica, Contax and Rollei. Many medium-format cameras are modular systems with interchangeable film backs, viewfinders and lenses.

Digital medium format

With the transition to digital imaging, medium format came to describe sensors larger than the 36×24 mm "full-frame" size, such as the 43.8×32.9 mm sensors used in Fujifilm's GFX system and Hasselblad's X system, and the larger 53.4×40 mm sensors in Phase One backs. These systems are used in fashion, advertising, landscape and studio photography where maximum resolution and dynamic range are required.
