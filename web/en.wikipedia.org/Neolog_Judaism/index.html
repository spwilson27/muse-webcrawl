Neolog Judaism

Neolog Judaism (Hungarian: Neológia) is a movement of Judaism that emerged in 19th-century Hungary as a moderate reform tendency, positioned between traditional Orthodoxy and the more radical Reform movement. Following the Hungarian Jewish Congress of 1868–1869, Neolog communities formed their own communal organisation.

Characteristics

Neolog practice retained Hebrew liturgy and traditional observance in many areas while introducing innovations such as sermons in the vernacular, organ music in some synagogues, and mixed seating in some congregations later. The Dohány Street Synagogue in Budapest, the largest synagogue in Europe, is Neolog. The Jewish Theological Seminary of Budapest, founded in 1877, trained Neolog rabbis.

Today

Neolog remains the historically dominant denomination of Hungarian Jewry, organised under the Federation of Hungarian Jewish Communities (Mazsihisz).
