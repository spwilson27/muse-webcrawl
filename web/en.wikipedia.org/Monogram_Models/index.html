Monogram Models

Founded: 1945
Founders: Jack Besser, Rylee Miller
Headquarters: Morton Grove, Illinois, United States
Products: Plastic model kits
Parent: Revell (later combined as Revell-Monogram)

Monogram Models was an American manufacturer of plastic scale model kits, founded in Chicago, Illinois, in 1945 by Jack Besser and Rylee Miller. The company began by producing wooden model ship and aircraft kits before transitioning to plastic models in the early 1950s.

History

Monogram's first plastic kits appeared around 1954, and the company became one of the "big four" American model kit manufacturers alongside Revell, Aurora, and Lindberg. Monogram was known for high-quality moulding, detailed aircraft kits in 1/48 scale, and a wide range of car models. Notable product lines included its 1/48 scale military aircraft, 1/24 and 1/25 scale automobiles, and large kits such as the 1/48 B-17 Flying Fortress and B-29 Superfortress bombers.

In 1968 the toy conglomerate Mattel acquired Monogram. In 1986, Monogram was sold and merged with Revell to form Revell-Monogram, Inc., headquartered in Morton Grove, Illinois. The combined company continued to market kits under both brand names. In 2007 Revell-Monogram was acquired by Hobbico, and after Hobbico's 2018 bankruptcy the Revell assets passed to new ownership, with the Monogram brand largely retired or used selectively on reissued kits.

Legacy

Monogram kits remain popular with collectors and modellers, and many of its classic toolings have been reissued for decades, valued for their accuracy and detail for their era.
