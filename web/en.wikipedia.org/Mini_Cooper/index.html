Mini Cooper

The Mini Cooper is the performance version of the Mini, originally a line of small British cars manufactured by the British Motor Corporation (BMC) and its successors from 1961 to 1971 and from 1990 to 2000. Developed with racing driver and engineer John Cooper, whose Cooper Car Company had won the Formula One constructors' championships in 1959 and 1960, the Cooper and the more powerful Cooper S became celebrated rally cars.

History

John Cooper recognised the competition potential of Alec Issigonis's 1959 Mini, with its transverse engine, front-wheel drive and go-kart-like handling. The 997 cc Cooper debuted in 1961, followed by the Cooper S (1963) with a 1071 cc engine, larger brakes and twin fuel tanks. Cooper S versions won the Monte Carlo Rally in 1964, 1965 and 1967 — and famously finished first in 1966 before being disqualified on a headlamp technicality. The classic Mini Cooper was revived by Rover in 1990, and the Cooper name continues on BMW's modern MINI range, including the John Cooper Works models.

Legacy

The Mini Cooper is regarded as an icon of 1960s Britain and of giant-killing motorsport design; in 1999 the original Mini was voted the second most influential car of the 20th century in the Car of the Century competition.
