Trade secret

A trade secret is information — such as a formula, process, design, customer list or business strategy — that derives economic value from not being generally known, and that its holder takes reasonable measures to keep secret. Unlike a patent, a trade secret requires no registration and can last indefinitely, but protection is lost if the information becomes public or is independently discovered or lawfully reverse-engineered.


Trade secrets are protected by secrecy measures, not registration

Definition

Article 39.2 of the international TRIPS Agreement defines protectable information as that which is secret, has commercial value because of its secrecy, and has been subject to reasonable steps to keep it secret. Famous examples include the Coca-Cola formula, Google's search ranking methods, and the blends of herbs and spices used by food brands.

Legal protection

In the United States, trade secrets are governed mainly by state law based on the Uniform Trade Secrets Act, supplemented by the federal Defend Trade Secrets Act of 2016, which created a federal civil cause of action; the Economic Espionage Act of 1996 makes trade-secret theft — especially for foreign governments — a federal crime. The European Union harmonised protection in its Trade Secrets Directive (2016/943). Remedies include injunctions, damages and, in criminal cases, imprisonment. Misappropriation covers acquisition by theft, bribery, breach of confidence or espionage; non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) and employee confidentiality obligations are the main practical tools.

Trade secrets versus patents

Firms choose between patent and secrecy protection: patents give a time-limited monopoly (typically 20 years) but require public disclosure of the invention, whereas trade secrets can protect information that is not patentable, or keep processes hidden indefinitely — at the risk that a competitor patents the same invention or discovers it independently. Many recipes, manufacturing know-how and algorithms are protected as secrets for this reason.
