Afonso III (5 May 1210 – 16 February 1279), nicknamed the Boulonnais (o Bolonhês), was the fifth King of Portugal and the Algarve. He succeeded his brother Sancho II, who was deposed by Pope Innocent IV, taking full control of the kingdom in 1248.

Reign

Raised in France after his marriage to Matilda II, Countess of Boulogne, Afonso returned to Portugal during the civil conflict against his brother. As king he completed the Portuguese Reconquista by conquering Faro in 1249, ending Muslim rule in the Algarve, and settled the frontier with Castile by the Treaty of Badajoz (1267), under which the Algarve was recognized as Portuguese. He moved the court to Lisbon, convoked the first Cortes to include commoners at Leiria in 1254, and was succeeded by his son Denis.
