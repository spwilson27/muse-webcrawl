Science News is an American bi-weekly magazine devoted to short articles about new scientific and technical developments, typically gleaned from recent scientific publications. It has been published since 1922 by the Society for Science, a non-profit organisation founded in 1920 by journalist Edward W. Scripps and zoologist William Emerson Ritter.

History

The magazine began as the Science News-Letter, prepared by the Science Service wire agency for newspaper syndication, and became a magazine for general readers in its own right. Watson Davis, director of Science Service for decades, shaped its mission of bringing accurate science reporting to the public. The publication took its current name in 1966. A companion publication, Science News Explores (formerly Science News for Students), serves younger readers.

Content

Each issue covers developments across physics, biology, medicine, astronomy, earth science, and technology, written for a lay audience by staff writers and freelancers. The magazine's website publishes daily news, features, and blogs, and its archive stretching back to 1922 is a notable record of 20th-century science journalism.
