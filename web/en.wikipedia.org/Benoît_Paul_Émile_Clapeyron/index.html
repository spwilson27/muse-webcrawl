Benoît Paul Émile Clapeyron (26 January 1799 – 28 January 1864) was a French engineer and physicist, one of the founders of thermodynamics. He is best known for the Clapeyron equation and the Clausius–Clapeyron relation describing phase transitions, and for rescuing Sadi Carnot's work on heat engines from obscurity.

Thermodynamics

In his 1834 memoir on the motive power of heat, Clapeyron gave a mathematical formulation of Carnot's theory and represented the Carnot cycle graphically on a pressure–volume diagram. Combining the gas laws of Boyle, Charles, and Gay-Lussac, he arrived at the combined gas law pV = nRT, the form of the ideal gas law. The Clausius–Clapeyron equation, later refined by Rudolf Clausius, relates vapour pressure to temperature during a phase change.

Engineering

Trained at the École Polytechnique, Clapeyron worked on railway engineering in Russia and France, designing locomotives and metal bridges, and taught at the École des Ponts et Chaussées. His name is among the 72 inscribed on the Eiffel Tower.
