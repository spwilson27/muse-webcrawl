Script supervisor

A script supervisor (also called continuity supervisor or continuity person) is a member of a film or television crew responsible for maintaining internal continuity and for recording the production's progress in shooting the script.

Role

The script supervisor watches each take, noting details of action, dialogue, props, costumes, makeup, hair, set dressing and camera position so that scenes shot out of order, or retaken later, match one another when edited. They keep the lined script — a copy of the screenplay marked with vertical lines showing which portions of dialogue were covered by each camera setup — and prepare daily reports for the editor and production office.

Duties

  • Timing the script in pre-production to estimate running length.
  • Recording take numbers, lens information and director's comments on set.
  • Noting deviations from the script and ensuring improvised lines are logged for legal and editing purposes.
  • Tracking continuity errors and prompting actors who forget lines.
  • Preparing the daily production report and editor's log.

Department

On most productions the script supervisor works as a department of one, reporting to the director and working closely with the editor, cinematographer and assistant directors. Traditionally the role was filled predominantly by women and was once called "script girl", a term now considered outdated.
