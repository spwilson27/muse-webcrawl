General ledger

A general ledger is a bookkeeping ledger in which accounting data are posted from journals and aggregated from subledgers, such as accounts payable, accounts receivable, cash management, and fixed assets. The general ledger is the core of a company's financial records and the basis from which the trial balance and financial statements are prepared.

Structure

The ledger contains accounts arranged according to the chart of accounts: assets, liabilities, equity, revenue, and expenses. Under the double-entry system, every transaction is recorded with equal debits and credits, and each posting is transferred ("posted") from a journal — the book of first entry — to the relevant ledger accounts, with a folio reference linking the two. Subsidiary ledgers keep detail for high-volume accounts, and their totals are posted to a single control account in the general ledger.

Use

At the end of an accounting period the ledger accounts are balanced off and a trial balance is extracted to check the arithmetic of the records; adjusting and closing entries are then posted before the income statement and balance sheet are drawn up. In modern practice the general ledger is maintained by accounting software or an ERP system, which posts transactions automatically and maintains an audit trail of entries.
