6061 is a precipitation-hardened aluminium alloy containing magnesium and silicon as its major alloying elements. Originally developed in 1935, it is one of the most versatile and widely used heat-treatable aluminium alloys, valued for its medium-to-high strength, good corrosion resistance, and excellent weldability and machinability.

Properties

In the common T6 temper (solution heat-treated and artificially aged), 6061 has a yield strength of about 276 MPa — comparable to mild steel at roughly one third of the weight. Unlike high-strength 2000- and 7000-series alloys, 6061 can be welded by all conventional methods, though welding locally softens the heat-affected zone. It is easily extruded into complex profiles and anodizes well.

Applications

6061 is used in aircraft fittings, bicycle frames, camera lenses, marine fittings, structural tubing and extrusions, truck frames, rail car components, and consumer products such as the unibody enclosures of some laptops and phones. It is the standard choice where a structure must be strong, light, corrosion resistant, and easy to fabricate.
