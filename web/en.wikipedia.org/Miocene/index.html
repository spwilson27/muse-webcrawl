The Miocene is the first geological epoch of the Neogene Period, extending from about 23.03 to 5.333 million years ago. It was named by Charles Lyell; the name combines Greek words meaning "less" and "recent", reflecting its smaller proportion of modern marine invertebrates compared with the following Pliocene.

Climate and geography

The Miocene began with warming, peaked in the Miocene Climatic Optimum (about 17–14.5 million years ago), then cooled toward the icehouse conditions of the later Cenozoic. Continents approached their modern configuration: India had collided with Asia, the Mediterranean formed as Tethys closed, and North and South America were nearing connection. Grasslands expanded as climates dried, driving the evolution of grazing animals.

Life

Kelp forests appeared, greatly enriching coastal ecosystems. Fauna became recognizably modern: horses, deer, camels and early elephants diversified in the Americas and Eurasia, giant predatory sharks such as megalodon patrolled oceans, and apes diversified in Africa — the lineage eventually leading to hominins. The epoch ended with the Messinian salinity crisis, when the Mediterranean largely dried out.
