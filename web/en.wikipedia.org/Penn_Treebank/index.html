Penn Treebank

The Penn Treebank is a large annotated corpus of English text, created at the University of Pennsylvania beginning in 1989, in which sentences are annotated with part-of-speech tags and syntactic parse trees. It became the standard training and evaluation resource for statistical natural language processing.

Contents

The best-known component comprises about one million words of the 1989 Wall Street Journal, tagged and parsed in a phrase-structure style using the bracketing format familiar to NLP researchers (e.g., (S (NP ...) (VP ...))). The full Treebank also includes material from the Brown Corpus, the ATIS spoken-language corpus, and Switchboard. Its part-of-speech tagset of about 36–48 tags (including punctuation) and its conventions for empty categories and co-indexation shaped decades of parser development.

Impact

Trained and tested on the Wall Street Journal sections, statistical parsers of the 1990s and 2000s — including the influential Collins and Charniak parsers — reported results against Penn Treebank benchmarks, making the corpus one of the most cited resources in computational linguistics. Derivatives include the Penn Discourse Treebank and PropBank, which add discourse and semantic role annotations over the same sentences.
