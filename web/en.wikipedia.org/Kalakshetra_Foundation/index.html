Kalakshetra Foundation, formerly simply Kalakshetra, is an arts and cultural academy dedicated to the preservation of traditional Indian art forms, especially the Bharatanatyam dance form and Gandharvaveda music. Based in Besant Nagar, Chennai, India, it was founded in January 1936 by Rukmini Devi Arundale and her husband George Arundale.

History

Rukmini Devi, a theosophist who had trained in ballet in her youth, learned Bharatanatyam — then still associated with hereditary temple dancers and widely disdained — and gave her first public performance in 1935. In 1936 she established Kalakshetra to teach and refine the dance, removing erotic elements and systematising its technique and pedagogy. The institution began at the Theosophical Society campus in Adyar and later moved to its present 40-hectare campus in Besant Nagar. In 1993 the Parliament of India recognised Kalakshetra as an Institute of National Importance, and it was renamed the Kalakshetra Foundation, functioning as an autonomous body under the Ministry of Culture.

Activities

The foundation runs the Rukmini Devi College of Fine Arts, offering diploma and post-diploma courses in Bharatanatyam, Carnatic music, visual arts and crafts. It is renowned for its dance dramas, staged with live music, elaborate sets and the distinctive Kalakshetra style of costume and choreography. The campus also houses a weaving centre producing handloom saris, a Kalamkari unit, a research library and the Rukmini Devi Museum.

Legacy

Kalakshetra played a central role in the revival and mainstreaming of Bharatanatyam in the 20th century, training generations of dancers and teachers. Its annual December dance festival in Chennai is a major event in the Indian classical arts calendar.
