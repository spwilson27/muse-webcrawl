Twistor space

Twistor space
Introduced by: Roger Penrose (1967)
Relates to: complex projective 3-space, Minkowski space
Field: mathematical physics

In mathematical physics, twistor space is the complex vector space of solutions of the twistor equation, introduced by Roger Penrose in 1967 as part of twistor theory. It provides a geometric framework in which points of Minkowski spacetime correspond to complex projective lines.

Correspondence

Projective twistor space is complex projective 3-space. A point in (complexified, compactified) Minkowski space corresponds to a projective line in twistor space, while a twistor itself corresponds to a light ray in spacetime. This swaps the roles of points and lines in a way that makes conformal symmetry manifest.

Applications

Twistor methods have been used to construct self-dual Yang–Mills fields and gravitational instantons, in the Penrose transform, and more recently in computing scattering amplitudes in gauge theory through twistor string theory.
