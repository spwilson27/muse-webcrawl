The Grand Canal (Italian: Canal Grande) is a channel in Venice, Italy. It forms one of the major water-traffic corridors in the city, winding through the lagoon city in a reverse S-shape from the basin of Saint Mark to the railway station and the mainland causeway.

Some 3.8 kilometres long and between 30 and 90 metres wide, with an average depth of about five metres, the canal follows the course of an ancient river channel. Its banks are lined with more than 170 buildings, most dating from the 13th to the 18th centuries—palazzi built by the wealthy families of the Venetian Republic, including the Ca' d'Oro, Ca' Rezzonico, and Palazzo Barbarigo, their water entrances opening directly onto the canal.

Bridges and transport

For centuries the Rialto Bridge (completed 1591) was the only bridge across the canal; it has been joined by the wooden Accademia Bridge, the stone Scalzi Bridge, and Santiago Calatrava's Constitution Bridge (2008). Public transport runs along the canal in the form of water buses (vaporetti) and water taxis, while the gondola survives chiefly as a tourist attraction; the traghetti ferry gondolas still carry standing passengers across at fixed points.

Events

Each September the Regata Storica, a procession of historic boats followed by competitive rowing races, is held on the canal. On 21 November the city gives thanks for deliverance from plague at the church of Santa Maria della Salute, reached by a temporary pontoon bridge across the canal.
