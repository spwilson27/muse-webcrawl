The Handley Page Victor is a British jet-powered strategic bomber developed and produced by Handley Page. It was the third and final V bomber approved by the Royal Air Force (RAF), the others being the Vickers Valiant and Avro Vulcan. Entering service in 1958 as part of the United Kingdom's airborne nuclear deterrent, the Victor is notable for its distinctive crescent-shaped wing, which combined sweep angles suitable for both high-subsonic cruise and low-speed handling.

History

The Victor began as a response to specification B.35/46 for a jet bomber able to deliver a nuclear weapon from high altitude. Victor B.1 and B.2 bombers carried free-fall bombs including Blue Danube and Yellow Sun, and later the Blue Steel stand-off missile. After the nuclear deterrent passed to the Royal Navy's Polaris submarines in 1969, surviving Victors were converted to aerial refuelling tankers.

Later service

Victor tankers supported operations including the 1982 Falklands War — refuelling the famous Black Buck Vulcan raids — and the 1991 Gulf War. The type was retired in 1993, outlasting the other V bombers in RAF service.
