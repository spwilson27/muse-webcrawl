Theorema Egregium

The Theorema Egregium (Latin for "remarkable theorem") is a major result in differential geometry proved by Carl Friedrich Gauss in 1827. It states that the Gaussian curvature of a surface is an intrinsic property: it can be determined entirely by measurements of distances within the surface itself, without reference to the surrounding space.

Meaning

Curvature is defined as the product of the two principal curvatures at a point. Gauss's theorem shows that this product does not change when a surface is bent without stretching — so a flat sheet of paper can be rolled into a cylinder (whose curvature remains zero) but never wrapped onto a sphere without distortion. This explains why flat maps of the Earth always distort shapes or distances.

Significance

The theorem launched the intrinsic geometry of surfaces and paved the way for Riemann's generalization to higher-dimensional manifolds, which underlies Einstein's general theory of relativity. See also Gaussian curvature and Carl Friedrich Gauss.
