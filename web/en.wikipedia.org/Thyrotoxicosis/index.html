Thyrotoxicosis

SpecialtyEndocrinology
SymptomsHeat intolerance, weight loss, tremor, palpitations, anxiety, increased sweating
CausesGraves' disease, toxic multinodular goitre, thyroiditis, excess thyroid hormone ingestion
Diagnostic methodBlood tests: suppressed TSH, elevated T3 and T4
TreatmentAntithyroid drugs, beta blockers, radioactive iodine, surgery

Thyrotoxicosis is a clinical syndrome resulting from excess thyroid hormone action in the tissues. It is often — but not always — caused by overactivity of the thyroid gland itself, which is termed hyperthyroidism; thyrotoxicosis can also result from release of preformed hormone in thyroiditis or from exogenous hormone intake, in which the gland is not overactive.

Signs and symptoms

Symptoms reflect a hypermetabolic state: weight loss despite increased appetite, heat intolerance, sweating, tremor, palpitations, anxiety, irritability, frequent bowel movements, and muscle weakness. Signs include tachycardia or atrial fibrillation, fine tremor, warm moist skin, lid lag, and in Graves' disease, ophthalmopathy and pretibial myxedema. In the elderly, presentations may be “apathetic”, dominated by weight loss and atrial fibrillation.

Causes

The commonest cause is Graves' disease, an autoimmune disorder in which antibodies stimulate the TSH receptor. Other causes include toxic multinodular goitre, toxic adenoma, the early phase of thyroiditis (including subacute and postpartum thyroiditis), iodine-induced thyrotoxicosis, excess levothyroxine, and rare TSH-secreting pituitary tumors.

Thyroid storm

Thyroid storm is a rare, life-threatening exacerbation of thyrotoxicosis with fever, severe tachycardia, heart failure, and altered mental state, often precipitated by infection, surgery, or trauma. It is a medical emergency requiring intensive treatment.

Diagnosis

Diagnosis is confirmed by blood tests showing a suppressed TSH with elevated free T4 and/or T3. The underlying cause is investigated with TSH-receptor antibody testing, thyroid ultrasound, and radioactive iodine uptake and scan.

Treatment

Symptoms are controlled with beta blockers. Definitive treatments include antithyroid drugs (methimazole or propylthiouracil), radioactive iodine ablation, and thyroidectomy, chosen according to the cause and patient preference.
