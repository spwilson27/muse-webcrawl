G4 nations

G4 nations
Members: Brazil, Germany, India, Japan
Formed: 2004
Purpose: Support for each other's bids for permanent UN Security Council seats

The G4 nations comprise Brazil, Germany, India, and Japan, which are four countries that support each other's bids for permanent seats on the United Nations Security Council. Unlike the G7, where the common denominator is the economy and long-term political motives, the G4's primary aim is the permanent member seats on the Security Council.

Each of these four countries has figured among the elected non-permanent members of the council since the UN's establishment. Their economic and political influence has grown significantly in the last decades, reaching a scope comparable to the permanent members (P5). However, the G4's bids are often opposed by the Uniting for Consensus movement, and particularly by their economic competitors or political rivals.

Background

The UN currently has five permanent members with veto power in the Security Council: China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom, and the United States. The Security Council's structure reflects the geopolitical situation at the end of the Second World War, and reform proposals argue that it no longer represents the world's political and economic realities.

Reform proposals

In 2004 the four countries formed the G4 and jointly proposed expanding the Security Council with six new permanent seats, including two for African states, together with additional non-permanent seats. In 2005 they presented a draft resolution to the General Assembly. While many countries — including permanent members France and the United Kingdom — have expressed support for the G4 bids, opposition from regional rivals (such as Pakistan opposing India, China opposing Japan, and Argentina and Mexico opposing Brazil) and from the Uniting for Consensus group has kept comprehensive reform from being adopted. The G4 leaders continue to meet on the margins of the UN General Assembly to press their case.
