Ka-Bar
Type: Fighting/utility knife
Adopted: 1942 (USMC / USN)
Blade length: 7 in (17.8 cm), clip point
Manufacturer: Ka-Bar Knives (Cutco)

Ka-Bar (trademarked KA-BAR) is the name of the famous fighting/utility knife first issued to the United States Marine Corps in World War II as the 1219C2 combat knife, and subsequently adopted by the United States Navy as the Mark 2 utility knife. Renamed the 7" USMC Fighting/Utility Knife, it has been in production ever since and remains an icon of the Marine Corps.

Design

The knife features a 7-inch clip-point blade of 1095 carbon steel with a flat grind and a blackened finish, a stacked leather-washer grip, and a steel crossguard and pommel. It was designed to serve simultaneously as a combat knife and a general camp and utility tool for opening crates and cans, digging, and cutting wire — tasks for which earlier Mark 1 trench knives were ill suited.

History and name

Production began in 1942, with Camillus Cutlery Company the first and largest wartime maker; other producers included Union Cutlery, whose brand name for its line of knives, "Ka-Bar," came — according to company lore — from a barely legible testimonial letter from a trapper who had killed a wounded bear ("kill a bear," compressed to "ka-bar") with one of its knives. The name became generic for the pattern. Ka-Bar Knives, based in Olean, New York, continues to manufacture the knife for military and civilian markets.
