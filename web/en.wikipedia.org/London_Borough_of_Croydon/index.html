The London Borough of Croydon is a London borough in South London, England, forming part of Outer London. It was formed in 1965 from the merger of the County Borough of Croydon with the Urban District of Coulsdon and Purley. Its main district is Croydon, a large town that became a major commercial and retail centre in the second half of the twentieth century.

History

Croydon was recorded in the Domesday Book of 1086 and grew as a market town on the Roman road from London to the south coast. The borough grew rapidly with the coming of the railways in the nineteenth century. During the 1960s and 1970s Croydon developed a dense cluster of office buildings, earning a skyline sometimes compared to Manhattan.

Governance

Croydon London Borough Council is the local authority, meeting at Croydon Town Hall. Since October 2022 the borough has had a directly elected executive mayor, the first in its history, following a referendum in 2021. The borough returns members to several parliamentary constituencies including Croydon Central, Croydon South and Croydon North (later Croydon West).

Transport

The borough is served by East Croydon station, one of the busiest railway stations in Britain outside central London, and by the Tramlink light rail network, which opened in 2000 and links Croydon with Wimbledon, Beckenham and New Addington.
