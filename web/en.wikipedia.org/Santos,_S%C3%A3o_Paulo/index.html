Santos is a municipality in the state of São Paulo, Brazil, located partly on the island of São Vicente and partly on the mainland, about 80 kilometres from the city of São Paulo. Founded in 1546, it is home to the Port of Santos, the busiest container port in Latin America, and is internationally famous as the home of Santos Futebol Clube, the club of Pelé.

History

Portuguese settlement of the São Vicente coast dates to 1532; the village of Santos was founded by Braz Cubas in 1546 around the Santa Casa de Misericórdia hospital. The town grew with sugar, then whale oil, and above all coffee: in the nineteenth century the completion of the São Paulo Railway over the Serra do Mar made Santos the great outlet for São Paulo's coffee boom, and its merchants built the ornate Coffee Exchange (Bolsa do Café, 1922). Coffee wealth, immigration and epidemics — Santos was ravaged by yellow fever and plague — shaped the modern city.

The port and economy

The organised port, inaugurated in 1892, today extends for many kilometres along both banks of the Santos estuary, handling coffee, soy, sugar, containers and much of Brazil's foreign trade; it accounts for roughly a quarter of the country's trade by value. Tourism is drawn by Santos's long beachfront, whose seaside gardens are among the largest in the world, and by attractions such as the Coffee Museum, the aquarium and the memorial to Pelé at the Vila Belmiro stadium of Santos FC.

Geography and character

Santos enjoys a humid subtropical climate moderated by the Atlantic. Its beachfront skyline is noted for several apartment buildings that visibly lean because of foundations in the soft clay soil — a local curiosity that engineers have worked to stabilise. The city also anchors the Baixada Santista metropolitan region with neighbouring São Vicente, Guarujá, Cubatão and Praia Grande.
