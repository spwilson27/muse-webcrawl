Bottleneck (guitar)

A bottleneck is a type of slide used for playing guitar, originally made from the neck of a glass bottle. In slide guitar playing, the bottleneck is worn on one finger of the fretting hand and glided along the strings to change pitch continuously, rather than pressing the strings against the frets.

History and technique

The technique developed in the early 20th century among blues musicians in the American South, influenced by Hawaiian steel guitar and single-string African instruments. Players improvised slides from bottle necks, knife blades, and medicine bottles; the glass neck of a wine bottle, smoothed and cut, became standard. Modern slides are manufactured from glass, metal (steel or brass), or ceramic, each material giving a different tone — glass is smoother and warmer, metal brighter and with more sustain.

Use

Slide playing is usually done in open tunings, allowing full chords to be played with the slide barred across the strings. Bottleneck style is a hallmark of Delta blues and Southern rock; celebrated exponents include Robert Johnson, Muddy Waters, Elmore James, and Duane Allman, who famously used a glass Coricidin medicine bottle.
