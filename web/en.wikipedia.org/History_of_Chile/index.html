History of Chile

Spanish conquestFrom 1536; Santiago founded 1541
IndependenceDeclared 1818
Military dictatorship1973–1990

The history of Chile spans from the settlement of the region by Indigenous peoples, including the Mapuche, to the modern republic. Spanish conquistadors under Pedro de Valdivia founded Santiago in 1541, but fierce Mapuche resistance in the Arauco War kept the south beyond colonial control for centuries. Chile was part of the Spanish Empire as the Captaincy General of Chile.

Independence and the 19th century

Chile declared independence in 1818 after the armies of Bernardo O'Higgins and José de San Martín defeated royalist forces. In the War of the Pacific (1879–1884) Chile defeated Peru and Bolivia and annexed nitrate-rich northern territories, fuelling an export-led economy.

20th century and dictatorship

After decades of parliamentary and presidential politics, the socialist Salvador Allende was elected in 1970. His government was overthrown in the military coup of 11 September 1973, bringing Augusto Pinochet to power. The dictatorship (1973–1990) combined severe human-rights abuses with market-oriented economic reforms. Democracy was restored in 1990, and Chile has since been known for relative institutional stability alongside persistent debates over inequality and the legacy of the Pinochet-era constitution.
