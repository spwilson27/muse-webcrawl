Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs

Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs (OIRA)
Formed: 1981
Parent agency: Office of Management and Budget (OMB)
Headquarters: Washington, D.C.
Role: Central review of U.S. federal regulations

The Office of Information and Regulatory Affairs (OIRA) is a division of the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) within the Executive Office of the President of the United States. Created by the Paperwork Reduction Act of 1980, OIRA reviews draft regulations from federal agencies, coordinates federal information policy, and oversees government statistics and information collection.

Regulatory review

Under Executive Order 12866 (1993), as supplemented by later orders, executive agencies must submit "significant regulatory actions" to OIRA before publication. OIRA reviews proposed and final rules for consistency with presidential priorities and with principles of cost-benefit analysis, and it may return rules to agencies for reconsideration. The review process, including meetings with outside parties, is documented publicly via Reginfo.gov and regulations.gov.

Other functions

OIRA also implements the Paperwork Reduction Act by approving agency information-collection requests, sets policies for federal statistical agencies, and issues government-wide guidance on information quality, privacy, and regulatory analysis — notably OMB Circular A-4 on regulatory impact analysis.

Criticism and debate

OIRA's centralised review has been both praised as a check ensuring coherent, cost-effective regulation and criticised as a source of delay and of political influence over agency expertise. Its administrator, appointed by the President (with Senate confirmation since 1989), is sometimes called the U.S. "regulatory czar".
