Vistula–Oder Offensive
Date12 January – 2 February 1945
LocationCentral Poland and eastern Germany
ResultSoviet victory

The Vistula–Oder Offensive was a successful Red Army operation on the Eastern Front in the European theatre of World War II in January 1945. The army made a major advance into German-held territory, capturing Kraków, Warsaw, and Poznań, and reached the Oder River less than 70 kilometres from Berlin.

Background

After Operation Bagration in the summer of 1944, Soviet forces had advanced to the Vistula River and halted to regroup and resupply. The offensive, conducted principally by the 1st Belorussian Front under Marshal Georgy Zhukov and the 1st Ukrainian Front under Marshal Ivan Konev, massed some 2.2 million men against roughly 450,000 German defenders of Army Group A.

The offensive

The attack opened on 12 January 1945 from bridgeheads at Sandomierz and Magnuszew. Warsaw was liberated on 17 January; Kraków fell two days later. Soviet spearheads advanced up to 500 kilometres in under three weeks, liberating the Auschwitz concentration camp on 27 January and establishing bridgeheads over the Oder by early February. The speed of the advance overwhelmed German defences and precipitated a mass flight of German civilians westward.

Aftermath

The offensive brought the Red Army to within striking distance of Berlin and set the stage for the final Battle of Berlin in April 1945. German casualties were estimated in the hundreds of thousands; Soviet losses were also heavy, commonly cited at around 194,000 killed, wounded, and missing.
