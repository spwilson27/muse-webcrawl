Antikythera is a small Greek island lying at the edge of the Aegean Sea, between Kythera and Crete. Its name means "opposite Kythera". The island is best known as the site of the shipwreck, discovered by sponge divers in 1900–1901, from which the Antikythera mechanism and the Antikythera Ephebe were recovered.

History

Known in antiquity as Aegilia, the island was settled from the Neolithic period and was later a base for Cilician pirates until Pompey cleared the Mediterranean of piracy. In the modern era it belonged successively to Venetian and Ottoman spheres before joining Greece. The wreck found off its north-east coast, dated to the first century BC, yielded bronze and marble statues, coins, and the famous geared astronomical mechanism, now in the National Archaeological Museum of Athens.

The island today

The island's main settlement is the port of Potamós. Antikythera is a notable bird-observatory site on migratory routes and home to a population of wild goats. In recent years the island has attracted attention for schemes offering incentives to new residents to counter depopulation.
