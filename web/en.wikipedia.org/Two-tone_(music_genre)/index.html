Two-tone (music genre)

The black-and-white checkerboard became the visual signature of 2 Tone.

Two-tone or 2 Tone is a genre of British popular music of the late 1970s and early 1980s that fused traditional Jamaican ska, rocksteady, and reggae music with elements of punk rock and new wave music. Its name derives from 2 Tone Records, a record label founded in 1979 by Jerry Dammers of the Specials, and references a desire to transcend and defuse racial tensions in Thatcher-era Britain: many two-tone bands featured a mix of Black, white, and multiracial line-ups.

History

The two-tone sound originated with young musicians in Coventry, England, who grew up listening to 1960s Jamaican music. Key bands included the Specials, Madness, the Selecter, the Beat (known in North America as the English Beat), Bad Manners, and the Bodysnatchers. Signature songs included "A Message to You Rudy", "Ghost Town", "One Step Beyond", and "Mirror in the Bathroom".

Style and legacy

The movement was associated with black-and-white checkerboard graphics and the "Walt Jabsco" rude boy figure. Although the original wave faded by the mid-1980s, two-tone strongly influenced the third wave of ska in the United States during the 1990s and remains a touchstone of British popular culture.
