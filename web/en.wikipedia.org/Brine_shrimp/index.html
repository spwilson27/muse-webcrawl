Brine shrimp are small aquatic crustaceans of the genus Artemia, the only genus in the family Artemiidae. They inhabit hypersaline lakes and salt pans around the world and are notable for thriving in water far saltier than seawater, where most predators cannot survive.

Biology

Adult brine shrimp are typically 8–10 mm long. Females can reproduce either by laying dormant cysts or by giving birth to live nauplii. The cysts can survive desiccation for years and hatch within hours when placed in salt water.

Uses

Brine shrimp nauplii are widely used as live food for fish and shellfish larvae in aquaculture and the aquarium trade. Their cysts are harvested commercially from places such as the Great Salt Lake in Utah. They are also sold to children as novelty pets under the name "Sea-Monkeys".
