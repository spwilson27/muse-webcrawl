Casualty (person)

DefinitionA person killed, injured, captured, or missing as a result of war or disaster
Military categoriesKIA, WIA, MIA, POW, DOW
Civilian useVictims of accidents, disasters, and attacks

A casualty, as a term in military usage, is a person in military service, not necessarily a combatant, who becomes unavailable for duty due to any of several circumstances, including death, injury, illness, capture, or desertion. In civilian usage, a casualty is a person who is killed or injured by an accident or in war.

Military usage

In military terminology, a casualty is a person who is lost to an organization by having been declared dead, duty status whereabouts unknown (missing), or having been seriously ill or injured and evacuated. Casualties are commonly reported in categories:

  • KIA — killed in action
  • WIA — wounded in action
  • MIA — missing in action
  • POW — prisoner of war
  • DOW — died of wounds

For armed forces, becoming a casualty has legal significance under the law of armed conflict: personnel who are wounded, sick, shipwrecked, or captured are protected persons under the Geneva Conventions, and hors de combat fighters may not be attacked.

Civilian usage

In news reports of disasters, accidents, and attacks, "casualties" may refer to dead and injured together, and sometimes loosely to fatalities alone — an ambiguity that frequently causes confusion. Emergency services instead tend to speak of injuries and fatalities separately, and many style guides advise avoiding the word "casualty" when a precise figure for deaths or injuries is available.

Etymology

The word derives from the Latin casualis and casus ("chance", "fall"), entering English through the sense of a chance occurrence; its specific meaning of a killed or wounded soldier developed in the fifteenth and sixteenth centuries.
