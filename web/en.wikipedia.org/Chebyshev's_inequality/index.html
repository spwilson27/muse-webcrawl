Chebyshev's inequality

Chebyshev's inequality is a result in probability theory that gives a lower bound on the proportion of values of a random variable that lie within a certain number of standard deviations of its mean, for any distribution with finite mean and variance.

Specifically, for a random variable X with mean μ and standard deviation σ, and any k > 1: P(|X − μ| ≥ kσ) ≤ 1/k². Thus at least 1 − 1/k² of values lie within k standard deviations of the mean — for example, at least 75% within two standard deviations, and at least 88.9% within three.

First stated by Irénée-Jules Bienaymé (1853) and proved by Pafnuty Chebyshev (1867), the inequality is a corollary of Markov's inequality and is used to prove the weak law of large numbers. Though not sharp for specific distributions, its generality makes it fundamental.
