Screenplay

A screenplay, or script, is a written work for a film, television programme or video game, written by a screenwriter. It describes the movement, actions, expressions and dialogues of the characters, together with scene directions, and serves both as the blueprint from which a production is planned and budgeted and as the primary creative document of the work. A screenplay written on speculation, in the hope of being sold, is called a spec screenplay; others are commissioned as works for hire.

Format

Screenplays follow a highly standardised format, traditionally typed in 12-point Courier so that one page corresponds roughly to one minute of screen time. A typical feature script runs 90–120 pages. Scene headings (slug lines) state interior or exterior, location and time: for example,

INT. COFFEE SHOP - DAY

Below the heading come action lines describing what is seen, then character names in capitals above their dialogue, with parentheticals for brief performance notes. Transitions such as CUT TO: are used sparingly in modern practice. Software such as Final Draft automates the formatting, and the standard is enforced because producers, actors and department heads all estimate cost and schedule from page counts.

Structure and types

Commercial screenwriting commonly follows the three-act structure popularised by Syd Field's Screenplay (1979): setup, confrontation and resolution, organised around plot points and a midpoint. Other frameworks include the hero's journey and various sequence and beat-sheet models. Distinct forms include the shooting script (numbered scenes, with camera directions for production), the spec script, television scripts with act breaks for commercials, and teleplays for episodic and streaming series. Stage plays, by contrast, are called playscripts and are dialogue-centred.

History and craft

Early silent films used brief scenarios; the continuity script developed in the 1910s added the detailed scene-by-scene breakdown that remains the basis of production planning. Screenwriting is recognised by major awards — the Academy Awards for Best Original and Best Adapted Screenplay, and honours from the Writers Guild of America, whose members' credits and residuals are governed by guild agreements.
