Huqin (Chinese: 胡琴; pinyin: húqín) is a family of bowed string instruments used in Chinese music. The word literally means "barbarian instrument," reflecting the family's origin among peoples to the north and west of China during the Tang and Song dynasties. Members of the family share a characteristic construction: a round, square, hexagonal, or octagonal sound box covered with snakeskin (usually python) or thin wood, a long fretless neck, and typically two strings played with a bow whose hair passes between the strings.

History

The huqin family is generally believed to have developed from the xiqin, an instrument associated with the Xi people of Central Asia, around the time of the Tang dynasty (618–907). By the Song dynasty the instrument had become established in Chinese court and folk music. Over the following centuries regional variants proliferated: the two-stringed erhu became the most widely known member, while the jinghu, with its bamboo body and piercing tone, became the lead melodic instrument of Peking opera. The banhu, with a coconut-shell resonator and wooden face, is prominent in northern Chinese folk traditions.

Construction and playing technique

Most huqin are held vertically on the left thigh, with the sound box resting against the player's leg. The left hand stops the strings by pressing them without the aid of frets or a fingerboard—the string does not touch the neck—which permits the wide vibrato, slides, and pitch inflections characteristic of Chinese string playing. The bow is inseparable from the strings; its hair is threaded between them, and the player tensions the hair with the fingers of the right hand.

Members of the family

Principal members include the erhu (二胡), gaohu (高胡, developed in the 1920s for a higher range), zhonghu (中胡, alto), jinghu (京胡), banhu (板胡), yehu (椰胡), and the four-stringed sihu (四胡) of Mongolian and northern Chinese music. In the modern Chinese orchestra, the erhu, gaohu, and zhonghu form the bowed-string sections analogous to violins and violas in a Western orchestra.
