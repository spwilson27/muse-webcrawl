Punishment

Punishment, in the legal and ethical sense, is the imposition of a penalty or deprivation on a person by an authority in response to an act judged to be wrongdoing. It ranges from fines and community service to imprisonment and, in some jurisdictions, capital punishment. Outside the criminal law, punishment also occurs in families, schools, workplaces, and religious practice, and in psychology it denotes any consequence that decreases the likelihood of a behavior.

Justifications

Philosophers have defended punishment on several grounds. Retribution holds that wrongdoers deserve to suffer in proportion to their offense. Deterrence aims to discourage the offender (specific deterrence) and others (general deterrence) from similar acts. Incapacitation prevents further harm by restraining the offender. Rehabilitation seeks to reform the offender into a law-abiding citizen, and restoration focuses on repairing harm to victims and communities. Critics argue that some justifications are inconsistent with human dignity or ineffective in practice, and criminological evidence suggests that the certainty of punishment deters more than its severity.

History

Early legal codes such as the Code of Hammurabi prescribed corporal and capital penalties, often on a lex talionis ("eye for an eye") basis. Public corporal punishment, mutilation, and execution were common through the early modern period. Enlightenment reformers such as Cesare Beccaria, in On Crimes and Punishments (1764), argued for proportionate, swift, and certain penalties, and the nineteenth century saw the rise of the penitentiary as the dominant sanction. Twentieth-century developments included probation, parole, juvenile justice, and human-rights limits on cruel and unusual punishment.

Types

Common forms include fines, restitution, community service, probation, imprisonment, corporal punishment (still legally used in some countries), and the death penalty, retained by a minority of states. Collateral forms—loss of licenses, disenfranchisement, deportation—may accompany formal sentences.
