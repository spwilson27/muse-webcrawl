Francesco Sforza

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Francesco I Sforza (23 July 1401 – 8 March 1466) was an Italian condottiero who founded the Sforza dynasty and became Duke of Milan in 1450. One of the most successful mercenary captains of the Italian Wars' early phase, he rose from peasant stock to rule one of Italy's great states.

Early life and career

Francesco was the son of Muzio Attendolo, called "Sforza" ("Force"), a celebrated condottiero from Romagna. He campaigned from boyhood in his father's company and inherited its command after Muzio drowned in 1424. Over the following decades he served, in turn and sometimes simultaneously, the Visconti dukes of Milan, Florence, Venice, Naples and the Papal States, acquiring lands in the Marche and a reputation as an able and comparatively humane commander.

Duke of Milan

In 1441 he married Bianca Maria Visconti, the only (illegitimate) child of Filippo Maria Visconti, Duke of Milan. When Filippo Maria died in 1447 without a male heir, Milan proclaimed the Golden Ambrosian Republic. Sforza, hired as its captain-general, instead maneuvered against the republic; after a siege that reduced the city to famine, he entered Milan in triumph on 25 February 1450 and was proclaimed duke.

As duke he proved an energetic ruler: he rebuilt the Castello Sforzesco, founded the Ospedale Maggiore, dug the Martesana canal, and reformed taxation and administration. His diplomacy brought the Peace of Lodi (1454) and the Italian League, inaugurating a generation of relative equilibrium among the Italian states.

Legacy

Francesco's son Galeazzo Maria succeeded him in 1466; the dynasty he founded ruled Milan, with interruptions, until 1535. Niccolò Machiavelli treated Francesco Sforza in The Prince as the exemplary "new prince" who rose by his own arms and ability rather than by fortune alone.
