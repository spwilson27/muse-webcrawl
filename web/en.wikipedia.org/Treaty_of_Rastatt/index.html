Treaty of Rastatt

Signed7 March 1714
LocationRastatt, Margraviate of Baden
PartiesFrance; Austria (Habsburg monarchy)

The Treaty of Rastatt, signed on 7 March 1714, was a peace treaty between France and Austria that completed the settlement of the War of the Spanish Succession. It supplemented the Treaty of Utrecht of 1713, which Austria's ruler, Emperor Charles VI, had initially refused to accept. A companion Treaty of Baden, signed in September 1714, extended the peace to the Holy Roman Empire as a whole.

Terms

Austria received the Spanish Netherlands, Milan, Naples, and Sardinia from the former Spanish possessions, while France retained Landau and Alsace, including Strasbourg, but restored Freiburg and other places on the right bank of the Rhine. The electors of Bavaria and Cologne, France's allies, were restored to their territories and dignities.

Negotiations

The treaty was negotiated by Prince Eugene of Savoy for Austria and Marshal Villars for France, former battlefield opponents whose personal rapport helped bring the talks to a successful conclusion.
