Radio Caroline

Radio Caroline broadcast from ships anchored in international waters.

Radio Caroline is a British radio station founded in 1964 by Irish businessman Ronan O'Rahilly. It began as an unlicensed offshore "pirate" station broadcasting from a ship, the MV Fredericia (renamed Caroline), anchored in international waters off the Essex coast, outside British jurisdiction. It was created to circumvent the BBC's monopoly on radio broadcasting and the record companies' control of airplay, bringing pop music to British listeners at a time when the BBC broadcast only a few hours of it per week.

History

Caroline quickly attracted audiences of millions. In 1967 the Marine, &c., Broadcasting (Offences) Act outlawed offshore stations, and most closed — but Caroline continued, broadcasting from the Mi Amigo and later the Ross Revenge. The Mi Amigo sank in a storm in 1980; the Ross Revenge was raided in 1989 and finally towed to Tilbury in 1991. Many Caroline DJs, including Tony Blackburn and Johnnie Walker, later joined the new BBC Radio 1.

Today

Radio Caroline now broadcasts legally via satellite, the internet, DAB and a medium-wave transmitter on 648 kHz from Orford Ness, and the preserved Ross Revenge hosts special broadcasts. The station's story inspired the 2009 film The Boat That Rocked.
