Songwriter


A songwriter composing at the piano

A songwriter is a musician who professionally composes musical compositions or writes lyrics for songs. A songwriter can also be called a composer, although the latter term tends to be used mainly for individuals from the classical music genre and film scoring. A songwriter who mainly writes the lyrics for a song is referred to as a lyricist. The pressure from the music industry to produce popular hits means that song writing is often an activity for which the tasks are distributed among a number of people.

Songwriting process

Songwriters may work alone or in teams, writing melody, harmony, and lyrics in various orders — music first, words first, or simultaneously. In the modern pop industry, specialized "topliners" write vocal melodies and lyrics over existing backing tracks produced by others. Songwriters commonly demo their work, pitching songs to recording artists, publishers, and music supervisors for film and advertising.

Copyright and royalties

Songwriters hold copyright in the musical composition (as distinct from any particular sound recording) and earn royalties when a song is reproduced, performed publicly, broadcast, or streamed. Performance royalties are collected by performing rights organisations such as ASCAP, BMI, and PRS. In Nashville and other songwriting centres, staff writers sign exclusive publishing deals and write on assignment.

Singer-songwriters

A singer-songwriter performs their own material, accompanying themselves, usually on guitar or piano. The confessional singer-songwriter movement of the 1960s and 1970s — exemplified by Bob Dylan, Joni Mitchell, and Carole King — placed the writer's own voice and perspective at the centre of popular music.
