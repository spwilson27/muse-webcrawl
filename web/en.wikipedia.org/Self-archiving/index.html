Self-archiving

Self-archiving is the act of depositing a free copy of an electronic document, usually a scholarly article, on the open web in order to provide open access to it. It is one of the two main routes to open access publishing, often called the "green road," the other being publication in an open access journal (the "gold road").

Forms

Authors may deposit preprints or postprints in a disciplinary repository such as arXiv, in an institutional repository run by their university, or on a personal website. Publisher policies vary: many allow the accepted manuscript (postprint) to be archived after an embargo period, while the publisher's formatted version of record usually may not. The SHERPA/RoMEO service catalogues publisher self-archiving policies.

History and mandates

The practice grew with the preprint servers of the early 1990s and was formalised as a strategy in the open access movement, notably in the Budapest Open Access Initiative of 2002. Many research funders and universities now mandate self-archiving of publicly funded research outputs, sometimes with a requirement that deposit occur upon acceptance for publication.
