Psycho (1960 film)

Psycho

Directed by: Alfred Hitchcock

Screenplay by: Joseph Stefano

Based on: Psycho by Robert Bloch

Starring: Anthony Perkins, Janet Leigh, Vera Miles, John Gavin, Martin Balsam

Music by: Bernard Herrmann

Released: 1960

Budget: approx. $807,000

Psycho is a 1960 American horror film produced and directed by Alfred Hitchcock. The screenplay, written by Joseph Stefano, was based on the 1959 novel of the same name by Robert Bloch. The film stars Anthony Perkins as Norman Bates, the shy manager of a remote motel, and Janet Leigh as Marion Crane, a secretary on the run after embezzling $40,000 who stops at the motel for the night.

Plot

After stealing money from her employer, Marion Crane flees Phoenix and, caught in a rainstorm, takes a room at the Bates Motel. Its proprietor, Norman Bates, lives in the gothic house above the motel, apparently dominated by his invalid mother. Marion is murdered in the film's famous shower scene — a 45-second sequence of rapid cuts set to Bernard Herrmann's shrieking string score — and investigators, including private detective Milton Arbogast and Marion's sister Lila, trace her to the motel. The climax reveals that Norman, whose mother died years earlier, has preserved her corpse and developed a split personality in which "Mother" commits the murders.

Production and reception

Hitchcock financed the film himself and shot it in black and white with his television crew for under a million dollars to keep costs down and evade studio interference. He bought the rights to Bloch's novel anonymously and purchased as many copies as possible to preserve the twist. Audiences were famously instructed not to enter the theatre after the film had started. Initial reviews were mixed, but the film was a major box-office success and was nominated for four Academy Awards, including Best Director and Best Supporting Actress for Leigh.

Legacy

Psycho is now regarded as one of the greatest films ever made and a landmark of the horror genre; it is credited with popularizing the slasher film, loosening Hollywood censorship of violence and sexuality, and making the mid-film murder of its apparent protagonist a famous violation of narrative convention. It was selected for preservation in the U.S. National Film Registry in 1992 and spawned three sequels, a 1998 shot-for-shot remake by Gus Van Sant, and the television series Bates Motel (2013–2017).
