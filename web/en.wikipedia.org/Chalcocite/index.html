Chalcocite

Chalcocite is a copper sulfide mineral with the formula Cu2S. It is one of the most important ores of copper, prized for its very high copper content (about 80% by weight) and the ease with which the metal can be extracted.

FormulaCu2S
Crystal systemMonoclinic (below 103.5 °C)
Hardness2.5–3 (Mohs)
ColourDark grey to black

Occurrence

Chalcocite typically forms in the supergene enrichment zones of copper deposits, where oxidising groundwater redeposits copper above primary ores such as chalcopyrite and bornite. Classic localities include Cornwall in England, and Butte, Montana, and the copper districts of Arizona in the United States. It is commonly associated with covellite, digenite and native copper.

Properties

Fresh chalcocite is lead-grey and metallic but tarnishes to dull black or blue on exposure. It is soft, sectile, and conducts electricity; above about 103.5 °C its crystal structure changes to a hexagonal high-temperature form.
