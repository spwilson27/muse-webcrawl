Wastewater treatment

Wastewater treatment is a process used to remove contaminants from wastewater and convert it into an effluent that can be returned to the water cycle. Once back in the water cycle, the effluent creates an acceptable impact on the environment or is reused for various purposes (called water reclamation). The treatment process takes place in a wastewater treatment plant. There are several kinds of wastewater which are treated at the appropriate type of wastewater treatment plant. For domestic wastewater (also called municipal wastewater or sewage), the treatment plant is called a sewage treatment plant. For industrial wastewater, treatment either takes place in a separate industrial wastewater treatment plant, or in a sewage treatment plant (usually after some form of pre-treatment).

Stages of treatment

Preliminary treatment removes coarse solids and grit through screening and settling. Primary treatment uses sedimentation tanks to remove settleable organic and inorganic solids, typically reducing suspended solids and biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) by roughly a third to a half.

Secondary treatment uses biological processes — such as the activated sludge process, trickling filters, or rotating biological contactors — in which microorganisms consume dissolved and suspended organic matter. Secondary clarification then separates the biological floc from the treated water.

Tertiary treatment further polishes the effluent, removing nutrients such as nitrogen and phosphorus, and disinfecting the water with chlorine, ozone, or ultraviolet light before discharge or reuse.

Sludge treatment

Solids removed during treatment form sludge, which is commonly thickened, digested anaerobically (producing biogas), dewatered, and then disposed of, incinerated, or applied to land as biosolids.

Global situation

According to United Nations estimates, a large share of the world's wastewater — particularly in low-income countries — is discharged untreated, contributing to water pollution and the spread of waterborne disease. Expanding treatment capacity is part of Sustainable Development Goal 6 (clean water and sanitation).
