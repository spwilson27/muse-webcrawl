State Library of New South Wales
Location: Macquarie Street, Sydney, Australia
Established: 1826 (as the Australian Subscription Library)
Collection: over 5 million items
Buildings: Mitchell Library (1910) and Macquarie Street building (1988)

The State Library of New South Wales, part of which is known as the Mitchell Library, is a large heritage-listed special collections, reference and research library open to the public in Sydney, Australia. It is the oldest library in Australia, tracing its origins to the Australian Subscription Library founded in 1826.

History

The library began in 1826 as a subscription library for colonists. The New South Wales government purchased it in 1869, establishing the Sydney Free Public Library. In 1895 it was renamed the Public Library of New South Wales, and in 1975 it took its present name.

Mitchell Library

The Mitchell Library is named for David Scott Mitchell, whose bequest of some 60,000 items of Australiana—books, manuscripts, pictures, and maps—came with the condition that a dedicated building be constructed. The Mitchell Wing opened in 1910 and houses the famous Mitchell Reading Room. The library's holdings are especially rich in material relating to Captain James Cook and early European exploration of the Pacific.

Collections and services

The library holds more than five million items, including the papers of many notable Australians, extensive newspaper and photograph collections, and born-digital material collected through legal deposit and web archiving programs such as Pandora. It supports public libraries across New South Wales and offers exhibitions, learning programs, and digitisation services.
