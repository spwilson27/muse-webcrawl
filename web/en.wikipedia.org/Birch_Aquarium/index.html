Birch Aquarium at Scripps is the public aquarium and outreach centre of the Scripps Institution of Oceanography at the University of California, San Diego. Located on a bluff above the Pacific Ocean in La Jolla, it opened at its present site in 1992 and is named for Stephen Birch and his foundation, whose gift helped fund the building. It serves as the interpretive arm of Scripps, presenting the institution's ocean and earth science research to the public.

Exhibits

The aquarium houses more than 60 habitats of Pacific marine life, from the cold waters of the Pacific Northwest to California's kelp forests and the tropical seas of Mexico. Its signature exhibit is a large kelp forest tank, and other displays feature sharks and rays, coral reefs, seahorses and seadragons — for which Birch Aquarium runs a notable captive-breeding programme — and a tide-pool plaza overlooking the ocean. Exhibits also address climate science and Scripps research, and the museum includes the historic Scripps Hall of oceanography's legacy collections.

Education and outreach

Birch Aquarium runs school programmes, summer camps, tide-pooling and snorkelling activities, and public lectures, reaching hundreds of thousands of visitors and students each year. It also operates a seahorse and weedy seadragon conservation programme and participates in species survival efforts.
