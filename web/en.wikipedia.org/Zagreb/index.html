Zagreb is the capital and largest city of Croatia, located in the country's northwest along the Sava River, at the southern slopes of the Medvednica mountain. It is Croatia's political, economic, cultural, and scientific centre, home to about a quarter of the national population in the wider metropolitan area.

History

Zagreb grew from two medieval settlements: Gradec, a free royal city chartered in 1242, and Kaptol, the seat of the bishopric founded in 1094. The two were formally united with surrounding settlements in 1850. Zagreb was the capital of the Croatian lands within the Habsburg monarchy and Austria-Hungary, and after 1918 part of Yugoslavia, where it became the country's second city and a major industrial centre. In 1991 it became the capital of independent Croatia. A damaging earthquake in March 2020 caused extensive harm to the historic centre.

Geography and cityscape

The historic Upper Town (Gornji Grad) preserves medieval streets, the Stone Gate, St. Mark's Square with its tiled-roof church, and the Lotrščak Tower. The 19th-century Lower Town (Donji Grad) is laid out around the "Green Horseshoe" of parks and squares. The city expanded south across the Sava after 1945 with the construction of Novi Zagreb.

Culture

Zagreb hosts the University of Zagreb (founded 1669), the Croatian National Theatre, numerous museums including the Museum of Broken Relationships, and events such as the Zagreb Advent, repeatedly voted among Europe's best Christmas markets.
