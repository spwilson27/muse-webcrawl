Tenochtitlan

FoundedTraditionally 13 March 1325
LocationIsland in Lake Texcoco, Valley of Mexico (modern Mexico City)
StatusCapital of the Aztec Empire; destroyed 1521

Tenochtitlan, also known as Mexico-Tenochtitlan, was a large Mexican altepetl (city-state) located on an island in Lake Texcoco in the Valley of Mexico. Founded traditionally in 1325, it became the capital of the expanding Aztec Empire in the 15th century and was one of the largest cities in the world, with a population estimated at 200,000–300,000 at its peak.

Foundation and growth

According to Mexica tradition, their god Huitzilopochtli directed them to found a city where an eagle perched on a cactus devoured a serpent — an image now on the flag of Mexico. The city was joined to the mainland by three great causeways, divided into four quadrants, and threaded by canals. Floating gardens called chinampas in the surrounding lake produced abundant food.

The Great Temple

At its heart stood the Templo Mayor, a great pyramid with twin shrines to Huitzilopochtli and the rain god Tlaloc, within a walled sacred precinct that included a ball court and the calmecac school. The neighboring city of Tlatelolco housed the empire's greatest market, described in astonished detail by Spanish chroniclers.

Conquest and legacy

After a siege by Hernán Cortés and his Indigenous allies, Tenochtitlan fell on August 13, 1521, and was largely destroyed. Mexico City was built atop its ruins; excavations of the Templo Mayor, begun in 1978 after the chance discovery of the Coyolxauhqui Stone, continue to reveal the ancient city.
