Taboon bread

Taboon bread (Arabic: خبز طابون, khubz ṭābūn) is a traditional Middle Eastern flatbread baked in a taboon, a clay oven. It is a staple of Palestinian cuisine and is widely eaten throughout the Levant. The bread is soft and slightly chewy, with characteristic dimples formed when the dough is spread over small smooth stones lining the oven floor.

Taboon bread, dimpled from baking on hot stones in a clay oven

Preparation

The dough is made from wheat flour, water, yeast, and salt, and is left to rise before being rolled or stretched into large rounds. In a traditional taboon, a fire of wood or dried dung burns inside the clay oven; the dough is draped over a cushion and pressed onto the hot stones at the bottom, where it bakes quickly. The stones impart the bread's signature pockmarked surface.

Culinary use

Taboon bread is the foundation of musakhan, often described as the national dish of Palestine, in which the bread is topped with olive oil, sumac, caramelized onions, and roasted chicken. It is also served as an everyday accompaniment to dips such as hummus, to grilled meats, and as a wrap.

Cultural significance

The communal taboon oven was historically a social center of village life, and the bread remains a symbol of Palestinian culinary heritage. In 2024, the art of baking taboon bread was inscribed on UNESCO-related heritage lists proposed by Palestine celebrating traditional foodways, reflecting its enduring cultural importance.
