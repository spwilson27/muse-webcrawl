Oscar Zariski

Oscar Zariski (born Oscher Zaritsky; April 24, 1899 – July 4, 1986) was a Russian-born American mathematician and one of the founders of modern algebraic geometry. Born in Kobrin, in the Russian Empire (now Belarus), he studied in Kyiv and Rome, where he worked with the Italian school of algebraic geometry.

Career

Zariski emigrated to the United States in 1927, teaching at Johns Hopkins University before moving to Harvard University in 1937, where he remained until retirement. He recast algebraic geometry on rigorous foundations using commutative algebra.

Contributions

His name is attached to the Zariski topology, the Zariski tangent space, Zariski's main theorem, and the Zariski–Riemann space. His book Commutative Algebra, written with Pierre Samuel, became a standard text. His students included David Mumford and Robin Hartshorne.
