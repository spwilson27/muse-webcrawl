Chief of the Air Staff (United Kingdom)

The Chief of the Air Staff (CAS) is the professional head of the Royal Air Force and the most senior officer in the service. The post was created in 1918, the year the RAF was founded, and its holder is a member of the Chiefs of Staff Committee and the Defence Council, advising the government on air power matters. CAS is usually held by an officer of air chief marshal rank.

Chief of the Air Staff

Service: Royal Air Force

Established: 1918

First holder: Sir Hugh Trenchard

Reports to: Chief of the Defence Staff

History

The first Chief of the Air Staff was Major-General Sir Hugh Trenchard, often called the "father of the RAF", who shaped the independent air force between 1919 and 1930. During the Second World War, Sir Charles Portal held the post throughout most of the conflict. Successive holders have balanced the demands of the Cold War deterrent, expeditionary operations and the modern emphasis on space and information warfare.
