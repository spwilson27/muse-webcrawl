Arp 220

Constellation: Serpens

Type: Interacting galaxies / ULIRG

Distance: ~250 million light-years

Arp 220 is the result of a collision between two galaxies that are now in the process of merging. It lies about 250 million light-years away in the constellation Serpens and is the nearest ultraluminous infrared galaxy (ULIRG) to Earth.

Characteristics

The merger has triggered an intense burst of star formation. Arp 220 radiates most of its enormous luminosity in the infrared, with a total output exceeding a trillion times that of the Sun. Its two nuclei, the cores of the original galaxies, are separated by roughly 1,200 light-years and are surrounded by dense gas, dust and hundreds of massive star clusters.

Observations

Because it is the closest example of its class, Arp 220 serves as a local analogue for the luminous, dust-obscured mergers common in the early universe. Studies suggest it may harbour one or two active galactic nuclei buried within its dusty core.
