Gilles Brassard (born 20 April 1955) is a Canadian computer scientist at the Université de Montréal, recognised as one of the founders of quantum information science. He is best known for co-inventing quantum key distribution and quantum teleportation.

Work

In 1984, with Charles H. Bennett of IBM, Brassard devised the BB84 protocol, the first quantum cryptography protocol, which allows two parties to establish a secret key whose security rests on the laws of quantum mechanics — any eavesdropping attempt inevitably disturbs the transmitted quantum states and can be detected. In 1993, Bennett, Brassard and four colleagues published the protocol for quantum teleportation, showing how an unknown quantum state can be transferred using entanglement and classical communication. Brassard also contributed to quantum pseudo-telepathy, quantum coin flipping and early quantum algorithms.

Career and honours

Brassard received his PhD in computer science from Cornell University in 1979, working on cryptography with John Hopcroft. He has spent his career at the Université de Montréal, where he holds the Canada Research Chair in Quantum Information. He is a Fellow of the Royal Society and an Officer of the Order of Canada. He shared the 2018 Wolf Prize in Physics with Charles Bennett "for founding and advancing the discipline of quantum information science," and he and Bennett were jointly awarded the ACM A.M. Turing Award for 2025 for their foundational work in quantum information science.
