Otsego County is a county in the U.S. state of New York, located in the central part of the state. Its county seat is Cooperstown, a village on Otsego Lake famous as the home of the National Baseball Hall of Fame and Museum. The county's largest city is Oneonta.

History

The county was formed in 1791 from Montgomery County. The region was settled in the late eighteenth century, and the novelist James Fenimore Cooper, whose father founded Cooperstown, set several of his Leatherstocking Tales around Otsego Lake.

Geography

Otsego County covers roughly 1,000 square miles of rolling hills at the northern edge of the Catskill region. The Susquehanna River begins at Otsego Lake and flows through the county.

Communities

Communities include the city of Oneonta, the villages of Cooperstown, Richfield Springs, and Unadilla, and numerous small towns. Hartwick College and the State University of New York at Oneonta are major institutions.
