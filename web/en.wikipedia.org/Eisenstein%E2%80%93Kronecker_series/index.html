Eisenstein–Kronecker series

Eisenstein–Kronecker series

The Eisenstein–Kronecker series are a family of meromorphic functions on a complex torus (equivalently, functions associated with a lattice in the complex plane and a point on the corresponding elliptic curve) that generalise both the classical Eisenstein series and the theta functions. They were introduced in essence by Eisenstein and Kronecker in the nineteenth century and developed systematically by André Weil.

Definition

For a lattice Λ in the complex plane, a point z, and integers a, b ≥ 0, the Eisenstein–Kronecker series Ea,b(z) is defined by a conditionally convergent sum over non-zero lattice points w of terms of the form (conjugate of (z+w))a / (z+w)b, weighted by the inverse area of the fundamental parallelogram. The summation is performed in the Eisenstein ordering, which makes the series converge for b − a ≥ 2 and defines the remaining cases by analytic continuation.

Properties

The series satisfy distribution and symmetry relations analogous to those of theta functions, and their values at torsion points of the elliptic curve are algebraic numbers with deep arithmetic significance. Kronecker's limit formula expresses the constant term in the Laurent expansion of the real-analytic Eisenstein series in terms of the Dedekind eta function.

Applications

Eisenstein–Kronecker series appear in the theory of elliptic units and the Kronecker–Weber theorem for imaginary quadratic fields, in the construction of p-adic L-functions, and in work on special values of Hecke L-series. They also occur in string theory computations involving elliptic genera and modular graph functions.
