The iPhone 5s is a smartphone that was designed and marketed by Apple Inc. It is the seventh generation of the iPhone, succeeding the iPhone 5, and was announced on 10 September 2013 alongside the iPhone 5c. It was released on 20 September 2013.

The 5s maintained much of the design of its predecessor, but was distinguished internally by being the first smartphone to use a 64-bit processor — the Apple A7 system-on-chip — along with the M7 motion coprocessor. It introduced Touch ID, a fingerprint recognition sensor built into the home button, allowing users to unlock the phone and authorize iTunes purchases biometrically. It shipped in a new gold finish in addition to space gray and silver.

Features

  • Camera — an improved 8-megapixel iSight camera with larger sensor pixels, dual-LED "True Tone" flash, burst mode, and 120 fps slow-motion video at 720p.
  • Software — shipped with iOS 7, which introduced a complete visual redesign of the operating system.
  • Performance — Apple claimed the A7 doubled CPU and graphics performance over the A6.

Reception and legacy

The iPhone 5s was praised for Touch ID and its forward-looking 64-bit chip, and became the best-selling smartphone on major U.S. carriers in its launch month. It was discontinued in March 2016, when the first-generation iPhone SE adopted the same body design with upgraded internals. The 5s received software updates through iOS 12, released in 2018, making its six years of support unusually long for its era.
