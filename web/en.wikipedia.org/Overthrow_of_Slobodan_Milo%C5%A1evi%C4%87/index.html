Overthrow of Slobodan Milošević

Date: 5 October 2000

Location: Belgrade, FR Yugoslavia (Serbia)

Also known as: Bulldozer Revolution; 5 October Overthrow

Result: Milošević resigns; Vojislav Koštunica becomes president

The overthrow of Slobodan Milošević took place on 5 October 2000 in Belgrade, Yugoslavia. Mass protests followed the disputed federal presidential election of 24 September 2000, in which the opposition candidate Vojislav Koštunica of the Democratic Opposition of Serbia (DOS) coalition claimed outright victory, while the regime-controlled electoral commission insisted a run-off was required. After a general strike and the storming of the federal parliament by demonstrators, Milošević conceded defeat the next day, ending thirteen years of rule. The events are sometimes called the Bulldozer Revolution, after a wheel-loader operator who charged the state television building.

Background

By 2000, Serbia had endured a decade of wars in Croatia, Bosnia and Kosovo, international sanctions, hyperinflation and the 1999 NATO bombing campaign. Milošević, in power since 1987, had survived earlier waves of protest in 1991 and 1996–97. The student movement Otpor! ("Resistance"), founded in 1998, harassed the regime with street theatre and the slogan "Gotov je" ("He is finished"), while the fragmented opposition united into the 18-party DOS coalition behind the moderate nationalist lawyer Vojislav Koštunica. Western governments funded election monitoring, independent media and voter-mobilisation campaigns such as "It's Time" and rock concerts encouraging youth turnout.

The election and protests

Parallel vote counts by the opposition and the independent monitor CeSID showed Koštunica above 50 percent, avoiding a second round. The Federal Electoral Commission instead announced that Koštunica had fallen short and ordered a run-off, annulling part of the vote. DOS called a general strike; coal miners at Kolubara, who produced much of Serbia's electricity, struck and defied police attempts to retake the pits. On 5 October hundreds of thousands converged on Belgrade from across Serbia. Crowds stormed and set fire to the Federal Parliament building and then the headquarters of the state broadcaster RTS, a symbol of regime propaganda — the assault on RTS led by Ljubisav Đokić, whose wheel loader gave the revolution its nickname. Police largely stood aside or joined the protesters.

Outcome

That evening Koštunica addressed the crowd from the City Hall balcony, and on 6 October Milošević appeared on television conceding defeat and congratulating his opponent. Koštunica was sworn in as President of Yugoslavia on 7 October. Zoran Đinđić became Prime Minister of Serbia after DOS won the December Serbian parliamentary election. In April 2001 Milošević was arrested, and in June 2001 — over Koštunica's objections — he was transferred to the UN tribunal in The Hague, where he died in custody in 2006 during his trial for war crimes.

The "October 5th" uprising became a template studied by later colour revolutions in Georgia and Ukraine, whose activists were in some cases trained by Otpor veterans.
