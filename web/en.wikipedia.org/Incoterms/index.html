Incoterms

The Incoterms (International Commercial Terms) are a set of standardised three-letter trade terms published by the International Chamber of Commerce (ICC). They define the responsibilities of buyers and sellers in international transactions — in particular who arranges and pays for transport and insurance, and where the risk of loss passes from seller to buyer.

History

The first edition appeared in 1936. The rules have been revised periodically, most recently in Incoterms 2020, which contains eleven terms.

Common terms

Well-known terms include EXW (Ex Works), FOB (Free On Board), CIF (Cost, Insurance and Freight), DAP (Delivered at Place) and DDP (Delivered Duty Paid). Some terms apply only to sea and inland-waterway transport.

Use

Incoterms are incorporated by reference into sales contracts worldwide; they allocate costs and risks but do not by themselves govern transfer of title or payment terms.
