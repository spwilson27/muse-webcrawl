Article processing charge

An article processing charge (APC), also known as a publication fee, is a fee which is sometimes charged to authors to make a work available open access in either an open access journal or a hybrid journal. The fee is usually paid by the author's institution or research funder rather than by the author personally.

Background

APCs are the most common business model for gold open access publishing, replacing reader subscriptions with author-side payments to cover peer review administration, editing, typesetting, hosting, and archiving. Charges vary widely, from under $100 at some journals to more than $10,000 for prestigious titles; the most expensive APCs are associated with high-impact journals such as Nature.

Criticism

Critics argue that APCs create barriers for researchers without funding, particularly in developing countries, encourage predatory journals that charge fees without genuine peer review, and allow publishers to be paid twice through hybrid journals that collect both subscriptions and APCs. Waivers, institutional read-and-publish agreements, and diamond open access journals (which charge neither readers nor authors) are responses to these concerns.
