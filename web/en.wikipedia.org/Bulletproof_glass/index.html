Bulletproof glass

Bulletproof glass, ballistic glass, transparent armor, or bullet-resistant glass is a type of strong but optically transparent material that is particularly resistant to penetration by projectiles. Like any material, it is not completely impenetrable. It is usually made from a combination of two or more types of glass, one hard and one soft.

Construction

The softer layer makes the glass more elastic, so it can flex instead of shatter. A common construction is laminated glass, in which layers of glass are bonded with interlayers of polyvinyl butyral (PVB) or polycarbonate. When struck by a bullet, the outer glass layers absorb and spread the energy, while the plastic interlayer holds the fragments together and resists penetration. Thicknesses range from a few millimetres for protection against handguns to much thicker assemblies for rifle protection.

One-way bulletproof glass

One-way bulletproof glass is designed to resist fire from one side while allowing return fire from the other; it combines brittle glass on the outside with flexible polycarbonate inside. Such material is used in some VIP and military vehicles.

Uses

Bullet-resistant glass is used in vehicle armoring, bank and embassy glazing, military vehicles, and protective display cases. Its performance is rated under standards such as UL 752 and EN 1063.
