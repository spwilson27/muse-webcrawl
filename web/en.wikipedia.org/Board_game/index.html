Board game

Modern board games use boards, tokens, dice and cards, though boards are not strictly required.

A board game is a type of tabletop game that involves pieces moved or placed on a pre-marked surface or board, according to a set of rules. Some games are based on pure strategy, some involve an element of chance, and many combine the two; players usually compete to reach a goal, such as capturing opposing pieces, accumulating points, or being the first to complete an objective.

History

Board games have been played in most cultures throughout history. Ancient examples include Senet from Egypt (c. 3100 BCE), the Royal Game of Ur from Mesopotamia, and the Chinese game of Go, first referenced around the 4th century BCE. Mancala-type games, played by moving stones around pits, spread widely across Africa and Asia. Chess evolved from the Indian game chaturanga around the 6th century CE, and checkers, backgammon and nine men's morris all have medieval or earlier roots.

The 19th and 20th centuries saw the rise of mass-produced commercial board games, including The Landlord's Game (the ancestor of Monopoly), Scrabble, Clue (Cluedo), Risk and Trivial Pursuit. In the 1990s, German-style or "Eurogames" such as Catan (1995) emphasised indirect competition, resource management and reduced player elimination, reshaping the hobby worldwide. The 21st century has seen a major expansion of the hobby market, crowdfunding of new designs, and crossover with digital versions and apps.

Categories

Board games are classified in many ways: abstract strategy games (chess, Go), race games (backgammon, pachisi), roll-and-move games, wargames simulating military conflict, economic games, word games, trivia games, and cooperative games in which players work together against the game system. Elements of luck, such as dice rolls or card draws, may be contrasted with games of perfect information and pure strategy.

Research and culture

Board games are studied in game theory and used in education and therapy; playing them has been associated with cognitive and social benefits. The largest board game collection and major trade fairs such as the Spiel in Essen, Germany, reflect the medium's cultural importance. Classic games have also been milestones in artificial intelligence research, most famously chess and Go.
