Cochinillo asado

Cochinillo asado (Spanish for "roast suckling pig") is a traditional dish of Castilian cuisine, most famously associated with the city of Segovia in Spain. It consists of a whole suckling pig, typically two to three weeks old, roasted slowly in a wood-fired oven until the meat is tender and the skin crisp.

Preparation

The piglet, weighing roughly 4 to 6 kilograms, is cleaned, seasoned simply with salt and sometimes lard or olive oil, and roasted in an earthenware dish with a little water so that steam keeps the meat moist. Traditional ovens burn holm oak wood. The cooking is slow — often around three hours — producing meat so tender that in Segovia it is traditionally portioned with the edge of a plate rather than a knife.

Cultural significance

Cochinillo asado is the signature dish of Segovia, where restaurants such as Mesón de Cándido, serving since the 18th century, have made it famous. The dish is protected by a quality designation, Cochinillo de Segovia, which specifies the breed, feeding, age and weight of the animals. A related variant, lechazo, uses suckling lamb instead of pig.
