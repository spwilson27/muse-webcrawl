Nationalism

Nationalism
Core claimThe nation should be congruent with the political unit (state)
Key theoristsErnest Gellner, Benedict Anderson, Eric Hobsbawm, Anthony D. Smith, Elie Kedourie
Principal typesEthnic, civic, cultural, liberal, expansionist, banal

Nationalism is an idea and movement that holds that the nation should be congruent with the state. As a movement, it presupposes the existence of a nation and works toward either gaining or maintaining self-governance — national sovereignty — for it, or towards furthering its interests against other nations. Nationalism holds that each nation should govern itself, free from outside interference (self-determination), that a nation is a natural and ideal basis for a polity, and that the nation is the only rightful source of political power.

Definitions and scholarly debate

Scholars disagree about when nations and nationalism arose. Modernists such as Ernest Gellner (Nations and Nationalism, 1983) argue both are products of industrialisation, mass education, and print capitalism; Benedict Anderson described nations as "imagined political communities" made possible by print languages. Ethnosymbolists such as Anthony D. Smith stress the pre-modern ethnic myths, symbols, and memories from which nations were built, while primordialists and perennialists see nations as ancient or recurrent forms of community.

History

The ideas of popular sovereignty and national self-determination crystallised in the American and French Revolutions and spread through nineteenth-century Europe — in the unifications of Italy and Germany, the independence movements of Latin America, and national revivals among the peoples of the Habsburg, Ottoman, and Russian empires. President Woodrow Wilson's advocacy of self-determination after the First World War reshaped the map of Europe; after 1945 nationalism powered decolonisation across Asia and Africa.

Varieties

A common distinction opposes civic nationalism, based on citizenship, shared institutions, and voluntary allegiance, to ethnic nationalism, based on descent, language, and culture. Other forms include cultural nationalism, religious nationalism, economic nationalism, and Michael Billig's "banal nationalism" — the everyday flags, anthems, and habits that reproduce national identity unnoticed. Nationalism has underpinned liberation movements and democratic state-building, but also chauvinism, irredentism, and ethnic conflict; it remains a central force in world politics, including in contemporary populist movements.
