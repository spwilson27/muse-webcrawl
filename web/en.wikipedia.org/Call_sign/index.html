Call sign

In broadcasting and radio communications, a call sign (also known as a call name or call letters) is a unique designation for a transmitter station. Call signs are formally assigned by government authorities under the coordination of the International Telecommunication Union (ITU), which allocates prefix blocks identifying each country. A station typically identifies itself by its call sign at regular intervals during transmission.

Uses

Call signs are used by broadcast radio and television stations, amateur radio operators, aircraft and airlines in radiotelephony, ships, spacecraft, and military units. Aviation call signs combine an airline designator with a flight number (for example "Speedbird" for British Airways), while amateur radio call signs encode the operator's country and licence class. Informally, aviators and military personnel also use personal call signs as nicknames.

Structure

ITU call signs consist of a one- or two-character prefix denoting nationality followed by additional letters and digits. In the United States, broadcast call signs begin with W east of the Mississippi River and K to the west — a convention dating to 1912–1913 — with the FCC assigning call signs such as WABC or KQED. Australia uses the VL/VH/VJ/VK/VN/VZ series; Canada uses CF–CK, VA–VG, and other prefixes.
