Immanuel Kant (22 April 1724 – 12 February 1804) was a German philosopher and one of the central Enlightenment thinkers. Born in Königsberg in the Kingdom of Prussia, he spent his entire life and career in or near that city. Kant's comprehensive and systematic works in epistemology, metaphysics, ethics, and aesthetics have made him one of the most influential figures in modern Western philosophy, synthesizing early modern rationalism and empiricism.

Life

Kant was the fourth of nine children of a harness maker. He studied at the University of Königsberg, supported himself for years as a private tutor and lecturer (Privatdozent), and in 1770 became professor of logic and metaphysics there. Famously regular in his habits — local legend held that Königsberg's citizens set their clocks by his daily walks — he never married and rarely left his home province.

Critical philosophy

After a decade of silence, Kant published the Critique of Pure Reason (1781, revised 1787), arguing that the human mind actively structures experience through a priori forms of intuition (space and time) and categories of understanding (such as causality). We can know phenomena — things as they appear to us — but not noumena, things in themselves. This "Copernican revolution" in philosophy proposed that objects must conform to our cognition, rather than our cognition to objects, thereby securing the possibility of synthetic a priori knowledge in mathematics and natural science while limiting speculative metaphysics.

Ethics

In the Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals (1785) and the Critique of Practical Reason (1788), Kant grounded morality in reason alone. The categorical imperative commands us to act only according to maxims that can be willed as universal laws, and to treat humanity, in ourselves and others, always also as an end and never merely as a means. Autonomy — self-legislation of the rational will — is the basis of human dignity.

Other works and influence

The Critique of Judgment (1790) addressed aesthetics and teleology; essays such as "What Is Enlightenment?" (1784) and Perpetual Peace (1795) shaped political thought. Kant's early Universal Natural History (1755) proposed the nebular hypothesis of the solar system's formation. His philosophy directly influenced German idealism (Fichte, Schelling, Hegel), Schopenhauer, neo-Kantianism, and much subsequent philosophy, ethics and political theory.
