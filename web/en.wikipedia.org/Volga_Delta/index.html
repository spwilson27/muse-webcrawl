Volga Delta

The Volga Delta is the delta of the Volga River where it flows into the Caspian Sea, in the Russian oblast of Astrakhan. It is the largest river delta in Europe, covering roughly 27,000 square kilometres (about 10,400 square miles), and one of the largest inland deltas in the world. The delta begins near the city of Astrakhan, where the river divides into hundreds of channels and shallow bays before reaching the Caspian.

Geography

The delta is an exceptionally flat, low-lying landscape of distributary channels, oxbow lakes, reed beds, and seasonally flooded meadows. Because the level of the Caspian Sea fluctuates over time, the shape and extent of the delta have changed significantly: falling sea levels in the mid-20th century extended the delta seaward, while rising levels from the late 1970s flooded some outer areas. The Volga carries an enormous load of sediment, and the delta continues to build outward.

Ecology

The delta is one of the most important wetland ecosystems in Eurasia. It supports vast beds of lotus and reed, spawning grounds for sturgeon and other commercially valuable fish, and huge numbers of migratory and breeding waterbirds, including pelicans, herons, egrets, swans, and the Siberian crane on passage. The Astrakhan Nature Reserve, established in 1919, protects three sections of the delta and was among the first nature reserves in Russia. The delta is listed as a wetland of international importance under the Ramsar Convention.

Human activity

Fishing, particularly for sturgeon and caviar, has historically been the principal industry, though stocks have declined sharply due to overfishing, poaching, and damming of the Volga upstream. Agriculture in the upper delta produces watermelons, rice, and vegetables. The delta's wetlands are threatened by pollution, altered river flow from reservoirs, and fluctuations in the Caspian Sea level.
