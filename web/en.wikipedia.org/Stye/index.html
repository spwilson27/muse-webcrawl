Stye

A stye, medically known as a hordeolum, is a painful red lump near the edge of the eyelid caused by a bacterial infection of an oil gland or eyelash follicle. Styes are common, usually harmless, and typically resolve on their own within days to weeks.

Stye
Medical name: Hordeolum
Specialty: Ophthalmology
Cause: Staphylococcus infection
Treatment: Warm compresses, hygiene

Signs and symptoms

A stye appears as a tender, swollen bump on the eyelid margin, sometimes with a visible yellowish spot of pus. The eye may water, feel gritty, or be sensitive to light. Unlike a chalazion, which is a painless blocked gland deeper in the lid, a stye is acutely painful.

Causes

Most styes are caused by Staphylococcus aureus bacteria. Risk factors include touching the eyes with unwashed hands, using old cosmetics, leaving makeup on overnight, and chronic blepharitis.

Treatment

Warm compresses applied several times daily encourage the stye to drain naturally. Squeezing is discouraged. Persistent or recurrent styes may require antibiotic ointment or drainage by a clinician.
