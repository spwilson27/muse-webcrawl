Ferran Adrià

Ferran Adrià i Acosta (born 14 May 1962) is a Spanish chef considered one of the most influential cooks of the modern era. As head chef of elBulli in Roses, Catalonia — voted the world's best restaurant a record five times by Restaurant magazine — he pioneered techniques associated with molecular gastronomy, a label he himself resisted.

Early life and career

Adrià was born in L'Hospitalet de Llobregat, Barcelona. He began washing dishes in a hotel kitchen to earn money for a trip to Ibiza, then completed military service as a cook. In 1984 he joined elBulli, then a two-Michelin-star restaurant under chef Jean-Paul Vinay, and became head chef in 1987 alongside Albert Adrià, his younger brother, who developed its celebrated desserts.

El Bulli and deconstructivist cooking

Under Adrià, elBulli developed a style he called "deconstructivist": familiar dishes were taken apart and reassembled in surprising forms — his famous "spherical olives" burst like the real thing but were pure liquid. Signature techniques included culinary foams made with a siphon, spherification, hot gelatins, and savoury ice creams. The restaurant opened only about six months a year; Adrià spent the rest developing new dishes in his Barcelona workshop, the Taller. At its peak, elBulli received around two million reservation requests for roughly 8,000 seats per season.

After elBulli

Adrià closed elBulli as a restaurant on 30 July 2011, judging that it had reached its creative limit, and announced plans to convert it into the elBulli Foundation, a centre for culinary research and documentation, including the Bullipedia encyclopedia project. His influence is visible in kitchens worldwide through alumni such as René Redzepi, Massimo Bottura, and Andoni Luis Aduriz.
