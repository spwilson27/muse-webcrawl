Second Coming

The Second Coming (sometimes called the Second Advent or the Parousia) is the Christian belief that Jesus Christ will return to Earth after his ascension to heaven, an event anticipated since the earliest decades of Christianity. It forms a central part of Christian eschatology and is affirmed in the historic creeds of the church.

Christ's return in glory, as described in the New Testament.

Biblical basis

The expectation rests on passages throughout the New Testament: Jesus's own teaching in the Olivet Discourse (Matthew 24–25, Mark 13, Luke 21), the promise of the angels at the Ascension that Jesus would return "in the same way" (Acts 1:11), the letters of Paul (especially 1 and 2 Thessalonians and 1 Corinthians 15), and the Book of Revelation, which culminates in Christ's return as judge and king. The Greek term parousia, meaning "arrival" or "presence," is frequently used for the event.

Associated events

Traditions differ in how they order the events connected with the Second Coming: the resurrection of the dead, the Last Judgment, the rapture of believers (in some traditions), the millennium of Revelation 20, and the renewal of creation in a "new heaven and new earth." Schools of interpretation include premillennialism, postmillennialism, and amillennialism.

Views in other traditions

In Islam, Jesus (Isa) is also expected to return before the Day of Judgment, though in a role subordinate to God's final reckoning and distinct from Christian doctrine. The Bahá'í Faith interprets prophecies of the return symbolically. Within Christianity, most historic churches teach a future, visible, personal return, while discouraging speculation about its date, following Jesus's statement that "about that day or hour no one knows" (Matthew 24:36). Down the centuries, various movements have nonetheless predicted dates, most famously the Millerites, whose failure in 1844 became known as the Great Disappointment.
