Shugendō

Type: Japanese mountain asceticism
Practitioners: Yamabushi
Influences: Shinto, Buddhism, Taoism, shamanism
Legendary founder: En no Gyōja

Shugendō (修験道) is a Japanese religion of mountain asceticism that emerged from the blending of indigenous kami worship, Shinto, esoteric Buddhism, Taoist ideas and shamanic practices. Its practitioners, called yamabushi ("those who lie down in the mountains"), seek spiritual power and enlightenment through rigorous training in sacred mountains.

Practices

Yamabushi undertake austerities such as pilgrimage along mountain routes, meditation under waterfalls, fire rituals (goma), chanting and periods of seclusion. Major sacred centres include Mount Haguro and the Dewa Sanzan in the north, Mount Ōmine and Kumano in the Kii Mountains, and Mount Hiko in Kyushu.

History

Tradition credits the semi-legendary ascetic En no Gyōja (7th century) as founder. Shugendō flourished in the medieval period, was suppressed during the Meiji government's separation of Shinto and Buddhism, and revived after 1945, with yamabushi training and pilgrimages continuing today.
