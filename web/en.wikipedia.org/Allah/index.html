Allah

Allah (Arabic: الله, Allāh) is the Arabic word for God, used principally in Islam to refer to the one God, creator and sustainer of the universe. The word is standardly understood as a contraction of al-ilāh, "the god" or "the deity." Arabic-speaking Christians and Jews also use Allah for God, and the word appears in Arabic translations of the Bible. In Islamic theology Allah is unique (tawhid), eternal, omnipotent, and utterly unlike created things; the Quran's 112th chapter, Al-Ikhlas, states: "He is Allah, the One… He begets not, nor is He begotten, and there is none like unto Him."

Etymology and pre-Islamic usage

The term was used by pre-Islamic Arabs, including polytheists who regarded Allah as a creator and high god, and by Arab monotheists. Cognates exist in other Semitic languages: Hebrew Eloah and Aramaic Alaha. Inscriptions from late antique Arabia attest the name in monotheistic contexts shortly before Islam.

In Islam

Muslims believe Allah is the same God worshipped in Judaism and Christianity, the God of Abraham, Moses, and Jesus, while rejecting the doctrines of the Trinity and Incarnation. The Quran describes Allah with many attributes — the Most Merciful (ar-Rahman), the Most Compassionate (ar-Rahim), the King, the Holy, the All-Knowing, the All-Powerful. Tradition enumerates 99 names of Allah (asma' al-husna, "the most beautiful names"), recited in devotion and prayer beads (tasbih).

The oneness of Allah in his lordship, worship, and names and attributes is the core of the Islamic creed, expressed in the shahada: "There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah." Associating partners with Allah (shirk) is regarded as the gravest sin in Islam.

In other traditions

Arab Christians commonly refer to God the Father as Allah al-Ab. In Maltese, a language descended from Arabic, God is Alla. In Malaysia and Indonesia the use of "Allah" by non-Muslims has been a recurring legal and political controversy. Sikhs also occasionally use the term in the Guru Granth Sahib, where Allah appears among the names of God.

Calligraphy

Because Islam discourages figurative depictions of God, the written name of Allah became a central subject of Arabic calligraphy, appearing in ornate forms on mosques, manuscripts, and amulets. Unicode includes a dedicated ligature for the word (U+FDF2).
