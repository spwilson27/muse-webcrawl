Semper fidelis

Semper fidelis is a Latin phrase that means "always faithful" or "always loyal". It is the motto of the United States Marine Corps, usually shortened to Semper Fi. It is also used as a motto by other military units, cities, and families worldwide.

United States Marine Corps

The Marine Corps adopted Semper Fidelis as its official motto in 1883, replacing earlier mottos such as Fortitudine ("with fortitude") and the phrase Per Mare, Per Terram borrowed from the British Royal Marines. The motto appears on the Marine Corps emblem, where the eagle holds a scroll bearing the words in its beak. "Semper Fi" is used among Marines as a greeting and a sign of mutual loyalty; the response traditionally is the same phrase, or "Oorah".

The march

"Semper Fidelis" is also the title of the official march of the Marine Corps, composed in 1888 by John Philip Sousa, then leader of the United States Marine Band. Sousa regarded it as his best march.

Other users

The phrase has served many organisations. It is the motto of the city of Exeter in England (granted around 1660), of the Devonshire Regiment, of the West Nova Scotia Regiment of Canada, of cadet and school bodies, and of families including the Lynch family of Galway, Ireland. Variants of the phrase appear as unit mottos across European and Commonwealth forces, reflecting its long heraldic history.
