Franz Xaver Süssmayr (1766 – 17 September 1803) was an Austrian composer and conductor, a pupil of Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart, best known for completing Mozart's Requiem in D minor (K. 626) after the composer's death in 1791.

Life

Born in Schwanenstadt, Upper Austria, Süssmayr was educated at the Kremsmünster Abbey gymnasium and studied composition, becoming a popular composer of Singspiele in Vienna. From about 1790 he was associated with Mozart, copying scores and assisting with rehearsals of La clemenza di Tito and The Magic Flute.

The Requiem completion

Mozart left the Requiem unfinished at his death. After an initial attempt by Joseph Eybler, the completion was entrusted to Süssmayr, who claimed to have discussed the work's plan with Mozart. He orchestrated the movements drafted by Mozart and composed the concluding sections — Sanctus, Benedictus, and Agnus Dei — as well as finishing the Lacrymosa. The exact extent of his contribution has been debated ever since, and several modern alternative completions exist, but the Süssmayr version remains the most widely performed.

Other works

Süssmayr wrote numerous operas, including Der Spiegel von Arkadien (1794), sacred music, and instrumental works. He served as Kapellmeister at the Vienna Kärntnertortheater. He died of tuberculosis in 1803 and was buried in an unmarked grave, like Mozart.
