Peter Saville (graphic designer)

Peter Saville (born 9 October 1955) is an English art director and graphic designer. He is best known for the record sleeves he designed for Factory Records in Manchester, most famously for Joy Division and New Order, whose minimalist, appropriated imagery made him one of the most influential sleeve designers of the post-punk era.

Factory Records

Saville co-founded Factory Records in 1978 with Tony Wilson and others, becoming the label's in-house art director. His cover for Joy Division's Unknown Pleasures (1979) reproduced the stacked radio-wave pulses of the first discovered pulsar, an image he found in a scientific encyclopedia; it became one of the most iconic album covers ever made. For New Order's Power, Corruption & Lies (1983) he combined a reproduced still-life painting by Henri Fantin-Latour with a colour code based on the record's catalogue number. His elegant, often typography-free designs notoriously ran over budget and deadline, contributing to Factory's financial chaos.

Later work

After Factory, Saville worked through his agency Peter Saville Associates and the studio Pentagram for clients including Roxy Music, Peter Gabriel, Pulp, Givenchy, Stella McCartney, Burberry and Adidas. He designed the England football team's home shirt in 2010 and served as creative director of the city of Manchester, originating the "Original Modern" branding concept. In 2020 he redesigned the Burberry monogram and visual identity.
