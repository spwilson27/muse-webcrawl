Assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand

Date28 June 1914
LocationSarajevo, Bosnia and Herzegovina (Austria-Hungary)
VictimsArchduke Franz Ferdinand and Sophie, Duchess of Hohenberg
AssassinGavrilo Princip
OutcomeJuly Crisis; outbreak of the First World War

The assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir presumptive to the Austro-Hungarian throne, and his wife Sophie, Duchess of Hohenberg, took place on 28 June 1914 in Sarajevo. The couple were shot dead by Gavrilo Princip, a 19-year-old Bosnian Serb and member of Young Bosnia, a group seeking to unite Bosnian Serbs with Serbia, which had been supplied with weapons by elements of Serbian military intelligence through the Black Hand organization. The assassination set off the July Crisis, a chain of alliances and ultimatums that led within weeks to the First World War.

The attack

The Archduke was visiting Sarajevo to inspect army manoeuvres on Vidovdan (St Vitus Day), a date of deep significance to Serbs. That morning, conspirator Nedeljko Čabrinović threw a bomb at the motorcade, wounding members of the entourage; the Archduke continued to the city hall and then decided to visit the wounded in hospital. Owing to a communication error, his driver took a wrong turn onto Franz Josef Street and stopped in front of a shop where Princip happened to be standing; Princip stepped forward and fired two shots from an FN Model 1910 pistol, fatally wounding both Franz Ferdinand and Sophie.

Aftermath

Princip and his accomplices were arrested; too young for the death penalty under Austro-Hungarian law, Princip received twenty years' imprisonment and died of tuberculosis in 1918. Austria-Hungary, backed by Germany, issued a deliberately harsh ultimatum to Serbia; Serbia's partial rejection led Austria-Hungary to declare war on 28 July 1914. Russia mobilized in Serbia's defence, Germany declared war on Russia and France, and Britain declared war on Germany after the invasion of Belgium — turning a Balkan crisis into a world war.
