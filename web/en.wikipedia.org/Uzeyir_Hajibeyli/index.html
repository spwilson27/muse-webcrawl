Uzeyir Hajibeyli (Azerbaijani: Üzeyir Hacıbəyli; 1885–1948) was an Azerbaijani composer, conductor, musicologist, publicist and teacher. He is regarded as the founder of modern Azerbaijani classical music and opera, and he composed the music of the national anthem of Azerbaijan.

Career

Born in the village of Aghjabadi and raised in Shusha, the cultural heart of Karabakh, Hajibeyli was educated at the Gori Teachers' Seminary and later studied in Moscow and St. Petersburg. In 1908 his Leyli and Majnun, based on the classic love poem, premiered in Baku — the first opera in the Muslim East — fusing traditional Azerbaijani mugham improvisation with European operatic form, a synthesis he theorized in his treatise on Azerbaijani folk music.

His operetta Arshin Mal Alan (1913), a comic tale of a merchant who disguises himself as a cloth peddler to find a bride, became enormously popular across the Caucasus, Central Asia and beyond, and was filmed several times. His grand opera Koroghlu (1937) is considered a summit of Azerbaijani music. Hajibeyli also founded music schools and the Azerbaijani State Conservatory (now the Baku Academy of Music, named after him), and served as a deputy of the Supreme Soviet of the USSR. He died in Baku in 1948.

Legacy

Hajibeyli's birthday, 18 September, is celebrated in Azerbaijan as National Music Day. His house-museums in Baku and Shusha, and the conservatory he founded, preserve his legacy as the central figure of Azerbaijani music.
