Autofocus

Autofocus (AF) is a feature of cameras and other optical systems that automatically adjusts the focus of the lens so that a selected subject appears sharp, without the photographer manually turning a focusing ring.

Active and passive systems

Active autofocus systems measure distance to the subject using infrared light, ultrasound, or laser time-of-flight, and then drive the lens to that distance. Passive systems analyze the image itself. Two principal passive methods are phase detection, which compares light passing through opposite sides of the lens, and contrast detection, which moves the lens to maximize local image contrast.

Modern developments

Mirrorless cameras commonly combine phase-detection pixels embedded in the image sensor with contrast detection, and use subject-recognition software to track eyes, faces, animals, and vehicles. Autofocus performance is measured by speed, accuracy, and low-light sensitivity.

Limitations

Autofocus can struggle with low-contrast subjects, repetitive patterns, subjects behind glass, and fast-moving subjects in poor light, in which cases manual focus may be preferred.
