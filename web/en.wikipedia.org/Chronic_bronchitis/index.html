Chronic bronchitis

Chronic bronchitis is a type of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) characterised by long-term inflammation of the bronchi, the airways that carry air to the lungs. It is clinically defined as a productive cough lasting at least three months in each of two successive years, where other causes of chronic cough have been excluded.

Causes

The most common cause is cigarette smoking; other risk factors include long-term exposure to air pollution, occupational dusts and chemicals, and repeated respiratory infections. Inhaled irritants stimulate hypertrophy of the mucus-secreting glands of the bronchi and increase mucus production, while damaging the cilia that normally clear secretions from the airways.

Symptoms and complications

Symptoms include a persistent cough with sputum production ("smoker's cough"), wheezing, breathlessness and frequent chest infections. Chronic bronchitis is one of the two main conditions comprising COPD, the other being emphysema; many patients have features of both. Progressive airflow limitation can lead to hypoxaemia, pulmonary hypertension and right-sided heart failure (cor pulmonale).

Treatment

There is no cure, but stopping smoking halts much of the accelerated decline in lung function. Bronchodilators, inhaled corticosteroids, pulmonary rehabilitation and vaccination against influenza and pneumococcus are mainstays of management; acute exacerbations may require antibiotics, steroids or hospital care, and some patients benefit from long-term oxygen therapy.
