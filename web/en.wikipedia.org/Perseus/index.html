Perseus

In Greek mythology, Perseus was the legendary founder of Mycenae and of the Perseid dynasty. He was a demigod, the son of Zeus and the mortal Danaë, and famous for slaying the Gorgon Medusa, whose head he used as a weapon before giving it to Athena.

Myth

Acrisius, king of Argos, locked his daughter Danaë away after an oracle foretold her son would kill him, but Zeus reached her as a shower of gold. Acrisius set mother and child adrift in a chest; they were rescued on Seriphos, where King Polydectes later, desiring Danaë, sent Perseus to fetch the head of Medusa to be rid of him.

Aided by Athena and Hermes, Perseus obtained winged sandals, a mirrored shield and an adamantine sickle from the Graeae and the nymphs, slew Medusa by viewing her reflection, and on his return rescued Andromeda from the sea monster Cetus and married her. He turned Polydectes to stone with the Gorgon's head and later accidentally killed Acrisius with a discus, fulfilling the prophecy.

Legacy

Perseus is commemorated in the constellation Perseus, and his story has been retold in works from Ovid's Metamorphoses to Benvenuto Cellini's bronze statue in Florence and the film Clash of the Titans.
