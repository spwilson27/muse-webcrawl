The 2015 FIFA corruption case, sometimes called "FIFAgate", was a criminal investigation into systemic corruption within FIFA, world football's governing body. On 27 May 2015, Swiss police arrested seven senior football officials at the Baur au Lac hotel in Zurich, acting on an indictment unsealed by the United States Department of Justice that charged fourteen defendants with racketeering, wire fraud and money laundering in connection with more than US$150 million in alleged bribes and kickbacks over 24 years.

The arrests and indictments

The arrests came two days before the FIFA Congress at which Sepp Blatter was seeking a fifth term as president. Those detained included FIFA vice-presidents Jeffrey Webb and Eugenio Figueredo and former vice-president Jack Warner was indicted in absentia in Trinidad. A parallel Swiss investigation examined the awarding of the 2018 and 2022 World Cups to Russia and Qatar. Former CONCACAF official Chuck Blazer, who had secretly pleaded guilty and cooperated with the FBI, emerged as a key witness.

Fall of Blatter

Blatter was re-elected on 29 May but announced his resignation four days later amid escalating pressure, after the indictment of his deputy Jérôme Valcke was foreshadowed by reports of a $10 million payment routed through FIFA. In December 2015 the FIFA Ethics Committee banned both Blatter and UEFA president Michel Platini from football for eight years (later reduced) over a "disloyal payment" of CHF 2 million. Gianni Infantino was elected FIFA president in February 2016.

Trials and aftermath

A second, larger indictment followed in December 2015, bringing the total number of defendants to more than forty individuals and entities. In 2017, former officials Juan Ángel Napout and José Maria Marin were convicted at trial in New York. The case triggered governance reforms at FIFA, criminal proceedings in several countries, and a lasting public reckoning over the organisation's culture; its repercussions continued through subsequent indictments related to World Cup broadcasting rights.
