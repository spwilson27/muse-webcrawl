Ioannis Metaxas

Born12 April 1871, Ithaca, Greece
Died29 January 1941, Athens, Greece
OfficePrime Minister of Greece, 1936–1941

Ioannis Metaxas (Greek: Ιωάννης Μεταξάς; 12 April 1871 – 29 January 1941) was a Greek military officer and politician who served as Prime Minister of Greece from 1936 until his death in 1941. He governed as the head of an authoritarian regime known as the 4th of August Regime, established after he suspended parliament and the constitution in 1936 with the backing of King George II.

Military career

Born on Ithaca, Metaxas studied military engineering in Germany and served as a staff officer in the Greco-Turkish War of 1897, the Balkan Wars, and the First World War. A staunch royalist, he opposed Eleftherios Venizelos during the National Schism, was exiled to Corsica in 1917, and was implicated on the royalist side during the Asia Minor Campaign. He later founded the Freethinkers' Party and served in coalition governments of the 1920s and 1930s.

The 4th of August Regime

Appointed prime minister in April 1936 amid political deadlock and labour unrest, Metaxas proclaimed a state of emergency on 4 August 1936 and ruled by decree. The regime censored the press, banned political parties and strikes, exiled opponents, and promoted a nationalist ideology of the "Third Hellenic Civilization" with a state youth organization, EON. Economically it combined public works and social insurance reforms with repression of the communist movement. Though authoritarian and influenced by contemporary European dictatorships, the regime remained pro-British in foreign policy.

"Ohi" and death

In the early hours of 28 October 1940, Metaxas rejected the Italian ambassador's ultimatum demanding occupation rights — the famous "Ohi" ("No") — and Greece repelled Mussolini's invasion in the Greco-Italian War, inflicting the first major land defeat on the Axis. Metaxas died in Athens in January 1941, shortly before the German invasion of April 1941.
