Financial risk

Main typesMarket risk, credit risk, liquidity risk, operational risk
Common measuresVolatility, value at risk (VaR), beta
Management toolsDiversification, hedging, insurance, derivatives

Financial risk is any of various types of risk associated with financing, including financial transactions involving company or government loans that risk default. Broadly, it is the possibility of losing money on an investment or business venture, or of a borrower failing to meet obligations. Financial risk is commonly managed by banks, insurers, investors and regulators.

Main types

Market risk is the risk of losses from movements in market prices — interest rates, exchange rates, equity and commodity prices. Credit risk is the risk that a counterparty defaults on a debt. Liquidity risk covers both the inability to fund obligations as they fall due and the inability to sell an asset quickly without a large price concession. Operational risk arises from failed internal processes, people, systems or external events, including fraud and legal risk.

Measurement

Risk is often quantified by the volatility (standard deviation) of returns; portfolio theory, from Harry Markowitz's work in 1952, relates risk to diversification and correlation. Value at risk (VaR) estimates the maximum loss not exceeded over a horizon at a given confidence level, though it was criticised after the 2008 financial crisis for understating tail risks, motivating stress testing and measures such as expected shortfall.

Risk management and regulation

Institutions manage financial risk through diversification, hedging with derivatives (futures, options, swaps), insurance, capital buffers and limits. Bank regulation — notably the Basel Accords — imposes minimum capital requirements calibrated to risk-weighted assets.
