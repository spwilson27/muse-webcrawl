Ilya Iosifovich Kabakov (30 September 1933 – 27 May 2023) was a Soviet and American conceptual artist of Ukrainian origin. He is regarded as one of the most important Russian artists of the late 20th century, best known for his large-scale installations evoking life in the Soviet Union.

Life and work

Kabakov studied at the Surikov Art Institute in Moscow and supported himself as a children's book illustrator while producing unofficial art. A leading figure of Moscow Conceptualism, he emigrated from the Soviet Union in 1987 and later settled in the United States, working in close collaboration with his wife Emilia Kabakov.

Installations

His "total installations" recreate communal apartments, schoolrooms, and other Soviet environments populated by fictional inhabitants. Notable works include The Man Who Flew into Space from His Apartment (1985) and Ten Characters (1988).
