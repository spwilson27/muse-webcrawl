Battleship

A battleship is a large armoured warship with a main battery consisting of large-calibre guns, designed to engage enemy surface vessels in line of battle. Battleships were the most powerful type of warship from the late 19th century until the Second World War, when they were eclipsed by the aircraft carrier. The term came into use in the 1880s with the advent of steel, steam-powered "ironclad" capital ships.

Development

The launch of HMS Dreadnought in 1906, with an all-big-gun armament and steam-turbine propulsion, rendered existing battleships obsolete and sparked a naval arms race. "Dreadnoughts" and their cruiser counterparts fought at Jutland in 1916, the largest battleship action in history. The Washington Naval Treaty of 1922 limited battleship size and numbers; the biggest ever built, Japan's Yamato class, displaced over 70,000 tons and mounted 18.1-inch guns.

Decline

The Second World War showed that aircraft could sink battleships beyond gun range — as at Taranto, Pearl Harbor and the sinking of Yamato and Musashi — and after 1945 no new battleships were completed. The United States recommissioned its four Iowa-class ships several times, lastly for missile-era service in the 1980s; the final one was struck from the naval register in 2006.

Characteristics

Battleships combined three attributes: heavy guns (up to 460 mm), armour protection up to 16 inches thick on the belt and turrets, and speeds that rose from around 18 knots for pre-dreadnoughts to over 30 knots for fast battleships. Secondary and anti-aircraft batteries grew enormously during the Second World War.
