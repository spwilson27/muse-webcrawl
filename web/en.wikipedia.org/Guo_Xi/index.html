Guo Xi (Chinese: 郭熙; pinyin: Guō Xī; c. 1020 – c. 1090) was a Chinese landscape painter of the Northern Song dynasty, one of the most celebrated masters of monumental landscape painting (shanshui). He served as a court painter under Emperor Shenzong, who greatly admired his work.

Early Spring

His masterpiece, Early Spring (1072), now in the National Palace Museum in Taipei, is a towering hanging scroll signed and dated by the artist. It shows a great central mountain dissolving into mist, with writhing pine trees, waterfalls and tiny travellers below. The painting exemplifies Guo Xi's technique of layered ink washes and his theory of multiple viewpoints within a single scene.

Theory

Guo Xi's treatise The Lofty Message of Forest and Streams (Linquan Gaozhi), compiled by his son Guo Si, is one of the most important texts of Chinese painting theory. It describes the "three distances" (high distance, deep distance and level distance) for composing landscapes, and insists that a landscape should be something the viewer can "walk in, wander in, and live in" — a painting to dwell within rather than merely look at. Guo Xi compared the painter's task to experiencing the mountains in all seasons and moods before putting brush to silk.
