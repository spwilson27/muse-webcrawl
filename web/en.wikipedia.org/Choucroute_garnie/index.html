Choucroute garnie (French for "dressed sauerkraut"; in Alsatian Sürkrüt) is an Alsatian dish of sauerkraut slowly braised (traditionally in white wine) and served garnished with a rich assortment of sausages, salted and smoked meats, and potatoes. It is the signature festive dish of Alsace in northeastern France — memorably served in the great Parisian brasseries that Alsatian brewers founded in the 19th century — and is typically a winter dish meant for sharing.

Preparation

Sliced cabbage is salted and lactic-fermented for several weeks to make the sauerkraut (choucroute). It is then rinsed, seasoned with juniper berries, caraway, bay leaves, cloves, and peppercorns, and braised gently with goose or pork fat and onions, moistened with Riesling wine or stock. On top are arrayed the garnishes: knack (Alsatian frankfurters), Montbéliard and Morteau sausages, smoked bacon, salted pork knuckle, Kassler-style loin, blood sausage, and liver dumplings in some versions, with boiled potatoes alongside. A seafood variation, choucroute de la mer, substitutes fish and a beurre blanc.

History

Fermented cabbage was known in China (where workers on the Great Wall were fed cabbage fermented in rice wine) and reached Europe centuries ago; sauerkraut became central to Alsatian peasant cookery, where cabbage was a winter staple preserved through fermentation. The elaborate garnished version developed as Alsace prospered, and after the Franco-Prussian War of 1870, Alsatian brasseries in Paris made choucroute garnie a Parisian institution, classically paired with Alsace white wines — Riesling and Gewürztraminer — or beer.

Cultural status

The dish anchors festivals across Alsace (such as Krautergersheim, the "cabbage capital") and family gatherings in winter. Since 2018, Alsace choucroute has EU Protected Geographical Indication status for the fermented cabbage itself.
