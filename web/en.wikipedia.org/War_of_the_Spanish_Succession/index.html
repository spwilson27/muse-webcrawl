The War of the Spanish Succession was a European great-power conflict fought between 1701 and 1714, triggered by the death in November 1700 of the childless Charles II of Spain, who willed his empire to Philip of Anjou, grandson of Louis XIV of France.

Course of the war

Fearing a union of the French and Spanish crowns, England, the Dutch Republic and Austria formed the Grand Alliance in support of the Habsburg claimant, Archduke Charles. Allied armies under the Duke of Marlborough and Prince Eugene of Savoy won famous victories at Blenheim (1704), Ramillies (1706), Oudenarde (1708) and Malplaquet (1709), while the war also spread to Italy, Spain itself, and North America.

Peace and consequences

The Peace of Utrecht (1713) recognised Philip V as King of Spain on condition that the French and Spanish crowns never unite. Britain gained Gibraltar, Minorca, Newfoundland, Acadia and the asiento contract to supply slaves to Spanish America; Austria received the Spanish Netherlands and Spanish possessions in Italy; Savoy gained Sicily. The settlement reshaped the European balance of power and confirmed Britain's rise as a naval and commercial power under the successors of William III.
