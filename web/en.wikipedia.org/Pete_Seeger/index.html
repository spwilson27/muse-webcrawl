Pete Seeger

Peter Seeger (May 3, 1919 – January 27, 2014) was an American folk singer and social activist. A fixture of American music for seven decades, he popularised the five-string banjo, co-founded the Almanac Singers and the Weavers, and helped spark the mid-twentieth-century folk revival. Songs associated with him include "Where Have All the Flowers Gone?", "If I Had a Hammer", "Turn! Turn! Turn!" and his adaptation of "We Shall Overcome".

Career

Seeger travelled with Woody Guthrie in 1940, collecting and performing folk songs. The Weavers' 1950 recording of "Goodnight, Irene" topped the charts, but the group was blacklisted during the McCarthy era; Seeger himself was convicted of contempt of Congress in 1961 for refusing to answer questions about his associations (the conviction was overturned on appeal). Solo concerts, often for children and campuses, rebuilt his career.

Activism

Seeger's music was intertwined with labour, civil-rights, anti-war and environmental causes. He co-founded the Hudson River Sloop Clearwater organisation, whose campaigning helped clean up the Hudson River. He remained a prominent protest singer into his nineties, performing at the 2009 presidential inauguration concert. Among many honours, he was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1996 and received a Grammy Lifetime Achievement Award.
