Quotations from Chairman Mao Tse-tung (Chinese: 毛主席语录) is a book of statements from speeches and writings by Mao Zedong, founding leader of the People's Republic of China. Universally known in English as the Little Red Book, it is among the most printed books in history — estimates of copies printed during the 1960s–70s run into the billions (including pamphlet editions) — and became the central icon of the Cultural Revolution.

Compilation and publication

The book was compiled by the General Political Department of the People's Liberation Army under the direction of Lin Biao, initially for political education within the army; the first edition appeared in May 1964. From 1966, at the launch of the Cultural Revolution, it was distributed nationwide to workers, peasants, students, and Red Guards, who carried, quoted, and waved it at mass rallies. Its 33 chapters address topics including the Communist Party, class struggle, war and peace, imperialism, ideology, culture, and study.

Role in the Cultural Revolution

Possession and quotation of the book became tests of political loyalty; study sessions structured daily life, and quotations were invoked to settle arguments and denounce opponents. After Lin Biao's death in 1971 — he had written the famous calligraphic foreword urging readers to "study Chairman Mao's writings, follow his teachings" — copies of the foreword were often removed or defaced. Mass printing ceased after Mao's death in 1976 and the reform era began.

Legacy

Foreign-language editions, published by Beijing's Foreign Languages Press, spread Maoist thought to movements worldwide. Today the book survives as a historical artefact and tourist souvenir in China and a studied primary source on Maoism and the Cultural Revolution abroad.
