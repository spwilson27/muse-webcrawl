Conveyancing

Conveyancing is the legal process of transferring legal title of real property from one person or entity to another. In common law jurisdictions it is typically performed by a licensed conveyancer or solicitor for the buyer and seller. A typical conveyancing transaction has two major phases: the exchange of contracts (when equitable title passes) and completion (when legal title passes), at least in England and Wales, the jurisdiction whose terminology is most widely used.

The process

The buyer's conveyancer investigates the seller's title, raises enquiries, and carries out searches with local authorities, water companies and other bodies to reveal matters such as planning restrictions, flood risk, contamination and rights of way. A survey and mortgage valuation are arranged if the buyer is borrowing. When both parties are ready, contracts are exchanged and a deposit paid; on completion the balance is transferred, the transfer deed executed and stamp duty land tax (or a devolved equivalent) paid. The transaction is then registered with the land registry.

Other jurisdictions

In much of the United States the analogous process is a real estate closing or settlement, administered by a title company or escrow agent, with title insurance commonly provided instead of the reliance on public registers seen elsewhere. Scotland uses its own system of missives and disposition, historically handled by solicitors, and many civil law jurisdictions require a notary to authenticate the transfer.
