Super Bowl I

Super Bowl I was the first AFL–NFL World Championship Game, played on January 15, 1967, at the Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum in Los Angeles, California. The National Football League (NFL) champion Green Bay Packers defeated the American Football League (AFL) champion Kansas City Chiefs by a score of 35–10.

Super Bowl I
Date: January 15, 1967
Stadium: Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum
Result: Green Bay Packers 35, Kansas City Chiefs 10
MVP: Bart Starr (Packers, quarterback)
Attendance: 61,946

Background

The game arose from the June 1966 merger agreement between the rival NFL and AFL, which provided for a season-ending championship between the two leagues before they fully merged in 1970. The Packers, coached by Vince Lombardi, had won the NFL Championship, while the Chiefs, coached by Hank Stram, had won the AFL title.

Game summary

Green Bay led only 14–10 at halftime after a Chiefs touchdown pass from Len Dawson to Curtis McClinton. In the second half, the Packers pulled away: an interception by Willie Wood set up a touchdown, and receiver Max McGee — who had caught only four passes all season and had been out the night before — finished with seven receptions for 138 yards and two touchdowns. Quarterback Bart Starr was named Most Valuable Player.

Legacy

The game was televised by both CBS and NBC, the only Super Bowl broadcast by two American networks, and is remembered for establishing the championship spectacle that grew into the Super Bowl tradition.
