Road of Life

The Road of Life (Russian: Дорога жизни, Doroga zhizni) was the supply route across frozen Lake Ladoga that kept Leningrad fed and supplied during the Nazi siege of the city in the Second World War. It operated chiefly in the winters of 1941–42 and 1942–43, when trucks drove over the ice between the lake's western and eastern shores.

Operation

When German and Finnish forces encircled Leningrad in September 1941, the only connection to the rest of the Soviet Union lay across Lake Ladoga. In summer, supplies moved by barge; in winter, an ice road was laid out, with traffic controllers, anti-aircraft guns, and repair teams stationed along it. Despite bombardment and the dangers of thin ice, the road delivered hundreds of thousands of tonnes of food and fuel and evacuated well over a million civilians and wounded soldiers.

Legacy

The Road of Life is commemorated by memorials along its route, including the "Broken Ring" monument at the lake shore. It remains a powerful symbol of the endurance of Leningrad's population during the 872-day siege.
