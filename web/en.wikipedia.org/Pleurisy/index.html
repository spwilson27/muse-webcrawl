Pleurisy

Pleurisy
SpecialtyPulmonology
SymptomsSharp chest pain worsened by breathing, cough, fever
CausesViral or bacterial infection, pneumonia, pulmonary embolism, autoimmune disease
SynonymsPleuritis

Pleurisy, also known as pleuritis, is inflammation of the pleura — the two thin layers of tissue that line the chest cavity and surround the lungs. Its hallmark is a sharp chest pain, called pleuritic pain, that worsens with breathing, coughing, or sneezing as the inflamed layers rub against each other.

Causes

Pleurisy most often results from a viral infection, but it can also be caused by pneumonia and other bacterial infections, tuberculosis, a pulmonary embolism, chest trauma, or autoimmune conditions such as lupus and rheumatoid arthritis. Fluid may accumulate between the pleural layers, forming a pleural effusion, which can paradoxically ease the pain while impairing breathing.

Diagnosis and treatment

A clinician may hear a characteristic friction rub through a stethoscope; diagnosis is usually confirmed with chest imaging, such as X-ray, ultrasound, or CT. Treatment is directed at the underlying cause — antibiotics for bacterial infection, for example — combined with anti-inflammatory pain relievers such as ibuprofen. Historically, pleurisy was a common and sometimes fatal illness; it is recorded among the causes of death of figures such as Catherine de' Medici and Benjamin Franklin.
