Terceira is an island in the Azores archipelago, in the middle of the North Atlantic Ocean. It is one of the larger islands of the archipelago, with a population of roughly 53,000 in an area of approximately 400 square kilometres. Its historical capital, Angra do Heroísmo, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

History

"Terceira" means "third" in Portuguese, as it was the third island of the archipelago to be discovered, in the mid-15th century. Angra's harbor became a crucial stopping point for Portuguese fleets returning from India and the Americas. During the Portuguese succession crisis of 1580, Terceira resisted Spanish rule, defeating a Spanish fleet at the Battle of Salga Bay in 1581. In the 20th century the island became strategically important again with the establishment of Lajes Field, an air base used by Portugal and the United States.

Geography and culture

The island is volcanic, formed by several stratovolcanoes; the Algar do Carvão is a visitable volcanic chimney. Terceira is known for the Sanjoaninas festivals in June and for its tradition of impromptu sung duels (cantoria) and street bull-running (tourada à corda).
