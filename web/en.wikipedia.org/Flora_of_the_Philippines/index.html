Flora of the Philippines

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The flora of the Philippines is one of the richest in Asia, with an estimated 10,000–14,000 species of vascular plants, of which roughly a third to a half are endemic to the archipelago. The Philippines is recognised as one of the world's 17 megadiverse countries and a global biodiversity hotspot, its plant life shaped by a complex geological history of volcanic island arcs, shifting sea levels, and a humid tropical climate.

Vegetation types

Lowland forests were historically dominated by the Dipterocarpaceae — the great timber trees such as narra (the national tree, Pterocarpus indicus), molave, lauan, and apitong. Montane mossy forests clothe the Cordillera and other ranges; mangrove forests of Rhizophora and Sonneratia fringe the coasts; and distinctive ultramafic forests grow on metal-rich soils, particularly on Palawan and in eastern Mindanao. Palawan's flora has strong affinities with Borneo, while much of the rest of the archipelago shows Malesian relationships with endemic radiations.

Notable plants

Endemic highlights include the jade vine (Strongylodon macrobotrys) with its turquoise hanging flowers, pitcher plants (Nepenthes) — the Philippines is a centre of their diversity — and the world's largest flower genus Rafflesia, represented by species such as Rafflesia speciosa. The sampaguita (Jasminum sambac) is the national flower. Orchids number over a thousand species, including the waling-waling (Vanda sanderiana).

Conservation

More than 90 per cent of the original forest cover has been lost to logging and conversion, making the Philippine flora among the most threatened on Earth. Remaining forests survive mainly in protected areas such as the Sierra Madre Natural Park and Mt. Kitanglad, and hundreds of species are listed as threatened.
