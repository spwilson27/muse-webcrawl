Dan Shechtman (born 1941) is an Israeli materials scientist at the Technion – Israel Institute of Technology who won the 2011 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the discovery of quasicrystals — solid materials with an ordered structure that, unlike conventional crystals, lacks periodic translational symmetry.

Discovery of quasicrystals

On April 8, 1982, while on sabbatical at the U.S. National Bureau of Standards, Shechtman observed an electron diffraction pattern from a rapidly cooled aluminium–manganese alloy showing sharp spots with tenfold (icosahedral) symmetry — a symmetry then considered impossible for crystals. His 1984 publication of the result met years of skepticism and ridicule, most famously from double Nobel laureate Linus Pauling. The discovery ultimately forced a redefinition of the concept of a crystal by the International Union of Crystallography and opened a new field of materials research.

Later life

Shechtman has received numerous honors, including the Wolf Prize in Physics (1999). In 2014 he ran for President of Israel, finishing third in the Knesset vote.
