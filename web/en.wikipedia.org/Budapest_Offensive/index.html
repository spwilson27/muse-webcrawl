Budapest Offensive
Date: 29 October 1944 – 13 February 1945
Location: Budapest and Transdanubia, Hungary
Result: Soviet victory

The Budapest Offensive was an offensive conducted by the Soviet Union's Red Army against Nazi Germany and its Hungarian allies on the Eastern Front of the Second World War. The operation, which ran from 29 October 1944 to 13 February 1945, culminated in the Siege of Budapest and the capture of the Hungarian capital.

Course of the operation

The offensive was conducted by the 2nd Ukrainian Front under Marshal Rodion Malinovsky and the 3rd Ukrainian Front under Marshal Fyodor Tolbukhin. After advancing across the Hungarian plain, Soviet forces encircled Budapest on 26 December 1944. The Axis garrison, which included German SS formations and Hungarian troops, defended the city under Hitler's orders to hold it as a fortress.

Several German attempts to relieve the city (Operations Konrad I, II and III) failed, and the garrison's attempted breakout on 11 February 1945 was largely annihilated. Budapest was captured on 13 February 1945. The fighting caused heavy military and civilian casualties and devastated much of the city.

Aftermath

The fall of Budapest opened the way for the Soviet advance on Vienna. The offensive was followed by the German Operation Spring Awakening around Lake Balaton in March 1945, the last major German offensive of the war.
