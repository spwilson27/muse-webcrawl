Human head

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

In human anatomy, the head is at the top of the human body. It supports the face and is maintained by the skull, which itself encloses the brain. The head rests on the neck, supported by the cervical spine, and contains the organs of sight, hearing, smell, and taste, as well as the mouth used for eating and speech.

[Image: Lateral view of the human head and skull]

Structure

The human skull consists of 22 bones — 8 cranial and 14 facial — fused at sutures (the mandible remains movable). Inside the cranium, the brain is protected by the meninges and cerebrospinal fluid. The face features the eyes in their orbits, the nose, cheeks, and jaws; the teeth bite and chew food. Twelve pairs of cranial nerves emerge from the brain through the skull.

Function

The head houses the brain, the centre of the nervous system, and the special sense organs. It is central to communication — through speech produced by the mouth and vocal tract, and facial expressions produced by dozens of small muscles — and to social identity and recognition.

Cultural significance

Across cultures the head is treated as the seat of intellect and spirit; it features prominently in art, portraiture, and sculpture, and idioms in many languages reflect its symbolic primacy ("head of state", "head of the family").
