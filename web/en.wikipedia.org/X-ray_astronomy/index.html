X-ray astronomy

X-ray astronomy is the branch of astronomy that studies astronomical objects at X-ray wavelengths, revealing extremely hot and energetic processes: accreting black holes and neutron stars, supernova remnants, galaxy clusters and the Sun's corona.

History

Because the atmosphere blocks X-rays, observations require rockets and satellites. The field began in 1962 when a sounding rocket led by Riccardo Giacconi discovered the first cosmic X-ray source, Scorpius X-1. Major observatories include Uhuru, Einstein, ROSAT, ASCA, Chandra and XMM-Newton.

Methods

X-ray telescopes use grazing-incidence mirrors to focus photons; detectors record spectra and rapid variability, probing matter at millions of degrees and the environments of compact objects.
