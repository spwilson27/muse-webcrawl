Séance

A séance (from French séance, "session", from seoir, "to sit") is an attempt to communicate with spirits or the dead, typically conducted by a medium with a group of participants sitting together, often around a table in dim light.

Practice

Séances became central to the religion of Spiritualism, which emerged in the United States in the late 1840s with the Fox sisters. In a typical séance, participants might join hands, ask questions of spirits, and interpret responses such as raps, table-turning, automatic writing or apparent materializations. Some sittings employ props such as Ouija boards, trumpets or cabinets.

Skepticism

Stage magicians and investigators, most famously Harry Houdini in the 1920s, exposed fraudulent mediums who used concealed accomplices, sleight of hand and cold reading. Psychologists attribute many séance phenomena to suggestion, the ideomotor effect and expectation. Nonetheless, séances remain popular in Spiritualist churches and popular culture.
