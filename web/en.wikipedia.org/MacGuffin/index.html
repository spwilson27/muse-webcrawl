MacGuffin

In fiction, a MacGuffin (sometimes McGuffin) is an object, device, or event that is necessary to the plot and the motivation of the characters, but insignificant, unimportant, or irrelevant in itself. The term was popularised by Alfred Hitchcock, who credited his screenwriter Angus MacPhail with coining it.

Hitchcock's usage

Hitchcock explained the idea with a joke about a mysterious device carried on a train: a passenger asks what the package contains and is told it is a "MacGuffin" used for trapping lions in the Scottish Highlands; when the questioner objects that there are no lions in the Highlands, the reply is, "Well, then, that's no MacGuffin." The point is that the object matters only because the characters care about it — the microfilm in North by Northwest, the "government secrets" of Notorious, or the stolen money in Psycho, which is discarded once its narrative purpose is served.

Examples

Famous MacGuffins include the One Ring in The Lord of the Rings (unusual in that it is also thematically central), the briefcase in Pulp Fiction, the Maltese Falcon in the film of that name, the plans and later the Death Star data files in Star Wars, and Rosebud's backstory in Citizen Kane. George Lucas credited the concept for the Ark of the Covenant in Raiders of the Lost Ark.

Criticism

Some writers regard an arbitrary MacGuffin as lazy plotting, arguing that effective stories make the sought object meaningfully connected to theme and character; others defend it as a clean way to focus attention on the characters' pursuit rather than the prize.
