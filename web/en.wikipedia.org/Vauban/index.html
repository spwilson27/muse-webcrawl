Vauban

Sébastien Le Prestre de Vauban (May 1633 – 30 March 1707), commonly known simply as Vauban, was a French military engineer and Marshal of France who revolutionised both siege warfare and fortification design under Louis XIV. Over a fifty-year career he directed some 53 sieges, all successful, and designed or rebuilt more than 160 fortresses.

Fortifications

Vauban refined the bastioned star fort into flexible systems adapted to terrain, with layered defences of ravelins, hornworks, glacis and covered ways designed to absorb artillery fire and channel attackers into killing zones. He built a double line of fortresses — the pré carré — along France's northern frontier. Twelve of his sites were inscribed together as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2008.

Siegecraft

Vauban's methodical attack relied on parallels — successive trenches dug parallel to the fortress walls and connected by zigzag saps — minimising casualties while making the fall of a fortress a matter of calculation. He also developed ricochet fire, in which cannonballs were bounced along the length of enemy parapets. His maxim held that a siege conducted by the rules was inevitable: "more powder, less blood."

Other work

Beyond engineering, Vauban wrote widely on economics, taxation and colonial policy; his privately circulated Projet d'une dîme royale (1707), proposing a single proportional tax, displeased the king and was suppressed shortly before his death.
