External fertilization

External fertilization is a mode of reproduction in which a male organism's sperm fertilizes a female organism's egg outside of the female's body. It contrasts with internal fertilization, in which sperm are introduced inside the female. External fertilization typically occurs in aquatic environments, where both eggs and sperm are released into the water in spawning events.

Spawning

Many fish, amphibians such as frogs, corals, and other marine invertebrates reproduce by broadcast spawning, releasing large numbers of gametes so that some zygotes survive. Environmental cues such as water temperature and lunar phase often synchronise spawning within a population.

Advantages and disadvantages

External fertilization allows large numbers of offspring and wide dispersal, but gametes and embryos are exposed to predation and environmental hazards, and fertilisation success can be low in dilute conditions.
