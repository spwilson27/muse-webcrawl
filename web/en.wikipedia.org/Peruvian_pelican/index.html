Peruvian pelican

The Peruvian pelican (Pelecanus thagus) is a member of the pelican family found along the Pacific coast of South America, in the cold, productive waters of the Humboldt Current, from northern Peru to southern Chile.

Description

It is a large, dark pelican with a wingspan over 2.2 metres. The plumage is mostly dark brown-black with a white stripe along the neck, a white head with a yellowish crest in breeding season, and a huge bill with a brightly coloured pouch — blue, red and orange — during courtship. It was formerly considered a subspecies of the brown pelican but is now generally treated as a separate species.

Behaviour and diet

Peruvian pelicans feed mainly on anchovetas and other schooling fish, plunge-diving or surface-seizing prey, often in association with guanay cormorants and Peruvian boobies. They nest in colonies on rocky islands and headlands, where their accumulated droppings historically formed valuable guano deposits.

Conservation

The species is listed as Near Threatened. Its population fluctuates dramatically with El Niño events, which disperse the anchoveta schools; the severe 1997–98 El Niño caused mass starvation. Overfishing of anchoveta and disturbance at colonies are continuing threats, though numbers recovered to several million birds in good years.
