Bread


Various breads
TypeStaple food
Main ingredientsFlour, water, yeast or leavening
OriginFertile Crescent, Neolithic

Bread is a staple food prepared from a dough of flour (usually wheat) and water, usually by baking. Throughout recorded history it has been a prominent food in large parts of the world. It is one of the oldest human-made foods, having been of significance since the dawn of agriculture, and plays an essential role in both religious rituals and secular culture.

History

Evidence from charred crumbs at Shubayqa 1 in Jordan shows bread-making from wild grains about 14,500 years ago, before farming. Ancient Egyptians developed leavened bread and large-scale baking; Greeks and Romans refined milling and produced many loaf types. Sourdough fermentation and later commercial yeast (19th century) shaped modern practice. The Chorleywood bread process (1961) industrialized soft sliced loaves, while artisan baking revived long-fermentation methods in the late 20th century.

Types and production

Breads range from flatbreads (pita, naan, tortilla, chapati) to enriched loaves (brioche, challah), steamed breads (mantou), and crispbreads. Leavening may be by yeast, sourdough cultures, baking soda, or steam. Key steps include mixing, kneading to develop gluten, bulk fermentation, shaping, proofing, baking, and cooling. Staling is driven by starch retrogradation and is slowed by refrigeration avoidance, emulsifiers, or reheating.

Nutrition and culture

Whole-grain breads provide fibre, B vitamins, and minerals; refined white breads are often fortified. Bread carries deep symbolic weight—"daily bread" in the Lord's Prayer, the Eucharist, challah on Shabbat—and appears in idioms worldwide ("breadwinner", "breaking bread").
