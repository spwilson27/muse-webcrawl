Pierre Auger Observatory

The Pierre Auger Observatory is an international cosmic ray observatory in Argentina designed to detect ultra-high-energy cosmic rays — the highest-energy particles ever observed, with energies above 1018 electronvolts. It is located near Malargüe in Mendoza Province, on a vast plain called the Pampa Amarilla.

Design

The observatory combines two techniques. A surface array of about 1,600 water Cherenkov detector tanks, spaced 1.5 km apart over 3,000 square kilometres, samples the particles of extensive air showers directly. Four fluorescence detector sites on the array's perimeter observe the faint ultraviolet light emitted as showers develop in the atmosphere on clear, moonless nights. This hybrid design allows precise measurement of both the energy and arrival direction of each cosmic ray.

Results

Named after French physicist Pierre Victor Auger, the observatory was completed in 2008 and is operated by a collaboration of more than 400 scientists from 17 countries. Its key findings include confirmation of the suppression of the cosmic ray flux at the highest energies (consistent with the GZK effect) and evidence that the highest-energy cosmic rays arrive preferentially from directions associated with nearby extragalactic objects, indicating sources outside the Milky Way.
