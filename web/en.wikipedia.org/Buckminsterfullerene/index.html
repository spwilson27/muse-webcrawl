Buckminsterfullerene

Buckminsterfullerene is a type of fullerene with the formula C60, composed of 60 carbon atoms arranged in a truncated icosahedron — a cage of 12 pentagons and 20 hexagons resembling a soccer ball and the geodesic domes of architect Buckminster Fuller, for whom it is named.

It was discovered in 1985 by Harold Kroto, Robert Curl, and Richard Smalley at Rice University in experiments simulating carbon chemistry in red giant stars; the discovery earned them the 1996 Nobel Prize in Chemistry.

C60 was later found in soot, lightning strikes, and interstellar space. Its derivatives are studied for superconductivity (when doped with alkali metals), lubricants, drug delivery, and photovoltaics.
