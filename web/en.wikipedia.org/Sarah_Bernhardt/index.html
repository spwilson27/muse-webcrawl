Sarah Bernhardt

BornHenriette-Rosine Bernard, 22/23 October 1844, Paris, France
Died26 March 1923 (aged 78), Paris, France
OccupationActress
Years active1862–1923

Sarah Bernhardt (born Henriette-Rosine Bernard; 22 or 23 October 1844 – 26 March 1923) was a French stage actress who starred in some of the most popular French plays of the late 19th and early 20th centuries, including La Dame aux Camélias by Alexandre Dumas fils, Ruy Blas by Victor Hugo, Fédora and La Tosca by Victorien Sardou, and L'Aiglon by Edmond Rostand. She also played male roles, including Hamlet in a prose translation by Marcel Schwob and Eugène Morand. Rostand called her "the queen of the pose and the princess of the gesture", Hugo praised her "golden voice", and she was often simply known as "the Divine Sarah".

Early life

Bernhardt was the daughter of a Dutch Jewish courtesan and an unknown father. Educated in convent schools, she entered the Paris Conservatoire in 1860 and made her debut at the Comédie-Française in 1862 in the title role of Racine's Iphigénie. Her early career was turbulent — she slapped a senior actress and was forced to resign — but she built her reputation at the Odéon, triumphing in 1869 as the troubadour Zanetto in François Coppée's Le Passant, her first real success.

Career

Returning to the Comédie-Française in 1872, Bernhardt achieved fame in the tragedies of Racine and Voltaire and in Hugo's Hernani (1877). In 1880 she resigned again and formed her own company, touring Europe and the Americas to enormous acclaim; her American tour of 1880–81 made her an international celebrity. She became manager of the Théâtre de la Renaissance in 1893 and in 1899 took over the theatre she renamed the Théâtre Sarah-Bernhardt.

Among her famous roles were Phèdre, Marguerite Gautier in La Dame aux Camélias, Sardou's Tosca and Fédora (written for her), Rostand's L'Aiglon (1900), in which she played the young Duke of Reichstadt, and Hamlet (1899), one of her celebrated male roles. She also appeared in early films, including Le Duel d'Hamlet (1900), La Tosca (1908) and Queen Elizabeth (1912), whose American success helped finance Adolph Zukor's Famous Players company.

Later life

In 1915 her right leg was amputated after a knee injury sustained years earlier became gangrenous; remarkably, she continued to perform, touring the front lines to entertain French troops during the First World War and acting seated or supported. She died of uraemia in Paris in 1923 and was buried in Père Lachaise Cemetery after a funeral attended by enormous crowds.

Legacy

Bernhardt was one of the first modern international theatre stars and a pioneer of celebrity self-promotion; her posters by Alphonse Mucha helped define Art Nouveau graphic style. Her summer home on Belle-Île in Brittany is preserved as a museum. She was made a member of the Legion of Honour in 1914.
