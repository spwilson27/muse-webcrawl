Susanoo

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Susanoo (スサノオ; historical orthography スサノヲ; Susano-o no Mikoto) is a kami in Japanese mythology. The younger brother of Amaterasu, goddess of the sun and legendary ancestress of the imperial line, and of Tsukuyomi, god of the moon, Susanoo is the storm god of the sea and storms. He is depicted as a wild, impetuous deity whose misbehaviour leads to his banishment from heaven, after which he performs heroic deeds on earth, most famously slaying the eight-headed serpent Yamata no Orochi.

Birth

According to the Kojiki, Susanoo was born from the nose of the creator god Izanagi as Izanagi cleansed himself after returning from Yomi, the land of the dead; Amaterasu was born from his left eye and Tsukuyomi from his right. Izanagi assigned Amaterasu the heavens, Tsukuyomi the night, and Susanoo the seas. Dissatisfied and weeping for his dead mother, Susanoo refused his domain and was banished by his father.

Conflict with Amaterasu

Before departing, Susanoo ascended to Takamagahara to bid farewell to Amaterasu, who suspected an invasion. In a ritual contest (ukehi) the two produced deities from each other's possessions — Amaterasu's jewels and Susanoo's sword — and Susanoo declared victory. In celebration he went on a rampage: he broke down the ridges of his sister's rice paddies, skinned a heavenly horse and threw it through the roof of her weaving hall, killing one of her attendants. Enraged and grieving, Amaterasu hid inside the Ame-no-Iwahato cave, plunging the world into darkness until the other gods lured her out. Susanoo was punished with the removal of his beard and fingernails and expelled from heaven.

Slaying of Yamata no Orochi

Descended to Izumo Province, Susanoo met an elderly couple whose seven daughters had been devoured, one each year, by the eight-headed, eight-tailed serpent Yamata no Orochi; the eighth, Kushinadahime, was about to be taken. Susanoo transformed the girl into a comb, instructed the couple to prepare eight vats of strong sake, and when the serpent drank itself senseless, cut it to pieces. In its middle tail he found the sword Ame-no-Murakumo (later called Kusanagi no Tsurugi), which he presented to Amaterasu in reconciliation; the sword became one of the Three Imperial Regalia of Japan. He then married Kushinadahime at Suga in Izumo, composing a poem on the "eightfold fence" (yakumo) that is traditionally regarded as the first waka in Japanese literature.

Descendants and worship

Susanoo's descendants include Ōkuninushi, the central deity of the Izumo myths. He is enshrined at many shrines, including Yasaka Shrine in Kyoto (where the Gion Matsuri honours him), Tsushima Shrine, and shrines throughout the Izumo region. In the medieval period he was syncretised with Gozu Tennō, a plague deity, and he remains a popular figure in Japanese religion, literature, and popular culture.
