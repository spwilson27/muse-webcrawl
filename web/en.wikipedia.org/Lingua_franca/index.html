Lingua franca
A language systematically used for communication between groups of people who do not share a native language.

A lingua franca is a language or dialect systematically used to make communication possible between groups of people who do not share a native language or dialect — in particular when it is a third language that is distinct from both of the speakers' native languages. The term derives from the Mediterranean Lingua Franca (Sabir), a pidgin based mainly on Italian and used for trade around the Mediterranean from the Middle Ages into the 19th century.

Examples

Historical lingua francas include Aramaic in the ancient Near East, Koine Greek in the Hellenistic world, Latin in medieval and early modern Europe, and Arabic across the Islamic world. Swahili serves East Africa, Hindi-Urdu much of South Asia, Malay/Indonesian Southeast Asia, and French large parts of West Africa. English is the dominant global lingua franca of science, aviation, diplomacy and business.

Characteristics

A lingua franca may be any language, native to some community or a constructed or pidgin language. When it becomes nativised by a community it may develop into a creole. Unlike a vernacular, a lingua franca's defining function is inter-group communication rather than ethnic identity.
