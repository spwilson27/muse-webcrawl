California State Route 1

Length655.8 mi (1,055.4 km)
South endI-5 in Dana Point
North endUS 101 in Leggett

State Route 1 (SR 1) is a major north–south state highway in California that runs along much of the Pacific coastline of the state. At 655.8 miles (1,055 km), it is the longest state route in California and the second-longest highway of any type in the state. SR 1 has several names, the best known being Pacific Coast Highway (PCH) in Southern California, Cabrillo Highway, and Shoreline Highway; much of the route, including the famed Big Sur coast, is designated an All-American Road.

Route description

The highway begins at Interstate 5 in Dana Point and runs north through Orange and Los Angeles counties (where it passes through Malibu as the PCH), Ventura, and along the Central Coast through Big Sur, Monterey, and Santa Cruz to San Francisco, crossing the Golden Gate Bridge concurrently with US 101. North of San Francisco it hugs the Marin, Sonoma, and Mendocino coasts before ending at US 101 in Leggett.

History

The route was built in stages, with the dramatic Big Sur segment completed in 1937 and the Bixby Creek Bridge opened in 1932. Portions near Devil's Slide were replaced by a tunnel in 2013. The road is periodically closed by landslides, notably the major Mud Creek slide of 2017, one of the largest in California history.
