General officer

A general officer is an officer of high rank in the armies, and in some nations' air or space forces, marines, or naval infantry. The term general is used in two ways: as the generic title for all grades of general officer, and as a specific rank. General officers typically command units larger than a regiment, from brigades upward to entire field armies and services.

Grades

Most modern systems use four or five general officer grades. In the United States these are brigadier general (one star), major general (two stars), lieutenant general (three stars), and general (four stars), with the rare five-star ranks of General of the Army and General of the Air Force reserved for wartime. British and Commonwealth systems include brigadier (not always classed as a general officer), major-general, lieutenant-general, general, and field marshal.

History

The title derives from the French capitaine général, shortened to général, used from the 16th century for commanders of whole armies. As armies grew during the 17th and 18th centuries, subordinate grades such as lieutenant general and major general emerged to structure the chain of command.

Appointment and command

General officers are usually appointed by the head of state, in some countries subject to legislative confirmation. They hold senior staff appointments — such as chief of staff of a service — or command major formations and joint commands. The naval equivalent is a flag officer, and officers of general or flag rank are collectively known as general and flag officers in United States usage.
