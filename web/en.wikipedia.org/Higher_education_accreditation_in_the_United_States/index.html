Higher education accreditation in the United States

Higher education accreditation in the United States is a peer review process by which the validity of degrees and credits awarded by higher education institutions is assured. It is coordinated by accreditation commissions made up of private, non-governmental organizations known as accrediting agencies, which are themselves recognized by the United States Secretary of Education or by the Council for Higher Education Accreditation (CHEA).

Role and importance

Unlike most countries, the United States has no federal ministry that directly controls universities. Accreditation fills this gap: it certifies that an institution meets standards of quality in areas such as curriculum, faculty, student support, and finances. Accreditation by a recognized agency is a prerequisite for an institution's students to receive federal student aid under Title IV of the Higher Education Act of 1965, and it strongly affects the transferability of credits and the recognition of degrees by employers and other institutions.

Types of accreditors

Institutional accreditors review entire institutions. They historically included regional accreditors — such as the Higher Learning Commission, the Middle States Commission on Higher Education, and the Southern Association of Colleges and Schools — though a 2020 regulatory change removed the regional/national distinction in favour of a single category of institutional accreditors. Programmatic (specialized) accreditors evaluate particular programs, such as ABET for engineering, the American Bar Association for law schools, and the LCME for medical schools.

Process and criticism

Accreditation proceeds through self-study, peer site visits, and periodic reaffirmation, usually on cycles of up to ten years. The system has been criticized for conflicts of interest, for acting as a barrier to innovation and new entrants, and for failing to prevent the collapse of some low-quality for-profit colleges; reforms and oversight actions by the Department of Education and the National Advisory Committee on Institutional Quality and Integrity (NACIQI) continue to be debated.
