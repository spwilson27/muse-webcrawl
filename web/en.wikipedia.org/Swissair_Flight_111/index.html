Swissair Flight 111

Date: 2 September 1998
Aircraft: McDonnell Douglas MD-11, HB-IWF
Route: New York (JFK) — Geneva
Location: Atlantic Ocean, near Peggy's Cove, Nova Scotia, Canada
Fatalities: 229 (all occupants)
A Swissair McDonnell Douglas MD-11 similar to the accident aircraft.

Swissair Flight 111 was a scheduled international passenger flight from John F. Kennedy International Airport in New York City to Geneva, Switzerland. On 2 September 1998 the McDonnell Douglas MD-11 operating the flight crashed into the Atlantic Ocean southwest of Halifax, Nova Scotia, near Peggy's Cove, killing all 229 people on board. It remains one of the deadliest MD-11 accidents and the deadliest aviation disaster on Canadian soil in terms of a single aircraft crash offshore.

Accident

About an hour after departure, while cruising off the coast of Nova Scotia, the crew smelled smoke in the cockpit and declared a "pan-pan", diverting toward Halifax International Airport. A rapidly spreading fire above the cockpit ceiling disabled aircraft systems progressively. While the crew worked through checklists and dumped fuel to reach landing weight, the fire intensified; the aircraft's flight recorders stopped about six minutes before impact. The MD-11 struck the sea at high speed and broke apart.

Investigation

The Transportation Safety Board of Canada's investigation — one of the largest in aviation history, involving recovery of about two million pieces of wreckage from the seabed — concluded that an arcing event in the wiring of the in-flight entertainment network (IFEN) most likely ignited flammable thermal-acoustic insulation blankets (made with metalized Mylar) above the cockpit. The fire spread undetected because the insulation materials met then-current flammability standards yet could propagate flame. The Board issued numerous recommendations; regulators ordered removal or replacement of the metallized insulation material across fleets and stricter flammability testing.

Aftermath

The victims included UN official Jonathan Mann and his wife Mary-Lou Clements-Mann. Memorials stand at Peggy's Cove and Bayswater, Nova Scotia. The disaster, combined with financial difficulties, contributed to the decline of Swissair, which ceased operations in 2002.
