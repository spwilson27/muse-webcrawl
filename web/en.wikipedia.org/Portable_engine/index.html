Portable engine
TypeSteam engine
IntroducedMid-19th century
Main usesAgriculture, threshing, sawmills
SuccessorTraction engine; stationary diesel and electric power

A portable engine is a steam engine mounted on wheels that can be towed from place to place by horses or a tractor but is not self-propelled. Portable engines supplied belt power to farm and industrial machinery in the 19th and early 20th centuries.

Design and use

A typical portable engine consists of a horizontal boiler, a single- or compound-cylinder steam engine mounted on top, and a large flywheel from which a flat belt drives equipment such as a threshing machine, circular saw, or stone crusher. Unlike a traction engine it carries no gearing to drive its own wheels and no steering for powered travel, making it simpler and cheaper.

History

Portable engines were produced in large numbers from the 1850s by firms such as Ransomes, Clayton & Shuttleworth, and Marshall of Gainsborough, and were exported worldwide. They were gradually replaced by tractors with power take-offs and by electric motors after the First World War, though many survive in preservation and appear at steam rallies.
