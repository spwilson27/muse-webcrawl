Laplace's equation

Type: Second-order partial differential equation
Named after: Pierre-Simon Laplace
Fields: Electrostatics, fluid dynamics, heat conduction

Laplace's equation is a second-order partial differential equation named after Pierre-Simon Laplace, who first studied its properties. It is written ∇²f = 0, where ∇² is the Laplace operator. Solutions of Laplace's equation are called harmonic functions and are important in many areas of physics.

Forms

In three-dimensional Cartesian coordinates the equation reads ∂²f/∂x² + ∂²f/∂y² + ∂²f/∂z² = 0. It also has forms in cylindrical and spherical polar coordinates, the latter being central to problems with spherical symmetry.

Applications

Laplace's equation describes the electrostatic potential in a charge-free region, steady-state temperature distributions, incompressible irrotational fluid flow, and gravitational potentials. Boundary-value problems for it include the Dirichlet and Neumann problems.
