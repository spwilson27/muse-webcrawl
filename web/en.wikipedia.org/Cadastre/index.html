A cadastre or cadaster is a comprehensive land recording system of a country. It is a register of the real property (land parcels) of an area, including the dimensions and location of land parcels, the identities of the owners, and the value and land use of each parcel. Historically, cadastres were created primarily for taxation purposes; today they serve legal, administrative and economic functions.

History

Land registers for taxation date back to antiquity, including Roman records and the English Domesday Book of 1086. The modern cadastre developed in continental Europe: the Austrian cadastre of the early 19th century and the Napoleonic cadastre in France systematically mapped and registered every parcel, becoming models for many other countries.

Components

A cadastre typically combines cadastral maps, which show the boundaries and identifiers of parcels at large scale, with descriptive registers recording attributes such as ownership, tenure, area, value and use. Cadastral surveying fixes parcel boundaries on the ground, and the records are maintained by a cadastral or land registry authority.

Uses

Cadastres underpin secure land tenure, property taxation, land-use planning, infrastructure development and environmental management. Modern systems are maintained as digital databases, often as part of a land information system integrated with geographic information systems (GIS). International bodies such as the International Federation of Surveyors (FIG) promote cadastral reform and publish statements on the role of the cadastre in sustainable development.

Types

Systems vary between countries. In some, the cadastre is primarily fiscal; in others it is legal, forming part of the land registration system that guarantees title. Multipurpose cadastres combine fiscal, legal and planning information in a single framework.
