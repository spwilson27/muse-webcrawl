Bachianas Brasileiras

The Bachianas Brasileiras are a series of nine suites for various instrumental and vocal combinations composed by the Brazilian composer Heitor Villa-Lobos between 1930 and 1945. Each suite fuses elements of Brazilian folk and popular music with the contrapuntal and formal procedures of Johann Sebastian Bach, and gives its movements double titles — one in the Baroque manner (Prelúdio, Fuga, Ária) and one Brazilian (Modinha, Desafio, Ponteio).

Composer: Heitor Villa-Lobos
Number of suites: 9
Composed: 1930–1945
Most famous: No. 5 (Ária, 1938)

The suites

The set ranges from chamber works, such as No. 1 for eight cellos (1930–32) and No. 6 for flute and bassoon, to large orchestral scores including No. 2 (with its celebrated final movement "O Trenzinho do Caipira", "The Little Train of the Caipira") and No. 7 and No. 8 for full orchestra. No. 9, for wordless chorus or string orchestra, was the last completed, in 1945.

No. 5

The best known of the set is No. 5 for soprano and orchestra of cellos. Its Ária (Cantilena), written in 1938 to words by Ruth Valadares Corrêa, is one of the most famous melodies in Brazilian classical music; the composer later added a second movement, Dança (Martelo), in 1945. The aria was popularised worldwide by the soprano Bidu Sayão, for whom Villa-Lobos wrote it.
