Tsardom of Russia

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Tsardom of Russia (Russian: Русское царство, Russkoye tsarstvo), also known as the Tsardom of Muscovy, was the centralised Russian state from the assumption of the title of tsar by Ivan IV in 1547 until the foundation of the Russian Empire by Peter the Great in 1721.

History

Ivan the Terrible's coronation as tsar in 1547 formalised Moscow's claims as heir to both Byzantium and the Kievan Rus'. His reign saw the conquest of the Kazan and Astrakhan khanates, the beginning of the conquest of Siberia, the Livonian War, and the terror of the oprichnina. After his dynasty died out, the Time of Troubles (1598–1613) brought famine, civil war, and Polish occupation before Michael Romanov was elected tsar, founding a dynasty that would rule for three centuries. The 17th century brought the legal codification of serfdom (1649), the schism of the Old Believers, Razin's revolt, and steady expansion across Siberia to the Pacific.

Government and society

The tsardom was an autocracy tempered by the boyar aristocracy, the Zemsky Sobor assembly, and a growing chancery bureaucracy. The Orthodox Church was central to the state's ideology. Peter the Great's reforms — a regular army and navy, the Table of Ranks, new taxation, and the founding of St Petersburg in 1703 — transformed the state and culminated in his proclamation as emperor in 1721, marking the conventional end of the tsardom.
