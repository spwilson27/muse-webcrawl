Vytautas (c. 1350 – 27 October 1430), known as Vytautas the Great (Lithuanian: Vytautas Didysis), was Grand Duke of Lithuania from 1392 until his death and the most celebrated ruler in Lithuanian history. Under his rule the Grand Duchy reached its greatest territorial extent, stretching from the Baltic to the Black Sea, and together with his cousin Jogaila he destroyed the power of the Teutonic Order at the Battle of Grunwald in 1410.

Struggle for power

Vytautas was the son of Kęstutis, co-ruler of Lithuania with his brother Algirdas. After Algirdas's death in 1377, his son Jogaila succeeded; the ensuing civil war saw Jogaila imprison Kęstutis (who died in captivity in 1382) and Vytautas, who escaped — according to tradition, disguised in women's clothing by his wife Anna. Vytautas twice allied with the Teutonic Order against Jogaila (1382–84 and 1389–92), and twice reconciled. In 1392 the Ostrów Agreement made him Governor of Lithuania under Jogaila, now King of Poland; he soon ruled as Grand Duke in all but subordination.

Reign

Vytautas centralised the duchy, replacing regional princes with appointed governors, and pushed Lithuanian influence east and south: Smolensk was annexed, alliances and campaigns reached toward Novgorod, Pskov, and the steppe, and in 1399 his drive for empire in the east ended in disaster at the Battle of the Vorskla River against the Golden Horde. He settled Karaite and Tatar communities in Lithuania, granted privileges to Jewish communities, and patronised both Catholic and Orthodox churches.

His greatest triumph came on 15 July 1410 at Grunwald, where he commanded the Lithuanian forces in the victory that broke the Teutonic Order. The Congress of Lutsk (1429) showcased his prestige; Emperor Sigismund offered him a royal crown, but the coronation was delayed by Polish opposition intercepting the crown's envoys, and Vytautas died at Trakai in October 1430, weeks before the rescheduled ceremony.

Legacy

Vytautas is the pre-eminent hero of Lithuanian national tradition; his name graces Lithuania's principal university in Kaunas (Vytautas Magnus University), its highest state decoration, and the 500th anniversary of his death in 1930 was a major national commemoration. He is also claimed by Belarusian historiography as a ruler of a state whose majority population and chancery culture were Ruthenian.
