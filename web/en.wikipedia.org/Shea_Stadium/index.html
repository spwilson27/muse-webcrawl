Shea Stadium

William A. Shea Municipal Stadium, usually shortened to Shea Stadium, was a multi-purpose stadium in Flushing Meadows–Corona Park in the borough of Queens, New York City. It was the home ballpark of the New York Mets of Major League Baseball from 1964 to 2008, and also hosted the New York Jets of the NFL from 1964 to 1983 and, at various times, the New York Yankees and New York Giants.

History

The stadium opened on April 17, 1964, next to the site of the 1964 New York World's Fair. It was named for attorney William A. Shea, who had led the effort to bring National League baseball back to New York after the departure of the Dodgers and Giants. Shea hosted the 1964 MLB All-Star Game and four World Series (1969, 1973, 1986 and 2000); the "Miracle Mets" won the 1969 Series there, and the 1986 Mets won their famous Game 6 comeback at Shea.

Other events

Beyond sport, Shea Stadium hosted landmark concerts — most famously the Beatles' concert on August 15, 1965, which drew a then-record crowd of about 55,600 and helped launch the era of stadium rock. The Mets played their final game at Shea on September 28, 2008; the stadium was demolished in 2008–2009, and the team moved to the adjacent Citi Field in 2009. The site is now used as parking for Citi Field, with markers for the former location of home plate and the bases.
