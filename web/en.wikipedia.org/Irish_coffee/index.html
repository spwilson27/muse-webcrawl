Irish coffee

TypeCoffee cocktail
IngredientsHot coffee, Irish whiskey, sugar, cream
OriginFoynes, County Limerick, Ireland (1940s)
ServedHot, in a stemmed glass; cream floated on top

Irish coffee is a cocktail of hot coffee, Irish whiskey, and sugar, topped with cream — traditionally floated so the coffee is drunk through the cream. It is served in a stemmed Irish coffee glass and is listed among the IBA official cocktails.

Origin

The drink is credited to Joe Sheridan, head chef at the flying-boat port of Foynes, County Limerick, who in the winter of 1943 added whiskey to coffee served to cold, delayed transatlantic passengers, replying when asked if it was Brazilian coffee: "No — Irish coffee." Stanton Delaplane, a San Francisco journalist, helped popularise it in the United States after 1951, working with the Buena Vista bar to recreate the cream-floating technique.

Preparation

Standard proportions are roughly two parts Irish whiskey and four parts hot coffee with brown sugar, finished with lightly whipped or heavy cream poured over the back of a spoon so it floats. The cream should not be stirred in. Variants include Baileys coffee (with Irish cream liqueur) and numerous national versions substituting local spirits, such as Gaelic coffee with Scotch whisky.
