François Achille Bazaine

Marshal Bazaine (1811–1888), commander of the Army of the Rhine in 1870

François Achille Bazaine (13 February 1811 – 23 September 1888) was a French general and Marshal of France. He rose from the ranks to high command, serving in Algeria, the Crimea, Italy, and Mexico, but is chiefly remembered for his role in the Franco-Prussian War of 1870 and his surrender of the fortress of Metz, for which he was tried and convicted of treason.

Early career

Born at Versailles, Bazaine enlisted as a private in 1831 and won rapid promotion in the Foreign Legion and in Algeria. He served in the Crimean War, in the Franco-Austrian War of 1859, and in the French intervention in Mexico, where he commanded the expeditionary corps and was made Marshal of France in 1864.

Franco-Prussian War and Metz

In August 1870 Bazaine took command of the Army of the Rhine. After battles around Metz — Borny, Mars-la-Tour, and Gravelotte–Saint-Privat — he allowed his army of some 180,000 men to be encircled in Metz and, after an ineffective siege, capitulated on 27 October 1870, one of the largest surrenders of the century. The release of German forces besieging Metz contributed to France's defeat.

Trial and exile

In 1873 a military court convicted Bazaine of treason for negotiating with the enemy and surrendering prematurely; he was sentenced to death, commuted to twenty years' imprisonment. In 1874 he escaped from the fortress on the Île Sainte-Marguerite and fled to Spain, where he lived in poverty in Madrid until his death in 1888. Historians continue to debate whether his conduct was treason, political calculation, or simple incompetence.
