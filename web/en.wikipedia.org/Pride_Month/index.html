Pride Month

Pride Month is a month-long celebration and commemoration of LGBTQ pride, culture, and activism, observed in June in the United States and many other countries. It honors the anniversary of the Stonewall riots, which began on 28 June 1969 in New York City.

Observance

Pride Month is marked by parades, festivals, concerts, memorials, and educational events celebrating lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender, and queer people and advocating for their rights. Pride parades in cities worldwide draw millions of participants; New York City's march is among the largest.

History

The first pride marches were held in June 1970 in New York, Chicago, Los Angeles, and San Francisco to mark the first anniversary of Stonewall. The rainbow flag, designed by Gilbert Baker in 1978, became the movement's best-known symbol. The U.S. federal government officially recognized June as Pride Month beginning in 1999.
