Macbeth (character)

Macbeth is the title character and protagonist of William Shakespeare's tragedy Macbeth (c. 1606). A brave Scottish general, he is spurred by a prophecy from three witches — and by the ambition of his wife, Lady Macbeth — to murder King Duncan and seize the throne, after which guilt, paranoia, and further murders drive him to tyranny and destruction. The character is based loosely on the historical Macbeth, King of Scotland.

Role in the play

Macbeth is introduced as a valiant thane who has crushed a rebellion and defeated the Norwegian invaders. Meeting the witches on a heath, he hears them hail him as Thane of Cawdor and future king. When the first title is promptly conferred on him, ambition takes hold. Encouraged by Lady Macbeth, he murders Duncan in his sleep and frames the king's guards. Crowned king, he orders the murders of Banquo and of Macduff's family to secure his throne. Haunted by Banquo's ghost and abandoned by his lords, he clings to the witches' equivocal prophecies — that none "of woman born" shall harm him and that he will not fall until Birnam Wood comes to Dunsinane — but both turn against him, and he is killed by Macduff.

Character analysis

Macbeth is one of Shakespeare's most studied tragic figures: a man of imagination and conscience whose ambition corrupts him step by step. His soliloquies, including "Is this a dagger which I see before me" and the despairing "Tomorrow, and tomorrow, and tomorrow", chart his psychological disintegration. Theatrical tradition holds the play to be cursed, and actors often refer to it only as "the Scottish play".

Notable portrayals

Actors who have played Macbeth include David Garrick, Edmund Kean, Laurence Olivier, Ian McKellen, Patrick Stewart, and Michael Fassbender in the 2015 film adaptation, and Denzel Washington in The Tragedy of Macbeth (2021).
