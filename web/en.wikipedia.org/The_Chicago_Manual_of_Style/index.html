The Chicago Manual of Style

The Chicago Manual of Style (abbreviated CMOS or CMS) is a style guide for American English published since 1906 by the University of Chicago Press. It is one of the most widely used editorial style guides in the United States, with the 18th edition published in 2024.

Contents

The manual covers grammar and usage, punctuation, capitalisation, treatment of numbers and foreign words, documentation, and the mechanics of manuscript preparation, proofreading, indexing and book production. Its documentation chapters prescribe two citation systems: the notes and bibliography system, favoured in the humanities, and the author–date system, used in the sciences and social sciences.

History

The first edition, in 1906, was a single style sheet expanded from the Press's in-house rules into a 203-page volume. Successive editions grew with publishing practice; the 12th edition (1969) adopted its present title, and the 15th (2003) addressed electronic publishing, with an online edition launched alongside the 16th in 2010.

Use

"Chicago style" underlies the Turabian guide for student writers and is the house style of many academic journals, trade publishers and magazines. Related guides include the AP Stylebook for journalism and the MLA and APA manuals.
