The forty-seven rōnin (四十七士, Shijūshichishi) were the masterless former samurai of Asano Naganori of Akō who avenged their lord's death in the Akō incident of 1701–1703. Having killed the shogunal official Kira Yoshinaka, they were sentenced to die by seppuku, a punishment that preserved their honour as samurai. Their story epitomizes the loyalty, sacrifice, and persistence celebrated in the bushidō tradition.

Background

Asano's retainers were left masterless when he was ordered to kill himself after wounding Kira Yoshinaka in Edo Castle in 1701. Some 47 of them, under the leadership of the chief retainer Ōishi Kuranosuke, plotted revenge over the following two years, concealing their intent through apparent idleness and dissipation.

The attack

On a snowy night in the twelfth month of Genroku 15 (early 1703 in the Western calendar), the rōnin stormed Kira's residence in Edo, killed him, and presented his head at their lord's grave at Sengaku-ji temple. They then awaited the authorities, and on the shogun's orders committed seppuku. They were buried alongside Asano at Sengaku-ji, where an annual festival on 14 December commemorates them.
