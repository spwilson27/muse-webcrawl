Paranormal

Paranormal events are phenomena described in popular culture, folklore, and other non-scientific bodies of knowledge whose existence is said to lie beyond the scope of normal scientific understanding. The term — coined in the early twentieth century from the Greek para, "beside", and "normal" — covers subjects as diverse as ghosts, hauntings, extrasensory perception, telepathy, clairvoyance, psychokinesis, cryptids such as Bigfoot, and unidentified flying objects.

The scientific consensus is that paranormal phenomena have not been demonstrated under controlled conditions, and reported experiences are generally attributed to misperception, cognitive biases, hoaxes, suggestion, and natural causes. Nevertheless, belief in the paranormal is widespread: surveys in the United States and Europe consistently find that a large minority, and on some questions a majority, of respondents endorse at least one paranormal belief.

Research

The systematic study of paranormal claims began with the Society for Psychical Research, founded in London in 1882, and continued in academic parapsychology laboratories, notably J. B. Rhine's at Duke University. Skeptical organisations such as the Committee for Skeptical Inquiry have investigated claims and offered large monetary prizes for a successful demonstration of paranormal ability under controlled conditions; none has been claimed.

Cultural impact

Paranormal themes are a staple of popular entertainment, from nineteenth-century Gothic fiction and spiritualist séances to television programmes, films, and "ghost hunting" tourism. Anthropologists note that such beliefs appear in some form in virtually every culture.
