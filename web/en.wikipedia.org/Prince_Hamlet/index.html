Prince Hamlet

Prince Hamlet is the title character and protagonist of William Shakespeare's tragedy Hamlet (c. 1600). He is the Prince of Denmark, nephew to the usurping King Claudius and son of King Hamlet, the previous king. Throughout the play he struggles with the task laid on him by his father's ghost — to avenge the murder — and his hesitation, real or feigned madness, and philosophical introspection have made him the most discussed character in English literature.

Character

Hamlet is a student at Wittenberg, recalled to Elsinore for his father's funeral, only to find his mother Gertrude remarried to his uncle Claudius within a month. The ghost's revelation that Claudius murdered the old king sets Hamlet on a course of simulated madness ("an antic disposition") while he seeks confirmation of the ghost's story, which he obtains by staging the play-within-the-play, The Mousetrap.

His famous soliloquies — above all "To be, or not to be" — explore death, conscience, and the difficulty of action. Critics have debated for centuries whether Hamlet's delay is caused by moral scruple, melancholy, Oedipal conflict, or simply the demands of the revenge-tragedy plot.

"To be, or not to be, that is the question: / Whether 'tis nobler in the mind to suffer / The slings and arrows of outrageous fortune, / Or to take arms against a sea of troubles / And by opposing end them."

Death

Manipulated into a fencing match with Laertes, Hamlet is wounded by a poisoned rapier; in the ensuing confusion the Queen drinks poisoned wine, Laertes and Claudius are killed, and Hamlet dies after naming Fortinbras of Norway as his successor, asking Horatio to tell his story.

Sources and legacy

Shakespeare's Hamlet descends from the legendary Danish prince Amleth in Saxo Grammaticus's Gesta Danorum, retold by François de Belleforest, and from a lost earlier play, the so-called Ur-Hamlet. The role is considered the summit of the classical actor's repertoire, famously played by Richard Burbage, David Garrick, Edmund Kean, Laurence Olivier, and many others.
