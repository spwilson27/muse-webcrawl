Combat

Combat is a purposeful violent conflict involving two or more opposing military forces, intended to harm, damage or kill the opposing force and to impose one's will upon the enemy. Combat may be conducted on land, at sea, in the air, in space or in cyberspace, and ranges in scale from individual hand-to-hand fighting to battles involving armies and fleets.

Forms of combat

Combat is commonly classified by environment and method: land warfare, naval warfare and aerial warfare; close combat and hand-to-hand combat; and specialised forms such as trench warfare, guerrilla warfare, siege warfare and armoured warfare. The conduct of combat is governed in international law by the laws of armed conflict, including the Geneva Conventions, which set limits on targets, weapons and the treatment of prisoners and the wounded.

Combat effectiveness

Military science studies the factors that determine outcomes in combat, including training, morale, leadership, logistics, intelligence, technology and terrain. The psychological effects of combat on participants, including combat stress reaction and post-traumatic stress disorder, have been studied intensively since the First World War.
