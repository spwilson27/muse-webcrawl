Arthur Ashe Stadium

Location: USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center, Flushing Meadows–Corona Park, Queens, New York City

Opened: 1997

Capacity: 23,771

Surface: DecoTurf (hard, outdoor)

Roof: Retractable roof added 2016

Tenant: US Open (tennis)

Arthur Ashe Stadium is a tennis stadium at Flushing Meadows–Corona Park in Queens, New York City. Part of the USTA Billie Jean King National Tennis Center, it is the main stadium of the US Open tennis tournament and has the largest seating capacity of any tennis stadium in the world, at 23,771 seats.

The stadium is named after Arthur Ashe (1943–1993), winner of the inaugural US Open in 1968 — the first edition at which the tournament was open to professional tennis players — and the first African American male tennis player to win a Grand Slam title.

History

Opened in 1997 at a cost of roughly $254 million, the stadium replaced Louis Armstrong Stadium as the primary venue of the US Open. Its size was frequently criticized for its vast upper deck and corporate suites, but its scale reflects the tournament's popularity. Weather delays, notably five consecutive years of rain-delayed men's finals (2008–2012), prompted the USTA to add a retractable roof.

Retractable roof

A lightweight retractable roof, designed by Rossetti Architects with an ETFE panel structure spanning the open bowl, was completed for the 2016 US Open as part of a major transformation of the National Tennis Center. The roof can close in about seven minutes, making Arthur Ashe Stadium the largest tennis venue with such a roof.

Other events

Beyond tennis, the stadium has hosted events including the first WNBA outdoor regular-season game (the 2008 Liberty Outdoor Classic) and esports events such as the Fortnite World Cup finals in 2019.
