Jim Yong Kim


Jim Yong Kim (born 8 December 1959) is a Korean-American physician who served as president of the World Bank Group from 2012 to 2019.

Jim Yong Kim (Korean: 짐용김; born 8 December 1959) is a Korean-American physician, anthropologist, and global health leader. He served as the 12th president of the World Bank Group from 2012 until his resignation in 2019, and before that as president of Dartmouth College from 2009 to 2012, becoming the first Asian American president of an Ivy League institution.

Early life and career

Kim was born in Seoul, South Korea, and grew up in Muscatine, Iowa, after his family immigrated to the United States. He earned an MD and a PhD in anthropology from Harvard University. As a co-founder (with Paul Farmer and others) of the health charity Partners In Health in 1987, he helped pioneer community-based treatment of multidrug-resistant tuberculosis and HIV/AIDS in poor settings such as Haiti and Peru. He later directed the World Health Organization's HIV/AIDS department, leading the "3 by 5" initiative to expand antiretroviral treatment.

World Bank presidency

Nominated by U.S. president Barack Obama, Kim took office on 1 July 2012 and was re-elected to a second term in 2016. He set twin goals for the Bank — ending extreme poverty by 2030 and boosting shared prosperity — and championed investment in pandemic preparedness, climate finance, and refugee support. His internal reorganisations drew staff criticism. He announced his resignation in January 2019, effective 1 February 2019, to join the investment firm Global Infrastructure Partners. Kim received the MacArthur "Genius" Fellowship in 2003.
