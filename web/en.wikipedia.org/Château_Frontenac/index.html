Château Frontenac

The Château Frontenac, currently branded as the Fairmont Le Château Frontenac, is a grand hotel in Quebec City, Canada. Standing atop Cap Diamant overlooking the St. Lawrence River and the Dufferin Terrace, its château-style silhouette is one of Canada's most recognisable landmarks and is said to be among the most photographed hotels in the world.

History

Built by the Canadian Pacific Railway and opened in 1893, the hotel was designed by Bruce Price in the Châteauesque style and named for Louis de Buade, Count of Frontenac, a governor of New France. It hosted the Quebec Conferences of 1943 and 1944, where Allied leaders including Roosevelt, Churchill, and Mackenzie King planned aspects of the Second World War.

Architecture

The hotel's steep copper roofs, turrets, and massive towers dominate the skyline of Old Quebec, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. It was designated a National Historic Site of Canada in 1981.
