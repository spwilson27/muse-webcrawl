The Noble Eightfold Path (Pali: Ariya Aṭṭhaṅgika Magga; Sanskrit: Āryāṣṭāṅgamārga) is an early summary of the path of Buddhist practice leading to liberation from samsara, the painful cycle of rebirth. It is the fourth of the Buddha's Four Noble Truths and a core element of his first sermon at Sarnath, and constitutes a central practice framework across Buddhist traditions.

The eight factors

The path consists of eight interdependent practices, traditionally grouped into three divisions:

  1. Right view (sammā diṭṭhi) — understanding the Four Noble Truths and the nature of reality. (Wisdom)
  2. Right intention (sammā saṅkappa) — resolve toward renunciation, goodwill and harmlessness. (Wisdom)
  3. Right speech (sammā vācā) — abstaining from lying, divisive, harsh and idle talk. (Ethical conduct)
  4. Right action (sammā kammanta) — abstaining from killing, stealing and sexual misconduct. (Ethical conduct)
  5. Right livelihood (sammā ājīva) — earning a living without harming others. (Ethical conduct)
  6. Right effort (sammā vāyāma) — cultivating wholesome states and abandoning unwholesome ones. (Meditation)
  7. Right mindfulness (sammā sati) — attentive awareness of body, feelings, mind and phenomena. (Meditation)
  8. Right concentration (sammā samādhi) — meditative absorption (jhāna). (Meditation)

Interpretation

Despite the sequential numbering, the factors are meant to be developed in parallel rather than strictly in order; "right" (sammā) is sometimes rendered "complete" or "perfect". The path is described as the "middle way" between sensual indulgence and self-mortification. In Mahayana traditions it is incorporated into the bodhisattva path, while Theravada commentary organises it around the threefold training of morality (sīla), concentration (samādhi) and wisdom (paññā).
