Experiential learning

Learners reflect on direct experience to form concepts they then test in new situations.

Experiential learning is the process of learning by doing — acquiring knowledge, skills and values from direct experience outside a traditional academic setting, or reflecting on experience within one. It underpins internships, field work, service learning, outdoor education, laboratory work and study abroad.

Kolb's model

David A. Kolb's influential model, set out in his 1984 book Experiential Learning, describes a four-stage cycle: concrete experience, reflective observation, abstract conceptualisation, and active experimentation, after which the learner re-enters the cycle. Kolb also derived a widely used inventory of learning styles from the cycle's two axes — feeling versus thinking, and watching versus doing. Earlier roots of the approach appear in the work of John Dewey, Kurt Lewin and Jean Piaget.

Applications and critique

Experiential methods are used in management training, medical and teacher education, and adventure education. Critics note that reflection is essential — raw experience alone does not guarantee learning — and question the empirical support for matching instruction to Kolb-style learning-style categories.
