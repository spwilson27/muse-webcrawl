Berlin S-Bahn

The Berlin S-Bahn (German: S-Bahn Berlin) is a rapid transit railway system in and around Berlin, the capital of Germany. Together with the U-Bahn (underground), trams, and buses, it forms the backbone of public transport in the German capital. The network comprises 16 lines and 168 stations over about 340 kilometres of track, and is operated by S-Bahn Berlin GmbH, a subsidiary of Deutsche Bahn, within the Verkehrsverbund Berlin-Brandenburg (VBB) transport association.

History

The S-Bahn grew out of steam suburban services on Berlin's Ringbahn (ring line, completed 1877) and the Stadtbahn cross-city line (opened 1882). The network was progressively electrified with 750 V DC third rail beginning in 1924; the term "S-Bahn" was coined in 1930. After the Second World War the Deutsche Reichsbahn of East Germany continued to operate the S-Bahn in both halves of the divided city, which led to a boycott in West Berlin and, after the building of the Berlin Wall in 1961, severed lines and "ghost stations." West Berlin's S-Bahn was transferred to the BVG in 1984. Following reunification in 1990 the network was reintegrated, severed links rebuilt, and ridership recovered to well over a million passengers a day.

Network

The system is characterized by the Ringbahn circle line (lines S41/S42 running clockwise and counterclockwise) intersecting with radial and cross-city routes via the Stadtbahn and the north–south tunnel. Suburban branches reach destinations in Brandenburg such as Potsdam, Bernau, Königs Wusterhausen, and Berlin Brandenburg Airport (BER). Trains run mostly at 10- or 20-minute intervals, with near-continuous service on core sections.

Rolling stock

Current fleets include the Class 481/482 trains built in the 1990s and 2000s, older Class 480 and 485 units, and the newest Class 483/484 trains, which entered service from 2021 with air conditioning, modern information systems, and a return to the traditional ochre-and-red livery.
