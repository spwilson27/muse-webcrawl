Neon

Neon is a chemical element with the symbol Ne and atomic number 10. It is a noble gas — colourless, odourless, and almost completely inert — discovered in 1898 by William Ramsay and Morris Travers while fractionally distilling liquid air, and named from the Greek néon, "new."

Properties

Neon is the fifth most abundant element in the universe but rare on Earth, making up about 18 ppm of the atmosphere. It gives a brilliant reddish-orange glow in discharge tubes, a property exploited in neon signs (popularized after Georges Muse's demonstrations in 1910) and in high-voltage indicator lamps, plasma displays, and helium–neon lasers. Liquid neon is also an efficient cryogenic refrigerant, with more than 40 times the cooling capacity per unit volume of liquid helium.

Chemistry

No stable chemical compounds of neon are known under ordinary conditions; it was the first element for which isotopes were shown to exist, when J. J. Thomson's 1913 experiments revealed ²⁰Ne and ²²Ne.
