Crowdfunding

Crowdfunding is the practice of funding a project or venture by raising money from a large number of people, typically via the internet. Crowdfunding is a form of crowdsourcing and alternative finance. In 2015, over US$34 billion was raised worldwide by crowdfunding. Although similar concepts can also be executed through mail-order subscriptions, benefit events and other methods, the term crowdfunding usually refers to internet-mediated registries.

Models

  • Reward-based — backers receive a product or other reward; major platforms include Kickstarter (founded 2009) and Indiegogo.
  • Donation-based — contributions for charitable or personal causes, e.g. GoFundMe.
  • Equity crowdfunding — investors receive shares in a company; enabled in the US by the JOBS Act of 2012 (Regulation Crowdfunding, effective 2016).
  • Debt-based (peer-to-peer lending) — lenders earn interest on loans to individuals or businesses.

History

Crowdfunding has historical roots in subscription publishing and cooperative lending; an early celebrated example is the 1885 public campaign that funded the pedestal of the Statue of Liberty. The modern internet form took shape in the 2000s with platforms such as ArtistShare (2001) and Kiva (2005). Notable campaigns include the Pebble smartwatch (over $10 million on Kickstarter in 2012) and the video game Star Citizen, which raised hundreds of millions of dollars.

Benefits and risks

Crowdfunding lets creators validate demand, build communities and bypass traditional gatekeepers, but campaigns may fail to deliver, and backers in reward-based models are generally consumers, not investors with legal protections. Regulators in many countries have introduced rules for equity crowdfunding and fraud prevention.
