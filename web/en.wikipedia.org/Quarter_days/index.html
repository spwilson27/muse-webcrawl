Quarter days

In British and Irish tradition, the quarter days are the four dates in each year on which servants were hired, rents were due, and leases traditionally began or ended. The English quarter days are Lady Day, Midsummer Day, Michaelmas and Christmas Day.

The four days

The English and Welsh quarter days are Lady Day (25 March), Midsummer Day (24 June), Michaelmas (29 September) and Christmas Day (25 December). Scotland historically used different term days — Whitsunday and Martinmas — while Ireland shared England's quarter days. Lady Day was the start of the legal and farming year and, until 1752, of the English calendar year.

Use today

Although largely superseded, quarter days still appear in older leases and some commercial tenancies, and rents on agricultural and commercial property in the United Kingdom are sometimes still payable on the traditional quarter days.
