Decommunization

Decommunization is the dismantling of the institutional, legal, and cultural legacies of communist state establishments and their ideology in the post-communist states of Central and Eastern Europe after the revolutions of 1989 and the dissolution of the Soviet Union. It is closely related to, but distinct from, lustration — the vetting and exclusion of former regime officials and secret-police collaborators from public office.

Measures

Decommunization measures have included:

  • Legal bans or restrictions on communist parties, symbols, and propaganda
  • Renaming of streets, squares, and cities named after communist figures
  • Removal of monuments, including Lenin statues (in Ukraine, the mass removal of such statues was dubbed "Leninfall")
  • Opening secret-police archives to the public and to victims
  • Rewriting of school curricula and national historical narratives
  • Restitution of property nationalised by communist regimes

By country

Czechoslovakia passed one of the earliest and strictest lustration laws in 1991. Poland created the Institute of National Remembrance (1998) and expanded lustration in the 2000s; its 2016–2018 decommunization law ordered the renaming of public places honouring communism. Ukraine adopted a package of decommunization laws in 2015 that banned communist and Nazi symbols, condemned the communist regime, and mandated the renaming of thousands of settlements and streets — a process accelerated after the Russian invasion of 2022 under broader "derussification" policies. The Baltic states treated the Soviet period as an occupation and removed many Soviet monuments, culminating in large-scale removals in 2022.

Debates

Supporters argue such measures are necessary for historical justice and national security; critics contend that blanket bans can infringe free expression, be applied selectively, or erase complex history. The pace and depth of decommunization varied widely: some states pursued it vigorously in the 1990s, while others — notably Russia and Belarus — largely abandoned it.
