Cyanoacrylate

Cyanoacrylates are a family of strong, fast-acting adhesives derived from cyanoacrylate esters such as ethyl 2-cyanoacrylate and methyl 2-cyanoacrylate. Common trade names include Super Glue and Krazy Glue. They polymerise almost instantly in the presence of moisture, forming strong bonds between many materials.

History

Cyanoacrylates were discovered in 1942 by Harry Coover and Fred Joyner at Eastman Kodak while searching for materials for gun sights; the sticky compounds were initially rejected as too adhesive, but Coover later recognised their commercial potential, and they were marketed as adhesives from 1958.

Uses

Besides household and industrial bonding, medical-grade cyanoacrylates (such as 2-octyl cyanoacrylate) are used to close wounds in place of sutures. In forensic science, cyanoacrylate fuming develops latent fingerprints on non-porous surfaces. Their bonds are strong in tension and shear but brittle, and they degrade with prolonged exposure to water and heat.
