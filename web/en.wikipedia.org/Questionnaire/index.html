Questionnaire

A questionnaire is a research instrument that consists of a set of questions (or other types of prompts) for the purpose of gathering information from respondents through survey or statistical study. A research questionnaire is typically a mix of close-ended questions and open-ended questions. Open-ended, long-term questions offer the respondent the ability to elaborate on their thoughts. Questionnaires were developed in 1838 by the Statistical Society of London.

Design

Questionnaires are used in many fields, including psychology, social research, marketing, medicine, and government statistics. Good design requires clear, unambiguous wording, a logical flow from general to specific questions, and attention to the order in which questions are asked, since earlier questions can influence answers to later ones. Common response formats include multiple choice, rating scales such as the Likert scale, ranking, and free text.

Administration

Questionnaires may be self-administered on paper, by post, online, or by telephone, or administered by an interviewer face to face. Each mode has advantages and disadvantages: self-completion is cheap but may yield low response rates, while interviewer administration improves completeness at greater cost. Key quality concerns include sampling, non-response bias, social desirability bias, and the validity and reliability of the measures used.

Analysis

Responses are coded and analysed statistically; open-ended responses may be analysed qualitatively. Pilot testing (pretesting) with a small sample is standard practice to identify confusing items before full deployment.
