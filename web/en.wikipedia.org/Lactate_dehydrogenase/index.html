Lactate dehydrogenase

Lactate dehydrogenase
EC number1.1.1.27
ReactionLactate + NAD⁺ ⇌ pyruvate + NADH + H⁺
SubunitsTetramer of H and M subunits

Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH or LD) is an enzyme found in nearly all living cells. LDH catalyzes the conversion of pyruvate to lactate and back, as it converts NAD⁺ to NADH and vice versa. It is a dehydrogenase — an enzyme that transfers a hydride from one molecule to another — and belongs to the enzyme family of oxidoreductases.

Function

LDH is expressed extensively in body tissues, such as blood cells and heart muscle. The enzyme is especially important in anaerobic glycolysis: when oxygen is scarce, it regenerates NAD⁺ from NADH so that glycolysis can continue, producing lactate as a by-product.

Isoenzymes

LDH is a tetramer composed of two kinds of subunits, H (heart) and M (muscle), giving five isoenzymes (LDH-1 to LDH-5) with different tissue distributions. Measuring their proportions in blood was historically used in diagnosing myocardial infarction and liver disease.

Clinical significance

Because LDH is released from damaged cells, elevated blood LDH is a nonspecific marker of tissue injury, haemolysis, and some cancers; it is also used as a prognostic marker in certain lymphomas and germ-cell tumours.
