Ian Rankin

Ian James Rankin (born 28 April 1960) is a Scottish crime writer and philanthropist, best known for his Inspector Rebus novels, set mainly in and around Edinburgh. A leading figure in the "Tartan noir" school of Scottish crime fiction, he has sold tens of millions of books worldwide and is one of the most successful crime novelists of his generation.

Early life and career

Rankin was born in Cardenden, Fife, and studied literature at the University of Edinburgh. Before writing full-time he worked as a grape-picker, swineherd, taxman, alcohol researcher, hi-fi journalist and punk musician. His first novel, The Flood, appeared in 1986.

Inspector Rebus

The first Rebus novel, Knots & Crosses, was published in 1987. The series follows Detective Inspector John Rebus of the Edinburgh police — stubborn, melancholic, fond of pubs, vinyl records and unsolved grievances — as the city changes around him across more than two dozen books, from Hide and Seek (1991) and Mortal Causes (1994) to Exit Music (2007), which saw Rebus retire, and later volumes in which he returns as a civilian investigator. From Standing in Another Man's Grave (2012) the series also incorporates Malcolm Fox, the protagonist of Rankin's The Complaints (2009). Several Rebus novels were adapted for television, with John Hannah and later Ken Stott in the title role; a further adaptation, Rebus (2024), starred Richard Rankin (no relation).

Rankin also wrote three thrillers under the pen name Jack Harvey in the 1990s, and standalone novels including Doors Open (2008) and The Impossible Dead (2011). In 2021 he completed William McIlvanney's unfinished Laidlaw prequel, The Dark Remains. His memoir of writing and his son's Angelman syndrome, and his lyrics for the band St Jude's Infirmary, form smaller parts of his output.

Honours

Rankin was appointed OBE in 2002 and has received honorary doctorates from several universities, including Edinburgh. He won the Crime Writers' Association Gold Dagger for Black & Blue (1997) and received the CWA Diamond Dagger for lifetime achievement in 2005. He was knighted in the 2023 New Year Honours for services to literature and charity.
