Yerkes Observatory is an astronomical observatory located in Williams Bay, Wisconsin, United States. Founded by astronomer George Ellery Hale with financing from transportation magnate Charles T. Yerkes, it was operated by the University of Chicago from its dedication in 1897 until 2020, when ownership passed to the Yerkes Future Foundation. The observatory is famous as the home of the 40-inch (102 cm) refracting telescope, the largest refractor ever used successfully for astronomy.

History

Hale, then a young University of Chicago astronomer, persuaded Yerkes to fund not merely a telescope but an entire observatory with laboratories for physics and chemistry. The great refractor's lenses were ground by Alvan Clark & Sons from blanks cast in France; the telescope was exhibited at the World's Columbian Exposition of 1893 before installation. Yerkes became a leading centre for the new science of astrophysics: work there included the founding of The Astrophysical Journal, E. E. Barnard's photographic studies of the Milky Way and comets, and the stellar spectral classification work that led to the Morgan–Keenan system.

The 40-inch refractor

The observatory's principal instrument has an aperture of 40 inches (1.02 m) and a focal length of about 19.3 m (62 feet). It surpassed the 36-inch refractor at Lick Observatory and remains the largest refracting telescope ever used for scientific research; larger refractors were built later only for exhibition. The telescope was used for important work on stellar parallax, double stars, and nebular photography well into the twentieth century.

Architecture and legacy

The observatory building, designed by Henry Ives Cobb, is an ornate Romanesque Revival structure rich in astronomical ornamentation, set in grounds landscaped by the Olmsted firm. Generations of notable astronomers worked or studied at Yerkes, including Edwin Hubble, Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar, Gerard Kuiper, and Carl Sagan. After the University of Chicago ended research operations in 2018 and closed the site, the Yerkes Future Foundation took ownership in 2020 and has undertaken restoration, reopening the observatory for tours, education, and events.
