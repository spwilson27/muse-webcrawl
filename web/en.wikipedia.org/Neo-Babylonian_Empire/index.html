The Neo-Babylonian Empire, also known as the Second Babylonian Empire or Chaldean Empire, was the last Mesopotamian empire, ruling from the revolt of Nabopolassar in 626 BCE until the Persian conquest of Babylon by Cyrus the Great in 539 BCE.

Rise

Nabopolassar, a Chaldean leader, seized Babylon from Assyria and, allied with the Medes, destroyed Nineveh in 612 BCE. His son Nebuchadnezzar II defeated Egypt at Carchemish (605 BCE), extended the empire over Syria and the Levant, destroyed Jerusalem and its Temple in 597 and again in 587/586 BCE, deporting much of Judah's population — the Babylonian captivity of biblical tradition.

Babylon at its height

Nebuchadnezzar rebuilt Babylon on a monumental scale: the Ishtar Gate and Processional Way, the great ziggurat Etemenanki, and vast walls made it the largest city of its age. The empire saw flourishing astronomy, mathematics, and commerce recorded on hundreds of thousands of clay tablets.

Fall

After Nebuchadnezzar the throne passed through weak successors to Nabonidus, whose religious eccentricities and long absence at Tayma alienated the priesthood. In 539 BCE Cyrus of Persia took Babylon with little resistance, ending native Mesopotamian rule.
