Smart card

A smart card with its visible chip contacts

A smart card (also chip card or integrated circuit card) is a physical electronic authentication device, typically the size of a credit card, used to control access to a resource. It carries an embedded integrated circuit — a secure microcontroller or memory chip — that can store data, perform cryptographic operations and interact with a card reader. Smart cards may provide identification, authentication, data storage and application processing.

Types and interfaces

Contact smart cards communicate through electrical contacts defined by the ISO/IEC 7816 standard and are inserted into a reader. Contactless smart cards use radio-frequency induction, following ISO/IEC 14443, and communicate when held near a reader; dual-interface cards support both. The chip in a SIM card is a small-format smart card, as are the chips in EMV payment cards.

Applications

Smart cards are used worldwide for payment (EMV "chip and PIN"), mobile telephony, public transport ticketing, electronic identity documents and passports, pay television, healthcare records and building access control. Because the chip can hold private keys and execute cryptographic algorithms internally, the secret key need never leave the card, which makes smart cards a strong form of two-factor authentication when combined with a PIN.

History

The concept was described in the late 1960s by German inventors Jürgen Dethloff and Helmut Gröttrup, and developed in France in the 1970s by Roland Moreno. Mass deployment began with French telephone cards and banking cards in the 1980s and 1990s, followed by the GSM SIM in 1991 and the global EMV migration in the 2000s and 2010s.
