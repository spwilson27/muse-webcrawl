Counterterrorism
DefinitionPractices and strategies to prevent or respond to terrorism
ActorsGovernments, military, law enforcement, intelligence agencies
Key bodiesUN Counter-Terrorism Committee; national CT centres

Counterterrorism (also spelled counter-terrorism) incorporates the practices, military tactics, techniques, and strategies that governments, militaries, law enforcement, and intelligence agencies use to combat or prevent terrorism.

Approaches

Counterterrorism spans prevention (intelligence gathering, disrupting plots, countering radicalisation), protection (securing infrastructure, aviation, and public spaces), pursuit (arrests, prosecutions, and in some contexts military action), and response and recovery after attacks. Strategies differ on the balance between criminal-justice and military models.

International framework

After the September 11 attacks in 2001, the UN Security Council adopted Resolution 1373, obliging all states to criminalise terrorist financing and improve cooperation. The United Nations later adopted a Global Counter-Terrorism Strategy, and bodies such as Interpol and FATF coordinate policing and financial measures.

Criticism and debate

Counterterrorism measures — surveillance powers, detention without trial, drone strikes, and watchlists — are frequently debated for their effects on civil liberties and their strategic effectiveness. Scholars also examine whether heavy-handed responses can fuel the grievances that drive recruitment.
