Sweet gale

Sweet gale (Myrica gale), also called bog-myrtle, is a species of flowering shrub in the family Myricaceae, native to northern and western Europe and parts of northern North America. It grows in bogs, fens and wet heaths.

Description

It is a deciduous shrub growing to about 1–2 metres, with spirally arranged, fragrant leaves and separate male and female catkins on different plants. The resinous leaves and fruit have a sweet, balsamic scent.

Uses

Sweet gale was traditionally used to flavour beer before hops became standard, as an ingredient of gruit, and as an insect repellent and moth deterrent. It is still used in some Scandinavian snaps and craft brewing, and symbolically in Scottish clan badges of Clan Campbell.
