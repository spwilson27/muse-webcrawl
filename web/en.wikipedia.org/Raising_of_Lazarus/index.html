Raising of Lazarus

The Raising of Lazarus, a long-favoured subject of Christian art.

The raising of Lazarus is a miracle of Jesus recounted in chapter 11 of the Gospel of John. Lazarus of Bethany, the brother of Mary and Martha, had been dead and entombed for four days when Jesus arrived. After declaring "I am the resurrection and the life" and weeping at the tomb (the shortest verse in the English Bible: "Jesus wept"), Jesus called "Lazarus, come out", and Lazarus emerged still bound in burial cloths. In John's Gospel the miracle is the last and greatest of the "signs" before the Passion, and it prompts the chief priests' plot against Jesus — and, in the following chapter, against Lazarus too.

Traditions and interpretation

The event is commemorated in Eastern Christianity on Lazarus Saturday, the day before Palm Sunday. Tradition holds that Lazarus later lived in Cyprus, where he is venerated as a saint and first bishop of Kition; the Church of Saint Lazarus in Larnaca is built over his reputed second tomb. Theologians read the story as a foreshadowing of Jesus's own resurrection and a demonstration of his divinity.

In art

The subject is one of the most frequently depicted episodes in Christian art, treated by Giotto, Duccio, Caravaggio, Rembrandt and Van Gogh, among many others; Sebastiano del Piombo's version was painted in competition with Raphael's Transfiguration.
