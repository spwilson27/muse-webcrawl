Pagani Automobili S.p.A. is an Italian manufacturer of high-performance sports cars and carbon-fibre components, based in San Cesario sul Panaro, near Modena, Italy. The company was founded in 1992 by Argentine-Italian engineer Horacio Pagani, who had previously managed the composites department at Lamborghini.

History

Horacio Pagani, who had worked on projects including the Lamborghini Countach Evoluzione — an early experiment in carbon-fibre construction — left Lamborghini in 1991 after disagreements over the company's investment in composite technology. With encouragement from racing driver Juan Manuel Fangio, he founded his own company, initially called Pagani Composite Research, and began designing his own mid-engined sports car.

Pagani's first production car, the Zonda C12, debuted at the 1999 Geneva Motor Show. Powered by a Mercedes-AMG V12 engine, the Zonda was built in numerous limited editions over more than a decade. Its successor, the Huayra, was introduced in 2011 with a twin-turbocharged AMG V12 and active aerodynamics. In 2022 Pagani unveiled its third model, the Utopia, again powered by a twin-turbo AMG V12 and offered with a manual gearbox.

Philosophy and technology

Pagani cars are noted for their extensive use of advanced materials, including Carbotitanium, a proprietary composite combining carbon fibre with titanium, first used on the Zonda R. Production volumes are extremely small — typically a few dozen cars per year — and each car is substantially hand-built and customised to its buyer. The company emphasises the union of art and engineering, a theme founder Horacio Pagani traces to the work of Leonardo da Vinci.

Models

  • Zonda (1999–2019): the original model line, with many one-off and limited editions including the Zonda R and Zonda Revolución.
  • Huayra (2011– ): twin-turbo V12 successor, including the Huayra BC and Huayra R track variants.
  • Utopia (2022– ): third-generation model with an emphasis on analogue driving feel.
