Riviera Maya

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

The Riviera Maya is a tourism district and resort corridor along the Caribbean coastline of the Mexican state of Quintana Roo, on the eastern Yucatán Peninsula. It stretches roughly 150 kilometres from Puerto Morelos, just south of Cancún, through Playa del Carmen and Tulum, to the Sian Ka'an Biosphere Reserve.

Attractions

The region takes its name from the Maya sites along the coast, above all the cliff-top fortress city of Tulum and the inland pyramid centre of Coba. Its beaches face the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef, the largest coral reef system in the northern hemisphere, which supports diving and snorkelling around Cozumel and Puerto Morelos. Inland, the limestone plateau is pierced by thousands of cenotes — natural sinkholes in the underground river system sacred to the Maya — popular for swimming and cave diving. Large eco-parks such as Xcaret and Xel-Há combine these features for visitors.

Development

Until the 1970s the coast was a remote region of fishing villages, coconut plantations, and chicle camps. The Mexican government's development of Cancún from 1970 transformed the area; the "Riviera Maya" brand was adopted in 1999 to market the corridor south of Cancún, and Playa del Carmen grew from a small ferry town into one of Mexico's fastest-growing cities. Tourism is now the dominant economy, bringing environmental pressures including groundwater pollution, sargassum seaweed influxes, and reef damage, alongside archaeological discoveries from construction of the Tren Maya railway.
