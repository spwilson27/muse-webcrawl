USS Constellation (1797)

USS Constellation was a nominally rated 38-gun wooden-hulled, three-masted frigate of the United States Navy, named by President George Washington after the flag of the United States. She was one of the original six frigates authorized by the Naval Act of 1794, designed by naval architect Joshua Humphreys, built by shipwright David Stodder at Baltimore, Maryland, and launched on 7 September 1797.

Service

Under Captain Thomas Truxtun, Constellation won the young Navy's first great victories during the Quasi-War with France: on 9 February 1799 she captured the French frigate L'Insurgente, the first capture of an enemy warship by a U.S. Navy vessel, and on 1–2 February 1800 she fought and badly mauled the frigate La Vengeance. In the First Barbary War she served in the Mediterranean squadron against Tripoli. During the War of 1812 she was blockaded in Norfolk and did not get to sea. She continued in service for decades, including a circumnavigation in the 1840s and duty as flagship of the East India Squadron, before being broken up at Gosport Navy Yard in 1853.

A long-running question surrounds the sloop-of-war USS Constellation built at Gosport in 1854 (now a museum ship in Baltimore): Navy records long treated her as a rebuild of the 1797 frigate, but modern scholarship, using dendrochronology and design evidence, concludes she was built as an essentially new vessel that reused the name and possibly some materials.
