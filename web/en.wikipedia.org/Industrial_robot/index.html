An industrial robot is a robot system used for manufacturing. The International Organization for Standardization defines it as an automatically controlled, reprogrammable, multipurpose manipulator programmable in three or more axes, which can be fixed in place or mobile for use in industrial automation applications. Industrial robots are typically programmable by a teach pendant or offline software and perform tasks such as welding, painting, assembly, pick and place, packaging, and inspection.

History

The first industrial robot, the Unimate, was installed on a General Motors die-casting line in Trenton, New Jersey, in 1961; it had been invented by George Devol and developed with Joseph Engelberger's company Unimation. Hydraulic and later electric robots spread through the automotive industry in the 1970s, especially in Japan, and microprocessor controls enabled more precise articulated arms from manufacturers such as KUKA, ABB, Fanuc, and Yaskawa.

Types and components

Common kinematic types include articulated (jointed-arm) robots, SCARA robots for high-speed assembly, Cartesian (gantry) robots, delta robots for rapid picking, and cylindrical robots. A system typically comprises the manipulator arm, servo motors and gears, a controller, an end-effector (such as a gripper or welding torch), sensors, and safety equipment such as light curtains or fenced cells.

Collaborative robots

Since the 2010s, collaborative robots ("cobots") with force-limiting and sensing features have been designed to work safely alongside humans without fencing, opening automation to smaller firms. Adoption continues to be led by the automotive and electronics industries, with China the largest national market.

Safety

Industrial robots move with enough force to cause serious injury, and standards such as ISO 10218 govern cell design, safeguarding, and risk assessment.
