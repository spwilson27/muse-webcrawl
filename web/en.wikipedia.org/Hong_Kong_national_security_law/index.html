Hong Kong national security law

Hong Kong national security lawFull title: Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region
Enacted: 30 June 2020
Enacted by: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress

The Hong Kong national security law is a piece of national security legislation concerning Hong Kong, formally the Law of the People's Republic of China on Safeguarding National Security in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region. It was passed by the Standing Committee of China's National People's Congress on 30 June 2020 and promulgated in Hong Kong the same day, bypassing the local Legislative Council.

Contents

The law criminalises four categories of offences: secession, subversion, terrorist activities, and collusion with a foreign country or external elements, with maximum penalties of life imprisonment. It establishes a national security committee in Hong Kong, a dedicated police unit, and an Office for Safeguarding National Security run by the central government, whose personnel are not subject to Hong Kong jurisdiction. In certain serious cases, suspects may be tried in mainland China.

Context and reactions

The law followed the 2019–20 pro-democracy protests and an earlier failed attempt at local security legislation in 2003. Beijing and the Hong Kong government described it as necessary to restore stability. Critics — including many Western governments, which responded with sanctions and suspension of extradition treaties — argued it undermined the "one country, two systems" framework and civil liberties. The law has been applied in prosecutions of activists, politicians and media figures, including the mass arrest of pro-democracy primaries participants and the closure of the Apple Daily newspaper.
