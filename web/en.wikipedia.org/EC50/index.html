EC50

EC50 is a measure of the concentration of a drug, antibody, or toxicant which induces a response halfway between the baseline and maximum after a specified exposure time. It is commonly used as a measure of a drug's potency and is defined as the molar concentration of an agonist that produces 50% of the maximal possible effect.

Dose–response curves

The EC50 is read from a graded dose–response curve, usually plotted as response against the logarithm of concentration. A lower EC50 indicates a more potent compound, since less of it is needed to produce half of the maximal effect. The corresponding value for an antagonist or inhibitor is the IC50, the concentration producing 50% inhibition.

Determination

EC50 values are usually estimated by fitting a four-parameter logistic (Hill) equation to experimental data. The value depends on the assay system: receptor density, signal amplification, and exposure time can all shift the measured EC50, which is why it is an operational rather than purely molecular measure of potency, unlike the equilibrium dissociation constant Kd.

Related measures

Related quantities include the LD50 (median lethal dose), ED50 (median effective dose in a population), and the therapeutic index, which compares toxic and effective doses.
