Mochi

Mochi
Place of originJapan
Main ingredientsGlutinous rice (mochigome)
VariationsDaifuku, kagami mochi, kirimochi, warabimochi

Mochi (Japanese: 餅) is a Japanese rice cake made of mochigome, a short-grain japonica glutinous rice, pounded into a paste and moulded into shape. Traditionally it is made in a ceremony called mochitsuki, in which steamed rice is pounded with wooden mallets in a mortar; modern production also uses automated machines. Mochi is a staple of Japanese New Year celebrations and is eaten year-round in many forms.

Forms and uses

Plain mochi may be grilled, boiled in soups such as zōni (the New Year soup), or coated with kinako (roasted soybean flour). Sweet filled forms include daifuku, mochi wrapped around fillings such as sweet red bean paste, and ichigo daifuku containing a strawberry. Kagami mochi — two stacked round cakes topped with a bitter orange — is a traditional New Year decoration. Mochi is also used in sweets such as mochi ice cream, and similar glutinous rice cakes exist across East and Southeast Asia.

Safety

Because of its sticky, elastic texture, mochi can obstruct the airway if not chewed thoroughly, and Japanese authorities issue annual warnings, particularly for elderly people, after choking incidents during the New Year season.
