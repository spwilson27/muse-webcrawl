EraMechanization from 19th century onward

Agricultural machinery relates to the mechanical structures and devices used in farming and other agriculture. Mechanization transformed farming during the Industrial Revolution and the 20th century, vastly increasing productivity per worker and enabling large-scale commercial agriculture.

Main types

The tractor is the central machine of modern farming, providing power for implements such as plows, harrows, seed drills, and balers. The combine harvester performs reaping, threshing, and winnowing of grain crops in a single operation. Other major classes include planters, sprayers, irrigation equipment, milking machines, and forage harvesters.

History

Key milestones included Jethro Tull's seed drill (1701), mechanical reapers in the 1830s, steam traction engines in the later 19th century, and the mass-produced gasoline tractor in the early 20th century. Since the late 20th century, agricultural machinery has increasingly incorporated electronics, GPS guidance, and automation — the basis of precision agriculture.
