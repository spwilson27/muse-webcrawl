Jack Bruce

John Symon Asher Bruce (1943 – 2014) was a Scottish musician, singer, songwriter and composer, best known as the bassist and lead vocalist of the rock supergroup Cream.

Born14 May 1943, Bishopbriggs, Scotland
Died25 October 2014, Suffolk, England
GenresBlues rock, jazz fusion, hard rock
InstrumentsBass guitar, vocals, piano, cello

Career

Trained as a classical cellist, Bruce played in jazz and blues bands including the Graham Bond Organisation and John Mayall's Bluesbreakers. In 1966 he formed Cream with Eric Clapton and Ginger Baker; the trio's improvisational blues rock produced songs such as "Sunshine of Your Love", "White Room" and "Crossroads", many co-written with lyricist Pete Brown.

Solo work

After Cream disbanded in 1968, Bruce released acclaimed solo albums including Songs for a Tailor (1969) and worked in jazz fusion with artists such as Tony Williams and John McLaughlin. He reunited with Cream for concerts in 2005 and continued recording until shortly before his death from liver disease in 2014.
