National Guard (United States)

The National Guard is a state-based military force of the United States that serves as a reserve component of the U.S. Army and U.S. Air Force. It consists of the Army National Guard and the Air National Guard, with units in all 50 states, the District of Columbia, and the territories of Guam, Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands.

Dual status

Guard members are under the command of their state's governor when serving in state status — for example, responding to natural disasters or civil disturbances — but can be federalised by the President to serve on active duty with the armed forces. This dual state-federal character distinguishes the Guard from other reserve components.

History

The Guard traces its lineage to colonial militias, with its birthday celebrated on 13 December 1636, when the Massachusetts Bay Colony organised militia regiments. The Militia Act of 1903 and subsequent legislation integrated the state militias into the national military structure. Since the September 11 attacks, Guard units have been deployed extensively in Iraq and Afghanistan as well as in domestic emergencies at home.
