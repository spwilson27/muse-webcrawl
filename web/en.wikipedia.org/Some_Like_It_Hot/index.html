Some Like It Hot is a 1959 American crime comedy film produced and directed by Billy Wilder, starring Marilyn Monroe, Tony Curtis, and Jack Lemmon. Set in 1929 during Prohibition, it follows two male musicians (Curtis and Lemmon) who witness the Saint Valentine's Day Massacre and flee Chicago disguised as women, joining an all-female band travelling to Florida, where both fall for the band's singer, Sugar Kane (Monroe).

Plot

Saxophonist Joe and bassist Jerry, on the run from mobster "Spats" Colombo, become "Josephine" and "Daphne" in Sweet Sue's orchestra. In Florida, Joe adopts a second disguise as a millionaire to woo Sugar, while Jerry, still as Daphne, is pursued by the genuinely wealthy Osgood Fielding III. The film culminates in a chase at the resort hotel and Osgood's immortal closing line, after Jerry finally reveals he is a man: "Well, nobody's perfect."

Reception and legacy

The film was a major critical and commercial success despite being made without Production Code approval. It won the Academy Award for Best Costume Design (black-and-white) and received five other nominations, including Best Actor for Lemmon. In 2000 the American Film Institute named it the greatest American comedy film of all time in its "100 Years...100 Laughs" list, and it was selected for preservation in the U.S. National Film Registry. A stage musical adaptation opened on Broadway in 2022.
