Perkins Brailler

The Perkins Brailler is a "braille typewriter" with a key corresponding to each of the six dots of the braille code, a space key, a backspace key, and a line space key. It was developed by David Abraham and first produced in 1951 by the Howe Press of Perkins School for the Blind in Massachusetts.

Design

Like a manual typewriter, it has a carriage that advances as the keys are pressed, and it embosses Braille onto heavy paper fed through the machine. Because Braille cells are fixed width, the carriage stops with a bell or mechanical halt at the right margin. The machine is entirely mechanical, durable and repairable, and remains in worldwide use despite the advent of electronic Braille note-takers.

Variants

An electric model and quieter next-generation models have been produced. The SMART Brailler, released in the 2010s, adds a digital display and audio feedback so sighted teachers and parents can follow what a student is writing. Perkins also runs repair and distribution programmes supplying Braillers to blind people in developing countries.
