Characterisation

Characterisation (or characterization) is the representation of characters in narrative and dramatic works of art. It encompasses the methods by which an author conveys a character's personality, appearance, motives and development to the audience, and the resulting impression the audience forms.

Methods

Characterisation is usually divided into direct (explicit) characterisation, in which the narrator or another character tells the audience what a character is like, and indirect characterisation, in which traits are shown through the character's actions, speech, thoughts, appearance and interactions with others — the "show, don't tell" principle.

Types of character

E. M. Forster famously distinguished flat characters, built around a single idea or quality, from round characters, who are complex and capable of surprising the reader convincingly. Characters may also be described as static or dynamic depending on whether they change over the course of the work (see character arc).
