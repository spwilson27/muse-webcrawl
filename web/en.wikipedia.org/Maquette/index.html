Maquette

Type: Scale model / sculptor's sketch
Materials: Clay, wax, plaster, plasticine
Related terms: Bozzetto, modello

A maquette (French for "scale model") is a small-scale preliminary model or sketch made by a sculptor for a work that will be executed at a larger size. Maquettes allow the artist to test composition, pose and proportion cheaply before committing to costly materials and enlargement.

Use in sculpture

Sculptors model maquettes in clay, wax, plasticine or plaster, sometimes in several versions. Once approved — historically, commissions for public monuments were often awarded on the basis of competition maquettes — the design is enlarged by pointing machines, pantographs or digital scanning and reproduced in stone or bronze. Because they capture the artist's first conception, maquettes are collected and exhibited in their own right; the Italian term bozzetto is used for similar sketch-models, especially in painting and Baroque sculpture.

Other fields

The word is also used for scale models in architecture, product and film design, and in animation, where sculpted character maquettes serve as three-dimensional reference for animators and modellers.
