Born rigidity

Born rigidity is a concept in special relativity introduced by the German physicist Max Born in 1909. It describes a condition under which a body can be accelerated or rotated without undergoing deformation as measured in its own instantaneous rest frame.

Definition

A motion of an extended body is said to be Born rigid if the distance between any two neighboring points of the body, measured in the body's instantaneous rest frame, remains constant throughout the motion. In mathematical terms, the expansion and shear of the body's world-line congruence must both vanish.

Consequences

Born rigidity is extremely restrictive. In particular, a body cannot be spun up from rest to a state of uniform rotation while remaining Born rigid, because different parts of the body would need to accelerate differently. This is related to the famous Ehrenfest paradox concerning the geometry of a rotating disc: Lorentz contraction acts along the circumference but not along the radius, so a Born-rigid disc cannot be set into rotation without stresses arising.

Herglotz–Noether theorem

The Herglotz–Noether theorem shows that in flat spacetime, a Born-rigid body has only three degrees of freedom: its motion is entirely determined by the motion of a single one of its points. This severely limits the possible rigid motions, and implies that perfectly rigid bodies cannot exist in relativity in the classical sense, since a rigid body would transmit signals instantaneously, violating causality.
