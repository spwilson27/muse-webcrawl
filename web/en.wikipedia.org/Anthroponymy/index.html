Anthroponymy

Anthroponymy (or anthroponomastics) is the study of the names of human beings. It is a branch of onomastics, the study of proper names. The words "anthroponymy" and "anthroponym" derive from the Greek ánthrōpos (human being) and ónoma (name).

Scope

Anthroponymy covers given names, surnames, nicknames, patronymics, matronymics, teknonyms, regnal names, pen names and stage names, among others. The sub-areas are: anthroponymy of given names; anthroponymy of surnames (family names); anthroponymy of nicknames; and ethnic anthroponymy, the study of a specific ethnic group's naming practices.

History and conventions

Naming conventions vary enormously across cultures: many European surnames originated in the Middle Ages from occupations, places or a father's name, while Icelandic names remain largely patronymic, Chinese names place the family name first, and many cultures use clan or caste names. Scholars use anthroponymic evidence in linguistics, history, genealogy and demography — for example, tracking migration through the distribution of surnames.
