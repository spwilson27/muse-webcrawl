The Me Too movement (often written #MeToo) is a social movement against sexual harassment and sexual assault that encourages survivors to speak publicly about their experiences. The phrase "Me Too" was coined in 2006 by activist Tarana Burke, who founded a programme supporting survivors, particularly young women of colour.

Viral spread in 2017

In October 2017, following investigative reports of sexual abuse allegations against film producer Harvey Weinstein, the actress Alyssa Milano encouraged survivors to post "me too" on social media. The hashtag was used millions of times within days, and accusations followed against powerful figures in entertainment, politics, media, sport and academia in many countries. Weinstein was later convicted of sex crimes; his conviction in New York was overturned on appeal in 2024, while a California conviction stood.

Effects

The movement led to workplace-policy changes, new legislation restricting non-disclosure agreements in several jurisdictions, the founding of the Time's Up Legal Defense Fund, and wider public debate about consent, power and due process. Related movements appeared internationally, including #BalanceTonPorc in France and campaigns in China, India, South Korea and elsewhere, sometimes facing official suppression. Time magazine named "The Silence Breakers" its Person of the Year for 2017.

Criticism and debate

Commentators have debated the movement's scope, the distinction between harassment and assault, standards of evidence in public accusations, and whether its benefits reached low-wage and marginalised workers as much as celebrities.
