Wombat

Wombats are short-legged, muscular quadrupedal marsupials native to Australia. They are about 1 metre (3 ft 3 in) in length with small, stubby tails and weigh between 20 and 35 kg (44 and 77 lb). Three species survive: the common wombat, the northern hairy-nosed wombat, and the southern hairy-nosed wombat.

KingdomAnimalia
OrderDiprotodontia
FamilyVombatidae
GeneraVombatus, Lasiorhinus

Characteristics

Wombats dig extensive burrow systems with their rodent-like front teeth and powerful claws. Unusually for a marsupial, the female's pouch opens backwards, protecting the young from soil while digging. Wombats are herbivorous, grazing on grasses, sedges, and roots, and are mostly nocturnal. Their droppings are characteristically cube-shaped.

Ecology and behaviour

Wombats are generally solitary and territorial, defending burrow territories by scent-marking and, if threatened, charging intruders. Their toughened backsides, largely made of cartilage, serve as a defence against predators in burrows.

Conservation

The common wombat is widespread, while the northern hairy-nosed wombat is critically endangered, surviving in only a few protected locations in Queensland.
