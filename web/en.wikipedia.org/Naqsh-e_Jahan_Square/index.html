Naqsh-e Jahan Square

Naqsh-e Jahan Square (Persian: میدان نقش جهان, Maidān-e Naqsh-e Jahān, "Image of the World Square"), officially Imam Square, is a vast public square at the centre of Isfahan, Iran. Constructed between 1598 and 1629 under Shah Abbas I of the Safavid dynasty, it is one of the largest city squares in the world and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Description

The square measures about 560 metres long by 160 metres wide and is surrounded by two-storey arcaded bazaars. At its ends and sides stand its great monuments: the Shah Mosque (now Imam Mosque) on the south, famed for its blue tilework and acoustics; the Sheikh Lotfollah Mosque, a royal chapel with a celebrated peacock dome, on the east; the Ali Qapu Palace on the west, from whose terrace the Shah watched polo matches played on the square; and the Qeysarieh gate to the Isfahan Grand Bazaar on the north.

History

When Abbas moved his capital to Isfahan in 1598, the square was laid out as the ceremonial and commercial heart of the new imperial city, hosting polo games, military parades, markets and public festivals. It remains a popular gathering place, ringed by shops selling Isfahan's handicrafts.
