Acceleration

In mechanics, acceleration is the rate of change of the velocity of an object with respect to time. It is a vector quantity, measured in the SI unit metre per second squared (m/s²). An object accelerates whenever it speeds up, slows down (deceleration), or changes direction, as in uniform circular motion, where the centripetal acceleration points toward the centre of the circle.

Newton's second law

By Newton's second law of motion, the net force on an object equals its mass multiplied by its acceleration (F = ma). Acceleration is thus the kinematical link between forces and motion: a constant force produces a constant acceleration in an inertial frame.

Average and instantaneous acceleration

Average acceleration over a time interval is the change in velocity divided by the interval; instantaneous acceleration is the limit as the interval approaches zero, i.e. the derivative of velocity with respect to time (and the second derivative of position). The derivative of acceleration is known as jerk.

Special cases

Near Earth's surface, freely falling bodies accelerate downward at approximately 9.81 m/s² — the acceleration due to gravity, denoted g. In relativity, proper acceleration is the acceleration an object experiences relative to free fall, measurable by an accelerometer; coordinate acceleration depends on the observer.
