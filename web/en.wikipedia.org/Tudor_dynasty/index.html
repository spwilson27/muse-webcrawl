The Tudor dynasty was a royal house of Welsh origin that held the English throne from 1485 until 1603. Its five monarchs — Henry VII, Henry VIII, Edward VI, Mary I and Elizabeth I — transformed England from a peripheral medieval kingdom into a major European power and a naval force of growing importance.

Origins

The Tudors descended from the Welsh Tudor family of Penmynydd on Anglesey. Henry Tudor, Earl of Richmond, defeated Richard III at the Battle of Bosworth Field in 1485, ending the Wars of the Roses, and married Elizabeth of York to unite the rival Lancastrian and Yorkist claims.

Reign and achievements

Henry VII restored royal finances and authority. His son Henry VIII broke with the Roman Catholic Church, initiating the English Reformation and dissolving the monasteries. Edward VI advanced Protestant reform, Mary I attempted a Catholic restoration, and Elizabeth I presided over a celebrated cultural renaissance, the defeat of the Spanish Armada in 1588, and the beginnings of English overseas colonisation.

End of the dynasty

Elizabeth I died childless in 1603, and the crown passed to James VI of Scotland, beginning the Stuart period. The Tudor era remains one of the most studied and dramatised periods in English history.
