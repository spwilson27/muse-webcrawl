Texas Revolution

The Alamo, besieged in February–March 1836

The Texas Revolution (2 October 1835 – 21 April 1836) was a rebellion of colonists from the United States and Tejanos (Texas Mexicans) against the centralist government of Mexico. It resulted in the establishment of the independent Republic of Texas.

Background

Mexico had encouraged American immigration to sparsely populated Texas in the 1820s, but clashes over slavery, customs duties, immigration restrictions, and the centralizing policies of President Antonio López de Santa Anna—who abrogated the federal Constitution of 1824—steadily inflamed tensions. Fighting began at the Battle of Gonzales on 2 October 1835, sparked by a dispute over a small cannon, and Texians soon seized San Antonio de Béxar.

Major events

The Texas Declaration of Independence was adopted at Washington-on-the-Brazos on 2 March 1836. Days later the Alamo fell (6 March) after a 13-day siege, its defenders killed to a man; later that month, several hundred Texian prisoners were executed in the Goliad massacre. General Sam Houston's army then destroyed Santa Anna's force in a surprise attack at the Battle of San Jacinto on 21 April 1836, capturing Santa Anna and effectively ending the war. Under the Treaties of Velasco, Mexico's government repudiated Santa Anna's concessions, but Texas remained de facto independent until U.S. annexation in 1845.
