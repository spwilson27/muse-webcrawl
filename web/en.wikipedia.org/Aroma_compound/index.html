Aroma compound

An aroma compound, also known as an odorant, aroma, fragrance or flavour, is a chemical compound that has a smell or odour. For a chemical compound to have a smell, it must be sufficiently volatile to be transported to the olfactory system in the upper part of the nose.

Odorant receptors

Odorants bind to specific receptors on olfactory sensory neurons. Humans have roughly 400 functional types of odorant receptors, and the pattern of activation across these receptors allows the brain to distinguish a very large number of different smells.

Aroma compounds by source

Many aroma compounds occur naturally in foods and plants. Examples include vanillin (vanilla), benzaldehyde (bitter almond), citral (lemon), eugenol (cloves) and menthol (mint). Esters such as isoamyl acetate give characteristic fruity smells. The food and perfume industries synthesise many of these compounds at scale.

Measurement

The concentration of an odorant is often compared with its odour threshold, the lowest concentration detectable by the human nose. Gas chromatography–olfactometry is used to identify which compounds in a mixture are responsible for its aroma.
