Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor

Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors
Drug classEnzyme inhibitors; diuretics; antiglaucoma agents
ExamplesAcetazolamide, methazolamide, dorzolamide, brinzolamide
TargetCarbonic anhydrase

Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors are a class of pharmaceuticals that suppress the activity of carbonic anhydrase, the enzyme that catalyses the reversible hydration of carbon dioxide to bicarbonate. By blocking this enzyme in the kidney, eye, and other tissues, they reduce bicarbonate reabsorption and fluid secretion.

Uses

Systemic agents such as acetazolamide are used for altitude sickness prophylaxis, some forms of epilepsy, idiopathic intracranial hypertension, and as weak diuretics. Topical agents such as dorzolamide and brinzolamide lower intraocular pressure in glaucoma by reducing aqueous humour production.

Side effects

Common adverse effects include tingling in the extremities, altered taste (especially for carbonated drinks), increased urination, fatigue, and metabolic acidosis; kidney stones are a recognised risk with long-term use.
